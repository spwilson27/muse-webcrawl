Edward C. Prescott

Edward C. Prescott
Born: 26 December 1940, Glens Falls, New York
Died: 6 November 2022
Field: Macroeconomics
Award: Nobel Prize in Economics (2004)

Edward Christian Prescott (1940–2022) was an American economist who shared the 2004 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Finn E. Kydland for their contributions to dynamic macroeconomics: the time consistency of economic policy and the driving forces behind business cycles.

Contributions

With Kydland, Prescott showed in 1977 how discretionary policy can be time-inconsistent, arguing for rules over discretion, and in 1982 developed real business cycle theory, modelling fluctuations as responses to real (technology) shocks. Their work reshaped central banking and macroeconomic modelling. The Hodrick–Prescott filter, widely used to extract trends, also bears his name.

Career

Prescott taught at the University of Pennsylvania, Carnegie Mellon, the University of Minnesota and Arizona State University, and advised the Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis. See also Real business cycle theory and Finn Kydland.
