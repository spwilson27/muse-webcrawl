Steve Wozniak

Stephen Gary Wozniak (born August 11, 1950), known mononymously as Woz, is an American technology entrepreneur, electronics engineer, computer programmer, and philanthropist. In 1976, he co-founded Apple Computer (now Apple Inc.) with Steve Jobs and Ronald Wayne. Through his work at Apple in the 1970s and 1980s, he is widely recognised as one of the most prominent pioneers of the personal computer revolution.

Early life

Wozniak was born in San Jose, California. His father was an engineer at Lockheed, and Wozniak developed an early fascination with electronics, building his own amateur radio equipment and winning science fairs. He attended Homestead High School in Cupertino. In 1971, with his friend Bill Fernandez, he built his first computer, the "Cream Soda Computer". The same year he met Steve Jobs, and the two collaborated on "blue boxes" — devices that exploited telephone network tones to make free long-distance calls.

Apple

While working at Hewlett-Packard designing calculators, Wozniak designed the Apple I in 1975–76, a single-board computer that he demonstrated at the Homebrew Computer Club. Apple Computer was founded on April 1, 1976. Wozniak then designed the Apple II (1977), one of the first highly successful mass-produced microcomputers, notable for its colour graphics, BASIC in ROM, and elegant, low-chip-count engineering. He also designed the Disk II floppy drive, dramatically improving the machine's storage.

Plane crash and return

In February 1981 Wozniak was injured when his private plane crashed during takeoff, suffering temporary amnesia. He left Apple to complete his computer engineering degree at UC Berkeley under the pseudonym "Rocky Raccoon Clark", then returned to Apple in 1983. He left full-time employment in 1985 but remains on the Apple payroll as an employee to this day, and he retained a close public association with the company he co-founded.

Later ventures and philanthropy

After Apple, Wozniak founded CL 9 (universal remote controls), Wheels of Zeus (wireless technology), Acquicor Technology, and the education company Privateer Space and Woz U. He has funded technology programmes in Los Gatos schools, supported museums, and is a prominent advocate for engineering education and the "right to repair". He was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2000 and received the National Medal of Technology in 1985 (jointly with Jobs).
