Rectangle

Image placeholder: a rectangle with its diagonals.

In Euclidean plane geometry, a rectangle is a quadrilateral with four right angles. It can also be defined as an equiangular quadrilateral, since equiangular means that all of its angles are equal (360°/4 = 90°), or as a parallelogram containing a right angle. A rectangle with four sides of equal length is a square. The term "oblong" is sometimes used for a non-square rectangle.

Characterizations

A convex quadrilateral is a rectangle if and only if it is any one of the following: a parallelogram with at least one right angle; a parallelogram with diagonals of equal length; an equiangular quadrilateral; or a quadrilateral with four right angles. Opposite sides of a rectangle are equal and parallel, and its diagonals are equal in length and bisect each other.

Formulas

If a rectangle has length l and width w, its area is A = lw, its perimeter is P = 2(l + w), and each diagonal has length d = √(l² + w²), by the Pythagorean theorem. A rectangle has an axis of symmetry through the midpoints of each pair of opposite sides, and rotational symmetry of order 2.

Special cases and related figures

The square is the special case in which all sides are equal. A rectangle whose side lengths are in the golden ratio is called a golden rectangle. Rectangles tile the plane easily and appear throughout architecture, design and everyday objects.
