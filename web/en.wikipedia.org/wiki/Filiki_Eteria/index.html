Filiki Eteria

The Filiki Eteria (Greek: Φιλική Εταιρεία, "Society of Friends") was a secret organization founded in 1814 in Odessa, in the Russian Empire, by Greek merchants and intellectuals. Its purpose was to overthrow Ottoman rule over Greece and establish an independent Greek state. The society recruited members throughout the Greek diaspora and the Balkans using rituals modelled on Freemasonry and the Carbonari, and its uprising in 1821 launched the Greek War of Independence.

Foundation and structure

The society was founded by Nikolaos Skoufas, Athanasios Tsakalov and Emmanuil Xanthos, three merchants of modest means. Members were initiated through elaborate rites and bound by a "Great Oath" of secrecy and dedication to the nation. Authority was attributed to an unseen supreme leadership, the "Invisible Authority", which lent the society an aura of powerful backing; in 1818 the headquarters moved to Constantinople, and the society spread rapidly among merchants, clergy, Phanariots and military men.

Leadership of Ypsilantis

After the statesman Ioannis Kapodistrias declined the leadership, it was accepted in April 1820 by Alexander Ypsilantis, a Greek officer in Russian service and aide-de-camp to the Tsar. Ypsilantis accelerated preparations for a general revolt.

The revolt of 1821

In February–March 1821 Ypsilantis crossed the Pruth into Moldavia, hoping to spark a Balkan-wide uprising; his campaign ended in defeat at Drăgășani in June. Meanwhile, in March 1821, the revolt broke out in the Peloponnese, where Filiki Eteria networks and local primates and klephts raised the banner of independence — the beginning of the Greek War of Independence, which the society itself did not survive as an organisation.
