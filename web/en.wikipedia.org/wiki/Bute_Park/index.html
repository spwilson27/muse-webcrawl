Bute Park

Placeholder image: the River Taff running through Bute Park.

Bute Park (Welsh: Parc Bute) is a major park in the city centre of Cardiff, capital of Wales. Extending to some 130 acres (53 hectares), it lies along the west bank of the River Taff adjacent to Cardiff Castle and forms the city's "green lung", one of the largest urban parks in the United Kingdom.

LocationCardiff, Wales
Area~130 acres (53 ha)
Created1949 (opened as public park)

History

The parkland was originally the grounds of Cardiff Castle, landscaped in the late 18th and early 19th centuries for the Marquesses of Bute. In 1947 the 5th Marquess of Bute gave the castle and its grounds to the people of Cardiff, and the park was opened to the public in 1949. It is now a Grade I listed historic park, containing a notable arboretum, champion trees and the remains of Blackfriars friary.

Features

Bute Park includes formal gardens, an arboretum with one of the largest collections of champion trees in Britain, playing fields, a visitor centre and cafés. The Taff Trail, a long-distance walking and cycling route, runs through the park. Together with the adjoining Pontcanna Fields and Sophia Gardens, it forms a continuous green corridor along the river.
