Superficial fibular nerve

The superficial fibular nerve (also called the superficial peroneal nerve) is a mixed nerve of the leg that provides motor innervation to the muscles of the lateral compartment of the leg and sensory innervation to the skin of the lower leg and most of the dorsum of the foot. It is one of the two terminal branches of the common fibular (peroneal) nerve, the other being the deep fibular nerve.

Course

The nerve arises at the neck of the fibula, where the common fibular nerve divides. It descends through the lateral compartment of the leg between the fibularis (peroneus) longus and brevis muscles, supplying both. In the lower third of the leg it pierces the deep fascia and becomes subcutaneous, dividing into the medial dorsal cutaneous nerve and the intermediate dorsal cutaneous nerve.

Function

Motor branches supply fibularis longus and fibularis brevis, the principal evertors of the foot. The terminal cutaneous branches supply sensation to the lower anterior leg and the dorsum of the foot and toes, except for the first web space (supplied by the deep fibular nerve) and the lateral border of the foot (supplied by the sural nerve).

Clinical significance

The nerve may be injured by fibular neck fractures, compartment syndrome of the lateral leg, ankle sprains, or surgical procedures around the ankle where its superficial course makes it vulnerable. Entrapment where it exits the fascia can cause pain and paraesthesia over the dorsum of the foot. Injury produces weakness of foot eversion and sensory loss over much of the dorsum of the foot, but foot drop — the hallmark of deep or common fibular nerve injury — does not occur from an isolated superficial fibular lesion.
