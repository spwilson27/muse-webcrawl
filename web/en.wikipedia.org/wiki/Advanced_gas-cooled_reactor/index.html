Advanced Gas-cooled Reactor

CountryUnited Kingdom
CoolantCarbon dioxide

The advanced gas-cooled reactor (AGR) is a type of nuclear reactor developed in the United Kingdom. It is the second generation of British gas-cooled reactors, succeeding the Magnox design, and uses graphite as the neutron moderator and carbon dioxide as the coolant. Unlike Magnox reactors, AGRs use slightly enriched uranium dioxide fuel clad in stainless steel, allowing much higher gas outlet temperatures (around 650 °C) and thermal efficiencies of about 40%.

Design

The AGR core consists of graphite bricks pierced by channels containing fuel assemblies. Carbon dioxide coolant is circulated through the core and transfers heat to steam generators housed inside the same prestressed concrete pressure vessel. The high operating temperature improves efficiency but, over decades of operation, has led to graphite cracking and other ageing issues that limit reactor lifetimes.

Stations

Fourteen AGRs were built at seven stations in the United Kingdom: Hinkley Point B, Hunterston B, Hartlepool, Heysham 1 and 2, Dungeness B, and Torness. Most entered service in the 1970s–1980s. The design was not exported, and the UK subsequently adopted pressurized water reactors. From the 2020s onward the AGR fleet has been progressively shut down for defuelling, beginning with Hunterston B and Hinkley Point B.
