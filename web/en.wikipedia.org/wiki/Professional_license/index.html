Professional license

A professional license is permission granted by a competent authority, typically a government agency or professional body, allowing the holder to practise a regulated profession. Licensing is a form of occupational regulation intended to protect the public by ensuring practitioners meet minimum standards of education, examination, and conduct.

Purpose and process

Professions commonly subject to licensing include medicine, law, engineering, architecture, accountancy, nursing, teaching, and many skilled trades. Obtaining a license usually requires completing accredited education, passing examinations, and demonstrating good character. Licenses often must be renewed periodically, sometimes conditional on continuing professional development.

Criticism

Economists and policy analysts have debated licensing: supporters argue it assures quality and safety, while critics contend it can restrict competition, raise prices, and create barriers to entry disproportionate to any public benefit. The proportion of workers requiring a license has grown substantially in many countries over the past century.
