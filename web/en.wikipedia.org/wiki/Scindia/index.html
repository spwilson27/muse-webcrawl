The Scindia dynasty (also spelled Sindia or Shinde) is a Maratha royal family that ruled the princely state of Gwalior in central India from the 18th century until Indian independence. Rising as military commanders in the Maratha Empire, the Scindias became one of its most powerful houses, controlling extensive territories in the Malwa region and beyond.

Origins and rise

The founder of the dynasty's fortunes was Ranoji Scindia (died 1745), a commander under the Peshwa Baji Rao I who established himself at Ujjain. His son Mahadji Scindia (1730–1794) greatly extended the family's power, building a modernised army trained by the French officer Benoît de Boigne and becoming the dominant power in northern India after the Third Battle of Panipat (1761); he effectively restored Mughal emperor Shah Alam II to Delhi under Maratha protection. Daulat Rao Scindia fought the British in the Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803–1805), after which the family lost much territory but retained Gwalior.

Gwalior State

During the Indian Rebellion of 1857, Maharaja Jayajirao Scindia remained loyal to the British, while his forces were briefly seized by rebels under Tatya Tope and the Rani of Jhansi. Gwalior became a 21-gun salute state, among the most prestigious princely states of British India. The family seat was the Jai Vilas Palace in Gwalior, completed in 1874.

After independence

Jiwajirao Scindia acceded to India in 1948. His son Madhavrao Scindia became a prominent Congress politician and Union minister, and his grandson Jyotiraditya Scindia has served as a Union minister and Rajya Sabha member, switching from the Indian National Congress to the Bharatiya Janata Party in 2020. Vijaya Raje Scindia, the Rajmata of Gwalior, was a leading figure of the Bharatiya Jana Sangh and BJP.
