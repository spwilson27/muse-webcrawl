Bachata (dance)

OriginDominican Republic

Related stylesTraditional, modern, sensual bachata

Bachata is a social dance originating in the Dominican Republic, danced to bachata music. It is danced in two-person pairs, typically in a side-to-side basic step, and has become popular worldwide in Latin dance scenes alongside salsa and merengue. In 2019, UNESCO inscribed the music and dance of Dominican bachata on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Basic steps

The basic bachata step is danced in 4/4 time over eight counts: three steps to one side followed by a tap or hip pop on the fourth count, then three steps back to the other side with another tap on the eighth count. Dancers may move in a square pattern or side to side, adding turns, dips, and syncopations as they advance.

Styles

Traditional (Dominican) bachata emphasizes intricate footwork, free-style interpretation, and a close embrace, reflecting the dance as practiced in the Dominican Republic. Modern bachata, developed in Spain and elsewhere from the early 2000s, incorporates turn patterns borrowed from salsa. Sensual bachata, popularized by dancers such as Korke and Judith, adds body isolations, rolls, and waves drawn from Brazilian zouk. Bachata fusion blends elements of hip-hop, tango, and other dances.

History

Bachata music emerged in the rural Dominican Republic in the early 20th century, blending bolero, son, and merengue influences. For decades it was associated with the rural poor and was marginalized by the Dominican establishment. Following the international success of artists like Juan Luis Guerra in the early 1990s and groups such as Aventura in the 2000s, both the music and the dance spread worldwide, and bachata festivals and congresses are now held on every continent.
