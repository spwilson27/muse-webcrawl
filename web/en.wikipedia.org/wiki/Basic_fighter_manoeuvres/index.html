Basic fighter manoeuvres

Basic fighter manoeuvres (BFM) are tactical movements performed by fighter aircraft during air combat manoeuvring (a dogfight) to gain a positional advantage over the opponent. BFM combines the fundamentals of aerodynamic flight and the geometry of pursuit with the physics of managing the aircraft's energy state — its altitude, airspeed, and turn performance.

Principles

BFM rests on managing energy: a fighter can trade altitude (potential energy) for speed (kinetic energy) and vice versa. Turning performance is governed by turn rate and turn radius, which depend on speed and load factor; every aircraft has a corner velocity at which it achieves its best instantaneous turn. Pilots fly pursuit curves — pure, lead, and lag pursuit — to control closure and angle-off relative to the opponent.

Offensive manoeuvres

Offensive BFM is flown when the attacker is in front of the defender's 3–9 line. Classic moves include the high yo-yo, which uses a vertical excursion to control overshoot; the low yo-yo, which cuts across the turn circle by diving; the barrel roll attack; and the lag displacement roll. The goal is to arrive in the defender's rear hemisphere within weapons range and parameters.

Defensive manoeuvres

A defender seeks to defeat the attacker's firing solution and force an overshoot: hard break turns, the scissors (a series of reversing turns), the Split S to disengage downward, and the Immelmann turn to reverse direction with altitude gain. Defensive success converts geometry so the defender can neutralise or reverse the fight.
