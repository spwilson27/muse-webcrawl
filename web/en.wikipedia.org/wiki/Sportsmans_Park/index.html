Sportsman's Park

LocationSt. Louis, Missouri, U.S.
Opened1881 (original); rebuilt 1909
ClosedMay 8, 1966
TenantsSt. Louis Browns (1882–1892, 1902–1953), St. Louis Cardinals (1920–1966), St. Louis Browns/Cardinals (NFL)
Later nameBusch Stadium (1953–1966)

Sportsman's Park was the name of several major league baseball ballparks in St. Louis, Missouri, all but one located on the same site at Grand Boulevard and Dodier Street. The final and best-known version, a steel-and-concrete stadium rebuilt after a fire in 1909, was home to both of the city's major league teams for decades: the St. Louis Browns of the American League and the St. Louis Cardinals of the National League, making St. Louis unusual in having two clubs share one park.

The Cardinals moved in as tenants of the Browns in 1920 after abandoning Robison Field. The park hosted the 1944 World Series, an all-St. Louis affair in which the Cardinals defeated the Browns in the Browns' only World Series appearance. In 1953 the Browns' owner Bill Veeck sold the park to Cardinals owner August A. Busch Jr.; the Browns moved to Baltimore the following year to become the Orioles, and the renamed Busch Stadium served the Cardinals until 1966, when they moved into the new downtown Busch Memorial Stadium. The site is now occupied by a community recreation centre and playing fields, with a historical marker noting the ballpark's location.

Football

The park also hosted professional football, serving as home field of the NFL's St. Louis All-Stars and later the St. Louis Gunners, and of the football Cardinals of the NFL from 1960 until their move to Busch Memorial Stadium in 1966.
