Naaman


Naaman
Occupation: Commander of the army of Aram (Syria)
Known for: Healing from leprosy in 2 Kings 5

Naaman (Hebrew: נַעֲמָן, "pleasantness") was a commander of the army of the king of Aram (Syria) in the Hebrew Bible. His story is told in 2 Kings 5, in which he is healed of leprosy through the prophet Elisha.

Biblical account

Naaman, though a leper, was a respected commander. A young Israelite servant girl captured in a raid told Naaman's wife that a prophet in Samaria could cure him. With a letter from his king and lavish gifts, Naaman travelled to Israel. Elisha did not meet him personally but sent word that he should wash seven times in the River Jordan. At first offended — he considered the rivers of Damascus, Abana and Pharpar, superior — Naaman was persuaded by his servants to try, and he was healed.

Grateful, Naaman confessed the God of Israel and offered gifts, which Elisha refused. Elisha's servant Gehazi then deceitfully obtained gifts from Naaman and was struck with Naaman's leprosy as punishment. Naaman asked for two mule-loads of Israelite earth so he could worship the God of Israel at home, and for pardon when he accompanied his king into the temple of Rimmon.

Reception

Naaman is mentioned in the New Testament (Luke 4:27), where Jesus cites his healing as an example of divine favour extended beyond Israel. The story has been a frequent subject of sermons on humility and grace, and of visual art depicting his immersion in the Jordan.
