Military uniform

A military uniform is a standardized outfit worn by members of the armed forces and paramilitary organizations. Uniforms identify their wearers as members of a unit and service, distinguish rank and role through insignia, and serve practical functions in protection and camouflage. Under the laws of armed conflict, wearing a distinctive uniform or sign recognizable at a distance is one of the traditional conditions for combatants to qualify for prisoner-of-war status.

Types

Full dress uniform is the most formal, often colorful order of dress, retained for ceremonial occasions. Mess dress is formal evening wear. Service dress is an intermediate uniform for office and less formal ceremonial duties. Combat uniform (battledress or fatigues) is designed for field use, and since the twentieth century has usually incorporated camouflage patterns adapted to woodland, desert, urban, or multi-environment use. Specialized clothing includes flight suits, naval working dress, and protective ensembles against chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear hazards.

History

While ancient armies such as Rome's used standardized equipment, true uniforms spread in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries as standing armies grew; recognizable regimental coats — British red, French blue or white, Prussian blue — aided identification amid gunpowder smoke. Industrial warfare in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries drove a shift to drab khaki and field-grey for concealment, and after the Second World War camouflage patterns proliferated. Modern combat uniforms integrate modular load-bearing equipment, body armor, and fabrics treated for flame resistance and reduced infrared signature.

Insignia

Rank insignia — worn on sleeves, shoulders, or chest — together with branch badges, qualification badges, and unit patches, communicate the wearer's rank, arm of service, and accomplishments.
