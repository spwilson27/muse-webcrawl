Onconeural antibodies are autoantibodies directed against antigens shared between tumours and the nervous system ("onconeural antigens"). They are strongly associated with paraneoplastic neurological syndromes (PNS), in which an immune response raised against a cancer cross-reacts with neuronal proteins, causing neurological damage often before the tumour is discovered.

Mechanism

Many onconeural antigens are intracellular RNA-binding or signalling proteins normally restricted to neurons but ectopically expressed by tumours such as small-cell lung cancer, breast and ovarian cancers, and testicular tumours. The resulting immune response, probably largely T-cell mediated, damages neurons; the antibodies serve as highly specific diagnostic markers. Because the antigens are intracellular, these syndromes typically respond poorly to immunotherapy, in contrast to syndromes caused by antibodies against neuronal surface antigens.

Major antibodies

  • Anti-Hu (ANNA-1) — paraneoplastic encephalomyelitis and sensory neuronopathy; small-cell lung cancer
  • Anti-Yo (PCA-1) — paraneoplastic cerebellar degeneration; ovarian and breast cancers
  • Anti-Ri (ANNA-2) — opsoclonus-myoclonus and brainstem encephalitis; breast cancer
  • Anti-CV2/CRMP5 — encephalomyelitis, chorea, optic neuritis; small-cell lung cancer, thymoma
  • Anti-Ma2 — limbic and brainstem encephalitis; testicular germ-cell tumours
  • Anti-amphiphysin — stiff-person syndrome and encephalomyelitis; breast and lung cancers

Diagnosis

Detection in serum or CSF prompts a search for the associated malignancy (CT/PET imaging). Updated diagnostic criteria (2021) stratify antibodies by their strength of cancer association and combine clinical phenotype, antibody and cancer findings into levels of diagnostic certainty for PNS.
