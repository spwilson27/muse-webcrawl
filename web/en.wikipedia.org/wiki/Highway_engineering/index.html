Highway engineering

Highway engineering (also known as roadway engineering) is a branch of civil engineering concerned with the planning, design, construction, operation, and maintenance of roads, highways, and related infrastructure so that traffic moves safely and efficiently.

Design

Geometric design sets the alignment, cross-section, lane and shoulder widths, sight distances, superelevation, and intersection layout for a chosen design speed and traffic volume, following standards such as the AASHTO "Green Book" in the United States. Structural design sizes the pavement — flexible asphalt or rigid concrete — to carry expected axle loads over the design life, building on soil (subgrade) investigations and drainage design, since water is the chief enemy of pavements.

Planning and operations

Highway engineers forecast traffic, evaluate capacity and level of service, and manage construction traffic control, signing, markings, lighting, and safety audits. Modern practice adds asset management of aging networks, work-zone safety, and increasingly, accommodation of intelligent transportation systems and electric vehicles.

History

The discipline descends from Roman roadbuilding and the turnpikes and macadam roads of the 18th–19th centuries, and professionalized with the automobile era and national highway programs such as the U.S. Interstate Highway System begun in 1956.
