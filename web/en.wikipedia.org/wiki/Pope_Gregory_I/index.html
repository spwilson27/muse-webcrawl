Pope Gregory I (Latin: Gregorius I; c. 540 – 12 March 604), commonly known as Saint Gregory the Great, was the bishop of Rome from 3 September 590 to his death. He is famous for instigating the first recorded large-scale mission from Rome, the Gregorian mission, to convert the pagan Anglo-Saxons in England to Christianity. Gregory is also well known for his writings, which were more prolific than those of any of his predecessors as pope; among them are the Pastoral Care (Regula Pastoralis), the Dialogues, and an enormous surviving correspondence of some 850 letters. The epithet "the Great" was accorded him by popular acclamation.

Early life

Gregory was born into a wealthy, senatorial Roman family on the Caelian Hill; his father Gordianus was a senator, and his family had already produced two popes (Felix III was his great-great-grandfather, and Agapetus I was also a relative). After serving as prefect of Rome (c. 573), he renounced public life, founded six monasteries on his Sicilian estates and the monastery of St Andrew on the Caelian, and became a monk. Pope Pelagius II made him one of the seven deacons of Rome and sent him as apocrisiarius (ambassador) to Constantinople (579–586). On Pelagius's death from plague in 590, Gregory was elected pope by acclamation, despite his own protests.

Pontificate

Gregory effectively administered Rome amid famine, plague, floods, and Lombard invasion: he used the revenues of the papal patrimony to feed the city, ransomed captives, negotiated truces with the Lombards independently of the Byzantine exarch, and appointed governors and commanders — steps that laid foundations for the temporal power of the medieval papacy. He styled himself servus servorum Dei ("servant of the servants of God"), a title popes still use. In 596 he dispatched Augustine of Canterbury and a party of monks to evangelize Kent, initiating the Christianization of Anglo-Saxon England. He also reformed the liturgy and church administration, corresponded across the Christian world, and wrote extensively — the Moralia in Job, homilies on Ezekiel and the Gospels, and the Dialogues, whose second book is the principal source for the life of St Benedict.

Legacy

Gregory is venerated as a saint in the Catholic, Orthodox, and several other churches, and is one of the four great Latin Doctors of the Church. Tradition attaches his name to "Gregorian chant", though the chant repertory was codified later in the Carolingian era. His feast day is 3 September in the modern Roman calendar. In John Calvin's estimation he was the last of the good popes — a measure of the breadth of his historical reputation.
