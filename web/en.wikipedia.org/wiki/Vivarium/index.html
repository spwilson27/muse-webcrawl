Vivarium

A vivarium (Latin: "place of life") is an enclosed area, usually a glass tank, designed to replicate a natural habitat for keeping and observing live animals or plants under semi-natural conditions.

Types

Related enclosures include the terrarium (for land animals and plants), aquarium (aquatic), paludarium (part land, part water), riparium (riverbank), and formicarium (ants). Vivariums are used by hobbyists for reptiles, amphibians, invertebrates and tropical plants, and by researchers and zoos. Modern bioactive vivariums incorporate living plants, microfauna such as springtails and isopods, and full-spectrum lighting to sustain a functioning mini-ecosystem.

History

The term echoes the ancient Roman vivarium, a preserve for wild animals; a famous monastery Vivarium was founded by Cassiodorus in 6th-century Italy — for its scriptorium rather than animals.
