The Dell was a football stadium in Southampton, England, that was the home ground of Southampton F.C. from 1898 to 2001. Built in a natural hollow — a dell — on land that had once been a wooded valley with a stream, it became famous for its tight, intimate atmosphere, its chocolate-box stands stacked almost vertically above the pitch, and the trees of the dell visible behind one end.

History

Southampton moved to The Dell from the Antelope Ground in 1898 for a rent of £250 a year. The ground was developed piecemeal: the West Stand was designed by the noted football architect Archibald Leitch, while the East Stand was built over the stream culvert. The ground hosted the club's rise, including the 1976 FA Cup-winning side of Lawrie McMenemy and the top-flight teams of the 1980s inspired by Kevin Keegan. In 1941 an unexploded German bomb damaged the pitch, and in 1993 The Dell became an all-seater stadium under the Taylor Report requirements, reducing capacity to about 15,200 — commercially untenable for a Premier League club.

Closure and legacy

The last competitive match at The Dell was played on 19 May 2001, a 3–2 Premier League win over Arsenal in which club legend Matt Le Tissier, a late substitute, scored the final goal with a trademark volley. Southampton moved to St Mary's Stadium in August 2001. The Dell was demolished and the site redeveloped for housing, with streets named for club figures and the site of the pitch preserved as a courtyard. It remains an affectionately remembered example of the old, characterful English football grounds chronicled in urban histories in the spirit of The Death and Life of Great American Cities.
