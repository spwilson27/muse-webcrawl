Indianapolis Motor Speedway

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indianapolis Motor Speedway is an automobile racing circuit located in Speedway, Indiana, an enclave suburb of Indianapolis. It is the home of the Indianapolis 500 and the Brickyard 400, and formerly hosted the United States Grand Prix of Formula One. Built in 1909, it is the third-oldest permanent automobile race track in the world and the highest-capacity sports venue anywhere, with permanent seating for around 235,000 and total capacity near 400,000.

History

Businessman Carl G. Fisher and partners built the speedway in 1909, first with a crushed stone-and-tar surface that proved deadly; the track was repaved with 3.2 million bricks, earning it the nickname "The Brickyard". The first Indianapolis 500 was held in 1911 and won by Ray Harroun. Tony Hulman purchased the speedway in 1945 and revived it after World War II, beginning the Hulman family's 75-year stewardship. In 2020 Roger Penske's Penske Corporation acquired the speedway and the IndyCar Series.

The track

The 2.5-mile rectangular oval has four quarter-mile turns connected by long straights and short chutes. Almost all of the bricks are now covered by asphalt, but a 36-inch strip of the original brick remains exposed at the start/finish line—the "Yard of Bricks"—which winning drivers traditionally kiss. An infield road course, built for Formula One in 2000, also hosts IndyCar's road-course race each May.

Events and traditions

The Indianapolis 500, held each Memorial Day weekend, is billed as "The Greatest Spectacle in Racing". Traditions include the singing of "Back Home Again in Indiana", the winner drinking milk in Victory Lane, and the Borg-Warner Trophy. The speedway also contains a museum and an 18-hole golf course, Brickyard Crossing, with holes inside the oval.
