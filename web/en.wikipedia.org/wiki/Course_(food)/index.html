Course (food)

A course is one stage of a meal served in sequence.

A course is a specific set of food items that are served together during a meal, all at the same time. A course may include multiple dishes, or a single dish, and the sequence of courses structures formal dining in many cuisines. A meal may consist of one course, or of many—historically up to a dozen or more at elaborate banquets.

Typical sequences

In modern Western formal dining a meal commonly proceeds: appetizer or soup; salad (served before the main course in North America, often after it in parts of Europe); the main course of meat, fish, or a vegetarian centrepiece with accompaniments; a cheese course in French tradition; and dessert, followed by coffee or digestifs. Common formats include the three-course meal (starter, main, dessert) and tasting menus of many small courses in fine dining.

History

Through the early modern period, European banquets used service à la française, in which a "course" was a large array of dishes set on the table at once, from which guests helped themselves; a grand dinner might have three such courses. In the 19th century this was gradually displaced by service à la russe, in which dishes are brought in sequence and portioned by servants—the origin of the modern meaning of a course as one stage of the meal. Auguste Escoffier and the grande cuisine tradition codified the progression of courses in classical French menus.

Other traditions

Japanese kaiseki presents a choreographed sequence of seasonal courses; Chinese banquets proceed through a series of shared dishes ending with rice or noodles; and the Italian meal is structured as antipasto, primo (pasta or rice), secondo (meat or fish) with contorno, and dolce. The full-course dinner remains a marker of ceremonial occasions such as weddings and state banquets.
