Władysław II Jagiełło

Władysław II Jagiełło
Reign (Poland): 1386–1434
Grand Duke of Lithuania: 1377–1401 (as Jogaila)
Spouse: Jadwiga of Poland
Dynasty: Jagiellonian

Władysław II Jagiełło (c. 1352/1362 – 1434), known in Lithuanian as Jogaila, was Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1377 and, by his marriage to Queen Jadwiga, King of Poland from 1386 until his death. He founded the Jagiellonian dynasty and the Polish–Lithuanian union.

Accession and union

Under the Union of Krewo (1385) Jogaila agreed to convert to Christianity, marry Jadwiga and unite his lands with Poland. He was baptised in Kraków in 1386, taking the name Władysław, and subsequently oversaw the Christianisation of Lithuania, the last pagan state in Europe.

War with the Teutonic Order

His reign culminated in the Polish–Lithuanian victory over the Teutonic Knights at the Battle of Grunwald (1410), one of the largest battles of medieval Europe, fought jointly with his cousin Vytautas the Great. Władysław refounded the University of Kraków in 1400 and died in 1434 at Gródek Jagielloński. See also Jagiellonian dynasty.
