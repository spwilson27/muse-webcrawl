Nasdaq-100

Type: Stock market index
Exchange: Nasdaq
Components: 100
Launched: 1985

The Nasdaq-100 (often written NDX) is a stock market index made up of 100 of the largest non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq stock exchange. It is heavily weighted toward large technology and growth companies and is one of the most widely followed equity benchmarks in the world.

Composition

The index includes companies across sectors such as technology, consumer discretionary, health care, telecommunications and industrials, but excludes financial companies, which are covered by a separate index. Its largest constituents have included Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta Platforms and Broadcom. The index is modified market-capitalization weighted and is rebalanced periodically.

History

The Nasdaq-100 was launched on January 31, 1985, alongside the Nasdaq Financial-100. It surged during the dot-com boom of the late 1990s, peaked in March 2000, and then fell sharply in the ensuing crash before recovering over the following decades. The Invesco QQQ exchange-traded fund, launched in 1999, tracks the index and is among the most traded ETFs in the world.

Investing

Investors gain exposure through index funds and ETFs such as QQQ, and derivatives including futures and options traded on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange.
