Ytterbium

Ytterbium is a chemical element with the symbol Yb and atomic number 70. It is a soft, silvery lanthanide metal — a rare-earth element — and is the penultimate member of the lanthanide series. Like the other lanthanides, its most common oxidation state is +3, although it also forms stable +2 compounds, reflecting its filled 4f electron shell.

Properties

Ytterbium is a bright, soft, malleable and ductile metal with a melting point of 824 °C. It tarnishes slowly in air and reacts with water. Natural ytterbium is a mixture of seven stable isotopes, with 174Yb the most abundant.

History

The element was discovered in 1878 by the Swiss chemist Jean Charles Galissard de Marignac, who separated it from "erbia" obtained from the mineral gadolinite found near the Swedish village of Ytterby — the source of the names of four elements (yttrium, terbium, erbium and ytterbium).

Applications

Ytterbium is used as a dopant in fibre lasers and amplifiers, in certain atomic clocks of exceptional precision (ytterbium lattice clocks), in stress gauges exploiting its electrical resistance changes under pressure, and historically in portable X-ray sources using the isotope 169Yb.
