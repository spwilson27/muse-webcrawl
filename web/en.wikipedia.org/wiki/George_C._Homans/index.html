George C. Homans

George Caspar Homans
Born: August 11, 1910, Boston, Massachusetts, U.S.
Died: May 29, 1989, Cambridge, Massachusetts, U.S.
Known for: Social exchange theory, The Human Group

George Caspar Homans (August 11, 1910 – May 29, 1989) was an American sociologist, a founder of behavioural sociology and social exchange theory, and a longtime professor at Harvard University.

Life and career

Homans was born in Boston into a prominent family — he was a descendant of presidents John Adams and John Quincy Adams. He graduated from Harvard College in 1932 in English literature, entered sociology through a seminar on Vilfredo Pareto, and became a junior fellow at Harvard. After service in the U.S. Navy during the Second World War he returned to Harvard, where he taught for the rest of his career. He served as president of the American Sociological Association in 1963–64.

Work

In The Human Group (1950), analysing classic small-group studies, Homans developed a conceptual scheme relating activities, interactions and sentiments. His major theoretical statement, Social Behavior: Its Elementary Forms (1961), grounded sociology in behavioural psychology: he proposed that social interaction is an exchange of material and non-material goods governed by propositions about reward, cost, success, stimulus, value, deprivation–satiation and distributive justice. Homans advocated "bringing men back in" against purely structural-functional theory, arguing that sociological explanations must ultimately rest on propositions about individual behaviour. His later writings, including The Nature of Social Science (1967) and an autobiography, Coming to My Senses (1984), defended this reductionist, deductive approach.
