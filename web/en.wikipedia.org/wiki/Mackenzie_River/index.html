Mackenzie River

The Mackenzie River is a major river system in western Canada and the largest and longest river system in Canada, flowing about 4,240 kilometres including its headstreams, the Peace and Finlay rivers. The Mackenzie proper flows northwest about 1,738 kilometres from Great Slave Lake in the Northwest Territories to the Beaufort Sea of the Arctic Ocean.

Its basin, covering roughly 1.8 million square kilometres, is the second-largest in North America after the Mississippi's. The river is named after explorer Alexander Mackenzie, who travelled its length in 1789 hoping to reach the Pacific. Communities along it include Fort Providence, Norman Wells and Inuvik, near its delta.

Environment

The Mackenzie delta is one of the world's great river deltas and an important habitat for migratory birds and beluga whales. The basin contains significant oil and gas deposits, and the river remains a summer transport artery for isolated northern communities supplied by barge.
