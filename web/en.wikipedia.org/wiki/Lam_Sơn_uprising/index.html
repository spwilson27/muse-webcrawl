Lam Sơn uprising

The Lam Sơn uprising (Vietnamese: Khởi nghĩa Lam Sơn) was a Vietnamese national resistance movement led by Lê Lợi against the occupation of Đại Việt by China's Ming dynasty. Beginning in 1418 at Lam Sơn in Thanh Hóa province, the ten-year struggle ended in 1428 with the withdrawal of Ming forces and the founding of the Later Lê dynasty.

Background

The Ming had annexed Vietnam in 1407 after defeating the Hồ dynasty, administering it as the province of Jiaozhi. Heavy taxation, forced labour, and cultural assimilation provoked widespread resentment and numerous revolts.

Course of the war

Lê Lợi, a landowner from Lam Sơn, raised the banner of revolt in early 1418 with a small force, initially suffering defeats and retreating into the mountains of Thanh Hóa. Guided by strategists such as Nguyễn Trãi, whose writings rallied popular support, the movement grew through guerrilla warfare. Victories at Tốt Động–Chúc Động (1426) and the decisive siege of Đông Quan (Hanoi) forced the Ming to negotiate; in 1427 the Ming commander Wang Tong agreed to withdraw, and the last Ming troops left in early 1428.

Aftermath

Lê Lợi ascended the throne as Emperor Lê Thái Tổ, restoring Vietnamese independence. Nguyễn Trãi's Bình Ngô đại cáo (Great Proclamation upon the Pacification of the Wu), written to mark the victory, is regarded as a landmark of Vietnamese literature and a declaration of national identity.
