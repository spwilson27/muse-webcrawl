Concentus Musicus Wien

Concentus Musicus Wien (CMW) is an Austrian Baroque music ensemble founded by Nikolaus Harnoncourt and his wife Alice Harnoncourt in 1953. It was one of the pioneering ensembles of the historically informed performance movement, performing early music on period instruments.

History

Harnoncourt, then a cellist with the Vienna Symphony Orchestra, gathered colleagues to explore the sound world of Baroque and earlier music using original instruments and historical performance practices. The ensemble gave its first public concerts after several years of private rehearsal and made its formal debut in Vienna in 1957. It became internationally known through landmark recordings, including a complete cycle of Bach's cantatas shared with the Leonhardt Consort, and recordings of Monteverdi, Handel, Telemann, Mozart, and Haydn.

Style and influence

The ensemble's rhetorical, expressive approach to early music — treating music as speech — profoundly influenced subsequent generations of performers. Concentus Musicus Wien toured worldwide and recorded extensively for Teldec's Das Alte Werk label. After Nikolaus Harnoncourt's retirement from conducting in 2015 and his death in 2016, the ensemble continued performing, led by concertmaster Erich Höbarth and others.
