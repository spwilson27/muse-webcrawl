Journal (accounting)


Journal
Type: Book of original entry
Used in: Double-entry bookkeeping
Followed by: Posting to the ledger

A journal in accounting is a book — today usually a database record — in which financial transactions are first recorded in chronological order before being posted to the nominal ledger. It is known as the book of original entry.

Contents

Each journal entry records the date of the transaction, the accounts debited and credited, the amounts, and a short description. In double-entry bookkeeping the total debits must equal the total credits for the books to balance.

Types of journals

Businesses commonly keep specialised journals (also called day books) for high-volume transactions, such as the sales journal, purchases journal, cash receipts journal and cash payments journal, alongside a general journal for transactions that fit nowhere else, such as depreciation, accruals and corrections.

Process

Recording a transaction in the journal is called journalising. Periodically, the entries are summarised and posted to the individual accounts in the general ledger, from which the trial balance and financial statements are prepared.
