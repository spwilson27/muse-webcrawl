Emergency medicine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A hospital emergency department

Emergency medicine is the medical speciality concerned with the care of illnesses or injuries requiring immediate medical attention. Emergency physicians (or emergency medicine physicians) specialise in providing unscheduled care for undifferentiated patients of all ages, and primarily work in hospital emergency departments. They are responsible for the immediate recognition, evaluation, care, stabilization, and disposition of patients in response to acute illness and injury.

Scope

Emergency medicine encompasses the initial assessment and treatment of conditions ranging from life-threatening emergencies such as cardiac arrest, stroke, major trauma and sepsis, to minor injuries and urgent symptoms. Core skills include advanced airway management, resuscitation, focused ultrasound, procedural sedation, fracture and wound management, and the use of triage systems to prioritize patients by acuity. Emergency physicians coordinate with inpatient specialties and emergency medical services (EMS), which deliver prehospital care.

History

Although emergency care has ancient roots, emergency medicine developed as a distinct specialty in the 1960s and 1970s, particularly in the United States and the United Kingdom. The first full-time emergency department staffing groups formed in the US in the 1960s; the American College of Emergency Physicians was founded in 1968, and emergency medicine was recognized as a specialty by the American Board of Medical Specialties in 1979. In the UK, the specialty grew out of casualty departments following the 1962 Platt Report.

Training

Training pathways vary by country. In the United States, physicians complete a three- or four-year residency in emergency medicine after medical school, with optional fellowships in areas such as emergency medical services, critical care, toxicology, pediatric emergency medicine, and ultrasound.
