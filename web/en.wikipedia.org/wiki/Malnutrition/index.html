Malnutrition

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Malnutrition is a condition resulting from a diet that lacks, or has too much of, the nutrients needed for health. In its broad modern sense it covers undernutrition (wasting, stunting and underweight, and micronutrient deficiencies), overweight and obesity, and diet-related noncommunicable diseases — the 'triple burden' many countries now face simultaneously.

Undernutrition

Undernutrition in children under five remains a major global health problem: stunting (low height for age) affects roughly 150 million children, and wasting (low weight for height) tens of millions, increasing deaths from infections such as diarrhoea, pneumonia and measles. Severe acute malnutrition is treated with ready-to-use therapeutic foods. Deficiencies of vitamin A, iron, iodine and zinc are the most consequential micronutrient gaps.

Causes and responses

Causes combine poverty, food insecurity, poor water and sanitation, infection, conflict and unequal food distribution rather than simple global scarcity. Responses span nutrition-specific programmes (breastfeeding promotion, supplementation, fortification) and nutrition-sensitive ones (agriculture, education, social protection).
