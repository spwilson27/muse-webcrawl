Bylina

Placeholder image: the three great bogatyrs of the byliny.

A bylina (Russian: были́на) is a traditional East Slavic oral epic poem. Byliny are narrative songs about the deeds of heroic figures — the bogatyrs — most famously Ilya Muromets, Dobrynya Nikitich and Alyosha Popovich, who serve Prince Vladimir of Kiev and defend the Rus' land against steppe invaders and supernatural foes.

Form and performance

Byliny are sung or chanted in a distinctive unrhymed verse with a flexible rhythm, typically in lines of roughly three stresses, with characteristic formulaic epithets — "the open field", "the glorious city of Kiev". They were preserved by peasant singers in the Russian North, around Lake Onega and the White Sea coast, far from the events they describe, and collected by folklorists from the 18th and 19th centuries onward.

Cycles

Scholars group the byliny into cycles: the Kievan cycle, centred on Vladimir's court; the Novgorod cycle, featuring the merchant-hero Sadko and Vasily Buslayev; and older "mythological" songs about heroes such as Svyatogor and Volkh Vseslavyevich whose roots may reach back to pre-Christian tradition. Although many byliny preserve echoes of real 10th–13th century history, they mix heroes from different eras freely.
