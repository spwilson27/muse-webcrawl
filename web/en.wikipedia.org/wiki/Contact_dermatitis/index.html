Contact dermatitis

Contact dermatitis
SpecialtyDermatology
SymptomsRed, itchy, scaly skin; blisters
CausesIrritants; allergens (nickel, latex, fragrances)
TreatmentAvoidance, emollients, topical corticosteroids

Contact dermatitis is a type of eczema — inflammation of the skin — caused by contact with irritants or allergens. It is one of the most common skin diseases and a leading cause of occupational illness.

Types

  • Irritant contact dermatitis (about 80% of cases) — direct chemical or physical injury, e.g. from detergents, solvents, friction, or repeated wet work.
  • Allergic contact dermatitis — a delayed type IV hypersensitivity reaction to a specific sensitizer such as nickel, latex, hair dyes, or poison ivy urushiol.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is clinical, based on the distribution matching the contact site. The patch test applies suspected allergens to the back for 48 hours to identify the culprit in allergic cases.

Treatment and prevention

The mainstay is avoiding the offending substance, plus emollients, topical corticosteroids for flares, and protective gloves or barrier creams occupationally.
