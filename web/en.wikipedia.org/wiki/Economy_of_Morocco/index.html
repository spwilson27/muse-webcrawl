The economy of Morocco is a market economy characterised by a large agricultural sector, the world's biggest phosphate reserves, growing automotive and aeronautics industries, tourism, and substantial remittances from Moroccans abroad. Morocco is a lower-middle-income country with a population of roughly 37 million.

Phosphates

Morocco holds around 70 percent of the world's known phosphate rock reserves. The state-owned OCP Group is the world's largest exporter of phosphate rock and phosphoric acid, and phosphate derivatives — used mainly in fertilisers — are among the country's top export earners. Prices surged during the 2021–2022 global fertiliser shock.

Agriculture

Agriculture employs around a third of the workforce and contributes roughly 12–15 percent of GDP, with output highly sensitive to rainfall. Morocco exports citrus fruit, tomatoes and other vegetables, olives, and fish products, largely to the European Union. Recurrent drought in the 2020s has intensified pressure on water resources and driven investment in desalination and dam projects.

Industry

Since the 2010s Morocco has built a significant automotive industry around plants near Tangier and Kenitra, with car production and components becoming the country's leading export sector. An aeronautics cluster around Casablanca supplies parts to global manufacturers. The Tanger Med port complex is the largest port in Africa and the Mediterranean by capacity and anchors export-oriented industrial zones.

Services and energy

Tourism — Marrakesh, Fes, Agadir and the coast — is a major source of foreign exchange, as are remittances from the large Moroccan diaspora in Europe. Morocco imports most of its fossil fuels but has invested heavily in solar power, including the Noor solar complex near Ouarzazate, one of the largest concentrated solar plants in the world.

Trade

The European Union is Morocco's dominant trading partner; the country also pursues trade and investment links across West Africa. Growth has generally run at 3–4 percent a year outside drought and pandemic years, with inflation and youth unemployment remaining key challenges.
