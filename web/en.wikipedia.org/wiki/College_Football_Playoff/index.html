The College Football Playoff (CFP) is an annual postseason tournament used to determine a national champion of the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) — the highest level of college football in the United States. It replaced the Bowl Championship Series after the 2013 season. A 13-member selection committee ranks teams and selects the field; from the 2024 season the playoff expanded from four teams to twelve, comprising the five highest-ranked conference champions plus seven at-large teams, with the four highest-ranked champions receiving first-round byes in the initial format.

History

For decades the FBS champion was decided by polls after the bowl games, producing frequent disputes; the BCS (1998–2013) matched No. 1 vs. No. 2 by formula. The four-team playoff debuted in the 2014 season, with Ohio State winning the inaugural title game over Oregon in January 2015. The semifinals rotated among the six "New Year's Six" bowls (Rose, Sugar, Orange, Cotton, Fiesta, Peach). Alabama under Nick Saban dominated the era with three titles; Clemson won two; Georgia won back-to-back titles for the 2021 and 2022 seasons, and Michigan won the final four-team edition for 2023.

Expanded playoff

The 12-team format began in December 2024: first-round games are played on campus sites, quarterfinals and semifinals at bowl venues, and the championship game at a neutral stadium. The first 12-team champion was Ohio State, which beat Notre Dame in the January 2025 title game. Media rights, chiefly with ESPN, are worth well over a billion dollars annually under the expanded deal, and further format adjustments (including seeding changes from 2025) have continued to be debated.

Criticism

The playoff has been criticised for selection controversies (notably the exclusion of undefeated Florida State in 2023), for accelerating conference realignment and player movement, and for devaluing regular-season and traditional bowl games, while supporters credit it with fairer access and record television audiences.
