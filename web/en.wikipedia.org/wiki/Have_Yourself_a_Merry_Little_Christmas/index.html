"Have Yourself a Merry Little Christmas" is a song written by Hugh Martin and Ralph Blane and introduced by Judy Garland in the 1944 MGM musical film Meet Me in St. Louis. It has become one of the most beloved and melancholy of all Christmas standards, recorded by hundreds of artists.

Origin and the lyric changes

In the film, Garland's character sings the song to her little sister (played by Margaret O'Brien) on Christmas Eve as the family faces a dreaded move from St. Louis to New York. Martin's original lyrics were strikingly bleak — beginning "Have yourself a merry little Christmas / It may be your last" — and Garland, director Vincente Minnelli, and co-star Tom Drake asked him to soften them. Martin rewrote lines such as "Next year we may all be living in the past" and produced the film version, including "Let your heart be light / Next year all our troubles will be out of sight."

Sinatra revision

In 1957 Frank Sinatra, recording the song for his album A Jolly Christmas, asked Martin to further brighten the line "Until then we'll have to muddle through somehow," objecting that the album should be jolly. Martin replaced it with "Hang a shining star upon the highest bough," the version most commonly sung today. Garland's film version and Sinatra's revision coexist, and performers mix elements of both.

Legacy

The song was written during the Second World War, and its wistful hope for reunion resonated with servicemen overseas; Garland sang it for troops at the Hollywood Canteen. It ranks near the top of the American Society of Composers, Authors and Publishers' most-performed Christmas songs, with notable versions by Bing Crosby, Ella Fitzgerald, James Taylor (whose 2001 recording revived the original wartime mood), and Sam Smith.
