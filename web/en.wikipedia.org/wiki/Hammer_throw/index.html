Hammer throw

The hammer throw is one of the four throwing events in regular track and field competitions, along with the discus throw, shot put and javelin. The "hammer" used in this sport is not like any of the tools also called by that name. It consists of a metal ball attached by a steel wire to a grip. The size of the ball varies between men's and women's competitions.

History

With roots in Scotland and Ireland dating back to at least the 16th century, the hammer throw is one of the oldest of the field events. A precursor competition, throwing a sledgehammer, was popular in Britain. The modern hammer throw became an Olympic event for men in 1900 and for women in 2000.

Technique

The thrower stands in a concrete circle 2.135 metres (7 feet) in diameter and spins the hammer around the body in a series of preliminary swings before completing typically three or four full rotations, releasing the implement into a marked sector. The throw is measured from the inside of the circle to the nearest mark made by the hammer's fall.

Equipment

The men's hammer weighs 7.26 kilograms (16 lb) and the women's 4 kilograms (8.8 lb). The wire and handle bring the total length to about 1.2 metres. The world records are held by Yuriy Sedykh (86.74 m, 1986) for men and Anita Włodarczyk (82.98 m, 2016) for women.
