Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park

Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park in Cornish, New Hampshire, preserves the home, gardens, and studios of Augustus Saint-Gaudens (1848–1907), one of America's foremost sculptors. The park, administered by the National Park Service, was originally established as a National Historic Site in 1964 and redesignated a National Historical Park in 2019.

Aspet, the Saint-Gaudens home in Cornish, New Hampshire.

History

Saint-Gaudens purchased the Cornish property, an old tavern, in 1885 at the urging of his friend Charles Cotesworth Beaman, and named it Aspet after his father's birthplace in France. It became the centre of the Cornish Art Colony, attracting artists, writers, and musicians each summer. Saint-Gaudens worked here on major commissions, including the Robert Gould Shaw Memorial and the standing figure of Abraham Lincoln.

Features

The park covers about 190 acres (77 ha) and includes the Aspet house with original furnishings, the Little Studio and the larger Studio, formal gardens designed with the sculptor's wife Augusta, and reproductions of Saint-Gaudens's major works, including the Shaw Memorial, the Adams Memorial, and the Diana figure. Hiking trails lead to the Blow-Me-Down mill pond and other parts of the estate. It is the only National Park Service site in New Hampshire.
