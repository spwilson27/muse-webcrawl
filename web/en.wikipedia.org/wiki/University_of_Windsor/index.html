University of Windsor

The University of Windsor (UWindsor) is a public research university in Windsor, Ontario, Canada, near the Canada–United States border. It was founded in 1957 as Assumption University and became the independent University of Windsor in 1963. Its location across the Detroit River from Detroit, Michigan, shapes its strong programs in automotive engineering, law, and cross-border studies.

History

The university traces its origins to Assumption College, founded in 1857 by the Basilian Fathers and affiliated for decades with the University of Western Ontario. It became Assumption University in 1956 and federated with Essex College before the province chartered the secular, independent University of Windsor in 1963, with Assumption continuing as a federated college. In the 2010s the university expanded into downtown Windsor, moving programs such as social work, law-related studies, and the School of Creative Arts into heritage buildings in the city core.

Academics

UWindsor offers undergraduate and graduate programs through faculties including Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences; Science; Engineering; the Odette School of Business; Law; Education; Nursing; and Human Kinetics. It operates one of Canada's only automotive engineering programs and the Cross-Border Institute, reflecting its border location beside the busiest commercial crossing between Canada and the United States. Its law school is known for access-to-justice and clinical legal education. The university's athletic teams, the Lancers, have won national titles in track and field and women's basketball.
