The Mediterranean Lingua Franca, or Sabir, was a pidgin language used as a lingua franca in the Mediterranean Basin from the 11th to the 19th centuries. It was the original basis for the term "lingua franca", which has since come to mean any common language used between speakers of different native tongues.

Characteristics

Lingua Franca was a contact language based largely on northern Italian languages, especially Venetian and Genoese, and Occitano-Romance languages in the western Mediterranean, with a substantial admixture of vocabulary from Spanish, Portuguese, Catalan, French, Arabic, Turkish, Berber, Persian and Greek. Its grammar was greatly simplified: verbs were typically used in a single invariant form derived from the Romance infinitive, and articles and inflections were minimal.

Use

The language was used for trade, diplomacy and maritime communication between Christians and Muslims, between European merchants and North African ports, and notably in the Barbary states, where it was spoken by slaves, renegades, merchants and officials. The French playwright Molière parodied it in Le Bourgeois gentilhomme (1670), in which the Turkish ceremony features phrases such as "Se ti sabir, ti respondir" — "if you know [Sabir], I answer you".

Decline

Sabir declined during the 19th century as European colonial powers imposed their own languages — particularly French in Algeria after 1830 — and as mass education and steam navigation changed Mediterranean communication. Scattered traces survive in loanwords and in the usage of some port communities.
