Mother goddess

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A "Venus figurine" of the type often interpreted as a mother goddess.

A mother goddess is a goddess who represents a personified deification of motherhood, fertility, creation, destruction, or the earth goddess who embodies the bounty of the earth or nature. When equated with the earth or the natural world, such goddesses are sometimes referred to as the Mother Earth or Earth Mother.

Mother goddesses have been worshipped in many cultures throughout history. In the ancient Near East and Mediterranean world, prominent examples included the Egyptian Isis, the Mesopotamian Ishtar and Ninhursag, the Phrygian Cybele, and the Greek goddesses Gaia, Demeter, and Rhea. In Hinduism, the divine feminine is worshipped as Shakti and in forms such as Durga, Kali, and Lakshmi. In Chinese folk religion and Taoism, Mazu and the Queen Mother of the West (Xiwangmu) occupy similar roles.

Prehistoric figurines

Small statuettes of voluptuous female figures, often called Venus figurines, are known from the Upper Paleolithic, roughly 40,000 to 10,000 years ago. The most famous example is the Venus of Willendorf, carved from oolitic limestone around 28,000–25,000 BCE. Scholars have long debated whether these figures represent goddesses, fertility charms, self-portraits, or dolls. In the Neolithic, sites such as Çatalhöyük in Anatolia yielded seated female figures flanked by animals, which the archaeologist James Mellaart interpreted as evidence of a mother goddess cult.

Interpretations and debate

The archaeologist Marija Gimbutas argued that Neolithic "Old Europe" was a peaceful, matrifocal civilization centred on goddess worship, later overthrown by patriarchal Indo-European invaders. Her theories were influential in the feminist spirituality and Goddess movement of the 1970s, but have been criticized by many archaeologists as over-interpretations of ambiguous evidence. Peter Ucko and others cautioned that figurines could serve many purposes and that a universal prehistoric mother goddess religion cannot simply be assumed.

Modern worship

The mother goddess remains a central figure in modern Pagan and Neopagan religions, particularly Wicca, where the Goddess is honoured alongside the Horned God, and in the Goddess movement, which treats the divine feminine as a focus of worship and a symbol of women's spirituality.
