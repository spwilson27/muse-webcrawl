Evesham is a market town and parish in the Wychavon district of Worcestershire, in the West Midlands of England. It is located roughly equidistant between Worcester, Cheltenham, and Stratford-upon-Avon, in the valley of the River Avon, and is known for the fertile market gardening of the surrounding Vale of Evesham, particularly its asparagus and plums.

History

Evesham developed around Evesham Abbey, founded in the early eighth century, traditionally in about 701, by Ecgwine, Bishop of Worcester, after a vision of the Virgin Mary said to have been experienced by a swineherd named Eof — hence "Eof's ham". The abbey grew into one of the wealthiest Benedictine houses in England before its dissolution in 1540, after which most of its buildings were destroyed. Surviving structures include the detached bell tower (the Bell Tower of Evesham Abbey, built under Abbot Clement Lichfield in the early sixteenth century), the almonry, and the town's two parish churches, All Saints and St Lawrence, which stand side by side in the former abbey precinct.

The town's name is also attached to the Battle of Evesham, fought nearby on 4 August 1265 during the Second Barons' War. Simon de Montfort, Earl of Leicester, who had effectively ruled England after his victory at Lewes, was defeated and killed by the forces of the future Edward I. The battle is regarded as one of the decisive engagements of the medieval period and de Montfort was buried at the abbey.

Geography and economy

Evesham sits on a meander of the River Avon, which encircles much of the historic town centre. The surrounding Vale is celebrated for its orchards and market gardens, producing asparagus (locally called "gras"), apples, and the yellow Pershore plum. From the nineteenth century, market gardening and fruit growing shaped the local economy, supported by the town's railway station on the Cotswold line between London Paddington and Worcester. Today the town is also a retail and service centre for the surrounding villages, and tourism is drawn by the river, the abbey remains, and annual events including an asparagus festival and a medieval festival commemorating the battle.

The river has periodically flooded the town, notably in the severe floods of July 2007, when Evesham was among the places in England badly affected, with homes and businesses inundated and road and rail links severed.

Transport

Evesham railway station offers direct trains to Worcester, Oxford, and London Paddington via the Cotswold line. The A46 bypasses the town to the west, connecting it to the M5 motorway near Tewkesbury. The River Avon is navigable and is used by leisure boats, with a marina and moorings serving the town.

Notable people

The broadcaster and cookery writer Clement Freud's contemporary names aside, Evesham's notable residents have included the writer John Drinkwater's circle and, in more recent times, the comedian Alistair McGowan, who was born in the town in 1964. The hymn writer and botanical author connections of the abbey's chroniclers, such as Thomas of Marlborough, are also recorded in local histories.
