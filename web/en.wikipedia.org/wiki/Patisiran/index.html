Patisiran

Trade nameOnpattro
TypeSmall interfering RNA (siRNA)
DeveloperAlnylam Pharmaceuticals
RouteIntravenous infusion
IndicationPolyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis
ApprovalFDA, 10 August 2018

Patisiran, sold under the brand name Onpattro, is a medication used to treat the polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis (hATTR) in adults. It is a small interfering RNA (siRNA) formulated in a lipid nanoparticle that delivers it to the liver, where it silences production of transthyretin (TTR). Approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in August 2018, patisiran was the first RNA interference (RNAi) therapeutic ever approved, a landmark for a technology that had taken two decades to reach the clinic.

Medical uses

Patisiran is indicated for adults with stage 1 or stage 2 polyneuropathy caused by hATTR amyloidosis, an inherited disease in which mutant transthyretin forms amyloid deposits that damage peripheral nerves (and, in many patients, the heart). By reducing both mutant and wild-type TTR synthesis in hepatocytes, patisiran lowers circulating TTR by roughly 80 per cent and slows or partially reverses neuropathy progression.

Mechanism of action

The drug substance is a double-stranded siRNA whose guide strand is complementary to a conserved sequence in the 3′ untranslated region of TTR messenger RNA. Encapsulated in a lipid nanoparticle, patisiran is taken up by hepatocytes via apolipoprotein E–mediated endocytosis. Inside the cell the siRNA is loaded into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), which cleaves the target mRNA and prevents transthyretin synthesis — a natural gene-silencing pathway first described by Andrew Fire and Craig Mello, who received the 2006 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the discovery of RNA interference.

Clinical evidence

Approval rested mainly on the phase III APOLLO trial (published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2018), which randomised 225 patients with hATTR amyloidosis and polyneuropathy. Patisiran significantly improved the modified Neuropathy Impairment Score +7 (mNIS+7) and quality-of-life measures compared with placebo at 18 months, with benefits also reported for gait speed and other endpoints.

Administration and adverse effects

Patisiran is given by intravenous infusion once every three weeks, dosed by body weight, with premedication (a corticosteroid, paracetamol/acetaminophen, and H1/H2 blockers) to reduce infusion-related reactions. Common adverse effects include upper respiratory tract infections and infusion reactions; because lowering TTR reduces retinol transport, patients are advised to take vitamin A supplements.

History and significance

Patisiran was developed by Alnylam Pharmaceuticals of Cambridge, Massachusetts. Its 2018 approval — alongside the antisense drug inotersen the same year — validated RNA-targeted therapeutics as a drug class and opened the way to later siRNA medicines such as givosiran, lumasiran, inclisiran and vutrisiran, a longer-acting successor for transthyretin amyloidosis given by subcutaneous injection quarterly.
