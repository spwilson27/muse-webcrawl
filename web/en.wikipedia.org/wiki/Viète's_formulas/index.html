Viète's formulas

In mathematics, Viète's formulas are formulas that relate the coefficients of a polynomial to sums and products of its roots. They are named after François Viète (more commonly referred to by the Latinised form of his name, "Franciscus Vieta").

The simplest versions state that for a quadratic polynomial with roots r₁ and r₂, the sum of the roots equals the negative of the coefficient of x divided by the leading coefficient, and the product of the roots equals the constant term divided by the leading coefficient.

x² + px + q = (x − r₁)(x − r₂), so r₁ + r₂ = −p and r₁r₂ = q.

General statement

For a polynomial of degree n with roots r₁, …, rₙ, the k-th elementary symmetric sum of the roots equals (−1)ᵏ times the ratio of the coefficient of xⁿ⁻ᵏ to the leading coefficient. These relations hold in any commutative ring in which the polynomial splits, and they are a consequence of expanding the factored form of the polynomial.

Example

For the cubic x³ − 2x² − x + 2 with roots 1, 2, and −1, Viète's formulas give: sum of roots 1 + 2 + (−1) = 2; sum of pairwise products (1·2) + (1·−1) + (2·−1) = −1; and product of roots 1·2·(−1) = −2, matching the coefficients with alternating signs.

History

Viète published the relations for positive roots in the late 16th century; the general form for all roots was established by Albert Girard in 1629. The formulas are fundamental in algebra and are used widely in solving polynomial equations and in the theory of symmetric polynomials.
