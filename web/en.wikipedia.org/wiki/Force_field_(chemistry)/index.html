Force field (chemistry)

FieldComputational chemistry / molecular modelling
PurposeEstimate energies and forces between atoms in molecular simulations
ExamplesAMBER, CHARMM, OPLS, GROMOS, MMFF

In the context of chemistry and molecular modelling, a force field is a computational method used to estimate the forces between atoms within molecules and between molecules. More precisely, it refers to the functional form and parameter sets used to calculate the potential energy of a system of atoms in molecular mechanics or molecular dynamics simulations.

Functional form

A typical force field sums bonded terms — harmonic bond stretching and angle bending, periodic torsional (dihedral) terms, and improper dihedrals — with non-bonded terms: Lennard-Jones potentials for van der Waals interactions and Coulomb potentials between partial charges for electrostatics. Cross terms and special corrections may be added in more sophisticated fields.

Parameterisation and use

Parameters (equilibrium lengths, force constants, charges) are fitted to experimental data and high-level quantum-mechanical calculations. Widely used families include AMBER and CHARMM for biomolecules, OPLS for organic liquids, GROMOS, and MMFF for small drug-like molecules. Because bonds cannot break in classical force fields, reactive systems need special methods such as ReaxFF. Force fields underpin simulations of protein folding, drug binding and materials properties.
