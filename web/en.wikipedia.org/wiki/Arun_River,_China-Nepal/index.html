Arun River, China–Nepal

The Arun River gorge in eastern Nepal

The Arun River (Nepali: अरुण नदी) is a transnational river that flows through Tibet (China) and eastern Nepal. It is the largest of the three main tributaries of the Sapta Koshi river system. In Tibet it is known as the Phung Chu (or Peng Qu), rising near Mount Shishapangma in Nyalam County, before cutting south through the Himalayas in some of the deepest gorges on Earth.

Course

From its source in the Tibetan Himalaya, the river flows east and then south, crossing into Nepal near Kimathanka. It then runs south through Sankhuwasabha and Bhojpur districts, dropping from high Himalayan valleys to the subtropical hills. The Arun contributes roughly a third — about 37% — of the Sapta Koshi's discharge, the largest share of any tributary, reflecting its great drainage of snow- and glacier-fed waters north of the main Himalayan crest. Near Tribeni it joins the Sun Koshi and Tamur rivers to form the Sapta Koshi, which flows on into India as the Koshi and ultimately the Ganges.

Hydropower

The steep gradient of the Arun has made it a focus of hydropower development. The Arun III project (900 MW) in Sankhuwasabha District, being built by an Indian public-sector developer, is among the largest hydroelectric projects in Nepal, with further projects (including Upper Arun) planned in the basin. Development has been accompanied by debate about the remoteness of the valley, road access (the Koshi highway corridor), environmental effects and benefits for local communities.

The Arun valley

The Arun valley is notable for its exceptional range of ecosystems — from alpine terrain near Makalu to subtropical forest — and lies adjacent to Makalu Barun National Park. The valley is home to Rai, Sherpa, Tamang and other communities; Tumlingtar, the valley's main town, has an airport linking the region to Kathmandu.
