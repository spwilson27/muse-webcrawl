Lake Dunstan

Lake Dunstan is a man-made lake and reservoir in the Central Otago region of New Zealand's South Island. It was created on the Clutha River (Mata-Au) by the Clyde Dam, a hydroelectric dam completed in 1992, with the lake filling between 1992 and 1993.

Geography

The lake extends from Clyde through the Cromwell Gorge to Bannockburn and up the Kawarau Arm toward the outlet of Lake Wakatipu. The town of Cromwell sits on its shore; the original old town of Cromwell was partly flooded by the reservoir, and its historic precinct was preserved above the new shoreline.

Recreation

Lake Dunstan is used for boating, rowing, fishing for brown and rainbow trout, and swimming. The Lake Dunstan Trail, a cycling and walking track along the Cromwell Gorge opened in 2021, is a popular attraction.

Environmental issues

The lake has experienced growth of the invasive aquatic weed Lagarosiphon major, which is managed by the regional council and Land Information New Zealand.
