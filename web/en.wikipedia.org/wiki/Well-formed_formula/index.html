Well-formed formula

In mathematical logic, a well-formed formula (often abbreviated wff, pronounced "wiff") is a finite sequence of symbols from a given alphabet that forms part of the grammar of a formal language. A formal language is usually thought of as the set of all its well-formed formulas.

Definition

Well-formedness is defined inductively: in propositional logic, propositional variables are wffs; if φ is a wff then so is ¬φ; and if φ and ψ are wffs, so are (φ ∧ ψ), (φ ∨ ψ), and (φ → ψ). In first-order logic, terms and atomic formulas are built from variables, constants, function symbols, and predicates, and quantifiers (∀, ∃) applied to formulas again yield formulas.

Role

Only well-formed formulas can be assigned truth values, appear as axioms, or be manipulated by rules of inference; the concept separates meaningful sentences of the language from arbitrary symbol strings.
