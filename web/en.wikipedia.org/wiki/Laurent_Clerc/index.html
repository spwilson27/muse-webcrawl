Laurent Clerc

Laurent Clerc (1785–1869).
Born26 December 1785, Balme-les-Grottes, France
Died18 July 1869, Hartford, Connecticut
Known forCo-founding the first school for the deaf in North America

Louis Laurent Marie Clerc (26 December 1785 – 18 July 1869) was a French deaf educator, regarded as the founder of deaf education in the United States. Deafened in infancy, he was educated at the Royal Institution for the Deaf in Paris under the Abbé Sicard, where he became a teacher.

America

In 1816 Clerc travelled to the United States with Thomas Hopkins Gallaudet, who had come to Europe to learn methods of deaf education. During the voyage Clerc taught Gallaudet French Sign Language while Gallaudet taught him English. Together they raised funds and in 1817 opened the American Asylum for the Deaf (now the American School for the Deaf) in Hartford, Connecticut — the first permanent school for deaf students in North America — with Clerc as its first deaf teacher.

Legacy

The sign language Clerc taught, blending French Sign Language with local signs, evolved into American Sign Language (ASL). Clerc taught in Hartford for over fifty years and is honoured as a central figure in Deaf history and culture.
