Kaddish (Aramaic: קַדִּישׁ, "holy" or "sanctification") is a hymn praising God that is recited during Jewish prayer services. The central theme of the Kaddish is the magnification and sanctification of God's name. In the liturgy, different versions of the Kaddish are used functionally as separators of the different sections of the service.

The best-known version is the Mourner's Kaddish, recited by mourners for a deceased parent during the eleven months after burial and on the anniversary of death (yahrzeit), as well as by mourners for other close relatives. Notably, the prayer itself contains no mention of death or mourning; it is an affirmation of life and of God's greatness in the face of loss.

Forms

  • Half Kaddish (Chatzi Kaddish) – the core text, used as a divider within services.
  • Full Kaddish (Kaddish Shalem) – adds a prayer for the acceptance of prayers, concluding major sections.
  • Rabbis' Kaddish (Kaddish de-Rabbanan) – adds a blessing for teachers and students of Torah, recited after study.
  • Mourner's Kaddish – the Rabbis' version without the Torah-study paragraph.
  • Burial Kaddish (Kaddish de-Ithadata) – the longest form, recited at funerals and at the completion of a tractate, with a passage on resurrection and the world to come.

Text and origins

The prayer is written mostly in Aramaic, the vernacular of Babylonian Jewry, with Hebrew phrases drawn from Ezekiel 38:23 ("I will magnify Myself and sanctify Myself") and Daniel 2:20. Its opening line, Yitgadal v'yitkadash sh'mei raba ("Magnified and sanctified be His great name"), is answered by the congregation with Yehei sh'mei raba m'vorach ("May His great name be blessed for ever and for all eternity"). The Kaddish took shape in the post-Talmudic period and was associated with mourning from the medieval period onward.

Cultural references

The Kaddish has inspired works including Allen Ginsberg's poem "Kaddish" (1961) for his mother Naomi, Leonard Bernstein's Symphony No. 3 "Kaddish" (1963), and Maurice Ravel's Kaddisch from the Deux mélodies hébraïques.
