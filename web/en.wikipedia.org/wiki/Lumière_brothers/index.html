Lumière brothers

Auguste and Louis Lumière
BornAuguste: 19 October 1862; Louis: 5 October 1864 — Besançon, France
DiedAuguste: 10 April 1954; Louis: 6 June 1948
Known forInventing the Cinématographe; pioneering cinema and colour photography (Autochrome)

The Lumière brothers — Auguste Marie Louis Nicolas Lumière (1862–1954) and Louis Jean Lumière (1864–1948) — were French manufacturers of photography equipment, best known for their Cinématographe motion picture system and the short films they produced between 1895 and 1905. They are often credited as the inventors of cinema as a public medium.

The Lumière brothers and their Cinématographe

The Cinématographe

Working in their father's photographic factory in Lyon, the brothers patented the Cinématographe in 1895 — a single lightweight device that functioned as camera, printer and projector, using 35 mm film with round perforations pulled through by a claw mechanism inspired by a sewing machine. On 28 December 1895, at the Salon Indien of the Grand Café in Paris, they gave the first commercial public screening of projected motion pictures to a paying audience, with a programme including Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory and L'Arroseur arrosé, often called the first comedy film.

Later work

The brothers sent cameramen around the world to film actualities and exhibit their device, effectively launching both cinema and newsreel. Believing film was "an invention without any future" commercially, they turned back to photography research and in 1907 introduced the Autochrome process, the first commercially successful colour photography method. Their former villa in Lyon now houses the Institut Lumière museum.
