Westport (Irish: Cathair na Mart, "stone fort of the beeves") is a town in County Mayo, Ireland, at the southeast corner of Clew Bay on the west coast. It is one of Ireland's few planned towns, laid out in the late 18th century in a Georgian style, and is a popular tourist base for Croagh Patrick, Clew Bay's islands, and the Great Western Greenway.

History

The original settlement, Cathair na Mart, stood near the coast. In the 1780s the Browne family, Marquesses of Sligo, commissioned the architect James Wyatt to design a new town beside their seat, Westport House, on the Carrowbeg River. The town was built around a tree-lined canalised river (the Mall) and an octagonal centre (the Octagon), replacing the older village. Westport House, designed by Richard Cassels and later Wyatt, remains a major visitor attraction.

Tourism and culture

Westport has repeatedly won Irish Tidy Towns awards and was named among Ireland's best places to live and retire. Pilgrims climbing Croagh Patrick — Ireland's holy mountain, associated with Saint Patrick — often start from nearby Murrisk. The Great Western Greenway, a 42 km walking and cycling trail to Achill Island along a disused railway, begins in the town. The town is also known for its pubs and traditional music sessions, and for Matt Molloy's pub, owned by the Chieftains' flautist.

Transport

Westport railway station is the terminus of the Dublin–Westport line, and the town is served by the N5 and N59 national roads. Ireland West Airport (Knock) lies about 60 km to the east.
