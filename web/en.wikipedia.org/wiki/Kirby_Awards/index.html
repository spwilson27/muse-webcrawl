Kirby Awards

Jack Kirby Awards Full name: Jack Kirby Award for Best Single Issue
Presented for: Excellence in American comic books
First awarded: 1985
Last awarded: 1987
Succeeded by: Eisner Awards and Harvey Awards (1988)

The Jack Kirby Awards were a series of awards in the American comic book industry, presented from 1985 to 1987 and named after the influential writer-artist Jack Kirby. Voted on by comics professionals and administered by Amazing Heroes magazine, they recognised achievement in categories such as best single issue, best continuing series, best black-and-white series, best finite series, best writer and best artist.

History

The awards were sponsored by Fantagraphics Books' magazine Amazing Heroes. Winners included Alan Moore and Dave Gibbons' Watchmen and Frank Miller's The Dark Knight Returns in the finite/limited series categories, reflecting the celebrated output of mid-1980s American comics.

Discontinuation and successors

The Kirby Awards were discontinued after 1987 following a dispute over ownership of the awards. In 1988 the honours split into two successor awards: the Eisner Awards, named for Will Eisner and administered in connection with San Diego Comic-Con, and the Harvey Awards, named for Harvey Kurtzman and voted on by professionals. Both remain among the most prestigious awards in the comics field.
