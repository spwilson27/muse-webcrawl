Guangdong (Chinese: 广东; pinyin: Guǎngdōng; historically romanised as Kwangtung or Canton Province) is a coastal province in South China, on the north shore of the South China Sea. The provincial capital is Guangzhou. With a population of 127 million as of 2021 and an area of 179,800 km², Guangdong is the most populous province of China and among the most densely populated and richest. Its economy is larger than any other province in the nation and the largest sub-national economy in the world.

The province borders Fujian to the northeast, Jiangxi and Hunan to the north, Guangxi to the west, and the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macau to the south. The Pearl River Delta, encompassing Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Dongguan, Foshan and other cities, is one of the largest urban agglomerations on Earth and a global hub of manufacturing and technology.

History

The region was incorporated into the Chinese empire under the Qin dynasty after the Qin conquest of the Baiyue peoples in 214 BCE, and Guangzhou (then Panyu) became an early centre of maritime trade on the Maritime Silk Road. During the Qing dynasty, Guangzhou was for decades the only Chinese port open to Western trade under the Canton System. Guangdong was a centre of anti-Qing revolutionary activity, and Sun Yat-sen, founder of the Republic of China, was born in the province.

After 1978, Guangdong was at the forefront of China's reform and opening up: three of the first four Special Economic Zones — Shenzhen, Zhuhai and Shantou — were established in the province. Migrants from throughout China have made it the country's most populous province since 2005.

Economy

Guangdong has had the largest provincial GDP in China since 1989. The Pearl River Delta is a manufacturing powerhouse, producing a large share of the world's electronics, textiles, toys and consumer goods, while Shenzhen has become a leading technology centre, home to companies such as Huawei, Tencent and DJI. Guangzhou hosts the Canton Fair, China's largest trade fair. The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge, opened in 2018, links the province with the two special administrative regions, and the Greater Bay Area initiative aims to integrate the region further.

Culture

Guangdong is the home of Cantonese (Yue) language and culture, as well as Hakka and Teochew (Chaozhou) communities. Cantonese cuisine is one of the great culinary traditions of China, famed for dim sum, roast meats and delicate soups. Cantonese opera, Cantonese popular music (Cantopop, produced largely in Hong Kong) and lion dance are prominent cultural exports. The province is also the ancestral home of much of the historic Chinese diaspora in Southeast Asia and North America.
