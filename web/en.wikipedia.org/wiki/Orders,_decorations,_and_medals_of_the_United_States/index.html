Orders, decorations, and medals of the United States

Highest military award: Medal of Honor

Highest civilian awards: Presidential Medal of Freedom; Congressional Gold Medal

Administered by: Federal departments and the armed services

The United States maintains no orders of chivalry in the European sense — the Constitution forbids titles of nobility — but it awards a wide range of decorations and medals through the armed forces and federal agencies to recognise heroism, meritorious service and campaign participation.

Military awards

The highest American military decoration is the Medal of Honor, awarded by the President in the name of Congress for conspicuous gallantry above and beyond the call of duty. Below it stand the service crosses (Distinguished Service Cross, Navy Cross, Air Force Cross and Coast Guard Cross), the Distinguished Service Medals, the Silver Star, the Legion of Merit, the Distinguished Flying Cross, the Bronze Star Medal and the Purple Heart for those wounded or killed in action. Campaign and service medals, such as the National Defense Service Medal and expeditionary medals, mark participation in particular operations.

Civilian awards

The Presidential Medal of Freedom, established in 1963, is the nation's highest civilian honour, alongside the Congressional Gold Medal, struck by act of Congress for distinguished achievement. Other civilian honours include the National Medal of Arts, the National Medal of Science and agency awards such as NASA's Distinguished Service Medal.

Wearing and precedence

Each service publishes an order of precedence for uniforms. Awards are worn as ribbons and medals, with devices such as oak leaf clusters and service stars denoting repeat awards.
