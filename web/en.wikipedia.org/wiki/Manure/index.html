Manure

Manure is organic matter used as organic fertiliser in agriculture. Most manure consists of animal faeces; other sources include compost and green manure. Manures contribute to soil fertility by adding organic matter and nutrients, such as nitrogen, that are utilised by bacteria, fungi and other organisms in the soil food web.

Types

Animal manure comes principally from cattle, horses, pigs, sheep, chickens and other livestock, often mixed with bedding material ("farmyard manure"). Green manures are crops grown specifically to be ploughed into the soil, such as legumes that fix nitrogen. Compost is decomposed organic material from mixed sources.

Use and management

Manure is usually aged or composted before application to reduce pathogens, weed seeds and odour, and to stabilise nitrogen. It is spread on fields with a manure spreader or applied as slurry. Poor management can cause nutrient runoff and water pollution, ammonia and greenhouse gas emissions, and contamination of produce; many jurisdictions regulate storage (in pits, lagoons or covered heaps) and spreading times. Biogas can be captured by anaerobic digestion of manure, which also reduces methane emissions.

History

Farmers have used animal dung to fertilise fields for millennia; it was the main source of plant nitrogen before the development of synthetic fertilisers in the 20th century, and remains essential to mixed farming systems worldwide.
