Squad

In military terminology, a squad is among the smallest sub-units of an army, typically consisting of roughly eight to fourteen soldiers and commanded by a non-commissioned officer such as a sergeant or corporal. Several squads make up a platoon. In British and Commonwealth usage, the equivalent unit is usually called a section.

Organisation

A United States Army infantry squad has nine soldiers led by a staff sergeant and is divided into two fire teams of four, each led by a team leader. Other armies vary: the British Army section has eight soldiers commanded by a corporal, split into two four-man fire teams ("Charlie" and "Delta"). Squad organisation balances the span of control of a single leader with enough personnel to manoeuvre, provide supporting fire and absorb casualties.

Role

The squad is the smallest unit expected to manoeuvre as a coherent body in combat, employing fire and movement: one element provides covering fire while the other advances. Squad weapons typically include rifles, a light machine gun or automatic rifle, and grenade launchers.

Other uses

The word has been extended beyond the military: police and emergency services organise squads for specialised duties, and in sport a squad denotes the full roster of players from which a team is selected for a match.
