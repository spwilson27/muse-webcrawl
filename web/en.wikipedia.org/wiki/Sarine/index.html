The Sarine (French: Sarine; German: Saane) is a river of Switzerland, 128 kilometres long, that flows through the cantons of Valais, Bern, Fribourg, Vaud and Aargau before joining the Aare. It is one of the principal rivers of the Swiss Plateau's western region and gives its name to the Sarine district of the canton of Fribourg.

The Sarine winding through the old town of Fribourg in its deeply incised meanders.

Course

The Sarine rises at the Sanetsch Glacier in the Bernese Alps and flows north through the resort of Gstaad in the Saanenland. It then turns through the Pays-d'Enhaut and the Gruyère region, where it is dammed to form Lake Gruyère (Lac de la Gruyère), one of Switzerland's larger artificial lakes. Below the lake it cuts a deep gorge through the city of Fribourg, whose medieval old town occupies a rocky spur inside one of its meanders, before flowing onward past Schiffenen (where it is again dammed) and joining the Aare downstream of Bern.

Significance

The river forms part of the traditional language boundary between French-speaking and German-speaking Switzerland, sometimes called the "Röstigraben". Several hydroelectric dams on its course supply power to the canton of Fribourg, and its gorges and lakes are popular recreation areas.
