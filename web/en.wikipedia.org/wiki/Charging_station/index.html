A charging station, also known as a charge point or electric vehicle supply equipment (EVSE), is a piece of equipment that supplies electrical power for recharging plug-in electric vehicles (including battery electric cars, trucks, buses, and plug-in hybrids). Charging stations range from simple home wall connectors to public rapid-charging hubs along highways.

Levels and speeds

Level 1 charging uses a standard household outlet (120 V in North America) and adds a few miles of range per hour. Level 2 uses 240 V AC (or 400 V three-phase in Europe) and typically delivers 7–22 kW. DC fast charging bypasses the vehicle's onboard charger, delivering 50 kW to over 350 kW and can charge a compatible vehicle to 80% in 20–40 minutes.

Connectors and networks

Connector standards include Type 1 (J1772) and Type 2 (Mennekes) for AC, and CCS, CHAdeMO, China's GB/T, and Tesla's North American Charging Standard (NACS, standardized as SAE J3400) for DC. Public charging is provided by networks such as Tesla Supercharger, Ionity, Electrify America, and ChargePoint, often accessed via apps or RFID cards. Battery swapping and inductive (wireless) charging are alternative approaches.
