Adherens junction

Adherens junctions (or zonula adherens, intermediate junction, or "belt desmosome") are protein complexes that occur at cell–cell junctions in epithelial and endothelial tissues, usually more basal than tight junctions. They consist of cadherin transmembrane proteins whose extracellular domains bind cadherins on the neighbouring cell and whose cytoplasmic tails link, via catenins, to the actin cytoskeleton.

Structure

The core of the junction is formed by classical cadherins such as E-cadherin in epithelia and VE-cadherin in endothelia. Calcium-dependent homophilic binding between cadherin ectodomains on opposing membranes holds the cells together. Inside the cell, the cadherin tail binds β-catenin and p120-catenin; β-catenin in turn binds α-catenin, which connects the complex to actin filaments, allowing the junction to bear and transmit mechanical tension.

Function

Adherens junctions maintain tissue integrity, establish cell polarity in cooperation with tight junctions, and participate in signalling pathways — notably the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, since β-catenin released from junctions can act as a transcriptional co-activator. They are essential in embryonic development and morphogenesis, and their disassembly is a hallmark of the epithelial–mesenchymal transition; loss of E-cadherin is associated with tumour invasion and metastasis.
