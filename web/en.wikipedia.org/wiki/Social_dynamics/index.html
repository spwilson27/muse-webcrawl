Social dynamics

Social dynamics is the study of how individuals and groups interact, influence one another and change over time. Drawing on sociology, social psychology, economics, network science and complexity theory, it examines phenomena such as the spread of opinions and behaviours, the formation and dissolution of groups, cooperation and conflict, and the emergence of norms and institutions.

Approaches

Classic work includes Kurt Lewin's group dynamics and field theory, Jacob Moreno's sociometry, and studies of conformity, obedience and social influence. Modern research uses social network analysis and agent-based modelling to study diffusion, segregation, polarisation and tipping points: Thomas Schelling's models showed how mild individual preferences can produce strong collective segregation, and Mark Granovetter's threshold models explained how collective behaviour can cascade once a critical mass is reached.

Applications

Understanding social dynamics informs public-health campaigns, marketing, organisational design, crowd management and the study of online communities and misinformation, where information spreads through network structures subject to homophily and filter effects.
