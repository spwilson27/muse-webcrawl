The Goodyear Tire & Rubber Company is an American multinational tire manufacturing company founded in 1898 by Frank Seiberling and headquartered in Akron, Ohio. Goodyear manufactures tires for automobiles, commercial trucks, light trucks, motorcycles, SUVs, race cars, airplanes, farm equipment and heavy earth-mover machinery. It also makes and sells rubber-related chemicals for various applications.

Though the company was named after Charles Goodyear, the inventor of vulcanized rubber, it was not founded by him nor by any member of his family. Goodyear was founded in 1898 and initially made bicycle and carriage tires, growing with the advent of the automobile. It became the world's largest tire company in 1916 and adopted the slogan "More people ride on Goodyear tires than on any other kind."

History

The first Goodyear factory opened in Akron, Ohio, in 1898. The thirteen original employees manufactured bicycle and carriage tires, rubber horseshoe pads, and poker chips. The company grew with the ascent of the horseless carriage. In 1901, founder Frank Seiberling provided racing tires to Henry Ford, and in 1908 Ford fitted the Model T with Goodyear tires.

In 1911, Goodyear's winged-foot trademark became an iconic symbol. In 1926, Goodyear was the world's largest rubber company. During the Second World War, Goodyear manufactured F4U Corsair fighter planes under license as the Goodyear FG Corsair, and its blimps were used for reconnaissance and convoy escort.

Goodyear blimp

The company is famous worldwide for the Goodyear Blimp. The first blimp, Pilgrim, flew in 1925. The airships became a familiar sight at major American sporting events, providing aerial television coverage. Goodyear has operated a fleet of blimps for promotional purposes for nearly a century, and modern semi-rigid airships such as Wingfoot One continue the tradition.

Products and brands

Goodyear sells tires under the Goodyear, Dunlop (in certain regions), Kelly, Fulda, Debica, Sava and Cooper brands; it acquired Cooper Tire & Rubber Company in 2021 for about $2.5 billion. It also produces aviation tires, off-the-road tires for mining and construction, and racing tires — for many years it was the sole tire supplier to NASCAR and a major supplier in Formula One until 1998.
