Filippo Tommaso Emilio Marinetti (22 December 1876 – 2 December 1944) was an Italian poet, editor, art theorist, and founder of the Futurist movement. Born in Alexandria, Egypt, to an Italian lawyer father, he was educated in Alexandria, Paris, and Italy, and began his career writing French-language symbolist poetry and editing the journal Poesia.

Futurism

On 20 February 1909, Marinetti published The Founding and Manifesto of Futurism on the front page of the Paris newspaper Le Figaro. The manifesto glorified speed, machinery, youth, and violence, and declared war on museums, libraries, and the cult of the past: "We want to glorify war — the world's only hygiene." It launched Futurism, an avant-garde movement that spread through painting (Boccioni, Balla, Carrà, Severini), sculpture, music, theatre, architecture, and literature across Europe.

Marinetti tirelessly promoted the movement through manifestos, provocative public performances (serate), and international tours. His literary experiments culminated in the "words-in-freedom" (parole in libertà) style, exemplified by the sound-poem novel Zang Tumb Tumb (1914), which rendered the Battle of Adrianople typographically. He also published manifestos on Futurist theatre, cinema, dance, cooking (the Manifesto of Futurist Cooking, 1930, notoriously attacked pasta), and lust.

Politics

Marinetti was an ardent Italian nationalist and interventionist who volunteered in the First World War. In 1919 he co-authored the programme of the Fasci Italiani di Combattimento with Benito Mussolini, and later co-wrote the Fascist Manifesto. Relations with the regime cooled as Futurism competed with officially favoured styles, but Marinetti remained broadly loyal, accepted membership in the Royal Academy of Italy in 1929, and volunteered for service in Ethiopia and on the Eastern Front in the Second World War despite his age. His entanglement with Fascism has shadowed his reputation ever since.

Death and legacy

Marinetti died of a heart attack in Bellagio on 2 December 1944. He is regarded as one of the seminal figures of the historical avant-garde: Futurism's manifesto form, typographic experimentation, and performance practices influenced Dada, Surrealism, Russian Futurism, and much of 20th-century experimental art, even as his politics remain deeply controversial.
