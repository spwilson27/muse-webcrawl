The iPod is a discontinued series of portable media players designed and marketed by Apple Inc. The first version was released on October 23, 2001, about 8½ months after the Macintosh version of iTunes was released. Apple sold an estimated 450 million iPod products as of 2022, when the last model, the iPod Touch, was discontinued.

History

The original iPod offered 5 GB of storage — marketed as "1,000 songs in your pocket" — a mechanical scroll wheel, and FireWire connectivity, initially for Mac only. The line expanded to include the iPod Mini, the screenless iPod Shuffle, the tiny iPod Nano, the video-capable iPod Classic, and ultimately the iPod Touch, an iPhone-like device with a touchscreen and Wi-Fi introduced in 2007.

Combined with the iTunes Store (launched 2003), the iPod reshaped the music industry, making legal digital downloads mainstream and establishing Apple as a consumer-electronics leader — a trajectory that led directly to the iPhone.

Models

  • iPod (Classic) — hard-drive based flagship, six generations, 2001–2014.
  • iPod Mini — smaller anodised-aluminium model, 2004–2005.
  • iPod Nano — flash-memory successor to the Mini, seven generations, 2005–2017.
  • iPod Shuffle — ultra-compact, no screen, 2005–2017.
  • iPod Touch — touchscreen model running iOS, 2007–2022.

Legacy

The click wheel interface became an iconic piece of industrial design. As smartphones absorbed music playback, dedicated players declined, and Apple ended the iPod line in May 2022, closing a product family credited with saving and transforming the company.
