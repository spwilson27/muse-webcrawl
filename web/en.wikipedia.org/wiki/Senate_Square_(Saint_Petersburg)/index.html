Senate Square (Saint Petersburg)

Senate Square (Russian: Сенатская площадь, Senatskaya ploshchad) is a city square in Saint Petersburg, Russia, on the left bank of the Bolshaya Neva, in front of Saint Isaac's Cathedral. It takes its name from the Senate building, constructed in the Neoclassical style between 1829 and 1834.

The Bronze Horseman

The square is dominated by the Bronze Horseman, the equestrian monument to Peter the Great commissioned by Catherine the Great and sculpted by Étienne Maurice Falconet. Unveiled in 1782, the statue stands on the Thunder Stone, one of the largest stones ever moved by human effort, and became a symbol of the city, immortalised in Pushkin's 1833 poem The Bronze Horseman.

Decembrist revolt

On 26 December [O.S. 14 December] 1825, the square was the scene of the Decembrist revolt, when some 3,000 soldiers assembled to protest the accession of Nicholas I and demand constitutional reform. The uprising was suppressed by artillery fire loyal to the tsar. In Soviet times the square was renamed Decembrists' Square (1925), reverting to Senate Square in 2008.
