Historical revisionism

Historical revisionism is the reinterpretation of historical evidence and the re-examination of accepted accounts of the past. In its legitimate, scholarly sense, revision is the normal process of historiography: new documents come to light, surviving sources are reread, and new questions and methods change how events are understood. In popular and polemical usage, the term is often applied pejoratively to denialist or politically motivated distortions of the record — a usage historians distinguish from genuine revision by the revisionist's adherence to evidence and scholarly standards.

Legitimate revision

Canonical historical interpretations are routinely revised. Examples include the reevaluation of the Middle Ages (once dismissed as a "Dark Age"), reinterpretations of the causes of the First World War as archives opened, the social-history turn of the mid-20th century that refocused attention from statesmen to ordinary people, and postcolonial histories that restored the perspectives of colonised peoples. Such revisions proceed through peer-reviewed publication, citation of sources, and open scholarly debate.

Negationism and distortion

The term is also applied to pseudohistorical movements that deny established atrocities or rewrite the past for political ends — most notoriously Holocaust denial, which historians and courts have shown to rest on falsified or cherry-picked evidence. Because deniers borrow the label "revisionist," many historians prefer the term negationism for such literature and reserve "revisionism" for the ordinary scholarly process.

Why interpretations change

Revision arises from new evidence (declassified archives, archaeology, DNA analysis), new methods (cliometrics, oral history, environmental history), and new standpoints, as each generation asks questions shaped by its own concerns. The distinction between revision and denial is methodological: legitimate revisionists test claims against the full evidentiary record and accept unwelcome conclusions; denialists begin with the conclusion and discard contrary evidence.
