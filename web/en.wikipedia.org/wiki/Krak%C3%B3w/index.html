Kraków is the second-largest city in Poland and one of its oldest, situated on the Vistula River in the Lesser Poland region. It was the capital of the Kingdom of Poland until 1596 and remains a leading academic, cultural and economic centre.

History

Tradition dates the city's founding to the legendary ruler Krakus. Kraków became a bishopric in 1000 and the coronation city of Polish kings at Wawel Cathedral. The Jagiellonian University, founded in 1364, is among Europe's oldest.

Old Town

The Old Town, centred on the Main Market Square (Rynek Główny) — the largest medieval town square in Europe — was among the first sites inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1978. Landmarks include St Mary's Basilica, the Cloth Hall and Wawel Castle.

Twentieth century

Kraków's Jewish district of Kazimierz was devastated by the Holocaust. The socialist model district of Nowa Huta was built after the Second World War, and the city's Archbishop Karol Wojtyła later became Pope John Paul II.
