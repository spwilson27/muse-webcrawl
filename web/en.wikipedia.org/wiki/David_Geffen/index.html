David Lawrence Geffen (born February 21, 1943) is an American businessman, record executive, film producer and philanthropist. He founded Asylum Records in 1971 and Geffen Records in 1980, produced films and Broadway musicals, and in 1994 co-founded the studio DreamWorks SKG with Steven Spielberg and Jeffrey Katzenberg. He is regarded as one of the most powerful figures in the history of the American entertainment industry.

Early life and career

Geffen was born in Brooklyn to Jewish immigrant parents and grew up in Borough Park. After dropping out of college he started in the mailroom of the William Morris Agency and rose rapidly as a talent agent, with an uncanny ear for the singer-songwriter generation. As a manager he guided Laura Nyro and Crosby, Stills & Nash; in 1971 he founded Asylum Records, home to the Eagles, Jackson Browne, Linda Ronstadt and Joni Mitchell, and merged it with Elektra Records in 1973.

Geffen Records and film

After a period at Warner Bros. film studio and a mistaken diagnosis of bladder cancer that led him to retire briefly, Geffen founded Geffen Records in 1980. The label signed John Lennon — whose final album, Double Fantasy, was released weeks before his murder — and later Aerosmith, Guns N' Roses and Nirvana, whose breakthrough Nevermind appeared on Geffen's DGC imprint. Through the Geffen Film Company he produced hits including Risky Business, Beetlejuice and Interview with the Vampire, and on Broadway he backed Dreamgirls, Cats and Little Shop of Horrors. In 1990 he sold Geffen Records to MCA for stock that eventually made him a billionaire.

DreamWorks

In 1994 Geffen, Spielberg and Katzenberg launched DreamWorks SKG, the first major new Hollywood studio in decades, producing films such as Saving Private Ryan, American Beauty and the animated Shrek. The live-action studio was sold to Paramount in 2005.

Philanthropy and collecting

Geffen is one of America's most prominent philanthropists and art collectors. His gifts include $200 million to the UCLA medical school, renamed the David Geffen School of Medicine, a major donation to the Academy Museum of Motion Pictures, $100 million toward the renovation of Lincoln Center's Avery Fisher Hall — renamed David Geffen Hall — and a $150 million gift to build a new gallery building at the Los Angeles County Museum of Art. His collection of postwar American art, including works by Pollock, de Kooning and Johns, is among the finest in private hands.
