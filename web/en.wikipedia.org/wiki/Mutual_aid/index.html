Mutual aid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mutual aid is the voluntary reciprocal exchange of resources and services for mutual benefit. As a social and political concept it is central to anarchist theory through Peter Kropotkin's Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution (1902), which argued — from zoology, anthropology and history — that cooperation is a major factor in evolution and in human flourishing, and that mutual aid is practised spontaneously by animals, “savage” and medieval societies, and modern communities alike, independently of the state.

In organisational practice the term covers friendly societies, cooperatives, mutual insurance, community fridges and contemporary mutual-aid networks that mobilised notably during the COVID-19 pandemic. Kropotkin contrasted mutual aid with both competitive individualism and state welfare, seeing it as the living root of a free society; later writers in evolutionary biology (the study of cooperation, kin selection and reciprocity) have revisited his claims, usually concluding that he overstated mutual aid against competition but identified a real and neglected factor.
