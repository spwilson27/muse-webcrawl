Moto3 is the lightweight, entry-level class of the FIM Grand Prix motorcycle racing world championship, below Moto2 and MotoGP. Introduced in 2012, it replaced the 125cc two-stroke class with 250 cc four-stroke single-cylinder machinery. It is renowned for extremely close racing, with large packs of riders slipstreaming one another and races frequently decided by hundredths of a second at the final corner.

Regulations

Moto3 bikes use a single-cylinder 250 cc four-stroke engine with a maximum bore of 81 mm and a rev limit of 13,500 rpm, producing around 60 horsepower. The minimum combined weight of bike and rider is 152 kg. Unlike Moto2, the engine is not a single-make formula: KTM and Honda have been the principal manufacturers, with KTM and its sister brands Husqvarna and GasGas dominating entries. Riders must be at least 18 years old (with limited exceptions historically for winners of junior championships) and may not remain in the class beyond the age of 28.

Character of racing

Because the low-powered machines reward slipstreaming, qualifying and race tactics revolve around towing and positioning. It serves as the first world-championship step for teenage riders graduating from junior series such as the Red Bull MotoGP Rookies Cup and the FIM JuniorGP championship.
