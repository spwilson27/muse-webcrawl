Akio Morita

Akio Morita (盛田 昭夫, Morita Akio; 26 January 1921 – 3 October 1999) was a Japanese entrepreneur and co-founder of the Sony Corporation, together with Masaru Ibuka. One of the most prominent Japanese businessmen of the twentieth century, Morita built Sony from a small Tokyo electronics workshop into a global consumer-electronics and entertainment empire, personally championing products such as the transistor radio and the Walkman, and making "Sony" one of the world's best-known brands.

Early life

Morita was born in Nagoya into a family that had brewed sake for centuries; he was expected to inherit the business but instead studied physics at Osaka Imperial University, graduating in 1944. As a naval officer in the Second World War he worked on thermal guidance weapons, where he met the engineer Masaru Ibuka. In 1946 the two founded Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo (Tokyo Telecommunications Engineering Corporation) in a bomb-damaged Tokyo department store, repairing radios and building Japan's first tape recorders.

Building Sony

Morita handled the company's marketing, finance, and overseas expansion while Ibuka led engineering. After licensing the transistor from Western Electric, the company launched Japan's first transistor radio in 1955 and the pocket-sized TR-63 in 1957, a major export success. In 1958 the company renamed itself Sony — from the Latin sonus (sound) and the American slang "sonny" — a short name Morita insisted on so the brand would be recognisable worldwide. Morita founded Sony Corporation of America in 1960 and moved his family to New York for a year to learn American business; Sony became the first Japanese company to list its shares on the New York Stock Exchange, in 1970. He drove the company's move into entertainment, acquiring CBS Records in 1988 and Columbia Pictures in 1989.

The Walkman

Morita's most famous creation arose from his own wish to listen to music on long flights. Against internal scepticism — the device could not record and market research was unfavourable — he ordered the development of a playback-only portable stereo cassette player, launched in 1979 as the Walkman. It created an entirely new product category, sold in the hundreds of millions, and became a defining cultural artefact of the 1980s.

Public life and later years

Morita was Japan's most internationally prominent businessman, serving as vice-chairman of the Keidanren and co-authoring (with politician Shintaro Ishihara) the assertive 1989 book The Japan That Can Say No. His 1986 autobiography Made in Japan became an international bestseller. He suffered a stroke in 1993, resigned as chairman in 1994, and died of pneumonia in Tokyo in 1999.
