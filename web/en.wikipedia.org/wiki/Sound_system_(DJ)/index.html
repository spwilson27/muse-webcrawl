Sound system (DJ)

A sound system is a group of DJs and engineers who work together, and the large custom-built audio equipment they operate, playing reggae, dancehall, and related music at dances and festivals. The sound system concept originates in the ghetto areas of Kingston, Jamaica, in the late 1940s and 1950s, and has exerted a major influence on Jamaican culture and on popular music worldwide.

History

In post-war Kingston, operators such as Tom the Great Sebastian, Duke Reid (the Trojan), and Clement "Coxsone" Dodd built ever-larger rigs of amplifiers and speaker cabinets to attract crowds to outdoor dances, competing fiercely for volume, bass weight, and exclusive records. The sound system's resident disc jockey — the "selector" — played imported American rhythm and blues and later Jamaican recordings, while the deejay (MC) toasted over the records, a practice that became a direct ancestor of rap.

Culture

Sound systems are central to the development of ska, rocksteady, reggae, dub, and dancehall; dub producers such as King Tubby pioneered remixing at the mixing desk. The tradition also introduced the "sound clash", in which rival systems compete before a crowd, and the dubplate, a bespoke exclusive recording praising the system. Jamaican migrants carried the culture to the United Kingdom in the 1960s and 1970s, where systems such as Jah Shaka and Aba Shanti-I shaped British reggae, jungle, dubstep, and grime.

Equipment

A traditional system is built around preamplifiers, valve or transistor amplifiers, and tall stacks of speaker boxes with a strong emphasis on bass reproduction from large "scoop" bins. The rig is assembled and tuned by the crew for each session.
