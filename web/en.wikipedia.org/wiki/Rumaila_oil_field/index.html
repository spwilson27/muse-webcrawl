Rumaila oil field

The Rumaila oil field is a supergiant oil field located in southern Iraq, near the border with Kuwait, roughly 50 km west of Basra. Discovered in 1953 by the Basrah Petroleum Company, it is one of the largest oil fields in the world, with estimated proven reserves of around 17 billion barrels, and it accounts for a major share of Iraq's total oil production.

Geology

Rumaila is an elongated anticline some 80 km long. The principal producing reservoirs are the Cretaceous Mishrif limestone and the Main Pay (Zubair) sandstone, with additional production from other Cretaceous and Tertiary units. The field is divided operationally into North Rumaila and South Rumaila.

History

Production began in the 1950s under the Iraq Petroleum Company group. The field was nationalised in 1972. Output suffered during the Iran–Iraq War and the 1990–91 Gulf War; Iraq alleged that Kuwait was slant-drilling into the Iraqi portion of the reservoir, one of the grievances cited before the 1990 invasion of Kuwait. After 2003 the field was looted and damaged but production recovered.

Development

In 2009 Iraq awarded a technical service contract for Rumaila to a consortium of BP and PetroChina (CNPC), working with Iraq's South Oil Company; the operating organisation became the Rumaila Operating Organisation (ROO). The contract targeted raising production from around 1 million barrels per day toward a plateau of 2.85 million barrels per day, involving extensive drilling, water injection, and rehabilitation of wells and facilities. In 2022 BP and PetroChina established Basra Energy Company to manage their interests in the field.
