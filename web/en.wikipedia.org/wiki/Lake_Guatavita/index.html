Lake Guatavita

Lake Guatavita (Spanish: Laguna de Guatavita) is a small circular lake in the Colombian Andes, in the municipality of Sesquilé in Cundinamarca Department, about 57 kilometres northeast of Bogotá. It lies at an elevation of roughly 3,000 metres within a crater-like depression in a salt-bearing mountain range.

The El Dorado legend

The lake was sacred to the indigenous Muisca people. According to legend, a new Muisca ruler (the zipa) was covered in gold dust and, from a raft, made offerings of gold and emeralds into the lake. This ceremony is the origin of the El Dorado legend and is depicted by the famous golden Muisca raft, now in the Museo del Oro in Bogotá.

Attempts to drain the lake

From the Spanish colonial era onwards, treasure seekers repeatedly attempted to recover gold from the lake. In the 1580s Antonio de Sepúlveda drained part of it by cutting a notch in the rim, recovering some gold before the tunnel collapsed. A British company in the early twentieth century drained the lake completely but found only modest amounts of gold, much of it now in museum collections.

Protection

Since 1965 the lake and its surroundings have been protected by the Colombian government, and further excavation is prohibited.
