Turku

Turku (Swedish: Åbo) is a city in southwestern Finland, at the mouth of the Aura River on the Baltic Sea. Founded at the end of the 13th century, it is Finland's oldest city and was its most important centre — and de facto capital — for centuries. Today it has roughly 200,000 inhabitants and is the regional capital of Southwest Finland.

History

Turku grew around its cathedral, consecrated in 1300, and Turku Castle, founded around 1280. As the chief Swedish administrative and ecclesiastical centre in Finland, it hosted the Royal Academy of Turku, founded in 1640. After Finland became a Russian Grand Duchy in 1809, the capital moved to Helsinki in 1812; the Great Fire of 1827 destroyed most of the city, and the university moved to Helsinki, though the Åbo Akademi (1918) and the University of Turku (1920) later re-established it as a university city.

Economy and culture

Turku is a major port with ferry links to Stockholm and a significant shipbuilding industry — the Meyer Turku yard builds some of the world's largest cruise ships. The city was European Capital of Culture in 2011 and markets itself as Finland's Christmas city and food capital. The surrounding Turku archipelago of tens of thousands of islands is a noted tourist region.
