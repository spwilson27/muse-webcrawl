Ernest Edwin Evans (August 13, 1908 – October 25, 1944) was a United States Navy officer who received the Medal of Honor for his actions as commanding officer of the destroyer USS Johnston (DD-557) during the Battle off Samar in October 1944. Of Cherokee and Creek ancestry, Evans is famous for turning his small destroyer to attack a vastly superior Japanese fleet to protect American escort carriers.

Early life and career

Born in Pawnee, Oklahoma, Evans enlisted in the Navy in 1926 and won an appointment to the U.S. Naval Academy, graduating in 1931. He served in various ships and, after the attack on Pearl Harbor, commanded the elderly destroyer USS Alden in the East Indies campaign. He took command of the new Fletcher-class destroyer Johnston at her commissioning in October 1943, reportedly telling his crew that he intended to command "a fighting ship" and would never retreat.

Battle off Samar

On 25 October 1944, Admiral Kurita's Center Force — including the battleship Yamato — surprised the escort carrier group Taffy 3 off Samar. Without orders, Evans ordered Johnston to attack, laying smoke, firing torpedoes that blew the bow off the cruiser Kumano, and duelling Japanese heavy ships for over two hours. Mortally wounded, Evans continued to direct the fight; Johnston sank with heavy loss of life, and Evans was not among the survivors rescued. His attack, with those of the other Taffy 3 escorts, helped convince Kurita to withdraw. Evans received the Medal of Honor posthumously, and the destroyer escort USS Ernest E. Evans (DE-1023) was named for him.
