APA style (also known as APA format) is a writing style and format for academic documents such as scholarly journal articles and books, defined by the American Psychological Association in its Publication Manual. It is widely used in psychology and the other social sciences.

History

The style originated in a seven-page set of instructions published in 1929 in the Psychological Bulletin by a group of psychologists, anthropologists and business managers. The first full Publication Manual appeared in 1952 as a 61-page supplement; successive editions expanded with the growth of behavioural-science publishing. The 7th edition was released in October 2019.

Key features

APA style uses an author–date in-text citation system: sources are cited parenthetically — (Smith, 2020) — with a full alphabetical reference list. A typical journal article reference is:

Author, A. A., Author, B. B., & Author, C. C. (Year). Title of article. Title of Periodical, volume(issue), pages. https://doi.org/xx.xxxx/xxxxx

The manual also prescribes manuscript formatting (title page, abstract, headings hierarchy, double spacing in student papers), bias-free language guidelines, table and figure formats, and rules for reporting statistics. The 7th edition notably endorsed the singular "they", simplified running heads, and expanded guidance for citing online and social-media sources.
