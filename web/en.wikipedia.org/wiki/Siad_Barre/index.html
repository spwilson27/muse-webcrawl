Siad Barre

Mohamed Siad Barre
Born: c. 1910, Shilavo, Ethiopia
Died: 2 January 1995, Lagos, Nigeria
President of Somalia: 1969–1991
Party: Somali Revolutionary Socialist Party

Mohamed Siad Barre (c. 1910 – 2 January 1995) was a Somali military officer and politician who ruled Somalia from 1969 until 1991. He came to power in a coup d'état following the assassination of President Abdirashid Shermarke and headed the Supreme Revolutionary Council, later establishing the Somali Democratic Republic under a doctrine he called "scientific socialism".

Early career

Born to a pastoralist family of the Marehan clan, Barre served in the Italian colonial police and later the British administration, rising in the Somali National Army after independence in 1960 and becoming its commander in 1965.

Rule

Barre's early government promoted literacy — adopting a Latin-based orthography for Somali in 1972 — women's rights, and anti-clan ("tribalism") campaigns, alongside one-party rule and extensive security services. The disastrous Ogaden War against Ethiopia (1977–78) reversed Somalia's alliances from the Soviet Union to the United States. In the 1980s, armed clan-based rebellions grew; the regime's brutal counterinsurgency in the north, including the destruction of Hargeisa and Burao in 1988, killed tens of thousands of Isaaq civilians.

Overthrow and death

Rebel forces drove Barre from Mogadishu in January 1991, precipitating the collapse of the Somali state and civil war. He fled to Kenya and then Nigeria, dying in exile in Lagos in 1995.
