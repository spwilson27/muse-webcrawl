Derby (horse race)

A derby is a type of horse race named after the Epsom Derby, first run in England in 1780 and named for Edward Smith-Stanley, 12th Earl of Derby. The term has since been applied to many prestigious races for three-year-old Thoroughbreds around the world.

Notable derbies

The Kentucky Derby in the United States, first run in 1875, is the most famous American example. Other major derbies include the Irish Derby, the Prix du Jockey Club (French Derby), the Derby Italiano, and the Australian Derby.

The Epsom Derby

The original Derby is run at Epsom Downs over a mile and a half and is one of the five British Classics.
