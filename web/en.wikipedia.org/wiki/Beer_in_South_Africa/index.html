Beer in South Africa

Beer in South Africa has a long history, encompassing traditional sorghum beer brewed for centuries by indigenous peoples and a modern lager industry dominated for most of the 20th century by South African Breweries (SAB). South Africans consume several billion litres of beer per year, and lager accounts for the overwhelming majority of the market.

Traditional beer

Traditional beer, known as umqombothi in Xhosa and Zulu contexts, is brewed from maize meal, sorghum malt and water, and fermented to a low alcohol content (around 3%). It has an opaque, pinkish, yoghurt-like consistency, a sour taste, and an important social and ritual role in ceremonies including weddings, funerals and coming-of-age rites. Commercially produced sorghum beer is sold in cartons by companies such as United National Breweries under brands including Chibuku.

History of commercial brewing

European-style brewing arrived with Dutch settlement at the Cape in the 17th century. Charles Glass founded the Castle Brewery in Johannesburg in 1895 after discovering that the water of the gold-mining boom town suited lager brewing; the same year, South African Breweries was founded and listed in Johannesburg and London. SAB grew through acquisitions to achieve a near-monopoly of the formal beer market during the 20th century, and in 2002 it merged with Miller Brewing to form SABMiller, which became one of the world's largest brewing groups. In 2016 SABMiller was acquired by Anheuser-Busch InBev, with its South African operations continuing as SAB, a subsidiary of AB InBev.

During apartheid, Black South Africans were for decades prohibited from purchasing "European" liquor, and municipal beer halls selling sorghum beer were a major — and contentious — source of revenue for township administration; beer hall boycotts were a recurring form of protest.

Major brands

Leading South African lagers include Castle Lager (first brewed in 1895), Castle Lite, Hansa Pilsener, Carling Black Label, Amstel Lager (brewed under licence for many years), and Windhoek Lager from neighbouring Namibia, which is popular in the South African market. Heineken established a significant local presence, including a large brewery at Sedibeng opened in 2010.

Craft beer

Since the 1980s, and especially after 2010, a craft beer sector has grown around cities such as Cape Town and Johannesburg, with breweries including Mitchell's (founded 1983 in Knysna), Devil's Peak, Jack Black's and Darling Brew, producing ales, IPAs and other styles that depart from the lager-dominated mainstream.
