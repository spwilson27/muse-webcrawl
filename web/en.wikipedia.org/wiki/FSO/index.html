FSO

FSO (Fabryka Samochodów Osobowych, "Passenger Car Factory") was a Polish automobile manufacturer based in Warsaw. Founded in 1951, it produced cars for over sixty years, and its name became one of the best-known brands of the Polish motor industry.

History

FSO began production with the Warszawa, a licence-built version of the Soviet GAZ-M20 Pobeda. In 1957 it launched the small two-stroke Syrena, designed in-house. From 1967 the factory built the Polski Fiat 125p under licence from Fiat, followed by the FSO Polonez from 1978, a hatchback developed with Fiat underpinnings and styling input from Giorgetto Giugiaro.

Daewoo and later years

After the fall of communism, FSO was taken over by South Korea's Daewoo in 1996, producing models such as the Lanos, Matiz and Nubira. When Daewoo collapsed, production passed through Ukrainian ownership (UkrAVTO/ZAZ). Car production ended in 2011, though the FSO name survived in parts manufacture and the site in Żerań, Warsaw continued in industrial use.

Products

  • FSO Warszawa (1951–1973)
  • FSO Syrena (1957–1983)
  • Polski Fiat 125p / FSO 125p (1967–1991)
  • FSO Polonez (1978–2002)
