Penthouse apartment

A penthouse apartment or penthouse is an apartment or unit on the highest floor of an apartment building, condominium, hotel or tower. Penthouses are typically differentiated from other apartments by luxury features. The term 'penthouse' originally referred to, and sometimes still does, a separate smaller structure on the roof of a building.

History

The concept of the penthouse dates back to the 1920s during a period of great economic growth in the United States. High-rises began to be built in New York City, and with them came the idea of making the top floor — previously used for storage or servants' quarters — into a desirable private residence. Elevated living was promoted for its cleaner air, views, privacy, and freedom from noise and insects. By the 1920s and 1930s, penthouses had become fashionable status symbols among wealthy urban residents, especially in Manhattan.

Characteristics

Penthouses typically occupy the entire top floor or a large portion of it, and often include features not found in lower units: private elevators or dedicated elevator access, large terraces or rooftop gardens, floor-to-ceiling windows with panoramic views, high ceilings, and premium finishes and appliances. Because of their position, penthouses usually command significantly higher prices or rents than comparable units in the same building. In many cities the penthouse is regarded as the most prestigious residence in a building.

Types

In architectural terms, a penthouse may also refer to a structure on the roof of a building that does not occupy its entire footprint, set back from the edge of the roof, housing mechanical equipment, elevator machinery, or stair access. Building codes in many jurisdictions regulate how much of a roof a penthouse structure may cover and how tall it may be.
