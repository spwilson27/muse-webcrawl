Harland Sanders Café and Museum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Harland Sanders Café and Museum is a historic restaurant and museum in North Corbin, Kentucky. Built by Harland Sanders, the founder of Kentucky Fried Chicken, it is the site where Sanders developed his fried chicken seasoned with a secret blend of 11 herbs and spices in the 1930s and 1940s at his service station café on U.S. Route 25.

History

Sanders began selling meals to travellers at his Corbin service station in the 1930s, and his cooking gained such a reputation that he expanded into a café and motel across the road. The present café building was constructed in 1940 and rebuilt after a fire in 1942. As interstate highways drew traffic away from Corbin, Sanders sold the business and took his chicken recipe on the road, franchising it as Kentucky Fried Chicken.

Museum

The café was restored by KFC in 1990 and reopened as a working restaurant and museum displaying Sanders' kitchen, office, and period artefacts, including the original pressure-cooking equipment. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1990 and is regarded as the birthplace of the KFC chain.
