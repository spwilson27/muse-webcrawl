Bard College

Bard College is a private liberal arts college in Annandale-on-Hudson, New York. Founded in 1860 as St. Stephen's College, it is affiliated with the Episcopal Church and is known for its progressive curriculum, small classes, and emphasis on the arts and humanities.

History

Bard was founded in 1860 by John Bard, a philanthropist and civic leader, together with leaders of the Episcopal Church, as St. Stephen's College, a theological institution intended to educate men for the ministry. The college expanded its curriculum over the decades and, in 1934, became affiliated with Columbia University, at which point it changed its name to Bard College in honor of its founder. Bard became independent and coeducational in 1944. Under president Leon Botstein, who took office in 1975, the college greatly expanded its programs, finances, and international presence.

Academics

Bard offers Bachelor of Arts and Bachelor of Science degrees across four divisions: Arts; Languages and Literature; Science, Mathematics, and Computing; and Social Studies, as well as interdisciplinary programs. Students complete a common first-year program including Language and Thinking and the First-Year Seminar. Bard is also known for its graduate programs, including the Bard Graduate Center and the Levy Economics Institute, and for early college programs such as Bard College at Simon's Rock and the Bard Early College network.

Campus and arts

The college's campus overlooks the Hudson River and the Catskill Mountains. Notable facilities include the Richard B. Fisher Center for the Performing Arts, designed by architect Frank Gehry, and the Hessel Museum of Art, home of the Center for Curatorial Studies. Bard hosts the annual Bard Music Festival and is the summer home of the American Symphony Orchestra.
