Cessna is an American brand of general aviation aircraft manufactured by the Cessna Aircraft Company, now a subsidiary of Textron Aviation. Founded in 1927 by Clyde Cessna in Wichita, Kansas — long nicknamed the "Air Capital of the World" — the company is known above all for small, piston-powered single-engine aeroplanes and the Citation family of business jets. Cessna has delivered more than 200,000 aircraft, more than any other manufacturer.

History

Clyde Cessna, a Kansas farmer and mechanic, built and flew his own aircraft from 1911 and founded the company with his nephew Dwane Wallace playing a key later role. After surviving the Great Depression, Cessna boomed in the post-war general aviation market with models such as the 120/140, 170, 180 and 182. The Cessna 172 Skyhawk (1956) became the best-selling aircraft of all time, and the Cessna 152 became a standard flight trainer. The Citation I of 1969 began the highly successful Citation business jet line. General Dynamics acquired Cessna in 1985 and sold it to Textron in 1992; production of piston singles was suspended for a decade amid US product-liability costs before resuming under the General Aviation Revitalization Act of 1994. Since 2014 Cessna has been managed jointly with Beechcraft as Textron Aviation.

Notable aircraft

  • 172 Skyhawk — best-selling aircraft in history
  • 152 — two-seat trainer
  • 182 Skylane, 210 Centurion, 208 Caravan turboprop
  • Citation family — including Citation X and Longitude business jets
  • T-37 Tweet and A-37 Dragonfly military trainers
