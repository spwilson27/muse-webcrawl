Renzo Novatore

Renzo Novatore (May 12, 1890 – November 29, 1922) was the pen name of Abele Rizieri Ferrari, an Italian individualist anarchist, illegalist and anti-fascist writer and propagandist. He is best known for his writings in anarchist periodicals and for the posthumously published collection Toward the Creative Nothing (Verso il nulla creatore).

Life

Born in Arcola, Liguria, to a poor peasant family, Ferrari left school early and educated himself. He was repeatedly prosecuted for theft and insurrectionary activity, deserted from military service during the First World War, and took part in the unrest of the Biennio Rosso. Writing as Novatore, he fused the egoism of Max Stirner and the thought of Friedrich Nietzsche with insurrectionary anarchism, rejecting both bourgeois morality and mass collectivism in favour of the self-realising individual.

Death and legacy

Novatore was killed in an ambush by carabinieri near Teglia in November 1922, shortly after the fascist March on Rome. His works were later translated and republished by individualist and insurrectionary anarchist circles, where he remains an influential, if controversial, figure.
