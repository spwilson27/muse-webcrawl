Hiram Percy Maxim (September 2, 1869 – February 17, 1936) was an American radio pioneer and inventor, best known for founding the American Radio Relay League (ARRL) and for inventing the Maxim Silencer, an early commercially successful device for reducing firearm noise. He was the son of Hiram Maxim, inventor of the Maxim gun, and the nephew of Hudson Maxim, an inventor of explosives.

Early life and career

Maxim was born in Brooklyn, New York, and graduated from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology at the age of sixteen, among the youngest graduates of his era there. He worked in the electric utilities industry before turning to automobiles: in the 1890s he joined the Pope Manufacturing Company in Hartford, Connecticut, where he designed and raced early electric and gasoline-powered cars. His experience with noisy internal combustion engines led him to think about muffling sound generally.

The Maxim silencer

In the early 1900s Maxim developed a firearm suppressor that used a series of curved vanes to swirl and cool expanding propellant gases, slowing them before release. He patented the device in 1909 and sold it through the Maxim Silent Firearms Company; it was marketed under the trademarked name "Maxim Silencer," and "silencer" consequently became the common generic term for suppressors in the United States. Versions were offered for rifles and pistols, and Maxim also pursued related work on mufflers for engines and industrial noise reduction. The same principles informed his later company, the Maxim Silencer Company of Hartford.

Amateur radio

An enthusiastic amateur radio operator with the call sign 1AW (later W1AW), Maxim became alarmed when proposed legislation and the technical limits of early relay messaging threatened the hobby. In 1914, together with Clarence D. Tuska, he founded the American Radio Relay League in Hartford to organize amateurs into a message-relaying network. He served as the ARRL's first president, a post he held until his death, and he was a central figure in securing amateur radio's legal standing in the United States. The ARRL's headquarters station in Newington, Connecticut, still bears his call sign, W1AW, and the league's highest honor for young amateurs is the Hiram Percy Maxim Award.

Other interests

Maxim was an early experimenter in amateur cinematography and helped found the Amateur Cinema League in 1926. He wrote on his experiences in books including A Genius in the Family (1936), a memoir of his father. He died in 1936 while returning by train from a trip to the Lowell Observatory in Flagstaff, Arizona.
