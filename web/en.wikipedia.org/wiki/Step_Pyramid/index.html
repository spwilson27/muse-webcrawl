The Step Pyramid, or Pyramid of Djoser, is an archaeological site in the Saqqara necropolis, Egypt, northwest of the ruins of Memphis. It was built in the 27th century BC during the Third Dynasty for the burial of Pharaoh Djoser. Designed by Djoser's vizier and architect Imhotep, it is the earliest large-scale cut-stone construction in Egypt and the first Egyptian pyramid.

Design and construction

The pyramid began as a square mastaba tomb, which was enlarged in stages — first into a four-stepped pyramid and finally into a six-stepped pyramid 60 metres high, faced with fine white Tura limestone. Beneath it lies a labyrinth of tunnels and chambers totalling nearly 6 km, including burial chambers lined with blue faience tiles imitating reed matting. The pyramid stands within a vast mortuary complex of courtyards, temples and dummy chapels enclosed by a niched limestone wall 10.5 m high with fourteen false doors and one real entrance — a palace façade translated into stone.

Significance

The Step Pyramid marks a revolutionary step in monumental architecture: the first time the Egyptians built entirely in stone on a giant scale, and the prototype from which the later smooth-sided pyramids of Giza developed, via intermediate forms such as Sneferu's Bent and Red pyramids. Its architect Imhotep was later deified, a rare honour for a non-royal Egyptian. The complex forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site of Memphis and its Necropolis (inscribed 1979). After long conservation work, the pyramid reopened to visitors in 2020.
