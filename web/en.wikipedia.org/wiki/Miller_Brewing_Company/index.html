The Miller Brewing Company is an American brewery founded in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, in 1855 by German immigrant Frederick Miller. Its flagship brands include Miller High Life, "The Champagne of Beers" (introduced 1903), and Miller Lite (launched nationally in 1975), the first successful mainstream light beer, marketed with the famous "Tastes Great, Less Filling" campaign. The Miller business is today part of Molson Coors, headquartered in Chicago, with the historic Miller Valley brewery still operating in Milwaukee.

History

Frederick Miller leased and then bought the Plank Road Brewery in Milwaukee's Menomonee Valley in 1855, bringing lager yeast from Germany. The company survived Prohibition by producing near beer, malt syrup and soft drinks, and grew strongly after the Second World War under Frederick C. Miller. The conglomerate Philip Morris acquired Miller in 1969–1970, funding an aggressive expansion that made it the second-largest US brewer behind Anheuser-Busch. In 2002 Miller was sold to South African Breweries, forming SABMiller; a 2008 joint venture, MillerCoors, combined its US operations with Coors. When Anheuser-Busch InBev acquired SABMiller in 2016, the Miller brands passed entirely to Molson Coors.

Brands and marketing

Beyond High Life and Lite, Miller brands include Miller Genuine Draft (1985) and, historically, Milwaukee's Best and the malt liquor Olde English 800. Miller Lite's advertising — first the "Great Taste… Less Filling!" debates among retired athletes — effectively created the light beer category in the United States. The Milwaukee Brewers' former stadium name, Miller Park (now American Family Field), reflected the company's deep local ties.
