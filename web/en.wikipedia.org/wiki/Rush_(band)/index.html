Rush was a Canadian rock band formed in Toronto in 1968. Its classic line-up consisted of Geddy Lee (bass, vocals, keyboards), Alex Lifeson (guitars), and Neil Peart (drums, percussion, lyrics), which was in place from 1974 until the band's retirement in 2018. Rush is renowned for its musicianship, complex compositions, and Peart's literate, often science-fiction- and philosophy-influenced lyrics.

History

After an early hard-rock style, Rush embraced progressive rock on albums such as 2112 (1976) and Hemispheres (1978), then reached a commercial peak with Permanent Waves (1980) and Moving Pictures (1981), the latter featuring the hits "Tom Sawyer" and "Limelight". In the 1980s the band incorporated synthesizers heavily before returning to a guitar-oriented power-trio sound in the 1990s and 2000s.

Legacy

Rush sold more than 40 million records worldwide, was inducted into the Canadian Music Hall of Fame in 1994 and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2013, and is frequently ranked among the greatest live acts in rock. Peart's death in January 2020 effectively ended the band, though Lee and Lifeson have since collaborated on new projects.
