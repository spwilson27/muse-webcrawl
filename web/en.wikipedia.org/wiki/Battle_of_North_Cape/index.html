Battle of North Cape

Date26 December 1943
LocationBarents Sea, off North Cape, Norway
ResultBritish victory
BelligerentsRoyal Navy vs Kriegsmarine

The Battle of North Cape was a Second World War naval battle fought on 26 December 1943 in the Barents Sea, as part of the Arctic convoys. The German battleship Scharnhorst, attempting to attack convoy JW 55B bound for the Soviet Union, was intercepted and sunk by British forces under Admiral Sir Bruce Fraser, centred on the battleship HMS Duke of York.

Battle

In stormy Arctic darkness, British cruisers under Vice-Admiral Robert Burnett — Belfast, Norfolk, and Sheffield — twice engaged Scharnhorst, damaging her radar. Fraser's force then caught her in the evening; Duke of York's 14-inch guns disabled her, and destroyers closed in with torpedoes. Scharnhorst sank with the loss of 1,932 men; only 36 survived.

Significance

The battle was the last clash between big-gun capital ships of Britain and Germany, and it effectively ended the German surface threat to the Arctic convoys; Tirpitz, already damaged, never again put to sea against them.
