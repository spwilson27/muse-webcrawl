Charles-Maurice de Talleyrand-Périgord, 1st Prince of Benevento (2 February 1754 – 17 May 1838), known simply as Talleyrand, was a French statesman and diplomat who served under the Revolution, Napoleon, and the Bourbon Restoration — a career famous both for skill and for pragmatism verging on opportunism.

Career

A clubfoot ended hopes of a military career, and Talleyrand entered the Church, becoming Bishop of Autun in 1788. In the Estates-General of 1789 he sided with the Third Estate and supported the nationalisation of Church property, leading to his excommunication. As foreign minister under the Directory and Napoleon, he resigned in 1807, increasingly sceptical of Napoleon's ambitions, and later worked against him.

Congress of Vienna

Representing restored Bourbon France at the Congress of Vienna (1814–15), Talleyrand used the principle of legitimacy and skilful coalition-building to secure generous terms for a defeated France. He briefly served as France's first prime minister under the Restoration, later supported the July Revolution of 1830 and served as ambassador to London. He died in Paris in 1838.
