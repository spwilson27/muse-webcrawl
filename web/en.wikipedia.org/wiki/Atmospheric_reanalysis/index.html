Atmospheric reanalysis

Atmospheric reanalysis
Major productsERA5, MERRA-2, JRA-55, NCEP/NCAR
MethodData assimilation with a fixed forecast model

An atmospheric reanalysis is a scientific method for reconstructing a consistent, gridded record of past weather and climate. Historical observations — from surface stations, ships, balloons, aircraft and satellites — are combined with a fixed modern numerical weather prediction model through data assimilation, producing estimates of atmospheric variables (temperature, wind, humidity, pressure) on a regular grid at regular time intervals, in places and times where no direct observations exist.

Major reanalyses

  • ERA5 – produced by ECMWF for the Copernicus Climate Change Service; hourly data from 1940 onward at ~31 km resolution.
  • ERA-Interim and ERA-40 – earlier ECMWF reanalyses.
  • MERRA-2 – NASA's reanalysis, beginning in 1980, designed for aerosol and satellite-era studies.
  • NCEP/NCAR Reanalysis – the pioneering product, extending back to 1948.
  • JRA-55 – produced by the Japan Meteorological Agency.

Uses and limitations

Reanalyses are widely used to study climate variability and change, verify models, drive hydrological and renewable-energy studies, and provide boundary conditions for regional models. Because observing systems change over time, spurious jumps and trends can appear, and variables not directly observed (such as precipitation) are model-dependent, so reanalysis data must be interpreted with care.
