Six Arrows

The Six Arrows (Turkish: Altı Ok) are the founding principles of the Republican People's Party (CHP) of Turkey and the symbol of Kemalism, the ideology of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk. The six principles — Republicanism, Populism, Nationalism, Laicism, Statism, and Reformism — were adopted into the party programme in 1931 and written into the Turkish constitution in 1937.

The six principles

  • Republicanism (Cumhuriyetçilik): sovereignty belongs to the nation, embodied in a republic rather than the Ottoman monarchy or caliphate.
  • Populism (Halkçılık): equality of citizens before the law, rejection of class or communal privilege, and government in the interest of the whole people.
  • Nationalism (Milliyetçilik): the Turkish nation as a political and cultural unity, committed to progress within national culture.
  • Laicism (Laiklik): secularism — the separation of religion from state affairs and the freedom of conscience.
  • Statism (Devletçilik): an active state role in economic development, especially in industry and infrastructure, alongside private enterprise.
  • Reformism (İnkılapçılık): permanent commitment to revolution and modernisation, replacing traditional institutions with contemporary ones.

Symbolism

The six arrows are borne on the flag of the CHP, and the emblem is commonly explained by reference to a story that Atatürk, asked about the party's principles, sketched arrows one by one. The principles remain a touchstone of Kemalist politics, though their interpretation — particularly laicism and statism — has been contested throughout the history of the Turkish Republic.
