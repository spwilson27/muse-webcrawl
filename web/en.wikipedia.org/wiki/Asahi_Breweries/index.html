Asahi Breweries, Ltd. (アサヒビール株式会社) is a Japanese brewery and soft drink company headquartered in Sumida, Tokyo. It is the core brewing company of Asahi Group Holdings and one of Japan's largest brewers, long holding the largest share of the Japanese beer market. Its flagship product, Asahi Super Dry, launched in 1987, transformed Japanese beer tastes toward crisp, dry lagers and became a global brand.

History

The company traces its origins to the Osaka Beer Brewing Company founded in 1889 by Komakichi Torii; the Asahi brand was first brewed in 1892. Through mergers the firm became Dai Nippon Beer, which dominated the prewar market before being split under the Allied occupation in 1949 into Asahi Beer and Sapporo Beer. Asahi's market share declined through the 1970s and early 1980s, falling as low as roughly 10%, before the 1987 introduction of Super Dry — developed under president Tsutomu Murai and marketed as karakuchi (dry and crisp) — reversed its fortunes and reshaped the industry.

International expansion

Under Asahi Group Holdings, the company expanded abroad, notably acquiring in 2016 the Peroni, Grolsch, and Meantime brands sold by SABMiller as part of the AB InBev merger, and in 2019 purchasing Carlton & United Breweries in Australia from AB InBev. The group's portfolio also includes soft drinks, Calpis, and food businesses.

Headquarters

The Asahi Beer Hall on the bank of the Sumida River in Tokyo, completed in 1989 and designed with the French designer Philippe Starck, is famous for the giant golden sculpture on its roof, officially the Flamme d'Or ("golden flame"), meant to represent the burning heart of Asahi Beer and the foam of a freshly poured glass.
