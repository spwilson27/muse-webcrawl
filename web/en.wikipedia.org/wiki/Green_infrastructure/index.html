Green infrastructure

Related conceptsLow-impact development, sustainable urban drainage, nature-based solutions
ExamplesGreen roofs, rain gardens, bioswales, constructed wetlands, urban forests

Green infrastructure is a network that provides the "ingredients" for solving urban and climatic challenges by building with nature. More broadly, it describes strategically planned networks of natural and semi-natural areas — including water features, green space, and other environmental features — designed to deliver ecosystem services such as clean water, flood protection, air quality, and climate adaptation.

Concepts

The term emerged in the United States in the mid-1990s in discussions of land conservation as a complement to conventional "grey" infrastructure. In urban stormwater management, green infrastructure refers to practices that infiltrate, evapotranspirate, or harvest stormwater runoff: rain gardens, permeable pavements, green roofs, and bioswales.

European Union usage

The European Commission defines green infrastructure as a strategically planned network of natural and semi-natural areas designed and managed to deliver a wide range of ecosystem services. The EU adopted a Green Infrastructure Strategy in 2013.

Benefits

Advocates cite reduced flooding, improved water quality, urban cooling, biodiversity habitat, carbon sequestration, and health and recreation benefits, often at lower cost than purely engineered alternatives.
