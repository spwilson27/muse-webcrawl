Overview

Modibo Keïta (1915–1968 in office; died 1977) was a Malian politician who led Mali to independence and served as its first president from 1960 to 1968. A teacher and veteran of French politics (deputy in Paris, co-founder of the RDA), he first headed the short-lived Mali Federation with Senegal, then, after its collapse in August 1960, the Republic of Mali.

Rule

Keïta pursued an African socialism: state farms and enterprises, exit from the franc zone (1962, reversed in 1967), close ties with the USSR, China and Kwame Nkrumah's Ghana (with whom Mali and Guinea formed a union of states), and strong pan-African diplomacy. Economic failure and coercion (the Popular Militia) fed discontent; on 19 November 1968 Moussa Traoré's army overthrew him. Keïta died in detention in Bamako in 1977, and was rehabilitated after Mali's return to democracy in the 1990s — Bamako's airport bears his name.
