Statue

A statue is a free-standing sculpture in which the realistic, full-length figures of persons or animals are carved or cast in a durable material such as wood, metal or stone.

MaterialsStone, bronze, marble, wood
ExamplesStatue of Liberty, David, Great Sphinx

History

Statues date to prehistory, flourished in ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome for gods, rulers and athletes, and in religious traditions worldwide — though some traditions restrict figurative depiction. Renaissance sculptors like Michelangelo revived classical realism.

Uses

Typical statues are life-size or larger, commemorating historical events and persons, for religion, or as public art. A small statue is a figurine or statuette; a very large one is colossal. See Memorial.
