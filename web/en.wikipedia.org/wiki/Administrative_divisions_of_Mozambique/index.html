Mozambique is divided into eleven provinces (províncias), which are subdivided into districts (distritos), and these in turn into administrative posts and localities. The capital, Maputo, has the status of a province as well as being a municipality. Provinces are headed by governors; since 2019 provincial governors have been elected rather than appointed by the president, part of a decentralisation package that also created provincial assemblies.

Provinces

  • Niassa — capital Lichinga (largest province, in the far north-west)
  • Cabo Delgado — capital Pemba (far north-east)
  • Nampula — capital Nampula (most populous province)
  • Zambezia — capital Quelimane
  • Tete — capital Tete (on the Zambezi, coal-mining region)
  • Manica — capital Chimoio
  • Sofala — capital Beira (major port)
  • Inhambane — capital Inhambane
  • Gaza — capital Xai-Xai
  • Maputo Province — capital Matola
  • Maputo City — the national capital, a province-level city

Districts and municipalities

The provinces are divided into 154 districts, each administered by a district administrator and containing several administrative posts (postos administrativos) and localities. Alongside this deconcentrated state administration, Mozambique has autonomous municipalities (municípios or autarquias) — cities and some towns, numbering more than fifty — with elected mayors and assemblies responsible for local services. The creation of elected provincial governments followed the 2018 constitutional amendments negotiated as part of the peace process with RENAMO.
