Paine Field (IATA: PAE, ICAO: KPAE), also known as Snohomish County Airport, is a public airport serving Everett, Washington, United States, about 25 miles (40 km) north of downtown Seattle. It is named for Topliff Olin Paine, an Everett-born Army Air Corps officer killed in World War I. The field is dominated by the adjacent Boeing Everett Factory — the largest building in the world by volume — where Boeing wide-body airliners are assembled.

History

The airport was built in 1936 as a Works Progress Administration project and served as an Army Air Forces base during the Second World War. After the war it returned to county control; Boeing selected the site in the 1960s for the 747 plant, and the first flight of many Boeing types (747, 767, 777, 787 and 747-8) departed from Paine Field. Test and delivery flights remain a major share of its traffic, alongside general aviation and the Future of Flight Aviation Center and Boeing Tour.

Commercial service

After decades of debate, scheduled passenger service began on 4 March 2019 from a new two-gate terminal operated by Propeller Airports, with Alaska Airlines and (initially) United Airlines offering flights. The airport is marketed as "Seattle Paine Field International Airport".

Attractions

Around the field are the Future of Flight Aviation Center, Paul Allen's former Flying Heritage & Combat Armor Museum, and the Museum of Flight Restoration Center.
