Thesis statement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The thesis statement usually appears near the end of an essay's introductory paragraph

A thesis statement is a sentence or group of sentences that expresses the main idea, argument, or central point of an essay, research paper, or other piece of writing. It tells the reader what the writer intends to argue or demonstrate and forecasts the structure of the discussion that follows.

Position and form

In the five-paragraph essay format taught in many schools, the thesis statement is placed at the end of the introductory paragraph. It is typically a single declarative sentence. An effective thesis is specific, arguable, and supportable: it takes a position that a reasonable reader could dispute, so that the body of the essay must supply evidence and reasoning on its behalf.

"Although some regard homework as essential practice, excessive homework in elementary school yields little academic benefit while costing children sleep and unstructured play."

Some writing instructors distinguish between a working thesis, drafted early and revised as the research develops, and the final thesis, which reflects the conclusions the writer actually reached. In scientific writing, the analogous role is played by the hypothesis and the statement of results.

Criticism

Critics of formulaic writing instruction argue that rigid insistence on a single-sentence thesis early in the writing process can constrain exploration and produce mechanical prose. Many professional genres — narrative nonfiction, exploratory essays, and much academic writing in the humanities — develop their claims gradually rather than announcing them in advance.
