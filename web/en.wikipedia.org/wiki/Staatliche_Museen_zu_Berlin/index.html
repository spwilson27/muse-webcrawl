Staatliche Museen zu Berlin

The Staatliche Museen zu Berlin (Berlin State Museums) are a group of institutions in Berlin, Germany, comprising seventeen museums in five clusters, together with research and conservation institutes. They form part of the Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz (Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation), one of the largest cultural foundations in the world.

Museum Island

The historic core lies on Museum Island (Museumsinsel) in the Spree, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It comprises the Altes Museum (Greek and Roman antiquities), the Neues Museum — rebuilt under David Chipperfield and reopened in 2009, home to the bust of Nefertiti — the Pergamon Museum, famous for the Pergamon Altar and Ishtar Gate, the Bode Museum (sculpture and Byzantine art), and the Alte Nationalgalerie (nineteenth-century art). The James-Simon-Galerie (2019) serves as the island's central entrance building.

Other sites

At the Kulturforum near Potsdamer Platz stand the Gemäldegalerie (Old Master paintings), the Kunstgewerbemuseum (decorative arts), the Kupferstichkabinett (prints and drawings) and the Neue Nationalgalerie, Mies van der Rohe's 1968 temple of modern art. Further institutions include the Hamburger Bahnhof (contemporary art), the Ethnologisches Museum and Museum für Asiatische Kunst — whose collections moved to the Humboldt Forum in the rebuilt Berlin Palace — and museums in Dahlem and Charlottenburg, including the Brücke Museum and the Museum Berggruen.

History

The museums trace their origin to the royal art collections of Prussia, opened to the public with the Altes Museum in 1830. Divided during the Cold War between East and West Berlin, the collections were reunified after 1990, a process involving major restitution, rebuilding and reorganisation that continues today.
