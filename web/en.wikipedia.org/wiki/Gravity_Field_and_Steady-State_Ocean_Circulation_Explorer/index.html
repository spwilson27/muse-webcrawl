Gravity Field and Steady-State Ocean Circulation Explorer

The Gravity Field and Steady-State Ocean Circulation Explorer (GOCE) was the first of ESA's Living Planet Programme satellites intended to map in unprecedented detail the Earth's gravity field. The satellite's primary payload was the Electrostatic Gravity Gradiometer (EGG), which consisted of three pairs of triaxial accelerometers arranged in three dimensions. GOCE was launched on 17 March 2009 from Plesetsk Cosmodrome in Russia.

Mission

GOCE flew at an exceptionally low altitude of about 260 kilometres, later lowered to around 229 kilometres, to maximise sensitivity to gravity variations. An ion thruster provided drag compensation, counteracting atmospheric resistance. The satellite's sleek, arrow-like shape earned it the nickname "the Ferrari of space".

The measurements allowed scientists to construct a high-resolution model of the geoid — the surface of equal gravitational potential — to a precision of 1–2 cm at a spatial resolution of about 100 km, improving knowledge of ocean circulation, sea-level change, and processes in the Earth's interior.

End of mission

On 21 October 2013, GOCE ran out of the xenon propellant for its ion engine, and the satellite reentered Earth's atmosphere on 11 November 2013, disintegrating over the South Atlantic near the Falkland Islands. See also European Space Agency and GRACE.
