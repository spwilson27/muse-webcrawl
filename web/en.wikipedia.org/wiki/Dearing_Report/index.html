Dearing Report

The Dearing Report (1997), Higher Education in the Learning Society

The Dearing Report, formally Higher Education in the Learning Society, is the report of the National Committee of Inquiry into Higher Education, chaired by Sir Ron (later Lord) Dearing, published in the United Kingdom in July 1997. It was the largest review of British higher education since the Robbins Report of 1963, and its recommendations reshaped the funding, structure, and quality assurance of universities in the United Kingdom, most controversially by proposing that students contribute to the cost of tuition — leading directly to the introduction of tuition fees.

Background

The committee was set up in 1996 by the Conservative government with cross-party support, at a time when participation in higher education had expanded rapidly (from roughly one in eight to about one in three young people) while public funding per student had fallen sharply. Its remit covered the purpose, shape, structure, size and funding of higher education over the following twenty years. The report appeared shortly after the Labour government of Tony Blair took office in May 1997.

Recommendations

The report made 93 recommendations. Key proposals included:

  • Students should make a contribution of around £1,000 per year towards tuition costs, repayable through income-contingent loans after graduation.
  • Continued expansion of student numbers, with particular growth in sub-degree and part-time provision and stronger links with further education colleges.
  • A new quality assurance framework, leading to the creation of the Quality Assurance Agency (QAA), and a professional Institute for Learning and Teaching for university teachers.
  • Greater transparency and comparability of degree standards, including programme specifications and a framework of qualifications.
  • Expanded funding for research infrastructure and a move towards the "learning society", with lifelong learning supported by individual learning accounts and wider use of new technology.

Implementation and legacy

The government accepted the broad thrust of the report but applied the student contribution more quickly than Dearing proposed: the Teaching and Higher Education Act 1998 introduced means-tested tuition fees of £1,000 per year for students in England, ending the era of free university tuition. Fees were later raised substantially (to £3,000 under the Higher Education Act 2004, and to £9,000 in 2012), a trajectory frequently debated in relation to Dearing's more cautious model. Other legacies include the QAA, the qualifications frameworks used across the UK, and a lasting emphasis on widening participation and employability in university missions. Earlier Dearing reports on school examinations and the National Curriculum (1993–94) had also made Lord Dearing a central figure in British education policy.
