I Love Lucy

I Love Lucy
Genre: Sitcom
Created by: Jess Oppenheimer, Madelyn Pugh, Bob Carroll Jr.
Starring: Lucille Ball, Desi Arnaz, Vivian Vance, William Frawley
Network: CBS
Original run: 1951–1957

I Love Lucy is an American television sitcom that originally aired on CBS from October 15, 1951, to May 6, 1957, with 180 half-hour episodes spanning six seasons. It starred Lucille Ball, her husband Desi Arnaz, Vivian Vance and William Frawley, and followed the life of Lucy Ricardo (Ball), a housewife in New York City married to bandleader Ricky Ricardo (Arnaz), as she schemed to break into show business, aided and abetted by landlords and best friends Fred and Ethel Mertz.

Production innovations

Produced by Ball and Arnaz's Desilu Productions, the show pioneered techniques that became standard: filming before a live studio audience with three cameras, shooting on 35mm film rather than kinescope, and — because Desilu retained ownership of the film negatives — the profitable syndication rerun model. Episodes such as "Lucy Does a TV Commercial" (the Vitameatavegamin routine) and "Job Switching" (the chocolate factory) remain landmarks of television comedy.

Legacy

The series won five Emmy Awards, regularly tops lists of the greatest television shows of all time, and was one of the first programmes to be preserved through reruns across generations.
