Cortachy Castle

[Image: Cortachy Castle]

Cortachy Castle is a castellated mansion house near the village of Cortachy, in Angus, Scotland, about four miles north of Kirriemuir. It is the seat of the Earl of Airlie and the Ogilvy family.

History

The lands of Cortachy were granted to the Ogilvy family in the fifteenth century, and a castle is recorded on the site by the 1470s. The original tower house was remodelled and greatly extended over the following centuries, particularly in the seventeenth and nineteenth centuries.

The present house is largely the result of Victorian remodelling, which gave the building its Baronial character with crow-stepped gables, turrets and battlements. The castle stands near the confluence of the River South Esk and the Prosen Water, in landscaped parkland.

The Ogilvy family

The Ogilvys of Airlie were hereditary sheriffs of Angus. David Ogilvy, the "Bonnie Earl" of the ballad The Bonnie House o' Airlie, was a notable Royalist during the Wars of the Three Kingdoms. The family seat at Airlie Castle was destroyed by the Earl of Argyll's forces in 1640, after which Cortachy became the principal family residence.

Architecture

Cortachy Castle is a Category A listed building. It combines elements of a fifteenth-century tower house with extensive later additions, including work by the architects David Bryce and others in the nineteenth century. The interior contains state rooms with heraldic decoration and family portraits.
