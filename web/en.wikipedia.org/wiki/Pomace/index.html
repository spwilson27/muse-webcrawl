Pomace

Pomace (from Latin pomum, "fruit"), or marc, is the solid remains of grapes, olives, or other fruit after pressing for juice or oil. It contains the skins, pulp, seeds, and stems of the fruit.

Uses

Grape pomace is traditionally used to produce pomace brandy (such as grappa in Italy, marc in France, and orujo in Spain), grape seed oil, and pectin. Apple pomace is a source of pectin and is used as animal feed. Pomace is also composted or used as a fertilizer, and research has explored its use as a source of polyphenols and biofuel.

History

In ancient Greece and Rome, pomace was fermented with water to produce a low-alcohol drink for slaves and labourers known as lora or piquette.
