Frobenius endomorphism

In commutative algebra and algebraic geometry, the Frobenius endomorphism is a special endomorphism of rings of prime characteristic p. For such a ring R, it is the map F: R → R defined by F(r) = rp. It is named after Ferdinand Georg Frobenius.

Why it is a homomorphism

Raising to the p-th power is always multiplicative, but additivity fails in general. In characteristic p, however, the binomial coefficients C(p, k) for 0 < k < p are all divisible by p and hence vanish in R, so the "freshman's dream" holds: (x + y)p = xp + yp. The map is the identity on the prime field Fp.

Properties and uses

A ring for which the Frobenius map is injective is called reduced-like in this context (Frobenius injective implies the ring is reduced); surjectivity is related to perfect fields. The Frobenius endomorphism is central to the theory of finite fields—where it generates the Galois group of Fpn over Fp—to the study of algebraic groups in positive characteristic, Cartier's theorem on formal groups, Dieudonné theory, and the geometry of varieties in characteristic p, including notions such as F-pure and F-regular singularities in the theory of tight closure.
