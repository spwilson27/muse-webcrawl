Comerica Park

Comerica Park is a baseball stadium in downtown Detroit, Michigan, United States. It has been the home of the Detroit Tigers of Major League Baseball since it opened in 2000, replacing the historic Tiger Stadium. The park is named for Comerica Bank; its seating capacity is approximately 41,000.

History

Ground was broken in October 1997, and the stadium opened on 11 April 2000, with the Tigers defeating the Seattle Mariners. Comerica Park hosted the 2005 Major League Baseball All-Star Game and World Series games in 2006 and 2012. It was built partly with public financing and is owned by the Detroit-Wayne County Stadium Authority, with the Tigers as operator.

Features

The park is known for its fan-friendly atmosphere and amusements unusual among ballparks, including a Ferris wheel and a carousel in the outfield concourse area. A giant scoreboard in left field is topped by two large tiger statues; statues honouring Tigers greats — Ty Cobb, Hank Greenberg, Charlie Gehringer, Al Kaline, Hal Newhouser, Willie Horton, and broadcaster Ernie Harwell — line the concourse. The outfield walls frame a view of the downtown Detroit skyline and the neighbouring Ford Field. The playing surface is natural grass, and the park's dimensions have been adjusted several times, notably bringing in the left-centre field fence in 2003.

Other events

Beyond baseball, Comerica Park has hosted major concerts and, in 2014, an outdoor NHL hockey game (the Winter Classic alumni and related events at the park accompanied the main event at Michigan Stadium). It also hosts hockey's Great Lakes Invitational festivities and various community events.
