Countryside Act 1968

United Kingdom legislation, chapter 41 of 1968.

The Countryside Act 1968 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom (c. 41) that extended public provision for recreation in the countryside of England and Wales. Building on the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949, it established the Countryside Commission in place of the National Parks Commission and empowered local authorities to create country parks and picnic sites close to urban areas.

Provisions

The Act broadened the remit of the renamed Countryside Commission from national parks and areas of outstanding natural beauty to countryside recreation generally. Local authorities were given powers to acquire land for country parks—sites intended primarily for informal recreation near towns, taking pressure off more sensitive landscapes—and to provide facilities such as car parks and information points. The Act also addressed public rights of way, requiring highway authorities to signpost footpaths where they leave metalled roads, made provision for wardens, and placed a duty on public bodies, in exercising their functions, to have regard to the desirability of conserving the natural beauty and amenity of the countryside.

Legacy

Hundreds of country parks were created under the Act's framework in the following decades. The countryside bodies it shaped were later reorganised: in England, the Countryside Commission merged into the Countryside Agency and then Natural England, while aspects of the 1968 Act were amended by later legislation including the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 and the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000.
