Program optimization

In computer science, program optimization, generally speaking, is the process of modifying a software system to make some aspect of it work more efficiently or use fewer resources. In general, a computer program may be optimized so that it executes more rapidly, or to make it capable of operating with less memory storage or other resources, or draw less power.

Levels of optimization

Optimization can occur at several levels: during design, where algorithmic choices dominate; at source-code level, where programmers restructure code; at compile time, where optimizing compilers apply transformations such as loop optimization, inlining, and dead-code elimination; and at runtime, using profiling and just-in-time compilation. Donald Knuth famously cautioned that "premature optimization is the root of all evil", emphasizing that the small efficiency gains usually matter only in critical sections identified by profiling.

Trade-offs

Optimizations frequently trade space for time, or vice versa, and may reduce code readability or portability. Optimization targets are usually measured by benchmarks, and bottlenecks are often in input/output or memory access rather than raw computation.
