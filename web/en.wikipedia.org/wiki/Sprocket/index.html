Sprocket

A sprocket, or sprocket wheel, is a profiled wheel with teeth (cogs) that mesh with a chain, track or other perforated or indented material. Unlike a gear, a sprocket never meshes directly with another sprocket; unlike a pulley, it has teeth rather than a smooth groove, so it cannot slip against the chain it drives.

Applications

Sprockets are fundamental to chain drives. On a bicycle, the chainrings at the pedals and the sprockets of the rear cassette transfer the rider's power to the back wheel, and their relative sizes set the gear ratio. Motorcycles use front and rear sprockets in the same way. Sprockets also drive conveyor chains, the tracks of tanks and bulldozers, timing chains in engines, and the film transport mechanisms of movie cameras and projectors, where they engage the perforations along the film's edge.

Design

The tooth profile of a sprocket is shaped to engage smoothly with the rollers or links of its chain and to accommodate wear. Sprockets are specified by the chain pitch, the number of teeth and the bore. Changing sprocket sizes is a common way of altering the gearing of bicycles and motorcycles without changing the chain type.
