The Aegean Region (Turkish: Ege Bölgesi) is one of the seven geographical regions of Turkey. It is located in western Turkey, bordered by the Aegean Sea to the west, the Marmara Region to the north, the Central Anatolia Region to the east, and the Mediterranean Region to the south. The region takes its name from the Aegean Sea.

Geography

Unlike the mountainous terrain of much of Anatolia, the Aegean Region is characterized by mountain ranges running perpendicular to the coast, creating broad, fertile river valleys such as those of the Gediz and Büyük Menderes rivers. The indented coastline is fringed with peninsulas and bays. The largest city and principal port is İzmir.

Economy

The region is one of Turkey's most productive agricultural areas, known for figs, olives, grapes, cotton, and tobacco. Tourism is a major industry along the coast, with resorts such as Bodrum, Marmaris, and Çeşme, and important archaeological sites including Ephesus, Pergamon, and Aphrodisias.

Provinces

The region comprises the provinces of İzmir, Manisa, Aydın, Denizli, Muğla, Afyonkarahisar, Kütahya, and Uşak.
