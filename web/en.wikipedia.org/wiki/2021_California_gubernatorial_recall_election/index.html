The 2021 California gubernatorial recall election was a special election held on 14 September 2021 in which voters decided whether to recall Governor Gavin Newsom and, in a separate question, who would replace him if the recall succeeded. A majority voted against the recall, and Newsom remained in office.

Background

Recall petitions against Newsom circulated from early in his term; the successful effort, organised by critics citing taxes, homelessness and pandemic restrictions, gathered momentum during the COVID-19 pandemic and was boosted by photographs of Newsom dining at the French Laundry restaurant while restrictions were in place. It collected more than the required 1.5 million valid signatures.

Ballot and campaign

The ballot had two parts: whether Newsom should be recalled, and a choice among 46 replacement candidates, which would take effect only if the recall passed. Radio host Larry Elder emerged as the leading Republican candidate; other candidates included former San Diego mayor Kevin Faulconer, businessman John Cox and media personality Caitlyn Jenner. Newsom and state Democrats urged voters to vote "no" and skip the second question, framing the recall as a Republican power grab.

Result

The recall failed by roughly 62% to 38%. Among replacement candidates, Elder received the most votes, but because the recall failed the second question had no effect. The election cost an estimated $276 million to administer.
