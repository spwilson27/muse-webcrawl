Meniscal cartilage replacement therapy

Meniscal cartilage replacement therapy
SpecialtyOrthopedic surgery
IndicationPainful partial meniscal loss after meniscectomy
ApproachArthroscopic implantation

Meniscal cartilage replacement therapy refers to surgical techniques that aim to replace lost or damaged meniscal tissue in the knee — typically after partial meniscectomy — in order to relieve pain and restore the load-distributing function of the meniscus, thereby protecting the articular cartilage from premature osteoarthritis. The best-known devices are the Collagen Meniscus Implant (CMI, later marketed as Menaflex) and the Actifit polyurethane scaffold.

Background

The medial and lateral menisci are C-shaped fibrocartilages that deepen the tibial plateau, distribute load across the knee and absorb shock; removal of meniscal tissue increases contact stresses on the articular cartilage and markedly raises the risk of degenerative change. Historically, tears were treated by partial or total meniscectomy; the long-term results encouraged repair where possible and, for irreparable segmental defects in symptomatic patients, replacement with scaffold implants or allografts.

Technique

The scaffold — a resorbable, porous implant shaped to the missing segment — is trimmed to the defect and arthroscopically sutured to the remaining meniscal rim and capsule. Over the following years the scaffold is intended to be invaded by fibrochondrocytes and remodelled into meniscus-like tissue while the material itself degrades. Rehabilitation usually involves several weeks of limited weight-bearing and restricted flexion while the implant integrates.

Evidence

Clinical studies report improvements in pain and function scores for appropriately selected patients with chronic post-meniscectomy pain and stable, well-aligned knees with limited cartilage damage. However, imaging and second-look arthroscopy often show shrinkage or incomplete regeneration of the implant, and high-quality comparative evidence that scaffolds prevent osteoarthritis remains limited. Meniscal allograft transplantation is a separate, more established option for complete or near-complete meniscal deficiency.
