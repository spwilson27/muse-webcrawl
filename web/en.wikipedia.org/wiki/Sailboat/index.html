Sailboat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: White sailboats racing with heeled hulls and full sails on blue water]
Sailing yachts racing.

A sailboat (sailing boat in British English) is a boat propelled partly or entirely by sails. Most modern sailboats are sloops — a single mast with a mainsail and a jib — rigged Bermuda (triangular) style, used for recreation, racing and cruising. Sailing relies on the interaction of sails as aerofoils and the keel or centreboard resisting sideways drift, allowing boats to travel upwind by tacking at an angle to the wind.

How sailing works

Sails generate lift like wings; the keel converts sideways force into forward motion. Points of sail run from \'in irons\' (head to wind, stalled) through close-hauled, reaching, and running downwind. Spinnakers and gennakers add area downwind, and modern foiling boats (America\'s Cup AC75s) lift clear of the water to exceed 40 knots.

Types

Dinghies (Laser/ILCA, Optimist) teach beginners; keelboats and cruising yachts carry cabins for offshore passages; catamarans and trimarans add speed and space. Racing spans club regattas to the Vendée Globe solo round-the-world race and the America\'s Cup, first contested in 1851 — sport\'s oldest international trophy.

Cruising culture

Millions sail recreationally; long-distance \'bluewater\' cruising follows trade-wind routes such as the Atlantic circuit. Auxiliary engines, roller furling and GPS navigation have made shorthanded sailing widely accessible.
