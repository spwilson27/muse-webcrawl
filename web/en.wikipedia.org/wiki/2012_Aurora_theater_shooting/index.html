The 2012 Aurora theater shooting was a mass shooting that occurred on 20 July 2012 at a Century movie theater in Aurora, Colorado, United States, during a midnight screening of the film The Dark Knight Rises. The gunman, James Holmes, set off tear gas grenades in the auditorium and opened fire with multiple weapons, killing 12 people and injuring 70 others. At the time it was among the deadliest mass shootings in modern U.S. history by number of casualties.

Attack

Holmes purchased a ticket, entered theater 9, and propped open an emergency exit door before returning to his car to arm himself. Wearing a gas mask, ballistic helmet and body armor, he re-entered about 30 minutes into the film, threw tear-gas canisters into the audience and began firing with a shotgun, a semi-automatic rifle and a handgun. His rifle jammed partway through the attack. Police arrested him without resistance in the parking lot minutes later. His apartment was found rigged with explosive booby traps, which bomb squads dismantled over the following day.

Perpetrator

James Eagan Holmes (born 1987) had been a neuroscience doctoral student at the University of Colorado Anschutz Medical Campus before withdrawing in June 2012. He had no criminal record and had legally purchased the weapons and thousands of rounds of ammunition in the months before the attack. Psychiatrists later testified that he suffered from serious mental illness, including schizotypal personality disorder and schizoaffective symptoms.

Trial and sentencing

Holmes initially pleaded not guilty by reason of insanity. In July 2015 a jury convicted him on all 165 counts, including 24 counts of first-degree murder. After the jury could not reach a unanimous decision on the death penalty, he was sentenced to 12 consecutive life sentences without parole plus 3,318 years.

Legacy

The shooting intensified the national debate over gun control and mental health care in the United States and contributed to Colorado legislation in 2013 expanding background checks and limiting magazine capacity. The theater reopened in January 2013 under a new name, and memorials to the victims have been held on each anniversary.
