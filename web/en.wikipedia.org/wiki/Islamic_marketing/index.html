Islamic marketing

Islamic marketing is a marketing discipline concerned with how marketing theory and practice can be adapted to the needs, values and consumption behaviour of Muslim consumers and markets. It emerged as a distinct field in the 2010s, prompted by the size of the global halal economy — spanning food, finance, travel, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics and modest fashion — and by debates about whether marketing itself is value-neutral or culturally situated.

Background

The global Muslim population, estimated at around 1.9 billion, represents a substantial and growing consumer market. Halal consumption is not limited to food: Islamic finance prohibits interest (riba) and excessive uncertainty (gharar), halal tourism serves Muslim travellers' needs for prayer facilities and halal food, and modest fashion serves religious dress requirements. The annual Global Islamic Economy Report has tracked this market since 2013.

Key themes

Research in Islamic marketing addresses halal branding and certification, the role of religious identity and religiosity in consumer choice, marketing ethics from an Islamic perspective, and the marketing of Muslim-minority markets in Europe and North America. A recurring question is whether Islamic marketing is simply conventional marketing applied to Muslim segments, or a distinct approach grounded in Islamic ethics, including honesty, fairness and the avoidance of exploitation and deception.

Criticism

Critics argue that "Islamic" branding can be used as a marketing ploy without genuine compliance, a practice dubbed "halal-washing". Others note that Muslim consumers are heterogeneous in income, practice and culture, and that over-segmentation can essentialise them.
