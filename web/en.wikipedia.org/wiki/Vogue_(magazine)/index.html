Vogue is an American monthly fashion and lifestyle magazine that covers style, culture, beauty, runway fashion and celebrity news. Founded in New York City in 1892 as a weekly society newspaper, it was acquired by Condé Nast in 1909 and transformed into a monthly women's fashion magazine. Today Vogue is published in numerous national editions around the world and is widely regarded as the most influential fashion magazine in the world.

The magazine became known for its photography, employing celebrated photographers and illustrators and helping to establish fashion photography as an art form. Its September issues are traditionally the largest and most commercially important of the year, a status documented in the 2009 film The September Issue.

History

Vogue was founded by Arthur Baldwin Turnure as a weekly newspaper for New York's social elite. After his death, Condé Montrose Nast purchased the title in 1909 and gradually turned it toward fashion coverage for women. British Vogue, launched in 1916, was the first international edition, followed by French Vogue in 1920.

Under editors such as Edna Woolman Chase, Diana Vreeland and Grace Mirabella, the magazine tracked — and often set — the course of twentieth-century fashion. Anna Wintour became editor-in-chief of American Vogue in 1988; her first cover, mixing inexpensive jeans with a couture top, signalled a more accessible, celebrity-driven direction. Wintour later also became Condé Nast's global chief content officer and global editorial director of Vogue.

Influence

Beyond the magazine itself, Vogue is associated with the annual Met Gala in New York, the fundraising ball for the Costume Institute of the Metropolitan Museum of Art, chaired by Wintour from the 1990s. The brand has expanded into digital media, video, and events, and its national editions — including Vogue Paris, Vogue Italia and Vogue Japan — each carry distinct editorial identities.
