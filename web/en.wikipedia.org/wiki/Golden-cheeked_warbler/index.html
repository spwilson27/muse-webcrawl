Golden-cheeked warbler

The golden-cheeked warbler (Setophaga chrysoparia) is a small songbird of the New World warbler family that breeds exclusively in central Texas and winters in southern Mexico and Central America. It is recognised by its bright yellow cheeks bordered by a black eye-line, black crown and throat, and white underparts streaked with black.

Habitat

For nesting, the species depends on mature Ashe juniper (cedar) bark, which females strip to build nests, mixed with oak and other hardwoods in juniper-oak woodland on the Edwards Plateau. This specialised requirement makes it vulnerable to land clearing for development and agriculture.

Conservation

The golden-cheeked warbler was listed as endangered under the US Endangered Species Act in 1990. Habitat protection around military reservations and preserves such as Balcones Canyonlands remains central to its recovery; periodic petitions to delist the species have been contested.
