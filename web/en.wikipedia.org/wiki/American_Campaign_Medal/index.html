American Campaign Medal

The American Campaign Medal is a United States military decoration created in 1942 for service in the American Theater during the Second World War. It recognised personnel who served within the defined theatre boundaries, on anti-submarine patrols, or under qualifying conditions outside the continental United States.

Criteria

Eligibility generally required service between 7 December 1941 and 2 March 1946. The theatre included North and South America and surrounding waters within specified limits. Bronze service stars could be worn for participation in designated anti-submarine or convoy escort operations and other credited actions.

Design

The bronze medal depicts a Navy cruiser, a B-24 Liberator bomber and a submarine in an enemy context associated with coastal defence, with the words “American Campaign”. The ribbon uses red, white and blue with additional stripes echoing both Allied and Axis colours interpreted in a stylised scheme. It is grouped with the European-African-Middle Eastern and Asiatic-Pacific campaign medals.
