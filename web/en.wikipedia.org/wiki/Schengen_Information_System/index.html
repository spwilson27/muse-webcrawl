Schengen Information System

Schengen Information System (SIS)
Type: Government database
Operator: eu-LISA
Established: 1995 (SIS I); 2013 (SIS II)

The Schengen Information System (SIS) is a large-scale information system that supports external border control and law enforcement cooperation in the Schengen Area of Europe. It allows border guards, police, customs, and judicial authorities in participating countries to enter and consult alerts on persons and objects, for example concerning missing persons, persons wanted for arrest or extradition, third-country nationals to be refused entry, and stolen vehicles, documents, and firearms.

History

The first-generation system (SIS I) went live in 1995 alongside the abolition of internal border controls. Its successor, SIS II, entered operation in April 2013, adding capabilities such as biometric data (fingerprints and photographs), European Arrest Warrant data, and links between alerts. The system is managed operationally by the EU agency eu-LISA, based in Tallinn, with the central system in Strasbourg and a backup near Salzburg.

Use and safeguards

SIS is one of the most widely used law-enforcement databases in the world, queried billions of times per year. Because it processes sensitive personal data, its use is governed by data-protection rules, rights of access and correction for individuals, and supervision by national data-protection authorities and the European Data Protection Supervisor. A renewed SIS legal framework adopted in 2018 added new alert categories, including on unknown wanted persons and return decisions.
