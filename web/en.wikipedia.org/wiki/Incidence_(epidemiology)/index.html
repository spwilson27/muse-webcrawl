Incidence (epidemiology)

In epidemiology, incidence is a measure of the probability of occurrence of a given medical condition in a population within a specified period of time. It counts new cases arising during the period, in contrast to prevalence, which counts all existing cases at a point or during a period.

Measures

Cumulative incidence (incidence proportion) is the number of new cases divided by the number of people at risk at the start of the period. The incidence rate divides new cases by the total person-time at risk, and is often expressed per 1,000 or 100,000 person-years. Incidence density sampling and survival analysis are used when individuals are followed for varying lengths of time.

Relation to prevalence

For a condition in a steady state, prevalence is approximately the product of incidence and average duration of disease. A treatment that prolongs life without curing a disease can therefore raise prevalence while incidence stays constant.
