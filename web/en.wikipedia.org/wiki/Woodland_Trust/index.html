Woodland Trust

The Woodland Trust is the largest woodland conservation charity in the United Kingdom, founded in 1972 and headquartered in Grantham, Lincolnshire. It protects, restores and creates native woodland and campaigns for ancient woods and trees.

History

The Trust was founded by retired farmer and businessman Kenneth Watkins in Devon, initially to buy and protect threatened woods in the south-west of England, beginning with Avon Valley Woods. It grew steadily through acquisitions aided by public appeals.

Work

The Trust cares for more than 1,000 woods covering over 33,000 hectares, all open to the public free of charge. Its activities include planting millions of native trees, restoring plantations on ancient woodland sites, recording veteran trees through the Ancient Tree Inventory, and running citizen-science projects such as Nature's Calendar, which tracks the timing of seasonal events.

Campaigns

The Trust campaigns against woodland loss to development and infrastructure, notably pressing for stronger legal protection for ancient woodland in planning policy, and promotes large-scale tree planting as part of the response to climate change and biodiversity loss.

Organisation

The charity has several hundred thousand members and supporters and works in partnership with landowners, communities, schools and governments across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
