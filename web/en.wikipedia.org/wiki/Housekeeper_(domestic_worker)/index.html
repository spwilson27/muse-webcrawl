Housekeeper (domestic worker)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A housekeeper is a person employed to manage a household and the domestic staff. According to the 1861 Victorian manual Mrs Beeton's Book of Household Management, the housekeeper's role in large households was that of a deputy to the mistress of the house, responsible for the household's stores, the supervision of female servants, cleaning, and the preparation of certain foods and preserves. In modern usage, a housekeeper is typically employed to perform cleaning and other domestic tasks in a private home, hotel, or institution; in hotels, "housekeeping" refers to the department responsible for cleaning guest rooms and public areas.

History

In the great houses of early modern and Victorian Britain, the housekeeper was one of the most senior servants, ranking near the butler and holding the keys to the store rooms, linen, and still room. She interviewed and supervised maids, kept household accounts, and was responsible for valuable goods such as tea, sugar, and china. As large domestic staffs declined in the 20th century, the role merged with that of the general domestic worker or cleaner.

Duties

Typical duties of a housekeeper include cleaning rooms, making beds, laundry and ironing, dusting, vacuuming, cleaning kitchens and bathrooms, grocery shopping and cooking in some households, and supervising other staff where applicable. Housekeepers may live in (residential) or live out, and may work full-time or part-time. In hotels, executive housekeepers manage teams of room attendants and inspectors.

Legal status

Domestic workers historically were excluded from many labour protections. The International Labour Organization's Domestic Workers Convention (No. 189), adopted in 2011, set standards for decent work for domestic workers, including housekeepers, and has been ratified by numerous countries.
