Gerlachovský štít (Slovak pronunciation: [ˈɡerlaxɔʊ̯skiː ˈʃciːt]; translated into English as Gerlachov Peak, German: Gerlsdorfer Spitze, Hungarian: Gerlachfalvi-csúcs) is the highest peak in the High Tatras, in Slovakia, and in the whole 1,500 km (930 mi) long Carpathian mountain chain. It is also the highest point in Slovakia and in Central Europe north of the Alps.

The mountain rises to 2,654.4 metres (8,708 ft) above sea level. Its distinctive pyramidal shape is marked by the 300 m (980 ft) high cirque above the Velická valley. Despite its relatively modest height compared with the Alps, Gerlachovský štít dominates the surrounding landscape and is visible from great distances, which led early geographers to overestimate its elevation.

Name

The peak takes its name from the village of Gerlachov at its foot, which itself is named after its founder, the Spiš count Gerlach. In official contexts the summit has carried several names over the centuries, reflecting the political history of the region: it was called Franz Joseph's Peak under Austria-Hungary, and during the twentieth century it was briefly renamed for both Legionaries and for Stalin (Stalinov štít, 1949–1959) before reverting to Gerlachovský štít.

History

Gerlachovský štít was long thought to be the highest mountain in the Carpathians; measurements in the nineteenth century at first favoured the neighbouring Lomnický štít, but surveys by the Austrian military cartographers in the 1830s correctly identified Gerlach as the highest summit. The first recorded ascent is attributed to the New Lesná schoolteacher and mountain guide Ján Still in 1834, although the authenticity of this ascent has been questioned. The first confirmed winter ascent was made in 1905.

Access

Unlike many High Tatra peaks, Gerlachovský štít has no marked tourist trail to its summit; it may only be climbed in the company of a certified mountain guide, or by members of national mountaineering associations on approved routes. The usual starting point is the Sliezsky dom hotel at the end of the Velická valley. Popular routes include the Velická próba and the Batizovská próba, both involving sections of fixed chains and exposed scrambling.
