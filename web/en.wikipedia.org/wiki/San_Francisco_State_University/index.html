San Francisco State University

San Francisco State University (San Francisco State, SF State, or SFSU) is a public research university in San Francisco, California, and part of the California State University system. Founded in 1899 as a normal school for training teachers, it has grown into a comprehensive university enrolling roughly 26,000 students and is known for its programs in cinema, creative writing, ethnic studies, and the sciences.

History

The institution began as San Francisco State Normal School and was among the first normal schools west of the Rockies. It became San Francisco State College and joined the California State College system in 1960. The campus was a focal point of 1960s student activism: the 1968–69 student strike, led by the Third World Liberation Front, was the longest student strike in U.S. history and resulted in the creation of the first College of Ethnic Studies in the nation. The university attained its current name and university status in 1974.

Campus and academics

The main campus occupies about 144 acres in the southwestern part of San Francisco, near Lake Merced and the Stonestown shopping centre. The university comprises several colleges, including Liberal & Creative Arts, Business, Education, Ethnic Studies, Health and Social Sciences, and Science & Engineering. Its film school is consistently ranked among the best in the United States, and its Romberg Tiburon Campus supports marine and environmental research on San Francisco Bay.

Student life and athletics

SF State's athletic teams, the Gators, compete in NCAA Division II. The university's motto is Experientia Docet ("Experience Teaches"). Notable alumni include filmmaker Francis Ford Coppola (who attended graduate studies), actors Annette Bening and Danny Glover, and many figures in Bay Area politics and the arts.
