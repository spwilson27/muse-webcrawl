FIFO (computing and electronics)

In computing and systems theory, FIFO (an acronym for first in, first out) is a method of organising the manipulation of a data structure where the oldest entry, the one that has been in the structure longest, is processed first. It is analogous to a queue of people waiting in line.

Data structures

A FIFO queue supports enqueue and dequeue operations. FIFOs are used as buffers wherever data arrives and is consumed at different rates — for example in data communications, print spooling, and operating system message queues.

Electronics

In digital electronics, a hardware FIFO is a dedicated memory circuit that stores data between two subsystems operating at different clock rates or burst patterns. Hardware FIFOs commonly provide full, empty, and almost-full status flags, and may be synchronous (one clock domain) or asynchronous (separate read and write clocks). They are ubiquitous in serial interfaces, video frame buffering and network hardware.

Related concepts

The opposite discipline is LIFO (last in, first out), implemented by a stack. In accounting and inventory management, FIFO is also a method of valuing stock, in which the oldest inventory is assumed to be sold first.
