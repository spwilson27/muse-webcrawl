Campanile

A campanile (Italian pronunciation: [kampaˈniːle], plural campanili) is a freestanding bell tower, usually adjacent to a church or civic building. The term derives from campana, Italian for "bell". Campaniles are characteristic of Italian architecture, especially of the medieval and Renaissance periods.

Notable examples

Famous campaniles include St Mark's Campanile in Venice, nearly 99 metres high, which collapsed in 1902 and was rebuilt exactly as before; Giotto's Campanile in Florence, clad in coloured marble; and the Leaning Tower of Pisa, a campanile famous for its unintended tilt caused by soft ground. The Campanile di San Marco served as a lighthouse and watchtower as well as a belfry. In the modern era, university campuses in other countries adopted the form, such as the Sather Tower at the University of California, Berkeley, commonly called "The Campanile".

Function

Beyond housing bells that called worshippers and marked the hours, campaniles served as symbols of civic pride and wealth, watchtowers, and landmarks for navigation. Their height was often a matter of competition between Italian city-states and noble families.
