Morskie Oko

Morskie Oko ("Eye of the Sea" in Polish) is the largest and one of the most famous lakes in the Tatra Mountains, in southern Poland. It lies in the Rybi Potok valley within Tatra National Park, at an elevation of about 1,395 m above sea level, beneath the slopes of the Mięguszowiecki Summits and near Poland's highest peak, Rysy (2,499 m).

The lake covers about 34.5 hectares and reaches a depth of just over 50 metres, making it the fourth-deepest lake in the Tatras. Its name comes from an old legend that the lake was connected to the sea by an underground passage. Documents from the 16th century already mention the lake, and it was first described in detail in the 19th century.

Tourism

Morskie Oko is one of the most visited natural attractions in Poland, reached by a roughly 8 km asphalt road from Palenica Białczańska, which visitors walk or travel by horse-drawn wagon. A mountain chalet (schronisko) on the lake's moraine shore serves hikers; trails continue around the lake to Czarny Staw pod Rysami and up to Rysy. The lake's waters are exceptionally clear and are home to trout; its surroundings preserve alpine vegetation and wildlife, including marmots and chamois.
