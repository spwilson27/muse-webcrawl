Avidya (Hinduism)

Sanskritअविद्या (avidyā)
Literal meaning"Non-knowledge", ignorance
Related conceptsVidya (knowledge), Maya, Atman, Brahman, Moksha

Avidya (Sanskrit: अविद्या, avidyā) is a Sanskrit word meaning ignorance, misconception, or lack of knowledge. In Hindu philosophy, particularly in the Upanishads and in Advaita Vedanta, avidya is the fundamental ignorance of one's true nature — the mistaken identification of the self (Atman) with the body, mind, and ego — which binds the soul to the cycle of rebirth (samsara) and is removed only by knowledge (vidya or jnana) of the identity of Atman and Brahman.

In the Upanishads and Vedanta

The term appears in the early Upanishads, where the Mundaka Upanishad contrasts para vidya (higher knowledge of the Absolute) with apara vidya (lower knowledge, including the Vedas themselves as texts, ritual, and grammar). Ignorance, in this tradition, is not mere absence of information but a positive misapprehension: superimposing (adhyasa) the unreal upon the real.

In Adi Shankara's Advaita Vedanta, avidya is closely associated with Maya, the power by which the one reality appears as the manifold world. Avidya operates both cosmically and individually: it veils (avarana) the true nature of the self and projects (vikshepa) the world of names and forms. Because it is beginningless yet terminable by knowledge, later Advaitins debated its locus and ontological status extensively. In Dvaita and Vishishtadvaita schools, ignorance is real but subordinate to God's grace, and liberation comes through devotion (bhakti) rather than knowledge alone.

In Yoga and Samkhya

In Patanjali's Yoga Sutras, avidya is the first of the five afflictions (kleshas) and the root of the others — egoism, attachment, aversion, and clinging to life. It is defined as mistaking the non-eternal for the eternal, the impure for the pure, suffering for pleasure, and the non-self for the self. The Samkhya school similarly treats non-discrimination between spirit (purusha) and matter (prakriti) as the cause of bondage.
