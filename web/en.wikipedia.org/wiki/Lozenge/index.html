Lozenge

A lozenge (◊) is a diamond-shaped figure, usually a rhombus with acute angles of 45° and 135°. The word also refers to a small medicated tablet or candy — such as a throat lozenge — that dissolves slowly in the mouth, historically often made in this shape.

Geometry and symbol

The lozenge shape appears widely in decoration, tiling and playing-card suits (diamonds). In Unicode it is represented by U+25CA ◊ LOZENGE. A related character appears on some keyboards and in proofreading marks, and the symbol is used in modal logic for the possibility operator.

Heraldry

In heraldry, the lozenge is a standard charge and a frequent field division. A coat of arms borne by a woman was traditionally displayed on a lozenge rather than a shield, and a "lozengy" field is patterned with lozenge shapes.

Medicine and confectionery

Throat lozenges, or cough drops, are small tablets containing soothing or antiseptic ingredients such as menthol, benzocaine or honey, intended to dissolve slowly and relieve irritation of the throat. The name carries over from the diamond shape in which such pastilles were originally cut.
