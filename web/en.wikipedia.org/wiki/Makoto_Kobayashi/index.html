Makoto Kobayashi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Makoto Kobayashi
Born7 April 1944, Nagoya, Japan
Known forCKM matrix; CP violation with Toshihide Maskawa
AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (2008)
[Image: Photograph of Makoto Kobayashi at the 2008 Nobel press conference]
Kobayashi in 2008.

Makoto Kobayashi (born 7 April 1944) is a Japanese theoretical physicist known, with his mentor Toshihide Maskawa, for the 1973 paper 'CP Violation in the Renormalizable Theory of Weak Interaction', which explained CP violation by extending the quark mixing matrix to three generations — thereby predicting the existence of a third generation of quarks years before the bottom (1977) and top (1995) quarks were found.

The Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa (CKM) matrix became a central element of the Standard Model, and the Kobayashi–Maskawa mechanism — requiring a complex phase in the mixing matrix, impossible with only two generations — is the Standard Model's sole source of CP violation, implicated (though insufficiently) in the universe's matter–antimatter asymmetry. Kobayashi shared the 2008 Nobel Prize in Physics with Maskawa and Yoichiro Nambu. Experiments at the 'B factories' (BaBar and Belle, the latter at Kobayashi's home institution KEK) confirmed the mechanism's predictions in beautiful detail around 2001–2004.

Career

Kobayashi studied at Nagoya University (PhD 1972), where Maskawa was his senior collaborator; their famous paper appeared when Kobayashi was 28. He later worked at KEK (the High Energy Accelerator Research Organization) in Tsukuba, directing its Institute of Particle and Nuclear Studies. The paper they wrote is among the most cited in particle physics history.
