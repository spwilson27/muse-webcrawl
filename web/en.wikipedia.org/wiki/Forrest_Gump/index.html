Forrest Gump

Forrest Gump is a 1994 American comedy-drama film directed by Robert Zemeckis, written by Eric Roth, and based on Winston Groom’s 1986 novel. Tom Hanks stars as Forrest Gump, a kind-hearted Alabama man with a low IQ who unwittingly influences decades of American history while pining for his childhood love Jenny (Robin Wright).

Forrest Gump
Director: Robert Zemeckis
Starring: Tom Hanks, Robin Wright, Gary Sinise
Released: 1994
Box office: ~$678 million
Awards: 6 Academy Awards incl. Best Picture

Plot

Sitting at a Savannah bus stop, Forrest narrates his life: childhood leg braces, college football, Vietnam service where he saves Lt. Dan Taylor, ping-pong diplomacy, shrimp boating with Bubba’s dream, a cross-country run, and reunion with Jenny and their son. Famous lines include “Life is like a box of chocolates” and “Run, Forrest, run!”

Reception and legacy

The film was the top-grossing film in North America in 1994, won Academy Awards for Best Picture, Director and Actor, and pioneered digital effects inserting Forrest into archival footage with figures such as President Kennedy. It entered the National Film Registry in 2011, though critics debate its politics and sentimentality.
