Christoph Hoffmann

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christoph Hoffmann (2 December 1815 – 8 December 1885) was a German religious leader and founder of the Templer Society (Tempelgesellschaft), a Pietist movement that established settlements in the Holy Land in the second half of the nineteenth century.

Life

Hoffmann was born in Leonberg in the Kingdom of Württemberg, the son of Gottlieb Wilhelm Hoffmann, a notary and mayor who founded the Pietist settlement of Korntal. Christoph Hoffmann studied theology and became a teacher and writer, and in 1854, together with like-minded associates, he founded the Society for the Gathering of the People of God in Jerusalem — the Templers — which sought to realize a Christian communal life in Palestine.

Settlements in Palestine

After an initially difficult relationship with the Württemberg church, Hoffmann and his followers emigrated to the Holy Land beginning in 1868. They founded colonies at Haifa, Jaffa, Sarona (now part of Tel Aviv), Jerusalem and elsewhere, introducing modern agriculture, crafts, mills, transport services and schools. Hoffmann settled in Jerusalem, where he died in 1885. The Templer colonies flourished under Ottoman and later British Mandate rule; most German Templers were interned and deported during the Second World War, and their property was later transferred to the State of Israel.
