Rayleigh number

In fluid mechanics, the Rayleigh number (Ra, after the British physicist Lord Rayleigh) is a dimensionless number associated with buoyancy-driven flow, also known as free (or natural) convection. It characterises the fluid's flow regime: below a critical value, heat transfer occurs primarily by conduction; above it, convection dominates.

Definition

For free convection near a vertical surface, the Rayleigh number is the product of the Grashof number (Gr) and the Prandtl number (Pr): Ra = Gr · Pr. In terms of fluid properties it combines the thermal expansion coefficient, the temperature difference, the characteristic length cubed, gravity, and the fluid's viscosity and thermal diffusivity: Ra = g β (Ts − T∞) L3 / (ν α).

Critical values

For a fluid layer heated from below, convection begins at a critical Rayleigh number of about 1,708. In the Earth's mantle, the Rayleigh number is estimated to be of order 106–107, explaining why the mantle convects despite its enormous viscosity. See also Rayleigh–Bénard convection.
