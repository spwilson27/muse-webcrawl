Scaling and root planing

Scaling and root planing, also known as conventional periodontal therapy, non-surgical periodontal therapy, or dental deep cleaning, is a procedure involving the removal of dental plaque and calculus (scaling) from the surfaces of teeth, including below the gumline, and the smoothing (planing) of the exposed root surfaces to remove bacterial toxins and discourage further plaque accumulation.

Procedure

The procedure is the primary non-surgical treatment for periodontitis. Using hand instruments such as curettes and scalers, and ultrasonic scalers, the clinician cleans out the periodontal pockets that form between teeth and gums in gum disease, removing the biofilm and hardened tartar that brushing cannot reach. Local anaesthetic is commonly used, and treatment is often performed quadrant by quadrant over several visits.

Effectiveness and aftercare

Successful scaling and root planing reduces pocket depths, bleeding on probing and inflammation, and can halt the progression of periodontitis, though lost bone does not regenerate. Effectiveness depends heavily on patient plaque control afterwards: regular brushing, interdental cleaning and periodic professional maintenance are essential. Patients may experience temporary tooth sensitivity and gum soreness after treatment. Where pockets remain deep after healing, surgical periodontal therapy may be considered.
