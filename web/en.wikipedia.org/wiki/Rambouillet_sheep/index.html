Rambouillet sheep

The Rambouillet is a breed of sheep also known as the Rambouillet Merino or the French Merino. It was developed in France from Spanish Merino stock beginning in 1786, when Louis XVI established a flock at Rambouillet, near Paris.

Characteristics

Rambouillets are large, rugged, white-faced sheep prized for both fine wool and meat, making them a classic dual-purpose breed. Ewes typically weigh 70–90 kg and rams 110–135 kg. The fleece is dense and fine, comparable to Merino wool, while the carcass qualities exceed those of the pure Merino.

Distribution

Exported widely from the mid-19th century, the Rambouillet became the foundation of much of the range sheep industry in the western United States, where it has been crossed with other breeds to produce sheep adapted to harsh range conditions. It remains one of the most influential wool breeds worldwide.
