The Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) is a United States government agency within the Department of Commerce that provides economic statistics, most notably the gross domestic product (GDP) of the United States. Its mission is to promote a better understanding of the U.S. economy by providing the most timely, relevant, and accurate economic data in a clear and cost-effective manner.

Principal statistics

BEA produces the national income and product accounts (NIPA), including quarterly GDP in advance, second and third estimates; monthly personal income and consumption expenditures (the source of the PCE price index watched by the Federal Reserve); the U.S. balance of payments and international investment position; GDP by state and by industry; and input–output tables showing how industries interact.

History

BEA traces its origins to 1820, when the Secretary of the Treasury was directed to prepare statistics on commerce and navigation. The modern bureau was created in 1972 from the Office of Business Economics, which had developed the national accounts under Simon Kuznets's intellectual influence in the 1930s and 1940s.

Relationship to other agencies

BEA is one of the three principal U.S. economic statistical agencies, alongside the Census Bureau (also in Commerce) and the Bureau of Labor Statistics (in Labor). It relies heavily on source data collected by those agencies and by the Internal Revenue Service and Federal Reserve.
