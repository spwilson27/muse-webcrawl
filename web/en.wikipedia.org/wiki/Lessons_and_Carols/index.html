Lessons and Carols

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A service of Lessons and Carols is a format of Christian worship, most often celebrated during Advent and Christmas, in which a sequence of short Bible readings (the "lessons") is interspersed with Christmas carols, hymns, and choral music. The best known version is the Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols held each Christmas Eve in the chapel of King's College, Cambridge, and broadcast worldwide by the BBC.

Origins

The format is generally credited to Edward White Benson, later Archbishop of Canterbury, who devised a service of nine lessons and carols for Christmas Eve 1880 at Truro Cathedral in Cornwall, partly to draw worshippers away from public houses on the holiday. The format spread through the Church of England and beyond. In 1918 Eric Milner-White, Dean of King's College, Cambridge, adapted the service for the college chapel, revising it in 1919; that order of service, with minor changes, is still used at King's today.

The King's College service

The Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols begins, by tradition, with a solo boy chorister singing the first verse of "Once in Royal David's City" unaccompanied. The nine lessons — read by members of the college, the city, and the university, ending with the Provost — trace the story of the fall of humanity, the promise of a Messiah, and the birth of Jesus, drawing on Genesis, the prophets, and the Gospels. The service closes with the hymn "Hark! The Herald Angels Sing." King's has broadcast the service since 1928, and it is carried by the BBC World Service and public radio networks in the United States, making it one of the most widely heard Christmas broadcasts in the world. Since 1982 the college has commissioned a new carol each year for the service.

Variants

Many churches, schools, and universities hold their own lessons and carols services, ranging from the full nine-lesson format to shorter Advent services. Similar services are also adapted for other seasons and occasions, and similar patterns of readings and music are used in secular settings such as school Christmas celebrations.
