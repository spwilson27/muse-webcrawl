Solesmes Abbey

Solesmes Abbey, or Saint Peter's Abbey, Solesmes (French: Abbaye Saint-Pierre de Solesmes), is a Benedictine monastery in Solesmes, Sarthe, France. It is famous as the centre of the restoration of Gregorian chant in the 19th and 20th centuries, and is the mother house of the Solesmes Congregation of Benedictine monasteries.

History

A priory dedicated to Saint Peter was founded at Solesmes around 1010, dependent on the abbey of Le Mans. Like other French monasteries it was suppressed during the French Revolution, and its buildings were sold. In 1833 the young priest Prosper Guéranger purchased the surviving buildings and refounded monastic life there; in 1837 the priory was raised to an abbey by Pope Gregory XVI, with Guéranger as its first abbot, and it became the head of a revived French Benedictine congregation.

Under the French anti-clerical laws of 1901 the community was expelled and lived in exile on the Isle of Wight, at Quarr Abbey, returning to Solesmes after the First World War. Nearby, the abbey of Sainte-Cécile serves the congregation's nuns.

Gregorian chant

Guéranger set out to restore the authentic chant of the Roman liturgy from medieval manuscripts. Successive Solesmes monks — Dom Joseph Pothier, Dom André Mocquereau, and later Dom Eugène Cardine — developed methods of chant scholarship and performance, editing the chant books used throughout the Roman Catholic Church, including the Liber Usualis and the Graduale Romanum (1974). The "Solesmes method," with its rhythmic signs, became the standard interpretation worldwide, and the abbey's recordings brought its choir international fame.

Architecture

The abbey church is renowned for its Renaissance sculpture groups, the "Saints of Solesmes" — elaborate carved ensembles of the Entombment of Christ (1496) and the Dormition of the Virgin (1554) in the transepts, regarded as masterpieces of French late-Gothic and Renaissance sculpture.
