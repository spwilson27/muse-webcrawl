Erdős–Szekeres theorem

In mathematics, the Erdős–Szekeres theorem asserts that, given r and s, any sequence of distinct real numbers with length at least (r − 1)(s − 1) + 1 contains a monotonically increasing subsequence of length r or a monotonically decreasing subsequence of length s. The theorem, proved by Paul Erdős and George Szekeres in 1935, is one of the founding results of Ramsey theory and extremal combinatorics.

Proof idea

A classic proof labels each term of the sequence with the pair (a, b), where a is the length of the longest increasing subsequence ending at that term and b the length of the longest decreasing subsequence ending there. Distinct terms receive distinct pairs, so if no increasing subsequence of length r and no decreasing subsequence of length s existed, there would be at most (r − 1)(s − 1) terms — a contradiction when the sequence is longer.

Related results

The same 1935 paper founded the study of convex polygons in point sets and led to the famous "happy ending problem". Mirsky's theorem gives an equivalent formulation in terms of partially ordered sets. The result also underlies patience sorting and algorithms for longest increasing subsequences.
