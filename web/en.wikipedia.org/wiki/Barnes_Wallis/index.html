Barnes Wallis

Sir Barnes Neville Wallis (26 September 1887 – 30 October 1979) was an English scientist, engineer and inventor. He is best known for inventing the bouncing bomb used by the Royal Air Force's No. 617 Squadron in the famous "Dambusters" raid (Operation Chastise) of May 1943 against the dams of Germany's Ruhr valley.

Early career and airships

Wallis began his career in marine engineering before moving into aircraft design at Vickers. He pioneered geodetic (lattice) construction in the airship R100, which crossed the Atlantic in 1930, and applied the same lightweight, strong framework to the Wellesley and Wellington bombers.

Second World War

His bouncing bomb, code-named Upkeep, was spun backwards and dropped at low level so that it skipped across the water, sank against the dam wall and exploded at depth. After the war Wallis designed the huge "earthquake bombs" Tallboy and Grand Slam. He later worked on swing-wing aircraft, hypersonic designs, and the Barnes Wallis "wild goose" variable-geometry concepts.

Legacy

Wallis was knighted in 1968. He was portrayed by Michael Redgrave in the 1955 film The Dam Busters. A statue of him stands in Herne Bay, Kent, where trials of the bouncing bomb were conducted.
