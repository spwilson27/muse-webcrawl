World War II or the Second World War was a global conflict between two coalitions: the Allies and the Axis powers. Nearly all of the world's countries participated, with many nations mobilising all resources in pursuit of total war. Tanks and aircraft played major roles, as did radar, codebreaking and the first computers such as those described in the Computer article. The war saw the only use of nuclear weapons in armed conflict, and the Holocaust, in which Nazi Germany murdered about six million Jews and millions of others.

Course of the war

The war in Europe is generally held to have begun on 1 September 1939 with Germany's invasion of Poland, prompting declarations of war by Britain and France; the Second Sino-Japanese War had begun in 1937. Germany conquered much of Europe in 1939–41, then invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941 (Operation Barbarossa). Japan's attack on Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941 brought the United States into the war. Turning points included El Alamein and Stalingrad (1942–43), the Allied invasion of Normandy on D-Day, 6 June 1944, and island-hopping across the Pacific. Germany surrendered in May 1945; Japan surrendered on 2 September 1945, after the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet invasion of Manchuria.

Aftermath

The war reshaped the world: the United Nations was founded; the United States and Soviet Union emerged as superpowers, soon locked in the Cold War; European colonial empires unravelled; Germany and Korea were divided; and war-crimes trials at Nuremberg and Tokyo set precedents in international law. Figures such as Lincoln's era had reshaped nations before; WWII's leaders — Churchill, Roosevelt, Stalin, Hitler, Hirohito — are detailed in linked biographies from the History portal.

Sources and remembrance

Extensive archives, memoirs and scholarship document the war; contemporary reporting in papers such as The New York Times and broadcasts by the BBC (whose wartime services are part of its history) remain primary sources studied by historians.
