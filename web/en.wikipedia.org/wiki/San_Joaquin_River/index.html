San Joaquin River

San Joaquin River
Length: 366 miles (589 km)
Source: Sierra Nevada, California
Mouth: Suisun Bay (with the Sacramento River)
Basin: San Joaquin Valley

The San Joaquin River is the longest river of Central California. Rising in the high Sierra Nevada near Thousand Island Lake, the 366-mile (589 km) river flows generally northwest through the San Joaquin Valley — the agricultural southern half of the Central Valley — past Fresno, Modesto, and Stockton, joining the Sacramento River in the Delta before reaching San Francisco Bay.

Course and watershed

The river's headwaters lie in the Ansel Adams Wilderness. It descends through hydroelectric reservoirs, enters the valley floor near Friant Dam northeast of Fresno, and is joined by the Fresno, Merced, Tuolumne, and Stanislaus rivers. The basin drains about 32,000 square miles.

Use and restoration

The San Joaquin is one of California's most heavily managed rivers. The Central Valley Project's Friant Dam (completed 1942) and the Friant-Kern and Madera canals divert most of its flow to irrigation, which caused a roughly 60-mile stretch below the dam to run dry in many years and eliminated its historic Chinook salmon runs. Litigation by environmental groups (NRDC v. Rodgers) led to a 2006 settlement and the San Joaquin River Restoration Program, which releases restoration flows and aims to reintroduce salmon. The lower river also carries significant agricultural drainage and has long-standing water-quality problems, including selenium and salinity.
