Maurice Maeterlinck

Maurice Polydore Marie Bernard Maeterlinck (29 August 1862 – 6 May 1949) was a Belgian playwright, poet, and essayist who wrote in French. A leading figure of Symbolism, he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1911 "in appreciation of his many-sided literary activities, and especially of his dramatic works."

Life

Maeterlinck was born in Ghent, Belgium, to a wealthy francophone family. He studied law at the University of Ghent and briefly practiced law before turning to literature. He lived for many years in France, notably at the Abbaye de Saint-Wandrille in Normandy, and spent the Second World War in the United States. He was made a count by King Albert I of Belgium in 1932.

Works

Maeterlinck's early plays, written in a static, dreamlike Symbolist style, include La Princesse Maleine (1889), L'Intruse (1890), Les Aveugles (1890), and Pelléas et Mélisande (1893), the last of which inspired musical settings by Fauré, Debussy, Sibelius, and Schoenberg. His greatest popular success was the fairy play The Blue Bird (L'Oiseau bleu, 1908). He also wrote celebrated philosophical nature essays, including The Life of the Bee (1901), The Intelligence of Flowers (1907), and The Life of Termites (1926).
