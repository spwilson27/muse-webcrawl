Worms Cathedral (Wormser Dom), formally St Peter's Cathedral, is a Roman Catholic church in Worms, Germany, and one of the great masterpieces of Romanesque architecture. Together with Speyer and Mainz cathedrals, it is one of the three "imperial cathedrals" (Kaiserdome) of the Rhineland. It is recognizable by its four slender round towers and two domes.

History

A bishopric existed at Worms from the early Middle Ages. Bishop Burchard of Worms built an early Romanesque cathedral in the early 11th century; the present church was begun around 1130 and constructed in stages through the 12th century, with the east choir and transept completed first and the nave and west choir following. The building was consecrated in 1181, though work on the west end continued into the 13th century.

Architecture

The cathedral is a double-choired basilica built of red sandstone, richly decorated with dwarf galleries and sculpted portals. The south portal is framed by elaborate Romanesque and early Gothic sculpture. The Nicholas Chapel and the Gothic Chapel of St Anne were later additions. The church preserves important Baroque furnishings, including a gilded high altar designed by Balthasar Neumann.

Historical associations

Worms was the scene of major events of imperial history, including the Concordat of Worms (1122) that ended the Investiture Controversy, and the Diet of Worms of 1521 at which Martin Luther defended his writings — though Luther's hearing took place in the bishop's palace beside the cathedral, since destroyed. The cathedral suffered severe damage in the Thirty Years' War, the Nine Years' War of 1689, and Second World War bombing, and has been extensively restored.
