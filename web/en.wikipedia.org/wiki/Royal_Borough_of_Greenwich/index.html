Royal Borough of Greenwich

The Royal Borough of Greenwich is a London borough in south-east England, created in 1965 by merging the former Metropolitan Boroughs of Greenwich and Woolwich. It was granted royal borough status in 2012, during the Diamond Jubilee of Elizabeth II, in recognition of its historic royal links.

Geography and landmarks

The borough stretches along the south bank of the River Thames from Deptford to Thamesmead and includes Eltham, Charlton, Plumstead and Woolwich. Its centre, Greenwich town, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (Maritime Greenwich) containing the Royal Observatory — the site of the Prime Meridian and Greenwich Mean Time — the National Maritime Museum, the Queen's House, the Old Royal Naval College and the tea clipper Cutty Sark. Elsewhere stand Eltham Palace, the Thames Barrier and the O2 arena on the Greenwich Peninsula.

Population and governance

The borough has a population of around 290,000, administered by Greenwich Council from Woolwich Town Hall. The University of Greenwich and the Royal Arsenal development are major employers and regeneration anchors.
