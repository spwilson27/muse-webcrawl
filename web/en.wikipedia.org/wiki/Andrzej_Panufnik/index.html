Andrzej Panufnik

Born24 September 1914, Warsaw, Poland
Died27 October 1991, Twickenham, London, England
OccupationComposer, conductor
ChildrenRoxanna Panufnik, Jeremy Panufnik

Sir Andrzej Panufnik (24 September 1914 – 27 October 1991) was a Polish composer and conductor. He became a British citizen in 1961 and is regarded as one of the most significant Polish composers after Chopin.

Life

Panufnik studied composition and conducting at the Warsaw Conservatory and continued his conducting studies in Vienna with Felix Weingartner. During the Second World War he played piano duos in Warsaw cafés with Witold Lutosławski; nearly all of his early works were destroyed in the Warsaw Uprising of 1944. After the war he became chief conductor of the Kraków Philharmonic and later of the Warsaw Philharmonic, but increasing interference with his work under the Stalinist regime led him to defect to Britain in 1954 while on a conducting tour in Switzerland.

Career in Britain

In England Panufnik served as chief conductor of the City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra (1957–1959) and then devoted himself chiefly to composition. His output includes ten symphonies, concertos for violin, cello and piano, chamber music, and vocal works. Works such as the Tragic Overture, Sinfonia Sacra and the Universal Prayer (to a text by Alexander Pope) won him wide recognition. He was knighted in 1991, the year of his death. His daughter Roxanna Panufnik is also a composer.
