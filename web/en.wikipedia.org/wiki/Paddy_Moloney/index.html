Paddy Moloney (Irish: Pádraig Ó Maoldomhnaigh; 1 August 1938 – 12 October 2021) was an Irish musician, composer and record producer. He co-founded and led the traditional Irish music band The Chieftains in 1962, serving as its principal composer and arranger, and played the uilleann pipes and tin whistle.

Early life

Moloney was born in Donnycarney, Dublin. He began playing the tin whistle at the age of six and took up the uilleann pipes at eight, learning from the noted piper Leo Rowsome. In his teens he played with several groups before joining Seán Ó Riada's ensemble Ceoltóirí Chualann in the early 1960s.

The Chieftains

In 1962 Moloney formed The Chieftains with Sean Potts and Michael Tubridy. The band became one of the most influential groups in Irish traditional music, bringing the genre to concert halls worldwide and collaborating with artists including Van Morrison, The Rolling Stones, Madonna and many country and classical performers. Under Moloney's direction the group won six Grammy Awards from eighteen nominations, performed for Pope John Paul II in Phoenix Park, Dublin, in 1979 before an audience of more than a million, and played on the Great Wall of China.

Other work

Moloney founded the Claddagh Records label with Garech Browne in 1959. He contributed music to films including Treasure Island (1950 version score work in his early career), Barry Lyndon (with The Chieftains, 1975) and The Grey Fox (1982). He also produced albums for artists such as Mike Oldfield.

Death and legacy

Moloney died suddenly on 12 October 2021, aged 83. Tributes were paid by the President of Ireland, Michael D. Higgins, and by musicians worldwide, recognising his central role in popularising Irish traditional music internationally.
