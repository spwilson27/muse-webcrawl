Northern Yuan

The Northern Yuan was the Mongol-ruled state that succeeded the Yuan dynasty of China after its court was expelled from China proper by the Ming dynasty in 1368. Ruled by the Borjigin khans from the Mongolian plateau, it retained the Yuan dynastic claim and imperial institutions while reverting largely to a steppe-based, nomadic polity. It lasted until 1635, when the last khan, Ligdan, submitted to the Later Jin (Qing).

History

After the Ming founder Zhu Yuanzhang took Dadu (Beijing) in 1368, Toghon Temür withdrew to Mongolia, where the Mongol regime continued as a rump empire. Under Ayurshiridara and Tögüs Temür, the Northern Yuan resisted Ming campaigns into the steppe, winning notable victories such as the battle of Lake Buir in 1388.

From the 15th century the khanate fragmented among powerful appanage princes, was overshadowed at times by the Oirat confederation (whose leader Esen briefly captured the Ming emperor in 1449), and was partly reunified by Dayan Khan around 1500. Altan Khan (1507–1582) reintroduced Tibetan Buddhism on a large scale and founded Hohhot. Ligdan Khan's centralising policies alienated the eastern Mongol tribes, many of which submitted to Nurhaci and Hong Taiji; in 1635 Ligdan's son surrendered the Yuan imperial seal, ending the Northern Yuan.
