The International Narcotics Control Board (INCB) is an independent and quasi-judicial monitoring body for the implementation of the United Nations international drug control conventions. It was established in 1968 under the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs of 1961, which merged earlier treaty bodies, and its responsibilities were extended by the Convention on Psychotropic Substances of 1971 and the Convention against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances of 1988.

Functions

The Board administers the system of estimates of national legitimate requirements for narcotic drugs and the statistical returns system for psychotropic substances, monitors licit trade through import/export authorisations, and approves estimates so that medical and scientific supplies are adequate but not diverted. For precursors under the 1988 Convention it operates the PEN (pre-export notification) Online system and PICS, the Precursors Incident Communication System. If a government fails to comply with the treaties, the Board may call the matter to the attention of the parties, ECOSOC and the Commission on Narcotic Drugs, and may recommend that countries stop drug imports from or exports to the defaulting country.

Membership and operation

The Board consists of 13 members elected by the UN Economic and Social Council for five-year terms: three members with medical, pharmacological or pharmaceutical experience from a list nominated by the World Health Organization, and ten from lists nominated by governments. Members serve in their personal capacity, not as government representatives. The INCB secretariat is based in Vienna and is administratively part of the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC), while remaining substantively independent. The Board publishes an annual report surveying the world drug situation and compliance, sometimes singling out countries whose policies it judges contrary to the conventions — a stance that has drawn criticism over issues such as harm reduction, capital punishment for drug offences and cannabis legalisation.
