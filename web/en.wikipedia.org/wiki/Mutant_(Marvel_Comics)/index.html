Mutant (Marvel Comics)

In the Marvel Universe, mutants are humans (and sometimes other species) born with a genetic trait called the X-gene, which grants them superhuman powers and abilities that usually manifest at puberty. Mutants are depicted as an evolutionary next step of humanity (Homo superior), and they are the focus of the X-Men franchise and its many related series.

Concept and creation

Editor/writer Stan Lee conceived mutants as a way of creating large numbers of superheroes without having to invent a separate origin for each one: they were simply born with their powers. The first Marvel mutant in publication terms was Namor the Sub-Mariner (1939), later retconned as a mutant, but the concept was formalized with the debut of the X-Men in 1963.

The X-gene

The X-gene (or X-factor gene) is an anomalous gene found in mutants. In Marvel's fictional cosmology it derives from experiments by the Celestials on early humanity. Carriers gain powers that vary from minor cosmetic differences to reality-warping abilities; mutants are conventionally rated on power scales, with so-called Omega-level mutants possessing the highest potential in their power category.

Mutants in Marvel society

Mutants are widely feared and discriminated against by ordinary humans, a status used as a metaphor for real-world minority groups. Key factions include Professor Charles Xavier's X-Men, who promote peaceful coexistence; Magneto's Brotherhood of Mutants, which has often pursued mutant supremacy or separatism; and human organizations such as the Sentinels program, which builds giant robots to hunt mutants.

Major story arcs include the near-extinction of the mutant population in House of M (2005), when the Scarlet Witch's declaration "No more mutants" reduced the mutant population to fewer than two hundred, and the Krakoa era (from 2019's House of X / Powers of X), in which mutants founded a sovereign island nation open to all mutants and developed resurrection protocols that restored millions of deceased mutants.
