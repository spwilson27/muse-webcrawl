Peter Brown (historian)

Born: July 26, 1935, Dublin, Ireland

Fields: Late antiquity, early Christianity, social history

Institutions: Princeton University, Oxford University, UC Berkeley

Peter Robert Lamont Brown (born July 26, 1935) is an Irish-born historian of late antiquity, widely credited with having helped create the modern field through his studies of the religious and social world of the later Roman Empire and early Middle Ages.

Life and career

Brown was born in Dublin to a Protestant family and educated at Shrewsbury School and New College, Oxford, where he studied history and was influenced by Arnaldo Momigliano. He became a fellow of All Souls College, Oxford, in 1956. He later taught at Royal Holloway, University of London, and the University of California, Berkeley, before becoming the Philip and Beulah Rollins Professor of History at Princeton University in 1986, a post he held until retirement in 2011.

Scholarship

Brown's biography Augustine of Hippo (1967) remains a standard work. His The World of Late Antiquity (1971) reframed the centuries after Rome's decline not as an age of decay but as a period of vibrant cultural and religious transformation. The Cult of the Saints (1981) analyzed how the veneration of saints and relics reshaped late antique society, while The Body and Society (1988) examined sexual renunciation in early Christianity. Later works include Authority and the Sacred (1995), The Rise of Western Christendom (1996, revised 2003), Poverty and Leadership in the Later Roman Empire (2002), Through the Eye of a Needle (2012), a study of wealth and the church, and The Ransom of the Soul (2015). Journeys of the Mind, his intellectual autobiography, appeared in 2023.

Honors

Brown received a MacArthur Fellowship ("genius grant") in 1982, the Balzan Prize in 2011, and the Kluge Prize in 2008. He is a fellow of the British Academy and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.
