Sangiovese

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sangiovese grapes ripening in Tuscany

Sangiovese is a red Italian wine grape variety that derives its name from the Latin sanguis Jovis, "the blood of Jupiter". It is Italy's most widely planted red grape and the principal variety behind many of the country's most celebrated wines.

Wines

Sangiovese is the dominant grape of Chianti and Chianti Classico in Tuscany, the sole permitted variety in Brunello di Montalcino, and a main component of Vino Nobile di Montepulciano, Morellino di Scansano and many "Super Tuscan" blends. Outside Tuscany it is important in Emilia-Romagna (Sangiovese di Romagna) and appears in Umbria, Marche and, as Nielluccio, on Corsica.

Viticulture and character

The variety is late-ripening, thin-skinned and prone to mutation, with many recognised clones and local synonyms including Brunello, Prugnolo Gentile and Morellino. DNA profiling has identified its parents as the Tuscan variety Ciliegiolo and the obscure southern Italian grape Calabrese Montenuovo. Typical Sangiovese wines are medium-bodied with high acidity and firm tannins, and flavours of red cherry, plum, dried herbs and earth; the best examples age gracefully for decades.

History

The grape is documented in Tuscany at least from the sixteenth century, and early references praise wines from the vine in the region. Its reputation rose in the twentieth century as Chianti's quality improved and Brunello di Montalcino became one of the world's most collectible wines.
