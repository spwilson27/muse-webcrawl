Shaykh al-Islām

Shaykh al-Islām (Arabic: شيخ الإسلام, romanized: Shaykh al-Islām; Ottoman Turkish: شيخ الاسلام, romanized: Şeyḫülislām) was a title applied in the premodern Islamic world to outstanding scholars of the Islamic sciences. It first emerged in Khurasan towards the end of the 4th Islamic century (10th–11th century CE). In the Ottoman Empire, it came to denote the empire's chief jurisconsult and highest religious authority, an office of great political weight.

Ottoman officeholders of the title ranked among the highest dignitaries of the empire.

As an honorific title

In the medieval period, Shaykh al-Islām ("Elder of Islam") was an honorific bestowed on leading jurists and theologians, among them figures such as Ibn Taymiyyah, al-Nawawi, and Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani. Its use was informal and could be claimed by or attributed to more than one scholar in the same era; Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Abu Hamid al-Ghazali were among those so described by admirers.

Ottoman office

From the 15th century the title attached to the chief mufti of Istanbul, appointed by the sultan. The Ottoman Shaykh al-Islam issued fatwas that legitimated state policy, supervised the learned hierarchy (ilmiye), the judiciary, and religious education, and could in principle depose a sultan through a fetva declaring him unfit. Noted holders include Ebussuud Efendi under Suleiman the Magnificent, who harmonised imperial kanun with the Sharia. The office grew in importance as the empire aged and was abolished in 1924 with the founding reforms of the Republic of Turkey, its functions transferred to the Directorate of Religious Affairs (Diyanet).

Regional uses

Comparable titles and offices existed elsewhere: in Safavid and Qajar Iran the Shaykh al-Islām was a senior judicial-religious official in major cities; in Southeast Asia the title was used in the Malay world; and under the Mughals and in later Indian usage it remained a scholarly honorific. In the modern period the title continues to be used honorifically for senior scholars in several countries.
