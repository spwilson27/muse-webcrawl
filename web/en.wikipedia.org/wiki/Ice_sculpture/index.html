Ice sculpture

Ice sculpture is a form of sculpture that uses ice as the raw material. Because the material is ephemeral, works are usually associated with special or festive events, from elegant table centrepieces at banquets to the monumental constructions of winter festivals.

Techniques

Sculptors work with large, clear blocks of ice — often produced in moulds or harvested from frozen lakes — using chainsaws, chisels, die grinders, hand saws and sometimes computer-controlled routers. Clarity matters: ice frozen slowly from filtered, agitated water is transparent and carves predictably. Sculptors exploit refraction, coloured lighting and fusing pieces with water to assemble complex figures.

Events

Major ice events include the Harbin International Ice and Snow Festival in China, the Sapporo Snow Festival in Japan, and competitions such as Ice Alaska's World Ice Art Championships. Small commercial sculptures remain a staple of weddings, corporate events and seafood displays.
