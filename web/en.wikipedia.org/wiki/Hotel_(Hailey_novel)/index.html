Hotel (Hailey novel)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

First edition cover of Hotel

Hotel is a 1965 novel by British-Canadian author Arthur Hailey. Set over five days in the fictional St. Gregory Hotel in New Orleans, it follows the interlocking dramas of the hotel's staff and guests as the ageing independent hotel faces financial crisis and a possible takeover by a chain run by the ruthless Curtis O'Keefe. The novel spent 48 weeks on the New York Times Best Seller list.

Plot

General manager Peter McDermott struggles to keep the St. Gregory solvent while handling a succession of crises: a hit-and-run scandal involving a guest, a would-be rapist confronted by staff, an ageing duke and duchess concealing their ruin, a thief working the rooms, and a union dispute. Hotel owner Warren Trent must decide whether to sell to O'Keefe's chain or accept a rescue offer from a guest, and the outcome turns on a dramatic elevator accident and the revelations that follow.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into the 1967 film Hotel, starring Rod Taylor and Karl Malden, and later into the Aaron Spelling television series Hotel (1983–1988), relocated to San Francisco and starring James Brolin and Connie Sellecca. As with Hailey's other "institutional" novels such as Airport and Wheels, the book was researched in the industry it depicts and is credited with popularising the behind-the-scenes ensemble format.
