Norteño (music)


Labelled placeholder: norteño conjunto

Norteño (Spanish pronunciation: [noɾˈteɲo], "northern") or música norteña ("northern music") is a genre of Mexican music related to polka, banda, and corridos. The typical norteño ensemble consists of accordion, bajo sexto (a twelve-string bass guitar), tololoche (upright bass) or electric bass, and drums. The music originated in northern Mexico and south Texas in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, blending Mexican folk forms with the polka, waltz and mazurka brought by German, Polish and Czech immigrants.

History

Central European settlers in Texas and northern Mexico introduced the button accordion and dance rhythms such as the polka in the mid-19th century. Mexican musicians adapted these into a distinctive regional style. Early recording artists included Bruno Villarreal ("El Azote de los Bravos"), the first recorded accordion conjunto performer, and Narciso Martínez, the "father of conjunto music." Lydia Mendoza and families such as Los Alegres de Terán helped popularise the style in the 1930s–1950s.

Development

Post-war groups modernised the instrumentation and repertoire. Los Relámpagos del Norte (with Cornelio Reyna and Ramón Ayala) defined the modern norteño sound in the 1960s, and Los Tigres del Norte, from 1968 onward, made norteño corridos internationally famous, addressing themes of immigration, work and social justice. The genre also underpins narcocorridos and modern fusions such as corridos tumbados, which merge norteño and banda instrumentation with hip-hop influences.
