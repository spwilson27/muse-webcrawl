Euro disco

Euro disco is a form of electronic dance music that emerged in Europe in the 1970s, characterised by a steady four-on-the-floor beat, lush orchestration, simple catchy melodies and lyrics often sung in English by non-native speakers. It is a root of later European dance styles including Italo disco, Euro house and Eurodance.

History

The genre crystallised in Munich around producer Giorgio Moroder, whose work with Donna Summer — "Love to Love You Baby" (1975) and the fully electronic "I Feel Love" (1977) — defined the sequencer-driven sound. German groups such as Boney M. and Silver Convention, France's Cerrone and Space, and Sweden's ABBA carried Euro disco to charts worldwide. As American disco declined after 1979, European production continued unbroken, evolving into the synthesiser-based Italo disco of the early 1980s and the Eurobeat and hi-NRG styles popular in gay clubs and Japanese para para dancing.

Characteristics

Euro disco typically favours drum machines and synthesisers alongside strings and brass, an emphasis on chorus hooks, and tempos around 100–130 BPM. Compared with its American counterpart it is often described as more electronic, melodic and camp. Its influence runs through house music, techno and modern pop production.
