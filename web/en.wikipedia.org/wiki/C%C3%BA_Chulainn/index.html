Cú Chulainn is an Irish mythological demigod who appears in the stories of the Ulster Cycle, as well as in Scottish and Manx mythology. He is said to be an incarnation of the Irish god Lugh, who is also his father. His mother is the mortal Deichtine, sister of Conchobar mac Nessa, king of Ulster.

Name and boyhood deeds

Born Sétanta, he gained his better-known name as a child after killing Culann's fierce guard dog in self-defence and offering to take its place until a replacement could be reared — hence Cú Chulainn, "Culann's Hound". The boyhood deeds (Macgnímrada Con Culainn) recount how he took up arms on a day prophesied to bring glory but a short life.

The Táin

In the epic Táin Bó Cúailnge ("Cattle Raid of Cooley"), the seventeen-year-old Cú Chulainn single-handedly defends Ulster against the armies of Connacht, fighting a series of single combats at fords, including a tragic duel with his foster-brother Ferdiad. In battle he undergoes a terrifying battle frenzy, the ríastrad or "warp spasm", which distorts his body into a monstrous form. He dies young, as prophesied, betrayed through the breaking of his geisi (taboos), and ties himself to a standing stone so that he dies on his feet.
