Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women

The Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW) is an international treaty adopted in 1979 by the United Nations General Assembly. Described as an international bill of rights for women, it was instituted on 3 September 1981 and has been ratified by 189 states. Over fifty countries that have ratified the convention have done so subject to certain declarations, reservations, and objections, including 38 countries who rejected the enforcement article 29, which addresses means of settlement for disputes concerning the interpretation or application of the convention.

Overview

The convention defines discrimination against women as "...any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the recognition, enjoyment or exercise by women, irrespective of their marital status, on a basis of equality of men and women, of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil or any other field."

The substantive provisions require states parties to eliminate discrimination in political and public life, education, employment, health care, economic and social life, and marriage and family relations. The convention affirms women's reproductive rights and targets culture and tradition as influential forces shaping gender roles and family relations. It also obliges states to suppress trafficking in women and exploitation of prostitution.

The CEDAW Committee

Implementation is monitored by the Committee on the Elimination of Discrimination against Women, a body of 23 experts on women's rights from around the world, established under Article 17 of the convention. States parties must submit national reports at least every four years on measures they have taken to comply with their treaty obligations, and the committee issues concluding observations and general recommendations.

Optional Protocol

The Optional Protocol to CEDAW, adopted on 6 October 1999 and in force from 22 December 2000, establishes an individual complaints mechanism and an inquiry procedure allowing the committee to investigate grave or systematic violations. As of recent counts it has over 110 parties.

Reservations

CEDAW has attracted a large number of reservations, many of them based on religious or cultural grounds, particularly concerning Articles 2 and 16 (equality in marriage and family life). The committee has repeatedly stated that reservations to these articles are incompatible with the object and purpose of the convention. Notably, the United States signed the convention in 1980 but has never ratified it, and a small number of UN member states, including Iran and Sudan, have neither signed nor ratified.
