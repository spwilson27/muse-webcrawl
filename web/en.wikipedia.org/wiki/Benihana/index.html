Benihana

Benihana Inc. is an American restaurant company best known for its chain of Japanese teppanyaki restaurants, in which chefs cook at a hibachi grill in front of diners, combining food preparation with theatrical knife skills and tricks. Founded in New York City in 1964, Benihana is credited with popularising teppanyaki dining in the United States.

History

Benihana was founded by Hiroaki Aoki, a Japanese wrestler who had come to the United States with the Japanese Olympic wrestling team in 1960. With money saved from selling ice cream in Harlem, he opened the first Benihana of Tokyo restaurant on West 56th Street in New York in 1964. The name Benihana means "red flower" (a safflower), after his parents' coffee shop in Tokyo.

The restaurant's format — communal tables around a steel grill, a chef performing while cooking steak, shrimp and vegetables, and signature touches such as the flaming "onion volcano" — was a novelty in 1960s America and drew celebrity patrons and press attention. The chain expanded by franchising and company-owned stores through the 1970s and 1980s. Aoki remained a flamboyant public face of the brand until his death in 2008.

Operations

Benihana Inc. operates and franchises Benihana restaurants in the United States, the Caribbean and Central and South America, and also owns the RA Sushi and Haru Sushi concepts. The company was taken private in 2012 by the private equity firm Angelo Gordon & Co.

Cultural impact

The Benihana format has been widely imitated and frequently referenced in film and television, making "Benihana-style" a generic description of theatrical teppanyaki dining in the United States.
