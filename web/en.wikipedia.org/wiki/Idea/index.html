Idea

In philosophy, ideas are usually taken to be mental representational images of some object. Ideas can also be abstract concepts that do not present themselves as mental images. Many philosophers have considered ideas to be a fundamental ontological category of being. The capacity to create and understand the meaning of ideas is considered to be an essential and defining feature of human beings. In a popular sense, an idea arises in a reflexive, spontaneous manner, even without thinking or serious reflection, for example, when we talk about the idea of a person or a place. A new or an original idea can often lead to innovation.

Our ability to produce ideas is essential to the formation of culture, and concepts of ontology and epistemology study how ideas are formed, what they represent, and how they relate to reality and to the mind.

Etymology

The word idea comes from Greek ἰδέα idea, "form, pattern", from the root of ἰδεῖν idein, "to see". The term entered philosophy in the sense of a fundamental reality as distinct from philosophical realism, and later entered common language meaning a thought or plan.

History of the concept

Plato

Plato argued that ideas, or forms, are the most fundamental kind of reality. For Plato, the physical world is a shadow of the eternal, unchanging world of forms, and true knowledge consists in apprehending these forms through reason rather than the senses.

Descartes

René Descartes used the term idea for any mental content, distinguishing innate ideas, which are inborn in the mind, from adventitious ideas, which come from experience, and factitious ideas, which the mind constructs itself.

British empiricists

John Locke defined an idea as whatever the mind perceives in itself or is the immediate object of perception, thought, or understanding. David Hume distinguished ideas from impressions: ideas are the faint copies of the lively perceptions of sense.

Kant

Immanuel Kant reserved the term Idea (Idee) for concepts of reason — such as God, freedom, and immortality — that transcend all possible experience yet regulate inquiry.

Ideas in science and innovation

In science, an idea may develop into a hypothesis to be tested. Invention and innovation are often described as beginning with an idea, which is then developed, protected (for example by patent), and realised in practice.
