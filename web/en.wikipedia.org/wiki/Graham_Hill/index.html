Norman Graham Hill OBE (15 February 1929 – 29 November 1975) was a British racing driver and team owner, who was Formula One World Champion twice, winning in 1962 and 1968. Hill is one of the most famous British racing drivers and remains the only driver ever to win the Triple Crown of Motorsport—the 24 Hours of Le Mans, the Indianapolis 500, and the Monaco Grand Prix, the last of which he won five times.

Early life

Hill was born in Hampstead, London. He did not learn to drive until he was 24 and came to motor racing relatively late. He worked as a mechanic and later as an apprentice, servicing in the Royal Navy, before persuading Colin Chapman to give him a job at Lotus, initially as a mechanic and then as a driver.

Formula One career

Hill made his Formula One debut with Lotus at the 1958 Monaco Grand Prix. In 1960 he moved to BRM, with whom he won his first World Championship in 1962. He returned to Lotus in 1967, and after the death of his teammate Jim Clark in 1968, Hill led the team to his second championship that year. He won the Monaco Grand Prix five times (1963, 1964, 1965, 1968 and 1969), earning him the nickname "Mr Monaco".

In 1966 Hill won the Indianapolis 500 in a Lola, and in 1972 he won the 24 Hours of Le Mans with Henri Pescarolo in a Matra, completing his unique Triple Crown.

Team owner and death

After leaving Lotus, Hill founded his own team, Embassy Hill, in 1973. He retired from driving in 1975 to concentrate on running the team and mentoring his protégé Tony Brise. On 29 November 1975, Hill was killed, along with Brise and four other team members, when the Piper Aztec aircraft he was piloting crashed in fog near Arkley golf course in north London while returning from a test session at the Paul Ricard circuit in France.

Legacy

Hill's son Damon Hill won the Formula One World Championship in 1996, making them the first father and son to both win the title. Graham Hill is remembered for his wit, his moustache, and his determination, and he remains one of Britain's most celebrated racing drivers.
