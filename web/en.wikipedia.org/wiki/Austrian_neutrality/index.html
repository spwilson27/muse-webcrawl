The Declaration of Neutrality was a declaration by the Austrian Parliament on 26 October 1955, the day after the last Allied occupation forces left the country, in which Austria declared its perpetual neutrality. Enacted as a federal constitutional law, it states that Austria will never join a military alliance and will not allow foreign military bases on its territory, and that it will maintain and defend its neutrality with all means at its disposal.

Background

After the Second World War, Austria was divided into four occupation zones like Germany. Negotiations for a state treaty stalled for a decade; in 1955, Austrian diplomacy — led by Chancellor Julius Raab and Foreign Minister Leopold Figl, with state secretary Bruno Kreisky — offered the Soviet Union a commitment to Swiss-style permanent neutrality in the Moscow Memorandum of April 1955. The Austrian State Treaty was signed on 15 May 1955 at the Belvedere Palace in Vienna, restoring Austria as a free, independent, and democratic state, and the neutrality law followed that October.

Neutrality in the Cold War and after

During the Cold War, neutral Austria positioned itself as a bridge between East and West, hosting international organisations in Vienna (the IAEA, and a UN headquarters from 1979) and summit meetings such as the 1961 Kennedy–Khrushchev encounter. Austria joined the European Union in 1995 and participates in the EU's Common Security and Defence Policy and NATO's Partnership for Peace, arrangements debated against the neutrality law. After Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Austria maintained military neutrality while politically supporting EU sanctions, and polls have consistently shown majority public support for retaining neutrality.
