Li Yuanhong

Li Yuanhong (Chinese: 黎元洪; pinyin: Lí Yuánhóng; 19 October 1864 – 3 June 1928) was a Chinese politician during the Qing dynasty and the Republic of China. He served as President of the Republic of China twice, from 1916 to 1917 and from 1922 to 1923, and as vice president under Yuan Shikai.

Early career

Born in Huangpi, Hubei, Li graduated from the Tianjin Naval Academy and served as an engineer in the Beiyang fleet during the First Sino-Japanese War. He later trained Hubei's New Army troops in Wuchang and rose to command a mixed brigade.

Revolution and presidency

When the Wuchang Uprising broke out on 10 October 1911, revolutionaries compelled the reluctant Li to head the Hubei military government, making him a figurehead of the Xinhai Revolution. He became vice president of the republic in 1912. On Yuan Shikai's death in June 1916, Li succeeded him as president, but real power lay with Premier Duan Qirui. Forced out by Zhang Xun's Manchu restoration attempt and Duan's refusal to restore him, Li resigned in 1917. Recalled by the Zhili clique in 1922, he served a second, equally powerless term before being driven out in 1923.

Later life

Li retired to the British concession in Tianjin, invested in industry, and died there in 1928.
