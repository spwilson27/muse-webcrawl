Trump–Ukraine scandal
Date:2019–2020
Key figures:Donald Trump, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, Rudy Giuliani
Outcome:Trump impeached by the House; acquitted by the Senate

The Trump–Ukraine scandal was a political scandal that arose in 2019 over allegations that U.S. president Donald Trump pressured Ukraine to investigate former vice president Joe Biden, a leading candidate for the Democratic nomination in the 2020 presidential election, and his son Hunter Biden. The scandal was sparked by an intelligence-community whistleblower complaint in August 2019 concerning a 25 July 2019 telephone call between Trump and Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

Background

Trump's personal lawyer Rudy Giuliani and others had for months urged Ukrainian officials to open investigations into Burisma, a Ukrainian energy company on whose board Hunter Biden had served, and into a discredited theory that Ukraine, rather than Russia, had interfered in the 2016 U.S. election. In July 2019 the Trump administration suspended nearly $400 million in congressionally approved military aid to Ukraine shortly before the leaders' call.

The July 25 call

A memorandum of the call released by the White House showed Trump asking Zelenskyy to "do us a favor" and to work with Giuliani and Attorney General William Barr on investigations. Critics characterised the request as an abuse of power and a solicitation of foreign interference in an election; Trump and his defenders maintained the call was "perfect" and denied any quid pro quo, while several officials testified that aid and a White House meeting were conditioned on announcing the investigations.

Impeachment

The House of Representatives opened an impeachment inquiry in September 2019. On 18 December 2019 the House impeached Trump on articles of abuse of power and obstruction of Congress. Following a trial, the Senate acquitted Trump on 5 February 2020. The episode was the first impeachment of Donald Trump; he was impeached again in 2021 over the January 6 Capitol attack.
