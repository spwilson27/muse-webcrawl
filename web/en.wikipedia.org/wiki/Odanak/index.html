Odanak

Country: Canada

Province: Quebec

Region: Centre-du-Québec

First Nation: Odanak First Nation (Abenaki)

Reserve: Odanak 12

Odanak is an Abenaki First Nations reserve and community in the Centre-du-Québec region of Quebec, Canada. It lies on the eastern bank of the Saint-François River, near its confluence with the St. Lawrence River at Pierreville, about 32 kilometres east of Sorel-Tracy. The name Odanak means "at the village" in the Abenaki language.

History

The community traces its origins to the mission village of Saint-François-de-Sales, established around 1700 for Abenaki and other Indigenous refugees displaced from New England by the colonial wars. Known in English records as St. Francis, the village's warriors took part in raids on New England settlements during the French and Indian War, most famously the 1759 retaliatory attack by Robert Rogers's rangers, which destroyed much of the village — an event commemorated on both sides of the conflict. The community rebuilt and endured; its inhabitants include descendants of the Abenaki artist and cultural figures who kept Wabanaki traditions alive in Quebec.

Present day

Odanak is home to the Odanak First Nation, governed by a band council, and to institutions such as the Musée des Abénakis (founded 1965, the first Indigenous museum in Quebec) and Kiuna Institution, a post-secondary college serving First Nations students, which opened in 2011. The community is known for its ash and sweetgrass basketry and for its powwow and cultural programming. Together with the nearby Abenaki community of Wôlinak, Odanak forms part of the Grand Conseil de la Nation Waban-Aki.
