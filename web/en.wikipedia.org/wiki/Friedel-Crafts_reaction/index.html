Friedel-Crafts reaction

The Friedel-Crafts reactions are a set of reactions developed by Charles Friedel and James Crafts in 1877 to attach substituents to an aromatic ring. The two main types are Friedel-Crafts alkylation, in which an alkyl group is added using an alkyl halide and a strong Lewis acid catalyst such as aluminium chloride, and Friedel-Crafts acylation, in which an acyl group is added using an acyl chloride.

Mechanism and limitations

Both reactions proceed by electrophilic aromatic substitution: the Lewis acid generates a powerful electrophile (a carbocation or an acylium ion) which attacks the ring. Alkylation suffers from rearrangements and multiple substitution, because the alkylated product is more reactive than the starting material; acylation stops after one substitution because the acyl group deactivates the ring, and the ketone product can be reduced to an alkyl group, making acylation a cleaner route to alkylbenzenes. Strongly deactivated rings, such as nitrobenzene, do not undergo Friedel-Crafts reactions.
