Octavio Paz Lozano (1914–1998) was a Mexican poet, essayist and diplomat, regarded as one of the most influential Spanish-language writers of the 20th century. He received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1990.

Life

Paz was born in Mexico City during the Mexican Revolution and published his first book of poems, Luna silvestre, in 1933. In 1937 he travelled to Spain in support of the Republic during the Civil War. He entered the Mexican diplomatic service in 1945, serving in Paris, where he associated with the surrealists, and later as ambassador to India (1962–1968), a period that deeply shaped his work. He resigned in protest at the Tlatelolco massacre of students in 1968.

Works

His long poem Sunstone (Piedra de sol, 1957), structured around the Aztec calendar, is among his most celebrated. His essay The Labyrinth of Solitude (1950) is a landmark analysis of Mexican identity. Other major works include the poetry collections Eagle or Sun? and East Slope, and essays such as The Bow and the Lyre and Conjunctions and Disjunctions. He founded and edited the literary journals Plural and Vuelta, which shaped Latin American intellectual life.
