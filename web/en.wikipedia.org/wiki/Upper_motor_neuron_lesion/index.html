Upper motor neuron lesion

The corticospinal tract: damage anywhere along it produces UMN signs

An upper motor neuron lesion (also known as an UMNL) is a lesion of the neural pathway above the anterior horn cell of the spinal cord or the motor nuclei of the cranial nerves. It contrasts with a lower motor neuron lesion, which affects nerve fibres travelling from the anterior horn or cranial nerve nuclei to the relevant muscles. Damage to the motor cortex, internal capsule, brainstem, or the descending corticospinal and corticobulbar tracts produces a characteristic pattern of clinical signs.

Features

Spasticity in an upper motor neuron lesion is velocity-dependent and shows a "clasp-knife" quality, in which resistance suddenly gives way. Weakness preferentially affects the extensors of the upper limb and the flexors of the lower limb on the affected side — the "pyramidal pattern". Superficial reflexes, such as the abdominal reflexes, are lost. In the acute phase after a sudden lesion such as a stroke, there may be an initial period of flaccidity and areflexia ("spinal shock" for cord lesions) before spasticity develops.

Causes

Common causes include stroke, multiple sclerosis, traumatic injury to the brain or spinal cord, cerebral palsy, tumours, and motor neuron disease, in which upper and lower motor neuron signs coexist. A lesion of the motor cortex or internal capsule causes contralateral signs, whereas a lesion of the spinal cord causes signs below the level of the lesion on the same side (with pain and temperature loss on the opposite side in Brown-Séquard syndrome).
