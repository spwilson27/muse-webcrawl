The Mesabi Range — also called the Mesabi Iron Range — is a mining district in northeastern Minnesota, United States, following an elongated trend of iron ore deposits that extends about 110 miles from Grand Rapids to Babbitt. It is the largest of the four iron ranges of Minnesota (the others being the Vermilion, Cuyuna, and Gunflint ranges) and has been the chief source of iron ore in the United States since the 1890s.

Geology

The ore occurs in the Biwabik Iron Formation, a Precambrian banded iron formation about 1.9 billion years old. High-grade natural ores were progressively depleted during the twentieth century, and since the 1950s the industry has relied on taconite — a hard, low-grade rock that is crushed, magnetically concentrated, and fired into pellets.

History

Ore was first shipped from the Mesabi in 1892. Open-pit mines such as the Hull–Rust–Mahoning mine at Hibbing — once the largest open-pit iron mine in the world — supplied the steel industry through both world wars. The range gave rise to "Iron Range" culture and towns built around mining, and inspired Bob Dylan, who grew up in Hibbing, and the hockey tradition of Eveleth.

Economy

Today a handful of taconite plants operated by companies such as Cleveland-Cliffs and U.S. Steel ship pellets by rail to ports at Duluth–Superior and Two Harbors for lake freighter transport to lower Great Lakes steel mills.
