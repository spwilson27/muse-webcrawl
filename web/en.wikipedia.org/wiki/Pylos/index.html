Pylos

Pylos (Greek: Πύλος), historically also known as Navarino, is a town and former municipality in Messenia, Peloponnese, Greece. It sits on the Bay of Pylos (Navarino Bay) on the Ionian Sea. Pylos is famous in Greek tradition as the home of King Nestor, the wise elder of the Iliad, and archaeologically for the nearby Mycenaean Palace of Nestor — the best-preserved Mycenaean palace in Greece and a principal source of Linear B tablets.

Bronze Age: the Palace of Nestor

On the hill of Ano Englianos, some 9 km north-east of the modern town, Carl Blegen and Konstantinos Kourouniotis began excavations in 1939 and uncovered a large Mycenaean palace, identified with the Pylos of Nestor, home of Neleus and his son Nestor in Homer. On the very first day of digging, the archive room yielded hundreds of clay tablets in Linear B — administrative records of a palace economy (land, personnel, livestock, textiles, perfumed oil) written in the earliest known Greek. The palace, with its megaron, painted plaster floors, frescoes and a remarkably preserved bathtub, was destroyed by fire around 1200 BC; the fire baked and preserved the tablets.

Classical and later history

In 425 BC, during the Peloponnesian War, Athenian forces fortified the uninhabited promontory of Pylos (Coryphasium) and won the ensuing Battle of Pylos, trapping Spartan troops on the island of Sphacteria — a celebrated episode in Thucydides. In the medieval and Ottoman periods the bay was known as Navarino, guarded by the castles of Old and New Navarino (Paliokastro and Niokastro). On 20 October 1827, in the bay of Navarino, a combined British, French and Russian fleet destroyed the Ottoman–Egyptian fleet in the Battle of Navarino, a decisive event in the Greek War of Independence.

Modern town

Modern Pylos was built after 1828 to plans of French engineers of the Morea expedition, around a central square shaded by plane trees overlooking the bay. It is now a port and tourist town serving the south-western Peloponnese, near the wetlands of Gialova lagoon and the celebrated Voidokilia beach.
