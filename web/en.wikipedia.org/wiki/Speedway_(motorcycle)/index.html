Motorcycle speedway

Governing bodyFédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM)
TrackFlat oval, loose dirt or shale surface, 260–425 m
Motorcycle500 cc single-cylinder, no brakes, no gears, methanol fuel
FormatFour riders, four laps per heat

Motorcycle speedway, usually referred to simply as speedway, is a motorcycle sport involving four and sometimes up to six riders competing over four anti-clockwise laps of an oval circuit. The motorcycles are highly specialised: they use 500 cc single-cylinder engines fuelled by methanol, have no brakes and only a single gear, and are ridden with dramatic powerslides — known as "broadsiding" — around the loose-surface corners, with the rider's steel-shod left boot skimming the ground for balance.

History

Speedway originated in the 1920s, with early meetings in Australia (notably organised by Johnnie Hoskins at Maitland, New South Wales, in 1923) and in the United Kingdom, where the first meeting at High Beech, Essex, in 1928 launched a boom that made speedway one of Britain's most popular spectator sports in the post-war years. The first World Championship was held at Wembley in 1936 and won by Australian Lionel Van Praag.

Competition

Team league racing is the backbone of the sport in Britain, Poland, Sweden, and Denmark. A match consists of a series of heats (usually 15), each scoring 3 points for a win, 2 for second, 1 for third, and 0 for last place or exclusion. Poland's Ekstraliga draws the world's best riders and large crowds. At the individual level, the Speedway Grand Prix series, run since 1995, determines the world champion over a season of rounds; the team world title is contested in the Speedway World Cup and its successor, the Speedway of Nations.

Riders and machinery

Engines produce around 75–80 horsepower and accelerate the bikes to about 110 km/h (70 mph) on the straights; with no brakes, riders slow by sliding the machine sideways and shutting the throttle. Prominent riders in the sport's history include Ivan Mauger of New Zealand (six world titles), Tony Rickardsson of Sweden (six titles), Hans Nielsen of Denmark, Greg Hancock of the United States, and Polish star Bartosz Zmarzlik.
