Domestic partnership

A domestic partnership is a relationship, usually a legal one, between two individuals who live together and share a common domestic life, but are not married (to each other or to anyone else). People in domestic partnerships receive legal benefits that guarantee right of survivorship, hospital visitation, and other rights similar to married couples.

History and usage

The term originated in the United States in the early 1980s, when some municipalities and employers began extending benefits to the unmarried partners of employees, including same-sex partners who then could not marry. Berkeley, California, was among the first cities to adopt a domestic partnership policy (1984). Several U.S. states later created statewide registries or analogous statuses such as civil unions and registered partnerships.

After marriage equality

Following the nationwide legalisation of same-sex marriage in the U.S. by Obergefell v. Hodges (2015), some jurisdictions closed their registries or converted partnerships into marriages, while others retained domestic partnership as an option for both same-sex and opposite-sex couples who prefer a status with fewer legal consequences than marriage. Comparable institutions exist in many countries under names such as civil partnership, registered partnership and civil solidarity pact (France's PACS).
