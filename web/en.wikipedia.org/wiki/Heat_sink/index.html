Heat sink

A heat sink (also commonly spelled heatsink) is a passive heat exchanger that transfers heat generated by an electronic or mechanical device to a fluid medium, often air or a liquid coolant, where it is dissipated away from the device. Heat sinks are used with high-power semiconductor devices such as power transistors and optoelectronics, and in computers to cool CPUs and GPUs.

Design

Heat sinks are usually made of aluminium or copper, metals with high thermal conductivity, and maximise surface area through fins or pins. Thermal interface material fills microscopic air gaps between the device and the sink. Performance is measured by thermal resistance in °C/W.

Methods of heat transfer

Heat moves through the sink by conduction and leaves its surfaces by natural or forced convection and a small amount of radiation. Fans mounted on sinks (active cooling), heat pipes and vapour chambers extend performance; liquid cooling blocks act as heat sinks coupled to pumps and radiators.

Applications

Beyond computing, heat sinks cool LED lighting, power supplies, audio amplifiers, laser diodes and thermoelectric devices.
