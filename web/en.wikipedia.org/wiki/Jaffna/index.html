Jaffna (Tamil: யாழ்ப்பாணம், romanized: Yāḻppāṇam) is the capital city of the Northern Province of Sri Lanka. It is the administrative headquarters of the Jaffna District, situated on a peninsula at the northern tip of the island. A predominantly Tamil city, Jaffna was historically the capital of the Jaffna Kingdom and remains the cultural heartland of Sri Lankan Tamils.

History

The Jaffna peninsula was ruled by the Aryacakravarti dynasty from the 13th century until the Portuguese conquest in 1619, after which it passed to the Dutch (1658) and then the British (1796). Under colonial rule Jaffna became known for its schools and high literacy. The city was devastated during the Sri Lankan Civil War (1983–2009): the burning of the Jaffna Public Library in 1981 destroyed nearly 100,000 books and manuscripts, and the population was displaced en masse in 1995 when the LTTE ordered an evacuation ahead of a government offensive. Since the war's end in 2009, the city has slowly rebuilt, and the library has been restored.

Geography and culture

Jaffna lies on flat, arid limestone terrain dotted with palmyrah palms and shallow lagoons. Landmarks include the Nallur Kandaswamy Temple, one of Sri Lanka's most significant Hindu temples; the Dutch-era Jaffna Fort; Sangiliyan Minister's residence ruins; and the Jaffna Palace complex. The region is noted for its distinctive cuisine, including Jaffna crab curry and kool seafood soup, and for the University of Jaffna, founded in 1974.
