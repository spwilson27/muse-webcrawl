Rashid Rida

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Muhammad Rashid Rida (23 September 1865 – 22 August 1935) was a Syrian-Egyptian Islamic scholar, reformer and journalist, and one of the most influential figures in the development of the Salafi movement. As editor of the journal Al-Manar ("The Lighthouse") in Cairo, he promoted the ideas of Jamal al-Din al-Afghani and his teacher Muhammad Abduh throughout the Muslim world.

Life and work

Rida was born near Tripoli, in Ottoman Syria (now Lebanon), and received a traditional religious education before being drawn to the reformist ideas circulated in the journal Al-Urwah al-Wuthqa. He moved to Cairo in 1897 to work with Abduh and founded Al-Manar in 1898. Through it he published Abduh's Qur'an commentary and his own extensive exegesis, the Tafsir al-Manar, and corresponded with readers across the Islamic world.

Thought and influence

Rida argued that Islam's decline resulted from abandoning the example of the earliest generations (salaf) and from blind imitation (taqlid); he called for renewed independent reasoning (ijtihad) and purification of practice from what he considered innovations. After the First World War and the abolition of the Ottoman caliphate, he became more politically focused, advocating an Arab caliphate and writing treatises on Islamic government. His thought influenced later movements across the spectrum, from Muslim modernists to Hasan al-Banna, founder of the Muslim Brotherhood.
