A jet aircraft is an aircraft propelled by jet engines. Whereas propulsion by propellers is limited in speed and altitude by the physics of the propeller, jet aircraft can fly at high subsonic and supersonic speeds and at high altitudes, typically cruising between 10,000 and 15,000 metres for large airliners.

History

The jet engine was developed independently in the 1930s by Frank Whittle in Britain and Hans von Ohain in Germany. Von Ohain's engine powered the Heinkel He 178, which made the world's first jet-powered flight on 27 August 1939. The German Messerschmitt Me 262 became the first operational jet fighter in 1944, followed by Britain's Gloster Meteor.

Postwar development

After World War II, jet fighters proliferated during the Korean War, where the American F-86 Sabre met the Soviet MiG-15. In civil aviation, the British de Havilland Comet entered service in 1952 as the first commercial jetliner, and the Boeing 707 and Douglas DC-8 made jet travel mainstream from 1958. The wide-body Boeing 747 (1970) and the supersonic Concorde (1976) marked further milestones.

Types

Jet aircraft include airliners powered by high-bypass turbofans, military fighters and bombers, business jets, and regional jets. Turbofan engines are more efficient and quieter than pure turbojets and dominate modern aviation.

Characteristics

Jet engines develop thrust by expelling exhaust gases at high velocity. Jet aircraft generally require longer runways and higher landing speeds than propeller aircraft, but offer far greater speed, range and altitude performance. Fuel is typically kerosene-based Jet A or Jet A-1.
