Common Foreign and Security Policy

The Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) is the organised, agreed foreign policy of the European Union (EU) for mainly security and diplomatic actions. Established by the Treaty of Maastricht in 1993 as one of the "three pillars" of the EU, the CFSP deals with diplomacy, conflict prevention, crisis management, and restrictive measures (sanctions), and includes the Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP) for military and civilian missions. Unlike most EU policy areas, CFSP remains largely intergovernmental: decisions in the Council generally require unanimity, and the European Commission and Parliament have limited roles.

The CFSP coordinates EU diplomacy and security action from Brussels.

Institutions

The CFSP is steered by the European Council and the Council of the European Union (in its Foreign Affairs Council formation, chaired by the High Representative). The High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy — who is also a Vice-President of the European Commission — represents the Union externally and heads the European External Action Service (EEAS), the EU's diplomatic corps, which operates delegations worldwide. The Political and Security Committee (PSC), made up of member-state ambassadors, monitors the international situation and exercises political control of crisis-management operations.

Instruments

CFSP tools include joint positions and actions, diplomatic démarches, statements, sanctions regimes (asset freezes, travel bans, trade restrictions), political dialogues with third countries, and CSDP missions — ranging from military operations (such as anti-piracy naval operations) to civilian rule-of-law, policing, border assistance, and training missions. Major sanctions programmes have targeted Russia (especially after the 2014 annexation of Crimea and the 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine), Iran, Syria, and others.

Development

The Lisbon Treaty (2009) abolished the pillar structure, created the permanent President of the European Council and the EEAS, and gave the EU a single legal personality, but kept CFSP's special unanimity procedures. Member states such as France have periodically advocated extending qualified majority voting to CFSP; others resist surrendering their veto on foreign policy. The EU's global strategy documents describe ambitions of "strategic autonomy", accelerated after 2022 by the war in Ukraine and the creation of instruments such as the European Peace Facility.
