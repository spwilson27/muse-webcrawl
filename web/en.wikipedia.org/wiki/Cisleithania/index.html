Cisleithania

Capital: Vienna
Period: 1867–1918
Official name: The Kingdoms and Lands Represented in the Imperial Council

Cisleithania, officially The Kingdoms and Lands Represented in the Imperial Council (German: Die im Reichsrat vertretenen Königreiche und Länder), was the northern and western part of Austria-Hungary, the Dual Monarchy created in the Compromise of 1867—as distinguished from Transleithania (i.e., the Hungarian Lands of the Crown of Saint Stephen east of [“beyond”] the Leitha River). This division did not emerge until a decade after the Compromise, because the Hungarian government had considered the name derogatory.

The Cisleithanian capital was Vienna, the residence of the Austrian emperor. The territory had a population of 28,571,900 in 1910. It reached from Vorarlberg in the west to the Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria and the Duchy of Bukovina (today part of Ukraine and Romania) in the east, as well as from the Kingdom of Bohemia in the north to the Kingdom of Dalmatia (today part of Croatia) in the south. It comprised the current states of Austria (except for Burgenland), as well as most of the territories of the Czech Republic and Slovenia (except for Prekmurje), southern Poland and parts of Italy (Trieste, Gorizia, Tarvisio, Trentino, and South Tyrol), Croatia (Istria, Dalmatia), Montenegro (Kotor Bay), Romania (southern Bukovina), and Ukraine (northern Bukovina and Galicia).

Term

The Latin name Cisleithania ('on this side of the Leitha') derives from the Leitha River, a tributary of the Danube forming the historical boundary between the Archduchy of Austria and the Kingdom of Hungary in the area southeast of Vienna (on the way to Budapest). Much of Cisleithania's territory lay west or north of the Leitha, but several of its constituent lands were actually east of it; the name was therefore somewhat imprecise even in geographic terms.

Crown lands

Cisleithania consisted of 15 crown lands which had representatives in the Imperial Council (Reichsrat), the Cisleithanian parliament in Vienna. The crown lands centred in the Archduchy of Austria (Erzherzogtum Österreich) were not states but provinces in the modern sense. However, they were areas with unique historic political and legal identities and were therefore more than mere administrative districts. They have been conceived of as “historical-political entities”.

Each crown land had a regional assembly, the Landtag, which enacted laws for regional and local matters, and a governor (Landeshauptmann or Statthalter) appointed by the emperor.

Politics

According to the December Constitution of 1867, Cisleithania was governed by a cabinet headed by a minister-president, responsible to the emperor and, increasingly, to the Reichsrat. Universal male suffrage for elections to the lower house of the Reichsrat was introduced in 1907. Politics in Cisleithania was dominated by struggles among its many nationalities — Germans, Czechs, Poles, Ruthenians (Ukrainians), Slovenes, Croats, Serbs, Italians, and others — and by the rise of mass parties such as the Social Democrats and Christian Socials.
