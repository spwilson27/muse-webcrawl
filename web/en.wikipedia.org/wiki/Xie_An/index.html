Xie An

Xie An (謝安, 320 – 12 October 385), courtesy name Anshi (安石), formally Duke Wenjing of Luling (廬陵文靖公), was a statesman of the Eastern Jin dynasty of China. He is celebrated for preserving the dynasty when the Former Qin under Fu Jian threatened to destroy it at the Battle of Fei River in 383, and for his famously unhurried, cultivated lifestyle.

Early life and reclusion

Xie An came from the prominent Xie clan of Chen Commandery. Though talented from youth, he famously refused office for many years, living in reclusion at Dongshan (East Mountain) in Shanyin, associating with literati such as the calligrapher Wang Xizhi and the monk Zhi Dun. The idiom "making a comeback from Dongshan" (東山再起) later derived from his eventual return to public life. Only around age 40, for the sake of his clan's standing after his brother Xie Yi's death, did he finally accept office.

Political career

Xie An rose through the administration and became one of the dominant figures at court, balancing the ambitions of the powerful general Huan Wen, who may have intended to usurp the throne; Huan died in 373 without doing so. Xie An organised the empire's defences against the northern Di-led state of Former Qin, entrusting the field command to his nephew Xie Xuan and the elite Beifu Army that Xie Xuan had raised.

Battle of Fei River

In 383, Fu Jian of Former Qin invaded with an enormous army — tradition puts it at several hundred thousand men. Against it, Xie Xuan's 80,000 Beifu troops won a decisive victory at the Fei River, shattering Former Qin, which soon fragmented; the Eastern Jin survived for another four decades. A famous anecdote relates that when news of the victory reached Xie An as he played weiqi with a guest, he merely remarked "the boys have beaten the foe" and continued playing, betraying excitement only afterward when he tripped over his clogs.

Death and legacy

His prestige after the victory aroused the suspicions of Emperor Xiaowu, and in 385, at his own request amid growing court tension, Xie An left the capital to take up a posting at Guangling, dying that autumn. He was posthumously honoured with the title Grand Tutor. Xie An remains a byword in Chinese culture for imperturbable composure and refined statesmanship.
