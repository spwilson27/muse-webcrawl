Parosmia

Parosmia is a distorted sense of smell, in which familiar odours are perceived as unpleasant or different from how they are remembered — commonly described as burnt, rotten, chemical or faecal — even though the odorant itself has not changed. It is a qualitative olfactory disorder, distinct from anosmia (loss of smell) and hyposmia (reduced smell).

Causes

Parosmia most often follows an upper respiratory tract viral infection that damages olfactory receptor neurons; recovery of the neurons can produce mis-wiring, so signals are misinterpreted by the brain. It gained wide attention during the COVID-19 pandemic, when many patients developed parosmia weeks or months after SARS-CoV-2 infection, often as their sense of smell returned. Other causes include head injury, sinus disease, toxins, and neurological conditions.

Features and management

Trigger foods frequently include coffee, meat, onions, garlic, eggs and chocolate. There is no proven drug treatment; management includes olfactory (smell) training, avoiding triggers, and time, as many cases improve gradually over months. Persistent parosmia can significantly affect appetite, nutrition and quality of life.
