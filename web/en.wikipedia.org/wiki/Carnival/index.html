Carnival or Shrovetide is a festive season that occurs at the close of the Christian pre-Lenten period, consisting typically of public celebrations, parades, street parties, masquerades and feasting, combining elements of a circus, a public street party and ritualised social inversion. Carnival culminates on Mardi Gras (Fat Tuesday / Shrove Tuesday), the day before Ash Wednesday opens the fasting season of Lent. The word is commonly traced to the Latin carne levare — "to remove meat".

History

Carnival traditions descend from ancient seasonal festivals such as the Roman Saturnalia and the Greek Dionysian rites, which were absorbed into the Christian calendar in late antiquity and the Middle Ages. Medieval carnival featured masked balls, mock battles, and licensed mockery of authorities. In the early modern period, the Venetian Carnival became famed across Europe for its masks; it was suppressed under Napoleonic rule and revived in the late 20th century. Through colonisation and the African diaspora, Carnival spread to the Americas, where it blended with African, Indigenous and European traditions — producing celebrations such as those of Rio de Janeiro, Trinidad and Tobago, New Orleans (Mardi Gras) and Barranquilla.

Celebrations around the world

The Rio Carnival in Brazil, with its samba school parades at the Sambadrome, is often called the world's largest carnival. Other notable celebrations include the Carnival of Venice (Italy), the Rhineland carnivals of Cologne, Düsseldorf and Mainz (Germany), the Carnival of Santa Cruz de Tenerife (Spain), the Carnival of Binche (Belgium, a UNESCO-listed tradition of the Gilles), the Québec Winter Carnival (Canada), and Mardi Gras in New Orleans (United States). Common elements include parades of floats, elaborate costumes, the election of carnival kings and queens, and satirical floats lampooning public figures.

Social function

Scholars such as Mikhail Bakhtin have interpreted carnival as a ritual of temporary liberation from hierarchy: during carnival, distinctions of rank are suspended, mockery of the powerful is licensed, and the "world turned upside down" is celebrated — after which the discipline of Lent restores the old order.
