William C. Schutz (August 19, 1925 – November 9, 2002) was an American psychologist who created the FIRO (Fundamental Interpersonal Relations Orientation) theory of interpersonal behaviour and its widely used measurement instrument, FIRO-B. He was also a prominent figure in the human potential movement of the 1960s and 1970s.

FIRO theory

Working in the 1950s, including research for the U.S. Navy on the compatibility of small crews, Schutz proposed that interpersonal behaviour is organised around three basic needs: inclusion (belonging and recognition), control (influence and structure), and affection (closeness and warmth). The FIRO-B questionnaire, published in 1958, measures both the behaviour a person expresses toward others and the behaviour they want from others in each area, and remains in use in organisational development and team building.

Human potential movement

In the 1960s Schutz joined the Esalen Institute at Big Sur, California, where he became a leading practitioner of encounter groups. His bestselling book Joy: Expanding Human Awareness (1967) and the follow-up Here Comes Everybody (1971) popularised open-encounter methods combining verbal honesty with physical exercises. In his later work, including The Human Element (1994), he applied his theory to organisational performance, arguing that self-awareness and truth-telling improve teamwork.
