The Minute Man is an 1874 statue in Concord, Massachusetts, created by sculptor Daniel Chester French (1850–1931) from a design by Franklin Simmons. It stands in Minute Man National Historical Park near the North Bridge, where the first shots of the American Revolutionary War were fired by colonial militiamen on 19 April 1775.

Description and history

The bronze statue depicts a young farmer leaving his plough behind and taking up a musket, representing the citizen-soldiers — minutemen — who were ready to fight at a minute's notice. It is traditionally said to represent Isaac Davis, captain of the Acton minute company, who was killed at the North Bridge. The statue was cast from Civil War-era cannon donated by Congress and was unveiled on 19 April 1875, the centenary of the battles of Lexington and Concord, at a ceremony attended by President Ulysses S. Grant.

It was French's first major commission; he later designed the seated Abraham Lincoln for the Lincoln Memorial in Washington, D.C. The base bears lines from Ralph Waldo Emerson's 1837 "Concord Hymn", which coined the phrase "the shot heard round the world". The image of the statue has been used extensively by the Massachusetts National Guard and on commemorative coins and postage stamps.
