The Flower-class corvette was a British class of 294 corvettes used during the Second World War by Allied navies, principally the Royal Navy and Royal Canadian Navy, as convoy escorts in the Battle of the Atlantic. The design was based on a commercial whale-catcher, allowing rapid construction in small civilian shipyards.

Design

Ships of the class were named after flowers, such as HMS Pansy and HMCS Snowberry. They were armed with a single 4-inch gun, depth charges and later Hedgehog anti-submarine mortars and radar. Their merchant-style triple-expansion engines gave about 16 knots — barely enough to catch a surfaced U-boat.

Service

Flowers escorted convoys across the Atlantic throughout the war; 36 were lost to enemy action. Nicholas Monsarrat, who served in them, immortalised the type in his novel The Cruel Sea. The last survivor, HMCS Sackville, is a museum ship in Halifax, Nova Scotia.
