Mount Wudang

Mount Wudang

Native name: 武当山 (Wǔdāng Shān)

Location: Shiyan, Hubei, China

Highest peak: Tianzhu Peak, 1,612 m (5,289 ft)

UNESCO World Heritage Site: designated 1994 (Ancient Building Complex in the Wudang Mountains)

Mount Wudang (simplified Chinese: 武当山; traditional Chinese: 武當山; pinyin: Wǔdāng Shān) is a mountain range in the northwestern part of Hubei Province, China. The range is famous as one of the most important centres of Taoism in China, traditionally associated with the legendary Taoist priest Zhang Sanfeng, who is credited in popular tradition with the creation of tai chi. Together with Mount Longhu, it is one of the principal sacred mountains of Chinese Taoism.

Geography

The Wudang range covers roughly 400 square kilometres (150 sq mi) and is known for its 72 peaks, 36 cliffs and 24 streams. Its highest summit, Tianzhu Peak ("Heaven's Pillar"), rises to about 1,612 metres and is crowned by the gilded Golden Hall (Jindian), a bronze temple cast in 1413 during the Ming dynasty.

History

Taoist temples existed on the mountain from the Tang dynasty onward, but the great building campaign came under the Yongle Emperor of the Ming dynasty, who in 1412 ordered the construction of a vast complex of palaces, temples and bridges, employing some 200,000 labourers. The emperor claimed the mountain's deity, Zhenwu (the Perfected Warrior), had aided his seizure of the throne. Over the following centuries the Wudang complex grew to include nine palaces, nine monasteries, 72 temples and numerous bridges and pavilions.

In 1994 the Ancient Building Complex in the Wudang Mountains was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. The site covers the surviving Ming-dynasty religious buildings, including the Forbidden City at the summit, the Purple Cloud Temple (Zixiao Gong) and the Nanyang Palace.

Martial arts

Wudang is regarded, alongside the Shaolin Monastery, as one of the two great centres of Chinese martial arts. The "internal" (neijia) styles associated with the mountain — tai chi, xingyiquan and baguazhang — emphasise softness, circular movement and the cultivation of qi, in contrast to the "external" Shaolin styles. The popular Wudang schools of kung fu derive their lineage claims from Zhang Sanfeng, though historians regard his role as legendary.
