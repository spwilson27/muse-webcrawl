Kamakura (Japanese: 鎌倉) is a city in Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan, about 50 km south-southwest of Tokyo. Though a modest city today, Kamakura was the political centre of Japan in the late 12th and 13th centuries as the seat of the Kamakura shogunate, the first samurai government, founded by Minamoto no Yoritomo. It is one of Japan's most popular tourist destinations, famous for its Buddhist temples and Shinto shrines, its beaches, and the Great Buddha of Kamakura.

History

Minamoto no Yoritomo established his military government at Kamakura in 1192, after defeating the Taira clan in the Genpei War, taking the title of shōgun. The shogunate ruled Japan from Kamakura until 1333, when Nitta Yoshisada captured the city and destroyed the regime in the fighting that brought down the Hōjō regents. The Kamakura period saw the flowering of Zen Buddhism, introduced from China, and of distinctive Kamakura culture and scholarship under warrior patronage.

Sights

  • Kōtoku-in — home of the Great Buddha (Daibutsu), a monumental bronze statue of Amida Buddha about 11.4 m tall (13.35 m with its base), cast in 1252 and originally housed in a great hall destroyed by storms and a tsunami.
  • Tsurugaoka Hachimangū — the city's most important Shinto shrine, founded by Yoritomo and dedicated to Hachiman, patron of the Minamoto and of warriors.
  • The five great Zen temples (Gozan) — Kenchō-ji, Engaku-ji, Jufuku-ji, Jōchi-ji and Jōmyō-ji.
  • Hase-dera — famous for its eleven-headed wooden statue of Kannon.
  • Yuigahama beach and the nearby island of Enoshima.

Transport

Kamakura Station is served by the JR Yokosuka Line from Tokyo (about one hour) and by the scenic Enoden (Enoshima Electric Railway), a tram-like line running along the coast to Fujisawa.
