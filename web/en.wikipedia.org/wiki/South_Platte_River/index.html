South Platte River

The South Platte River is a major river in the U.S. states of Colorado and Nebraska, and one of the two main tributaries of the Platte River. It rises in the Colorado Rocky Mountains and flows about 439 miles (707 km) northeast to join the North Platte River near the city of North Platte, Nebraska.

Course

The river forms in Park County, Colorado, in the high alpine basin of South Park, and descends through dramatic canyons — including Waterton Canyon — onto the plains at Denver, which was founded at its confluence with Cherry Creek during the 1858 gold rush. From Denver it flows northeast across the plains, receiving Cache la Poudre River near Greeley, before crossing into Nebraska.

Water use

The South Platte is heavily managed: reservoirs such as Eleven Mile, Spinney Mountain, Antero and Chatfield store water for the Denver metropolitan area, while extensive ditch systems irrigate farms along its valley. Trans-mountain diversions bring additional water from the Colorado River basin. Competing demands among cities, agriculture and instream flows have made it one of the most litigated and closely administered rivers in the American West.

History

The valley was a corridor for Indigenous peoples, fur traders and emigrant trails, and the river's flood of 1864 devastated early Denver. In September 2013, severe flooding along the South Platte and its Front Range tributaries caused extensive damage across northeastern Colorado.
