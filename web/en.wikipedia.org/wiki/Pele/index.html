Pele

Kīlauea, regarded as the home of Pele.

Pele is the Hawaiian goddess of volcanoes and fire and the creator of the Hawaiian Islands. In Hawaiian religion she is a powerful and revered deity, known as "Pele-honua-mea" (Pele of the sacred land) and "Ka wahine ‘ai honua" (the woman who devours the land). This article concerns the goddess; for the Brazilian footballer, see Pelé.

Mythology

According to legend, Pele travelled to Hawaii from Kahiki (Tahiti), fleeing her elder sister Nā-maka-o-Kaha‘i, a sea goddess. She carried her favourite younger sister, Hi‘iaka, in egg form, and sought a home where she could dig her fire pits. Pursued by her sister, she moved from island to island—from Ni‘ihau and Kaua‘i southeastward—each former home becoming an older, eroded volcano. She finally settled in the crater of Halema‘uma‘u at the summit of Kīlauea on the island of Hawai‘i, where her spirit is said to reside today.

Worship and cultural significance

Pele remains a living presence in Hawaiian culture. Devotees make offerings at Kīlauea, and hula traditions, chants (mele), and stories preserve her cycle of legends, including the epic of Hi‘iaka. Sightings of a mysterious woman in white—an old woman or a beautiful young woman—near volcanic areas are attributed to her, and a popular belief warns that removing lava rocks from Hawaii brings her displeasure.

Volcanic activity

Eruptions of Kīlauea, including the long-lived Pu‘u ‘Ō‘ō eruption (1983–2018) and the destructive lower Puna eruption of 2018, are widely interpreted by practitioners as manifestations of Pele, and her role in creating new land is central to Native Hawaiian understandings of the volcanic landscape.
