Chinese zodiac

The twelve zodiac animals in order

The Chinese zodiac is a traditional classification scheme based on the Chinese calendar that assigns an animal and its reputed attributes to each year in a repeating twelve-year cycle. The twelve animals are, in order: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig.

Origins

The zodiac animals correspond to the twelve Earthly Branches of the traditional calendar and were fixed in their modern form by the Han dynasty. A popular folk legend, the "Great Race", explains the ordering: the Jade Emperor invited the animals to race across a river, and the Rat won by riding on the Ox's ear and leaping ahead at the finish.

Uses

A person's zodiac sign is determined by birth year; the year of one's own sign (benmingnian) recurs every twelve years and is traditionally considered unlucky, warded off by wearing red. The zodiac is also used in Chinese astrology and fortune-telling, and similar twelve-animal cycles are used in other East and Southeast Asian cultures, sometimes with substituted animals (for example, the Cat replaces the Rabbit in Vietnam).
