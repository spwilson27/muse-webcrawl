Fansub

A fansub (short for fan-subtitled) is a version of a foreign film or foreign television program, typically anime, which has been translated by fans and subtitled into a language other than that of the original. The practice emerged in the 1980s within anime fandom and became a major channel through which Japanese animation reached international audiences before official licensed releases were widely available.

History

Early fansubs were distributed on VHS tapes. Fan groups obtained raw Japanese recordings, translated scripts, timed subtitles, and used genlock hardware to superimpose the text before dubbing copies for distribution through anime clubs and conventions, usually at no profit beyond the cost of tapes and postage. The rise of the internet in the 2000s shifted distribution to digital "digisubs": video files encoded with soft or hard subtitles and shared via IRC, BitTorrent, and direct download, allowing episodes to appear within hours or days of the Japanese broadcast.

Production

A fansub group typically divides labour among a translator, a timer who synchronises subtitle lines with the audio, a typesetter responsible for on-screen signs and karaoke effects for opening and ending songs, an editor, and quality checkers. Releases often include translator's notes explaining cultural references, honorifics, and wordplay.

Ethics and legality

Fansubbing involves the unauthorised copying and translation of copyrighted works, and its legal status is contested. An informal code within the community held that distribution should cease once a work was licensed in the target region, and that fansubs should not be sold for profit. As licensed simulcasting services such as Crunchyroll became widespread in the 2010s, much of the fansub scene declined or shifted toward works without official releases.
