Culture of Hong Kong

The culture of Hong Kong is a hybrid of traditional Chinese culture and the legacy of British colonial rule, shaped by the city's history as a trading port and financial centre. Hong Kong is often described as a place where "East meets West", and its residents commonly identify as "Hongkongers" with a distinct local identity.

Language

Cantonese is the mother tongue of the great majority of residents and the dominant language of everyday life, popular culture, and local media. English is a co-official language, widely used in government, law, and business. Written Chinese in Hong Kong uses traditional characters. Code-switching between Cantonese and English is common, as is the mixture of both in popular song lyrics and advertising.

Cinema and popular music

Hong Kong cinema was once the third-largest film industry in the world, famous for martial arts films, action cinema, and directors and stars such as Bruce Lee, Jackie Chan, Chow Yun-fat, and Wong Kar-wai. Cantopop dominated regional popular music from the 1970s, with stars such as Leslie Cheung and Anita Mui achieving fame across East and Southeast Asia.

Food

Hong Kong food culture ranges from dai pai dong street stalls and cha chaan teng tea restaurants — serving local adaptations of Western dishes such as Hong Kong-style milk tea and pineapple buns — to dim sum lunches and Michelin-starred fine dining. The city's wet markets and dried seafood shops preserve older Cantonese culinary traditions.

Festivals

Public life follows both the Chinese and Western calendars. Major celebrations include Lunar New Year, the Mid-Autumn Festival, the Dragon Boat Festival, and the Cheung Chau Bun Festival, alongside Christmas and Halloween events in commercial districts.
