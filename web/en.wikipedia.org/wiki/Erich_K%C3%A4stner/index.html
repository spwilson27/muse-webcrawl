Emil Erich Kästner (23 February 1899 – 29 July 1974) was a German writer, poet, screenwriter and satirist, known especially for his humorous, socially critical poems and for children's books such as Emil and the Detectives (1929).

Life and work

Born in Dresden, Kästner served as a soldier in the First World War and then studied German studies, history and philosophy in Leipzig, earning a doctorate in 1925. He worked as a journalist and freelance writer in Berlin during the Weimar Republic, publishing satirical verse and the novel Fabian (1931), a portrait of a moralist in a decadent society.

Nazi era

Kästner's books were among those burned by the Nazis in May 1933, and he was banned from publishing literature in Germany, although he continued to write under pseudonyms and for film. Unusually, he remained in Germany throughout the Second World War, later explaining that he wished to witness events and care for his mother.

Post-war years

After 1945 Kästner became a leading figure in Munich's cultural life, editing the children's magazine Pinguin and writing cabaret. His children's novel Lottie and Lisa (1949) has been adapted for film many times, most famously as The Parent Trap. He died in Munich in 1974.
