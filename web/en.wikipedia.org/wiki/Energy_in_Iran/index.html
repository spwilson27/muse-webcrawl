Energy in Iran

Energy in Iran is dominated by the country's enormous hydrocarbon endowment: Iran holds among the world's largest proven reserves of natural gas (second only to Russia) and the third- or fourth-largest reserves of crude oil. Fossil fuels supply the overwhelming share of domestic energy, and oil and gas exports have historically provided the bulk of government revenue.

Oil and gas

Production centres on the giant fields of Khuzestan and the offshore South Pars gas field, shared with Qatar. The National Iranian Oil Company (NIOC) controls the sector. International sanctions have repeatedly constrained investment and exports, cutting output after 2012 and again after 2018, though production later recovered to around 3 million barrels per day. Domestic demand for gasoline has often exceeded refining capacity, requiring imports and prompting price reforms that sparked protests in 2019.

Electricity

Electricity generation relies mainly on natural gas, supplemented by hydroelectricity and oil-fired plants; chronic summer peak demand has caused blackouts. The Bushehr Nuclear Power Plant, built with Russian assistance, began commercial operation in 2013 as the Middle East's first civilian nuclear power station. Renewables — wind, solar and small hydro — remain a small share despite high potential.

Subsidies and consumption

Iran has long had some of the world's cheapest domestic fuel and electricity prices due to heavy subsidies, driving high per-capita consumption and energy intensity. Subsidy reform began in 2010 under the Targeted Subsidies Plan, replacing price supports with cash transfers, but inflation eroded much of the effect.
