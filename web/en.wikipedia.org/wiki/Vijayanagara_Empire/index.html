Vijayanagara Empire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The stone chariot and gopurams of the Vittala Temple at Hampi]

The Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646) was a Hindu empire in southern India, founded by the brothers Harihara I and Bukka Raya I amid the collapse of Delhi Sultanate power in the Deccan, and named for its capital Vijayanagara ('City of Victory') on the Tungabhadra River — whose ruins at Hampi are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. For two centuries it was the dominant power of the south, the great bulwark against the Deccan sultanates, and under Krishnadevaraya (r. 1509–1529) a byword for wealth, temple-building and Telugu and Sanskrit literature. Catastrophic defeat at Talikota (1565) destroyed the capital, though successor courts lingered for decades.

Rise and zenith

The Sangama brothers, guided in tradition by the sage Vidyaranya, built a state that absorbed the Hoysala and other southern kingdoms and fought the Bahmani Sultanate for the rich Raichur doab between the Krishna and Tungabhadra rivers. Successive dynasties (Sangama, Saluva, Tuluva) ruled through nayaka military chiefs. Krishnadevaraya defeated the Gajapatis of Orissa and the Deccan sultans, patronised the Ashtadiggajas (eight great Telugu poets), endowed temples from Tirupati to Hampi, and received the Portuguese — newly arrived at Goa — as horse-trading partners; travellers like Paes and Nuniz described a capital of half a million people, diamonds sold in open bazaars.

Talikota and after

The regent Aliya Rama Raya's meddling diplomacy united the Deccan sultanates against Vijayanagara; at Talikota (23 January 1565) the imperial army was annihilated and Rama Raya captured and beheaded mid-battle. The victors occupied and systematically wrecked the capital — a ruin ever since. The court fled south (Penukonda, Chandragiri, Vellore) as the Aravidu dynasty; nayaka states (Madurai, Thanjavur, Mysore's Wodeyars among them) became independent, and the empire faded out by 1646.
