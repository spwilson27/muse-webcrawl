Contraction (grammar)

A contraction is a shortened version of the spoken and written forms of a word, syllable, or word group, created by omission of internal letters and sounds. In English, contractions typically combine a pronoun or noun with a verb, or a verb with the negation not, and the missing letters are conventionally marked with an apostrophe: do not → don't, I have → I've, it is → it's.

English contractions

Common forms include:

  • With be: I'm, you're, he's, we're, they're
  • With auxiliaries: I'll, we'd, they've, she'd
  • Negative contractions: isn't, aren't, can't, won't (irregular, from will not), shan't
  • Archaic or dialectal: 'tis, o'clock (of the clock), ne'er-do-well

Some contractions are homophones of possessives, a frequent source of error: it's (it is) versus its; you're versus your; they're versus their/there. Double contractions such as I'd've are common in speech but rare in formal writing, where contractions in general are often avoided — though this stylistic rule has softened over the past century.

Other languages

Contraction is widespread. French elides vowels before vowels (je aime → j'aime) and mandates fused forms such as au (à + le) and du (de + le). Italian has elisions (l'amico) and articulated prepositions (del, nel). Portuguese fuses prepositions with articles (do, na, pelo). In Sanskrit grammar, sandhi systematically merges sounds across word boundaries. In historical linguistics, contraction also describes the merging of two vowels or syllables within a word over time.

Contraction and abbreviation

A contraction is distinguished from an abbreviation in that the shortened form is pronounced as a word in its own right within the flow of speech; in medieval manuscripts, scribal contractions (special marks for omitted letters) were a standard space-saving device.
