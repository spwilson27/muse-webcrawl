Tefillin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Type: Jewish ritual object
Materials: Leather, parchment
Used in: Weekday morning prayers

Tefillin (Hebrew: תְּפִלִּין), also called phylacteries, are a set of small black leather boxes containing scrolls of parchment inscribed with verses from the Torah. They are worn by observant adult Jews during weekday morning prayers.

Description

Tefillin consist of two units: one worn on the arm (shel yad, "of the hand") and one on the head (shel rosh, "of the head"). Each contains four passages from the Torah (Exodus 13:1–10, Exodus 13:11–16, Deuteronomy 6:4–9, and Deuteronomy 11:13–21) that mention the commandment to bind words as a sign on the hand and between the eyes.

Manufacture

The boxes are made from the hide of a kosher animal and must be perfectly square at the base. The parchment scrolls are handwritten by a trained scribe (sofer) using a quill and special ink. Leather straps, blackened on one side, attach the boxes to the arm and head.

Customs

The arm tefillin is placed on the weaker arm (the left arm for right-handed people) opposite the heart, and the strap is wound seven times around the forearm. Tefillin are not worn on Shabbat or most Jewish festivals. Traditionally worn by men from the age of bar mitzvah, they are also worn by some women in egalitarian movements.
