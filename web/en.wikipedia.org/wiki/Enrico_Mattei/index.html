Enrico Mattei

Enrico Mattei (29 April 1906 – 27 October 1962) was an Italian public administrator and entrepreneur who built the state energy company Eni into a major international oil and gas group. A former partisan commander and Christian Democrat deputy, he is remembered as one of the most powerful and controversial figures of postwar Italy.

From AGIP to Eni

In 1945 Mattei was appointed to liquidate AGIP, the Fascist-era state oil agency. Instead of winding it down he expanded it, encouraged by natural gas discoveries in the Po Valley, and in 1953 he persuaded parliament to create Ente Nazionale Idrocarburi (Eni), with a monopoly on hydrocarbons in the Po Valley and wide freedom abroad.

Challenging the "Seven Sisters"

Mattei coined the phrase "Seven Sisters" for the Anglo-American oil majors that dominated world markets. To break into their cartel he offered producing countries unusually generous terms — notably 75/25 profit-sharing deals in Iran, Egypt and elsewhere — and bought crude from the Soviet Union, infuriating Western governments and companies. At home he built a web of political influence, founding the newspaper Il Giorno and financing factions across the political spectrum.

Death

Mattei died on 27 October 1962 when his company jet crashed near Bascapè, outside Milan, in bad weather; the journalist Mauro De Mauro, who was investigating the crash, disappeared in 1970. Decades later, exhumations and forensic work led Italian authorities to conclude the plane had been sabotaged with an explosive device, though responsibility was never definitively established — a story retold in Francesco Rosi's 1972 film The Mattei Affair.
