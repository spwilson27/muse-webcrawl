Silicon Docks is a nickname for the area in Dublin, Ireland, around the Grand Canal Dock and the Docklands, which is home to the European headquarters of many large technology companies. The name is a riff on Silicon Valley in California, substituting the historic docks of the River Liffey and Grand Canal for the "silicon" of the tech industry.

Development

The regeneration of the Dublin Docklands began with the International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) in 1987, attracting finance firms to the north bank of the Liffey. Technology companies followed in the 2000s, drawn by Ireland's low corporation tax, English-speaking workforce, and EU membership. Google established its European headquarters at Gordon House on Barrow Street in 2004 and expanded across the Grand Canal Dock area, buying the Montevetro building in 2011. Meta (Facebook), LinkedIn, Twitter (later scaled back), Salesforce, Airbnb, TikTok, and Stripe all took major offices around the docks, alongside the city centre tech scene.

Character and criticism

The district mixes glass office towers with converted warehouses, the Bord Gáis Energy Theatre, and new apartment blocks. Its growth has been criticised for driving up housing costs and displacing or overshadowing the long-standing working-class communities of Ringsend, Irishtown, and Pearse Street, and for the dependence of the Irish economy on a small number of US multinationals. Ireland's corporate tax reforms (including the global minimum tax agreed in 2021) are watched closely for their effect on the cluster.
