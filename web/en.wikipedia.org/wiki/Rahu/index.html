Rahu

Rahu (Sanskrit: राहु) is one of the nine major celestial bodies (navagraha) in Hindu texts and is regarded as a shadow entity that causes eclipses of the Sun and Moon. In Hindu mythology, Rahu is the severed head of the asura Svarbhanu, who drank the nectar of immortality (amrita) and was beheaded by Vishnu. His head became Rahu and his body became Ketu, the complementary shadow planet.

Mythology

According to the legend of the churning of the ocean of milk (Samudra Manthana), the demon Svarbhanu disguised himself among the gods to drink the amrita distributed by Vishnu in the form of Mohini. The Sun and Moon noticed the deception and alerted Vishnu, who cut off the demon's head with his discus — but the nectar had already reached his throat, so the head became immortal. In revenge, Rahu periodically swallows the Sun and Moon, causing eclipses, but they escape because he has no body.

Astronomy and astrology

Astronomically, Rahu corresponds to the ascending (northern) lunar node — one of the two points where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic — while Ketu corresponds to the descending node. In Vedic astrology, Rahu is considered a malefic influence associated with materialism, obsession, and sudden events; its period of influence (dasha) lasts 18 years. Rahu is traditionally depicted as a dragon's head without a body, riding a chariot drawn by dark horses.

Worship

Rahu is worshipped particularly during Rahukaalam, a daily inauspicious period calculated by Hindu astrologers, and at temples such as the Thirunageswaram Naganatha Swamy Temple in Tamil Nadu, where devotees offer prayers to mitigate Rahu's adverse effects.
