The Naval Criminal Investigative Service (NCIS) is the primary federal law enforcement agency of the United States Department of the Navy. It conducts criminal investigations, counterintelligence, and force-protection activities concerning the United States Navy and Marine Corps, and is staffed predominantly by civilian special agents.

History

The agency traces its lineage to the Office of Naval Intelligence, founded in 1882. Naval investigative functions were consolidated during the Second World War, and after the war the organisation became known as the Naval Investigative Service (NIS). Following the 1991 Tailhook scandal and criticism of its handling, the agency was reorganised and renamed the Naval Criminal Investigative Service in 1992, placing it under civilian leadership.

Mission and organisation

NCIS investigates felony-level crimes involving Navy and Marine Corps personnel, including homicide, fraud, espionage, and terrorism, and provides protective services and criminal intelligence. It operates field offices around the world, including afloat aboard major vessels. The agency is headquartered at the Russell-Knox Building at Marine Corps Base Quantico, Virginia. NCIS is widely known through the television drama series NCIS, first broadcast in 2003, and its spin-offs.
