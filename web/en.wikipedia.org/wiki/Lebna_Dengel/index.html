Lebna Dengel (Ge'ez: ልብነ ድንግል, Ləbnä Dəngəl), also known by his regnal name Dawit II, was Emperor of Ethiopia from 1508 until his death in 1540, a member of the Solomonic dynasty. He is best known to Europeans as the ruler who received the Portuguese embassy of 1520 and as the emperor during the devastating invasion of the Adal Sultanate under Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi.

Reign

Lebna Dengel came to the throne as a boy; his grandmother, Empress Eleni, acted as regent and pursued diplomacy with Portugal. Early in his reign he campaigned successfully against the Adal Sultanate, winning a major victory in 1517, the year Adal fell into internal turmoil. In 1520 a Portuguese fleet reached Ethiopia and an embassy under Rodrigo de Lima remained at his court for six years; its chaplain, Francisco Álvares, wrote the first detailed European account of Ethiopia, Verdadeira Informação das Terras do Preste João das Índias.

War with Adal

From 1529 the Imam Ahmad ibn Ibrahim launched a jihad that overran the Ethiopian highlands. Lebna Dengel was defeated at Shimbra Kure (1529) and spent the rest of his reign as a fugitive, watching churches and monasteries—including the treasures of the Aksumite era—be destroyed. Portuguese military aid arrived only after his death; he died in 1540 near Debre Damo and was succeeded by his son Gelawdewos, who, with Cristóvão da Gama's musketeers, reversed the conquest.
