Trans-Iranian Railway

The line crosses the Zagros and Alborz mountain ranges by hundreds of bridges and tunnels.

The Trans-Iranian Railway (Persian: راه‌آهن سراسری ایران) was a major railway building project started in Pahlavi Iran in 1927 and completed in 1938, under the direction of the then Persian monarch Reza Shah. It was entirely built with indigenous capital and connects the capital Tehran with Bandar Shahpur (now Bandar-e Emam Khomeyni) on the Persian Gulf in the south and Bandar Shah (now Bandar Torkaman) on the Caspian Sea in the north, via Ahvaz and Qom.

Construction

The line runs for 1,394 kilometres (866 mi) and was built to standard gauge (1,435 mm). Its construction was an extraordinary feat of engineering: the route crosses two mountain ranges, the Zagros and the Alborz, and required 4,100 bridges, 224 tunnels, and extensive rock cuttings. Financing came from domestic taxes on tea and sugar rather than foreign loans, a point of national pride stressed by Reza Shah.

Foreign engineers and contractors from several countries participated, coordinated by an international syndicate; the Danish firm Kampsax supervised much of the work. The line was formally opened on 25 August 1938.

Second World War and legacy

After the Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran in 1941, the railway became a vital artery of the Persian Corridor, through which Allied war materiel was shipped to the Soviet Union. In 1961, under Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, extensions toward Tabriz, Mashhad and the Turkish border were completed. In 2021 the railway was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
