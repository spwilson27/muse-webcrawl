Black caiman

Black caiman
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)
Scientific nameMelanosuchus niger (Spix, 1825)
FamilyAlligatoridae

The black caiman (Melanosuchus niger) is a species of large crocodilian and, along with the American alligator, is one of the largest extant members of the family Alligatoridae. It is a carnivorous reptile that lives along slow-moving rivers, lakes, seasonally flooded savannas of the Amazon basin, and other freshwater habitats of northern South America. It is the largest predator in the Amazon ecosystem.

Description

The black caiman has darkly coloured, black skin with white or yellow banding on the flanks and a distinctly broad head. Adults commonly reach 2.8 to 4.3 metres (9 to 14 feet) in length, and exceptionally large males may exceed 5 metres (16 feet) and weigh more than 1,000 kilograms. Like other caimans, it has a bony ridge between the eyes. Its dark colouring helps it camouflage during nocturnal hunts and may assist in absorbing heat.

Distribution and habitat

The species is found in Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador, French Guiana, Guyana, Peru, and Venezuela, throughout the Amazon, Essequibo, and Orinoco river basins. It inhabits flooded forests, oxbow lakes, and slow rivers, moving into flooded savannas during the wet season.

Behaviour and diet

Black caimans are nocturnal apex predators. Juveniles feed on crustaceans, insects, and small fish; adults take fish such as piranhas and catfish, birds, turtles, mammals including capybaras and deer, and occasionally larger prey. Females build mound nests during the dry season and lay 30 to 65 eggs, guarding them until they hatch after about six weeks.

Conservation

Black caiman populations were severely depleted by commercial hunting for their skins from the 1940s to the 1970s. Legal protection and the decline of the skin trade allowed populations to recover in many areas, and the species is now listed as Least Concern, although populations remain dependent on conservation and the species is still threatened by habitat loss and illegal hunting in parts of its range.
