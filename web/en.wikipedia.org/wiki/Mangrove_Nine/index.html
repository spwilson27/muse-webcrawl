The Mangrove Nine were nine Black British activists tried at the Old Bailey in 1971 on charges arising from a protest march against police harassment of the Mangrove restaurant in Notting Hill, London. The defendants included Barbara Beese, Rupert Boyce, Frank Crichlow, Rhodan Gordon, Darcus Howe, Anthony Innis, Altheia Jones-LeCointe, Rothwell Kentish and Godfrey Millett. Their case became a landmark in British legal and civil-rights history: the judge and defence both publicly acknowledged evidence of racial prejudice within the Metropolitan Police.

Background

The Mangrove, a Caribbean restaurant at 8 All Saints Road owned by Frank Crichlow, was a meeting place for Notting Hill's Black community — and the target of repeated police drugs raids, none of which found evidence of drug dealing. On 9 August 1970, 150 people marched in protest; clashes with police led to arrests and charges of incitement to riot, an unusually serious charge that took the case to the Old Bailey.

Trial

The 55-day trial in 1971 was marked by challenges from the defence, including demands for an all-Black jury (rejected), attempts to have the judge recuse himself, and two defendants — Howe and Jones-LeCointe — representing themselves. All nine were acquitted of the principal charge of incitement to riot; four, plus one other defendant, received suspended sentences for lesser offences such as affray and assaulting police. In his summing-up the judge remarked that evidence showed racial hatred on both sides — the first judicial acknowledgment of racism in the Metropolitan Police.

Legacy

The trial is regarded as a foundational moment for Black British activism and was dramatised in Mangrove, the first film of Steve McQueen's Small Axe anthology (2020).
