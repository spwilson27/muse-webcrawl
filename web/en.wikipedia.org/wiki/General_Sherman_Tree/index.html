General Sherman Tree

The General Sherman Tree is a giant sequoia (Sequoiadendron giganteum) located in the Giant Forest of Sequoia National Park in Tulare County, California. By volume, it is the largest known living single-stem tree on Earth, and one of the tallest, widest and longest-lived organisms on the planet.

Height83.8 m (274.9 ft)
Ground circumference31.3 m (102.6 ft)
Maximum trunk diameter11 m (36.5 ft)
Estimated bole volume1,487 m³ (52,508 cu ft)
Estimated age2,300–2,700 years

Although it is neither the tallest tree (a distinction held by coast redwoods such as Hyperion) nor the widest, its massive trunk gives it the greatest volume of any living single-stem tree. In 2006 the tree shed its largest branch, a limb over 2 metres in diameter and roughly 30 metres long, which shattered the surrounding fence and walkway; scientists regarded the loss as a natural ageing phenomenon rather than a sign of ill health.

History

The tree was named in 1879 by naturalist James Wolverton, who had served as a lieutenant under General William Tecumseh Sherman in the American Civil War. In the early twentieth century it briefly bore the name "Karl Marx Tree" when the surrounding land was home to the Kaweah Colony, a utopian socialist community. The tree stands at about 2,109 metres (6,919 ft) elevation on the western slope of the Sierra Nevada.

Visiting

A paved trail from the Wolverton Road parking area leads to the tree, which sits at the heart of the Giant Forest grove alongside other notables such as the Congress Group. The General Sherman Tree is among the most visited natural landmarks in the national park system.
