Prime Directive

The Prime Directive is a guiding principle of the United Federation of Planets in the fictional Star Trek universe. It prohibits Starfleet personnel and spacecraft from interfering with the natural development of alien civilizations, particularly those that have not yet developed faster-than-light travel. Formally known as Starfleet General Order 1, it is described as the Federation's supreme guiding principle, violations of which can end a Starfleet officer's career.

Origins

The concept was introduced in the original Star Trek series in the 1960s and is generally attributed to producer Gene L. Coon, with influences from creator Gene Roddenberry. It is often read as a commentary on United States involvement in the Vietnam War, and on earlier real-world policies. In the Enterprise-era prequel fiction, the directive grew out of the Vulcan practice of non-interference with less advanced species, and was formalized after early disastrous first contacts.

Content of the directive

The directive forbids interference with the internal and natural development of alien civilizations below a certain threshold of technological, scientific and cultural development. In practice this has been applied to pre-warp societies, and Starfleet officers are expected to allow such societies to develop on their own, even when interference might appear morally tempting. Captains including James T. Kirk, Jean-Luc Picard and Kathryn Janeway have debated, bent and occasionally violated the directive in dramatic storylines.

Reception and influence

The Prime Directive has become one of the most widely discussed ethical ideas in science fiction, raising questions about colonialism, cultural relativism and humanitarian intervention. It has been referenced in legal scholarship, philosophy courses and debates about real-world space policy and contact with isolated peoples.
