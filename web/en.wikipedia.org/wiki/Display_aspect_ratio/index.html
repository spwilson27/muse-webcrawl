Display aspect ratio

The display aspect ratio (DAR) is the aspect ratio of a display device and is the proportional relationship between the width and the height of the display. It is expressed as two numbers separated by a colon (x:y), where x corresponds to the width and y to the height. Common aspect ratios for displays, past and present, include 5:4, 4:3, 16:10, 16:9, and 21:9.

Common ratios
4:3Traditional television and early computer monitors
16:10Widescreen computer monitors of the 2000s
16:9Modern HDTV and most monitors
21:9Ultrawide monitors

History

Early television and computer displays used the 4:3 ratio inherited from Academy ratio film. As high-definition television was standardized, 16:9 became the dominant ratio for televisions and later for computer monitors and smartphone screens. Ultrawide 21:9 and taller phone ratios such as 19.5:9 appeared in the 2010s.

DAR versus PAR and SAR

The display aspect ratio combines the storage aspect ratio (SAR), the ratio of pixel dimensions of the stored image, with the pixel aspect ratio (PAR). When pixels are square, DAR equals SAR; formats such as anamorphic DVD video use non-square pixels, so the three quantities differ.
