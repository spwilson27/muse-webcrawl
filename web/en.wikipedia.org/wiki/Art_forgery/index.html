Art forgery

Art forgery is the creation and sale of works of art falsely attributed to another artist, usually a famous one, with intent to deceive. Forgery ranges from copies of individual paintings to fabricated provenances and entirely invented oeuvres.

Notable forgers

Famous cases include Han van Meegeren, whose fake Vermeers were sold to Nazi leaders including Hermann Göring; Elmyr de Hory, who sold forged Matisses and Picassos; and Wolfgang Beltracchi, whose forged modernist paintings deceived major auction houses. The Knoedler Gallery scandal in New York, revealed in the 2010s, involved tens of millions of dollars of fake Abstract Expressionist paintings.

Detection

Authentication combines provenance research, connoisseurship and scientific analysis: pigment and binder analysis, X-ray and infrared imaging, canvas thread counting, radiocarbon dating and dendrochronology can expose anachronistic materials. A single anachronistic pigment — such as titanium white in a purported 17th-century painting — can be decisive.

Legal aspects

Selling a knowing forgery is fraud in most jurisdictions, though making a copy is not itself illegal. The market's reliance on expertise and catalogues raisonnés makes attribution disputes a recurring feature of art law.
