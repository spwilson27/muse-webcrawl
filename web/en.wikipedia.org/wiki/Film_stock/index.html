Film stock

Film stock is an analog medium used for recording images, whether for still photography or motion pictures. It consists of a transparent film base coated with an emulsion containing light-sensitive silver halide crystals. Colour film uses multiple emulsion layers sensitive to different parts of the spectrum, coupled with dye-forming chemicals.

Characteristics

Film stocks are defined by their format (gauge), speed (sensitivity), colour balance (daylight or tungsten), and whether they are negative or reversal (positive) stocks. Motion picture gauges include 8mm, Super 8, 16mm, 35mm, and 65/70mm; still photography formats include 35mm (135), medium format (120), and large format sheet film.

Manufacturers and history

For most of the twentieth century the market was dominated by Kodak, Fujifilm, and Agfa. The digital revolution of the 2000s drastically reduced demand; Fujifilm largely exited motion picture stock production in 2013, leaving Kodak as the principal supplier of motion picture film, though a revival of interest among filmmakers led Kodak to reintroduce stocks such as Ektachrome.

Archival properties

On a polyester base and stored cold and dry, black-and-white film can last for centuries; colour dyes fade faster. Unexposed stock degrades with age and heat, and is refrigerated by professional users.
