Landsat 9 is an Earth observation satellite launched on 27 September 2021 from Vandenberg Space Force Base. It continues the Landsat program, the longest-running enterprise for acquiring satellite imagery of Earth, begun in 1972. Landsat 9 is nearly identical to its predecessor Landsat 8, carrying improved versions of the same instruments, and together the two satellites image the entire Earth every eight days.

Instruments

The Operational Land Imager 2 (OLI-2) records nine spectral bands in the visible, near-infrared and shortwave infrared at 30 m resolution (15 m panchromatic) with higher radiometric precision (14-bit quantisation) than earlier sensors. The Thermal Infrared Sensor 2 (TIRS-2) measures two thermal bands at 100 m resolution, with design improvements addressing the stray-light problem that affected the original TIRS on Landsat 8. Both instruments use push-broom sensors, improving signal-to-noise compared with the whisk-broom design of Landsat 7.

Mission

NASA built and launched the satellite; the USGS operates it and distributes the data free of charge, continuing the open-data policy in place since 2008. Landsat 9 data support agriculture, forestry, water management, disaster response (wildfire, flood and volcano monitoring), glacier and coastal-change studies, and the monitoring of land use and land cover change worldwide. The spacecraft has a design life of five years with consumables for more than ten.

Development and launch

The satellite was built by Northrop Grumman, with Ball Aerospace supplying OLI-2 and NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center building TIRS-2 in-house. Launch aboard a United Launch Alliance Atlas V in the 401 configuration occurred on 27 September 2021; first images were released in November 2021, and operational hand-over to the USGS followed in 2022.
