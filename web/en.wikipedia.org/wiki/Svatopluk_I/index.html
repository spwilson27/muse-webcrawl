Svatopluk I

Statue of Svatopluk at Bratislava Castle

Reign: 870/871 – 894
Title: Duke, later King of Great Moravia
Predecessor: Rastislav
Successors: Mojmir II and Svatopluk II
Died: 894

Svatopluk I (also Svätopluk, Sventopluk, or Zwentibold in Frankish sources) was the greatest ruler of Great Moravia, reigning from about 870 until his death in 894. Under him Moravia reached its largest extent, dominating Bohemia, parts of Poland, Pannonia, and Slovakia, and became the strongest Slavic state of its era.

Rise to power

Nephew of Prince Rastislav, Svatopluk governed the Nitra region as a subordinate duke. In 870 he seized Rastislav and handed him to the East Franks, receiving Moravia as a fief; when the Franks then imprisoned Svatopluk himself on suspicion of treason, a Moravian revolt under Slavomir forced his release, and Svatopluk turned on his former patrons, destroying a Frankish army in 871. He made peace with Louis the German in 874 at Forchheim while keeping effective independence.

Reign

Svatopluk expanded relentlessly: he subdued the Vistulans in Lesser Poland, annexed Bohemia (whose duke Bořivoj accepted baptism under his sway), and fought and negotiated with King Arnulf of East Francia. In church affairs he expelled the disciples of Methodius after the archbishop's death in 885, favouring the Latin clergy under Wiching, yet obtained papal recognition of his rule; Pope Stephen V addressed him as "king" (rex). Later Slovak national tradition celebrated him as a king and lawgiver, while the legend of his deathbed warning to his three sons — illustrated by a bundle of three rods that together cannot be broken — became a famous parable of unity. On his death in 894 the realm passed to his sons Mojmir II and Svatopluk II, whose quarrels contributed to Moravia's collapse before the Hungarians within a generation.
