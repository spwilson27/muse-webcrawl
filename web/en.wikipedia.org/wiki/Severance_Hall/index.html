Severance Hall, formally the Severance Music Center, is a concert hall in the University Circle neighborhood of Cleveland, Ohio, and the home of the Cleveland Orchestra. Opened in 1931, it is widely regarded as one of the most beautiful concert halls in the United States, noted for its blend of Neoclassical and Art Deco design and for its distinctive interior decoration in materials including aluminium, marble, and silver.

History

The hall was built with a gift from philanthropist John Long Severance, president of the Musical Arts Association, and his wife Elisabeth. Originally intended as a memorial to Elisabeth's love of music, it became a memorial to her after she died in 1929, before the hall was completed. Severance ultimately contributed around $2.6 million toward construction. The hall opened on 5 February 1931 with a gala concert conducted by Nikolai Sokoloff, the orchestra's first music director.

Architecture

The Cleveland firm of Walker and Weeks designed the building. The Euclid Avenue facade is neoclassical, with a colonnade and broad steps, while the interiors are richly decorated in an Egyptian Revival and Art Deco-influenced manner, featuring papyrus and lotus motifs, aluminium leaf, and woods from around the world. The main auditorium seats about 2,000, and the smaller Reinberger Chamber Hall seats about 400. The stage shell was remodelled in 1958 under music director George Szell to improve the hall's acoustics.

Organ and renovations

The hall's Norton Memorial Organ, built by the E. M. Skinner Organ Company and later rebuilt by Schantz, is one of the largest concert organs in the United States; it was restored and reinstalled around 2000 as part of a major two-year renovation and expansion (1998–2000) that restored the original interiors, modernized facilities, and enlarged backstage and patron amenities while keeping the orchestra in residence only a short distance away during construction.

Today

Severance Hall hosts the Cleveland Orchestra's subscription season, chamber concerts, recitals, and community events. In the 2020s the building was renamed the Severance Music Center to reflect its role as a broader campus for the orchestra's activities, while the historic hall retains the Severance name.
