Nitra (river)

The Nitra (Slovak: Nitra, German: Neutra, Hungarian: Nyitra) is a river in western Slovakia, a left tributary of the Váh. It is about 197 km long, with a basin of roughly 4,500 km².

Course

The Nitra rises in the Malá Fatra mountains near Kľačno and flows south through Prievidza, Topoľčany, the city of Nitra — which takes its name from the river — and Nové Zámky, before joining the Váh near Komoča. Its basin, the Nitra valley, is an agricultural lowland corridor between the Tribeč and Žitava hill country.

History

The river valley was an early centre of Slavic settlement and of Great Moravia; the city of Nitra hosts one of Slovakia's oldest churches. Industrial development in the upper valley around Prievidza and Nováky historically affected water quality, long a subject of environmental concern.
