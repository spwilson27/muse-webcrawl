House of Plantagenet

House of Plantagenet
Country: England, France (claims)
Founded: 12th century
Founder: Geoffrey Plantagenet, Count of Anjou
Final ruler: Richard III (died 1485)
Cadet branches: House of Lancaster, House of York

The House of Plantagenet was a royal house that originated in Anjou, France. Its members held the English throne from 1154, with the accession of Henry II, until 1485, when Richard III was killed at the Battle of Bosworth Field.

The name derives from Geoffrey of Anjou's nickname, from the sprig of broom (planta genista) he reportedly wore. Through marriage to Empress Matilda, Geoffrey's son became Henry II of England, founder of the Angevin Empire stretching from Scotland to the Pyrenees.

Kings

Plantagenet monarchs include Henry II, Richard I (the Lionheart), John, Henry III, Edward I, Edward II, Edward III, and Richard II. After 1399 the throne passed between the cadet houses of Lancaster (Henry IV, V, VI) and York (Edward IV, Edward V, Richard III) in the Wars of the Roses.

Legacy

The Plantagenet era saw Magna Carta (1215), the development of Parliament and English common law, the Hundred Years' War with France, and the Black Death. The dynasty ended with the Tudor victory in 1485.
