Richard Sapir

Richard Ben Sapir (July 27, 1936 – January 27, 1987) was an American author best known as the co-creator, with Warren Murphy, of The Destroyer series of paperback adventure novels featuring Remo Williams.

Career

Sapir worked as a journalist and editor before turning to fiction. He and Murphy created The Destroyer in the 1960s; the first novel, Created, the Destroyer, was published in 1971. The series, blending action, satire, and martial-arts mythology around the fictional assassin Remo Williams and his Korean master Chiun, eventually ran to more than 150 volumes and inspired the 1985 film Remo Williams: The Adventure Begins.

Other work

Under his own name Sapir wrote standalone novels including The Body (1983), a thriller about a skeleton believed to be that of Jesus, which was made into a 2001 film. He also wrote the novel Spies and served as a reporter and editor in New York and New Jersey. He died of a heart attack in New Hampshire in 1987.
