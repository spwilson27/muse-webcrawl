Astarte (Phoenician: 𐤏𐤔𐤕𐤓𐤕, ʿAštart; Greek: Ἀστάρτη) was the principal goddess of the Phoenician world, worshipped across the Levant and the Mediterranean colonies of Tyre and Sidon. A goddess of love, fertility and war, associated with the planet Venus, she is the West Semitic counterpart of the Mesopotamian Ishtar and Inanna.

Worship

Astarte was the patroness of Sidon, whose kings served as her priests, and of Tyre, where she was consort of Melqart. Her temples stood in Byblos, Cyprus, Sicily, Sardinia and Carthage, and the Phoenician foundation legend told how a falling star marked the site of her temple at Eryx. In Egypt, where she was adopted during the New Kingdom, she appears as a war goddess on horseback, consort of Seth or Resheph. Hebrew scripture condemns her worship as Ashtoreth — a name deliberately revocalised with the vowels of boshet, "shame" — and the "Queen of Heaven" of Jeremiah's polemic is often identified with her.

Relation to Aphrodite

The Greeks identified Astarte with Aphrodite; Herodotus traced the cult of Aphrodite Urania at Paphos and Ascalon to Phoenician origins, and the myths of Adonis travelled with her rites from Byblos westward. In the Hellenistic period she merged further with Isis and Atargatis, and her astral symbolism — the eight-pointed star and the dove — passed into later iconography.
