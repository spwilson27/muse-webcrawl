Rent control

Rent control is a system of rent regulation in which a government imposes controls on the rents that landlords may charge tenants. Rent regulation encompasses laws that set maximum rents ("first-generation" rent control, or rent ceilings/floors) as well as systems that limit rent increases within and sometimes between tenancies while allowing initial rents to be set by the market ("second-generation" controls, often called rent stabilisation).

History

Rent controls were widely introduced during and after the First and Second World Wars in Europe and North America, in response to housing shortages and inflation. Many jurisdictions dismantled strict controls in the postwar decades, while cities such as New York retain large regulated sectors. Several U.S. states preempt local rent control, while others — including Oregon (2019) and California (2019) — have adopted statewide caps on rent increases.

Debate

Supporters argue rent regulation protects tenants from displacement and preserves affordable housing and neighbourhood stability. Many economists argue it reduces the quantity and quality of rental housing over time, discouraging construction and maintenance; empirical studies show effects vary with design — modern stabilisation systems with exemptions for new construction show smaller supply effects than strict ceilings.
