Studio Ghibli

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Ghibli Museum in Mitaka, Tokyo: a whimsical cream building with a life-size robot soldier from Laputa on its roof garden]

Studio Ghibli is a Japanese animation studio founded on 15 June 1985 by directors Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata and producer Toshio Suzuki, following the success of Miyazaki's Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind (1984). Headquartered in Koganei, Tokyo, it is the most celebrated animation studio in the world, famed for hand-drawn craftsmanship, ecological and pacifist themes, and heroines of unusual independence.

Its films include Castle in the Sky (1986), My Neighbor Totoro and Grave of the Fireflies (both 1988), Kiki's Delivery Service (1989), Porco Rosso (1992), Princess Mononoke (1997), Spirited Away (2001), Howl's Moving Castle (2004), Ponyo (2008), Takahata's The Tale of the Princess Kaguya (2013) and Miyazaki's The Boy and the Heron (2023, Academy Award for Best Animated Feature).

Founding ethos

Ghibli (named by Miyazaki for an Italian aircraft, itself from the Libyan word for a hot desert wind — pronounced 'jiburi' in Japanese) was founded to let its directors make the films they wanted at the highest craft standard: no merchandising-led production, stories without villains-of-convenience, flight and wind as spiritual weather. Totoro became the studio's logo and Japan's most beloved character; the Ghibli Museum (Mitaka, opened 2001) and Ghibli Park (Aichi, from 2022) extend the world physically.

Succession

Takahata died in 2018; Miyazaki has twice retired and twice returned — The Boy and the Heron's triumph at 82/83 postponed the succession question again. In 2023 Nippon TV acquired a controlling stake, with Ghibli continuing as its subsidiary — the studio's independence traded for permanence, in the founders' judgement.
