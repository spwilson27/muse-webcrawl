Pop rock

Pop rock (also typeset as pop/rock) is a fusion genre that combines elements of pop and rock music, characterised by catchy melodies, accessible song structures, and guitar-based instrumentation performed with a lighter, smoother approach than mainstream rock. Songs are typically built around a verse–chorus form and aimed at a broad commercial audience.

History

The term came into use in the late 1960s and 1970s for rock acts with a strong pop sensibility, including performers such as Elton John, Paul McCartney and Wings, Fleetwood Mac, and the Bee Gees in their early work. In the 1980s and 1990s pop rock encompassed acts from Billy Joel and Huey Lewis and the News to Alanis Morissette and Sheryl Crow, while the 2000s brought bands such as Maroon 5, Kelly Clarkson in her rock-leaning work, and OneRepublic. Critics have sometimes used "pop rock" pejoratively to imply a compromise of rock's edge, while others treat it as a natural and enduring mainstream idiom.

Characteristics

Pop rock generally emphasises professional songwriting, polished studio production, prominent hooks, and themes of romance and everyday life. Related and overlapping styles include soft rock, power pop, new wave, and pop-punk.
