Thăng Long

Thăng Long (Vietnamese: Thăng Long, literally "ascending dragon") was the former name of Hanoi, the capital of Vietnam, used during the Lý, Trần, Lê and Nguyễn dynasties. The name survives today in references to the Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

LocationBa Đình District, Hanoi, Vietnam
Founded1010 (as capital of Đại Việt)

History

In 1010, Emperor Lý Thái Tổ issued the Edict on the Transfer of the Capital, moving the seat of government from Hoa Lư to the citadel of Đại La. According to legend, the king saw a golden dragon rising from the Red River and renamed the city Thăng Long, "ascending dragon."

Imperial Citadel

The central sector of the Imperial Citadel preserves remains from successive dynasties, including foundations of palaces, the Đoan Môn gate, the Flag Tower of Hanoi and the North Gate. Excavations since 2002 have uncovered large quantities of ceramics and architectural remains.

Legacy

The millennium of Thăng Long–Hanoi was celebrated in October 2010. Thăng Long remains a poetic and historical name for the capital, appearing in literature, street names and institutions.
