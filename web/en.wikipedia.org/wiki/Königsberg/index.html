Königsberg

Königsberg (German: [ˈkøːnɪçsbɛʁk]) was the historic Prussian city on the Pregel River that is now Kaliningrad, Russia. Founded in 1255 by the Teutonic Knights during the Northern Crusades and named in honour of King Ottokar II of Bohemia, it became the capital of the Duchy of Prussia and later of East Prussia.

History

The city — originally three towns, Altstadt, Löbenicht and Kneiphof, united in 1724 — was a major Baltic port and member of the Hanseatic League. Albertina University, founded in 1544, made it an intellectual centre: the philosopher Immanuel Kant was born there, spent his whole life there, and is buried at the cathedral. The city's seven bridges posed the famous mathematical problem solved by Leonhard Euler in 1736, founding graph theory.

Destruction and renaming

Königsberg was devastated by British bombing in August 1944 and by the Red Army's siege in April 1945. Awarded to the Soviet Union at Potsdam, it was renamed Kaliningrad in 1946, its surviving German population expelled and replaced by Soviet settlers. The cathedral on Kneiphof island was restored in the 1990s with Kant's tomb.
