The Rabbit, also called the Hare, is the fourth animal in the twelve-year cycle of the Chinese zodiac, associated with the fourth Earthly Branch (卯, mǎo) and the hours from five to seven in the morning. People born in a Year of the Rabbit are traditionally said to be gentle, quiet, elegant, alert, quick, skilful, kind, and patient, though sometimes prone to superficiality and self-indulgence.

Years and elements

The sign repeats every twelve years, combined with the five elements in a sixty-year cycle: recent Rabbit years include the Wood Rabbit (1975), Fire Rabbit (1987), Earth Rabbit (1999), Metal Rabbit (2011), and Water Rabbit (2023–24). The Year of the Rabbit is widely celebrated across East and Southeast Asia and in Chinese communities worldwide; in 2023, for example, it began on 22 January.

The Cat in the Vietnamese zodiac

In the Vietnamese zodiac, the Rabbit is replaced by the Cat — one of only a few differences from the Chinese cycle. Explanations vary: a popular folk tale holds that the cat missed its place in the Jade Emperor's race (in versions of the legend the rat tricked it), while linguistically the archaic Chinese name of the rabbit branch (mǎo) may have been confused with a word for cat as the tradition spread south.

In folklore

In Chinese mythology the Rabbit is intimately linked with the Moon: the Jade Rabbit lives on the Moon with the goddess Chang'e, pounding the elixir of immortality with a mortar and pestle — a figure visible in the lunar markings and honoured at the Mid-Autumn Festival. India's Jataka tales and the derived Japanese and Korean folklore likewise tell of a self-sacrificing rabbit on the Moon.
