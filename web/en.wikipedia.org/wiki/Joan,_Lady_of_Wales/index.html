Joan, Lady of Wales and Princess of North Wales (Welsh: Siwan; c. 1191 – 2 February 1237) was an illegitimate daughter of King John of England and the wife of Llywelyn the Great, Prince of Gwynedd and de facto ruler of much of Wales. She played a significant diplomatic role between the Welsh principality and the English crown.

Life

Joan's mother is not named in contemporary sources; later tradition gives her name as Clemence. Around 1204–1205 Joan was married to Llywelyn ab Iorwerth as part of a settlement between John and the Welsh prince. In 1226 Pope Honorius III declared her legitimate at the request of her husband and father-in-law's petitioners, though the decree expressly barred her from any claim to the English throne.

Joan acted as a trusted intermediary, travelling to the English court in 1228 and helping to negotiate settlements during periods of conflict. In 1230 her reputation was damaged when William de Braose, a Marcher lord held prisoner by Llywelyn, was discovered in her chamber and hanged by Llywelyn; Joan was placed under house arrest for about a year, but was later restored to favour and resumed her diplomatic role.

Death and burial

Joan died at the court of Abergwyngregyn in February 1237 and was buried at Llanfaes on Anglesey, where Llywelyn founded a Franciscan friary in her memory. Her stone coffin survives and is displayed at Beaumaris Castle. Llywelyn retired to the Cistercian abbey of Aberconwy a few years later and died in 1240.
