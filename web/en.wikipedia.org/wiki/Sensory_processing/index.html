Sensory processing

Sensory processing is the way the nervous system receives messages from the senses and turns them into responses. Through the senses—vision, hearing, touch, taste, smell, balance (vestibular) and body awareness (proprioception)—the brain detects, modulates, interprets and organises sensations from the body and the environment to produce adaptive behaviour.

Process

Sensory processing involves reception of a stimulus by sensory receptors, transduction into neural signals, transmission to the central nervous system, and integration with other sensory information, memory and motor planning. Effective integration underlies everyday functions such as maintaining posture, filtering background noise in a classroom, and coordinating hand–eye movements. The framework of "sensory integration" was developed by occupational therapist A. Jean Ayres in the 1960s and 1970s.

Differences and disorders

Individuals differ in sensory thresholds and responsiveness: some are hyper-responsive (over-sensitive) to sound, texture or light, others hypo-responsive and sensation-seeking. Marked difficulties are described under the proposed diagnosis of sensory processing disorder, and atypical sensory responses are a recognised feature of autism spectrum disorder in the DSM-5. Occupational therapy using sensory-integration techniques is a common, though debated, intervention.
