Wrocław (German: Breslau) is a city in southwestern Poland and the capital of the Lower Silesian Voivodeship. Situated on the Oder River, it is one of Poland's largest cities, with a population of roughly 670,000, and serves as a major centre of finance, technology and higher education.

History

A settlement existed at the site by the 10th century; the bishopric of Wrocław was founded in the year 1000. The city belonged successively to the Polish Piast dynasty, the Kingdom of Bohemia, the Habsburg monarchy and, from 1741, Prussia and later the German Empire, under the name Breslau. It became one of the largest German cities and an important industrial and university centre.

At the end of the Second World War, Breslau was besieged and heavily damaged as "Festung Breslau", surrendering on 6 May 1945. Under the Potsdam Agreement the city was transferred to Poland; its German population was expelled westward and replaced largely by Poles, including many displaced from Lwów (now Lviv). Rebuilt Wrocław hosted the 1948 World Congress of Intellectuals and the Exhibition of the Recovered Territories.

Landmarks and culture

The city's sights include the Gothic Old Town Hall on the Market Square, Ostrów Tumski (Cathedral Island) with the twin-spired Cathedral of St John the Baptist, the Centennial Hall (1913, a UNESCO World Heritage Site) designed by Max Berg, and the Panorama of Racławice painting. Wrocław was a European Capital of Culture in 2016 and is noted for its hundreds of small dwarf statues placed around the city since 2005.

Education

The University of Wrocław, founded in 1702 as a Jesuit academy, is one of Poland's largest universities; alumni include nine Nobel laureates associated with the former German university.
