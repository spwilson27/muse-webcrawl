Maggie's (formally the Maggie Keswick Jencks Cancer Caring Trust) is a charity in the United Kingdom that runs a network of drop-in centres, known as Maggie's Centres, which aim to help people with cancer and their families and friends. The centres are renowned for their innovative architecture and are built in the grounds of NHS cancer hospitals.

Origin

The concept came from Maggie Keswick Jencks, a writer, artist, and garden designer, who conceived the idea while being treated for cancer. Together with her husband, the architectural historian Charles Jencks, she developed a blueprint for non-clinical, domestic-feeling spaces where patients could obtain information, psychological support, and a calm environment. The first centre opened in Edinburgh in 1996, in converted stable buildings designed by Richard Murphy.

Architecture

Maggie's commissions prominent architects for each new building; centres have been designed by Frank Gehry (Dundee), Zaha Hadid (Kirkcaldy), Rem Koolhaas (Glasgow), Norman Foster (Manchester), and Snøhetta (Aberdeen), among others. The buildings typically centre on a kitchen table as a social heart and avoid corridors and signage that would evoke a hospital.

Services

Centres offer free practical, emotional, and social support — benefits advice, psychological therapy, nutrition and exercise classes, and support groups — delivered by cancer support specialists, and they complement rather than replace NHS medical treatment.
