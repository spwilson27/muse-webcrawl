First United Front

Formed1923–1924
Dissolved1927
PartiesKuomintang; Chinese Communist Party
PurposeEnd warlordism and unify China

The First United Front was an alliance between the Kuomintang (KMT) and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) formed in 1923–1924 to end warlordism and unify China. Under the arrangement, negotiated with the encouragement of the Soviet Union and the Comintern, CCP members joined the KMT as individuals while retaining their party membership.

Formation

The alliance was formalized after meetings between Sun Yat-sen and Soviet envoy Adolph Joffe, which produced the Sun–Joffe Manifesto of January 1923. Soviet advisers, notably Mikhail Borodin, helped reorganize the KMT along Leninist lines, and Soviet support underwrote the founding of the Whampoa Military Academy, whose first commandant was Chiang Kai-shek.

Collapse

After Sun's death in 1925, tensions between the KMT right wing and the Communists grew. In April 1927, during the Northern Expedition, Chiang ordered a purge of Communists in Shanghai, and the alliance collapsed, beginning the Chinese Civil War. A Second United Front was formed a decade later to resist Japanese invasion.
