Bachelor of Arts

A Bachelor of Arts (BA or AB, from the Latin baccalaureus artium) is an undergraduate academic degree awarded for completing a programme in the liberal arts, humanities or social sciences, and in some systems also in natural sciences and mathematics. It is one of the oldest and most common first degrees worldwide, typically requiring three to four years of full-time study; the science counterpart is the Bachelor of Science (BS/BSc).

History

The degree descends from the medieval university curriculum of the seven liberal arts — the trivium (grammar, rhetoric, logic) and quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy). At Oxford and Cambridge the BA remains the standard first degree in nearly all subjects, and graduates may later proceed to the MA by seniority without further study. In Scotland the ancient universities award the MA as a first degree in the humanities, while the BA is used elsewhere in the UK.

By country

In the United States the BA is a four-year degree usually combining a major with general-education requirements; the distinction from the BS often rests on foreign-language or breadth requirements rather than subject. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland it is normally three years, with honours classification (first, upper second, lower second, third). In the European Higher Education Area, the Bologna Process standardised the BA as a three-year (180 ECTS) first-cycle degree.
