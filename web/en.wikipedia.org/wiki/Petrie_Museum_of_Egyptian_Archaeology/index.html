The Petrie Museum of Egyptian Archaeology in London is part of University College London (UCL). It houses around 80,000 objects, one of the world's largest collections of Egyptian and Nubian/Sudanese material, and is particularly renowned for its everyday objects, textiles, and papyri rather than monumental art.

History

The museum was established in 1892 alongside UCL's Edwards Professorship of Egyptian Archaeology, endowed by the writer Amelia Edwards, who bequeathed her collection of some 900 objects. It is named for Flinders Petrie, the first Edwards Professor, whose excavations across Egypt over four decades brought tens of thousands of finds to UCL under the excavation divisions of the time. The collection has been housed in Malet Place since the 1950s, in a former stables building.

Collection highlights

Notable holdings include one of the earliest pieces of Egyptian linen (c. 5000 BC) from Tarkhan, the Tarkhan dress — among the oldest surviving woven garments in the world — the oldest known wills written on papyrus, a unique bead-net dress, fragments of the Kahun medical and veterinary papyri, objects from Amarna and Lahun, and material from Petrie's excavations in Palestine and Sudan. The museum has published much of its collection online and is known for its deliberately dense, study-oriented displays and its "Visible Storage" character.
