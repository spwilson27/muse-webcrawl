Windsor Castle is a royal residence at Windsor in the English county of Berkshire, notable for its long association with the English and later British monarchy and for its architecture. The original castle was built in the 11th century after the Norman Conquest, and it has been used by the reigning monarch since the reign of Henry I. It is the largest castle in the world still in regular royal use and the longest-occupied palace in Europe.

History

William the Conqueror chose the site, a day's march from London and on the River Thames, around 1070. Successive monarchs rebuilt it in stone: Edward III created the great St George's Chapel (completed under Edward IV and Henry VII), home of the Order of the Garter. The castle survived sieges in the First Barons' War, served as a prison and court during the Civil War, and was lavishly remodelled by George IV. Queen Victoria made it the centre of court life; Prince Albert died there in 1861.

Fire and restoration

On 20 November 1992 a major fire, begun by a spotlight igniting a curtain in the Queen's Private Chapel, destroyed or damaged more than 100 rooms, including St George's Hall. A five-year restoration, completed in 1997, used traditional crafts and cost about £36.5 million, funded partly by opening Buckingham Palace to the public.

Today

Windsor was a principal residence of Elizabeth II, who died in 2022 and is buried in the King George VI Memorial Chapel within St George's Chapel. The castle remains in regular use for state ceremonies and is open to the public much of the year.
