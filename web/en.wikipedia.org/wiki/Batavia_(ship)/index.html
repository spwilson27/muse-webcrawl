Batavia (ship)

Batavia was a Dutch East India Company (VOC) ship built in Amsterdam in 1628, the flagship of a fleet bound for the Dutch East Indies. On her maiden voyage she was wrecked on 4 June 1629 on Morning Reef near Beacon Island in the Abrolhos Islands off the coast of Western Australia. The wreck was followed by one of the most notorious episodes in maritime history: a mutiny and massacre among the survivors led by Jeronimus Cornelisz, in which more than 100 men, women and children were murdered.

Voyage and wreck

Batavia sailed from Texel on 29 October 1628 under the command of Francisco Pelsaert, with Ariaen Jacobsz as skipper and the under-merchant Jeronimus Cornelisz second in authority. She carried some 340 people and a fortune in trade goods and cash, including silver coins and jewels intended for the Mughal court. After separating from the fleet, the ship struck the reef in the early hours of 4 June 1629. Most passengers and crew reached the nearby islands, but fresh water was scarce.

Mutiny and massacre

Pelsaert and Jacobsz sailed the ship's longboat to Batavia (modern Jakarta) to seek rescue, reaching it after a remarkable open-boat voyage. Left in charge, Cornelisz established a reign of terror on the islands, systematically murdering survivors — eventually more than 100 people — to reduce competition for supplies and to consolidate his planned mutiny. A group of soldiers under Wiebbe Hayes, exiled to a nearby island, found water and food, resisted Cornelisz's attacks, and captured him shortly before Pelsaert returned in September 1629 aboard the Sardam. Cornelisz and several accomplices were tried and hanged on Seal Island; two mutineers were marooned on the Australian mainland, the first recorded European residents of Australia.

Legacy

The wreck site was located in 1963, and artefacts — including the ship's stern transom, cannons and a reconstructed portico of stone blocks intended for Batavia — are displayed at the Western Australian Museum in Fremantle and Geraldton. A full-scale sailing replica was built at Lelystad in the Netherlands between 1985 and 1995 and is now a museum ship.
