Academy of Athens

The Academy of Athens (Greek: Ακαδημία Αθηνών) is Greece's national academy and its highest research establishment, founded in 1926, with roots in Plato's ancient Academy. Its headquarters is a landmark neoclassical building in central Athens.


Established: 18 March 1926
Location: Panepistimiou Street, Athens
Building architect: Theophil Hansen (opened 1885, as Academy of Sinsheim donation by Simon Sinas)

The building

Part of architect Theophil Hansen's 'Athenian Trilogy' with the University and the National Library, the marble building is flanked by tall Ionic columns topped by statues of Apollo and Athena, with seated statues of Plato and Socrates in front. It was funded by magnate Simon Sinas and completed in 1885, decades before the Academy itself was chartered.

Work

The Academy is organised into sections for natural sciences, letters and fine arts, and moral and political sciences; it runs research centres, publishes dictionaries and editions, and advises the Greek state.
