Elementary embedding

Elementary embedding
Field: model theory, set theory
Notation: j : M → N
Key concept: critical point
Related: large cardinal axioms

In model theory and set theory, an elementary embedding is a map between structures that preserves the truth of all first-order statements: for every formula φ and parameters from the domain, φ holds in the source structure exactly when it holds of their images in the target structure.

In model theory

A structure A is an elementary substructure of B if the inclusion map is an elementary embedding. The downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem guarantees small elementary substructures of any structure in a countable language, and diagrams of such maps are central to the study of saturated and homogeneous models.

In set theory

Non-trivial elementary embeddings j : V → M from the universe of sets into a transitive inner model are the defining tool of large cardinal theory. The least ordinal moved by such an embedding is its critical point, which must be a measurable cardinal; stronger embeddings characterise huge, supercompact, and extendible cardinals. The Kunen inconsistency theorem shows no non-trivial elementary embedding of V into itself can exist in ZFC.
