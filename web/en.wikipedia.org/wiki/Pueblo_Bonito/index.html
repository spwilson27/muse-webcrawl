Pueblo Bonito

Pueblo Bonito (Spanish for "beautiful town") is the largest and best-known great house of Chaco Canyon, in present-day north-western New Mexico, United States. Built and occupied by Ancestral Puebloans between about AD 828 and 1126, the D-shaped sandstone complex rose four to five storeys, contained roughly 650–800 rooms, and enclosed about 3 acres (1.2 ha), with more than thirty circular kivas including two great kivas.

Construction and layout

The building grew in stages over three centuries, its curved back wall aligned to the cardinal directions and its geometry incorporating solar and lunar alignments shared with other Chacoan sites. Timber for its approximately 50,000 roof beams was hauled from mountain forests 50–75 km away, as no suitable trees grow in the canyon. Roads radiating from Chaco — some 9 m wide and running dead straight for tens of kilometres — linked Pueblo Bonito to outlying great houses across the San Juan Basin.

Excavation and finds

The Hyde Exploring Expedition, directed by George Pepper, excavated 190 rooms in 1896–1900, recovering turquoise by the tens of thousands of pieces, jet, shell ornaments, macaw feathers and cylindrical ceramic vessels with residues of cacao — evidence of trade reaching Mesoamerica. Room 33 held an elite burial crypt of fourteen individuals; ancient DNA analysis published in 2017 showed a matrilineal dynasty spanning the building's occupation. In 1941 a rockfall from the cliff's "Threatening Rock" destroyed part of the rear wall.

Interpretation

Pueblo Bonito is thought to have housed a small resident population but served as a ceremonial, political and storage centre for the Chacoan regional system, which collapsed amid drought in the twelfth century. It lies within Chaco Culture National Historical Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1987.
