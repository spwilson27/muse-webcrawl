La Selva Biological Station is a protected area and research station in the Caribbean lowlands of Costa Rica, near the town of Puerto Viejo de Sarapiquí. Run by the Organization for Tropical Studies (OTS), it encompasses 1,536 hectares (3,800 acres) of tropical wet forest, most of it primary forest, and is one of the most productive field stations in the world for research in tropical ecology.

History

The land was purchased in 1953 by tropical forester Leslie Holdridge, who farmed part of it as a mixed plantation experiment called "La Selva." In 1968 the property was sold to the OTS, which developed it into a research station and reserve. La Selva is connected by a biological corridor to Braulio Carrillo National Park, giving forest organisms access to a continuous gradient from lowland rainforest to montane forest.

Biodiversity

The station's flora includes more than 2,000 plant species, among them some 400 tree species. Its recorded fauna includes over 500 bird species, about 125 mammal species (including more than 60 bats), roughly 120 reptiles and amphibians, and tens of thousands of insect species. Annual rainfall averages about 4 metres (13 feet).

Research

La Selva hosts hundreds of visiting scientists and students each year and has generated thousands of scientific publications. Long-running projects include studies of forest carbon dynamics, tree growth and mortality plots, leaf-cutter ant ecology, and migratory birds. The station has also documented worrying declines in some amphibian and reptile populations, making it an important sentinel site for tropical ecological change.
