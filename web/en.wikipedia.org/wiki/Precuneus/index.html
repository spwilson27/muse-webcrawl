Precuneus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Precuneus
Part ofParietal lobe
ArteryParietal branches of the middle cerebral artery; posterior cerebral artery

The precuneus is the portion of the superior parietal lobule on the medial surface of each cerebral hemisphere. It is bounded in front by the marginal branch of the cingulate sulcus, behind by the parieto-occipital sulcus, and below by the subparietal sulcus. It corresponds largely to Brodmann area 7 on the medial surface.

Function

Functional imaging studies implicate the precuneus in visuospatial imagery, episodic memory retrieval, and self-referential processing — the mental representation of the self. Together with the posterior cingulate cortex it forms a central hub of the default mode network, showing high activity during rest and deactivation during externally directed tasks. It has one of the highest resting metabolic rates in the brain.

Clinical significance

Reduced glucose metabolism in the precuneus and posterior cingulate is among the earliest functional changes detected in Alzheimer's disease. Lesions of the region can disturb spatial orientation and the sense of agency over one's own actions.
