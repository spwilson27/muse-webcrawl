Truck classification

Trucks range from light pickups to heavy tractor-trailers

Truck classification is the grouping of trucks into categories for regulatory, commercial and licensing purposes. Systems differ between countries; the best-known is that of the United States, which classifies trucks by gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) into eight classes.

In the US, a commercial driver's licence is generally required for vehicles over 26,000 lb GVWR. Trucks are also informally described as light-duty, medium-duty and heavy-duty, and by body type (pickup, box truck, flatbed, tanker and so on).

Europe and elsewhere

The European Union classifies goods vehicles by maximum authorised mass in driving-licence categories (for example category B up to 3.5 tonnes, C1 up to 7.5 tonnes, and C above that) and by vehicle category designations such as N1, N2 and N3. Other countries use broadly similar weight-based schemes.
