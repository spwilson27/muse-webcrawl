Violin Concerto (Elgar)

ComposerEdward Elgar
KeyB minor
OpusOp. 61
Composed1909–1910
Premiere10 November 1910, London
SoloistFritz Kreisler

The Violin Concerto in B minor, Op. 61, by Edward Elgar is one of the composer's longest orchestral works and one of the most demanding concertos in the violin repertoire, lasting around 50 minutes. It was composed in 1909–1910 and dedicated to the Austrian violinist Fritz Kreisler, who gave the premiere in London on 10 November 1910 with the composer conducting the London Symphony Orchestra.

Elgar's concerto is among the longest and most lyrical in the repertoire

Background

Elgar, himself a violinist, inscribed the score with a Spanish epigraph from Gil Blas: "Aquí está encerrada el alma de ..... " ("Herein is enshrined the soul of ....."). The five dots have prompted speculation about whose soul is enshrined — commonly identified with Alice Stuart-Wortley, a close friend of the composer, whom he called "Windflower". The concerto is written on a grand scale in three movements: Allegro, Andante, and Allegro molto, the finale containing an unusually long accompanied cadenza in which the soloist reflects on themes from earlier movements, with the strings marked to be played with a "thrummed" pizzicato effect.

Reception

The premiere was a triumph, and the concerto quickly entered the repertoire. Kreisler compared Elgar in stature to Beethoven and Brahms. Notable recordings include the 1932 version by the 16-year-old Yehudi Menuhin conducted by Elgar himself, a landmark of the gramophone era.
