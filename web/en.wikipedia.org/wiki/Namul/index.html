Namul (Korean: 나물) is a general term in Korean cuisine for dishes made of seasoned vegetables, grasses, or leaves. Namul are among the most common banchan (side dishes) served with rice, and they form the vegetable components of mixed dishes such as bibimbap.

Preparation

Depending on the ingredient, vegetables may be blanched, steamed, stir-fried, or used raw, then seasoned with some combination of sesame oil, soy sauce (or fish sauce), minced garlic, sesame seeds, scallions, vinegar, and chilli. The term originally referred to wild greens gathered from fields and mountains; the gathering and eating of such greens, especially in spring, has deep roots in Korean food culture.

Common varieties

Well-known namul include sigeumchi-namul (seasoned spinach), kongnamul (soybean sprouts), sukju-namul (mung bean sprouts), gosari-namul (bracken fern), doraji-namul (balloon flower root), chwinamul (from the aster species Aster scaber), and gaji-namul (steamed eggplant). For the Daeboreum festival, families traditionally eat boreum-namul made from dried vegetables stored over the winter, together with five-grain rice (ogokbap), in the belief that eating them helps one withstand the summer heat.
