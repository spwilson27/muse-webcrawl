Kahlúa

Kahlúa
Type: Coffee liqueur
Origin: Veracruz, Mexico
Introduced: 1936
Alcohol by volume: 20%

Kahlúa is a coffee liqueur produced in Veracruz, Mexico. Made from rum, sugar, vanilla and arabica coffee beans grown in the mountains of Veracruz, it is one of the best-known coffee liqueurs in the world and a key ingredient in cocktails such as the White Russian, Black Russian, Espresso Martini and B-52.

History

The liqueur was created in 1936 by Pedro Domecq. Its name derives from an Arabic slang word for coffee. The brand passed through several owners and is now produced by Pernod Ricard. Production from bean to bottle takes several years, most of it spent growing and maturing the coffee cherries.

Varieties and use

Besides the original, flavoured variants such as vanilla, hazelnut and salted caramel have been marketed. Kahlúa is served neat, on ice, in coffee and in a wide range of mixed drinks, and is used in desserts such as tiramisu-style preparations.
