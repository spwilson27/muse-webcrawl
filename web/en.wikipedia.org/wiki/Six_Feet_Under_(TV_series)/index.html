Six Feet Under is an American drama television series created by Alan Ball for HBO. It premiered on June 3, 2001, and concluded on August 21, 2005, after five seasons comprising 63 episodes. The series depicts the lives of the Fisher family, who run a funeral home in Los Angeles, along with their friends and lovers. Its ensemble cast included Peter Krause, Michael C. Hall, Frances Conroy, Lauren Ambrose, Freddy Rodriguez, Mathew St. Patrick, and Rachel Griffiths.

Premise

The pilot opens with the death of funeral director Nathaniel Fisher (Richard Jenkins) on Christmas Eve 2000, which brings his prodigal elder son Nate (Krause) home from Seattle. Nate and his younger brother David (Hall), a closeted gay man, inherit the business, Fisher & Sons, while their widowed mother Ruth (Conroy) and teenage sister Claire (Ambrose) navigate grief and reinvention. Nearly every episode begins with a death — often of a stranger whose funeral the family then arranges — which sets the thematic terms for the episode, and features conversations between the living characters and imagined versions of the dead.

Reception and legacy

The series was widely acclaimed for its writing, acting, and unflinching treatment of mortality, sexuality, and family dysfunction. It won numerous awards, including three Golden Globes and nine Emmy Awards from 44 nominations; Frances Conroy won a Golden Globe and the cast won Screen Actors Guild ensemble awards. The series finale, "Everyone's Waiting" — closing with a montage, set to Sia's "Breathe Me", that follows each main character to their death — is frequently cited as one of the greatest television finales ever made. Six Feet Under is regularly ranked among the finest TV dramas of all time and is considered a landmark of HBO's prestige-television era.
