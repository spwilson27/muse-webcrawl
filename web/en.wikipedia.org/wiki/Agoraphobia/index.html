Agoraphobia

SpecialtyPsychiatry, clinical psychology

Agoraphobia is an anxiety disorder characterised by marked fear or anxiety about situations — such as public transport, open spaces, enclosed places, crowds, or being outside the home alone — where escape might be difficult or help unavailable in the event of a panic attack. The word derives from the Greek agora (marketplace).

Signs and symptoms

People with agoraphobia often avoid feared situations, sometimes to the point of becoming largely housebound. Anticipatory anxiety, panic symptoms (palpitations, breathlessness, dizziness), and safety behaviours such as travelling only with a companion are common.

Crowded public places may provoke severe anxiety

Causes

The disorder is thought to arise from an interaction of genetic vulnerability, temperament, stress and learning: an initial panic attack in a particular setting can condition avoidance of similar settings, which then generalises.

Diagnosis

The DSM-5 requires persistent fear or avoidance of two or more agoraphobic situations for at least six months, causing clinically significant distress or impairment, and not better explained by another condition.

Treatment

Cognitive behavioural therapy, particularly graduated exposure combined with panic-management techniques, is the treatment of choice. SSRIs and other antidepressants are also effective for many patients.

Epidemiology

Lifetime prevalence is roughly 1–2 % of adults in Western surveys; onset is typically in late adolescence or early adulthood, and the disorder is diagnosed about twice as often in women as in men.
