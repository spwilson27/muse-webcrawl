Nicomedes (mathematician)

Bornc. 280 BC
Diedc. 210 BC
Known forConchoid of Nicomedes; angle trisection

Nicomedes (c. 280 – c. 210 BC) was an ancient Greek mathematician best known for discovering the curve called the conchoid of Nicomedes, which he used to solve the classical problems of trisecting an angle and doubling the cube.

The conchoid

The conchoid is generated by fixing a point (the pole) and a line (the directrix): from the pole, lines are drawn through the directrix, and points at a constant distance from the intersection are marked, tracing the curve. Nicomedes devised a mechanical instrument to draw it and used the curve's intersection properties to perform constructions impossible with straightedge and compass alone.

Life

Little is known of his life; he lived after Eratosthenes, whom he criticised, and before or contemporary with Apollonius. His treatise On Conchoid Lines is lost and known only through later authors such as Pappus.
