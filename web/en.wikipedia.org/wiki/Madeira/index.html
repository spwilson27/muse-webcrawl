Madeira

Madeira is a Portuguese archipelago in the North Atlantic Ocean, about 900 kilometres southwest of mainland Portugal and 600 kilometres west of Morocco, comprising the main island of Madeira, Porto Santo and the uninhabited Desertas and Savage Islands. It is one of Portugal's two autonomous regions, with a population of about 250,000 and its capital at Funchal.

Discovered (or rediscovered) by Portuguese sailors in 1419, Madeira was settled and planted with sugarcane and vines — Madeira wine became world-famous, toasted at the signing of the US Declaration of Independence. The island rises steeply to 1,862 metres (Pico Ruivo), is laced with levada irrigation channels used as hiking paths, and preserves the laurisilva laurel forest (UNESCO). Tourism and the Zona Franca free zone drive the economy; footballer Cristiano Ronaldo, born in Funchal, is its most famous son.
