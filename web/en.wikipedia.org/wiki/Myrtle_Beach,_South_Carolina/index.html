Myrtle Beach is a coastal city and vacation resort in Horry County, South Carolina, United States. It is the largest city in the Grand Strand, a 60-mile arc of beaches on the Atlantic coast, and one of the major centres of tourism in the United States, attracting millions of visitors each year.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Waccamaw people. The modern resort grew in the early 20th century after the Burroughs and Collins Company built a railroad to the coast and developed the beachfront; in 1900 it won a naming contest with the name "Myrtle Beach", after the abundant southern wax myrtle shrubs. A building boom followed the Second World War, and the city became known for golf, beach tourism, and live entertainment theatres.

Tourism and economy

Tourism dominates the economy. Attractions include the Myrtle Beach Boardwalk and Promenade, the SkyWheel observation wheel, Broadway at the Beach, dozens of miniature golf courses, and more than 90 championship golf courses in the surrounding area, earning it the nickname "Golf Capital of the World". The climate is humid subtropical, with hot summers and mild winters, and the area is occasionally affected by Atlantic hurricanes.
