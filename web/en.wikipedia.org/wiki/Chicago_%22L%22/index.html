The Chicago "L" (short for "elevated") is the rapid transit system serving the city of Chicago and some of its surrounding suburbs in the U.S. state of Illinois. Operated by the Chicago Transit Authority (CTA), it is the fourth-largest rapid transit system in the United States, with a total route length of over 100 miles and 145 stations.

History

The first elevated line, the South Side Elevated Railroad, opened in 1892 and ran from downtown Chicago south to Jackson Park, in time for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition. The famous Loop, a rectangle of elevated track encircling the downtown business district, was completed in 1897 and gives downtown Chicago its name, "the Loop."

Lines

The system has eight lines, designated by colors: Red, Blue, Brown, Green, Orange, Pink, Purple, and Yellow. The Red and Blue lines operate 24 hours a day, making Chicago one of the few U.S. cities with round-the-clock rapid transit service.

Rolling stock

The CTA operates a fleet of railcars of several generations, including the 2600-series, 3200-series, 5000-series and the newer 7000-series cars.
