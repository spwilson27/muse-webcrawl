Alum Bay

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The coloured sand cliffs of Alum Bay, Isle of Wight

Alum Bay is a bay on the western coast of the Isle of Wight, England, near the Needles headland. It is famous for its multicoloured sand cliffs, whose vertically tilted strata display bands of white, red, brown, yellow and grey sands and clays.

Geology and tourism

The bay exposes Eocene sediments of the Bracklesham Group and Barton beds, folded nearly vertical during the Alpine orogeny. Since the Victorian era the coloured sands have been collected and sold in glass bottles and ornaments as souvenirs. A chairlift carries visitors between the beach and the cliff-top attractions near the Needles, and pleasure boats run from the pier to the Needles rocks and lighthouse.
