Nilometer

A nilometer was a structure used to measure the clarity and water level of the Nile River during the annual flood season in ancient and medieval Egypt. Because the height of the inundation determined crop yields, nilometer readings were used to predict harvests, set tax levels and administer grain storage.

Nilometer

Purpose: Measuring the Nile flood level

Notable examples: Elephantine Island; Rhoda Island, Cairo

Period of use: Pharaonic era to 19th century

Design

Nilometers took several forms: vertical columns marked with graduations, staircases descending to the river from which marks on the walls could be read, or deep wells connected to the Nile by culverts with a central measuring pillar. Readings were kept by priests and officials, and in antiquity were treated as closely guarded state information.

History

Nilometers are attested from the Pharaonic period, with famous examples on Elephantine Island at Aswan. The best-preserved example is the nilometer on Rhoda Island in Cairo, whose present structure dates to 861 CE under the Abbasid caliph al-Mutawakkil; its conical dome was added in the 19th century. Measurements from such devices were recorded for centuries and provide one of the longest hydrological records of the pre-modern world, falling out of use only with the construction of the Aswan dams.
