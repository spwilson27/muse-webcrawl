Cohort (statistics)

In statistics, demography, and epidemiology, a cohort is a group of subjects who have a defining characteristic in common, usually that they experienced a common event in a selected time period, such as birth or graduation. Cohorts are used in cohort studies, in which the group is followed over time to observe outcomes such as the development of disease.

Types of cohort

A birth cohort consists of people born during a specified period, such as the British 1946, 1958, and 1970 birth cohort studies that have tracked participants' health and social circumstances for decades. An exposure cohort groups people by a shared exposure—an occupation, a treatment, or an environmental agent—while an inception cohort is assembled at an early, uniform point in the course of a disease.

Cohort studies

In a cohort study, researchers classify subjects by exposure status and follow them to measure the incidence of outcomes, yielding measures such as relative risk. Prospective cohorts are enrolled before outcomes occur; retrospective (historical) cohorts reconstruct exposure and outcomes from existing records. Cohort designs can establish the temporal sequence of exposure and outcome—an advantage over case-control studies—but they are expensive, slow, and inefficient for rare diseases.

Cohort effects

Demographers distinguish cohort effects—enduring influences of the era in which a group came of age—from age effects and period effects. Identifying which of the three explains a trend is known as the age-period-cohort problem, complicated by the exact linear dependence among the variables. In marketing and product analytics, "cohort analysis" groups users by sign-up period to compare retention and behaviour over time.

Etymology

The term derives from the Latin cohors, a unit of a Roman legion (roughly a tenth of a legion, about 480 men), passing into statistical usage in the early 20th century.
