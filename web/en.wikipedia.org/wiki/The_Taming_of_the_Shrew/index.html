The Taming of the Shrew

The Taming of the Shrew is a comedy by William Shakespeare, believed to have been written between 1590 and 1592. It is one of Shakespeare's most popular and most controversial plays, famous for the battle of wills between the spirited Katherina and her suitor Petruchio.

Plot

In a framing "Induction," a drunken tinker, Christopher Sly, is tricked into believing he is a lord and watches the play performed for him. In Padua, the wealthy Baptista Minola will not let his younger daughter, the sweet Bianca, marry until his elder daughter, the sharp-tongued Katherina — the "shrew" — has a husband. Petruchio, a fortune-hunting gentleman from Verona, agrees to woo Katherina. After a stormy courtship and a chaotic wedding, he carries her to his country house and undertakes to "tame" her through deprivation of food and sleep and systematic contradiction, mirroring and outdoing her temper. Meanwhile, Bianca's suitors Lucentio and Hortensio plot in disguise to win her. In the final scene, at a banquet, Katherina — now apparently transformed — delivers a speech on wifely obedience and wins Petruchio a wager against the other husbands, whose seemingly pliant wives refuse to come when called.

Themes and criticism

The play's depiction of marriage, gender roles and Petruchio's treatment of Katherina has generated centuries of debate. Modern productions variously play the ending straight, ironically, or as a shared private joke between the couple. Some critics link the play to the anonymous contemporary play The Taming of a Shrew, whose relationship to Shakespeare's text remains disputed.

Adaptations

Adaptations include Cole Porter's musical Kiss Me, Kate (1948), Franco Zeffirelli's 1967 film starring Elizabeth Taylor and Richard Burton, and the 1999 teen comedy 10 Things I Hate About You.
