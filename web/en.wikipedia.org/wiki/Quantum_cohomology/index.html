Quantum cohomology

Quantum cohomology is a generalization of ordinary cohomology theory in mathematics, originally arising from the study of symplectic geometry and string theory. It deforms the classical cup product on the cohomology ring of a symplectic manifold by incorporating counts of pseudoholomorphic curves, encoded in Gromov–Witten invariants.

Quantum cohomology blends classical intersection theory with counts of rational curves in a symplectic manifold.

Definition

Given a closed symplectic manifold X, its (small) quantum cohomology ring QH*(X) is, as a vector space, the ordinary cohomology H*(X) tensored with a Novikov ring of formal power series. The product of two classes is defined using three-point Gromov–Witten invariants, which count pseudoholomorphic maps from the Riemann sphere into X passing through cycles representing the classes. When the curve class is zero, the product reduces to the classical cup product.

History

The subject grew out of Edward Witten's work on topological sigma models in the early 1990s and Maxim Kontsevich's enumerative predictions. Yuri Manin, Kontsevich, and others developed the algebraic foundations, and the Frobenius manifold structure on quantum cohomology was axiomatized by Dubrovin, Manin, and others. The theory became a central tool in symplectic topology and mirror symmetry.

Examples

For complex projective space, the quantum cohomology ring is a simple quotient of a polynomial ring in one variable tensored with the Novikov ring; its relations encode the numbers of rational curves of each degree passing through given linear spaces. For Grassmannians, quantum cohomology is related to quantum Schubert calculus and to integrable systems such as the Toda lattice.
