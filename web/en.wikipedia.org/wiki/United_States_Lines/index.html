United States Lines

United States Lines was a transatlantic shipping company that operated cargo services from 1921 to 1989 and passenger liner services from 1921 to 1969. Created by the US Shipping Board to operate government-owned liners on the North Atlantic, it became the principal American-flag passenger line, famous for the record-breaking superliner SS United States, which captured the Blue Riband for the fastest Atlantic crossing on her maiden voyage in 1952 and still holds the record.

Early years

The company began operations on 27 August 1921 with former German liners seized during the First World War, including the huge Leviathan (the former Vaterland), which became its flagship on the New York–Southampton–Cherbourg route. In 1929 the line was sold to private interests; despite Depression-era difficulties it commissioned the Manhattan and Washington in 1932–33, the largest American-built liners of their time, and the America in 1940.

SS United States and decline

Launched in 1951 and built with heavy government subsidy as a convertible troopship, the 53,000-ton United States crossed the Atlantic in 3 days, 10 hours and 40 minutes in July 1952. Together with America she maintained the line's prestige service until jet airliners destroyed the transatlantic liner trade in the 1960s; passenger service ended in 1969 when United States was withdrawn.

Container era and bankruptcy

United States Lines pioneered container shipping under owner Malcom McLean, who took control in 1978. It built a fleet of large "Econship" container vessels for round-the-world service, but overexpansion and falling rates led to one of the largest bankruptcies in US shipping history in November 1986. Remaining services were sold or wound down, and the brand briefly reappeared under new ownership in 2000–01 for a short-lived Hawaii cruise venture.
