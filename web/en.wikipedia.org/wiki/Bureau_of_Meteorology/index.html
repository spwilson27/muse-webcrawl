Bureau of Meteorology

The Bureau of Meteorology (BOM, known as "the Bureau") is an executive agency of the Australian Government responsible for providing weather, climate, water, and space weather services to Australia and surrounding regions. It is one of the country's most-used public services, issuing forecasts and severe-weather warnings nationwide.

History

Before Federation, each Australian colony ran its own meteorological service, with notable colonial astronomer-meteorologists such as Charles Todd in South Australia and Henry Chamberlain Russell in New South Wales. The Meteorology Act 1906 centralized weather services, and the Bureau began operations in 1908 under its first director, Henry Hunt.

Services

The Bureau operates weather radar and satellite receiving networks, automatic weather stations, and numerical weather prediction models run on supercomputers in Melbourne. It issues public forecasts, tropical cyclone and flood warnings, tsunami advisories (through the Joint Australian Tsunami Warning Centre), aviation weather services, climate records and outlooks, and water information including streamflow data.

Role in emergencies

Bureau warnings are central to Australia's response to bushfires, floods, heatwaves, and severe thunderstorms. Its role has been scrutinized after events such as the 2019–20 Black Summer bushfires and the 2022 eastern Australian floods, prompting investment in forecasting technology and communications.
