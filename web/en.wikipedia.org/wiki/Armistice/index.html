Armistice

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An armistice is a formal agreement of warring parties to stop fighting. Not necessarily the end of a war, it may constitute a total cessation of hostilities while an attempt is made to negotiate a lasting peace. The word derives from Latin arma (arms) and -stitium (a stopping).

Examples

The archetype is the Armistice of 11 November 1918 — signed in a railway carriage at Compiègne in the early morning, effective at 11 a.m. ("the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month") — ending the fighting of the First World War months before the Versailles Treaty. The Italian (1943), Japanese-instrument (1945) and Korean (1953) cases span armistice shading into surrender and into permanent limbo: the Korean Armistice Agreement, signed by military commanders (not governments), demilitarised a divided peninsula and left the two Koreas technically at war — supervised for decades by the Neutral Nations Supervisory Commission and the Military Armistice Commission. Under modern international humanitarian law the armistice/ceasefire vocabulary is less legally rigid than in Hague-era treatises; the parties' terms govern.
