Ferdinand Alexander Porsche (11 December 1935 – 5 April 2012), nicknamed "Butzi" Porsche, was a German automobile designer best known as the creator of the original Porsche 911. The grandson of company founder Ferdinand Porsche and the son of Ferry Porsche, he shaped the design language of the sports-car maker for decades and later founded the industrial design firm Porsche Design.

Early life

Born in Stuttgart, Porsche spent much of his childhood in the workshops of the family engineering office. After being expelled from the Ulm School of Design (Hochschule für Gestaltung Ulm) after his first year — to his later amusement, given his subsequent fame — he joined the family firm's body design department in 1957, working under design chief Erwin Komenda.

The Porsche 911

In 1962 Porsche took over the design studio. His most famous task was the body of the successor to the Porsche 356, developed as the Type 901 and presented at the Frankfurt Motor Show in 1963 before being renamed the 911 after a trademark objection from Peugeot. His design combined a fastback silhouette, integrated bumpers, and a smooth, unornamented surface that broke with the separate-fender styling of earlier cars. The 911's basic form has been retained through every subsequent generation and is regarded as one of the most enduring designs in automotive history. He also styled the racing Porsche 904 Carrera GTS, which he considered among his most beautiful works.

Porsche Design

When the Porsche company was converted into a public limited company in 1972 and family members withdrew from management, Ferdinand Alexander left and founded the Porsche Design Studio in Stuttgart (later based in Zell am See, Austria). The studio applied his functionalist credo — "If you analyse the function of an object, its form often becomes obvious" — to products including the Chronograph I (1972), the first wristwatch with a black-coated case, titanium watches for IWC, sunglasses, pens, luggage, and consumer electronics. Porsche Design products are characterized by matte black finishes, technical materials, and restrained forms.

Death and legacy

Porsche died in Salzburg on 5 April 2012, aged 76, having been named honorary chairman of Porsche AG's supervisory board in 2005. He is remembered, in the words of the company's obituary, as the man who "created a design philosophy that will define our vehicles well into the future."
