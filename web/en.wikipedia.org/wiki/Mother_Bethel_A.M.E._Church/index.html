The Mother Bethel African Methodist Episcopal Church is a historic congregation and church building in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Founded in 1794 by Richard Allen, it is the mother church of the African Methodist Episcopal (AME) denomination, the first independent Black denomination in the United States, and the oldest church property in the United States continuously owned by African Americans.

History

Allen, a formerly enslaved preacher, purchased a blacksmith's shop at Sixth and Lombard Streets and converted it into a church after Black worshippers were segregated at St. George's Methodist Episcopal Church. In 1816 delegates from Black Methodist congregations met at Bethel to organise the AME Church, electing Allen its first bishop.

The building

The present Romanesque Revival building, erected in 1889–1890, is the fourth church on the site. Its basement houses a museum and the tomb of Richard Allen. The church was a station on the Underground Railroad and a centre of abolitionist and civil rights organising.

Recognition

Mother Bethel was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1972 and remains an active congregation and pilgrimage site.
