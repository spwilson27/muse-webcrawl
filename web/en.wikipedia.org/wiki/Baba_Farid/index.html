Baba Farid

Bornc. 1173, Kothewal, Punjab
Died1266, Pakpattan, Punjab
Known forSufi saint and Punjabi poet
TitleGanj-e-Shakar ("Treasure of Sugar")

Baba Farid (Punjabi: بابا فرید; c. 1173–1266), born Fariduddin Masud, was a revered Punjabi Muslim mystic, saint and poet of the Chishti Order of Sufism. He is honoured by Muslims, Sikhs and Hindus alike, and is regarded as one of the founding figures of Punjabi literature, being among the earliest poets to write in the Punjabi language.

Life

Farid was born in the village of Kothewal near Multan in the Punjab. After early education in Multan, he travelled to Delhi, where he became a disciple of the Chishti master Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki. He later settled at Ajodhan, now Pakpattan in present-day Pakistan, where his hospice attracted large numbers of followers and visitors, including many who came to embrace Islam.

He is popularly known as Baba Farid Ganj-e-Shakar — "Treasury of Sugar" — a title linked in legend to a miracle in which stones or earth in his mouth turned to sugar during a fast.

Legacy

More than a hundred of Farid's hymns (shlokas and shabads) were preserved in the Guru Granth Sahib, the holy scripture of Sikhism, and he is traditionally honoured as the first major poet of Punjabi. His shrine at Pakpattan remains a major pilgrimage site, famous for the Bahishti Darweza (Gate of Paradise), through which devotees pass during the annual urs festival. The city of Faridkot in Indian Punjab is named after him.
