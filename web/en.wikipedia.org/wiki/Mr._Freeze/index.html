Mr. Freeze (Dr. Victor Fries) is a supervillain appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. Created by writer Dave Wood and artist Sheldon Moldoff, he first appeared in Batman #121 (1959) as the ice-based criminal "Mr. Zero", and was renamed Mr. Freeze for the 1966 Batman television series, in which he was played by George Sanders, Otto Preminger and Eli Wallach. He is one of Batman's most enduring enemies.

Fictional character biography

In his modern origin, established by writer Paul Dini in the acclaimed Batman: The Animated Series episode "Heart of Ice" (1992), Victor Fries was a brilliant GothCorp cryogenics scientist whose terminally ill wife, Nora, was placed in suspended animation while he sought a cure. When company head Ferris Boyle shut down the experiment, a lab accident exposed Fries to cryogenic chemicals, mutating him so that he can survive only at sub-zero temperatures. Wearing a refrigerated suit of powered armour and wielding a freeze gun, he commits crimes to fund his research and keep Nora alive, making him one of Batman's most sympathetic adversaries — a cold, emotionless exterior concealing single-minded devotion. The "Heart of Ice" origin was subsequently adopted into the comics continuity.

In other media

Mr. Freeze was portrayed by Arnold Schwarzenegger in the film Batman & Robin (1997), a performance remembered for its ice puns, and by Nathan Darrow in the television series Gotham. Michael Ansara's voice performance in the DC Animated Universe is widely praised. The character has appeared throughout Batman video games, including the Arkham series, where his boss battle in Arkham City — which forces the player to vary tactics as Freeze adapts — is frequently cited among the best in gaming.
