2023 Turkey–Syria earthquakes

Earthquake details
Date6 February 2023, 04:17 and 13:24 TRT
Magnitudes7.8 Mw and 7.5 Mw
EpicentresNear Pazarcık and Elbistan, Kahramanmaraş Province, Turkey
DeathsMore than 59,000 (approx. 50,800 in Turkey, 8,500 in Syria)
DamageOver US$100 billion (Turkey); hundreds of thousands of buildings destroyed or damaged

The 2023 Turkey–Syria earthquakes were two powerful earthquakes that struck southern Turkey and northern Syria on 6 February 2023. The first, of magnitude 7.8, struck at 04:17 local time near Pazarcık in Kahramanmaraş Province, rupturing the East Anatolian Fault; nine hours later a magnitude 7.5 shock hit near Elbistan on a neighbouring fault. Together with thousands of aftershocks, they killed more than 59,000 people, injured over 120,000, and devastated a region of some 14 million inhabitants, making them the deadliest earthquakes worldwide since 2010 and the most destructive natural disaster in modern Turkish history.

The earthquakes

The magnitude 7.8 mainshock was among the strongest ever recorded in Turkey, comparable to the 1939 Erzincan earthquake, and produced surface rupture over roughly 300 kilometres. It was felt across the eastern Mediterranean, from Cyprus to Egypt. The second major shock, initially startling seismologists, ruptured the Çardak–Sürgü fault zone. Building collapses concentrated in cities including Kahramanmaraş, Hatay (especially Antakya), Gaziantep, Adıyaman, Malatya and, in Syria, Aleppo and Idlib.

Impact and response

Entire neighbourhoods of mid-rise apartment blocks pancaked, trapping residents in winter cold. Turkey declared a level-four emergency and a three-month state of emergency in ten provinces; more than 140,000 search-and-rescue personnel, including teams from over 90 countries, joined the response, and rescues continued for nearly two weeks. In war-divided Syria, aid delivery was hampered by damaged border crossings and sanctions disputes, drawing sharp criticism of the international response. Millions were left homeless in tents and container settlements.

Causes of the destruction

Subsequent investigations focused on widespread non-compliance with earthquake building codes introduced after the 1999 İzmit earthquake, including the effects of repeated construction amnesties. Arrests of contractors and officials followed, and the disaster became a central issue in Turkey's May 2023 elections. Reconstruction pledges exceeded $100 billion, with the World Bank estimating direct physical damage in Turkey at about $34 billion and total economic losses far higher.
