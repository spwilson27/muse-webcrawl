The Scombridae family of the mackerels, tunas, and bonitos includes many of the world's most important food fishes. The family contains 51 species in 15 genera and two subfamilies. All species are in the subfamily Scombrinae, except the butterfly kingfish, which is the sole member of subfamily Gasterochismatinae.

Atlantic bluefin tuna (Thunnus thynnus).

Characteristics

Scombrids have two dorsal fins and a series of small finlets behind the second dorsal and anal fins. The caudal fin is strongly forked, and the body is streamlined for fast, sustained swimming. Many species are partially warm-bodied (regional endotherms), allowing them to hunt in cold water. The largest member is the Atlantic bluefin tuna, which can exceed 3 m in length and 600 kg in weight; among the smallest are island mackerels of under 20 cm.

Importance to humans

Tunas and mackerels support major commercial fisheries worldwide, for canning, fresh consumption and sashimi. Several tuna stocks have been heavily overfished, and conservation of bluefin tunas in particular is a longstanding concern.
