Kinsey scale

The Kinsey scale, also called the Heterosexual–Homosexual Rating Scale, is a scale used in research to describe a person's sexual orientation based on their experience or response at a given time. It was introduced by Alfred Kinsey and his co-authors in Sexual Behavior in the Human Male (1948).

The scale

The scale ranges from 0 (exclusively heterosexual) through 3 (equally heterosexual and homosexual) to 6 (exclusively homosexual), with intermediate grades reflecting varying degrees of experience or attraction to each sex. An additional category, X, denoted no socio-sexual contacts or reactions. Kinsey argued against viewing sexual orientation as a simple binary, writing that "the world is not to be divided into sheep and goats" and that sexuality exists on a continuum.

Influence and criticism

The scale was influential in shifting scientific and public thinking toward a spectrum model of sexual orientation. It has also been criticised for conflating behaviour, attraction, and identity on a single dimension; later instruments, such as the Klein Sexual Orientation Grid and the Storms Scale, extended the idea to multiple dimensions and separate axes.
