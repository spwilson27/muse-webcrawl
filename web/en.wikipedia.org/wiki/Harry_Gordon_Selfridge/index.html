Harry Gordon Selfridge Sr. (January 11, 1858 – May 8, 1947) was an American retail magnate who founded the London-based department store Selfridges. His 20-year leadership of Selfridges led to his becoming one of the most respected and wealthy retail magnates in the United Kingdom. He was known as the "Showman of Shopping".

Born in Ripon, Wisconsin, Selfridge delivered newspapers and left school at 14 when he found work at a bank in Jackson, Michigan. After various jobs, Selfridge found a position at Marshall Field's in Chicago, where he worked for the next 25 years. In 1890 he married Rosalie Buckingham of the prominent Chicago Buckingham family.

In 1906, following a trip to London, Selfridge invested £400,000 to build a new department store in Oxford Street. The new store, Selfridges, opened to the public on 15 March 1909, and Selfridge remained its managing director until his retirement in 1941. In later years, Selfridge lost much of his fortune and was removed from the Selfridges board.

Early life

Selfridge was born to Robert Oliver Selfridge and Lois Frances Selfridge (née Fagg) in Ripon, Wisconsin, on January 11, 1858. He was one of three boys: Charles (who died in infancy), Robert Jr. (who died at five while Harry was young), and Harry. Shortly after Harry was born, the family moved to Jackson, Michigan, because his father wanted to open a general store there. At the outbreak of the Civil War, his father joined the Union Army, rising to the rank of Major. The three boys were raised by their mother after the war; Robert Jr. and Charles died shortly after the war's end. Harry's father had chosen not to return home after his military service; he abandoned his wife and child.

Selfridge's mother found work as a school teacher. The family lived in humble circumstances; they never had much money, but never went hungry. The two became close, enjoying reading and card games. At ten years of age, Selfridge began contributing to the household income by delivering newspapers. At 12, he got a job at a local bank, and at 14 he left school to work at the bank. When he failed the entrance examinations to the Naval Academy at Annapolis, Selfridge became a bookkeeper for a local furniture factory. The factory closed a few months later, and Selfridge moved to Grand Rapids and began working in insurance.

Marshall Field's

In 1876, Selfridge went to Chicago and obtained a job working as a stock boy in the wholesaling department of Marshall Field and Company for $10 per week. Over the next 25 years, Selfridge worked his way up. He was eventually appointed a junior partner, married Rosalie Buckingham (of the prominent Chicago Buckingham family), and amassed a considerable personal fortune.

In 1884, Selfridge pitched to Field the idea of increasing the advertising budget. He later originated the concepts of bargain basements and annual sales, as well as the notion that department stores should be located in central, downtown areas accessible by public transportation rather than in outlying districts.

Selfridge is credited with popularising the saying "The customer is always right." Marshall Field's is also thought to have popularised the saying. Selfridge took the concept of the department store to a new level, making shopping an entertaining, social activity rather than a chore.

Selfridges, London

In 1906, Selfridge travelled to London for a holiday with his wife. Unimpressed with the quality of existing British stores, he noticed that the best in London could not compare with Marshall Field's or the great stores of Paris. He decided to invest £400,000 in a new department store of his own, in the then unfashionable western end of Oxford Street but directly opposite the Bond Street tube station.

The new store was designed by Daniel Burnham, the architect of Marshall Field's main building in Chicago. It opened to the public on 15 March 1909, setting a new standard for retailing in the UK. The store's innovative marketing techniques, including the use of elaborate window displays and a wide range of departments and services, helped to establish Selfridges as one of London's premier department stores.

Selfridge's vision was to create a store that offered not just goods, but an experience. He introduced many innovative ideas, including:

  • A library and reading room for customers
  • Restaurants and cafes within the store
  • A roof garden with various attractions
  • Educational and scientific exhibits, such as the first public demonstration of television by John Logie Baird in 1925

Later years and death

During the Depression years Selfridge's fortunes rapidly declined and in 1939 he owed the store £250,000 in taxes. He had been removed as chairman of Selfridges in 1941, and his pension was reduced from £6,000 a year to £450 a few years later.

Selfridge died in Putney, south-west London, on 8 May 1947 at the age of 89. He was buried at St Mark's Churchyard in Highcliffe, Dorset, next to his wife and mother.

In popular culture

The ITV and PBS drama series Mr Selfridge (2013–2016) tells the story of Harry Selfridge and his store, with Jeremy Piven in the title role.
