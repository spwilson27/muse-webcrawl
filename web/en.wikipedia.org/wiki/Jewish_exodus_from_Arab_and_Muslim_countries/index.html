The Jewish exodus from Arab and Muslim countries was the departure, flight, expulsion, evacuation, and migration of about 850,000 Jews, primarily of Sephardi and Mizrahi descent, from Arab and Muslim-majority countries from 1948 to the early 1970s.

Background and course

Ancient Jewish communities had lived across the Middle East and North Africa for millennia. The creation of Israel in 1948, the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, discriminatory legislation, riots such as the 1941 Farhud in Baghdad, and state expulsions drove the exodus. Large communities vanished almost entirely: Iraq's roughly 135,000 Jews mostly left in 1950–51 in Operation Ezra and Nehemiah; Yemen's Jews were airlifted in Operation Magic Carpet (1949–50); Egypt expelled or pressured out its Jews after 1956; and Algeria's Jews largely left for France in 1962. Most of the migrants settled in Israel, where their descendants form a large share of the Jewish population.

Legacy

The event is commemorated in Israel on 30 November, and the property left behind by the departing communities remains a contested element of wider refugee debates.
