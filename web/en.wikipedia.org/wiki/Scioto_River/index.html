Scioto River

The Scioto River is a river in central and southern Ohio, more than 231 miles (372 km) long. It rises in Hardin County in west-central Ohio and flows generally southeast and then south through Columbus, the state capital, before emptying into the Ohio River at Portsmouth.

Course

The river's principal tributaries include the Olentangy River, which joins it in Columbus, Big Walnut Creek and Darby Creek. It passes through the cities of Kenton, Marion, Delaware, Columbus, Circleville, Chillicothe, Waverly and Portsmouth. Several reservoirs on the river and its tributaries, including Griggs and O'Shaughnessy reservoirs north of Columbus, supply drinking water and recreation.

History

The Scioto valley was home to Native American peoples for thousands of years, including the mound-building Hopewell culture, whose earthworks survive along its banks. The Shawnee had important towns in the valley in the 18th century. The name is derived from a Wyandot word meaning "deer". Severe floods, notably the Great Flood of 1913, led to extensive flood-control works around Columbus. In recent decades the Scioto riverfront in downtown Columbus has been redeveloped into the Scioto Mile park system.
