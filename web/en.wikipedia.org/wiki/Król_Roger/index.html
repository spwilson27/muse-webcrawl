Król Roger

ComposerKarol Szymanowski
LibrettistsKarol Szymanowski and Jarosław Iwaszkiewicz
LanguagePolish
PremiereJune 19, 1926, Grand Theatre, Warsaw

Król Roger (King Roger) is an opera in three acts by the Polish composer Karol Szymanowski, to a libretto by the composer and his cousin Jarosław Iwaszkiewicz. It was premiered at the Grand Theatre in Warsaw on June 19, 1926. It is widely considered Szymanowski's masterpiece and one of the finest Polish operas.

Background and plot

The opera is set in 12th-century Norman Sicily at the court of King Roger II. In the first act, the court and church demand the punishment of a mysterious Shepherd who preaches a joyful, Dionysian faith; Roger instead orders him brought before the court. In the second act, the Shepherd's allure draws away Queen Roxana and the court; Roxana's aria and the Shepherd's dance lead Roger to follow them. In the final act, set among ancient ruins at dawn, Roger — having resisted the temptation to surrender wholly to the Shepherd, who is revealed as Dionysus — greets the rising sun in a hymn of renewed self-knowledge and balance between reason and ecstasy.

Music and reception

Szymanowski's score blends lush late-Romantic orchestration with Byzantine, Arabic, and ancient Greek colour, orientalizing modes, and shimmering choral writing; the libretto draws on Euripides' The Bacchae and on Szymanowski's travels in Sicily and North Africa and his novel Efebos. Neglected for decades, the opera has been widely revived since the late 20th century and recorded and staged by major houses, and is now regarded as a central work of 20th-century Polish music.
