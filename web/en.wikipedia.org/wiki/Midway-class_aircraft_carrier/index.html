Midway-class aircraft carrier

The Midway-class was a class of three aircraft carriers of the United States Navy. Designed during World War II but completed just after it ended, they were the largest carriers in the world when commissioned and the first American carriers too large to transit the Panama Canal. The class comprised USS Midway (CV-41), USS Franklin D. Roosevelt (CV-42) and USS Coral Sea (CV-43); three additional ships were cancelled.

Design

The Midways were designed with an armoured flight deck — a British practice adopted after wartime experience — and a very large air group of up to 130 aircraft in their original configuration. As designed they displaced about 45,000 tons, and they carried a heavy anti-aircraft armament including twin 5-inch mounts.

Service

Extensive modernisations (the SCB-110 and SCB-101 programmes) gave them angled flight decks, steam catapults, hurricane bows and enlarged lifts, allowing them to operate jet aircraft. Franklin D. Roosevelt was decommissioned in 1977 and Coral Sea in 1990. Midway, extensively rebuilt, served in the Vietnam War and as flagship during the 1991 Gulf War; she was decommissioned in 1992 after 47 years of service, among the longest-serving carriers in US Navy history.

USS Midway is now a museum ship at San Diego, California, one of the most visited naval museums in the United States.
