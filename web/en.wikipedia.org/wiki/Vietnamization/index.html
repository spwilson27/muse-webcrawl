Vietnamization

Vietnamization was the United States policy, adopted under President Richard Nixon in 1969, of withdrawing American ground combat troops from the Vietnam War while expanding, equipping and training the armed forces of South Vietnam (ARVN) to assume the fighting themselves.

Implementation

Announced as part of the Nixon Doctrine, the policy reduced US troop strength from a peak of 549,000 in 1969 to about 69,000 by mid-1972. Massive transfers of equipment accompanied the drawdown, alongside intensified bombing and incursions into Cambodia (1970) and Laos (Lam Son 719, 1971) intended to buy time.

Outcome

ARVN performed credibly in the 1972 Easter Offensive with US air support, but after the Paris Peace Accords (1973) and subsequent American aid cuts it collapsed during North Vietnam's 1975 Spring Offensive, ending with the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975. Historians debate whether Vietnamization was a genuine strategy or a "decent interval" cover for withdrawal.
