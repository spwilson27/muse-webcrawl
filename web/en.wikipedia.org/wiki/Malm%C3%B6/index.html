Malmö is the largest city in the Swedish county of Scania, the third-largest city in Sweden, and the sixth-largest in the Nordic countries. It lies at the southern tip of Sweden, across the Öresund strait from Copenhagen, Denmark.

History

Founded in the 13th century as a fortified ferry port under Danish rule, Malmö grew wealthy on the herring trade and was one of Denmark's most important towns before passing permanently to Sweden under the Treaty of Roskilde in 1658. Industrialization in the 19th century brought shipbuilding — the Kockums shipyard dominated the harbour — but the yards' decline in the 1970s and 1980s plunged the city into a prolonged downturn.

Regeneration

The opening of the Öresund Bridge in 2000 integrated Malmö into the Copenhagen metropolitan region, and the former shipyard district of Västra Hamnen was redeveloped as a sustainable housing area, anchored by the twisting Turning Torso skyscraper (2005), designed by Santiago Calatrava. Malmö University was founded in 1998.

Demographics and culture

Malmö is one of Sweden's most multicultural cities, with residents born in more than 170 countries. It hosted the Eurovision Song Contest in 1992, 2013, and 2024, and its football club, Malmö FF, reached the European Cup final in 1979.
