Donald "Ginger" McCain (21 September 1930 – 19 September 2011) was an English racehorse trainer. He is best known as the trainer of Red Rum, the only horse to win the Grand National at Aintree three times (1973, 1974 and 1977), and also of Amberleigh House, winner of the 2004 Grand National.

Career

McCain worked as a taxi driver and used-car dealer in Southport before taking out a training licence in 1953, famously training his small string on the sands of Southport beach behind his car showroom. He bought Red Rum for 6,000 guineas for owner Noel Le Mare in 1972. Despite the horse's pedal osteitis, a debilitating bone disease of the hoof, McCain's regime of galloping in seawater on the beach managed the condition, and Red Rum became a national celebrity — also finishing second in the 1975 and 1976 Nationals.

McCain later moved his training operation to Cholmondeley in Cheshire. In 2006 he handed the licence to his son, Donald McCain Jr., himself later a Grand National-winning trainer with Ballabriggs (2011). Outspoken and a champion of the Grand National against its critics, McCain died in 2011, days before his 81st birthday.
