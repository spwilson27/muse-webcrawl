King George VI and Queen Elizabeth Stakes

The King George VI and Queen Elizabeth Stakes is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to horses aged three years or older. It is run at Ascot Racecourse over a distance of 1 mile 3 furlongs and 211 yards (2,406 metres), and it is scheduled to take place each year in July.

History

The event was founded in 1951 to mark the Festival of Britain, combining two separate races named after King George VI and Queen Elizabeth. It quickly established itself as Britain's most prestigious all-aged flat race, often described as the country's midsummer middle-distance championship, and forms part of a celebrated international series of races alongside the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe and the Breeders' Cup Turf.

Notable winners

Past winners include many of the greatest middle-distance horses of the modern era: Ribot, Nijinsky, Brigadier Gerard, Shergar, Dancing Brave, Galileo, Montjeu, Enable — the only three-time winner of the race (2017, 2019 and 2020) — and City of Troy. The most successful jockey in the race's history is Lester Piggott, with seven victories.
