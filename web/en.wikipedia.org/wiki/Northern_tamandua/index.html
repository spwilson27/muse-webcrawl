Northern tamandua

The northern tamandua (Tamandua mexicana) is a species of tamandua, an anteater in the family Myrmecophagidae. It ranges from southern Mexico, through Central America, to northwestern South America west of the Andes, in Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador and Peru.

Description

It is a medium-sized anteater with a prehensile tail, small eyes and ears, and a long snout. The fur is pale yellow over most of the body, with a distinctive black vest over the back, sides and shoulders. Adults weigh roughly 3 to 5 kg and measure about 1 metre including the tail.

Behaviour

The northern tamandua is both arboreal and terrestrial, active mainly at night. It feeds on ants, termites and bees, tearing open nests with strong foreclaws and lapping insects with a tongue that can extend 40 cm. Solitary except when mating or rearing young, it produces a single offspring that rides on the mother's back.

Conservation

The species is listed as Least Concern, though it is threatened locally by habitat loss, roadkill and hunting.
