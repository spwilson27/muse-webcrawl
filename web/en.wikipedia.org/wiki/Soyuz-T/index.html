The Soyuz-T (Russian: Союз-Т, "Union-T", T for transport) was a Soviet crewed spacecraft, the third generation of the Soyuz programme, flown between 1979 and 1986. It was developed for ferrying crews to the Salyut 6, Salyut 7, and Mir space stations.

Design

Soyuz-T restored the three-person crew capacity lost after the Soyuz 11 disaster, thanks to the lighter Sokol pressure suits. It replaced the earlier Argon computer with a digital flight computer, regained solar panels (which the Soyuz Ferry variant had omitted), and introduced a new Kurs-free Igla rendezvous system refinements, solid-fueled escape and soft-landing improvements, and a launch escape tower usable over more of the ascent.

Missions

The type first flew uncrewed as Kosmos 1001 in 1978 and as Soyuz T-1 in 1979. Crewed flights began with Soyuz T-2 in June 1980. Notable missions include Soyuz T-6, carrying the first French cosmonaut Jean-Loup Chrétien (1982); Soyuz T-7, with Svetlana Savitskaya, the second woman in space; and Soyuz T-15 (1986), the last flight, which visited both Mir and Salyut 7 — the only mission ever to travel between two space stations. The Soyuz-T was succeeded by the Soyuz-TM.
