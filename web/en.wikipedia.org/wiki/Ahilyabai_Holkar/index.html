Ahilyabai Holkar

Born: 31 May 1725, Chandi, Ahmadnagar district
Died: 13 August 1795, Maheshwar
Reign: 1767–1795 (Holkar state, from Maheshwar)

Ahilyabai Holkar (31 May 1725 – 13 August 1795) was the queen of the Holkar state of the Maratha Confederacy in central India, ruling from 1767 until her death. Widely remembered as an exemplary administrator and a pious builder of temples and public works, she is one of the most revered women rulers in Indian history.

Early life and accession

Born in the village of Chandi in present-day Maharashtra, Ahilyabai was spotted by Malhar Rao Holkar, founder of the Holkar dynasty, who arranged her marriage to his son Khanderao. Khanderao was killed at the Battle of Kumher in 1754, and Malhar Rao died in 1766; after the death of her son Male Rao in 1767, Ahilyabai petitioned the Peshwa for permission to rule the Holkar territories herself, which was granted despite opposition from some nobles.

Reign

From her capital at Maheshwar on the Narmada River, Ahilyabai administered justice personally, kept efficient accounts, promoted trade and textile manufacture (Maheshwar saris remain famous), and maintained a well-regarded military under commanders such as Tukoji Rao Holkar. She is celebrated for building and restoring temples, ghats, wells, and rest houses across India — including work at Varanasi, where she rebuilt the Kashi Vishwanath temple in 1780, as well as at Somnath, Dwarka, Gaya, and Rameswaram. Her reign is remembered as a period of peace, prosperity, and good governance. Indore's airport is named Devi Ahilya Bai Holkar Airport in her honour.
