Induced subgraph

Induced subgraph
FieldGraph theory
NotationG[S]

In the mathematical field of graph theory, an induced subgraph of a graph is another graph, formed from a subset of the vertices of the graph and all of the edges (from the original graph) connecting pairs of vertices in that subset. Formally, for a graph G = (V, E) and a subset S of V, the induced subgraph G[S] has vertex set S and edge set { (u, v) ∈ E : u, v ∈ S }.

Properties and examples

Induced subgraphs preserve all adjacencies among the chosen vertices, unlike a general subgraph, which may omit edges between vertices it keeps. Important classes defined through induced subgraphs include perfect graphs, characterised by forbidding induced odd holes and odd anti-holes, and hereditary graph classes in general: a class of graphs is hereditary if it is closed under taking induced subgraphs, and every hereditary class can be characterised by its set of forbidden induced subgraphs. Cliques and independent sets are induced subgraphs that are complete or empty, respectively.

Problems

Deciding whether a graph contains an induced subgraph isomorphic to a given pattern graph is NP-complete in general (it includes the clique and induced path problems), a fact that drives much research on special graph classes. The induced subgraph isomorphism problem contrasts with the (also NP-complete) subgraph isomorphism problem, which allows the target to omit edges.
