Hypercube

A projection of an 8-cube (octeract)

In geometry, a hypercube or n-cube is an n-dimensional analogue of a cube: just as a square is a 2-dimensional cube and a cube is 3-dimensional, the hypercube generalises the pattern to any number of dimensions. The 4-dimensional hypercube is commonly called a tesseract. The hypercube is a special case of a hyperrectangle and a member of the family of regular polytopes known as cross-polytopes' duals.

Construction

A hypercube can be built recursively: a 0-cube is a point; moving a point a unit distance gives a line segment (1-cube); sweeping a segment perpendicular to itself gives a square (2-cube); sweeping a square gives a cube (3-cube); and sweeping a cube into a fourth direction gives a tesseract (4-cube). An n-cube has 2n vertices, each corresponding to a binary string of length n, and its graph is the hypercube graph Qn, in which vertices are connected when their strings differ in exactly one coordinate.

Elements

The number of m-dimensional faces of an n-cube is given by 2n−m · C(n, m). Thus a tesseract has 16 vertices, 32 edges, 24 square faces and 8 cubic cells. The hypercube with side length s has n-dimensional volume sn and surface "volume" 2n sn−1. Its long diagonal has length s√n.

Applications

Hypercube graphs are used as interconnection networks in parallel computing, since they offer low diameter with regular structure; each of the 2n processors differs from its neighbours in one bit of its address. Hypercubes also appear in coding theory, in the geometry of data (as the space of binary feature vectors), and in popular culture, where the tesseract has featured in fiction and film as a symbol of four-dimensional space.
