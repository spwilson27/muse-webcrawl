Misa de Gallo (Spanish for "Rooster's Mass") is the name given in Spanish-speaking countries to the Christmas Mass celebrated at midnight or in the early hours of Christmas Day. In the Philippines, the term is intertwined with Simbang Gabi ("Night Mass"), a devotional novena of nine dawn Masses beginning on 16 December and culminating in the Misa de Gallo on Christmas Eve.

Origins

The name derives from the tradition that a rooster crowed on the night of Christ's birth, and from the early-morning hour of celebration. In Spain, the Misa del Gallo is the popular name for the midnight Mass of Christmas Eve. Spanish missionaries carried the practice to the colonies, where in the Philippines it was adapted during the 17th century so that farmers could attend Mass before dawn and still work the rice harvest season days; Pope Sixtus V granted special permission for the dawn schedule.

Philippine observance

Church bells ring before dawn — often as early as 3 or 4 a.m. — and families attend on nine consecutive mornings, believing a completed novena grants a special wish. After Mass, vendors outside churches sell traditional rice cakes such as bibingka and puto bumbong, eaten with salabat (ginger tea) or hot chocolate. Parishes that cannot accommodate dawn crowds also hold anticipated evening Simbang Gabi Masses. Filipino diaspora communities worldwide maintain the devotion.
