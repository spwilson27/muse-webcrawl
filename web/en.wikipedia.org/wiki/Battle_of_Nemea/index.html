The Battle of Nemea (394 BC) was a land battle of the Corinthian War, fought between Sparta and a coalition of Athens, Thebes, Corinth, and Argos. It took place in the dry bed of the Nemea River, on the territory of Corinth, and ended in a Spartan victory despite the coalition's numerical superiority.

Hoplites in combat during the Corinthian War.

Background

The Corinthian War (395–387 BC) pitted Sparta against a coalition of Greek states supported by Persian gold. In 394 BC the allied army, which Xenophon numbers at some 24,000 hoplites, advanced from Corinth to meet a Spartan army of about 18,000 hoplites under the command of Aristodemus, regent for the young king Agesipolis I.

The battle

As was typical of hoplite warfare, both armies drifted to the right as they advanced. The Spartans defeated the Athenians facing them on the allied left, while on the other wing the Thebans and Argives routed the Spartan allies opposite them. The Spartans then wheeled along the allied line, catching each returning contingent in the flank as it came back from pursuit. Allied losses were heavy — around 2,800 killed by Xenophon's account — against roughly 1,100 Spartan and allied dead.

Aftermath

Although the coalition lost the battle, the Spartan victory could not break the alliance, which continued the war from the shelter of Corinth's walls. The fighting of 394 BC also included the Spartan defeat at sea at Cnidus the same summer, which undid Spartan naval supremacy in the Aegean.
