Frequency-division multiple access

Frequency-division multiple access (FDMA) is a channel access method used in some multiple-access protocols. FDMA allows multiple users to send data through a single communication channel, such as a radio frequency band or a coaxial cable trunk, by dividing the bandwidth of the channel into separate non-overlapping frequency sub-channels and allocating each sub-channel to an individual user.

Method

In an FDMA system, the available spectrum is partitioned into channels of equal or specified bandwidth. Each user is assigned a dedicated frequency channel for the duration of a transmission, and guard bands are placed between adjacent channels to reduce interference. Users transmit continuously on their assigned carriers, so no synchronization of transmission times between users is required, unlike time-division multiple access (TDMA).

Applications

FDMA was a fundamental technique of first-generation (1G) analog cellular telephone systems such as AMPS and TACS, in which each telephone call occupied its own pair of 30 kHz channels (one for each direction). It is also used in satellite communications, in cable television distribution, and in some radio systems. The related technique of frequency-division duplexing (FDD), in which uplink and downlink traffic use separate frequency bands, is widely used in cellular networks.

Advantages and disadvantages

FDMA offers simple implementation and continuous transmission, which suits analog signals and reduces the need for synchronization. Its disadvantages include inefficient use of spectrum when users are idle, since a channel assigned to an inactive user cannot be reassigned easily, and susceptibility to nonlinear effects such as intermodulation in shared amplifiers.
