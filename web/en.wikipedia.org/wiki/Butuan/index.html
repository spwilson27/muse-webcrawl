Butuan, on the Agusan River in northeastern Mindanao.

Butuan, officially the City of Butuan, is a highly urbanised city in the Caraga region of the Philippines and the regional centre of Caraga. It lies in the northeastern part of Mindanao, in the Agusan Valley near the mouth of the Agusan River.

History

Butuan was an ancient trading polity long before Spanish colonisation. Archaeological finds include the balangay plank boats — the oldest Philippine watercraft, dating back over a millennium — and gold artefacts indicating trade with China and Southeast Asia; a wooden coffin and the Butuan silver strip with an ancient inscription were also recovered. Butuan figures in early Spanish accounts and in the historiographical debate over the site of the first Mass in the Philippines, traditionally assigned to Limasawa but argued by some historians to have been in Butuan's vicinity (Masao).

Economy

Once famous as the "Timber City of the South" for its logging industry, Butuan's economy now centres on commerce, agriculture (rice, bananas, and agro-processing), gold trading, and services as the gateway to the Caraga region. It celebrates the Balangay Festival each May and the Kahimunan Festival in honour of its patron, Santo Niño.
