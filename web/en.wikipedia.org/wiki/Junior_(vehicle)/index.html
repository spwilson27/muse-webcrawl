Junior (vehicle)

ManufacturerDKW / Auto Union
Production1959–1963
ClassCompact car
EngineTwo-stroke three-cylinder

The Junior was a small car produced by the West German manufacturer DKW, part of the Auto Union group, between 1959 and 1963. It was unveiled at the Frankfurt Motor Show in 1959 and was notable for its modern, American-influenced styling, including modest tailfins and a panoramic windscreen.

Design

The Junior was powered by a water-cooled two-stroke three-cylinder engine of 741 cc producing around 34 PS, driving the front wheels. In 1961 the larger-engined Junior de Luxe appeared with an 897 cc engine, and the range was succeeded in 1963 by the DKW F12.

Production and successors

The Junior was built at Auto Union's new plant in Ingolstadt, which became a cornerstone of the later Audi company. Its two-stroke engine technology, increasingly uncompetitive against four-stroke rivals, was phased out after Daimler-Benz sold Auto Union to Volkswagen in 1964, and the DKW F102 became the last model to carry the two-stroke engine.
