Crater (Aden)

Crater
District of Aden, Yemen
Coordinates: 12.7778° N, 45.0333° E

Crater (Arabic: كريتر, Kraytar) is a district of the city of Aden, Yemen. Its official name is Seera (Arabic: صيرة, Ṣīrah). It is situated in a crater of an ancient volcano which forms the Shamsan Mountains around Aden Harbour.

History

Crater was the original port city of Aden. After the British East India Company captured Aden in 1839, the town grew as a coaling station and free port on the route to India. The old town sits inside the bowl of the extinct volcano, protected by steep rocky slopes on nearly all sides, with the harbour on its northern edge.

During the Aden Emergency of the 1960s, Crater was a centre of unrest. In June 1967, following a mutiny by South Arabian police and army units, British forces temporarily withdrew from the district; it was reoccupied in July 1967 by the Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders under Lieutenant-Colonel Colin Mitchell, an episode that attracted wide press attention in Britain.

Landmarks

Landmarks of Crater include the Aden Tanks (ancient cisterns cut into the rock to collect rainwater, also known as the Tawila Tanks), the Sira Fortress on Sira Island, the minaret of Al-Aidrus Mosque, and the main commercial streets of the old town, including the market area around the main post office.
