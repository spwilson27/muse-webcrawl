Falkland Islands sovereignty dispute

The Falkland Islands sovereignty dispute is an ongoing territorial dispute between the United Kingdom and Argentina over the Falkland Islands (Spanish: Islas Malvinas), an archipelago in the South Atlantic. Britain administers the islands as a British Overseas Territory, while Argentina claims sovereignty, maintaining that it inherited them from Spain upon independence and that the British reassertion of rule in 1833 was illegitimate.

Background

France founded the first settlement at Port Louis in 1764; Britain established Port Egmont in 1765, and Spain later acquired the French colony. Spain expelled the British garrison in 1770, nearly causing war, but Britain withdrew in 1774 while maintaining its claim. After Spanish withdrawal in 1811, the islands were used by whalers and sealers until the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata appointed governors in the 1820s. In January 1833 a British naval force re-established British rule, removing the Argentine garrison, an event Argentina regards as a usurpation.

Falklands War and after

On 2 April 1982 Argentina's military junta invaded the islands, beginning the Falklands War. Britain dispatched a naval task force and retook the islands by 14 June 1982, at the cost of 255 British and 649 Argentine military deaths and three islander civilians. Since then Britain has strengthened its garrison at RAF Mount Pleasant and maintained that sovereignty is not negotiable without the islanders' consent. In a 2013 referendum, 99.8% of Falkland Islanders voted to remain a British Overseas Territory. Argentina, whose constitution mandates recovery of the islands by peaceful means respecting international law, continues to press its claim at the United Nations.
