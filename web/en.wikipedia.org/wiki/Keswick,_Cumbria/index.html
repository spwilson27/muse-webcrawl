Keswick is a market town and civil parish in the Borough of Allerdale in Cumbria, England, lying within the Lake District National Park. It is situated just north of Derwentwater and a short distance from Bassenthwaite Lake, surrounded by fells including Skiddaw and Catbells. Keswick is a major centre for tourism in the northern Lake District.

History

The name Keswick derives from the Old English cēse-wīc, meaning "cheese farm". The town received its market charter in 1276 from Thomas de Derwentwater. In the Tudor period the area became important for mining — copper, lead and graphite were extracted nearby — and German miners brought in by the Company of Mines Royal introduced new smelting techniques. The local graphite ("wad") gave rise to Keswick's famous pencil industry, commemorated by the Derwent Pencil Museum.

Tourism and culture

Tourism has been the town's main industry since the Victorian era, when the railway (closed in 1972) brought visitors attracted by the scenery celebrated by the Lake Poets. Samuel Taylor Coleridge lived at Greta Hall in Keswick from 1800, and Robert Southey resided there for forty years. Attractions include the Theatre by the Lake on the shore of Derwentwater, the Keswick Museum, and boat launches giving access to the lake. The town is a base for walking on Skiddaw, Blencathra and the Borrowdale fells, and hosts the annual Keswick Mountain Festival and the Keswick Convention, an evangelical Christian gathering founded in 1875.

Geography

Keswick sits on the River Greta, which flows into the River Derwent. The town suffered significant flooding during Storm Desmond in December 2015. The A66 trunk road connects it to Penrith and the M6 motorway to the east and to Workington on the coast to the west.
