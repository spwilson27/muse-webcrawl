Solar home system

A solar home system (SHS) is a stand-alone photovoltaic system that supplies electric power to households not connected to the electricity grid. A typical system comprises one or more solar panels, a charge controller, a rechargeable battery, and appliances such as LED lights, phone chargers, radios, fans, and small televisions, usually running on 12 V DC.

Scale and deployment

Solar home systems became one of the principal routes to rural electrification in developing countries from the 1990s onward. Bangladesh's IDCOL programme installed over four million systems, making it one of the largest off-grid solar programmes in the world; large programmes also operated in Kenya, India, Nepal, Indonesia, and across sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America. Small "pico-solar" products — solar lanterns and multi-light kits — serve the smallest end of the market.

Business models

Systems have been sold for cash, on microcredit, through fee-for-service energy companies, and increasingly via pay-as-you-go (PAYGO) models in which mobile-money payments unlock the system day by day until it is paid off. Pay-as-you-go firms such as M-KOPA brought solar home systems, and later televisions and other appliances, to millions of East and West African households.

Benefits and limitations

Compared with kerosene lamps, solar home systems provide brighter, cheaper, safer light, extend study and working hours, and enable phone charging and information access. Their power capacity is limited, however; meeting needs such as cooking, irrigation, or heavy appliances requires larger systems or mini-grids. Battery replacement, e-waste, product quality, and after-sales service remain recurring challenges, addressed in part by quality standards from Lighting Global / VeraSol.
