Seven bowls

The seven bowls (Greek: φιάλας, phialas) are a set of plagues mentioned in the Book of Revelation, chapters 15 and 16. They are the final and most severe series of judgements in the book, poured out by seven angels upon the earth as "the seven last plagues" containing the wrath of God. The bowls follow the seven seals and the seven trumpets in the structure of Revelation.

The plagues

  1. Foul and loathsome sores appear on those bearing the mark of the beast.
  2. The sea turns to blood and every living creature in it dies.
  3. Rivers and springs of water turn to blood.
  4. The sun is given power to scorch people with fire.
  5. The kingdom of the beast is plunged into darkness.
  6. The Euphrates dries up, preparing the way for the kings from the east and the gathering at Armageddon.
  7. A voice from the temple declares "It is done"; there is a great earthquake, hail, and the fall of Babylon the Great.

Interpretation

Interpretations vary by school of eschatology. Preterists read the bowls as judgements on first-century Rome or Jerusalem; futurists expect literal end-time plagues; idealists treat them symbolically as recurring patterns of divine judgement throughout history. The imagery of the bowls draws heavily on the plagues of Egypt in Exodus, with sores, bloodied waters and darkness paralleling the earlier account.
