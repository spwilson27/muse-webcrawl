Trinomial

A trinomial is a polynomial with three terms

In elementary algebra, a trinomial is a polynomial consisting of three terms or monomials. Examples include 3x + 5y + 8z with variables x, y, z, and the quadratic trinomial ax² + bx + c, where a, b and c are constants and a ≠ 0.

Types

Common special cases include:

  • Quadratic trinomials, of the form ax² + bx + c, which frequently arise when expanding the product of two binomials: (x + m)(x + n) = x² + (m + n)x + mn.
  • Perfect square trinomials, which are the squares of binomials, e.g. x² + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)².

Factorisation

Trinomials are often studied in connection with factorisation: a monic quadratic trinomial x² + bx + c can be factored into binomials when two numbers can be found whose sum is b and whose product is c. More general trinomials may be factored by grouping or by using the quadratic formula to find their roots.

In other fields

The term is also used outside algebra. In taxonomy, a trinomial name is a three-part name for a subspecies. In probability, the trinomial distribution generalises the binomial distribution to outcomes with three categories.
