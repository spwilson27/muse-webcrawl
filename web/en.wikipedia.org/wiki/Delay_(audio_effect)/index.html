Delay (audio effect)

Delay is an audio effect which records an input signal to an audio storage medium and plays it back after a period of time. The delayed signal may be played back multiple times, or fed back into the recording, to create the sound of a repeating, decaying echo.

History

Early delay effects were achieved with tape loops, such as the Echoplex and the Roland Space Echo, in which a loop of magnetic tape passed over recording and playback heads; varying tape speed or head distance changed the delay time. Oil-can delays used a rotating disc in electrostatic oil. In the 1970s analogue bucket-brigade devices (BBDs) made compact pedal delays possible, and digital delays followed in the 1980s, offering longer and cleaner repeats.

Parameters

Typical controls include delay time (from a few milliseconds to several seconds), feedback (the proportion of the delayed signal re-fed into the input, controlling the number of repeats), and mix (the balance of dry and delayed signal). Short delays produce doubling, slapback echo and flanging-like comb-filtering; longer delays create rhythmic echo patterns.

Uses

Delay is a staple of rock, dub, ambient and electronic music. Dub producers such as King Tubby and Lee "Scratch" Perry made feedback echo a central instrument; guitarists such as The Edge of U2 built signature sounds from dotted-eighth-note digital delay.
