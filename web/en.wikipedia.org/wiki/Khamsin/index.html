Khamsin

Khamsin, khamaseen or chamsin (Arabic: خمسين, "fifty") is a dry, hot, sandy local wind affecting Egypt and the Levant. The name derives from the Arabic word for "fifty", referring to the roughly fifty-day period in spring during which these winds blow. Similar winds in the region include the sirocco and the harmattan.

RegionEgypt, Libya, the Levant
SeasonSpring (roughly March–May)
DirectionSouth to south-east
EffectsHeat, dust storms, low visibility

Characteristics

The khamsin blows when a low-pressure system tracks eastward across North Africa or the eastern Mediterranean, drawing hot air northward from the Sahara. Temperatures can rise by 20 °C or more within a few hours, sometimes exceeding 45 °C, while humidity falls below 5%. The wind carries large quantities of sand and dust, reducing visibility to a few metres and turning the sky yellow or orange. Episodes typically last from several hours to a few days.

Effects

Khamsin conditions aggravate respiratory illnesses, damage crops, disrupt air and road traffic, and are associated in local tradition with irritability and ill health. In Egypt the season falls between Easter and the Coptic Pentecost period, and the winds are a noted feature of the spring months. Historical accounts describe khamsin storms hampering military campaigns in North Africa during the Second World War.
