Villa Hügel

Villa Hügel is a 19th-century mansion in Bredeney, Essen, Germany, built in 1870–1873 for industrialist Alfred Krupp as the residence of the Krupp family. With 269 rooms and extensive parkland above Lake Baldeney, it symbolised the wealth of the Ruhr steel dynasty.

History

Alfred Krupp designed much of the house himself, insisting on modern fireproofing, ventilation and guest facilities. It remained the family seat until 1945, when it was seized by Allied occupation authorities and used as offices. The Alfried Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach Foundation opened it to the public in 1984.

Today

The villa now hosts exhibitions on Krupp and Ruhr industrial history, concerts and corporate events, and its park is open to visitors.
