Dulwich College

Dulwich College
Type: Public school (independent, day and boarding)
Founded: 1619, by Edward Alleyn
Location: Dulwich, London, England
Pupils: approx. 1,800 boys aged 7–18

Dulwich College is a public school (an independent, fee-charging day and boarding school for boys) in Dulwich, south London. It was founded in 1619 by the Elizabethan actor Edward Alleyn as the College of God's Gift, originally educating twelve poor scholars, and is one of the largest independent schools in the United Kingdom.

History

Alleyn, a successful actor-manager and contemporary of Shakespeare, endowed the college with his Dulwich estate. The school was reconstituted in the 1850s under the Alleyn's College of God's Gift foundation, and moved to new buildings designed by Charles Barry Jr. on Dulwich Common in 1870. The foundation also supports Alleyn's School and James Allen's Girls' School, and the Dulwich Picture Gallery.

School life

Dulwich is notable for its "Dulwich Experiment" of the mid-20th century, which admitted large numbers of local-authority-funded pupils on merit, and for strong traditions in rugby, rowing and music. It runs international partner schools under the Dulwich College International banner in Asia.

Notable alumni

Old Alleynians include the explorer Sir Ernest Shackleton, writers P. G. Wodehouse and Raymond Chandler, and comedian Bob Monkhouse.
