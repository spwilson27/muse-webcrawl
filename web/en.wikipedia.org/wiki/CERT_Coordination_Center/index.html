CERT Coordination Center

Founded: November 1988
Parent: Software Engineering Institute, Carnegie Mellon University
Location: Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania
Focus: Computer security incident response and vulnerability coordination

The CERT Coordination Center (CERT/CC) is the computer emergency response team for the United States' internet infrastructure, housed within the CERT Division of the Software Engineering Institute (SEI), a federally funded research and development center at Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania.

History

The center was created by the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA) in November 1988, in direct response to the Morris worm incident, which had exposed the absence of any central point of coordination for internet security emergencies. It was the first organisation of its kind; the generic term "CERT" (Computer Emergency Response Team) derives from its name, and CERT/CC's example led to the creation of hundreds of incident response teams worldwide and to the Forum of Incident Response and Security Teams (FIRST).

Activities

CERT/CC coordinates the disclosure of software vulnerabilities between discoverers and vendors, publishes vulnerability notes (the VU# series) and advisories, and develops tools and practices for network security, including the OCTAVE risk assessment method and tools for network traffic analysis such as SiLK. It has historically operated the federally supported vulnerability coordination function for the United States and works closely with the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA).
