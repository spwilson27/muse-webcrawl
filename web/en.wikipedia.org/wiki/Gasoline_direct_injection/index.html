Gasoline direct injection

Direct injection of fuel into the combustion chamber
TypeFuel injection system
ApplicationSpark-ignition internal combustion engines

Gasoline direct injection (GDI or FSI) is a fuel delivery system used in modern gasoline engines, in which fuel is injected at high pressure directly into each combustion chamber, rather than into the intake port upstream of the intake valve as in port (manifold) injection.

Operation

A GDI system uses an engine-driven high-pressure fuel pump (commonly delivering 35–200 bar) feeding a common rail and solenoid or piezoelectric injectors mounted in the cylinder head. Precise electronic control of injection timing and quantity allows multiple injections per cycle and stratified-charge operation, in which a rich, ignitable mixture is formed near the spark plug within an overall lean mixture. Cooling of the charge by fuel evaporation in the cylinder permits higher compression ratios and greater turbocharger boost without knock.

Advantages and drawbacks

Compared with port injection, GDI typically improves fuel efficiency by roughly 3–5 percent (more in stratified-charge designs), increases specific power output, and reduces cold-start hydrocarbon emissions because fuel is not lost to intake-wall wetting. However, because fuel does not wash over the intake valves, GDI engines are prone to carbon deposits on the valves; they also emit more particulate matter, which has led to the fitment of gasoline particulate filters in some markets, and their high-pressure pumps and injectors are costlier and can contribute to low-speed pre-ignition in downsized turbocharged engines.

History

Direct injection was used in the 1955 Mercedes-Benz 300SL, the first production car with a gasoline direct injection engine, and in earlier aircraft engines, but cost limited its use. Mitsubishi's GDI engine of 1996 in the Galant/Legnum was the first modern mass-produced GDI system, followed by Toyota's D-4 and Volkswagen/Audi's FSI systems. Since the 2010s GDI has become the dominant fuel system for new gasoline cars, often combined with port injection in dual-injection systems to mitigate carbon build-up.
