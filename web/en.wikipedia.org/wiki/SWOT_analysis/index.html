SWOT analysis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

SWOT analysis (or SWOT matrix) is a strategic planning and strategic management technique used to help a person or organization identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats related to business competition or project planning. It is sometimes called situational assessment or situational analysis.

Overview

Strengths and weaknesses are internal factors — attributes of the organization itself, such as its resources, reputation, skills, and processes. Opportunities and threats are external factors arising from the environment, such as market trends, competitors, regulation, and economic conditions. A SWOT analysis is typically drawn as a two-by-two matrix, with each quadrant listing the relevant factors.

The technique is intended as an early stage of decision-making: by matching internal strengths against external opportunities, and by confronting weaknesses and threats, planners can build a strategy that exploits advantages and defends against risks.

History

The origins of SWOT are debated. It is often associated with work at the Stanford Research Institute in the 1960s and 1970s, led by Albert S. Humphrey, who studied why corporate planning at Fortune 500 companies failed. An earlier related framework, SOFT analysis, examined what was Satisfactory, an Opportunity, a Fault, or a Threat. SWOT became a standard element of business-school teaching and consulting practice.

Uses

SWOT analysis is used in business strategy, marketing planning, product development, and also in community organizations, public policy, and personal career planning. Variants include TOWS analysis, which reverses the order to emphasize strategy generation from the external environment inward.

Criticism

Critics note that SWOT analyses often generate long, unweighted lists without prioritization, that factors can be ambiguous or fit more than one category, and that the exercise by itself produces no strategy. Used carelessly it can become a substitute for deeper analysis rather than a starting point for it.
