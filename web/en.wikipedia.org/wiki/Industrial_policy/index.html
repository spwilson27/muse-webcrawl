Industrial policy

Industrial policy is the deliberate effort by a government to shape or promote the development of particular sectors of the economy, or of industry in general, through measures such as subsidies, tax incentives, tariffs, directed credit, public research funding, state ownership and regulation.

Rationale

Supporters argue that industrial policy can correct market failures, nurture infant industries, capture strategic capabilities in areas such as defence, energy and semiconductors, and speed transitions such as decarbonisation. Critics argue that governments are poor at "picking winners", that support invites rent-seeking, and that failures waste public money.

Examples

Post-war Japan's MITI, South Korea's support for chaebol-led heavy industry, and China's state-led manufacturing programmes are classic examples of successful (though debated) industrial strategy. In the 21st century, measures such as the United States' CHIPS and Science Act (2022) and the European Union's green industrial plans mark a revival of industrial policy in advanced economies.
