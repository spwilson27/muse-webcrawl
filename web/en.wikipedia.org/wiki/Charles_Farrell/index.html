Charles Farrell

Charles Farrell (August 9, 1900 – May 6, 1990) was an American stage, film and television actor, who also served as mayor of Palm Springs, California. He was popular as a leading man in the late 1920s and early 1930s, particularly in a series of romantic dramas opposite Janet Gaynor — among them 7th Heaven (1927), Street Angel (1928), and Lucky Star (1929) — and later played Vern Albright on the television series My Little Margie (1952–1955).

Career

Farrell began in Hollywood as an extra and stuntman before signing with Fox Film Corporation. His pairing with Janet Gaynor in Frank Borzage's 7th Heaven made both stars; they made twelve films together and were billed as "America's favorite lovebirds." Farrell's career declined with the advent of sound, though he continued in films through the 1930s and served in naval aviation during the Second World War.

Palm Springs and later life

Farrell settled in Palm Springs, co-founded the Palm Springs Racquet Club, and became a leading civic figure, serving as mayor of the city from 1947 to 1955. He returned to acting for the popular early television sitcom My Little Margie, playing the widowed father of Gale Storm's Margie. He later hosted The Charles Farrell Show (1956), filmed at the Racquet Club.
