Remittance

[Image: a migrant worker sending money home at a money-transfer shop]

A remittance is money sent by a migrant worker to family or others in their home country. Remittances are among the largest international financial flows to developing countries — officially recorded flows to low- and middle-income countries run at roughly US$650–670 billion a year in the early 2020s, exceeding foreign direct Investment and dwarfing foreign aid — and they form a credit in the current account (secondary income) of the balance of payments.

Significance and costs

India is the largest recipient (over $100 billion in recent years), followed by Mexico, China and the Philippines; for some economies (Nepal, Lebanon, Tonga, several Central American and Central Asian states) remittances exceed a quarter of GDP. Evidence links them to reduced Poverty, spending on health, education and housing, and counter-cyclical support in crises. The average cost of sending money remains around 6% — well above the UN Sustainable Development Goal target of 3% — because of fees, exchange margins and de-risking by banks; mobile money and digital channels are cutting costs. Hawala and other informal channels mean true flows are larger than recorded ones.
