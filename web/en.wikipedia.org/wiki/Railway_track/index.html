Railway track

A railway track (British English) or railroad track (American English) consists of rails, fasteners, sleepers (ties) and ballast (or slab track), plus the underlying formation. It is used to guide trains by constraining their flanged wheels to follow a fixed path with very low rolling resistance.

Structure

Two steel rails are set at a fixed distance apart, the track gauge — standard gauge is 1,435 mm (4 ft 8½ in) — and are supported on sleepers of timber, concrete or steel, which rest in a bed of crushed-stone ballast that distributes load and drains water. Modern main lines use continuously welded rail to eliminate the expansion gaps of jointed track. Slab track, in which rails are fixed to a solid concrete foundation, is used on many high-speed lines and metros.

Maintenance

Track geometry is monitored for alignment, gauge and cross level; maintenance includes tamping ballast, rail grinding and renewal of worn components.
