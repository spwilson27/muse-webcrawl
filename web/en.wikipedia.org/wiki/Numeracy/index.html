Numeracy

Numeracy is the ability to understand, reason with, and apply simple numerical concepts — the mathematical equivalent of literacy. It involves recognising and using numbers, arithmetic, proportions, percentages, and basic statistics in everyday life, at work, and in interpreting information such as graphs, risks, and financial products.

Definitions and assessment

The term was popularised by the 1959 Crowther Report in the United Kingdom. International surveys such as the OECD's Programme for the International Assessment of Adult Competencies (PIAAC) measure numeracy across countries, consistently finding large differences between and within nations and a strong association between numeracy and earnings, employment, and health outcomes. Research suggests roughly half of working-age adults in England have numeracy skills no better than those expected of a primary-school child.

Development and innumeracy

Humans and many animals possess an approximate number sense from infancy, but exact arithmetic is a cultural skill built by education. Innumeracy, the term popularised by mathematician John Allen Paulos in his 1988 book Innumeracy: Mathematical Illiteracy and its Consequences, describes the inability to reason with numbers and probability; it has been linked to poor financial decisions, susceptibility to misleading statistics in media and medicine, and difficulty weighing risks. Related concepts include health numeracy, statistical literacy, and the specific learning disorder dyscalculia.
