Brahma is the Hindu creator god, first member of the Trimūrti alongside Vishnu the preserver and Shiva the destroyer. He is depicted with four faces — gazing in the four directions — and four arms, seated on a lotus, with his consort Saraswati, goddess of learning, and his vehicle the swan (haṃsa). (He is not to be confused with Brahman, the impersonal absolute of Vedanta philosophy, nor with brāhmaṇa, the priestly class.)

Mythology and cosmology

In Purāṇic cosmology Brahma is born from a lotus springing from the navel of the sleeping Vishnu at the beginning of each cosmic cycle, and proceeds to create the worlds. Time itself is measured against his life: a day of Brahma (a kalpa) is 4.32 billion years, followed by a night of equal length in which creation dissolves; his hundred-year lifespan spans the existence of the universe before it is remade. His myths include the pride that cost him his fifth head (cut off by Shiva), the incest-tinged story of his desire for his daughter-creation, told and handled variously, and the lie about finding the top of Shiva's infinite pillar of fire, for which his worship was curtailed.

Worship

Paradoxically for a creator, Brahma has almost no independent cult: only a handful of temples are dedicated to him, most famously at Pushkar in Rajasthan, where an annual fair and pilgrimage centre on his temple and the sacred lake. Explanations offered within the tradition include curses from Shiva or from his consort Savitri. His functions are largely absorbed into the other great gods, while his name lives on in the priestly tradition and in the concept of the brahmānda, the "egg of Brahma" — the cosmos.
