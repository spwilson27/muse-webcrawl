EAA AirVenture Oshkosh (informally known simply as Oshkosh) is an annual air show and gathering of aviation enthusiasts held each summer at Wittman Regional Airport and adjacent Pioneer Airport in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, United States. Organised by the Experimental Aircraft Association (EAA), it is the world's largest fly-in event: more than 10,000 aircraft arrive each year, and during the show the airport's control tower becomes the busiest in the world. Attendance in recent years has approached or exceeded 650,000 people, with visitors from more than 90 countries.

Aircraft camping grounds at AirVenture, Wittman Regional Airport.

History

The EAA was founded in 1953 by Paul Poberezny in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and held its first fly-in that year at what is now Timmerman Airport, drawing about 150 visitors. The annual convention grew steadily, moving to Rockford, Illinois, in 1959, where it remained through the 1960s. In 1970, having outgrown Rockford, the convention moved to Oshkosh — chosen for its large airport and open land — and has been held there ever since. The event was officially renamed "EAA AirVenture Oshkosh" in 1998. The 2020 edition was cancelled because of the COVID-19 pandemic, the first cancellation in the event's history; it returned in 2021.

The event

AirVenture runs for seven days, typically beginning on the last Monday of July. Its scale is unmatched in general aviation:

  • More than 10,000 aircraft fly in, from homebuilts and warbirds to modern military jets; many visitors camp beneath their aircraft's wing.
  • Over 2,500 showplanes are registered for judging in categories including homebuilt, vintage, warbird, aerobatic and seaplane.
  • Roughly 15,000 camping sites fill the grounds known as Camp Scholler.
  • More than 1,400 forums, workshops and presentations cover building, restoring and flying aircraft.
  • Daily afternoon air shows and two night air shows feature aerobatic performers, military demonstration teams and one of the largest fireworks displays in the United States.

During the week, the Federal Aviation Administration staffs the Wittman tower with its best controllers, handling more movements than any other tower in the world during that period. Arriving pilots follow a special published procedure — famously including the visual approach along the Fisk railroad tracks — and receive instructions by rocking their wings in acknowledgement, since controllers transmit almost continuously.

Highlights and traditions

Major aircraft debuts are a tradition at Oshkosh: the Rutan VariEze (1975), the first public appearance of the Lockheed F-117 Nighthawk (1990), the B-2 Spirit, and numerous homebuilt designs were introduced there. Mass arrivals by type clubs — Bonanzas, Cessnas and others flying in formation by the dozen — open the event weekend. The seaplane base on Lake Winnebago hosts hundreds of floatplanes, and the ultralight area operates its own grass strip. Aircraft manufacturers, including Boeing, Airbus, Cessna and Garmin, use AirVenture for product announcements.

Economic impact

A University of Wisconsin–Oshkosh study estimated the event generates more than $170 million in economic activity for the Fox Valley region each year, supporting thousands of jobs. The EAA Aviation Museum, located on the grounds, is open year-round and houses more than 200 historic aircraft.
