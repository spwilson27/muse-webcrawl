List of Indian reservations in the United States

This is a list of Indian reservations in the United States. An Indian reservation is an area of land reserved for a tribe or tribes under treaty or other agreement with the United States, executive order, or federal statute, and managed by a Native American tribal government under the supervision of the U.S. Bureau of Indian Affairs. There are approximately 326 Indian reservations in the United States, associated with the 574 federally recognized tribes.

Overview

Reservations vary enormously in size and population. The largest is the Navajo Nation Reservation, spanning parts of Arizona, Utah, and New Mexico at over 27,000 square miles; other large reservations include the Uintah and Ouray in Utah, the Tohono O'odham Nation and Fort Apache in Arizona, and the Pine Ridge Reservation in South Dakota. Many reservations are small rancherias in California. The list gives each reservation's tribe, state, land area, and population.

Organization of the list

The full list is ordered alphabetically by reservation name, with columns for the associated federally recognized tribe(s), the state(s) in which the reservation lies, its total land area (including trust lands), and census population. Alaska Native villages and Oklahoma tribal statistical areas are covered separately.

History

The reservation system developed through nineteenth-century treaties, forced removals, and statutes such as the Indian Removal Act of 1830 and the Dawes Act of 1887, which broke up much reservation land into allotments. Modern reservations are governed by tribal governments exercising inherent sovereignty, subject to federal law.
