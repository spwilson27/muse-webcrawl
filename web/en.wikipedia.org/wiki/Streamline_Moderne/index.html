Streamline Moderne

Streamline Moderne is an international style of Art Deco architecture and design that emerged in the 1930s. It was inspired by aerodynamic design. Streamline architecture emphasized curving forms, long horizontal lines, and sometimes nautical elements. In industrial design, it was used in railroad locomotives, telephones, toasters, buses, appliances, and other devices to give the impression of sleekness and modernity.

The style was influenced by modern aerodynamic principles developed for aviation and ballistics, as well as the streamlined forms of ocean liners and airships. Architects applied the horizontal orientation, rounded corners, glass brick walls, and porthole windows of ships to buildings.

Characteristics

  • Smooth, plastered (often stucco) walls with rounded corners
  • Flat roofs with little or no ornamentation
  • Strong horizontal emphasis, with long lines and "speed whiskers"
  • Porthole windows, glass blocks, and neon lighting
  • Nautical references such as railings resembling those of ships

Examples

Notable examples include the Coca-Cola Building in Los Angeles, the Pan-Pacific Auditorium, Greyhound bus stations across the United States, the Normandie Hotel in San Juan, Puerto Rico, and numerous cinemas, diners, and railway stations of the 1930s and 1940s. The style also shaped the design of locomotives such as the streamlined steam trains of the New York Central Railroad.
