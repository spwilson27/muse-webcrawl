Cinderella (sports)

A Cinderella team celebrating an unexpected win

In American sports, a "Cinderella" team or competitor is one that achieves far greater success than expected, advancing deep into a tournament despite a low seeding or an unheralded reputation. The term alludes to the fairy tale of Cinderella, in which a neglected heroine is transformed and triumphs. The related expression "Cinderella story" or "Cinderella run" describes the sequence of upsets itself; the clock metaphor — the run ending when the clock "strikes midnight" — is often invoked when such a team is finally eliminated.

Usage

The term is particularly associated with the NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament ("March Madness"), where mid-major schools regularly defeat power-conference opponents. Famous Cinderella runs include the 1983 NC State Wolfpack, who won the national championship as a sixth seed; the 1985 Villanova Wildcats, an eighth seed and the lowest seed ever to win the title; George Mason's 2006 Final Four run as an eleventh seed; UMBC's 2018 upset of top overall seed Virginia, the first victory by a sixteenth seed over a first seed; and Saint Peter's 2022 run to the Elite Eight as a fifteenth seed.

The concept appears in other sports as well: Leicester City's 2015–16 Premier League title, won against bookmakers' odds of 5000–1, is frequently described as the greatest Cinderella story in football; wild-card and low-seeded teams winning American professional championships, and underdog nations advancing at the FIFA World Cup, attract the same description.

Cultural significance

Cinderella runs are credited with much of the appeal of knockout tournaments, drawing casual audiences, inflating television ratings, and boosting applications and donations to the schools involved — the so-called "Flutie effect". Critics note that the label can be applied loosely and that, in expanded tournaments, deep runs by lower seeds are a statistical expectation rather than a miracle.
