Rescue of Jews during the Holocaust

During the Holocaust, some individuals, groups and governments rescued Jews from Nazi persecution and genocide, at great personal risk. Rescuers hid Jews in homes, convents and cellars, forged documents, smuggled children to safety, and organised escapes to neutral countries.

Notable rescues

In October 1943 the Danish resistance evacuated some 7,200 of Denmark's Jews by boat to neutral Sweden. The Swedish diplomat Raoul Wallenberg issued protective passports in Budapest in 1944; Oskar Schindler employed and protected about 1,200 Jews in his factories; and the Kindertransport brought some 10,000 mostly Jewish children to Britain before the war. Whole communities, such as the French village of Le Chambon-sur-Lignon, sheltered refugees.

Righteous Among the Nations

Israel's Yad Vashem honours non-Jewish rescuers as 'Righteous Among the Nations'; more than 28,000 individuals have been recognised, including the largest national groups from Poland and the Netherlands.
