Celestial coordinate system

In astronomy, a celestial coordinate system is a spherical coordinate system used to specify the positions of satellites, planets, stars, galaxies, and other celestial objects, as viewed from a given vantage point. Coordinates are given by a pair of angular measurements relative to a chosen fundamental plane and direction, analogously to latitude and longitude on Earth.

Coordinate systems

Equatorial system

The equatorial system uses right ascension and declination, projected from Earth's equator and poles onto the celestial sphere. It is the standard system for star catalogues, specified for a given epoch such as J2000.0.

Horizontal system

The horizontal (alt-azimuth) system uses altitude and azimuth relative to the observer's horizon, convenient for pointing telescopes but varying with time and location.

Ecliptic and galactic systems

The ecliptic system is based on the plane of Earth's orbit and is natural for Solar System bodies; the galactic system is based on the plane of the Milky Way and is used in galactic astronomy.

Transformations

Converting between systems requires spherical trigonometry and corrections for precession, nutation, aberration, and atmospheric refraction. Modern practice relies on the International Celestial Reference System and its realisation, the International Celestial Reference Frame.
