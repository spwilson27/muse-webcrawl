Senusret III (also written Sesostris III) was a pharaoh of the Twelfth Dynasty of ancient Egypt, reigning for roughly four decades in the nineteenth century BC. He is regarded as one of the most powerful kings of the Middle Kingdom, and his reign marks its military and administrative high point.

Reign

Senusret III campaigned extensively in Nubia, leading at least four expeditions that pushed Egypt's southern frontier to the Second Cataract at Semna. There he built a chain of massive mudbrick fortresses and set up boundary stelae forbidding Nubians to pass north except for trade. He is also credited with digging a canal through the First Cataract at Aswan to ease the movement of ships and troops. Within Egypt he reorganized provincial administration, curtailing the power of the regional nomarchs whose large tombs largely disappear from the record during his reign.

Pyramid and legacy

His pyramid was built at Dahshur, north of his father's; its complex included the burials of royal women whose jewellery, found in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, is among the finest of the Middle Kingdom. Statues of Senusret III are famous in Egyptian art for their strikingly weary, careworn expressions, departing from the idealized imagery of earlier kings. In later tradition he was remembered as a great conqueror and lawgiver, was deified in Nubia, and likely contributed to the Greek legend of Sesostris.
