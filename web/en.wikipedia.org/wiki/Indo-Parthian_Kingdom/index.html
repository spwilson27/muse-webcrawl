Indo-Parthian Kingdom

Period: c. 12 BCE – c. 130 CE

Founder: Gondophares I

Territory: Parts of modern Iran, Afghanistan, Pakistan, and northwest India

The Indo-Parthian Kingdom was a Parthian kingdom founded by Gondophares around 12 BCE. Also called the Gondopharid dynasty, it ruled over parts of present-day eastern Iran, Afghanistan, Pakistan, and northern India.

History

Gondophares appears to have taken over territories from the Indo-Scythian king Azes II. In later Christian tradition, Gondophares is associated with the visit of the apostle Thomas to India. The kingdom later lost ground to the expanding Kushan Empire under Kujula Kadphises and Kanishka.

Coinage

Indo-Parthian coinage, known from hoards and excavations, typically shows the king on horseback and blends Parthian, Greek, and Indian motifs, reflecting the kingdom's position on Silk Road trade routes.
