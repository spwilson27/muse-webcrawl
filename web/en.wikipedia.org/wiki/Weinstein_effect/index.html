Weinstein effect


The scandal prompted a global reckoning over sexual harassment in the workplace.

The Weinstein effect is a global trend in which people come forward to accuse famous or powerful men of sexual misconduct. The term came into use in October 2017, when media outlets reported numerous sexual abuse allegations against film producer Harvey Weinstein. The reports were followed by a wave of similar allegations against other prominent figures across entertainment, media, politics, technology, and other industries, many of whom subsequently lost their positions.

The effect is closely associated with the Me Too movement, which encouraged survivors of sexual harassment and assault to share their experiences. Activist Tarana Burke had coined the phrase "Me Too" years earlier, but it went massively viral after actress Alyssa Milano encouraged its use on social media in the wake of the Weinstein reports.

Background

On October 5, 2017, The New York Times published an investigation by Jodi Kantor and Megan Twohey detailing decades of allegations against Weinstein, including settlements with accusers. Days later, The New Yorker published Ronan Farrow's reporting, which included allegations of rape. Weinstein was fired from The Weinstein Company, expelled from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, and later convicted of sex crimes in New York and California.

Impact

In the months that followed, allegations surfaced against numerous high-profile men, and the phrase "Weinstein effect" was used by outlets such as USA Today to describe the phenomenon of powerful figures facing consequences once considered unlikely. The effect extended internationally, prompting debates about workplace protections, nondisclosure agreements, statutes of limitations, and the treatment of accusers.
