Art market

Segments: Primary, secondary (auction, dealer)
Major hubs: New York, London, Hong Kong, Paris
Major houses: Sotheby's, Christie's

The art market is the marketplace of buyers and sellers trading commodities, services and works of art. It comprises a primary market, where works enter the market for the first time through galleries and dealers, and a secondary market of resale through auction houses, dealers and private sales.

Structure

Galleries represent living artists and set primary prices; auction houses such as Sotheby's and Christie's dominate high-value resale, while art fairs (Art Basel, Frieze) concentrate dealers and collectors. Online sales grew sharply after 2020. The market is notably opaque: private prices are rarely disclosed, and attribution, provenance and condition strongly affect value.

Economics

Global sales have been estimated in the tens of billions of US dollars annually, concentrated in the United States, China and the United Kingdom. A tiny fraction of artists accounts for a large share of auction value. Art is bought for aesthetic, social and investment motives, and indices track repeat sales despite high transaction costs and illiquidity.

Issues

Debates concern money laundering and sanctions evasion, looted antiquities and restitution, forgery, speculation and flipping of young artists' work, and the brief boom and collapse of NFT art in 2021–22. See Art auction and Provenance.
