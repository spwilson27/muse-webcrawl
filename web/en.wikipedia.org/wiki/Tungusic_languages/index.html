The Tungusic languages (also known as Manchu-Tungus and Tungus) form a language family spoken in Eastern Siberia and Manchuria by Tungusic peoples. Many Tungusic languages are endangered, and the long-term future of the family is not assured. There are about a dozen living languages in the family, with in total roughly 55,000 native speakers.

Classification

Linguists commonly divide Tungusic into a Northern branch — including Evenki (the most widely spoken, spread across a vast area of Siberia), Even, Negidal and Oroqen — and a Southern branch, including Nanai, Ulch, Orok, and the Manchuric group: Manchu itself and its close relatives Xibe and the extinct Jurchen, the language of the Jin dynasty. Manchu was the language of the Manchu rulers of Qing-dynasty China, but today has very few native speakers, surviving most vigorously as Xibe in Xinjiang.

Features

Tungusic languages are agglutinative, with vowel harmony, subject–object–verb word order, and extensive systems of suffixation and converbs. The family was once grouped with Turkic and Mongolic in the proposed Altaic family, a hypothesis now largely rejected by mainstream historical linguists, who attribute the similarities to prolonged areal contact.
