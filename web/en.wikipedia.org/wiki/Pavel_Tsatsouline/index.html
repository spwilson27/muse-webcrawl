Pavel Tsatsouline (born 23 August 1969) is a Belarusian-American strength coach and author. A former physical training instructor for the Soviet special forces (Spetsnaz), Tsatsouline is credited with introducing the Russian kettlebell to the West in the late 1990s, sparking a worldwide revival of kettlebell training. He founded the StrongFirst school of strength and previously ran the Russian Kettlebell Challenge (RKC) instructor certification.

Background

Tsatsouline was born in Minsk and studied sports science at the Institute of Physical Culture there. In the Soviet Union he served as a physical training instructor for Spetsnaz units, and after emigrating to the United States in the 1990s he consulted for American military and law-enforcement special operations units, including Marine Corps and Secret Service personnel.

Kettlebell popularisation

Tsatsouline's 1998 article "Vodka, Pickle Juice, Kettlebell Lifting, and Other Russian Pastimes" in MILO magazine introduced American strength athletes to the kettlebell. Working with publisher John Du Cane of Dragon Door, he began manufacturing Russian-style kettlebells in the United States and in 2001 launched the RKC, the first kettlebell instructor certification in the West. His books The Russian Kettlebell Challenge (2001) and Enter the Kettlebell! (2006) codified the hardstyle method — emphasising maximal tension, the swing, get-up, press, snatch and goblet squat. After a split with Dragon Door in 2012 he founded StrongFirst, which continues his certification system.

Other work

Tsatsouline's other books include Power to the People! (1999) on minimalist barbell strength, The Naked Warrior (2003) on bodyweight training, Relax into Stretch on flexibility, and Kettlebell Simple & Sinister (2013), which popularised a simple program of swings and get-ups. His training philosophy stresses strength as a skill, frequent low-repetition practice ("greasing the groove"), and anti-glycolytic endurance methods drawn from Soviet sports science.
