Arena Stage

Arena Stage at the Mead Center for American Theater, Washington, D.C.

Arena Stage is a not-for-profit regional theatre company located in Washington, D.C. Founded in 1950, it was one of the first nonprofit theatres in the United States and a pioneer of the American regional theatre movement. In 1976 it became the first regional theatre to receive the Tony Award for regional theatre.

History

Arena Stage was founded by Zelda Fichandler, Tom Fichandler and Edward Mangum, opening in August 1950 in a converted movie theatre. It took its name from its original theatre-in-the-round configuration. In 1961 the company moved to a purpose-built home in Southwest Washington, which included the Fichandler Stage, a theatre in the round, and later the Kreeger Theater, a modified proscenium space.

Under Zelda Fichandler, the company maintained a resident acting ensemble — unusual among American theatres — and championed new American plays alongside classics and the first major American productions of European dramatists. Productions originating at Arena Stage have regularly transferred to Broadway and beyond; the theatre was an early champion of works that went on to win Pulitzer Prizes and Tony Awards. Fichandler was succeeded as artistic director by Molly Smith in 1998.

The Mead Center

A major renovation and expansion, completed in 2010 to a design by architect Bing Thom, enclosed the two existing theatres and added a third, the Arlene and Robert Kogod Cradle, a 200-seat experimental space, under a sweeping glass envelope. The complex, renamed Arena Stage at the Mead Center for American Theater, kept the historic stages intact within the new building.

Facilities

  • Fichandler Stage — theatre in the round, approximately 680 seats
  • Kreeger Theater — modified proscenium, approximately 510 seats
  • Arlene and Robert Kogod Cradle — flexible experimental space, approximately 200 seats
