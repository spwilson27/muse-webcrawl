Carnival of Venice

The Carnival of Venice (Italian: Carnevale di Venezia) is an annual festival held in Venice, Italy, ending on Shrove Tuesday, the day before the start of Lent on Ash Wednesday. It is famous worldwide for its elaborate masks.

History

The carnival is documented from 1094, when the Doge Vitale Faliero mentioned public entertainments before Lent. Mask-wearing became so widespread that the Venetian Republic repeatedly legislated to restrict it. After the fall of the Republic in 1797, the Austrians banned the festivities; the carnival virtually disappeared until its revival in 1979, when the Italian government encouraged its return as part of efforts to restore Venetian culture.

Masks

Traditional masks include the white bauta (with its protruding chin allowing the wearer to eat and speak), the oval moretta, the beaked plague-doctor (medico della peste), and lavish modern creations. Mask makers (maschereri) have held a guild statute since 1436.

Events

Festivities span roughly two weeks: the Flight of the Angel from the campanile of St Mark's, costume contests in Piazza San Marco, the Festa delle Marie, water parades on the Cannaregio canal, and masked balls in palazzi.
