Education in Michigan

Education in Michigan comprises public and private primary, secondary and higher education institutions across the U.S. state of Michigan. The state is home to more than 1.4 million public school students and several major research universities, including the University of Michigan.

Primary and secondary

Public education is administered by local school districts and intermediate school districts, overseen by the Michigan Department of Education and the elected State Board of Education. Michigan also has a large charter school sector, called public school academies, established in the 1990s. School funding was substantially restructured by Proposal A in 1994, which shifted funding from local property taxes to state sources.

Higher education

The state has 15 public universities, including the University of Michigan, Michigan State University and Wayne State University, along with numerous community colleges and private institutions such as Calvin University and Hillsdale College.
