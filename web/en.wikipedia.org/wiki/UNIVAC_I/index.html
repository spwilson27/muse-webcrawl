UNIVAC I

The UNIVAC I (Universal Automatic Computer I) was the first general-purpose electronic digital computer designed for business and administrative use — and the first American computer built for commercial sale. It was designed by J. Presper Eckert and John Mauchly, the inventors of the ENIAC, through their Eckert–Mauchly Computer Corporation, which was acquired by Remington Rand in 1950. The first UNIVAC I was accepted by the United States Census Bureau on March 31, 1951, and dedicated on June 14, 1951.

Technical description

The UNIVAC I used 5,200 vacuum tubes, weighed about 13 tonnes, consumed 125 kW of power, and occupied over 35 square metres of floor space. Its main memory consisted of mercury delay lines storing 1,000 words of 12 characters each. Unusually for the time it used magnetic tape units (the Uniservo) for bulk storage, and its input/output included a typewriter-like console and Unityper. It could perform roughly 1,905 operations per second. In total, 46 UNIVAC I systems were built and delivered, at prices exceeding one million dollars each, to government agencies, large corporations and universities.

Public fame

The UNIVAC became a household name on election night, November 4, 1952, when a UNIVAC I used by CBS correctly predicted Dwight D. Eisenhower's landslide victory over Adlai Stevenson from early returns — a forecast so contrary to expectations that it was initially withheld from broadcast. The feat made "Univac" a generic term for computers in popular usage for years afterward.
