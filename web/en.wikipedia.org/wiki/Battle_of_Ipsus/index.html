Battle of Ipsus

The Battle of Ipsus was fought in 301 BC in Phrygia, Asia Minor, between the Diadochi — the successors of Alexander the Great. The aged Antigonus I Monophthalmus and his son Demetrius were defeated by a coalition of Seleucus, Lysimachus, Cassander and Ptolemy; Antigonus was killed, ending the last realistic attempt to reunite Alexander's empire.

Background

Antigonus, in his eighties, controlled Asia Minor and the Levant and aimed at supremacy over the other successors. Seleucus brought hundreds of war elephants obtained from Chandragupta Maurya in exchange for territory in India.

Battle

Demetrius's cavalry routed the opposing horse under Antiochus but pursued too far; Seleucus's elephants formed a barrier preventing his return. Meanwhile the allied phalanx pressed Antigonus's infantry, many of whom surrendered or fled. Antigonus, hoping his son would return, was killed by javelins.

Aftermath

The victors partitioned Antigonus's realm: Lysimachus took much of Asia Minor, Seleucus took Syria and the east, and the Hellenistic kingdoms took the shape they would broadly keep for a century. Demetrius escaped with part of his fleet and remained a restless power.
