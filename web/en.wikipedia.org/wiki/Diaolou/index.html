Diaolou

LocationKaiping and surrounding counties, Guangdong, China
BuiltMainly late 19th – early 20th century
UNESCO World HeritageInscribed 2007 ("Kaiping Diaolou and Villages")

Diaolou (simplified Chinese: 碉楼; traditional Chinese: 碉樓; pinyin: diāolóu) are fortified multi-storey watchtowers, generally made of reinforced concrete, found mainly in Kaiping and neighbouring counties of Guangdong province in southern China. Some 1,800 diaolou survive in the region. They were built primarily from the late 19th century to the 1930s, funded largely by overseas Chinese emigrants, and served as defensive refuges against bandits and floods as well as prestigious residences.

History

Multi-storey defensive towers had existed in the area since the Ming dynasty, but the great age of diaolou construction came with the wealth sent home by emigrants who had left Guangdong for North America, Australia and Southeast Asia during the gold rushes and railway-building era. Returning emigrants and remittances financed towers that combined Chinese architectural elements with Western features — Greek columns, Roman arches, Gothic and Baroque details — in a distinctive fusion style.

Types

Diaolou are commonly grouped into three types: communal towers built by several families as shared refuges; residential towers built by a single wealthy family, combining defence with comfortable living quarters; and watchtowers built at village entrances or high points as lookouts. The Ruishi Diaolou in Jinjiangli village, built in 1921, is among the finest residential examples, while the Zili Village cluster is the best-known group of towers amid rice fields.

Heritage status

In 2007, UNESCO inscribed "Kaiping Diaolou and Villages" on the World Heritage List, recognising the towers as an outstanding and complex fusion of Chinese and Western architectural traditions and as testimony to the role of emigration in the region's history. The towers have since become a significant tourist attraction and a symbol of the overseas Chinese connection to Guangdong.
