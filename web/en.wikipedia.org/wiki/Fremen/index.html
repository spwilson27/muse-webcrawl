The Fremen are a fictional people in Frank Herbert's Dune universe. They inhabit the deserts of the planet Arrakis, the only known source of the spice melange, and are renowned as unsurpassed desert fighters and survivalists, shaped by millennia of hardship and by their Zensunni religious heritage.


Fremen crossing the deep desert

Origins and culture

The Fremen descend from Zensunni Wanderers — religious exiles who migrated across many worlds before reaching Arrakis. In the desert they developed a culture entirely organised around water conservation: they wear stillsuits that reclaim bodily moisture, live in cave communities called sietches, store water in underground caches (qanats), and observe strict rituals, including reclaiming the water of their dead. Their eyes are stained entirely blue ("the eyes of Ibad") by chronic exposure to spice.

Society and warfare

Fremen society is tribal, led by naibs such as Stilgar of Sietch Tabr. Matters of leadership and honour are settled by ritual single combat with the crysknife, a blade made from the tooth of a sandworm. Fremen ride the giant sandworms of Arrakis using maker hooks, harvest spice, and pursue a secret long-term plan — guided by planetologist Liet-Kynes — to terraform Arrakis into a greener world. Despite their small numbers, their ferocity makes them superior even to the Emperor's Sardaukar troops.

Role in the novels

In Dune (1965), Paul Atreides and his mother Jessica take refuge with the Fremen; Paul, as Muad'Dib, unites the tribes and leads a jihad that overthrows the Emperor. Fremen figures such as Chani and Stilgar remain central through the later novels, as the greening of Arrakis steadily erodes the old Fremen way of life.
