Jerry Jones

Born: October 13, 1942, Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Occupation: Businessman; owner, president and general manager of the Dallas Cowboys
Known for: Owning the Dallas Cowboys since 1989

Jerral Wayne Jones Sr. (born October 13, 1942) is an American businessman who has owned the Dallas Cowboys of the National Football League (NFL) since 1989, also serving as the team's president and general manager. Under his ownership the Cowboys won three Super Bowls in the 1990s and became one of the most valuable sports franchises in the world.

Early life and business career

Jones was born in Los Angeles and raised in North Little Rock, Arkansas. He played football at the University of Arkansas, where he was an offensive lineman and co-captain of the Razorbacks' 1964 national championship team, alongside future coaching greats Jimmy Johnson and Barry Switzer. After graduating, he built a fortune in oil and gas exploration with his company Jones Oil and Land Lease, and in insurance.

Dallas Cowboys

In February 1989 Jones purchased the Cowboys from H. R. "Bum" Bright for $140 million. He immediately fired legendary head coach Tom Landry and hired his former college teammate Jimmy Johnson, a move initially unpopular with fans. The team won Super Bowls XXVII, XXVIII, and XXX (the last under Barry Switzer) in the 1990s. Jones is known as one of the NFL's most hands-on owners, serving as his own general manager, and as an influential figure in league business, marketing, and television negotiations.

In 2009 the Cowboys opened AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas, a landmark venue noted for its enormous video board. The franchise's value under Jones has been estimated in excess of $9 billion. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2017 as a contributor.
