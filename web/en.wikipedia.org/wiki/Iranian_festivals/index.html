Iranian festivals

Iranian festivals consist of ancient cultural celebrations, many of Zoroastrian origin, that have been kept alive by Iranians of all religions for thousands of years, together with the public holidays of the Islamic Republic. The festivals closely follow the Solar Hijri calendar and the cycle of the seasons.

Principal festivals

  • Nowruz — the New Year, beginning on the vernal equinox (around March 20–21). Families clean their homes, set the haft-seen table with seven items beginning with the Persian letter sin, exchange visits, and on the thirteenth day picnic outdoors for Sizdah Bedar.
  • Chaharshanbe Suri — the festival of fire held on the eve of the last Wednesday before Nowruz, when bonfires are lit and jumped over while chanting "give me your redness, take away my pallor".
  • Yalda Night (Shab-e Yalda) — the winter solstice celebration, the longest night of the year, spent with family eating pomegranate and watermelon and reading the poetry of Hafez.
  • Mehregan — the autumn festival of thanksgiving associated with the deity Mithra, second in importance to Nowruz in ancient times.
  • Sadeh — a mid-winter fire festival held fifty days before Nowruz, maintained especially by Zoroastrian communities.
  • Tirgan — a midsummer water festival celebrating rain and the legend of Arash the Archer.

Religious observances

Official holidays also include Islamic feasts such as Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Ashura and Tasua (commemorating the martyrdom of Imam Hussein at Karbala), and the Prophet's birthday, as well as national days such as Islamic Republic Day and Oil Nationalisation Day. Because Islamic holidays follow the lunar calendar, they migrate through the solar year.
