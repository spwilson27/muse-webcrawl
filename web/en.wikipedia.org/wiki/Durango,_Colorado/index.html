Durango is a home rule city and the county seat of La Plata County, Colorado, United States. Located in the Animas River valley in southwestern Colorado, it is the most populous city in the county, with a population of about 19,000 at the 2020 census. The city is named after Durango, Mexico, which in turn took its name from Durango in Spain.

History

Durango was founded in 1880 by the Denver and Rio Grande Railroad as a depot town serving the mining district of Silverton to the north. The railroad reached Silverton in 1882, and Durango grew as a smelting and supply centre for the San Juan Mountains' gold and silver mines. A smelter operated in the town for decades, processing ore from throughout the region.

Geography and climate

The city sits at an elevation of about 6,512 feet (1,985 m) where the Animas River emerges from the San Juan Mountains onto the Colorado Plateau. It has a semi-arid high-desert climate with cold, snowy winters and warm summers. Nearby attractions include Mesa Verde National Park, the San Juan National Forest and the Purgatory Resort ski area.

Transportation and culture

Durango is the southern terminus of the Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad, a heritage railway that operates the steam-hauled line built in 1882 and is one of Colorado's most popular tourist attractions. The city is also home to Fort Lewis College and serves as a hub for mountain biking — it hosted the first UCI Mountain Bike World Championships in 1990 — rafting, skiing and other outdoor recreation.
