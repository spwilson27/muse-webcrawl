Multivariate normal distribution

The multivariate normal distribution is a generalisation of the one-dimensional normal distribution to higher dimensions. A random vector is said to be k-variate normally distributed if every linear combination of its k components has a univariate normal distribution. It is a standard model in multivariate statistics, used in discriminant analysis, regression, and Gaussian processes.

Definition

A random vector X = (X1, …, Xk)T has a multivariate normal distribution with mean vector μ and covariance matrix Σ, written X ~ N(μ, Σ), if its probability density function is

f(x) = (2π)−k/2 det(Σ)−1/2 exp( −½ (x − μ)T Σ−1 (x − μ) ).

The exponent involves the squared Mahalanobis distance from x to μ; contours of constant density are ellipsoids centred at the mean.

Properties

Much of the distribution's tractability comes from its closure under affine transformations: if Y = AX + b, then Y is again multivariate normal. Marginal distributions and conditional distributions of subsets of variables are also normal. For jointly normal variables, zero correlation between two components implies their independence — a special property not shared by distributions in general.

Estimation

Maximum likelihood estimates of the mean and covariance are the sample mean and the (biased) sample covariance matrix. Tests of multivariate normality include Mardia's test and the Henze–Zirkler test; samples can be generated by applying the Cholesky decomposition of Σ to independent standard normal variates.
