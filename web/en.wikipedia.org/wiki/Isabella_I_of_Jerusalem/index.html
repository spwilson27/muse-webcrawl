Isabella I of Jerusalem

Reign: 1190/1192 – 1205

Born: c. 1172, Nablus

Died: 5 April 1205, Acre

House: Anjou

Isabella I (c. 1172 – 5 April 1205) was queen of Jerusalem from 1190 to her death. She was the daughter of King Amalric I of Jerusalem and his second wife, Maria Komnene. She became heir to the throne after the death of her half-sister Sibylla during the Third Crusade.

Marriages and reign

Isabella was married four times, each marriage shaped by the politics of the crusader kingdom. Her first husband, Humphrey IV of Toron, was set aside so she could marry Conrad of Montferrat, who was assassinated in 1192 days after being elected king. She then married Henry II of Champagne, and after his death in 1197, Aimery of Lusignan, king of Cyprus, uniting the two crowns in practice.

Succession

Isabella's daughter by Conrad, Maria of Montferrat, succeeded her in 1205. Through her daughters, Isabella's descendants included future rulers of Jerusalem, Cyprus, and Armenia, making her a central figure in the dynastic history of the Latin East.
