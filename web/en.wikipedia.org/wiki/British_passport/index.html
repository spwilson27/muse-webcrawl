British passport

A British passport is a travel document issued by the United Kingdom to British nationals. Since 2020 it has been issued in a dark blue cover bearing the royal coat of arms, returning to the colour used before 1988. Passports are issued by HM Passport Office, an agency of the Home Office, and in the Crown Dependencies and overseas territories by their respective governments.

The blue British passport introduced from 2020.

History

British passports in recognisably modern form date from 1915, when the first photographic passport was introduced. The standard blue book-style passport was created in 1921 following a League of Nations standard. In 1988 the UK adopted the burgundy machine-readable passport in the European Community format; biometric e-passports with a contactless chip followed in 2006. After the Brexit referendum, the words "European Union" were removed from covers in 2019, and the blue design — now made from polycarbonate data pages — was phased in from March 2020.

Types and entitlement

Different classes of British nationality (British citizens, British Overseas Territories citizens, British Overseas citizens, British subjects and others) receive passports with endorsements reflecting their right of abode. Only British citizens' passports confer the right to live and work in the UK. Inside the front cover appears the traditional request from the Secretary of State "in the name of His Majesty" to allow the bearer to pass freely without let or hindrance.
