The Toyota Corolla (Japanese: トヨタ・カローラ) is a series of compact cars manufactured and marketed globally by Toyota since 1966. The nameplate is one of the most successful in automotive history: in 1997 the Corolla became the best-selling nameplate in the world, surpassing the Volkswagen Beetle, and cumulative sales passed 50 million units in 2021. "Corolla" is Latin for "small crown", fitting Toyota's naming theme for its sedans.

History

Introduced in November 1966, the first-generation Corolla (E10) was a small rear-wheel-drive sedan aimed at the Japanese public, and its success helped motorize Japan. Exports established the model in North America, Europe and Australia. Later generations moved to front-wheel drive with the fifth generation (E80, 1983) for most models, though the rear-drive AE86 coupe variants of that generation became cult classics.

Successive generations — E90 (1987), E100 (1991), E110 (1995), E120 (2000), E140 (2006), E160/E170 (2012) and the E210 twelfth generation (2018) — progressively added size, safety equipment and refinement. The current range includes sedan, hatchback and estate ("Touring Sports") body styles, hybrid powertrains, the GR Corolla high-performance model, and the Corolla Cross crossover derivative.

Production

Corollas have been manufactured in Japan and in plants in the United States, Canada, Brazil, China, Turkey, South Africa, Thailand, the United Kingdom and many other countries, often under joint ventures. Its reputation for reliability and low running costs has made it a staple of private buyers and fleets alike.
