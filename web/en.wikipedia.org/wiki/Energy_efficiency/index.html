Efficient energy use

Efficient energy use, or energy efficiency, is the process of reducing the amount of energy required to provide products and services. There are many technologies and methods available that are more energy efficient than conventional systems. For example, insulating a building allows it to use less heating and cooling energy to achieve and maintain a thermal comfort. The goal is to reduce the amount of energy input to a system without negatively affecting its outputs.

Overview

Improving energy efficiency lowers energy costs, reduces greenhouse gas emissions and other pollution, and decreases dependence on imported fuels. According to the International Energy Agency, improved energy efficiency in buildings, industrial processes and transportation could reduce a large share of the world's energy needs and help control global emissions of carbon dioxide. Energy efficiency and renewable energy are often described as the twin pillars of sustainable energy policy.

Appliances and buildings

Modern appliances such as refrigerators, heat pumps and LED lighting use far less electricity than earlier models, driven by labelling schemes such as Energy Star and the EU energy label, and by minimum efficiency standards. In buildings, measures include insulation, high-performance windows, efficient heating and cooling, and passive design. Whole-building standards such as Passivhaus reduce heating demand dramatically.

Transport and industry

In transport, efficiency measures include fuel-economy standards, electric vehicles, lightweight materials and modal shift to public transport, walking and cycling. In industry, combined heat and power, efficient motors, waste-heat recovery and process optimisation are major sources of savings. Economists note the rebound effect — efficiency gains can partly be offset by increased use — and the Jevons paradox, though direct rebound is usually well below 100%.
