Louis Leo Prima (December 7, 1910 – August 24, 1978) was an American trumpeter, singer, entertainer and bandleader. A flamboyant performer rooted in New Orleans jazz, he led successful swing and jump-blues bands and, in the 1950s, became a star of the Las Vegas lounge scene with his wife Keely Smith and saxophonist Sam Butera's band, the Witnesses.

Early life and career

Prima was born in New Orleans to Sicilian immigrant parents. He learned violin, then switched to cornet, and began leading bands in the 1920s. In the 1930s his New Orleans Gang combo became popular in New York, and he wrote the swing standard "Sing Sing Sing", later made famous by Benny Goodman.

Big band and Vegas years

In the 1940s Prima led a popular big band with hits such as "Angelina" and "Civilization (Bongo, Bongo, Bongo)". In 1954 he teamed with singer Keely Smith and Sam Butera to create a wildly energetic lounge act at the Desert Inn in Las Vegas. Their recordings — including "That Old Black Magic", "Just a Gigolo / I Ain't Got Nobody", "Jump, Jive, an' Wail" and "Buona Sera" — became signature songs.

Later work

Prima voiced King Louie the orangutan in Disney's animated film The Jungle Book (1967), singing "I Wan'na Be Like You". He remained a popular live act until surgery for a brain tumour in 1976 left him in a coma; he died in 1978.

Legacy

Prima's recordings influenced later artists including David Lee Roth, who covered his medley of "Just a Gigolo / I Ain't Got Nobody", and Brian Setzer, whose revival recording of "Jump, Jive an' Wail" won a Grammy. He was inducted into the Big Band and Jazz Hall of Fame.
