Kew is a district in the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames, in southwest London, England. Its population at the 2011 census was 11,436. Kew is the location of the Royal Botanic Gardens ("Kew Gardens"), now a World Heritage Site, which includes Kew Palace. Kew is also the home of important historical buildings such as the National Archives and St Anne's Church.

Kew sits beside a bend of the River Thames, opposite Brentford, and is connected to the north bank by Kew Bridge. It is known for its village-like atmosphere around Kew Green, its Victorian and Edwardian housing, and Kew railway station on the District line and London Overground.

History

Kew is first recorded in the 14th century as Cayho. It was a rural hamlet until it became fashionable in the 18th century, when Frederick, Prince of Wales, and his wife Augusta developed the royal gardens; Augusta founded the botanic garden in 1759 and commissioned structures including the Great Pagoda. Kew Palace was a residence of George III. The gardens passed to the nation in 1840 and grew into the world's leading botanic research institution.
