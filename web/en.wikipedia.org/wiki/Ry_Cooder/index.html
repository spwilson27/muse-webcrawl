Ryland Peter Cooder (born 15 March 1947) is an American musician, songwriter, film score composer, record producer, and writer. A multi-instrumentalist, he is celebrated for his slide guitar work, his deep engagement with American roots music, and his collaborations with traditional musicians from many countries. Rolling Stone ranked him eighth on its list of "The 100 Greatest Guitarists of All Time" in 2003, and in 2011 ranked him 31st.

Early career

Born in Los Angeles and raised in Santa Monica, Cooder began playing guitar at age three and lost the sight in his left eye in a childhood accident. As a teenager he played in blues and folk clubs, and in 1966–67 he played with Taj Mahal in the Rising Sons. He then worked with Captain Beefheart on Safe as Milk (1967) and became an in-demand session musician, contributing to recordings by the Rolling Stones (including Let It Bleed and Sticky Fingers), Randy Newman, Little Feat, and Van Morrison.

Solo career

Cooder's self-titled debut album (1970) began a series of records exploring American vernacular music: Into the Purple Valley (1972), Paradise and Lunch (1974), Chicken Skin Music (1976), which fused Tex-Mex and Hawaiian music, and the digitally recorded Bop till You Drop (1979). Later solo works include the acclaimed social-commentary albums Chávez Ravine (2005), My Name Is Buddy (2007), I, Flathead (2008), Pull Up Some Dust and Sit Down (2011), and The Prodigal Son (2018), as well as duo records with his son, drummer Joachim Cooder, and with banjoist Ricky Skaggs and others.

Film scores and world music

Cooder composed celebrated film scores, above all for Wim Wenders's Paris, Texas (1984), whose sparse slide guitar became iconic, and for The Long Riders (1980), Southern Comfort (1981), Crossroads (1986), and Johnny Handsome (1989).

His global collaborations include A Meeting by the River (1993) with Indian slide guitarist Vishwa Mohan Bhatt and Talking Timbuktu (1994) with Malian guitarist Ali Farka Touré, both Grammy winners. In 1996 he travelled to Havana to record with veteran Cuban musicians, producing the album Buena Vista Social Club (1997) — one of the best-selling world music albums of all time — and Wenders's 1999 documentary of the same name, which revived the careers of Ibrahim Ferrer, Compay Segundo, and Rubén González.

Honours

Cooder has won multiple Grammy Awards across folk, world music, and Americana categories, and in 2022 received the Premio Tenco in Italy. His short-story collection Los Angeles Stories was published in 2011.
