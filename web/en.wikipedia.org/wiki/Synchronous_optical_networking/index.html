Synchronous optical networking

Synchronous optical networking (SONET) and synchronous digital hierarchy (SDH) are standardized protocols that transfer multiple digital bit streams synchronously over optical fiber using lasers or LEDs. SONET was developed in North America (ANSI) and SDH internationally (ITU-T); the two are largely interoperable, and together they formed the backbone multiplexing technology of telephone networks from the late 1980s onward.

Frame structure

The basic SONET signal, STS-1, runs at 51.84 Mbit/s; higher rates are formed by byte-interleaving N STS-1s (STS-N/OC-N). SDH's base unit is STM-1 at 155.52 Mbit/s. Frames repeat every 125 microseconds, matching the sampling rate of digital voice. Extensive overhead bytes support operations, administration, maintenance, and provisioning (OAM&P), enabling rapid fault detection and protection switching within 50 ms in ring topologies.

Legacy

SONET/SDH carried plesiochronous digital hierarchy (PDH) tributaries as well as ATM and, later, Ethernet via generic framing procedure (GFP). As packet traffic outgrew circuit traffic, networks migrated toward OTN and pure Ethernet/IP over DWDM, but large SONET/SDH installations remain in service.
