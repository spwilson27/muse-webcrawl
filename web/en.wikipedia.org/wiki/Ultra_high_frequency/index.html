Ultra high frequency

Ultra high frequency (UHF) is the ITU designation for radio frequencies in the range between 300 megahertz (MHz) and 3 gigahertz (GHz), also known as the decimetre band as the wavelengths range from one metre to one tenth of a metre (one decimetre).

Characteristics

UHF signals propagate mainly by line of sight and are easily blocked by buildings and terrain, but can penetrate foliage and building materials to a degree. Antennas are small because of the short wavelengths.

Applications

UHF is used for television broadcasting (channels 14–36 in the United States after repacking), mobile phones (including LTE and 5G bands), Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, GPS, two-way radios, cordless phones, RFID and microwave ovens (2.45 GHz, in the ISM band). Amateur radio has UHF allocations at 70 cm, 33 cm, 23 cm and shorter wavelengths.
