Diversion program

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Diversion programs are administered through the courts

A diversion program, also known as a pretrial diversion program or pretrial intervention program, in the criminal justice system is a form of pretrial sentencing designed to enable offenders of criminal law to avoid criminal charges and a criminal record. The purposes of diversion are generally thought to include relief to courts and prosecutors, better outcomes for offenders compared with incarceration, and restitution to victims.

How diversion works

Eligible defendants, typically first-time or low-level offenders, agree to complete specified conditions, which may include community service, counselling, substance abuse treatment, drug testing, educational or vocational training, and payment of restitution. Upon successful completion, charges may be dismissed or reduced and the arrest record may be expunged; failure to complete the program usually returns the case to normal prosecution.

Types

Specialized diversion programs include drug courts and drug treatment diversion for substance-related offences, mental health diversion, juvenile diversion, and programs for veterans. In juvenile justice, diversion is strongly favoured because it avoids the labelling and detention associated with formal processing.
