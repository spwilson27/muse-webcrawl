Pendolino (from Italian pendolo, "pendulum", with the diminutive suffix -ino) is a family of high-speed tilting trains developed by Fiat Ferroviaria in Italy and now produced by Alstom. Pendolino trains tilt into curves, allowing them to run 20–30% faster on conventional winding track than non-tilting trains, and are in service in Italy and numerous other countries.

History

Fiat began tilting-train experiments in the 1960s. The prototype ETR 401 entered service in 1976 on the Rome–Ancona line. Mass production began with the nine-car ETR 450 in 1988, running Rome–Milan services. Subsequent generations include the dual-voltage ETR 460, the ETR 470 (used for Cisalpino services into Switzerland), and the "New Pendolino" ETR 600/610 family ordered from Alstom after its 2000 acquisition of Fiat Ferroviaria. Trenitalia's ETR 675 "Pendolino Evo" followed in the 2010s.

Technology

The Pendolino tilting system uses accelerometers and gyroscopes to sense curve entry and commands hydraulic actuators to tilt each car body inward by up to 8 degrees, compensating for lateral acceleration so passengers feel comfortable at higher curve speeds. Early Italian designs tilt around a pivot above the centre of gravity ("pendulum" style).

Operators

Outside Italy, Pendolino derivatives have operated in Spain (as the Alaris and Série 114), Portugal (Alfa Pendular), Slovenia, Finland and Russia (the Sm6 Allegro), the Czech Republic and Poland (ED250), Switzerland, China (CRH5, non-tilting derivative), and Great Britain, where the Class 390 Pendolino has run West Coast Main Line services since 2002. Greece and Croatia also operate Pendolino-family trains.
