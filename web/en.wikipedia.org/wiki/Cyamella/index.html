Cyamella


Location of the cyamella in the popliteus tendon (placeholder image)

Classification: Sesamoid bone; anatomical variant

The cyamella is a small sesamoid bone embedded in the tendon of the popliteus muscle at the back of the knee. It is a normal variant in many non-human primates and other mammals, in which it may articulate with the lateral femoral condyle and play a role in knee mechanics, but in humans it is rare.

In humans

In humans the cyamella is an uncommon incidental finding on knee radiographs or MRI, with reported prevalence well under one percent in most series. Because it lies near the posterolateral corner of the knee it can be mistaken for a loose body, fracture fragment or other ossicle. Rarely, a cyamella may become symptomatic or be associated with popliteus tendon pathology; symptomatic cases have occasionally been treated by excision. It should not be confused with the fabella, a much commoner sesamoid in the lateral head of the gastrocnemius.
