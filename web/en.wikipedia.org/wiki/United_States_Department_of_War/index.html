United States Department of War

The United States Department of War, also called the War Department, was a cabinet department of the United States government responsible for the operation and maintenance of the United States Army. It existed from 1789 until 1947, when postwar reorganization divided its functions and placed the armed services under a unified defence establishment.

History

Congress created the department on 7 August 1789. Henry Knox, formerly George Washington's chief of artillery, became the first Secretary of War. The department originally also handled naval affairs until a separate Department of the Navy was created in 1798. Through the nineteenth century it administered the expanding regular army, frontier posts, military engineering, arsenals and relations connected with Native American affairs for a time.

The War Department mobilized mass armies for the Mexican-American War, the Civil War, the Spanish-American War and the two world wars. During the Second World War it grew into an enormous global organization headquartered in the newly constructed Pentagon. Its responsibilities included procurement, logistics, military aviation through the Army Air Forces, and administration of occupied and liberated areas.

Abolition

The National Security Act of 1947 abolished the War Department as a cabinet-level entity, splitting it into the Department of the Army and the Department of the Air Force and subordinating both, with the Navy, to the new National Military Establishment headed by a Secretary of Defense. In 1949 that establishment was renamed the Department of Defense. The Secretary of War was succeeded by the Secretary of the Army, no longer a cabinet officer in the same sense.
