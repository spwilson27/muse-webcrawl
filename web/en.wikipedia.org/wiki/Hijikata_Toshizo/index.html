Born: 31 May 1835, Hino, Musashi Province
Died: 20 June 1869, Hakodate, Hokkaido

Unit: Shinsengumi (vice-commander)

Hijikata Toshizō (土方 歳三, 31 May 1835 – 20 June 1869) was a Japanese warrior of the late Edo period who served as vice-commander of the Shinsengumi, the Tokugawa shogunate's special police force in Kyoto. Known for his strict discipline — he was nicknamed the "demon vice-commander" — he authored the group's internal code, the Kyokuchū Hatto.

Early life

Hijikata was born in Hino, a farming village in Musashi Province (now Tokyo), the youngest son of a wealthy farmer. He worked as an itinerant medicine seller while studying Tennen Rishin-ryū swordsmanship at the Shieikan dōjō in Edo, where he met Kondō Isami.

Shinsengumi and Boshin War

In 1863 he travelled to Kyoto with Kondō's Rōshigumi, which became the Shinsengumi. After the shogunate's defeat in the Boshin War began in 1868, Hijikata fought at Toba–Fushimi, in Aizu, and finally in Hokkaido, where he served the short-lived Republic of Ezo. He was shot and killed during the Battle of Hakodate in June 1869, aged 34.

Legacy

Hijikata is one of the most popular figures of the bakumatsu period in Japanese fiction, film and manga, frequently portrayed as a stern but loyal swordsman.
