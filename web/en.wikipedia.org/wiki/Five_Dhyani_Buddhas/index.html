Five Dhyani Buddhas

The Five Dhyani Buddhas, also known as the Five Wisdom Buddhas or Five Tathagatas (Sanskrit: pañcatathāgata), are five Buddhas central to the iconography of Vajrayana (Tantric) Buddhism. They are not historical figures but meditational deities, each representing an aspect of the enlightened mind and a wisdom that transmutes a particular mental poison. In mandalas they are arranged with one Buddha at the centre and the others at the four cardinal points.

Symbolism

Each Buddha is associated with a family (such as lotus or vajra), a mudra (hand gesture), a mount, a syllable, and one of the five aggregates (skandhas) that constitute a person, as well as one of the five elements and five seasons in some schemes. The system teaches that the ordinary, afflicted components of the mind are, when purified, nothing other than the wisdoms of a Buddha. The five Buddhas appear prominently in the Bardo Thodol (Tibetan Book of the Dead), where they are envisioned during the intermediate state after death, and in the Shingon Buddhism of Japan, where they fill the Diamond Realm mandala.

Origins

The grouping developed in the Mahayana and early Tantric scriptures of India, including the Guhyasamaja Tantra and the Sarvatathagata Tattva Sangraha, and spread with Vajrayana Buddhism to Tibet, Nepal, China and Japan. Sets of the five are found carved and painted throughout the Buddhist world, notably on the terraces of Borobudur in Java.
