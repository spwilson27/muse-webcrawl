Mother India

Mother India is a 1957 Indian Hindi-language epic drama film directed by Mehboob Khan and starring Nargis, Rajendra Kumar, Sunil Dutt, and Raaj Kumar. A remake of Khan's earlier film Aurat (1940), it tells the story of a poverty-stricken village woman, Radha, who struggles to raise her sons while enduring hardship and exploitation by a moneylender, and who ultimately upholds moral duty at a terrible personal cost.

Plot

Radha, a poor peasant woman, is left to fend for her family after her husband is maimed and abandons the family. Indebted to the moneylender Sukhilala, she works tirelessly to raise her sons Ramu and Birju. While Ramu grows into a dutiful farmer, Birju becomes a rebellious outlaw. When Birju attempts to abduct the moneylender's daughter, Radha shoots him to defend the village's honor, embodying the idealized, self-sacrificing Indian mother.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major commercial success and is regarded as one of the greatest Indian films ever made. It was India's first submission for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film and was nominated at the 30th Academy Awards, losing by a single vote. Nargis won the Filmfare Award for Best Actress and acclaim at the Karlovy Vary International Film Festival. The film's title references Katherine Mayo's controversial 1927 book of the same name, which it implicitly answered with a dignified portrayal of Indian womanhood. "Mother India" also became a defining symbol of the young Indian nation.
