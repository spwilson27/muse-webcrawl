Vladimir Vasilyevich Markovnikov (Russian: Владимир Васильевич Марковников; 25 December 1837 – 11 February 1904) was a Russian organic chemist, best known for formulating Markovnikov's rule describing the addition of protic acids to alkenes.

Early life and education

Markovnikov was born in Knyaginino, in the Nizhny Novgorod Governorate. He studied economics and law at Kazan University before turning to chemistry, where he worked under Alexander Butlerov, a founder of the theory of chemical structure. He completed his studies in Germany, working in the laboratories of Emil Erlenmeyer in Heidelberg and Hermann Kolbe in Leipzig.

Career

Markovnikov became a professor at Kazan University, then moved to Odessa (Novorossiysk University), and in 1873 to Moscow University, where he spent the rest of his career. In his 1869 doctoral dissertation and an 1870 paper, he generalized observations on the regiochemistry of electrophilic addition: when a protic acid HX adds across a carbon–carbon double bond, the hydrogen attaches to the carbon already bearing more hydrogen atoms — summarized as "the rich get richer." This empirical generalization, Markovnikov's rule, remains a standard predictive tool in introductory organic chemistry.

Beyond the addition rule, Markovnikov made major contributions to the chemistry of petroleum: he studied the naphthenes (cycloalkanes) of Caucasian oil, recognized the presence of cyclohexane derivatives, and worked on the composition of brine lakes and mineral waters. He also investigated isomerism in organic compounds and the mutual influence of atoms within molecules, an early structural principle.

Legacy

During the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, Markovnikov organized disinfection work for hospitals. He championed the development of Russian chemical industry and science education, and his name is commemorated by the rule taught to generations of chemistry students.
