The Douro (Portuguese: Rio Douro; Spanish: Río Duero) is the largest river by discharge in western Iberia and one of the major rivers of the Iberian Peninsula. It rises in the Sierra de Urbión in Soria Province, north-central Spain, flows generally westward for about 897 kilometres (557 mi) across northern Spain and Portugal, and empties into the Atlantic Ocean at Porto.

The river is best known internationally for the wine country of its middle and lower valley. The Alto Douro wine region of Portugal, demarcated in 1756, is among the oldest regulated wine regions in the world and is the home of Port wine; it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001.

Course

The Douro springs from the glacial lagoons of the Sierra de Urbión at about 2,160 metres above sea level. In Spain (where it is called the Duero) it flows west through the Meseta Norte, passing Soria, Aranda de Duero, Tordesillas and Zamora. Its major Spanish tributaries include the Pisuerga, the Esla, the Tormes and the Águeda.

For roughly 110 kilometres the river forms the border between Spain and Portugal, running through the dramatic canyons of the Arribes del Duero. Fully within Portugal, it receives the Côa, Tua, Sabor, Tâmega and Sousa before reaching its mouth between Porto on the north bank and Vila Nova de Gaia on the south.

Wine region

The Demarcated Region of the Douro was established in 1756 under the Marquis of Pombal, making it one of the world's oldest appellation systems. Grapes grown on the steep, terraced schist slopes of the Alto Douro produce both Port, a fortified wine traditionally shipped from the lodges of Vila Nova de Gaia, and unfortified Douro DOC table wines.

Navigation and dams

A series of dams built in the twentieth century for hydroelectric power — including Miranda, Picote, Bemposta, Aldeadávila and Saucelle on the international stretch — tamed the river's formerly dangerous rapids. Locks on the Portuguese section allow navigation by river cruise and cargo vessels from the Atlantic as far upstream as Barca d'Alva, near the Spanish border.
