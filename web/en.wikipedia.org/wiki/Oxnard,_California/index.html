Oxnard is a city in Ventura County in the U.S. state of California, located on the Oxnard Plain along the Pacific coast west of Los Angeles. It is the most populous city in Ventura County and one of the most populous in the Central Coast region. Oxnard is a major agricultural centre, famed for its fertile soil, strawberries, and lima beans, and it hosts two large U.S. Navy bases nearby: Naval Base Ventura County at Port Hueneme and Point Mugu.

History

The area was inhabited by Chumash people for thousands of years. The city was founded in 1898 when Henry T. Oxnard, a sugar beet magnate, built a processing plant there; the town that grew around it took his name and was incorporated in 1903. In 1903 Oxnard was also the site of a landmark strike by the Japanese-Mexican Labor Association, one of the first successful multiracial farm labour unions in California.

Economy and culture

Agriculture remains central, and Oxnard celebrates the California Strawberry Festival each spring. The city is also known for its Channel Islands Harbor, the Carnegie Art Museum, its historic Victorian architecture in the Henry T. Oxnard Historic District, and as the former off-season training camp home of the Dallas Cowboys.
