The Croix-de-Feu was one of the largest right-wing leagues in interwar France.

The Croix-de-Feu ("Cross of Fire") was a French nationalist movement and veterans' association of the interwar period, led by Colonel François de La Rocque. Founded in 1927 as an association of decorated First World War veterans, it grew in the early 1930s into one of the largest of the French right-wing leagues, numbering perhaps hundreds of thousands of members.

History

The Croix-de-Feu first attracted national attention during the riots of 6 February 1934 in Paris, in which it participated alongside other leagues; the events shook the Third Republic and brought down the government of Édouard Daladier. La Rocque's movement combined nationalism, anti-communism, hostility to parliamentary politics, and calls for a strengthened executive, though historians continue to debate whether it should be classed as fascist.

After the Popular Front government dissolved the paramilitary leagues in June 1936, La Rocque refounded the movement as a political party, the Parti social français (PSF), which became the largest right-wing party in France before the Second World War, before being overtaken by events after 1940.
