Lord Emsworth

Clarence Threepwood, 9th Earl of Emsworth, known as Lord Emsworth, is a fictional character created by P. G. Wodehouse. He is the amiable, absent-minded master of Blandings Castle in Shropshire and the central figure of the Blandings Castle stories and novels. First appearing in Something Fresh (1915), he featured in Wodehouse's fiction for nearly sixty years.

Character

Lord Emsworth is a tall, thin, benevolent peer in his late fifties with a vague manner, a love of gardening, and an intense devotion to his prize pig, the Empress of Blandings, a black Berkshire sow who repeatedly wins the Fat Pigs class at the Shropshire Agricultural Show. His chief desire in life is to potter about his garden in old clothes and be left in peace — a desire constantly frustrated by his formidable sisters, visiting guests, secretaries, and the schemes of his younger son Freddie.

Family and household

His sisters include the imperious Lady Constance Keeble, who effectively runs the castle, and his brother is the Hon. Galahad Threepwood, a man-about-town with scandalous memoirs. His sons are George, the heir, and Freddie, whose romances and dog-food business ventures drive many plots. Recurring antagonists include the efficient secretary Rupert Baxter and the neighbouring landowner Sir Gregory Parsloe-Parsloe, his pig rival.

Appearances

Lord Emsworth appears in novels including Leave It to Psmith (1923), Summer Lightning (1929), Heavy Weather (1933), Uncle Fred in the Springtime (1939), Full Moon (1947) and Pigs Have Wings (1952), and in numerous short stories. He has been played on television by Ralph Richardson, Peter O'Toole and Timothy Spall.
