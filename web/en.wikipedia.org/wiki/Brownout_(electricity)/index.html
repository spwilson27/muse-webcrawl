Brownout (electricity)

A brownout causes lights to dim as supply voltage sags.

A brownout is a drop in voltage in an electrical power supply system, either intentional or unintentional. The term derives from the dimming experienced by lighting when voltage sags. Brownouts contrast with blackouts, in which power is lost entirely.

Causes

Intentional brownouts are sometimes imposed by utilities as an emergency measure to reduce load and prevent a total blackout when demand threatens to exceed generating or transmission capacity. Unintentional brownouts arise from faults, overloaded circuits, storms and high demand — for example during heat waves when air-conditioning loads peak.

Effects

Different equipment responds differently to reduced voltage. Incandescent lamps dim noticeably; motors may overheat because they draw more current to maintain power, and can stall or burn out; some electronics shut down or malfunction, while switch-mode power supplies often compensate by drawing higher current. Repeated or prolonged brownouts can damage sensitive equipment, and "brownout protection" circuits typically disconnect loads when voltage falls outside a safe range.
