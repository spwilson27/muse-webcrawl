Om

ॐ

Om (or Aum; Sanskrit: ॐ, ओम्, romanized: Ōṁ) is a sacred sound, syllable, mantra and invocation in Hinduism. It is also revered in Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism. In Hinduism, Om is one of the most important spiritual symbols (pratika), believed to be the primordial sound from which the universe arose, and it is traditionally chanted at the beginning and end of prayers, mantras and scriptural recitations.

Meaning and symbolism

The syllable is commonly analyzed as comprising three sounds — a, u and m — which are said to symbolize many triads: the three states of consciousness (waking, dreaming and deep sleep), the three Vedas, the three worlds (earth, atmosphere and heaven), and the Hindu Trimurti of Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva. The Mandukya Upanishad, one of the shortest Upanishads, is devoted entirely to the exposition of Om, identifying it with the whole of reality and the self (atman).

In the scriptures

Om is first mentioned in the Upanishads and is prescribed in the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali as the designation of Ishvara and a means of attaining samadhi. The Bhagavad Gita calls it the one-syllabled Brahman ("om ity ekaksharam brahma"). It precedes many mantras, most famously in the Buddhist mantra Om mani padme hum.

Written forms

The Om ligature is written ॐ in Devanagari and has distinct forms in other Indic scripts, as well as in Tibetan and Southeast Asian scripts. It is a ubiquitous religious symbol across South Asia, appearing on temples, printed texts, jewellery and, in the modern era, on clothing and merchandise worldwide. The syllable is included in Unicode as U+0950 (Devanagari) among other code points.
