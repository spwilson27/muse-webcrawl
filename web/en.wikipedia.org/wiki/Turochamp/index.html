Turochamp

Turochamp is a chess program developed by Alan Turing and David Champernowne in 1948. It was created before any computer capable of running it existed, so Turing simulated its calculations by hand. It is among the earliest examples of a chess-playing algorithm.

Design

Turochamp evaluated positions using a scoring system based on material values and simple positional heuristics such as mobility, piece safety and threats of checkmate. It searched two plies (half-moves) deep, considering all of its own moves and only "significant" replies by the opponent, such as captures and checks.

The 1952 game

In 1952 Turing played a game against his colleague Alick Glennie, executing Turochamp's algorithm by hand with pencil and paper — each move took Turing up to half an hour. Turochamp lost in 29 moves. The score of the game survives and the program has since been reconstructed and run on modern computers, including a 2012 recreation in which it faced Garry Kasparov in Manchester.
