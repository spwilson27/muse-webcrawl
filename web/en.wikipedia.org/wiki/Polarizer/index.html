Polarizer

A polarizer or polariser is an optical filter that lets light waves of a specific polarization pass through while blocking light waves of other polarizations. It can convert a beam of light of undefined or mixed polarization into a beam of well-defined polarization, known as polarised light. The common types of polarizers are linear polarizers and circular polarizers.

Linear polarizers

Linear polarizers can be divided into two general categories: absorptive polarizers, where the unwanted polarization states are absorbed by the device, and beam-splitting polarizers, where the unpolarized beam is split into two beams with opposite polarization states. The best-known absorptive polarizer is the Polaroid sheet, invented by Edwin H. Land, made of polymer chains aligned so that they absorb one component of the field. Crystalline polarizers such as the Nicol prism and Glan–Taylor prism work by birefringence, separating the two polarizations spatially.

Circular polarizers

A circular polarizer consists of a linear polarizer combined with a quarter-wave plate oriented at 45° to it. It converts linearly polarised light into circularly polarised light, and is used in photography and in 3D display technology.

Uses

Polarizers are used in sunglasses, camera filters, LCD screens, optical instruments such as the polarimeter, and in scientific experiments studying polarization and stress birefringence.
