"Rapunzel" is a German fairy tale recorded by the Brothers Grimm and first published in 1812 in Children's and Household Tales. It belongs to Aarne–Thompson type 310, "The Maiden in the Tower". The tale's name comes from the rapunzel plant (rampion bellflower) that the heroine's mother craves during pregnancy.

Plot

A childless couple longs for a baby. When the wife becomes pregnant, she craves the rapunzel growing in the garden of a sorceress (in later editions, a witch named Dame Gothel). Her husband steals the plant twice; the second time he is caught and must promise the unborn child in exchange for his life. The sorceress takes the girl, names her Rapunzel, and at the age of twelve locks her in a tower with neither stairs nor door — only one window. The sorceress visits by calling, "Rapunzel, Rapunzel, let down your hair," and climbing the girl's long golden hair.

A prince overhears the call, climbs the hair himself, and the two fall in love and plan her escape. When Rapunzel carelessly betrays the visits (in the 1812 edition, by asking why her clothes have grown tight — a pregnancy the Grimms edited out of later versions), the sorceress cuts off her hair, banishes her to a wilderness, and tricks the prince, who falls from the tower onto thorns and is blinded. After years of wandering he finds Rapunzel, now mother of twins; her tears restore his sight, and they return to his kingdom.

Sources and adaptations

The Grimms' immediate source was Friedrich Schulz's version (1790), itself derived from Charlotte-Rose de Caumont de La Force's Persinette (1698); an earlier Italian analogue is Giambattista Basile's Petrosinella (1634). The story has been adapted many times, most famously in Disney's animated film Tangled (2010), which made Rapunzel the tenth Disney Princess.
