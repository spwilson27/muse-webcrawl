Purine

Chemical formulaC₅H₄N₄

Purine is a heterocyclic aromatic organic compound consisting of a six-membered pyrimidine ring fused to a five-membered imidazole ring, with the formula C₅H₄N₄. It is the parent compound of the purines, a family of nitrogen-containing bases of fundamental biological importance: the nucleobases adenine and guanine, which are constituents of DNA and RNA, are purines, as are caffeine, theobromine, xanthine and uric acid. The name was coined by the German chemist Emil Fischer, who first synthesised purine in 1898.

Biological role

In nucleic acids, the purine bases adenine (A) and guanine (G) pair with the pyrimidines thymine/uracil and cytosine respectively. Purine nucleotides also serve as energy currency (ATP, GTP), signalling molecules (cAMP), and components of coenzymes such as NAD and FAD. Cells can synthesise purines de novo from amino acids and other small precursors, or salvage them by recycling free bases and nucleosides — the enzyme HGPRT, defective in Lesch–Nyhan syndrome, is a key salvage enzyme.

Metabolism and medicine

In humans and other primates, purine degradation ends at uric acid, because the enzyme uricase is absent. Excess uric acid can crystallise in joints as gout, and its production is increased by purine-rich foods such as red meat, offal, some seafood and beer. The drug allopurinol treats gout by inhibiting xanthine oxidase, the enzyme that produces uric acid. Many important drugs are purine analogues: the immunosuppressant azathioprine, the antiviral aciclovir, and the anticancer agent mercaptopurine.

Occurrence and synthesis

Purines occur widely in nature and have been detected in meteorites; experiments show they can form under plausible prebiotic conditions, lending interest to their role in the origin of life. Industrially and in the laboratory, purine itself is made by heating formamide or by the Traube synthesis — Fischer's historic route built the molecule from uric acid isolated from guano. Purine is moderately soluble in water and is both weakly acidic and weakly basic.
