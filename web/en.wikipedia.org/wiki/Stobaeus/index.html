Stobaeus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Joannes Stobaeus (Greek: Ἰωάννης ὁ Στοβαῖος), from Stobi in Macedonia, was the compiler of a valuable series of extracts from Greek authors. The work originally consisted of four books, of which the first two are known as the Eclogae and the latter two as the Florilegium. In most modern editions the two are combined under the title Anthology (or Anthologion). The work was compiled in the 5th century AD, apparently for the education of his son Septimius.

The Anthology

The extracts number in the thousands and are drawn from more than 500 poets and prose writers, including Homer, Euripides, Sophocles, Plato, and many authors whose works are otherwise lost, such as early Stoics and Presocratics. The first book treats physics, cosmology, and natural philosophy; the second book deals with logic and epistemology; the third and fourth books cover ethics and politics. Stobaeus's anthology is an essential source for fragments of lost Greek literature and for quotations from works that survive only in part.

Editions and transmission

The work circulated widely in Byzantine manuscripts and was much used by later excerptors and gnomologists. The Florilegium was first printed in the 16th century, and the Eclogae appeared in print in 1575. The standard modern edition is that of Kurt Wachsmuth and Otto Hense (1884–1923).
