Gerard Groote

Gerard Groote (October 1340 – 20 August 1384), also Geert Grote or Gerardus Magnus, was a Dutch Roman Catholic deacon, preacher, and founder of the Brethren of the Common Life. He was the originator of the Devotio Moderna ("Modern Devotion"), a movement of spiritual renewal that profoundly influenced late medieval piety and produced The Imitation of Christ, traditionally attributed to his follower Thomas à Kempis.

Life

Groote was born in Deventer, in the bishopric of Utrecht, the son of a wealthy cloth merchant and city magistrate. He studied at Paris, where he took a master of arts degree, and also studied medicine, astronomy, and canon law, acquiring lucrative church benefices. After a serious illness and a conversation with the prior of the Carthusian house at Monnikhuizen around 1374, he underwent a religious conversion, renounced his revenues, and gave his house in Deventer to poor women wishing to live a devout common life — the first of the Sisters of the Common Life.

Preaching and the Common Life

Ordained a deacon in 1379, Groote preached powerful sermons in the vernacular throughout the diocese of Utrecht, denouncing clerical corruption, simony, and concubinage, until the bishop suspended his licence to preach in 1383 — an action he accepted. Around him gathered a community of disciples, including Florens Radewyns, who copied books and lived a shared devotional life without monastic vows. After Groote's death from plague in 1384, Radewyns organised these followers into the Brethren of the Common Life and founded the Windesheim congregation of Augustinian canons (1387), which spread the Modern Devotion across the Netherlands and Germany.
