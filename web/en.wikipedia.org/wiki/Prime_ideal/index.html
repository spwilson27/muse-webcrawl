In ring theory, a prime ideal is an ideal P of a ring R such that if the product ab of two elements belongs to P, then at least one of a or b belongs to P — generalising the defining property of prime numbers. Equivalently, the quotient ring R/P is an integral domain.

Examples

In the ring of integers, the prime ideals are (0) and the ideals (p) generated by a prime number p; the quotient ℤ/(p) is the field with p elements. In a polynomial ring k[x] over a field, the prime ideals are (0) and those generated by irreducible polynomials. In any field, the zero ideal is prime. Every maximal ideal is prime, though the converse does not hold in general: (0) in ℤ is prime but not maximal.

The prime spectrum

The set of all prime ideals of a ring, called its spectrum (Spec R), carries the Zariski topology and is the foundational object of modern algebraic geometry: points of a scheme correspond to prime ideals, so the notion links algebra to geometry. In ℤ, for example, Spec ℤ consists of the generic point (0) together with a closed point for each prime number.

Properties

An element whose principal ideal is prime relates to the notion of a prime element; in unique factorisation domains, irreducible elements generate prime ideals. Prime ideals are used to define localisation of rings, Krull dimension, and in the Nullstellensatz connecting ideals of polynomial rings to algebraic sets.
