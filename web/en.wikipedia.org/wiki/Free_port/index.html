Free port

Free port
DefinitionPort or area where goods may be stored or processed without customs duty
Historical examplesSingapore, Hong Kong, Hamburg, Livorno
Modern formsFree trade zones, bonded warehouses, art freeports

A free port is a port or designated area where goods may be landed, handled, manufactured or re-exported without payment of customs duties, or with duties deferred until the goods enter the domestic market. The concept is closely related to the free trade zone and the bonded warehouse.

History

The Italian port of Livorno was among the first to be declared a free port, in the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries, attracting merchants of many nations. In the nineteenth century the great entrepôt free ports of Singapore (founded 1819) and Hong Kong (ceded to Britain in 1841) built their prosperity on duty-free transshipment, and Hamburg retained a free port zone within the German customs union from 1888 until 2013.

Modern usage

Today the term covers several distinct arrangements: foreign-trade zones in the United States, special economic zones with broader tax and regulatory concessions, and — in a narrower sense — high-security storage facilities for valuable art and collectibles, such as the Geneva Free Ports in Switzerland and similar facilities in Singapore and Luxembourg. These art freeports allow works to be bought, sold and stored indefinitely without import taxes or VAT being triggered, a feature that has attracted criticism over transparency and the risk of facilitating money laundering and the storage of looted antiquities. Governments have also revived the term politically: after Brexit, the United Kingdom designated a number of "freeports" combining customs sites with tax reliefs.
