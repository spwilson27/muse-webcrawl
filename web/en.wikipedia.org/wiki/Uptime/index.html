Uptime

The Unix uptime command reports how long a system has been running

Uptime is a measure of system reliability, expressed as the length of time a computer or other system has been continuously working without crashing or requiring a restart. It is typically expressed as a percentage of total time over a period — for example, "five nines" availability (99.999%) permits only about 5.26 minutes of downtime per year.

Measurement

On Unix and Unix-like systems, the uptime command displays the current time, how long the system has been running, the number of users logged in, and the system load averages for the past 1, 5, and 15 minutes. Hosting providers and cloud services commonly guarantee uptime in service-level agreements (SLAs), with credits issued when availability falls below the agreed figure.

Records

Long-running servers have achieved uptimes of many years; such records are less common today, since security patching frequently requires reboots. Uptime is also tracked informally as a point of pride among system administrators.
