Brennivín

Brennivín is an Icelandic distilled spirit, a variety of akvavit flavoured with caraway, considered Iceland's signature alcoholic drink. Its name literally means "burning wine", and it is traditionally nicknamed "Black Death" (Svarti Dauði), a label that originated with the stark black label of the state-produced bottle.

Production and use

Brennivín is made from fermented grain or potato mash and distilled, then flavoured principally with caraway. It is traditionally consumed during the midwinter festival of Þorrablót, often alongside hákarl (fermented shark), and is also drunk as a shot throughout the year. The spirit is typically around 37.5–40% alcohol by volume.
