Tribute act

A tribute act (or tribute band) is a musical act that performs the songs and reproduces the style, appearance, and stage show of a specific well-known artist or group. Tribute acts range from amateur productions to elaborate professional touring shows featuring costume changes, replica instruments, and actors impersonating individual band members.

History and culture

The phenomenon grew with classic rock fandom and the casino, theatre, and festival circuits. Tributes to the Beatles, ABBA, Queen, Elvis Presley, Pink Floyd, and Led Zeppelin are among the most numerous; successful productions such as the Australian Pink Floyd Show and "Beatlemania"-style revues have toured internationally. Tribute acts are sometimes distinguished from cover bands, which play their own arrangements of many artists' songs, whereas a tribute act recreates one artist's catalogue and persona as faithfully as possible.

Legal issues

Tribute acts occupy a grey area of intellectual property law. Performance of songs generally requires venue licences from collecting societies, but use of a famous act's name, trade dress, or likeness can raise trademark and publicity-rights questions, leading many acts to use punning names — for example "Nearly Neil" or "By Jovi" — to signal imitation without claiming identity. Some original artists endorse particular tribute acts or even perform with them.
