Phyllis Schlafly

Phyllis Stewart Schlafly (born Phyllis McAlpin Stewart; August 15, 1924 – September 5, 2016) was an American attorney, conservative activist, and author. She is best known for her successful campaign against ratification of the Equal Rights Amendment (ERA) to the U.S. Constitution in the 1970s, and for her 1964 self-published book A Choice Not an Echo, which supported Barry Goldwater and attacked the liberal Republican establishment. She founded the Eagle Forum in 1972 and remained a leading figure of the American conservative movement and the "pro-family" movement for over half a century.

Early life and education

Schlafly grew up in St. Louis during the Great Depression and worked her way through Washington University, graduating Phi Beta Kappa in 1944. She earned a master's degree in political science from Radcliffe College in 1945 and worked during the war as a model and researcher, including testing firearms at a munitions plant. She married attorney Fred Schlafly in 1949 and raised six children in Alton, Illinois, later earning a law degree in 1978.

Political career

She ran unsuccessfully for Congress in 1952 and 1970. Her book A Choice Not an Echo (1964) sold millions of copies and helped propel Goldwater to the Republican nomination. She wrote on national defence, opposing arms control agreements, and served as a delegate to Republican national conventions for decades.

STOP ERA

When Congress passed the Equal Rights Amendment in 1972, Schlafly founded "Stop ERA" (later the Eagle Forum), arguing that the amendment would strip women of privileges and protections, subject them to the draft and combat duty, and transfer family law to the federal government. Her network of largely homemaker volunteers lobbied state legislatures effectively; although 35 states ratified, the amendment fell three states short by the extended 1982 deadline. Schlafly's victory is regarded as a founding achievement of the modern social-conservative movement.

Later years and legacy

Schlafly continued to campaign against abortion, same-sex marriage, immigration liberalisation, and international treaties. Her final book, The Conservative Case for Trump, was published the day after her death in 2016. Historians credit her with mobilising evangelical and homemaker constituencies central to the Republican coalition from the Reagan era onward; critics charge her with obstructing gender equality. The 2020 miniseries Mrs. America dramatised her ERA campaign.
