Eiffel Tower

The Eiffel Tower (French: Tour Eiffel) is a wrought-iron lattice tower on the Champ de Mars in Paris, France. It is named after the engineer Gustave Eiffel, whose company designed and built the tower from 1887 to 1889.

History

Locally nicknamed "La dame de fer" (French for "Iron Lady"), it was constructed as the centerpiece of the 1889 World's Fair (Exposition Universelle), marking the centennial of the French Revolution. The design by Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier, refined by architect Stephen Sauvestre, was selected from 107 entries. Construction used 18,038 prefabricated puddled-iron pieces joined by about 2.5 million rivets, assembled by some 300 workers; remarkably, only one fatality occurred during construction.

Standing 330 metres (1,083 ft) tall including its antenna, it was the tallest human-made structure in the world for 41 years, until the Chrysler Building in New York City surpassed it in 1930. It remains the tallest structure in Paris.

Many Parisians and artists initially condemned the tower as an eyesore — the 1887 "Artists' Protest" called it a "useless and monstrous" construction — and it was intended to be dismantled in 1909. It was saved by its value as a radiotelegraph station: the military used it for wireless communications, and Eiffel funded scientific experiments on the tower, including meteorology and aerodynamics, inscribing the names of 72 French scientists and engineers on its sides.

Design and structure

The tower has three levels for visitors, with restaurants on the first and second levels. The top level's upper platform is 276 m (906 ft) above the ground — the highest public observation deck in the European Union. The iron structure weighs about 7,300 tonnes, and the entire structure about 10,100 tonnes. The tower is repainted every seven years with roughly 60 tonnes of paint; its colour has evolved from Venetian red to the current "Eiffel Tower brown." Thermal expansion makes the tower lean slightly away from the sun and grow by up to about 15 cm in summer heat.

Tourism and cultural significance

The Eiffel Tower is the most-visited paid monument in the world, receiving around seven million visitors annually, and had welcomed its 300 millionth visitor by 2017. It is one of the most recognizable structures in the world and a global cultural icon of France, reproduced in replicas from Las Vegas to Tokyo. The tower's nighttime lighting and sparkling beacon are protected as an artistic work under French copyright law in some interpretations, a point of recurring debate.
