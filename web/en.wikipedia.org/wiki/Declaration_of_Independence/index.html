Declaration of Independence

A declaration of independence, or declaration of statehood, is a statement by a polity, nation, or region asserting its independence and statehood, and formally severing its political ties to a parent state or empire. Such declarations often — but do not always — follow a period of conflict, and they may or may not be recognized by other states. The most famous example in the English-speaking world is the United States Declaration of Independence of 1776.

History

Early examples include Scotland's Declaration of Arbroath (1320), which asserted Scottish independence from England, and the Dutch Act of Abjuration (1581), by which the provinces of the Netherlands renounced allegiance to Philip II of Spain — a document that influenced later declarations. During the era of decolonization and the world wars of the 19th and 20th centuries, declarations of independence proliferated: the Spanish American wars of independence produced declarations throughout Latin America, and dozens of new states declared independence as the European colonial empires dissolved after the Second World War.

Legal aspects

Declarations of independence are generally political acts rather than legal instruments recognized under international law, which contains neither a right to unilateral secession nor a general prohibition of declarations. In 2010, the International Court of Justice advised that Kosovo's 2008 declaration of independence did not violate international law because international law contains no prohibition on such declarations. A unilateral declaration of independence (UDI) — one made without the consent of the parent state — may result in anything from full international recognition, as with the United States, to armed conflict and lasting non-recognition, as with Rhodesia's UDI in 1965.

Notable declarations

  • Declaration of Arbroath — Scotland, 1320
  • Act of Abjuration — Netherlands, 1581
  • United States Declaration of Independence — 4 July 1776
  • Declarations of the Spanish American republics, 1810s–1820s
  • Israeli Declaration of Independence — 1948
  • Declarations marking the dissolution of the Soviet Union, 1990–1991
