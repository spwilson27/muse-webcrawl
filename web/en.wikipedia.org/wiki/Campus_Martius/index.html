Campus Martius
Meaning: "Field of Mars"
Location: floodplain west of ancient Rome's hills
Modern rioni: Campus Martius (IV) and neighbours

The Campus Martius (Latin for "Field of Mars") was a publicly owned area of ancient Rome, a floodplain of about 2 square kilometres outside the Servian Walls between the Capitoline and Quirinal hills and the Tiber River. In the Republic it was used for military training and assemblies; under the emperors it filled with temples, theatres, baths, and monuments, and today it forms much of Rome's historic centre.

Republican era

Traditionally the property of the Tarquin kings, the field became public land after their expulsion. It hosted the comitia centuriata, the census, army musters, and exercise grounds, with the Villa Publica and the Altar of Mars among its early structures. Its open space also made it Rome's place of recreation, where, as Horace and Martial describe, people rode, fenced, and played ball games.

Imperial building

Pompey built Rome's first permanent stone theatre there (55 BC). Agrippa added the original Pantheon, baths, and gardens; Augustus raised the Mausoleum of Augustus, the Ara Pacis, and a giant sundial. Later emperors added the Stadium of Domitian (now Piazza Navona's outline) and Hadrian's rebuilt Pantheon.
