2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine

On 24 February 2022, Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, escalating the Russo-Ukrainian War that had begun in 2014. Russian president Vladimir Putin announced a "special military operation", citing goals of "demilitarisation and denazification" and the protection of Russian speakers in Donbas; the invasion was widely condemned internationally as a war of aggression and the largest attack on a European country since the Second World War.

Russian invasion of Ukraine

Date: 24 February 2022 – ongoing
Location: Ukraine (and border regions of Russia)
Belligerents: Russia (with Belarus support) vs. Ukraine (with extensive Western military aid)
Consequences: Millions displaced; annexation claims over four Ukrainian oblasts

Background

Russia had annexed Crimea in 2014 and supported separatists in the Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. During 2021 and early 2022 Russia massed troops near Ukraine's borders while demanding that NATO rule out Ukrainian membership. On 21 February 2022 Russia recognised the separatist republics and days later launched the invasion.

Course of the invasion

Russian forces attacked from the north (including via Belarus toward Kyiv), east and south. The attempt to seize Kyiv failed amid stiff Ukrainian resistance, and Russia withdrew from northern Ukraine in early April 2022, refocusing on the east and south. Evidence of mass killings of civilians in Bucha and elsewhere drew global condemnation. Ukraine retook large areas in counteroffensives around Kharkiv in September 2022 and liberated Kherson city in November. Russia announced the annexation of four partially occupied oblasts in September 2022, a move rejected by the UN General Assembly. The front subsequently settled into attritional warfare, including the long battle for Bakhmut, as Western countries supplied Ukraine with heavy weapons, air defences and financial support, and imposed sweeping sanctions on Russia.

Impact

The invasion triggered Europe's largest refugee crisis since the Second World War, with millions of Ukrainians fleeing abroad and millions more internally displaced. Casualties on both sides are estimated in the hundreds of thousands. The war disrupted global food and energy markets and led Finland and Sweden to join NATO.
