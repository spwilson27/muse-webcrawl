Worcestershire Beacon, also known as Worcester Beacon or locally simply the Beacon, is the highest point in the Malvern Hills and the highest point in the English county of Worcestershire, at 425 metres (1,394 feet) above sea level. Standing immediately west of the spa town of Great Malvern, it is one of the most popular walking destinations in the West Midlands, with a toposcope at the summit indicating landmarks visible on clear days across as many as thirteen counties.

Geography

The beacon forms the northern high point of the Malvern ridge, a narrow range of ancient Precambrian igneous and metamorphic rocks running roughly north–south for about 13 km. Its prominence of about 337 metres qualifies it as a Marilyn. The summit carries a toposcope erected for the Diamond Jubilee of Queen Victoria in 1897 and replaced for later jubilees. On exceptionally clear days the view extends to the Cotswolds, the Black Mountains and Pen y Fan in Wales, the Shropshire Hills, and the cathedrals of Worcester, Gloucester, and Hereford.

History

The hill takes its name from the warning beacons lit on its summit, part of a chain used from at least the Elizabethan era — including at the time of the Spanish Armada in 1588 — and rekindled for national celebrations ever since. The nearby Iron Age hillfort of British Camp crowns the neighbouring Herefordshire Beacon. The Malvern Hills were designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty in 1959 and are managed by the Malvern Hills Trust. The Worcestershire Beacon is the goal of the Malvern Hills end-to-end walk, and William Langland's 14th-century poem Piers Plowman opens with a dream vision on the hills.
