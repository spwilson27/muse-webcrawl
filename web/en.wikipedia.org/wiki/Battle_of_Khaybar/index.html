Battle of Khaybar

The Battle of Khaybar was fought in 628 CE (7 AH) between the early Muslims led by the prophet Muhammad and the Jewish tribes inhabiting the oasis of Khaybar, a fertile valley of date palms and fortresses in the Hejaz, about 150 kilometres north of Medina.

Background

Khaybar sheltered leaders of the Banu Nadir, exiled from Medina, who had helped organise the confederate army defeated at the Battle of the Trench (627). After the Treaty of al-Hudaybiya freed him from the threat of Quraysh, Muhammad marched on Khaybar.

The battle

The oasis was defended by a series of separate fortresses — among them Naim, al-Qamus and al-Sa'b ibn Mu'adh — which the Muslims reduced one by one over several weeks. Islamic tradition gives a prominent role to Ali ibn Abi Talib, credited with defeating the champion Marhab and tearing a gate from its hinges to use as a shield.

Aftermath

The defenders surrendered on terms: they kept their lands and date groves in exchange for half the produce paid annually to Medina — an arrangement often cited as a model for later agreements with non-Muslim subjects. Among the captives was Safiyya bint Huyayy, who married Muhammad. The victory greatly strengthened the finances and prestige of the young Muslim community.
