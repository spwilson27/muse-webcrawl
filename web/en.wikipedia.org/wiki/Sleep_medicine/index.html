Sleep medicine

Sleep medicine
Focus: Sleep disorders and sleep health
Key test: Polysomnography
Classification: ICSD-3 (International Classification of Sleep Disorders)

Sleep medicine is a medical specialty devoted to the diagnosis and treatment of sleep disturbances and disorders. It draws on neurology, pulmonology, psychiatry, otolaryngology, and psychology, and developed as a distinct discipline in the second half of the 20th century following the discovery of REM sleep and the recognition of sleep apnoea.

Disorders

The International Classification of Sleep Disorders groups conditions into insomnias; sleep-related breathing disorders such as obstructive sleep apnoea; central disorders of hypersomnolence including narcolepsy; circadian rhythm sleep–wake disorders; parasomnias such as sleepwalking and REM sleep behaviour disorder; and sleep-related movement disorders such as restless legs syndrome.

Diagnosis

The central diagnostic tool is polysomnography, an overnight recording of brain waves (EEG), eye movements, muscle tone, breathing, oxygen saturation, and heart rhythm, from which sleep stages and disturbances are scored. Home sleep apnoea testing, actigraphy (wrist-worn movement recording over days or weeks), sleep diaries, and the multiple sleep latency test are also widely used.

Treatment

Treatments include cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) for sleep apnoea, oral appliances and surgery, chronotherapy and light therapy for circadian disorders, and medications ranging from melatonin to wake-promoting agents for narcolepsy.

Professional organisation

In the United States, sleep medicine is a recognised subspecialty with board certification, and the American Academy of Sleep Medicine accredits sleep centres. William C. Dement, who founded the first sleep disorders clinic at Stanford in 1970, is often called the father of sleep medicine.
