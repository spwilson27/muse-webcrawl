Portuguese Restoration War

The Portuguese Restoration War (Portuguese: Guerra da Restauração) was a series of conflicts between the Kingdom of Portugal and the Crown of Spain from 1640 to 1668 that ended the Iberian Union, the sixty-year dynastic union of the two crowns under the Spanish Habsburgs. It began with the revolution of 1 December 1640, when Portuguese nobles stormed the royal palace in Lisbon and acclaimed John, Duke of Braganza, as King John IV. Spain finally recognized Portuguese independence in the Treaty of Lisbon (1668).

Background

Portugal had been joined to Spain since 1580, when Philip II of Spain claimed the Portuguese throne. Resentment grew under the Count-Duke of Olivares, whose fiscal demands, Spanish officials, and entanglement of Portugal in Spain's wars — leaving Portuguese colonies exposed to Dutch attack — provoked revolt, simultaneously with the Catalan Reapers' War of 1640.

The war

Fighting concentrated on the Alentejo frontier. Major Portuguese victories included Montijo (1644), the Lines of Elvas (1659), Ameixial (1663), Castelo Rodrigo (1664), and decisively Montes Claros (1665), where an army under the Marquis of Marialva and the Count of Schomberg destroyed a Spanish invasion force. Portuguese diplomacy secured French support and an English alliance sealed by the marriage of Catherine of Braganza to Charles II in 1661.

Outcome

By the Treaty of Lisbon in February 1668, Spain recognized the House of Braganza as Portugal's ruling dynasty and Portuguese sovereignty, retaining only Ceuta. The revolution of 1640 is commemorated in Portugal's Restoration Day holiday on 1 December.
