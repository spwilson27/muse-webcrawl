Sutomo

Sutomo (3 October 1920 – 7 October 1981), more popularly known as Bung Tomo, was an Indonesian leader during the Indonesian National Revolution. He is best known for his role in the Battle of Surabaya in November 1945, when his fiery radio broadcasts rallied Indonesian fighters against British and Dutch forces; the battle is commemorated annually in Indonesia as Heroes' Day on 10 November.

Life

Born in Surabaya, East Java, Sutomo worked as a journalist and was active in nationalist organisations under Dutch rule and during the Japanese occupation. After the proclamation of Indonesian independence in August 1945, he founded the Indonesian People's Revolutionary Front (BPRI) and used radio broadcasts from Surabaya to urge resistance when British forces, tasked with disarming the Japanese and repatriating prisoners, clashed with Indonesian republicans following the killing of Brigadier Mallaby. His famous broadcasts during the ensuing three-week battle made him a symbol of the revolution.

Later years

After the revolution Sutomo served as a minister in the early 1950s under President Sukarno but later fell out of favour and became a critic of both Sukarno's Guided Democracy and aspects of Suharto's New Order; he was imprisoned from 1978 to 1979 after signing a petition critical of the government. He died in 1981 while on the Hajj pilgrimage in Mecca. In 2008 he was posthumously awarded the title of National Hero of Indonesia.
