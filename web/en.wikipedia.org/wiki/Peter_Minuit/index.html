Peter Minuit (1580 or 1585 – 1638) was a Walloon-Dutch colonial administrator, director of the Dutch colony of New Netherland from 1626 to 1631, famous for the purchase of Manhattan Island. He later founded the Swedish colony of New Sweden on the Delaware River.

Manhattan purchase

Arriving in New Netherland in May 1626, Minuit, acting for the Dutch West India Company, negotiated with representatives of the Lenape (the precise group is debated) for Manhattan Island. The only documentary source, a letter by Pieter Schaghen, reports goods valued at 60 guilders — the basis for the famous but misleading later conversion to "$24 worth of beads". The transaction is better understood against the parties' very different concepts of land tenure: the Dutch believed they had bought exclusive ownership, while the Lenape likely understood a grant of shared use and alliance. Minuit built Fort Amsterdam at the island's tip, founding the town of New Amsterdam, later New York City.

Later career

Recalled to the Netherlands in 1631, Minuit entered Swedish service and in 1638 led an expedition that founded Fort Christina (now Wilmington, Delaware), the first settlement of New Sweden. He died that year in a hurricane near Saint Kitts while returning to Europe.
