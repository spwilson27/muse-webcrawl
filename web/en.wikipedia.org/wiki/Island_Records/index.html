Island Records is a Jamaican multinational record label owned by Universal Music Group. It was founded in 1959 by Chris Blackwell, Graeme Goodall and Leslie Kong in Jamaica, and was eventually sold to PolyGram in 1989. The label is widely associated with reggae music through its work with Bob Marley and the Wailers, and later with rock and pop acts including U2, Cat Stevens, Roxy Music, Grace Jones and Amy Winehouse.

History

Blackwell founded the label in Jamaica in 1959, recording local musicians; its first hit was Laurel Aitken's "Boogie in My Bones". In 1962 Blackwell moved the operation to London, where Island sold Jamaican music — ska, rocksteady and reggae — to Britain's Caribbean immigrant community and, increasingly, to a wider audience. The label had a major hit with Millie Small's "My Boy Lollipop" (1964).

Rock era

In the late 1960s Island diversified into British rock, signing acts such as Traffic, Jethro Tull, King Crimson, Free, Nick Drake, Fairport Convention and Cat Stevens. Its Compass Point Studios in the Bahamas became a noted recording location in the 1980s.

Reggae and beyond

Island's signing of Bob Marley and the Wailers in 1972 brought reggae to a global rock audience with albums such as Catch a Fire and Exodus. The label later released landmark records by U2, whose The Joshua Tree (1987) was one of the best-selling albums of its era.

Subsequent ownership

Blackwell sold Island to PolyGram in 1989; PolyGram was in turn acquired by Seagram and folded into Universal Music Group in 1998. Island continues to operate as a frontline label within UMG in the US and UK, with a roster that has included Amy Winehouse, Mumford & Sons, The Weeknd (via licensing arrangements), Shawn Mendes and Sabrina Carpenter.
