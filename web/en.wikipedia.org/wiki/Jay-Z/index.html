Jay-Z

Image placeholder: Jay-Z

Shawn Corey Carter (born December 4, 1969), known professionally as Jay-Z, is an American rapper, businessman, and record executive. Rooted in East Coast hip hop, he was crucial in the commercial and creative success of numerous artists. Jay-Z is one of the best-selling music artists of all time, with more than 140 million records sold worldwide, and has won 24 Grammy Awards, the most by any rapper.

Career

Raised in Brooklyn's Marcy Houses, Jay-Z co-founded the record label Roc-A-Fella Records in 1994 and released his debut studio album, Reasonable Doubt, in 1996 to critical acclaim. His subsequent albums include Vol. 2... Hard Knock Life (1998), The Blueprint (2001), The Black Album (2003), American Gangster (2007), The Blueprint 3 (2009), Magna Carta Holy Grail (2013), and 4:44 (2017). His collaborative albums include Watch the Throne (2011) with Kanye West and Everything Is Love (2018) with his wife Beyoncé, as The Carters.

Jay-Z served as president of Def Jam Recordings from 2004 to 2007. His business ventures include the entertainment company Roc Nation, the streaming service Tidal, and investments in champagne (Armand de Brignac) and cognac (D'Usse). In 2019 he became the first hip-hop artist to be declared a billionaire by Forbes. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2021.

Personal life

Jay-Z married singer Beyoncé in 2008; they have three children. He is known for philanthropic work through the Shawn Carter Foundation and criminal justice reform advocacy, including co-founding the REFORM Alliance.
