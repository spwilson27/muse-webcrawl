Wakayama Prefecture (和歌山県) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Kansai region of Honshu, occupying the southwestern part of the Kii Peninsula. Its capital is the city of Wakayama. The prefecture corresponds largely to the historical province of Kii.

Geography and economy

Wakayama is mountainous and heavily forested, with a coastline on the Pacific Ocean and the Kii Channel. It is one of Japan's leading producers of mikan (satsuma mandarins) and ume (for umeboshi pickles), and the coastal town of Kushimoto and the Shirahama resort area are known for fishing and hot springs. Traditional industries include textiles and steel around Wakayama city.

Culture and sights

The prefecture contains the sacred mountain area of Mount Kōya (Kōyasan), headquarters of Shingon Buddhism founded by Kūkai, and much of the Kumano Kodō pilgrimage network, including the Kumano Nachi Taisha shrine beside Nachi Falls, Japan's tallest waterfall in a single drop (133 m). These sites form part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range" (2004). Wakayama Castle crowns the capital city.
