Oscar Zariski

Oscar Zariski (1899–1986).

Oscar Zariski (April 24, 1899 – July 4, 1986) was an American mathematician who was one of the founders of modern algebraic geometry. Born in Kobryn, in what was then the Russian Empire (now Belarus), he studied in Kyiv and Rome, where he worked with the Italian school of algebraic geometry, before emigrating to the United States in 1927.

Work

Zariski's mission was to rebuild algebraic geometry on rigorous foundations, replacing the Italian school's brilliant but loose geometric intuition with the methods of commutative algebra. His achievements include foundational work on the resolution of singularities, the Zariski topology (now fundamental to scheme theory), Zariski's main theorem, and the theory of holomorphic functions on varieties. His textbook Commutative Algebra (1958–1960), written with Pierre Samuel, trained generations of mathematicians. He taught at Johns Hopkins University and, from 1947, at Harvard University, where his students included David Mumford and Heisuke Hironaka. Zariski received the National Medal of Science in 1965 and the Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 1981.
