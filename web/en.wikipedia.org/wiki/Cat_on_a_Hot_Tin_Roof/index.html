Cat on a Hot Tin Roof is a three-act play written by Tennessee Williams, based on his 1952 short story "Three Players of a Summer Game". It premiered on Broadway in 1955, directed by Elia Kazan, and won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama that year. The play is considered one of Williams's finest works and his personal favourite of his plays.

Synopsis

The play is set in the Pollitt family's plantation home in the Mississippi Delta on the evening of the 65th birthday of its patriarch, "Big Daddy" Pollitt, a wealthy cotton planter who is dying of cancer — a fact concealed from him and his wife "Big Mama" but known to the rest of the family. At the centre of the drama are Big Daddy's favourite son, Brick, a former football hero who has become an alcoholic since the suicide of his best friend Skipper, and Brick's wife Maggie — "Maggie the Cat" — who is desperate to secure the inheritance for herself and Brick and to restore their childless, sexless marriage. Around them circle Brick's elder brother Gooper and his wife Mae, who scheme to cut Brick out of the estate. In the celebrated second act, Big Daddy confronts Brick about his drinking, forcing both men toward painful truths about "mendacity" — the lies the family lives by.

Themes

The play explores themes of mendacity, greed, repression, family conflict, and mortality. Williams explained that the "cat" of the title refers to Maggie's tenacious determination to stay on the "hot tin roof" of her unbearable situation rather than jump off.

Adaptations

The play was adapted into a 1958 film directed by Richard Brooks, starring Elizabeth Taylor as Maggie, Paul Newman as Brick, and Burl Ives reprising his stage role as Big Daddy. The film was a major commercial and critical success, nominated for six Academy Awards, although its treatment of the play's themes was softened under the Hollywood Production Code. The play has been frequently revived on Broadway and in London's West End.
