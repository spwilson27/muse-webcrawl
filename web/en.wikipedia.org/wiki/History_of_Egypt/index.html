History of Egypt

The history of Egypt is one of the longest continuous histories of any country, extending from predynastic cultures along the Nile to the modern Arab Republic of Egypt.

Ancient Egypt

Agriculture developed in the Nile valley in the Neolithic, and around 3100 BC Upper and Lower Egypt were unified under the pharaohs. The Old Kingdom (c. 2686–2181 BC) built the pyramids of Giza; the Middle Kingdom (c. 2055–1650 BC) and New Kingdom (c. 1550–1070 BC) saw Egypt become a great imperial power under rulers such as Hatshepsut, Thutmose III, Akhenaten and Ramesses II. Later periods brought rule by Libyans, Nubians (the 25th Dynasty), Assyrians and Persians, before Alexander the Great conquered Egypt in 332 BC. The Ptolemaic dynasty — Cleopatra VII its last ruler — ended with Roman annexation in 30 BC.

Roman to modern Egypt

Egypt became a province of Rome and then of Byzantium, a granary of the empire, and an early centre of Christianity. Arab armies conquered it in 639–642 AD, beginning its gradual Islamisation and Arabisation. Successive dynasties — Tulunids, Fatimids (who founded Cairo in 969), Ayyubids under Saladin, and the Mamluks — made Egypt a centre of Islamic civilisation. The Ottomans ruled from 1517 until the era of Muhammad Ali (from 1805), who modernised the country; his descendants ruled, under increasing British influence and, from 1882, occupation, until the monarchy was overthrown in 1952–53. The republic proclaimed then was led by Gamal Abdel Nasser, architect of Arab nationalism and of the Aswan High Dam, followed by Anwar Sadat — who made peace with Israel in 1979 — and Hosni Mubarak, whose thirty-year rule ended in the Egyptian revolution of 2011 during the Arab Spring.
