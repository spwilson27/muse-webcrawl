Fortrose

Fortrose is a town on the Black Isle in the Highland council area of Scotland, on the Moray Firth about 11 miles northeast of Inverness. It is best known for the ruins of Fortrose Cathedral and for Chanonry Point, one of the best places in Britain to view bottlenose dolphins from the shore.

History

Fortrose grew around the cathedral of the Diocese of Ross, founded in the 13th century by David I's successors; the red sandstone cathedral was largely built in the 1300s. After the Scottish Reformation the building decayed, and legend holds that Cromwell's troops removed stone to build a citadel at Inverness. Only the south aisle and chapter house survive under the care of Historic Environment Scotland. The town was made a royal burgh in 1590, and for centuries a famous academic tradition surrounded Fortrose Academy and its schoolmasters.

Tradition associates the town with the Brahan Seer (Coinneach Odhar), the 17th-century Highland prophet said to have been burned at Chanonry Point for witchcraft.

Geography

Fortrose sits on a ridge above the Moray Firth; its harbour and beach at Rosemarkie Bay look across to Fort George. Dolphins, seals, and porpoises are frequently sighted at the point where tides churn through the narrows.
