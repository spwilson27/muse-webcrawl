Foster Botanical Garden

LocationHonolulu, Hawaii
Area13.5 acres (5.5 ha)
Established1853
OperatorCity and County of Honolulu

Foster Botanical Garden, measuring 13.5 acres (5.5 ha), is one of five public botanical gardens on Oahu, Hawaii. It is located at 50 North Vineyard Boulevard, near Chinatown in downtown Honolulu, and is the oldest of the Honolulu Botanical Gardens.

History

The garden's site was originally planted in 1853 by German physician and botanist William Hillebrand, who built his home there and stocked the grounds with tropical plants new to Hawaii. After passing to Thomas Foster and his wife Mary, the property was bequeathed to the City of Honolulu in 1930 and opened to the public in 1931.

Collections

The garden contains a mature canopy of tropical trees, an orchid conservatory, a palm garden, a butterfly garden, and an "economic garden" of herbs, spices, and dye plants. Its Exceptional Tree collection includes a kapok, a rainbow eucalyptus, and a bo tree grown from a descendant of the tree under which the Buddha is said to have attained enlightenment.
