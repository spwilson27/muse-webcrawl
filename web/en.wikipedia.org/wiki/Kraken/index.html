Kraken

The kraken (/ˈkrɑːkən/) is a legendary sea monster of enormous size, said to appear in the sea between Norway and Iceland. It is described as a cephalopod-like creature in the folklore of Scandinavia, and has been popularised in literature and film since the 18th century.


The kraken attacking a ship, in a popular 19th-century style illustration.

History

The kraken is first mentioned in the Örvar-Oddr saga and in the Norwegian natural history written by Erik Pontoppidan in 1753, the Natural History of Norway, which described it as an island-sized floating creature that could drag down the largest warships. Carl Linnaeus included the kraken as Microcosmus marinus in the first edition of his Systema Naturae (1735), though he later removed it.

Origins

Sightings of the kraken are generally thought to be based on encounters with the giant squid (Architeuthis dux), which can grow to 12–15 metres in length, or on colossal squid and large octopuses. Volcanic activity and floating accumulations of seaweed may also have contributed to the legend.

In popular culture

The kraken appears in Alfred Tennyson's 1830 sonnet "The Kraken", in Jules Verne's Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (in which the giant squid attack is central), in John Wyndham's novel The Kraken Wakes, and in the Pirates of the Caribbean films. The phrase "Release the Kraken!" from Clash of the Titans (1981) became a popular catchphrase.
