Canadian Silver Maple Leaf

Country: Canada
Value: 5 Canadian dollars (face value)
Mass: 31.11 g (1 troy oz)
Purity: 99.99% silver
Years of minting: 1988–present

The Canadian Silver Maple Leaf is a silver bullion coin issued annually by the Government of Canada and produced by the Royal Canadian Mint. Introduced in 1988, it is one of the best-selling silver bullion coins in the world and is noted for the purity of its silver — 99.99 per cent fine — higher than that of many competing bullion coins.

Design

The obverse bears a portrait of the reigning monarch of Canada — Elizabeth II until her death in 2022, and subsequently Charles III. The reverse depicts a sugar maple leaf, Canada's national symbol, designed by Walter Ott. The one-ounce coin has a face value of five Canadian dollars, the highest face value among major one-ounce silver bullion coins, though its market price follows the price of silver and is typically far higher.

Security features

The Mint has added a series of anti-counterfeiting measures. In 2014 it introduced radial lines on both faces and MintShield surface protection was later applied to reduce white spotting ("milk spots"), a long-standing complaint among collectors. In 2015 coins began to carry a small privy mark with a laser-engraved maple leaf containing the last two digits of the year, part of the Mint's MINTSHIELD and Bullion DNA anti-counterfeit technology.

Variants

Besides the standard one-ounce coin, the Mint has issued fractional sizes, multi-coin sets, privy-marked and coloured special editions, and larger formats. The Silver Maple Leaf is the silver counterpart of the Gold Maple Leaf, first issued in 1979.
