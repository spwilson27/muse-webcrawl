Content marketing

Content marketing is a form of marketing focused on creating, publishing and distributing content for a targeted audience, with the aim of attracting and retaining customers and ultimately driving profitable customer action — rather than directly pitching products. The content is intended to be valuable, relevant and consistent: articles, videos, podcasts, infographics, white papers, newsletters and social media posts that educate or entertain the audience and build trust in the brand.

History

Although the term became widespread with digital media, the practice is old. John Deere launched the agricultural magazine The Furrow in 1895 to educate farmers (and, indirectly, sell equipment); it is often cited as a pioneering example of brand publishing. The Michelin Guide, first issued in 1900 to encourage motoring, and soap manufacturers' sponsorship of radio and television "soap operas" are other early instances. The rise of the web, blogs and search engines in the 2000s, and later social platforms, dramatically lowered distribution costs and moved content marketing into the mainstream; Joe Pulizzi founded the Content Marketing Institute in 2007 and popularised the term.

Approach

  • Audience definition: building personas and mapping content to stages of the buying process.
  • Owned, earned and paid media: content distributed via a brand's own channels, amplified by coverage and sharing, or promoted with advertising.
  • Search visibility: content optimised for search engines (SEO) to capture information-seeking traffic.
  • Measurement: metrics such as traffic, engagement, lead generation, conversion and retention are used to evaluate return on investment.

Criticism

Sceptics note the difficulty of attributing revenue to content, the glut of low-quality "content farm" material, and the blurring of editorial and advertising (native advertising) that can mislead readers when sponsorship is not disclosed.
