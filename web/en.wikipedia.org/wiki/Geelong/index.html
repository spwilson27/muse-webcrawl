Geelong is a port city in the state of Victoria, Australia, located on Corio Bay, an arm of Port Phillip Bay, about 65 kilometres southwest of Melbourne. It is Victoria's second-largest city and the largest non-capital city in Australia by some measures, governed by the City of Greater Geelong.

History

The area was first surveyed by Europeans in 1802 and named after the Wathaurong word "Djilong" ("place of the native companion" or "land of the bird"). Geelong grew rapidly after the Victorian gold rush of the 1850s, when it served as the port for the Ballarat goldfields, and became a manufacturing centre for wool, rope, paper and automobiles; the Ford Australia plant at Norlane operated from 1926 to 2016.

Economy and culture

Modern Geelong's economy centres on health care, education (including Deakin University), advanced manufacturing and tourism. The city is home to the Geelong Football Club, the "Cats", of the Australian Football League, based at Kardinia Park. The waterfront precinct, with its pier, bollard trail and Eastern Beach swimming enclosure, is a major attraction, and Geelong serves as a gateway to the Bellarine Peninsula and the Great Ocean Road.
