Egyptian revolution of 2011

Egyptian revolution of 2011
Date: 25 January – 11 February 2011
Location: Egypt
Result: Resignation of President Hosni Mubarak; dissolution of the ruling National Democratic Party; military assumption of power

The Egyptian revolution of 2011, also known as the 25 January Revolution, was a series of mass protests, demonstrations, marches, and acts of civil disobedience that took place in Egypt beginning on 25 January 2011 and culminating in the resignation of President Hosni Mubarak on 11 February 2011, ending his nearly 30-year rule.

The uprising was part of the wider Arab Spring wave of protests across the Middle East and North Africa, which had begun in Tunisia the previous month. In Egypt, grievances included police brutality, state-of-emergency laws in force almost continuously since 1967, political corruption, restrictions on free speech, high unemployment, food price inflation, and low wages.

Background

Hosni Mubarak had ruled Egypt since 1981, following the assassination of Anwar Sadat. His government was supported by a long-standing emergency law that curtailed civil liberties. Opposition movements such as Kefaya (the Egyptian Movement for Change) and the April 6 Youth Movement had campaigned against corruption and the widely expected succession of Mubarak's son Gamal. The death of Khaled Mohamed Saeed, a young man beaten to death by police in Alexandria in June 2010, became a rallying symbol, amplified by the Facebook page "We are all Khaled Said".

Protests

Protests began on 25 January 2011, a national holiday honouring the police, with tens of thousands marching in Cairo, Alexandria, Suez, and other cities. The largest demonstrations centred on Tahrir Square in downtown Cairo, which protesters occupied for 18 days. On 28 January, the "Friday of Rage", the government shut down internet and mobile networks and deployed the army after police failed to contain the crowds. Clashes on 2 February, the so-called "Battle of the Camel", saw pro-Mubarak attackers ride camels and horses into the square.

Resignation and aftermath

On 11 February 2011, Vice President Omar Suleiman announced that Mubarak had resigned and handed power to the Supreme Council of the Armed Forces. Celebrations erupted across Egypt. In the following months the ruling National Democratic Party was dissolved, Mubarak was put on trial, and constitutional amendments were approved by referendum. Parliamentary elections in late 2011 and early 2012 were won by Islamist parties, and Mohamed Morsi of the Muslim Brotherhood was elected president in June 2012. Morsi was himself removed by the military in July 2013 after renewed mass protests.
