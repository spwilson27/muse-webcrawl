Bangladeshi cuisine

Core staplesRice, river fish, lentils
Signature dishesHilsa curry, biryani, bharta, pitha
Key flavouringsMustard oil and seed, panch phoron, green chilli
InfluencesBengali, Mughlai, Persian, Arab, British

Bangladeshi cuisine is the culinary tradition of Bangladesh, shaped by the country's riverine geography, monsoon climate, and history as part of greater Bengal and the Mughal Empire. It is dominated by rice and freshwater fish — reflected in the proverb "machh-e bhat-e Bangali" ("fish and rice make a Bengali").

Staples and techniques

A meal typically combines steamed rice with dal (lentils), fried or curried vegetables, and fish such as rohu, catla, and the prized hilsa, the national fish, celebrated in mustard preparations like shorshe ilish. Mashed preparations called bharta — of potato, aubergine, or dried fish — are sharpened with raw onion, green chilli, and mustard oil. The five-spice mixture panch phoron and mustard in many forms give the cuisine its characteristic pungency. Regional specialities include the fiery beef kala bhuna of Chittagong, Sylheti shatkora citrus curries, and Khulna's prawns.

Mughlai and festive food

Dhaka's Old Town preserves Mughlai-influenced dishes: kacchi biryani, morog polao, kebabs, and the Ramadan iftar market of Chawkbazar. Weddings feature multi-course feasts built around rich, ghee-based rice dishes.

Sweets and snacks

Bangladesh is renowned for milk sweets—rosogolla, chomchom, sandesh, and the yoghurt mishti doi—and for winter rice cakes (pitha) made with fresh date-palm jaggery. Street snacks include fuchka (pani puri), jhalmuri (spiced puffed rice), and singara.
