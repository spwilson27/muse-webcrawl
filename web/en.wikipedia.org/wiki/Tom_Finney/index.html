Sir Thomas Finney (5 April 1922 – 14 February 2014) was an English footballer who played his entire professional career for Preston North End. Renowned for his versatility, dribbling and sportsmanship, he is regarded as one of the greatest English players of all time.

Career

Finney was born in Preston and, having served in the Royal Armoured Corps during the Second World War — including at the Battle of El Alamein — made his league debut for Preston in 1946. Equally effective on either wing or at centre-forward, he was twice named Footballer of the Year (1954 and 1957), becoming the first player to win the award twice. He famously continued to work as a plumber alongside his football career, earning the nickname "the Preston Plumber".

For England, Finney won 76 caps between 1946 and 1958 and scored 30 goals, playing at the 1950, 1954 and 1958 World Cups. His tally of caps was a record for an outfield player at the time.

Later life and honours

After retiring in 1960 he ran his plumbing business and remained a beloved figure in Preston, serving as club president. He was knighted in 1998. The Sir Tom Finney Stand at Deepdale and the famous "Splash" statue — depicting Finney sliding through a puddle in a celebrated 1956 photograph — commemorate him in his home city.
