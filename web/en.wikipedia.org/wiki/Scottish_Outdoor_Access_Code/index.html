Scottish Outdoor Access Code
Effective9 February 2005
Legal basisLand Reform (Scotland) Act 2003
Issued byScottish Natural Heritage (now NatureScot)
ScopeStatutory access rights to land and inland water in Scotland

The Scottish Outdoor Access Code provides detailed guidance on the exercise of the ancient tradition of universal access to land in Scotland, which was formally codified by the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003. Under Scots law everyone has statutory access rights to most land and inland water for recreational purposes, subject to the responsibilities in the Code.

Principles

The Code rests on three key principles: respect the interests of other people, care for the environment, and take responsibility for your own actions. Access rights apply to walking, cycling, horse riding, wild camping and non-motorised water activities, but not to motorised vehicles, hunting, shooting or fishing. Rights do not extend to houses and gardens, farm land where crops are growing, sports fields in use, or land being paid for entry.

Responsibilities

Those exercising access must keep dogs under proper control, avoid disturbing livestock and wildlife, leave no trace — the Code underpins Scotland's permissive wild-camping guidance — and follow local advice during deer-stalking and lambing seasons. Land managers, in turn, must respect access rights and not obstruct or deter access unreasonably; local authorities must uphold access and may appoint access officers and local access forums.

Significance

Scotland's regime is among the most permissive access systems in the world and is frequently compared with the Scandinavian allemansrätten (freedom to roam). It is credited with supporting Scotland's outdoor recreation and tourism economy while framing access as a reciprocal right-and-responsibility relationship.
