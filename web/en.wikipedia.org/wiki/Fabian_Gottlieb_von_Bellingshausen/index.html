Fabian Gottlieb von Bellingshausen

Born20 September 1778, Saaremaa, Governorate of Livonia, Russian Empire
Died25 January 1852, Kronstadt, Russian Empire
OccupationNaval officer, explorer

Fabian Gottlieb Thaddeus von Bellingshausen (20 September 1778 – 25 January 1852) was a Russian naval officer and explorer of Baltic German descent. He commanded the Russian expedition of 1819–1821 that circumnavigated the globe and made one of the first sightings of the Antarctic mainland in January 1820.

Expedition

Sailing with the sloops Vostok and Mirny (the latter under Mikhail Lazarev), Bellingshausen crossed the Antarctic Circle repeatedly, discovered Peter I Island and Alexander Island, and approached the Antarctic continent's ice shelf — a sighting now credited among the first of the mainland, alongside those of Edward Bransfield and Nathaniel Palmer the same year.

Later life and legacy

Bellingshausen later fought in the Russo-Turkish War and served as military governor of Kronstadt. The Bellingshausen Sea in Antarctica, a crater on the Moon and a Russian Antarctic research station bear his name.
