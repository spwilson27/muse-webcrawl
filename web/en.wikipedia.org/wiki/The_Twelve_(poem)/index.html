The Twelve (poem)

AuthorAlexander Blok
WrittenJanuary 1918
LanguageRussian (Двенадцать)
FormLong poem in twelve cantos

The Twelve (Russian: Двенадцать, Dvenadtsat) is a long poem by the Russian Symbolist poet Alexander Blok, written in January 1918, weeks after the October Revolution. It is Blok's most famous work and one of the most debated poems of the revolutionary era. (For the Roman emperors' biographies of the same numeral, see The Twelve Caesars.)

Content

Through a blizzard in Petrograd, twelve Red Guards patrol the streets, looting and shooting, marching "with a sovereign tread". One of them, Petka, has murdered his former lover Katka, now with a bourgeois officer. Around them the "old world" — a priest, a bourgeois at a crossroads, a lady in furs — cringes in the snow. In the astonishing final image, ahead of the twelve, invisible in the storm, walks Jesus Christ carrying a blood-red flag — an ending that bewildered Bolsheviks and horrified Blok's former Symbolist admirers alike.

Style and reception

The poem blends literary verse with soldiers' slang, marching songs, chastushki and criminal argot, its rhythms shifting like the storm it describes. Blok insisted he had written it in a state of heightened listening to "the music of the revolution". It was acclaimed at once and remains a central text of Russian modernism, though Blok reportedly came to be disturbed by its Christ figure.
