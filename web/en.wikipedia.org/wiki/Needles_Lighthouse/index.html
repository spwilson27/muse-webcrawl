The Needles Lighthouse is an offshore lighthouse standing at the outermost end of the Needles, a row of chalk stacks at the western extremity of the Isle of Wight in England. The present granite tower, designed under James Walker for Trinity House, was completed in 1859 and marks the western approach to the Solent.

History

A light was first established on the cliff above the Needles in 1786, but it frequently stood above low cloud and fog. Trinity House therefore built a new tower on the outermost rock at sea level. The circular granite lighthouse rises 31 metres; its light has a range of about 17 nautical miles. A helipad was added above the lantern in 1987, and the station was automated in 1994, when the last keepers left; it is now monitored from the Trinity House operations centre at Harwich.

Setting

The lighthouse is one of the most photographed maritime landmarks on the English coast. The surrounding waters are hazardous, and the nearby wreck of the Varvassi, lost in 1947, remains a hazard to yachts racing in the annual Round the Island Race.
