eThekwini Metropolitan Municipality (Zulu: UMasipala waseThekwini) is a metropolitan municipality created in 2000 that administers the city of Durban and surrounding suburbs and townships in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa. Its name derives from the Zulu word itheku, meaning "bay" or "lagoon", the name of Durban Bay.

Geography and demography

The municipality covers about 2,556 square kilometres along the Indian Ocean coast, from Umkomaas in the south to Tongaat in the north and inland to the Valley of a Thousand Hills. It includes the port of Durban — the busiest general cargo port in Africa — as well as major townships such as Umlazi, KwaMashu, Phoenix and Inanda, and suburbs including Pinetown, Umhlanga and Hillcrest. With more than four million residents, it is the third most populous metropolitan municipality in South Africa, after Johannesburg and Cape Town.

Government

eThekwini is governed by a municipal council of 222 councillors, half elected in the municipality's 111 wards and half from party lists, headed by an executive mayor. The municipality is responsible for services including water and sanitation, electricity distribution, roads, public transport, housing, waste management and the Durban metropolitan police.

History

The metropolitan municipality was established on 5 December 2000, amalgamating the former Durban transitional metropolitan council with neighbouring local and rural councils as part of South Africa's post-apartheid local government reorganization. In April 2022 the municipality was severely affected by the KwaZulu-Natal floods, among the deadliest natural disasters in the country's recorded history, which caused extensive damage to infrastructure in and around Durban.
