FieldPhysics
SubjectBehaviour and properties of light; its interaction with matter; optical instruments
Main branchesGeometrical optics, physical (wave) optics, quantum optics

Optics is the branch of physics that studies the behaviour and properties of light, including its interactions with matter and the construction of instruments that use or detect it. Optics usually describes the behaviour of visible, ultraviolet and infrared light. Because light is an electromagnetic wave, other forms of electromagnetic radiation such as X-rays and radio waves exhibit analogous properties.

History

Optics began with the ancient Greeks and was developed substantially in the medieval Islamic world, notably by Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen), whose Book of Optics (c. 1021) established an experimental, intromission-based theory of vision. Spectacles appeared in Italy in the late thirteenth century; the telescope and microscope followed around 1600. In the seventeenth century Kepler, Snell, Descartes, Fermat, Newton and Huygens laid the foundations of geometrical optics and rival corpuscular and wave theories. Young's interference experiments and Fresnel's work established the wave theory in the nineteenth century; Maxwell showed light to be an electromagnetic wave, and Einstein's 1905 explanation of the photoelectric effect founded the quantum view, in which light also behaves as particles (photons).

Branches

Geometrical optics treats light as rays that travel in straight lines and bend at surfaces according to the laws of reflection and refraction; it underlies the design of lenses, mirrors, cameras and telescopes. Physical optics uses the wave model to explain interference, diffraction, polarisation and scattering. Quantum optics studies light at the level of photons, underpinning lasers, fibre-optic communications and quantum information science. Applied optics overlaps with physiology (vision and ophthalmic optics) and engineering disciplines such as photonics and optoelectronics.
