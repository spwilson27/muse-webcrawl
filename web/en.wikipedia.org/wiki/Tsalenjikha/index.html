Tsalenjikha (Georgian: წალენჯიხა) is a town in the Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti region of western Georgia, located on the Enguri River near the foothills of the Greater Caucasus. It serves as the administrative centre of the Tsalenjikha Municipality.

History

The area around Tsalenjikha has been inhabited since ancient times and was historically part of the principality of Mingrelia. The town developed as a local market and administrative centre during the Soviet period, when it gained town status. The monumental Tsalenjikha Cathedral Church of the Saviour, dating from the 14th century, stands in the town and is noted for its cycle of Byzantine-style frescoes commissioned by the Dadiani princes.

Geography

Tsalenjikha lies in the Colchic lowland foothills, in a humid subtropical climate zone characterised by heavy rainfall. The surrounding municipality borders Abkhazia to the northwest and includes mountainous terrain rising toward Svaneti. Tea plantations and hazelnut cultivation have traditionally been important to the local economy.
