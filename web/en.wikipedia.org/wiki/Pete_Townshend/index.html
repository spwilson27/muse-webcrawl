Peter Dennis Blandford Townshend (born 19 May 1945) is an English musician and songwriter, best known as the guitarist, co-lead vocalist, and principal songwriter of The Who, one of the most influential rock bands of the twentieth century. Not to be confused with the BBC dance DJ Pete Tong.

Career with The Who

Townshend formed the band that became The Who in the early 1960s with Roger Daltrey, John Entwistle, and Keith Moon. He wrote most of the band's catalogue, including the anthem "My Generation" (1965) and the rock operas Tommy (1969) — about a deaf, dumb and blind pinball prodigy — and Quadrophenia (1973). His windmill guitar style, feedback experimentation, and on-stage guitar smashing defined the band's live image, while his songwriting addressed adolescence, identity, and spirituality, the latter influenced by the teachings of Meher Baba.

Solo work and writing

Townshend has released solo albums including Empty Glass (1980, with the hit "Let My Love Open the Door"), founded Eel Pie Publishing and a bookshop, and edited works including a volume of Meher Baba's writings. His memoir Who I Am appeared in 2012, and his novel The Age of Anxiety in 2019. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with The Who in 1990 and received Kennedy Center Honors in 2008.
