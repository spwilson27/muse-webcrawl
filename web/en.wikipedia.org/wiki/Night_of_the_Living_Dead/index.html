Night of the Living Dead is a 1968 American independent horror film directed, photographed and edited by George A. Romero. Starring Duane Jones and Judith O'Dea, it follows seven people trapped in a farmhouse besieged by reanimated corpses.

Night of the Living Dead

Night of the Living Dead is a 1968 American black-and-white horror film directed by George A. Romero, who co-wrote it with John Russo. Made in Pittsburgh on a budget of about $114,000, it became one of the most influential films of the twentieth century, effectively creating the modern zombie genre.

Plot

Siblings Barbra and Johnny are attacked at a rural cemetery by a strange, violent man; Barbra flees to a farmhouse, where she is joined by Ben (Duane Jones), a resourceful drifter, and several others hiding from an outbreak in which the recently dead return to life and devour the living. Broadcasts attribute the phenomenon to radiation from a returning space probe. As the ghouls mass outside, conflicts within the house prove as deadly as the threat without, and in the morning Ben — the sole survivor — is shot dead by a sheriff's posse that mistakes him for one of the undead.

Reception and legacy

Initially controversial for its graphic violence and bleak ending, and screened controversially at children's matinees, the film was a major box-office success and was gradually recognised as a landmark. Its casting of a Black actor as the competent hero, and its apocalyptic tone amid the Vietnam War era, have been read as social commentary, though Romero said the casting was not intended as a statement. Because its distributor omitted the copyright notice on the prints, the film entered the public domain, enabling countless re-releases. Romero followed it with sequels including Dawn of the Dead (1978) and Day of the Dead (1985), and it was remade in 1990 by Tom Savini. In 1999 it was added to the United States National Film Registry.
