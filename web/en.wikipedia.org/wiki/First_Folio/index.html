First Folio

The First Folio is a collection of William Shakespeare's plays — officially titled Mr. William Shakespeares Comedies, Histories, & Tragedies — published in 1623, about seven years after Shakespeare's death. Prepared by his fellow actors John Heminges and Henry Condell, it contains 36 plays, 18 of them printed for the first time; without it, plays including Macbeth, Julius Caesar, Twelfth Night, The Tempest and Antony and Cleopatra might have been lost.

Publication

The volume was printed in the workshop of William and Isaac Jaggard and entered in the Stationers' Register in November 1623. It sold for about one pound unbound — expensive for the time. Around 750 copies are thought to have been printed; 235 are known to survive, most incomplete. The Folger Shakespeare Library in Washington, D.C., holds 82 copies, the largest collection.

Contents and importance

The plays are grouped as Comedies, Histories and Tragedies. The texts were set from Shakespeare's manuscripts ("foul papers"), fair copies, prompt books and earlier quartos. The Folio is the sole source for 18 plays and the authoritative text for many others, making it one of the most influential books in English literature. Later folios (Second, 1632; Third, 1663; Fourth, 1685) reprinted it with additions and corrections. Complete copies now sell for millions of pounds at auction.
