Ibn Tibbon

The Ibn Tibbon family (Hebrew: ابن طبون) was a family of Jewish grammarians, translators and philosophers active in southern France and Spain during the 12th and 13th centuries. Their translations of Arabic-language works of science and philosophy into Hebrew were crucial in transmitting Greek and Islamic learning to Jewish communities in Christian Europe.

Notable members

Judah ben Saul ibn Tibbon (c. 1120 – c. 1190), born in Granada, settled in Lunel, France. He translated Bahya ibn Paquda's Duties of the Heart, Judah Halevi's Kuzari, and works of Saadia Gaon and Jonah ibn Janah.

Samuel ben Judah ibn Tibbon (c. 1150 – c. 1230), Judah's son, is best known for his Hebrew translation of Maimonides' Guide for the Perplexed, completed in 1204, and for his own philosophical commentary. He corresponded with Maimonides about translation questions.

Moses ibn Tibbon (fl. 1240–1283), Samuel's son, translated works of astronomy, mathematics and medicine, including writings of Euclid, Averroes and al-Farabi.

Legacy

The Tibbonids created much of the Hebrew philosophical and scientific vocabulary still in use, coining new terms to render Arabic concepts. Their translations helped make the works of Aristotle, Averroes and Maimonides accessible to Hebrew readers.
