Blood vessel

Cross-section of an artery showing its three layers

Blood vessels are the components of the circulatory system that transport blood throughout the human body. These vessels transport blood cells, nutrients, and oxygen to the tissues of the body. They also take waste and carbon dioxide away from the tissues. Blood vessels are needed to sustain life, because all of the body's tissues rely on their functionality.

Types

There are five types of blood vessels: the arteries, which carry blood away from the heart; the arterioles; the capillaries, where the exchange of water and chemicals between the blood and the tissues occurs; the venules; and the veins, which carry blood from the capillaries back towards the heart.

Structure

The arteries and veins have three layers. The middle layer is thicker in the arteries than it is in the veins:

  • The inner layer, the tunica intima, is the thinnest layer, formed of a single layer of endothelial cells.
  • The middle layer, the tunica media, is the thickest layer, composed of smooth muscle cells and elastic tissue.
  • The outer layer, the tunica adventitia, is made of collagen fibres and provides structure and support.

Capillaries consist of a single layer of endothelial cells with a supporting basement membrane, allowing easy exchange of substances with surrounding tissues.

Function

Blood vessels function to transport blood throughout the body. Arteries carry oxygenated blood away from the heart (except for the pulmonary arteries), while veins return deoxygenated blood to the heart (except for the pulmonary veins). The contraction of smooth muscle in vessel walls — vasoconstriction and vasodilation — regulates blood pressure and directs flow to where it is needed most. If laid end to end, the blood vessels of an adult human would stretch for roughly 100,000 kilometres.

Disease

Blood vessel diseases include atherosclerosis, in which fatty deposits narrow the arteries; aneurysms, in which a weakened wall bulges and may rupture; and vasculitis, inflammation of the vessel wall. Such diseases are major causes of heart attack and stroke worldwide.
