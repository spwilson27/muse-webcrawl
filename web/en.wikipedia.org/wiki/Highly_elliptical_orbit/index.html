Highly elliptical orbit

A highly elliptical orbit (HEO) is an elliptic orbit with high eccentricity, usually referring to one around the Earth. Examples include the Molniya orbit and the tundra orbit.

Characteristics

By Kepler's second law, a satellite in a highly elliptical orbit moves slowly near apogee and quickly near perigee. It therefore spends most of its orbital period high over one hemisphere, making such orbits useful for communications and surveillance of high-latitude regions that geostationary satellites serve poorly. The Molniya orbit, with a period of about half a sidereal day and an inclination of about 63.4 degrees, keeps its apogee over the northern hemisphere.

Uses

Highly elliptical orbits have been used for communications satellites serving Russia and northern latitudes, for the Sirius Satellite Radio constellation, and for some military early-warning and signals-intelligence satellites.
