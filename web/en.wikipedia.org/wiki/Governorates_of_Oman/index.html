Governorates of Oman

Oman is divided into eleven governorates (muḥāfaẓāt, singular muḥāfaẓa), the first-level administrative subdivisions of the Sultanate. The governorates are in turn subdivided into provinces known as wilayats (singular wilaya), of which there are 63. The current system dates from a royal decree of 2011, which reorganized the country's previous regions and governorates into eleven governorates in order to promote decentralization and balanced regional development. Each governorate is headed by a governor appointed by the Sultan, and governorates have been given growing responsibility for local development planning, municipal affairs, and the administration of government services.

History

Before 2011, Oman was divided into a mixture of regions (manāṭiq) and governorates that had evolved over decades: the governorates of Muscat, Dhofar and Musandam, and the regions of Ad Dakhiliyah, Al Batinah, Ash Sharqiyah, Ad Dhahirah and Al Wusta. Royal Decree 114/2011, issued in October 2011, restructured the system: Al Batinah was split into North and South Al Batinah, Ash Sharqiyah into North and South Ash Sharqiyah, and Ad Dhahirah was divided into Ad Dhahirah and Al Buraimi, producing the present eleven governorates. The reform accompanied a broader program of administrative decentralization, expanded under Sultan Haitham bin Tariq, including a System of Governorates and Municipal Affairs (2020) that strengthened governors' authority over local development budgets.

Administration

Governors are appointed by royal decree and report within a framework coordinated by the Ministry of Interior; municipal councils, first elected in 2012, advise on local services. The 2020 System of Governorates and Municipal Affairs (Royal Decree 101/2020) granted governorates legal personality and financial and administrative independence in managing local development, consistent with Oman Vision 2040's decentralization goals. National statistics (such as census data from the National Centre for Statistics and Information) are reported by governorate, making them the standard frame for regional comparisons of population, housing and employment.
