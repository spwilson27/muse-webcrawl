Wang Shu

Wang Shu (Chinese: 王澍; born 4 November 1963) is a Chinese architect, co-founder with his wife Lu Wenyu of Amateur Architecture Studio in Hangzhou. In 2012 he became the first Chinese citizen to receive the Pritzker Architecture Prize, honoured for work that opens "new horizons while at the same time resonating with place and memory" — notably his use of recycled materials and traditional craftsmanship amid China's rapid urbanisation.

Career

Wang Shu studied at the Nanjing Institute of Technology (now Southeast University) and, unusually, spent much of the 1990s not designing but working on building sites with craftsmen, learning construction first-hand — an experience that shaped his material philosophy. In 1997 he founded Amateur Architecture Studio with Lu Wenyu; the name rejects the "professional, soulless" architecture of China's building boom in favour of an amateur's spontaneity and attention to everyday life.

Major works

The Xiangshan Campus of the China Academy of Art in Hangzhou (2004–07), where Wang Shu heads the architecture department, arranges rammed-earth and tile-roofed buildings around the hill like a Chinese landscape scroll. The Ningbo Museum (2008) rises like a mountain or fortress, its walls built by the traditional wapan technique from millions of recycled bricks and tiles salvaged from demolished villages — a memorial, in material, to the history China's cities were erasing. Other works include the Ningbo Tengtou Pavilion for the Shanghai Expo (2010) and the renovation of Wencun village in Fuyang.

Philosophy

Wang Shu insists that "before being an architect, one must be a scholar" and that architecture should recover the continuity of Chinese building culture — its courtyards, recycled materials, and cooperation with nature — rather than imitate Western icons. His Pritzker acceptance was widely read in China as a turning point for the country's own architectural culture.
