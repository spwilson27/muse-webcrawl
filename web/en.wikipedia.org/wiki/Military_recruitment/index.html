Military recruitment

Recruiting stations are the public face of volunteer armed forces.

Military recruitment is the process by which armed forces attract, select, and enlist personnel. It encompasses voluntary enlistment in professional forces, conscription (compulsory service), and hybrid systems combining the two. Recruitment policy shapes the size, quality, age profile, and social composition of a state's military.

Voluntary recruitment

Many states, including the United States (since 1973), the United Kingdom, India, and Japan, rely on all-volunteer forces. Recruiters use advertising, school and community outreach, and incentives such as enlistment bonuses, education benefits, and citizenship pathways to meet annual accession goals. The I Want YOU Uncle Sam poster of the First World War remains the iconic image of American recruiting.

Conscription

Other states maintain compulsory service, including Israel, South Korea, Singapore, Switzerland, Finland, and, since its 2022 full-scale invasion, Ukraine and Russia. Conscription systems typically require registration of young men (and, increasingly, women, as in Norway and Sweden) and call-up for training periods ranging from a few months to several years.

Eligibility and controversies

Minimum enlistment ages vary; the Optional Protocol to the Convention on the Rights of the Child sets standards against the military use of children, yet child soldiers remain a documented problem in some conflicts. Recruitment debates include the socioeconomic profile of volunteers, recruiting in schools, medical and fitness standards, and shortfalls during strong labor markets.
