Electrical wiring in the United Kingdom

Electrical wiring in the United Kingdom is commonly understood to be an electrical installation for operation by end users within domestic, commercial, industrial, and other buildings, and also in special installations and locations, such as marinas or petrol filling stations. It does not normally cover the transmission or distribution of electricity to them.

Standards

Installations are governed by BS 7671, "Requirements for Electrical Installations", published by the Institution of Engineering and Technology (IET), currently in its 18th edition. In England and Wales, most electrical work in dwellings is controlled under Part P of the Building Regulations as "notifiable" work requiring certification by a competent person.

Key features

The UK uses a nominal mains voltage of 230 V AC at 50 Hz. Distinctive features include the ring final circuit for socket outlets, wired in a loop and protected by a 32 A breaker, and the BS 1363 plug, which incorporates an internal fuse (3 A or 13 A) and shuttered sockets. Modern wiring uses harmonised cable colours: brown for line, blue for neutral, and green/yellow for protective earth. Distribution boards (consumer units) typically include residual-current devices (RCDs) protecting groups of circuits.

Terminology

British practice uses terms such as "consumer unit" for the distribution board, "ring main" colloquially for the ring final circuit, and "fused spur" for a fused connection unit — reflecting the UK's distinctive combination of high-current ring circuits and individually fused plugs.
