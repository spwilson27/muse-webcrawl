EDVAC

EDVAC (Electronic Discrete Variable Automatic Computer) was one of the earliest electronic computers. It was built by the University of Pennsylvania's Moore School of Electrical Engineering for the United States Army's Ballistics Research Laboratory.

EDVAC
Developer: Moore School, University of Pennsylvania
Released: 1951
Predecessor: ENIAC

History

EDVAC was designed by John Mauchly, J. Presper Eckert and others. John von Neumann's 1945 First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC popularised the stored-program concept, often called the von Neumann architecture.

Design

Unlike ENIAC, EDVAC was binary rather than decimal, used mercury delay-line memory of about 5.5 kilobytes in modern terms, and was a stored-program computer. It was delivered in 1949 and operated until 1961.
