Saint Stephen's Day, also called the Feast of Saint Stephen, is a Christian saint's day on 26 December in the Western Church (27 December in the East) commemorating Saint Stephen, the first martyr of Christianity, stoned to death in Jerusalem around AD 34 as recorded in the Acts of the Apostles. It is a public holiday in many European countries and coincides with Boxing Day in the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth.

Customs

In Ireland the day is called Wren Day (Lá an Dreoilín): "wren boys" in straw costume go from house to house with music, traditionally re-enacting the hunting of the wren, the "king of all birds", which folklore blamed for betraying Stephen's hiding place. In Catalonia, Sant Esteve is a major family feast day, often featuring canelons made from Christmas leftovers. In Finland (Tapaninpäivä) and Sweden (Annandag jul), the day was traditionally for sleigh rides, visiting and dances — and in the Åland and Finnish tradition, the "Stephen's ride" to farms and weddings. In Hungary and Croatia (Štefanje), name-day visiting for men named Stephen/István/Stjepan is customary.

In song

The carol "Good King Wenceslas", written by John Mason Neale in 1853, is set on "the Feast of Stephen", and tells of the Bohemian duke Wenceslaus braving winter weather to give alms to a poor peasant.
