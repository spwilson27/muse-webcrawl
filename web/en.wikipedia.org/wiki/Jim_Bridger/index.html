James Felix "Jim" Bridger (March 17, 1804 – July 17, 1881) was an American mountain man, trapper, Army scout, and wilderness guide who explored and trapped in the Western United States during the first half of the 19th century. He was known as Old Gabe in his later years. Bridger is known for his expeditions' findings, among them — according to tradition — being among the first non-Native Americans to see the geysers and other natural wonders of the Yellowstone region, and for his reports of the Great Salt Lake.

Life and career

Orphaned young in St. Louis, Bridger joined William Ashley and Andrew Henry's fur-trapping expedition up the Missouri River in 1822, alongside other notable mountain men such as Jedediah Smith and Hugh Glass. He spent more than two decades trapping beaver throughout the Rocky Mountains and became a partner in the Rocky Mountain Fur Company. In 1843 he and Louis Vasquez established Fort Bridger on the Green River in present-day Wyoming, which became an important supply point on the Oregon, California, and Mormon trails.

Bridger served as a guide for U.S. Army expeditions, including the surveying expeditions of Captain Howard Stansbury and the Raynolds Expedition, and acted as an intermediary in dealings with Native American tribes, among whom he had extensive family ties. He scouted routes that influenced later road and rail construction, and Bridger Pass in Wyoming bears his name. He died in 1881 near Kansas City, Missouri.

Legacy

Numerous places are named for Bridger, including the Bridger Mountains and Bridger-Teton National Forest in Wyoming, Fort Bridger State Historic Site, and the town of Bridger, Montana.
