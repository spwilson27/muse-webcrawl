Lodovico Ferrari
Born: 2 February 1522, Bologna
Died: 5 October 1565 (aged 43), Bologna
Known for: Solution of the quartic equation

Lodovico Ferrari (2 February 1522 – 5 October 1565) was an Italian mathematician best known today for solving the quartic (fourth-degree) polynomial equation.

Ferrari, Cardano's brilliant young protégé.

Life

Born in Bologna to a poor family, Ferrari was taken into the household of the polymath Gerolamo Cardano as a servant at the age of fourteen. Cardano recognised his quick intelligence, taught him mathematics, and employed him as his secretary. By the age of eighteen Ferrari had worked out an algebraic method for solving quartic equations by reducing them to a cubic, building on the cubic solution associated with Cardano, Tartaglia and del Ferro.

The Tartaglia controversy

Ferrari became Cardano's champion in the famous priority dispute with Niccolò Tartaglia over publication of the cubic solution in Cardano's Ars Magna (1545), which also printed Ferrari's quartic method. The two exchanged vitriolic mathematical challenges (cartelli), culminating in a public contest in Milan in August 1548, from which Ferrari emerged the effective victor; Tartaglia left after the first day.

Later years and death

The contest won Ferrari fame and lucrative offers; he became tax assessor to the governor of Milan and later professor of mathematics at Bologna (1564). He died in Bologna in 1565, aged only 43 — according to Cardano, poisoned by his own sister, though the story is doubtful.

Legacy

Ferrari's method remains the standard classical solution of the quartic: by introducing an auxiliary parameter, the quartic is reduced to a cubic (the resolvent) and the rest follows by quadratic equations.
