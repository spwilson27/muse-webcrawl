Ekati Diamond Mine

Ekati Diamond Mine
Location: Lac de Gras, Northwest Territories, Canada
Opened: 1998
Products: Diamonds

The Ekati Diamond Mine, often simply called Ekati, is Canada's first surface and underground diamond mine, located at Lac de Gras in the Northwest Territories, approximately 300 kilometres northeast of Yellowknife and about 200 kilometres south of the Arctic Circle.

History

Diamonds were discovered in the Lac de Gras region in 1991 by geologists Chuck Fipke and Stewart Blusson, sparking one of the largest staking rushes in Canadian history. The mine was developed by BHP (later BHP Billiton) and officially opened in October 1998. The name "Ekati" is a Tłı̨chǫ (Dogrib) word meaning "fat lake", referring to the white quartz veins in the area that resemble caribou fat.

Operations

Production began with open-pit mining of several kimberlite pipes, including Panda, Koala, Fox, and Misery, later supplemented by underground operations beneath Koala and Koala North. Ore is processed on site and the diamonds are sorted and valued in Yellowknife. The mine is accessible year-round by air and during winter by the Tibbitt to Contwoyto Winter Road. Ownership has changed several times: Dominion Diamond Corporation acquired the mine in 2013, and it later passed to the Arctic Canadian Diamond Company.
