Marcomanni

The Marcomanni in Bohemia, early 1st century AD

The Marcomanni were a Germanic people, part of the Suebi group, first mentioned by Julius Caesar in connection with the campaigns of Ariovistus in Gaul (58 BC). Their name is usually interpreted as "men of the borderland" or "march-men".

Kingdom of Maroboduus

Around 9 BC, under their king Maroboduus, the Marcomanni migrated from the Main valley into Bohemia, where Maroboduus built a powerful kingdom that at its height dominated many neighbouring tribes. Rome regarded him as a serious threat; Tiberius planned a major invasion in AD 6 that was abandoned because of the revolt in Pannonia. Maroboduus maintained an uneasy peace with Rome, defeated an attempt by Arminius to extend his power eastward, and was eventually overthrown in AD 19 by the exile Catualda, fleeing to Roman protection at Ravenna.

Marcomannic Wars

The Marcomanni are best known for the Marcomannic Wars (AD 166–180), when, together with the Quadi, Sarmatians, and other peoples, they crossed the Danube and invaded the Roman Empire during the reign of Marcus Aurelius. Epidemic disease, Roman counteroffensives, and diplomacy eventually restored the frontier, and the emperor died at Vindobona (Vienna) in 180 while planning further campaigns. The wars are depicted on the Column of Marcus Aurelius in Rome.

Later history

In the 4th and 5th centuries the Marcomanni appear allied with the Quadi and later movements of peoples; some followed Radagaisus into Italy in 405/406. Their name disappears from the record during the Migration Period, and their territory in Bohemia was eventually settled by Slavs.
