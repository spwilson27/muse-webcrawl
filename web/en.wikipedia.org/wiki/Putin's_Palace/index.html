Putin's Palace

"Putin's Palace"
LocationCape Idokopas, near Gelendzhik, Krasnodar Krai, Russia
ConstructionBegan c. 2005
GroundsAbout 68 hectares
Alleged ownerBeneficially Vladimir Putin (per FBK); denied by Putin; claimed by Arkady Rotenberg

"Putin's Palace" is the popular name for a large Italianate palace complex on Cape Idokopas, on the Black Sea coast near the resort town of Gelendzhik in Krasnodar Krai, Russia. The estate became internationally known after a 2021 investigation by Alexei Navalny's Anti-Corruption Foundation (FBK) alleged that the residence — estimated by the investigators to have cost around 100 billion rubles (about US$1.35 billion) — was built for the personal benefit of Russian president Vladimir Putin through a corrupt scheme involving his inner circle. Putin has denied the palace belongs to him, and businessman Arkady Rotenberg subsequently claimed ownership, saying he was developing the site as an apart-hotel.

The estate

The heavily guarded complex, surrounded by a no-fly zone and Federal Security Service (FSB) land, includes a main palace of roughly 17,700 square metres, an underground complex, a hockey rink, an amphitheatre, greenhouses, a helipad, a church, a tunnel to the beach with a tasting room overlooking the sea, and vineyards with a winery. The FBK film reconstructed the interiors from leaked architectural plans, showing amenities such as a hookah lounge, a theatre, a casino room, a swimming pool and a pole-dancing-equipped "aqua-discotheque" fountain.

Allegations and history

Claims about the palace first surfaced in 2010 in an open letter by businessman Sergei Kolesnikov to President Dmitry Medvedev, describing a project funded through diverted donations. The FBK film, Putin's Palace: The Story of the World's Largest Bribe, was released on 19 January 2021, two days after Navalny's arrest on returning to Russia, and quickly accumulated over 100 million views on YouTube, becoming one of the most-watched investigative reports in Russian history. It contributed to nationwide protests in January 2021.

Reactions

The Kremlin rejected the allegations as nonsense and a compilation of publicly known material. Days after release, state television aired footage showing the palace's interior under renovation and largely unfinished, and Rotenberg, a long-time associate of Putin, announced in an interview that he was the true owner and had bought the property to convert into a hotel. Independent journalists noted continuing tight security and FSB control of the surrounding land. In 2024, further investigations by Navalny's team published updated 3D reconstructions claiming luxury interiors had since been completed or remodelled, including new amenities added after the start of the war in Ukraine.
