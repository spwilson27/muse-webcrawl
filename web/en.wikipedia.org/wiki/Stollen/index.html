Stollen

Stollen (German pronunciation: [ˈʃtɔlən]) is a fruit bread of nuts, spices, and dried or candied fruit, coated with powdered sugar or icing sugar and often containing marzipan. It is a traditional German Christmas bread, known as Weihnachtsstollen or Christstollen.

Stollen
Type: Fruit bread
Place of origin: Germany (Dresden, Saxony)
Main ingredients: Flour, butter, dried fruit, candied peel, marzipan, powdered sugar
Occasion: Christmas / Advent

History

Stollen is documented in Dresden from the 15th century; early versions, baked during Advent fasting, were plain until Saxon rulers obtained a papal "Butter Letter" permitting butter. Dresden's Stollenfest celebrates a giant stollen each December, recalling a huge cake baked for Augustus the Strong in 1730.

Varieties

Types include Dresdner Christstollen (protected designation, with a seal), Mandelstollen (almond), Mohnstollen (poppy seed), Quarkstollen (curd cheese) and Butterstollen. The folded shape is said to evoke the swaddled Christ child.

Preparation

A rich yeast dough with rum-soaked raisins and candied citrus peel is baked, brushed with melted butter and dredged in sugar, then matured for weeks.
