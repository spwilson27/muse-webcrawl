Energy drink

An energy drink is a type of functional beverage containing stimulant compounds, usually caffeine, marketed as providing mental and physical stimulation. Typical ingredients include caffeine (commonly about 80 mg per 250 ml can, similar to a cup of coffee), taurine, B vitamins, sugars or sweeteners, and herbal extracts such as guarana and ginseng.

History and market

Precursors include Japan's Lipovitan D (1962) and Thailand's Krating Daeng (1976), from which Red Bull was adapted and launched in Austria in 1987. The global market grew to tens of billions of dollars, dominated by brands such as Red Bull, Monster, and Rockstar, with aggressive marketing aimed at young consumers, extreme sports, gaming, and esports sponsorships.

Effects

Caffeine improves alertness and reaction time and can modestly enhance endurance performance; sugar contributes readily available calories. Claims for taurine and B vitamins at typical doses are weakly supported. Adverse effects include insomnia, anxiety, palpitations and raised blood pressure, especially in children, adolescents and people sensitive to caffeine. Mixing energy drinks with alcohol is associated with riskier behaviour because stimulants mask perceived intoxication.

Regulation

The EU requires drinks with more than 150 mg/l of caffeine to carry a "high caffeine content" warning and a statement that they are not recommended for children or pregnant women. Lithuania and Latvia banned sales to under-18s, and the UK government has legislated to prohibit sales to under-16s in England. Some countries cap caffeine content per serving.
