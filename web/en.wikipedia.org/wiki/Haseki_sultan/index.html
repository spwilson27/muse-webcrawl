Haseki sultan (Ottoman Turkish: خاصكي سلطان) was the title used for the chief consort of an Ottoman sultan. Created in the 1530s for Hürrem Sultan, the legal wife of Suleiman the Magnificent, it ranked the holder above other consorts (kadin) and second in the imperial harem only to the valide sultan, the sultan's mother. The word haseki derives from the Arabic khāṣṣa, denoting someone especially close or favoured.

History

Before Hürrem, no sultan had legally married a consort since the 15th century, and the mother of the heir held no special chief-consort rank. Suleiman's marriage to Hürrem and the creation of the haseki title broke with tradition and signalled the rising political importance of the imperial women during the period historians call the Sultanate of Women. A haseki received a large daily stipend, a fur coat, and her own apartments and household; if she bore the heir and survived the sultan, she might become valide sultan.

Notable holders

Famous haseki sultans include Hürrem Sultan (Suleiman I), Nurbanu Sultan (Selim II), Safiye Sultan (Murad III), Kösem Sultan (Ahmed I) — who later became one of the most powerful valide sultans — Turhan Sultan (Ibrahim I), and Rabia Gülnuş Sultan (Mehmed IV). From the later 17th century the title fell out of use and the rank of chief consort was expressed simply as the senior kadin.
