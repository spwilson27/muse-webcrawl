Fountain

A fountain, from the Latin "fons" (genitive "fontis"), meaning source or spring, is a decorative reservoir used for discharging water. It is also a structure that jets water into the air for a decorative or dramatic effect. Fountains are used for decoration and celebration of their builders. Ancient fountains were powered by gravity, today fountains are powered by pumps.

History

The earliest known fountains were stone basins in ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt used to collect drinking and ritual water. Greek and Roman cities developed public fountains fed by aqueducts; the Romans built elaborate monumental fountains (nymphaea) and celebrated spring-fed shrines. In the Islamic world, fountains became central features of courtyards, mosques and gardens, while medieval European towns built communal fountains as water sources and symbols of civic pride, such as the Schöner Brunnen in Nuremberg.

Renaissance and Baroque designers — notably in Rome and at Versailles under André Le Nôtre — made the fountain a grand artistic form, combining sculpture, hydraulics and allegory. Famous examples include the Trevi Fountain in Rome (1762) and the fountains of the Palace of Versailles.

Types

Fountains include wall fountains, tiered fountains, drinking fountains, musical fountains that synchronise water, light and music, and dry-deck or splash-pad fountains. Modern large-scale examples include the Bellagio Fountains in Las Vegas and The Dubai Fountain.
