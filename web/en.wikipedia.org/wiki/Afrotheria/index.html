OriginAfrica, Late Cretaceous (~100 mya)

Afrotheria is a superorder of placental mammals whose living members originated in Africa. Molecular phylogenetic studies since the late 1990s have shown that seemingly disparate animals — elephants, manatees, hyraxes, aardvarks, elephant shrews, golden moles, and tenrecs — actually form a single evolutionary clade that diverged from the rest of the placentals around 100 million years ago, when Africa was becoming isolated as a continent.

Members

Living afrotherians include:

  • Proboscidea — elephants
  • Sirenia — manatees and dugongs
  • Hyracoidea — hyraxes
  • Macroscelidea — elephant shrews (sengis)
  • Tubulidentata — the aardvark
  • Afrosoricida — tenrecs and golden moles

Elephants, manatees, and hyraxes are often grouped as the clade Paenungulata ("almost ungulates"), long suspected on morphological grounds before the larger Afrotheria clade was established on DNA evidence.

Evolution

The afrotherian radiation is considered an example of an isolated continental fauna maturing in place — comparable to marsupials in Australasia or xenarthrans in South America. Fossil afrotherians were diverse, including the giant horned embrithopods and marine desmostylians. Because the group is defined mostly by molecular synapomorphies, its anatomical recognition in fossils remains difficult.

Significance

Afrotheria was one of the first major placental clades recognized primarily through molecular rather than anatomical evidence, helping to reorganize early placental evolution around continental fragmentation. It is one of four great placental superorders, alongside Xenarthra, Euarchontoglires, and Laurasiatheria.
