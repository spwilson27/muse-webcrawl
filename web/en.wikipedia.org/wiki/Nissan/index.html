Nissan

Nissan Motor Co., Ltd.
Founded: 26 December 1933
Headquarters: Yokohama, Japan
Industry: Automotive
Brands: Nissan, Infiniti, Datsun (discontinued)
Alliance: Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi

Nissan Motor Co., Ltd. is a Japanese multinational automobile manufacturer headquartered in Yokohama, Kanagawa, Japan. One of the largest car manufacturers in the world, it sells vehicles under the Nissan and Infiniti brands and was for decades associated with the Datsun nameplate. Since 1999 it has been part of the Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance.

History

The company's roots go back to the Kwaishinsha works founded by Masujiro Hashimoto in 1911, which produced the DAT cars. Nissan Motor was formally established in 1933 under Yoshisuke Aikawa, taking over the automobile operations of Tobata Casting, and began mass production of Datsun cars at Yokohama. After the Second World War Nissan expanded internationally, establishing Nissan Motor Corporation USA in 1960 and plants in Mexico, the United States, and at Sunderland in the United Kingdom. By the late 1990s the company was near bankruptcy; an alliance with Renault in 1999 under Carlos Ghosn led to the "Nissan Revival Plan" and a return to profitability.

Products

Nissan's range includes the Micra/March, Note, Leaf, Juke, Qashqai, X-Trail/Rogue, Patrol, and the GT-R sports car. The Leaf, launched in 2010, was among the first mass-market battery electric vehicles and for years the best-selling electric car in history. Nissan's luxury division Infiniti was launched in 1989. The company has a long motorsport heritage, including success in rallying, touring cars, and endurance racing.

Alliance and recent developments

The arrest of Ghosn in 2018 triggered a prolonged restructuring of the alliance; in 2023 Renault and Nissan agreed to rebalance their cross-shareholdings at 15% each. Nissan has invested heavily in electrification under its "Ambition 2030" plan and its e-Power hybrid technology.
