George Bellows
Born: August 12, 1882, Columbus, Ohio, U.S.
Died: January 8, 1925 (aged 42), New York City, U.S.
Known for: Painting, lithography
Movement: Ashcan School, American realism

George Wesley Bellows (August 12 or August 19, 1882 – January 8, 1925) was an American realist painter, known for his bold depictions of urban life in New York City, and a leading figure of the Ashcan School. He is best remembered for his vigorous paintings of prizefighting — above all Stag at Sharkey's (1909) and Dempsey and Firpo (1924) — as well as for street scenes, portraits, landscapes, and an influential body of lithographs.

Early life and training

Bellows was born in Columbus, Ohio, and attended Ohio State University, where he played baseball and basketball well enough to be offered a professional baseball contract; he declined it to pursue art. In 1904 he moved to New York and studied at the New York School of Art under Robert Henri, whose circle of urban realists — the group later nicknamed the Ashcan School — encouraged painting modern city life in all its roughness.

Career

Bellows achieved success quickly: his painting River Rats was shown in 1907, and by 1909, at age 27, he was elected an associate member of the National Academy of Design, then one of the youngest ever. His boxing pictures, painted at a time when public prizefighting was illegal in New York and bouts were staged as private "athletic club" events, captured the violence and theatre of the ring. He also painted the excavation of Pennsylvania Station (Pennsylvania Excavation), tenement life, and later, in summer colonies in Maine and California, seascapes and landscapes influenced by colour theory and dynamic symmetry. During the First World War he produced a series of paintings and lithographs about German atrocities in Belgium.

Bellows was also one of the foremost American lithographers of his generation, producing over 190 lithographs, and he taught at the Art Students League. His retrospective at the Metropolitan Museum of Art was being prepared when he died suddenly of peritonitis following a ruptured appendix in January 1925, aged 42.

Legacy

Bellows's work is held by major American museums, and his prices have set records for American art of his period; Men at Work and Polo Crowd achieved notable auction results. A major retrospective toured in 2012–13 to the National Gallery of Art, the Metropolitan Museum, and the Royal Academy in London — the first comprehensive exhibition of his work in a generation.
