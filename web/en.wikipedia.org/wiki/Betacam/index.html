Betacam is a family of half-inch professional videocassette products developed by Sony, introduced on 7 August 1982. Using cassettes derived mechanically from the consumer Betamax format but recording component video at much higher tape speed, Betacam became the dominant format for television news gathering and studio production worldwide for more than two decades.

Variants

The original Betacam was followed by Betacam SP (Superior Performance, 1986), which used metal-particle tape and became the broadcast standard of the 1990s. Digital successors included Digital Betacam (1993), Betacam SX (1996), MPEG IMX (2001), and the high-definition HDCAM family (1997 onward). The formats share cassette shells in two sizes, S and L.

Use

Betacam camcorders combined camera and recorder in a single unit, making them ideal for electronic news gathering; tapes could be edited directly in broadcast suites. Although digital file-based workflows displaced tape in production, vast broadcast archives remain stored on Betacam-family cassettes, and the format name became a generic term for professional broadcast video.
