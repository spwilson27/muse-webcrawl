Testosterone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A molecular model of testosterone, balls and sticks in dark colours
A molecular model of testosterone, balls and sticks in dark colours

Testosterone is the principal male sex hormone and an anabolic steroid. In humans it drives the development of male reproductive tissues, secondary sexual characteristics (facial hair, deepened voice, muscle mass) and libido, and is produced mainly by the testes (in females, in smaller amounts, by the ovaries and adrenal glands). Isolated and synthesised in 1935, it is used medically for hypogonadism and widely misused for performance enhancement.

Physiology

Testosterone circulates mostly bound to SHBG and albumin; a fraction converts to dihydrotestosterone (the potent androgen of prostate and hair follicles) and to estradiol (essential for bone and brain in both sexes). Levels peak in late adolescence, decline about 1% a year from midlife, and respond to sleep, obesity and illness. The hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis regulates it via LH.

Medicine and misuse

Testosterone replacement treats documented deficiency; its extension into lifestyle 'low T' treatment for ageing men — a marketing-driven boom of the 2010s — has been tempered by cardiovascular and prostate safety trials (TRAVERSE, 2023, broadly reassuring on major cardiac events). As a drug of sport it anchors the anabolic-steroid family and anti-doping's history.
