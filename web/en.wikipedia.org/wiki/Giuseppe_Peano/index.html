Giuseppe Peano

Giuseppe Peano (27 August 1858 – 20 April 1932) was an Italian mathematician and glottologist who made foundational contributions to mathematical logic and set theory. He is best known for the Peano axioms, a formal axiomatization of the natural numbers that remains standard today, and for the existence of space-filling curves, the first example of which is called the Peano curve.

Life and career

Peano was born in Spinetta, near Cuneo in Piedmont, and studied at the University of Turin, where he graduated in 1880 and spent nearly his entire career, becoming professor of infinitesimal calculus in 1890. His clarity as a teacher was famous, although later in his career his insistence on teaching his own symbolic notation and his auxiliary language alienated some students and colleagues.

Work

In Arithmetices principia, nova methodo exposita (1889), written in Latin, Peano presented his axioms for arithmetic and introduced a logical symbolism that profoundly influenced Bertrand Russell and the development of modern logic. His monumental Formulario mathematico, published in several editions between 1894 and 1908, attempted to present all of mathematics in his symbolic language, with the final edition written in Latino sine flexione, the international auxiliary language Peano himself created in 1903.

Peano also discovered a continuous mapping from the unit interval onto the unit square (1890) — the first space-filling curve — provided a precise definition of a vector space, and gave an existence theorem for solutions of systems of ordinary differential equations (the Peano existence theorem). More than 200 books and papers bear his name.
