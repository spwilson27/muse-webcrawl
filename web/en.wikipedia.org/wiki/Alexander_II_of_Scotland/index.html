Alexander II (24 August 1198 – 6 July 1249) was King of Scots from 1214 until his death. The son of William the Lion and Ermengarde de Beaumont, he consolidated royal authority, settled the northern border with England, and sought to extend Scottish control over the Hebrides.

Early reign and England

Succeeding his father at sixteen, Alexander allied with the English barons rebelling against King John, invading northern England in support of their cause and in pursuit of Scottish claims to Northumbria. After John's death he made peace with Henry III, and in 1221 married Henry's sister Joan at York. The Treaty of York (1237) largely fixed the Anglo-Scottish border, with Alexander abandoning the old claims to the northern English counties.

Consolidation of the kingdom

Alexander suppressed revolts in Galloway, Moray, Caithness and Argyll, strengthening crown control over the kingdom's fringes. He founded or refounded religious houses and promoted burghs and royal justice.

The Hebrides and death

In 1249 Alexander sailed west to compel Ewen of Argyll to renounce his allegiance to Norway and to press Scottish claims over the Norwegian-held Hebrides. He fell ill and died on the island of Kerrera in the Inner Hebrides on 6 July 1249, and was buried at Melrose Abbey. His seven-year-old son succeeded as Alexander III.
