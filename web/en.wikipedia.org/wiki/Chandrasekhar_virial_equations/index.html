Chandrasekhar virial equations

The Chandrasekhar virial equations are a hierarchy of equations, developed by the astrophysicist Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar (initially in collaboration with Enrico Fermi, and later Norman Lebovitz), that describe the equilibrium and oscillations of self-gravitating fluid masses. They generalise the scalar virial theorem to tensor form and to successively higher orders, linking the moment of inertia tensor of a rotating or oscillating star to its kinetic, internal, magnetic, and gravitational potential energy tensors.

Background

The ordinary (scalar) virial theorem, due to Clausius, relates the time-averaged kinetic energy of a bound system to its potential energy; for a self-gravitating body in equilibrium it yields the condition 2T + W = 0 (augmented by pressure and other terms), from which, for example, the mass of a galaxy cluster can be estimated. Chandrasekhar's insight was to take successive moments of the equations of motion, producing an infinite hierarchy: the first-order equations express conservation of momentum; the second-order (tensor virial) equations govern the equilibrium figures and second-harmonic oscillations of rotating masses; and the third-order equations involve third moments of the density distribution and are needed to study certain classes of pulsation and the effects of rotation on stability.

Second-order (tensor virial) equation

The second-order virial equation, in the absence of magnetic fields and surface terms, can be written in the schematic form

½ d²Iij/dt² = 2𝒯ij + Π δij + Wij

where Iij is the moment of inertia tensor, 𝒯ij the kinetic energy tensor (including ordered rotation and internal motions), Π the volume integral of the pressure, and Wij the (Chandrasekhar) gravitational potential energy tensor. In equilibrium the left-hand side vanishes, giving exact integral constraints that any configuration must satisfy. Chandrasekhar and Fermi (1953) extended the theory to include magnetic fields, introducing the magnetic energy ℳ — the basis of the Chandrasekhar–Fermi method for estimating magnetic field strengths in interstellar clouds.

Applications

  • Figures of equilibrium: The tensor virial theorem determines the equilibrium shapes of homogeneous rotating masses (Maclaurin spheroids and Jacobi ellipsoids) and the points at which secular and dynamical instabilities set in, including the bifurcation to triaxial figures.
  • Stellar and galactic dynamics: Higher-order virial equations constrain the oscillations of rotating stars and the structure of ellipsoidal galaxies.
  • Stability theory: Chandrasekhar used the equations with a variational principle to analyse the pulsations and stability of rotating gaseous masses, work summarised in his monograph Ellipsoidal Figures of Equilibrium (1969).
