Digital Research

Industry: Software
Founded: 1974
Founder: Gary Kildall
Defunct: 1991 (acquired by Novell)
Headquarters: Pacific Grove / Monterey, California

Digital Research, Inc. (DR or DRI) was an American software company created by Gary Kildall to market and develop his CP/M operating system and related 8-bit, 16-bit and 32-bit systems software. It was the first large software company in the microcomputer world and, for a time, the largest dedicated microcomputer software company.

History

Kildall wrote CP/M in 1974 while consulting for Intel, and founded Intergalactic Digital Research — later shortened to Digital Research — with his wife Dorothy McEwen Kildall to license it. CP/M became the dominant operating system for Intel 8080 and Zilog Z80 based microcomputers, with variants including multi-user MP/M (1979) and CP/M-86 for the Intel 8086.

The IBM PC episode

In 1980 IBM approached Digital Research about licensing CP/M-86 for its forthcoming personal computer. The negotiations famously failed — accounts differ on why — and IBM instead turned to Microsoft, which supplied PC DOS (derived from 86-DOS). CP/M-86 was eventually offered on the IBM PC but priced far above PC DOS, and the DOS market consolidated around Microsoft. Digital Research responded with the multitasking Concurrent DOS and, later, DR DOS.

Later products and acquisition

DR DOS, first released in 1988, was a functionally compatible but feature-enhanced DOS clone that gained significant market share and provoked competitive responses from Microsoft. Digital Research also developed the GEM graphical environment. The company was acquired by Novell in 1991 for about $80 million; Novell later sold the DR DOS line to Caldera in 1996.
