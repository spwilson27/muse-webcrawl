The Arctic skua (Stercorarius parasiticus), also known as the parasitic jaeger in North America, is a seabird in the skua family Stercorariidae. It breeds in the far north of Eurasia and North America and winters at sea in the Southern Hemisphere.

Description

It is a medium-sized skua, 41–48 cm long, with pointed central tail feathers in adults. It occurs in pale, dark, and intermediate colour morphs. Its flight is falcon-like, and it is known for kleptoparasitism: chasing gulls, terns, and other seabirds to force them to drop their catch.

Breeding and migration

Arctic skuas breed on coastal moorland and tundra in Iceland, Scandinavia, Scotland (notably Shetland and Orkney), Russia, Alaska, and northern Canada. They lay two eggs in a ground scrape and defend the nest aggressively. In winter they migrate to southern oceans off South America, Africa, and Australasia.

Conservation

Declines linked to reduced availability of the seabirds it parasitises, and to sandeel shortages, have led to concern in parts of its range; in the UK it is red-listed.
