Larne

Larne (from Irish Latharna, a Gaelic territory) is a town on the east coast of County Antrim, Northern Ireland, about 25 miles (40 km) north of Belfast. It had a population of about 18,000 at the 2021 census. Larne has been used as a seaport for over 1,000 years and is the terminus of the shortest sea crossing between Ireland and Scotland.

History

The area around Larne Lough was settled early; the medieval territory of Latharna gave the town its name. In 1914 Larne was the landing place for arms for the Ulster Volunteer Force in the Larne gun-running, a major episode in the Home Rule crisis. During the Second World War the port served naval and troop movements.

Port and transport

The Port of Larne operates roll-on/roll-off ferry services for passengers and freight to Cairnryan in Scotland, one of the busiest such routes in Ireland. The town is connected to Belfast by the A8 road and the Belfast–Larne railway line, and marks the southern end of the Antrim Coast Road. Islandmagee lies across Larne Lough.
