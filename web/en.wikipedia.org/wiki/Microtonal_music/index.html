Microtonal music

Microtonal music or microtonality is the use in music of microtones — intervals smaller than a semitone, which is the smallest interval in conventional Western twelve-tone equal temperament. Microtones may be produced by retuning instruments, building special instruments, or using the voice and unfretted strings; many musical traditions outside Western art music, such as Indian classical music, Arabic maqam and Turkish makam, routinely use intervals other than the twelve semitones.

Tuning systems

Microtonal systems include equal temperaments dividing the octave into 19, 24 (quarter tones), 31 or more steps, as well as just intonation networks of pure intervals. Quarter-tone scales (24 equal divisions) were explored by composers such as Alois Hába and Ivan Wyschnegradsky. Mexican composer Julián Carrillo developed his Sonido 13 system with divisions as fine as sixteenth-tones.

Composers

Notable microtonal composers include Harry Partch, who built a famous orchestra of custom instruments tuned to a 43-tone just-intonation scale; Ben Johnston; Lou Harrison; and La Monte Young. Specialised notation systems using additional accidentals were developed to write microtonal music, and modern software and electronic instruments have made microtonal experimentation far more accessible.
