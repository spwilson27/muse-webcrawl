Battle of Alcácer Quibir

The Battle of Alcácer Quibir (also known as the Battle of the Three Kings), fought on 4 August 1578 in northern Morocco, was a crushing defeat for a Portuguese crusading army. King Sebastian of Portugal, the deposed Moroccan sultan Abu Abdallah Mohammed II whom he supported, and the reigning Saadian sultan Abd al-Malik all died in or after the battle — hence its name.

Background and battle

The young, zealously crusading Sebastian landed in Morocco to restore Abu Abdallah against his uncle Abd al-Malik, an Ottoman-backed ruler. Ignoring counsel, Sebastian marched inland in summer heat to Alcácer Quibir (Ksar el-Kebir). Abd al-Malik, though terminally ill, concealed his condition and deployed a larger army with artillery and arquebusiers in a crescent that enveloped the Portuguese. Abu Abdallah drowned fleeing, Abd al-Malik died (possibly poisoned or of illness) during the fight, and Sebastian was killed; much of the Portuguese nobility died or was captured for ransom.

Consequences

With Sebastian childless, the Portuguese succession passed briefly to Cardinal Henry, then to Philip II of Spain in 1580, beginning the Iberian Union until 1640. Sebastian's missing body bred the myth of Sebastianism — the king's prophesied return. Victorious Ahmad al-Mansur became Morocco's great Saadian sultan. See also history of Portugal.
