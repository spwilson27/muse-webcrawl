Haru no Umi

Haru no Umi (Japanese: 春の海, "The Sea in Spring") is a piece of Japanese music composed in 1929 by the koto player and composer Michio Miyagi (1894–1956). Originally written as a duet for koto and shakuhachi, it is one of the most famous works of modern Japanese traditional music and is frequently heard in Japan during the New Year season.

Background

Miyagi, blind since childhood, was inspired by memories of the sea at Tomonoura on the Seto Inland Sea, which he had seen as a boy before losing his sight. The music evokes waves, seagulls, and the work of fishermen in a series of flowing passages: a calm opening suggests the sea at rest, followed by livelier sections depicting boats and birds.

Arrangements and legacy

Although composed for koto and shakuhachi, Haru no Umi has been arranged for many combinations of instruments, most famously for violin and piano after Miyagi performed it with the French violinist Renée Chemet, with whom he recorded the piece in 1932. It remains a staple of the sankyoku and modern Japanese music repertoire and is widely studied by koto students as an essential standard.
