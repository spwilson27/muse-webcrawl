State Council (Russian Empire)

The State Council (Russian: Государственный совет, Gosudarstvennyy sovet) was the supreme state advisory body to the emperor of Russia from 1810 to 1906, and the upper house of the Russian legislature from 1906 until the fall of the monarchy in 1917.

Advisory period, 1810–1906

The Council was created by Alexander I in 1810 as part of Mikhail Speransky's reform program, replacing the earlier Permanent Council. Its members were appointed by the emperor from among senior officials, ministers, and grandees. The Council examined draft legislation, budgets, and administrative questions before the emperor's final decision; its opinions were advisory, and the emperor could and did overrule majority views, but its deliberations gave the government a measure of procedural regularity. It met in the Mariinsky Palace in Saint Petersburg, and Ilya Repin's enormous canvas of its ceremonial centenary session (1901–1903) is among the most famous paintings of imperial Russia.

Upper house, 1906–1917

Under the constitutional settlement following the Revolution of 1905 and the Fundamental Laws of 1906, the State Council was reconstituted as an upper legislative chamber alongside the elected State Duma. Half its members were appointed by the emperor and half elected by institutions including the zemstvos, the nobility, the clergy, universities, and chambers of commerce. Legislation required the approval of both houses and the emperor. The Council generally acted as a conservative brake on the Duma. It ceased to function with the February Revolution of 1917 and was formally abolished later that year.
