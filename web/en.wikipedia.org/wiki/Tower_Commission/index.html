Tower Commission

President's Special Review Board
FormedDecember 1, 1986
ChairmanJohn Tower
MembersEdmund Muskie, Brent Scowcroft

The Tower Commission, formally the President's Special Review Board, was a commission appointed by U.S. President Ronald Reagan on December 1, 1986, to investigate the Iran–Contra affair, in which senior administration officials had secretly facilitated the sale of arms to Iran and diverted proceeds to the Nicaraguan Contras. The commission was chaired by former Senator John Tower of Texas, and its other members were former Secretary of State Edmund Muskie and former National Security Advisor Brent Scowcroft.

Investigation

Unlike the congressional committees that investigated the scandal in parallel, the Tower Commission had no subpoena power and relied on voluntary cooperation and access to White House documents. Significantly, its investigators recovered deleted computer records from the National Security Council's PROFS email system, which provided a detailed paper trail of the operation — including the role of NSC staffer Oliver North — that participants had believed destroyed.

The Tower Report

The commission issued its report on February 26, 1987. It was sharply critical of the Reagan administration's management style, concluding that the President's detached approach to foreign policy had allowed the National Security Council staff to run a covert operation without adequate oversight. The report faulted Chief of Staff Donald Regan and others, described the arms-for-hostages dealing as fundamentally flawed, and recommended reforms to the NSC system, including that the National Security Advisor should not engage in operational activities. Reagan accepted the report's conclusions in a televised address on March 4, 1987, acknowledging that the Iran initiative had deteriorated into an arms-for-hostages trade.

Legacy

The Tower Commission is often cited as a model of the independent presidential review board: quick, bipartisan and substantive. Its findings shaped subsequent debate about covert action oversight and the proper role of the National Security Council.
