Lost city

A lost city is an urban settlement that fell into terminal decline and became extensively or completely uninhabited, with its former location forgotten or mythologised before rediscovery by archaeologists.

Lost city
Examples: Petra, Machu Picchu, Pompeii, Troy, Angkor
Causes: War, disaster, climate, economic collapse

Causes and categories

Cities were lost through catastrophe (Pompeii), conquest, shifting trade routes, disease or environmental change. Some, like Troy and El Dorado, straddle archaeology and legend; El Dorado and Atlantis remain unlocated or fictional.

Famous rediscoveries

  • Petra, Jordan — reintroduced to the West by Burckhardt, 1812
  • Machu Picchu, Peru — brought to international attention by Hiram Bingham, 1911
  • Angkor, Cambodia, and Maya cities found by lidar surveys

In fiction

Lost cities are a staple of adventure fiction, from H. Rider Haggard to Indiana Jones.
