Functional gastrointestinal disorder

Functional gastrointestinal disorders (FGIDs), now often called disorders of gut–brain interaction, are a group of disorders in which chronic gastrointestinal symptoms occur without structural or biochemical abnormalities that explain them. They include irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), functional dyspepsia, functional constipation, and functional bloating, and they are among the most common reasons for gastroenterology consultations.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on symptom-based Rome criteria (currently Rome IV, published 2016), which classify more than thirty adult and paediatric disorders by the affected region of the gut, together with limited testing to exclude alarm features and organic disease such as coeliac disease or inflammatory bowel disease.

Mechanisms and treatment

Mechanisms are multifactorial: visceral hypersensitivity, altered motility, changes in the gut microbiota and mucosal immune function, and dysregulation of the gut–brain axis, with symptoms often worsened by stress. Treatment is tailored to the dominant symptom and includes dietary modification (such as a low-FODMAP diet), antispasmodics, laxatives or antidiarrhoeals, neuromodulators, and gut-directed psychological therapies including hypnotherapy and cognitive behavioural therapy.
