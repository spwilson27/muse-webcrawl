The Montreal Metro (French: Métro de Montréal) is a rubber-tyred underground rapid transit system serving Greater Montreal, Quebec, Canada. Operated by the Société de transport de Montréal, it opened in 1966 for Expo 67 and is Canada's busiest metro system by daily ridership, and the third busiest in North America after New York City and Mexico City.

Network

The network has four lines identified by colour and number: Line 1 (Green), Line 2 (Orange), Line 4 (Yellow), and Line 5 (Blue) — there is no Line 3, a designation reserved for a planned surface line that was never built. The system has 68 stations over 69.2 km of track, entirely underground. The Orange Line was extended to Laval in 2007, the system's first extension beyond the island of Montreal.

Technology and architecture

Modelled on the Paris Métro, Montreal's trains run on rubber tyres over concrete rollways, chosen for quiet operation and the ability to climb steep grades. Stations were each designed by different architects and are famous for their variety and integrated public art — the network is often described as an underground art gallery. The original MR-73 cars are being supplemented and progressively replaced by the newer Azur (MPM-10) trains.

Because the system is entirely underground, it connects directly with Montreal's "underground city" (RÉSO), a network of tunnels linking downtown stations, offices, and shopping centres.

History

Proposals for a Montreal subway date to 1910, but construction only began in 1962 under mayor Jean Drapeau, initially for the 1967 World Exhibition (Expo 67). The Yellow Line was built specifically to serve the Expo site on Île Sainte-Hélène. The Blue Line opened in stages in the 1980s; further extensions, including a Blue Line extension eastward, have been under construction.
