Al-Ula

Al-Ula (Arabic: العلا), also spelled AlUla, is a city and oasis in the Medina Region of north-western Saudi Arabia, and the capital of Al-Ula Governorate. Situated on the ancient Incense Road in the Hejaz, it is best known for the nearby archaeological site of Hegra (Mada'in Salih), the first Saudi site inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

The oasis, ancient Dedan, was the capital of the Lihyanite kingdom in the first millennium BCE and later fell under Nabataean influence; the Nabataeans built Hegra, 20 km to the north, as their second city after Petra, carving monumental tombs into its sandstone outcrops. Al-Ula itself prospered as a stop on the incense and pilgrimage routes, and a station of the Ottoman-era Hejaz Railway, whose station and workshops survive. The walled old town, occupied until the late 20th century, is a dense labyrinth of mud-brick houses.

Development and tourism

Since 2017 the Royal Commission for AlUla has developed the region as Saudi Arabia's flagship heritage-tourism destination under Vision 2030, opening Hegra, the Dedan and Jabal Ikmah inscription sites, the Elephant Rock formation and the Maraya concert hall — the world's largest mirrored building — to visitors, and hosting the Winter at Tantora festival.

Geography

The oasis lies in a valley between sandstone massifs about 300 km north of Medina; date palms and citrus groves are fed by groundwater, and summer temperatures are extreme.
