The Genpei War (1180–1185) was a national civil war between the Taira and Minamoto clans during the late Heian period of Japan. It resulted in the downfall of the Taira and the establishment of the Kamakura shogunate under Minamoto no Yoritomo, Japan's first shogunate, in 1192. The name 'Genpei' is the Sino-Japanese reading of the characters for Minamoto (Genji) and Taira (Heike).

Background

The Taira under Taira no Kiyomori had dominated the imperial court since victories in the Hōgen (1156) and Heiji (1160) rebellions. In 1180 Kiyomori placed his infant grandson Antoku on the throne, prompting Prince Mochihito and Minamoto no Yorimasa to issue a call to arms; their defeat at the first Battle of Uji opened the war.

Course

Minamoto no Yoritomo raised an army in the Kantō, while his cousin Yoshinaka seized Kyoto in 1183, driving the Taira west with the child emperor. Yoritomo's half-brother Minamoto no Yoshitsune then won a string of brilliant victories — Ichi-no-Tani (1184), Yashima, and finally the naval Battle of Dan-no-ura (1185), where the Taira were annihilated and Emperor Antoku drowned.

Legacy

The war marks the rise of the samurai class to political power and the beginning of nearly seven centuries of military government in Japan. Its tragedies are retold in the epic The Tale of the Heike, a cornerstone of Japanese literature, and in countless plays, prints, and films.
