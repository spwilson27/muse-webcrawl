Wadi El Hitan

Wadi El Hitan (Valley of the Whales)
Location: Faiyum Governorate, Egypt
Designated: UNESCO World Heritage Site, 2005
Age of fossils: ~37–40 million years (Eocene)

Wadi El Hitan (Arabic: وادي الحيتان, "Valley of the Whales") is a paleontological site in the Faiyum Governorate of Egypt, some 150 km southwest of Cairo. It contains hundreds of fossils of early whales (archaeocetes) that document the transition of whales from land mammals to ocean-going animals.

Fossils

The site preserves skeletons of Basilosaurus and the smaller Dorudon, including specimens with vestigial hind limbs, feet and toes — key evidence of whale evolution — as well as fossils of sea cows, sharks, turtles and ancient mangrove environments from when the area lay under the Tethys Sea.

Protection

Wadi El Hitan was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2005 and is managed as part of the Wadi El Rayan protected area, with an open-air museum and visitor facilities. It is regarded as one of the most important sites in the world for demonstrating the evolution of cetaceans.
