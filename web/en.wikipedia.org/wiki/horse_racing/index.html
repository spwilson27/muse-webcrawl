Horse racing

Horse racing is an equestrian performance sport, typically involving two or more horses ridden by jockeys (or driven without riders in harness racing) over a set distance for competition. It is one of the most ancient of all sports, and its basic premise — to identify which of two or more horses is the fastest over a set course — has remained largely unchanged.

Types

The main forms are flat racing on turf or dirt, jump racing over hurdles and steeplechase fences, harness racing with sulkies, and endurance racing over long distances. Thoroughbreds dominate flat racing, while Standardbreds are used in harness racing.

Major races

Prestigious events include the Kentucky Derby, the Epsom Derby, the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe, the Melbourne Cup, and the Dubai World Cup. Breeding, training, and wagering form a global industry worth hundreds of billions of dollars.

Betting

Gambling, typically through parimutuel pools, has been intertwined with horse racing since its earliest days and remains a principal source of its revenue in many jurisdictions.
