Paraganglioma

A paraganglioma is a rare neuroendocrine tumour that arises from paraganglia — clusters of chromaffin and related cells dispersed along the autonomic nervous system. The same kind of tumour arising in the adrenal medulla is called a phaeochromocytoma; paragangliomas are the extra-adrenal equivalents. They occur most often in the abdomen, chest, and head and neck, where typical forms include carotid body tumours, glomus jugulare tumours and vagal paragangliomas.

Features

Some paragangliomas secrete catecholamines (noradrenaline and related hormones) and cause episodic headache, palpitations, sweating and dangerously high blood pressure; head and neck tumours are usually non-secretory and present as a slowly growing painless mass, sometimes with tinnitus or cranial nerve deficits. Most are benign, but a minority metastasise, and malignancy can only be judged by spread rather than by appearance under the microscope.

Genetics and treatment

A substantial fraction — around a third or more — are hereditary, most often due to mutations in the succinate dehydrogenase genes (SDHA, SDHB, SDHC, SDHD and SDHAF2), but also in VHL, RET and other genes; genetic testing is recommended for most patients. Diagnosis combines biochemical testing (plasma or urine metanephrines), cross-sectional imaging and functional scans such as DOTATATE PET. Surgical resection is the main treatment for localised tumours, with careful pre-operative alpha-blockade for secreting tumours; radiotherapy is used for inoperable or residual disease.
