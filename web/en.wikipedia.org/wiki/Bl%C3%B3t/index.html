Blót

Blót (Old Norse: blót) is the term for a sacrificial blood rite in Norse paganism and Germanic paganism more generally. The blót was dedicated to the gods, the elves, or the ancestors, and usually involved the slaughter of animals whose blood was sprinkled on altars, idols, and participants, followed by a communal sacrificial feast in which the meat was eaten.

Practice

The blood, called hlaut, was collected in bowls and sprinkled with twigs likened to aspergilla. Major seasonal blóts were held at winter nights (autumn), midwinter (Yule), and the beginning of summer (Sigrblót), as described in Snorri Sturluson's Ynglinga saga. Temples and sacred enclosures hosting blóts are described in sources such as Adam of Bremen's account of the Temple at Uppsala.

Modern practice

Modern Heathenry (Germanic neopaganism) revives the blót as a ritual of offering, typically substituting mead or other drink for animal blood, which is poured out or sprinkled while toasts are offered to the gods and ancestors.
