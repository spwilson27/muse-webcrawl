Leipzig is the largest city in the German state of Saxony and, with more than 600,000 inhabitants, one of the fastest-growing cities in Germany. Situated at the confluence of the White Elster, Pleiße and Parthe rivers, it has been a centre of trade, printing and music for centuries.

History

Leipzig was first mentioned in 1015 in the chronicle of Thietmar of Merseburg as urbs Libzi. It received city and market privileges in 1165, and its location at the crossing of the Via Regia and Via Imperii trade routes made it a major commercial hub. Leipzig University, founded in 1409, is one of Europe's oldest universities. The city hosted one of the first daily newspapers (1660) and became Germany's printing and publishing capital.

In October 1813 Leipzig was the site of the Battle of the Nations, the largest battle in Europe before the First World War, in which Napoleon's forces were decisively defeated; the enormous Monument to the Battle of the Nations (Völkerschlachtdenkmal), completed in 1913, commemorates it. In 1989 the Monday demonstrations in Leipzig, centred on the Nikolaikirche, played a pivotal role in the peaceful revolution that brought down the East German regime.

Culture

Johann Sebastian Bach worked in Leipzig from 1723 until his death in 1750 as Thomaskantor, directing the St. Thomas Choir (Thomanerchor). Felix Mendelssohn founded the Leipzig Conservatory in 1843 and conducted the Gewandhaus Orchestra, one of the world's oldest civic orchestras. The city was also home to composers Robert Schumann and Richard Wagner (born here in 1813).

Economy

Modern Leipzig is a logistics hub — DHL's European air freight hub is at Leipzig/Halle Airport — and hosts plants of BMW and Porsche. The Leipzig Trade Fair (Leipziger Messe) is among the oldest trade fairs in the world.
