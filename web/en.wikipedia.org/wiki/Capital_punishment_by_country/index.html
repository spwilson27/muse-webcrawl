Capital punishment by country

Capital punishment by country describes the worldwide variation in the use of the death penalty. A majority of the world's countries have abolished capital punishment in law or practice, while a minority of states — including China, Iran, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, and the United States — continue to carry out executions.

Capital punishment worldwide
Abolitionist for all crimes: over 110 countries
Retentionist: roughly 55 countries
Trend: continued global decline in executions

Overview

Amnesty International classifies countries as abolitionist for all crimes, abolitionist for ordinary crimes, abolitionist in practice, or retentionist. Europe is almost entirely abolitionist, with the Council of Europe requiring abolition as a condition of membership. In the United States, the death penalty is a matter of state law, and roughly half the states have abolished it.

Methods

Methods of execution still in use include hanging, shooting, lethal injection, beheading, and electrocution. China is believed to execute more people than the rest of the world combined, though figures are a state secret.
