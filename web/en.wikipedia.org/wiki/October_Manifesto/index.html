The October Manifesto, officially the Manifesto on the Improvement of the State Order, was a document issued by Emperor Nicholas II of Russia on 17 October 1905 (Old Style), drafted with the advice of Sergei Witte, in response to the Russian Revolution of 1905. It promised civil liberties and an elected legislative assembly, the State Duma, without whose approval no law would take effect — the first formal limitation on the tsarist autocracy.

Background

Defeat in the Russo-Japanese War, Bloody Sunday in January 1905, and months of strikes, mutinies and peasant unrest culminated in a general strike in October 1905 that paralysed railways and industry. Witte presented Nicholas with a choice between military dictatorship and political reform; the emperor chose the manifesto.

Contents

The manifesto pledged:

  • "The fundamental principles of civil liberty" — inviolability of the person, and freedoms of conscience, speech, assembly and association;
  • Extension of the franchise for elections to the State Duma toward universal male suffrage;
  • That no law should take effect without the Duma's approval, and that elected representatives would supervise the legality of the administration.

Reaction and consequences

The manifesto split the opposition: liberals of the Constitutional Democratic (Kadet) and Octobrist parties accepted it and prepared to contest elections, while the Social Democrats and Socialist Revolutionaries largely repudiated it as insufficient. Street violence followed, including pogroms by right-wing "Black Hundreds" against Jews and radicals. The government arrested the St. Petersburg Soviet in December, and Witte became the first chairman of the Council of Ministers — effectively Russia's first prime minister.

Limits of the settlement

The Fundamental Laws of April 1906, issued before the First Duma met, reasserted the emperor's "supreme autocratic power", reserved control of ministers, foreign policy and the armed forces to the crown, and gave Nicholas the power to dissolve the Duma — which he used twice. The electoral coup of June 1907 sharply narrowed the franchise. Historians debate how far Russia might have evolved toward constitutional monarchy had war not intervened in 1914.
