The H3 is a Japanese expendable launch system developed by the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI) to succeed the H-IIA/H-IIB rockets as Japan's flagship launcher. It is designed to offer flexible configurations at substantially lower cost — with a target launch price of about ¥5 billion for the basic variant, roughly half that of the H-IIA.

Design

The H3 uses two or three LE-9 engines on its first stage. The LE-9 is the first large rocket engine to use the expander bleed cycle, chosen for simplicity and reliability. The second stage uses an improved LE-5B-3 engine. Configurations vary by the number of SRB-3 solid rocket boosters (zero, two, or four) and fairing length, denoted in designations such as H3-303 and H3-24L. The rocket stands up to 63 metres tall with the long fairing.

Launch history

Development began in 2013–2014. The maiden flight on 7 March 2023, carrying the ALOS-3 (DAICHI-3) Earth observation satellite, failed when the second-stage engine did not ignite, and the vehicle was destroyed by command. After an investigation and corrective measures, the second flight on 17 February 2024 succeeded, reaching orbit. The third flight in July 2024 successfully carried the ALOS-4 satellite. H3 is intended to launch Japanese government payloads, commercial satellites, and cargo missions to the International Space Station aboard the HTV-X spacecraft.
