Woomera Prohibited Area

The Woomera Prohibited Area (WPA) is a military testing range in the Far North region of South Australia, northwest of Woomera. Covering about 122,188 square kilometres, it is the world's largest land-based weapons test range — roughly the size of England. It is administered by the Royal Australian Air Force as part of the Woomera Range Complex.

History

The range was established in 1947 under the Anglo-Australian Joint Project for testing long-range missiles and rockets. It supported Britain's rocket programmes, the Europa launcher of the European Launcher Development Organisation, and in 1967 the launch of Australia's first satellite, WRESAT. Between 1955 and 1963, Britain conducted a series of nuclear weapons tests at Maralinga and Emu Field on and near the range, whose contamination has been the subject of long remediation and compensation efforts.

Current use

Today the range hosts trials of missiles, drones, aircraft and electronic warfare systems for Australia and allied nations. Much of the area overlaps mineral-rich land and Aboriginal heritage sites; since 2014, a coexistence regime permits mining exploration and some public access under permit, with exclusion periods during tests.
