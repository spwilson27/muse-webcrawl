Wrestling is a martial art and combat sport that involves grappling techniques such as clinch fighting, throws and takedowns, joint locks, pins, and other holds. It is among the oldest recorded sports in the world, with contests appearing in ancient Egyptian and Greek art and in the earliest Olympic Games.

History

Depictions of wrestlers appear in French cave paintings some 15,000 years old, in Babylonian and Egyptian reliefs, and in the epics of India and Greece. Wrestling was a centerpiece of the ancient Olympic Games from 708 BC and of the pentathlon. Styles developed regionally across the world — from Mongolian bökh and Turkish oil wrestling to Indian pehlwani and Japanese sumo.

International styles

Two styles are contested at the modern Olympic Games under United World Wrestling. In Greco-Roman wrestling, holds below the waist are forbidden and the legs may not be used to trip or lift. In freestyle wrestling, wrestlers may attack the opponent's legs and use their own legs for throws and trips. Bouts are won by pinning the opponent's shoulders to the mat (a fall) or by outscoring them with takedowns, exposures, and reversals across two three-minute periods.

Other forms

American collegiate (folkstyle) wrestling emphasizes control and riding time; beach wrestling is a simplified outdoor variant. The term also encompasses professional wrestling, a form of sports entertainment with predetermined outcomes that developed from 19th-century carnival strongman acts and catch wrestling exhibitions.
