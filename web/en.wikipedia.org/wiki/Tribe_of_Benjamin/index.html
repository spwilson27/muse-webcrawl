Tribe of Benjamin

According to the Torah, the Tribe of Benjamin was one of the Twelve Tribes of Israel, descended from Benjamin, the youngest son of the patriarch Jacob and the second (and last) son of Rachel. The tribe's territory, allotted in the Book of Joshua, lay just north of Judah, in a small but strategically important strip of land around Jerusalem's northern approaches, including Jericho, Bethel and Gibeon.

Biblical narrative

The tribe nearly perished in the civil war described in Judges 19–21, when the other tribes united against Benjamin after the outrage at Gibeah; the Book of Judges reports that only 600 Benjamite men survived, and wives had to be found for them from Jabesh-Gilead and Shiloh to preserve the tribe.

Benjamin nevertheless recovered to play a leading role: Israel's first king, Saul, was a Benjamite, and Jerusalem lay at the tribe's border with Judah. After the division of the monarchy, Benjamin remained loyal to the southern Kingdom of Judah, unlike the ten tribes that formed the northern Kingdom of Israel. Later members of the tribe in the Bible include Mordecai in the Book of Esther, and the apostle Paul described himself as "of the tribe of Benjamin" (Romans 11:1, Philippians 3:5).

Character

Jacob's blessing in Genesis 49 calls Benjamin "a ravenous wolf," and the tribe had a reputation for skilled warriors, including celebrated left-handed slingers and archers (Judges 20:16).
