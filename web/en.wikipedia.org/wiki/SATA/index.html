SATA

Full nameSerial Advanced Technology Attachment
Introduced2003
PredecessorPATA (IDE)
Speeds1.5, 3.0 and 6.0 Gbit/s

SATA (Serial ATA, Serial Advanced Technology Attachment) is a computer bus interface that connects host adapters to mass storage devices such as hard disk drives, solid-state drives and optical drives. Standardised from 2003, it replaced the older parallel ATA (PATA/IDE) interface.

Features

SATA uses serial signalling over thin seven-conductor cables up to one metre long, with point-to-point links rather than the shared bus of PATA. It supports hot swapping, Native Command Queuing, and link speeds of 1.5 Gbit/s (SATA I), 3 Gbit/s (SATA II) and 6 Gbit/s (SATA III). The related eSATA variant serves external drives, and the compact mSATA and M.2 form factors carry SATA signals in small devices.

Adoption and successors

SATA became the dominant storage interface for desktops and laptops in the 2000s and 2010s. For high-performance SSDs it has increasingly been displaced by NVMe over PCI Express, though SATA drives remain common for bulk storage.
