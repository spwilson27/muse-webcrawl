Baby Boomers

Baby boomers, often shortened to boomers, are the demographic cohort following the Silent Generation and preceding Generation X. In the United States the generation is generally defined as people born from 1946 to 1964, during the mid-20th-century baby boom that followed the Second World War. The term is used in a similar sense in other Western countries that experienced large post-war birth surges, though the exact dates vary by country.

Characteristics

The boom began as soldiers returned home in 1946 and birth rates remained elevated until 1964, when contraception, delayed marriage and rising female employment brought them down. Boomers grew up amid suburbanisation, television, rock and roll and unprecedented economic growth; as young adults they were associated with the counterculture, anti-war protest and the civil-rights and feminist movements. Demographers sometimes split the cohort into "leading-edge" boomers (1946–1955) and "trailing-edge" or "Generation Jones" boomers (1956–1964), whose formative experiences differed significantly.

Economic significance

Because of their size, boomers have exerted outsized influence on markets and politics at every life stage, from the expansion of schools in the 1950s to housing demand in the 1980s. Their mass retirement in the 2010s–2030s places heavy demands on Social Security, Medicare and pension systems, and they hold a majority share of U.S. household wealth.
