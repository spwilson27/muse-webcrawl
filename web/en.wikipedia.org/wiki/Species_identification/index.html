Species identification

Species identification is the process of assigning an observed organism to a known species. It is a fundamental task in biology, ecology, agriculture, medicine and conservation, since knowing which species an organism belongs to connects the observation to the accumulated knowledge about that species.

Methods

Traditional identification relies on morphological characters — the size, shape, colour and structure of the organism — compared against published descriptions and dichotomous identification keys, in which a series of paired choices leads progressively to a name. Field guides and taxonomic monographs support this process, and voucher specimens may be deposited in museums or herbaria for verification.

Molecular methods

DNA barcoding uses a short standardized gene region, such as the COI gene in animals or rbcL and matK in plants, to match a specimen against reference libraries of sequences. Environmental DNA (eDNA) techniques extend this approach to samples of water or soil, allowing species to be detected without direct observation.

Automated identification

Computer vision systems and citizen science platforms use images and sounds to suggest identifications, and acoustic monitoring can identify birds, bats and frogs by their calls. Automated tools assist, but difficult groups still require expert examination because of cryptic species and incomplete reference data.
