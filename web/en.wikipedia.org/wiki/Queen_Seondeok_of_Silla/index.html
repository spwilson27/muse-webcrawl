Queen Seondeok of Silla (Korean: 선덕여왕; c. 595/610 – 17 February 647) reigned as Queen Regent of Silla, one of the Three Kingdoms of Korea, from 632 to 647. She was Silla's twenty-seventh ruler and its first reigning queen, ascending the throne because her father, King Jinpyeong, had no sons; her selection was possible under Silla's bone-rank system, in which she held the highest "sacred bone" rank.

Reign

Seondeok's reign was dominated by war and diplomacy among the three kingdoms: Silla, pressed by Baekje in the west and eyeing Goguryeo in the north, deepened its alliance with Tang China. She relied on able officials and generals, most famously Kim Yu-sin, and promoted Buddhism as a unifying state ideology. Major building works associated with her reign include the nine-storey wooden pagoda of Hwangnyongsa (built 645, later destroyed by the Mongols), the temple Bunhwangsa, and the Cheomseongdae astronomical observatory in the capital, Gyeongju — among the oldest surviving observatories in East Asia.

Legends

The Samguk yusa preserves famous anecdotes of her prescience: predicting an attack from the scent of peonies sent without bees or butterflies (hence, she reasoned, from a hostile source), and foreseeing the day of her own death. In 647, amid the rebellion of her relative Bidam — the largest revolt of her reign — she died before its suppression was complete; she was succeeded by her cousin, Queen Jindeok.

Legacy

Seondeok is remembered as a wise and benevolent ruler who laid foundations for Silla's later unification of the peninsula in 668. She has been the subject of popular Korean historical dramas, notably the 2009 television series Queen Seondeok, which was a major ratings success across Asia.
