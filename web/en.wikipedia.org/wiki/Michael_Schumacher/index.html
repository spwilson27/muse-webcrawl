Michael Schumacher

Born: 3 January 1969, Hürth, West Germany

Championships: 7 (1994, 1995, 2000, 2001, 2002, 2003, 2004)

Career wins: 91

Teams: Jordan, Benetton, Ferrari, Mercedes

Michael Schumacher (born 3 January 1969) is a German former racing driver, who competed in Formula One from 1991 to 2006 and from 2010 to 2012. He won seven World Drivers' Championship titles — tied with Lewis Hamilton for the most in history — including a record five consecutive titles with Ferrari from 2000 to 2004, and holds or has held records for wins (91), pole positions, and fastest laps.

Career

Schumacher began karting at his family's track in Kerpen and progressed through German Formula Three, winning the title in 1990. He made his Formula One debut with Jordan at the 1991 Belgian Grand Prix and was immediately signed by Benetton, with whom he won the 1994 and 1995 World Championships — the first, in 1994, amid controversy over a title-deciding collision with Damon Hill in Adelaide, and with Benetton under suspicion of illegal driver aids.

In 1996 he joined a struggling Ferrari and, with technical director Ross Brawn, designer Rory Byrne, and team principal Jean Todt, rebuilt the team into a dominant force. After title fights in 1997 (where his collision with Jacques Villeneuve at Jerez led to his disqualification from the championship standings) and 1998–99, Schumacher won five consecutive championships from 2000 to 2004, including the utterly dominant 2002 and 2004 seasons. He retired at the end of 2006 with a then-record 91 victories.

Comeback and accident

Schumacher returned to Formula One with Mercedes from 2010 to 2012, achieving one podium before retiring again. In December 2013, he suffered a severe head injury in a skiing accident in Méribel, France, and was placed in a medically induced coma. He was brought home to continue rehabilitation in September 2014; his family has kept his condition private. His son Mick Schumacher raced in Formula One for Haas in 2021–2022, and his brother Ralf won six Grands Prix.

Legacy

Schumacher is widely regarded as one of the greatest drivers of all time, credited with raising standards of fitness, preparation, and technical feedback in Formula One, and his era transformed the sport's popularity in Germany. He was also a major philanthropist, donating tens of millions of dollars to causes including UNESCO education projects and disaster relief.
