Chase Bank, formally JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A., is the American consumer and commercial banking subsidiary of the multinational financial services firm JPMorgan Chase. It is one of the largest banks in the United States by deposits and operates roughly 4,700 branches and about 15,000 automated teller machines nationwide.

History

The Chase name descends from the Chase National Bank, founded in 1877 and named after Salmon P. Chase, the former Secretary of the Treasury and Chief Justice of the United States — although Chase himself had no connection with the bank. Chase National merged with the Bank of the Manhattan Company in 1955 to form the Chase Manhattan Bank. Through a series of mergers — with Chemical Bank in 1996, with the original J.P. Morgan & Co. in 2000, with Bank One in 2004, and the acquisition of most of Washington Mutual's banking operations during the 2008 financial crisis — the institution became the retail banking arm of today's JPMorgan Chase.

Operations

Chase provides personal banking (checking and savings accounts), credit cards (including the Sapphire and Freedom product lines), home lending, auto finance, and small and mid-sized business banking. It is one of the largest credit card issuers in the United States and a major mortgage originator and servicer.
