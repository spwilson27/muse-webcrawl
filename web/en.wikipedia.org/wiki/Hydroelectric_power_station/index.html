Hydroelectric power station

A hydroelectric power station is a facility that generates electricity by using the gravitational force of falling or flowing water to turn turbines coupled to generators. It is the most widely used form of renewable energy generation worldwide.

Types

Stations are classed as conventional (dam-based, storing water in a reservoir), run-of-the-river (using a river's natural flow with little storage), pumped-storage (water is pumped uphill off-peak and released at peak demand, acting as a giant battery), and tidal or diversion schemes.

Operation

Water under pressure is delivered through penstocks to turbines — commonly Francis, Kaplan, or Pelton types depending on head and flow — whose rotation drives generators. Output depends on head (vertical drop) and volumetric flow. The world's largest station by installed capacity is the Three Gorges Dam in China.
