5G is the fifth generation of cellular network technology, succeeding 4G/LTE. Standardised by the 3GPP as 5G NR (New Radio) and defined by the ITU's IMT-2020 requirements, it offers higher data rates, lower latency and the capacity to connect far more devices per unit area than earlier generations. Commercial networks launched from 2019.

Technology

5G operates across three broad spectrum ranges: low-band (below 1 GHz) for coverage, mid-band (roughly 1–6 GHz) balancing speed and range, and high-band millimetre wave (24 GHz and above) for very high speeds over short distances. Key techniques include massive MIMO antenna arrays, beamforming, carrier aggregation and small cells. Standalone (SA) networks add network slicing and ultra-reliable low-latency communication modes aimed at industrial and Internet-of-Things applications.

Deployment and debate

Rollout proceeded rapidly in China, South Korea, the United States and Europe, though mid-band spectrum availability varied widely by country. 5G also became entangled in geopolitics, notably restrictions on Huawei equipment, and attracted unfounded health conspiracy theories which scientific reviews have not substantiated.
