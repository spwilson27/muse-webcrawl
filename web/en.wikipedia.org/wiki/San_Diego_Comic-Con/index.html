San Diego Comic-Con

San Diego Comic-Con (SDCC), officially Comic-Con International: San Diego, is an annual entertainment and comic book convention held in San Diego, California, United States. Founded in 1970 by a group of comics fans including Shel Dorf, it has grown into one of the largest popular-culture conventions in the world, drawing well over 130,000 attendees in recent years.

History

The first San Diego comics convention, a one-day "minicon" in March 1970, led to a three-day event that August attended by about 300 people, with guests including Forrest J Ackerman and Mike Royer. Through the 1970s and 1980s the convention grew as comics professionals and science-fiction authors made it a fixture of fandom. It moved to the San Diego Convention Center in 1991.

From the 2000s onward, major film and television studios began using Comic-Con — particularly the cavernous Hall H — to unveil trailers and footage, transforming it into a central event of the entertainment industry calendar. Badges now sell out within hours, and the surrounding Gaslamp Quarter fills with off-site activations and promotional displays.

Programming and features

The convention includes panels with creators and actors, workshops, an exhibit hall, the Eisner Awards for comics achievement, a masquerade costume competition, film screenings, and an anime programme. Cosplay is a prominent feature. The event was held virtually as "Comic-Con@Home" in 2020 and 2021 during the COVID-19 pandemic, returning in person in 2022.
