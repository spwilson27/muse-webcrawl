Colombian coffee growing axis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Coffee fincas in the hills of the Eje Cafetero.

The Colombian coffee growing axis (Spanish: Eje Cafetero), also known as the Coffee Triangle (Triángulo del Café), is a part of the Colombian Paisa region in the rural area of Colombia. It is famous for growing and producing most Colombian coffee, considered by some to be the best coffee in the world. The region comprises the departments of Caldas (capital Manizales), Quindío (capital Armenia), and Risaralda (capital Pereira), together with adjacent areas of Tolima, Valle del Cauca, and Antioquia.

History

Coffee arrived in the region in the late 19th century as settlers from Antioquia — the colonización antioqueña — cleared the mountain forests and planted small farms. The volcanic soils, altitude of roughly 1,000–2,000 metres, and evenly distributed rainfall of the central cordillera proved ideal for arabica coffee. Coffee income built railways, towns with distinctive bahareque architecture, and institutions such as the National Federation of Coffee Growers of Colombia (founded 1927), whose fictional spokesman, Juan Valdez, became a global brand icon.

Coffee Cultural Landscape

In 2011 UNESCO inscribed the Coffee Cultural Landscape of Colombia on the World Heritage List, recognising the continuing tradition of smallholder coffee farming on steep mountain plots, the region's architecture and folk culture, and institutions such as the National Coffee Park near Montenegro, Quindío. The regional economy supplements coffee with plantain, citrus, and increasingly rural tourism centred on working coffee farms (fincas).
