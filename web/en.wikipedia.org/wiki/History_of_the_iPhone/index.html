History of the iPhone

The iPhone, developed by Apple Inc., was introduced by CEO Steve Jobs on January 9, 2007, at Macworld in San Francisco and released on June 29, 2007. It combined a mobile phone, an iPod with a touch interface, and an internet communicator in a single device with a 3.5-inch multi-touch display and no physical keyboard.

Development

Development began around 2004–2005 under the secret "Project Purple," growing out of Apple's tablet research and its partnership with Motorola (the ROKR E1). The original iPhone ran OS X-derived software, lacked 3G and third-party apps, and cost $499–$599 with a two-year AT&T (Cingular) contract in the United States.

Evolution

The iPhone 3G (2008) added the App Store, which transformed the software industry. Later milestones include the Retina display and FaceTime (iPhone 4, 2010), Siri (4S, 2011), Touch ID (5S, 2013), larger "Plus" models (2014), the removal of the headphone jack (iPhone 7, 2016), and Face ID with a full-screen design (iPhone X, 2017). The iPhone became Apple's most important product and helped define the modern smartphone, with over two billion units sold by the early 2020s.
