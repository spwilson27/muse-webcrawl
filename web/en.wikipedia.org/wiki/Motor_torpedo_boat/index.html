A motor torpedo boat is a fast torpedo boat, especially of the mid-20th century. Designed to operate at high speed, the small, wooden-hulled craft attacked larger warships with torpedoes, relying on speed and surprise rather than armour. The term is generally used for vessels of the Royal Navy (MTB) and the United States Navy (known as PT boats, for "patrol torpedo").

Development

The concept emerged from British Coastal Motor Boats of the First World War. Between the wars, firms such as Vosper, Thornycroft, British Power Boat and the American Elco company refined hard-chine planing hulls powered by petrol engines. During the Second World War MTBs saw extensive action in the English Channel, Mediterranean and Pacific.

Operations

MTBs escorted convoys, attacked enemy shipping, landed raiders and rescued downed airmen. Famous PT boat exploits include the evacuation of General Douglas MacArthur from the Philippines and the service of PT-109 under John F. Kennedy. German counterparts, the S-boats (called E-boats by the Allies), fought a long campaign against British coastal traffic.

Decline

After 1945 the anti-ship missile transformed fast attack craft, and the classic motor torpedo boat gave way to missile boats and modern patrol vessels.
