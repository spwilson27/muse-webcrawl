The Lieutenant Governor of California is the second-highest executive officer of the U.S. state of California. The lieutenant governor is elected separately from the governor — unlike many states, California does not use a joint ticket — for a four-year term, and is first in the line of succession should the governorship become vacant.

Duties

The office's formal powers are modest: the lieutenant governor serves as president of the California State Senate (casting tie-breaking votes), acts as governor when the governor is absent from the state or unable to serve, and succeeds to the office permanently upon a vacancy. By statute the lieutenant governor sits on numerous boards, including the Regents of the University of California, the California State University trustees, the State Lands Commission (which the office often chairs), and the Ocean Protection Council, and serves as a member of the State Board of Equalization for some purposes.

History

Because the governor and lieutenant governor are elected independently, they have sometimes belonged to different parties, leading to friction — notably when Republican lieutenant governor Mike Curb exercised gubernatorial powers while Democratic governor Jerry Brown travelled out of state. Several lieutenant governors have gone on to become governor, among them Goodwin Knight (who succeeded in 1953) and, by election, Gray Davis (lieutenant governor 1995–1999, then governor). Eleni Kounalakis, elected in 2018, is the first woman to hold the office.
