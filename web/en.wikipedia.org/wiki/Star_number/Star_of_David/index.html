Star of David

The Star of David (Hebrew: מָגֵן דָּוִד, Magen David, "Shield of David") is a six-pointed star formed by two overlapping equilateral triangles — one pointing up, one down — creating a hexagram. It is the most universally recognized symbol of Judaism and Jewish identity, and appears at the center of the flag of Israel.

Origins and history

The hexagram is an ancient geometric motif found in many cultures and religions, including Hinduism and early Islamic art. Its specifically Jewish association developed gradually: it appeared on Jewish seals and synagogue decorations in late antiquity and the Middle Ages, and was used in medieval Kabbalah as a protective amulet. The name "Shield of David" derives from the tradition that King David's shield bore the symbol, or that the monogram of his name (two interlocking Hebrew dalets) formed it.

In 17th-century Prague the hexagram became a communal emblem of the Jewish community, and from there it spread across Europe. The Zionist movement adopted it in 1897, and it was placed on the flag of the State of Israel in 1948.

Dark chapter and modern use

Under Nazi persecution, Jews in Germany and occupied Europe were forced to wear a yellow Star of David badge marked "Jude," turning the symbol into an instrument of exclusion; after the Holocaust it was reclaimed as an emblem of survival and pride. Today the Star of David appears on synagogues, tombstones, jewelry, and the red Magen David Adom, Israel's national emergency medical service, which uses a red version analogous to the Red Cross.
