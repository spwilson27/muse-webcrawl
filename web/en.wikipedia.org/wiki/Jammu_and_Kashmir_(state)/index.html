Jammu and Kashmir (state)

CountryIndia
Existed1952 – 31 October 2019
CapitalsSrinagar (summer); Jammu (winter)

Jammu and Kashmir was a state of India from 1952 until 2019, comprising the southern portion of the larger Kashmir region, which has been the subject of a territorial dispute between India and Pakistan since 1947 and between India and China since 1962. It consisted of three regions: the predominantly Muslim Kashmir Valley in the north, the Hindu-majority Jammu region in the south, and sparsely populated Ladakh in the east.

History

The princely state of Jammu and Kashmir, ruled by Maharaja Hari Singh of the Dogra dynasty, acceded to India in October 1947 amid invasion by tribal militias from Pakistan, sparking the Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–1948. Under Article 370 of the Indian constitution, the state enjoyed a special autonomous status, with its own constitution and flag and limits on the Indian Parliament's power to legislate for it.

A prolonged insurgency in the Kashmir Valley beginning in 1989, and continuing conflict along the Line of Control with Pakistan, made the state one of South Asia's most militarised regions.

Reorganisation

On 5 August 2019 the Government of India revoked the state's special status under Article 370, and Parliament passed the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019. With effect from 31 October 2019 the state was dissolved and replaced by two union territories: Jammu and Kashmir, with a legislature, and Ladakh, without one.

Geography and demography

The state spanned the Himalaya, Karakoram and Pir Panjal ranges and the fertile Jhelum valley. Its population at the 2011 census was about 12.5 million. Srinagar was the summer capital and Jammu the winter capital, a practice known as the Darbar Move.
