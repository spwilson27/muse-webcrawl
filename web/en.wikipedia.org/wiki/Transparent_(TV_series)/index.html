Transparent is an American comedy-drama television series created by Joey Soloway for Amazon Studios, which premiered in 2014. The title is a play on "trans parent". The series follows the Pfefferman family in Los Angeles after the discovery that the family's patriarch, retired professor Mort Pfefferman — played by Jeffrey Tambor — is a trans woman, who comes out and begins living as Maura. The story was inspired by Soloway's own parent coming out as trans.

Plot and characters

Maura's coming out acts as a catalyst for her self-absorbed adult children — Sarah (Amy Landecker), Josh (Jay Duplass) and Ali (Gaby Hoffmann) — and her ex-wife Shelly (Judith Light), each of whom confronts secrets about their own identities, sexuality and family history. Later seasons interweave flashbacks to the family's past and to queer and Jewish history in 1930s Berlin, and a trip to Israel in the fourth season. The show is noted for its naturalistic, semi-improvised tone and for employing trans people extensively in front of and behind the camera, which Soloway called the "transfirmative action program".

Reception and awards

Transparent was widely acclaimed and is credited with bringing transgender stories into mainstream television. It won the Golden Globe Award for Best Television Series – Musical or Comedy in 2015, and Tambor won the Golden Globe and two Primetime Emmy Awards for his performance; Soloway won two Emmys for directing. Critics praised its writing, performances and treatment of gender, family and Jewish identity, though some debate surrounded a cisgender actor playing the lead trans role.

Conclusion

In 2017 Amazon fired Tambor following allegations of sexual harassment on set, which he denied. The series concluded in September 2019 with a feature-length musical finale, Transparent: Musicale Finale, which addressed Maura's death off-screen.
