Keith Moon

Moon performing with the Who in the 1970s

Keith John Moon (23 August 1946 – 7 September 1978) was an English musician, best known as the drummer of the rock band the Who. Noted for his unique, explosive drumming style — emphasising tom-toms, cymbal crashes and drum fills — he has been widely acclaimed by critics and fellow musicians as one of the greatest drummers in rock history. He was equally famous for his flamboyant, destructive behaviour, earning the nickname "Moon the Loon".

Career with the Who

Moon joined the Who in 1964 after famously approaching the band at a gig and claiming he could play better than their then-drummer. He played on all of the band's classic albums, from My Generation (1965) through Tommy (1969), Who's Next (1971) and Quadrophenia (1973) to Who Are You (1978). His playing — influenced by jazz drummers such as Gene Krupa as much as by rock and roll — avoided conventional timekeeping in favour of surging, lead-instrument interplay with Pete Townshend's guitar and John Entwistle's bass.

Wild behaviour and solo work

Moon became notorious for smashing his kit on stage, blowing up hotel toilets with explosives, and driving a car into a hotel swimming pool. He released one solo album, Two Sides of the Moon (1975), and acted in films including That'll Be the Day (1973) and Ken Russell's Tommy (1975), in which he played Uncle Ernie.

Death

Years of alcohol and drug abuse badly damaged Moon's health and playing. On 7 September 1978, after attending a party hosted by Paul McCartney, he died in his sleep at his Mayfair flat from an overdose of clomethiazole, a sedative prescribed to help him withdraw from alcohol. He was 32. The Who continued with drummer Kenney Jones, but Moon's death marked the end of the band's classic era. He was posthumously inducted into the Modern Drummer Hall of Fame in 1982 and, as a member of the Who, into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1990.
