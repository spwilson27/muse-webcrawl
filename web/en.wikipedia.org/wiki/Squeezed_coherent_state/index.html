Squeezed coherent state

In physics, a squeezed coherent state is a quantum state of light (or another bosonic field) whose uncertainty in one quadrature is reduced below that of an ordinary coherent state, at the expense of increased uncertainty in the conjugate quadrature, in accordance with the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.

Description

Coherent states, produced by ideal lasers, distribute quantum noise equally between the amplitude and phase quadratures. Squeezing redistributes this noise, "squeezing" the uncertainty ellipse in phase space so that one observable is known more precisely than the standard quantum limit allows, while the total area of the ellipse is preserved.

Applications

Squeezed light is generated nonlinear-optically, for instance in parametric down-conversion. Its most prominent application is in gravitational-wave detectors: injecting squeezed vacuum into the interferometers of LIGO and Virgo reduces quantum noise and extends the detectors' range. Squeezed states are also used in precision spectroscopy and quantum information processing with continuous variables.
