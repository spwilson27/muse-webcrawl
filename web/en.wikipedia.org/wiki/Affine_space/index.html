Affine space

In mathematics, an affine space is a geometric structure that generalises some properties of Euclidean spaces in such a way that these are independent of the concepts of distance and measure of angles, keeping only the properties related to parallelism and ratios of lengths for parallel line segments.

Definition

An affine space consists of a set of points, a vector space of displacements, and an action of the vector space on the points that is free and transitive: for any two points P and Q there is exactly one vector PQ→ translating P to Q. Unlike a vector space, an affine space has no distinguished origin; choosing an origin (a coordinate frame) identifies it with its vector space.

Examples and uses

Ordinary Euclidean points form an affine space over the real numbers. Affine subspaces — points closed under taking affine combinations — are the translates of linear subspaces, i.e. points, lines, and planes not necessarily through the origin. Affine spaces are fundamental in affine geometry, algebraic geometry, and computer-aided design.
