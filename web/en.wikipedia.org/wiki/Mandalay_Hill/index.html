Mandalay Hill

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mandalay Hill is a 240-metre (790 ft) hill located northeast of the city centre of Mandalay in Myanmar. The city took its name from the hill. Mandalay Hill has been a major pilgrimage site for Burmese Buddhists for nearly two centuries.

Religious sites

According to legend, the Buddha visited the hill and prophesied that a great city would be founded at its foot in the Buddhist year 2400 (1857 CE), the year King Mindon founded Mandalay. A covered stairway of 1,729 steps climbs the hill past shrines and monasteries to the Sutaungpyei Pagoda at the summit, which offers panoramic views of the Mandalay plain, the Irrawaddy River and the Sagaing and Shan hills.

History

During the Second World War, the hill was a strongpoint in the Battle of Mandalay in March 1945, when Gurkha troops of the British 14th Army stormed its Japanese-held positions in fierce fighting.
