The Anderton Boat Lift is a two-caisson boat lift near the village of Anderton in Cheshire, England, linking the River Weaver with the Trent and Mersey Canal, some 50 feet (15.2 m) above it. Opened in 1875, it was the first commercially successful boat lift in the world and is often called the "Cathedral of the Canals." It is a Scheduled Monument and is graded I on the National Heritage List for England.

The Anderton Boat Lift, with its two water-filled caissons

Design and history

The lift was built to avoid transshipment of salt and other cargoes between river barges and canal narrowboats. Two water-filled iron caissons, each able to carry two narrowboats, moved vertically in counterbalance, originally by hydraulic rams designed by Edwin Clark: as one caisson descended, the other rose. In 1908, corrosion of the hydraulic system led to conversion to electric motors and a system of pulleys and counterweights carried on a forest of steel A-frames, which gives the structure its distinctive appearance.

Decline and restoration

Commercial traffic faded in the twentieth century and the lift was closed in 1983 after corrosion was found in the structure. A long campaign by the Friends of Anderton Boat Lift and others raised funds, and after a £7 million restoration the lift reopened in 2002, once again working on hydraulic power. It now carries leisure boats and hosts a visitor centre with trip boats.
