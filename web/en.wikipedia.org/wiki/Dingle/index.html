Dingle

Dingle (Irish: An Daingean or Daingean Uí Chúis) is a town in County Kerry, Ireland, and the principal settlement of the Dingle Peninsula. Situated on a natural harbour below Slievanea mountain, it is the westernmost town of Ireland and a centre of the Kerry Gaeltacht, where Irish is spoken daily.

Dingle prospered in the Middle Ages as a port trading with Spain and France, and its walled town was incorporated in the 1580s. The harbour and the surrounding peninsula are rich in early Christian and medieval remains, including the Gallarus Oratory, beehive huts, and ogham stones, which draw large numbers of visitors each year.

The town became internationally known for Fungie, a wild bottlenose dolphin who took up residence in the harbour mouth in the 1980s and remained, greeting boats, for over three decades until his disappearance in 2020. Today Dingle's economy rests largely on tourism, fishing, and distilling; the Dingle Distillery, founded in 2012, produces Irish whiskey and gin. The town name was the subject of a public dispute in the 2000s when official signage rendered it only as An Daingean; a 2011 plebiscite restored the bilingual "Dingle / Daingean Uí Chúis".
