Modiolus (face)

In facial anatomy, the modiolus is a point at the corner (angle) of the mouth where several muscles of facial expression converge. It is a dense, mobile, fibromuscular mass, likened in shape to the hub of a wheel (Latin modiolus, the nave of a wheel), into which the surrounding muscles insert like spokes.

Muscles involved

The muscles that meet at or near the modiolus include:

  • Orbicularis oris
  • Buccinator
  • Zygomaticus major
  • Levator anguli oris
  • Depressor anguli oris
  • Risorius
  • Levator labii superioris
  • Depressor labii inferioris
  • Platysma (in part)

Function

Because so many muscles with different lines of pull insert into it, the modiolus acts as an anchor and movable attachment that positions the corner of the mouth. It is essential to smiling, pursing the lips, speech, mastication and the expression of emotions. Its position and mobility vary between individuals and contribute to the presence or absence of cheek dimples.

Clinical significance

The modiolus region is important in dental prosthetics, orthodontics and reconstructive and aesthetic facial surgery, since altering the balance of forces at the corner of the mouth affects both appearance and oral function. It is supplied by branches of the facial artery and innervated by branches of the facial nerve (CN VII).
