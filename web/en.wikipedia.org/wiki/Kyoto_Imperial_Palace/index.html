Kyoto Imperial Palace

The Kyoto Imperial Palace (京都御所, Kyōto Gosho) is the former residence of Japan's Imperial Family in Kyoto, used from the move of the capital to Heian-kyō in 794 until the transfer of the capital to Tokyo during the Meiji Restoration in 1869. The present palace, completed in 1855, stands within the Kyoto Gyoen National Garden in Kamigyō Ward.

Kyoto Imperial Palace Location: Kamigyō, Kyoto, Japan
Completed: 1855 (current buildings)
Grounds: Kyoto Gyoen, approx. 63 ha
Access: Open to the public

History

The palace was repeatedly destroyed by fire and rebuilt; its location shifted slightly over the centuries and the current buildings date to a reconstruction ordered in the 1850s following a fire in 1854. Even after the court moved to Tokyo, enthronement ceremonies were held at the palace for the Taishō and Shōwa emperors, and the palace remains an official venue for imperial events in Kyoto.

Buildings

The complex includes the Shishinden, the hall for state ceremonies such as enthronements, with the Takamikura throne; the Seiryōden, the emperor's former daily residence, displaying the distinctive cypress-bark roofs and shinden-zukuri style; the Kogosho council hall; and the Ogakumonjo study hall. The layout balances Chinese-influenced formalism with the more relaxed shoin style of later buildings.

Access

Since 2016 the grounds have been open to walk-in visitors without prior application, administered by the Imperial Household Agency.
