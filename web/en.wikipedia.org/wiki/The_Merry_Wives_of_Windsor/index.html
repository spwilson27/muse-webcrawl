The Merry Wives of Windsor

Author: William Shakespeare
First performed: c. 1597
First published: 1602 (quarto)
Genre: Comedy

The Merry Wives of Windsor is a comedy by William Shakespeare, first published in 1602, though believed to have been written in or before 1597. It features the fat knight Sir John Falstaff, transplanted from the Henry IV plays into a contemporary, middle-class setting in the town of Windsor.

Plot

Short of money, Falstaff decides to court two wealthy married women, Mistress Ford and Mistress Page, sending them identical love letters. The wives discover the scheme and conspire to humiliate him: he is lured into a laundry basket and dumped in the Thames, beaten while disguised as a woman, and finally frightened in Windsor Forest dressed as Herne the Hunter. A subplot follows the courtship of Anne Page, whose parents favour different suitors while she chooses her own.

Origins and legacy

Tradition holds that Queen Elizabeth I so enjoyed Falstaff in Henry IV that she commanded a play showing him in love. The comedy is unusual in Shakespeare for its prose, its English provincial setting, and its bourgeois characters. It has inspired operas by Otto Nicolai and, most famously, Verdi's Falstaff (1893).
