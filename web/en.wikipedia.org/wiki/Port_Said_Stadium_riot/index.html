Port Said Stadium riot

On 1 February 2012, a massive riot occurred at Port Said Stadium in Port Said, Egypt, following an Egyptian Premier League football match between Al Masry and Al Ahly. More than 70 people — 74 by the most commonly cited count — were killed and over 500 injured, making it the deadliest disaster in Egyptian football history and one of the deadliest in world football. The violence occurred a year after the Egyptian revolution of 2011, and many Al Ahly supporters accused the police and military authorities of standing by, or worse, in revenge for the role of organised fans (ultras) in the revolution.

The match and the violence

Al Masry, the home team, won the match 3–1. As the final whistle blew, thousands of Al Masry supporters stormed the pitch and stands, attacking Al Ahly players and fans with stones, bottles, fireworks, and knives. Many Al Ahly fans were crushed or fell to their deaths in a narrow, reportedly locked or poorly lit exit corridor as they tried to escape; others were stabbed or beaten. Al Ahly players fled to their dressing room, and the match's aftermath was broadcast live on Egyptian television. Witnesses and survivors criticised the small police presence and its failure to intervene, noting that the gates were not opened and searches at entry had been lax.

Reactions and aftermath

The disaster provoked nationwide outrage. Protests and clashes erupted in Cairo and other cities in the following days, leaving more dead, as demonstrators blamed the ruling Supreme Council of the Armed Forces and the interior ministry. The Egyptian Football Association suspended the league (the 2011–12 season was cancelled), the federation's board resigned, and senior security officials in Port Said were dismissed. Egyptian league football was played largely behind closed doors for years afterward.

Trials

Seventy-three defendants, including Al Masry officials and police officers, were put on trial. In January 2013 a court sentenced 21 defendants — mostly Al Masry fans — to death, sparking riots in Port Said in which dozens more people were killed. In March 2013 the death sentences were upheld for 21 defendants while others received prison terms and nine, including security officials, were acquitted. After appeals and a retrial, a court in 2015 again confirmed death sentences against 11 defendants and imposed prison sentences on others; the verdicts were upheld on final appeal in 2017.
