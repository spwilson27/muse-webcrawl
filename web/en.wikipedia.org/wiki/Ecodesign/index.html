Ecodesign

Ecodesign, also known as design for the environment (DfE) or environmentally sustainable design, is an approach to designing products and services that gives special consideration to the environmental impacts of the product during its whole life cycle. It aims to reduce resource consumption, emissions and waste from raw-material extraction through manufacture, distribution, use and end-of-life treatment.

Principles

Ecodesign strategies include selecting low-impact and renewable materials, reducing the quantity of material used, optimising production techniques, reducing the impacts of distribution, lowering energy and resource consumption during the use phase, extending product lifetime through durability and repairability, and designing for reuse, remanufacturing and recycling at end of life. Life-cycle assessment (LCA) is a common tool used to compare design options.

Regulation

In the European Union, ecodesign requirements were first set under the Ecodesign Directive of 2005, recast in 2009, which established minimum energy-efficiency and environmental performance standards for energy-related products such as boilers, televisions and lighting. The Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation, adopted in 2024, broadened the framework to cover most physical goods, introducing digital product passports and rules against the destruction of unsold consumer products.

Related concepts

Ecodesign is closely related to the circular economy, cradle-to-cradle design, sustainable design and biomimicry. It differs from simple "green" marketing in that environmental performance is integrated into the design process itself, alongside cost, quality, safety and aesthetics.
