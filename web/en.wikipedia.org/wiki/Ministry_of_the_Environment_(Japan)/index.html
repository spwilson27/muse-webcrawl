Ministry of the Environment (Japan)

The Ministry of the Environment (Japanese: 環境省, Kankyō-shō) is a cabinet-level ministry of the Government of Japan responsible for environmental protection and policy. It was established on 6 January 2001, when the former Environment Agency (created in 1971) was upgraded to a full ministry as part of the central government reorganisation.

History and responsibilities

The Environment Agency was created in 1971 in response to severe pollution problems, including the "four big pollution diseases" such as Minamata disease and Yokkaichi asthma. Elevated to ministry status in 2001, the Ministry of the Environment oversees air and water quality, waste management and recycling, chemical safety, conservation of wildlife and natural habitats, Japan's national parks and quasi-national parks, and the decontamination and reconstruction work following the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster of 2011. It also leads Japanese climate-change policy, including the country's commitments under the Paris Agreement and its goal of carbon neutrality by 2050.

Organisation

The ministry includes the Minister's Secretariat and bureaus covering global environment, environmental management, water and air, nature conservation, and environmental restoration and resource circulation. The Nuclear Regulation Authority was established in 2012 as an external agency of the ministry to oversee nuclear safety independently of the bodies promoting nuclear power.
