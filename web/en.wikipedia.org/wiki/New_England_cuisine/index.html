New England cuisine

New England cuisine is the regional cooking of the six northeastern US states — Maine, Vermont, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Rhode Island and Connecticut. It developed from English colonial cooking combined with Native American ingredients such as corn, beans, squash and cranberries, and it is strongly shaped by the region's Atlantic fisheries.

Characteristic dishes

Seafood is central: clam chowder (the creamy New England style, distinct from Manhattan's tomato-based version), lobster rolls, fried clams, cod and haddock, and the traditional clambake of lobster, shellfish, corn and potatoes cooked in seaweed. Baked beans sweetened with molasses, brown bread, and succotash reflect the colonial pantry, while maple syrup from Vermont and New Hampshire sweetens breakfasts and desserts.

Desserts include apple cider doughnuts, Indian pudding, whoopie pies and Boston cream pie, the official dessert of Massachusetts. Later immigration — Portuguese fishermen, French Canadians, Italians and Irish — added dishes such as linguica, tourtière, and Rhode Island's coffee milk.
