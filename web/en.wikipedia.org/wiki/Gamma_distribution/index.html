Gamma distribution

Gamma distribution
Parameters: shape k > 0, scale θ > 0 (or rate β)
Support: x ∈ (0, ∞)
Mean: kθ
Variance: kθ²
Special cases: Exponential, Erlang, chi-squared

In probability theory and statistics, the gamma distribution is a versatile two-parameter family of continuous probability distributions. The exponential distribution, Erlang distribution, and chi-squared distribution are special cases of the gamma distribution. It is widely used to model waiting times and the time until an event occurs a specified number of times.


Gamma distribution densities

Definitions

With shape parameter k and scale parameter θ, the probability density function is f(x) = xk−1 e−x/θ / (Γ(k) θk) for x > 0, where Γ is the gamma function. An equivalent parameterisation uses a rate parameter β = 1/θ. The distribution arises as the sum of k independent exponentially distributed random variables.

Applications

The gamma distribution models rainfall amounts, insurance claims, queuing times and survival times. In Bayesian statistics it is the conjugate prior for several likelihood functions, including the Poisson rate and the normal precision. It is also used in the generalised linear model with a gamma error distribution.
