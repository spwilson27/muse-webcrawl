Rubber band ligation

Rubber band ligation (RBL) is an outpatient procedure for the treatment of internal haemorrhoids (piles). A small elastic band is applied at the base of the haemorrhoid, cutting off its blood supply; the strangulated tissue withers and sloughs off within a few days, leaving a small scar that fixes the remaining tissue to the underlying rectal wall.

Procedure

The procedure is performed through an anoscope or proctoscope, usually without anaesthesia because the band is placed above the dentate line, where there are few pain-sensitive nerve endings. One to three haemorrhoids may be banded at a single session. It is most effective for grade I to III internal haemorrhoids.

Complications

Common after-effects include a feeling of fullness and mild discomfort. Rare but serious complications include severe pain if a band is placed too low, urinary retention, bleeding when the tissue sloughs, and the rare but life-threatening condition of pelvic sepsis.
