Taborites

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Taborites (Czech: Táborité) were a radical faction within the Hussite movement of 15th-century Bohemia. They took their name from the town of Tábor in south Bohemia, founded in 1420 as a model Christian community named after Mount Tabor in the Holy Land. The Taborites combined theological radicalism — including rejection of purgatory, prayers for the dead and much of the traditional sacramental system — with social egalitarianism, including an early experiment in a form of communal property.

Origins and beliefs

After the execution of Jan Hus in 1415, his followers divided between moderate Utraquists, centred on Prague, who demanded communion in both kinds for the laity, and radicals who gathered at hilltop assemblies. At Tábor, priests such as Václav Koranda and the movement's theologians taught that Scripture alone was authoritative, anticipated an imminent millennium, and abolished private wealth among the first settlers, who deposited their goods in communal chests. The military organisation of the community fell to the one-eyed commander Jan Žižka, whose war wagons and disciplined peasant infantry defeated repeated crusades.

The Hussite Wars

Taborite armies, later led by Prokop the Great ("the Shaven"), carried the war beyond Bohemia in the so-called "beautiful rides" into Germany, Poland and Hungary. Internal tensions persisted: the most extreme chiliast groups, the Adamites, were suppressed by Žižka himself in 1421. After the Council of Basel opened negotiations with the moderates, the combined Utraquist and Catholic forces defeated the Taborites and their allies at the Battle of Lipany on 30 May 1434, where Prokop was killed.

Decline

Under the Compactata of Basel (1436) the Utraquist church was recognised, but the Taborites rejected the settlement. Their last stronghold, Tábor itself, fell to George of Poděbrady in 1452, and the separate Taborite church organisation was dissolved. Their legacy influenced later Bohemian reform movements, including the Unity of the Brethren.
