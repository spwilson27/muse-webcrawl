South India

South India is the southern region of India, generally comprising the states of Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu, together with the union territories of Puducherry and Lakshadweep. It occupies the peninsular Deccan plateau and the coastal plains on either side, bounded by the Bay of Bengal, the Arabian Sea and the Indian Ocean.

Geography

The region is framed by the Western and Eastern Ghats, which meet at the Nilgiri Hills. Major rivers — the Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri and Tungabhadra — flow eastward to the Bay of Bengal. The climate is tropical, with monsoon rains from both the southwest and northeast monsoons.

Languages and culture

The principal languages belong to the Dravidian family: Telugu, Tamil, Kannada and Malayalam, each with its own script and a long literary tradition. Tamil, one of the world's oldest continuously used classical languages, anchors a distinctive temple architecture, Carnatic music and Bharatanatyam dance.

History

South India was ruled by dynasties including the Cholas, Cheras, Pandyas, Pallavas, Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas and the Vijayanagara Empire, and later by the Marathas, Mysore, Hyderabad and the British Madras Presidency. The Chola navy projected power as far as Southeast Asia, and the region's ports traded with Rome, Arabia and China for two millennia.

Economy

Modern South India is a hub of information technology (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai), automobiles, textiles and film industries in several languages, alongside agriculture in rice, spices, coffee and coconuts.
