José Dirceu

José Dirceu de Oliveira e Silva (born 16 March 1946) is a Brazilian politician. A leading figure in the Workers' Party (PT), he served as Chief of Staff (Casa Civil) to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva from 2003 until 2005, when he resigned amid the Mensalão corruption scandal.

Early life and exile

Dirceu was born in Passa Quatro, Minas Gerais. As a student leader in São Paulo in the 1960s he joined the armed resistance to Brazil's military dictatorship. Arrested in 1968 at a clandestine student congress in Ibiúna, he was released in 1969 as one of the political prisoners exchanged for the kidnapped U.S. ambassador Charles Burke Elbrick. He went into exile, received guerrilla training in Cuba, and lived clandestinely in Brazil in the 1970s, even undergoing plastic surgery to conceal his identity, before an amnesty allowed him to return to public life in 1979.

Political career

Dirceu helped found the Workers' Party in 1980 and became its national president (1995-2002), running Lula's successful 2002 presidential campaign. As Chief of Staff he was regarded as the government's chief political operator. In 2005 the Mensalão scandal - allegations of monthly payments to congressional allies - forced his resignation; the Chamber of Deputies stripped him of his seat and political rights for eight years.

Trials and imprisonment

In 2012 Brazil's Supreme Federal Court convicted Dirceu of corruption in the Mensalão trial, and he served prison time from 2013. In 2015 he was arrested again in the Operation Car Wash (Lava Jato) investigation and convicted in further cases, receiving long sentences; some of these were later modified or annulled on appeal as the Car Wash judgments were reviewed. Dirceu has consistently maintained his innocence, describing himself as a political prisoner.
