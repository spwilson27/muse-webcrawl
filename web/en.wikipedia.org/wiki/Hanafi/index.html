Hanafi

Hanafi school
Named for: Abu Hanifa (699–767)
Branch: Sunni Islam
Regions: Central and South Asia, Turkey, Balkans, Egypt, Levant

The Hanafi school (Arabic: حنفي, Ḥanafī) is one of the four major schools (madhhabs) of Islamic jurisprudence (fiqh) within Sunni Islam. Founded on the teachings of the Kufan jurist Abu Hanifa (699–767 CE), it is the oldest and, by number of adherents, the largest of the four schools.

Methodology

Hanafi jurisprudence is noted for its structured use of legal reasoning: the Qur'an and Sunnah, consensus (ijma), analogical reasoning (qiyas), juristic preference (istihsan), and local custom (urf). Abu Hanifa's disciples Abu Yusuf and Muhammad al-Shaybani systematised the school.

Distribution

The Hanafi school predominates in Turkey, the Balkans, Central Asia, Afghanistan, Pakistan, India and Bangladesh, and is followed by a large share of Muslims in Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon and Syria. It was the official school of the Abbasid, Ottoman and Mughal empires.
