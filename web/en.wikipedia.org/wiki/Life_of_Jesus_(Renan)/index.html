Life of Jesus (Renan)

Vie de Jésus (Life of Jesus)

Author: Ernest Renan

Language: French

Published: 1863

Series: Histoire des origines du christianisme

Life of Jesus (French: Vie de Jésus) is a book by the French historian and philosopher Ernest Renan, published in 1863. It was the first volume of Renan's History of the Origins of Christianity and became one of the best-selling books in nineteenth-century France. Presenting Jesus as an extraordinary but wholly human figure, the work was a landmark of the historical-critical study of the Bible and a sensation — and scandal — in its day.

Content

Drawing on the Gospels treated as historical sources to be critically sifted, and on his own travels in Palestine during a French archaeological mission to Phoenicia, Renan portrayed Jesus as a gentle, charismatic preacher from Galilee whose message of love was progressively distorted — first by the expectations of his followers and later by the Church. Miracles were explained as legendary accretions or as misunderstandings by credulous witnesses. Renan's lyrical evocation of the Galilean landscape and his portrait of Jesus as "an incomparable man" made the book widely read beyond scholarly circles.

Reception

The book provoked fierce opposition from the Catholic Church, which condemned it, while Renan was suspended from his chair at the Collège de France after his inaugural lecture described Jesus as an incomparable man rather than a god. Despite the controversy — or because of it — the Life of Jesus sold tens of thousands of copies, was translated into many languages, and influenced subsequent "lives of Jesus" and the broader quest for the historical Jesus, alongside the earlier controversial work of David Friedrich Strauss.
