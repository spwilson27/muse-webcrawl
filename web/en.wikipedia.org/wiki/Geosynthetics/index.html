Geosynthetics

Geosynthetics are synthetic (polymeric) products used to solve civil engineering problems involving soil, rock, and other geotechnical materials. The field emerged in the second half of the 20th century and geosynthetics are now standard components in roads, dams, landfills, canals, coastal works, and foundations, where they perform functions such as separation, reinforcement, filtration, drainage, and containment.

MaterialsPolypropylene, polyester, polyethylene, PVC and other polymers
First uses1950s–1960s (filter fabrics, canal liners)
FunctionsSeparation, reinforcement, filtration, drainage, barrier, protection, erosion control

Types

TypeDescription and typical use
GeotextilesWoven or nonwoven permeable fabrics; separation, filtration, drainage, protection.
GeogridsOpen grid structures of tensioned ribs; reinforcement of roads, slopes, and retaining walls.
GeomembranesEssentially impermeable sheets; liners for landfills, reservoirs, and canals.
Geonets / geocompositesNet-like drainage cores, often combined with geotextiles, for in-plane drainage.
Geosynthetic clay linersBentonite clay sandwiched between geotextiles; hydraulic barriers.
Geocells, geofoam, geopipesHoneycomb confinement systems, lightweight EPS fill blocks, and buried plastic pipes.

History

Precursors include the use of woven cotton fabrics in road construction in the 1920s–30s and plastic films as canal liners in the 1950s. In the 1960s the Dutch engineer work on filter fabrics and the French use of nonwovens in dams and roads established modern practice; the term "geotextile" was coined by J. P. Giroud, who also organized the first international conference on the subject in Paris in 1977. The broader term "geosynthetics" followed as membranes, grids, and composites proliferated. The International Geosynthetics Society was founded in 1983.

Applications and advantages

Geosynthetics can replace or reduce the use of natural materials such as aggregate filter layers and clay liners, often lowering cost, construction time, and haulage. Reinforced soil structures built with geogrids or geotextiles allow steeper slopes and walls on weak ground. In environmental engineering, geomembrane and clay-liner systems are the regulated standard for containing waste and leachate. Design relies on polymer durability testing, since the products must often perform for decades underground.
