Santa María (ship)

Santa María
Type: Carrack (nao)
Launched: c. 1480, Pontevedra, Galicia
Fate: Ran aground and wrecked, 25 December 1492, off Haiti
Role: Flagship of Columbus's first voyage

La Santa María de la Inmaculada Concepción, commonly called the Santa María, was the largest of the three ships used by Christopher Columbus in his first voyage across the Atlantic Ocean in 1492, serving as his flagship. Her companion vessels were the caravels Niña and Pinta.

Description

The Santa María was a carrack (in Spanish, a nao) of roughly 150 tons, about 19–24 metres long, built in Pontevedra, Galicia. She was owned by Juan de la Cosa, who sailed as her master under Columbus. Slow and cumbersome compared with the caravels, she was nevertheless the expedition's principal cargo carrier.

Voyage and loss

The fleet sailed from Palos de la Frontera on 3 August 1492 and reached the Bahamas on 12 October. On Christmas night 1492, while off the northern coast of Hispaniola, the Santa María drifted onto a reef or sandbank and was wrecked. Her timbers were salvaged and used to build the settlement of La Navidad, the first European settlement in the Americas, where Columbus left 39 men — all found dead on his return the following year. Several replicas of the ship have since been built and displayed.
