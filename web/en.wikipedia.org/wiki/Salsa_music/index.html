Salsa music is a popular dance music genre that initially arose in New York City during the 1960s. Salsa is the product of various musical genres including the Cuban son montuno, guaracha, cha-cha-chá, mambo, and to a certain extent, the Puerto Rican plena and the Latin jazz. Latin jazz, which was also developed in New York City, has had a platinum influence on salsa, and salsa bands often feature jazz players.

Origins

The word "salsa" (Spanish for "sauce") was popularised as a marketing term by Fania Records, the New York label founded in 1964 by Johnny Pacheco and Jerry Masucci. Fania's artists — Celia Cruz, Héctor Lavoe, Willie Colón, Rubén Blades, Ray Barretto, Eddie Palmieri and others — codified a sound built on Cuban rhythmic frameworks (clave, tumbao, montuno) performed by big bands with piano, brass sections and layered percussion.

Characteristics

Salsa is organised around the clave pattern (2–3 or 3–2 son clave), with a repeating piano montuno, a syncopated bass tumbao, and call-and-response coro–pregón vocals. Songs typically alternate a verse (canto) with an extended montuno section featuring improvisation and horn mambos.

Development

In the 1980s, salsa romántica softened the sound with romantic lyrics and smoother production. Cuban timba developed the genre in a more rhythmically aggressive direction, while Cali, Colombia became a world salsa capital. Salsa dancing developed parallel regional styles (New York "on 2", Los Angeles "on 1", Cuban casino).
