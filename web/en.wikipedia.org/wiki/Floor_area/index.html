Floor area

Floor area (also floor space or floorspace) is the area of a building taken up by its floors, and is a fundamental measure of building size used in architecture, construction, real estate, and planning. Different conventions define which parts of a building are counted, leading to distinct measures such as gross floor area and net floor area.

Definitions

Gross floor area (GFA), also called gross internal area, is the total floor area contained within a building, measured to the external face of the external walls on each floor. Net floor area (net internal area, or usable floor area) excludes areas occupied by walls, structural elements, and in many conventions also circulation space, toilets, and plant rooms, counting only the space usable by occupants. The exact rules vary by jurisdiction and standard-setting body, including the International Property Measurement Standards (IPMS).

Uses

Floor area figures determine rental and sale values in commercial real estate, where prices are commonly quoted per square metre or square foot. In zoning and planning law, the floor area ratio (FAR) — the ratio of a building's gross floor area to the area of its plot — is a key control on development density. Floor area is also used in building energy ratings, occupancy limits, and per-capita space statistics.
