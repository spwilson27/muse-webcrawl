The Battle of Rorke's Drift, also known as the Defence of Rorke's Drift, was an engagement in the Anglo-Zulu War. On 22–23 January 1879, a small British garrison of about 150 men successfully defended a mission station against repeated attacks by 3,000–4,000 Zulu warriors. Eleven Victoria Crosses were awarded, the most ever for a single action by one regiment.

Background

Earlier on 22 January the main British column had been annihilated at the Battle of Isandlwana. A Zulu force under Prince Dabulamanzi kaMpande, acting against King Cetshwayo's orders not to cross into Natal, advanced on the supply depot and hospital at Rorke's Drift, on the Buffalo River crossing into the colony. Warned of the approach, Lieutenants John Chard (Royal Engineers) and Gonville Bromhead prepared defences of mealie bags and biscuit boxes with the men of B Company, 2nd Battalion, 24th Regiment of Foot, and a number of patients and auxiliaries.

The battle

The attacks began in the late afternoon of 22 January and continued through the night. Wave after wave of Zulu riflemen and spearmen assaulted the perimeter, set the hospital thatch alight and fought hand to hand through its rooms as the defenders evacuated the patients. Chard steadily contracted the perimeter around a final redoubt of mealie bags. By dawn on 23 January the Zulu force, exhausted and having lost several hundred men, withdrew as a British relief column approached. Seventeen defenders were killed.

Aftermath and legacy

The defence offset, in the British press, the humiliation of Isandlwana, and the unusually large number of Victoria Crosses — including Chard, Bromhead, and Privates such as Henry Hook and John Williams — has been the subject of debate ever since. The battle was immortalised in the 1964 film Zulu.
