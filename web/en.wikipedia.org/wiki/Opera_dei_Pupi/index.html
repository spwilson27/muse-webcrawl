Opera dei Pupi

The Opera dei Pupi (Italian for "Puppet Opera") is a marionette theatrical tradition of Sicily, originating in the early nineteenth century. Performances dramatise medieval chivalric literature, especially Frankish romantic epics such as The Song of Roland and Ariosto's Orlando Furioso, with armoured knights such as Charlemagne's paladins fighting Saracens in elaborate staged battles.

The puppets (pupi) are large wooden marionettes, roughly a metre tall in the Palermo tradition, manipulated by rods and strings by puppeteers (pupari) who also supply the voices. Two main schools exist, those of Palermo and Catania, differing in puppet size, jointing and staging.

In 2001 UNESCO proclaimed the Opera dei Pupi a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, and it was inscribed on the Representative List in 2008. The tradition declined with the arrival of cinema and television, but family companies and museums, such as the Antonio Pasqualino International Puppet Museum in Palermo, keep it alive.
