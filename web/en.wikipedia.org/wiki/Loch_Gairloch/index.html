Loch Gairloch is a sea loch on the north-west coast of Scotland, in Wester Ross in the Highland council area. The loch takes its name from the village of Gairloch, which sits at its head, and lends the wider parish its name.

The loch opens into the Minch, the strait separating the mainland from the Outer Hebrides. Its shores are indented by smaller inlets, including Loch Kerry and the bay at Badachro, and it shelters a number of islands near its mouth, among them Fraoch-eilean and Eilean Horrisdale.

Settlements and activities

The principal settlement on the loch is Gairloch, a fishing and holiday village at the loch's south-eastern end. Smaller communities, including Charlestown, Badachro, Port Henderson and Strath, line its shores. Fishing for shellfish, small-scale aquaculture, and tourism — including whale- and dolphin-watching trips departing from Gairloch — are the main local activities. On a clear day the mountains of Torridon and the island of Skye are visible across the loch.

The loch also gave its name to Gairloch's heritage museum and to a number of local institutions in the parish.
