The Grambling State Tigers football program represents Grambling State University in college football in Grambling, Louisiana. The Tigers compete in the NCAA Division I Football Championship Subdivision (FCS) as a member of the Southwestern Athletic Conference. The program is one of the most storied in historically Black college football, built to national prominence by legendary head coach Eddie Robinson.

History

Football at Grambling began in 1928. Eddie Robinson took over as head coach in 1941 and remained for 57 seasons, retiring in 1997 with 408 victories — then a record for a college football coach. Under Robinson, Grambling won numerous SWAC championships and Black college national championships, and sent more than 200 players to professional football, including Pro Football Hall of Famers Buck Buchanan, Willie Brown, Charlie Joiner and Willie Davis. Quarterback Doug Williams, a Grambling product, became the first Black quarterback to start and win a Super Bowl.

Rivalries

Grambling's best-known rivalry is with the Southern Jaguars, contested annually as the Bayou Classic in New Orleans, one of the most celebrated events in HBCU sports.

Championships

The Tigers have claimed multiple Black college football national championships (including 1955, 1967, 1972, 1974, 1975, 1977, 1980, 1983, 1992, 2000, 2001, 2005 and 2016) and many SWAC conference titles. They won the Celebration Bowl in 2016.
