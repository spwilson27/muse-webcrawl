Black Lives Matter

Black Lives Matter (BLM) is a decentralised political and social movement that emerged in the United States in 2013, advocating against violence and systemic racism towards Black people and, more broadly, against police violence. The hashtag #BlackLivesMatter was created by Alicia Garza, Patrisse Cullors, and Opal Tometi after the acquittal of George Zimmerman in the killing of Trayvon Martin.

Development

The movement gained national prominence in 2014 through protests in Ferguson, Missouri, after the police killing of Michael Brown, and in New York after the death of Eric Garner. Local chapters organised protests, die-ins, and campaigns around cases of police violence, while the slogan spread internationally. The associated Black Lives Matter Global Network Foundation became the movement's best-known formal organisation.

2020 protests

After the murder of George Floyd by Minneapolis police officer Derek Chauvin in May 2020, protests erupted in thousands of places across the US and around the world; estimates suggest 15 million to 26 million participants in the US alone, making it possibly the largest protest movement in the country's history. Demands ranged from police reform and accountability to "defund the police" and broader anti-racist change. The protests prompted policy changes, corporate and cultural responses, and also counter-mobilisation and criticism, including the counter-slogan "All Lives Matter" and debates over protest tactics and the foundation's finances and governance.

Impact and criticism

Supporters credit BLM with transforming public discussion of race and policing; public opinion shifted markedly in 2020 before receding in following years. Critics have raised concerns about some protests turning violent, the movement's decentralised structure, and the Global Network Foundation's spending, which drew scrutiny and leadership changes after 2021.
