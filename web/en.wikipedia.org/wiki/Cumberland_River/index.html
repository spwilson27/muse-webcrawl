The Cumberland River is a major waterway of the southeastern United States, about 688 miles (1,107 km) long. It drains an area of roughly 17,700 square miles in southern Kentucky and north-central Tennessee, flowing generally westward to join the Ohio River at Smithland, Kentucky. It is the Ohio's second-largest tributary by discharge after the Tennessee River.

Course

The river begins in the Appalachian Mountains of Letcher and Harlan counties, Kentucky, where the Poor Fork and Clover Fork join. It flows southwest through Kentucky — past Pineville and the Cumberland Falls, a 68-foot waterfall on the river famous for its moonbow — then into Tennessee, where it passes through Nashville, the state capital, before turning north, crossing back into Kentucky past the Land Between the Lakes, and joining the Ohio.

Dams and navigation

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers has dammed the river and its tributaries for flood control, hydroelectric power and navigation. Wolf Creek Dam impounds Lake Cumberland, one of the largest man-made lakes in the eastern United States; other projects include Dale Hollow Lake, Center Hill Lake, Old Hickory Lake and the Barkley Dam, whose canal connects the Cumberland to the Tennessee River. Barges carry coal, limestone and other bulk goods through the lock system.

History

The river is named for Prince William, Duke of Cumberland, the name having been given by explorer Thomas Walker in 1750. It was a vital artery for early settlement of Kentucky and Tennessee and a contested strategic waterway during the American Civil War, when Union victories at Forts Henry and Donelson opened the river in 1862. Severe flooding, notably at Nashville in May 2010, has periodically devastated its banks.
