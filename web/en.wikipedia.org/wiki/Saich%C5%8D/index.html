Saichō

Saichō (最澄)
Born: 15 September 767, Ōmi Province, Japan
Died: 26 June 822, Mount Hiei
Posthumous name: Dengyō Daishi
Known for: Founder of Tendai Buddhism in Japan

Saichō (最澄, September 15, 767 – June 26, 822) was a Japanese Buddhist monk credited with founding the Tendai school of Buddhism based on the Chinese Tiantai tradition. He founded the temple and monastery of Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei near Kyoto, which became one of the most powerful and influential institutions in Japanese history.

Life

Ordained as a novice at a young age in Nara, Saichō withdrew to Mount Hiei, where he studied the Tiantai teachings centred on the Lotus Sutra. In 804 he travelled to Tang China as part of an official embassy, studying Tiantai, esoteric Buddhism, Zen and the precepts. On his return in 805 he established the Tendai school, which synthesised these elements.

Legacy

Saichō was posthumously given the title Dengyō Daishi. Many later founders of Japanese Buddhist schools, including Hōnen, Shinran, Eisai, Dōgen and Nichiren, trained at Enryaku-ji, making Saichō's foundation a seedbed of Kamakura Buddhism.
