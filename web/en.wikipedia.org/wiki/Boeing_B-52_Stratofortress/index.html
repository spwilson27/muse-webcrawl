Boeing B-52 Stratofortress

The Boeing B-52 Stratofortress is an American long-range, subsonic, jet-powered strategic bomber. The B-52 was designed and built by Boeing, which has continued to provide support and upgrades. It has been operated by the United States Air Force (USAF) since the 1950s. The bomber can carry up to 70,000 pounds (32,000 kg) of weapons, has a typical combat range of around 8,800 miles (14,200 km) without aerial refueling, and is expected to remain in service into the 2050s — a service life approaching a century.

Development

The B-52 originated in a 1946 requirement for a new strategic bomber. Boeing's design evolved from a straight-wing turboprop proposal into an eight-engine swept-wing jet, influenced by the B-47 Stratojet, and the YB-52 prototype first flew on 15 April 1952. Production ran from 1952 to 1962 across models A through H; the B-52H, powered by eight Pratt & Whitney TF33 turbofans, is the only variant still in service, with about 76 aircraft in the inventory.

Operational history

B-52s formed the airborne leg of the American nuclear deterrent throughout the Cold War, flying airborne alert and later ground alert under Strategic Air Command. In the Vietnam War, B-52s flew massive conventional bombing campaigns (Operation Arc Light, and the Linebacker operations of 1972). They launched cruise missiles in the 1991 Gulf War and flew long-range strikes in Kosovo, Afghanistan, Iraq, and Syria, including precision-guided munitions drops in close air support roles. The aircraft — nicknamed the "BUFF" ("Big Ugly Fat Fellow") — has been crewed in some cases by grandchildren of earlier crew members.

Modernization

Successive upgrade programs have added GPS-guided weapons, new communications and defensive systems, and a planned radar replacement. A major re-engining program will replace the TF33s with Rolls-Royce F130 engines, along with a digital cockpit upgrade; with these changes the type (to be redesignated in its upgraded form) is projected to serve alongside the B-21 Raider into the 2050s, making the B-52 the longest-serving combat aircraft in history.

Specifications (B-52H)

Crew: 5. Length: 48.5 m (159 ft 4 in); wingspan: 56.4 m (185 ft); height: 12.4 m (40 ft 8 in). Maximum takeoff weight: 220,000 kg (488,000 lb). Powerplant: 8 × Pratt & Whitney TF33-P-3/103 turbofans, 17,000 lbf (75.6 kN) each. Maximum speed: 1,050 km/h (650 mph, Mach 0.86). Range: about 14,162 km (8,800 mi). Service ceiling: 15,000 m (50,000 ft). Armament: up to 31,500 kg (70,000 lb) of bombs, mines, and missiles, including AGM-86 ALCM cruise missiles; tail gun removed from the H model.
