Sudoku

Sudoku (数独, "single number") is a logic-based, combinatorial number-placement puzzle. In its classic form the objective is to fill a 9 × 9 grid with digits 1–9 so that each column, each row and each of the nine 3 × 3 sub-grids contains every digit exactly once. The puzzle setter provides a partially completed grid, which for a well-posed puzzle has a single solution.

History

Number puzzles of this type appeared in French newspapers in the 19th century, and the modern game was designed by the American architect Howard Garns, published by Dell Magazines in 1979 as "Number Place". The puzzle was introduced in Japan by Nikoli in 1984 under the name Sudoku, a shortening of a phrase meaning "the digits must remain single". It achieved international popularity in 2004–2005 after Wayne Gould supplied puzzles to The Times of London.

Mathematics

A completed Sudoku grid is a Latin square with an additional block constraint. The number of possible completed 9 × 9 grids is 6,670,903,752,021,072,936,960 (about 6.67 × 1021), computed by Felgenhauer and Jarvis in 2005. Research published in 2012 proved that the minimum number of clues for a unique solution is 17. Sudoku solving is an NP-complete problem in its generalised form, though the fixed 9 × 9 puzzle is readily solved by backtracking algorithms or constraint propagation.

Variants and competitions

Numerous variants exist, including Killer Sudoku, Samurai Sudoku (overlapping grids), diagonal Sudoku and larger 16 × 16 "hexadoku" grids. The World Sudoku Championship has been organised by the World Puzzle Federation since 2006. Solving techniques range from simple candidate elimination to advanced chains, fish patterns and forcing chains.
