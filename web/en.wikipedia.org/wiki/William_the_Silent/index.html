William I, Prince of Orange (24 April 1533 – 10 July 1584), commonly known as William the Silent (Dutch: Willem de Zwijger), was the main leader of the Dutch Revolt against the Spanish Habsburgs that set off the Eighty Years' War (1568–1648) and resulted in the formal independence of the United Provinces in 1581. He is regarded as the Vader des Vaderlands — the Father of the Fatherland — of the Netherlands.

Early life

William was born at Dillenburg in the County of Nassau, Germany, the son of William I, Count of Nassau-Dillenburg. At eleven he inherited the Principality of Orange in southern France from his cousin René of Chalon, becoming a prince and a favourite at the Brussels court of Emperor Charles V, who personally oversaw his upbringing. His nickname "the Silent" traditionally derives from a hunting conversation in which Henry II of France revealed plans against the Protestants and William concealed his alarm.

Opposition to Spanish rule

Under Philip II, William opposed the introduction of the Spanish Inquisition and the curtailing of the privileges of the Netherlands. With the Counts of Egmont and Horn he withdrew from the Council of State, and in 1567 fled to Germany as the Duke of Alba arrived with an army and a reign of terror.

Revolt

William's invasions of 1568 mark the start of the Eighty Years' War. After years of failure, the capture of Brill by the Sea Beggars in 1572 turned the tide, and Holland and Zeeland elected him stadtholder. He led the relief of Leiden (1574) and promoted the Union of Utrecht (1579). In his Apology of 1581 he defended the revolt against Philip's ban outlawing him.

Assassination

Philip II placed a price of 25,000 crowns on William's head. After earlier attempts, the French Catholic Balthasar Gérard shot him at the Prinsenhof in Delft on 10 July 1584 — one of the first heads of government assassinated with a handgun. His reported last words were in French: "My God, have pity on my soul; my God, have pity on this poor people." Leadership passed to his son Maurice of Nassau.

Legacy

William is honoured in the Dutch national anthem, the Wilhelmus, the oldest national anthem in the world, written in his honour. His tomb in Delft's Nieuwe Kerk became the burial place of the Dutch royal family.
