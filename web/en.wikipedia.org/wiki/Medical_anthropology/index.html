Medical anthropology

Medical anthropology studies "human health and disease, health care systems, and biocultural adaptation". It is a subfield of anthropology that uses ethnographic and other methods to understand how culture and society shape experiences of illness, healing practices and the organisation of medical care.

Scope

Medical anthropologists examine explanatory models of illness, the distinction between disease (biological pathology) and illness (lived experience), medical pluralism in which biomedicine coexists with traditional and alternative healing, and the social determinants of health. Applied medical anthropology contributes to public-health programmes, global health and clinical care.

History

The field coalesced in the 1960s–70s, drawing researchers such as Arthur Kleinman, whose work on explanatory models and the cultural construction of mental illness was influential, and Paul Farmer, who linked structural violence to unequal health outcomes in Haiti and elsewhere.
