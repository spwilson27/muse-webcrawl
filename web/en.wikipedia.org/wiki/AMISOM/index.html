The African Union Mission in Somalia (AMISOM) was a regional peacekeeping mission operated by the African Union with the approval of the United Nations, active in Somalia from 2007 to 2022. It was deployed to support the Transitional Federal Government, protect key infrastructure in Mogadishu, and combat the Islamist militant group al-Shabaab, in a country that had lacked an effective central government since 1991.

History

AMISOM was authorised in early 2007, and the first Ugandan troops arrived in Mogadishu in March of that year, initially confined largely to the airport, seaport, and presidential palace under frequent mortar attack. The mission expanded slowly until 2010–2011, when, reinforced to over 9,000 troops, it helped drive al-Shabaab out of the capital during fighting in 2011. Kenyan forces intervened separately in southern Somalia in October 2011 and were later incorporated into AMISOM, which reached an authorised strength of about 22,000. In subsequent years AMISOM and the Somali National Army recovered many towns and cities, though al-Shabaab retained control of much of rural southern and central Somalia and continued to mount major attacks, including on AMISOM bases at El Adde (2016) and elsewhere, inflicting heavy casualties on the mission.

Transition

AMISOM's mandate ended on 31 March 2022, when it was replaced by the African Union Transition Mission in Somalia (ATMIS), mandated to hand over security responsibilities progressively to Somali forces, itself followed by a further successor mission (AUSSOM) from 2025. AMISOM is widely regarded as one of the deadliest peacekeeping missions in history, though the African Union has not published comprehensive casualty figures.
