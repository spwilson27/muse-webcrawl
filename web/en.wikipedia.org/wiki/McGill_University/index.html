McGill University

McGill University is a public research university in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. Founded in 1821 by royal charter, it is one of Canada's best-known institutions of higher learning and one of the leading universities in the world. McGill offers degrees and diplomas in over 300 fields of study, and its students come from over 150 countries.

History

The university was founded with a bequest from James McGill, a Scottish-Canadian merchant, whose estate and funds were used to establish McGill College. It received its royal charter from King George IV in 1821, and its first classes in medicine began in 1829. McGill was the birthplace of ice hockey rules (1877), and its graduates include Ernest Rutherford, who conducted his Nobel Prize-winning research on radioactivity there in 1898–1907.

Academics and alumni

McGill counts 12 Nobel laureates and 145 Rhodes Scholars among its alumni — the most Rhodes Scholars of any Canadian university. Notable alumni include former Prime Minister Justin Trudeau, singer-songwriter Leonard Cohen, and architect Moshe Safdie. The university comprises 11 faculties, including one of Canada's oldest medical schools, and two campuses: the downtown campus at the foot of Mount Royal and the Macdonald Campus in Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue.
