Color field

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


In optics and in painting, a "color field" is a broad, uniform area of colour.

The term color field refers, in its most general sense, to a broad area or expanse of a single colour or of gradually varying colour. In painting it gives its name to Color Field painting, the mid-20th-century abstract style built from such expanses. In vision science and display technology, the term describes the extent or the appearance of coloured regions within the visual field.

In art

A colour field in a painting dispenses with figure-ground contrast and linear drawing in favour of an all-over composition in which colour itself carries the structure and emotional weight of the work. Artists of the Color Field movement — Rothko, Newman, Louis, Noland — used very large canvases so the field of colour would fill the viewer's peripheral vision and create an immersive effect.

In optics and imaging

In photometry and colorimetry, the size of a colour field (for example, the 2° and 10° fields used in defining standard observers) affects measured colour appearance, because the distribution of cone types differs between the centre and the edge of the retina. In television and video test signals, "color field" sequences display full-frame single colours for calibrating and testing displays and transmission chains.
