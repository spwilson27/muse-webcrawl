Permeability (Earth sciences)

In the Earth sciences, permeability is a measure of the ability of a porous material — rock or unconsolidated sediment — to allow fluids to pass through it. It depends on the size, shape, and connectivity of the pore spaces, but not on the fluid itself; it is an intrinsic property of the medium.

Units and measurement

Permeability has dimensions of area and is commonly expressed in darcies (D) or millidarcies (mD), named after Henry Darcy, whose law describes flow through porous media. Laboratory measurements use core samples and permeameters; field estimates come from pumping and well tests.

Importance

Permeability controls groundwater movement in aquifers, the migration and recovery of oil and natural gas from reservoir rocks, and the suitability of formations for carbon dioxide storage. It is related to, but distinct from, porosity: a rock can be highly porous yet nearly impermeable if its pores are not connected, as in many clays.
