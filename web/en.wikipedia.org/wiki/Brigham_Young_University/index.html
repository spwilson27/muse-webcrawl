Brigham Young University (BYU) is a private research university in Provo, Utah, United States. It is sponsored by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and is the flagship institution of the Church Educational System. With around 35,000 students, it is one of the largest private universities in the United States.

History

The school was founded in 1875 by Brigham Young, the second president of the Church, as Brigham Young Academy. Under principal Benjamin Cluff it became a university in 1903. Church president George H. Albertson and later presidents, notably Dallin H. Oaks and Rex E. Lee, expanded its graduate and professional programmes, including the J. Reuben Clark Law School and the Marriott School of Business.

Campus and student life

The 560-acre campus sits at the foot of the Wasatch Range. Students agree to abide by an Honor Code covering academic honesty, dress and grooming standards, and abstinence from alcohol, tobacco and extramarital sex, reflecting the sponsoring church's teachings. A large proportion of students serve church missions and study foreign languages, making BYU one of the most multilingual campuses in the country. Sister institutions include BYU–Idaho and BYU–Hawaii.

Athletics

The BYU Cougars compete in NCAA Division I, joining the Big 12 Conference in 2023. The football team won the national championship in 1984 under coach LaVell Edwards.
