Horrible Histories is a British series of children's history books written by Terry Deary and illustrated by Martin Brown, published by Scholastic. First appearing in 1993 with The Terrible Tudors and The Awful Egyptians, the series presents "history with the nasty bits left in", combining gruesome facts, quizzes, cartoons, and comic strips to make the past entertaining for young readers.

Books

The main series spans topics from the Stone Age (The Savage Stone Age) through the Rotten Romans, Vicious Vikings, and Groovy Greeks to the Woeful Second World War and beyond, alongside specials on cities, countries, and themes such as sport and science. The books have sold tens of millions of copies worldwide and have been translated into dozens of languages.

Adaptations

The series has been adapted into an animated television series (2001), stage productions, and most famously the BAFTA-winning CBBC sketch-comedy series Horrible Histories (2009–2015), whose ensemble cast and songs — many performed by the troupe later known for Ghosts — won a large adult following. A feature film, Horrible Histories: The Movie — Rotten Romans, followed in 2019.

Reception

Teachers and historians have generally praised the series for engaging reluctant readers with history, while occasionally debating its simplifications. Deary retired from writing new main-series titles in the 2020s, three decades after the first books appeared.
