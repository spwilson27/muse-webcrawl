D-subminiature
Type: Electrical connector
Invented: 1952, by ITT Cannon
Common types: DE-9, DB-25, DE-15 (VGA)

The D-subminiature, or D-sub, is a common family of electrical connectors distinguished by a D-shaped metal shell that ensures correct orientation. It was introduced by Cannon in 1952.

Types and uses

D-subs come in several shell sizes with pin counts including 9, 15, 25, 37 and 50. They were long standard for serial ports (DE-9, DB-25), parallel printer ports, game ports and analogue video: the DE-15 connector carried VGA signals for decades. High-density variants add more pins in the same shell.

Decline and persistence

USB, HDMI and other smaller connectors have displaced D-subs in consumer computing, but they remain common in industrial control, aviation, test equipment and embedded hardware for their robustness and locking screws.
