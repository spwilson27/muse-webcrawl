Hôtel de la Marine

The Hôtel de la Marine is a historic palace on the Place de la Concorde in Paris, built between 1757 and 1774 by the architect Ange-Jacques Gabriel. Together with its twin, the Hôtel des Monnaies (now the Hôtel de Crillon), it forms the northern side of the square. Originally the Garde-Meuble de la Couronne, the royal furniture storehouse, it later housed the headquarters of the French Navy for more than two centuries, and reopened to the public in 2021 after a major restoration as a museum and cultural venue.

History

The building was erected as part of the Place Louis XV (now Place de la Concorde). As the Garde-Meuble, it stored and displayed the Crown's furniture, tapestries, arms and the French Crown Jewels. In September 1792, during the Revolution, thieves stole most of the crown jewels from the building, including the Regent and French Blue diamonds.

From 1789 the Ministry of the Navy progressively took over the palace, and from 1806 until 2015 it served as the navy's headquarters. The ministry's departure to the Hexagone Balard defence complex freed the building for restoration.

Restoration and museum

Restored by the Centre des monuments nationaux, the Hôtel de la Marine opened to visitors in June 2021. Its 18th-century intendant's apartments were refurbished to recreate their appearance in the 1780s, the state rooms were restored, and the building also hosts the Paris galleries of the Al Thani Collection, bookable offices, restaurants and a bookshop.
