Halter (horse show)

Halter is a type of horse show class in which horses are shown "in hand" — led, not ridden — and judged on their conformation and suitability as breeding stock. The horse wears only a halter or bridle, and the handler presents it to the judge, typically walking and trotting it in a pattern and then setting it up standing squarely for inspection.

Judging

Judges evaluate balance, structural correctness, muscling, breed and sex characteristics, quality, and way of going. Classes are divided by breed, age, and sex, and grooming and turnout are expected to be immaculate. Halter competition is especially prominent in American Quarter Horse, Arabian, and other stock and pleasure breeds; specialist "halter horses" are bred and conditioned for the discipline. The practice has attracted controversy where extreme muscling or training practices in some breeds have been linked to genetic conditions such as HYPP in Quarter Horses, prompting rule changes by breed associations.

Related classes

Related in-hand classes include showmanship, in which the handler rather than the horse is judged, and in-hand trail and conformation classes at breed inspections in Europe.
