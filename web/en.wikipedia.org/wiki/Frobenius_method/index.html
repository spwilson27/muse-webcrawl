Frobenius method

The Frobenius method, named after Ferdinand Georg Frobenius, is a way to find an infinite series solution of a second-order ordinary differential equation of the form

z2u″ + p(z)zu′ + q(z)u = 0,

in the vicinity of the regular singular point z = 0. The method generalises the ordinary power series method, which works only at ordinary points, by allowing the series to begin with a non-integer power of the variable.

Outline

One divides the equation by z2 to obtain an equation with a pole of order at most 1 in the first-derivative term and order at most 2 in the zeroth-order term. One then assumes a solution of the form

u(z) = Σn=0∞ an zn+r,   a0 ≠ 0,

where the exponent r is to be determined. Substituting the series and equating coefficients of the lowest power of z yields the indicial equation, a quadratic in r whose roots determine the possible leading behaviour of the solutions.

Roots of the indicial equation

If the roots are distinct and do not differ by an integer, each root gives an independent Frobenius series solution. If the roots coincide, or differ by an integer, the second solution generally involves a logarithmic term, obtained for example by differentiating the series solution with respect to the parameter r. Many of the classical special functions of mathematical physics — including the Bessel functions and the hypergeometric function — arise as Frobenius series solutions of their defining equations.
