Home Office

Home Office
Formed: 27 March 1782
HQ: 2 Marsham Street, London
Minister: Secretary of State for the Home Department (Home Secretary)

The Home Office (HO), legally the Home Department, is a ministerial department of the Government of the United Kingdom, responsible for immigration, security, and law and order. It is headed by the Secretary of State for the Home Department, commonly called the Home Secretary, one of the great offices of state.

Responsibilities

The department oversees policing in England and Wales, the UK Border Force, HM Passport Office, UK Visas and Immigration, and the Security Service (MI5), and it leads policy on drugs, crime reduction, counter-terrorism, and fire and rescue services in England. Executive agencies and bodies include the Disclosure and Barring Service and the Independent Office for Police Conduct.

History

The Home Office was created in 1782 when the Southern Department was renamed, taking charge of domestic affairs. Over the following two centuries it accumulated and shed many functions: the factory inspectorate, broadcasting, prisons (transferred to the Ministry of Justice in 2007), and the administration of the Channel Islands and Isle of Man's link to the Crown.

Headquarters

Since 2005 the department has been based at 2 Marsham Street in Westminster, having previously occupied 50 Queen Anne's Gate.
