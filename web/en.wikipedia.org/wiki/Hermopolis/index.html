Hermopolis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hermopolis (Ancient Greek: Ἑρμούπολις, "city of Hermes") was a major city in ancient Egypt, known in Egyptian as Khmun ("Eight-Town"), the cult centre of the Ogdoad, a group of eight primordial deities, and of the ibis- and baboon-associated god Thoth, whom the Greeks identified with Hermes. The site lies near the modern town of el-Ashmunein in Minya Governorate on the west bank of the Nile.

History

Hermopolis was the capital of the 15th (Hare) nome of Upper Egypt and a prosperous provincial and religious centre from the Old Kingdom onward. Its priests developed the Hermopolitan creation myth, in which the world emerged from the primeval waters of Nun through the Ogdoad. The nearby necropolis of Tuna el-Gebel contains the vast ibis catacombs and the tomb of the high priest Petosiris.

Remains

Surviving remains include two colossal quartzite statues of baboons representing Thoth, erected under Amenhotep III, the foundations of temples of Thoth and of Nero, and a later Christian basilica. Excavations have been conducted by German and Egyptian missions since the early 20th century.
