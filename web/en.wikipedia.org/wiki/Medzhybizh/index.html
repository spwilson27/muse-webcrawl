Medzhybizh (Ukrainian: Меджибіж; Yiddish: מעזשיבאָזש, Mezhibozh) is a rural settlement in Khmelnytskyi Oblast, western Ukraine, situated where the Buzhok river joins the Southern Bug. It is best known for its medieval fortress and as the birthplace of Hasidic Judaism.

History

First mentioned in 1146, Medzhybizh was an important fortified town of the Podolia region, contested between Kievan Rus', Lithuania, Poland, and the Ottoman Empire. Its massive stone castle, repeatedly rebuilt, guarded the frontier against Tatar raids. The town was devastated during the Khmelnytsky Uprising of 1648–49 and changed hands several times before passing to the Russian Empire in the Second Partition of Poland (1793).

Jewish history

Jews lived in Medzhybizh from the 16th century, and the town became a major Jewish center. Around 1740 Rabbi Israel ben Eliezer, the Baal Shem Tov — founder of Hasidic Judaism — settled there, and his grave remains a pilgrimage site. The town was also home to other Hasidic leaders, including Baruch of Medzhybizh and the Degel Machaneh Ephraim. The Jewish community was destroyed in the Holocaust: in 1942 the remaining Jews of Medzhybizh were murdered in mass shootings nearby.

Landmarks

The Medzhybizh Castle, now a museum reserve, dominates the settlement. The Baal Shem Tov's restored synagogue and cemetery draw Hasidic pilgrims from around the world each year.
