Fast food restaurant

A fast food restaurant emphasises speed of service, standardised menus and counter or drive-through ordering.

A fast food restaurant, also known as a quick service restaurant (QSR), is a type of restaurant that serves fast food cuisine and has minimal table service. Food is typically ordered and paid for at a counter or drive-through window, prepared quickly from pre-processed ingredients using standardised procedures, and served packaged for take-out or rapid on-premises consumption.

Characteristics

Fast food restaurants are defined by speed, uniformity and low prices. Menus are limited and engineered for fast assembly; cooking processes are simplified and often centralised, with items partially prepared at commissaries. Many outlets belong to large franchise chains — such as McDonald's, Burger King, Subway, KFC and Taco Bell — whose franchise models allow rapid expansion while maintaining consistent branding, decor and product.

History

Precursors include ancient Roman thermopolia and British fish and chip shops. The modern format emerged in the United States in the 1920s with White Castle (founded 1921), which standardised the hamburger stand. A&W introduced drive-in service, and in the 1940s–1950s the McDonald brothers' "Speedee Service System" applied assembly-line principles to the kitchen; Ray Kroc franchised the concept from 1955. Car culture, suburbanisation and the Interstate Highway System fuelled explosive growth, and the format spread worldwide from the 1970s onward.

Variations

Related formats include fast-casual restaurants, which offer higher-priced, freshly prepared items with somewhat more service; food trucks; and takeaway shops specialising in pizza, fried chicken, sandwiches, kebabs, sushi or regional cuisines. Drive-through service accounts for a majority of sales at many American chains.

Criticism

The industry has been criticised for the nutritional profile of its food — high in calories, sodium, added sugars and saturated fat — and its association with obesity; for low-wage, precarious employment; and for environmental impacts of packaging and meat supply chains. Chains have responded with salads, grilled items, nutritional labelling, and commitments on packaging and sourcing, while governments have introduced measures such as menu calorie labelling and advertising restrictions.
