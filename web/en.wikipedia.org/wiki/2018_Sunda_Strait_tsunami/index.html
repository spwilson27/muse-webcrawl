2018 Sunda Strait tsunami

On 22 December 2018, a tsunami struck the coasts of Banten and Lampung in Indonesia along the Sunda Strait, killing at least 437 people and injuring more than 14,000. The tsunami was caused by an undersea landslide triggered by the eruption of Anak Krakatoa volcano, whose flank partially collapsed into the sea. Because it was not preceded by an earthquake, no tsunami warning was issued.

Event

Anak Krakatoa ("Child of Krakatoa"), which grew from the caldera of the 1883 Krakatoa eruption, had been erupting since June 2018. At about 21:03 local time on 22 December, roughly two-thirds of the cone above sea level — an estimated 0.2 km³ of material — slid into the ocean. Tsunami waves reached the coast within about 25 minutes, striking beaches, resorts and villages on both sides of the strait at night and without warning. Among the victims were members of the Indonesian pop band Seventeen, who were performing at a beach resort in Tanjung Lesung when the wave hit the stage.

Aftermath

Indonesia's disaster agency reported 437 deaths, more than 14,000 injuries, and hundreds of homes, hotels and vessels damaged or destroyed; tens of thousands were displaced. The disaster exposed gaps in Indonesia's warning system, which relied on seismic buoys designed for earthquake-generated tsunamis; the government subsequently pledged to develop early-warning capability for volcanic and landslide tsunamis. The collapse reduced Anak Krakatoa's height from about 338 m to about 110 m.
