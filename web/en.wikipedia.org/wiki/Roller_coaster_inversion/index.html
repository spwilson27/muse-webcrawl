Roller coaster inversion

A roller coaster inversion is an element of a roller coaster in which the track turns riders upside down and then returns them to an upright position. Inversions are counted as a headline statistic for many coasters, and the record for the most inversions on a single ride has been contested by manufacturers since the 1970s.

Types

Common inversion elements include the vertical loop, corkscrew, zero-g roll, Immelmann, dive loop, cobra roll, batwing, sea serpent, pretzel loop (on flying coasters) and the heartline roll. Modern steel coasters combine these in sequences; wooden coasters historically lacked inversions until modern track engineering (such as Rocky Mountain Construction's I-Box track) made them feasible.

History

Early looping coasters of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, such as the Flip Flap Railway, used circular loops that produced dangerous g-forces. The modern era began in 1975 with Corkscrew at Knott's Berry Farm, the first coaster with (two) corkscrew inversions using a clothoid-based design, and Revolution at Magic Mountain (1976) with the first modern vertical loop. Multi-inversion coasters escalated through the 1980s and 1990s; as of the 2020s, rides such as The Smiler (14 inversions) hold the world record.
