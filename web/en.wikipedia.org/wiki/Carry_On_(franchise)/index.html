Carry On is a British comedy franchise of 31 low-budget films produced between 1958 and 1978 (with one revival in 1992), together with television series and stage shows. Built on innuendo, double entendres and seaside-postcard humour, the films are a cornerstone of British popular culture.

The films

Producer Peter Rogers and director Gerald Thomas launched the series with Carry On Sergeant (1958), set in a National Service camp. The repertory company of regulars — Kenneth Williams, Sid James, Joan Sims, Charles Hawtrey, Kenneth Connor, Hattie Jacques, Bernard Bresslaw and, later, Barbara Windsor — romped through institutions and historical settings: hospitals (Carry On Nurse, Carry On Doctor), schools, the police, ancient Rome (Carry On Cleo, 1964) and the Khyber Pass (Carry On Up the Khyber, 1968), widely regarded as the series' peak. Carry On Camping (1969) supplied the famous scene of Barbara Windsor's bikini top flying off during exercises.

Style and legacy

Made quickly on small budgets at Pinewood Studios, the films relied on wordplay, farcical plots and a cheerfully bawdy attitude rooted in music-hall and variety traditions. Critical opinion was initially dismissive, but the films were box-office staples and are now celebrated as a record of post-war British attitudes, endlessly repeated on television.
