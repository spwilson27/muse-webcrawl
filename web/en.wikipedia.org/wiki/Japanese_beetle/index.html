Japanese beetle

The Japanese beetle (Popillia japonica) is a species of scarab beetle native to Japan. It is a highly destructive pest when introduced, feeding on the leaves, flowers and fruit of more than 300 plant species in North America and parts of Europe.

Japanese beetle
Scientific namePopillia japonica Newman, 1841
FamilyScarabaeidae
Native rangeJapan
Introduced rangeNorth America, parts of Europe

Description

Adults are about 15 mm long, with an iridescent copper-coloured elytra and metallic green thorax and head, and distinctive tufts of white hair along the abdomen. Adults emerge in summer and feed gregariously, skeletonising leaves.

Life cycle and damage

Females lay eggs in turf; the white C-shaped grubs feed on grass roots before overwintering deep in the soil. Adults defoliate roses, lindens, grapes and many crops. First detected in New Jersey in 1916, the beetle has spread across much of eastern North America despite quarantines, pheromone traps and biological control with milky spore disease.
