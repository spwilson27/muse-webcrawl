Kaohsiung

Kaohsiung (Chinese: 高雄; pinyin: Gāoxióng) is a special municipality in southern Taiwan and the country's third most populous city, with around 2.7 million inhabitants. It is Taiwan's largest port city and a major industrial centre, and serves as the political and economic hub of southern Taiwan.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Makatau people; the settlement was known as Takao (打狗), from a Makatau word for bamboo forest. Under Japanese rule (1895–1945) the name was rendered with characters read "Takao" in Japanese and the harbour was developed into a major naval and commercial port. After 1945 the city became part of the Republic of China. In 1979 the Kaohsiung Incident, a pro-democracy demonstration and subsequent crackdown, became a landmark in Taiwan's democratisation. In 2010, Kaohsiung City merged with Kaohsiung County to form the present enlarged special municipality.

Geography and economy

The municipality extends from the port and dense urban core on the Taiwan Strait inland to Jade Mountain (Yushan), the highest peak in Taiwan. The Port of Kaohsiung is one of the largest container ports in the world and Taiwan's busiest. Heavy industries — steel, shipbuilding, petrochemicals and refining — anchor the economy, alongside a growing cultural and tourism sector.

Sights and transport

Notable attractions include the Lotus Pond with its Dragon and Tiger Pagodas, the Love River, the Pier-2 Art Center, the Fo Guang Shan Buddha Museum, and the 85 Sky Tower. Kaohsiung is served by Kaohsiung International Airport, the Kaohsiung MRT (including the widely photographed Formosa Boulevard station), light rail, and the high-speed rail terminus at Zuoying.
