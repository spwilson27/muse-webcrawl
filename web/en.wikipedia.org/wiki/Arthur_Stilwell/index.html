Arthur Edward Stilwell (1859–1928)

Arthur Edward Stilwell (October 21, 1859 – September 26, 1928) was the founder of the Kansas City Southern Railway and an influential railroad promoter in the United States at the turn of the 20th century. He founded more than 40 cities in the region and created two major port cities, Port Arthur, Texas, and Lake Charles, Louisiana.

Career

Stilwell began his career in insurance, inventing a form of annuity policy, before turning to railroads. He completed the Kansas City Suburban Belt Railway and then built the Kansas City, Pittsburg and Gulf Railroad southward from Kansas City to the Gulf of Mexico. When Galveston proved unsuitable as a terminus, he founded Port Arthur on Sabine Lake and built a ship canal to the Gulf.

Financial difficulties forced the line into receivership, and Dutch financiers reorganized it as the Kansas City Southern Railway in 1900, ending Stilwell's control. He subsequently promoted railways in Mexico, including the Kansas City, Mexico and Orient Railway, aimed at the Pacific port of Topolobampo.

Spiritualism

Stilwell attributed many of his business decisions to guidance from "brownies" or spirit voices, which he described in his writings, including the autobiographical To a Brown Study and the novel The Light of the World. He died in New York City in 1928.
