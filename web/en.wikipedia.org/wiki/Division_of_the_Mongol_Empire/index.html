Division of the Mongol Empire

The division of the Mongol Empire began when Möngke Khan died in 1259 with no declared successor, leading to a civil war between his brothers Kublai and Ariq Böke (the Toluid Civil War, 1260–1264) and the effective fragmentation of the unified empire into four khanates.

Division of the Mongol Empire
Date: 1259–1260s onwards
Result: Yuan dynasty, Ilkhanate, Golden Horde, Chagatai Khanate

The four khanates

  • Yuan dynasty — Kublai Khan's realm in Mongolia and China, founder of the Yuan dynasty (1271).
  • Ilkhanate — Hulegu's realm in Persia and the Middle East.
  • Golden Horde — Jochi's ulus on the Kipchak steppe and Rus' lands.
  • Chagatai Khanate — in Central Asia.

Causes

Mongol succession customs required a kurultai election, and Genghis Khan's sons' appanages had already formed semi-autonomous uluses. Rivalries — notably Berke of the Golden Horde versus Hulegu over the Caucasus, sharpened by Berke's conversion to Islam — erupted into open wars.

Aftermath

The khanates developed independently, adopting local religions and cultures: Islam in the western khanates, Tibetan Buddhism and Chinese traditions in Yuan. A nominal unity persisted and was briefly reasserted in the 1304 peace, but the empire never reunified.
