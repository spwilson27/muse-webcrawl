New Classical architecture

New Classical architecture is a contemporary movement in architecture that applies the vocabulary, proportions, and compositional principles of classical architecture — the architecture of ancient Greece and Rome and its Renaissance, Baroque, and Neoclassical successors — to new buildings, using both traditional and modern construction techniques. It emerged as a reaction against modernist and postmodernist architecture and is closely associated with traditional urbanism and the New Urbanism movement in planning.

Characteristics

New Classical buildings typically employ load-bearing or masonry-clad construction, symmetrical façades, the classical orders, pitched roofs, and durable natural materials such as stone, brick, and timber. Practitioners hold that these forms are not mere decoration but embody tested principles of proportion, hierarchy, and human scale, and that classical architecture is a living language capable of development rather than a fixed historical style. The movement distinguishes itself from earlier "revivalist" styles by its self-conscious theoretical basis and from postmodernism's often ironic quotation of classical motifs.

History and practitioners

The movement gathered force from the 1980s, supported by figures such as Léon Krier, whose masterplans include Poundbury, the model village in Dorset developed with the patronage of Charles III (then Prince of Wales), whose 1989 book A Vision of Britain attacked the modernist architectural establishment. Architects associated with the movement include Quinlan Terry, Robert Adam, Demetri Porphyrios, Allan Greenberg, and John Simpson, architect of the Queen's Gallery at Buckingham Palace. Institutional support has come from the University of Notre Dame School of Architecture, which teaches classical design, and from organisations such as INTBAU and the Institute of Classical Architecture & Art. The Driehaus Architecture Prize, founded in 2003, honours leading traditional architects. Prominent works elsewhere include large parts of the reconstruction of Berlin's historic centre and new classical buildings across Europe, the United States, and beyond.
