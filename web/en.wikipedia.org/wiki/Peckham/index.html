Peckham (/ˈpɛkəm/) is a district in south-east London, within the London Borough of Southwark. It is located 3.5 miles (5.6 km) south-east of Charing Cross. At the 2001 Census the Peckham ward had a population of about 14,720; the wider area is known for its large West African community, its market street Rye Lane, and a growing reputation for arts venues, bars and restaurants.

History

Peckham appears in Domesday Book as Pecheham; the name probably means "village by a hill." It remained a rural village, celebrated in the eighteenth century for its gardens and orchards, until the coming of the railways in the 1860s turned it into a dense Victorian suburb and shopping centre. Henry Bessemer had an estate at nearby Denmark Hill, and the area developed large houses and terraces for the prosperous middle classes before declining in the twentieth century as housing was subdivided. The North Peckham Estate became notorious in 2000 after the killing of the schoolboy Damilola Taylor. Extensive regeneration since then, including the Stirling Prize-winning Peckham Library (2000), designed by Will Alsop, and the restoration of Peckham Rye station, has revived the district.

Culture and community

Rye Lane is one of London's most multicultural shopping streets, with African, Caribbean, and Asian grocers, fabric shops, and market stalls alongside newer cafés and venues such as the Bussey Building and Bold Tendencies, a multi-storey car park turned arts space famous for its rooftop views. Peckham is widely recognised as the setting of the sitcom Only Fools and Horses, though it was filmed mainly in Bristol. The Victorian novelist's connection is older: Charles Dickens referenced the area, and Charles Haddon Spurgeon preached nearby. Peckham Rye Park and Common provide its main green space, where William Blake is said to have seen his vision of angels in a tree.

Transport

Peckham Rye and Queens Road Peckham stations provide London Overground (Windrush line) and National Rail services to London Bridge, Victoria, and Clapham Junction. Numerous bus routes run along Rye Lane and Peckham High Street.
