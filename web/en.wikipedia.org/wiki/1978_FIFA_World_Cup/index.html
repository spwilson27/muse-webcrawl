The 1978 FIFA World Cup was the eleventh FIFA World Cup, held in Argentina from 1 to 25 June 1978. Hosts Argentina won their first World Cup, defeating the Netherlands 3–1 after extra time in the final at the Estadio Monumental in Buenos Aires. The tournament took place under Argentina's military junta, which had seized power in 1976, and was dogged by controversy over human-rights abuses by the regime and allegations surrounding Argentina's decisive 6–0 second-round win over Peru, which sent them to the final at Brazil's expense.

Tournament summary

Sixteen teams competed in a format of two group phases followed by the final and third-place play-off. Argentina, coached by César Luis Menotti and built around striker Mario Kempes and captain Daniel Passarella, recovered from a group-stage loss to Italy to reach the second phase. There, level with Brazil on points, they needed to beat Peru by four goals in the final group match; they won 6–0, a result long dogged by unproven claims of a deal between the Argentine and Peruvian governments. The Netherlands, without Johan Cruyff — who had withdrawn from international football — reached their second consecutive final through the goals of Rob Rensenbrink.

Final

In the final, Kempes put Argentina ahead before Dick Nanninga equalised late in normal time for the Dutch, and Rensenbrink struck the post in the dying seconds. In extra time, Kempes scored his second and Daniel Bertoni added a third to seal a 3–1 victory amid cascades of ticker tape.

Legacy

Argentina's win sparked nationwide celebrations but remains inseparable from the junta's attempt to use the tournament to legitimise itself during the "Dirty War". Kempes finished as top scorer and was named the tournament's best player. It was the last World Cup with 16 teams; Spain 1982 expanded the field to 24.
