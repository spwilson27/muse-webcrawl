Bioenergy with carbon capture and storage

Bioenergy with carbon capture and storage (BECCS) is the process of extracting bioenergy from biomass and capturing and storing the carbon, thereby removing it from the atmosphere. It is a form of carbon dioxide removal.

BECCS
Type: Carbon dioxide removal / negative emissions technology
Inputs: Biomass
Storage: Geological formations

How it works

Plants absorb CO2 as they grow. When biomass is burned for power, fermented to biofuels, or gasified, the CO2 released is captured and injected into geological storage rather than emitted, yielding net negative emissions if supply chains are low-carbon and storage permanent.

Role in climate scenarios

Many IPCC integrated assessment scenarios limiting warming to 1.5–2 °C rely on large-scale BECCS later this century. A notable operating example is ethanol production with capture in Illinois, USA; Drax power station in the UK has piloted capture on biomass units.

Concerns

Critics cite land, water and fertiliser demands competing with food and ecosystems, uncertain net negativity, storage permanence, and the risk of delaying emissions cuts.
