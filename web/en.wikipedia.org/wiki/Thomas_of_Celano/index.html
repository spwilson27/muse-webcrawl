Thomas of Celano

Bornc. 1185–1190, Celano, Abruzzo, Italy
Diedc. 1260, Tagliacozzo, Italy
OccupationFranciscan friar, writer, poet
Notable worksVita Sancti Francisci; Dies Irae (attrib.)

Thomas of Celano (Italian: Tommaso da Celano; c. 1185 – c. 1260) was an Italian Franciscan friar, a poet, and the author of three hagiographies about Saint Francis of Assisi. He was one of the earliest followers of the Franciscan Order and accompanied missions to Germany and elsewhere. Thomas is often attributed with composing the famous medieval sequence Dies Irae ("Day of Wrath"), sung in the Requiem Mass, though the attribution is debated.

Life

Thomas was born at Celano in the Abruzzi region of central Italy, probably between 1185 and 1190. He joined the Order of Friars Minor around 1215, possibly in the lifetime of Francis himself, and in 1221 he took part in the Franciscan mission to Germany under Caesar of Speyer. By 1223 he was back in Italy, holding offices as custos (guardian of a group of friaries) in the Rhineland province towns of Worms, Mainz, Speyer and Cologne. He spent his later years in Italy, serving as spiritual director to a convent of Poor Clares at Tagliacozzo, where he died around 1260.

Writings

After Francis's canonisation in 1228, Pope Gregory IX commissioned Thomas to write the official life of the new saint. The resulting Vita Prima Sancti Francisci ("First Life of Saint Francis", 1228–1229) is the earliest full biography of Francis and a foundational source for his life and miracles, including the stigmata received on Mount La Verna. In the 1240s, at the request of the order's general chapter, Thomas produced a second, shorter life, the Vita Secunda (1246–1247), incorporating material sent in by Francis's surviving companions. His third work, the Tractatus de miraculis ("Treatise on the Miracles", c. 1250–1252), collected accounts of posthumous miracles.

Thomas's vivid, devout prose shaped the popular image of Francis for centuries. The Dies Irae, traditionally ascribed to him, became one of the most famous poems of the Latin Middle Ages and its melody has been quoted by composers from Mozart and Berlioz to Rachmaninoff.

Legacy

Because so many early Franciscan writings were later suppressed as the order standardised its official biography, Thomas's works are of exceptional importance to scholars of medieval spirituality and of the Franciscan movement.
