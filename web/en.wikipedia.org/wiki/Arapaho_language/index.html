The Arapaho language (Hinónoʼeitíít) is a Plains Algonquian language spoken by the Arapaho people of Wyoming and formerly Oklahoma. Closely related to Gros Ventre, it is polysynthetic, with complex verb morphology and a pitch-accent system unusual among Algonquian languages.

Status

Fluent speakers are now mostly elders of the Northern Arapaho at the Wind River Reservation; the Southern Arapaho variety in Oklahoma is nearly extinct. Immersion schools, master-apprentice programmes and digital archives aim to transmit the language to children.

Features

Arapaho has a small consonant inventory, phonemic vowel length and pitch, and nouns classified as animate or inanimate. Verbs encode rich information about participants, giving the language very free word order.
