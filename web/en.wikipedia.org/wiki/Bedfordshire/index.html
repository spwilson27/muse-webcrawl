Bedfordshire (abbreviated Beds) is a ceremonial county in the East of England. It borders Cambridgeshire to the north-east, Northamptonshire to the north, Buckinghamshire to the west, Hertfordshire to the south-east, and the Greater London area lies to the south-east beyond Hertfordshire. The county town is Bedford, and the largest town is Luton.

Bedfordshire is one of the smallest English counties, but is densely populated in its south, where Luton forms part of a conurbation extending toward London. The county is largely governed by three unitary authorities: Bedford, Central Bedfordshire and Luton.

Geography

The southern end of the county lies on the chalk ridge of the Chiltern Hills, whose highest point in Bedfordshire is Dunstable Downs at 243 metres. The rest of the county forms part of the drainage basin of the River Great Ouse and its tributaries. The Greensand Ridge runs across the county from near Leighton Buzzard to near Gamlingay in Cambridgeshire. The northern part of the county is a broad clay vale.

History

Bedfordshire was first recorded as a county in the early 11th century, taking its name from Bedford, a burh founded at a crossing of the Great Ouse. The county was historically divided into nine hundreds. Bedford Castle was destroyed after a siege in 1224. During the English Civil War the county generally supported Parliament.

In the 19th century the county became known for straw plaiting and hat-making, centred on Luton, which grew rapidly after the arrival of the railway and later became known for motor manufacturing with Vauxhall Motors. Brick-making was also significant, with the London Brick Company operating large works in the Marston Vale.

Settlements

The principal settlements are Luton, Bedford, Dunstable, Leighton Buzzard, Biggleswade, Sandy, Ampthill, Flitwick, Kempston and Houghton Regis. London Luton Airport, one of the United Kingdom's busiest airports, is located just south of Luton.

Places of interest

  • Whipsnade Zoo, on the Dunstable Downs
  • Wrest Park, near Silsoe
  • The Shuttleworth Collection of vintage aircraft at Old Warden
  • Bedford's Embankment along the Great Ouse
  • Dunstable Downs and the Chilterns escarpment

Transport

The M1 motorway runs through the south of the county, and major rail routes include the Midland Main Line through Bedford and Luton, the West Coast Main Line (with a station at Leighton Buzzard just across the border area), and the East Coast Main Line at Sandy and Biggleswade. The Marston Vale line links Bedford with Bletchley.
