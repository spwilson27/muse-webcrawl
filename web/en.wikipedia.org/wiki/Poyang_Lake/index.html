Poyang Lake

Poyang Lake is a critical wintering ground for Siberian cranes

LocationJiangxi Province, China
TypeFreshwater lake
Max. areaAbout 4,400 km² (wet season, fluctuating)
InflowGan, Fu, Xin, Rao, Xiu rivers
OutflowYangtze River

Poyang Lake (Chinese: 鄱阳湖; pinyin: Póyáng Hú), located in Jiangxi Province, is the largest freshwater lake in China. It is fed by the Gan, Fu, Xin, Rao, and Xiu rivers and drains north into the Yangtze near Hukou. The lake's surface area fluctuates dramatically with the seasons — expanding to several thousand square kilometres in the wet summer months and shrinking to a fraction of that, with exposed grasslands and mudflats, in the dry winter.

Ecology

Poyang is one of the most important wetland ecosystems in Asia. Its seasonal shallows host immense numbers of migratory waterbirds, including the great majority of the world's critically endangered Siberian cranes, as well as Oriental white storks, swan geese, and tundra swans. The lake historically supported the Yangtze finless porpoise in significant numbers. The Poyang Lake Nature Reserve, centred on the northwestern shore, is a Ramsar wetland of international importance.

Environmental pressures

Sand dredging, water extraction, the effects of the Three Gorges Dam on Yangtze flows, drought associated with climate change, and land reclamation have lowered dry-season water levels and shrunk the lake earlier in the year, causing ecological stress and periodic public controversy over proposals to build a sluice regulating the lake's outlet. In the severe drought of 2022 the lake contracted to near-record lows, exposing its bed and the medieval "Thousand Eyes" bridge. In history, the lake was the site of the Battle of Lake Poyang in 1363, one of the largest naval battles ever fought, in which Zhu Yuanzhang defeated his rival Chen Youliang on the way to founding the Ming dynasty.
