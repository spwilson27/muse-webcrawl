Pulp (band)

Pulp are an English rock band formed in Sheffield in 1978. At their critical and commercial peak, the band consisted of Jarvis Cocker (vocals, guitar), Russell Senior (guitar, violin), Candida Doyle (keyboards), Nick Banks (drums), Steve Mackey (bass) and Mark Webber (guitar). They were among the leading acts of the Britpop movement of the 1990s.

History

Cocker formed the band, originally called Arabicus Pulp, while at school in Sheffield. After more than a decade of obscurity and several independent releases, including It (1983), Freaks (1987) and Separations (1992), Pulp signed to Island Records and released His 'n' Hers (1994), which was nominated for the Mercury Prize.

Their breakthrough came with Different Class (1995), which debuted at number one in the UK and won the Mercury Prize in 1996. Its singles "Common People" and "Disco 2000" became anthems of the era; "Common People," a sharply observed song about class tourism, reached number two on the UK Singles Chart and is widely regarded as one of the defining songs of Britpop. The band headlined Glastonbury in 1995 as last-minute replacements for the Stone Roses.

The darker follow-up This Is Hardcore (1998) was critically acclaimed but commercially weaker, and We Love Life (2001), produced by Scott Walker, was their final album before a hiatus in 2002. Pulp reunited for live performances in 2011–2012 and again from 2022, and released More, their first studio album in 24 years, in 2025. Bassist Steve Mackey died in March 2023.
