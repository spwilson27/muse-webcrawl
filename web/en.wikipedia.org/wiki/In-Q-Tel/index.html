In-Q-Tel, Inc. (IQT) is an American not-for-profit venture capital firm based in Arlington, Virginia. It invests in companies that develop technologies of potential use to the United States Intelligence Community, particularly the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA), from which it originated.

History

In-Q-Tel was founded in 1999, during the tenure of CIA director George Tenet, in response to concern that the rapid pace of innovation in Silicon Valley was outstripping the government's ability to develop comparable capabilities in-house. Its original name was Peleus; the current name is a play on the fictional Q, the quartermaster who supplies gadgets to James Bond, combined with "intel" (intelligence). The model was novel: rather than funding classified internal development, the firm would make strategic investments in commercial startups, giving intelligence agencies early access to emerging technology.

Investments

IQT has backed hundreds of companies in fields including data analytics, cybersecurity, geospatial intelligence, artificial intelligence, and biotechnology. Well-known early investments include the geospatial software company Keyhole, whose technology became the basis of Google Earth, and the data analytics firm Palantir Technologies. Although its portfolio companies typically serve commercial markets first, IQT works to adapt their products for government missions and also supports other agencies including the FBI, NSA, and Department of Defense organizations.
