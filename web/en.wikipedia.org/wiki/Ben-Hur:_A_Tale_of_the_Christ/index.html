Ben-Hur: A Tale of the Christ is a historical novel by the American author Lew Wallace, published in 1880. Set in the Roman province of Judea at the time of Jesus, it tells the story of Judah Ben-Hur, a Jewish prince falsely condemned to the galleys through the betrayal of his Roman friend Messala, who survives, grows rich, and returns to defeat Messala in a spectacular chariot race — while his encounters with Jesus transform his thirst for revenge into faith. The novel became the best-selling American novel of the 19th century, surpassing Uncle Tom's Cabin, and remained in print continuously.

Plot

Judah Ben-Hur, heir of a wealthy Jerusalem family, is arrested after a tile falls from his roof onto the Roman governor. His boyhood friend, the Roman tribune Messala, refuses to help; Judah is sentenced to slavery in the galleys and his mother and sister are imprisoned. Aboard ship he saves the commander Quintus Arrius, who adopts him. Trained as a charioteer, Judah returns east, defeats Messala in the great race at Antioch, discovers his mother and sister alive but leprous, and follows Jesus of Nazareth, witnessing the miracles and the Crucifixion, at which his family is healed and his hatred dissolves.

Reception and legacy

Written by Wallace — a Union general in the American Civil War and later governor of New Mexico Territory — after a celebrated conversation about religion with the agnostic orator Robert Ingersoll, the book's research and storytelling made it a cultural phenomenon, blessed by Pope Leo XIII and read across the world. The stage adaptation (1899), which ran for over 6,000 performances, staged the chariot race with live horses on treadmills. MGM's silent film of 1925 and William Wyler's 1959 epic Ben-Hur with Charlton Heston — winner of a record 11 Academy Awards — made the chariot race one of cinema's most famous sequences. A further film adaptation appeared in 2016.
