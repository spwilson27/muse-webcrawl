Zirconium is a chemical element with the symbol Zr and atomic number 40. It is a lustrous, grey-white, strong transition metal resembling hafnium and, to a lesser extent, titanium. Zirconium is exceptionally resistant to corrosion and is used mainly as a refractory, an opacifier, and — because of its very low neutron absorption — in alloys for nuclear reactor components.

Properties and production

The metal forms a protective oxide layer in air and resists acids and alkalis. Commercial zirconium always contains 1–3% hafnium, from which it must be painstakingly separated for nuclear use, since hafnium absorbs neutrons strongly. Most zirconium comes from the mineral zircon (ZrSiO4), mined chiefly in Australia and South Africa, and is produced by the Kroll process.

Applications

Zirconium alloys (zircaloys) clad nuclear fuel rods because they are transparent to neutrons yet withstand hot water. Zirconium dioxide (zirconia) is used in ceramics, dental crowns, oxygen sensors, and as a gemstone simulant (cubic zirconia). Zirconium compounds serve in deodorants, water treatment, and catalysts. Finely divided zirconium is pyrophoric and burns with one of the hottest flames of any metal.
