Teenage pregnancy, also known as adolescent pregnancy, is pregnancy in a female under the age of 20. It can occur after the onset of ovulation, which precedes the first menstrual period in some girls. Worldwide, complications in pregnancy and childbirth are a leading cause of death for girls aged 15–19, and babies born to adolescent mothers face higher risks of low birth weight, preterm birth and severe neonatal conditions.

Rates

Adolescent birth rates vary enormously: they exceed 100 births per 1,000 girls aged 15–19 in much of sub-Saharan Africa, while rates in much of Europe and East Asia are below 10 per 1,000. In the United States, the teen birth rate peaked in the early 1990s and has since fallen by more than two-thirds, a decline demographers attribute chiefly to improved contraceptive use, including long-acting reversible contraception.

Causes and consequences

Drivers include early and child marriage (still the context of most adolescent births in some regions), sexual violence, poverty, school dropout and lack of access to contraception and comprehensive sexuality education. Consequences for mothers include interrupted education, reduced earnings, eclampsia and other obstetric risks, and social stigma.

Prevention

Public-health bodies such as the World Health Organization and UNFPA recommend keeping girls in school, ending child marriage, providing youth-friendly reproductive health services and comprehensive sexuality education — measures associated with sustained declines in adolescent pregnancy where applied.
