Hallam family

The Hallams were a family of English actors who played a foundational role in the early history of theatre in colonial America. Led by Lewis Hallam (c. 1714–1756) and his wife, the actress Sarah Hallam, the London Company of Comedians arrived in Virginia in 1752 and gave some of the first professional theatrical performances in the Thirteen Colonies.

The American tours

Lewis Hallam, a former actor at London's Goodman's Fields Theatre, organised a company that opened in Williamsburg, Virginia, in September 1752 with The Merchant of Venice. The troupe toured Williamsburg, New York, Philadelphia and Charleston, performing the standard London repertory of Shakespeare and popular plays despite colonial anti-theatrical sentiment in some colonies. After Lewis Hallam's death in Jamaica, his widow married David Douglass, and the company, renamed the American Company, continued performing; her son by her first marriage, Lewis Hallam Jr. (1740–1808), became its leading actor.

Legacy

The Hallam company is regarded as the seed of the professional American theatre, establishing the touring circuit and repertory on which later companies built.
