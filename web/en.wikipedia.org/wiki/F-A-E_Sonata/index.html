Genre: Violin sonata
Composed: October 1853
Composers: Albert Dietrich, Robert Schumann, Johannes Brahms
Dedicatee: Joseph Joachim
Scoring: Violin and piano

F-A-E Sonata

The F-A-E Sonata is a four-movement sonata for violin and piano composed jointly in October 1853 by Albert Dietrich, Robert Schumann and the young Johannes Brahms. It was written as a gift for the violinist Joseph Joachim, who was expected to guess which composer had written each movement.

Background

The sonata is built around the musical motto F–A–E, standing for Joachim's personal motto "Frei aber einsam" ("free but lonely"). Dietrich wrote the opening Allegro in A minor, Schumann contributed an Intermezzo and the Finale, and Brahms composed the Scherzo in C minor — an early work (WoO 2) that became a favourite calling card for the twenty-year-old composer. Schumann later replaced the other two movements with his own, issuing the result as his Violin Sonata No. 3.

Movements

  1. Allegro (Dietrich)
  2. Intermezzo: Bewegt, doch nicht zu schnell (Schumann)
  3. Scherzo: Allegro (Brahms)
  4. Finale: Markiertes, ziemlich lebhaftes Tempo (Schumann)

The complete collaborative sonata was not published until 1935. Joachim identified the authors of all movements correctly at the Düsseldorf presentation.
