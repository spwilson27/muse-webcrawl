Strata Florida Abbey

Strata Florida Abbey (Welsh: Abaty Ystrad Fflur, "Vale of Flowers") is a former Cistercian abbey near Pontrhydfendigaid in Ceredigion, Wales. Founded in the twelfth century, it became one of the most important religious and cultural centres of medieval Wales — a place of pilgrimage, the reputed burial place of the poet Dafydd ap Gwilym, and a house closely associated with the native princes of Deheubarth, several of whom are buried there.

History

The abbey was founded in 1164 by the Norman lord Robert FitzStephen at a site a short distance from the present ruins, and the community moved to the current site around 1184. Under the patronage of Rhys ap Gruffydd, the Lord Rhys, Strata Florida grew rich on sheep farming and the wool trade, holding vast granges across mid Wales. A version of the Welsh chronicle Brut y Tywysogion (Chronicle of the Princes) is believed to have been compiled at the abbey. The house suffered in the wars of Edward I — it was burned in 1294–95 during the revolt of Madog ap Llywelyn, after which Edward contributed to rebuilding — and again during the Glyndŵr rising, when it was occupied by the forces of Henry IV.

Dissolution and remains

The abbey was dissolved in 1539 under Henry VIII, and its estates passed to lay owners; the site later came to the Powell family of Nanteos. The church was robbed for building stone, and today the ruins — in the care of Cadw — consist chiefly of the abbey church's plan, its celebrated carved west doorway, fragments of the cloister and chapter house, and the consecrated ground of the cemetery, traditionally containing the graves of eleven princes of Deheubarth and of Dafydd ap Gwilym, the greatest Welsh poet of the Middle Ages.
