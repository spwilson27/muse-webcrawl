Surgeon's knot

A surgeon's knot is a modification of the reef (square) knot used in surgery and in fly fishing. It consists of two overhand throws on the first pass instead of one, followed by a single overhand throw in the opposite direction on the second pass. The extra twist increases friction in the first throw, holding tension on the suture while the knot is completed — a valuable property when tying slippery monofilament or closing tissue under tension.

Use

In surgery, the surgeon's knot is a standard technique for ligating vessels and approximating wound edges, although its security depends on material and additional throws are often added. In fly fishing it is used to join lengths of leader and tippet of similar or differing diameters.
