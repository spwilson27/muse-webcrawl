George Joseph Stigler (17 January 1911 – 1 December 1991) was an American economist and a leading figure of the Chicago school of economics. He received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1982 for his studies of industrial structures, the functioning of markets, and the causes and effects of public regulation.

Life and career

Stigler studied at the University of Washington and Northwestern University before earning his PhD at the University of Chicago in 1938, where his teachers included Frank Knight and Jacob Viner. He taught at Iowa State, the University of Minnesota, Brown, and Columbia before returning to Chicago in 1958, where he remained for the rest of his career. At Minnesota he was a colleague and close friend of Milton Friedman, with whom he wrote a famous critique of rent control, "Roofs or Ceilings?" (1946).

Contributions

Stigler founded the modern economics of information with his 1961 paper "The Economics of Information", which analysed search costs in markets — for example, why prices for the same good differ — and made information itself an economic good. His 1971 paper "The Theory of Economic Regulation" proposed capture theory: regulation is typically acquired by the industry it governs and operated for its benefit, rather than for the public interest. He also edited the Journal of Political Economy, wrote a widely used textbook The Theory of Price, and produced influential histories of economic thought. His humorous "Stigler's law of eponymy" holds that no scientific discovery is named after its original discoverer.
