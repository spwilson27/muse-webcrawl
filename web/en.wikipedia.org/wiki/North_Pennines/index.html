North Pennines

The North Pennines is an upland area in northern England, forming the northern part of the Pennine chain between the Yorkshire Dales to the south and Hadrian's Wall country to the north. It spans parts of County Durham, Northumberland, Cumbria and North Yorkshire. Much of the area is designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty (AONB) and, since 2003, a UNESCO European and Global Geopark in recognition of its geological heritage.

Geology and landscape

The landscape is built of Carboniferous limestones, sandstones and shales, intruded by the dolerite of the Great Whin Sill, which forms dramatic outcrops such as High Cup Nick. The highest point is Cross Fell (893 m), the highest summit in England outside the Lake District. Rivers including the Tyne, Wear and Tees rise here; the Tees plunges over the Whin Sill at High Force, one of England's largest waterfalls.

History

The North Pennines were a major centre of lead mining from the eighteenth to the late nineteenth century, especially around Weardale, Teesdale and Alston, leaving a rich industrial archaeology of mines, smelt mills and miners' villages. Today the economy rests on sheep farming, grouse moor management and tourism.
