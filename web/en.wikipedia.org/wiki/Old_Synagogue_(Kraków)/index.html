The Old Synagogue (Polish: Synagoga Stara) is an Orthodox Jewish synagogue in the Kazimierz district of Kraków, Poland. Built in the 15th century, it is the oldest synagogue still standing in Poland and one of the most precious landmarks of Jewish architecture in Europe. It now serves as a division of the Historical Museum of Kraków devoted to the history and culture of Kraków's Jews.

History

The original two-nave Gothic hall was constructed in the 15th century, modelled in part on Prague's Old New Synagogue. After a fire in 1557, the Florentine architect Matteo Gucci rebuilt it in Renaissance style in 1570, preserving the characteristic twin-nave, rib-vaulted interior supported on two pillars. A women's prayer room and other annexes were added in the following centuries. During the Second World War the Nazis devastated the interior and used the building as a warehouse; its treasures were looted or destroyed. Restored in the late 1950s, the synagogue opened as a museum in 1958–1959.

Architecture and museum

The fortress-like exterior, with its crenellated attic rebuilt after wartime damage, reflects the defensive character of early synagogues. Inside, the museum displays Torah ornaments, textiles, ritual objects, and documentation of Kraków's Jewish community — once one of the largest in Europe — and of its destruction in the Holocaust, when most of Kazimierz's Jews were deported to the Kraków Ghetto and murdered at Płaszów, Auschwitz, and Bełżec.
