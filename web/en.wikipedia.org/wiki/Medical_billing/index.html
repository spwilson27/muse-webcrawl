Medical billing

Medical billing is the process of submitting and following up on claims with health insurance companies in order to receive payment for services rendered by a healthcare provider.

Process

The billing cycle generally begins when a patient registers and receives care. The provider documents the visit, and a coder assigns standardized codes: diagnosis codes from the ICD system and procedure codes from CPT or HCPCS. These codes, together with patient and provider identifiers, are assembled into a claim, most commonly the CMS-1500 form for professional services or the UB-04 for institutional services.

The claim is transmitted electronically to a clearinghouse, which checks it for errors and forwards it to the payer. The payer adjudicates the claim, determining what is covered under the patient's plan, and returns a remittance advice explaining what was paid, denied, or applied to the deductible. Any patient responsibility — copayments, coinsurance, or remaining deductible — is then billed to the patient.

Roles and systems

  • Medical coders translate clinical documentation into standardized codes.
  • Medical billers prepare and submit claims, track denials, and post payments.
  • Revenue cycle management covers the full cycle from scheduling and eligibility verification through final payment.

Common issues

Claims are frequently denied for reasons such as missing information, coding errors, lack of prior authorization, or services deemed not medically necessary. Denials are appealed or corrected and resubmitted. The complexity of the system, especially in the United States, has made medical billing a significant administrative cost for providers and a source of patient confusion over bills, with phenomena such as balance billing and surprise billing attracting regulatory attention.
