Manuel Azaña Díaz (1880–1940) was a Spanish politician, writer and journalist who served as Prime Minister of the Second Spanish Republic and later as its second and last President, during the Spanish Civil War.

Career

Azaña came to prominence as minister of war and then prime minister in the reformist first biennium of the Republic (1931–1933), driving military reform, secularisation, Catalan autonomy and agrarian changes that sharply polarised Spanish politics. After the Popular Front victory of February 1936 he returned as prime minister, and in May 1936 succeeded Niceto Alcalá-Zamora as President of the Republic.

Civil War and exile

During the Civil War Azaña remained head of state but with diminishing real power, based successively in Valencia and Barcelona. As Republican defeat became certain he crossed into France in February 1939 and resigned the presidency. He died in exile at Montauban in November 1940, and was buried there under a Mexican flag, the Spanish Republic's flag being forbidden by the Vichy authorities.
