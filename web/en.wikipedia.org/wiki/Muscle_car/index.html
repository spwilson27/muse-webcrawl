Muscle car

Muscle car is a term for a category of high-performance American automobiles, typically rear-wheel-drive coupes fitted with large, powerful V8 engines and sold at relatively affordable prices. The classic muscle car era ran from the mid-1960s to the early 1970s, beginning, by most accounts, with the 1964 Pontiac GTO, which put a big-car engine in an intermediate-size body.

History

Antecedents include the 1949 Oldsmobile Rocket 88 and the high-performance models of the 1950s. After the GTO's success, rival manufacturers answered with cars such as the Ford Mustang (a smaller "pony car"), Chevrolet Chevelle SS and Camaro, Dodge Charger and Challenger, and Plymouth Road Runner. Competition escalated into a horsepower war, with special drag-racing packages and limited-production models.

The era waned in the early 1970s under rising insurance rates, emissions regulations, and the 1973 oil crisis. The nameplate tradition revived in the 21st century with retro-styled, high-horsepower versions of the Mustang, Camaro, and Challenger, some producing well over 700 horsepower.

Characteristics

Muscle cars prioritize straight-line acceleration and aggressive styling over handling finesse, though modern examples incorporate sophisticated suspension and electronics. They occupy a distinct place in American car culture, celebrated at drag strips, cruise nights, and collector auctions.
