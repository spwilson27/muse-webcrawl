Parallel key

In music, a parallel key is the major or minor scale that shares the same tonic (keynote) as another key. For example, C major and C minor are parallel keys, as are G major and G minor. Parallel keys are distinguished from relative keys, which share the same key signature but have different tonics (such as C major and A minor).

Characteristics

Moving from a major key to its parallel minor changes the third, sixth and seventh scale degrees, which are lowered by a semitone. This gives the parallel minor its characteristically darker sound while retaining the same tonal centre. In chord notation, the parallel minor of a major key is sometimes indicated with a lowercase tonic.

Use in composition

Composers frequently borrow chords from the parallel key — a practice known as modal mixture or borrowed chords — such as using a minor iv chord or a flat-VI chord in a major key. Whole sections of a piece may also shift to the parallel key for contrast, a device common in classical sonata movements and in popular music alike.
