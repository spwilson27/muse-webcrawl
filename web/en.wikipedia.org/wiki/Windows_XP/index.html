Windows XP is an operating system produced by Microsoft as part of the Windows NT family. It was released to manufacturing on August 24, 2001, and to retail on October 25, 2001. The name "XP" derives from the word experience. Windows XP merged Microsoft's consumer (Windows 9x/Me) and business (Windows NT/2000) product lines onto the NT kernel, and introduced the colourful "Luna" visual style and a redesigned Start menu. Its default wallpaper, Bliss—a photograph of a green hill in Sonoma County, California—is often called the most viewed image in the world.

Development and editions

Codenamed "Whistler", XP was developed from the cancelled Neptune and Odyssey projects. It shipped in editions including Home Edition and Professional, later joined by Media Center Edition, Tablet PC Edition, and the 64-bit editions. Windows XP required product activation, a then-controversial anti-piracy measure.

Service packs and security

Three service packs were released (2002, 2004, 2008). Service Pack 2 (2004), developed under Microsoft's Trustworthy Computing initiative after damaging worm outbreaks such as Blaster and Sasser, enabled the firewall by default and added the Security Center and Data Execution Prevention support. Internet Explorer 6, bundled with XP, was widely criticized for security flaws but retained enormous market share for years.

Reception and longevity

Windows XP was a major commercial success and was praised for its stability and usability relative to Windows Me. It remained the world's most-used desktop operating system until about 2012, when it was overtaken by Windows 7. Mainstream support ended in 2009 and extended support on April 8, 2014, yet the system lingered in ATMs, medical devices, industrial systems, and some government computers—an enduring problem highlighted when the WannaCry ransomware attack of 2017 forced Microsoft to issue an exceptional out-of-band patch for XP.
