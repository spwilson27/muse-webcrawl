Ulysses (spacecraft)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ulysses (spacecraft)
OperatorESA / NASA
Launch6 October 1990 (STS-41)
MissionFirst probe over the Sun's poles
End30 June 2009
Ulysses over the solar pole (artist's concept).

Ulysses was a robotic space probe built by the European Space Agency and launched by NASA in 1990 to study the Sun from a unique vantage: a polar orbit that took it over the Sun's north and south poles, regions never before observed directly. A Gravity assist at Jupiter in 1992 flung it out of the ecliptic plane into its 6.2-year solar orbit.

For over 18 years — nearly four times its design life — Ulysses measured the solar wind, magnetic field, and cosmic rays from all latitudes, discovering among other things that the solar wind's behaviour over the poles differs fundamentally between solar minimum and maximum. It survived three passes over each pole, plus an unplanned comet-tail crossing.

End of mission

Power from its decaying nuclear generator slowly starved the craft; when its hydrazine fuel lines threatened to freeze, controllers shut Ulysses down on 30 June 2009. The mission remains the only one to have surveyed the heliosphere from above and below, and its dataset is the reference for the Sun's three-dimensional environment.
