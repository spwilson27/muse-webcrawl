National Police Corps

The National Police Corps (Dutch: Korps Nationale Politie), commonly known simply as the Dutch Police (Politie), is the national police force of the Netherlands. It was formed on 1 January 2013, when the country's 25 regional police forces and the national police services agency were merged into a single organisation under the Police Act 2012. The force employs roughly 63,000 people, of whom about 40,000 are sworn officers, and is headed by a Commissioner (chief constable) answerable to the Minister of Justice and Security.

History

Before 2013 policing in the Netherlands was divided between 25 regional forces, each governed jointly by the region's mayors and public prosecutor, and a national agency providing specialist services such as motorway patrols, railway police and aviation support. Critics argued the structure was fragmented and inefficient. The Police Act 2012, pushed through under Minister Ivo Opstelten, created a single national force despite concerns from mayors about losing local control. The first chief commissioner was Gerard Bouman (2013–2016), followed by Erik Akerboom and, from 2020, Henk van Essen.

Organisation

The force is organised into ten regional units (eenheden) — including Amsterdam, Rotterdam, The Hague, East Netherlands and North Netherlands — a national unit responsible for specialist and cross-border operations, and a police services centre handling support functions. Within districts, basic police teams provide neighbourhood policing through community officers (wijkagenten). Specialist national capabilities include the Dienst Speciale Interventies (DSI) for counter-terrorism and high-risk arrests, the National Criminal Investigation Division, and the Netherlands Forensic Institute partners closely with the police on forensic work.

Roles and powers

Dutch police are responsible for maintaining public order, enforcing the law, investigating crime and assisting the public. Officers are generally armed with a service pistol (the Walther P99Q NL), baton and pepper spray, and a growing number carry conducted energy devices. Day-to-day authority over deployments rests with the local "triangle" of mayor, chief public prosecutor and police unit chief. The Royal Marechaussee, a separate gendarmerie branch of the armed forces, retains distinct duties such as border security and policing Schiphol Airport.
