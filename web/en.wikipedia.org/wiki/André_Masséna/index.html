André Masséna, Prince of Essling, Duke of Rivoli (born Andrea Massena; 6 May 1758 – 4 April 1817) was a French military commander during the French Revolutionary Wars and the Napoleonic Wars. He was one of the original 18 Marshals of the Empire created by Napoleon in 1804, and was nicknamed l'Enfant chéri de la Victoire ("the Dear Child of Victory").

Career

Born in Nice to a family of Italian origin, Masséna served as a common soldier in the Royal Italian Regiment before the Revolution and rose rapidly thereafter. He distinguished himself under Napoleon Bonaparte in the Italian campaign of 1796–97, notably at Rivoli, from which his ducal title derives. In 1799 his victory at the Second Battle of Zurich saved France from invasion and is considered one of the greatest defensive campaigns of the era.

Created marshal in 1804 and Prince of Essling after the campaigns of 1809 (Aspern-Essling and Wagram), he commanded the Army of Portugal in 1810–11 but was checked by Wellington behind the Lines of Torres Vedras and defeated at Fuentes de Oñoro, after which Napoleon relieved him of command. He held no major field command afterwards, governed Marseille during the Hundred Days, and died in Paris in 1817. Many historians rank him second only to Napoleon among French commanders of the period.
