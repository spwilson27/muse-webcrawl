Civics

Civics is the study of the rights and obligations of citizens in society. The term derives from the Latin word civicus, meaning "relating to a citizen". Civic education is the study of the theoretical, political and practical aspects of citizenship, as well as its rights and duties; the duties of citizens to each other as members of a political body and to the government.

Content

Civics education typically covers the structure and functions of government, constitutions and the rule of law, voting and elections, civil rights and civil liberties, and the responsibilities of citizenship such as jury service, taxation, and public participation. In many countries civics is a school subject intended to prepare young people for democratic participation.

Debates

Scholars debate how civics should be taught — whether it should emphasise knowledge of institutions, civic skills and participation, or critical engagement with social issues. Declines in civic knowledge and voter turnout in several democracies have prompted renewed investment in civic education and research on effective teaching methods.
