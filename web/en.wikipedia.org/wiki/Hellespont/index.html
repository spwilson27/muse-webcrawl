The Hellespont is the ancient Greek name for the narrow strait in northwestern Turkey now known as the Dardanelles, connecting the Aegean Sea to the Sea of Marmara and, via the Bosphorus, to the Black Sea. Together these Turkish Straits separate Europe from Asia and form the only sea route between the Black Sea and the Mediterranean.

Name and myth

Greek myth derived the name from Helle, who fell from the flying golden ram while escaping with her brother Phrixus — the sea where she drowned. The strait is also the setting of the myth of Hero and Leander: Leander swam nightly across to Hero at Sestos until he drowned in a storm; Lord Byron famously repeated the swim in 1810.

History

The Hellespont was a strategic crossroads in antiquity: Troy guarded its Aegean entrance, Xerxes I crossed it in 480 BC on twin pontoon bridges to invade Greece, and Alexander the Great crossed in the opposite direction in 334 BC. In the First World War the strait was the objective of the 1915 Gallipoli campaign and the failed Allied naval attack at the Battle of Çanakkale. Passage is now governed by the Montreux Convention (1936). Since 2022 the 1915 Çanakkale Bridge — the world's longest suspension bridge — has spanned the strait near its western end.
