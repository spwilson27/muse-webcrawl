Johnny B. Goode

"Johnny B. Goode" is a 1958 rock and roll song written and first recorded by Chuck Berry. Released as a single by Chess Records, it reached No. 2 on Billboard's Hot R&B Sides and No. 8 on the Hot 100. The song, about a poor country boy who plays guitar "just like ringing a bell", is one of the most recognised and covered songs in popular music, and Berry's recording is on the Voyager Golden Record.

Composition and recording

Berry wrote the semi-autobiographical song — the opening guitar riff adapted from Louis Jordan's "Ain't That Just Like a Woman" (1946), played by Carl Hogan. Pianist Johnnie Johnson inspired the name. Recorded in Chicago in January 1958 with Lafayette Leake/Johnnie Johnson on piano, Willie Dixon on bass, and Fred Below on drums, it became Berry's signature.

Legacy

Covered by Jimi Hendrix, the Beatles, Elvis Presley, the Grateful Dead, and many others, and famously performed by Michael J. Fox in Back to the Future (1985). It ranks near the top of Rolling Stone's greatest songs lists, was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame, and was selected for the Voyager Golden Record as an example of Earth's music.
