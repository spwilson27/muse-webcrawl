Vorticity equation

The vorticity equation is an equation of fluid dynamics that describes the evolution of the vorticity ω of a fluid particle as it moves through the flow. Vorticity, defined as the curl of the velocity field, ω = ∇ × v, measures the local rotation of the fluid. The equation is obtained by taking the curl of the momentum (Navier–Stokes) equation.

The equation

For a compressible fluid, the equation takes the form:

Dω/Dt = (ω · ∇)v − ω(∇ · v) + (∇ρ × ∇p)/ρ² + ∇ × (F/ρ) + ν∇²ω

where D/Dt is the material derivative, ρ is density, p is pressure, ν is kinematic viscosity, and F represents body forces such as gravity per unit volume.

Physical interpretation of the terms

  • Vortex stretching and tilting, (ω · ∇)v — in three dimensions, stretching of vortex lines amplifies vorticity; this term is absent in two-dimensional flow and is central to the energy cascade in turbulence.
  • Compression, −ω(∇ · v) — converging flow concentrates vorticity, as when a spinning figure skater pulls in her arms; it vanishes for incompressible flow.
  • Baroclinic torque, (∇ρ × ∇p)/ρ² — where surfaces of constant density and pressure are misaligned, vorticity is generated from nothing; this drives sea breezes and much atmospheric motion. It vanishes in barotropic flow.
  • Viscous diffusion, ν∇²ω — viscosity diffuses vorticity from regions of high to low concentration, and at boundaries it is the ultimate source of vorticity in flows started from rest.

Applications

The vorticity equation is fundamental in meteorology, where its vertical component (in quasi-geostrophic form) underlies numerical weather prediction, and in aerodynamics and turbulence theory. For inviscid, barotropic flow with conservative body forces it reduces to the statement that vorticity is "frozen into" the fluid, yielding Kelvin's circulation theorem and the conservation of potential vorticity.
