Akhtamar (Turkish: Akdamar; Armenian: Աղթամար, Aghtamar) is the second largest of four islands in Lake Van, in eastern Turkey. The island is famous for the Armenian Cathedral of the Holy Cross, built in the tenth century, and its name is attached to a well-known Armenian legend of star-crossed lovers.

The legend

According to legend, a youth named Tamar lived on the island, and a young man from the mainland swam nightly across the lake guided by a light she lit for him. Her father, discovering the affair, shattered the lamp one stormy night; the swimmer drowned crying "Akh, Tamar!" ("Oh, Tamar!") — giving the island its name.

Cathedral of the Holy Cross

The church was built between 915 and 921 by the architect-monk Manuel for King Gagik I Artsruni of Vaspurakan. Built of pink tuff, it is celebrated for the extensive band of relief carvings around its exterior depicting biblical scenes, saints, and court life — among the masterpieces of medieval Armenian art. The island was the seat of the Armenian Catholicosate of Aghtamar from 1113 until the early twentieth century.

Abandoned after 1915, the church was restored by the Turkish government and reopened as a museum in 2007; an annual liturgy has been permitted since 2010.
