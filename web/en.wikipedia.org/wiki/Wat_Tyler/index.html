Wat Tyler

Walter "Wat" Tyler (c. 1341 – 15 June 1381) was a leader of the English Peasants' Revolt of 1381. He led the Kentish rebels to London and was killed during negotiations with King Richard II at Smithfield.

The revolt

Tyler emerged as leader of the Kentish rebels after the imposition of a third poll tax provoked uprisings in Essex and Kent in May–June 1381. The rebels marched on London, executed unpopular officials including Archbishop Simon Sudbury and treasurer Robert Hales, and forced the young Richard II to meet them at Mile End, where he conceded many demands. The following day, at Smithfield, a second meeting turned violent: Tyler was struck down — by the Lord Mayor William Walworth in most accounts — and died shortly afterwards. Leaderless, the revolt collapsed, and the king's concessions were revoked.

Legacy

Tyler became a folk hero for later radicals; his name is commemorated in places such as Wat Tyler Country Park in Essex.
