Are You Being Served? is a British television sitcom that was first broadcast on BBC1 from 1972 to 1985. Created and written by Jeremy Lloyd and David Croft, it is set in the ladies' and gentlemen's clothing departments of Grace Brothers, a fictional London department store, and follows the comic interactions of its staff.

The clothing department of Grace Brothers

Premise and characters

The staff of the clothing department include the formidable Mrs Slocombe (Mollie Sugden), senior assistant in ladies' wear, famous for her ever-changing hair colour and references to her cat; the effeminate Mr Humphries (John Inman), whose catchphrase "I'm free!" became widely known; Captain Peacock (Frank Thornton), the pompous floor walker; Miss Brahms (Wendy Richard); and Mr Rumbold (Nicholas Smith), the jug-eared department manager. The store's owner, Young Mr Grace, appears in early series with the catchphrase "You've all done very well!"

Production and popularity

The series drew on Jeremy Lloyd's experience working in a department store. It became one of the BBC's most popular comedies, noted for its double entendres and innuendo-laden humour. The show was sold internationally and became especially popular in the United States through PBS broadcasts.

Film, spin-offs and remakes

A film version was released in 1977, sending the staff on holiday to the fictional Costa Plonka. A sequel series, Grace & Favour (also known as Are You Being Served? Again!), aired in 1992–1993. An Australian version, remade using scripts from the original, aired in 1980–1981. A one-off revival episode was broadcast in 2016 as part of the BBC's Landmark Sitcom Season.
