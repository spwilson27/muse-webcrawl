Tonyukuk

Tonyukuk (Old Turkic: 𐱃𐰆𐰪𐰸𐰸; c. 646 – c. 726) was a strategist and commander of the Second Turkic Khaganate, serving four khagans. He is credited with masterminding the restoration of Turkic power in the Mongolian steppe.

Born among the Ashide clan and educated partly in Tang China, Tonyukuk became the chief counsellor of Elteriš (Ilterish) Qaghan, who founded the Second Turkic Khaganate in 682. Tonyukuk directed campaigns that subdued rival tribes and defeated Chinese armies, and he cautioned the khagans against becoming dependent on Chinese wealth and city life, arguing that the Turks' strength lay in their mobile way of life.

Inscriptions

The Bain Tsokto inscriptions, erected near the Tuul River in Mongolia around 720–725, record Tonyukuk's own account of his life and campaigns. Together with the Orkhon inscriptions for Kül Tigin and Bilge Khagan, they are among the oldest surviving texts in a Turkic language and a key source for early Turkic history.
