Hesychius of Alexandria

Hesychius of Alexandria (Ancient Greek: Ἡσύχιος ὁ Ἀλεξανδρεύς) was a Greek grammarian and lexicographer who probably lived during the 5th or 6th century AD. He compiled a rich Greek lexicon, the Synagoge Pasōn Lexeōn kata Stoicheion ("Alphabetical collection of all words"), which survives in a single 15th-century manuscript.

The Lexicon

The lexicon contains roughly 51,000 entries: rare and obscure words, many from classical authors, forms from Greek dialects, words from Jewish and Christian scriptures, and explanations of phrases and proverbs. It is an invaluable source for modern scholars of Ancient Greek philology because it preserves readings and glosses from lost works, and it documents words from dialects such as Cypriot, Cretan and Laconic that are otherwise poorly attested.

The surviving manuscript (Marcianus Graecus 622) was printed at Venice in 1514 by Marcus Musurus in the Aldine edition. Modern critical editions include those of Kurt Latte (covering alpha to omicron) and Peter Allan Hansen and Ian Cunningham.
