Characteristic polynomial

Characteristic polynomial
FieldLinear algebra
DefinitionpA(λ) = det(λI − A)
RootsEigenvalues of A

In linear algebra, the characteristic polynomial of a square matrix A is a polynomial which is invariant under matrix similarity and has the eigenvalues of A as roots. It is defined as pA(λ) = det(λI − A), where I is the identity matrix and det denotes the determinant. The polynomial's degree equals the size of the matrix, and its coefficients encode information such as the trace (sum of diagonal entries, giving the negative of the coefficient of λn−1) and the determinant (the constant term, up to sign).

Properties

By the Cayley–Hamilton theorem, a matrix satisfies its own characteristic polynomial: pA(A) = 0. Similar matrices share the same characteristic polynomial, though the converse is false (matrices can share the polynomial without being similar; the minimal polynomial gives finer information). The characteristic polynomial factors over an algebraically closed field into linear factors whose roots are the eigenvalues, with multiplicities equal to the algebraic multiplicities of those eigenvalues.

Generalizations

The concept extends to linear operators on finite-dimensional vector spaces (independent of the basis chosen), to the characteristic polynomial of a graph (of its adjacency matrix), and to recurrence relations, where the characteristic polynomial determines closed-form solutions of linear recurrences.
