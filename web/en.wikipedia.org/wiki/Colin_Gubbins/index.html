Colin Gubbins

Major-General Sir Colin McVean Gubbins (1896–1976), head of SOE from 1943.

Major-General Sir Colin McVean Gubbins KCMG, DSO, MC (2 July 1896 – 11 February 1976) was a British Army officer who was the driving force and, from 1943, chief of the Special Operations Executive (SOE), the Second World War organisation charged with espionage, sabotage and support to resistance movements in occupied Europe—Winston Churchill's directive to "set Europe ablaze".

Early career

Born in Japan, where his father served in the consular service, Gubbins was commissioned into the Royal Artillery and served on the Western Front in the First World War, winning the Military Cross. Between the wars he studied guerrilla warfare at first hand, observing the Irish War of Independence, the Russian Civil War and conflicts in India, and wrote influential manuals on partisan warfare and resistance to occupation.

Second World War

In 1939–40 Gubbins organised the Independent Companies (forerunners of the Commandos) for Norway and then the Auxiliary Units, a stay-behind resistance network for a German-invaded Britain. Joining SOE at its creation in 1940 as director of operations and training, he codenamed himself "M" and built its agent-training, supply and country sections; he became executive head in September 1943 and led SOE until its dissolution in 1946.

Later life

After the war Gubbins worked in industry and remained an elder statesman of the special-operations community. He was knighted for his wartime service and died in the Hebrides in 1976.
