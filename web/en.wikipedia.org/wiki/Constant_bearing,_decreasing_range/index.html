Constant bearing, decreasing range

For the related risk-avoidance doctrine, see collision avoidance.

Constant bearing, decreasing range (CBDR) is a term in navigation for a situation in which another vessel (or aircraft, or other vehicle) is maintaining the same relative bearing to one's own vessel, but the distance (range) between them is decreasing. Under these conditions, if neither vessel changes course or speed, a collision is likely to occur. CBDR is a cornerstone of radar-based collision avoidance and of the "rules of the road" at sea.

Detection

Mariners and watch officers can detect CBDR by taking successive bearings of an approaching vessel with a hand-bearing compass or by watching compass points on a radar display. If the bearing to the other vessel stays the same while its distance closes, the aspect of the two vessels is on a collision course. Even without instruments, an observer can use a fixed reference point: if another vessel appears to hang against a fixed point in the background but grows larger, the vessels may be on a collision course.

Application

The concept is taught to navigators, pilots, and drone operators as a quick mental check for collision risk. In maritime practice it underpins the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs), which state that risk of collision shall be deemed to exist when the compass bearing of an approaching vessel does not appreciably change. In aviation, pilots are trained that a target aircraft which remains fixed in the windshield and grows in size is on a collision course.
