Ford Australia (formally the Ford Motor Company of Australia Pty Ltd) is the Australian subsidiary of the American Ford Motor Company. Founded in 1925, it designed and manufactured vehicles in Australia for more than ninety years, most famously the Falcon family car, before ending local production in 2016 and becoming a full-line importer.

History

Ford's Australian operation began in Geelong, Victoria, in 1925, assembling Model Ts from imported kits. It went on to build its own body plant and, during the Second World War, produced military vehicles and aircraft engines. In 1934 Ford Australia engineer Lewis Bandt created the coupe utility — the "ute" — a uniquely Australian vehicle type combining a car cabin with a cargo tray. The Falcon, launched in 1960 as a local version of the American compact, became an Australian icon; from the 1972 XA model it was designed and engineered entirely in Australia. Its decades-long rivalry with the Holden Commodore defined the local car market and the V8 Supercars racing series.

End of manufacturing

Declining sales of large Australian-built sedans and the high cost of local production led Ford to announce in May 2013 that it would close its Broadmeadows assembly plant and Geelong engine plant. The last Australian-built Falcon rolled off the line on 7 October 2016, ending 91 years of Ford manufacturing in Australia; Holden and Toyota followed in 2017, closing the Australian car industry. Ford Australia continues as a sales, engineering and design operation — its Australian design centre developed the Ranger pickup and Everest SUV for global markets — and employs several thousand people in Victoria.

Models

Notable Australian Ford models include the Falcon (1960–2016) and its derivatives the Fairmont, Fairlane and LTD; the Territory SUV (2004–2016); the Cortina and Escort assembled from British designs; and the long-running ute line. Imported models such as the Ranger, Mustang, Focus and Fiesta have since formed the core range.
