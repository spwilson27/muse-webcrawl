Heavy-tailed distribution

A heavy-tailed distribution is a probability distribution whose tail decays more slowly than any exponential.

In probability theory, heavy-tailed distributions are probability distributions whose tails are not exponentially bounded: they decay more slowly than the exponential distribution, so extreme values occur with non-negligible probability. Formally, a distribution of a random variable X is heavy-tailed if the tail probability P(X > x) decays slower than exp(−ax) for every a > 0 as x grows.

Classes and examples

An important subclass is the subexponential distributions, in which the tail of a sum of independent variables is dominated by the largest single value — the "one big jump" principle. Classic heavy-tailed families include the Pareto distribution and power laws, the Cauchy distribution, Lévy stable distributions with index below 2, the log-normal and heavy-tailed Weibull distributions. Fat tails (positive excess kurtosis) are related but not identical: a fat-tailed distribution may still decay exponentially.

Applications

Heavy tails appear in insurance claims and reinsurance pricing, finance (asset returns show far more large moves than the normal distribution predicts), internet traffic, file sizes, city sizes, and earthquake magnitudes. Modelling such data with light-tailed assumptions dramatically underestimates risk; moment-based methods may fail entirely when variance or even the mean is infinite, as with the Cauchy distribution.
