Cayley's formula

In mathematics, Cayley's formula states that the number of trees on n labelled vertices is nn−2. Equivalently, it counts the spanning trees of a complete graph with labelled vertices. The result is named after Arthur Cayley, who published it in 1889, though equivalent statements were known earlier through the work of Carl Wilhelm Borchardt.

Proofs

Many proofs are known. A particularly elegant one uses Prüfer sequences, which establish a bijection between labelled trees on n vertices and sequences of length n−2 drawn from the vertex labels; since there are nn−2 such sequences, the formula follows. Other proofs use Kirchhoff's matrix tree theorem, recurrence relations, and double counting.

Generalizations

The formula generalizes to counting spanning forests with specified root sets (Cayley's multinomial formula) and to complete multipartite graphs.
