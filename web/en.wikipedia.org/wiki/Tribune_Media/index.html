Tribune Media Company, also known as Tribune Media, was an American multimedia conglomerate headquartered in Chicago, Illinois. It was the successor of the Tribune Company, one of the oldest media businesses in the United States, founded with the Chicago Tribune newspaper in 1847. After emerging from a lengthy bankruptcy and spinning off its publishing division as Tribune Publishing in 2014, the company focused on television broadcasting, operating 42 owned-or-operated television stations, the cable channel WGN America, and digital and real-estate interests.

History

The Tribune Company grew from the Chicago Daily Tribune, founded in 1847, and under Joseph Medill and later Robert R. McCormick became a politically influential conservative newspaper empire. In the twentieth century it expanded into broadcasting with WGN radio (1924) and WGN-TV (1948) — the call letters stood for "World's Greatest Newspaper" — and acquired the Los Angeles Times, Newsday, and other papers. In 2007, real-estate investor Sam Zell took the company private in a leveraged buyout that loaded it with debt; Tribune filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in December 2008, one of the largest media bankruptcies in American history.

The company emerged from bankruptcy at the end of 2012 under the control of its creditors. In 2013 it bought Local TV Holdings' 19 stations, and in August 2014 it spun off its newspapers into a separate company, Tribune Publishing (later Tronc), retaining the broadcasting assets under the name Tribune Media.

Sale to Nexstar

In May 2017, Sinclair Broadcast Group agreed to acquire Tribune Media for about $3.9 billion, a merger that would have created the largest local-television group in the United States. The deal collapsed in 2018 amid regulatory problems at the FCC, and Tribune sued Sinclair for breach of contract. In December 2018, Nexstar Media Group agreed to buy Tribune for about $6.4 billion including debt assumption; the acquisition closed on September 19, 2019, making Nexstar the largest U.S. television station operator and ending the Tribune name as an independent company.

Assets

At its dissolution Tribune Media's flagship assets included Chicago's WGN-TV and WGN America (since rebranded NewsNation by Nexstar), KTLA in Los Angeles, WPIX in New York, and stations in most major American markets, many affiliated with The CW — a network in which Tribune had also held an interest through its earlier ownership of The WB.
