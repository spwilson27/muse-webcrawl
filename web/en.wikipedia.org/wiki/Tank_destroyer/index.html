Tank destroyer

A tank destroyer, also known as a tank hunter or (in German service) Panzerjäger, is a type of armoured fighting vehicle designed specifically for anti-tank warfare. Tank destroyers typically carry a high-velocity anti-tank gun, often in a casemate or open-topped turret, and sacrifice the all-round capability and protection of a main battle tank for firepower and, often, lower cost or greater speed.

History

The concept flourished during the Second World War. The United States developed turreted, lightly armoured tank destroyers such as the M10, M18 Hellcat, and M36, under a doctrine that envisaged them massing against enemy armoured breakthroughs. Germany produced both turretless assault-gun-derived vehicles (Jagdpanzer IV, Jagdpanther, Jagdtiger) and improvised open designs (Marder series, Nashorn), while the Soviet Union fielded casemate vehicles such as the SU-85, SU-100, and ISU-152. After the war, the spread of guided anti-tank missiles and the universality of the main battle tank rendered the dedicated gun-armed tank destroyer largely obsolete, though wheeled missile carriers and gun-armed tank destroyers remain in some armies.
