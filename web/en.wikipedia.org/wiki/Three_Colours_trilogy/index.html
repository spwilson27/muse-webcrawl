The Three Colours trilogy (French: Trois couleurs; Polish: Trzy kolory) is a series of three films directed by Polish filmmaker Krzysztof Kieślowski: Three Colours: Blue (1993), Three Colours: White (1994), and Three Colours: Red (1994). The trilogy, a French-Polish-Swiss co-production, takes its titles from the colours of the French flag, and each film explores one of the ideals of the French Republic: liberty, equality, and fraternity.

The trilogy's three colours correspond to liberty, equality, and fraternity.

Blue follows Julie, a woman who loses her composer husband and daughter in a car accident and attempts to free herself from all emotional ties. White, a dark comedy, tells of Karol, a Polish hairdresser divorced by his French wife, who plots an elaborate revenge after returning to Poland. Red centres on Valentine, a young model in Geneva, and her friendship with a retired judge who eavesdrops on his neighbours' telephone calls.

Connections and reception

The films are linked by shared characters, repeated motifs, and a celebrated concluding sequence in Red that brings characters from all three films together. Blue won the Golden Lion at Venice; White won the Silver Bear for Best Director at Berlin; Red was nominated for Academy Awards for Best Director, Best Original Screenplay, and Best Cinematography, and is frequently ranked among the greatest films ever made. Kieślowski announced his retirement after the trilogy and died in 1996.
