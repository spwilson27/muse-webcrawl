Long non-coding RNA

Long non-coding RNA (lncRNA)
DefinitionRNA transcript longer than ~200 nucleotides not translated into protein
Number in humansTens of thousands of loci (estimates vary)
Well-known examplesXIST, HOTAIR, MALAT1, H19

Long non-coding RNAs (long ncRNAs, lncRNA) are a large and diverse class of RNA molecules, defined as transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides that are not translated into protein. Like messenger RNAs, most lncRNAs are transcribed by RNA polymerase II, capped, spliced, and polyadenylated, but they tend to be expressed at lower levels and in more tissue-specific patterns. Tens of thousands of lncRNA genes have been annotated in the human genome, though the fraction with demonstrated function is debated.

Functions

Known lncRNAs act through diverse mechanisms: as signals, decoys, guides, and scaffolds for proteins and chromatin. The paradigmatic example is XIST, which coats the inactive X chromosome and silences it in female mammals. HOTAIR recruits chromatin-modifying complexes (PRC2, LSD1) to silence target loci such as HOXD, while others regulate gene expression in cis or trans, modulate splicing, or organise nuclear structures such as paraspeckles (NEAT1).

Disease

Dysregulation of lncRNAs has been implicated in cancers and neurological disorders, and some, such as MALAT1 and the imprinted H19, are studied as biomarkers and therapeutic targets.
