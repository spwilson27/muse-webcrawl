Bupropion, sold under the brand names Wellbutrin and Zyban among others, is an atypical antidepressant medication. It is used primarily to treat major depressive disorder and to support smoking cessation. Unlike most antidepressants, bupropion does not act on serotonin; instead it is a norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor (NDRI) and a nicotinic acetylcholine receptor antagonist. Consequently it does not usually cause the sexual dysfunction or weight gain associated with selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, and it is often mildly weight-reducing.

Medical uses

Bupropion is approved for major depressive disorder and for prevention of seasonal affective disorder, and (as Zyban) as an aid to smoking cessation, where it reduces cravings and withdrawal symptoms. It is also used off-label for attention deficit hyperactivity disorder and as an augmenting agent in depression. A combination product with naltrexone is marketed for weight management in some countries.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include dry mouth, insomnia, headache, nausea, and agitation. The most important risk is a dose-dependent increase in seizure incidence, which led to the drug's temporary withdrawal from the US market in the 1980s before its reintroduction with lower maximum doses; it is contraindicated in people with seizure disorders and in those with current or prior anorexia or bulimia nervosa, in whom seizures have been more frequent. Bupropion can raise blood pressure and should be used cautiously with other stimulant-like agents. It is a strong inhibitor of the liver enzyme CYP2D6, creating interactions with many other drugs.

Pharmacology

Bupropion is an aminoketone (a substituted cathinone derivative) chemically related to amphetamine-class stimulants but without significant direct releasing action at usual doses. It is metabolised in the liver mainly by CYP2B6 to hydroxybupropion, an active metabolite that contributes substantially to its effects. Its antagonism at nicotinic receptors underlies its usefulness in nicotine dependence.

History

Bupropion was invented by chemist Nariman Mehta at Burroughs Wellcome and patented in 1974. It was first approved in the United States in 1985, withdrawn in 1986 over seizure concerns, and reintroduced in 1989 with a lower maximum recommended dose. Sustained- and extended-release formulations followed, and it became one of the most widely prescribed antidepressants in the United States.
