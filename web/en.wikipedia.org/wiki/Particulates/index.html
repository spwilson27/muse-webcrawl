Particulates

Particulates, also known as particulate matter (PM), atmospheric aerosol particles, or suspended particulate matter, are microscopic particles of solid or liquid matter suspended in a gas, chiefly the air. They range in size from visible dust down to clusters of molecules, and are among the most health-relevant components of air pollution worldwide.

Size classes

Size categories
PM10Particles ≤ 10 µm — inhalable coarse particles (dust, pollen, mould)
PM2.5Particles ≤ 2.5 µm — fine particles from combustion
PM0.1Ultrafine particles ≤ 0.1 µm

Regulators classify particulates by aerodynamic diameter. Coarse particles (PM10) are trapped mainly in the upper airways; fine particles (PM2.5) penetrate deep into the lungs and alveoli; ultrafine particles may pass into the bloodstream. For scale, a human hair is about 70 µm wide and a grain of beach sand about 90 µm.

Sources

Natural sources include wind-blown dust, sea salt, wildfires, pollen, and volcanic eruptions. Human sources are dominated by combustion: vehicle exhaust (especially diesel), coal and biomass burning, residential wood heating, industrial processes, construction, and agriculture. Many particles are secondary — formed in the atmosphere when sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, ammonia, and volatile organic compounds react to form sulphates, nitrates, and organic aerosols.

Health and environmental effects

Epidemiological studies consistently associate PM exposure with aggravated asthma, reduced lung function, cardiovascular disease, and premature mortality; fine particulate pollution is estimated by the World Health Organization to contribute to millions of premature deaths annually. Particulates also scatter and absorb sunlight, affecting climate directly and by altering clouds, and they reduce visibility, producing haze.

Regulation

The United States regulates PM under the Clean Air Act's National Ambient Air Quality Standards; the European Union sets limit values in its air-quality directives; and the WHO issues guideline levels that many countries have not yet met. Controls include filters and scrubbers, cleaner fuels, dust suppression, and emission standards for engines.
