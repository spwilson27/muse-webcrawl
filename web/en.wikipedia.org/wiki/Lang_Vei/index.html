Lang Vei (Vietnamese: Làng Vây) was a United States Army Special Forces camp in northwestern Quảng Trị Province, South Vietnam, near the village of the same name on Route 9, about 7 km west of the Khe Sanh Combat Base and close to the border with Laos. It is known for the Battle of Lang Vei on the night of 6–7 February 1968, during the Vietnam War, in which North Vietnamese forces overran the camp in the first battle in which the People's Army of Vietnam committed tanks.

The camp

Lang Vei was built in 1967 to replace the earlier camp at Lang Vei village destroyed by a sapper attack that May; the new camp, some 400 metres away, was fortified with bunkers and wire and garrisoned by Detachment A-101, 5th Special Forces Group, together with Civilian Irregular Defense Group (CIDG) strikers — Montagnard (Bru) and Vietnamese troops — and a Mobile Strike Force company. The camp guarded the western approaches to Khe Sanh along Route 9 and monitored infiltration from the Ho Chi Minh Trail in Laos.

Battle of Lang Vei

During the siege of Khe Sanh, in the early hours of 7 February 1968 the PAVN 66th Regiment, supported by a company of PT-76 amphibious light tanks of the 202nd Armored Regiment — the first use of North Vietnamese armour in the war — assaulted the camp after artillery preparation. The tanks broke through the wire; the defenders fought back with LAW rockets (some of which failed), recoilless rifles and claymore mines, knocking out several tanks, but the bunkers were progressively destroyed with satchel charges and tank fire. The survivors, led by the detachment commander and his executive officer, held a last position in and around the command bunker until morning, when a relief effort from Khe Sanh — a road march that had to fight through an ambush — and heavy airstrikes enabled the remaining defenders to be extracted. Of the garrison of roughly 500, more than 300 CIDG troops were killed or missing and 14 of the 24 Americans present were killed or wounded; PAVN losses were also heavy. Fourteen North Vietnamese tanks were claimed destroyed in total by ground fire and airstrikes. The battle was the only occasion in the war in which a US Special Forces camp was overrun by an armoured assault.

Aftermath

The fall of Lang Vei intensified concern at Khe Sanh and in Washington that the combat base would be attacked in force; it was not, and Khe Sanh was relieved in April. Sergeant First Class Eugene Ashley Jr., who repeatedly led counterattacks and called in airstrikes during the battle, was posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor.
