Karuta

Karuta (かるた, from Portuguese carta, "card") are Japanese playing cards, introduced by Portuguese merchants in the 16th century. Karuta decks fall broadly into two families: those derived from Portuguese-suited cards, and e-awase ("picture matching") games derived from the shell-matching game kai-awase.

Types

Best known is uta-garuta, based on the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu anthology of one hundred classical waka poems. It uses two sets of 100 cards: yomifuda (reading cards) bearing the full poems, and torifuda (grabbing cards) bearing only the poems' concluding lines. Competitive karuta (kyōgi karuta), played on tatami with 50 cards per side, is a fast memory-and-reflex sport governed by the All Japan Karuta Association, with national championships held at Ōmi Jingū in Ōtsu.

Other popular forms include iroha karuta, a proverb-matching game for children, and hanafuda ("flower cards"), a distinct 48-card deck of monthly flower suits also sometimes classed under karuta.

Cultural presence

Karuta is traditionally played at New Year gatherings. The manga and anime series Chihayafuru (2011 onward) significantly raised the profile of competitive karuta among young people in Japan and abroad.
