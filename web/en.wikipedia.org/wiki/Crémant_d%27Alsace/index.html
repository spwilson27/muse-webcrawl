Crémant d'Alsace is a protected designation of origin (AOC) sparkling wine produced in the Alsace wine region of north-eastern France. Made by the traditional method — a second fermentation in the bottle, as in Champagne — it is the most popular Crémant in France and accounts for a large share of all French sparkling wine consumed domestically outside Champagne itself.

Sparkling wine has been made in Alsace since the nineteenth century, when houses such as Dopff au Moulin adopted the méthode traditionnelle. The Crémant d'Alsace appellation was officially recognised in 1976.

Production

White Crémant is typically based on Pinot blanc, often blended with Auxerrois, Pinot gris, Riesling, or Chardonnay; rosé Crémant d'Alsace must be made entirely from Pinot noir. The wine must age on its lees for a minimum period before disgorgement. Production exceeds 30 million bottles a year, roughly a quarter of Alsace's total wine output.
