Cthulhu is a fictional cosmic entity created by American writer H. P. Lovecraft. Introduced in the short story "The Call of Cthulhu", published in Weird Tales in 1928, Cthulhu is a colossal, ancient being — part octopus, part dragon, part human caricature — that lies in deathlike sleep in the sunken city of R'lyeh beneath the Pacific Ocean, awaiting the time "when the stars are right" to rise again.

Description and role

Lovecraft's narrator pieces together the cult of Cthulhu from manuscripts, police raids, and sailors' accounts: cultists chant "Ph'nglui mglw'nafh Cthulhu R'lyeh wgah'nagl fhtagn" — "In his house at R'lyeh, dead Cthulhu waits dreaming." Cthulhu is the best-known of the Great Old Ones and the emblem of Lovecraft's cosmicism: humanity is insignificant before indifferent, incomprehensibly powerful beings.

The Cthulhu Mythos

After Lovecraft's death, August Derleth and others expanded his interconnected stories into the "Cthulhu Mythos", which has since grown into a vast shared universe of novels, anthologies, films, and games — notably the role-playing game Call of Cthulhu (1981) — with Cthulhu appearing in countless works across popular culture, from heavy metal to plush toys.

Pronunciation

Lovecraft rendered the name variously and suggested something like "Khlûl'-hloo", while noting the name was never meant to be pronounced by human tongues; the common modern pronunciation is "kuh-THOO-loo".
