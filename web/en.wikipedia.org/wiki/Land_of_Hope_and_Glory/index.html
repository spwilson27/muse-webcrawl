Land of Hope and Glory

Land of Hope and Glory
Music: Edward Elgar (1901)
Lyrics: A. C. Benson (1902)
Based on: Pomp and Circumstance March No. 1

"Land of Hope and Glory" is a British patriotic song, with music by Edward Elgar, written in 1901 and with lyrics by A. C. Benson added in 1902. The melody comes from the trio section — "the big tune" — of Elgar's Pomp and Circumstance March No. 1.

History

Elgar's march was an immediate success at its 1901 premiere. At the suggestion that words be fitted to the trio melody, Elgar invited Benson to write lyrics, and the song was incorporated into Elgar's Coronation Ode for Edward VII in 1902. The American soprano Clara Butt popularised it as a standalone song.

The refrain begins "Land of Hope and Glory, Mother of the Free" and includes the famous lines "Wider still and wider shall thy bounds be set; God, who made thee mighty, make thee mightier yet." Because of its imperial-era references to the widening bounds of empire, the lyrics have periodically drawn criticism and calls for replacement as an English anthem.

Usage

The song is traditionally performed at the Last Night of the Proms at the Royal Albert Hall, alongside "Rule, Britannia!" and "Jerusalem". It has often been proposed as an anthem for England at sporting events, where England — unlike Scotland and Wales — has no official anthem of its own and usually uses "God Save the King". It is the anthem of the Conservative Party conference tradition and is used at Commonwealth Games ceremonies by Team England in some contexts historically.

In the United States, the same Elgar tune, titled "Pomp and Circumstance", is the traditional processional at high-school and university graduation ceremonies.
