HER2

HER2 (human epidermal growth factor receptor 2), also known as ERBB2 or Neu, is a protein encoded by the ERBB2 gene that belongs to the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR/ErbB) family of receptor tyrosine kinases. HER2 has no known direct activating ligand; instead it is the preferred dimerisation partner for the other ErbB receptors, and these heterodimers trigger signalling pathways that promote cell growth and division.

Role in cancer

Amplification or overexpression of ERBB2 occurs in roughly 15–20% of breast cancers and in subsets of gastric, ovarian, lung and other tumours. HER2-positive cancers tend to grow aggressively, but the receptor is a major therapeutic target: the monoclonal antibody trastuzumab (Herceptin), together with pertuzumab, the antibody–drug conjugates trastuzumab emtansine and trastuzumab deruxtecan, and small-molecule inhibitors such as lapatinib and tucatinib, have greatly improved outcomes.

Testing

HER2 status is determined by immunohistochemistry (IHC) and in situ hybridisation (ISH/FISH) on tumour tissue and guides treatment selection. More recently, tumours classified as "HER2-low" (IHC 1+ or 2+ without amplification) have been shown to benefit from trastuzumab deruxtecan, expanding the treatable population.
