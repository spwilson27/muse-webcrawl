Friis formulas for noise

The Friis formulas for noise, named after Danish-American engineer Harald T. Friis, give the total noise factor of a cascade of amplifiers or other two-port devices in terms of the noise factors and gains of the individual stages. They are fundamental in radio-frequency and telecommunications engineering for designing low-noise receivers.

Formulation

For a cascade of n stages, the total noise factor F is approximately F1 + (F2 − 1)/G1 + (F3 − 1)/(G1G2) + …, where Fi is the noise factor and Gi the available power gain of the i-th stage. The formula shows that the noise contribution of later stages is divided by the gain of the stages before them, so the first stage — typically a low-noise amplifier — dominates the overall noise performance of the chain.

Applications

Engineers use the Friis noise formula when allocating noise budgets in receiver front ends, satellite links and radio telescopes, where a noisy first stage cannot be compensated by later amplification because the signal-to-noise ratio is already degraded.
