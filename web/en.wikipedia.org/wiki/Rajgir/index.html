Rajgir (historically known as Rajagriha, meaning "the City of Kings") is a city and a notified area in Nalanda district in the Indian state of Bihar. It is an ancient city that was the first capital of the kingdom of Magadha, a state that would later evolve into the Mauryan Empire. Rajgir is associated with the founders of both Buddhism and Jainism: Gautama Buddha spent many years preaching here, and Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara of Jainism, attained nirvana at nearby Pawapuri closely linked to the Rajgir region.

History

Rajagriha was the capital of Magadha until Ajatashatru founded Pataliputra (modern Patna). The city is surrounded by five hills — Ratnagiri, Udayagiri, Vaibhavgiri, Songiri and Vipulagiri — ringed by cyclopean walls that are among the oldest structures of their kind in India. Buddha is said to have delivered many sermons and discourses here, including at Vulture Peak (Griddhakuta), where he set in motion the second turning of the wheel of dharma according to Mahayana tradition. The First Buddhist Council was held in the Saptaparni Cave at Rajgir after Buddha's death, under the patronage of King Ajatashatru.

Places of interest

  • Vishwa Shanti Stupa — a peace pagoda on Ratnagiri hill, accessible by a ropeway.
  • Vulture Peak (Griddhakuta) — a sacred site associated with Buddha's sermons.
  • Brahmkunda — hot springs sacred to Hindus.
  • Son Bhandar Caves — rock-cut caves with inscriptions.
  • Jarasandha ka Akhara — ruins associated with the Mahabharata king Jarasandha.
  • Rajgir Wildlife Sanctuary — a forested protected area in the surrounding hills.

Geography and climate

Rajgir lies in a valley surrounded by rocky hills. Summers are hot, with temperatures reaching above 40 °C; winters are mild. The city is roughly 110 km from Patna and is connected by road and rail.

Transport

Rajgir railway station connects the city with Nalanda, Bihar Sharif and Patna. The nearest airport is in Patna. A ropeway carries visitors up to the Vishwa Shanti Stupa.
