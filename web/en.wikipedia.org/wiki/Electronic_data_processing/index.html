Electronic data processing

Electronic data processing (EDP) is the processing of data by a computer, including the collection, recording, manipulation and dissemination of information. The term dates from the early decades of commercial computing, when it distinguished computer-based methods from earlier manual and electromechanical ("unit record") methods using punched cards and tabulating machines.

History

Data processing began with Herman Hollerith's punched-card tabulators, first used for the 1890 United States census. Mid-twentieth-century businesses adopted unit record equipment for payroll, billing and inventory, and from the 1950s electronic computers such as the UNIVAC I and IBM 650 took over these tasks. "EDP" became the standard name for corporate computing departments and job titles through the mainframe era, before being largely supplanted by the broader term information technology (IT).

Process

Electronic data processing is conventionally described in stages: input (data capture and validation), processing (sorting, calculating, summarising and updating master files), storage, and output (reports, documents and transactions). Early systems were dominated by batch processing, in which transactions accumulated and were processed in runs; interactive and real-time processing later became standard.
