Castra Praetoria

BuiltAD 21–23
Built byTiberius, on the advice of Sejanus

The Castra Praetoria was the ancient Roman barracks (castra) of the Praetorian Guard, the emperor's bodyguard, in Rome. Established between AD 21 and 23 under the emperor Tiberius at the instigation of the prefect Sejanus, it concentrated the previously scattered Praetorian cohorts in a single permanent camp on the Viminal Hill, just outside the Servian Wall in the north-east of the city.

Description

The camp formed a rectangle of about 440 by 380 metres, enclosed by high concrete walls faced with brick, parts of which still survive, later incorporated into the Aurelian Walls. Within stood barracks, an armamentarium, a shrine of the standards and quarters for the urban cohorts and other units stationed in the capital.

History

From the Castra Praetoria the Guard exercised decisive influence on imperial politics, proclaiming emperors and deposing them; here Claudius was found hiding after Caligula's assassination and was proclaimed emperor in AD 41. In AD 193 the Guard notoriously auctioned the empire to Didius Julianus from its walls. Constantine I disbanded the Guard after the Battle of the Milvian Bridge in 312 and demolished much of the camp. Today the grounds are occupied by the Castro Pretorio barracks of the Italian Army and the National Central Library of Rome.
