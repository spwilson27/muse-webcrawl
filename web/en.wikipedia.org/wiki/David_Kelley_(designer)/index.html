David M. Kelley (born February 10, 1951) is an American designer, businessman and educator. He is the founder of the design consultancy IDEO and a professor at Stanford University, where he co-founded the Hasso Plattner Institute of Design (the "d.school"). Kelley is widely credited with popularising the methodology of design thinking and human-centred design in business.

Early life and education

Kelley was born in Barberton, Ohio. He studied electrical engineering at Carnegie Mellon University and worked briefly as an engineer at Boeing and NCR before enrolling in Stanford's joint design programme, where he embraced the product-design philosophy of Robert McKim and the workshop culture of the mechanical engineering department. He later joined the Stanford faculty, becoming the Donald W. Whittier Professor of Mechanical Engineering.

IDEO

In 1978 Kelley co-founded David Kelley Design in Palo Alto; the firm designed the first mouse for Apple — the rolling-ball mouse shipped with the Apple Lisa and Macintosh — as well as early laptop details and medical devices. In 1991 the firm merged with firms led by Bill Moggridge and Mike Nuttall to form IDEO. IDEO's celebrated portfolio includes the Palm V handheld, the Leap chair for Steelcase, shopping carts redesigned in a famous ABC Nightline segment, and service innovations for banks, hospitals and governments. Its method — interdisciplinary teams, rapid prototyping, observation of users and brainstorming under rules of deferred judgment — became a template for innovation consultancies worldwide.

Design thinking and the d.school

With his brother Tom Kelley, general manager of IDEO, Kelley articulated "design thinking" as a teachable, empathy-based problem-solving discipline, set out in Tom Kelley's The Art of Innovation (2001) and the brothers' Creative Confidence (2013), which argues that creativity is a universal capacity rather than a rare gift. In 2005, with backing from SAP co-founder Hasso Plattner, he founded Stanford's d.school, which teaches design methodology to graduate students from all disciplines.

Honours

Kelley has been elected to the National Academy of Engineering and received numerous design awards, including the Cooper Hewitt National Design Award for lifetime achievement. He survived a diagnosis of throat cancer in 2007, an experience he has cited in discussing empathy in design.
