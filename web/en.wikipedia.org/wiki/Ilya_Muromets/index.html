Ilya Muromets

Ilya Muromets is a mythical Kievan Rus' hero. He is celebrated in many Russian byliny (epic poems) and is the greatest of the legendary bogatyrs (knights-errant) of Kyivan Rus, alongside Dobrynya Nikitich and Alyosha Popovich. His name means "Ilya of Murom", after the city of Murom in present-day Russia.

Legend

According to the byliny, Ilya was a peasant's son who was unable to walk until the age of 33, when he was miraculously healed by wandering pilgrims (kaliki) and granted superhuman strength. He became a defender of Kyiv and of Prince Vladimir, defeating monsters such as Nightingale the Robber and the idol Idolishche. Unlike noble-born heroes, Ilya embodied the strength and loyalty of the common people.

Historical basis

The tales may preserve memories of a historical warrior; a monk named Ilya, venerated as Saint Ilya of Murom, is buried in the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, and his relics have been linked to the legendary figure, though this identification is debated.

In culture

Ilya Muromets has inspired works including Reinhold Glière's Third Symphony (1911), paintings by Viktor Vasnetsov, and the 1956 film Ilya Muromets. The name was also given to the Sikorsky four-engine aircraft designed by Igor Sikorsky before the First World War.
