Pepperoni is a variety of spicy salami made from cured pork and beef seasoned with paprika or other chili pepper. It is characteristically soft, slightly smoky and bright red in color. Thinly sliced pepperoni is one of the most popular pizza toppings in American pizzerias, where it cups and chars at the edges during baking.

History

Pepperoni was created by Italian-American immigrants in the United States in the early twentieth century, drawing on the spicy dry sausages of southern Italy such as salsiccia piccante and the Neapolitan soppressata. The word derives from peperoni, the plural of the Italian peperone, meaning bell pepper. The first known printed use of "pepperoni" to describe a sausage dates to 1919 in New York City, where Italian butchers and pizzerias were concentrated.

Production

Pepperoni is made from a mixture of ground pork and beef (sometimes all beef or turkey for dietary variants) blended with salt, curing agents such as sodium nitrite, and spices including paprika, cayenne, anise seed, garlic and black pepper. A lactic acid bacterial culture lowers the pH, giving pepperoni its tang and aiding preservation. The meat is stuffed into casings — often collagen — fermented, dried and sometimes lightly smoked.

Use

Beyond pizza, pepperoni is used in sandwiches, calzones, stromboli, pasta dishes and snack items such as pepperoni rolls, a regional specialty of West Virginia. On pizza, pepperoni slices famously curl into "cups" as they cook, a behavior prized by many regional styles, notably in Buffalo, New York.

Nutrition

Pepperoni is high in fat, sodium and protein, and like other processed meats is classified by the WHO's IARC as a Group 1 carcinogen with respect to colorectal cancer risk when consumed in quantity.
