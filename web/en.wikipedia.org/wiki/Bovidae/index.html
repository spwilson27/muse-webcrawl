The Bovidae comprise the biological family of cloven-hoofed, ruminant mammals that includes cattle, bison, buffalo, antelopes, sheep and goats. With around 279 species in some 146 genera, Bovidae is the largest family of hoofed mammals. Bovids occur naturally across Africa, Eurasia and North America, and domestic cattle, sheep and goats are now kept worldwide.

Characteristics

All bovids are ruminants with a four-chambered stomach that allows them to digest cellulose through microbial fermentation. Males — and in many species females — bear horns: permanent, unbranched keratin sheaths over bony cores, unlike the annually shed antlers of deer. Body size ranges from the tiny royal antelope (about 2 kg) to the gaur and wild buffalo (over 1,000 kg).

Evolution

Bovids first appeared in the early Miocene, about 20 million years ago, and diversified rapidly as grasslands spread. Africa remains the centre of bovid diversity, home to the great antelope radiations of the savanna. During the Pleistocene, bison and muskoxen spread into North America across the Bering land bridge.

Subfamilies

Traditional classifications recognise subfamilies including Bovinae (cattle, buffalo, bison), Caprinae (sheep, goats, muskox, takin), Antilopinae (gazelles and small antelopes), Alcelaphinae (wildebeest, hartebeest), Hippotraginae (oryx, roan and sable antelopes), Reduncinae (reedbucks, waterbuck), Cephalophinae (duikers), Aepycerotinae (impala) and Peleinae (rhebok). Molecular studies continue to revise the relationships between these groups.

Relation to humans

Cattle, sheep and goats were among the first livestock domesticated, in the Fertile Crescent around 10,000 years ago, and remain central to agriculture as sources of milk, meat, wool, leather and traction. Wild bovids are hunted for food and trophies and are flagship species for savanna conservation.
