Rover Company

The Rover Company Limited was a British motor manufacturer founded in 1878 in Coventry as a bicycle maker. John Kemp Starley and William Sutton's Rover safety bicycle of 1885 set the pattern for the modern bicycle, and the word "Rover" became generic for bicycle in several languages.

Cars

Rover built its first car in 1904 and became known for solid, middle-class saloons. After the Second World War it launched the Land Rover (1948), whose success transformed the company, and later the innovative Rover P6 (1963) and the SD1 (1976), both European Car of the Year winners. The Range Rover followed in 1970.

Decline and succession

Rover was absorbed into the Leyland Motor Corporation in 1967 and passed through British Leyland, nationalisation, a partnership with Honda, and ownership by BMW (1994–2000). After BMW's break-up, the MG Rover Group continued production at Longbridge until its collapse in 2005. The Rover marque itself is now owned by Jaguar Land Rover (Tata), while Land Rover continues as a major brand.
