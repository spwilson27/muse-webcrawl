Zinoviev letter

The Zinoviev letter was a forged document published in the British Daily Mail on 25 October 1924, four days before the general election of that year. Purporting to be a letter from Grigory Zinoviev, head of the Communist International in Moscow, to the Communist Party of Great Britain, it urged British communists to prepare for revolution, including subversion of the armed forces.

Background

Ramsay MacDonald's first Labour government had recognised the Soviet Union and negotiated controversial loans and treaties with it, and had recently collapsed after the Campbell Case. The letter appeared to confirm Conservative claims that Labour was soft on Bolshevism. The Foreign Office, under pressure, published the letter with a note of protest to the Soviet chargé d'affaires, effectively authenticating it before its provenance could be established.

Authenticity

The letter is now generally accepted as a forgery, most likely produced by White Russian émigrés in Berlin and passed to British intelligence and the press through intermediaries. An official historical study commissioned by the Foreign Office, published in 1999 by historian Gill Bennett, concluded the letter was forged, though questions remain about the roles of individual intelligence officers and Conservative Party figures in its dissemination.

Impact

The Conservatives won the 1924 election in a landslide. Historians debate how far the letter itself decided the result — Labour's vote share actually rose, and the collapse of the Liberal vote mattered more to the seat outcome — but the affair poisoned Anglo-Soviet relations, reinforced Labour's suspicion of the security services for a generation, and remains a byword for election-time dirty tricks.
