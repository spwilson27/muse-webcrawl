Census of India

The Census of India is a decennial population census conducted by the Office of the Registrar General and Census Commissioner, under the Ministry of Home Affairs, Government of India. It is the largest administrative exercise of its kind in the world, enumerating every resident of India and collecting data on demography, housing, language, religion, caste, literacy, occupation, and migration. The census is conducted under the Census Act of 1948.

History

The first complete census of India was taken in 1881 under British rule, following earlier non-synchronous counts in 1871–72; censuses have been held every ten years since. The first census of independent India took place in 1951. The 2011 census, the fifteenth overall and seventh since independence, counted a population of 1.21 billion people. The 2021 census was postponed because of the COVID-19 pandemic — the first break in the unbroken decennial sequence since 1881.

Organisation

The census is conducted in two phases: a house-listing and housing census, followed by population enumeration. In 2011 it engaged roughly 2.7 million enumerators and supervisors, mostly schoolteachers and government employees, covering some 640,000 villages and 8,000 towns. Data on caste beyond the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes were collected in the separate Socio Economic and Caste Census of 2011. The census also builds the National Population Register in parallel with the house-listing phase.

Findings of 2011

The 2011 census recorded a population of 1,210,854,977, with a decadal growth of 17.7%. The literacy rate rose to 73.0%, the sex ratio improved to 943 females per 1,000 males, and population density reached 382 persons per km². Uttar Pradesh was the most populous state with nearly 200 million inhabitants; Sikkim was the least populous.
