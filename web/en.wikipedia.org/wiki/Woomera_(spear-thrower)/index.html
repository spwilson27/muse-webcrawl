Woomera (spear-thrower)

A woomera is an Australian Aboriginal spear-throwing device, similar to an atlatl. It is a wooden implement, typically about 60–90 cm long, with a hook or peg at one end engaging the butt of a spear, extending the thrower's leverage so the spear can be cast faster and farther.

Description

Many woomeras are broad, leaf-shaped boards, concave on one side, sometimes fitted with a stone adze at the handle end and used as a multi-purpose tool: a shield, a dish, a fire-making saw, and a weapon in close combat.

Etymology and culture

The word comes from the Dharug language of the Sydney region. Woomeras feature in Aboriginal art and story, and the South Australian town of Woomera — later a rocket testing range — took its name from the device, symbolising throwing power.
