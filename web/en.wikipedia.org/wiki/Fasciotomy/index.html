Fasciotomy

Fasciotomy or fasciectomy is a surgical procedure where the fascia is cut to relieve tension or pressure in order to treat the resulting loss of circulation to an area of tissue or muscle. Fasciotomy is a limb-saving procedure when used to treat acute compartment syndrome, in which rising pressure within a closed fascial compartment compromises blood flow and nerve function.

Indications

The main indication is acute compartment syndrome, which may follow fractures, crush injuries, burns, or vascular injury and reperfusion, most commonly in the lower leg and forearm. Emergency fasciotomy releases all affected compartments. Fasciotomy is also performed electively for chronic exertional compartment syndrome, and modified forms are used in treating plantar fasciitis and Dupuytren's contracture (fasciectomy).

Procedure

In the lower leg, incisions are made to decompress the four compartments (anterior, lateral, superficial posterior, and deep posterior). Wounds are typically left open initially, covered with dressings, and later closed or skin-grafted. Delayed treatment can result in permanent muscle and nerve damage, contracture, or limb loss, so timely diagnosis and surgery are critical.

Complications

Complications can include infection, damage to nerves or vessels, the need for skin grafting, chronic pain, and altered sensation around scars.
