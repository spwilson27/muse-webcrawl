Kate Sessions

Katherine Olivia Sessions (8 November 1857 – 24 March 1940) was an American botanist, horticulturist and landscape architect known as the "Mother of Balboa Park" for her role in planting and shaping San Diego's Balboa Park and much of the city's urban forest.

Life

Born in San Francisco, Sessions graduated from the University of California, Berkeley in 1881 with a degree in natural science, one of the first women to do so. She moved to San Diego in 1883 to teach, then turned to horticulture, leasing land from the city in what is now Balboa Park for her nursery in exchange for planting 100 trees a year in the park and 300 elsewhere in the city.

Legacy

Sessions introduced many species to Southern California, including the jacaranda, poinsettia, bougainvillea and various palms and eucalypts, and co-founded the San Diego Floral Association. Her plantings for the 1915 Panama–California Exposition gave Balboa Park much of its character. Kate Sessions Park in Pacific Beach and a statue in Balboa Park honour her; she was inducted into the California Horticultural Hall of Fame.
