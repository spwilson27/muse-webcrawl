Baler

Baler (image placeholder)

A baler or hay baler is a piece of farm machinery used to compress a cut and raked crop (such as hay, cotton, flax straw, salt marsh hay, or silage) into compact bales that are easy to handle, transport, and store. Several different types of balers are in common use.

Types

Round balers produce cylindrical bales, typically 4–5 feet in diameter, formed by rolling the crop inside a chamber of belts or rollers; the finished bale is wrapped in twine, netting, or plastic film. Square (rectangular) balers produce dense rectangular bales, from small two-string bales handled by hand up to large bales weighing half a ton or more that require a loader. Industrial balers are also used to compact waste materials such as cardboard, plastic, and scrap metal for recycling.

History

Stationary hay presses date to the 19th century; mobile pick-up balers drawn by tractors appeared in the 1930s and 1940s, with the automatic knotter making field baling far more efficient. Round balers became widespread in the 1970s, in part because their bales shed rain and can be left in the field.
