Shinano River

The Shinano River (信濃川, Shinano-gawa), known as the Chikuma River (千曲川, Chikuma-gawa) in its upper reaches, is the longest and widest river in Japan and the third largest by basin area (after the Tone and Ishikari). It is located in northeastern Honshu, rising in the Japanese Alps and flowing generally northeast through Nagano and Niigata Prefectures before emptying into the Sea of Japan at Niigata City. Its length of 367 kilometres (228 mi) makes it the longest river entirely within Japan.

Course

The river rises on Mount Kobushi on the border of Saitama, Yamanashi, and Nagano Prefectures. As the Chikuma River, it flows northwest through Nagano Prefecture, gathering tributaries including the Sai River near Nagano City. On entering Niigata Prefecture it takes the name Shinano and crosses the Echigo Plain, Japan's largest alluvial plain area for a single river and a major rice-producing region. Historically the lower river flooded frequently; the great Ōkōzu Diversion Channel, completed in 1922 (with the Sekiya diversion near the mouth added in 1972), diverts much of the flow directly to the sea and transformed flood control and land use on the plain.

Significance

The Shinano system has long been central to transport (river boats carried rice to the port of Niigata), irrigation, hydroelectric power, and regional identity; its discharge is the largest of any Japanese river by mean annual flow. The river's upper course as the Chikuma is celebrated in Japanese poetry and song. In June 2011, following a major storm, flooding and sediment issues again highlighted the river's importance to Niigata's infrastructure, and the basin remains a focus of flood-defence engineering.
