The Chūgoku region (Japanese: 中国地方, Chūgoku-chihō), also known as the San'in-San'yō region, is the westernmost region of Honshu, the largest island of Japan. It consists of the prefectures of Hiroshima, Okayama, Shimane, Tottori and Yamaguchi. Its largest city is Hiroshima. In 2010 the region had a population of about 7.56 million.

Geography

The Chūgoku Mountains run east–west through the region, dividing it into two sub-regions: the San'yō region along the mild, industrialised coast of the Seto Inland Sea to the south, and the San'in region along the colder, less developed Sea of Japan coast to the north, noted for heavy winter snow.

History

"Chūgoku" means "middle country", a name dating from the old provincial system in which provinces were ranked by distance from the capital at Kyoto; the western provinces beyond it were the "far country" of Kyushu. During the Edo period the region was divided among domains such as Chōshū (Yamaguchi), whose samurai played a leading role in the Meiji Restoration. Hiroshima became the first city subjected to atomic bombing, on 6 August 1945.

Economy

The San'yō industrial belt along the Inland Sea hosts shipbuilding, steel, petrochemicals and automobile manufacture (notably Mazda in Hiroshima), while the San'in coast depends more on agriculture, fisheries and tourism.
