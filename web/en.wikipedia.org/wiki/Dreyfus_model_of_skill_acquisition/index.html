Dreyfus model of skill acquisition

The Dreyfus model of skill acquisition is a model of how learners acquire skills through formal instruction and practice, proposed by brothers Stuart and Hubert Dreyfus in 1980 based on studies of chess players, pilots and other practitioners. It describes five developmental stages: novice, advanced beginner, competence, proficiency and expertise.

The five stages

Novice — learners follow context-free rules rigidly and need monitoring. Advanced beginner — they begin to recognise situational aspects from experience. Competence — they plan deliberately, choose among rules and feel responsibility for outcomes. Proficiency — they perceive situations holistically but still decide analytically. Expertise — performance becomes fluid and intuitive; experts act on deep, tacit understanding rather than explicit rules.

Implications

The model has influenced professional education — notably Patricia Benner's adaptation for nursing — as well as debates in artificial intelligence, where the Dreyfus brothers argued that expert judgement cannot be fully captured by rule-based systems.
