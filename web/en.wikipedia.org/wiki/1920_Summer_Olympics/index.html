The 1920 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the VII Olympiad, were held in Antwerp, Belgium, from April to September 1920. Antwerp was awarded the Games as a tribute to the Belgian people, who had suffered heavily during the First World War. The Games were the first to feature the Olympic flag — designed by Pierre de Coubertin, with its five interlaced rings — and the first at which the Olympic Oath was taken by an athlete (fencer Victor Boin) and doves were released as a symbol of peace.

Background

The 1916 Games, planned for Berlin, had been cancelled because of the war. Hungary, Germany, Austria, Bulgaria, and Ottoman Turkey — the defeated Central Powers — were not invited to Antwerp, and Soviet Russia did not participate. The budget was limited, facilities were makeshift, and many events (including sailing in the Netherlands and ice hockey and figure skating in April) were spread out in time and place.

Highlights

Finnish runner Paavo Nurmi made his Olympic debut, winning three gold medals; his compatriot Hannes Kolehmainen won the marathon. Italian fencer Nedo Nadi won gold in all five fencing events. American swimmer Ethelda Bleibtrey won three golds, and Swedish shooter Oscar Swahn, aged 72, became the oldest Olympic medallist in history by winning silver. Ice hockey (won by Canada) and figure skating appeared as part of the programme, several years before the first Winter Olympics.

Medal table

The United States topped the medal table with 41 gold and 95 total medals, followed by Sweden and Great Britain. Host nation Belgium finished fifth.
