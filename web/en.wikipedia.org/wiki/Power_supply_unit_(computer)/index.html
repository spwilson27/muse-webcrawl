Power supply unit (computer)

Overview
Function:Converts AC mains to regulated DC
Outputs:+3.3 V, +5 V, +12 V, −12 V, +5 VSB
Common standard:ATX / ATX12V
Efficiency ratings:80 Plus tiers

A power supply unit (PSU) converts mains AC power to low-voltage regulated DC power for the internal components of a desktop computer. Modern personal computers universally use switched-mode power supplies, which are lighter and more efficient than older linear designs. Most desktop PSUs conform to the ATX standard, which specifies dimensions, mounting, and connectors, including the 24-pin main motherboard connector and 4- or 8-pin CPU power connectors.

Rails and connectors

Desktop components primarily draw on the +12 V rail, from which motherboards derive other voltages; +3.3 V and +5 V supply logic and some peripherals, and a +5 V standby rail powers wake-up circuitry when the machine is off. Graphics cards may require dedicated 6- or 8-pin PCIe power connectors (and newer high-power 12VHPWR connectors on high-end models). Modular PSUs allow unused cables to be detached to improve airflow and cable management.

Efficiency and wattage

PSUs are rated by maximum output wattage and by efficiency certification under the voluntary 80 Plus programme, with tiers from standard through Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, and Titanium. Efficiency peaks near mid-load, and modern units use active power factor correction.
