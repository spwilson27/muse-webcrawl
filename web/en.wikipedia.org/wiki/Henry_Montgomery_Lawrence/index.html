Brigadier-General Sir Henry Montgomery Lawrence KCB (28 June 1806 – 4 July 1857) was a British military officer and administrator in India, best remembered for his defence of the Residency at Lucknow during the Indian Rebellion of 1857, in which he was killed. He was the elder brother of John Lawrence, later Viceroy of India.

Early career

Born in Ceylon to a military family, Lawrence was commissioned in the Bengal Artillery in 1823. He served in the First Anglo-Burmese War, then in revenue survey work, and as a political officer on the North-West Frontier. After the First Anglo-Sikh War he became Resident at Lahore and president of the council governing the Punjab for the young Maharaja Duleep Singh, earning a reputation for energy and an unusually sympathetic interest in Indian welfare. He clashed with his brother John over Punjab policy — Henry favouring conciliation of the Sikh aristocracy — and was moved to Rajputana.

Oudh and the rebellion

In 1857 Lawrence was appointed Chief Commissioner of the recently annexed province of Oudh and Resident at Lucknow. Foreseeing trouble, he prudently stocked and fortified the Residency and gathered supplies. When the rebellion reached Lucknow in June 1857, he took command of the defence of the Residency compound, which housed over a thousand European and loyal Indian residents. On 2 July he was mortally wounded by a shell that burst in his room and died two days later; tradition gives his last instruction as "Here lies Henry Lawrence who tried to do his duty" — the epitaph he had chosen for himself.

Legacy

Lawrence founded a series of military asylums and schools for the children of British soldiers — at Sanawar in the Himalayas (1847), Mount Abu, Lovedale and Ghora Gali — which survive as the Lawrence Schools. The besieged Residency at Lucknow was held until finally relieved by Colin Campbell in November 1857, and its ruins became a celebrated memorial of the rebellion.
