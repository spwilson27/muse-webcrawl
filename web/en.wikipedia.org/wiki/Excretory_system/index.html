Excretory system

The excretory system is a passive biological system that removes excess, unnecessary materials from the body fluids of an organism, so as to help maintain internal chemical homeostasis and prevent damage to the body. Its functions include removing nitrogenous wastes (such as urea, uric acid, and ammonia), regulating water and electrolyte balance, and helping control blood pressure and pH.

In humans

The human excretory (urinary) system consists of two kidneys, two ureters, the urinary bladder, and the urethra. Each kidney contains about a million nephrons, which filter blood at the glomerulus and reabsorb useful substances along the tubule; the resulting urine passes to the bladder for storage and voiding. The liver also plays an excretory role by converting ammonia to urea and detoxifying other compounds, while the lungs excrete carbon dioxide and the skin excretes salts in sweat.

In other animals

Invertebrates use specialized structures: flame cells and protonephridia in flatworms, metanephridia in annelids, and Malpighian tubules in insects. Animals differ in their main nitrogenous waste — aquatic animals often excrete ammonia directly (ammonotelic), mammals excrete urea (ureotelic), and birds and reptiles excrete uric acid (uricotelic), conserving water.
