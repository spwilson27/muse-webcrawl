Blockade runner

A blockade runner of the American Civil War era

A blockade runner is a merchant vessel used for evading a naval blockade of a port or strait. It is usually light and fast, using stealth and speed rather than armour or armament to evade blockading forces. Blockade runners usually carry cargo, for example bringing food or arms to a blockaded city. They also may carry mail in and out of the blockade.

The term is often applied to vessels that evaded the Union blockade during the American Civil War, running between the Confederate States and British ports such as Bermuda, Nassau in the Bahamas, and Havana in Cuba. British-built steamers, many constructed on the Clyde, made hundreds of successful runs, although the odds of capture grew as the war progressed and the Union Navy expanded.

History

Blockade running predates the Civil War: during the Napoleonic Wars and the War of 1812, fast schooners and privateers slipped through British and French blockades. In the Second World War, Swedish and other neutral vessels ran the German blockade to supply Britain, and German and Italian submarines and surface ships attempted to break the Allied blockade to reach Japan and Southeast Asia, carrying strategic raw materials such as rubber and tungsten.

Design

Successful blockade runners were designed for speed and a low profile. Civil War runners were typically side-wheel steamers with shallow drafts, painted grey to blend with the sea and haze, burning smokeless anthracite coal to avoid detection. Speeds of 14–17 knots allowed them to outrun most blockading warships on moonless nights.

Economics

Because a single successful voyage could pay for the vessel itself, blockade running attracted heavy speculative investment. Cotton exported from the Confederacy sold at many times its domestic price in Liverpool, while imported goods such as rifles, medicine, and luxuries fetched enormous prices in the South. Crews were paid in gold, and captains could earn fortunes, but capture meant imprisonment and the loss of ship and cargo.
