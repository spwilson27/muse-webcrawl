Trilemma

A trilemma is a difficult choice from three options, each of which is (or appears) unacceptable or unfavourable. It generalises the dilemma, which forces a choice between two options. The term is a coinage modelled on dilemma, from the Greek prefix tri- (three) and lemma (a proposition or assumption).

Famous trilemmas

Lewis's trilemma, popularised by C. S. Lewis, argues that someone claiming to be God, as Jesus is portrayed as doing, must be "lunatic, liar, or Lord" — mad, evil, or genuinely divine — and therefore cannot be merely a great moral teacher.

The impossible trinity (Mundell–Fleming trilemma) in international economics says a country cannot simultaneously maintain a fixed exchange rate, free capital movement and independent monetary policy; it must give up one of the three.

The Münchhausen trilemma in epistemology holds that any attempted proof of a claim ends in one of three unsatisfying outcomes: circular reasoning, infinite regress, or dogmatic stopping at an unproven axiom.

Other examples include the Zooko's triangle for naming systems, the scalability trilemma in blockchain design, and trilemmas in political philosophy about liberty, equality and community.
