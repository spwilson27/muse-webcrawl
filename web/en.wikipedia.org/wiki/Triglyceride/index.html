Triglyceride

A triglyceride (triacylglycerol, TAG) is an ester derived from glycerol and three fatty acids. Triglycerides are the main constituents of body fat in humans and other vertebrates and of vegetable fat.

Triglyceride
TypeLipid, ester
ComponentsGlycerol + 3 fatty acids
FunctionEnergy storage

Metabolism

Dietary triglycerides are hydrolysed by pancreatic lipase, absorbed, and transported in chylomicrons; the liver exports them in VLDL. Hormone-sensitive lipase mobilises stored fat. Blood triglyceride levels are measured in a lipid panel; high levels (hypertriglyceridemia) are associated with cardiovascular disease and pancreatitis.

Chemical structure

Fatty acid chains may be saturated or unsaturated, determining whether a fat is solid or an oil. See Fatty acid and Lipid.
