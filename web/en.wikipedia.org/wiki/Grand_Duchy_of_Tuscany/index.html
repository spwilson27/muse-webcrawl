The Grand Duchy of Tuscany (Italian: Granducato di Toscana) was a central Italian state founded in 1569 when Pope Pius V granted Cosimo I de' Medici the title of Grand Duke of Tuscany. It endured, with interruptions during the Napoleonic era, until its annexation to the Kingdom of Sardinia in 1860 during the unification of Italy.

Medici rule

Under the Medici, Florence continued as a major centre of art, finance and early science — Galileo Galilei served as court mathematician. The grand duchy's ports, especially Livorno, prospered as free ports, while the dynasty's fortunes waned in the later 17th and early 18th centuries.

Habsburg-Lorraine rule

When the Medici line died out in 1737, Tuscany passed to Francis Stephen of Lorraine, husband of Maria Theresa of Austria. The Lorraine grand dukes carried out noted reforms; Leopold II of Tuscany made the grand duchy the first modern state in the world to abolish the death penalty, in 1786. The last grand duke, Ferdinand IV, was deposed in 1859 and Tuscany voted for annexation to Piedmont-Sardinia in 1860.
