Auto racing

Auto racing (also known as car racing, motor racing, or automobile racing) is a motorsport involving the racing of automobiles for competition. It is one of the world's most watched and commercially successful sports, encompassing a wide variety of formats, from single-seater open-wheel racing such as Formula One to stock car, touring car, sports car, rallying and drag racing disciplines.

History

Racing began soon after the invention of the internal combustion engine. The first organised contest is generally regarded as the 1894 Paris–Rouen trial, organised by the newspaper Le Petit Journal, which demonstrated the reliability and speed of the new automobiles. The first true race, in which entrants competed purely for position, was the Paris–Bordeaux–Paris event of 1895, won by Émile Levassor. Inter-city races in Europe gave way to closed-circuit events after accidents involving spectators, notably the disastrous 1903 Paris–Madrid race.

In the United States, racing developed on oval dirt tracks and at purpose-built speedways such as the Indianapolis Motor Speedway, opened in 1909 and host of the Indianapolis 500 from 1911. Grand Prix racing in Europe produced the modern Formula One World Championship, inaugurated in 1950.

Categories

Open-wheel racing

Open-wheel cars, including Formula One, IndyCar and Formula E, have their wheels outside the main bodywork. Formula One, governed by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA), is the highest class of international single-seater racing and the most commercially prominent form of the sport.

Stock car racing

Stock car racing, dominated in the United States by NASCAR, uses production-based cars on oval tracks. Its premier event is the Daytona 500.

Sports car and endurance racing

Endurance races such as the 24 Hours of Le Mans, held since 1923, test speed and reliability over long distances, typically with multiple drivers sharing a car. Prototype and grand tourer classes compete under the FIA World Endurance Championship.

Rallying and off-road

Rallying is conducted on public or private roads using modified production cars, with drivers following pacenotes read by a co-driver. The World Rally Championship has run since 1973. Off-road events include the Dakar Rally, originally run from Paris to Dakar.

Other forms

Drag racing features short, straight-line acceleration contests; touring car racing uses modified saloon cars; kart racing is a common entry point for professional drivers.

Safety

Safety has improved dramatically since the sport's dangerous early decades, through roll cages, fire-resistant clothing, crash structures, the HANS device and, in open-wheel series, the halo cockpit protection introduced in Formula One in 2018. Governing bodies mandate medical facilities and rigorous circuit inspection.
