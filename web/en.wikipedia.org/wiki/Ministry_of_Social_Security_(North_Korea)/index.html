Ministry of Social Security (North Korea)

Formed1951 (as Ministry of Public Security lineage)
HeadquartersPyongyang
JurisdictionNorth Korea (DPRK)

The Ministry of Social Security (Korean: 사회안전성) is the law enforcement and internal security agency of North Korea, responsible for policing, public order, and the surveillance of the civilian population. Its personnel — "social security agents" — function as the country's ordinary police force, distinct from the Ministry of State Security, which handles political crimes and espionage.

Functions

The ministry maintains police stations throughout the country, enforces criminal law and traffic regulations, issues travel permits for movement between provinces, conducts household registration checks, and supervises resident registration and the songbun-related records of citizens. It also guards key facilities and is reported by defectors and human-rights investigators to administer ordinary correctional camps (kyohwaso) for non-political prisoners.

History

The agency descends from security bureaus established after Korea's liberation in 1945 and was long known as the Ministry of People's Security (인민보안성). In 2020 it was renamed the Ministry of Social Security, restoring a name used briefly decades earlier. It is subordinate to the State Affairs Commission and operates under the direction of the Workers' Party of Korea.
