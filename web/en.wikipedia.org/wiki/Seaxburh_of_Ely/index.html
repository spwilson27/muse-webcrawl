Seaxburh (Old English: Sexburga; died 6 July 699), also known as Saint Sexburga of Ely, was a queen of Kent and later an abbess and saint of Anglo-Saxon England. She was a daughter of King Anna of East Anglia and the sister of Æthelthryth (Saint Audrey), founder of the double monastery at Ely.

Life

Seaxburh married Eorcenberht, king of Kent (r. 640–664), and was the mother of kings Ecgberht and Hlothhere of Kent, and of Saints Eorcengota and Ermenilda. After her husband's death she acted as regent of Kent during the minority of her son Ecgberht — one of the earliest recorded female regencies in English history. She founded the abbey of Minster-in-Sheppey on the Isle of Sheppey, but later joined her sister Æthelthryth's monastery at Ely. On Æthelthryth's death in 679, Seaxburh succeeded her as abbess.

The translation of Æthelthryth

As abbess, in about 695 Seaxburh oversaw the translation of her sister's body into a new church at Ely. According to Bede's Ecclesiastical History of the English People, the body was found incorrupt sixteen years after burial, a sign of sanctity that greatly promoted the cult of Saint Audrey. Seaxburh died on 6 July 699 and was buried at Ely beside her sister. Her daughter Ermenilda later also became abbess of Ely.

Veneration

Seaxburh's feast day is 6 July. She is one of a remarkable family of East Anglian royal saints that includes her sisters Æthelthryth, Æthelburh of Faremoutiers and Withburga of Dereham.
