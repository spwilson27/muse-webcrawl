Diseconomies of scope

Diseconomies of scope
FieldMicroeconomics, business strategy
OppositeEconomies of scope
CausesComplexity, coordination failures, cultural clashes, resource contention

Diseconomies of scope are an economic concept describing a situation in which the combined cost of producing or providing two or more products or services within a single firm exceeds the cost of producing them separately in specialised firms. It is the converse of economies of scope, in which joint production is cheaper.

Causes

Diseconomies of scope can arise from managerial complexity and the dilution of focus when a firm diversifies into unrelated lines of business; from conflicting organisational cultures and incentives between divisions; from shared resources becoming bottlenecks; and from the difficulty of allocating overhead and capital across dissimilar activities. In finance, the phenomenon is sometimes discussed in connection with the "conglomerate discount" — diversified firms trading at lower valuations than the sum of comparable specialised firms.

Strategic implications

The prospect of diseconomies of scope argues for corporate focus, spin-offs, and divestitures, and is a standard consideration in diversification strategy: a firm should expand into new product areas only when genuine synergies — shared inputs, distribution, technology, or brand — outweigh the coordination costs of managing a broader scope.
