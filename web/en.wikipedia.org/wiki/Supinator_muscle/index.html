Supinator muscle

The supinator is a broad muscle in the posterior compartment of the forearm, curving around the upper third of the radius. Its contraction supinates the forearm — rotating it so that the palm faces upward or forward — working with the biceps brachii, which is the more powerful supinator when the elbow is flexed.

OriginSupinator crest of the ulna, lateral epicondyle of the humerus, radial collateral and annular ligaments
InsertionLateral, posterior and anterior surfaces of the proximal radius
ArteryRadial recurrent artery
NerveDeep branch of the radial nerve / posterior interosseous nerve (C5–C6)
ActionSupinates the forearm

Structure

The muscle has superficial and deep layers arising from the supinator crest and fossa of the ulna and from the structures around the elbow joint, wrapping around the neck and shaft of the radius to insert on its upper third. The deep branch of the radial nerve passes between the two layers of the supinator, emerging as the posterior interosseous nerve; the fibrous proximal edge of the superficial layer is known as the arcade of Frohse.

Clinical significance

The posterior interosseous nerve can be compressed as it traverses the supinator, producing posterior interosseous nerve syndrome — weakness of finger and wrist extension without the pain typical of lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow), with which it is sometimes confused. Repetitive pronation–supination is among the activities implicated in such entrapment.
