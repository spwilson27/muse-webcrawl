The Irish Times is an Irish daily broadsheet newspaper, founded in Dublin on 29 March 1859. It is widely regarded as Ireland's newspaper of record. Since 1974 it has been owned by The Irish Times Trust, a governance structure designed to guarantee its editorial independence.

History

The paper was founded by Major Lawrence Knox as a Protestant nationalist and unionist-leaning title. In the twentieth century, under editors such as R. M. Smyllie, it broadened its outlook; the playwright and novelist Brian O'Nolan (Flann O'Brien) wrote the long-running "Cruiskeen Lawn" column as Myles na gCopaleen. Douglas Gageby, editor from 1963, transformed the paper into a liberal, pluralist voice for the whole island, broadening its Catholic readership. The paper has employed many distinguished journalists and publishes celebrated columns, letters pages and the Crosaire crossword.

Modern era

The Irish Times launched irishtimes.com in 1994, among the earliest newspaper websites, and later a metered digital subscription model. Its weekly editions cover news, business, property, sport and culture, and it publishes an edition for Northern Ireland. Notable editors have included Conor Brady, Geraldine Kennedy, Kevin O'Sullivan and Paul O'Neill.
