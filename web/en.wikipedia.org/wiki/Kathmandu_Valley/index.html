Kathmandu Valley

Kathmandu Valley
Location: Bagmati Province, Nepal
Area: about 900 km²
Major cities: Kathmandu, Lalitpur (Patan), Bhaktapur
UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979

The Kathmandu Valley (Nepali: काठमाडौं उपत्यका), historically known as Nepal Valley, is a bowl-shaped valley in the Himalayan foothills of north-central Nepal, at an elevation of roughly 1,300–1,400 metres. It contains the capital city Kathmandu and the historic cities of Lalitpur and Bhaktapur, and is the political, cultural and economic centre of the country.

Geography

The valley is drained by the Bagmati River and its tributaries, including the Vishnumati and Manohara. Tradition holds that it was once a great lake, drained — in legend by the bodhisattva Manjushri — leaving fertile lacustrine soils that supported early settlement.

History

The valley was ruled successively by the Licchavi and Malla dynasties, whose patronage produced an extraordinary concentration of palaces, temples and stupas. After rivalry between the valley's three kingdoms, Prithvi Narayan Shah of Gorkha conquered it in 1768–69 and made Kathmandu the capital of a unified Nepal.

Heritage

The UNESCO World Heritage listing "Kathmandu Valley" comprises seven monument zones: the Durbar Squares of Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur, the stupas of Swayambhunath and Boudhanath, and the Hindu temples of Pashupatinath and Changu Narayan. Many monuments were damaged in the April 2015 earthquake and have been under restoration since.
