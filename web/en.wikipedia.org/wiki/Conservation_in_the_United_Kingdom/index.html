Conservation in the United Kingdom

Conservation in the United Kingdom encompasses the protection of wildlife, habitats, landscapes and the built heritage through legislation, designated sites and voluntary organisations. The UK has a long conservation history, from 19th-century commons preservation and bird protection to modern biodiversity strategies.

Designations

Key protections include Sites of Special Scientific Interest (SSSIs), Special Areas of Conservation and Special Protection Areas, Ramsar wetlands, National Nature Reserves, national parks, Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty (National Landscapes in England), scheduled monuments and listed buildings. Statutory agencies include Natural England, NatureScot, Natural Resources Wales and the Environment Agency.

Organisations and law

Major charities include the National Trust, RSPB, the Wildlife Trusts, Woodland Trust and Plantlife. Landmark legislation includes the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949, the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, and the Environment Act 2021, which introduced biodiversity net gain and legally binding targets after Brexit replaced much EU-derived habitat law.
