Disaster

A disaster is a serious problem occurring over a period of time that causes widespread human, material, economic or environmental loss which exceeds the ability of the affected community or society to cope using its own resources. Disasters are routinely divided into two major categories: natural disasters, arising from geophysical, meteorological, hydrological, climatological or biological hazards, and human-made (anthropogenic or technological) disasters, such as industrial accidents, transport crashes, armed conflict, and structural failures.

Definitions and classification

The United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction defines a disaster as a serious disruption of the functioning of a community or society involving widespread losses and impacts, which exceeds the community's ability to cope using its own resources. Modern disaster studies emphasize that disasters result from the interaction of a hazard with exposure and vulnerability: the same earthquake may be a catastrophe in a densely settled, poorly built region and a minor event in an unpopulated one. The EM-DAT international disaster database classifies events by hazard type and records those causing at least ten deaths, affecting at least one hundred people, or prompting a state of emergency or call for international assistance.

Impacts

Disasters kill and injure people, destroy housing and infrastructure, interrupt water, power and health services, displace populations, and cause long-term economic and psychological harm. Their burden falls disproportionately on low-income countries and marginalized groups, where housing quality, early-warning systems, and state capacity are weakest. Indirect effects — disease outbreaks, food insecurity, lost schooling — may exceed the direct toll.

Management

Emergency management addresses the full cycle of mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery. International frameworks such as the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030 set targets for reducing mortality, affected populations, and economic losses through risk assessment, resilient infrastructure, early warning, and community preparedness. Humanitarian relief is coordinated by agencies including the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs and the Red Cross and Red Crescent movement.
