The Perfect Storm (book)

The Perfect Storm: A True Story of Men Against the Sea is a 1997 creative nonfiction book by Sebastian Junger. It reconstructs the "Perfect Storm" of October–November 1991 and the loss of the swordfishing boat Andrea Gail and her six-man crew from Gloucester, Massachusetts.

AuthorSebastian Junger
Published1997 (W. W. Norton)
GenreCreative nonfiction

Content

Junger interweaves the Andrea Gail's final voyage with accounts of other vessels and rescuers, the physics of extreme waves, and the history and dangers of the North Atlantic swordfishery. Because no one survived to tell the crew's story, the book pieces together their last days from radio contacts, weather data and the experiences of other fishermen.

Adaptation

The book was a bestseller and was adapted into the 2000 film The Perfect Storm, starring George Clooney and Mark Wahlberg. Families of some crew members disputed aspects of the portrayals, while the book is credited with popularising the meteorological term "perfect storm".
