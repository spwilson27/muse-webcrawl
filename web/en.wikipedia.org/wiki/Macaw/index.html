Macaw

Macaws are a group of New World parrots that are long-tailed and often colourful. They are popular in aviculture and as companion parrots, although there are conservation concerns about several species in the wild.

Description

Macaws are native to Central and South America (and formerly the Caribbean). They range from the small red-shouldered macaw (about 30 cm) to the hyacinth macaw, the largest flying parrot at about 100 cm long. Macaws have large, powerful beaks adapted for cracking hard nuts and seeds, long tails, and bare facial patches. Well-known species include the scarlet macaw, blue-and-yellow macaw, green-winged macaw, military macaw and Spix's macaw, the latter extinct in the wild but the subject of reintroduction efforts in Brazil.

Conservation

Habitat loss and capture for the pet trade have threatened many macaw species; several, including the glaucous macaw and Cuban macaw, are extinct.
