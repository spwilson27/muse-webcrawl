Quetta after the earthquake of 31 May 1935

The 1935 Quetta earthquake struck Quetta and the surrounding region of Baluchistan, British India (now Pakistan), in the early hours of 31 May 1935. Measuring 7.7 on the moment magnitude scale, it destroyed the city and killed between 30,000 and 60,000 people, making it one of the deadliest earthquakes in South Asian history and one of the deadliest of the 20th century.

Earthquake

The shock struck at 3:02 a.m. local time, when most residents were asleep. Its epicentre was near Ali Jaan, southwest of Quetta, along the Chaman fault system. Ground shaking lasted about 30 seconds; virtually every building in Quetta — largely of mud brick and rubble — collapsed. Fires broke out among the ruins, and aftershocks continued for days. The neighbouring towns of Mastung and the military cantonment also suffered severe damage; the nearby RAF station was heavily hit, with many airmen killed.

Casualties and relief

Estimates of the death toll range from 30,000 to 60,000; around 20,000 bodies were recovered in Quetta alone. British and Indian troops organised the rescue, clearing rubble, establishing camps, and sealing off the ruined city against epidemic disease. The dead were buried or cremated in mass ceremonies in the days that followed.

Reconstruction

Quetta was rebuilt under one of British India's first enforced seismic building codes: new construction used lighter materials, reinforced frames, and height limits, so that the rebuilt city was markedly more earthquake-resistant than its predecessor. The 1935 disaster remains the benchmark seismic event for Balochistan, and the region was struck again by a major earthquake near Ziarat in 2008.
