The Neon Museum in Las Vegas, Nevada, United States, is a non-profit organisation dedicated to collecting, preserving, studying and exhibiting iconic Las Vegas signs. Founded in 1996, the museum opened at its current downtown site in October 2012. Many of its signs are displayed outdoors in the roughly two-acre Neon Boneyard, where retired casino, hotel and business signs from the 1930s onwards are preserved, a number of them restored and illuminated.

Campus

The museum's visitors' centre is housed in the restored former lobby of the La Concha Motel, a mid-century, shell-shaped ("Googie") concrete building designed by architect Paul Revere Williams, which was moved from the Las Vegas Strip to the museum site in 2006 and reassembled. The main Boneyard holds more than 200 signs, including those of the Stardust, Moulin Rouge, Desert Inn, Golden Nugget and Aladdin. A second space, the North Gallery, hosts exhibitions and events. Restored signs are also displayed as public art along Las Vegas Boulevard and in the Fremont Street area.

History and collection

For decades, retired signs in Las Vegas were typically destroyed or left to decay in the storage yards of sign companies, notably YESCO (Young Electric Sign Company). Concern over the loss of this heritage led preservationists — working with the Allied Arts Council of Southern Nevada and the city — to found the museum in 1996. YESCO donated much of its retired-sign inventory. Famous items in the collection include the original "Welcome to Fabulous Las Vegas"–era signage, the Stardust's giant lettering, the lamp of the Aladdin and the guitar sign of the Hard Rock Cafe, restored in 2019.

Recognition

The museum is one of Las Vegas's most visited cultural attractions and has become a symbol of historic preservation in the city; its night-time guided tours of the illuminated Boneyard are especially popular. In 2018 the museum expanded with an immersive audiovisual show, "Brilliant!", which uses projection mapping to re-light unrestored signs.
