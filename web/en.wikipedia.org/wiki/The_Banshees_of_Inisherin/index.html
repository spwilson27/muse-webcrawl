The Banshees of Inisherin is a 2022 black tragicomedy film written, directed, and co-produced by Martin McDonagh. Set on a remote island off the west coast of Ireland, the film follows two lifelong friends, Pádraic Súilleabháin (Colin Farrell) and Colm Doherty (Brendan Gleeson), who find themselves at an impasse when Colm abruptly ends their friendship. It also stars Kerry Condon and Barry Keoghan.

The film had its world premiere at the 79th Venice International Film Festival on 5 September 2022, where Farrell won the Volpi Cup for Best Actor and McDonagh won the Golden Osella for Best Screenplay. It received nine nominations at the 95th Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor for Farrell, and Best Supporting Actor nominations for Gleeson and Keoghan, as well as Best Supporting Actress for Condon.

Plot

In April 1923, on the fictional island of Inisherin off the Irish coast, fiddler Colm Doherty abruptly begins ignoring his best friend and drinking companion Pádraic Súilleabháin. Colm explains that he finds Pádraic too dull and wishes to spend his remaining years composing music. When Pádraic refuses to accept the rejection, Colm issues an ultimatum: every time Pádraic speaks to him, Colm will cut off one of his own fingers with a pair of sheep shears. The feud escalates tragically, drawing in Pádraic's sister Siobhán and the troubled local lad Dominic Kearney, and ends in arson, loss, and an unresolved standoff on the beach as the two men face each other one final time.

Cast

Colin Farrell as Pádraic Súilleabháin; Brendan Gleeson as Colm Doherty; Kerry Condon as Siobhán Súilleabháin; Barry Keoghan as Dominic Kearney; Gary Lydon as Peadar Kearney; Pat Shortt as Jonjo Devine; Sheila Flitton as Mrs McCormick; David Pearse as the priest.

Production

Principal photography began in August 2021 on Inishmore and Achill Island in Ireland and wrapped in October 2021. The film reunited Farrell and Gleeson with McDonagh, who had directed them in In Bruges (2008). McDonagh originally conceived the story as the third part of his Aran Islands trilogy of plays, after The Cripple of Inishmaan and The Lieutenant of Inishmore.

Reception

The film was acclaimed by critics for its screenplay, direction, and performances, particularly those of Farrell and Condon. It grossed over $52 million worldwide against a $20 million budget and was named one of the top ten films of 2022 by the National Board of Review. At the 80th Golden Globe Awards it won Best Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy, Best Actor for Farrell, and Best Screenplay.
