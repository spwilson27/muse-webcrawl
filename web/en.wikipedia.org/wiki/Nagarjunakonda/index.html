Nagarjunakonda (meaning "Nagarjuna Hill") is a historical site and island located near Nagarjuna Sagar in Palnadu district of the Indian state of Andhra Pradesh. It is one of India's richest Buddhist sites, and now lies almost entirely beneath the reservoir of the Nagarjuna Sagar Dam.

The site was the capital of the Ikshvaku dynasty (c. 225–325 CE), successors of the Satavahanas in the eastern Deccan. It was named after Nagarjuna, the celebrated 2nd-century Mahayana Buddhist philosopher and founder of the Madhyamaka school, who is said to have lived there and presided over its monastic community. In its day the city was known as Vijayapuri.

History

Nagarjunakonda was a major centre of Buddhist learning, attracting monks and scholars from as far as Sri Lanka, Bengal, Kashmir, Gandhara, China, and Syria. Excavations revealed more than 30 Buddhist monasteries (viharas), stupas, chaitya halls, temples, and sculptures of the Amaravati school of art, alongside Hindu temples and secular structures including a bathing ghat, an amphitheatre, and a Roman-style wharf. Inscriptions mention artisan guilds from various parts of India, attesting to the city's prosperity as a commercial as well as religious hub.

Excavation and submergence

The ruins were discovered in 1926 by A. R. Saraswati. Extensive salvage excavations were carried out by the Archaeological Survey of India between 1954 and 1960, ahead of the construction of the Nagarjuna Sagar Dam. Because the entire valley was to be flooded, the most important monuments were dismantled, reconstructed on the hilltop island that now forms Nagarjunakonda, and many sculptures and artefacts were moved to the island museum, opened in 1966. Smaller relics were relocated to a museum on the mainland at Anupu.

Today the island, accessible by ferry from the dam area, contains reconstructed monasteries, stupas, and temples spanning Buddhist, Hindu, and medieval periods, together with reproductions of megalithic burials and a sound-and-light presentation of the site's history.
