Cefn-coed-y-cymmer

Cefn-coed-y-cymmer (also written Cefn Coed y Cymer) is a small village in the county borough of Merthyr Tydfil, Wales, at the confluence (cymer) of the River Taff and the Nant Morlais, just west of Merthyr Tydfil town. It is best known for the Cefn-Coed Viaduct, one of the largest stone railway viaducts in Wales.

History

The village grew during the industrial revolution as part of the ironworking district around Merthyr Tydfil, then the iron capital of the world. Workers' housing served the nearby Cyfarthfa and Penydarren ironworks, and the Brecon and Merthyr Railway crossed the Taff gorge here.

Cefn-Coed Viaduct

The Cefn-Coed Viaduct, opened in 1866, carries the former railway 35 metres (115 ft) above the River Taff on fifteen stone arches spanning some 238 metres (781 ft), curving across the gorge. Designed by engineers Alexander Sutherland and Henry Conybeare for the Brecon and Merthyr Tydfil Junction Railway, it is the third-largest masonry viaduct in Wales and a Grade II* listed structure. The line closed in the 1960s and the viaduct now carries the Taff Trail, the long-distance walking and cycling route between Cardiff and Brecon.
