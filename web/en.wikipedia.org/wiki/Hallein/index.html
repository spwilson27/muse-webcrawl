Hallein is a historic town in the Austrian state of Salzburg, about 15 km south of the city of Salzburg on the Salzach river. It is the capital of the Hallein district (Tennengau) and the second-largest town in the state.

History

Hallein's wealth rested on salt: the Dürrnberg salt mine above the town has been worked since Celtic times, and salt extraction shaped the town from the Middle Ages until the mine closed in 1989. The Celtic Museum (Keltenmuseum) in Hallein displays finds from the Dürrnberg, including a famous beaked flagon.

Culture

Franz Xaver Gruber, composer of "Silent Night", lived and worked in Hallein and is buried there; the Silent Night Museum occupies his former home. The former salt works on Pernerinsel island now host productions of the Salzburg Festival.
