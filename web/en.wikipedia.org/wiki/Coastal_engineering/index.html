Coastal engineering

Coastal engineering is a branch of civil engineering concerned with the management of coastal areas and the construction of works in and near the sea. It combines knowledge of hydrodynamics, sediment transport, geomorphology, and structural engineering to address coastal erosion, flooding, harbour and marina construction, and the protection of shorelines.

Topics

Coastal engineers study wave mechanics, tides, storm surge, and longshore drift, and how these processes move sediment and reshape beaches and cliffs. Interventions are often divided into "hard" and "soft" engineering. Hard structures include seawalls, breakwaters, groynes, revetments, and storm surge barriers such as the Thames Barrier and the Dutch Delta Works. Soft engineering includes beach nourishment, dune restoration, and managed retreat, which work with natural processes and are often favoured for environmental reasons.

Challenges

Climate change and sea-level rise have made coastal defence a growing concern: rising seas increase erosion and flood risk for low-lying cities and deltas worldwide. Modern practice increasingly integrates numerical modelling, remote sensing, and ecosystem-based approaches such as the restoration of salt marshes and mangroves as natural buffers.
