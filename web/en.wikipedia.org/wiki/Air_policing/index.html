Air policing

Air policing is the use of interceptor aircraft, and in some contexts surface-to-air missiles, as a peacetime activity to maintain the integrity of a state's airspace and enforce air law. It involves quick reaction alert aircraft scrambling to intercept, identify and, if necessary, escort aircraft that enter controlled airspace without permission, fail to respond to radio calls, or are suspected of hostile intent.

NATO air policing

NATO air policing is a collective peacetime mission safeguarding the airspace of Alliance members. Well-known examples include Baltic Air Policing, under which allies deploy fighters on rotation to Šiauliai Air Base in Lithuania to protect the airspace of Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, which lack their own fighter aircraft; Benelux air policing; Icelandic Air Policing; and Slovenian and Albanian arrangements. Intercepts of Russian military aircraft near allied airspace are a regular feature of the mission.

Other applications

Air policing also refers historically to the colonial use of air power for internal security, notably by the Royal Air Force in Iraq and elsewhere in the 1920s and 1930s.
