Citizenship education

Citizenship education is the teaching of the knowledge, skills and values that prepare people to participate actively in democratic civic life. It typically covers government and law, rights and responsibilities, voting and political processes, media literacy, and community engagement. The concept overlaps with, and is sometimes called, civics education or education for democratic citizenship.

Approaches

Approaches range from formal instruction in civics and national history to participatory methods such as mock elections, school councils, debates and service learning. International bodies including the Council of Europe and UNESCO promote "global citizenship education", which extends the framework to human rights, sustainability and intercultural understanding.

By country

In England, citizenship became a compulsory subject in secondary schools in 2002 following the Crick Report (1998). In the United States, civics requirements vary by state; civic knowledge and youth voter turnout are recurring policy concerns. International comparative studies, such as the IEA's International Civic and Citizenship Education Study (ICCS), assess students' civic knowledge and attitudes across countries.

Criticisms

Scholars debate whether curricula stress passive obedience over critical engagement, how national identity should be taught in diverse societies, and whether classroom instruction translates into adult participation.
