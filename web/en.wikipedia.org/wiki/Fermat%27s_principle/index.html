Fermat's principle

Fermat's principle, also known as the principle of least time, is the principle, first stated by Pierre de Fermat in the 17th century, that the path taken by a ray of light between two given points is the path that can be traveled in the least time.

Derivation of the laws of optics

From Fermat's principle one can derive the law of reflection (angle of incidence equals angle of reflection) and Snell's law of refraction, by minimizing the optical path length between two points. In its modern form the principle is one of stationary time: the optical path is stationary with respect to small variations, not necessarily a strict minimum.

Relation to other principles

Fermat's principle is an early example of a variational principle in physics, anticipating the principle of least action in mechanics.
