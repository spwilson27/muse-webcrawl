Dragutin Dimitrijević

Born 17 August 1876, Belgrade, Serbia
Died 24 June 1917 (aged 40), Salonica, Greece
Nickname Apis
Allegiance Kingdom of Serbia
Rank Colonel

Dragutin Dimitrijević (Serbian Cyrillic: Драгутин Димитријевић; 17 August 1876 – 24 June 1917), known by his nickname Apis, was a Serbian army officer and a leading figure in the secret nationalist organisation the Black Hand. He played a central role in the 1903 May Coup that overthrew the Obrenović dynasty in Serbia.

Career

Dimitrijević was among the conspirators who assassinated King Alexander I and Queen Draga in 1903, bringing the Karađorđević dynasty to the throne. In 1911 he helped found Ujedinjenje ili smrt ("Union or Death"), the Black Hand, which sought the unification of all Serbs. As head of the intelligence section of the Serbian General Staff, he was connected to the network that armed and assisted the young assassins who murdered Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 — the event that precipitated the First World War. The precise extent of his involvement remains debated by historians.

Death

Arrested by the Serbian government-in-exile on charges of plotting against Prince Regent Alexander, Dimitrijević was convicted at the Salonica Trial and executed by firing squad on 24 June 1917. In 1953 a Yugoslav court posthumously retried and acquitted him.
