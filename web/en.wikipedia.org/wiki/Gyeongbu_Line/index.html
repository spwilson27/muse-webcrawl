Gyeongbu Line

The Gyeongbu Line (Korean: 경부선) is the most important railway line in South Korea, connecting Seoul Station in the capital with Busan Station in the country's second city and principal port. Operated by Korail, it carries a dense mix of passenger services — including Mugunghwa and Saemaeul local and intercity trains — and freight traffic along the nation's most populous corridor.

History

Construction of the line began under the Korean Empire, and the Seoul–Busan route was completed in 1905, shortly before Korea became a Japanese protectorate. Under Japanese rule it formed the trunk of the peninsula's railway network, linking to ferries for Japan at Busan and northward to Manchuria. The line suffered heavy damage during the Korean War (1950–1953) and was subsequently rebuilt, double-tracked and fully electrified by the mid-1980s.

Services and upgrading

The opening of the Gyeongbu high-speed railway (KTX) in phases from 2004 diverted most long-distance express traffic to a new alignment, but the conventional Gyeongbu Line remains heavily used for regional passenger trains, commuter services in the Seoul metropolitan area (where it shares tracks with Seoul Subway Line 1), and freight. Major stations include Seoul, Yongsan, Suwon, Cheonan, Daejeon, Dongdaegu, Gumi and Busan. Sections of the line through Daejeon and Daegu have been progressively elevated or relocated to ease urban congestion.
