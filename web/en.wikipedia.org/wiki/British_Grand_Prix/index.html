The British Grand Prix is a Formula One motor race held annually in Great Britain. It is one of the oldest continuously staged Grands Prix: the first was held at Brooklands in 1926, and the race at Silverstone on 13 May 1950 was the very first race of the inaugural Formula One World Championship, won by Giuseppe Farina for Alfa Romeo.

History

After Brooklands, the race moved between Donington Park, Silverstone, Aintree and Brands Hatch. From 1987 Silverstone became the permanent home, although the event's future was repeatedly in doubt: a move to Donington was announced for 2010 but collapsed, and in 2017 the British Racing Drivers' Club activated a break clause in its contract before a new long-term deal was agreed in 2019.

Notable races

Mansell-mania peaked in 1992, when Nigel Mansell won dominantly and gave a lift on his slowing-down lap to Ayrton Senna in 1991. The rain-soaked 2008 race announced Lewis Hamilton's mastery in wet conditions. Hamilton has won the race more times than any other driver, including a remarkable 2019 victory and the controversial 2021 race that followed his first-lap collision with Max Verstappen.

The circuit

Silverstone, built on a former RAF bomber station, is famed for the high-speed Maggotts–Becketts–Chapel sequence and Copse corner. The current "Arena" layout, in use since 2010–11, is 5.891 km long and the race runs over 52 laps.
