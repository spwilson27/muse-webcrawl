Town and Country Planning Act 1990

Long title: An Act to consolidate certain enactments relating to town and country planning in England and Wales
Citation: 1990 c. 8
Royal assent: 24 May 1990
Territorial extent: England and Wales

The Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (c. 8) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom regulating the development of land in England and Wales. It is the central piece of legislation underpinning the planning system in those countries, consolidating earlier statutes reaching back to the Town and Country Planning Act 1947, which first required planning permission for development.

Provisions

Part II of the Act provides for the preparation of development plans by local planning authorities. Part III covers control over development: section 55 defines "development" (building, engineering, mining or other operations in, on, over or under land, or a material change of use), and section 57 provides that planning permission is required for development. Section 106 allows planning obligations ("section 106 agreements") under which developers agree to contributions such as affordable housing or infrastructure.

The Act also deals with enforcement (Part VII), including enforcement notices and breach of condition notices, listed buildings being covered by the companion Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990.

Amendments

The Act has been extensively amended, notably by the Planning and Compensation Act 1991, the Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act 2004 (which restructured plan-making), and the Localism Act 2011 (which introduced neighbourhood planning).
