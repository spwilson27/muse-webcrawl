The Northrop B-2 Spirit, also known as the Stealth Bomber, is an American heavy strategic bomber, featuring low-observable stealth technology designed to penetrate dense anti-aircraft defenses. A subsonic flying wing with a crew of two, the plane was designed by Northrop (later Northrop Grumman).

Development

The B-2 originated in the classified "Advanced Technology Bomber" program of the late 1970s and was publicly unveiled in 1988. Originally planned at 132 aircraft, production was cut after the Cold War to 21 aircraft. The bomber's unit cost — roughly $2.1 billion including development — made it the most expensive aircraft ever built.

Design

The B-2 is a pure flying wing with blended edges and radar-absorbent materials and coatings that give it a very small radar cross-section. Four General Electric F118 non-afterburning turbofans are buried within the wing. The aircraft is inherently unstable and is flown with a quadruplex fly-by-wire system. It can carry up to 40,000 lb (18,000 kg) of ordnance, including both conventional precision-guided bombs and nuclear weapons such as the B61 and B83.

Operational history

B-2s are based at Whiteman Air Force Base, Missouri. The type made its combat debut in the Kosovo War in 1999 and has flown missions over Afghanistan, Iraq, Libya, and Yemen, often on global strike sorties lasting more than 30 hours with aerial refueling. The fleet is expected to be replaced by the B-21 Raider in the 2030s.

Specifications

Crew: 2. Length: 69 ft (21 m). Wingspan: 172 ft (52.4 m). Maximum speed: Mach 0.95. Range: about 6,000 nmi (11,000 km) unrefueled. Service ceiling: 50,000 ft (15,200 m).
