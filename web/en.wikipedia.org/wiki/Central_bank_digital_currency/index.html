Central bank digital currency

A central bank digital currency (CBDC) is a digital form of a country's fiat currency that is a direct liability of the central bank. Unlike commercial bank deposits or electronic money issued by private firms, a CBDC represents a claim on the central bank itself, in the same sense as physical banknotes and coins, but held and transferred in digital form.

Types

CBDCs are commonly divided into retail (or general-purpose) CBDCs, available to households and businesses for everyday payments, and wholesale CBDCs, restricted to financial institutions for settling large-value interbank transactions. Designs vary: accounts may be held directly at the central bank or through intermediaries in a "two-tier" model, and the underlying technology may use distributed ledger technology or conventional centralized databases.

Implementations

Among the first live retail CBDCs were the Bahamas' Sand Dollar (2020), Nigeria's eNaira (2021), and Jamaica's JAM-DEX (2022). China's e-CNY, piloted from 2020, is the largest CBDC project of a major economy, while the European Central Bank has investigated a digital euro and many other central banks — well over one hundred jurisdictions in total, according to tracking by the Atlantic Council — have conducted research, pilots, or proofs of concept.

Debates

Proponents argue CBDCs can improve payment efficiency, financial inclusion, resilience of payment systems, and the transmission of monetary policy, and can counter the risks of private cryptocurrencies and foreign digital currencies. Critics raise concerns about privacy and surveillance, the risk of disintermediating commercial banks and accelerating bank runs in crises, cybersecurity, and the costs of implementation.
