Town square

A town square (or public square, plaza, or market square) is an open public space, usually rectangular, found in the heart of a town and used for community gatherings. Town squares are typically surrounded by shops, civic buildings such as the town hall, and places of worship, and often contain a market cross, fountain, war memorial or statue at their centre.

The town square descends from the agora of ancient Greek cities and the forum of Roman towns, which combined commercial, political and social functions. In medieval Europe the market square was laid out at the foundation of a town and served as its commercial heart, hosting weekly markets and fairs.

Functions

Squares have historically been used for markets, public announcements, ceremonies, festivals, protests and, in earlier times, public punishments. They remain focal points for civic life: examples include Red Square in Moscow, St Mark's Square in Venice, the Rynek Główny in Kraków — one of the largest medieval town squares in Europe — and Trafalgar Square in London.

By region

In Spain and Latin America the equivalent is the plaza mayor or zócalo; in German towns the Marktplatz; and in New England the town common or village green serves a similar role. Colonial American courthouse squares developed their own distinctive form in the southern and midwestern United States.
