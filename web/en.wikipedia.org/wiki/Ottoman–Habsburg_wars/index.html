Date1526 – 1791
LocationHungary, the Balkans, the Mediterranean

The Ottoman–Habsburg wars were a series of conflicts between the Ottoman Empire and the Habsburg monarchy—initially both its Spanish and Austrian branches—fought from the 1520s to the 1790s over control of Hungary, the western Balkans and the Mediterranean. For two centuries the military frontier between the two empires ran across Croatia and Hungary, shaping the development of south-eastern Europe.

Ottoman advance, 1526–1566

The Ottoman victory at the Battle of Mohács in 1526 destroyed the medieval Kingdom of Hungary and brought Suleiman the Magnificent to the gates of Vienna, besieged unsuccessfully in 1529. Central Hungary became an Ottoman province, Transylvania an Ottoman vassal, and a strip of "Royal Hungary" remained under the Habsburgs, whose Spanish branch fought the Ottomans at sea, most famously at the Battle of Lepanto in 1571.

Stalemate and the turn, 1568–1699

The Long Turkish War (1593–1606) ended in stalemate confirmed by the Peace of Zsitvatorok. The balance shifted decisively after the failed Ottoman siege of Vienna in 1683: the Holy League counter-offensive of the Great Turkish War (1683–1699) retook Buda and most of Hungary, and the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699) formally ceded them to Austria.

Later wars

Further wars followed: the Austro-Turkish War of 1716–1718, in which Prince Eugene of Savoy won at Petrovaradin and Belgrade (Treaty of Passarowitz); the war of 1737–1739, which returned Belgrade to the Ottomans; and the last Habsburg–Ottoman war of 1788–1791, closed by the Treaty of Sistova. Thereafter the two declining empires increasingly faced common threats, and the frontier stabilised.
