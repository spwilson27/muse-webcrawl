Triduum

A triduum is a religious observance lasting three days. The best-known is the Paschal Triduum (or Easter Triduum), the summit of the Christian liturgical year, which recalls the Passion, Death, burial and Resurrection of Jesus Christ. In the Roman Catholic Church and many other Western churches it begins with the evening Mass of the Lord's Supper on Maundy Thursday, continues through Good Friday and Holy Saturday, and reaches its climax in the Easter Vigil, concluding with evening prayer on Easter Sunday.

The Paschal Triduum

Though counted as three days, the Triduum is liturgically a single celebration. Maundy Thursday commemorates the Last Supper and the institution of the Eucharist, including the washing of feet. Good Friday observes the crucifixion with the reading of the Passion, veneration of the cross and a communion service without a Mass. Holy Saturday is a day of quiet waiting, ended after dark by the Easter Vigil: the lighting of the Paschal candle, a series of readings tracing salvation history, baptism and confirmation of new Christians, and the first Eucharist of Easter.

Other triduums

The word also denotes any three-day period of prayer or preparation, such as a triduum before a saint's feast or in honour of a particular devotion, common in Catholic parishes and schools.
