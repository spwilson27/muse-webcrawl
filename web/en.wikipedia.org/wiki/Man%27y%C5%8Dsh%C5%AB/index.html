The Man'yōshū (Japanese: 万葉集, "Collection of Ten Thousand Leaves") is the oldest existing collection of Japanese poetry, compiled sometime after AD 759 during the Nara period. It contains more than 4,500 poems in twenty books.

Contents

The poems span roughly 130 years, from the early 7th century to 759. They include long poems (chōka), short poems (tanka), and the head-repeated sedōka form, attributed to emperors, courtiers, soldiers, farmers, and anonymous authors. Ōtomo no Yakamochi, whose own poetry forms a substantial part of the final books, is generally regarded as the collection's final compiler.

Man'yōgana

The anthology is written in man'yōgana, an early system using Chinese characters for their phonetic values to record Japanese — the writing practice from which the kana syllabaries later developed.

Significance

The Man'yōshū occupies a foundational place in Japanese literature for its emotional directness and breadth of social voices. The name of Japan's Reiwa era, announced in 2019, was drawn from a passage in its fifth book — the first era name taken from a Japanese rather than a Chinese classic.
