Strain (injury)

A strain is an acute or chronic soft tissue injury that occurs to a muscle, tendon, or both. The comparable injury to a ligament is a sprain. A strain generally occurs as a result of fatigue, overuse, or improper use of a muscle, and can result from any activity, including work or sports, or sudden trauma.

Signs and symptoms

Typical symptoms include pain, muscle spasm, loss of strength, and limited range of motion. Severe strains in which the muscle or tendon is partially or completely torn may be very painful and disabling, with swelling and bruising. Strains are graded from mild (first-degree, overstretched fibres) through partial tears (second-degree) to complete rupture (third-degree).

Causes and common sites

Acute strains often occur during eccentric contraction — when a muscle lengthens under load — as in sprinting or lifting. The hamstrings, quadriceps, gastrocnemius ("tennis leg"), groin muscles, rotator cuff, and lower back are frequent sites. Chronic strains result from repetitive overuse. Risk factors include inadequate warm-up, muscle fatigue and imbalance, previous injury, and poor conditioning.

Treatment

First aid follows rest, ice, compression, and elevation, with protection of the injured part. Diagnosis is usually clinical, supplemented by ultrasound or MRI for severe cases. Most strains heal with progressive rehabilitation emphasising controlled loading and strengthening; complete tendon ruptures may require surgery. Prevention centres on conditioning, flexibility, and gradual increases in training load.
