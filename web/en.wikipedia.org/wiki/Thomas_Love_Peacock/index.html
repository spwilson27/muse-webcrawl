Thomas Love Peacock (18 October 1785 – 23 January 1866) was an English novelist, poet, and official of the East India Company. He was a close friend of Percy Bysshe Shelley, and they influenced each other's work.

Novels of talk

Peacock's mature novels are satirical 'novels of talk' in which eccentric characters, each embodying an idea or obsession, converse at country houses: Headlong Hall (1815), Melincourt, Nightmare Abbey (1818, gently satirising Shelley and Byron), Maid Marian, The Misfortunes of Elphin, Crotchet Castle and his late masterpiece Gryll Grange (1861). His essay "The Four Ages of Poetry" provoked Shelley's A Defence of Poetry.

Career

From 1819 Peacock worked at India House, succeeding James Mill and overseeing dispatches on finance and steam navigation; he championed steamship routes to India.
