Chhattisgarhi is an Indo-Aryan language spoken by about 16 million people in the Indian state of Chhattisgarh and neighbouring areas of Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, and Maharashtra. It belongs to the Eastern Hindi group and is written in the Devanagari script. In 2007 Chhattisgarh designated it an additional official language of the state, and there is a continuing movement for its inclusion in the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution.

Dialects

Chhattisgarhi varieties include Surgujia in the north, varieties of the central Mahanadi plain, and southern forms influenced by Odia and Marathi. Grammarian George Grierson classified it under Eastern Hindi in the Linguistic Survey of India.

Literature and media

The language has a rich folk tradition of songs and epics such as the Pandwani narrative singing, made famous by Teejan Bai. Modern Chhattisgarhi cinema ("Chhollywood"), All India Radio broadcasts, and newspapers in the language have grown in recent decades.
