Rachel (Hebrew: רָחֵל, "ewe") is a figure in the Book of Genesis, the younger daughter of Laban and the beloved wife of the patriarch Jacob. She is the mother of Joseph and Benjamin and one of the four matriarchs of Israel.

Biblical narrative

Jacob meets Rachel at a well in Haran and agrees to work seven years for her hand; deceived by Laban into marrying her elder sister Leah first, he works seven more. Rachel, long barren while Leah bears many sons, eventually gives birth to Joseph. She dies giving birth to Benjamin on the road to Ephrath and is buried there, near Bethlehem. Her tomb has been a place of pilgrimage for Jews, Christians and Muslims for centuries.

Legacy

The prophet Jeremiah pictures Rachel weeping for her exiled children — an image quoted in the Gospel of Matthew in connection with the Massacre of the Innocents. In Jewish tradition Rachel is a symbol of maternal compassion and intercession.
