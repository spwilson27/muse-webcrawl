Jameson Irish Whiskey

Jameson is a blended Irish whiskey produced by Irish Distillers, a subsidiary of Pernod Ricard. It is the world's best-selling Irish whiskey, with annual sales of over 8 million cases.

History

The company was founded by John Jameson, a Scotsman who married into the Haig whisky family and in 1780 took over the Bow Street Distillery in Dublin, founded in 1780 by previous owners. "John Jameson & Son" grew into one of the giants of Irish distilling. The Irish industry collapsed in the early 20th century under the effects of Prohibition in the United States, trade wars with Britain and competition from blended Scotch. In 1966 Jameson merged with Cork Distilleries and John Power to form Irish Distillers; the new Midleton distillery in County Cork opened in 1975, and production of Jameson moved there.

Production

Jameson is a blend of pot still whiskey, made from both malted and unmalted barley, and grain whiskey, all triple-distilled and matured in oak casks for at least four years. The core expression is bottled at 40% ABV and is known for its smoothness and notes of vanilla, spice and sherry fruit.

Sales

Following Pernod Ricard's acquisition of Irish Distillers in 1988, Jameson was marketed aggressively abroad, particularly in the United States and South Africa, becoming one of the world's fastest-growing whiskey brands.
