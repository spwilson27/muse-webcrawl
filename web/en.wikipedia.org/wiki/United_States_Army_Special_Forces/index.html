The United States Army Special Forces (SF), colloquially known as the "Green Berets" due to their distinctive service headgear, are a special operations force of the United States Army. Their core mission is unconventional warfare: working by, with, and through indigenous forces in denied or politically sensitive areas.

Missions

Special Forces soldiers are trained in unconventional warfare, foreign internal defense, special reconnaissance, direct action, counter-terrorism, counter-proliferation, and information operations. Their hallmark capability is building and advising partner forces, which requires intensive language and cultural training for a specific region of the world.

History

The first Special Forces unit, the 10th Special Forces Group (Airborne), was activated at Fort Bragg, North Carolina, on 19 June 1952 under Colonel Aaron Bank, drawing on the traditions of the wartime Office of Strategic Services and the First Special Service Force. Special Forces expanded during the early 1960s under President John F. Kennedy, who authorized the green beret as their headgear in 1961 and championed them as counterinsurgency specialists. Green Berets served extensively in the Vietnam War (including in the MACV-SOG reconnaissance program), and later in El Salvador, Panama, the Gulf War, Afghanistan, and Iraq.

Organization

There are five active-duty Special Forces Groups (1st, 3rd, 5th, 7th, and 10th) and two Army National Guard groups (19th and 20th), each oriented toward a geographic combatant command. The basic operational unit is the 12-man Special Forces Operational Detachment Alpha (ODA, or "A-Team"), led by a captain (18A) and a warrant officer, with two specialists in each of several fields: weapons (18B), engineering (18C), medical (18D), and communications (18E). Selection and training — the "Q Course" — is conducted by the John F. Kennedy Special Warfare Center and School at Fort Liberty.
