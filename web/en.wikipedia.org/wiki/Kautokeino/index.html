Kautokeino (Northern Sámi: Guovdageaidnu) is a municipality in Finnmark county, Norway. One of Norway's largest municipalities by area but sparsely populated, it is a major centre of Sámi culture: the great majority of its roughly 2,900 inhabitants are Sámi, and Northern Sámi is an official language alongside Norwegian.

Culture and institutions

Kautokeino hosts the Sámi University of Applied Sciences (Sámi allaskuvla), the Sámi National Theatre Beaivváš, and the Sámi Easter Festival, famous for reindeer races, snowmobile races, and ice-fishing competitions. Reindeer herding remains a central livelihood, and the village of Kautokeino on the Altaelva river serves as a winter gathering point for herding families.

History

The municipality is remembered for the Kautokeino rebellion of 1852, in which a group of Sámi influenced by Laestadian revivalism attacked officials and merchants; its leaders were executed and the event was later dramatised in the 2008 film The Kautokeino Rebellion. In the late 1970s, protests against the damming of the Alta–Kautokeino river system, which began here, became a landmark of Norwegian environmental and Sámi-rights politics.

Geography

The municipality covers a vast inland plateau (part of the Finnmarksvidda), with hundreds of lakes and the upper course of the Altaelva. Winters are long and among the coldest in Norway; the village lies about 100 km south of Alta.
