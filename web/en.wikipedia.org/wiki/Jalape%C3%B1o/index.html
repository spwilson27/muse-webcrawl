Jalapeño

SpeciesCapsicum annuum
OriginMexico (named for Xalapa, Veracruz)
HeatMedium — about 2,500–8,000 Scoville units
Ripe formChipotle (smoke-dried)

The jalapeño is a medium-sized chili pepper cultivar of the species Capsicum annuum. Named after Xalapa (Jalapa), the capital of the Mexican state of Veracruz, it is one of the most widely grown and eaten chili peppers in the world, a staple of Mexican and Tex-Mex cuisine.

Characteristics

The fruit is usually harvested while green, when it is about 5–10 cm long, firm and glossy; left to ripen it turns red, and ripe red jalapeños that are smoke-dried are sold as chipotles. Its heat is moderate, typically ranging from about 2,500 to 8,000 Scoville heat units, though it varies with growing conditions.

Culinary use

Jalapeños are eaten fresh in salsas and salads, pickled (often as sliced rings), stuffed (as in jalapeño poppers), and used to flavour sauces, nachos and countless other dishes. Mexico grows the great majority of the crop, particularly in Veracruz, Chihuahua and the Papaloapan basin; the pepper was also famously taken into space aboard NASA missions as a condiment.
