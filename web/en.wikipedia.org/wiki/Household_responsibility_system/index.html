Household responsibility system

The household responsibility system (Chinese: 家庭联产承包责任制), or contract responsibility system, was an economic reform practice adopted in agriculture in China from 1978–1979 onward, under which individual households contracted with the collective (later the village) for land and production quotas, keeping output above the quota for themselves or for sale. It effectively dismantled the people's communes and became the cornerstone of Deng Xiaoping-era rural reform.

Origins

The practice began secretly in 1978 in Xiaogang Village, Fengyang County, Anhui, where 18 households signed a clandestine pact dividing collective land among families. Grain output surged, and after initial controversy the experiment won the support of reformers including Wan Li and Deng Xiaoping. In 1982 the central government's Document No. 1 officially endorsed the system, and by the end of 1983 nearly all production teams had adopted it; the commune system was formally abolished by 1984.

Effects

Agricultural output grew at roughly 7–8 percent annually in 1978–84, far above the preceding decades, and studies attribute a large share of the gain to the incentive effects of the household system. Freed surplus labour migrated to township and village enterprises, fuelling China's broader reform takeoff. Land remained collectively owned by the village, contracted to households — initially for short terms, later extended to 30 years and beyond.
