Düsseldorf is the capital city of North Rhine-Westphalia, the most populous state of Germany. It is the second-largest city in the state and the seventh-largest city in Germany, with a population of about 630,000. Düsseldorf lies at the confluence of the Rhine and its tributary the Düssel, in the centre of the Rhine-Ruhr metropolitan region.

History

First mentioned in 1135, Düsseldorf received town privileges in 1288 from Count Adolf V of Berg after the Battle of Worringen. It grew as the residence of the dukes of Berg and later of the Electoral Palatinate; under Johann Wilhelm II ("Jan Wellem") it became a notable centre of art and culture in the late 17th and early 18th centuries. The city was heavily bombed in World War II and rebuilt after becoming the state capital in 1946.

Economy and culture

Düsseldorf is a major centre for finance, fashion, trade fairs (Messe Düsseldorf), telecommunications, and advertising, and hosts a large Japanese community, the biggest in Germany. The Königsallee ("Kö") is a famous luxury shopping boulevard. The Altstadt (Old Town) is nicknamed "the longest bar in the world" for its concentration of pubs serving the local Altbier. The city is known for its carnival (Karneval), its art academy (Kunstakademie Düsseldorf), the Rheinturm telecommunications tower, and the MedienHafen harbour district with buildings by Frank Gehry.
