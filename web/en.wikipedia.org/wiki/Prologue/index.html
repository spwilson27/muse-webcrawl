Prologue

A prologue or prolog (from Greek πρόλογος, prologos, from πρό, pro, "before" and λόγος, logos, "word") is an opening to a story that establishes the context and gives background details, often some earlier story that ties into the main one, and other miscellaneous information.

History

In ancient Greek drama, the prologue was the opening portion of the play before the entrance of the chorus, delivered by an actor who explained the situation and background. The prologue was used extensively in Latin drama and in medieval and Renaissance theatre; Geoffrey Chaucer's Canterbury Tales features a famous General Prologue, and Shakespeare's plays include prologues spoken by a chorus, as in Romeo and Juliet and Henry V.

In modern works

In novels and films, a prologue may present events that occurred before the main narrative, introduce a frame story, or hook the reader with a dramatic scene. Its opposite is the epilogue, which concludes or comments on the work.
