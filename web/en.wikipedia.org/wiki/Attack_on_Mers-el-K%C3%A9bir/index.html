Attack on Mers-el-Kébir
Date3 July 1940
LocationMers-el-Kébir, French Algeria
ResultBritish victory; French fleet largely neutralised

The attack on Mers-el-Kébir (Operation Catapult) was a British naval attack on French Navy ships at the port of Mers-el-Kébir near Oran, on the coast of French Algeria, on 3 July 1940, during the Second World War.

Background

After the Franco-German armistice of June 1940, Britain feared that the powerful French fleet might fall into German hands. The British gave the French squadron at Mers-el-Kébir several options, including joining the British, sailing to a British or neutral port, or scuttling; when negotiations failed, Force H under Vice-Admiral James Somerville opened fire.

Outcome

The battleship Bretagne exploded and sank, and the battlecruiser Dunkerque and battleship Provence were badly damaged; the battlecruiser Strasbourg escaped to Toulon. Nearly 1,300 French sailors were killed. The attack severely strained Anglo-French relations and was used extensively in Vichy and German propaganda.
