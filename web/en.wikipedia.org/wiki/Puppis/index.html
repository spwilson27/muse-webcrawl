Puppis

Puppis (Latin for "the stern" or "poop deck") is a constellation in the southern sky. It was originally part of the much larger constellation Argo Navis, the ship of Jason and the Argonauts, which the astronomer Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille divided in 1752 into Puppis (the stern), Carina (the keel), and Vela (the sails). Puppis is the largest of the three. It contains no star brighter than second magnitude; its brightest star is Naos (Zeta Puppis), a hot blue supergiant and one of the most luminous stars in the Milky Way.

Notable features

Because the Milky Way runs through Puppis, the constellation is rich in open clusters and nebulae, including the open clusters Messier 46, Messier 47, and Messier 93. M46 contains the planetary nebula NGC 2438. Pi Puppis is the brightest star of the Pi Puppids meteor shower, and the constellation also hosts the unusual hypergiant and luminous blue variable systems, as well as the neutron star RX J0822-4300, a candidate "cosmic cannonball" moving at exceptionally high speed.

Neighbouring constellations

Puppis borders Canis Major to the west — Sirius, the night sky's brightest star, lies near its western edge — and Carina and Vela to the south and east, with Pyxis, Hydra, Monoceros, and Columba also adjoining it.
