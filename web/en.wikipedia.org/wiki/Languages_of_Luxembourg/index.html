The languages of Luxembourg are characterized by an institutionalised multilingualism unusual in Europe. The Grand Duchy has three administrative languages — Luxembourgish, French, and German — allocated to different domains of public life, while its citizens move between them in everyday practice, a situation often described as functional trilingualism.

Legal status

Under the Language Law of 24 February 1984, Luxembourgish (Lëtzebuergesch), a Moselle Franconian language closely related to German, is the national language; French is the language of legislation; and Luxembourgish, French, and German may all be used in administration and the courts, with citizens entitled to be answered in the language they use. A 2018 law further promoted Luxembourgish, including a commissioner for the language and standardisation of spelling.

Domains of use

Luxembourgish is the dominant spoken language of daily life, media chat shows, and increasingly of texting and social media. German predominates in early primary schooling literacy and much of the print press; French dominates secondary and legal-administrative writing, commerce in many sectors, and communication with the large cross-border workforce. Education is famously bi- and trilingual: children are first taught to read and write in German, with French introduced soon after, and secondary subjects split between the two — a system debated for its demands on immigrant children.

Immigrant and other languages

With nearly half of residents being foreign nationals, many languages are spoken. Portuguese is by far the largest immigrant language, reflecting a community that is the biggest minority in the country; Italian and the languages of former Yugoslavia are also established, and English is widely used in the international financial sector and among EU institution staff in Luxembourg City, seat of several European Union bodies. Luxembourgish Sign Language was recognised in 2018.
