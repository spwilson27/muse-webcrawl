Dubbel

A dubbel (also double) is a Belgian beer style: a strong brown ale, typically of 6–7.5% alcohol by volume, characterised by a malty sweetness, dark fruit flavours (raisin, plum, fig), and a dry finish. The name means "double" in Dutch and Flemish, in reference to its strength relative to a brewery's everyday beer.

Origins

The style is most associated with Trappist breweries. Westmalle's Dubbel, first brewed in the 19th century and reformulated in 1926, is considered the defining commercial example; its success popularised the naming convention by which abbey and Trappist breweries grade their beers as enkel (single), dubbel, tripel, and sometimes quadrupel. Earlier monastic breweries brewed stronger "double" beers for special occasions and export, but Westmalle fixed the modern profile: a reddish-brown ale brewed with dark candi sugar, which lightens the body while adding caramel and dried-fruit flavours.

Characteristics

  • Appearance: dark amber to brown, with a large, dense, tan head.
  • Aroma and flavour: rich malt, caramel, dark candi sugar, raisins and other dark fruit esters, hints of spice and alcohol warmth; hop bitterness low to moderate, keeping the finish drier than the aroma suggests.
  • Strength: typically 6–7.6% ABV.

Examples

Notable examples include Westmalle Dubbel, Chimay Red (Première), Rochefort 6, La Trappe Dubbel, and the abbey-style dubbels brewed by secular breweries such as Affligem and St. Bernardus; American craft versions include Ommegang Abbey Ale and Allagash Dubbel.
