Prusa Research

Prusa Research is a Czech 3D printer manufacturer based in Prague, founded in 2012 by Josef Průša, a prominent figure of the RepRap open-source movement. The company is known for its "Original Prusa" line of FDM 3D printers and for releasing its designs, firmware and software under open-source licences. It grew from a one-person operation into one of the largest 3D printer manufacturers in the world, employing over a thousand people and shipping hundreds of thousands of printers.

History

Josef Průša began selling printer kits (the "Prusa Mendel") around 2010–2012. The Prusa i3 design of 2012 became one of the most cloned 3D printers ever. The Original Prusa i3 MK2 (2016) introduced automatic mesh bed levelling and a magnetic heatbed in later versions (MK2.5/MK3, 2017), winning widespread industry awards. The company expanded into resin printing with the Original Prusa SL1 (2018), launched the smaller MINI/MINI+ (2019), the large-format XL with tool changer, and the MK4 (2023) featuring a next-generation extruder, loadcell bed probing and input-shaper speed. In 2024 it released the enclosed Core One.

Business and community

Prusa Research manufactures in-house in Prague, including operating a large "print farm" of hundreds of its own printers producing parts for new machines — one of the largest 3D printing farms in the world. The company develops the PrusaSlicer slicing software (based on Slic3r) and operates Printables.com (formerly PrusaPrinters), a model-sharing community launched to rival Thingiverse. It also sells filaments under the Prusament brand, manufactured in its own facility for tight diameter tolerances.

COVID-19 response

During the COVID-19 pandemic the company famously designed and open-sourced a 3D-printed face shield, coordinating production by the maker community worldwide, and delivered large quantities of protective equipment to Czech medical staff.
