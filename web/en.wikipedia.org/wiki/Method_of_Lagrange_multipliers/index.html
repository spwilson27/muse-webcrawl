Method of Lagrange multipliers

In mathematical optimization, the method of Lagrange multipliers is a strategy for finding the local maxima and minima of a function subject to equation constraints (i.e., subject to the condition that one or more equations have to be satisfied exactly by the chosen values of the variables). It is named after the mathematician Joseph-Louis Lagrange.

Idea

The basic idea is to convert a constrained problem into a form such that the derivative test of an unconstrained problem can still be applied. At a constrained extremum, the gradient of the objective function must be parallel to the gradient of the constraint function, because the level curves of the two functions are tangent there.

Statement

To maximize f(x, y) subject to g(x, y) = c, introduce the Lagrangian function:

L(x, y, λ) = f(x, y) − λ (g(x, y) − c)

The critical points of L, found by setting its partial derivatives to zero, include the constrained extrema of f. The scalar λ is called a Lagrange multiplier. The conditions generalize to several constraints and to functions of many variables, and the necessary conditions are a special case of the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions.

Applications

The method is used throughout physics and economics: finding the maximum entropy distribution subject to moment constraints, deriving the equations of constrained mechanics, and in consumer theory, where the multiplier on a budget constraint is interpreted as marginal utility of income. It also underlies support vector machines in machine learning.
