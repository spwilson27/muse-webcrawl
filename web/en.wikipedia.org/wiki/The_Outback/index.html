The Outback

The Outback is a remote and sparsely populated region of Australia, comprising the vast, arid interior of the continent — often called "the bush" — beyond the closely settled coastal fringe where most Australians live. While definitions vary, the Outback is generally taken to cover some 5.6 million square kilometres (about 70 percent of Australia's land area) across the Northern Territory, Western Australia, South Australia, Queensland, and New South Wales, yet it is home to well under 5 percent of the national population.

Geography and climate

The Outback includes great deserts — the Great Victoria, Great Sandy, Tanami, Gibson, and Simpson deserts — along with semi-arid shrublands, spinifex grasslands, and mountain ranges such as the MacDonnell Ranges. Landmarks include Uluru (Ayers Rock) and Kata Tjuta in Uluṙu-Kata Tjuṯa National Park, the opal-mining town of Coober Pedy, where many residents live underground, and Lake Eyre (Kati Thanda), Australia's lowest point and largest lake when filled. Rainfall is low, erratic, and evaporation high; temperatures can exceed 50 °C in summer and drop below freezing on winter nights.

History and people

Aboriginal Australians have inhabited the interior for tens of thousands of years, and many Outback communities remain predominantly Indigenous; Aboriginal languages such as Pitjantjatjara and Arrernte are spoken there, and large areas are Aboriginal-owned land under native title. European exploration in the 19th century (Burke and Wills, John McDouall Stuart) opened the interior to pastoralism, the overland telegraph, and the Afghan cameleers who supplied inland settlements. Enormous cattle stations, some larger than small European countries, characterize the pastoral economy, alongside mining (iron ore, gold, opals, uranium) and tourism.

Life and services

Isolation shapes Outback life: the Royal Flying Doctor Service (founded 1928 by John Flynn) provides medical care by air; the School of the Air delivers lessons by radio and internet; and road trains — trucks pulling multiple trailers — freight goods along highways such as the Stuart and Eyre highways. The term "outback" has become central to Australian national identity and popular culture, appearing in films from Crocodile Dundee to The Adventures of Priscilla, Queen of the Desert.
