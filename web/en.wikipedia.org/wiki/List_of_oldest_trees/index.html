List of oldest trees

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

This is a list of the oldest trees in the world, verified by dendrochronology (tree-ring dating) or radiocarbon dating. A distinction is drawn between individual (non-clonal) trees and clonal colonies, in which many stems arise from one root system and which can be far older.

Oldest clonal colonies

Clonal colonies reproduce asexually, so the genetic individual may be much older than any living stem. Old Tjikko, a Norway spruce in Sweden, grows from roots radiocarbon-dated to about 9,550 years. Pando, a quaking aspen colony in Utah, covers about 43 hectares with some 47,000 stems from a single root system, with age estimates ranging from thousands to tens of thousands of years. King Clone, a creosote bush ring in the Mojave Desert, is estimated at about 11,700 years.

Dating methods and caveats

Ring counting gives the most reliable ages for non-clonal trees, but hollow trunks (common in ancient yews and olives) prevent exact counting, so some famous ages — for olive trees and baobabs — are estimates based on size and growth rates and remain debated.
