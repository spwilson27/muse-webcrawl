John Desmond Bernal

John Desmond Bernal (1901–1971), known as "Sage", was an Irish scientist who pioneered the use of X-ray crystallography in molecular biology and wrote influentially on the social function of science.

Science

Educated at Cambridge, Bernal worked at the Royal Institution under William Henry Bragg and at Cambridge's Cavendish Laboratory before becoming professor at Birkbeck College, London. He determined the structure of graphite, studied the molecular structure of water, sterols and proteins, and mentored a generation including Dorothy Hodgkin, Aaron Klug and Max Perutz.

Science and society

A committed Marxist, Bernal's The Social Function of Science (1939) founded the modern sociology of science. His speculative essay The World, the Flesh and the Devil (1929) anticipated space habitats (the "Bernal sphere"). He was a controversial figure for his defence of the Soviet Union and of Lysenko.
