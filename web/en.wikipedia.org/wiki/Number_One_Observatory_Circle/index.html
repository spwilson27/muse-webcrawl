Number One Observatory Circle

Number One Observatory Circle is the official residence of the Vice President of the United States. Located on the northeast grounds of the United States Naval Observatory in Washington, D.C., the white-painted Queen Anne-style house was built in 1893 for the observatory's superintendent and has housed every vice president since Walter Mondale in 1977.


Address: 1 Observatory Circle NW, Washington, D.C.
Built: 1893
Architect: Leon E. Dessez
Architectural style: Queen Anne

History

The house was designed by architect Leon E. Dessez and built in 1893 at a cost of about $20,000. It originally served as the home of the superintendent of the Naval Observatory, and from 1923 it was used by the Chief of Naval Operations. In 1974, Congress designated it the official temporary residence of the vice president, following security concerns after presidential assassinations; the first vice president to reside there full time was Walter Mondale, under President Jimmy Carter. Before this, vice presidents lived in their own homes in Washington.

Description

The three-storey Victorian house stands on a 13-acre (5.3 ha) wooded site inside the 72-acre observatory grounds in the Glover Park neighbourhood. It contains a living room, dining room, kitchen, library, and several bedrooms, with additions made by successive occupants — a small gym under George H. W. Bush, a swimming pool installed during Dan Quayle's tenure, and redecoration by each new family. Kamala Harris moved in in April 2021 after renovations, becoming the first woman and first person of colour to live in the residence.
