Mount Erebus

Mount Erebus is the second-highest volcano in Antarctica (after Mount Sidley), the highest active volcano in Antarctica, and the southernmost active volcano on Earth. Rising 3,794 metres (12,448 ft) on Ross Island, it is noted for its persistent convecting phonolitic lava lake, one of only a few long-lived lava lakes in the world.

History

The volcano was discovered in 1841 by Sir James Clark Ross, who named it and neighboring Mount Terror after his ships HMS Erebus and HMS Terror. Members of Shackleton's Nimrod Expedition made the first ascent in 1908. The modern eruptive period began in 1972, when the lava lake was rediscovered.

Geology

Erebus is a polygenetic stratovolcano whose anorthoclase-phyric phonolite lavas are unusual in composition. Strombolian eruptions from the lava lake eject distinctive "Erebus crystals" of anorthoclase. The volcano emits measurable quantities of gold-bearing particles in its gas plume.

1979 disaster

On 28 November 1979, Air New Zealand Flight 901, a sightseeing flight, crashed into the slopes of Mount Erebus, killing all 257 people on board — New Zealand's worst peacetime disaster.
