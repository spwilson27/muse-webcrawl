The Port of Algeciras (Spanish: Puerto de Algeciras) is a major seaport in the Bay of Algeciras, in the province of Cádiz, Andalusia, southern Spain. Situated on the northern shore of the Strait of Gibraltar, it is one of the largest ports in Spain and in the Mediterranean, and one of Europe's principal transshipment hubs for container traffic between Asia, Europe, Africa and the Americas.

Facilities

The port complex includes the Isla Verde and Juan Carlos I container terminals, operated with major global terminal companies, extensive ro-ro facilities for the heavy truck and passenger traffic across the strait to Tangier and Ceuta, liquid-bulk terminals serving the nearby refinery and petrochemical plants, and a fishing harbour and marina at La Galera. Its location at the meeting point of the Atlantic and the Mediterranean, on the main east–west shipping lane, makes it a natural first/last port of call in the Mediterranean for deep-sea services.

Traffic

Total traffic exceeds 100 million tonnes a year, with container throughput around 4.7–4.8 million TEU in recent peak years, placing Algeciras among the Mediterranean's top container ports alongside Valencia, Piraeus and Tanger Med, its fast-growing Moroccan competitor directly across the strait. Millions of passengers and vehicles cross between Algeciras and North African ports annually, especially during the summer Operación Paso del Estrecho.

History

Although the bay has been used since Phoenician and Roman times (the ancient port of Portus Albus), the modern port was developed from 1906 with the creation of a port works board, and expanded massively from the 1960s onwards, when containerisation and the industrialisation of the Campo de Gibraltar transformed it into a global hub.
