Scrapbooking

Scrapbooking is a method of preserving, presenting and arranging personal and family history in the form of a book, box or card. Typical memorabilia include photographs, printed media, and artwork. Scrapbook albums are often decorated and frequently contain extensive journal entries or written descriptions.

History

The keeping of commonplace books and friendship albums dates back several centuries. In the 19th century, printed scrap became widely available and albums were filled with chromolithographed images, calling cards and newspaper clippings. Mark Twain patented a self-pasting scrapbook in 1873. The modern hobby took off in the 1980s and 1990s with the availability of acid-free, photo-safe materials and dedicated supplies, after concern grew that older albums' adhesives and papers damaged photographs.

Materials

Basic materials include background papers (patterned and solid cardstock), photo corner mounts, adhesives, scissors and decorative punches and dies. Embellishments include stickers, rub-ons, stamps, eyelets, brads, chipboard elements and ribbon. Preservation-minded scrapbookers use lignin-free, acid-free and buffered papers to protect photographs.

Digital scrapbooking

Digital scrapbooking uses computer software to arrange photographs, journaling and digital embellishments into page layouts, which may be shared online or printed. Hybrid scrapbooking combines printed digital elements with physical albums.
