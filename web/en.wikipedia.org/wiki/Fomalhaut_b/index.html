Distance25 light-years

Fomalhaut b, formally named Dagon, is a former candidate exoplanet and a directly imaged object near the star Fomalhaut, about 25 light-years away in the constellation Piscis Austrinus. Announced in 2008 on the basis of Hubble Space Telescope images, it was celebrated as one of the first exoplanets directly photographed.

Observations and reinterpretation

The object lay inside Fomalhaut's prominent debris ring, at a projected distance of about 115 AU. Oddities accumulated: it was bright in visible light but undetectable in the infrared, suggesting it was not a self-luminous giant planet, and its apparent orbit crossed the ring without disturbing it. In 2020 astronomers concluded that Fomalhaut b had faded and expanded because it was never a planet, but rather the slowly dispersing dust cloud from a collision between two large planetesimals.

Name

The name Dagon, after the ancient Semitic deity, was selected in the IAU's NameExoWorlds campaign in 2015 and remains attached to the object.
