Garrett Birkhoff


Garrett Birkhoff (1911–1996), American mathematician.

Garrett Birkhoff (January 19, 1911 – November 22, 1996) was an American mathematician best known for his work in lattice theory and universal algebra. The son of mathematician George David Birkhoff, he spent almost his entire career at Harvard University, where he joined the faculty in 1936 and became a full professor in 1946. He was a member of the National Academy of Sciences.

Work

Birkhoff founded lattice theory as a major branch of algebra; his representation theorem states that every finite distributive lattice is isomorphic to the lattice of lower sets of a partially ordered set. In 1935 he introduced the concept of a universal algebra, and with John von Neumann he proposed quantum logic in a celebrated 1936 paper. His textbook Lattice Theory (1940) and, with Saunders Mac Lane, A Survey of Modern Algebra (1941) — the first undergraduate abstract algebra text in English — shaped generations of students.

Later career

During the Second World War and afterwards, Birkhoff worked on applied mathematics, including hydrodynamics and early computing; his book Hydrodynamics (1950) and his supervision of Harvard's computing laboratory reflected this turn. He also wrote on the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem on doubly stochastic matrices.
