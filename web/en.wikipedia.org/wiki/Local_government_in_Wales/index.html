Local government in Wales

Local government in Wales is organised into 22 unitary principal areas — counties and county boroughs — each governed by a principal council responsible for services such as education, social services, highways, housing, and refuse collection. Below them, in many areas, sit community and town councils.

History

The Local Government Act 1972 created eight Welsh counties (such as Dyfed, Gwynedd, and Mid Glamorgan) subdivided into districts from 1974. A further reorganisation under the Local Government (Wales) Act 1994 replaced this two-tier system with the present 22 unitary authorities on 1 April 1996. Since devolution in 1999, oversight of Welsh local government has rested with the Welsh Government and the Senedd.

Principal areas

The 22 principal areas range from large city councils such as Cardiff, Swansea, and Newport to rural counties such as Powys, the largest by area. Some retain the names of the historic counties, and the eight preserved counties (based on the 1974 counties) survive for ceremonial purposes such as lieutenancy.

Community councils

More than 700 community and town councils represent smaller localities; they have limited powers covering matters such as community halls, footpaths, and local amenities, and are funded mainly through a precept on council tax.
