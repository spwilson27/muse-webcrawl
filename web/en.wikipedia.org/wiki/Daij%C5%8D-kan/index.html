The Daijō-kan (太政官, Council of State) was the central administrative body of the imperial government of Japan under the ritsuryō system, formally established by the Taihō Code of 701 and reshaped by the Yōrō Code. It stood above the eight ministries and supervised the whole bureaucracy.

Structure

The council was headed by the Daijō-daijin (Chancellor of the Realm), assisted by the Sadaijin (Minister of the Left), Udaijin (Minister of the Right), Naidaijin (Minister of the Centre), and the senior and middle counsellors (dainagon and chūnagon). Beneath it stood eight ministries covering central affairs, ceremonies, civil affairs, popular affairs, war, justice, the treasury and the imperial household, along with agencies such as the Jingikan, which handled Shinto ritual and ranked alongside the council.

Decline and revival

As Fujiwara regents (sesshō and kampaku) and later retired emperors and military governments took real power, the Daijō-kan became largely ceremonial. It was briefly revived after the Meiji Restoration in 1868 as Japan's governing body, before being replaced by the Western-style Cabinet in 1885.
