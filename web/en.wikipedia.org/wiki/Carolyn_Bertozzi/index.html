Carolyn Ruth Bertozzi (born 10 October 1966) is an American chemist and Nobel laureate, the Anne T. and Robert M. Bass Professor in the School of Humanities and Sciences at Stanford University. She founded the field of bioorthogonal chemistry—chemical reactions that can occur inside living organisms without interfering with native biochemistry—and shared the 2022 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Morten Meldal and K. Barry Sharpless "for the development of click chemistry and bioorthogonal chemistry."

Career

Bertozzi studied chemistry at Harvard University, where she worked on photoacoustic calorimetry and played keyboards in a college band with future Rage Against the Machine guitarist Tom Morello, and earned her Ph.D. at UC Berkeley in 1993, working on oligosaccharide synthesis. After postdoctoral research at UC San Francisco on cell adhesion, she joined the Berkeley faculty in 1996, becoming a Howard Hughes Medical Institute investigator and a leading figure in glycobiology—the study of the sugars (glycans) coating cells.

Bioorthogonal chemistry

Frustrated by the inability of conventional microscopy and chemistry to visualize glycans in living systems, Bertozzi developed a two-step strategy: feed cells sugars bearing a small chemical "handle" (such as an azide), which their metabolic machinery installs into cell-surface glycans, then attach probes using reactions—first the Staudinger ligation, then strain-promoted azide–alkyne cycloaddition ("copper-free click chemistry")—that work selectively in water, in cells, and in living animals. The approach has been used to image glycans in zebrafish and mice, to study cancer and infection, and to build targeted therapeutics; Bertozzi has co-founded several biotechnology companies.

Honors

She received a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship in 1999, is a member of the National Academy of Sciences, and in 2022 became one of only a handful of women to win the Nobel Prize in Chemistry. From 2021 she has also served as editor-in-chief of ACS Central Science.
