Christopher Latham Sholes

Born: February 14, 1819, Mooresburg, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Died: February 17, 1890, Milwaukee, Wisconsin, U.S.
Known for: Inventing the QWERTY keyboard layout and co-inventing the first commercial typewriter

Christopher Latham Sholes (February 14, 1819 – February 17, 1890) was an American inventor who invented the QWERTY keyboard, and, along with Samuel W. Soulé, Carlos Glidden and John Pratt, has been contended as one of the inventors of the first typewriter in the United States.

Career

Sholes was a printer, newspaper publisher, and Wisconsin politician who served in the state senate and assembly. Working in Milwaukee in the 1860s, he and his collaborators developed a "writing machine" with a piano-like keyboard; their 1868 patent led to further designs featuring an upstrike mechanism and a cylindrical platen.

QWERTY

Sholes arranged the keys in the QWERTY order, partly to separate common letter pairs and reduce jamming of the type bars. In 1873 he sold his rights to E. Remington and Sons, which refined the design and marketed the Sholes and Glidden typewriter (Remington No. 1) in 1874. The layout, with minor variations, remains the standard for English-language keyboards today.
