Harry Secombe

Sir Harry Donald Secombe (8 September 1921 – 11 April 2001) was a Welsh actor, comedian, singer and television presenter. Secombe was a member of the British radio comedy programme The Goon Show (1951–1960), playing many roles, most notably Neddie Seagoon. An accomplished tenor, he also appeared in musicals and films, and towards the end of his career became a presenter of religious television programmes.

Born: Harry Donald Secombe, 8 September 1921, Swansea, Wales

Died: 11 April 2001 (aged 79), Guildford, Surrey, England

Occupation: Comedian, actor, singer, presenter

Known for: The Goon Show; Songs of Praise; Highway

Early life and war service

Secombe was born in Swansea, the son of a travelling salesman, and sang in church choirs as a boy. He served in the Royal Artillery in North Africa and Italy during the Second World War. In Italy he met Spike Milligan after an incident in which a howitzer rolled down a hill towards Milligan's position; their friendship, and later meeting with Peter Sellers and Michael Bentine, led to The Goon Show.

The Goon Show and comedy career

Secombe was a founder member of The Goon Show on BBC radio, playing the hero Neddie Seagoon around whom the surreal plots revolved; his hearty laugh and falsetto interjections became hallmarks of the programme. In parallel he developed a stage career as a singer and variety performer and appeared in pantomime each Christmas season.

Acting and singing

On stage he starred in musicals including Pickwick (1963), whose song "If I Ruled the World" became his signature recording and a UK chart hit in 1963. In film he played Mr Bumble in the acclaimed musical Oliver! (1968). His powerful tenor voice led to numerous albums of ballads and sacred music.

Secombe was knighted in 1981. In later years he presented the ITV religious programme Highway (1983–1993) and episodes of Songs of Praise, drawing on his deep Christian faith. He died of prostate cancer in 2001. His epitaph, by his own request, echoed the Goons' humour; his gravestone records that he was "a man of many talents, none of them much use" in spirit if not in words — in fact it reads with typical warmth about a life lived for laughter and faith.
