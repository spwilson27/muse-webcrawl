Plush

Plush is a textile having a cut nap or pile the same as fustian or velvet. Its softness gives it an important role in upholstery and decorative fabrics. Originally the pile of plush consisted of mohair or worsted yarn, but now it can also be made of silk, cotton, or synthetic fibres such as polyester.

Plush is used for stuffed toys, upholstery, clothing (such as jackets and robes), and various accessories. It is also one of the main materials from which plush toys — often called "plushies" — are made.

Manufacture

Plush is manufactured by weaving two thicknesses of fabric face to face, held together by an extra warp thread, and then cutting the connecting pile threads apart. This process is similar to that used to make velvet, but plush has a longer and less dense pile than velvet or velour. Modern plush is commonly knitted rather than woven, particularly for toy manufacture.

Uses

  • Stuffed toys: Plush fabric is the standard covering for teddy bears and other soft toys.
  • Upholstery: Used on furniture, especially in the 19th and early 20th centuries.
  • Clothing: Robes, jackets, and costume trims.
  • Cleaning: Microfibre plush cloths are used for polishing and dusting because the long fibres trap particles.

Types

Common varieties include minky plush, a very soft short-pile polyester fabric widely used for baby blankets and toys; crystal or "boa" plush with longer fibres used for costumes; and mohair plush traditionally used for high-quality teddy bears.
