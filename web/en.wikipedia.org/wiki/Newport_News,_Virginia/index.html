Newport News is an independent city in southeastern Virginia, United States, located on the northern shore of Hampton Roads at the mouth of the James River. With a population of 186,247 at the 2020 census, it is one of the principal cities of the Hampton Roads metropolitan area and forms the northern end of the Virginia Peninsula.

History

The area was settled by English colonists shortly after the founding of Jamestown in 1607. The modern city, however, owes its existence to railroad magnate Collis P. Huntington, who in the 1880s extended the Chesapeake and Ohio Railway to a new deep-water coal port on Hampton Roads and in 1886 founded the shipyard that became Newport News Shipbuilding. The origin of the name is disputed; one tradition links it to Captain Christopher Newport, who brought news and supplies to Jamestown.

Newport News Shipbuilding, now a division of Huntington Ingalls Industries, is the largest industrial employer in Virginia and the sole designer and builder of United States Navy aircraft carriers, as well as one of two builders of nuclear submarines. During both World Wars the city was a major port of embarkation.

Attractions

  • The Mariners' Museum and Park, one of the largest maritime museums in the world, home to the USS Monitor Center.
  • The Virginia Living Museum.
  • Newport News Park, one of the largest municipal parks in the United States.
  • The United States Army Transportation Museum at Fort Eustis.

Notable people

Singer Ella Fitzgerald was born in Newport News in 1917. Other natives include football player Michael Vick and basketball player Allen Iverson (raised in nearby Hampton).
