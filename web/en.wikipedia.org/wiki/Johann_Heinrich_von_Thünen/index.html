Johann Heinrich von Thünen

Johann Heinrich von Thünen
BornJune 24, 1783, Canarienhausen, Oldenburg
DiedSeptember 22, 1850, Tellow, Mecklenburg
Known forVon Thünen model of agricultural land use

Johann Heinrich von Thünen (24 June 1783 – 22 September 1850) was a prominent 19th-century German economist, agriculturalist and landowner. His masterwork, The Isolated State (1826), developed an early theory of spatial economics and land rent that anticipated marginal productivity theory.

Thünen managed an estate at Tellow in Mecklenburg, where he kept meticulous accounts over many years. His model imagines an isolated city surrounded by a featureless plain, and shows how agricultural land use arranges itself in concentric rings around the market according to transport costs and perishability: intensive dairying and horticulture near the centre, then timber, grain, and finally ranching at the margin.

Influence

The von Thünen model became a foundation of location theory, agricultural geography and regional science, and his analysis of wages and marginal productivity earned him recognition as a forerunner of neoclassical economics.
