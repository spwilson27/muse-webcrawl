The Alpine Club is a British mountaineering club based in London, founded in 1857 by a group of British climbers including William Mathews, and generally regarded as the world's first mountaineering club. Its first president was John Ball, author of the first systematic guide to the Alps. Club members drove the "golden age of alpinism", making many first ascents of the great Alpine peaks in the 1850s–60s, and later pioneered exploration and climbing in the Himalaya, Karakoram and other greater ranges.

Activities and collections

The club publishes the Alpine Journal, the world's oldest mountaineering journal, maintains one of the foremost mountaineering libraries and photo archives, supports expeditions through its Mount Everest Foundation (jointly with the Royal Geographical Society), and administers the Himalayan Index of ascents. Membership traditionally required proven experience of Alpine or high-mountain climbing; women were excluded until 1974, prompting the separate Ladies' Alpine Club (1907), which merged with the Alpine Club in 1975.
