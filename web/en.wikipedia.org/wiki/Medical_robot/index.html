Medical robot

A teleoperated surgical robot in use in an operating room

A medical robot is a robot used in the medical sciences, including surgical robots, rehabilitation robots, hospital service robots, and robots for disinfection, pharmacy automation, and telepresence. Medical robots are intended to improve the precision, consistency, and capability of medical procedures, extend the reach of specialists, and reduce risks to patients and staff.

Surgical robots

The most prominent medical robots are surgical systems that allow a surgeon to operate through small incisions using robotic arms controlled from a console, with magnified three-dimensional vision and instruments that scale and filter the surgeon's hand movements. The da Vinci Surgical System, introduced commercially in 1999, is the most widely used, applied to prostatectomy, gynaecological surgery, cardiac procedures, and general surgery. Earlier milestones included the PUMA 560 arm used to position a needle for a brain biopsy in 1985 and the ROBODOC system for orthopaedic surgery. Critics note the high cost of systems and the mixed evidence for superiority over conventional laparoscopy for many procedures.

Rehabilitation and assistance

Rehabilitation robots assist patients recovering from stroke or spinal cord injury in performing repetitive therapeutic movements, and robotic exoskeletons help people with paralysis to stand and walk. Socially assistive robots have been trialled in elder care and in therapy for children with autism. Telepresence robots let physicians examine and converse with patients remotely.

Hospital logistics

Autonomous mobile robots transport linen, meals, medication, and laboratory samples around hospitals, while ultraviolet-light robots disinfect wards. During the COVID-19 pandemic, such robots were deployed to limit staff exposure. Capsule robots and microrobots remain largely experimental areas of research for diagnosis and targeted drug delivery.
