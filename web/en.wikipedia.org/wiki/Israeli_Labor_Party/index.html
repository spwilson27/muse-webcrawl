The Israeli Labor Party (Hebrew: מפלגת העבודה הישראלית, HaAvoda) is a social-democratic political party in Israel. Descended from Mapai, the party of David Ben-Gurion, Labor and its predecessors governed Israel continuously from independence in 1948 until 1977 and led the governments that signed the Oslo Accords and the peace treaty with Jordan in the 1990s. In recent decades its parliamentary strength has declined sharply.

History

Labor was formed in 1968 by the merger of Mapai with Ahdut HaAvoda and Rafi. From 1969 it stood in the Alignment with Mapam, winning the elections of 1969 and 1973 under Golda Meir and Yitzhak Rabin. The 1977 election, won by Menachem Begin's Likud, ended three decades of Labor dominance. Shimon Peres led the party through national-unity governments in the 1980s; Rabin returned it to power in 1992, negotiated the Oslo Accords with the PLO and the Israel–Jordan peace treaty, and was assassinated in 1995. Ehud Barak won decisively in 1999 but his government collapsed within two years amid the Second Intifada.

Decline

From the 2000s Labor's vote eroded as security concerns, economic liberalisation and demographic change reshaped Israeli politics; the party fell to single-digit Knesset seat totals in several elections, sometimes standing in joint lists such as the Zionist Union (2015) and with Gesher and Meretz. In 2024 Labor agreed to merge with Meretz to form a new party, The Democrats, under Yair Golan.

Ideology

Labor combines social-democratic economic policies with Labour Zionism and support for a two-state solution negotiated with the Palestinians, territorial compromise, and a strong welfare state and trade-union movement (the Histadrut).
