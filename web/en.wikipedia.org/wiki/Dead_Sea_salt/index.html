Dead Sea salt

Dead Sea salt is the mineral salt extracted from the Dead Sea, the hypersaline lake bordered by Jordan, Israel and the West Bank. Its composition differs markedly from ordinary sea salt: whereas oceanic salt is roughly 85–90% sodium chloride, Dead Sea salt contains a much larger share of magnesium, potassium, calcium and bromide salts, with sodium chloride making up only about 30–35%.

Production and uses

Salt and other minerals are recovered by solar evaporation in shallow ponds at the southern end of the sea, notably by works in Israel and Jordan; the same evaporation systems produce potash and bromine on an industrial scale. Refined Dead Sea salt is sold for culinary use in small quantities, but it is best known in cosmetics and bath products, where it is marketed for skin conditions such as psoriasis and eczema. Bathing in the Dead Sea itself has a long therapeutic tradition ("climatotherapy"), and clinical studies have found some benefit from Dead Sea salt baths for skin barrier function and inflammation, though evidence for many cosmetic claims is limited.

Environmental note

The Dead Sea has been shrinking for decades because of diversion of the Jordan River and mineral extraction, its level falling by roughly a metre per year, which has altered the shoreline salt formations and created thousands of sinkholes around the coast.
