National Car Rental

National Car Rental is an American car rental company founded in 1947 and headquartered in Clayton, Missouri. Catering especially to business travellers, it is best known for its Emerald Club loyalty programme, whose "Emerald Aisle" service lets members bypass the counter and choose any car from a designated row. National is owned by Enterprise Mobility, which also operates the Enterprise Rent-A-Car and Alamo brands.

History

National was founded in St. Louis in 1947 by a group of 24 independent car rental operators, including Joe James, as a national cooperative network; it is sometimes described as the first car rental "franchise" system in the United States. Through the post-war decades it grew into one of the country's largest rental companies, pioneering airport locations and, in 1987, the Emerald Club, one of the industry's first loyalty programmes, built around speed of service for frequent renters.

Ownership

The company passed through several owners: it was bought by Paine Webber interests in the 1980s, sold to General Motors in 1986, spun off in the 1990s, and acquired by an investment group before being combined with Alamo under Vanguard Automotive Group. In 2007 Enterprise Rent-A-Car's parent, the Taylor family of St. Louis, bought Vanguard, bringing National and Alamo under Enterprise Holdings (now Enterprise Mobility). Under common ownership the three brands are positioned by market segment, with National aimed at frequent business travellers and corporate accounts.

Services

National operates thousands of locations in North America and, through partners, in Latin America, the Caribbean, Europe and Asia. Its hallmark services are the Emerald Aisle, one-way rentals, and expedited return. It has frequently ranked at or near the top of J. D. Power's rental car satisfaction studies in North America.
