Lysergic acid

Lysergic acid, also known as D-lysergic acid and (+)-lysergic acid, is a precursor for a wide range of ergoline alkaloids produced by the ergot fungus Claviceps purpurea and found in the seeds of some plants, including morning glories. Its molecular formula is C16H16N2O2.

Chemistry

Lysergic acid has a tetracyclic ergoline skeleton incorporating an indole ring system, with a carboxylic acid group at the 8 position. It can be obtained by alkaline hydrolysis of naturally occurring ergot alkaloids such as ergotamine and ergonovine, or synthesised from tryptophan-derived intermediates; the first total synthesis was reported by Robert Burns Woodward's group at Harvard in 1956.

Derivatives and regulation

Amides of lysergic acid (lysergamides) include the pharmaceutical ergonovine and the psychedelic drug LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide), first synthesised by Albert Hofmann at Sandoz in 1938. Because of its use as an LSD precursor, lysergic acid is listed as a Table I precursor under the United Nations Convention Against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances and is regulated as a List I chemical in the United States.
