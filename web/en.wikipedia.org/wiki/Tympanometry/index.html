Tympanometry

Tympanometry is an acoustic measurement of the condition of the middle ear and the mobility of the eardrum (tympanic membrane) and conduction bones, performed by varying air pressure in the ear canal. A probe tone, usually 226 Hz in adults, is presented while pressure is swept, and the reflected sound is analysed to produce a graph called a tympanogram.

Interpretation

Tympanograms are commonly classified following the Jerger system: type A indicates normal middle-ear pressure and mobility; type B is a flat tracing suggesting fluid in the middle ear, a perforated eardrum or impacted wax; type C shows significantly negative pressure, often associated with Eustachian tube dysfunction. Tympanometry is not a hearing test itself but is used alongside audiometry in the assessment of conditions such as otitis media.
