Jason is a hero of Greek mythology, the leader of the Argonauts whose quest for the Golden Fleece is one of the best-known tales of the ancient world, told at length in the Argonautica of Apollonius Rhodius and summarised by Apollodorus. Unlike many Greek heroes, Jason succeeded chiefly through the help of others — above all the sorceress Medea — and his story ends in betrayal and ruin.

Jason with the Golden Fleece.

Early life

Jason was the son of Aeson, rightful king of Iolcus, whose half-brother Pelias seized the throne. Sent for safety to Mount Pelion, Jason was raised by the wise centaur Chiron. Warned by an oracle to beware a man wearing one sandal, Pelias confronted Jason when he returned as a young man having lost a sandal while helping an old woman — in fact the goddess Hera in disguise — cross a river. Pelias promised to yield the throne if Jason brought him the Golden Fleece from distant Colchis, expecting the quest to kill him.

The voyage of the Argo

Jason assembled the greatest heroes of the age — including Heracles, Orpheus, Castor and Pollux, Atalanta in some versions, and the shipwright Argus, who built the fifty-oared Argo. After adventures with the Harpies, the clashing Symplegades rocks, and the Amazons, the Argonauts reached Colchis, where King Aeëtes set impossible tasks. Medea, the king's daughter, fell in love with Jason and used her magic to help him complete them and steal the Fleece, fleeing with him aboard the Argo.

Later life and death

Back in Iolcus, Medea engineered Pelias's death, but the couple were exiled to Corinth. There, after ten years, Jason abandoned Medea to marry the Corinthian princess Glauce — provoking Medea's revenge, in which she killed Glauce, Creon and her own children by Jason. Jason's end is ignominious: in one tradition he died when a rotting timber of the Argo fell on him as he slept in its shadow.
