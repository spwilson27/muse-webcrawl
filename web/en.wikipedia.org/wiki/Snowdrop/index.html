Snowdrop

KingdomPlantae
OrderAsparagales
FamilyAmaryllidaceae
GenusGalanthus

Snowdrops are flowering plants of the genus Galanthus, in the family Amaryllidaceae — some 20 species of bulbous perennial herbaceous plants native to Europe and the Middle East, prized as among the earliest spring flowers, often blooming while snow still lies on the ground.

The most common species, Galanthus nivalis, bears a single nodding white flower with three outer tepals and a shorter inner crown marked with green. Snowdrops produce the alkaloid galantamine, used in the treatment of Alzheimer's disease, and all parts of the plant are poisonous if eaten.

Cultivation and galanthophilia

Snowdrops are widely naturalised in gardens and woodlands outside their native range. Enthusiastic collectors, known as galanthophiles, seek out rare cultivars; single bulbs of unusual varieties have sold for hundreds of pounds. "Snowdrop walks" are a popular late-winter attraction at British gardens.

Conservation

All Galanthus species are listed in CITES Appendix II, restricting international trade in wild-collected bulbs, since over-collection threatened wild populations, particularly in Turkey and the Caucasus.
