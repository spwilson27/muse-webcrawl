Owain Gwynedd

Owain ap Gruffudd
King of Gwynedd: 1137–1170
Born: c. 1100, Anglesey
Died: 23 November 1170
Buried: Bangor Cathedral
Father: Gruffudd ap Cynan

Owain ap Gruffudd (c. 1100 – 23 November 1170), known as Owain Gwynedd, was King of Gwynedd in north Wales from 1137 until his death. He was the first Welsh ruler to style himself princeps Wallensium ("Prince of the Welsh") and is often considered the greatest of the medieval Welsh princes before Llywelyn the Great.

Reign

Owain succeeded his father Gruffudd ap Cynan and, with his brother Cadwaladr, exploited England's civil war (the Anarchy) to recover Welsh territory. In 1157 Henry II of England invaded and forced Owain to submit and surrender Rhuddlan and land in the north-east; but when Henry returned in 1165, Owain led a grand alliance of Welsh princes that defied the king's army in the Berwyn mountains — one of the few occasions when Wales acted in unity against an English king. By the end of his reign he had recovered most of his losses and extended Gwynedd's influence.

Legacy

Owain patronized court poets (the Gogynfeirdd), notably Gwalchmai ap Meilyr and Cynddelw Brydydd Mawr. His many children by several marriages — including Hywel ab Owain Gwynedd, the warrior-poet — fought a bloody succession struggle after his death, and no will of his designated an heir. He was buried in Bangor Cathedral despite excommunication over his marriage to his cousin Cristin.
