The Northrop Grumman (formerly Grumman) EA-6B Prowler is a twin-engine, four-seat, mid-wing electronic-warfare aircraft derived from the A-6 Intruder airframe. Operated by the United States Navy and United States Marine Corps, it was designed to jam enemy radar systems and gather radio intelligence, and it could also attack radar sites with AGM-88 HARM anti-radiation missiles.

Development and design

The Prowler was developed from the A-6 by stretching the fuselage to accommodate a crew of four: one pilot and three electronic countermeasures officers (ECMOs). Its distinctive features include a pod on top of the vertical stabilizer housing receiver antennas, five ALQ-99 tactical jamming pods carried on wing and fuselage stations, and gold-film-coated canopies that shield the crew from the aircraft's own powerful electromagnetic emissions. Two Pratt & Whitney J52 turbojet engines power the aircraft to subsonic speeds.

Operational history

The EA-6B entered service in 1971 and flew in the later stages of the Vietnam War. It supported virtually every major US combat operation thereafter, including the Gulf War, the Kosovo War, the Iraq War, and operations in Afghanistan, providing suppression of enemy air defenses (SEAD) for Navy, Marine Corps, and Air Force strike aircraft — especially after the retirement of the EF-111A Raven left it as the sole US tactical radar jammer.

Retirement and replacement

The US Navy retired the Prowler in 2015, replacing it with the carrier-based EA-18G Growler. The Marine Corps, which used the type for land-based electronic attack, retired its last Prowlers in March 2019, ending 48 years of service.
