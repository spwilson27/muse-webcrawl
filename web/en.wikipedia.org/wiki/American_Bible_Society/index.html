The American Bible Society is a U.S.-based nonprofit, interconfessional organisation dedicated to translating, publishing, and distributing the Christian Bible and to helping people engage with its contents. Founded in New York City on May 11, 1816, it is one of the oldest Bible societies in the world and a founding member of the United Bible Societies fellowship.

History

The society was organised by prominent American Christians, including Elias Boudinot, its first president, and John Jay, its first vice-president, with the goal of distributing Bibles "without note or comment". In its early decades it undertook ambitious campaigns to place a Bible in every American household and supplied scriptures to soldiers, immigrants, and enslaved people. In the 19th century it promoted the production of stereotype plates to mass-print inexpensive Bibles, and it supported translation work into Native American languages and many foreign tongues.

For most of its history the society was based in New York, long occupying the Bible House in Manhattan. In 2015 it relocated its headquarters to Philadelphia.

Work

The American Bible Society publishes and distributes Bibles and scripture portions in English and other languages, including the Contemporary English Version and the Good News Translation, whose New Testament ("Good News for Modern Man") first appeared in 1966. It supports Bible translation projects worldwide, provides scriptures to the U.S. armed forces and to people affected by trauma and poverty, and conducts research such as the annual State of the Bible survey on American attitudes toward scripture.
