Educational acceleration

Educational acceleration is the practice of allowing a student to progress through an educational programme at a faster pace than is typical, in order to match teaching to the student's readiness and motivation. It is one of the principal forms of provision for gifted and talented students, and research reviews have generally found positive academic effects when acceleration is carefully matched to the individual.

Forms of acceleration

Acceleration takes many forms: whole-grade skipping, early entrance to kindergarten, school or university, subject-matter (partial) acceleration, curriculum compacting, telescoping several years into fewer, dual enrolment in secondary and tertiary courses, credit by examination, and advanced programmes such as Advanced Placement and the International Baccalaureate. The Iowa Acceleration Scale is a widely used tool to guide decisions about whole-grade acceleration.

Research and debate

Reports such as A Nation Deceived (2004) and A Nation Empowered (2015) argue that acceleration is underused in the United States despite strong evidence for its effectiveness. Concerns commonly raised include possible social and emotional effects of being younger than classmates, though longitudinal studies of accelerated gifted students generally report favourable outcomes.
