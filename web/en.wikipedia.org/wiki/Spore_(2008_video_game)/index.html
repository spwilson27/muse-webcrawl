Spore (2008 video game)

DeveloperMaxis
PublisherElectronic Arts
DesignerWill Wright
ReleaseSeptember 7, 2008
GenreLife simulation, real-time strategy, god game
PlatformsMicrosoft Windows, Mac OS X

Spore is a 2008 life simulation real-time strategy god game developed by Maxis and published by Electronic Arts for Microsoft Windows and Mac OS X. Designed by Will Wright, it allows players to guide the development of a species from its beginnings as a microscopic organism through evolution into an intelligent and social creature, then to interstellar exploration.

Gameplay

The game is divided into five stages: Cell, Creature, Tribal, Civilization, and Space. In each stage players use editors to design creatures, buildings, and vehicles; creations are procedurally animated and were shared online via the Sporepedia, an early large-scale example of user-generated content in a mainstream game. The Space stage, the longest, has the player's species explore a galaxy of procedurally generated planets and confront the Grox, a hostile cyborg empire near the galactic core.

Development and reception

Spore was announced at the 2005 Game Developers Conference and became one of the most anticipated games of its era, celebrated for its procedural generation technology. On release it received generally positive reviews praising its creativity and editors, though some criticized the shallowness of individual stages. It sold millions of copies and spawned expansions including Galactic Adventures (2009). The game also attracted controversy over its SecuROM digital rights management, which became the subject of a class-action lawsuit and made Spore one of the most pirated games of 2008.
