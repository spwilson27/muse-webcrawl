Voice type

A voice type is a classification of the human singing voice into categories based on vocal range, vocal weight, tessitura, vocal timbre, and vocal transition points (passaggio). The classification system is used most rigorously in classical music and opera, where assigning the right roles — the German Fach system — protects singers from damaging their voices and matches voices to parts they can sing safely and effectively throughout a career.

Characteristics considered

Voice classification weighs several factors together: the extremes of range the singer can reach; the tessitura, where the voice sounds best and can sing for long periods; vocal weight, the relative lightness or heaviness of the sound; timbre or color; and the location of the passaggi between registers. Age and training alter the voice, so young singers are often advised to delay definitive classification.

Principal voice types

From highest to lowest, the standard types are: soprano, mezzo-soprano, contralto, countertenor, tenor, baritone, and bass. Each subdivides further: sopranos include coloratura, lyric, spinto, and dramatic varieties; tenors include leggiero, lyric, spinto, heldentenor, and character types; the bass family includes bass-baritone, basso cantante, and basso profondo. Contralto is the rarest female type; the countertenor sings in the alto range using a developed falsetto or head voice.

The Fach system

German opera houses codified the Fach (literally "compartment") system, in which singers are engaged for a defined category of roles and are contractually protected from being assigned parts outside it. The system has been influential in European houses and in how roles are catalogued, though lighter versions of classification are used elsewhere.

In popular music

Popular music classifies voices loosely, if at all, since amplification removes the need to fill an opera house. Nevertheless, descriptions such as "high tenor" or "alto" are used informally, and vocal range is a popular measure of pop singers' abilities.
