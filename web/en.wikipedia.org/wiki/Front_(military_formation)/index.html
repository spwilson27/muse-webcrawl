Front (military formation)

A front (Russian: фронт, front) is a type of military formation used by the Soviet Union and, before it, the Imperial Russian Army: a large military organisation roughly equivalent to an army group in the military forces of other countries, particularly during the Second World War. In the Red Army, a front was generally made up of several armies (typically between four and nine), together with supporting aviation, artillery and logistical formations, and was commanded by a senior officer, often of the rank of Colonel General or Army General.

Second World War

During the Great Patriotic War (1941–1945), fronts were the largest operational-strategic formations of the Red Army. They were created at the beginning of the war from the border military districts and were frequently renamed, split or consolidated as the strategic situation changed. Famous fronts include the Western Front, the 1st, 2nd and 3rd Ukrainian Fronts, the 1st and 2nd Belorussian Fronts, and the Leningrad and Karelian Fronts. By 1945 a major front could field hundreds of thousands of men, thousands of tanks and guns, and its own air army.

Organisation

A front was directly subordinated to the Stavka, the Soviet high command. Its headquarters coordinated the operations of its subordinate combined-arms and tank armies, an air army, and front-level reserves. Fronts conducted strategic offensive and defensive operations across sectors that might extend for hundreds of kilometres. The concept survives in the military doctrine of Russia and several other post-Soviet states, where a front would be formed in wartime from the forces of a military district.
