Polish space

In the mathematical discipline of general topology and descriptive set theory, a Polish space is a separable completely metrizable topological space; that is, a space homeomorphic to a complete metric space that has a countable dense subset. Polish spaces are so named because they were first extensively studied by Polish topologists and logicians of the Polish School of Mathematics, including Sierpiński, Kuratowski and Tarski.

Examples and properties

Examples include the real line, the Cantor set, the Baire space (the space of sequences of natural numbers with the product topology), and every separable Banach space; non-examples include any uncountable discrete space. A subspace of a Polish space is Polish exactly when it is a Gδ set, and every uncountable Polish space has the cardinality of the continuum and contains a homeomorphic copy of the Cantor set.

Applications

Polish spaces are the standard setting for descriptive set theory, because their Borel structure is tame enough to admit a rich theory of analytic sets and measurable selection. Probability theory uses them as state spaces: weak convergence of measures, regular conditional probabilities and existence theorems for stochastic processes are all developed most naturally on Polish spaces, as are the standard Borel spaces of ergodic theory.
