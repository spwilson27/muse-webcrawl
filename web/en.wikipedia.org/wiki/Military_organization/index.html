Military organization

Military organization (or military organisation) is the structuring of the armed forces of a state so as to offer the military capability required by national defence policy. In some countries paramilitary forces are included in a nation's armed forces, though not considered military. Armed forces that are not part of the state — irregular military forces such as militias and private military companies — are also organised along broadly similar lines.

Hierarchy of units

Modern armies organise forces into a hierarchy of formations and units of increasing size, each with a commander, a headquarters and subordinate elements. Typical echelons for land forces include:

Command and control

Units are directed through a chain of command, supported by staff officers organised into branches dealing with personnel, intelligence, operations, logistics and planning. Standardised staff systems, such as the continental and NATO staff systems, allow officers to move between headquarters. Naval and air forces use corresponding hierarchies of fleets, squadrons, wings and groups.

Organisation by service

Armed forces are normally divided into services — army, navy, air force and, in some states, marines, space forces or gendarmeries — coordinated at the top by a ministry of defence and a joint staff.
