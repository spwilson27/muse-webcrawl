Non-self-governing territory

A non-self-governing territory (NSGT) is a territory "whose people have not yet attained a full measure of self-government", in the terms of Chapter XI of the Charter of the United Nations. Member states administering such territories accept the obligation to develop self-government, take due account of the political aspirations of the peoples, and transmit information on the territory to the Secretary-General.

The UN list

When the UN was founded in 1945, some 750 million people lived in colonies or dependent territories. The General Assembly maintains a list of territories to which the 1960 Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples (Resolution 1514) applies. Since 1946, 80 former colonies have gained independence and been removed from the list. As of the 2020s, 17 territories remain listed, administered mainly by the United Kingdom, the United States, and France, plus Western Sahara, whose status is disputed.

The remaining territories include American Samoa, Anguilla, Bermuda, the British Virgin Islands, the Cayman Islands, the Falkland Islands, French Polynesia, Gibraltar, Guam, Montserrat, New Caledonia, Pitcairn, Saint Helena, Tokelau, the Turks and Caicos Islands, and the US Virgin Islands, along with Western Sahara.

Process

Oversight is provided by the Special Committee on Decolonization (C-24), established in 1961. A territory is removed from the list when it attains independence, free association with an independent state, or integration with one, as determined by the General Assembly — in several cases (American Samoa, Guam, Gibraltar, the Falkland Islands) the administering power and local majorities have at times differed with the committee over whether continued listing is appropriate.
