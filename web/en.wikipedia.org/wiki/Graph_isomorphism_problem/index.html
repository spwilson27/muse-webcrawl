The graph isomorphism problem is the computational problem of determining whether two finite graphs are isomorphic. Although it is a problem in the complexity class NP, it is not known whether it is NP-complete or solvable in polynomial time; it is one of a small number of natural problems in NP that occupy this intermediate status (assuming P ≠ NP). The problem is known to lie in the low hierarchy and in co-AM, which is strong evidence against its being NP-complete.

Two graphs G and H are isomorphic if there is a bijection between their vertex sets that preserves adjacency: vertices u and v are adjacent in G if and only if their images are adjacent in H. The problem asks for an algorithm to decide whether such a bijection exists.

History and Babai's result

In November 2015, László Babai announced a quasi-polynomial-time algorithm for graph isomorphism, running in time exp((log n)O(1)), a dramatic improvement over the previous best bound of exp(O(√(n log n))) by Eugene Luks (1983). An error in the analysis found by Harald Helfgott in 2017 was corrected by Babai, restoring the quasi-polynomial bound with a slightly weaker exponent. The announcement was widely covered as a landmark in theoretical computer science.

Special cases

Polynomial-time algorithms are known for many restricted classes, including planar graphs, bounded-degree graphs, bounded-treewidth graphs, and interval graphs. The related graph automorphism problem and the canonization problem are closely connected; canonical labelling tools such as nauty solve typical instances very quickly in practice.

Applications

Graph isomorphism arises in chemical informatics (comparing molecular structures), pattern recognition, electronic design automation, and the verification of combinatorial objects. The Weisfeiler–Lehman colour-refinement heuristic solves most random instances but fails on strongly regular graphs and other highly symmetric structures.
