Great Rose Bowl Hoax

The hijacked card stunt at the 1961 Rose Bowl.

The Great Rose Bowl Hoax was a prank executed by students from the California Institute of Technology (Caltech) at the 1961 Rose Bowl game in Pasadena, California, one of the most famous practical jokes in American college history. The game, played on 2 January 1961 between the Washington Huskies and the Minnesota Golden Gophers, was nationally televised by NBC.

The prank

Washington's cheerleaders coordinated a flip-card display in which students in the stands held up coloured cards following instruction sheets to form images and words. Caltech students — a group later dubbed the "Fiendish Fourteen" — obtained the instruction sheets by posing as a high-school reporter interviewing the head cheerleader, then altered and reprinted them. When the sequence ran during the second half, the first two images appeared correctly, but the third, intended to show a husky, came out distorted; the final display, expected to spell "WASHINGTON", instead read "CALTECH" in huge letters, live on national television before an audience of millions.

Aftermath

The prank cemented Caltech's reputation for ingenious hacks and is still celebrated by the institute; some of the university's own literature recounts it as a defining moment of its student culture. Washington won the game 17–7. The hoax inspired imitators, including the 1984 Caltech prank that altered the Rose Bowl scoreboard to show "Caltech 38, MIT 9", and Yale's 2004 "We Suck" Harvard card-stunt prank.
