Sulaymaniyah (Kurdish: سلێمانی, Silêmanî; Arabic: السليمانية, as-Sulaymāniyyah) is a city in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, capital of Sulaymaniyah Governorate. Surrounded by the Azmar, Goyzha, and Baranan mountain ranges, it is considered the cultural capital of Iraqi Kurdistan and a centre of Kurdish literature, publishing, and education.

History

The city was founded in 1784 by the Kurdish prince Ibrahim Pasha of the Baban dynasty, who named it after his father, Sulaiman Pasha. As the last capital of the Baban principality, it attracted poets and scholars, and the Sorani Kurdish dialect of the region became the basis of the standard literary Sorani. In the early twentieth century the city was the seat of Sheikh Mahmud Barzanji's short-lived Kingdom of Kurdistan (1922–24). Under Ba'athist rule the city suffered repression; the Amna Suraka ("Red Security") prison in the city, now a museum, was a notorious interrogation centre. Sulaymaniyah was a centre of the 1991 Kurdish uprising and has since grown rapidly as part of the autonomous Kurdistan Region.

Culture and education

The city hosts the University of Sulaymaniyah (founded 1968) and the American University of Iraq, Sulaimani (founded 2007). It is associated with poets such as Nali, Goran, and Sherko Bekas, and is sometimes called the "Paris of Kurdistan" for its liberal, intellectual atmosphere.
