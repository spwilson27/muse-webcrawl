The K League 1 (Korean: K리그1) is the top division of the South Korean football league system, contested by twelve professional clubs. It is the first division of the K League, above K League 2, with promotion and relegation between them.

History

The league was founded in 1983 as the Korean Super League, making it the first professional football league in East Asia. The original five member clubs included company teams such as Hallelujah FC, Yukong Elephants and POSCO Dolphins. It was renamed the K League in 1998 as part of reforms following the 1997 Asian financial crisis and the build-up to the 2002 FIFA World Cup, which Korea co-hosted. After the establishment of a second division in 2013, the top flight was rebranded — first as the K League Classic, and since the 2018 season as K League 1.

Competition format

The twelve clubs play a split season: in the regular round each team plays the others three times (33 matches), after which the league divides into a top group (Final A) and bottom group (Final B) of six teams each, which play five further matches among themselves. The champion and other leading clubs qualify for the AFC Champions League Elite, while the bottom club is relegated directly to K League 2 and the eleventh-placed club enters a promotion–relegation play-off.

Clubs and rivalries

Notable clubs include Jeonbuk Hyundai Motors, the most successful club in league history with numerous titles and two AFC Champions League trophies; Ulsan HD (formerly Ulsan Hyundai), also continental champions; FC Seoul; Suwon Samsung Bluewings; and Pohang Steelers, record Asian champions of the 1990s. Famous fixtures include the "Super Match" between FC Seoul and Suwon Samsung Bluewings and the Hyundai derby between Jeonbuk and Ulsan. The league is also known for clubs' mixed ownership between large corporations (chaebol) and citizen-supported community clubs.
