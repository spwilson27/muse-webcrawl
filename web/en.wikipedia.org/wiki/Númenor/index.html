Númenor, also called Elenna or the Land of the Star, is a fictional island kingdom in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, the great civilisation of Men in the Second Age of Middle-earth. It appears in The Silmarillion, Unfinished Tales, and appendices to The Lord of the Rings, and is a central setting of the television series The Rings of Power.

History

The island, shaped like a five-pointed star, was raised from the sea by the Valar as a gift to the Edain — the Men who had fought against Morgoth in the First Age — at the beginning of the Second Age. Its people, the Númenóreans or Dúnedain, were granted lives far longer than those of ordinary Men. Their first king was Elros, brother of Elrond. From their capital Armenelos and the holy mountain Meneltarma, the Númenóreans became the greatest mariners of the age, voyaging across the seas and establishing colonies and dominions in Middle-earth. They aided the Elves against Sauron and later humbled and captured him — but Sauron corrupted the kingdom from within, turning its people to the worship of Morgoth and resentment of the Ban of the Valar, which forbade them to sail to the Undying Lands.

The Downfall

The last king, Ar-Pharazôn, broke the Ban and sailed with a great fleet to invade Valinor in 3319 of the Second Age. In response, Eru Ilúvatar changed the shape of the world and Númenor was swallowed by the sea — the Downfall, told in the Akallabêth. Only a remnant, the Faithful led by Elendil and his sons Isildur and Anárion, escaped on nine ships and founded the kingdoms of Arnor and Gondor in Middle-earth. Tolkien's story deliberately echoes the myth of Atlantis, and he referred to Númenor in his letters as his Atlantis legend.
