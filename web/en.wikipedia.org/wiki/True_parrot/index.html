True parrot

The true parrots are members of the superfamily Psittacoidea, one of the three superfamilies in the order Psittaciformes (parrots), alongside the cockatoos (Cacatuoidea) and the New Zealand parrots (Strigopoidea). There are roughly 350 species of true parrots, distributed across tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, with the greatest diversity in South America and Australasia.

Characteristics

True parrots typically have a strong, curved bill, an upright stance, clawed zygodactyl feet, and often vividly coloured plumage. They range in size from small pygmy parrots to large macaws. Well-known groups include macaws, Amazon parrots, conures, parakeets, lovebirds, and the African grey parrot, which is noted for its intelligence and ability to mimic human speech.

Families

The superfamily is divided into the families Psittacidae (African and New World parrots) and Psittaculidae (Old World parrots of Asia and Australasia), with some classifications recognising additional families. Many species are threatened by habitat loss and the wild bird trade.
