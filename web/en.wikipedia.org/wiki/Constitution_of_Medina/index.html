Constitution of Medina

The Constitution of Medina (Arabic: دستور المدينة, Dustūr al-Madīna; also known as the Charter of Medina, صحيفة المدينة, Ṣaḥīfat al-Madīna, "the Book/scroll of Medina") is a document traditionally attributed to the Islamic prophet Muhammad, drawn up shortly after his arrival (the Hijra) in Medina in 622 CE. It established a confederation among the tribes of Medina — the Emigrants (Muhajirun) from Mecca, the Helpers (Ansar), and the Jewish tribes of the oasis — under Muhammad's leadership and arbitration.

Sources and authenticity

The text survives in the Sira (biography) of Ibn Ishaq as transmitted by Ibn Hisham, and in Abu Ubayd's Kitab al-Amwal. Although no original exists, most historians, Muslim and non-Muslim alike, accept the document as substantially authentic and among the earliest documents of Islam; the prevailing scholarly view, following Wellhausen, Serjeant and Watt, is that it may combine several agreements concluded at different moments in the first years at Medina rather than a single act.

Content

The charter's provisions include:

  • The signatory groups form a single community (umma) distinct from outsiders, bound to mutual defence of Medina.
  • Blood-money (diya) and ransom of prisoners remain obligations of each clan, now organized on a more equitable basis.
  • The Jewish tribes are recognized as a community "alongside the believers," retaining their religion; several clauses guarantee them protection and equal political rights in return for loyalty and support in war.
  • Medina (Yathrib) is declared a sanctuary (haram) in which bloodshed among the parties is forbidden.
  • Disputes are to be referred "to God and to Muhammad" for arbitration, making Muhammad the supreme arbiter of the confederation.

Significance

The charter is often described as one of the earliest written constitutions and is frequently cited in modern debates about pluralism and citizenship in Islam, since it recognized the Jewish tribes as part of the political community of Medina rather than requiring their conversion. Its provisions were progressively overtaken by events: relations with the Jewish tribes of Banu Qaynuqa, Banu Nadir and Banu Qurayza broke down amid the conflict with Mecca, ending in their expulsion or destruction between 624 and 627 CE.
