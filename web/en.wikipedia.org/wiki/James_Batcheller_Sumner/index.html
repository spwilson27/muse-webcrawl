James Batcheller Sumner (19 November 1887 – 12 August 1955) was an American biochemist who first crystallised an enzyme, demonstrating that enzymes are proteins. For this work he shared the 1946 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with John Howard Northrop and Wendell Meredith Stanley.

Work

Sumner lost his left arm in a hunting accident as a teenager and nonetheless pursued laboratory chemistry, earning his doctorate at Harvard Medical School. At Cornell University, in 1926, he isolated and crystallised the enzyme urease from jack beans and showed that the crystals were protein and catalysed the enzyme's reaction. The claim was controversial — Richard Willstätter and others held that enzymes were small molecules carried on inert colloids — but it was vindicated when Northrop crystallised pepsin and other enzymes in the 1930s. Sumner later crystallised catalase and wrote a standard textbook of biological chemistry.
