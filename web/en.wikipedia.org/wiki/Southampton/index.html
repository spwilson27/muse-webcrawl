Southampton is a port city in Hampshire, on the south coast of England. It lies at the northern end of Southampton Water, between the mouths of the rivers Test and Itchen. One of Britain's major ports, Southampton is the country's principal cruise port and a major container terminal.

History

The Roman settlement of Clausentum and the later Saxon port of Hamwic preceded the medieval walled town, whose surviving town walls and Bargate date from the Norman period onwards. Southampton was the departure point of the Mayflower in 1620 and of RMS Titanic on her maiden voyage in 1912. In the 19th and 20th centuries it became the gateway for ocean liners crossing the Atlantic. The Supermarine Spitfire was designed and first built in the city, and Southampton was heavily bombed during the Southampton Blitz of 1940–41. The city was a major embarkation point for the D-Day landings in 1944.

Economy and culture

The Port of Southampton handles millions of tonnes of cargo, over a million cruise passengers a year, and the majority of the UK's car exports. The University of Southampton is a leading research university, noted for engineering, oceanography (the National Oceanography Centre is in the city) and computer science — Tim Berners-Lee held a chair there. Southampton F.C., nicknamed "The Saints", play at St Mary's Stadium. The city received city status in 1964 and was a candidate for UK City of Culture 2025.

Transport

Southampton Central station links the city to London Waterloo, and Southampton Airport serves domestic and European destinations. The M27 motorway passes north of the city, connecting with the M3 to London.
