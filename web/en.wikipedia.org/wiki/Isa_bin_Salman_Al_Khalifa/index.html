Isa bin Salman Al Khalifa

Isa bin Salman Al Khalifa
Emir of Bahrain
Reign: 2 November 1961 – 6 March 1999
Born: 3 June 1933, Jasra, Bahrain
Died: 6 March 1999 (aged 65), Manama, Bahrain
House: Khalifa
Successor: Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa

Isa bin Salman Al Khalifa (3 June 1933 – 6 March 1999) was the Emir of Bahrain from 1961 until his death in 1999. He was the first emir of Bahrain following the end of the British protectorate, overseeing the country's independence in 1971 and its transformation into a wealthy oil-producing state and regional financial centre.

Reign

Isa succeeded his father, Salman bin Hamad Al Khalifa, in 1961. On 15 August 1971 Bahrain declared independence from Britain, and Isa became Emir of the new State of Bahrain. In 1973 a constitution was promulgated and elections held for a National Assembly, but in 1975 the Emir dissolved the assembly and suspended the constitutional clauses after disputes over legislation, ruling by decree thereafter.

Development and foreign policy

During his reign Bahrain developed its petroleum industry, built the causeway to Saudi Arabia (opened 1986), and established itself as a banking and services hub for the Gulf. Bahrain hosted the United States Navy's Fifth Fleet, and during the 1990–91 Gulf War it was a member of the coalition against Iraq. Relations with Qatar were strained by the dispute over the Hawar Islands.

Death and succession

Isa died of a heart attack on 6 March 1999 and was succeeded by his eldest son, Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa, who later proclaimed himself King of Bahrain.
