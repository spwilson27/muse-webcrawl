Rainbow Books

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Rainbow Books are a collection of specification documents defining the formats of the various types of compact discs. Each standard is named for the colour of the cover of its specification book. The series was begun by Philips and Sony with the Red Book for audio CDs and has grown to cover data, recordable, and high-definition optical formats.

OriginatorsPhilips and Sony
First bookRed Book (CD-DA), 1980
ScopeCompact disc format specifications

The books

Red Book (1980) defines the Compact Disc Digital Audio (CD-DA) format: 16-bit PCM audio at 44.1 kHz. Yellow Book (1983) defines CD-ROM, adapting the audio disc for computer data with additional error correction. Green Book (1987) covers CD-i, Philips's interactive multimedia format. Orange Book (1989) defines recordable discs, CD-MO and CD-R/CD-RW. White Book (1993) defines Video CD. Blue Book (1995) defines Enhanced Music CD (CD+). Beige Book (1992) defines the Photo CD. Scarlet Book (1999) defines the Super Audio CD, and Purple Book (2000) defines the Double Density CD.

Significance

The Rainbow Books made the compact disc an open, licensed ecosystem: any manufacturer could build players or press discs conforming to a published specification, while Philips and Sony collected royalties through licensing programmes. The naming convention has since been imitated by other consortia; the DVD and Blu-ray formats, by contrast, are documented in specification books without the colour naming tradition.
