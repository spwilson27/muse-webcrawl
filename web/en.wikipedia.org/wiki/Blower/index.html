Blower

An industrial centrifugal blower.
Blower
TypeMachine for moving air or gas
Pressure riseLow to moderate (between fan and compressor)
Common typesCentrifugal, positive-displacement (Roots, screw, lobe)
ApplicationsHVAC, pneumatic conveying, supercharging, wastewater aeration

A blower is a machine that increases the pressure of air or gas by moving it through rotating impellers or positive-displacement elements. Blowers occupy an intermediate position between fans, which move air at very low pressure rise, and compressors, which achieve much higher pressures. The term is used both in industry and for hand-held devices such as leaf blowers.

Types

Centrifugal blowers

Centrifugal blowers use a rotating impeller inside a volute (spiral) housing. Air enters axially near the hub and is flung outward by centrifugal force, exiting at higher velocity and pressure. They are widely used in heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) systems, dust collection and industrial drying.

Positive-displacement blowers

Positive-displacement blowers trap a fixed volume of gas and force it toward the discharge. The best-known design is the Roots blower, invented by Philander and Francis Roots in 1860, which uses two counter-rotating lobed rotors. Roots blowers are used in wastewater treatment aeration, pneumatic conveying and as superchargers on internal combustion engines. Screw blowers, using helical rotors, offer higher efficiency at moderate pressures.

Applications

Blowers are used for ventilation, combustion air supply to furnaces and boilers, fluidised-bed systems, vacuum generation (when operated in reverse as exhausters), cooling of electronic equipment, and air knives in manufacturing. A supercharger is often called a blower in automotive slang, especially the Roots-type units mounted on drag-racing engines. Hand-held and backpack leaf blowers use small centrifugal or axial fans powered by electric motors or two-stroke engines to clear leaves and debris.
