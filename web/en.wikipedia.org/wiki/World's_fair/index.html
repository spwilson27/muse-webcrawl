A world's fair, also known as a universal exhibition or an expo, is a large international exhibition designed to showcase the achievements of nations. These exhibitions vary widely in character and are held in varying parts of the world at specific times of the year, attracting millions of visitors. Since 1928 they have been regulated by the Bureau International des Expositions (BIE) in Paris, which distinguishes large registered "World Expos" (held every five years, lasting up to six months) from smaller recognised "Specialised Expos".

History

19th century

The tradition began with the Great Exhibition of 1851 in London's Crystal Palace, organised by Prince Albert and Henry Cole, which drew six million visitors and displayed over 100,000 exhibits. Paris answered with a series of Expositions Universelles; the 1889 edition left the Eiffel Tower as its emblem. American fairs included Philadelphia's Centennial Exposition (1876) and Chicago's World's Columbian Exposition (1893), whose neoclassical "White City" influenced American urban planning. Fairs became showcases of industrial technology — telephones, electric light, and moving pictures all reached mass audiences at expositions.

20th century

New York hosted influential fairs in 1939–40 ("The World of Tomorrow") and 1964–65, which introduced the Bell System Picturephone and popularised futuristic design. Expo 67 in Montreal and Expo '70 in Osaka marked the format's postwar peak, with attendance of 50 and 64 million respectively. Expo 2000 in Hanover and Expo 2010 in Shanghai (73 million visitors, the largest ever) extended the tradition into the 21st century, with Expo 2020 in Dubai delayed a year by the COVID-19 pandemic.

Themes and legacies

National pavilions compete in architecture and spectacle, while host cities use expos to drive infrastructure investment and tourism. Enduring legacies include the Crystal Palace (destroyed 1936), the Eiffel Tower, Seattle's Space Needle (1962), the Atomium in Brussels (1958), and San Antonio's Tower of the Americas (1968). Critics have questioned the fairs' cost, their colonial-era ethnographic displays ("human zoos") in the early period, and their environmental footprint.

Types of exhibition

The BIE recognises World Expos, Specialised Expos, Horticultural Expos (with AIPH), and the Triennale di Milano. In the United States, "world's fair" remains the common term regardless of BIE sanction.
