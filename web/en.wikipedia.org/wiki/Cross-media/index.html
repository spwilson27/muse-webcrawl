Cross-media

Cross-media refers to communication, storytelling, marketing or production in which content, services or experiences are distributed across multiple media platforms — such as print, television, film, the web, social media and mobile apps — in an integrated way, so that each channel contributes something distinctive to a unified whole. The term is used across the creative industries, advertising and media studies.

Concepts

Cross-media production is distinguished from simple adaptation or repurposing (the same content moved between media) by the aim that each platform adds new material or functionality suited to that medium, and that audiences can enter the work through any of the channels. Related concepts include transmedia storytelling, popularised by Henry Jenkins, in which elements of a fiction are dispersed systematically across media to create an integrated entertainment experience; media convergence; and cross-media marketing, in which campaigns coordinate print, broadcast and digital advertising and measure response across channels with variable-data and personalised media.

Applications

Examples include entertainment franchises that extend a film story into games, comics and web series; educational publishing combining textbooks with online exercises and video; and journalism projects in which a broadcaster, newspaper and website each publish complementary parts of an investigation. Production techniques such as shared asset workflows and 360-degree commissioning developed as broadcasters and publishers adapted to multi-platform audiences in the 2000s and 2010s.
