Dumbarton Castle

Dumbarton Castle
Location: Dumbarton, West Dunbartonshire, Scotland
Site: Dumbarton Rock, a volcanic plug
Managed by: Historic Environment Scotland

Dumbarton Castle stands on Dumbarton Rock, a volcanic plug of basalt at the confluence of the River Leven and the River Clyde in western Scotland. It has the longest recorded history of any stronghold in Scotland, documented from the 5th century AD.

History

Known in Welsh tradition as Alt Clut, "the rock of the Clyde", the fortress was the capital of the Brittonic Kingdom of Strathclyde. It withstood a famous four-month Viking siege in 870–871, after which Olaf the White carried off its treasure and captives to Dublin. In the medieval Wars of Scottish Independence the castle changed hands several times; the young Mary, Queen of Scots sheltered here in 1548 before sailing to France, and it remained a royal stronghold and state prison in later centuries.

The castle today

The surviving defences are largely 18th-century artillery fortifications built around the twin peaks of the rock, the White Tower Crag and the Beak. A long stair of 557 steps climbs to the summit. The castle is in the care of Historic Environment Scotland and open to the public.
