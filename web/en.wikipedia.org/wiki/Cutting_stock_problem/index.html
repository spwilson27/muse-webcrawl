Cutting stock problem

FieldOperations research, combinatorial optimisation
GoalCut stock material into required pieces with minimum waste
ComplexityNP-hard
Classic methodGilmore–Gomory column generation (1961–1963)

The cutting stock problem is an optimisation problem in operations research: given a supply of stock material of fixed size (such as rolls of paper, sheets of metal, or lengths of timber) and a demand for smaller pieces of specified sizes, determine how to cut the stock so as to meet demand while minimising waste or, equivalently, the number of stock pieces used.

Formulation

In the one-dimensional version, stock rolls of length L must be cut into items of lengths li with demands di. A cutting pattern describes how many items of each size are obtained from one stock piece. The problem is then to choose non-negative integers of patterns covering all demands at minimum cost. Because the number of possible patterns is enormous, practical solution uses the pattern-based linear programming formulation of Gilmore and Gomory, in which patterns are generated as needed by solving a knapsack problem (column generation). The two-dimensional and three-dimensional analogues—cutting sheets or packing boxes—are closely related to the bin packing problem.

Applications

The problem arises in the paper, steel, glass, textile, and timber industries, where even small reductions in trim waste translate into large savings. It was one of the earliest practical applications of linear programming; Kantorovich formulated related problems in 1939, and the Gilmore–Gomory papers of the 1960s made large industrial instances tractable.
