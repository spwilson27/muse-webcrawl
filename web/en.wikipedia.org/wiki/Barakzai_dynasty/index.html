Barakzai dynasty

The Barakzai dynasty was the ruling dynasty of the Emirate and later Kingdom of Afghanistan from 1823 (or 1826) until the monarchy was abolished in 1973. The Barakzai are a Pashtun tribe of the Durrani confederation; after the decline of the Durrani (Sadozai) Empire, the Barakzai sardar Dost Mohammad Khan seized Kabul and founded an emirate, and his descendants — with interruptions — ruled Afghanistan through the "Great Game", both World Wars, and the modernising reigns of the 20th century, ending with King Mohammed Zahir Shah's deposition in 1973.

History

The Barakzai rose as kingmakers under the Sadozai shahs: Fateh Khan Barakzai served as wazir before his blinding and murder in 1818 sparked a Barakzai takeover of the Durrani territories. His brother Dost Mohammad took Kabul in 1826 and the title of Amir in 1835. His reign was interrupted by the First Anglo-Afghan War (1839–1842), after which he was restored and reunified much of the country before his death in 1863.

Later rulers included Sher Ali Khan, Abdur Rahman Khan (the "Iron Amir", r. 1880–1901), Habibullah Khan, Amanullah Khan (who won full independence in 1919 and proclaimed a kingdom in 1926), the brief Musahiban restoration under Nadir Shah from 1929, and finally Mohammed Zahir Shah (r. 1933–1973), whose forty-year reign ended when his cousin Mohammed Daoud Khan proclaimed a republic.
