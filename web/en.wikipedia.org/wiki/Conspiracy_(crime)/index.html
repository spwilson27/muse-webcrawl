Conspiracy (criminal)

In criminal law, a conspiracy is an agreement between two or more people to commit an unlawful act, or to commit a lawful act by unlawful means. Conspiracy is an inchoate offence: it is complete once the agreement is made (and, in many jurisdictions, once an overt act in furtherance of the agreement is committed), even if the planned crime is never carried out.

Elements

The traditional elements of conspiracy are (1) an agreement between two or more persons, (2) an intent to agree, (3) an intent that the underlying crime be committed, and (4) in many jurisdictions, an overt act by at least one conspirator in furtherance of the plan. Under the Pinkerton doctrine in United States federal law, a conspirator may also be liable for foreseeable offences committed by co-conspirators in furtherance of the conspiracy.

History

The offence developed in English common law, where it was originally concerned with combinations to abuse legal process, and was later extended to agreements to commit crimes generally. Statutory versions now exist in most common-law jurisdictions, and analogous offences — such as criminal association — exist in civil-law systems.
