Saint Anthony of Padua

Saint Anthony of Padua (Portuguese: Santo António de Pádua; born Fernando Martins de Bulhões; 15 August 1195 – 13 June 1231) was a Portuguese Catholic priest and friar of the Franciscan Order. Born and raised in Lisbon, he died in Padua, Italy. Renowned for his powerful preaching and knowledge of Scripture, he was canonised less than a year after his death and later proclaimed a Doctor of the Church. He is the patron saint of lost things and lost people.

Saint Anthony is traditionally depicted holding the Christ Child and a lily.

Life

Fernando was born to a wealthy Lisbon family and educated at the cathedral school. He joined the Augustinian Canons Regular at the Abbey of Santa Cruz in Coimbra, where he devoted himself to study and prayer. Moved by the relics of the first Franciscan martyrs, killed in Morocco, he joined the Franciscans in 1220, taking the name Anthony in honour of Anthony the Great, and set out for Morocco himself, returning to Europe through illness.

In Italy his gifts as a preacher were discovered almost by accident, when he was called upon to preach at an ordination without preparation. Saint Francis of Assisi appointed him the first teacher of theology for the friars, and Anthony spent his remaining years preaching across northern Italy and southern France against heresy and usury, to such effect that he was called the "Hammer of Heretics".

Death and legacy

Anthony died at the Poor Clare monastery of Arcella, near Padua, on 13 June 1231. His tomb in the great Basilica of Saint Anthony in Padua remains a major pilgrimage destination; his tongue, found incorrupt when his remains were exhumed, is kept there as a relic. Pope Pius XII declared him a Doctor of the Church in 1946, with the title Doctor Evangelicus. In popular devotion he is invoked above all for the recovery of lost objects.
