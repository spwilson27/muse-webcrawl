Dunkeld

Dunkeld (Scottish Gaelic: Dùn Chailleann, "fort of the Caledonians") is a small town in Perth and Kinross, Scotland, on the north bank of the River Tay. Together with neighbouring Birnam it is known for Dunkeld Cathedral, its restored 18th-century streets, and associations with Shakespeare's Macbeth (Birnam Wood).

History

Dunkeld was an early centre of the Scottish church; relics of St Columba were brought here in the 9th century and Kenneth MacAlpin built a church in 848. The cathedral, begun in the 13th century, was partly ruined at the Reformation. In 1689 Jacobites were defeated in the Battle of Dunkeld by the Cameronians, though much of the town was burned. The Atholl Street and Cathedral Street houses were restored by the National Trust for Scotland in the 20th century and form one of Scotland's best-preserved 18th-century town centres.

Surroundings

The area is known for the Hermitage woodland with its folly Ossian's Hall and some of Britain's tallest trees, and for the Parent Larch, a surviving original of the larches from which Highland plantations were seeded.
