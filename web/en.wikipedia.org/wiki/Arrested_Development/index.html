Arrested Development is an American television sitcom created by Mitchell Hurwitz. Set in Newport Beach, California, it follows the Bluths, a formerly wealthy and habitually dysfunctional family, centred on the comparatively level-headed Michael Bluth (Jason Bateman), who tries to hold the family together after its patriarch is imprisoned for corporate fraud.

Production and style

The series is distinguished by its dense, interwoven plotting, running gags, foreshadowing, rapid-fire wordplay and extensive use of narration by executive producer Ron Howard. It was filmed single-camera without a laugh track, a comparatively unusual format for network comedy at the time. Executive producers included Brian Grazer and Ron Howard of Imagine Entertainment.

Run and reception

The show aired three seasons on Fox from 2003 to 2006. Despite widespread critical acclaim — including the Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Comedy Series in 2004 and six Emmys overall — ratings were low and Fox cancelled it. It developed a strong cult following on DVD. Netflix revived it for a fourth season in 2013, structured around interlocking episodes focused on individual characters, and a fifth season in 2018–2019. The series frequently appears on critics' lists of the greatest television comedies.
