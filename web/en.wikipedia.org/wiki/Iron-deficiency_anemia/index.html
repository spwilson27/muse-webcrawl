Iron-deficiency anemia

Iron-deficiency anemia is anemia caused by a lack of iron. Anemia is defined as a decrease in the number of red blood cells or the amount of hemoglobin in the blood. When onset is slow, symptoms are often vague, including feeling tired, weakness, shortness of breath or headaches. It is the most common cause of anemia worldwide.

Causes

Causes include blood loss (such as from heavy menstrual periods, gastrointestinal bleeding or frequent blood donation), poor dietary intake, malabsorption (for example coeliac disease or after gastric surgery), and increased requirements during pregnancy or growth. In the developing world, parasitic worms and malaria are additional factors.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is typically by blood tests showing a low hemoglobin with small red blood cells (microcytosis) and low serum ferritin, which reflects iron stores. Identifying the source of blood loss is an important part of evaluation.

Treatment and epidemiology

Treatment involves addressing the underlying cause and iron supplementation, orally or intravenously; severe cases may require transfusion. Iron-deficiency anemia affected about 1.5 billion people globally in recent estimates and is a major cause of disability, particularly in women of reproductive age.
