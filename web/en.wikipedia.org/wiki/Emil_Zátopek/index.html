Emil Zátopek

Emil Ferdinand Zátopek (19 September 1922 – 21 November 2000) was a Czech long-distance runner best known for winning three gold medals at the 1952 Summer Olympics in Helsinki. He won gold in the 5,000 metres and 10,000 metres runs, and given the last-minute decision to compete in the first marathon of his life, he won that, too.

Career

Zátopek first won gold in the 10,000 m at the 1948 London Olympics, also taking silver in the 5,000 m. He set numerous world records at distances from 5,000 m to 30 km and was the first runner to break 29 minutes for the 10,000 metres (1954). Nicknamed the "Czech Locomotive", he was renowned for his punishing interval training method and his agonized facial expressions while racing.

Later life

After the 1968 Prague Spring, which he supported, Zátopek was expelled from the army and the Communist Party and spent years in manual work before being rehabilitated. He died in Prague in 2000 and is regarded as one of the greatest distance runners of all time.
