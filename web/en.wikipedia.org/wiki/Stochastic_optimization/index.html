Stochastic optimization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stochastic optimization (SO) are optimization methods that generate and use random variables. For stochastic problems, the random variables appear in the formulation of the optimization problem itself, which involves random objective functions or random constraints. Stochastic optimization methods also include methods with random iterates. Some stochastic optimization methods use random iterates to solve stochastic problems, combining both meanings of stochastic optimization.

Stochastic optimization methods generalize deterministic methods for deterministic problems. They are widely applied in machine learning, simulation-based engineering design, finance, and control, where objectives can only be estimated through noisy observations or Monte Carlo simulation.

Methods for stochastic functions

Partially random input data arise in such areas as real-time estimation and control, simulation-based optimization for function fitting, experiments in science and engineering, and finance. Methods in this class include stochastic approximation (such as the Robbins–Monro algorithm), stochastic gradient descent, finite-difference and simultaneous perturbation methods (SPSA), and stochastic programming approaches that optimize expected performance over distributions of scenarios.

Randomized search methods

On the other hand, even when the data set consists of precise measurements, some methods introduce an inherent randomness into the search process to accelerate progress or to escape local optima. Examples include simulated annealing, cross-entropy method, random search, genetic algorithms and other evolutionary algorithms, and randomized coordinate descent. Such methods are valued for problems that are discontinuous, multimodal, or otherwise hostile to gradient-based optimization.

Applications

Stochastic optimization underpins the training of large statistical models, portfolio selection under uncertainty, inventory and supply-chain planning, and the design of experiments in which each objective evaluation requires a costly simulation.
