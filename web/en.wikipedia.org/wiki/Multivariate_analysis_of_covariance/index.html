Multivariate analysis of covariance

Multivariate analysis of covariance (MANCOVA) is an extension of analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) to cases where there is more than one dependent variable. It is a generalized form of multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA), in which one or more continuous covariates are included in the model so that differences among groups on the dependent variables can be assessed after adjusting for the covariates' effects, reducing error variance and removing confounding influences.

Model and assumptions

Like MANOVA, MANCOVA models a matrix of dependent variables as a function of categorical factors, adding continuous covariates whose regression slopes are assumed equal across groups (homogeneity of regression slopes). Key assumptions include multivariate normality of residuals, homogeneity of variance–covariance matrices across groups (tested with Box's M), linear relationships between each covariate and the dependent variables, independence of the covariates from the treatment (ideally measured before treatment), and reliable measurement of covariates, since error in a covariate biases adjustment.

Test statistics

Omnibus group effects are tested with multivariate statistics such as Wilks' lambda, Pillai's trace, Hotelling–Lawley trace, and Roy's greatest root, computed from hypothesis and error sum-of-squares-and-cross-products matrices after adjustment for the covariates. Significant multivariate results are typically followed by univariate ANCOVAs on each dependent variable, with adjusted (estimated marginal) means reported.

Uses

MANCOVA is used in experimental and quasi-experimental research — in psychology, education, medicine, and the social sciences — when several correlated outcome measures are taken and one or more baseline or nuisance variables must be statistically controlled. It is implemented in general linear model procedures in major statistical packages.
