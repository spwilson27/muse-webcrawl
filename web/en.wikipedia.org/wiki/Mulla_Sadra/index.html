Mulla Sadra

Ṣadr ad-Dīn Muḥammad Shīrāzī, more commonly known as Mullā Ṣadrā (Persian: ملا صدرا; Arabic: صدر المتألهين; c. 1571/2 – 1640), was a Persian Muslim philosopher, theologian and 'Ālim who led the Iranian cultural renaissance in the 17th century. According to Oliver Leaman, Mulla Sadra is arguably the single most important and influential philosopher in the Muslim world in the last four hundred years.

Born in Shiraz, he studied in Isfahan under Mir Damad and Shaykh Baha'i, and after years of retreat and study at Kahak near Qom he returned to teach in Shiraz at the Khan School. His magnum opus is Al-Hikma al-muta'aliya fi-l-asfar al-'aqliyya al-arba'a ("The Transcendent Philosophy of the Four Journeys of the Intellect"), known as the Asfar.

Sadra's "transcendent theosophy" (al-hikmat al-muta'aliyah) synthesised Avicennan philosophy, Suhrawardi's Illuminationism, Ibn Arabi's Sufism and Twelver Shi'a theology. Its signature doctrines include the primacy of existence over essence, substantial motion (al-harakat al-jawhariyya) — the idea that substance itself is in constant flux — and a graded, unified ontology of being. His system remains the dominant philosophical tradition taught in the seminaries of Iran and Iraq.
