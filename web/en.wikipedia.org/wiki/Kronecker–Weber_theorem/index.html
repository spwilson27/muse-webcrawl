In algebraic number theory, the Kronecker–Weber theorem states that every abelian extension of the rational numbers is contained in a cyclotomic field. Equivalently, every algebraic integer whose Galois group over ℚ is abelian is expressible as a sum of roots of unity with rational coefficients.

Statement

Let K/ℚ be a finite Galois extension with abelian Galois group. Then there exists a positive integer n such that K ⊆ ℚ(ζn), where ζn is a primitive n-th root of unity. The smallest such n relates to the conductor of K.

History

Leopold Kronecker announced the result in 1853 and proved it for extensions of degree a power of 2 and for quadratic extensions; Heinrich Martin Weber gave an attempted complete proof in 1886 that contained a gap. David Hilbert supplied the first complete proof in 1896, in the course of writing his Zahlbericht. The theorem realised part of Kronecker's youthful dream (his Jugendtraum) of generating all abelian extensions of a number field by special values of analytic functions — a programme later extended by class field theory.

Significance

The theorem completely describes the abelian extensions of ℚ and is the prototype for explicit class field theory. Its analogue holds for imaginary quadratic fields using elliptic curves with complex multiplication, but fails for general number fields, where the corresponding questions belong to Hilbert's twelfth problem.
