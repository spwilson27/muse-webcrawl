Laser guidance

Laser guidance is a method of guiding a missile, bomb, or other projectile to a target by means of a laser beam. The target is illuminated ("painted") by a laser designator, and the weapon carries a seeker that detects the reflected laser light, which is pulsed in a specific coded pattern, and steers toward the centre of the reflection. Laser-guided weapons are a class of precision-guided munitions.

How it works

A designator — mounted on an aircraft, vehicle, or carried by ground troops — emits pulses of infrared laser light at the target. The weapon's seeker, typically a quadrant detector in the nose, measures the direction of the reflected energy. Guidance electronics compute steering commands so the weapon flies toward the spot. Because the designator must keep the beam on target until impact ("buddy lasing" if a different platform designates), laser guidance is a form of semi-active homing.

History and use

Laser-guided bombs were first developed in the 1960s; the Paveway series was used extensively in the Vietnam War, famously destroying the Thanh Hóa Bridge in 1972 after years of failed conventional attacks. Laser guidance is now used in missiles such as the AGM-65 Maverick and Hellfire, and in artillery shells and rockets. Limitations include degraded performance in smoke, dust, fog, or rain, which scatter the beam.
