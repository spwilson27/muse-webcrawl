Yoga as exercise

Yoga as exercise is a physical activity consisting largely of postures (asanas), sometimes accompanied by breathing exercises and ending with relaxation or meditation. It is the form of yoga most widely practised in the Western world and in modern India, and differs from traditional haṭha yoga in its emphasis on fitness, flexibility and stress relief rather than spiritual liberation. Its best-known styles include Iyengar yoga, Ashtanga (vinyasa) yoga and Bikram (hot) yoga.

Origins

Modern postural yoga was shaped in early twentieth-century India, where teachers such as Tirumalai Krishnamacharya combined medieval haṭha yoga postures with Western gymnastics and bodybuilding practices then current in India. His students B. K. S. Iyengar, K. Pattabhi Jois and Indra Devi carried the practice worldwide from the middle of the century, and it grew into a global industry of classes, studios, teacher training and apparel.

Practice

A typical class works through a sequence of standing, seated, balancing and inverted postures, often flowing between them in time with the breath (vinyasa), and finishes with Savasana (corpse pose) for relaxation. The repeated sequence Surya Namaskar (sun salutation) is a common warm-up. Classes vary widely in pace, heat, precision of alignment and the degree of spiritual content.

Health effects and risks

Evidence suggests yoga as exercise can improve balance, flexibility and strength and may modestly help with chronic low back pain and stress, though trials are often small and at risk of bias. Injuries, usually strains from overstretching, occur at rates comparable to other forms of exercise, with rare serious events reported mainly in intensive practice.
