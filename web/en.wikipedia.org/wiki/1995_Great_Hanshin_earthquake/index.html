The Great Hanshin earthquake, also known as the Kobe earthquake, occurred on 17 January 1995 at 05:46 JST in the southern part of Hyōgo Prefecture, Japan, including the city of Kobe. Measuring 6.9 on the moment magnitude scale, it was the deadliest earthquake in Japan since the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake.

The quake struck along the Nojima Fault on Awaji Island, close to the densely populated cities of Kobe and Osaka. More than 6,400 people died, most of them in Kobe, and over 43,000 were injured. Around 200,000 buildings were damaged or destroyed, and large fires broke out in several districts. A section of the elevated Hanshin Expressway toppled over in a scene that became emblematic of the disaster.

Aftermath

The earthquake exposed weaknesses in Japan's disaster preparedness and building codes for older structures. It led to major revisions of seismic design standards, improvements in emergency response, and the establishment of 17 January as Disaster Prevention and Volunteer Day in Japan. Economic losses were estimated at around ten trillion yen.
