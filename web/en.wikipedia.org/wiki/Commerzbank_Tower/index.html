Commerzbank Tower

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Commerzbank Tower is a 56-storey skyscraper in the banking district of Frankfurt am Main, Germany. Completed in 1997 and designed by Foster and Partners, it rises to 259 metres (850 ft), or 300 metres including its antenna spire. On completion it was the tallest building in Europe, a title it held until 2003, and it remained the tallest building in the European Union until the Shard in London surpassed it in 2011. It serves as the headquarters of Commerzbank.

Design

The tower has a triangular plan with rounded corners arranged around a full-height central atrium. Its most distinctive feature is a series of planted "sky gardens", each four storeys high, spiralling up the building on different sides; the gardens provide natural light and ventilation to the offices and social space for occupants. The building was widely noted as an early example of an ecological high-rise: offices are designed so that a large proportion can be naturally ventilated for much of the year, reducing reliance on air conditioning.

Construction and status

The tower stands on Kaiserplatz in central Frankfurt and is floodlit at night, giving it a prominent place on the city's skyline. A yellow signal light at the top is part of its architectural lighting scheme. The Commerzbank Tower remains one of the most recognisable buildings in Germany and a landmark of the Frankfurt skyline.
