Platform game

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A platform game (often simplified as platformer and sometimes called a jump 'n' run game) is a sub-genre of action video games in which the core objective is to move the player character between points in an environment. Platform games are characterized by levels with uneven terrain and suspended platforms of varying height that require jumping and climbing to traverse. Other acrobatic maneuvers may factor into the gameplay, such as swinging from objects like vines or grappling hooks, jumping off walls, air-dashing, gliding through the air, and being shot from cannons or bouncing off springboards or trampolines.

Although commonly associated with two-dimensional video games, the genre also exists in three dimensions. The genre's best-known representative is Nintendo's Super Mario series, while other prominent examples include Sonic the Hedgehog, Donkey Kong, Mega Man, Rayman, and Crash Bandicoot.

History

Single-screen era

The platform game genre originated in the early 1980s. Space Panic (1980), an arcade game by Universal, is sometimes credited as the first platform game, though it lacked a jumping mechanic — players instead dug holes to trap enemies. Nintendo's Donkey Kong (1981), designed by Shigeru Miyamoto, established the template of running and jumping over obstacles and across gaps, and introduced "Jumpman", later renamed Mario.

Scrolling platformers

Side-scrolling platformers emerged as home hardware improved. Pitfall! (1982) for the Atari 2600 featured a running and jumping character across connected screens. Super Mario Bros. (1985) for the Nintendo Entertainment System perfected smooth scrolling levels and became one of the best-selling games of all time, defining the genre for a generation. Sega's Sonic the Hedgehog (1991) emphasized speed as a core mechanic.

3D platformers

The transition to three-dimensional graphics in the mid-1990s produced landmark titles such as Super Mario 64 (1996), which established conventions for camera control and analog movement in 3D space, and Crash Bandicoot (1996) and Spyro the Dragon (1998) on the PlayStation.

Modern era

Independent developers revived the 2D platformer in the 2000s and 2010s with titles such as Braid (2008), Super Meat Boy (2010), Shovel Knight (2014), Ori and the Blind Forest (2015), Hollow Knight (2017), and Celeste (2018), the latter two frequently incorporating Metroidvania exploration elements.

Gameplay

Platform games typically challenge the player to traverse an environment by jumping between platforms, avoiding obstacles and enemies, and often collecting items. Precision jumping — sometimes demanding pixel-perfect landings — is a hallmark of the genre. Subgenres include puzzle-platformers, run-and-gun platformers, and "cinematic platformers" such as Prince of Persia (1989) and Another World (1991), which emphasized realistic animation and deliberate movement.
