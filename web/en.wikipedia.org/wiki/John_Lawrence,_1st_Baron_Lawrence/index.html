John Laird Mair Lawrence, 1st Baron Lawrence (March 4, 1811 – January 27, 1879) was a British administrator in India who served as Viceroy of India from 1864 to 1869. A leading figure of the "Punjab school" of administration, he was widely credited with holding the Punjab during the Indian Rebellion of 1857.

Career in India

Lawrence joined the East India Company and went to India in 1830, working in the Delhi territory and, after the Anglo-Sikh wars, on the Board of Administration for the newly annexed Punjab alongside his brother Henry. As Chief Commissioner from 1853 he reorganised the province's revenue, policing and public works. During the 1857 rebellion he kept the Punjab loyal, disarming suspect regiments and sending troops that helped relieve Delhi — earning him the epithet "Saviour of India".

Viceroy

As Viceroy, Lawrence pursued a policy of cautious non-intervention in Afghanistan, known as "masterly inactivity", and promoted irrigation, railways and famine relief administration. He was raised to the peerage as Baron Lawrence on leaving India in 1869.
