Palm Beach, Florida

Palm Beach is an incorporated town in Palm Beach County, Florida, United States. Located on a barrier island in east-central Palm Beach County, the town is separated from the mainland city of West Palm Beach by the Lake Worth Lagoon. With a population of about 9,000 in the 2020 census, Palm Beach is known as one of the wealthiest communities in the United States, famous for its Gilded Age mansions, upscale shopping on Worth Avenue, and historic resorts.

History

The area's modern development began in the 1870s and 1880s, when settlers established coconut palm groves — legend holds that coconuts washed ashore from the wreck of the Spanish ship Providencia in 1878 and were planted along the coast, giving the town its name. Railroad magnate Henry Flagler transformed Palm Beach into a premier winter resort in the 1890s, building the Royal Poinciana Hotel and The Breakers hotel, and extending his Florida East Coast Railway across the lagoon. His estate, Whitehall, is now the Flagler Museum.

During the Florida land boom of the 1920s, architect Addison Mizner reshaped the town with Mediterranean Revival mansions and the shopping arcades of Worth Avenue. Landmarks include Mar-a-Lago, built for Marjorie Merriweather Post in 1927 and later owned by Donald Trump.

Geography and climate

Palm Beach occupies the northern portion of an 18-mile-long barrier island. It has a tropical rainforest climate with hot, humid summers and warm, dry winters, moderated by the Atlantic Ocean and the nearby Gulf Stream.
