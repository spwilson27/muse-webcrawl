Star of David

The Star of David (Hebrew: מָגֵן דָּוִד, Magen David, "Shield of David") is a hexagram formed by two overlapping equilateral triangles. It is the most widely recognised symbol of Judaism and Jewish identity, and appears in blue on the flag of Israel.

History

Hexagrams occur in many cultures and religions. Jewish use dates to late antiquity, when the symbol appeared on synagogues and seals, but it became a distinctly Jewish emblem mainly in the early modern period in Prague and was adopted by the Zionist movement in 1897. During the Holocaust, Jews in Nazi-occupied Europe were forced to wear a yellow Star of David badge.

Interpretation

Various meanings have been proposed: the interlocking triangles represent the relationship between God and the Jewish people, or fire and water, or the six directions of space plus the centre.
