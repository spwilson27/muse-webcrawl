Local government in the Republic of Ireland

Local government in the Republic of Ireland is provided by 31 local authorities, comprising county councils, city councils and city and county councils. Most local authorities are responsible for a range of functions including housing, planning, roads, water services, environmental protection and recreation.

History

The system derives from the Local Government (Ireland) Act 1898, which established elected county and district councils based on the traditional counties. Following independence, the system was retained with modifications. The Local Government Reform Act 2014, implemented in June 2014, abolished town councils, merged several city and county councils (Limerick, Waterford, and North and South Tipperary), and introduced municipal districts within counties.

Structure

There are 26 county councils, 3 city councils (Dublin, Cork and Galway) and 2 city and county councils. Councillors are elected every five years by proportional representation using the single transferable vote. Each local authority is headed by a chief executive, appointed by the council, alongside an elected cathaoirleach (chairperson) or mayor.

Below county level, municipal districts are areas within a county for the purpose of local elections and the performance of certain functions. Certain towns also retain ceremonial mayors.

Functions and finance

Local authorities derive income from commercial rates, the Local Property Tax, service charges such as waste and parking, and grants from central government. Ireland has one of the most centralised local government systems in Europe, with local authorities accounting for a comparatively small share of public spending; responsibilities such as health and policing are administered nationally.
