Turkic peoples

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Turkic horsemen in traditional dress riding across the Central Asian steppe
Turkic horsemen in traditional dress riding across the Central Asian steppe

The Turkic peoples are a collection of diverse ethnic groups of West, Central, East and North Asia and parts of Europe who speak Turkic languages — some 35 related tongues with roughly 200 million speakers, the largest groups being Turks, Azerbaijanis, Uzbeks, Kazakhs, Uyghurs, Tatars and Kyrgyz. Emerging from the Altai-steppe world, Turkic peoples built empires that reshaped Eurasia (Göktürks, Seljuks, Ottomans) and today form six sovereign states and numerous autonomous regions, notably in Russia and China.

History

The Göktürk Khaganate (sixth century) first bore the name 'Türk'; Turkic expansions carried the languages across the steppe to Anatolia (after Manzikert, 1071) and Siberia. Islamisation (from the Karakhanids onward) tied most Turkic peoples to the Muslim world, while Siberian groups kept shamanic traditions and the Chuvash and Gagauz became Orthodox Christians.

Modern world

The Organization of Turkic States (founded 2009, renamed 2021) institutionalises Ankara-led cooperation among Türkiye, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan — an alphabet, summits and a 'Turkic world' rhetoric — while the largest Turkic tragedies of the era (the Uyghurs' mass detention in Xinjiang) divide the bloc's solidarity from its China policy.
