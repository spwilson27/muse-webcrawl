BMW M

BMW M GmbH, formerly BMW Motorsport GmbH, is a subsidiary of the German car manufacturer BMW AG responsible for high-performance automobiles and motorsport activities. Cars developed by the division carry the "M" (for Motorsport) badge — such as the M3 and M5 — and are distinguished by more powerful engines, uprated chassis and brakes, and distinctive M styling in the division's colours of light blue, dark blue (often read as violet) and red.

History

BMW Motorsport GmbH was founded in 1972 to run BMW's racing programmes, achieving early success in touring car racing with the 3.0 CSL "Batmobile". Its first road car was the mid-engined M1 supercar of 1978, developed for Group 4 racing. The division then applied its expertise to saloons: the M535i, the first M5 (E28) of 1985, and the original M3 (E30) of 1986, built as a homologation special for touring car racing and one of the most successful touring cars of all time.

Model range

The full M range grew to include M versions of the 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8 Series coupés and saloons and the X-series SUVs, as well as the standalone XM. Below the full M cars, BMW markets "M Performance" models (such as the M240i and M340i) with milder tuning. In 2022 the division celebrated its 50th anniversary and has since begun electrification, with plug-in hybrid powertrains in the XM and the contemporary M5.
