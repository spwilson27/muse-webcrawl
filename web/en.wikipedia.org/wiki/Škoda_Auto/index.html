Škoda Auto a.s. is a Czech automobile manufacturer established in 1895 as Laurin & Klement and headquartered in Mladá Boleslav, Bohemia. It is one of the world's oldest car manufacturers still producing vehicles. Since 2000 it has been a wholly owned subsidiary of the German Volkswagen Group.

History

Václav Klement, a bookseller, and Václav Laurin, a bicycle mechanic, began making bicycles under the name Slavia in 1895, then motorcycles in 1899, and automobiles from 1905. In 1925 the firm was acquired by the industrial conglomerate Škoda Works of Plzeň, and the cars took the Škoda name. The Popular, Rapid, and Superb models of the 1930s built the marque's reputation.

After the Second World War the company was nationalised in communist Czechoslovakia, producing cars such as the Octavia and Felicia for Eastern Bloc markets. Following the Velvet Revolution, Volkswagen acquired a stake in 1991 and full ownership by 2000.

Models

The modern range is built around the Fabia hatchback, Scala, the Octavia liftback and estate, the Superb flagship, the Kamiq, Karoq, and Kodiaq SUVs, and the all-electric Enyaq. Škoda sells more than a million cars a year in over 100 markets, with production in the Czech Republic, India, China, Slovakia, and elsewhere.

Motorsport

Škoda Motorsport has a long rallying heritage, achieving particular success in the WRC2 category with the Fabia R5 and Fabia RS Rally2 cars.
