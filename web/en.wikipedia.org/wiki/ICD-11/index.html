ICD-11

ICD-11
Full name: International Classification of Diseases, 11th Revision
Publisher: World Health Organization
Released: 18 June 2018
In effect: 1 January 2022
Predecessor: ICD-10

The ICD-11 is the eleventh revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD), the global standard for diagnostic health information maintained by the World Health Organization. It replaced ICD-10 as the global standard.

Development

Work began in 2007 with more than 300 specialists from 55 countries. The WHO released the completed version on 18 June 2018; the World Health Assembly adopted it in May 2019, and it came into effect on 1 January 2022. Unlike previous revisions it is fully digital, born as an online platform with an API, coding tool and foundation component of about 80,000 entities.

Changes from ICD-10

ICD-11 reorganised many categories: stroke moved from circulatory to nervous-system diseases; gaming disorder and compulsive sexual behaviour disorder were added; gender incongruence moved out of mental disorders into a sexual-health chapter; immune-system diseases and sleep–wake disorders gained their own chapters; and a chapter on traditional medicine was included. Codes use a new alphanumeric structure and allow 'cluster coding' combining stem codes with extension codes.

Adoption

Countries transition at different speeds; mortality and morbidity reporting, insurance and statistics worldwide are migrating gradually, with ICD-10 still in use in several health systems including much of the United States.
