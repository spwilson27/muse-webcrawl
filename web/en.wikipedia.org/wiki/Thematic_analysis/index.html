Thematic analysis

FieldQualitative research
Key authorsVirginia Braun and Victoria Clarke
Key paper"Using thematic analysis in psychology" (2006)

Thematic analysis is a method of qualitative research for identifying, analysing and reporting patterns ("themes") within data such as interview transcripts, focus groups and documents. It is among the most widely used qualitative techniques, particularly in psychology, health research and the social sciences.

Braun and Clarke's approach

The method was systematised by Virginia Braun and Victoria Clarke in their 2006 paper "Using thematic analysis in psychology", one of the most cited methods papers in the discipline. They described six phases: (1) familiarisation with the data; (2) generating initial codes; (3) searching for themes; (4) reviewing themes; (5) defining and naming themes; and (6) producing the report. Codes capture interesting features of the data; themes are broader patterns of meaning built up from codes.

Reflexive thematic analysis

In later work Braun and Clarke developed "reflexive thematic analysis", stressing that themes are actively constructed by the researcher rather than "emerging" from data, and distinguishing their approach from coding-reliability and codebook variants that use structured codebooks, multiple coders and inter-rater agreement. Key debates include how far analysis should stay close to participants' language (semantic) versus interpret underlying assumptions (latent), and whether analysis is inductive (data-driven) or theoretical (driven by the research question).
