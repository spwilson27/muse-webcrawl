Ben-Gurion University of the Negev

Established: 1969

Location: Beersheba, Israel

Students: approx. 19,000–20,000

Named for: David Ben-Gurion

Ben-Gurion University of the Negev (BGU) is a public research university in Beersheba, Israel. Founded in 1969 as the University of the Negev, it was renamed after Israel's first prime minister, David Ben-Gurion, following his death in 1973, in honour of his vision of developing the Negev desert through science and education.

Campuses and faculties

The university's main Marcus Family Campus is in Beersheba, with additional campuses and institutes including the Jacob Blaustein Institutes for Desert Research at Sde Boker, near Ben-Gurion's home and grave. BGU has five faculties: Natural Sciences, Engineering Sciences, Health Sciences (working closely with Soroka Medical Center), Humanities and Social Sciences, and the Guilford Glazer Faculty of Business and Management, as well as a school for desert studies and the Ben-Gurion Research Institute.

Research

The university is internationally known for research in desert agriculture, water resources and desalination, solar energy, cybersecurity, and robotics. It is a leading partner of the CyberSpark innovation park in Beersheba, adjacent to the campus and to Israel Defense Forces technology units. The medical school pioneered community-oriented and global-health curricula in Israel.
