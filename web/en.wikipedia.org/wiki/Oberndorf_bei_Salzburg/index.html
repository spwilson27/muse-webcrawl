Oberndorf bei Salzburg is a small town in the Austrian state of Salzburg, about 17 kilometres north of the city of Salzburg, on the east bank of the river Salzach, which forms the border with Bavaria, Germany. Its twin town of Laufen lies directly across the river.

Silent Night

Oberndorf is world-famous as the birthplace of the Christmas carol "Silent Night" (Stille Nacht, heilige Nacht). On Christmas Eve 1818, in the town's St Nicholas Church, the assistant priest Joseph Mohr and the schoolteacher-organist Franz Xaver Gruber first performed the song: Mohr had written the words two years earlier, and Gruber composed the melody, according to legend hurriedly, for voice and guitar, after the church organ was found to be damaged. The original church was damaged by flooding and demolished in the early twentieth century; the small Silent Night Chapel, built on its site and completed in 1937, together with an adjacent museum, draws visitors from around the world, and a memorial service is held there each Christmas Eve.

History

Oberndorf's prosperity long rested on salt shipping down the Salzach, a trade dominated by neighbouring Laufen, of which Oberndorf was originally a suburb. When the Napoleonic-era border settlements of 1816 split the town along the river, Oberndorf passed to Austria and developed as an independent municipality, reorienting itself toward the new railway in the later nineteenth century.
