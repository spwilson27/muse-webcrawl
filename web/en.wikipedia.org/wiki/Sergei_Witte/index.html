Count Sergei Yulyevich Witte (29 June 1849 – 13 March 1915) was a Russian statesman who presided over extensive industrialization and economic modernization of the Russian Empire as Minister of Finance from 1892 to 1903. He later became the first Prime Minister of Russia following the Revolution of 1905.

Career

Born in Tiflis (now Tbilisi) to a family of Baltic German and Dutch descent, Witte studied mathematics at Novorossiysk University before entering railway administration, where he rose rapidly through the southwestern railways. As Minister of Finance under Alexander III and Nicholas II, he pursued a programme of state-led industrialization: he secured large foreign loans (chiefly from France), put Russia on the gold standard in 1897, raised protective tariffs, promoted the construction of the Trans-Siberian Railway, and encouraged foreign investment in heavy industry. Industrial output roughly doubled during his tenure.

Prime minister

After negotiating the Treaty of Portsmouth that ended the Russo-Japanese War in 1905 — an achievement that earned him the title of Count — Witte drafted the October Manifesto promising civil liberties and an elected Duma, and became Russia's first prime minister. Frustrated by court intrigue and his loss of the Tsar's confidence, he resigned in April 1906.

Legacy

Witte's memoirs, published posthumously, are a major source for the late imperial period. Historians regard him as one of the most able statesmen of tsarist Russia, whose programme of modernization from above was undermined by political stagnation.
