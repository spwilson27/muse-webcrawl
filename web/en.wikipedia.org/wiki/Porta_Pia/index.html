Porta Pia

Porta Pia

Location: Rome, Italy
Designer: Michelangelo
Built: 1561–1565
Named for: Pope Pius IV

Porta Pia is a gate in the Aurelian Walls of Rome, Italy. It was designed by Michelangelo and built between 1561 and 1565 on the orders of Pope Pius IV, from whom it takes its name. It replaced the ancient Porta Nomentana, which stood nearby.

History

The gate stands at the end of the Via Pia (now Via XX Settembre). Its inner facade, facing the city, is Michelangelo's last architectural work, completed shortly before his death in 1564. The outer facade, in a neoclassical style, was designed by Virginio Vespignani and completed in 1869.

Porta Pia is famous above all for the events of 20 September 1870, when Italian troops of the Kingdom of Italy breached the Aurelian Walls just west of the gate — the so-called Breach of Porta Pia — and entered Rome, completing the unification of Italy and ending the temporal power of the popes. A monument and a museum near the gate commemorate the Bersaglieri who took part in the assault.

Architecture

Michelangelo's facade is playful and theatrical, mixing classical elements with mannerist invention: a central arch flanked by columns, topped by a curved element resembling a draped cloth or a basin and soap, which Michelangelo reportedly sketched as a witty reference to the pope's family, the Medici barber-surgeons. The design shows his late, inventive handling of classical forms.
