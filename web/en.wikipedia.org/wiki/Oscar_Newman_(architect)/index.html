Oscar Newman (architect)

Oscar Newman (born 1935) is a Canadian-American architect and urban theorist best known for formulating the theory of defensible space in his 1972 book Defensible Space: Crime Prevention Through Urban Design. Comparing crime rates in New York public housing, Newman argued that high-rise blocks surrounded by anonymous, undifferentiated public areas suffered far more crime than low-rise housing whose spaces were clearly assigned to residents.

Defensible space theory

Newman's principles — territoriality, natural surveillance, image and milieu — hold that residential environments should be designed so residents identify with and informally monitor their surroundings: subdividing grounds into zones belonging to small groups of households, orienting windows over entrances and walkways, and avoiding designs that stigmatise a project. The work influenced crime prevention through environmental design (CPTED) and housing policy in the United States and Britain, including later Newman-influenced programmes applying defensible space concepts in public housing communities.
