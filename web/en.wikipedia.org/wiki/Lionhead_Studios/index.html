Lionhead Studios was a British video game developer based in Guildford, Surrey. It was founded in 1997 by Peter Molyneux after he left Bullfrog Productions, the studio he had co-founded and sold to Electronic Arts. Microsoft acquired Lionhead in April 2006 and closed it in 2016.

History and games

Lionhead's first title was the god game Black & White (2001), in which players ruled a world as a deity with a giant trainable creature. It followed with the business simulation The Movies (2005) and, most successfully, the action role-playing game Fable (2004) for Xbox, developed with satellite studio Big Blue Box. Fable II (2008) and Fable III (2010) were major Xbox 360 releases; the studio also produced the Kinect title Fable: The Journey (2012) and the remaster Fable Anniversary (2014).

Closure

Lionhead was developing the free-to-play cooperative game Fable Legends when, on 7 March 2016, Microsoft announced the proposed closure of the studio and the cancellation of the game. After a consultation period, Lionhead Studios closed on 29 April 2016. Co-founder Molyneux had already departed in 2012 to found 22cans.
