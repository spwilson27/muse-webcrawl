5000 metres

Sport: Athletics (track and field)

Distance: 5,000 m (12.5 laps of a 400 m track)

Men's world record: 12:35.36 — Joshua Cheptegei (2020)

Women's world record: 14:00.21 — Gudaf Tsegay (2023)

Olympic event since: 1912 (men), 1996 (women)

The 5000 metres or 5000-metre run (approximately 3.1 miles) is a common long-distance running event in track and field. It is one of the Olympic track events and is contested outdoors as 12.5 laps of a standard 400-metre track. Along with the 10,000 metres and the marathon, it is one of the three long-distance track events at the Olympics and World Championships.

History

The 5000 metres has been an Olympic event for men since the 1912 Stockholm Games. The women's equivalent at championship level was long the 3000 metres, until the 5000 metres replaced it at the World Championships in 1995 and the Olympics in 1996. Finnish runners dominated the early decades; in recent decades the event has been led by East African athletes, particularly from Ethiopia and Kenya, including Kenenisa Bekele, Haile Gebrselassie, Mo Farah, and Joshua Cheptegei.

Records

Uganda's Joshua Cheptegei set the men's world record of 12:35.36 in Monaco in 2020, breaking Bekele's long-standing mark. Ethiopia's Gudaf Tsegay set the women's world record of 14:00.21 in Eugene in 2023. The event demands a blend of aerobic endurance and tactical speed, with races often decided by a fast final lap after a cagey early pace.
