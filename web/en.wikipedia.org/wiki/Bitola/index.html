Bitola

Bitola (Macedonian: Битола) is a city in the southwestern part of North Macedonia. It is located in the southern part of the Pelagonia valley, surrounded by the Baba, Nidže and Kajmakčalan mountain ranges, 14 kilometres (9 mi) north of the border with Greece. With a population of roughly 70,000, it is the second-largest city in the country and a major administrative, cultural and educational centre.

History

Near the modern city lie the ruins of Heraclea Lyncestis, founded by Philip II of Macedon in the 4th century BC and famed for its Roman-era mosaics. Bitola developed in the medieval period and flourished under Ottoman rule, when it was known as Manastır and became a major military and consular centre — the "city of consuls" — with numerous European consulates. The first film footage in the Balkans was shot in Bitola in 1905 by the Manaki brothers, commemorated by the annual Manaki Brothers Film Festival.

Landmarks

The wide pedestrian street Širok Sokak, lined with neoclassical consular-era buildings, is the city's social centre. Other landmarks include the Clock Tower, the Yeni and Isak mosques, the covered bazaar (bezisten), and the archaeological site of Heraclea. Nearby Pelister National Park protects Mount Pelister and its glacial lakes.
