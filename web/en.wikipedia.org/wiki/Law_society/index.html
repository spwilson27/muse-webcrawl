Law society

A law society is an association of lawyers with a regulatory role that includes the right to supervise training, qualifications and conduct. Where a country or jurisdiction has a fused legal profession (solicitors and barristers combined), a law society may represent the whole profession. In jurisdictions with a split profession, solicitors are typically organised in a law society, while barristers belong to a bar association or bar council.

Examples

The Law Society of England and Wales is the professional body for solicitors in that jurisdiction; since 2007 its regulatory functions have been exercised independently by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, leaving the Law Society principally as a representative body. Other examples include the Law Society of Scotland, the Law Society of Ireland, and the law societies of Canadian provinces, such as the Law Society of Ontario, which both represent and regulate lawyers and paralegals in their jurisdictions.

Functions

Depending on the jurisdiction, law societies may set standards for admission to practice, maintain rolls of practising lawyers, issue practising certificates, make professional conduct rules, operate compensation funds, and provide continuing professional development and member services.
