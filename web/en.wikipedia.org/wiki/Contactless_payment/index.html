Contactless payment is a secure method for consumers to purchase products or services using a debit, credit, or smart card — or another payment device such as a smartphone or wearable — by holding it near a point-of-sale terminal equipped with contactless technology. Transactions use near-field communication (NFC) or radio-frequency identification (RFID) and follow the EMV contactless specifications; payment is made by tapping or waving the device within a few centimetres of the reader, without inserting the card or entering a PIN for low-value transactions.

History

Closed-loop transit cards pioneered mass contactless payment in the 1990s, led by Hong Kong's Octopus card (1997) and later London's Oyster card (2003). Open-loop bank card schemes followed: contactless credit and debit cards rolled out in the United Kingdom from 2007, and the UK transaction limit rose in steps from £10 to £100 in 2021. Mobile wallets such as Apple Pay (2014) and Google Pay brought tokenized contactless payment to phones and watches. Transport for London's acceptance of ordinary contactless bank cards from 2012–2014 was a landmark in open-loop transit payment.

Technology and security

Contactless cards contain an embedded chip and antenna communicating at 13.56 MHz under ISO/IEC 14443. Each transaction generates a unique dynamic cryptogram, and mobile wallets substitute device-specific tokens for the real card number, adding biometric or passcode authentication. Because no PIN is required below regional floor limits, issuers mitigate risk through transaction caps, cumulative spend checks that periodically force a chip-and-PIN transaction, and real-time fraud monitoring. The very short read range makes casual eavesdropping impractical, though researchers have demonstrated relay and skimming attacks under laboratory conditions.

Adoption

Contactless became the dominant face-to-face card payment method in much of Europe, Australia, and Canada during the 2010s, and adoption accelerated worldwide in 2020 as merchants and customers preferred touch-free payment during the COVID-19 pandemic. In the United States, issuance of contactless cards and transit acceptance expanded rapidly from 2019 onward.
