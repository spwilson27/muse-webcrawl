Battle of Belleau Wood

The Battle of Belleau Wood (1–26 June 1918) was a major engagement of the First World War fought near the Marne River in France during the German spring offensive. United States Army and Marine Corps forces — principally the 4th Marine Brigade of the U.S. 2nd Division, under the overall command associated with General James Harbord — attacked German positions in and around Belleau Wood, north-west of Château-Thierry. The battle became one of the most celebrated in the history of the U.S. Marine Corps and a symbol of the American Expeditionary Forces' arrival as a decisive force on the Western Front.

The battle

As German forces drove toward Paris in late May 1918, the U.S. 2nd Division was rushed to block the Paris–Metz road. On 6 June the Marines launched their first major attack across open wheat fields against German machine guns in the wood, suffering the heaviest single day of casualties in Marine Corps history to that point — over 1,000 men. Fighting then degenerated into weeks of brutal close combat among the rocks and trees, with the wood changing hands repeatedly. Famous episodes include Gunnery Sergeant Dan Daly's rallying cry, "Come on, you sons of bitches, do you want to live forever?", and the Germans' reported nickname for the Marines, Teufel Hunden ("Devil Dogs"). On 26 June the Marines finally reported the message: "Woods now U.S. Marine Corps entirely."

Legacy

American casualties in the battle were about 9,800, including some 1,800 killed; German losses were comparable, with over 1,600 taken prisoner. In gratitude, France renamed the wood Bois de la Brigade de Marine ("Wood of the Marine Brigade"), and the nearby Aisne-Marne American Cemetery holds more than 2,200 graves. Belleau Wood remains a pilgrimage site for Marines, and the battle is commemorated annually on Memorial Day at the cemetery.
