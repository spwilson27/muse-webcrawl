Ginkaku-ji (銀閣寺, "Temple of the Silver Pavilion"), officially named Jishō-ji (慈照寺), is a Zen temple in the Sakyo ward of Kyoto, Japan. It is one of the constructions that represents the Higashiyama culture of the Muromachi period. Originally built as a retirement villa for the shōgun Ashikaga Yoshimasa, it was converted into a Zen temple after his death.

History

Ashikaga Yoshimasa began building his retirement villa in 1482 on the site of the ruined temple Jōdo-ji. The two-storey Kannon-den, known as the Silver Pavilion, was modelled on the Golden Pavilion (Kinkaku-ji) built by his grandfather Ashikaga Yoshimitsu. Despite its popular name, the pavilion was never actually covered in silver leaf. After Yoshimasa's death in 1490, the villa became a Zen temple of the Shōkoku branch of Rinzai Zen.

Garden

The temple is renowned for its dry sand garden, known as the "Sea of Silver Sand", with a massive sand cone named the "Moon-Viewing Platform" (Kōgetsudai). The moss garden, which Yoshimasa is said to have designed, is considered a classic of its kind. The grounds epitomise the aesthetics of wabi-sabi and Higashiyama culture, which also gave rise to the tea ceremony, flower arranging, and Noh theatre developments of the era.

Status

Ginkaku-ji is designated a Special Place of Scenic Beauty and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto (Kyoto, Uji and Otsu Cities)", inscribed in 1994.
