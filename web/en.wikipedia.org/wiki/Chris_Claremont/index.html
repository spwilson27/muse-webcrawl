Chris Claremont (born 25 November 1950) is an American comic book writer and novelist, best known for his 16-year run on Marvel Comics' Uncanny X-Men from 1975 to 1991, during which the title became one of the best-selling in the industry. Working with artists including Dave Cockrum and John Byrne, he co-created many of the X-Men's most famous characters and storylines.

X-Men

Claremont took over the revived X-Men with Giant-Size X-Men #1's new international team (1975). With John Byrne he produced the landmark "Dark Phoenix Saga" (1980) and "Days of Future Past" (1981). He co-created characters including Rogue, Psylocke, Kitty Pryde, Emma Frost, Mystique's modern characterisation, Gambit, Jubilee and Mister Sinister, and launched spin-offs such as New Mutants and Excalibur, as well as Wolverine's first solo series. His writing emphasised character-driven melodrama, strong female characters, and themes of prejudice and outsider identity.

Later work

After leaving Marvel in 1991 amid creative disputes, Claremont worked for other publishers and wrote novels, including the Chronicles of the Shadow War trilogy with George Lucas. He returned to Marvel several times, writing titles such as X-Treme X-Men and Exiles. He was inducted into the Will Eisner Award Hall of Fame in 2015.
