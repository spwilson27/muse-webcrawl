Lake Bolsena (Italian: Lago di Bolsena) is a lake of volcanic origin in the northern part of the province of Viterbo in the Lazio region of central Italy. It is the largest volcanic lake in Europe, with a surface area of about 113.5 km², and was formed about 370,000 years ago following the collapse of a caldera of the Vulsini volcanic complex.

Geography

The lake is almost circular, set among the Vulsini hills. Its only emissary is the River Marta, which flows from the town of Marta on the southern shore to the Tyrrhenian Sea. Two islands rise from the lake: Bisentina, the larger, with churches and gardens, privately owned but visitable; and Martana, smaller and uninhabited. Towns around the shore include Bolsena on the north-east, Montefiascone in the south-east, Capodimonte and Marta in the south.

History

The shores were settled from the Neolithic period, and in Etruscan times the area lay near the important city of Velzna (Volsinii), whose exact site — Etruscan Volsinii, destroyed by Rome in 264 BC — has been debated between Bolsena and Orvieto. Underwater archaeology has revealed the submerged village of Gran Carro, an Iron Age settlement now below the lake's surface on the eastern side.

The lake's town of Bolsena is also famous for the "Miracle of Bolsena" of 1263, when a Bohemian priest who doubted transubstantiation is said to have seen blood issue from a consecrated host; the event led Pope Urban IV to institute the feast of Corpus Christi, and the blood-stained altar linens are kept in Orvieto Cathedral.

Environment and use

Lake Bolsena is noted for the clarity and quality of its water, used for drinking water, irrigation and fishing (whitefish, eel, perch, tench and pike). Its dark volcanic sand beaches make it a popular holiday destination, especially for visitors from Italy and northern Europe, although tourism is quieter than at Italy's great northern lakes.
