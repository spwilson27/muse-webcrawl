Yandere

Yandere (ヤンデレ) is a Japanese term for a character archetype in anime, manga, games, and related media: a person who is initially sweet, gentle, and loving, but whose devotion becomes obsessive and deranged, sometimes turning to stalking or extreme violence toward rivals or the beloved themselves. The word is a portmanteau of yanderu (病んでる, "being sick") and deredere (でれでれ, "being lovestruck").

Characteristics

A yandere character's affection is unconditional but possessive: the beloved is the center of their world, and any threat — real or imagined — to the relationship may provoke jealousy, manipulation, or murder. Characteristic visual cues in illustration include hollow or darkened eyes, an unblinking smile, and clutching a bladed weapon. The archetype is usually female in mainstream usage but applies to any gender.

Notable examples

Frequently cited examples include Yuno Gasai of Future Diary (Mirai Nikki), often treated as the archetype's emblem; Kotonoha Katsura of School Days; and Rena Ryūgū of Higurashi When They Cry. The term is commonly paired with tsundere (aloof on the surface, affectionate beneath) and other "-dere" archetypes in fan taxonomies.

Cultural discussion

Commentary on the archetype ranges from treating it as a stylized fantasy of total devotion to criticizing it for romanticizing controlling behaviour. Real-life incidents in Japan have occasionally been described in the press using the word, a usage some critics consider loose. In English-speaking fandom the word is well established and has spread into game titles such as Yandere Simulator.
