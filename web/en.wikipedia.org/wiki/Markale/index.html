Markale

Markale is a historic marketplace (Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian: Gradska tržnica Markale) in Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina. The neo-Renaissance market hall, completed in 1895 during the Austro-Hungarian period, stands in the Stari Grad (Old Town) area of the city and remains a functioning food market.

The Markale massacres

Markale became internationally known during the siege of Sarajevo for two mortar attacks on civilians gathered at the market. The first, on 5 February 1994, killed 68 people and wounded some 200. The second, on 28 August 1995, killed 43 and wounded around 75–90. The second attack helped precipitate NATO's Operation Deliberate Force, the alliance's air campaign against Bosnian Serb positions, which contributed to the end of the war.

Responsibility for the attacks was contested during the war, but subsequent investigations and judgements of the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia, including in the cases of Stanislav Galić and Radovan Karadžić, attributed the shelling to Bosnian Serb forces surrounding the city.

Today

A memorial at the site commemorates the victims. The market still functions as an ordinary urban market selling produce, meat and flowers, and the surrounding square is a gathering place in central Sarajevo.
