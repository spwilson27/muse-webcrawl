Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson

Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson (29 September 1758 – 21 October 1805) was a British flag officer in the Royal Navy, famed for his inspirational leadership, grasp of strategy and unconventional tactics — and for victories at the Nile (1798), Copenhagen (1801) and Battle of Trafalgar (1805), where he was killed. He remains Britain's archetypal naval hero.

Rise

Nelson went to sea at twelve. In the wars against revolutionary France he distinguished himself at Cape St Vincent (1797), boarding two Spanish ships in succession. At Santa Cruz de Tenerife his arm was shattered and amputated. In 1798 his squadron destroyed the French fleet at anchor in Aboukir Bay — the Battle of the Nile — stranding Napoleon's army in Egypt and making Nelson a European celebrity; the Neapolitan episode that followed, and his liaison with Emma Hamilton, complicated his legend. At Copenhagen (1801) he famously put his telescope to his blind eye to disregard a signal to withdraw.

Trafalgar and death

After two years blockading Toulon and chasing Villeneuve across the Atlantic and back, Nelson met the combined fleets off Trafalgar on 21 October 1805. His two-column attack broke the allied line; his signal — "England expects that every man will do his duty" — became canonical. Wearing his decorations on the quarterdeck of Victory, he was shot by a French marksman and died that afternoon, in time to hear of victory. His body was preserved in a cask of spirits and given a state funeral in St Paul's.

Legacy

Nelson's Column in Trafalgar Square (completed 1843) anchors his memory in London. Historians credit his combination of aggression, delegation to his "band of brothers", and personal charisma with creating the Royal Navy's century of dominance.
