Earring

An earring is a piece of jewellery attached to the ear via a piercing in the earlobe or another external part of the ear, or, less commonly, by some other means such as a clip. Earrings have been worn across multiple different cultures and historic periods, often with cultural significance. Locations for piercings other than the earlobe include the rook, tragus, and across the helix (see the article on body piercing).


Gold hoop earrings

Materials used in earrings include metal, plastic, glass, precious stone, beads, wood, bone, and other materials. Designs range from small loops and studs to large plates and dangling items. The size is ultimately limited by the physical capacity of the earlobe to hold the earring without tearing. However, heavy earrings worn over extended periods of time may lead to stretching of the earlobe and the piercing.

History

Ear piercing is one of the oldest known forms of body modification, with artistic and written references from cultures around the world dating back to early history. Gold earrings, along with other jewellery of gold, silver and bronze, were common in the Aegean world from the Bronze Age. In ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, earrings signified wealth and status; in ancient Rome and Greece they were worn primarily by women, while in many other cultures they were worn by both sexes. In parts of the modern West, earrings became mainstream for men from the 1960s and 1970s onward.

Types

Common types include stud earrings, hoop earrings, dangle earrings, chandelier earrings, huggie earrings, and ear cuffs. Earlobes are commonly pierced with a piercing gun or a sterile needle, and earrings may be secured with butterfly backs, screw backs, or lever backs.
