Champs-Élysées

The Avenue des Champs-Élysées is a 1.9-kilometre avenue in the 8th arrondissement of Paris, running between the Place de la Concorde and the Place Charles de Gaulle, where the Arc de Triomphe stands. It is famous for its theatres, cafés and luxury shops, and is often described as one of the world's most famous streets.

History

Originally fields, the avenue was laid out in 1667 by André Le Nôtre as an extension of the Tuileries Garden axis and took its name from the Elysian Fields of Greek mythology. It forms part of Paris's historic axis. It hosts the Bastille Day military parade each 14 July and the finish of the Tour de France, and was the scene of liberation celebrations in 1944.

Description

The lower section near Concorde is bordered by gardens and palaces such as the Grand Palais and Petit Palais; the upper section is lined with cinemas, flagship stores and cafés. Rents are among the highest in the world.
