Experimental aircraft

An experimental aircraft is an aircraft that has not been fully proven in flight, or one operated under an experimental airworthiness certificate issued by a national aviation authority. In the United States, the Federal Aviation Administration issues experimental certificates for purposes including research and development, showing compliance with regulations, air racing, and amateur-built (homebuilt) aircraft.

The amateur-built category is the largest: builders construct aircraft from plans or kits for education and recreation, and such aircraft must be inspected before flight and flown for a test period in a limited area. Famous research experimental aircraft include the series of X-planes, such as the Bell X-1 that first broke the sound barrier in 1947.

Rules vary by country; in the United Kingdom similar aircraft operate under a Permit to Fly administered with the Light Aircraft Association.
