County Antrim

County Antrim
Irish: Contae Aontroma
Country: Northern Ireland, United Kingdom
Province: Ulster
County town: Antrim
Largest city: Belfast (partly)
Area: approx. 3,046 km²

County Antrim (from Irish Aontroim, meaning "lone ridge") is one of the six counties that form Northern Ireland and one of the thirty-two traditional counties of Ireland. It lies in the province of Ulster, in the north-east of the island of Ireland. Adjoining counties are County Londonderry to the west, County Down to the south, and County Tyrone to the south-west. The eastern and northern coasts look out over the North Channel and the Atlantic Ocean towards Scotland.

Geography

The county contains a varied landscape of coastal cliffs, glens, and uplands. The Glens of Antrim, nine valleys running down to the sea, are a well-known feature of the north coast, which also includes the Giant's Causeway, a UNESCO World Heritage Site famed for its interlocking basalt columns, and the Carrick-a-Rede rope bridge. In the south-west, the county borders Lough Neagh, the largest lake in the British Isles. Belfast, the capital of Northern Ireland, lies partly within County Antrim and partly within County Down, divided by the River Lagan.

History

The area was settled from early times and later formed part of the medieval Irish kingdom and earldom of Ulster. In the early seventeenth century, much of the county was settled during the Plantation of Ulster, although Antrim and Down were largely colonised by private enterprise rather than official plantation. The county town is Antrim; the historic county administration was based for many years in Belfast and later in Ballymena. Following the partition of Ireland in 1921, County Antrim became part of Northern Ireland.

Settlements

Major towns in the county include Antrim, Ballymena, Ballymoney, Carrickfergus, Larne, Lisburn (partly), Newtownabbey, and Portrush. The county town is Antrim, while Belfast is the largest settlement.
