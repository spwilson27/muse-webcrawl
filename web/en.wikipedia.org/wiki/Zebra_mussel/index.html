Zebra mussel

The zebra mussel (Dreissena polymorpha) is a small freshwater mussel, a bivalve mollusk in the family Dreissenidae. It is native to fresh waters of Eastern Europe and Western Asia, around the Black and Caspian Seas, and has become a notorious invasive species in North America and Western Europe.

Description

Zebra mussels are typically 2–5 cm long, with a distinctive striped pattern of dark and light bands on the shell that gives the species its name, though some individuals are uniformly dark or light. They attach to hard surfaces using strong byssal threads, often forming dense clusters.

Invasive spread

The species was first discovered in North America in Lake St. Clair in 1988, having arrived in the ballast water of transoceanic ships. It spread rapidly through the Great Lakes and the Mississippi River basin. Zebra mussels are filter feeders that can dramatically alter aquatic ecosystems, outcompete native mussels, and cause extensive economic damage by clogging water intake pipes and fouling boats and infrastructure.
