Republic of Afghanistan

The Republic of Afghanistan (Pashto: د افغانستان جمهوریت; Dari: جمهوری افغانستان) was the first republic in Afghanistan, established in July 1973 after Mohammed Daoud Khan seized power from his cousin, King Mohammed Zahir Shah, in a bloodless coup, ending the Afghan monarchy.

History

On 17 July 1973, while Zahir Shah was abroad, former prime minister Mohammed Daoud Khan, supported by army officers and the communist People's Democratic Party of Afghanistan (PDPA), proclaimed a republic with himself as president. Daoud abolished the monarchy and the 1964 constitution, and ruled through a central committee of his National Revolutionary Party, the only legal party after 1977.

Government and policies

Daoud pursued modernisation, land reform and closer ties initially with the Soviet Union, though after 1975 he attempted to distance Afghanistan from Moscow, purging leftists from government and seeking support from Iran, Egypt and the West. A new constitution in 1977 established a one-party presidential system.

Fall

Growing unrest, the arrest of PDPA leaders following the assassination of a party ideologue, and fear of a purge led PDPA-aligned military officers to overthrow the regime in the Saur Revolution of 27–28 April 1978. Daoud and most of his family were killed, and the Democratic Republic of Afghanistan was proclaimed.
