Cog (ship)

A cog was a type of single-masted cargo ship built with clinker (overlapping) planking, used in northern European waters from about the 10th to the 14th centuries. Cogs were the workhorse vessels of the Hanseatic League, carrying bulk trade around the North and Baltic Seas.

Design

Cogs had flat bottoms for beaching and shallow harbours, high sides, a straight stem and sternpost, and — from the 12th century — a stern-mounted rudder replacing the steering oar. Later cogs added fore- and stern-castles for defence. The remarkably preserved Bremen cog of 1380, found in the Weser in 1962, anchors the type archaeologically.

Use

With capacities growing to about 200 tons, cogs hauled grain, timber, salt, wool and wine between Hanseatic ports and could be armed as warships, their castles serving as fighting platforms, before being superseded by the larger hulk and carrack.
