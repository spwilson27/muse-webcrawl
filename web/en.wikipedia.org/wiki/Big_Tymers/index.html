The Big Tymers were an American hip hop duo from New Orleans, Louisiana, composed of Birdman (then known as Baby), co-founder of Cash Money Records, and producer-rapper Mannie Fresh. The duo epitomized the flamboyant "bling-bling" era of late-1990s and early-2000s Southern hip hop.

Big Tymers, Birdman and Mannie Fresh.

Career

Originally part of a larger Cash Money collective in the early 1990s, Baby and Mannie Fresh formalized the Big Tymers name with their debut album, How You Luv That (1998), reissued in a second volume the same year as Cash Money's national distribution deal with Universal took effect. Their breakthrough came with I Got That Work (2000), which reached the top five of the Billboard 200 and included the hit singles "#1 Stunna" and "Big Ballin'."

The duo's most successful album, Hood Rich (2002), debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 and produced the hit "Still Fly," a tongue-in-cheek ode to maintaining an image of wealth. The follow-up, Big Money Heavyweight (2003), continued the formula of Mannie Fresh's bouncy, synth-heavy production paired with the pair's boasts about cars, jewelry, and real estate.

Style and legacy

Big Tymers popularized the vocabulary of hip hop conspicuous consumption; the phrase "bling-bling," associated with the Cash Money camp, entered the mainstream lexicon in this period. Mannie Fresh's production — built on drum machines, brass stabs, and rolling hi-hats — defined the Cash Money sound that also carried labelmates such as Juvenile and Lil Wayne.

The duo effectively ended when Mannie Fresh left Cash Money Records in 2005 over financial disputes. Birdman continued as a solo artist and label executive, while Fresh pursued production and solo work. Birdman later released collaborative music under the Big Tymers name with other artists, though the original duo did not reunite as an ongoing act.

Discography

Studio albums: How You Luv That (1998), How You Luv That Vol. 2 (1998), I Got That Work (2000), Hood Rich (2002), Big Money Heavyweight (2003).
