Canticle

A canticle (from Latin canticulum, a little song) is a hymn, psalm or other song of praise taken from the Bible or holy texts other than the Psalms. Canticles form a regular part of Christian liturgy, especially in the daily office.

Major canticles

The best-known canticles are from the Gospel of Luke, sung daily in many traditions: the Benedictus (Song of Zechariah) at morning prayer, the Magnificat (Song of Mary) at evening prayer, and the Nunc dimittis (Song of Simeon) at night prayer. Other canticles include the songs of Moses, Hannah, Isaiah, and the Te Deum and Phos Hilaron.

Use

In Roman Catholic Liturgy of the Hours and Anglican Morning and Evening Prayer, canticles are sung or said between the readings and prayers, often to chant or in metrical and choral settings by composers across the centuries.

In other contexts

The word is also used more loosely for a song or poem of praise, and for works such as St Francis's Canticle of the Sun.
