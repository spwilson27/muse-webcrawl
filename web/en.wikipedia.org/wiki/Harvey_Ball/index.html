Harvey Ross Ball (July 10, 1921 – April 12, 2001) was an American commercial artist. He is recognized as the designer of a popular smiley face graphic picture, which became an international symbol. He is widely considered the designer of the iconic "smiley" badge, although his authorship was only legally accepted years later because he failed to trademark the image.

Overview

Ball was born and raised in Worcester, Massachusetts, by his parents Ernest G. Ball and Christine ("Kitty") Ross Ball. He had five siblings: Merrit, Virginia, Raymond, Robert, and Barbara. During his time at South High School, he became an apprentice to a local sign painter and later attended Worcester Art Museum School, where he studied fine arts.

After graduating, Ball served in Asia during World War II and was awarded the Bronze Star for heroism during the Battle of Okinawa. After the war, Ball worked for a local advertising firm before opening his own business, Harvey Ball Advertising, in 1959. He served on the Board of Directors of the Worcester Historical Museum.

Smiley face

In 1963, Ball was assigned a client, the State Mutual Life Assurance Company of Worcester, Massachusetts (now known as Hanover Insurance). The company had acquired the Ohio-based Guarantee Mutual Company of Ohio, and the merger resulted in low employee morale. In an attempt to solve this, Ball was employed in 1963 as a freelance artist to create an image to increase staff morale. Ball started with a yellow circle, drew two eyes, and then a smile — reportedly completing the design in about ten minutes. He was paid $45 (equivalent to about $450 in 2024) for his work.

The State Mutual Life Assurance Company made posters, buttons, and signs with Ball's design, giving them to employees in an effort to improve morale. The design, however, turned out to be so successful that Ball was overwhelmed by requests for more buttons and posters. Later, in the 1970s, Philadelphia brothers Bernard and Murray Spain designed the smiley face themselves (unaware of Ball's earlier design), adding the phrase "Have a nice day". They trademarked the combination and produced large numbers of bumper stickers and other merchandise bearing it.

Ball never applied for a trademark or copyright for the smiley face design and earned only $45 for his efforts. (According to Ball's son, Charles, his father never regretted not registering the design, saying "Hey, I can only eat one steak at a time, I can only drive one car at a time.")

Neither Ball nor the State Mutual Life Assurance Company applied to trademark or copyright the design. By the 1970s, the smiley had become one of the most popular symbols in the world. Ball later founded the World Smile Foundation, a non-profit charitable trust that supports children's causes. The group licenses the name and image of the smiley to raise money for the foundation.

World Smile Day

Ball is also credited with creating World Smile Day, which is observed on the first Friday of October each year. The idea came to him in 1999, as he worried that the commercialization of the smiley face had caused it to lose its original meaning. World Smile Day is devoted to smiles and kind acts.

Death

Ball died on April 12, 2001, after a short illness, at the age of 79. He left behind his wife of 41 years, Winifred Trudell, and four children.
