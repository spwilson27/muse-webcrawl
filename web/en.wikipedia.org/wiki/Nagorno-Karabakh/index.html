Nagorno-Karabakh

Nagorno-Karabakh (Russian: Нагорный Карабах, "mountainous Karabakh"; Armenian: Լեռնային Ղարաբաղ, Lernayin Gharabagh) is a mountainous region in the South Caucasus, internationally recognised as part of Azerbaijan. For three decades it was governed by the ethnic Armenian majority as the self-declared Republic of Artsakh, until Azerbaijan regained control of the territory in September 2023 and the Armenian population fled.

Geography

The region covers roughly 4,400 square kilometres of rugged upland in the Lesser Caucasus, with elevations rising above 3,000 metres. Its historic capital was Stepanakert (Khankendi). The terrain is a mixture of oak and hornbeam forest, alpine meadow and deep river gorges, drained by rivers such as the Tartar and Khachen.

History

The area formed part of ancient Caucasian Albania and later of the Armenian principality of Khachen. Under the Russian Empire it belonged to the Elizavetpol Governorate. In 1923 the Soviet government created the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast within the Azerbaijan SSR, with an overwhelmingly Armenian population.

The conflict

In 1988 the regional soviet voted to join the Armenian SSR, sparking the First Nagorno-Karabakh War (1988–1994), which ended with Armenian forces controlling the region and surrounding districts; some 30,000 people died and hundreds of thousands were displaced. The self-proclaimed republic remained unrecognised by any UN member state. Fighting flared again in the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War of 2020, in which Azerbaijan recovered much of the surrounding territory and part of the region itself under a Russian-brokered ceasefire.

Following a nine-month blockade of the Lachin corridor, Azerbaijan launched an offensive on 19 September 2023. The Artsakh authorities capitulated within a day, and over the following week more than 100,000 ethnic Armenians — nearly the entire population — fled to Armenia. The Republic of Artsakh formally dissolved itself effective 1 January 2024.
