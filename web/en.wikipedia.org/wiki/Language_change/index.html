Language change is the process by which a language evolves over time, through variation and the gradual adoption of new forms in pronunciation, grammar, vocabulary and meaning. All living languages change continuously; only dead languages, no longer spoken natively, remain fixed. Language change is studied in the field of historical linguistics.

Causes

Several factors drive change. Economy of effort leads speakers to shorten and simplify frequent forms, producing assimilation and elision. Analogy regularises irregular patterns (as when English speakers began forming some plurals and past tenses by productive rules). Language contact introduces loanwords and structural features, as when Norse and Norman French transformed English. Social factors — prestige, identity, migration and generational difference — determine which innovations spread through a speech community. Children acquiring language may reanalyse ambiguous structures, seeding grammatical change.

Types of change

Sound change affects phonology, often with great regularity: the Neogrammarians of the 19th century showed that changes such as Grimm's law, which shifted the consonants of Germanic languages, applied systematically across the vocabulary. Semantic change alters word meanings through processes such as broadening, narrowing, pejoration and amelioration. Grammaticalisation turns content words into grammatical markers — for example, English will, once a verb meaning "want", became a future auxiliary. Lexical change adds words through coinage, compounding and borrowing, and loses others into obsolescence.

Study and attitudes

The comparative method reconstructs unattested ancestor languages by comparing systematic correspondences among descendants. Although speakers often complain that their language is decaying, linguists treat change as neither progress nor deterioration but a neutral, inevitable property of language in use. Variation at any given moment — across regions, classes and generations — is the visible raw material from which long-term change arises.
