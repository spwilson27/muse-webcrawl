Whiskey Sour

Whiskey Sour
Type: Cocktail
Base spirit: Whiskey (usually bourbon)
Served: On the rocks or straight up
Garnish: Orange slice and maraschino cherry
Drinkware: Old fashioned glass or sour glass
IBA specified ingredients: 45 ml bourbon, 25 ml lemon juice, 20 ml sugar syrup

The whiskey sour is a classic mixed drink containing whiskey (often bourbon), lemon juice, sugar, and optionally a dash of egg white. It is one of the best-known members of the sour family of cocktails, which combine a base spirit with citrus juice and a sweetener. The drink is an official cocktail of the International Bartenders Association (IBA).

History

The earliest known mention of a whiskey sour appeared in an 1870 issue of the Waukesha Plain Dealer, a Wisconsin newspaper. The drink also appears in Jerry Thomas's 1862 bartender's guide How to Mix Drinks in the broader family of sours. Sours were valued partly because citrus juice helped prevent scurvy among sailors. An often-repeated claim holds the drink was invented in Iquique, Peru (now Chile), by English steward Elliott Stubb in the early 1870s, though printed references predate this.

Preparation

The ingredients are shaken with ice and strained over fresh ice in an old fashioned glass, or served straight up. When egg white is included — a style associated with the "Boston sour" — it is often dry-shaken first to create a smooth foam. The drink is traditionally garnished with a half orange slice and a maraschino cherry, and sometimes a few drops of bitters are floated on the foam.

Variations

The New York sour adds a float of dry red wine. Other variations substitute different whiskeys or add amaretto. International Whiskey Sour Day is celebrated on 25 August.
