Famagusta (Greek: Αμμόχωστος, Ammochostos; Turkish: Mağusa) is a city on the east coast of Cyprus. It has the island's deepest harbour and one of its best-preserved medieval walled towns.

History

Founded around 274 BC by Ptolemy II of Egypt near the ruins of ancient Salamis, Famagusta grew rich on trade after the fall of Acre in 1291, becoming a major entrepôt between East and West under Lusignan rule, when the elaborate Gothic cathedral of St Nicholas was built. Genoa seized it in 1372; Venice took control in 1489 and strengthened its walls. After an eleven-month siege, the city fell to the Ottoman Empire in 1571, an event that precipitated the Battle of Lepanto.

Modern era

Under British rule Famagusta's port expanded. After Cypriot independence in 1960 it became a major tourist centre, with the Greek Cypriot resort suburb of Varosha. During the 1974 Turkish invasion the inhabitants of Varosha fled; the district was fenced off by the Turkish military and remained largely abandoned for decades, becoming an emblem of the Cyprus dispute. Parts of Varosha were reopened to visitors beginning in 2020. The walled old town contains notable monuments including Othello Castle, named after the Shakespeare play partly set in Cyprus.
