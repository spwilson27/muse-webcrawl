Regal blue tang

Scientific name: Paracanthurus hepatus
Family: Acanthuridae (surgeonfish)
Range: Indo-Pacific coral reefs
Length: Up to about 30 cm

The regal blue tang (Paracanthurus hepatus) is a species of surgeonfish known by many common names, including regal tang, palette surgeonfish, blue tang and hippo tang. It is the only member of its genus and is one of the most recognisable coral reef fish, with a royal blue body, a black palette-shaped marking and a yellow tail.

Description

Adults reach about 30 cm in length and have a laterally compressed, oval body. Like other surgeonfish, it has a sharp, venom-free spine on each side of the caudal peduncle, which it can use defensively. Juveniles are predominantly yellow with blue markings.

Distribution and habitat

The species is widely distributed across the Indo-Pacific, from East Africa to Japan and Samoa, living on outer reef slopes at depths of roughly 2 to 40 metres. Juveniles shelter among branching corals, while adults feed largely on zooplankton; algae become more important in the diet with age.

In popular culture

The species gained worldwide fame as Dory, the forgetful fish in the animated films Finding Nemo (2003) and Finding Dory (2016). The films raised demand in the aquarium trade, a concern for conservationists because the species is almost entirely wild-caught.
