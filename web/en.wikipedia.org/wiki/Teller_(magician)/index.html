Teller (magician)

Teller (born Raymond Joseph Teller; February 14, 1948) is an American magician and writer. He is half of the comedy magic duo Penn & Teller, along with Penn Jillette. Known for performing silently while his partner speaks, Teller is also a director, an author and a former high school Latin teacher.

Early life

Teller was born in Philadelphia to parents of Russian Jewish and Cuban descent. He attended Amherst College, graduating in 1969, and taught Latin at Lawrence High School in New Jersey. He began performing magic with his friend Weir Chrisemer under the name of the Othmar Schoeck Memorial Society for the Preservation of Unusual and Disgusting Music.

Career

Teller met Penn Jillette in 1974, and the two — with Chrisemer, later solo as a duo — developed the Penn & Teller act, which combines elaborate illusions with comedy, scepticism and occasional political commentary. The duo's long-running Las Vegas residency at the Rio All-Suite Hotel and Casino began in 2001. Teller's signature tricks include "Shadows", in which he prunes the shadow of a rose and the real flower loses its petals.

The silent persona

Teller generally does not speak during performances, a choice he has explained as a strategic one formed in his early career: performing silently at rowdy venues kept audiences from heckling and kept their attention. He does speak in interviews, films and other appearances outside the act.

Other work

Teller has directed stage productions, including a notable staging of Macbeth and a production of The Tempest incorporating stage magic, and co-wrote books including Penn & Teller's How to Play in Traffic. He has defended the secrecy of his tricks in court, successfully suing a performer who offered to sell the secret of "Shadows".
