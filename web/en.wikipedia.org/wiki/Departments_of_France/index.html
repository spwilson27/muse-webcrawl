Departments of France

The departments of France (French: départements) are the principal administrative subdivisions of the French Republic, functioning simultaneously as units of local government and as constituencies of the central state. There are 101 departments: 96 in metropolitan France and five overseas departments (Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Réunion and Mayotte).

History

The departments were created by decree of the revolutionary National Constituent Assembly on 4 March 1790, replacing the provinces of the Ancien Régime with rationally drawn territories sized so that their capital could be reached on horseback within a day. They were named mainly after rivers and mountains rather than historical identities. The number grew with annexations and administrative changes; Mayotte became the 101st department in 2011.

Administration and numbering

Each department is administered by an elected departmental council (conseil départemental) and represented by a prefect, the central government's delegate. Departmental numbers — assigned roughly alphabetically, from 01 Ain to 95 Val-d'Oise, with Corsica's 2A and 2B and overseas numbers 971–976 — appear in postal codes and vehicle registration plates, making them a familiar part of everyday French geography.
