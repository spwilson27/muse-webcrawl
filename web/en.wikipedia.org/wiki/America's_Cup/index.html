America's Cup

The America's Cup is an international sailing match race and the oldest trophy in international sport still contested. It began in 1851 when the schooner America won a race around the Isle of Wight; the trophy later took the yacht's name and was deeded for challenge competition.

Format

Unlike most championships, the America's Cup is a challenger-versus-defender contest governed by the Deed of Gift and negotiated protocols. Challengers usually race in a selection series, historically the Louis Vuitton Cup and later the Prada Cup, before facing the defender in a match. The boats have evolved from gaff schooners to monohulls and then to foiling catamarans and AC75 monohulls.

History

The New York Yacht Club defended the cup for 132 years until Australia II won in 1983, one of sport's most famous streaks ending. Later editions brought professional syndicates, nationality disputes, court cases over the Deed of Gift, and rapid technological change. Winning teams have come from the United States, Australia, New Zealand, Switzerland and the United Kingdom's challenger tradition remains central to its lore.
