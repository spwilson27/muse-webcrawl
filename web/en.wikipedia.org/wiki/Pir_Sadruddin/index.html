Pir Sadruddin

Pir Sadruddin (also spelled Sadardin) was a fourteenth-century Nizari Ismaili missionary (pir) credited with spreading Ismaili Islam in the Indian subcontinent, particularly in Sindh, Punjab, and Gujarat. He is revered by the Satpanth tradition as the founder of the Khoja Ismaili community.

Mission and teachings

According to tradition, Sadruddin was appointed pir by the Ismaili imam of the time in Persia and travelled to Sindh, where he established the first jamaatkhana (congregational hall) at Kotda. Preaching in local languages and idioms, he presented the imam within a framework intelligible to Hindu audiences, identifying him with the awaited tenth avatar (Dashavatara) of Vishnu — a synthesis embodied in the Satpanth ("true path") tradition.

Ginans and legacy

Sadruddin is traditionally credited with composing many ginans — devotional hymns in vernacular languages that form the sacred literature of the Khoja Ismailis — and with devising the Khojki script for recording them. Works attributed to him include the Das Avatar and the long poem Bujh Niranjan. He died, by tradition, in the late fourteenth or early fifteenth century and is buried at Uch in present-day Pakistan.
