Kaarlo Jaakko Juhani Hintikka (12 January 1929 – 12 August 2015) was a Finnish philosopher and logician, widely regarded as the founder of formal epistemic logic and of game-theoretical semantics for logic. He studied under Georg Henrik von Wright at the University of Helsinki.

Work

Hintikka's early book Knowledge and Belief (1962) laid the foundations of epistemic logic, the formal study of knowledge and belief statements. He developed the possible-worlds semantics for modal and epistemic logics through the notion of model sets, and later created game-theoretical semantics, in which the meaning of a sentence is given by a game between a verifier and a falsifier. With his collaborators he also developed independence-friendly logic, which extends first-order logic to express independence relations among quantifiers.

Career

Hintikka taught at the University of Helsinki, Stanford University, Florida State University and, from 1990, Boston University. He edited the journal Synthese for many years and was the first recipient of the Rolf Schock Prize in Logic and Philosophy in 2005. He published more than thirty books and hundreds of papers on logic, philosophy of language, philosophy of science and the history of philosophy, including studies of Aristotle, Kant, Wittgenstein and Descartes.
