The New York Giants are a professional American football team based in the New York metropolitan area. A member of the National Football Conference (NFC) East division of the National Football League, the franchise was founded in 1925 and plays its home games at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey, which it shares with the New York Jets.

The Giants are among the NFL's oldest and most storied franchises.

History

The Giants won four NFL championships in the pre-Super Bowl era, including the famous 1934 "Sneakers Game" and the 1956 title behind Frank Gifford and Sam Huff. Their 1958 championship game loss to the Baltimore Colts — the first sudden-death overtime final — is often called "The Greatest Game Ever Played" and credited with popularising professional football.

Super Bowl era

The team won its first Super Bowl, XXI, under coach Bill Parcells and MVP Phil Simms, and again in XXV. Under Tom Coughlin and quarterback Eli Manning, the Giants twice upset the New England Patriots in Super Bowls XLII — ending New England's perfect season in one of the greatest upsets in sports history — and XLVI. The franchise, long owned by the Mara family and later jointly with the Tisch family, ranks among the most valuable in the NFL.
