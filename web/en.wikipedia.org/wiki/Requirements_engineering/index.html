Requirements engineering

Requirements engineering (RE) is the process of defining, eliciting, documenting, analysing, validating and managing the requirements of a system — originally in software engineering, but also applied in systems and product engineering generally. A requirement is a documented representation of a condition or capability that a system must possess.

Activities

  • Elicitation: discovering requirements from stakeholders through interviews, workshops, observation, prototyping and document analysis.
  • Analysis and modelling: checking requirements for conflicts, gaps and feasibility; modelling with use cases, user stories, scenarios and formal models.
  • Specification: recording requirements, traditionally in a Software Requirements Specification (SRS) following standards such as IEEE 830, or as a backlog of user stories in agile development.
  • Validation and verification: confirming that requirements describe the intended system, through reviews, prototyping and acceptance criteria.
  • Management: maintaining traceability between requirements, design and tests, and controlling changes over the project life cycle.

Types of requirements

Functional requirements describe what the system should do; non-functional requirements (quality attributes) constrain how — performance, security, usability, reliability and so on. Business and stakeholder requirements capture higher-level goals. Poor requirements engineering is consistently cited among the leading causes of project failure, cost overrun and systems that fail to meet user needs.
