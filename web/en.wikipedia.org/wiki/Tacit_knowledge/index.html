Tacit knowledge

Tacit knowledge or implicit knowledge is knowledge that is difficult to express or extract, and thus more challenging to transfer to others through verbalization or documentation. Examples include riding a bicycle, playing a musical instrument, or recognising a face.

Origins

Philosopher Michael Polanyi introduced the concept in The Tacit Dimension (1966), summarised as "we can know more than we can tell". It contrasts with explicit knowledge that can be codified in manuals and databases.

Applications

In management, Ikujiro Nonaka's SECI model describes conversion between tacit and explicit knowledge. Tacit knowledge is central to expertise, apprenticeship, and debates about artificial intelligence and whether such knowledge can be captured by machines. See also Intellectual.

Examples

  • Language fluency and intuition
  • Clinical judgement
  • Craft and sporting skill
