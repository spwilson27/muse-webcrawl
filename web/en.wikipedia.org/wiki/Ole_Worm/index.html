Ole Worm

Ole Worm (1588–1654), known in Latin as Olaus Wormius, was a Danish physician, natural historian, antiquarian, and professor at the University of Copenhagen. He assembled one of Europe's most famous cabinets of curiosities, the Museum Wormianum, and conducted pioneering studies of runestones.

Collections and research

Worm's museum contained minerals, plants, animals, and ethnographic artifacts from around the world. He famously demonstrated that the supposed unicorn horns in circulation were actually narwhal tusks, and he investigated the lemurs, birds of paradise, and Greenland artifacts in his collection. The posthumous catalogue Museum Wormianum (1655) became a classic of early museology.

Antiquarian work

Worm collected and published runic inscriptions across Denmark and Norway and corresponded with scholars throughout Europe. He died in 1654 while treating patients during a plague epidemic in Copenhagen.
