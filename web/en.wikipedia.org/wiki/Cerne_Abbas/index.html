Cerne Abbas

Cerne Abbas is a village and civil parish in the county of Dorset in southern England. It lies in the valley of the River Cerne, about 7 miles (11 km) north of Dorchester. The village is best known for the Cerne Abbas Giant, a huge hill figure of a naked man carved into the chalk hillside above the village.

History

The village grew up around Cerne Abbey, a Benedictine monastery founded in 987 AD on the site of an earlier religious community traditionally associated with St Edwold. After the Dissolution of the Monasteries under Henry VIII in 1539, the abbey was largely demolished, though parts survive, including the abbey gatehouse, the guest house and the church of St Mary, which contains notable medieval wall paintings.

The Giant

The Cerne Abbas Giant is a 55-metre (180 ft) tall hill figure cut into the turf and chalk of Giant Hill, depicting a naked man wielding a large club. Its origins were long debated, with theories ranging from a Roman depiction of Hercules to a seventeenth-century political caricature. Scientific dating of soil samples reported in 2021 indicated that the figure was most likely cut in the late Saxon period, possibly around the time of the abbey's foundation. The figure is maintained by the National Trust and is re-chalked by volunteers roughly every decade.

Geography

Cerne Abbas sits within the Dorset Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. The River Cerne, a chalk stream, flows through the village and was historically used to power watercress beds, for which the village was once noted.
