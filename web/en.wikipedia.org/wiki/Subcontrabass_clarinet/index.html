Subcontrabass clarinet

The subcontrabass clarinet is the largest and lowest-pitched member of the clarinet family in regular — though rare — manufacture. Pitched in B♭, it sounds two octaves below the standard soprano clarinet and one octave below the contrabass clarinet, reaching the bottom of the human hearing range.

ClassificationWind, woodwind, single-reed
KeyB♭
Related instrumentsContrabass clarinet, Contra-alto clarinet, Octocontrabass clarinet

Description

Because of its great length, the subcontrabass clarinet's tube is folded into a shape resembling a large paperclip or trombone-like loop, and most examples stand taller than the player. Instruments have been built experimentally since the nineteenth century; in the 2010s the German maker Benedikt Eppelsheim and others produced subcontrabass clarinets to low C for specialist ensembles and clarinet choirs.

Use

The instrument is used almost exclusively in clarinet choirs and wind ensembles seeking an extreme bass foundation, and by contemporary composers and instrument builders exploring the limits of the woodwind family. It is not part of the standard orchestral clarinet section.
