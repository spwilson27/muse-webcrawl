AGM-86 ALCM

AGM-86 ALCM
Type: Air-launched cruise missile
Manufacturer: Boeing
In service: 1982–present
Platform: B-52H Stratofortress

The AGM-86 ALCM (Air-Launched Cruise Missile) is a subsonic, long-range cruise missile built by Boeing and operated by the United States Air Force. Launched from the B-52H Stratofortress, it forms part of the air leg of the United States nuclear triad.

Variants

The AGM-86B carries a W80 thermonuclear warhead and navigates by terrain-contour matching (TERCOM). The AGM-86C/D, known as the Conventional Air-Launched Cruise Missile (CALCM), carried conventional explosive warheads and GPS guidance and was used in combat beginning with Operation Desert Storm in 1991; the CALCM was retired in 2019.

Role

A B-52H can carry up to twenty AGM-86B missiles on internal rotary launchers and underwing pylons. The missile is planned to be supplemented and eventually replaced by the Long Range Standoff (LRSO) weapon.
