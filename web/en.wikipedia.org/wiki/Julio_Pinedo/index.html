Julio Pinedo (born 1942) is the ceremonial king of the Afro-Bolivian community, a largely symbolic monarchy recognised as part of Bolivia's cultural heritage. He lives in the village of Mururata in the Nor Yungas Province of the department of La Paz, where he works as a farmer growing coca, citrus and coffee.

Background

The Afro-Bolivian monarchy traces its origins to the descendants of Africans brought to Bolivia during the Spanish colonial period. According to tradition, Prince Uchicho, of Congolese royal origin, was brought as a slave to work in the silver mines of Potosí and later recognised by the enslaved community as their king because of marks on his body interpreted as royal signs. His descendants, the Pinedo family, continued the royal line in the Yungas region.

Reign

Julio Pinedo was crowned in 1992, succeeding his grandfather Bonifacio Pinedo, who had been crowned in 1932. The coronation ceremony was held in Mururata with traditional Afro-Bolivian music and dance, including the saya.

Although the title carries no political power, the Bolivian state formally recognised the Afro-Bolivian monarchy as part of the nation's intangible cultural heritage in 2007, in legislation incorporating the 2009 constitution's recognition of Afro-Bolivian rights. Pinedo represents the Afro-Bolivian people at cultural events and has received official visits from government representatives.

Personal life

Pinedo is married to Angélica Larrea, recognised as queen, and leads a modest life as a peasant farmer, a contrast often noted between his royal title and his humble circumstances.
