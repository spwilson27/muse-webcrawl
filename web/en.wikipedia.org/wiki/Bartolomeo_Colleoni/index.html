Bartolomeo Colleoni

Bartolomeo Colleoni (c. 1400 – 2 November 1475) was an Italian condottiero (mercenary captain) who became captain-general of the Republic of Venice, the most prestigious military post in Italy. A native of Bergamo, he is remembered as one of the most capable and comparatively humane commanders of the Italian Renaissance wars.

Career

Colleoni began his career in the service of various captains, including Braccio da Montone and Carmagnola, and alternated between Venetian and Milanese service under Filippo Maria Visconti and Francesco Sforza. In 1455 he was appointed captain-general of Venice for life, leading the republic's armies on the terraferma frontier for twenty years. He fortified the Venetian border, campaigned in Lombardy, and in 1467 fought the inconclusive Battle of Molinella. From his castle at Malpaga he administered a large estate, patronized agriculture and building, and amassed great wealth.

Legacy

Colleoni left the bulk of his fortune to Venice on condition that an equestrian statue of him be erected in Piazza San Marco. The republic, unwilling to place a personal monument in the square, satisfied the letter of the bequest by placing Andrea del Verrocchio's famous equestrian statue — one of the masterpieces of Renaissance sculpture — outside the Scuola Grande di San Marco, near the church of Santi Giovanni e Paolo, where it still stands. His burial chapel, the Cappella Colleoni in Bergamo, is a jewel of Lombard Renaissance architecture.
