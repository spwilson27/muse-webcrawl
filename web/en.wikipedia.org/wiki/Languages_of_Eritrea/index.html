Eritrea is a multilingual country in the Horn of Africa. The constitution recognises the equality of all Eritrean languages, and no single language has official status, although Tigrinya and Arabic serve as the main working languages of government and commerce. Nine languages are recognised as the languages of Eritrea's nine recognised ethnic groups, and primary education is in principle offered in the child's mother tongue.

Languages

  • Tigrinya — an Ethiosemitic language written in the Ge'ez script; the most widely spoken language, predominant in the highlands and the capital, Asmara.
  • Tigre — a related Ethiosemitic language spoken by the Tigre people of the northern and western lowlands, also written in Ge'ez script (and historically in Arabic).
  • Arabic — spoken along the Red Sea coast and by the Rashaida; widely used in trade and religion (Islam).
  • Afar and Saho — Cushitic languages of the Danakil lowlands and the southern Red Sea region.
  • Kunama and Nara — Nilo-Saharan languages of the western lowlands near the Sudanese border.
  • Bilen — a Cushitic (Agaw) language spoken around Keren.
  • Hidareb (Beja) — a Cushitic language of the northwest, related to Beja in Sudan and Egypt.

Foreign languages

English is the medium of instruction in secondary schools and the main foreign language of government and higher education. Italian, the language of the former colonial power (1890–1941), is still spoken by some older Eritreans and survives in Asmara's commercial vocabulary and architecture.
