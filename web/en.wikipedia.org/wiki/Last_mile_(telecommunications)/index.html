Last mile (telecommunications)

In telecommunications, the last mile (or last kilometre) is the final leg of a network that delivers service to the end user — the segment between the provider's local exchange, cabinet or node and the customer's premises.

Technologies used for the last mile include twisted-pair copper (DSL), coaxial cable (DOCSIS), fibre to the home (FTTH), fixed wireless and satellite. Although short in distance, the last mile is typically the most expensive part of a network per user and the bandwidth bottleneck, because its cost is not shared among many customers.

The term is borrowed from logistics, where the last mile of delivery is likewise disproportionately costly.
