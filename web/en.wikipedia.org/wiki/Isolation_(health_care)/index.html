Isolation (health care)

Isolation (health care)

Field: Infection control

Categories: Contact, droplet, airborne precautions

Related: Quarantine, reverse isolation

In health care facilities, isolation represents one of several measures that can be taken to implement infection control: the prevention of contagious diseases from being spread from a patient to other patients, health care workers, and visitors, or from outsiders to a particular patient (reverse isolation).

Types of precautions

Modern isolation practice combines standard precautions applied to all patients with transmission-based precautions for known or suspected infections: contact precautions for organisms spread by touch (such as MRSA), droplet precautions for respiratory pathogens spread over short distances (such as influenza), and airborne precautions, including negative-pressure rooms, for diseases such as tuberculosis, measles, and COVID-19.

Isolation versus quarantine

Isolation separates people who are ill with a contagious disease from those who are not ill, whereas quarantine restricts the movement of people who were exposed but may not be infected. Both measures gained wide public prominence during the COVID-19 pandemic. Isolation can have significant psychological effects on patients, which health systems attempt to mitigate through communication technology and compassionate care practices.
