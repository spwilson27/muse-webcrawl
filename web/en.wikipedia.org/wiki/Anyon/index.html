Anyon

An anyon is a type of quasiparticle in two-dimensional systems whose exchange statistics are neither bosonic nor fermionic. When two identical bosons are exchanged, the quantum wavefunction is unchanged; when two fermions are exchanged, it changes sign. Anyons can acquire any phase under exchange, a possibility first explored in the late 1970s and early 1980s by Jon Magne Leinaas, Jan Myrheim and Frank Wilczek.

Statistics and braiding

In three dimensions, exchanging two particles twice returns the system to its original configuration, which limits exchange phases to +1 or −1. In two dimensions a double exchange need not be contractible to a point: the world lines of particles can wind around one another. The resulting phases are governed by the braid group rather than the permutation group, so anyons are also called braid statistics particles.

Abelian anyons pick up a single phase under braiding. Non-Abelian anyons have a degenerate manifold of states that transforms by a matrix when particles are braided, a property proposed as a basis for fault-tolerant topological quantum computation.

Physical realisations

The best established anyons occur as quasiparticle excitations in the fractional quantum Hall effect, where electrons in a strong magnetic field form strongly correlated liquids. Certain fractional Hall states, such as the ν = 5/2 state, have been proposed to host non-Abelian quasiparticles, though the interpretation of experiments remains an active research area.
