Columbia Law School (CLS) is the law school of Columbia University, a private Ivy League university in New York City. Founded in 1858, it is one of the oldest and most prestigious law schools in the United States, consistently ranked among the top law schools nationally. It is known particularly for corporate and business law, international law and its placement of graduates into large law firms, federal clerkships and public service.

History

Legal teaching at Columbia dates to the 18th century, when John Jay and others lectured at King's College. The law school was formally established in 1858. In the early 20th century, under dean Harlan Fiske Stone, it became a centre of legal realism, a movement that reshaped American jurisprudence. Its main building, Jerome Greene Hall, opened in 1961 on the Morningside Heights campus.

Academics and notable alumni

Columbia offers the J.D., LL.M. and J.S.D., along with numerous clinics, centres and journals, including the Columbia Law Review. Alumni include United States presidents Theodore Roosevelt and Franklin D. Roosevelt (both attended without graduating), Supreme Court justices including Chief Justices Charles Evans Hughes and Harlan Fiske Stone, and Ruth Bader Ginsburg, who graduated in 1959 tied for first in her class.
