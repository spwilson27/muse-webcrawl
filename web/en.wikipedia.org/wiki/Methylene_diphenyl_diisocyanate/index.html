Methylene diphenyl diisocyanate

Methylene diphenyl diisocyanate
AbbreviationMDI
FormulaC15H10N2O2
Molar mass250.26 g/mol
AppearanceWhite to pale yellow solid (pure 4,4′ isomer)

Methylene diphenyl diisocyanate (MDI) is an aromatic diisocyanate. Three isomers are common, varying in the positions of the isocyanate groups around the rings: 2,2′-MDI, 2,4′-MDI, and 4,4′-MDI. The 4,4′ isomer, also known as pure or monomeric MDI, is the most widely produced. MDI is a key precursor in the manufacture of polyurethanes.

Production

MDI is produced from aniline, which is condensed with formaldehyde to give methylenedianiline (MDA); phosgenation of MDA then yields a mixture of MDI isomers and polymeric MDI (PMDI), which together account for the great majority of world diisocyanate output alongside toluene diisocyanate (TDI). Major producers include Covestro, BASF, Huntsman, Dow, and Wanhua Chemical.

Uses

Reacted with polyols, MDI forms polyurethanes used in rigid insulation foams for buildings and appliances, flexible foams, elastomers, adhesives, sealants, coatings, and binders for composite wood products such as oriented strand board.

Safety

MDI is a potent respiratory sensitiser; inhalation of vapours or aerosols can cause occupational asthma. It reacts with water to release carbon dioxide. Handling requires protective equipment and exposure controls, and spray-foam application is a particular focus of occupational-health guidance.
