Overview

The Senedd building in Cardiff Bay houses the debating chamber (Siambr) and committee rooms of Senedd Cymru, the Welsh Parliament. Designed by the Richard Rogers Partnership and opened by Queen Elizabeth II on St David's Day 2006, it is one of the emblematic buildings of Welsh devolution, notable for its sweeping Welsh-oak-and-steel roof, glass walls symbolising democratic transparency, and pioneering environmental design (ground-source heating, rainwater harvesting).

Context

The Senedd (then National Assembly for Wales, renamed Senedd Cymru/Welsh Parliament in 2020) first met in 1999 after the 1997 devolution referendum, initially in the neighbouring Crickhowell House. The building stands on the redeveloped Cardiff Bay waterfront beside the Wales Millennium Centre and the Pierhead Building. A debating chamber expansion is planned as membership rises from 60 to 96 members at the 2026 election under Welsh electoral reform.
