Pirogue

A traditional dugout pirogue

A pirogue (also piragua or piraga) is a long, narrow canoe-like watercraft propelled by paddles, traditionally made from a single hollowed-out tree trunk as a dugout canoe. The word comes from the Spanish piragua, itself borrowed from a Carib term. Pirogues are used by communities throughout the Caribbean, West Africa, Madagascar, Louisiana and parts of Asia and the Pacific.

Construction and use

Traditional pirogues are carved from a single log and are valued for their shallow draft, which suits swamps, rivers, estuaries and lagoons. Many modern pirogues are built from planks or plywood to the same basic design, and larger seagoing versions — such as the plank-built and outrigger pirogues of Madagascar and Senegal — are important artisanal fishing boats, often fitted with sails or outboard motors.

Regional forms

In Louisiana bayous, flat-bottomed pirogues of cypress or fibreglass are used for hunting, fishing and trapping in water too shallow for larger boats. In West Africa, long dugout pirogues (French: pirogues) are central to inshore fisheries in Senegal, Mali, Nigeria and elsewhere. Military and exploratory expeditions historically adopted pirogues for river travel, notably in North America and Africa.
