An orchard is an intentional plantation of trees or shrubs maintained for food production. Orchards comprise fruit- or nut-producing trees that are generally grown for commercial purposes, but are also sometimes part of large gardens. Most temperate-zone orchards are laid out in a regular grid, with a grazed or mown grass or bare soil base, to make maintenance and fruit gathering easy.

Types

Orchards are often classified by their crop: pome fruit orchards (apples, pears), stone fruit orchards (peaches, plums, cherries), citrus groves, and nut orchards (almonds, walnuts, hazelnuts). In the tropics, plantation crops such as mango, banana, and oil palm are often managed in orchard-like systems.

Management

Orchard management includes pruning to shape trees and maximize yield, irrigation, fertilization, pest and disease control, and pollination management — many orchardists rent honeybee colonies during bloom. Modern high-density orchards plant dwarf or semi-dwarf trees on grafted rootstocks at close spacing, supported by trellises, enabling earlier harvests and mechanized work.

History

Fruit trees were cultivated in the ancient Near East and Mediterranean worlds; the Romans spread orchard practice across Europe, and monasteries preserved varieties through the Middle Ages. Johnny Appleseed became a folk symbol of orchard planting on the American frontier. Traditional low-density orchards with standard trees and pasture beneath (such as the German Streuobstwiese) are now valued for biodiversity.
