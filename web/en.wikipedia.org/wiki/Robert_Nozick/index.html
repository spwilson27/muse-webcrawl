Robert Nozick

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Robert Nozick (16 November 1938 – 23 January 2002) was an American philosopher and professor at Harvard University. His book Anarchy, State, and Utopia (1974), written partly in response to his Harvard colleague John Rawls's A Theory of Justice (1971), mounted the most influential philosophical defence of libertarianism and the minimal state, and won the National Book Award.

Nozick argued from a theory of individual rights as side-constraints: a state more extensive than the “night-watchman” state — limited to protection against force, theft and fraud and to the enforcement of contracts — cannot be justified, because taxation of earnings beyond that point is “on a par with forced labor”. His entitlement theory judges distributions just if they arise from just acquisition and voluntary transfer (summed up in his slogan about Wilt Chamberlain: from each as they choose, to each as they are chosen). The book revived political philosophy's debate over distributive justice and remains the standard philosophical statement of right-libertarianism.

Later work

Nozick's subsequent books ranged widely — Philosophical Explanations (1981) on knowledge, free will and value; The Examined Life (1989); The Nature of Rationality (1993); and Invariances (2001). In The Examined Life he distanced himself from the strictest libertarian conclusions, writing that the position had seemed to him seriously inadequate — a passage often quoted by critics, though he did not repudiate the earlier work wholesale.
