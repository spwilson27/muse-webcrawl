The Dreamcast is a home video game console released by Sega on November 27, 1998, in Japan, September 9, 1999, in North America and October 14, 1999, in Europe. It was the first of the sixth-generation consoles, preceding the PlayStation 2, GameCube and Xbox, and was Sega's final home console.

Features

The Dreamcast was the first console to include a built-in modem for internet play, and its controller came with the Visual Memory Unit (VMU), a memory card with its own small screen. Its library included acclaimed titles such as Sonic Adventure, Soulcalibur, Crazy Taxi, Jet Set Radio, Shenmue and the pioneering online RPG Phantasy Star Online.

Discontinuation and legacy

Despite a strong North American launch, the console struggled against the anticipation and market dominance of the PlayStation 2, and Sega's losses from the preceding Saturn era mounted. Sega discontinued the Dreamcast in 2001 and exited the console hardware business to become a third-party software publisher. The system retains a devoted following and an active homebrew scene, and is widely remembered as ahead of its time.
