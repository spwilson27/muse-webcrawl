Memorial Cup

The Memorial Cup is a trophy awarded annually to the Canadian Hockey League (CHL) champion. It is contested in a tournament between the champions of the CHL's three member leagues — the Ontario Hockey League (OHL), the Quebec Maritimes Junior Hockey League (QMJHL), and the Western Hockey League (WHL) — plus a host team.

History

The trophy was donated in 1919 by the Ontario Hockey Association in memory of Canadian soldiers who died in the First World War; it was first awarded to the Toronto Canoe Club Paddlers in 1920 as the national junior championship of Canada. Initially an east-versus-west championship series, the modern round-robin tournament format with a host team dates from 1972, and the trophy is now dedicated to all Canadian Armed Forces members who have died in service.

Format

The tournament is held each year in late May in a pre-selected host city. The four teams play a round-robin; the top team advances to the final and the second- and third-placed teams meet in a semi-final. The Stafford Smythe Memorial Trophy is awarded to the tournament's most valuable player.

Notable records

The Kamloops Blazers and the Brandon Wheat Kings are among the most successful clubs; the London Knights, Spokane Chiefs, and Windsor Spitfires have each won multiple modern-era titles. The trophy is regarded as one of the most difficult in Canadian sport to win, given the narrow age window of junior eligibility.
