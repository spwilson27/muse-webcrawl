Army of the Andes

The Army of the Andes (Spanish: Ejército de los Andes) was a military force created by the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata and commanded by General José de San Martín. In 1817 it crossed the Andes mountains to liberate Chile from Spanish rule.

Formation

San Martín organised the army at Mendoza between 1814 and 1816, with support from Supreme Director Juan Martín de Pueyrredón. It numbered about 5,000 men, including soldiers freed from slavery, Chilean exiles under Bernardo O'Higgins, artillery, and thousands of mules and horses.

Crossing and campaign

In January–February 1817 the columns crossed the high passes of Los Patos and Uspallata, losing many animals but arriving intact. On 12 February 1817 the army defeated the royalists at the Battle of Chacabuco, entering Santiago and securing Chilean independence, confirmed at Maipú in 1818.
