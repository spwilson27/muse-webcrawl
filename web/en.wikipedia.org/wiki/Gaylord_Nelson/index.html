Gaylord Anton Nelson (4 June 1916 – 3 July 2005) was an American politician from Wisconsin who served as a United States senator from 1963 to 1981 and as the 35th governor of Wisconsin from 1959 to 1963. He is best known as the founder of Earth Day, first held on 22 April 1970.

Early life and career

Nelson was born and raised in Clear Lake, Wisconsin. He graduated from San Jose State College and the University of Wisconsin Law School, served in the United States Army during the Second World War, and entered politics as a Wisconsin state senator in 1948. As governor, he pioneered a programme of outdoor recreation land acquisition funded by a cigarette tax, the Outdoor Resources Action Program (ORAP).

Senate career

In the Senate, Nelson became one of Congress's leading environmental voices. He promoted the Wilderness Act of 1964, worked to ban the insecticide DDT, supported the creation of the Apostle Islands National Lakeshore, and co-sponsored the National Wild and Scenic Rivers Act. Inspired by the anti-war teach-ins of the 1960s, he proposed a national environmental teach-in that became Earth Day 1970, an event involving some 20 million Americans and widely credited with launching the modern environmental movement and spurring the creation of the US Environmental Protection Agency and the Clean Air and Clean Water Acts.

Later life

Defeated for re-election in 1980, Nelson became counsellor of The Wilderness Society. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1995. The Gaylord Nelson Institute for Environmental Studies at the University of Wisconsin–Madison and the Gaylord Nelson Wilderness in the Apostle Islands bear his name.
