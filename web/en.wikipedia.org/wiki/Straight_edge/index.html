Straight edge
OriginEarly 1980s, hardcore punk scene, Washington, D.C.
Key tenetsAbstinence from alcohol, tobacco, and recreational drugs
SymbolX (often drawn on hands)

Straight edge (sometimes abbreviated as sXe) is a subculture within hardcore punk whose adherents refrain from using alcohol, tobacco, and recreational drugs — and in some interpretations also abstain from casual sex and follow vegetarian or vegan diets. It originated in the early 1980s in the Washington, D.C. hardcore scene.

Origins

The term comes from the 1981 song "Straight Edge" by Minor Threat, whose frontman Ian MacKaye expressed frustration with the self-destructive excesses of the punk scene. The X symbol derived from the practice of club staff marking under-age patrons' hands with an X so bartenders would not serve them alcohol; straight edge adherents adopted it voluntarily as a badge of the lifestyle.

Development

Bands such as Youth of Today and Earth Crisis spread the movement through the 1980s and 1990s, the latter emphasising veganism and animal rights. A militant minority gave rise to the hardline offshoot, criticised for intolerance. Straight edge has influenced wider "clean living" youth culture, and its adherents often treat the commitment as lifelong — "breaking edge" carries social stigma within the scene.
