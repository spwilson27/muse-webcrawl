Global optimization

Global optimization is a branch of applied mathematics and numerical analysis that attempts to find the global minimum or maximum of a function or set of functions on a given set. It is usually described as a minimization problem because the maximization of a real-valued function is equivalent to the minimization of its negative.

Global optimization is distinguished from local optimization by its focus: local optimization methods find a locally optimal solution, which for non-convex problems may be far from the global optimum. Given a possibly non-convex and possibly nonsmooth objective function, global optimization seeks a solution that is optimal over the entire feasible region, often with some guarantee of convergence or a bound on the optimality gap.

Approaches

Deterministic approaches include branch and bound methods, interval analysis, cutting plane methods, and, for special problem classes, convex relaxations and difference-of-convex decompositions. Stochastic and heuristic approaches include simulated annealing, evolutionary algorithms, particle swarm optimization, differential evolution, basin-hopping, and multistart local search. Applications arise in engineering design, economics, molecular geometry (such as protein structure prediction), and training of machine learning models.
