Banda Singh Bahadur

Banda Singh Bahadur (born Lachman Dev, 27 October 1670 – 9 June 1716), also called Banda Bairagi, was a Sikh military commander who established a Sikh state in the Punjab region of northern India. Appointed by Guru Gobind Singh, he led Khalsa forces against the Mughal Empire, conquered large territories, abolished the zamindari system in areas under his control, and issued coins in the names of Guru Nanak and Guru Gobind Singh.

Early life

Lachman Dev was born into a Hindu family in Rajouri. As a youth he became a Bairagi ascetic after an incident hunting a deer, taking the name Madho Das, and settled at Nanded on the banks of the Godavari River, where he acquired a reputation for occult powers.

Meeting with Guru Gobind Singh

In 1708 Guru Gobind Singh visited him at Nanded. Impressed, Madho Das became the Guru's disciple (banda, "slave"), was baptised into the Khalsa, and received the name Banda Singh. The Guru ordered him to Punjab to avenge the execution of the Guru's younger sons and to punish oppressive officials, giving him a drum, a banner and five arrows.

Campaigns

Banda Singh gathered an army in Punjab, defeated Mughal forces at Samana (1709) and Sadhaura, and in May 1710 stormed Sirhind, where Wazir Khan, the official responsible for the execution of the Guru's sons, was killed in the Battle of Chappar Chiri. He took control of a large territory south of the Sutlej, made his capital at Lohgarh, struck coins, and granted land to the cultivators, abolishing the zamindari landlord system.

Capture and death

After years of resistance, Banda Singh and his men were besieged at Gurdas Nangal in 1715 and starved into surrender. Taken to Delhi, he refused to convert to Islam and was executed on 9 June 1716 after witnessing the killing of his young son. He remains a revered hero in Sikh history.
