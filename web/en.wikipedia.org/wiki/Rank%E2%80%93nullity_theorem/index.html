Rank–nullity theorem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The rank–nullity theorem is a fundamental result in linear algebra. For a linear transformation T from a finite-dimensional vector space V to another vector space, it states that the dimension of V equals the sum of the dimension of the image of T (its rank) and the dimension of the kernel of T (its nullity): dim(V) = rank(T) + nullity(T).

Matrix form

For an m × n matrix A, the theorem says that the rank of A plus the dimension of its null space equals n, the number of columns. Consequently, a homogeneous system Ax = 0 has a nontrivial solution whenever A has more columns than its rank, and Ax = b is solvable for every b exactly when the rank equals the number of rows.

Proof idea

A standard proof extends a basis of the kernel by adding vectors whose images form a basis of the image, showing the counts add up to the dimension of the domain. The theorem is also a special case of the first isomorphism theorem for vector spaces, since V/ker(T) is isomorphic to the image of T.
