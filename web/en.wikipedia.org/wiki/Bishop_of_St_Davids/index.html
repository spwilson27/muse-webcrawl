Bishop of St Davids

The Bishop of St Davids is the ordinary of the Church in Wales Diocese of St Davids, one of the six dioceses of the Church in Wales. The bishop's seat (cathedra) is St Davids Cathedral in the city of St Davids, Pembrokeshire, and the diocese covers south-west Wales, including Pembrokeshire, Carmarthenshire and Ceredigion.

History

The see traces its origins to the 6th century and the monastic community founded by Saint David (Dewi), the patron saint of Wales, at Mynyw (St Davids). The medieval bishops of St Davids, among them Gerald of Wales (Giraldus Cambrensis), who campaigned unsuccessfully for St Davids to be recognised as a metropolitan archbishopric independent of Canterbury, were major figures in Welsh church life. The great Bishop's Palace beside the cathedral was built in the 14th century under Bishop Henry de Gower.

At the Reformation the diocese became part of the Church of England, and after disestablishment in 1920 it became one of the founding dioceses of the Church in Wales. Joanna Penberthy, consecrated in 2017, was the first woman to serve as a bishop in Wales.
