Porosity

Porosity or void fraction is a measure of the void (i.e. "empty") spaces in a material, and is a fraction of the volume of voids over the total volume, between 0 and 1, or as a percentage between 0% and 100%. Strictly, zero porosity represents a perfectly solid material, while a material with porosity approaching one would be mostly void.

Porosity is a key property in fields such as geology, hydrogeology, soil science, materials science, and petroleum engineering. In rocks and soils, porosity controls how much water or hydrocarbons a formation can store and, together with permeability, how readily fluids flow through it.

Types

Effective porosity counts only the interconnected pore volume through which fluid can pass, whereas total porosity includes isolated pores. Primary porosity is the original pore space formed when a sediment was deposited; secondary porosity is created later by dissolution or fracturing.

Measurement

Porosity can be measured by fluid displacement, gas expansion (helium porosimetry), mercury intrusion, or imaging techniques such as CT scanning and thin-section analysis.
