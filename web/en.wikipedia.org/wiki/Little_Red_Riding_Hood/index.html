Little Red Riding Hood

"Little Red Riding Hood" is a European fairy tale about a young girl and a sly wolf. Its origins can be traced to several European folk tales from the 10th century; the best-known versions are those by Charles Perrault (1697) and the Brothers Grimm (1812).

Plot

A girl nicknamed for the red hooded cloak she always wears is sent by her mother to bring food to her sick grandmother. A wolf intercepts her, learns her destination, and races ahead. In Perrault's version the wolf eats both grandmother and girl, ending with an explicit moral warning young girls against "wolves" of the human kind. In the Grimms' version, a huntsman rescues the two from the wolf's belly, and the wolf is killed by filling its stomach with stones.

History and interpretations

Earlier oral variants include tales of werewolves and the story in the 11th-century poem by Egbert of Liège. Scholars have interpreted the tale variously as a coming-of-age allegory, a warning against strangers, and a seasonal myth. The tale has been retold, parodied and adapted in countless books, films, operas and cartoons, and Little Red Riding Hood remains one of the most recognisable fairy-tale heroines in the world.
