French fries

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: French fries with salt in a paper cone]

French fries (chips in Britain and Ireland; frites in French) are batonnet-cut deep-fried potatoes, crisp outside and fluffy within, served hot with salt and condiments. They are a universal accompaniment to Hamburgers and fried fish, the centrepiece of Belgian friteries and of dishes such as poutine (Canada), chips with curry sauce, and loaded fries.

Belgium and France both claim their invention: Belgian tradition tells of Meuse valley villagers frying potatoes when the river froze (1680s), while France points to Parisian pont-neuf vendors of the revolutionary era; Thomas Jefferson served "potatoes served in the French manner" at the White House. The double-fry (blanch, then crisp) is the professional method.

1. Production

Production

Russet-type potatoes are cut, soaked, blanched in oil at moderate heat, rested, then fried hotter to crisp; frozen par-fried fries supply most of the world's fast food. Acrylamide and oil quality are monitored food-safety topics.
