Elektra Records (or Elektra Entertainment) is an American record label owned by Warner Music Group, founded in 1950 by Jac Holzman with a $600 investment. Named after Electra of Greek myth, it was spelled with a "k" to distinguish it.

History

Elektra began with folk, ethnic, and classical releases, and its Nonesuch subsidiary (1964) sold budget classical records. In the 1960s it became a leading folk-rock and rock label, signing Love, the Doors, Tim Buckley, and (in the UK licensing) acts that defined the era. In the 1970s under David Geffen's Asylum merger (Elektra/Asylum), it released the Eagles, Jackson Browne, Joni Mitchell, Queen (US), and the Cars.

Later years

After periods of restructuring within Warner, Elektra was revived as a standalone frontline label in 2018 under Atlantic Records Group, and in 2022 was folded into 300 Elektra Entertainment with 300 Entertainment and Fueled by Ramen.

Notable artists

Historic and later rosters include Metallica, Mötley Crüe, Björk, Missy Elliott, Bruno Mars (via Atlantic partnership), Ed Sheeran (outside US), and Twenty One Pilots affiliates.
