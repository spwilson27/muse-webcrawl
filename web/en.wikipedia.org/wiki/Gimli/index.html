Gimli

Race: Dwarf

Family: Glóin (father)

Home: Erebor; later the Glittering Caves of Aglarond

Weapon: Battle axe

First appearance: The Fellowship of the Ring (1954)

Gimli is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. A dwarf of the House of Durin, he is the son of Glóin and a member of the Fellowship of the Ring in The Lord of the Rings (1954–1955). Gimli represents the Dwarves in the Fellowship and becomes an unlikely close friend of the Elf Legolas.

Appearances

Gimli first accompanies his father Glóin to Rivendell, where Glóin reports to the Council of Elrond on conditions at Erebor and the threat from Mordor. Gimli is chosen to represent the Dwarves in the Fellowship sent to destroy the One Ring. During the journey he is the first of his people to enter Lothlórien in living memory, where the Lady Galadriel's courtesy overturns his prejudices; he asks for a single strand of her hair as a gift and treasures it thereafter.

He fights at the Battle of the Hornburg, competing with Legolas in counting slain orcs, and afterwards brings a colony of Dwarves from Erebor to restore the gates of Minas Tirith and to settle the Glittering Caves behind Helm's Deep, becoming their lord. After the death of King Elessar, Gimli is said to have sailed west over the Sea with Legolas — the only Dwarf ever admitted to the Undying Lands — because of his devotion to Galadriel.

Character

Gimli is blunt, proud, and quick-tempered, but also steadfast, humorous, and courtly when he chooses. Tolkien uses him to show the reconciliation of Dwarves and Elves after ages of mistrust. His name derives from Old Norse; a dwarf named Gimli does not appear in the Norse sources, though "Gimlé" is a hall in Norse mythology.

Adaptations

Gimli was played by John Rhys-Davies in Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings film trilogy (2001–2003), where the character provides much of the films' comic relief while remaining a formidable warrior. Rhys-Davies also voiced Treebeard in the same films.
