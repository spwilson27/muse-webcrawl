State-building

State-building is the process of creating or strengthening the institutions of a state — its executive, legislature, judiciary, security forces, and revenue systems — so that it can govern effectively and legitimately.

Concepts

Scholars distinguish state-building from nation-building: the former concerns institutions and state capacity, the latter shared identity. Historically, European states were built through war-making, extraction, and protection, as analysed by Charles Tilly. In development policy, state-building has guided international interventions in fragile and post-conflict countries such as Afghanistan, Iraq, and Somalia.

Criticism

Critics argue externally led state-building often produces institutions lacking domestic legitimacy, and that sustainable states emerge from internal political settlements rather than imported templates.
