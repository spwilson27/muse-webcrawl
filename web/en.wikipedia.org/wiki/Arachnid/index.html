Arachnid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A spider, the best-known order of arachnids

Arachnids are a class (Arachnida) of joint-legged invertebrates in the subphylum Chelicerata, characterised by eight walking legs, a body divided into a cephalothorax (prosoma) and abdomen (opisthosoma), and the absence of antennae and wings. The class includes spiders, scorpions, harvestmen, ticks and mites, solifuges, whip scorpions and several smaller orders — over 110,000 described species.

Biology

Arachnids bear two specialised pairs of appendages: chelicerae (used for feeding, and modified into fangs in spiders) and pedipalps (sensory, predatory or reproductive). Most are terrestrial predators feeding on liquefied prey; many produce venom, and spiders produce silk from spinnerets. Respiration is by book lungs, tracheae, or both. Ticks and many mites are parasitic and of major medical and agricultural importance. Arachnids are mostly solitary, though some spiders and harvestmen show social behaviour.

Evolution

The arachnid fossil record extends to the Silurian period, over 430 million years ago, with early scorpion-like forms among the first animals to colonise land. Molecular studies generally place arachnids as close relatives of horseshoe crabs within Chelicerata.
