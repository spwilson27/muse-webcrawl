Mineral (nutrient)

In nutrition, minerals are chemical elements, other than carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen and oxygen, that the body must obtain from food. They are classed as macrominerals (needed in larger amounts — calcium, phosphorus, potassium, sodium, magnesium, sulfur, chloride) and trace elements (iron, zinc, iodine, selenium, copper and others).

Functions

Minerals build structures (calcium and phosphorus in bone; see Skeleton), carry signals (sodium, potassium and calcium ions in the action potential), transport oxygen (iron in Haemoglobin) and serve as enzyme cofactors (zinc, copper, selenium).

Deficiency and excess

Important deficiencies: iron (see Anaemia), iodine (goitre and impaired brain development — prevented by iodised salt), zinc and vitamin-A-linked mineral interactions. Excess is also harmful (iron overload, fluorosis, sodium driving Hypertension), and supplements can unbalance absorption — calcium competes with iron, for example. Most people obtain enough from a varied diet; soil quality and food processing affect content.
