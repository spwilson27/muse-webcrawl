Sandbox game

Genre typeVideo game design mode / genre
Defining traitsOpen-ended play, player-set goals, creative tools
Early exampleSimCity (1989)
Popular examplesMinecraft, Garry's Mod, The Sims

A sandbox game is a video game, or a mode within a game, that gives the player a high degree of freedom and creativity with few or no imposed goals, allowing them to roam, experiment and reshape the game world. The term evokes a child's sandbox, in which the fun comes from the player's own creations rather than from winning.

Characteristics

Sandbox games typically feature open worlds, emergent systems — physics, crafting, economies, ecosystems — and tools the player can combine in unscripted ways. Progress, if defined at all, is self-directed: building structures, collecting resources, or simply surviving. Many sandboxes include a dedicated "creative mode" with unlimited resources alongside a survival mode with hazards and constraints.

History

Precursors include Will Wright's SimCity (1989), a goal-free city builder, and life simulators such as The Sims (2000). The genre's modern popularity surged with Minecraft (2011), a procedurally generated block world that became the best-selling video game of all time, alongside physics sandboxes like Garry's Mod and creative platforms such as Roblox and Dreams. The term is also applied loosely to open-world action games like Grand Theft Auto, though designers distinguish the sandbox's emphasis on creation from mere freedom of movement.

Design considerations

Designers of sandbox games focus on systemic depth — interactions that generate surprises — and on tools that are easy to learn but expressive. Challenges include guiding players who expect objectives, managing performance in player-modified worlds, and moderating user-generated content in shared online sandboxes.
