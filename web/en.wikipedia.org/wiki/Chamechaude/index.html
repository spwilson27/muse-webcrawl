Chamechaude

Chamechaude, the highest summit of the Chartreuse

Chamechaude is a mountain in the French Prealps located in the Isère department of southeastern France. At 2,082 metres (6,831 feet), it is the highest peak of the Chartreuse Mountains.

The mountain rises above the Col de Porte and overlooks the city of Grenoble to the south. Its distinctive profile, with a large monolithic eastern face, makes it one of the most recognizable summits around Grenoble.

Climbing

The standard hiking route ascends from the Col de Porte via the Jardin du Tour, reaching a distinctive breach (the Brèche Arnaud) before following the western slopes to the summit cross. The ascent is popular with hikers from Grenoble, and the mountain is also used for ski touring in winter.

Geology

Like the rest of the Chartreuse massif, Chamechaude is built of folded sedimentary limestones laid down in the ancient Tethys Sea and uplifted during the Alpine orogeny.
