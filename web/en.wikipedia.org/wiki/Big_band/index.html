Big band

A big band or jazz orchestra is a type of musical ensemble of jazz music usually consisting of ten or more musicians with four sections: saxophones, trumpets, trombones, and a rhythm section.

Big band
Stylistic origins: Jazz, swing, blues
Cultural origins: 1920s, United States
Typical instruments: Saxophone, trumpet, trombone, piano, double bass, drums, guitar

History

Big bands originated in the 1920s and dominated popular music during the Swing era of the 1930s and early 1940s. Leaders included Duke Ellington, Count Basie, Benny Goodman, Glenn Miller, Tommy Dorsey, and Fletcher Henderson.

Instrumentation

A typical big band has about 12 to 25 musicians. The rhythm section provides harmony and pulse, while brass and reeds play arranged ensemble passages, riffs and solos.

Swing era

During World War II big bands were central to dance halls and radio broadcasts. After the war, economics, changing tastes and bebop led to decline, though bands led by Ellington, Basie, Stan Kenton and Woody Herman continued.

Modern big bands

Contemporary ensembles include the Jazz at Lincoln Center Orchestra, Maria Schneider Orchestra, and many school and repertory bands preserving the repertoire.
