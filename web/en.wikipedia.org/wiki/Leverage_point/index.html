Leverage point

A leverage point is a place within a complex system (such as an economy, a living body, a city or an ecosystem) where a small shift in one thing can produce big changes in everything. The concept comes from systems thinking and was popularised by Donella Meadows.

Meadows's twelve leverage points

In her essay Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System (1999), Meadows listed twelve places to intervene, in increasing order of effectiveness: constants and parameters; the size of buffers; the structure of material stocks and flows; the length of delays; the strength of negative feedback loops; the gain around positive feedback loops; the structure of information flows; the rules of the system; the power to change the system's structure; the goals of the system; the mindset or paradigm from which the system arises; and the power to transcend paradigms.

Applications

Leverage points are used in environmental policy, organisational change and sustainability science to identify interventions with disproportionate effect.
