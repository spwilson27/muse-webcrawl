Fuxi

Fuxi (Chinese: 伏羲), also romanized as Fu Xi or Fu Hsi, is a culture hero in Chinese mythology, credited — together with his sister and wife Nüwa — with creating humanity and inventing hunting, fishing, cooking, and the Chinese writing system of characters along with the Cangjie system. Fuxi is counted among the Three Sovereigns, the mythical first rulers of China.

Legend

According to legend, Fuxi was born along the Yellow River. He taught humans to cook, to fish with nets, to hunt with iron weapons, and instituted marriage. He is also credited with inventing the guqin (a stringed instrument, with Shennong) and with deriving the eight trigrams (八卦, bāgùà) of the I Ching from patterns he observed in the sky and on the back of a mythical creature emerging from the Yellow River; the arrangement of trigrams attributed to him is known as the Fu Xi arrangement or Earlier Heaven sequence.

Iconography

Fuxi is commonly depicted with Nüwa, their bodies entwined with serpent or dragon tails, holding a carpenter's square (Fuxi) and compass (Nüwa), symbolizing order in heaven and earth. A temple of Fuxi in Tianshui, Gansu province, hosts an annual festival in his honor.
