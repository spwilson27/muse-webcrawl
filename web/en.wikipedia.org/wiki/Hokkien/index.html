Hokkien

Hokkien is a Southern Min Chinese language originating from the Minnan region of Fujian province in southeastern China. It is spoken by the Hokkien people in Fujian, Taiwan (where it is commonly called Taiwanese Hokkien), and throughout the Chinese diaspora in Southeast Asia, including the Philippines, Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia.

Varieties

Major varieties include the Quanzhou, Zhangzhou and Xiamen (Amoy) dialects of Fujian, Taiwanese Hokkien, Philippine Hokkien (Lán-lâng-ōe), Penang Hokkien and Medan Hokkien. These varieties are broadly mutually intelligible, though with local differences in pronunciation and vocabulary.

Writing and status

Hokkien has historically been written with Chinese characters, and romanisation systems such as Pe̍h-ōe-jī were developed by missionaries in the 19th century. In Taiwan it is one of the island's major languages and is taught in schools as a local language.
