Reinsurance Treaty

The Reinsurance Treaty was a secret agreement between the German Empire and the Russian Empire concluded on 18 June 1887. Drafted by German Chancellor Otto von Bismarck and the Russian ambassador Pavel Shuvalov, and signed in Berlin, it was one of the last products of Bismarck's complex system of alliances designed to isolate France and prevent a two-front war.

Terms

Each signatory promised to observe benevolent neutrality if the other became involved in a war with a third great power — except that Germany would not be bound if Russia attacked Austria-Hungary, and Russia would not be bound if Germany attacked France. A secret additional protocol recognised Russian influence in Bulgaria and the Eastern Balkans and promised German support for Russian interests regarding the Turkish Straits.

Non-renewal

After Bismarck's dismissal in 1890, the government of Kaiser Wilhelm II allowed the treaty to lapse — it was not renewed despite Russian willingness — a decision widely regarded as a major diplomatic blunder that pushed Russia toward the Franco-Russian Alliance of 1892, encircling Germany.
