Tom Kristensen (born 7 July 1967) is a Danish former racing driver. He holds the record for the most wins at the 24 Hours of Le Mans with nine victories, earning him the nickname "Mr. Le Mans". Kristensen spent most of his career associated with Audi, winning the French endurance classic eight times with the German manufacturer, including six consecutive victories from 2000 to 2005.

Early career

Kristensen began his career in karting before progressing through junior single-seater categories, including Formula Three in Germany and Japan. He also competed in the Japanese Formula 3000 championship and in touring cars in the Japanese JTCC series.

Le Mans success

Kristensen made his Le Mans debut in 1997 with Joest Racing, driving a Porsche WSC-95 alongside Michele Alboreto and Stefan Johansson, and won at his first attempt. After Audi entered the race with its R8 prototype, Kristensen became the cornerstone of the programme, winning in 2000, 2001, 2002, 2003 (with Bentley), 2004 and 2005. Further wins followed in 2008 with the diesel-powered Audi R10 TDI and in 2013 with the Audi R18 e-tron quattro.

World Endurance Championship

In 2013 Kristensen, together with Allan McNish and Loïc Duval, won the FIA World Endurance Championship drivers' title. He announced his retirement from racing at the end of the 2014 season.

Personal life

Kristensen was born in Hobro, Denmark. In 2010 he published his autobiography, Tom Kristensen: The Book, written with Dan Philipsen.
