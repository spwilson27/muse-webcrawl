Welsh independence

Welsh independence is the political movement advocating for Wales to leave the United Kingdom and become an independent sovereign state. It is a minority position within Welsh politics, though support has grown in the 21st century, particularly after the Brexit referendum and the Scottish independence campaigns.

History

Welsh nationalism in modern politics began with the formation of Plaid Cymru in 1925. For much of the 20th century the movement emphasised language preservation and home rule rather than independence. Plaid Cymru first won a parliamentary seat in 1966 (Gwynfor Evans at Carmarthen). Independence itself became a more prominent goal of the party from the 2000s, and in 2021 Plaid Cymru and Welsh Labour signed a cooperation agreement including an Independent Commission on the Constitutional Future of Wales, which reported in 2024 with options including independence alongside enhanced devolution and a federal UK.

Arguments

Supporters argue that independence would let Wales pursue distinct economic, energy, social and language policies, citing Ireland's success and Wales's natural resources and renewable energy potential. Opponents argue that Wales's large fiscal deficit and trade integration with England make independence economically risky, and that devolution within the UK better serves Welsh interests. Opinion polls typically put support for independence between roughly a quarter and a third of voters, higher among younger and Welsh-speaking respondents.
