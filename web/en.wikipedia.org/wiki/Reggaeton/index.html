Reggaeton

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Reggaeton is a modern musical genre that originated in Puerto Rico during the 1990s. It blends Jamaican dancehall and reggae rhythms — notably the "Dem Bow" riddim — with Latin American sounds and Spanish-language rapping influenced by hip hop. From an underground scene in San Juan it grew into one of the most commercially successful music genres in the world.

History

The genre's roots lie in Panama's Spanish-language reggae (reggae en español, pioneered by El General) and in Puerto Rico's underground rap scene, where DJs such as Playero and DJ Negro circulated mixtapes in the marquesinas (garage parties) of San Juan. Daddy Yankee's "Gasolina" (2004) brought reggaeton to global charts, and hits such as "Despacito" (2017) by Luis Fonsi featuring Daddy Yankee made it a dominant force in mainstream pop.

Musical characteristics

Reggaeton is built around the dembow rhythm, a 3+3+2 kick pattern derived from Shabba Ranks's 1990 dancehall track "Dem Bow". Songs typically run at 84–100 beats per minute, layering synthesised melodies over the looped riddim, with vocals alternating between rapped verses and sung choruses; the associated dance is known as perreo.
