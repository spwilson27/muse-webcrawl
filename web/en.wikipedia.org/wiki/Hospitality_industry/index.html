Hospitality industry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The hospitality industry is a broad category of fields within the service industry that includes lodging, food and drink service, event planning, theme parks, travel, and tourism. It is a multibillion-dollar sector that depends heavily on disposable income and leisure time, and it is one of the world's largest employers.

Sectors

Lodging covers hotels, motels, resorts, hostels, bed and breakfasts, and vacation rentals. Food and beverage includes restaurants, bars, catering, and cafes. Travel and tourism encompasses airlines, cruise lines, tour operators, and travel agencies, while recreation and entertainment includes casinos, theme parks, spas, and events venues.

Characteristics

Hospitality businesses sell experiences and services rather than durable goods; their product is perishable — an unsold hotel room for a night is revenue lost forever — and demand is highly seasonal. The industry is notably labour-intensive and sensitive to economic cycles, public health events, and geopolitical disruption, as demonstrated by the COVID-19 pandemic's severe impact on travel and hotels in 2020–2021.

Major companies

Major international groups include Marriott International, Hilton Worldwide, and InterContinental Hotels Group in lodging; McDonald's, Starbucks, and Yum! Brands in food service; and large cruise and tour operators such as Carnival Corporation and TUI Group.
