The Master Switch: The Rise and Fall of Information Empires is a 2010 non-fiction book by Columbia Law School professor Tim Wu, who also coined the term "net neutrality". The book argues that information industries follow a recurring historical pattern Wu calls "the Cycle": a new technology begins as an open, chaotic, decentralised medium and is eventually consolidated into a closed, centrally controlled empire.

Content

Wu traces the pattern through the history of the telephone (Bell's AT&T monopoly), radio broadcasting, the Hollywood studio system, television, and cable, before turning to the internet. He warns that the internet — built on open principles — risks the same fate of consolidation by a small number of dominant firms, and argues for structural safeguards, including a "separations principle" limiting vertical integration in information industries, alongside network neutrality rules.

Reception

The book was widely praised as an elegant and accessible work of media history and was named among the best books of 2010 by several publications. It won the 2011 Lowell Thomas Gold Medal and remains a standard reference in debates over platform regulation and internet policy.
