The 21st Lancers (Empress of India's) was a cavalry regiment of the British Army. Originally raised in 1858 as the 3rd Bengal European Light Cavalry for the East India Company, it passed through several redesignations as a hussar regiment before being converted to lancers in 1897 and granted the title "Empress of India's" by Queen Victoria.

History

The regiment served in India and was notable for long periods without active service, which earned it the jocular motto "Thou shalt not kill". Its one famous action came in the Mahdist War at the Battle of Omdurman on 2 September 1898, where it charged a concealed force of Mahdist warriors — a far larger body than expected — suffering significant casualties; three Victoria Crosses were awarded, and the young Winston Churchill took part in the charge as an attached officer and war correspondent.

Amalgamation

After limited service in the First World War, the regiment was amalgamated with the 17th Lancers in 1922 to form the 17th/21st Lancers.
