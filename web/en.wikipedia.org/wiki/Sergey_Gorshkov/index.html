Sergey Gorshkov

Sergey Georgyevich Gorshkov (Russian: Серге́й Георгиевич Горшко́в; 26 February 1910 – 13 May 1988) was a Soviet naval officer who served as Commander-in-Chief of the Soviet Navy from 1956 to 1985. He was the architect of the Soviet Union's transformation from a largely coastal defence force into a global blue-water navy capable of challenging Western sea power.

Early life and career

Gorshkov was born in Kamianets-Podilskyi, then in the Russian Empire (now Ukraine). He joined the Soviet Navy in 1927 and graduated from the M. V. Frunze Higher Naval School in Leningrad in 1931. He served in the Black Sea Fleet and later in the Pacific Fleet, rising through command of torpedo boats and destroyers before the Second World War.

Second World War

During the Great Patriotic War, Gorshkov commanded the Azov Flotilla and later the Danube Flotilla, supporting Red Army operations in Ukraine, Romania, Bulgaria, Yugoslavia and Hungary. He was noted for amphibious landings in the Black Sea theatre, including operations around Novorossiysk.

Commander-in-Chief

In January 1956, Nikita Khrushchev appointed Gorshkov Commander-in-Chief of the Soviet Navy, a post he would hold for nearly thirty years — the longest tenure of any Soviet naval commander. Under Gorshkov, the Navy expanded dramatically: nuclear-powered submarines, ballistic-missile submarines, guided-missile cruisers and the Kiev-class aircraft carriers all entered service. He pressed successfully for forward deployment of Soviet naval forces in the Mediterranean, the Indian Ocean and other theatres, presenting a permanent challenge to the United States Navy.

Gorshkov articulated his doctrine in the book The Sea Power of the State (1976) and in the article series "Navies in War and Peace" published in the 1970s. His ideas influenced Western thinking about maritime strategy and the concept of sea power.

Later life and legacy

Promoted to Admiral of the Fleet of the Soviet Union in 1967, Gorshkov retired in 1985 and died in Moscow in 1988. The Russian Navy's Project 22350 frigate Admiral Gorshkov is named in his honour.
