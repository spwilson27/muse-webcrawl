Music publisher

A music publisher is a person or company responsible for ensuring that songwriters and composers receive payment when their compositions are used commercially, and for promoting those compositions to recording artists, film and television producers, advertisers, and other users of music. In the music industry, the publisher typically owns or administers the copyright in the underlying musical composition (the melody and lyrics), as distinct from the sound recording, which is usually owned by a record label or the recording artist.

Sheet music, historically the core product of music publishers.

Role

A publisher's work includes signing songwriters (often under exclusive agreements with advance payments), registering works with collecting societies, licensing uses of the compositions, collecting royalties worldwide, and policing infringements. Revenue comes from mechanical royalties (for recordings), performance royalties (radio, television, streaming, live performance), synchronisation fees (film, TV, advertising, games), and print music sales. Publishers also employ song-pluggers and creative staff who pair songs and songwriters with recording artists.

History

Music publishing began with printed sheet music in the 16th century, and publishers such as Novello and Breitkopf & Härtel built businesses on editions of sheet music. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, New York's Tin Pan Alley publishers dominated popular song. The rise of recording and broadcasting shifted the business toward copyright administration and licensing.

Modern industry

The largest music publishers today are the publishing arms of the major record groups — Universal Music Publishing Group, Sony Music Publishing (the largest, after acquiring EMI Music Publishing), and Warner Chappell Music — alongside major independents such as Kobalt Music Group and BMG Rights Management. In the streaming era, the value of publishing catalogues rose sharply, and investment funds began acquiring song catalogues as financial assets.

Not to be confused with music publishing, the activity and business as a whole, or with a record producer.
