Stochastic calculus

Stochastic calculus is a branch of mathematics that operates on stochastic processes, allowing a consistent theory of integration to be defined for integrals of stochastic processes with respect to stochastic processes such as Brownian motion. It was created and started by Japanese mathematician Kiyosi Itô during and after World War II.

Stochastic calculus
Key concepts: Itô integral, Stratonovich integral, Itô's lemma, SDEs
Founder: Kiyosi Itô
Applications: Mathematical finance, physics

Itô integral

The Itô integral integrates a process against Brownian motion (Wiener process), defined as a limit of sums using non-anticipating evaluations. Because Brownian paths have unbounded variation, ordinary Riemann–Stieltjes integration fails. Itô's lemma — the chain rule of stochastic calculus — adds a second-order correction term involving quadratic variation.

Stochastic differential equations

SDEs model systems with noise, from diffusion processes to asset prices in the Black–Scholes model. The alternative Stratonovich integral obeys ordinary chain-rule calculus and is common in physics.

Applications

Stochastic calculus underpins modern quantitative finance, filtering theory, and stochastic control.
