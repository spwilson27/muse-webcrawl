Natzweiler-Struthof was a Nazi concentration camp located in the Vosges Mountains close to the villages of Natzwiller and Struthof in German-annexed Alsace (then administered as part of Germany, now in France). It operated from 21 May 1941 until September 1944 and was the only concentration camp established by the Nazis on present-day French territory.

History

The camp was established near a quarry of pink granite, and prisoners were subjected to brutal forced labour there and in the camp's network of roughly 70 subcamps. Of approximately 52,000 prisoners deported to Natzweiler-Struthof and its subcamps — many of them political prisoners and Resistance members, including Nacht und Nebel ("Night and Fog") prisoners — an estimated 22,000 died from exhaustion, disease, mistreatment and execution.

Medical crimes

The camp became notorious for medical experiments conducted under SS doctors including Eugen Haagen, Otto Bickenbach and August Hirt. Hirt assembled a collection of skeletons of Jewish prisoners murdered at the camp for the Reich University of Strasbourg; 86 Jewish men and women selected at Auschwitz were gassed at Natzweiler in August 1943 for this purpose. The camp had a small gas chamber used for experiments and killings.

Liberation and memorial

As Allied forces advanced, the SS evacuated the camp in September 1944; American forces reached the empty camp on 23 November 1944, the same day Strasbourg was liberated. The site is now preserved as a memorial and museum, with the European Centre of Deported Resistance Members (CERD) opened in 2005.
