Defence (legal)

In law, a defence is an argument made by or on behalf of a defendant in civil or criminal proceedings that seeks to defeat, in whole or in part, the claim or charge brought against them. A successful defence may result in the dismissal of the case, an acquittal, a reduction in liability, or a lesser penalty.

Criminal defences

Common criminal defences include alibi, self-defence, insanity, diminished capacity, duress, necessity, entrapment, intoxication, and mistake of fact. Some defences are complete, leading to acquittal; others are partial, such as provocation, which in some jurisdictions reduces murder to manslaughter. In many legal systems the prosecution must disprove a properly raised defence beyond reasonable doubt, although the evidential burden of raising certain defences may fall on the defendant.

Civil defences

In civil litigation, defences may deny the elements of the claimant's cause of action or raise affirmative defences such as limitation periods, contributory negligence, consent, or illegality. Pleading rules generally require defences to be set out in a formal document, the statement of defence.
