An academic degree is a qualification awarded to a student upon successful completion of a course of study in higher education, usually at a college or university. Degrees signify that the holder has achieved a standard of learning in a discipline and are often a prerequisite for professional employment.

History

The degree system originated in the medieval European university, where the licentia docendi (licence to teach) developed into the bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees. The structure spread worldwide through European colonial and academic influence.

Levels

Modern systems commonly comprise undergraduate degrees — associate and bachelor's degrees — and postgraduate degrees such as the master's degree and the doctorate (e.g. the PhD). Professional degrees, such as the MD and JD, prepare graduates for regulated professions. The Bologna Process harmonised a three-cycle system (bachelor, master, doctorate) across much of Europe.

Honorary degrees

Universities may also award honorary degrees to recognise distinguished achievement, without requiring coursework.
