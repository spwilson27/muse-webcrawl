Digital curation
ScopeSelection, preservation, maintenance and archiving of digital assets
Key modelDCC Curation Lifecycle Model; OAIS reference model
Applied inLibraries, archives, research data management
The digital curation lifecycle, from creation and appraisal through preservation and reuse

Digital curation is the selection, preservation, maintenance, collection and archiving of digital assets. It involves maintaining, preserving and adding value to digital research data and other digital material throughout its lifecycle. Unlike one-off backup or storage, digital curation is an active, managed process intended to keep digital objects authentic, intelligible and usable over decades despite changes in technology.

Activities

Core activities include appraisal and selection (deciding what to keep), ingest into a repository, creation of descriptive and preservation metadata, storage management, format migration or emulation to counter obsolescence, periodic integrity checking (fixity), and provision of access and reuse. The Digital Curation Centre (DCC), established in the UK in 2004, codified these steps in its Curation Lifecycle Model, while the OAIS (Open Archival Information System) reference model, ISO 14721, defines the functions of a trustworthy digital archive.

Context

Digital curation is practised by national libraries and archives preserving the digital cultural record, by universities managing research data under funder mandates for data sharing (often guided by the FAIR principles — Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable), and by organisations preserving records, media and software. Its challenges include exploding data volumes, fragile file formats and media, rights and privacy constraints, and the cost of active stewardship over very long timescales.
