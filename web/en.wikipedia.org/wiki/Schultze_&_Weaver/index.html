Schultze & Weaver

Schultze & Weaver was an American architectural firm established in New York City in 1921 by Leonard Schultze (1877–1951) and S. Fullerton Weaver (1879–1939). The firm became renowned in the 1920s and 1930s for the design of grand luxury hotels, apartment hotels and clubs, many of them landmarks of their cities.

Partners

Leonard Schultze had previously worked for Warren and Wetmore, where he was involved in Grand Central Terminal and several hotels. S. Fullerton Weaver was a real estate developer and engineer as well as a designer. Together they combined architectural design with hotel planning expertise, frequently collaborating with interior designer Lloyd I. Sexton and others.

Works

The firm's best-known commission is the Waldorf Astoria on Park Avenue in New York (1931), at the time the largest and tallest hotel in the world. Other New York works include The Pierre (1930), the Sherry-Netherland (1927), the Park Lane Hotel, and the Lexington Hotel. Outside New York they designed the Los Angeles Biltmore (with additions), the Breakers and the Everglades Club in Palm Beach, the Boca Raton Resort (Cloister Inn additions), the Atlanta Biltmore, the Sevilla-Biltmore in Havana, and numerous apartment buildings and private clubs.

Legacy

Schultze & Weaver's hotels exemplified the setback skyscraper hotel of the interwar boom years, blending Beaux-Arts planning with modern massing. Several of their buildings are designated landmarks and remain in operation as hotels.
