Terry Mitchell Riley (born June 24, 1935) is an American composer and performing musician best known as a pioneer of minimal music. His works In C (1964) and A Rainbow in Curved Air (1969) launched a style built on consonant harmony, steady pulse and gradual pattern repetition, profoundly influencing classical, electronic and popular music.

Early life and education

Riley was born in Colfax, California, and grew up in Redding. He studied composition at San Francisco State University and the University of California, Berkeley, where he met La Monte Young; both were influenced by their teacher Robert Erickson and by jazz. In the early 1960s Riley worked at the San Francisco Tape Music Center and experimented with tape loops in Paris, where, working as a studio assistant, he created loop-based pieces such as Music for The Gift (1963) — often cited as early tape minimalism.

Minimalism and major works

In C (premiered November 1964 in San Francisco, with Steve Reich, Jon Gibson and Pauline Oliveros among the performers) consists of 53 short melodic modules in C major that any number of musicians repeat at their own pace over a pulsing high C. Its open form, tonal materials and driving rhythm made it a landmark — frequently called the first minimalist masterpiece. The Columbia recordings In C (1968) and A Rainbow in Curved Air (1969), the latter an overdubbed keyboard improvisation, brought Riley wide recognition; The Who's "Baba O'Riley" is named partly in his honour. His "time-lag accumulator" tape-delay system powered all-night solo concerts of keyboard and soprano saxophone improvisation in the late 1960s.

Indian classical music and later work

In 1970 Riley began studying North Indian raga singing with Pandit Pran Nath, a discipleship that lasted until Pran Nath's death in 1996 and reshaped Riley's music toward long-form vocal and keyboard improvisation in just intonation. From the 1980s he wrote extensively for the Kronos Quartet, beginning with Sunrise of the Planetary Dream Collector, producing numerous string quartets and multimedia works. Later compositions include concertos, the opera collaborations, and works performed with his son, guitarist Gyan Riley. He has lived largely in rural California, teaching at Mills College in the 1970s and continuing to perform worldwide.

Legacy

Riley, together with La Monte Young, Steve Reich and Philip Glass, is regarded as one of the founders of musical minimalism. His emphasis on repetition, modal harmony and improvisation influenced generations of composers and electronic musicians, from progressive rock to ambient and techno.
