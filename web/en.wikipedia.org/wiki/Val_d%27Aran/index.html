The Val d'Aran (Aranese for "Valley of Valleys"; Spanish: Valle de Arán) is an administrative entity in the province of Lleida, Catalonia, Spain, in the Pyrenees. Uniquely within Spain, the valley lies on the northern slope of the Pyrenees and is drained by the Garonne River, which flows north into France.

Geography and language

The valley covers about 634 square kilometres and has a population of roughly 10,000. Its native language is Aranese, a variety of Gascon Occitan, which is co-official in the valley and recognised throughout Catalonia. Val d'Aran has its own governing council, the Conselh Generau d'Aran, with a degree of autonomy restored under Spanish and Catalan law.

History and economy

Historically tied to both France and the Crown of Aragon, the valley was definitively attached to Spain in the Middle Ages. Its economy today rests largely on tourism, especially the ski resort of Baqueira-Beret, one of the largest in the Pyrenees, and on hydroelectric power.
