Peginterferon alfa-2a

Peginterferon alfa-2a, sold under the brand name Pegasys, is a medication used to treat chronic hepatitis C and chronic hepatitis B. It consists of interferon alfa-2a covalently attached to a branched 40 kDa polyethylene glycol (PEG) chain; this "pegylation" greatly prolongs the drug's half-life, allowing once-weekly subcutaneous injection instead of the frequent dosing of standard interferon.

Medical uses

For hepatitis C, peginterferon alfa-2a was long combined with the antiviral ribavirin as standard therapy, achieving sustained virological responses in a substantial fraction of patients depending on viral genotype. Direct-acting antivirals introduced in the 2010s have since largely replaced interferon-based regimens for hepatitis C, but the drug remains an option for hepatitis B, where finite courses can produce durable immune control in some patients.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include flu-like symptoms (fever, fatigue, muscle aches), injection-site reactions, hair thinning, and mood changes including depression; blood-count suppression and thyroid dysfunction require monitoring. The drug was developed by Hoffmann-La Roche and approved in the early 2000s.
