RepRap project

The RepRap project is an open-source initiative to develop a low-cost 3D printer that can print most of its own components — a self-replicating manufacturing machine. Its name is short for replicating rapid prototyper. The project was founded in 2005 by Adrian Bowyer, a senior lecturer in mechanical engineering at the University of Bath, United Kingdom, and its designs are released under the GNU General Public License.

History and designs

The first RepRap machine, "Darwin" (2008), could print a substantial fraction of its own plastic parts. It was followed by "Mendel" (2009), the simplified "Prusa Mendel" developed by Josef Průša, and later designs such as the Mendel90, Wallace and Rostock-derived delta printers. Because a RepRap can print parts for another RepRap, the design spread virally and seeded the desktop 3D-printing industry.

Influence

RepRap's open-source ethos and its ecosystem of firmware (e.g. Marlin), electronics and slicer software directly inspired maker communities and companies such as Prusa Research and, indirectly, much of the consumer fused-filament-fabrication market.
