Piquet

Piquet is an early two-player trick-taking card game, played with a 32-card pack (the piquet pack, with cards below the seven removed). One of the oldest card games still widely recorded, it is first mentioned in print in 1535 in the works of Rabelais and was for centuries regarded as the finest two-player card game.

Play

Each player is dealt twelve cards in packets of two or three; eight cards remain as the talon, from which the players improve their hands by exchanging cards (the elder hand up to five, the younger up to as many as remain). Play proceeds through declarations before the trick play:

  • Point — the longest suit;
  • Sequences — runs such as tierces (three cards), quatrièmes (four) and longer;
  • Sets — trios and quatorzes (four of a kind) of queens, kings, aces, etc.

Tricks are then played out; scores accrue from the declarations, from winning tricks and cards, and from bonuses such as repique (reaching 30 points before the opponent scores at all) and pique (reaching 30 before the opponent scores once tricks have begun). A game traditionally consists of six hands (a partie), after which the higher total wins the difference.

History

Piquet originated in France and spread throughout Europe in the 16th and 17th centuries; Charles Cotton described it in The Compleat Gamester (1674). It remained a fashionable game of skill into the 19th century, and the auction-piquet variant popular in Britain introduced bidding and a modified scoring.
