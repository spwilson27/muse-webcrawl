Equality Act 2010

The Equality Act 2010 (c. 15) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that consolidated and replaced previous anti-discrimination legislation, including the Sex Discrimination Act 1975, the Race Relations Act 1976, and the Disability Discrimination Act 1995. It received royal assent on 8 April 2010, and most of its provisions came into force on 1 October 2010.

Protected characteristics

The Act prohibits discrimination, harassment, and victimisation on the basis of nine protected characteristics: age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, and sexual orientation. It covers employment, the provision of goods and services, education, housing, transport, and public functions, and imposes a public sector equality duty on public authorities.

Key features

The Act defines direct and indirect discrimination, requires reasonable adjustments for disabled people, restricts employers' ability to ask health questions before offering a job, and permits positive action in limited circumstances. Pay secrecy clauses that prevent employees from discussing pay in relation to discrimination are unenforceable. Enforcement in England, Scotland and Wales is supported by the Equality and Human Rights Commission.
