High Speed Packet Access (HSPA) is a family of two mobile telephony protocols — High Speed Downlink Packet Access (HSDPA) and High Speed Uplink Packet Access (HSUPA) — that extend and improve the performance of existing 3G mobile networks using the UMTS standard. Commercially introduced from 2005–2006, HSPA networks were widely marketed as "3.5G".

Technology

HSDPA, standardised in 3GPP Release 5, introduced a shared downlink channel (HS-DSCH), adaptive modulation including 16-QAM, hybrid automatic repeat request and fast scheduling in the base station, raising peak downlink rates initially to 14.4 Mbit/s. HSUPA (Release 6) applied similar techniques to the uplink, reaching 5.76 Mbit/s.

HSPA+

The further evolution, Evolved HSPA or HSPA+ (Releases 7 and later), added MIMO, 64-QAM and dual-carrier aggregation, pushing theoretical peak rates to 42 Mbit/s and beyond. HSPA and HSPA+ networks were deployed by hundreds of operators worldwide and served as the principal mobile broadband technology before the rollout of LTE (4G).
