Lake Petén Itzá (Spanish: Lago Petén Itzá) is a lake in the Petén lowlands of northern Guatemala, the third-largest lake in the country. On its southern shore stands the island town of Flores, connected by causeway to Santa Elena and San Benito, which together form the gateway for visitors to the Maya ruins of Tikal.

History

The lake was the heartland of the Itza Maya. Their capital, Nojpetén (also called Tayasal), stood on the island where Flores now stands and was the last independent Maya polity in Mesoamerica: it did not fall to the Spanish until 1697, nearly two centuries after Hernán Cortés passed through the region in 1525. The lake basin is dotted with Maya archaeological sites, including Topoxté, Zacpetén, and Yaxhá on its northeastern shore.

Geography

The lake lies in a limestone (karst) basin with no surface outlet. Its steep underwater sinkholes make it unusually deep for its size. The surrounding Petén forests, once part of a vast rainforest, have suffered heavy deforestation, and settlement and tourism development around Flores have raised concerns about water quality.
