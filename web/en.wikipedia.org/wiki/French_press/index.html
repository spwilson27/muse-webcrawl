French press

French press
TypeCoffee brewer
Other namesCafetière, coffee plunger

A French press, also called a cafetière or coffee plunger, is a coffee brewing device with a cylindrical beaker and a plunger fitted with a mesh filter that separates grounds from brewed coffee.

History

Early French designs date to the 1850s; the recognisable modern design was patented by Italians Attilio Calimani (1929) and Faliero Bondanini (1958), and popularised by Danish company Bodum.

Brewing

Coarse grounds are steeped in hot water for about four minutes, then the plunger is pressed down. Immersion brewing yields a full-bodied cup with more oils and sediment than paper-filtered coffee. It can also brew loose tea and cold brew.
