The Komandorski Islands, or Commander Islands (Russian: Командо́рские острова́), are a group of treeless, sparsely populated islands in the Bering Sea, about 175 km (109 mi) east of the Kamchatka Peninsula in the Russian Far East. The group consists principally of Bering Island and Medny (Copper) Island, together with several small islets and rocks. Administratively they form the Aleutsky District of Kamchatka Krai.

History

The islands are named for Commander Vitus Bering, whose second Kamchatka expedition was wrecked on Bering Island in 1741; Bering died there and is buried on the island. During the wreck's wintering, naturalist Georg Wilhelm Steller described the Steller's sea cow, a giant sirenian confined to these waters that was hunted to extinction by about 1768. In the 19th century, Aleut (Unangan) settlers were brought to the islands by the Russian-American Company to hunt fur seals and sea otters; their descendants form much of the population of Nikolskoye, the only inhabited settlement.

Geography and nature

The islands are the westernmost extension of the Aleutian arc, separated from the rest of the chain by the Near Strait. The climate is subarctic oceanic — cool, foggy and windy. The Komandorsky Zapovednik (nature reserve), established in 1993 and later designated a federal nature reserve and UNESCO biosphere reserve, protects huge colonies of northern fur seals and sea lions, sea otters, and nesting seabirds, as well as the marine waters around the islands.
