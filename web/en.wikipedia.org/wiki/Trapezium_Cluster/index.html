The Trapezium or Trapezium Cluster, also known by its Bayer designation Theta¹ Orionis, is a tight open cluster of massive young stars in the heart of the Orion Nebula, in the constellation Orion. It was discovered by Galileo Galilei, who sketched three of its stars in 1617; the grouping takes its name from the trapezoid formed by its four brightest members, conventionally labelled A, B, C, and D.

Members

The brightest component, Theta¹ Orionis C, is one of the most massive and luminous stars known (an O-type star) and supplies most of the ultraviolet radiation that ionises the surrounding nebula. The cluster contains on the order of a thousand young stars packed into a region a few light-years across, including numerous proplyds — protoplanetary discs being photoevaporated by the massive stars — imaged by the Hubble Space Telescope. Additional components (E, F, G, H) are fainter members of the multiple system.

Significance

At only about one to two million years old, the Trapezium is a key laboratory for studying star formation, massive-star feedback, and the early evolution of planetary systems. The wider Orion Nebula Cluster, of which it is the dense core, is among the nearest regions of active massive star formation to Earth.
