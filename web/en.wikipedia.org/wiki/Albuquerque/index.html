Albuquerque, New Mexico

City in Bernalillo County, New Mexico, United States
Founded: 1706 as La Villa de Alburquerque
Population: ~564,000 (city, 2020); ~916,000 metro
Elevation: ~5,312 ft (1,619 m)

Albuquerque (/ˈælbəˌkɜːrki/; Spanish: [alβuˈkeɾke]), abbreviated ABQ, is the most populous city in the U.S. state of New Mexico. Its nicknames, The Duke City and Burque, reference its founding in 1706 as La Villa de Alburquerque by Nuevo México governor Francisco Cuervo y Valdés, named in honour of the Duke of Alburquerque, viceroy of New Spain. The first "r" was later dropped in English usage.

Geography

Albuquerque lies in the Albuquerque Basin on the Rio Grande, flanked by the Sandia Mountains to the east and volcanic mesas (the West Mesa) to the west. It has a high-desert, arid climate with hot summers, mild winters and abundant sunshine. The Sandia Peak Tramway, one of the world's longest aerial tramways, climbs to 10,378 ft.

History

Pueblo peoples lived along the Rio Grande for centuries before Spanish colonisation; nearby sites include Petroglyph National Monument. The city grew with the arrival of the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway in 1880 (New Town, east of Old Town), and with Route 66 in the 20th century. After World War II, Kirtland Air Force Base, Sandia National Laboratories and the University of New Mexico (founded 1889) drove growth as a centre for defence, nuclear research and technology.

Culture

The Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta, held each October since 1972, is the largest hot-air balloon festival in the world, famous for mass ascensions and specially shaped balloons. The city is known for New Mexican cuisine featuring red and green chile ("Christmas" when both are ordered), Old Town Plaza, and as the setting of the television series Breaking Bad and Better Call Saul.

Economy and transport

Major employers include Sandia National Laboratories, Kirtland AFB, Presbyterian Healthcare and the university. Albuquerque International Sunport is the state's largest airport; I-25 and I-40 meet at the "Big I" interchange in the city.
