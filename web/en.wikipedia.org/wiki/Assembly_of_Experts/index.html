Assembly of Experts

Country: Iran

Members: 88 mujtahids

Term: Eight years

Function: Electing and supervising the Supreme Leader

The Assembly of Experts (Persian: مجلس خبرگان رهبری, Majles-e Khobregān-e Rahbari), also translated as the Assembly of Experts of the Leadership, is a deliberative body of Islamic theologians (mujtahids) in Iran charged with electing, supervising and potentially dismissing the Supreme Leader of Iran.

Functions

Under Articles 107, 109 and 111 of the Iranian constitution, the Assembly selects the Supreme Leader after the death, resignation or dismissal of the incumbent, monitors his continued fitness for office, and may remove him if he becomes incapable or loses the required qualifications. It famously acted in June 1989, electing Ali Khamenei to succeed Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini within a day of Khomeini's death.

Membership and elections

The Assembly has 88 members elected by direct public vote for eight-year terms from multi-member provincial districts. Candidates must be Islamic jurists and are vetted by the Guardian Council — itself partly appointed by the Supreme Leader — a process critics say limits genuine choice. The first Assembly was elected in December 1982. The body meets at least twice a year, generally in Tehran, and its deliberations about succession are secret.

Compare Assembly of Notables, a consultative body of pre-revolutionary France.
