Artificial general intelligence

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


AGI is the hypothetical intelligence of a machine that can perform any intellectual task a human can

Artificial general intelligence (AGI) is a type of artificial intelligence (AI) that matches or surpasses human capabilities across virtually all cognitive tasks. This contrasts with narrow AI, which is limited to specific tasks. AGI is considered one of the definitions of strong AI. AGI research is a primary goal of AI research and of technology companies such as OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Anthropic.

Besides AGI, other terms used to describe the concept include full AI, human-level AI, and general-purpose AI. Creating AGI is a long-term goal that has not yet been achieved; timelines for its arrival are contested, with surveys of experts giving estimates ranging from years to many decades, and some arguing it may never be achieved.

Characteristics

Various criteria for intelligence have been proposed, including the ability to learn from experience, solve novel problems, adapt to new situations, reason abstractly, and understand and produce language. Tests proposed for AGI range from the Turing test to more practical benchmarks such as performing a job at a human level or passing college-level examinations.

Potential risks and benefits

AGI is widely discussed in terms of both its transformative potential benefits — in science, medicine, and productivity — and its potential risks, including existential risk, labour displacement, and concentration of power. The field of AI alignment studies how to ensure that advanced AI systems act in accordance with human intentions.
