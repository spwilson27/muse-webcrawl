Reification (fallacy)

Reification (also known as concretism, hypostatization, or the fallacy of misplaced concreteness) is a fallacy of ambiguity in which an abstract belief, concept or hypothetical construct is treated as though it were a concrete, real event or physical entity.

Description

The term comes from the Latin res ("thing") and facere ("to make"): literally, "thing-making". Alfred North Whitehead described the fallacy of misplaced concreteness as the error of mistaking the abstract for the concrete. A common example is speaking of "Nature" or "the Market" as if it were a single agent with intentions and desires.

Uses of the concept

In Marxist theory, following Georg Lukács, reification refers to the process by which social relations between people come to appear as relations between things. In law and rhetoric, awareness of reification is used to criticise arguments that depend on treating constructs such as "society" or "the economy" as unitary actors.
