Christian Jürgensen Thomsen (29 December 1788 – 21 May 1865) was a Danish antiquarian and museum curator who developed early archaeological techniques and is best known for introducing the three-age system — the division of prehistory into the Stone Age, Bronze Age and Iron Age — in his 1836 book Ledetraad til Nordisk Oldkyndighed ("Guideline to Scandinavian Antiquity").

Museum work

Thomsen, the son of a wealthy merchant, became secretary to the Royal Commission for the Preservation of Antiquities and from 1816 organised the collection that became the National Museum of Denmark. Arranging the exhibits by the material of their cutting edges, he found that artefacts sorted naturally into groups made of stone, bronze and iron, and he backed the scheme with stratigraphic and closed-find evidence. He was also a leading numismatist, building an important collection of bracteates and coins.

Legacy

The three-age system was taken up across Europe and remains a basic framework of prehistoric archaeology. Thomsen pioneered the modern public museum, personally guiding visitors through the galleries on open days. His successor Jens Jacob Worsaae confirmed the system through excavation.
