Westminster

Westminster is the main district of the City of Westminster in central London, England, on the north bank of the River Thames. It is the historic political heart of the United Kingdom, containing the Palace of Westminster (the Houses of Parliament), Westminster Abbey, Buckingham Palace, and the government departments of Whitehall. The name derives from "West Minster"—the great abbey church west of the City of London.

History

Westminster grew up around the abbey founded in the early medieval period and refounded by Edward the Confessor in the 11th century. It became the seat of the royal court and of Parliament, which first met in Westminster Hall in the 13th century. The City of Westminster received city status in 1540 and today is one of London's 32 boroughs, governed by Westminster City Council.

Landmarks

Major landmarks include Big Ben (the Elizabeth Tower), Westminster Bridge, the Supreme Court, the Cenotaph, Downing Street, and St James's Park. The Palace of Westminster and Westminster Abbey together form a UNESCO World Heritage Site. "Westminster" is also used metonymically for the UK Parliament and government, giving its name to the Westminster system of parliamentary government used across the Commonwealth.
