Lycopene

Chemical formula: C40H56

Class: Carotenoid (tetraterpene)

Food sources: Tomatoes, watermelon, pink grapefruit, guava

Lycopene is an organic compound classified as a carotenoid, a bright red pigment found in tomatoes and other red and pink fruits and vegetables, such as red carrots, watermelons, grapefruits, and papayas. It is an acyclic isomer of beta-carotene, with the chemical formula C40H56. Although lycopene is chemically a carotene, it has no vitamin A activity.

Chemistry and sources

Lycopene is a highly unsaturated, symmetrical tetraterpene assembled from eight isoprene units, containing 11 conjugated and 2 non-conjugated double bonds; this conjugated system gives it its deep red colour and strong antioxidant capacity in vitro. In plants it functions in light harvesting and protects against photodamage. Tomatoes and tomato products — juice, sauce, and paste — provide more than 85 percent of the dietary lycopene for most people; cooking and processing increase its bioavailability, since heat breaks down cell walls and converts the trans form into more readily absorbed cis isomers. Because lycopene is fat-soluble, it is better absorbed when eaten with oils.

Health research

Lycopene has been extensively studied as an antioxidant. Observational studies have associated high tomato and lycopene intake with lower risks of certain cancers, particularly prostate cancer, and of cardiovascular disease, but regulatory bodies such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and the European Food Safety Authority have found the evidence insufficient to authorise health claims, regarding it as limited and inconclusive. Lycopene is approved as a food colouring (E160d) in many jurisdictions.
