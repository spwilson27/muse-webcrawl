House of Baden

The House of Baden is a German dynastic family that ruled the Margraviate, Electorate and Grand Duchy of Baden in southwestern Germany from the 11th century until the abolition of the monarchy in 1918. A branch of the House of Zähringen, the family took its name from Baden Castle (Hohenbaden) above the town of Baden-Baden.

History

Herman I of Baden (died 1074), son of Berthold I of Zähringen, is regarded as the founder of the line. His descendants ruled a territory along the upper Rhine that was repeatedly divided among branches — including Baden-Baden and Baden-Durlach — and reunified in 1771 under Charles Frederick, who became the first Grand Duke in 1806 as the territory expanded greatly during the Napoleonic era. Karlsruhe, founded in 1715, served as the capital.

Grand Dukes and later history

Grand Dukes of Baden included Charles Frederick, Charles, Louis I, Leopold, Louis II, Frederick I, Frederick II and Maximilian. Frederick I's marriage to Princess Louise of Prussia tied the house closely to the Hohenzollerns. The last Grand Duke, Frederick II, abdicated on 22 November 1918 during the German Revolution. The head of the house since 1963 was Maximilian, Margrave of Baden (died 2022), succeeded by his son Bernhard. Members of the family also married into the Swedish royal house: Queen Victoria of Sweden and Queen Louise of Sweden were Baden princesses.
