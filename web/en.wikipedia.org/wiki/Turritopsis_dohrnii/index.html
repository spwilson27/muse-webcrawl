Turritopsis dohrnii

Turritopsis dohrnii, also known as the immortal jellyfish, is a species of small, biologically immortal hydrozoan jellyfish found in temperate and tropical waters worldwide, originating in the Mediterranean Sea and the waters of Japan. Its bell is only about 4.5 millimetres (0.18 in) across.

Biological immortality

T. dohrnii is one of the few known animals capable of reverting completely to its sexually immature, colonial polyp stage after having reached sexual maturity as a solitary medusa. When injured, sick, or old, the jellyfish sinks to the ocean floor, transforms into a cyst-like ball, and its cells undergo transdifferentiation — converting, for example, muscle cells into nerve cells — to form a new polyp colony genetically identical to the original. In theory this cycle can repeat indefinitely, making the species biologically immortal, although in practice most individuals are eaten or succumb to disease.

Spread and research

The species has spread worldwide, probably via ballast water in ships, a dispersal researchers have called a "silent invasion." Because of its life-cycle reversal, T. dohrnii is studied for insights into cellular ageing, regeneration, and transdifferentiation.
