Möbius transformation

In geometry and complex analysis, a Möbius transformation of the complex plane is a rational function of the form

f(z) = (az + b) / (cz + d)

where z is a complex variable and a, b, c, d are complex coefficients with ad − bc ≠ 0. Named after the German mathematician August Ferdinand Möbius, these transformations are defined on the Riemann sphere (the extended complex plane) and form a group under composition.

Properties

Möbius transformations are conformal maps: they preserve angles. They map circles and lines to circles and lines (lines being circles through the point at infinity), and they preserve the cross-ratio of four points. Every Möbius transformation can be decomposed into a composition of simpler ones: translations, rotations and scalings, and inversion in the unit circle.

Classification and applications

According to their fixed points and multipliers, Möbius transformations are classified as elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic or loxodromic. The group of Möbius transformations is isomorphic to the projective linear group PGL(2, ℂ) and to the Lorentz group of special relativity. They are fundamental in complex analysis, hyperbolic geometry, and the theory of the modular group and elliptic curves.
