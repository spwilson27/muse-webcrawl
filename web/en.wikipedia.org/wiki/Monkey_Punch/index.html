Monkey Punch (Japanese: モンキー・パンチ) was the pen name of Japanese manga artist Kazuhiko Katō (26 May 1937 – 11 April 2019), best known as the creator of Lupin III, one of the most influential and long-running manga and anime franchises. His loose, energetic drawing style was influenced by Western artists such as Mort Drucker of Mad magazine.

Career

Katō began drawing manga while working as an X-ray technician, debuting professionally in the early 1960s under various pen names before adopting Monkey Punch. In 1967 he launched Lupin III in the first issue of Weekly Manga Action; the adult-oriented series, about the gentleman thief Arsène Lupin III and his gang, became a hit and spawned anime television series, films and specials spanning more than five decades, including Hayao Miyazaki's film The Castle of Cagliostro (1979).

Later work and teaching

Monkey Punch was a pioneer of digital manga production, using computers from the 1990s, and taught manga studies as a professor at Otemae University. He received numerous honours, including recognition at international comics festivals, and served as a director of the Japan Cartoonists Association.

Death and legacy

He died of pneumonia on 11 April 2019, aged 81. The Lupin III franchise continues in new productions, and his hometown of Hamanaka, Hokkaido, maintains a museum dedicated to his work.
