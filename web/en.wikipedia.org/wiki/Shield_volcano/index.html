A shield volcano is a type of volcano named for its low profile, resembling a warrior's shield lying on the ground. It is formed by the eruption of highly fluid (low viscosity) lava, which travels farther and forms thinner flows than the more viscous lava erupted from a stratovolcano. Repeated eruptions result in the steady accumulation of broad sheets of lava, building up the shield volcano's distinctive form.

Characteristics

Shield volcanoes are built almost entirely of fluid basaltic lava flows. Because the lava is so fluid, it spreads out in thin sheets over great distances before solidifying, producing slopes of only a few degrees. Individual flows may be only a few metres thick, but tens of thousands of superimposed flows can build an edifice hundreds of kilometres across. A central summit caldera, formed by collapse after magma drains from the chamber below, is a common feature, as are rift zones radiating from the summit along which much of the lava is erupted from fissures.

Eruptions from shield volcanoes are usually effusive rather than explosive, producing lava fountains, fast-moving channelised flows, and eventually lava tubes that can transport lava many kilometres with little cooling. Explosive activity can occur when water interacts with the magma, as at Kīlauea in 1924, or when gas-rich magma arrives rapidly from depth.

Distribution

Shield volcanoes occur wherever low-viscosity magma reaches the surface, most characteristically above mantle hotspots. The Hawaiian Islands are a chain of shield volcanoes built over the Hawaiian hotspot; Mauna Loa is the largest shield volcano on Earth. Other notable examples include the volcanoes of Iceland, the Galápagos Islands, Réunion, and many of the great volcanic edifices of the ocean floor. Olympus Mons on Mars, the tallest volcano in the Solar System, is an enormous shield volcano, its size attributed to the absence of plate tectonics and low gravity on Mars.

Hawaiian shields

Hawaiian shield volcanoes pass through a characteristic life cycle: a submarine pre-shield stage of alkalic pillow lavas, a main shield stage producing the great bulk of the edifice from tholeiitic lava, a caldera-forming and cap stage, and finally a rejuvenated stage of small, isolated eruptions after a period of erosion and quiet.
