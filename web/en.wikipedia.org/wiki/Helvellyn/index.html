Helvellyn

Helvellyn
Elevation: 950 m (3,117 ft)
Location: Lake District, Cumbria, England
Listing: Wainwright, Hewitt

Helvellyn is a mountain in the English Lake District, the third-highest point in England at 950 metres (3,117 ft) above sea level, after Scafell Pike and Scafell. It lies between Thirlmere and Ullswater.

Topography

The mountain is famous for its sharp arêtes, Striding Edge and Swirral Edge, which enclose the glacial coves containing Red Tarn. The summit plateau is broad and flat, and was the site of a celebrated early aeroplane landing by John Leeming and Bert Hinkler in 1926.

Literary associations

Helvellyn was a favourite of William Wordsworth, who climbed it many times and wrote of the death of the artist Charles Gough, whose body was found below the summit in 1805, guarded by his dog — commemorated in poems by Wordsworth and Walter Scott.

Recreation

It is one of the most climbed fells in the Lake District, with routes from Glenridding, Patterdale, Thirlmere and Wythburn; winter conditions on the edges can be serious and are monitored by the Lake District Weatherline fell-top assessors based on the summit.
