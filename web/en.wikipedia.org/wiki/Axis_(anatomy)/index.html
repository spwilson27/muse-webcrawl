Axis (anatomy)

Axis (C2 vertebra)

In anatomy, the axis (from Latin axis, "axle") or epistropheus is the second cervical vertebra (C2) of the spine, immediately inferior to the atlas, upon which the head rests. The axis is distinguished by its strong odontoid process (dens), a bony knob that projects upward from its body and forms the pivot around which the atlas and head rotate.

Structure

The axis consists of a body, heavy pedicles and laminae, a bifid spinous process that serves as an attachment for muscles of the neck, and transverse processes pierced by the transverse foramina for the vertebral arteries. The dens is held in place against the atlas by the transverse ligament of the atlas and the alar ligaments, forming the median atlanto-axial joint.

Clinical significance

Fractures of the dens are common in elderly patients after falls and in high-energy trauma; the "hangman's fracture" is a traumatic spondylolisthesis through the pars interarticularis of the axis, named for its association with judicial hanging. Instability of the atlanto-axial joint can endanger the spinal cord.
