The Battle of Boyacá, fought on 7 August 1819, was the decisive battle of the campaign of Simón Bolívar to liberate New Granada from Spanish rule. It secured the independence of Colombia and opened the road to Bogotá.

Background

After crossing the Andes at the Páramo de Pisba and fighting at Pantano de Vargas, Bolívar's army raced the royalist division of Colonel José María Barreiro toward Bogotá. Bolívar moved to cut him off at the bridge over the Teatinos River near Boyacá.

The battle

Patriot forces under Bolívar, Francisco de Paula Santander and José Antonio Anzoátegui engaged the royalists at the bridge. The royalist army was split in two and overwhelmed; Barreiro and over 1,600 men were captured.

Aftermath

The viceroy fled Bogotá, which Bolívar entered unopposed on 10 August. The victory led to the creation of the Republic of Gran Colombia later that year. 7 August is a national holiday in Colombia.
