Gamla stan

Gamla stan (Swedish for "The Old Town") is the old town of Stockholm, Sweden. It consists primarily of the island Stadsholmen, together with the islets Riddarholmen, Helgeandsholmen and Strömsborg. Dating from the 13th century, it is one of the largest and best-preserved medieval city centers in Europe, characterized by narrow cobbled streets and ochre-colored buildings in shades of gold.

Landmarks

Gamla stan contains Stockholm Palace (the Royal Palace), Stockholm Cathedral (Storkyrkan), the Nobel Prize Museum, Riddarholm Church — the burial church of Swedish monarchs — and the Swedish Parliament buildings on Helgeandsholmen. Stortorget, the large square at its center, was the site of the Stockholm Bloodbath of 1520. The main thoroughfare, Västerlånggatan, and the narrow Mårten Trotzigs gränd — the narrowest alley in Stockholm — are among its best-known streets.

History

Stockholm was founded around 1252, traditionally by Birger Jarl, at the strategic outlet of Lake Mälaren into the Baltic Sea. The medieval street plan of Gamla stan largely survives, although most buildings now date from the 17th and 18th centuries. Until the mid-19th century the old town was virtually synonymous with the city itself.
