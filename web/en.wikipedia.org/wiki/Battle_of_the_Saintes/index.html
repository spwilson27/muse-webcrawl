The Battle of the Saintes (French: Bataille de la Dominique) was a major naval battle of the American Revolutionary War, fought in the Caribbean between a British fleet under Admiral Sir George Rodney and a French fleet under the Comte de Grasse on 9–12 April 1782. It is famous for Rodney's manoeuvre of "breaking the line", which cut the French formation in two places and led to the capture of five French ships of the line, including de Grasse's flagship Ville de Paris.

Background

De Grasse sailed from Martinique intending to join Spanish forces for an attack on the rich British sugar colony of Jamaica. Rodney, forewarned, pursued from St Lucia. A partial action on 9 April damaged several ships on both sides; after two days of repairs and manoeuvring, the fleets met again on 12 April in the channel between Dominica and the Îles des Saintes.

The battle

As the two lines exchanged fire on opposite tacks, a shift of wind created gaps in the French line. Rodney's flagship Formidable and several following ships passed through one gap while another division broke through further ahead, throwing the French formation into confusion and allowing the British to engage from windward with devastating effect. By evening the Ville de Paris, after a heroic defence, struck her colours; de Grasse was taken prisoner. Four other French ships were captured the same day, and two more were taken by Hood's division at the Mona Passage on 19 April.

Significance

The victory saved Jamaica, restored British naval prestige after the loss of America, and strengthened Britain's hand in the peace negotiations of 1783. "Breaking the line" — hitherto an occasional improvisation — entered British naval doctrine and foreshadowed the tactics Nelson used at Trafalgar.
