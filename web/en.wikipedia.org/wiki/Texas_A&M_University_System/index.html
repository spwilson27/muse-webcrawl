The Texas A&M University System is a state university system in Texas and one of the largest systems of higher education in the United States. Established in 1948, it is governed by a nine-member Board of Regents appointed by the Governor of Texas, and is led by a chancellor. Its flagship institution is Texas A&M University at College Station, founded in 1876 as the Agricultural and Mechanical College of Texas under the Morrill Land-Grant Acts.

Members

The system comprises eleven universities, including Texas A&M University (College Station), Prairie View A&M University, Tarleton State University, Texas A&M University–Corpus Christi, Texas A&M University–Kingsville, Texas A&M International University (Laredo), West Texas A&M University, Texas A&M University–Commerce, Texas A&M University–Texarkana, Texas A&M University–San Antonio, and Texas A&M University–Central Texas, together with a comprehensive health science center (Texas A&M Health) operating campuses across the state, and branch campuses including Texas A&M at Galveston and a campus in Qatar.

Agencies

Unusually among university systems, the Texas A&M System also administers state agencies with statewide service missions: the Texas A&M AgriLife Extension Service and AgriLife Research, the Texas A&M Engineering Experiment Station and Engineering Extension Service, the Texas A&M Forest Service, the Texas A&M Transportation Institute, the Texas Division of Emergency Management (transferred to the system in 2019), and the Texas A&M Veterinary Medical Diagnostic Laboratory.

Scale and research

The system enrolls more than 150,000 students and conducts over a billion dollars in annual research. It shares in the Permanent University Fund with the University of Texas System. The system is noted for its role in disaster response (Task Force 1 urban search and rescue), agricultural outreach in every Texas county, and engineering and national-security research, including the RELLIS Campus in Bryan.
