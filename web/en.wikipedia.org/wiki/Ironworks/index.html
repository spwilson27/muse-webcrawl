Ironworks

An ironworks or iron works is an industrial plant where iron is smelted and worked into products. Historically, an integrated ironworks combined a blast furnace producing pig iron with forges and fineries that converted it into wrought iron bars, and later puddling furnaces, rolling mills and steelmaking shops.

History

Early ironworking used bloomeries and charcoal-fuelled forges. The blast furnace, spreading through Europe in the late Middle Ages, enabled larger-scale cast iron production. In Britain, Abraham Darby's use of coke for smelting at Coalbrookdale from 1709 freed the industry from charcoal supplies and helped launch the Industrial Revolution; sites such as Blaenavon and Ironbridge became emblematic of the new iron industry.

Processes

A traditional ironworks received iron ore, coke or charcoal and limestone, smelted them in the blast furnace, and refined the pig iron in finery or puddling furnaces before hammering or rolling it into bars, rails, plates and structural shapes. Water power, then steam engines, drove bellows, hammers and mills.

Modern integrated steelworks perform the same sequence at far greater scale, often combined with steelmaking and continuous casting on one site.
