James Thomson (poet, born 1700)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

James Thomson, author of The Seasons

James Thomson (11 September 1700 – 27 August 1748) was a Scottish poet and playwright, best known for his long descriptive poem The Seasons and for the lyrics of "Rule, Britannia!", written for the masque Alfred (1740), on which he collaborated with David Mallet.

The Seasons

Published in stages between 1726 and 1730—beginning with Winter (1726), followed by Summer, Spring and Autumn—The Seasons is a long poem in blank verse celebrating nature through the turning year, mingling landscape description, Newtonian science and moral reflection. It was enormously influential, helping to shape the nature poetry of the later eighteenth century and the Romantic movement, and it popularised the personal, reflective mode in landscape verse.

Other works

Thomson also wrote the philosophical poem Liberty (1735–36), plays including Sophonisba and Tancred and Sigismunda, and The Castle of Indolence (1748), a Spenserian allegory published shortly before his death at Richmond, Surrey.
