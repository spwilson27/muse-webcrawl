Degrowth

Degrowth is an academic and social movement arguing that wealthy societies should equitably downscale production and consumption to reduce ecological impact and improve well-being, challenging GDP growth as the overriding policy goal.

Ideas

Rooted in ecological economics, Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen's entropy work, and the 1972 Limits to Growth report, degrowth advocates propose work-time reduction, universal basic services, caps on resource use, wealth redistribution, and relocalised economies. The French term decroissance was popularised in the 2000s by thinkers such as Serge Latouche.

Criticism

Critics argue degrowth would harm the poor, is politically unfeasible, and that green growth via decoupling and technology can reconcile growth with sustainability; supporters reply that absolute decoupling at scale is unproven.
