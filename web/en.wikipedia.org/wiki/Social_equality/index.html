Social equality

Social equality is a state of affairs in which all individuals within a specific society have equal status in certain respects, including civil rights, freedom of speech, property rights and equal access to certain social goods and services.

Concepts

Concepts include equality before the law, equal opportunity, and equality of outcome. Social equality requires the absence of legally enforced social class or caste boundaries and discrimination motivated by inalienable characteristics.

History

Ideals of equality appear in ancient philosophy and were radicalized in the Enlightenment, the American and French Revolutions, and later movements for abolition, suffrage, labour rights and decolonization.

Measurement and debate

Inequality is measured by instruments such as the Gini coefficient and studies of distribution of wealth. Debates concern how far equality should extend and its relationship to liberty and merit.
