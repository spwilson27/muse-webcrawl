Vienna Opera Ball

The Vienna Opera Ball (German: Wiener Opernball) is an annual Austrian society event which takes place in the building of the Vienna State Opera in Vienna, Austria on the Thursday preceding Ash Wednesday, corresponding to the start of Lent. Together with the New Year's concert, the ball is one of the highlights of the Viennese carnival season and a major event in Austrian social and cultural life.

History

The tradition of balls at the Vienna Court Opera dates to the 19th century; the first ball in the present opera house was held in 1877 as a "soirée". The Opera Ball in its modern form was first held in 1935. Interrupted during the Second World War, it resumed in 1956 and has since been held annually, with exceptions such as 1991 (during the Gulf War) and 2021–2022 (during the COVID-19 pandemic).

The event

For the ball, the opera auditorium is transformed: the seats are removed and a level dance floor is built over the stalls at stage height, creating one large ballroom. The evening opens with the entry of around 150 debutante couples — young women in white gowns and tiaras, young men in tailcoats — who perform the opening polonaise and waltz. The call "Alles Walzer!" ("Everyone waltz!") invites all guests to dance.

The ball draws around 5,000 guests including the President of Austria, government members, business figures, and international celebrities, and is broadcast live on Austrian television to a large audience. It is also regularly the target of demonstrations by left-wing groups protesting its opulence.
