Bass horn

The bass horn was an early nineteenth-century brass instrument designed to provide a bass voice in military bands. It was an upright form of the serpent, built of wood or metal with finger holes and a cupped mouthpiece, held vertically with the bell pointing upward.

History

Forms of the instrument were developed in France around the turn of the nineteenth century — including Frichot's "basse militaire" of about 1800 — to improve on the serpent's awkward horizontal playing position and uneven tone for marching bands. The bass horn enjoyed a period of popularity in British and French service bands before being superseded.

Replacement

The invention of valves in the 1810s and the subsequent development of the ophicleide and then the tuba (patented in 1835) provided louder, more reliable and more chromatic bass instruments, and the finger-holed bass horn fell out of use by the mid-nineteenth century. Surviving examples are found in instrument museums, and reproductions are occasionally played by early-music ensembles.
