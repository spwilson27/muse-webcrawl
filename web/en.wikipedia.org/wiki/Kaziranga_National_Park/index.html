Kaziranga National Park

An Indian rhinoceros grazing in the park's tall elephant grass

Kaziranga National Park is a national park in the Golaghat and Nagaon districts of Assam, India, on the south bank of the Brahmaputra River. The park hosts two-thirds of the world's Indian rhinoceroses and was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985. Its landscape of tall elephant grass, marshland and tropical moist broadleaf forests is criss-crossed by the Brahmaputra and its channels, and the park is also a designated tiger reserve with one of the highest densities of Bengal tigers in the world.

History

The reserve originated in 1905, when Mary Curzon, wife of the Viceroy of India, Lord Curzon, visited the area and, famously failing to spot a single rhinoceros, persuaded her husband to protect the dwindling species. The Kaziranga Proposed Reserve Forest was created in 1905, became a reserve forest in 1908, a game sanctuary in 1916, and was opened to visitors in 1938. It was renamed Kaziranga Wildlife Sanctuary in 1950 and declared a national park in 1974. In 2006 it was also declared a tiger reserve.

Fauna

Besides the Indian rhinoceros (about 2,600 animals in recent censuses), Kaziranga supports large populations of wild water buffalo, Asian elephants, swamp deer (barasingha) and wild boar, as well as tigers, leopards, sloth bears and the rare Bengal florican. The park is recognised by BirdLife International as an Important Bird Area, with migratory waterfowl joining resident species such as pelicans, storks and hornbills. The rivers harbour the endangered Ganges river dolphin.

Conservation and challenges

Strict anti-poaching enforcement has made Kaziranga a conservation success story for the rhinoceros, though poaching for horn remains a threat. The annual Brahmaputra floods are ecologically essential — renewing the grasslands — but periodically drown large numbers of animals and drive them toward the Karbi Anglong hills across a busy highway. Expansions of the park through official "additions" have gradually increased its area to roughly 850–1,000 square kilometres including buffer zones.
