Renato Dulbecco

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Dulbecco at the Salk Institute

Renato Dulbecco (February 22, 1914 – February 19, 2012) was an Italian-American virologist who won the 1975 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for his work on oncoviruses, which are viruses that can cause cancer when they infect animal cells. He studied at the University of Turin under Giuseppe Levi, along with fellow students Salvador Luria and Rita Levi-Montalcini, who also moved to the United States with him. In 1949 he moved to Caltech, where he developed methods for culturing animal viruses in cell cultures and quantifying them by plaque assay.

Work

Dulbecco's group showed that certain viruses (polyomavirus and SV40) integrate their DNA into the host cell's chromosomes, leading to transformation of the cell into a cancer cell. He shared the Nobel Prize with Howard Temin and David Baltimore for "discoveries concerning the interaction between tumour viruses and the genetic material of the cell". Dulbecco was also a pioneer of the modern approach to cancer research at the molecular level and was an early advocate of sequencing the human genome, promoting the approach while he was president of the Salk Institute for Biological Studies (1988–1992).

Life

Dulbecco was born in Catanzaro, Italy, and earned his M.D. from the University of Turin in 1936. Drafted into the Italian army during World War II, he later joined the Resistance. After the war, at the invitation of Salvador Luria, he emigrated to the United States in 1947, working first at Indiana University and then at Caltech. He became a U.S. citizen in 1953. In the 1960s he joined the newly founded Salk Institute in La Jolla, California, with interludes at the Imperial Cancer Research Fund in London. He died at La Jolla in 2012, three days before his 98th birthday.
