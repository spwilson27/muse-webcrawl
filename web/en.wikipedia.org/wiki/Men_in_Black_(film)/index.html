Men in Black is a 1997 American science fiction action comedy film directed by Barry Sonnenfeld, written by Ed Solomon, and loosely based on the comic book series The Men in Black by Lowell Cunningham and Sandy Carruthers. It stars Tommy Lee Jones as Agent K and Will Smith as James Edwards / Agent J, members of a secret organisation that monitors and polices extraterrestrial activity on Earth.

Plot

NYPD detective James Edwards is recruited by Agent K into the Men in Black (MIB), a non-government agency that regulates some 1,500 aliens living secretly on Earth, erasing witnesses' memories with a device called the neuralyzer. The new partners hunt "Edgar", a giant alien cockroach (Vincent D'Onofrio) who has murdered an alien prince and stolen "the Galaxy" — a vast energy source hidden on the collar of the prince's cat, Orion — threatening an alien war if it is not recovered. In the climax at the 1964 World's Fair grounds in Flushing Meadows, the agents defeat Edgar and recover the Galaxy, which proves to contain an entire miniature cosmos. K retires, has his memory wiped, and deputy chief medical examiner Laurel Weaver (Linda Fiorentino) becomes J's new partner, Agent L.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major commercial success, grossing $589.4 million worldwide, and was praised for its humour, performances and effects; Rick Baker and David LeRoy Anderson won the Academy Award for Best Makeup. Will Smith's title song "Men in Black" was an international hit. The film launched a franchise including Men in Black II (2002), Men in Black 3 (2012), the spin-off Men in Black: International (2019), and an animated television series.
