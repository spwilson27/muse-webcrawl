Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) is an American multinational semiconductor company headquartered in Santa Clara, California. It develops computer processors and related technologies for business and consumer markets, and is the principal competitor to Intel in x86 microprocessors and to Nvidia in graphics processors and AI accelerators.

History

AMD was founded in 1969 by Jerry Sanders and seven colleagues from Fairchild Semiconductor. It initially produced logic chips and second-source processors, later becoming a major x86 vendor. In 2006 it acquired graphics company ATI Technologies, gaining the Radeon line. In 2009 it spun off its manufacturing operations as GlobalFoundries, becoming a fabless designer. Under CEO Lisa Su (appointed 2014), AMD's Zen architecture (2017) restored its competitiveness: Ryzen processors for desktops and laptops, Epyc processors for servers, and Instinct accelerators for data centres and artificial intelligence. In 2022 AMD completed its acquisition of FPGA maker Xilinx, the largest deal in semiconductor history at the time.

Products

AMD's lines include Ryzen (consumer CPUs), Ryzen Threadripper (workstations), Epyc (servers), Radeon (consumer graphics), Radeon Instinct/Instinct (compute and AI accelerators), and adaptive SoCs and FPGAs from Xilinx. Its semi-custom chips power the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S consoles.
