Charles I (German: Karl I.; Hungarian: IV. Károly; 17 August 1887 – 1 April 1922) was the last Emperor of Austria, the last King of Hungary (as Charles IV), the last King of Croatia, and the last monarch belonging to the House of Habsburg-Lorraine to rule over Austria-Hungary. He reigned from November 1916, succeeding his great-uncle Franz Joseph I, until the collapse and dissolution of the empire in November 1918.

Reign

Charles became heir presumptive after the assassination of his uncle, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, in 1914. Ascending the throne in the middle of the First World War, he sought a negotiated peace, most notably through the secret Sixtus Affair of 1917, in which he used his brother-in-law Prince Sixtus of Bourbon-Parma to sound out France. The affair's exposure in 1918 damaged his credibility. At home he attempted federal reform of the monarchy, issued in the October 1918 manifesto, but events outpaced him: on 11–12 November 1918 he renounced participation in state affairs in Austria and Hungary, carefully avoiding the word "abdication".

Restoration attempts and death

In 1921 Charles twice attempted to regain the Hungarian throne; the second attempt ended in armed clashes and his arrest. The Allies exiled him to the island of Madeira, where he died of pneumonia in poverty on 1 April 1922, aged 34.

Veneration

Charles was beatified by Pope John Paul II in 2004, who praised his efforts for peace and his Christian conduct of office; his feast day is 21 October, the anniversary of his marriage to Zita.
