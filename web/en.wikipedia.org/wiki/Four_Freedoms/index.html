Four Freedoms

The Four Freedoms were goals articulated by U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt on Monday, January 6, 1941, in his State of the Union address, proposing four fundamental freedoms that people "everywhere in the world" ought to enjoy:

  1. Freedom of speech and expression
  2. Freedom of worship
  3. Freedom from want
  4. Freedom from fear

Context and influence

Delivered eleven months before the American entry into the Second World War, the address looked beyond the immediate conflict to a postwar world "founded on four essential human freedoms". The Four Freedoms influenced the Atlantic Charter later that year and were echoed in the preamble of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948), whose drafting Eleanor Roosevelt helped lead.

Cultural legacy

Artist Norman Rockwell immortalized the Four Freedoms in a series of four paintings published in The Saturday Evening Post in 1943; reproduced as posters, they helped sell war bonds and became enduring images of American ideals. The Four Freedoms are commemorated by the Four Freedoms Monument in Florida and by Four Freedoms Plaza and Park in New York, and by the annual Four Freedoms Awards presented by the Roosevelt Institute.
