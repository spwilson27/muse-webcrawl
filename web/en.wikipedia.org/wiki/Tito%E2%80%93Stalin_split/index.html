Tito–Stalin split


Date: 1948
Parties: Josip Broz Tito (Yugoslavia) vs Joseph Stalin (USSR)
Result: Yugoslavia expelled from Cominform; pursues independent socialism

The Tito–Stalin split was the rupture in 1948 between Yugoslav leader Josip Broz Tito and Soviet leader Joseph Stalin, which ended with Yugoslavia's expulsion from the Cominform and its emergence as the first communist state to break from Soviet control.

Causes

Yugoslavia's partisans had liberated much of the country largely by their own efforts, giving Tito unusual independence. Friction grew over Yugoslav ambitions in Albania and Greece, Soviet economic demands and Stalin's attempts to subordinate the Yugoslav party and army. In June 1948 the Cominform condemned the Yugoslav leadership, expecting Tito to fall.

Consequences

Tito instead purged pro-Soviet cadres, accepted Western economic aid and developed "self-management" socialism. Yugoslavia later co-founded the Non-Aligned Movement, charting a course between East and West for the rest of the Cold War.
