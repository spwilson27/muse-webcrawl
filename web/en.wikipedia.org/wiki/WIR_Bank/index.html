The WIR Bank, formerly the Swiss Economic Circle (German: Wirtschaftsring-Genossenschaft), or WIR, is an independent complementary currency system in Switzerland that serves businesses, especially small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). It exists in parallel with the Swiss franc and functions both as a currency (the WIR franc, CHW) and as a bank providing conventional Swiss-franc services. It is based in Basel.

As of 2023 the network comprised tens of thousands of member businesses, predominantly in the construction, hospitality, manufacturing, retail and professional-services sectors. Members trade with one another partly in WIR francs and partly in Swiss francs.

History

The WIR system was founded in 1934 by Werner Zimmermann, Paul Enz and thirteen other businessmen in response to the currency shortages and deflationary pressures of the Great Depression. The founders drew on the ideas of the German economist Silvio Gesell, particularly his theory of "free money" (Freigeld), which proposed a currency that would circulate readily rather than be hoarded during economic downturns.

The founders' original goal was to create a system whereby member businesses would extend mutual credit to one another, reducing their dependence on scarce conventional money. The WIR cooperative received a Swiss banking licence in 1936. In 1998 it adopted the name WIR Bank and expanded its conventional banking services, offering accounts, mortgages and payment services in Swiss francs alongside the WIR clearing system.

The WIR franc

The WIR franc (currency code CHW) is an electronic complementary currency with no physical banknotes. It is pegged at par to the Swiss franc but is not convertible into it; it can only be spent within the WIR network. Loans in WIR are typically offered at low interest rates, encouraging members to accept WIR in trade. Economic studies have found the system to be countercyclical: usage tends to increase when the general economy contracts, providing liquidity to member businesses when conventional credit tightens.
