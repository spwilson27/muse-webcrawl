Roger Hilton CBE (1911–1975) was a British painter associated with the St Ives group of artists in Cornwall. One of the leading British abstract painters of the post-war period, he won the John Moores Painting Prize in 1963 and represented Britain at the Venice Biennale, where his work was awarded the UNESCO prize.

Life and work

Hilton studied at the Slade School of Fine Art and in Paris. During the Second World War he served as a commando and was captured in the Dieppe Raid in 1942, spending the rest of the war as a prisoner. In the 1950s he moved toward abstraction influenced by the Dutch De Stijl painter Piet Mondrian, whom he visited shortly before Mondrian's death. Hilton settled in Cornwall in 1965. Confined largely to bed in his final, illness-marked years, he produced a remarkable series of exuberant gouaches. His work is held by Tate and other major collections.
