Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC; French: Immigration, Réfugiés et Citoyenneté Canada) is the department of the Government of Canada with responsibility for matters dealing with immigration to Canada, refugees, and Canadian citizenship. The department was established in 1994 as Citizenship and Immigration Canada and was given its current name in 2015.

Mandate

IRCC develops and implements policies and programs governing the arrival and settlement of immigrants and refugees, the granting of citizenship, and the issuance of travel documents such as Canadian passports (a function transferred to the department in 2013). It administers the Express Entry system for skilled economic immigration, family sponsorship programs, study and work permits, and Canada's refugee protection and resettlement programs, in cooperation with the provinces, the Immigration and Refugee Board, and the Canada Border Services Agency.

Organisation

The department is headed by the Minister of Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship, supported by a deputy minister and a network of processing offices across Canada and at Canadian missions abroad. IRCC works closely with Employment and Social Development Canada on labour market needs and with settlement agencies that deliver language training and integration services to newcomers.

History

Responsibility for immigration has moved among several departments since Confederation in 1867. The modern department traces its institutional history through Citizenship and Immigration Canada (1994–2015). In November 2015, under the government of Justin Trudeau, the department was renamed to reflect the addition of the refugee portfolio in its title.
