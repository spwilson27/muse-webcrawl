Architecture of Hong Kong

The architecture of Hong Kong is characterized by one of the world's densest concentrations of skyscrapers, shaped by scarce flat land, a mountainous terrain, and a large population. Hong Kong has more buildings over 150 metres tall than any other city in the world.

Notable buildings: Bank of China Tower, HSBC Main Building, International Commerce Centre, Two International Finance Centre
Features: High-rise residential towers, public housing estates, tong lau

History

Early building included traditional Chinese village architecture in the New Territories and colonial structures after Hong Kong became a British colony in 1841. Tong lau shophouses mixed Chinese and European influences. After the Second World War, mass public housing estates were built for a rapidly growing population, followed by ever-taller commercial towers in Central and beyond.

Landmarks and design

Signature towers include I. M. Pei's Bank of China Tower (1990) and Norman Foster's HSBC Main Building (1985). Feng shui considerations influence many designs. The International Commerce Centre in West Kowloon is the city's tallest building at 484 metres.
