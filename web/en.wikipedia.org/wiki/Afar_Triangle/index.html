Afar Triangle

Afar Triangle / Afar Depression
Location: Ethiopia, Eritrea, Djibouti
Feature: Triple junction of rift systems

The Afar Triangle (also called the Afar Depression) is a geological depression caused by the Afar Triple Junction, which is part of the Great Rift Valley in East Africa. The region is home to some of the earliest hominid fossils and to active volcanism, extreme heat in the Danakil Depression, and salt flats lying below sea level.

Geology

Here the Red Sea Rift, the Gulf of Aden Rift, and the East African Rift meet, and the African continent is slowly splitting. Extension, volcanism (including Erta Ale), hot springs, and earthquakes mark an incipient oceanic basin. Part of the home region of the Afar people.

Palaeontology

Localities such as Hadar yielded Australopithecus afarensis, including the fossil "Lucy" (AL 288-1) discovered in 1974, and some of the oldest known stone tools and early Homo remains.

Climate

The Danakil Depression is one of the hottest inhabited places on Earth, with average annual temperatures around 34 °C and very low rainfall.
