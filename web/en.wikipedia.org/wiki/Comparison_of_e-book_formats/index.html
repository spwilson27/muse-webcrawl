Comparison of e-book formats

The following is a comparison of e-book formats used to create and publish e-books. E-book formats are the various file formats used by e-readers and other devices to store and display digital books. Formats differ in their support for digital rights management (DRM), multimedia, annotations, and reflowable text.

Formats

EPUB

EPUB (electronic publication) is a free and open standard developed by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), succeeding the Open eBook standard. An EPUB file is essentially a ZIP archive containing XHTML documents, CSS stylesheets, images, and fonts. EPUB 3, the current major version, adds support for HTML5, audio and video overlays, fixed-layout documents, and MathML. It is supported by most e-readers except Amazon Kindle.

Kindle formats (AZW, KF8, KFX)

Amazon's Kindle devices use proprietary formats derived from the Mobipocket format. AZW is essentially Mobipocket with a different DRM scheme. KF8 (Kindle Format 8, .azw3) adds HTML5 and CSS3 support. KFX (Kindle Format 10) introduces enhanced typesetting with improved hyphenation and justification, and supports the newer Enhanced Typesetting features.

Mobipocket

The Mobipocket format (.mobi, .prc) was developed by the French company Mobipocket SA, acquired by Amazon in 2005. It is based on the Open eBook standard using XHTML. Amazon deprecated sending MOBI files to Kindle devices in favour of EPUB through its Send to Kindle service.

PDF

The Portable Document Format (PDF), developed by Adobe, preserves fixed layout and fonts, which makes it well suited for print-like documents but less flexible on small screens because the layout does not reflow. Most e-readers support PDF natively.

Others

Other formats include FictionBook (FB2), an XML-based format popular in Russia; DjVu, designed for scanned documents; comic book archives (CBZ/CBR) for image-based publications; plain text; and the discontinued Microsoft Reader (.lit) and PalmDOC formats.
