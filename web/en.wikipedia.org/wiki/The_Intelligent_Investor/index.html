The Intelligent Investor is a book on value investing by Benjamin Graham, first published in 1949. Widely hailed as the "bible of stock market investing," it is the best-known work of Graham, the mentor of Warren Buffett, who has called it "by far the best book on investing ever written."

Content

Graham lays out his philosophy of "investment versus speculation," teaching investors to analyse businesses rather than chase price movements. Key ideas include the allegory of "Mr. Market," the manic-depressive partner whose mood swings the investor should exploit rather than follow; the "margin of safety" as the central concept of sound investment; and distinct strategies for the "defensive" and "enterprising" investor. The last revised edition supervised by Graham appeared in 1973; later reprints append commentary by Jason Zweig and a foreword by Buffett.
