Benjamin "Bob" Clark (August 5, 1939 – April 4, 2007) was an American film director, screenwriter and producer. Working across genres, he directed both the pioneering slasher film Black Christmas (1974) and the beloved family film A Christmas Story (1983).

Career

Clark began in low-budget horror, making Children Shouldn't Play with Dead Things (1972) and Deathdream (1974) in Florida. Black Christmas is credited as a foundational influence on the slasher genre. He then had a huge commercial hit with the raunchy teen comedy Porky's (1981), one of the most successful Canadian films ever made. A Christmas Story, adapted from Jean Shepherd's writings, initially performed modestly but became a perennial holiday favourite, airing in annual 24-hour television marathons.

Death

Clark and his son Ariel were killed in 2007 when a drunk driver struck their car head-on in Pacific Palisades, California.
