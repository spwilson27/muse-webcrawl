Daresbury Laboratory is a scientific research laboratory based near Daresbury in Cheshire, England, operated by the Science and Technology Facilities Council (STFC). Founded in 1962 as a nuclear physics laboratory, it became internationally known for the Synchrotron Radiation Source (SRS), the world's first dedicated synchrotron light source, which operated from 1980 to 2008.

Facilities

The laboratory hosts advanced facilities in accelerator science, high-performance computing and detector technology. The ALICE accelerator test facility and the CLARA free-electron laser test facility support research into future particle accelerators. The site also houses the Hartree Centre for supercomputing and forms the core of the Sci-Tech Daresbury campus, a science and innovation park hosting many technology companies.

History

The laboratory began with the NINA electron synchrotron for nuclear physics. After NINA closed, the SRS brought researchers from across the world to use intense X-ray and ultraviolet light for materials and life sciences. When the SRS closed in 2008, British synchrotron research moved to the Diamond Light Source in Oxfordshire, while Daresbury refocused on accelerator R&D and computing.
