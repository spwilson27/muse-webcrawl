The Rangrim Mountains are a mountain range in North Korea that runs north to south through the centre of the northern part of the Korean Peninsula. They form the watershed between the western lowlands draining to the Yellow Sea and the eastern side draining to the Sea of Japan, and are sometimes described as the "spine" of North Korea.

Geography

The range extends for roughly 500 kilometres, from the Paektu/Changbai region near the Chinese border southward into Kangwon Province. Its highest summit is Wagal Peak (와갈봉), at about 2,260 metres. Branch ranges extend east and west from the main divide — including the Kangnam, Myohyang, and Chogyuryong ranges — and the country's major rivers, including sources of the Taedong and Chongchon, rise in its slopes. Much of the range is rugged, forested, and sparsely populated, with a cold continental climate and heavy winter snow.

Significance

Because the Rangrim chain and its offshoots dominate the terrain, transport in North Korea relies on a limited number of passes and coastal routes, and several important military and industrial sites are located in the sheltered valleys of the interior. The mountains also figure in Korean revolutionary mythology associated with guerrilla activity in the region during the anti-Japanese struggle.
