Salting in

Salting in is the phenomenon in which the solubility of a protein (or other macromolecule) in water increases at low concentrations of added salt. It is the opposite of salting out, in which high salt concentrations precipitate proteins from solution.

Mechanism

At low ionic strength, added ions shield the charged groups on a protein's surface, reducing electrostatic attractions between protein molecules that would otherwise cause aggregation. The result is greater solubility. As salt concentration rises further, the ions compete with the protein for water of hydration, and salting out takes over — an effect exploited in techniques such as ammonium sulfate precipitation.

Applications

Salting in is important in protein purification, formulation of biopharmaceuticals, and food science, where modest salt levels can keep proteins dispersed and functional. The effect depends on the identity of the ions involved, following trends related to the Hofmeister series.
