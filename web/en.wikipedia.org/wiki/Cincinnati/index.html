Cincinnati

Cincinnati (nicknamed the "Queen City") is a city in the U.S. state of Ohio, located on the north bank of the Ohio River opposite Kentucky. It is the third-largest city in Ohio, with a city population of about 309,000 and a metropolitan population of roughly 2.3 million.

History

Founded in 1788 as Losantiville, the settlement was renamed Cincinnati in 1790 by governor Arthur St. Clair in honour of the Society of the Cincinnati. In the 19th century it became a major centre of pork packing ("Porkopolis"), riverboat trade and German immigration, and was one of the first American cities to establish public transit and major league sports clubs.

Economy and culture

Cincinnati is home to Fortune 500 headquarters including Procter & Gamble and Kroger, and the universities of Cincinnati and Xavier. Cultural institutions include the Cincinnati Symphony Orchestra, Cincinnati Ballet, the National Underground Railroad Freedom Center, and the Cincinnati Zoo. Professional teams include the Bengals (NFL) and Reds (MLB), the latter the oldest professional baseball club in the United States (1869).
