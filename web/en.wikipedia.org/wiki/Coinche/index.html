Coinche

TypeTrick-taking, point-trick
Players4 (two partnerships)
Cards32 (French piquet deck)
DeckFrench-suited
OriginFrance
Related gamesBelote, Contrée, Jass

Coinche (also called belote coinchée) is a French trick-taking card game for four players in two partnerships, played with a 32-card deck. It is a member of the Jass family and a close relative of belote and belote contrée, from which it differs principally in its bidding: in coinche, players bid freely on the number of points they contract to make and the trump suit (or no-trumps / all-trumps), and a defensive player may "coincher" — double — a bid, which the bidding side may redouble ("surcoinche").

Play

Each player is dealt eight cards. Card values follow the belote pattern: in trump, the Jack (20 points) and Nine (14) dominate; in plain suits the Ace (11) and Ten (10) are the main scorers, for 162 points in all in the pack, plus 10 for the last trick (dix de der). The declaring side must reach its contract — usually at least 82 points plus any announced combinations — or the opponents score the whole hand. Belote (King and Queen of trumps held by one player) scores a bonus. Games are typically played to 1,000 points. The freedom of the auction and the coinche/surcoinche doubles give the game its bluffing and tactical character.
