The Demerara River is a river in eastern Guyana that rises in the central rainforests of the country and flows north for 346 kilometres (215 mi) until it reaches the Atlantic Ocean. Georgetown, Guyana's capital and largest port, is situated on the east bank of the river's mouth.

Geography

The river's estuary is spanned by the Demerara Harbour Bridge, a floating bridge near Georgetown that was, at its opening in 1978, one of the longest floating bridges in the world. The river's deep-water port handles exports of sugar, timber, bauxite, gold, and diamonds. Linden, Guyana's second-largest town and a center of bauxite mining, lies about 105 kilometres (65 mi) upriver on its east bank.

History

The Demerara gave its name to the Dutch colony of Demerara, which was founded in the 18th century and later united with Essequibo and Berbice to form British Guiana. The name is also associated with Demerara sugar, a type of raw cane sugar originating in the region, and with the Demerara rum industry. The river figured in the 1763 Berbice slave uprising's wider regional context and in the 1823 Demerara rebellion of enslaved people, a significant event in the movement toward emancipation in the British Empire.

Tributaries and features

The river is navigable by oceangoing vessels as far as Georgetown and by smaller craft considerably further inland. Its banks support mangrove and tropical forest habitats, and its muddy, brown waters reflect the sediments carried from the interior highlands.
