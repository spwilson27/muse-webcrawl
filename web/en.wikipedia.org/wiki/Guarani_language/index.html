Guarani language

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Guarani (Guarani: avañeʼẽ, "the people's language") is a South American language of the Tupian language family. It is one of the official languages of Paraguay, together with Spanish, and is spoken by the majority of the Paraguayan population — a rare case of an indigenous American language being widely spoken by non-indigenous people. Varieties of Guarani are also spoken in northeastern Argentina, southeastern Bolivia and southwestern Brazil.

History

Guarani was the language of the Guaraní people encountered by Spanish colonisers. Jesuit missionaries in the reductions standardised a written form, and Antonio Ruiz de Montoya's Tesoro de la lengua guaraní (1639) was among the first grammars of an American language. After independence Paraguay retained Guarani alongside Spanish; the 1992 constitution made it co-official, and it is taught in schools.

Phonology and grammar

Guarani has six oral vowels, each with a nasal counterpart, and a phonology notable for nasal harmony, in which nasality spreads through whole words. The language is agglutinative and strongly head-marking: verbs agree with both subject and object, and nouns carry suffixes for tense-like notions such as former state (-kue) and prospective state (-rã). Word order is typically subject–verb–object, though flexible.

Jopará and usage

Everyday Paraguayan speech often mixes Guarani with Spanish loanwords and code-switching, a variety known as jopará ("mixture"). Guarani is used in government, media, literature and popular music, and in 2011 Paraguay created a Guarani Language Academy (Avañeʼẽ Rerekuapavẽ) to regulate the language.
