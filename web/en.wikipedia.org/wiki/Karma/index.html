Karma (Sanskrit: 'action, deed') is the spiritual principle of cause and effect in Indian religions: the moral quality of a person's intentional actions shapes their future experiences and the circumstances of their rebirth within samsara, the cycle of birth, death and rebirth. Liberation from the cycle — moksha or nirvana — is the soteriological goal of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism, each interpreting karma's mechanics differently.

Interpretations

In Hindu thought karma operates under divine order and devotion can mitigate it; in Jainism karma is a fine material substance that literally sticks to the soul and is burnt off by asceticism; Buddhism, denying a permanent soul, treats karma as intention (cetana) whose ripening conditions rebirth without a transmigrating self. In the modern West 'karma' has entered popular speech as a loose idea of cosmic payback — a simplification scholars contrast with the traditions' subtler accounts.
