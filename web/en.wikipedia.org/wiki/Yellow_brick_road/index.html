Yellow brick road

OriginThe Wonderful Wizard of Oz (1900)
AuthorL. Frank Baum
DestinationEmerald City, Land of Oz

The yellow brick road is a fictional road in the Land of Oz in L. Frank Baum's novel The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (1900) and its adaptations. Dorothy Gale is instructed to follow the road of yellow brick from Munchkin Country to the Emerald City to seek the Wizard's help in returning home to Kansas.

In the story

Along the road Dorothy meets her travelling companions — the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, and the Cowardly Lion. In Baum's book there are in fact several yellow brick roads, only one of which leads to the Emerald City. The road became iconic through the 1939 MGM film The Wizard of Oz, in which Judy Garland's Dorothy follows it in a famous musical sequence.

Cultural references

The phrase has passed into general use as a metaphor for a path to success or fortune. Elton John's 1973 album Goodbye Yellow Brick Road is named for it. Several real streets have been proposed as Baum's inspiration, including a yellow-brick street in Peekskill, New York, Baum's hometown of Chittenango (which has installed yellow sidewalks), and a road in Holland, Michigan.
