Council of States

[Image: Council of States]

The Council of States (German: Ständerat, French: Conseil des États, Italian: Consiglio degli Stati) is the upper house of the Federal Assembly of Switzerland, representing the cantons. Together with the National Council, the lower house, it forms the Swiss federal legislature.

Composition

The Council of States has 46 members: two from each of the twenty cantons and one from each of the six former half-cantons (Obwalden, Nidwalden, Basel-Stadt, Basel-Landschaft, Appenzell Ausserrhoden and Appenzell Innerrhoden). Unlike the National Council, whose seats are distributed by population, each full canton has equal representation in the Council of States regardless of its population, in keeping with Switzerland's federal structure.

Elections

Members are elected for four-year terms. The method of election is determined by each canton; most use a majoritarian system, while Jura and Neuchâtel use proportional representation. Elections generally take place at the same time as those for the National Council.

Powers

The two chambers of the Federal Assembly have equal powers: legislation must pass both in identical form, and the chambers jointly elect the Federal Council, federal judges and, in wartime, the general of the Swiss Army. The Council of States is presided over by a President elected for a one-year term.
