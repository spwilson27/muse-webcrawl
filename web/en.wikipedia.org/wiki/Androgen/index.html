An androgen (from Greek andr-, "man") is any natural or synthetic steroid hormone that regulates the development and maintenance of male characteristics by binding to androgen receptors. The principal androgens are testosterone and its more potent metabolite dihydrotestosterone (DHT); weaker androgens include androstenedione and dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), secreted largely by the adrenal glands. Androgens are present and physiologically important in both sexes.

Functions

Androgens drive prenatal differentiation of the male reproductive tract and genitalia, and at puberty the development of secondary sexual characteristics: deepening of the voice, growth of facial and body hair, increased muscle mass and bone growth. They maintain spermatogenesis, libido and bone density in adults, and contribute to scalp hair loss in genetically susceptible individuals. In females, androgens are precursors for estrogen synthesis and influence libido, bone and muscle.

Production and regulation

Testosterone is produced mainly by the Leydig cells of the testes under the control of luteinising hormone from the pituitary, itself regulated by hypothalamic GnRH and negative feedback. In target tissues, the enzyme 5α-reductase converts testosterone to DHT; aromatase can convert it to estradiol. Sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) determines the free, active fraction in blood.

Medical aspects

Androgen deficiency (hypogonadism) may be treated with testosterone replacement. Excess androgen production causes conditions such as polycystic ovary syndrome in women. Antiandrogens and 5α-reductase inhibitors are used in prostate cancer, benign prostatic hyperplasia and androgenetic alopecia. Synthetic anabolic-androgenic steroids are misused in sport for their muscle-building effects, with significant health risks.
