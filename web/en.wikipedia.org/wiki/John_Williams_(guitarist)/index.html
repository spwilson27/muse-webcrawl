John Christopher Williams (born April 24, 1941) is an Australian classical guitarist renowned for his ensemble playing and for his interpretation of the standard classical guitar repertoire. A student of Andrés Segovia, he has been one of the most recorded and influential guitarists of his generation.

Early life and training

Williams was born in Melbourne; his father, Len Williams, a jazz guitarist, founded the London Guitar School, and the family moved to London in 1952. The young Williams's talent brought him to the attention of Segovia, under whom he studied in Siena, and he made his professional debut at the Wigmore Hall in 1958.

Career

His repertoire ranges from Bach transcriptions and Barrios to contemporary concertos, including works written for him by André Previn and Stephen Dodgson. His recording of Stanley Myers's "Cavatina" became famous as the theme of the film The Deer Hunter (1978). Beyond the classical world he performed duets with Julian Bream, played with the fusion group Sky, and collaborated with musicians from many traditions. He was appointed OBE in 1980.
