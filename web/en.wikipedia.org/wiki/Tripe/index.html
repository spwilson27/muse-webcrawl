Tripe

Tripe is a type of edible lining of the stomach of various farm animals, most commonly cattle. Beef tripe is made from the first three chambers of a cow's stomach: the rumen (blanket or flat tripe), the reticulum (honeycomb tripe, generally considered the best), and the omasum (book or bible tripe). The abomasum, the fourth chamber, is less commonly eaten.

Preparation

Tripe requires thorough cleaning and long, slow cooking — often several hours of simmering — to become tender. It is sold fresh, pickled, or canned in some regions. Because it readily absorbs the flavours of the liquids and spices it is cooked with, it features in strongly seasoned dishes around the world.

Dishes

Famous tripe dishes include Italian trippa alla fiorentina and lampredotto, French tripes à la mode de Caen and andouille sausage, Spanish callos a la madrileña, Polish flaki, Turkish işkembe çorbası, Mexican menudo, Vietnamese phở with tripe, and Filipino kare-kare. In northern England, tripe was traditionally dressed with vinegar and eaten cold; the town of Bury was a noted centre of the trade.

Nutrition

Tripe is low in fat and a good source of protein, vitamin B12, selenium, and zinc, though it is moderately high in cholesterol.
