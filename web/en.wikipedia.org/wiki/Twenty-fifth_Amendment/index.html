Twenty-fifth Amendment to the United States Constitution

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Twenty-fifth Amendment (Amendment XXV) to the United States Constitution deals with presidential succession and disability. It clarifies that the vice president becomes president if the president dies, resigns, or is removed from office, and establishes how a vacancy in the office of the vice president can be filled. It also provides for the temporary transfer of the president's powers and duties to the vice president, either on the initiative of the president alone or on the initiative of the vice president together with a majority of the president's cabinet. It was adopted on February 10, 1967.

Sections

Section 1 provides that in the case of the removal of the President from office or of his death or resignation, the Vice President shall become President — resolving the ambiguity left by the original text, under which it had been debated whether the vice president merely acted as president.

Section 2 provides that whenever there is a vacancy in the office of the Vice President, the President shall nominate a Vice President who shall take office upon confirmation by a majority vote of both Houses of Congress.

Section 3 allows the President to voluntarily transfer power by informing Congress in writing that he is unable to discharge the powers and duties of the office, remaining in office but with the Vice President as Acting President until the President declares himself able to resume.

Section 4 allows the Vice President, together with a majority of the principal officers of the executive departments (or of such other body as Congress may provide), to declare the President unable to serve. The President may dispute the declaration, in which case Congress decides, requiring a two-thirds vote of both Houses to keep the Vice President as Acting President.

History and invocations

The amendment was spurred by the assassination of President John F. Kennedy in 1963, which exposed the lack of a clear procedure for presidential disability and left the vice presidency vacant for over a year. Section 2 was used twice in quick succession: in 1973, when Gerald Ford was nominated to replace Spiro Agnew, and in 1974, when Nelson Rockefeller replaced Ford, who had become President upon Richard Nixon's resignation. Section 3 has been invoked by Presidents Ronald Reagan (1985), George W. Bush (2002 and 2007) and Joe Biden (2021), each briefly transferring power during medical procedures. Section 4 has never been used, though it was discussed after the assassination attempt on Reagan in 1981 and during Donald Trump's presidency.
