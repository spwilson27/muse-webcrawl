Roncesvalles

Roncesvalles (Basque: Orreaga; French: Roncevaux) is a small village and municipality in Navarre, northern Spain, in the Pyrenees close to the French border. It is famous as the traditional site of the Battle of Roncesvalles (778), in which the rearguard of Charlemagne's army — including the knight Roland — was ambushed and destroyed, an event immortalised in the epic poem The Song of Roland.

History and pilgrimage

The pass of Roncesvalles (Ibañeta) has been a principal crossing of the Pyrenees since Roman times. A hospital and collegiate church for pilgrims were founded in the 12th century; the Royal Collegiate Church of Santa María, in early Gothic style, still stands. Today Roncesvalles is the classic starting point in Spain for the French Way (Camino Francés) of the Camino de Santiago, some 790 km from Santiago de Compostela, and its pilgrim hostel receives tens of thousands of walkers each year.

Geography

The municipality lies at about 900 metres above sea level in a high valley of beech forest, near the sources of the River Arga. Its population is only a few dozen residents.
