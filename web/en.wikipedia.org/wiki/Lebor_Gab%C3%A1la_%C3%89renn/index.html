Lebor Gabála Érenn (Modern Irish spelling: Leabhar Gabhála Éireann), known in English as The Book of Invasions or The Book of the Taking of Ireland, is a collection of poems and prose narratives in the Irish language intended to be a history of Ireland and the Irish from the creation of the world to the Middle Ages. There are a number of versions, the earliest of which was compiled by an anonymous writer in the 11th century.

Contents

The work traces the supposed origins of the Irish people back to Noah and the biblical Flood, and recounts a series of successive invasions or "takings" of Ireland: the people of Cessair, the Partholonians, the Nemedians, the Fir Bolg, the Tuatha Dé Danann, and finally the Milesians, the ancestors of the Gaels, who arrive from Iberia under the leadership of the sons of Míl Espáine.

Origins and purpose

Modern scholars regard the Lebor Gabála as pseudo-history: an attempt by medieval Irish scholars to provide their people with a written history comparable to those of the Israelites or Romans, weaving native myth and legend into a Christian biblical framework. It nonetheless remained influential for centuries, shaping Irish historical writing well into the 17th century, including Geoffrey Keating's Foras Feasa ar Éirinn.

Manuscripts

The text survives in several recensions preserved in manuscripts such as the Book of Leinster (c. 1160). R. A. S. Macalister produced the standard modern edition and translation for the Irish Texts Society (1938–1956).
