Summerhill School is an independent boarding school in Leiston, Suffolk, England, founded in 1921 by the Scottish educator Alexander Sutherland Neill (A. S. Neill). It is the world's best-known "democratic school": lessons are optional, and the school is governed by a weekly meeting in which every pupil and staff member has an equal vote. Summerhill has operated continuously for over a century and became internationally famous through Neill's 1960 book Summerhill: A Radical Approach to Child Rearing.

Philosophy

Neill's guiding conviction was that a child is innately wise and realistic, and that education should fit the child rather than forcing the child to fit the school. He held that children learn best when free from adult coercion: classes are offered, but attendance is voluntary, and many pupils spend their early years at play before choosing to study intensively when they wish to qualify for examinations. Neill distinguished freedom from licence — freedom ends where it infringes on others — and the school's communal rules are made and enforced by the community itself.

The School Meeting

Summerhill's distinctive institution is its general meeting, held weekly, where rules governing communal life are proposed, debated, and voted on. Each person present — child or adult, including the principal — has exactly one vote. The meeting elects committees and officials, hears complaints, and can impose fines or sanctions. Neill regarded this self-government as the heart of the school: children learn democracy by practising it.

History and inspection controversies

Neill's experiment began in Germany and Austria in 1921 before settling at Leiston in 1927 (with a wartime evacuation to Wales). Neill ran the school until his death in 1973; his wife Ena and then his daughter Zoë Readhead continued it. In the 1990s the school fought a famous battle with the British government: after a critical Ofsted report in 1999 demanded, among other things, that lessons cease to be voluntary, Summerhill appealed to an independent tribunal and won in 2000, an outcome widely reported as a landmark for alternative education. Subsequent inspections have been more favourable, and Summerhill celebrated its centenary in 2021.

Influence

Summerhill inspired democratic and free schools worldwide, from Sudbury Valley School in the United States to dozens of democratic schools in Europe, Israel, and Japan, and remains the archetype against which child-centred education is debated.
