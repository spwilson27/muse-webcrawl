Larisa Latynina

Larisa Semyonovna Latynina (Ukrainian: Лариса Семенівна Латиніна; born 27 December 1934) is a Soviet former artistic gymnast. Competing between 1956 and 1964, she won 18 Olympic medals — nine gold, five silver, and four bronze — a record total for any athlete that stood for 48 years until surpassed by American swimmer Michael Phelps in 2012. She remains the most decorated female Olympian of all time and holds the record for the most individual Olympic gold medals by a woman (nine, shared counting her team titles differently; she won five individual golds). At the World Championships she won 14 medals, including nine titles.

Career

Born in Kherson, Latynina first studied ballet before turning to gymnastics. She made her international debut at the 1954 World Championships in Rome, winning team gold. At the 1956 Melbourne Olympics, aged 21, she won the all-around title, vault, and floor exercise golds, plus team gold, in a celebrated duel with Hungary's Ágnes Keleti. At the 1958 World Championships in Moscow she won five of six titles while four months pregnant. She retained her Olympic all-around crown at Rome 1960 and led the Soviet team to gold at Tokyo 1964, where — now 29 — she added floor gold and finished second in the all-around to Věra Čáslavská. Latynina's elegance and consistency defined the Soviet school: she won the Soviet all-around championship repeatedly and never missed a podium in the all-around at a Worlds or Olympics during her prime.

Later life

After retiring in 1966, Latynina coached the Soviet national gymnastics team from 1967 to 1977, overseeing its dominance at three Olympics, and helped organise gymnastics at the 1980 Moscow Olympics. She was inducted into the International Gymnastics Hall of Fame in 1998 and received the Olympic Order. Her medal record — 18 Olympic and 14 World Championship medals, 32 in total at the two events — made her a standard for sporting longevity; Phelps broke her Olympic total record at London 2012, after which Latynina graciously posed with him.
