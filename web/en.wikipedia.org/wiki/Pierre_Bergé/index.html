Pierre Vital Georges Bergé (14 November 1930 – 8 September 2017) was a French industrialist, businessman, and arts patron. He is best known as the co-founder, with designer Yves Saint Laurent, of the Yves Saint Laurent fashion house, and as Saint Laurent's long-time business and romantic partner.

Early life

Bergé was born on the Île d'Oléron, off the Atlantic coast of France, the son of a tax official and an amateur operatic singer. He was educated in La Rochelle and moved to Paris as a young man, where he frequented the intellectual and artistic circles of the Left Bank, meeting figures such as Jean Cocteau and Albert Camus. In 1958 he met the young designer Yves Saint Laurent, who had recently taken over the house of Dior.

Yves Saint Laurent

After Saint Laurent was dismissed from Dior in 1960, Bergé helped him establish his own couture house, founded in 1961 with backing from American businessman J. Mack Robinson. Bergé ran the business side of the enterprise for four decades, building it into one of the world's leading luxury brands, encompassing ready-to-wear under the Rive Gauche label, perfumes, and licensing. He famously remarked that while Saint Laurent was born with a nervous breakdown, he himself supplied the discipline that allowed the house to function. The company was sold to the Gucci Group (now Kering) in 1999.

Art collection

Together Bergé and Saint Laurent assembled one of the great private art collections of the 20th century, housed in their Paris apartment on the Rue de Babylone and at the Château Gabriel in Normandy. Following Saint Laurent's death in 2008, Bergé consigned the collection to auction at Christie's in February 2009 — the so-called "sale of the century" — which realised some €374 million, with proceeds benefiting medical research and the battle against AIDS.

Cultural patronage and politics

Bergé was a major figure in French cultural life. He financed the magazine Globe, supported the politician François Mitterrand, co-founded the magazine Têtu, and in 2010 became a co-owner of the newspaper Le Monde. He served as president of the Opéra Bastille and supported numerous theatres, museums, and the restoration of historic gardens. A lifelong campaigner on gay rights and AIDS awareness, he co-founded the association Sidaction.

Later life and death

Bergé oversaw the creation of the Fondation Pierre Bergé – Yves Saint Laurent, which preserves the designer's archive, and the opening of museums dedicated to Saint Laurent in Paris and Marrakech, both inaugurated shortly after his death. He died on 8 September 2017 at Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, aged 86.
