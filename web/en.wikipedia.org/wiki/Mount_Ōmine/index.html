Mount Ōmine (大峯山, Ōmine-san) is a sacred mountain in the village of Tenkawa, Nara Prefecture, Japan. Its summit, Mount Sanjō (1,719 m), is the principal training site of Shugendō, the syncretic mountain religion established by the semi-legendary ascetic En no Gyōja in the late 7th century. In many sources the whole ridge between Yoshino and Kumano, the Ōmine Okugakemichi, is called Mount Ōmine.

Religious history

Tradition holds that En no Gyōja, after meditating on nearby Mount Yoshino, carved images of Zaō Gongen — the fierce guardian deity who became the central figure of Shugendō — and enshrined them on Mount Ōmine. The temple Ōminesan-ji on the summit administers the mountain to this day. In the medieval period Ōmine was linked with Yoshino to the north and the Kumano shrines to the south by the Okugakemichi, an arduous "inner path" of some 170 km whose 75 sacred stations (nabiki) ascetics traverse in the rite of passage called shōjin.

Ascetic practices

During the climbing season (3 May to late September) yamabushi arriving from the Dorogawa trailhead undergo ordeals on the mountain, most famously the Nishi no Nozoki, in which a practitioner is suspended head-down over a precipice by a rope while confessing his faults, and the ascent of iron chains and ladders on near-vertical rock at the summit approach. These trials symbolise passage through death and rebirth into the womb of the Buddha.

Women's exclusion

Mount Ōmine preserves the nyonin kinsei prohibition on women entering beyond its gates, a tradition once widespread on Japanese sacred mountains and defended by Ōminesan-ji as religious doctrine. It was the only such site retained at the time of the 2004 World Heritage listing of the "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range," a point of ongoing public discussion in Japan.

Access

The most-used trailhead is the hot-spring village of Dorogawa in Tenkawa, reached by bus from Shimoichiguchi Station on the Kintetsu Yoshino Line. The ascent to the summit takes about three hours. The massif also forms the northern anchor of the Ōmine Okugakemichi trail south to Kumano Hongū Taisha.
