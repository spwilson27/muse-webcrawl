The Lockheed Corporation was an American aerospace manufacturer founded in 1926. Lockheed became prominent during the Second World War and the Cold War for military aircraft such as the P-38 Lightning, the U-2 spy plane and the SR-71 Blackbird, and for the airliners of the Constellation series. In 1995 it merged with Martin Marietta to form Lockheed Martin.

History

Brothers Allan and Malcolm Loughead founded the Loughead Aircraft Manufacturing Company in 1916 and, after its failure, re-founded the business in Hollywood in 1926 as the Lockheed Aircraft Corporation — using the phonetic spelling of their surname. Detroid Aircraft acquired the company in 1929; after the parent company's collapse in the Depression, Robert E. Gross led a syndicate that bought Lockheed in 1932 for $40,000, moving it to Burbank.

Notable aircraft

  • Vega (1927) — record-setting monoplane flown by Amelia Earhart and Wiley Post
  • P-38 Lightning (1939) — twin-boom fighter of the Second World War
  • Constellation (1943) — pressurised propeller airliner series
  • P-80 Shooting Star (1944) — first US operational jet fighter
  • U-2 (1955) — high-altitude reconnaissance aircraft, designed by the secretive "Skunk Works" under Kelly Johnson
  • C-130 Hercules (1954) — military transport still in production
  • SR-71 Blackbird (1964) — Mach 3+ strategic reconnaissance aircraft
  • F-117 Nighthawk (1981) — first operational stealth aircraft
  • L-1011 TriStar (1970) — wide-body airliner

Skunk Works

Lockheed's Advanced Development Programs division, nicknamed the Skunk Works, developed many of the company's most famous aircraft in secrecy, including the U-2, SR-71 and F-117.

Merger

Following the post-Cold War consolidation of the US defence industry, Lockheed and Martin Marietta announced their "merger of equals" in August 1994. The combined company, Lockheed Martin, began operations on 15 March 1995.
