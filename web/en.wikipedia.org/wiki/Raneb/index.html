Raneb (also read Nebre) was an ancient Egyptian king (pharaoh) of the Second Dynasty, reigning around 2880 BC. He is known from seal impressions and stone vessels bearing his Horus name, and from king lists where he appears as the second ruler of the dynasty.

Attestations

Raneb's Horus name appears on seal impressions from the entrance galleries of the tomb complexes at Saqqara, including galleries beneath the Step Pyramid of Djoser, and on stone vessels. His name is preserved in the Abydos King List (as "Kakau") and the Turin King List.

Name and identity

The Horus name Rꜥ-nb ("Ra is lord" / "Lord of the sun") is one of the earliest royal names to incorporate the sun god Ra, a significant development in Egyptian royal ideology. Egyptologists debate whether Raneb and Nebre are variant readings of the same name or distinct kings; the prevailing view treats them as one ruler.

Reign

Manetho credits "Kaiechos" (Raneb) with a reign of 39 years, though the archaeological record suggests a shorter rule. Little is known of events during his reign; his tomb has not been identified with certainty.
