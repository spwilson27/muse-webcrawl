Shot (pellet)

Shot is a collective term for small round pellets, typically made of lead, steel, or other metals, that serve as the projectiles of a shotgun. A shotgun shell typically contains many individual shot pellets, which spread out after leaving the barrel to form a pattern, making shotguns effective against small, fast-moving targets such as birds in flight.

Sizes

Shot sizes are designated by numbers and letters, with larger numbers indicating smaller pellets. Birdshot ranges from small sizes such as No. 9 (about 2.0 mm diameter), used for clay targets and small birds, up to larger sizes for waterfowl and turkeys. Buckshot, designated with "0", "00" ("double-aught") and similar sizes, consists of much larger pellets (roughly 8–9 mm) used for hunting large game and for defensive applications. Shotgun slugs, by contrast, are a single solid projectile.

Materials

Traditional shot is made of lead, sometimes hardened with antimony and plated with copper or nickel. Because spent lead shot accumulating in wetlands poisoned waterfowl that ingested it, many jurisdictions — including the United States since 1991 for waterfowl hunting — require non-toxic shot, made of steel, bismuth, tungsten alloys, or tin. Steel is lighter and harder than lead, behaves differently in flight and in older barrels, and generally requires larger pellet sizes for equivalent performance.

Manufacture

Lead shot was historically produced by dropping molten lead from the top of a shot tower, allowing surface tension to form spheres that solidified during the fall into water below. Modern shot is also made by the Bleimeister process and by machining or rolling.
