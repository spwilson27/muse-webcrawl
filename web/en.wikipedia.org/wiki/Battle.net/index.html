Battle.net

Battle.net is an Internet-based online gaming, social networking, and digital distribution platform developed by Blizzard Entertainment. Launched on 30 December 1996 with the release of Diablo, it was among the first online gaming services incorporated directly into the games it serves, and it remains the platform for Blizzard titles such as World of Warcraft, StarCraft, Overwatch, and Hearthstone, as well as some Activision games.

History

Battle.net was revolutionary for being free and integrated into the game client, in contrast to contemporary per-hour online services; players simply connected to chat channels and joined games, initially with client-to-client hosting. It powered the multiplayer communities of Diablo, Warcraft II: Battle.net Edition, and StarCraft (1998), whose Korean esports boom ran largely through the service.

Battle.net 2.0

A reworked platform launched with World of Warcraft integration (2009) and StarCraft II (2010), adding unified accounts (BattleTags), cross-game friends lists and chat, achievements, and a storefront. A desktop launcher followed, consolidating all Blizzard games. In 2016 Blizzard announced renaming the service to "Blizzard Battle.net", but reverted to the Battle.net name in 2017. The platform has also hosted Activision titles such as Call of Duty games and Destiny 2 (2017–2020).
