Soul conjecture

The soul conjecture is a theorem in Riemannian geometry, conjectured by Jeff Cheeger and Detlef Gromoll in 1972 and proved by Grigori Perelman in 1994. It concerns complete, noncompact Riemannian manifolds of nonnegative sectional curvature.

Background

The soul theorem of Cheeger and Gromoll states that any such manifold contains a compact, totally geodesic submanifold called a soul, and that the whole manifold is diffeomorphic to the normal bundle over its soul. The soul conjecture sharpened this: if the curvature is strictly positive at even one point, then the soul must be a single point, and hence the manifold is diffeomorphic to Euclidean space.

Proof

Perelman proved the conjecture in a short paper in 1994, using ideas that later featured in his celebrated work on the geometrization and Poincaré conjectures. The result completed a major chapter in the study of open manifolds of nonnegative curvature.
