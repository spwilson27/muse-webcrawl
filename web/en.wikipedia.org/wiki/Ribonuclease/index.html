Ribonuclease (commonly abbreviated RNase) is a type of nuclease enzyme that catalyses the degradation of RNA into smaller components. Ribonucleases can be divided into endoribonucleases, which cleave internal phosphodiester bonds, and exoribonucleases, which remove nucleotides from the ends of RNA molecules. They are ubiquitous in living organisms and play essential roles in RNA maturation, turnover, defence against RNA viruses, and nutrient scavenging.

Ribonuclease A

Bovine pancreatic ribonuclease A is one of the most intensively studied enzymes in biochemistry. A small, robust protein of 124 amino-acid residues stabilised by four disulfide bonds, it cleaves single-stranded RNA after pyrimidine (C and U) residues via a 2′,3′-cyclic phosphate intermediate. RNase A was the first enzyme and the third protein whose amino-acid sequence was determined, and Christian Anfinsen's experiments on its oxidative refolding—which showed that sequence alone determines three-dimensional structure—earned a share of the 1972 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Stanford Moore and William Stein shared the same prize for work on the enzyme's active site.

Major families

  • RNase A superfamily — secreted vertebrate enzymes, including angiogenin and eosinophil-derived neurotoxin.
  • RNase H — degrades the RNA strand of RNA–DNA hybrids; essential to retroviral reverse transcription and a drug target.
  • RNase III (e.g. Dicer and Drosha) — double-stranded-RNA-specific enzymes central to RNA interference and microRNA processing.
  • RNase P — a ribozyme that matures tRNA, a landmark discovery of catalytic RNA.
  • RNase T1 and other microbial ribonucleases used in sequencing and structural studies.

Biological roles and laboratory importance

Cells regulate RNases carefully, and cytosolic RNase inhibitors bind RNase A–family enzymes with extraordinary affinity. In the laboratory, RNases are notorious contaminants: their stability (they survive boiling and refold) makes protecting experimental RNA difficult, so RNase-free technique and inhibitors are standard in molecular biology. Conversely, purified RNases (RNase A, T1, DNase-free RNase cocktails) are used deliberately to remove RNA from DNA preparations and in RNA mapping.

Medical relevance

Certain ribonucleases are cytotoxic and have been investigated as anticancer agents; ranpirnase (Onconase), an RNase from the leopard frog, was trialled against mesothelioma. RNase H activity is targeted in antisense ("gapmer") drug design, and engineered RNases figure in antiviral strategies.
