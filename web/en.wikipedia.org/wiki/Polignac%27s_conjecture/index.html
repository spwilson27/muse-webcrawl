Polignac's conjecture

Polignac's conjecture, proposed by Alphonse de Polignac in 1849, states that for every positive even integer k, there are infinitely many pairs of primes (p, p + k) whose difference is k. Such prime pairs are sometimes called cousin primes (for k = 4), sexy primes (for k = 6), and so on.

Statement

The case k = 2 is the twin prime conjecture. Polignac's conjecture generalises it to all even gaps. The conjecture also includes a quantitative form, related to the Hardy–Littlewood first conjecture, predicting the asymptotic density of prime pairs with a given gap.

Status

The conjecture remains unproven for every value of k. However, work by Yitang Zhang in 2013, and subsequent improvements by the Polymath project, proved that there are infinitely many prime pairs with a gap of at most 246, which implies the conjecture holds for at least one even gap not exceeding 246.
