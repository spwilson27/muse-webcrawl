Yupik languages

The Yupik languages are a family of languages spoken by the Yupik peoples of western and south-central Alaska and the Russian Far East (Chukotka). They belong to the Eskimo branch of the Eskimo–Aleut language family, and are closely related to, but distinct from, the Inuit languages spoken further north and east.

Classification

The Yupik family is generally divided into:

  • Central Alaskan Yup'ik — by far the largest, with roughly 10,000 speakers; one of the most widely spoken Indigenous languages in Alaska, with dialects including General Central Yup'ik, Norton Sound, Hooper Bay–Chevak, Nunivak, and Egegik.
  • Alutiiq (Pacific Gulf Yupik / Sugpiaq) — spoken on the Alaska Peninsula and Prince William Sound, with a few hundred speakers.
  • Central Siberian Yupik (St. Lawrence Island Yupik) — spoken on St. Lawrence Island and in Chukotka, with around a thousand speakers on each side of the Bering Strait.
  • Naukan Yupik — critically endangered, spoken near the Bering Strait in Russia.
  • Sirenik — extinct since 1997, sometimes classified as a separate branch.

Linguistic features

Like other Eskimo–Aleut languages, the Yupik languages are highly polysynthetic and agglutinative: complex words are built from a root followed by many derivational suffixes ("postbases") and inflectional endings, so that a single word can express the meaning of an English sentence. The languages are ergative–absolutive in alignment and have dual as well as singular and plural number. Central Alaskan Yup'ik uses a writing system developed by Moravian missionaries and refined by linguists at the University of Alaska Fairbanks, while Siberian varieties are written in Cyrillic.

Status and revitalisation

All Yupik languages are endangered to some degree, but Central Alaskan Yup'ik retains relatively strong intergenerational transmission in some villages and is taught in immersion and bilingual programmes. Since 1972, Yup'ik has been an official language of Alaska (alongside English and the state's other Indigenous languages).
