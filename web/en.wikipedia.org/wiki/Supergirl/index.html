Supergirl

Supergirl is the name of several superheroines appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. The best-known incarnation is Kara Zor-El, the cousin of Superman, who shares his Kryptonian origin, powers, and costume motifs. Created by writer Otto Binder and artist Al Plastino, Kara Zor-El debuted in Action Comics #252 (May 1959).

Fictional character biography

Kara Zor-El was born on Argo City, a fragment of Krypton that survived the planet's destruction. When Argo City itself was doomed, her parents sent the teenage Kara to Earth in a rocket, where she was raised in secret by her cousin Kal-El (Superman) and developed the same powers under a yellow sun: flight, superhuman strength, speed, invulnerability, heat vision, and super-hearing. Adopting the identity of Linda Lee Danvers, she operated first as Superman's "secret weapon" before being revealed to the world.

Death and return

In the 1985–86 series Crisis on Infinite Earths, Supergirl died heroically battling the Anti-Monitor, and the character was removed from continuity as DC streamlined Superman's origin to make him Krypton's sole survivor. Other characters later used the name, including the shapeshifting Matrix and Linda Danvers. A modern Kara Zor-El was reintroduced in 2004 in the Superman/Batman storyline "The Supergirl from Krypton" by Jeph Loeb and Michael Turner, restoring her as a central figure in the Superman family.

Other versions

Related characters include Power Girl (Kara Zor-L of Earth-Two), an adult counterpart of Supergirl, and various alternate-universe incarnations across DC's multiverse.

In other media

Supergirl was portrayed by Helen Slater in the 1984 film Supergirl, by Laura Vandervoort in Smallville, and by Melissa Benoist in the television series Supergirl (2015–2021), part of the Arrowverse. Sasha Calle played a version of the character in the 2023 film The Flash.
