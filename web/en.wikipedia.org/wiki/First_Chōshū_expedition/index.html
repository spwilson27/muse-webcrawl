First Chōshū expedition

The First Chōshū expedition (Japanese: 第一次長州征討, Dai-ichiji Chōshū seito) was a punitive military expedition by the Tokugawa shogunate against the feudal domain of Chōshū, which took place in late 1864 during the Bakumatsu period. The expedition followed the Kinmon incident of August 1864, in which Chōshū forces had attempted to seize control of the Imperial Court in Kyoto and were repelled.

Background

Chōshū Domain, under the Mōri clan, had become a centre of the sonnō jōi ("revere the Emperor, expel the barbarians") movement. In 1863 Chōshū forces fired on foreign shipping in the Shimonoseki Strait, and in August 1864 Chōshū troops marched on Kyoto, fighting shogunate and loyalist forces at the Forbidden Gate (the Kinmon or Hamaguri incident). Defeated and branded enemies of the Imperial Court, Chōshū faced a shogunal army drawn from numerous domains, commanded in the name of the shogun Tokugawa Iemochi, with Saigō Takamori of Satsuma playing a leading role in the campaign's direction.

Outcome

The expedition ended largely without major battle. Negotiations, in which Saigō pressed for a lenient settlement, led Chōshū to submit: the domain executed the officials held responsible for the attack on Kyoto, and its daimyō Mōri Takachika and his son performed ritual apologies and accepted punishment including the reduction of territory. The shogunate's show of force thus achieved a nominal victory, but the lenient terms allowed Chōshū to survive, reform its military, and return to opposition — leading to the Second Chōshū expedition of 1866, which the shogunate lost.
