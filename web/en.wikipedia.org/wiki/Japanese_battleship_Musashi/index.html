Japanese battleship Musashi

Musashi
Class: Yamato-class battleship
Laid down: 29 March 1938
Commissioned: 5 August 1942
Fate: Sunk 24 October 1944, Battle of Leyte Gulf

Musashi (武蔵) was one of two Yamato-class battleships built for the Imperial Japanese Navy during World War II, named after the former Musashi Province. Together with her sister ship Yamato, she was among the heaviest and most powerfully armed battleships ever built, displacing nearly 72,000 tonnes at full load and mounting nine 46 cm (18.1 in) main guns.

Design and construction

Musashi was laid down at the Mitsubishi Nagasaki shipyard on 29 March 1938 and secretly built behind a large camouflage curtain. She was launched on 1 November 1940 and commissioned on 5 August 1942.

Service

Musashi served as flagship of the Combined Fleet in early 1943. She saw limited action until October 1944, when she sailed as part of Vice Admiral Takeo Kurita's Centre Force in the Battle of Leyte Gulf. On 24 October 1944, during the Battle of the Sibuyan Sea, she was attacked by successive waves of American carrier aircraft and sunk after taking numerous bomb and torpedo hits; about half of her crew of some 2,400 were lost.

Wreck

The wreck was located in March 2015 by a team funded by Paul Allen aboard the research vessel Octopus, lying in the Sibuyan Sea at a depth of about 1,000 metres.
