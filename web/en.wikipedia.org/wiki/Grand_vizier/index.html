Grand vizier

Grand vizier (Ottoman Turkish: Sadrazam) was the title of the chief minister and effective head of government in the Ottoman Empire, serving as the sultan's absolute deputy and holder of the imperial seal. Comparable offices of a senior vizier existed in several other Islamic states.

Role and powers

The grand vizier presided over the Imperial Council (Divan), commanded armies in the field, and conducted the day-to-day administration of the empire from the Sublime Porte in Constantinople. Although appointed and dismissed at the sultan's pleasure — and not infrequently executed — powerful grand viziers such as Sokollu Mehmed Pasha and the Köprülü family dominated Ottoman politics for decades.

History

The office grew out of the vizierate of earlier Islamic states and was formalised under the early Ottomans. In the empire's later centuries the grand vizier's office at the Porte became synonymous with the Ottoman government itself. The last grand vizier, Ahmed Tevfik Pasha, left office in 1922 with the abolition of the sultanate.
