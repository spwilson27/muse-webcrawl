The secretary of state for foreign, Commonwealth and development affairs, commonly known as the foreign secretary, is a secretary of state in the Government of the United Kingdom, with responsibility for the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO). The incumbent is a member of the Cabinet of the United Kingdom, considered one of the most senior of the Great Offices of State, and is ranked third in the ministerial ranking, behind the prime minister and the chancellor of the exchequer.

History

The office of Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs was created in 1782, when the former Northern and Southern Departments were reorganised into the Home Office and the Foreign Office. The first holder was Charles James Fox. The post took on responsibility for the Commonwealth in 1966, when the Commonwealth Office merged with the Foreign Office to form the Foreign and Commonwealth Office, and for international development in 2020, when the Department for International Development was merged into the new FCDO.

Responsibilities

The foreign secretary is responsible for the United Kingdom's relations with foreign countries and governments, its network of embassies and consulates, British interests abroad, and — since 2020 — official development assistance. The postholder also has oversight of the Secret Intelligence Service (MI6) and the Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ).

Residences

The foreign secretary's official London residence is 1 Carlton Gardens, and the country residence is Chevening House in Kent, shared in practice with the prime minister's discretion. The office itself is based at the FCDO building in King Charles Street, Whitehall.
