Hungarian–Romanian War

The Hungarian–Romanian War was fought between Hungary and Romania from 13 November 1918 to 3 August 1919. One of the conflicts that reshaped Central Europe after the First World War, it arose from competing claims to Transylvania and ended with Romanian troops occupying Budapest and the collapse of the Hungarian Soviet Republic.

Background

Following Austria-Hungary's collapse, the Romanian National Council proclaimed the union of Transylvania with the Kingdom of Romania on 1 December 1918. The democratic Hungarian government of Mihály Károlyi, weakened and facing Allied-imposed territorial losses, resigned in March 1919; Béla Kun then proclaimed a Hungarian Soviet Republic, hoping Soviet Russian support would help recover lost territories.

Course of the war

Romanian forces advanced into Transylvania in late 1918 and pushed to the Tisza River by May 1919. The Hungarian Red Army under Aurél Stromfeld counterattacked northward into Slovakia, but under Allied pressure Kun withdrew. In July 1919 Hungary launched an offensive across the Tisza; it failed, and Romanian forces counterattacked, crossing the river and advancing on Budapest. Kun's government collapsed on 1 August, and Romanian troops entered Budapest on 3–4 August 1919.

Aftermath

The Romanian occupation lasted until early 1920 and included extensive requisitioning that embittered Hungarian opinion. Admiral Miklós Horthy's counter-revolutionary forces took power, and the Treaty of Trianon (1920) confirmed Transylvania's transfer to Romania, leaving Hungary stripped of roughly two-thirds of its former territory — a grievance that shaped Hungarian politics for decades.
