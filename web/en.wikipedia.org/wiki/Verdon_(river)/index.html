Verdon (river)

The Verdon is a 166-kilometre river in south-eastern France, a left tributary of the Durance. It rises in the Maritime Alps near the Col d'Allos and flows generally southwest through the Alpes-de-Haute-Provence and Var departments before joining the Durance near Vinon-sur-Verdon.

Verdon Gorge

The river is famous for the Verdon Gorge (Gorges du Verdon), a limestone canyon up to 700 metres deep between Castellane and Moustiers-Sainte-Marie, often described as one of Europe's most beautiful canyons. The striking turquoise-green colour of the water comes from glacial flour and minerals.

Dams and lakes

Five dams built for hydroelectricity and irrigation created a series of reservoirs, including Lac de Castillon, Lac de Chaudanne, Lac de Sainte-Croix, Lac d'Esparron and Lac de Quinson. The vast Lac de Sainte-Croix, completed in 1973, is a major centre for kayaking, rafting and climbing tourism.
