Prêt-à-porter

Prêt-à-porter
Literal meaning"Ready to wear" (French)
English termReady-to-wear (RTW), off-the-rack
ProductionFactory-made in standard sizes
ContrastHaute couture, bespoke tailoring

Prêt-à-porter (French for "ready to wear"; often abbreviated RTW) is factory-made clothing sold in standard sizes, as distinct from haute couture and bespoke garments made to an individual customer's measurements. Although the economics of ready-made clothing began with 19th-century mass production, the term acquired its modern fashion connotation after the Second World War, when Paris couturiers began producing designer ready-to-wear lines alongside their couture collections.

History

Standardised garments first became widespread for military uniforms and men's workwear. Department stores such as Le Bon Marché in Paris and Brooks Brothers in New York sold ready-made clothing through the 1800s, though fashionable womenswear remained largely made to order. The turning point came in the 1960s: in 1966 Yves Saint Laurent opened his Rive Gauche boutiques, the first major couturier line devoted entirely to ready-to-wear, shifting prestige away from bespoke couture toward designer garments anyone could buy off the rack. Italian houses in Milan built a parallel industry in the 1970s.

In the fashion system

Prêt-à-porter collections are presented at the major fashion weeks — Paris, Milan, New York and London — in two main seasons (spring/summer and autumn/winter), supplemented by resort and pre-fall lines. Luxury brands typically divide output into haute couture (where licensed), prêt-à-porter and diffusion lines, with ready-to-wear supplying most revenue. Below the designer tier, high-street and fast-fashion retailers apply the same off-the-rack principle at far lower price points and shorter cycles.

Terminology

In English, "ready-to-wear" and "off-the-rack/off-the-peg" are used interchangeably, though "prêt-à-porter" tends to signal designer-level collections. The phrase was borrowed into other languages and is also the title of Robert Altman's 1994 satirical film Prêt-à-Porter, set during Paris Fashion Week.
