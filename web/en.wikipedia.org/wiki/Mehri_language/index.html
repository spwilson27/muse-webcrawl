Mehri (Mehri: Məhrəyət) is a Modern South Arabian language spoken by the Mehri people (Al Mahrah tribe) in eastern Yemen, in Al Mahrah Governorate, and in the Dhofar region of western Oman. It is the most widely spoken of the Modern South Arabian languages, a group of endangered Semitic languages unrelated to Arabic despite the region's Arab identity; they descend from the ancient South Arabian linguistic area rather than from Classical Arabic.

Dialects

Two main dialect groups are recognised: Western Mehri, spoken in Yemen (Mehri of Al Mahrah), and Eastern Mehri, spoken in Oman (often called Mehreyyet or Dhofari Mehri). The varieties differ in phonology and lexicon; Yemeni Mehri has been more influenced by contact with Arabic dialects, while Omani varieties preserve some archaic features. Mutual intelligibility between the two groups is generally good.

Phonology

Like the other Modern South Arabian languages, Mehri preserves consonants lost in most other Semitic languages, including lateral fricatives and ejective (glottalised) consonants. Its consonant inventory includes the emphatic series characteristic of the family:

LateralsVoiceless lateral fricative /ɬ/ (ś) and its glottalised counterpart
Ejectives/tʼ/, /kʼ/, /sʼ/ and others
VowelsShort and long /a, e, i, o, u/ with dialectal variation

Grammar

Mehri is a verb-initial-to-SVO language with rich root-and-pattern morphology inherited from Semitic. It has dual number in pronouns and verbs, a conditional mood marked by suffixes, and a distinctive system of verbal stems. Because the language is primarily oral, most speakers are bilingual in Arabic, which is used for education, government and writing.

Status

Mehri is classified as endangered: intergenerational transmission is weakening, especially in Oman, where Arabic dominates schooling and media. Documentation projects by linguists in Oman and abroad have produced grammars, text collections and a dictionary, and there is growing local interest in recording oral literature, including poetry and traditional narrative.
