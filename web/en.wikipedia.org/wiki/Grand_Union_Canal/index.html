Grand Union Canal

Narrowboats on the Grand Union Canal, the main canal artery between London and Birmingham.

The Grand Union Canal in England is part of the British canal system. It is the principal canal link between London and the Midlands, running 137 miles (220 km) from the River Thames at Brentford to Birmingham, with several branches. Including its branches, the Grand Union system totals about 286 miles (461 km) and contains 166 locks.

History

The canal was created on 1 January 1929 by the amalgamation of several canal companies — principally the Grand Junction Canal and the Regent's Canal, together with the Warwick canals and others — and was further expanded in 1932 with the addition of the Leicester and Loughborough navigations and the Erewash Canal. The new company undertook a modernisation programme in the 1930s, widening locks and dredging channels in an attempt to compete with road and rail transport, though large-scale commercial traffic had largely ceased by the 1970s.

Route

The main line climbs from Brentford through the Chiltern Hills at the Marsworth and Braunston lock flights, passes through the 2,813-metre Blisworth Tunnel, and descends into Birmingham. Engineering features include the Iron Trunk Aqueduct over the River Great Ouse at Cosgrove and the Foxton Locks staircase in Leicestershire, on the Leicester branch. The Paddington Arm connects the main line to Paddington Basin in central London, where it meets the Regent's Canal at Little Venice.

Today

Today the canal is used almost entirely for leisure boating and is managed by the Canal & River Trust. Its towpaths are popular walking and cycling routes, and many of its structures are listed historic buildings.
