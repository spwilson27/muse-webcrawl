Gobi Desert

The Gobi is a large, cold desert and brushland region in northern China and southern Mongolia. It is one of the great deserts of the world — about 1.3 million square kilometres — but unlike classic sand deserts, much of its surface is bare rock and gravel plains (from which its name, meaning 'waterless place', derives).

The Gobi is famous in palaeontology for dinosaur fossils, including the first recognised dinosaur eggs, found by Roy Chapman Andrews' expeditions in the 1920s at the Flaming Cliffs. The Silk Road crossed its southern edge, and herders today graze Bactrian camels, goats (for cashmere) and sheep across its steppe margins. Desertification at its edges is a major concern for China, which has planted vast shelterbelts of trees.
