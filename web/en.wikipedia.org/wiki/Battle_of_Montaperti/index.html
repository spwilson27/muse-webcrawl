Battle of Montaperti

Date4 September 1260
LocationMontaperti, near Siena, Tuscany, Italy
ResultDecisive Sienese (Ghibelline) victory
BelligerentsSiena and Ghibelline allies, with troops of King Manfred vs. Florence and Guelph allies
CommandersProvenzano Salvani; Farinata degli Uberti vs. Iacopino Rangoni (killed)

The Battle of Montaperti was fought on 4 September 1260 between the forces of Ghibelline Siena and Guelph Florence near the hill of Montaperti in Tuscany. It was the bloodiest battle of medieval Italy and the high point of Sienese power; a Sienese and German cavalry victory destroyed the Florentine army and briefly put Florence itself at Siena's mercy.

Background

The rivalry between Florence and Siena was part of the wider Guelph and Ghibelline struggle. Siena sheltered exiled Florentine Ghibellines and received from King Manfred of Sicily a contingent of German knights. In 1260 a large Florentine-led Guelph army, perhaps 30,000 strong — roughly twice the Sienese force — marched on Siena and camped at Montaperti.

The battle

The Sienese attacked on 4 September. During the fighting, Florentine Ghibelline sympathisers within the Guelph army turned against it; in the most famous episode, Bocca degli Abati cut off the hand of the Florentine standard-bearer, bringing down the banner of the carroccio and sowing panic. A charge by the German cavalry completed the rout. Florentine losses were enormous by medieval standards — perhaps 10,000 killed and 15,000 captured.

Aftermath

Florence's government fell and its Guelph leaders fled; at a meeting of victorious Ghibellines, the proposal to raze Florence was famously resisted by Farinata degli Uberti. Sienese ascendancy proved brief: Manfred's death at Benevento in 1266 reversed the balance, and the Sienese were defeated at Colle Val d'Elsa in 1269. Dante remembered the slaughter in the Inferno, where the river Arbia "ran red" and Bocca degli Abati is damned among the traitors.
