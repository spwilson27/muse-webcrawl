Sphenoid bone

Sphenoid bone
Articulations: all other cranial bones
Features: sella turcica, greater and lesser wings, pterygoid processes

The sphenoid bone is an unpaired bone of the neurocranium. It is situated in the middle of the skull towards the front, in front of the temporal bone and the basilar part of the occipital bone. One of the most complex bones of the human body, it is often described as resembling a bat, butterfly or wasp with outstretched wings; the name derives from the Greek sphen, "wedge".

Structure

The sphenoid consists of a central body, two greater wings and two lesser wings, and two pterygoid processes pointing downward. The body contains the sphenoidal sinuses and bears on its upper surface the sella turcica, a saddle-shaped depression that cradles the pituitary gland, flanked by the anterior and posterior clinoid processes.

Foramina

The bone is pierced by several openings transmitting nerves and vessels, including the optic canal, the superior orbital fissure, the foramen rotundum, the foramen ovale and the foramen spinosum, carrying branches of the trigeminal nerve and the middle meningeal artery among other structures.

Function

The sphenoid articulates with all the other cranial bones and helps unite the cranium and facial skeleton. It contributes to the floor of the cranial cavity, the orbit, the nasal cavity and the infratemporal fossa, and provides attachment for muscles of mastication via its pterygoid processes.
