Baylor College of Medicine (BCM) is a private medical school and academic health science centre in Houston, Texas, located in the Texas Medical Center, the largest medical complex in the world. Although it shares its name and early history with Baylor University, it has been an independent institution since 1969.

History

The school was founded in Dallas in 1900 as the University of Dallas Medical Department and affiliated with Baylor University in 1903. In 1943 the M.D. Anderson Foundation offered support for it to relocate to Houston, and it became the first institution of the new Texas Medical Center. Under the leadership of heart surgeon Michael E. DeBakey, who served as president from 1969 to 1979, the college rose to national prominence in cardiovascular surgery and research. The university affiliation ended amicably in 1969, after which "Baylor College of Medicine" became a standalone name.

Education and research

BCM grants the M.D. degree and operates a Graduate School of Biomedical Sciences, a School of Health Professions and the National School of Tropical Medicine, founded in 2011. It is consistently ranked among the top American medical schools for research and primary care, and its Human Genome Sequencing Center was one of the principal centres of the Human Genome Project. Clinical training takes place at affiliated hospitals including Texas Children's Hospital and Ben Taub Hospital.

Notable people

Cardiac surgeon Denton Cooley trained and taught at Baylor, and the college's faculty have included leaders in cardiology, genetics and space medicine through its Center for Space Medicine partnership with NASA.
