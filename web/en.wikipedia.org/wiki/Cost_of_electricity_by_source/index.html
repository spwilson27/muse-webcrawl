Cost of electricity by source

Different methods of electricity generation can incur a significantly different cost of electricity, usually compared using the levelized cost of electricity (LCOE) — the average net present cost of generation over a plant's lifetime divided by its lifetime output, expressed per megawatt-hour. LCOE allows technologies with different lifetimes, capacities and cost structures to be compared on a common basis.

Trends

Since about 2010 the costs of renewable energy have fallen dramatically: the International Renewable Energy Agency (IRENA) reports that the global average cost of utility-scale solar photovoltaics fell by roughly 90% between 2010 and 2022, and onshore wind by around 70%, driven by cheaper modules and turbines, better capacity factors and competitive auctions. Studies by Lazard and others similarly find new wind and solar frequently undercutting the marginal cost of existing fossil and nuclear plants.

System and external costs

LCOE does not capture all costs: variable renewables impose balancing and grid-integration costs, while dispatchable plants provide capacity value. Studies also differ on whether to include externalities such as air pollution and carbon emissions — a carbon price raises fossil generation costs substantially — and decommissioning and waste costs for nuclear power.
