Turbine map

Image placeholder: turbine map

A turbine map is a performance chart used in turbomachinery engineering to describe the operating characteristics of a turbine across its range of conditions. It is analogous to a compressor map but applies to the expanding component of a gas turbine, steam turbine, turbocharger or expansion device.

Description

A turbine map typically plots corrected (or reduced) mass flow against pressure ratio — the ratio of inlet total pressure to exit static or total pressure — for a series of lines of constant corrected rotational speed. Contours of constant isentropic or total-to-total efficiency are superimposed on the map, allowing engineers to predict flow, efficiency and power output at any operating point.

Corrected parameters are used so that a single map represents performance at many inlet conditions. Corrected mass flow combines the actual mass flow with inlet temperature and pressure, while corrected speed divides the shaft speed by the square root of inlet temperature.

Characteristics

Unlike compressor maps, turbine maps often show mass flow becoming nearly constant once the turbine nozzles choke: beyond a certain pressure ratio the flow through the first-stage stator reaches Mach 1 and cannot increase further, so the constant-speed lines bunch together vertically. This choked behaviour simplifies matching a turbine with its compressor in a gas turbine engine, because the turbine swallowing capacity fixes the flow the compressor must deliver.

Applications

Turbine maps are essential in the design and off-design performance prediction of jet engines, industrial gas turbines, steam turbines, turbochargers and refrigeration expanders. Engine manufacturers use them in cycle-deck simulations to match components, predict surge margins, estimate fuel consumption and plan control schedules such as variable stator vane settings.
