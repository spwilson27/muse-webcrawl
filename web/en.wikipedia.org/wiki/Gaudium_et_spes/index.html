Gaudium et spes

TypePastoral constitution
Issued bySecond Vatican Council
Promulgated7 December 1965, by Pope Paul VI
SubjectThe Church in the modern world

Gaudium et spes ("Joy and Hope") is the Pastoral Constitution on the Church in the Modern World, one of the four constitutions of the Second Vatican Council and one of its principal achievements. Promulgated by Pope Paul VI on 7 December 1965, the last day of the Council, it addresses the whole of humanity — it is the Council's longest document and its most direct engagement with the social, political and economic life of the modern world.

Content

The constitution opens with the famous words: "The joys and the hopes, the griefs and the anxieties of the men of this age, especially those who are poor or in any way afflicted, these are the joys and hopes, the griefs and anxieties of the followers of Christ." Its first part develops a theological anthropology centred on human dignity, the vocation of the person, and the role of conscience; it famously rejects atheism while affirming religious freedom and the legitimate autonomy of earthly affairs.

The second part treats specific urgent problems: marriage and the family, culture, socio-economic life, the political community, and the promotion of peace, including a strong condemnation of total war and of the arms race. The document's call to read "the signs of the times" shaped subsequent Catholic social teaching and the theology of the Church's engagement with the world.
