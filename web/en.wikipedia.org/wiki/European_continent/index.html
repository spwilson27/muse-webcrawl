European continent

Image: Map of Europe (placeholder)

The European continent comprises the westernmost peninsulas of the continental landmass of Eurasia. It is conventionally bounded by the Arctic Ocean to the north, the Atlantic Ocean to the west, the Mediterranean Sea to the south, and — by long-standing convention — the Ural Mountains, Ural River, Caspian Sea, and Caucasus watershed to the east, separating it from Asia.

Geography

Covering about 10 million square kilometres, Europe is the second-smallest continent by surface area, yet it is the third most populous, with more than 740 million inhabitants. The continent is characterised by a deeply indented coastline of peninsulas — Iberian, Italian, Balkan, Scandinavian, and Jutland among them — and by large offshore islands including Great Britain, Ireland, and Iceland. Major mountain systems include the Alps, Pyrenees, Carpathians, Apennines, and Caucasus, whose Mount Elbrus is often cited as Europe's highest point.

Definitions

Because Europe and Asia form a single continuous landmass, their division is historical and cultural rather than strictly physical. Terms such as "continental Europe" or "the Continent" are used especially from British and island perspectives to refer to mainland Europe excluding the surrounding islands.
