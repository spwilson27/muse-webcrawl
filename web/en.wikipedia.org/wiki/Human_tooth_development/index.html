Human tooth development, or odontogenesis, is the complex process by which teeth form from embryonic cells, grow, and erupt into the mouth. It begins around the sixth week of development in the embryo, when the dental lamina forms within the primitive jaw.

Stages

Tooth development is conventionally divided into the bud, cap, bell and maturation stages. Interactions between the oral epithelium and the underlying mesenchyme produce the enamel organ, dental papilla and dental follicle, which give rise to enamel, dentine, pulp, cementum and the periodontal ligament. Ameloblasts secrete enamel, the hardest substance in the human body, while odontoblasts form dentine.

Eruption

The 20 primary (deciduous) teeth usually begin erupting around six months of age and are shed from about age six, being replaced by the permanent dentition of 32 teeth, including the third molars or "wisdom teeth", which erupt in late adolescence or adulthood.
