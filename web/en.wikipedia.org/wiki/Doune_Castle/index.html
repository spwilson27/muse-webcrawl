Doune Castle is a medieval stronghold near the village of Doune, in the Stirling council area of central Scotland. The castle is sited on a wooded bend where the Ardoch Burn flows into the River Teith. It lies 8 miles (13 km) north-west of Stirling, where the Teith flows into the River Forth.

Recent research has shown that Doune Castle was originally built in the 13th century, then probably damaged in the Scottish Wars of Independence, and subsequently rebuilt in its present form in the late 14th century by Robert Stewart, Duke of Albany (c. 1340–1420), the son of King Robert II of Scotland, and Regent of Scotland from 1388 until his death. The castle has survived relatively unchanged and complete.

History

As regent, Albany effectively ruled Scotland, and Doune was a principal seat of his power. After his son Murdoch was executed by James I in 1425, the castle passed to the Crown and served as a royal hunting lodge and dower house for widowed queens, including Mary of Guelders and Margaret Tudor. During the Jacobite risings it was garrisoned by government troops, and in 1745 it held prisoners captured at the Battle of Falkirk, some of whom escaped by knotting bedsheets. The castle fell into decay in the 18th century but was restored in the 1880s by the Earl of Moray; it passed into state care in 1984.

Layout

The castle consists of a courtyard enclosed by ranges of buildings on two sides, dominated by a large gatehouse tower containing the Lord's Hall and the lord's apartments, and a separate kitchen tower with one of the best-preserved medieval great kitchens in Scotland. Its great hall, with a minstrels' gallery and open hearth, is noted for its fine acoustics.

Filming location

Doune Castle is famous as a filming location. It stood in for several castles in Monty Python and the Holy Grail (1975), served as Winterfell in the pilot of Game of Thrones, and appears as Castle Leoch in the television series Outlander, bringing large numbers of visitors.
