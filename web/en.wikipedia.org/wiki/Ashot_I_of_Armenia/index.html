Ashot I of Armenia

Reign: 884/885 – 890

Predecessor: (kingdom restored)

Successor: Smbat I

House: Bagratuni

Died: 890

Ashot I (Armenian: Աշոտ Ա; died 890) was the first king of the medieval Bagratid Armenia, ruling from 884/885 until his death. A leading prince of the Bagratuni family, he restored the Armenian monarchy more than four centuries after the fall of the Arsacid kingdom, gaining recognition as king from both the Abbasid caliph al-Mu'tamid and the Byzantine emperor Basil I.

Rise

Ashot succeeded his father Smbat VIII the Confessor, who had been killed by Arab forces in 862, as head of the Bagratuni house and sparapet (commander-in-chief) of Armenia. Skillfully balancing between the Abbasid Caliphate and Byzantium, he expanded Bagratid power, subdued rival princely houses, and campaigned against Arab emirs in the region. His influence extended into Caucasian Iberia and Albania.

Kingship

In 884/885 the caliph sent Ashot a crown and recognised him as "king of Armenia"; Basil I sent a second crown soon after. Ashot established his court at Bagaran and later Kars. He founded the city of Shirakavan and promoted church building. He was called "the Great" by Armenian chroniclers. On his death in 890 he was succeeded by his son Smbat I, under whom the kingdom reached its greatest extent.
