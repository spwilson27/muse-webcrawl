David Leitch (born November 16, 1975) is an American filmmaker, stunt coordinator, and actor. He made his directorial debut as co-director of the action film John Wick (2014) with Chad Stahelski, and went on to direct Atomic Blonde (2017), Deadpool 2 (2018), Fast & Furious Presents: Hobbs & Shaw (2019), Bullet Train (2022), and The Fall Guy (2024). Before directing, Leitch was one of Hollywood's most sought-after stunt performers and fight choreographers, serving as a stunt double for Brad Pitt and Jean-Claude Van Damme.

Early life and stunt career

Leitch was born in Kohler, Wisconsin, and studied at the University of Minnesota, training in martial arts and competing before moving into stunt work. He performed stunts in films including Blade, Fight Club (doubling Brad Pitt), The Matrix Reloaded, 300, The Bourne Ultimatum, and TRON: Legacy. In 1997 he co-founded the action design company 87Eleven with Chad Stahelski, which became a leading provider of stunt choreography and second-unit direction for major productions.

Directing career

Leitch and Stahelski co-directed John Wick (2014), whose lean storytelling and long-take "gun-fu" fight choreography revitalised the Hollywood action genre. Although contractual rules meant only Stahelski received sole directorial credit, Leitch was widely credited as co-director. His solo directorial debut, the Cold War spy thriller Atomic Blonde (2017) with Charlize Theron, was noted for an extended stairwell fight staged as a single take. He then took over Deadpool 2 (2018) and directed the Fast & Furious spin-off Hobbs & Shaw (2019), both major box-office successes. Bullet Train (2022), an ensemble action comedy set on a Japanese shinkansen, starred Brad Pitt, and The Fall Guy (2024), a love letter to the stunt profession, starred Ryan Gosling and Emily Blunt.

Production

Through his production company 87North Productions, founded with Kelly McCormick, Leitch has produced action films including Nobody (2021), Violent Night (2022), and The Fall Guy. He has frequently advocated for formal recognition of stunt performers at the Academy Awards.
