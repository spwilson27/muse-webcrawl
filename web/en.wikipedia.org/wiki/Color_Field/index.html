Color Field

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Colour Field painting emphasises large, unified areas of colour.

Color Field painting is a style of abstract painting that emerged in New York City during the 1940s and 1950s. It was inspired by European modernism and closely related to Abstract Expressionism, and many of its leading artists were among the pioneers of that movement. Colour Field is all about colour: a painting consists of large fields of flat, solid colour spread across or stained into the canvas, creating areas of unbroken surface and a flat picture plane.

Characteristics

The movement prized consistency of form and process, psychological content, and large formats worked on at close range. The glow of enveloping colour was meant to produce a contemplative, even transcendent experience. Critics such as Clement Greenberg championed the style and associated it with artists including Mark Rothko, Barnett Newman, Clyfford Still, Robert Motherwell, and Adolph Gottlieb; a second generation — Helen Frankenthaler, Morris Louis, Kenneth Noland, Jules Olitski, and Sam Francis — developed techniques such as Frankenthaler's "soak stain" method of pouring thinned paint directly onto unprimed canvas.

Legacy

Colour Field painting paved the way for Post-painterly Abstraction, Lyrical Abstraction, and Minimalism. Rothko's hovering rectangles and Newman's "zips" remain among the most recognisable images of postwar American art.
