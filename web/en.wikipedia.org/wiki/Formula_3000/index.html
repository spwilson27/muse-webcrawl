Formula 3000 was a type of open-wheel, single-seater motor racing formula created by the FIA in 1984 to provide a final stepping stone for drivers aiming for Formula One. The name derives from the 3.0-litre (3000 cc) engine capacity permitted, initially using the abundant and inexpensive Cosworth DFV V8 engines made obsolete by turbocharged Formula One.

History

The International Formula 3000 championship replaced Formula Two in 1985. Early seasons featured a mix of ex-Formula One chassis and purpose-built cars from manufacturers such as March, Lola, Ralt and Reynard. Champions included Ivan Capelli, Stefano Modena, Jean Alesi, Erik Comas, Olivier Panis, Nick Heidfeld and Juan Pablo Montoya, many of whom graduated to Formula One.

Decline and replacements

From 1996 the international series became a one-make formula using Lola chassis and Zytek engines, which reduced costs but also diminished its appeal as grids shrank. The championship ended after 2004 and was replaced in 2005 by the GP2 Series. National Formula 3000 series continued in countries including Britain, Italy and Japan for some years.
