The Islamic Republic of Afghanistan was the government of Afghanistan between 2004 and 2021, established after the fall of the first Taliban regime in late 2001 and the interim administration created by the Bonn Agreement. It collapsed in August 2021 during the Taliban offensive that followed the withdrawal of United States and coalition forces.

History

Following the US-led invasion of 2001, an Afghan Interim Administration under Hamid Karzai was installed. A loya jirga in 2002 made Karzai head of a transitional administration, and a constitutional loya jirga adopted a new constitution in January 2004 creating a strong presidency. Karzai won the 2004 presidential election and was re-elected in 2009 amid widespread allegations of fraud. He was succeeded in 2014 by Ashraf Ghani, whose disputed election led to a US-brokered national unity government with Abdullah Abdullah as Chief Executive.

Throughout its existence the Republic fought a Taliban insurgency, supported by NATO's ISAF and later Resolute Support missions. The February 2020 Doha Agreement between the United States and the Taliban set a timetable for foreign withdrawal; as international forces left in 2021, Taliban forces rapidly overran the provinces, Kabul fell on 15 August 2021, and President Ghani fled the country.

Government and politics

The 2004 constitution established a presidential system with a bicameral National Assembly (the Wolesi Jirga and Meshrano Jirga), an independent judiciary headed by the Supreme Court, and Islam as the state religion, providing that no law could contradict the beliefs and provisions of Islam. Afghanistan was divided into 34 provinces administered by appointed governors.

Economy and society

The Republic's economy depended heavily on foreign aid and security spending; opium production remained a major illicit sector. Indicators in education and health improved markedly compared with the Taliban period, particularly in girls' schooling and urban access to services, though gains were uneven and corruption pervasive.
