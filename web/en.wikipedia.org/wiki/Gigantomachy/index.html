In Greek mythology, the Gigantomachy (Greek: Γιγαντομαχία, Gigantomachia) was the battle fought between the Giants and the Olympian gods for supremacy of the cosmos. It is one of the most important themes in Greek art and literature, surpassed in prominence only by the Titanomachy, the earlier war between the Olympians and the Titans.

Background

The Giants were the offspring of Gaia (Earth), born from the blood that fell from the castrated Uranus (Sky), or, in some accounts, born from Gaia and Tartarus. Gaia, angered at the imprisonment of the Titans, urged the Giants to attack the gods. Leading Giants included Alcyoneus, who was immortal in his homeland, and Porphyrion, who attacked Hera.

The battle

According to Apollodorus, the gods could not defeat the Giants without the aid of a mortal, so Zeus brought Heracles into the battle, whose arrows finished off the Giants wounded by the gods. Individual combats included Zeus striking Porphyrion with a thunderbolt, Apollo and Heracles shooting out the eyes of Ephialtes, and Athena burying Enceladus beneath Sicily. Athena also flayed the Giant Pallas and used his skin as a shield.

In art

The Gigantomachy was depicted on the east metopes of the Parthenon, the interior of Athena's shield, and most famously in the monumental frieze of the Pergamon Altar, now in Berlin. It was woven into the peplos presented to Athena at the Panathenaea.
