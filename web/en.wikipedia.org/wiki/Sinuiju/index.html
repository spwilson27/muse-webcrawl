Sinŭiju

Sinŭiju Country: North Korea
Province: North Pyongan (capital)
Location: On the Yalu River, opposite Dandong, China
Population: about 360,000 (2008 census)
Korean: 신의주 (新義州)

Sinŭiju (신의주, "new Ŭiju") is a city in North Korea and the capital of North Pyongan Province, situated on the left bank of the Yalu (Amnok) River directly across from the Chinese city of Dandong. With a population of roughly 360,000, it is one of North Korea's principal border cities and its most important overland gateway for trade with China.

History

The city developed under Japanese colonial rule as a railway and industrial centre at the Korean end of the bridge across the Yalu, eclipsing the older administrative town of Ŭiju inland; its name records that succession. It became the terminus of the Gyeongui railway line from Seoul and a hub for timber floated down the Yalu and for chemical and paper industries. During the Korean War the city was heavily bombed, including in the famous November 1950 air raids associated with General MacArthur's advance to the Yalu. It was rebuilt in the postwar decades on a grid plan.

Economy and border trade

Sinŭiju's economy revolves around the Sino-Korean Friendship Bridge to Dandong, through which the great majority of North Korea's legal foreign trade passes by rail and road. The city has light industry — textiles, cosmetics (the Pomhyanggi brand), enamelware and processed foods — and its markets have long been a channel for Chinese consumer goods. In 2002 North Korea designated a "Sinŭiju Special Administrative Region" intended as a semi-autonomous market enclave under Dutch-Chinese businessman Yang Bin; the project collapsed within weeks when Yang was arrested in China, and the scheme was quietly abandoned, though smaller border economic zones around Hwanggumpyong and Wihwa islands have been periodically promoted since.

Transport

The city is the northern terminus of North Korea's railway connection to China; trains between Pyongyang and Beijing cross at Sinŭiju, where passport and customs formalities for North Korea are conducted. A new road bridge downstream, the New Yalu River Bridge, was completed by China but long stood unused pending facilities on the Korean side.
