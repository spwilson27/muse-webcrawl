The Minitel was a videotex online service accessible through telephone lines, and is considered one of the world's most successful pre-World Wide Web online services. Developed in France by the Postes, Télécommunications et Télédiffusion (PTT) and launched commercially in 1982, it was distributed to telephone subscribers free of charge. Millions of terminals — small beige units with a built-in modem, a small monochrome screen, and a fold-away keyboard — were given away, allowing users to access an electronic telephone directory, make reservations, perform online banking, read news, shop, and exchange messages.

History

The French government, inspired by the 1978 Nora–Minc report on the computerisation of society, promoted Minitel as a way to modernise French telecommunications. The initial "killer application" was the electronic telephone directory, which let France Télécom save the cost of printing paper directories and running directory-enquiry services. From the mid-1980s, third-party services proliferated under the "Télétel" kiosk billing model, in which service charges were simply added to the user's telephone bill, with the operator keeping a share — an early and effective micropayment system.

Services and culture

At its peak the network offered some 25,000 services. Banking, travel booking, and professional databases were widely used, but the cultural phenomenon of the Minitel was the messageries: chat services, many of them flirtatious "Minitel rose" services, advertised all over Paris and generating enormous revenues. The system created an online culture in France a decade before the web became mainstream elsewhere.

Decline and closure

Minitel usage peaked in the late 1990s and early 2000s with around 25 million users, but steadily lost ground to the open internet. Its success is sometimes argued to have slowed French adoption of the web, because businesses and users already had a workable national online system. France Télécom (by then Orange) switched the service off on 30 June 2012, after thirty years of operation; at closure about 800,000 terminals were still in use, including by farmers checking market prices and other long-tail professional users.

Technology

Minitel terminals were "dumb" terminals communicating over the telephone network using modems with asymmetric speeds (1200 bit/s downstream, 75 bit/s upstream on the classic V.23 standard), displaying semigraphic text pages based on the Videotex standard. Later models added colour and faster modems. Services were reached by dialling short codes such as 3611 (directory) and 3615 (commercial services).
