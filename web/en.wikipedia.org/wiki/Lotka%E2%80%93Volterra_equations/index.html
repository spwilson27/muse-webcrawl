Lotka–Volterra equations

Lotka–Volterra equations
TypeSystem of ordinary differential equations
FieldMathematical biology, population dynamics
Introduced byAlfred J. Lotka (1925); Vito Volterra (1926)

The Lotka–Volterra equations, also known as the predator–prey equations, are a pair of first-order nonlinear differential equations frequently used to describe the dynamics of biological systems in which two species interact, one as a predator and the other as prey.

The equations

The populations change through time according to: dx/dt = αx − βxy and dy/dt = δxy − γy, where x is the prey population, y the predator population, and α, β, γ, δ are positive real parameters describing the interaction of the two species.

History

The model was proposed independently by Alfred J. Lotka in 1925 and Vito Volterra in 1926. Volterra developed it after studying fish catch statistics from the Adriatic Sea with his future son-in-law Umberto D'Ancona, who had observed that predator fish proportions rose during the First World War when fishing pressure fell.

Solutions and behaviour

Solutions of the equations are periodic: prey and predator populations oscillate out of phase, producing neutral cycles in the phase plane. The model assumes unlimited prey food supply and that predator food depends entirely on prey density, simplifications that later models such as the Rosenzweig–MacArthur model were developed to relax.

Applications

Beyond ecology, variants of the equations have been applied in economics, chemistry, and epidemiology wherever coupled growth-and-consumption dynamics arise.
