Keyhole, Inc. was a pioneering software development company based in Mountain View, California, founded in 2001. It developed the Earth Viewer application, a highly successful program for viewing satellite and aerial imagery of the Earth, which was later developed into Google Earth after the company was acquired by Google in 2004.

History

Keyhole was founded by Brian McClendon, who had previously worked at Silicon Graphics. The company's name is a homage to the KH-11 "Keyhole" series of military reconnaissance satellites. Its Earth Viewer software allowed users to zoom seamlessly from a view of the whole planet down to individual buildings, streaming vast amounts of imagery and terrain data over ordinary internet connections.

The company's technology attracted attention from the intelligence community. In 2003, In-Q-Tel, the venture capital arm of the Central Intelligence Agency, made a strategic investment in Keyhole, and Earth Viewer was used by news organisations during the Iraq War to visualise the conflict, greatly raising the company's profile.

Acquisition by Google

In October 2004, Google acquired Keyhole for an undisclosed sum. Keyhole's technology became the foundation of Google Earth, launched in June 2005. John Hanke, Keyhole's chief executive, went on to lead Google's Geo products division and later founded Niantic, the developer of Pokémon Go; Brian McClendon became a senior figure in Google's mapping efforts.

The Keyhole Markup Language (KML), developed by the company for expressing geographic annotation in Earth Viewer, survives as an international standard maintained by the Open Geospatial Consortium.
