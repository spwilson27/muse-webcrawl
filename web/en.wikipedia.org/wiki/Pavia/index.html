Pavia (Italian: [paˈviːa]) is a town and comune of south-western Lombardy in northern Italy, 35 kilometres (22 miles) south of Milan on the lower Ticino River near its confluence with the Po. It is the capital of the province of Pavia and is known for its ancient university and its medieval towers and churches.

History

Founded as the Roman municipium of Ticinum, Pavia became an important centre under the Ostrogoths — Theodoric the Great built a palace there — and then the capital of the Lombard Kingdom from 572 to 774. After Charlemagne's conquest it remained a significant city of the Kingdom of Italy, and imperial coronations were held in its basilica of San Michele Maggiore. In 1525 the decisive Battle of Pavia, fought near the city during the Italian Wars, saw Imperial-Spanish forces defeat the French and capture King Francis I of France.

University

The University of Pavia, founded in 1361, is one of the oldest universities in the world. Its alumni and professors include the physicist Alessandro Volta, the anatomist Antonio Scarpa, and the Nobel laureate Camillo Golgi. The university anchors the city's economy and student population.

Main sights

The Certosa di Pavia, a lavish Carthusian monastery founded in 1396, lies about 8 km north of the city. Within Pavia itself are the Romanesque basilicas of San Michele Maggiore and San Pietro in Ciel d'Oro (which holds the tomb of Saint Augustine), the covered bridge (Ponte Coperto) over the Ticino, the Visconti Castle, and the cathedral with one of the largest domes in Italy.
