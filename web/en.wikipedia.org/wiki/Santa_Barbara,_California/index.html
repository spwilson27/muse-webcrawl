Santa Barbara is a coastal city in Santa Barbara County, California, of which it is the county seat. Situated on a south-facing section of coastline, the longest such section on the West Coast of the United States, the city lies between the steeply rising Santa Ynez Mountains and the Pacific Ocean. Santa Barbara's climate is often described as Mediterranean, and the city has been promoted as the "American Riviera". Its population was 88,665 at the 2020 census.

History

The region was home to the Chumash people for over 13,000 years. Sebastián Vizcaíno gave the channel the name Santa Barbara in 1602. A Spanish presidio was founded in 1782, followed by Mission Santa Barbara in 1786, whose present church, built after the 1812 earthquake, is known as the "Queen of the Missions". After the devastating 1925 earthquake, the city rebuilt in a unified Spanish Colonial Revival style, establishing the white stucco walls, red tile roofs, and Mediterranean character for which it is famous. The city was also the site of the 1969 Santa Barbara oil spill, a formative event for the modern environmental movement and the first Earth Day.

Economy and education

Tourism, technology, education, and wine production in the nearby Santa Ynez Valley anchor the economy. The University of California, Santa Barbara, lies immediately west of the city in Isla Vista, and the city hosts Santa Barbara City College and Westmont College nearby. Stearns Wharf, State Street, and the Funk Zone are among its principal visitor districts.
