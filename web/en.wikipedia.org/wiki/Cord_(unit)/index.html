Cord (unit)

The cord is a unit of measure of dry volume used to measure firewood and pulpwood in the United States and Canada. A cord is the amount of wood that, when "racked and well stowed" (arranged so pieces are aligned, parallel, touching and compact), occupies a volume of 128 cubic feet (3.62 m3). This corresponds to a well-stacked woodpile 4 feet (122 cm) high, 8 feet (244 cm) wide, and 4 feet (122 cm) deep; or any other arrangement of linear measurements that yields the same volume.

Cord

Unit system: Imperial / US customary

Unit of: Volume

Symbol: cd

Conversions: 1 cord = 128 ft3 = 3.6246 m3

Definitions

The name "cord" probably comes from the use of a cord or string to measure it. In Canada, the cord is legally defined by Measurement Canada. In the United States, the cord is defined by statute in most states, and the National Institute of Standards and Technology provides guidance for its measurement.

Related measures

A face cord is a stack of wood 4 feet high and 8 feet wide, but of variable depth, typically 16 inches (the common length of a firewood log), giving a volume of about 42.7 cubic feet. Because the depth is not fixed, consumer-protection agencies often discourage selling wood by the face cord. Other informal terms include the rick, the running cord, and the bush cord.

Heating value

The heat content of a cord varies considerably with the species of wood and its moisture content; a cord of dense hardwood such as oak may yield heat roughly equivalent to 200 US gallons of fuel oil, while softwoods yield substantially less.
