The Hunt family is an American business family based in Texas, descended from oil wildcatter H. L. Hunt (1889–1974), who in the 1930s acquired interests in the East Texas Oil Field—the largest oil field then known in the continental United States—and became one of the richest men in the world. The family's holdings span oil and gas, real estate, sports, and hospitality.

Generations

H. L. Hunt had fifteen children by three families, a private arrangement that became public during litigation after his death. His first family, with Lyda Bunker, included Margaret Hunt Hill, oilman and philanthropist H. Hassell "Al" Hunt, Nelson Bunker Hunt, William Herbert Hunt, and sports pioneer Lamar Hunt. Children of his second family included Ray L. Hunt, who built Hunt Consolidated and led Hunt Oil for decades, and June Hunt, a religious broadcaster.

Business and sports

Nelson Bunker and William Herbert Hunt became infamous for attempting to corner the world silver market in 1979–80, culminating in "Silver Thursday" (27 March 1980) and their bankruptcies. Lamar Hunt founded the American Football League and the Dallas Texans/Kansas City Chiefs, coined the term "Super Bowl," co-founded World Championship Tennis and Major League Soccer (the U.S. Open Cup trophy bears his name); his son Clark Hunt now chairs the Chiefs and FC Dallas. Ray Hunt's branch developed Rosewood Hotels & Resorts and major Dallas real estate. Hunt descendants remain collectively among America's wealthiest families, with significant philanthropy through foundations such as the Hillcrest Foundation.
