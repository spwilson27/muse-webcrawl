Royal Mail

Royal Mail Group Limited Founded: 1516 (as a public service)
Headquarters: London, England
Industry: Postal services, courier
Parent: International Distribution Services
Employees: approx. 130,000 (UK)

Royal Mail is a British postal service and courier company, originally established in 1516, which provides mail collection and delivery services throughout the United Kingdom. It is the UK's designated universal postal service provider, obliged to deliver mail to every address in the country at a uniform price six days a week. Royal Mail's distinctive red pillar boxes, stamps bearing the monarch's head, and red delivery vans are among the most recognisable sights in Britain.

History

The service traces its origins to 1516, when Henry VIII appointed a "Master of the Posts", and was made available to the public by Charles I in 1635. The uniform penny post of 1840, championed by Rowland Hill, revolutionised communication by introducing pre-paid postage at a flat rate, accompanied by the world's first adhesive postage stamp, the Penny Black. The Post Office also pioneered services such as the telegram, the telephone network, and the Girobank.

For most of its life Royal Mail was a department of the state, latterly as part of the Post Office corporation. The Postal Services Act 2011 paved the way for privatisation, and the government floated the company on the London Stock Exchange in October 2013, selling its remaining stake by 2015. The parent company was renamed International Distribution Services in 2022; it also owns the international parcels business GLS. The Post Office—the network of post office branches—is a separate company, Post Office Limited, split from Royal Mail in 2012.

Services and challenges

Royal Mail delivers letters and parcels to around 32 million addresses. Letter volumes have fallen dramatically since their peak in the 2000s because of email and digital communication, while parcel volumes have grown with e-commerce. The company has faced intense competition in parcels, disputes over modernisation and working conditions (including major strike action in 2022–23), and ongoing debate about reforming the universal service obligation to reflect declining letter use. In 2024–25 the company was the subject of a takeover by EP Group, controlled by Czech billionaire Daniel Křetínský, approved by shareholders and the government subject to commitments protecting the service's universal character.
