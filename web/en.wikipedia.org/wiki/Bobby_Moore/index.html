Robert Frederick Chelsea Moore OBE (12 April 1941 – 24 February 1993) was an English professional footballer. He most notably played as a defender for West Ham United, captained the England national team to victory in the 1966 FIFA World Cup, and is regarded as one of the greatest defenders of all time. Pelé described him as the greatest defender he had ever played against.

Club career

Moore joined West Ham United in 1956 and made his debut in 1958. Over 16 seasons he made 647 appearances for the club, captaining them to victory in the 1964 FA Cup final and the 1965 European Cup Winners' Cup final. Renowned for his reading of the game, anticipation and immaculate tackling rather than pace or physicality, he captained West Ham for more than a decade. In 1974 he moved to Fulham, reaching the 1975 FA Cup final — against West Ham — and later played in the North American Soccer League with San Antonio Thunder and Seattle Sounders, finishing his career in Denmark with Herning Fremad.

International career

Moore won 108 caps for England between 1962 and 1973, captaining the side a then-record 90 times. Made captain by Alf Ramsey in 1963, he led England at three World Cups. On 30 July 1966 he received the Jules Rimet Trophy from Queen Elizabeth II at Wembley after England's 4–2 extra-time win over West Germany, the enduring image of English football history. His perfectly timed tackle on Jairzinho at the 1970 World Cup in Mexico is often cited as one of the finest ever seen. The same tournament was overshadowed by the "Bogotá Bracelet" incident in Colombia, in which Moore was falsely accused of theft and briefly detained before being cleared.

Later life and legacy

After retiring, Moore managed Oxford City and Southend United and worked as a sports columnist and radio commentator. He died of bowel and liver cancer in 1993, aged 51. He was the first member of the 1966 team to die. A statue of Moore stands outside Wembley Stadium, and another depicting him with Geoff Hurst, Martin Peters and Ray Wilson stands near West Ham's former ground at Upton Park. The Bobby Moore Fund, founded by his widow Stephanie, raises money for Cancer Research UK. Moore was an inaugural inductee into the English Football Hall of Fame in 2002.
