Anseba Region (Tigrinya: ዞባ ዓንሰባ) is one of the six administrative regions (zobas) of Eritrea, located in the north-western part of the country. Its capital is the city of Keren, the second-largest city in Eritrea. The region takes its name from the Anseba River, a seasonal tributary of the Barka River that flows through it.

Anseba is an inland region, bordered by Gash-Barka to the west, the Northern Red Sea Region to the north and east, the Central Region (Maekel) to the south-east, and Sudan to the north-west. Its terrain ranges from highland plateaus around Keren, at roughly 1,400 metres above sea level, down to hot lowland plains in the west.

Geography

The region covers about 23,200 square kilometres. The Anseba River rises in the highlands near Asmara and flows north-west through Keren before joining the Barka. Settlement is concentrated in the cooler highlands, while the western lowlands are sparsely populated and used largely for pastoralism. Wildlife historically found in the region includes elephants along the Gash-Setit lowlands on its borders.

Demographics

The population is ethnically mixed. The principal groups include the Bilen around Keren, the Tigre in the northern and western lowlands, Tigrinya speakers in the south, and smaller communities of Hedareb, Kunama and Rashaida. The main languages are Bilen, Tigre and Tigrinya, and both Christianity and Islam are widely practised.

Administration

The region is divided into sub-regions (sub-zobas) including Keren City, Adi Tekelezan, Elabered, Geleb, Hagaz, Halhal, Habero, Kerkebet, Sel'a and Keru. Regional administrators are appointed by the Eritrean central government.

Economy

Agriculture and livestock raising dominate the economy. The fertile areas around Keren and Elabered produce sorghum, fruits and vegetables, and Keren serves as a trading centre for the surrounding countryside. The Elabered estate is known for citrus and dairy production.
