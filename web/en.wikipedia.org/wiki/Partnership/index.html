Partnership

[Image: two business partners shaking hands across a desk in a small professional office]

A partnership is a business owned and run by two or more people (the partners) who agree to share its profits, losses, management and — in the classic general partnership — its liabilities, each partner being liable for the firm's debts, potentially without limit. It is the standard form for professional firms: law, Accounting, architecture and medicine were historically organised as partnerships.

Forms

Partnerships arise by agreement, formalised (wisely) in a written partnership deed covering capital, profit shares, decision-making and exit; in many jurisdictions (e.g. under the UK Partnership Act 1890 or US state law) a partnership is not a separate legal person distinct from its partners, unlike a Corporation. Variants soften the liability problem: limited partnerships (some partners passive with capped liability — the structure used by hedge and private equity funds) and limited liability partnerships (LLPs), now common for large professional firms. Partnership taxation is usually 'pass-through': the firm itself pays no income tax; partners are taxed individually on their shares.
