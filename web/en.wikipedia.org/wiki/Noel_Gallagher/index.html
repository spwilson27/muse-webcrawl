Noel Gallagher

Noel Thomas David Gallagher (born 29 May 1967) is an English musician, singer and songwriter, best known as the lead guitarist, chief songwriter and occasional lead vocalist of the rock band Oasis, which he formed with his younger brother Liam Gallagher. He later fronted Noel Gallagher's High Flying Birds.

Early life

Gallagher was born in Longsight, Manchester, to Irish parents, and grew up in Burnage. He began playing guitar at thirteen and, after a spell working for a building firm (where an injury to his foot led to a less physically demanding job that gave him time to write songs), he became a roadie and technician for the Inspiral Carpets.

Oasis

In 1991 Gallagher joined his brother's band The Rain, on condition that he take creative control and serve as sole songwriter; the band renamed themselves Oasis. Their debut album Definitely Maybe (1994) became the fastest-selling debut in UK history at the time, and (What's the Story) Morning Glory? (1995) became one of the best-selling albums of all time, propelled by songs such as "Wonderwall" and "Don't Look Back in Anger". The follow-up Be Here Now (1997) was the fastest-selling album in UK chart history on release. Noel's songwriting made him a central figure of the Britpop movement.

Relations between the brothers were famously volatile. Oasis split in 2009 after a backstage altercation in Paris shortly before a festival performance, with Noel announcing he could no longer work with Liam. The band's reunion was announced in 2024, with a world tour beginning in 2025.

High Flying Birds and solo work

Gallagher formed Noel Gallagher's High Flying Birds in 2011; their albums include the self-titled debut (2011), Chasing Yesterday (2015), Who Built the Moon? (2017) and Council Skies (2023).
