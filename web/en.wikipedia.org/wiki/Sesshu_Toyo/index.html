Sesshu Toyo

Born: 1420, Akahama, Bitchū Province, Japan
Died: 1506 (aged 85–86)
Known for: Ink painting (suiboku-ga)
Notable works: Long Scroll of Landscapes; Splashed-ink Landscape (Haboku-Sansui)

Sesshu Toyo (Japanese: 雪舟 等楊; 1420–1506), most commonly called simply Sesshū, was a Japanese Zen monk and painter regarded as the greatest master of Japanese ink painting (suiboku-ga). His work synthesised the Chinese ink traditions he studied first-hand in Ming China with a distinctly Japanese sensibility, and he founded a school of painting that dominated Japanese art for generations.

Life

Sesshū was born in Akahama in Bitchū Province (present-day Okayama Prefecture) and entered the Zen temple of Shōkoku-ji in Kyoto as an acolyte, studying painting under the monk-painter Shūbun. In 1467 he travelled to Ming China as part of a trade mission, spending about two years visiting temples and studying contemporary and Song-dynasty painting; he was received with honour and is said to have painted at the imperial court. On his return to Japan in 1469 he settled eventually in Yamaguchi under the patronage of the Ōuchi clan, establishing his studio, the Unkoku-an, and travelling widely in Japan to sketch real landscapes.

Works

Among his most celebrated works are the Long Scroll of Landscapes (Sansui Chōkan, 1486), a handscroll nearly sixteen metres long depicting the passage of the seasons; the Splashed-ink Landscape (Haboku-Sansui, 1495), executed in the spontaneous "splashed ink" (haboku) manner for his pupil Josui Sōen and accompanied by testimonials from his contemporaries; and views such as the View of Ama-no-Hashidate. His paintings are marked by powerful, angular brushstrokes, strong compositional structure, and a balance of bold ink masses with delicate atmospheric washes.

Legacy

Sesshū's pupils and followers, known collectively as the Unkoku school, preserved his style for centuries, and later masters such as Hasegawa Tōhaku acknowledged his influence. Six of his works are designated National Treasures of Japan. He remains the most famous name in Japanese ink painting, and his name is often used as an honorific comparison for later painters.

This article uses the unaccented rendering "Sesshu Toyo"; the painter is also known under the macronised form Sesshū Tōyō.
