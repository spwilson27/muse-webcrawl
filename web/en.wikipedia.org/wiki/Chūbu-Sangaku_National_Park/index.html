Chūbu-Sangaku National Park (中部山岳国立公園, Chūbu Sangaku Kokuritsu Kōen) is a national park of Japan in the Chūbu region of Honshu, encompassing much of the Hida Mountains, popularly known as the Northern Japanese Alps. It spans the prefectures of Nagano, Gifu, Toyama and Niigata and was designated in 1934, among the first national parks in the country.

Geography

The park protects a rugged chain of peaks around 3,000 metres high, including Mount Hotaka (3,190 m), Mount Yari (3,180 m), Mount Tate, Mount Tsurugi and Mount Shirouma, together with deep gorges such as Kurobe Gorge. The high valley of Kamikōchi, on the Azusa River, is the park's most famous scenic spot and a centre for mountaineering; the Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route crosses the range past the Kurobe Dam.

Nature

Alpine plants, Japanese serow, Asiatic black bear and the rock ptarmigan — a symbol of the park — inhabit its forests of beech and subalpine conifer and its high alpine zone. Volcanic features include the active Mount Yake and the hot springs of Okuhida. The English missionary Walter Weston popularised the region in the late 19th century and is commemorated as the father of mountaineering in Japan.
