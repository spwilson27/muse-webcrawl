Jan Evangelista Purkyne

Jan Evangelista Purkyne (without diacritics: an alternate English spelling of Jan Evangelista Purkyně, Czech: Purkyně, German: Johannes Evangelista Purkinje; 17 December 1787 – 28 July 1869) was a Czech anatomist and physiologist, one of the founders of modern experimental physiology.

Note on spelling

In English-language literature his surname appears both as Purkyně (the correct Czech spelling) and Purkyne. This page covers the same person as Jan Evangelista Purkyně.

Career and discoveries

He described Purkinje cells (1837) and cardiac Purkinje fibres (1839), discovered the Purkinje effect and Purkinje images, introduced the terms plasma and protoplasm, and proposed an early cell theory. He founded the first physiological institute at the University of Breslau and later taught at Charles University in Prague.
