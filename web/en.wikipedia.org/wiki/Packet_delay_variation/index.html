Packet delay variation

In computer networking, packet delay variation (PDV) is the difference in end-to-end one-way delay between selected packets in a flow, commonly understood as the variation of latency over time. In informal usage PDV is often called jitter, although jitter has several distinct technical definitions.

Definitions

The IETF defines several measures. RFC 3393 defines the IP Packet Delay Variation metric based on the difference between the one-way delay of a selected pair of packets. RFC 3550 (RTP) defines an interarrival jitter estimate used in RTCP receiver reports, an exponentially smoothed statistic of packet transit-time differences. ITU-T recommendation Y.1540 also defines packet delay variation for IP service performance.

Causes and effects

PDV arises from queuing in routers, variable path lengths, contention, and serialization effects. It matters most for real-time traffic such as voice over IP and video conferencing, where late-arriving packets cannot be used. Receivers typically use a jitter buffer (playout buffer) to smooth out variation, trading added delay for fewer dropouts. High PDV can also degrade TCP throughput in some conditions.
