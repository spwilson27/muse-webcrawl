Game of the Year

Game of the Year (often abbreviated GotY) is a title awarded annually by numerous video game publications and award ceremonies to the game considered the best of that calendar year. Hundreds of outlets give such awards, and tallying sites aggregate them each season.

Major award ceremonies

  • The Game Awards — produced by Geoff Keighley since 2014, its Game of the Year is voted on by an international jury of media outlets (90%) and public vote (10%). Winners include Dragon Age: Inquisition (2014), The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt (2015), Overwatch (2016), The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild (2017), God of War (2018), Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice (2019), The Last of Us Part II (2020), It Takes Two (2021), Elden Ring (2022), Baldur's Gate 3 (2023) and Astro Bot (2024).
  • Golden Joystick Awards — the longest-running public-voted games awards (since 1983); its Ultimate Game of the Year is chosen entirely by players.
  • D.I.C.E. Awards — the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences' peer-voted honours.
  • BAFTA Games Awards — the British Academy's Best Game award.
  • Game Developers Choice Awards — voted by industry peers at GDC.

Publication awards

Magazines and websites such as Edge, IGN, GameSpot, Eurogamer, Polygon and PC Gamer each name their own Game of the Year, often accompanied by ranked lists. Sites tracking the total number of wins across all outlets act as a form of consensus ranking for each year.

"Game of the Year Edition"

The phrase is also a marketing label: successful games are commonly re-released as a "Game of the Year Edition" bundling the base game with its downloadable content and expansions — examples include The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion, Fallout 3 and The Witcher 3.
