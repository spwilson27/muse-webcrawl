Gulf of Bothnia

Location: Northern Baltic Sea

Bordering countries: Sweden and Finland

Parts: Bothnian Sea (south), Bothnian Bay (north)

The Gulf of Bothnia (Swedish: Bottniska viken; Finnish: Pohjanlahti) is the northernmost arm of the Baltic Sea, lying between Sweden to the west and Finland to the east. It stretches roughly 725 km from the Åland Islands in the south to the head of the Bothnian Bay near Tornio and Kemi.

Geography

The gulf divides into the Bothnian Sea in the south and the Bothnian Bay in the north, separated by the Kvarken strait. Its water is brackish and becomes fresher northwards; the northern bay freezes over for several months each winter. Because of post-glacial land uplift — among the fastest on Earth around the High Coast and Kvarken, a UNESCO World Heritage area — the gulf is slowly shrinking.
