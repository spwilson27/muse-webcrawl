Frans Brüggen

Born30 October 1934, Amsterdam, Netherlands
Died13 August 2014, Amsterdam
OccupationConductor, recorder player, flautist

Frans Brüggen (30 October 1934 – 13 August 2014) was a Dutch conductor, recorder player and baroque flautist. He is regarded as one of the pioneers of the historically informed performance movement and was widely considered the world's leading virtuoso on the recorder during the 20th century.

Life and career

Brüggen studied musicology at the University of Amsterdam and the recorder with Kees Otten at the Amsterdam Conservatory, where he later became a professor. In 1962 he made his debut with the Concertgebouw Orchestra. Unusually for the time, he championed historical instruments and period performance practice, performing on original instruments from the 17th and 18th centuries as well as copies. With Gustav Leonhardt and others he helped establish Amsterdam as a centre of the early music revival.

Orchestra of the 18th Century

In 1981 Brüggen co-founded the Orchestra of the 18th Century, an ensemble of musicians from many countries specialising in music of the Baroque and early Classical periods performed on period instruments. The orchestra toured widely and made many acclaimed recordings of works by composers including Handel, Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven. Brüggen later also conducted modern symphony orchestras, including the Royal Concertgebouw Orchestra, and increasingly the repertoire of the 19th century, with interpretations noted for their period-informed approach.

Recordings and legacy

His discography includes landmark recordings of Bach's Brandenburg Concertos, the orchestral suites, Telemann's recorder concertos and much of the Baroque recorder repertoire. He died in Amsterdam in 2014, aged 79.
