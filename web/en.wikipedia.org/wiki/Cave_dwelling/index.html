Cave dwelling

A cave dwelling is a home constructed within a natural cave or excavated into rock. Cave dwellings are among the oldest forms of human shelter and remain in use in various parts of the world, ranging from natural caves occupied since prehistory to elaborate rock-cut architecture.

History

Natural caves sheltered early humans, as evidenced by sites such as Zhoukoudian in China. Later societies excavated dwellings into soft rock: the yaodong cave houses of China's Loess Plateau still house millions of people, the troglodyte villages of Cappadocia in Turkey were carved into volcanic tuff, and cliff dwellings of the Ancestral Puebloans occupy alcoves in the American Southwest.

Characteristics

Rock provides insulation, keeping interiors cool in summer and warm in winter, and offers protection from weather and enemies. Disadvantages include poor ventilation, dampness, and limited light. Famous examples include Matmata in Tunisia, Coober Pedy in Australia (where homes are dug underground to escape desert heat), and the cave churches and houses of Göreme.
