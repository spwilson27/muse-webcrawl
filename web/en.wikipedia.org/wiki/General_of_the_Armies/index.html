General of the Armies

Country: United States
Branch: United States Army
Rank group: Flag officer
Highest rank of the U.S. Army
Holders: John J. Pershing (1919); George Washington (posthumously, 1976); Ulysses S. Grant (posthumously, 2022/2024)

General of the Armies of the United States, more commonly referred to as General of the Armies, is the highest military rank in the United States Army. The rank has been conferred only three times in American history: upon John J. Pershing in 1919 for his service in World War I, and posthumously upon George Washington in 1976 and Ulysses S. Grant in 2024 (authorized in 2022). The rank is senior to the five-star rank of General of the Army.

John J. Pershing

On 3 September 1919, President Woodrow Wilson signed Public Law 66-45, promoting General John J. Pershing, commander of the American Expeditionary Forces, to the rank of General of the Armies of the United States in recognition of his wartime service. Pershing was permitted to design his own insignia and chose four gold stars. He retired in 1924 and died in 1948, the only holder to wear the rank in his lifetime.

George Washington

During the American Revolutionary War, Washington held the rank of "General and Commander in Chief of the Army of the United Colonies" — in effect a three-star equivalent — and was later commissioned Lieutenant General in 1798. For the United States Bicentennial, Congress passed Public Law 94-479 in January 1976, providing for Washington's posthumous appointment to General of the Armies, with the promotion effective 4 July 1976. The resolution specified that "no officer of the United States Army shall outrank Lieutenant General George Washington", ensuring he remains, in perpetuity, the senior officer of the Army.

Ulysses S. Grant

The James M. Inhofe National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023, signed in December 2022, authorized the President to appoint Ulysses S. Grant posthumously to General of the Armies. Grant, the commanding general who led the Union Army to victory in the Civil War and later the 18th President of the United States, had been the first officer promoted to the revived rank of Lieutenant General in 1864 and then to General of the Army in 1866. The formal appointment was executed in 2024.

Distinction from General of the Army

The singular title "General of the Armies" (plural) is distinct from the five-star rank "General of the Army" (singular), created in December 1944 and held by George C. Marshall, Douglas MacArthur, Dwight D. Eisenhower, Henry H. Arnold, and Omar Bradley. Pershing, as General of the Armies, was senior to all of them.
