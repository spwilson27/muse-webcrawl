Leven, Fife

Leven
Country: Scotland
Council area: Fife
Population: approx. 12,000

Leven (Scottish Gaelic: Inbhir Lìobhann) is a seaside town in Fife, Scotland, located on the north shore of the Firth of Forth at the mouth of the River Leven. Together with the adjoining settlements of Methil and Buckhaven it forms part of the Levenmouth conurbation.

History

Leven grew as a small port and market town, later developing linen weaving and other industries connected with the Fife coalfield. In the Victorian era it became a popular holiday resort, noted for its long sandy beach and two championship-standard golf courses, Leven Links and Lundin Links, which trace their origins to the Old Tom Morris era.

Transport

The town lost its railway station in 1969 as part of the Beeching cuts, but rail services returned with the Levenmouth rail link, which reopened in 2024 with new stations at Leven and Cameron Bridge, reconnecting the town to Edinburgh.

Recreation

Leven beach, the Fife Coastal Path, and the Silverburn parklands make the town a centre for walking and golf tourism in the East Neuk region.
