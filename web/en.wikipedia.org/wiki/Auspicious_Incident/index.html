The Auspicious Incident (or Auspicious Event; Turkish: Vaka-i Hayriye, "the Fortunate Event") was the forced disbandment of the Janissary corps by Ottoman Sultan Mahmud II on 15 June 1826. When the Janissaries mutinied against Mahmud's plan to form a new European-style army, the Sultan's loyal artillery bombarded their barracks in Constantinople, killing some 4,000–6,000 Janissaries and destroying the centuries-old corps that had become the chief obstacle to Ottoman military reform.

The suppression of the Janissary revolt, June 1826.

Background

Once the elite infantry of the empire, the Janissaries had by the 18th century become a hereditary, politically entrenched caste that deposed sultans — including Selim III in 1807 — and blocked reform while losing battles to European armies. Mahmud II spent nearly two decades preparing: securing support from the ulema and the populace, building a loyal artillery corps, and allying with key commanders. In May 1826 he announced a new drilled unit, the Eşkinci, to be drawn partly from the Janissaries themselves.

The revolt and its suppression

On the night of 14–15 June the Janissaries, following their traditional signal of overturning their soup cauldrons, marched on the Hippodrome and demanded the heads of the reformers. Mahmud, advised at the palace, unfurled the Sancak-ı Şerif (the Prophet's banner) and called loyal Muslims to arms. Artillery under Ağa Hüseyin Pasha bombarded the Etmeydanı barracks; fires consumed much of the quarter. Surviving Janissaries were executed, exiled or hunted down in the provinces in following months, the corps was formally abolished, and the affiliated Bektashi order was suppressed.

Consequences

Mahmud replaced the Janissaries with a new army, the Asakir-i Mansure-i Muhammediye ("Victorious Soldiers of Muhammad"), and the Incident cleared the path for the sweeping centralising reforms of the Tanzimat era. Its official name — "Auspicious" — reflects the victors' framing of a massacre as a blessing for the empire.
