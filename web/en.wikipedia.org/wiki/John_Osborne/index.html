John James Osborne (12 December 1929 – 24 December 1994) was an English playwright, screenwriter and actor. His 1956 play Look Back in Anger, first performed at the Royal Court Theatre in London, is credited with transforming British theatre and gave rise to the label "angry young man" for Osborne and his contemporaries.

Look Back in Anger

The play's protagonist, Jimmy Porter, a disaffected university-educated working-class man railing against the complacency of post-war Britain, struck a chord with a new generation. Critic Kenneth Tynan famously declared that he doubted he could love anyone who did not wish to see it. The play launched the English Stage Company's "kitchen sink" era of socially realist drama.

Later work

Osborne followed with The Entertainer (1957), starring Laurence Olivier as the failing music-hall performer Archie Rice, and plays including Luther (1961), Inadmissible Evidence (1964) and A Patriot for Me (1965). He won the Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay for the film Tom Jones (1963). His two volumes of autobiography, A Better Class of Person (1981) and Almost a Gentleman (1991), are noted for their candour and invective.

Osborne was married five times, including to actresses Mary Ure and Jill Bennett. He died of complications from diabetes in 1994.
