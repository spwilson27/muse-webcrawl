The British Medical Association (BMA) is the professional association and trade union for doctors and medical students in the United Kingdom. Founded in 1832 as the Provincial Medical and Surgical Association, it is one of the UK's oldest professional bodies and acts as both a learned society and a negotiating body for its members' pay and conditions.

History

The association was founded in Worcester in 1832 by the surgeon Sir Charles Hastings. It grew through the 19th century, published the British Medical Journal (now The BMJ) from 1840, and played a central role in the campaign for medical registration and the Medical Act 1858, which created the General Medical Council.

Role

The BMA negotiates with the governments of the UK's four nations on behalf of doctors, campaigns on public health issues, and provides members with legal, career and welfare support. Its annual representative meeting is the main policy-making body. The BMA is also a registered trade union; junior doctors' industrial action over pay and working conditions has been a recurring theme, most prominently in 2016 and again in the early 2020s.

Publications

BMJ Publishing Group, a subsidiary of the BMA, publishes The BMJ, one of the world's most influential general medical journals, along with a large portfolio of specialist journals.
