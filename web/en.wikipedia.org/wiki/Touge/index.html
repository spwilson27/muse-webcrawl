Touge

Touge (峠, tōge) is a Japanese word literally meaning "pass" — a mountain pass crossing a mountain ridge. In Japanese car culture, the term came to describe the narrow, winding mountain roads themselves, and the illegal street racing and drifting that took place on them, most famously during the 1980s and 1990s.

Motorsport culture

Touge racing typically involved two cars on a twisty mountain road, racing either in a "cat and mouse" format — where the lead car wins if the gap increases and the chasing car wins if it closes the gap or passes — or in time-attack runs over a set course. Because of the narrow roads and blind corners, grip (non-drifting) racing was common alongside drifting runs.

The culture was popularized internationally by the manga and anime series Initial D, which depicts street racers competing on the mountain passes of Gunma Prefecture, including Mount Haruna (fictionalized as Mount Akina), Mount Akagi, and the Irohazaka slope. Professional drifter Keiichi Tsuchiya, known as the "Drift King", began his career racing on touge roads.

Notable passes

  • Mount Haruna, Gunma Prefecture
  • Irohazaka, Nikkō, Tochigi Prefecture
  • Hakone Turnpike, Kanagawa Prefecture
  • Mount Akagi, Gunma Prefecture
