The Diamond Jubilee of Elizabeth II was a multinational celebration throughout 2012 marking the 60th anniversary of the accession of Queen Elizabeth II on 6 February 1952. Elizabeth was the second British monarch to reach a diamond jubilee, after Queen Victoria in 1897, and the celebrations combined state ceremonies, royal tours, community events and a long extended bank-holiday weekend in the United Kingdom in early June.

Background

Elizabeth II acceded to the throne on the death of her father, George VI, in February 1952, and was crowned in June 1953. By 2012 she was head of state of sixteen Commonwealth realms. A programme of tours was arranged so that members of the royal family visited every realm on the Queen's behalf, while the Queen and the Duke of Edinburgh toured England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Celebrations in the United Kingdom

The central weekend ran from 2 to 5 June 2012. On 2 June the Queen attended the Epsom Derby. On 3 June the Thames Diamond Jubilee Pageant saw a flotilla of about a thousand vessels sail down the River Thames in London, one of the largest such gatherings ever held on the river, with the Queen aboard the royal barge Spirit of Chartwell; the day also saw thousands of street parties and "Big Jubilee Lunches" across the country. On 4 June the Diamond Jubilee Concert, organised by Gary Barlow, was held outside Buckingham Palace, featuring artists including Paul McCartney, Elton John and Stevie Wonder, and more than 4,000 jubilee beacons were lit across the Commonwealth. The weekend concluded on 5 June with a service of thanksgiving at St Paul's Cathedral, a carriage procession and a balcony appearance with a flypast.

Commemorations

A Diamond Jubilee medal was struck for members of the armed forces, emergency services and others in several realms. Places renamed in honour of the jubilee included the clock tower of the Palace of Westminster, renamed the Elizabeth Tower, and the city status granted to several towns. Commemorative issues of stamps and coins were released across the Commonwealth.

Legacy

The Diamond Jubilee was widely seen as a high point of the Queen's popularity in the United Kingdom. Elizabeth went on to become the longest-reigning British monarch in September 2015 and celebrated her Platinum Jubilee in 2022, a few months before her death in September of that year.
