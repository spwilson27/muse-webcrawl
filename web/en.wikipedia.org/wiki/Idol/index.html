Idol

An idol is an image or representation of a god used as an object of worship. In Abrahamic religions, the term is associated with idolatry — the worship of something other than God — and such practices are generally prohibited.

Religious contexts

In Hinduism, images (murti) are treated as manifestations of the divine and play a central role in worship (puja). In East Asian Buddhism, statues of buddhas and bodhisattvas serve as objects of devotion and meditation. In ancient Greek, Roman, Egyptian and Mesopotamian religion, cult statues housed in temples received offerings.

Etymology

The word derives through Latin idolum from Greek εἴδωλον (eidolon), "image, phantom". In ordinary modern usage, "idol" may also denote a person greatly admired, such as a pop idol.
