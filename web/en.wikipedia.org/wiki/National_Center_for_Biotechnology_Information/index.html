National Center for Biotechnology Information

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) is part of the United States National Library of Medicine (NLM), itself a branch of the National Institutes of Health (NIH). It is located in Bethesda, Maryland, and was founded in 1988 through legislation sponsored by US Congressman Claude Pepper.

The NCBI houses a series of databases relevant to biotechnology and biomedicine and is an important resource for bioinformatics tools and services. Major databases include GenBank for DNA sequences and PubMed, a bibliographic database for biomedical literature. Other databases maintained by the NCBI include the NCBI Epigenomics database. All these databases are available online through the Entrez search engine.

History

NCBI was established by the Health Omnibus Programs Extension Act of 1988 as a division of the National Library of Medicine. Its founding director was David Lipman, a physician and computational biologist who led the centre from 1989 to 2017 and was one of the original authors of BLAST. Under Lipman the centre grew into the principal public repository of molecular biology data in the United States.

Databases and tools

  • GenBank — the NIH genetic sequence database, an annotated collection of all publicly available DNA sequences, maintained in collaboration with the DNA Data Bank of Japan and the European Nucleotide Archive.
  • PubMed — free search access to MEDLINE and other life-science citations; PubMed Central provides a free full-text archive of journal articles.
  • BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool) — compares nucleotide or protein sequences against sequence databases and calculates the statistical significance of matches.
  • Entrez — a cross-database retrieval system linking sequences, structures, genomes, taxonomy and literature.
  • Gene, Protein, Nucleotide, Taxonomy, GEO, SRA and ClinVar — specialised archives for genes, proteins, expression data, sequence reads and clinical variants.
  • Bookshelf — a collection of freely accessible online books and documents in life science and healthcare.

Mission

The NCBI's mission is to develop new information technologies to aid in the understanding of fundamental molecular and genetic processes that control health and disease, and to provide automated systems for storing and analysing knowledge about molecular biology, biochemistry and genetics.
