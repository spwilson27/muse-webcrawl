East Francia

East Francia (Latin: Francia orientalis) or the Kingdom of the East Franks (Regnum Francorum orientalium) was a successor state of Charlemagne's empire, created by the Treaty of Verdun in 843 and ruled by Louis the German. It comprised the eastern territories of the Carolingian Empire — including the stem duchies of Saxony, Franconia, Bavaria, Swabia, and later Lotharingia — and is generally regarded as the precursor of the medieval Kingdom of Germany and, ultimately, of modern Germany.

Formation

After the death of Louis the Pious in 840, civil war among his sons ended with the Treaty of Verdun (843), which divided the empire three ways: Charles the Bald received West Francia, Lothair I the Middle Kingdom, and Louis the German the lands east of the Rhine. Louis ruled from 843 to 876 and consolidated royal authority despite Viking and Slavic pressure on the frontiers.

Later Carolingians and transition

Under Louis's sons the kingdom was subdivided, and Charles the Fat briefly reunited the empire (884–887) before his deposition. Arnulf of Carinthia and the child king Louis the Child were the last East Frankish Carolingians. On Louis's death in 911, the magnates elected Conrad of Franconia, and in 919 Henry the Fowler, Duke of Saxony, founding the Ottonian dynasty. Henry's son Otto I was crowned emperor in 962, an event often taken as the beginning of the Holy Roman Empire.
