Periochae (Livy)

Periochae

Author: Anonymous (summarising Livy)

Subject: Ab Urbe Condita by Livy

Coverage: All 142 books (except 136–137)

Date: Late antiquity (probably 4th century AD)

The Periochae are ancient summaries (epitomes) of the Roman historian Livy's monumental history Ab Urbe Condita ("From the Founding of the City"). Livy's work originally comprised 142 books covering Roman history from the legendary founding of Rome to the reign of Augustus, but only 35 books survive complete. The Periochae preserve brief summaries of the contents of nearly all the books, including the 107 that are otherwise lost.

Content and value

Each periocha is a compressed list of the main events, speeches and prodigies of one book, sometimes only a few lines long. Despite their brevity, they are invaluable to historians: they provide the narrative outline of the lost books, covering events such as the later Roman civil wars and the reign of Augustus for which Livy's account is otherwise missing. Together with fragments, the Florus epitome and the Oxyrhynchus epitome, they allow partial reconstruction of the lost history.

Transmission

The summaries were probably composed in late antiquity and were transmitted alongside the surviving books of Livy in the manuscript tradition. The Periochae for books 136 and 137 are missing.
