Significant wave height

In physical oceanography, the significant wave height (Hs or Hsig) is defined traditionally as the mean height (trough to crest) of the highest third of waves in a given wave record. It is the standard measure reported in marine weather forecasts and by wave buoys.

Definition

The statistician Walter Munk proposed the definition during the Second World War, finding that the "significant height" best matched the wave height a trained observer would report visually. In modern practice, significant wave height is usually estimated from the wave spectrum:

Hm0 = 4√m0

where m0 is the zeroth moment (variance) of the surface-elevation spectrum. For a narrow-band Gaussian sea the two definitions agree closely.

Distribution and extremes

Individual wave heights approximately follow a Rayleigh distribution; in a record of a thousand waves the largest is expected to reach roughly 1.9 times Hs. Mariners' reports of rogue waves concern individual waves of at least twice the significant height.

Measurement

Significant wave height is measured by moored wave buoys, estimated from satellite altimetry, and hindcast by numerical wave models such as WAVEWATCH III. Values above 13 m occur in severe North Atlantic and Southern Ocean storms.
