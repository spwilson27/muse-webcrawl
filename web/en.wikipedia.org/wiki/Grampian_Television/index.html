Grampian Television

Grampian Television was the ITV franchise holder for northern Scotland, broadcasting from 1961 until its on-air identity was replaced by STV in 2006. The company, named after the Grampian mountain region, served Aberdeen, Inverness, Dundee and the Highlands and Islands — the largest geographic franchise area in the ITV network, though one of the smallest by population.

History

The franchise was awarded by the Independent Television Authority to North of Scotland Television Limited, a consortium of local business interests, and the station launched on 30 September 1961 from converted tramway-depot studios at Queen's Cross, Aberdeen, taking the name Grampian Television shortly before launch. Grampian survived the franchise rounds of 1967 and 1980 and won the 1991 auction with a modest bid, largely because it faced no rival applicant.

In June 1997 Grampian was bought by the Scottish Media Group (SMG), owner of Scottish Television. SMG progressively centralised operations; in May 2006 both SMG stations were rebranded as STV, with the Aberdeen service becoming STV North, ending 45 years of the Grampian name on screen though the legal licence name continues. The Queen's Cross studios were vacated in 2003 for smaller premises in the city's Tullos area.

Programming

Grampian produced regional news — its flagship bulletin North Tonight (launched 1980, later STV News at Six) was a consistent ratings success — as well as Scots and Gaelic programming, documentaries and light entertainment. Network contributions were modest but included the long-running children's favourite, through its successors' co-productions, and Grampian supplied the first series of some networked programmes filmed in Scotland. Its idents — notably the stylised Saltire "G" of the 1980s and the thistle ident — are fondly remembered by viewers in the region.

Transmission

The region's main transmitters included Durris (Aberdeen), Knockmore, Rosemarkie (Inverness), Angus (Dundee) and Eitshal (Stornoway), with dozens of relays serving the Highlands and Islands — a transmission challenge compounded by the mountainous terrain and sparse population.
