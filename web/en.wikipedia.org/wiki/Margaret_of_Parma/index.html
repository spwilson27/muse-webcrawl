Margaret of Parma (5 July 1522 – 18 January 1586) was the Governor of the Habsburg Netherlands from 1559 to 1567 and again, nominally, from 1578 to 1582. She was the illegitimate daughter of Emperor Charles V and his Flemish servant Johanna Maria van der Gheynst. Her governorship coincided with the opening phase of the Dutch Revolt against Spanish rule.

Early life and marriages

Margaret was raised in the Netherlands under the care of her great-aunt Margaret of Austria and later Mary of Hungary, both former regents of the Netherlands. Charles V acknowledged her as his daughter and arranged advantageous marriages: first to Alessandro de' Medici, Duke of Florence, who was assassinated in 1537, and then to Ottavio Farnese, Duke of Parma. Her son Alexander Farnese became one of the leading military commanders of the age.

Governor of the Netherlands

In 1559 her half-brother Philip II of Spain appointed her governor-general of the Netherlands. Although real authority was exercised by the Council of State and advisers such as Cardinal Granvelle, Margaret faced mounting opposition from the Netherlandish nobility — including William of Orange, Count Egmont and Count Horn — over religious persecution, Spanish troops and centralising policies. In April 1566 she received the petition of the Compromise of Nobles; an adviser reportedly dismissed the petitioners as "beggars" (gueux), a name the opposition adopted with pride.

The iconoclastic fury and resignation

In August 1566 the Iconoclastic Fury (Beeldenstorm) swept the Netherlands, with Calvinist crowds destroying images in churches. Margaret restored order temporarily, but Philip II resolved on military intervention under the Duke of Alba. Judging her position untenable, Margaret resigned and departed for Italy in late 1567 as Alba's regime of repression began. She was nominally reappointed co-governor with her son Alexander in 1578 but returned to Italy in 1582 and died at Ortona in 1586. See also Dutch Revolt and Philip II of Spain.
