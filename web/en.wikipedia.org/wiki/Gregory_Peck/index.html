Gregory Peck

Eldred Gregory Peck (April 5, 1916 – June 12, 2003) was an American actor and one of the most popular film stars from the 1940s to the 1970s. He won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his portrayal of Atticus Finch in To Kill a Mockingbird (1962), a role the American Film Institute later named the greatest hero in American film.

Career

Born in San Diego, California, Peck studied at the University of California, Berkeley, before training as an actor in New York. He made his film debut in 1944 and quickly became a leading man, earning Oscar nominations for The Keys of the Kingdom (1944), The Yearling (1946), Gentleman's Agreement (1947) and Twelve O'Clock High (1949). His major films include Hitchcock's Spellbound (1945), Duel in the Sun (1946), Roman Holiday (1953) opposite Audrey Hepburn, Moby Dick (1956), The Guns of Navarone (1961), Cape Fear (1962) and The Omen (1976). His Atticus Finch, a principled small-town lawyer defending a Black man falsely accused of rape, became an enduring emblem of integrity.

Public life

Peck was a prominent liberal activist, supporting civil rights and gun control and opposing the Vietnam War and McCarthy-era blacklisting. He served as president of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1969. He died in Los Angeles in 2003 at the age of 87.
