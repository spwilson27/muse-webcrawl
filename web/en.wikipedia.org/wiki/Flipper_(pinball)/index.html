Flipper (pinball)

Flipper
TypePinball machine component
Introduced1947 (Humpty Dumpty)
ActuationSolenoid, player-operated buttons

A flipper is a mechanical lever in a pinball machine that the player uses to strike the ball and keep it in play. Modern machines typically have two flippers near the bottom of the playfield, angled inward, controlled by buttons on the sides of the cabinet. Some games add extra flippers higher on the playfield.

History

Flippers were introduced in 1947 with Gottlieb's Humpty Dumpty, designed by Harry Mabs, which had six flippers powered by solenoids. Their invention transformed pinball from a largely chance-based game into a game of skill — a shift that helped overturn legal bans that had classified pinball as gambling. Early flippers were small ("zipper flippers" could close the gap between them), and by the 1970s the standard three-inch bat shape had become universal.

Technique

Skilled players use techniques such as dead flipper passes, live catches, drop catches and cradling to control the ball. The gap between the flippers, combined with the outlanes, determines how forgiving a machine is.
