The Quadrantids (QUA) are a meteor shower that peaks in early January. They are among the year's strongest showers, with zenithal hourly rates comparable to the Perseids and Geminids, but their peak is unusually sharp — lasting only a few hours — and January weather in northern latitudes often hides them.

Name and radiant

The shower is named after the obsolete constellation Quadrans Muralis, the "mural quadrant", which lay between Boötes and Draco and appeared on star charts from 1795 into the 19th century. The radiant now lies in northern Boötes, near the handle of the Plough. When the International Astronomical Union adopted its official list of meteor showers in 2009, the name Quadrantids was retained despite the constellation's abolition.

Parent body

The parent is generally identified as asteroid 2003 EH1, itself probably a fragment of the comet C/1490 Y1, observed in China, Korea and Japan in 1490–91. The narrowness of the stream suggests it is only a few hundred years old.
