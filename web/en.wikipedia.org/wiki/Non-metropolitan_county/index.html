Non-metropolitan county

A non-metropolitan county, or colloquially a shire county, is a county in England that is not a metropolitan county. The non-metropolitan counties were established by the Local Government Act 1972 and form the top tier of a two-tier local government structure in most of England outside London and the metropolitan areas. The term is used in legislation to distinguish these counties from the six metropolitan counties created for the large conurbations.

Administration

In the traditional two-tier arrangement, a non-metropolitan county council provides services such as education, social services, highways, libraries, and waste disposal, while district councils within the county handle housing, planning applications, and refuse collection. Since the 1990s, many parts of the shire counties have been reorganised into unitary authorities, where a single council performs both tiers of functions. Some unitary areas remain legally part of a non-metropolitan county for ceremonial purposes but have their own councils independent of the county council; Berkshire, uniquely, is a non-metropolitan county with no county council at all, its districts being unitary.

List and characteristics

There are 21 non-metropolitan counties with county councils following the 2023 reorganisations (which created new unitary councils in Cumbria, North Yorkshire, and Somerset), including counties such as Kent, Hampshire, Devon, Lancashire, Essex, Staffordshire, and Northumberland. Their combined areas are commonly described as the "shire counties", largely rural or mixed in character compared with the metropolitan counties. Each non-metropolitan county is also a ceremonial county (or shares its boundaries with one) with a lord-lieutenant and high sheriff.
