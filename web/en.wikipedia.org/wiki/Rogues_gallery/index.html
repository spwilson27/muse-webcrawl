Rogues gallery

A rogues gallery (or rogues' gallery) is a police collection of mug shots or other images of criminal suspects kept for identification purposes. The term dates to the nineteenth century, when photography first allowed police forces to assemble albums of known offenders for study by detectives and victims.

History

One of the earliest collections was assembled in the 1850s by Allan Pinkerton, whose detective agency photographed and catalogued criminals. In New York, police inspector Thomas F. Byrnes published Professional Criminals of America (1886), a printed rogues gallery of portraits and biographies of notorious offenders. Similar albums were kept by police departments across the United States, Britain and continental Europe; the practice complemented the Bertillon system of measurements and later fingerprinting.

Modern usage

Today the term is used loosely for any display of photographs of wanted or arrested persons — in police stations, post offices, and online "most wanted" lists — and figuratively for any collection of disreputable characters. In fiction, particularly comics, a hero's recurring set of villains is often called a "rogues gallery", as with Batman's adversaries.
