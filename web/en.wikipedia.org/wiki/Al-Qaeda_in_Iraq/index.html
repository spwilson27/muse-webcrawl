Al-Qaeda in Iraq

Al-Qaeda in Iraq (AQI)
Active: 2004 – 2006 (as AQI)
Predecessor: Jama'at al-Tawhid wal-Jihad
Successor: Islamic State of Iraq
Leaders: Abu Musab al-Zarqawi (until 2006); Abu Ayyub al-Masri
Area: Iraq

Al-Qaeda in Iraq (Arabic: القاعدة في العراق, al-Qāʿidah fī al-ʿIrāq), often abbreviated AQI, was a Salafi jihadist organisation that played a major role in the Iraqi insurgency following the 2003 invasion of Iraq. It was formed in 2004 when Abu Musab al-Zarqawi's Jama'at al-Tawhid wal-Jihad pledged allegiance to Osama bin Laden and al-Qaeda, becoming its Iraqi affiliate.

History

AQI became notorious for suicide bombings, beheadings of hostages, and attacks on Shia civilians and religious sites — most notably the 2006 bombing of the al-Askari mosque in Samarra, which helped ignite a sectarian civil war. Zarqawi was killed in a US airstrike in June 2006. In October 2006, the group announced the establishment of the Islamic State of Iraq (ISI), absorbing AQI's identity.

Decline and successors

From 2007, the Sunni "Awakening" movement and the US troop surge sharply weakened the organisation in Iraq. Its successor, ISI, later expanded into Syria and in 2013–2014 evolved into the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL/ISIS), which broke with al-Qaeda and declared a caliphate in 2014.
