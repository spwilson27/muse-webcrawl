The Beaufort family is an English noble family that rose to prominence in the 14th and 15th centuries. The name Beaufort refers to the castle of Beaufort in Champagne, France, once a possession of John of Gaunt, Duke of Lancaster. The Beauforts were Gaunt's children by his mistress and later third wife, Katherine Swynford; they were legitimised by royal and papal decrees in 1397, though Henry IV later barred them from succession to the throne.

Role in the Wars of the Roses

The Beauforts became leading supporters of the House of Lancaster during the Wars of the Roses. John Beaufort, 1st Duke of Somerset, and his brother Edmund Beaufort, 2nd Duke of Somerset, were key Lancastrian commanders; Edmund was killed at the First Battle of St Albans in 1455, and his son Henry Beaufort, 3rd Duke of Somerset, was executed after the Battle of Hexham in 1464.

Descent to the Tudors

Lady Margaret Beaufort, daughter of John Beaufort, 1st Duke of Somerset, married Edmund Tudor and was the mother of Henry VII, whose victory at the Battle of Bosworth in 1485 founded the Tudor dynasty — a claim to the throne that descended through the Beaufort line. The present-day Beaufort line continues through the Dukes of Beaufort, descended from the Somerset family of Raglan.
