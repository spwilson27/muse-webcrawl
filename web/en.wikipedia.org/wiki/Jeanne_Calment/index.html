Jeanne Calment

Jeanne Calment at age 120, the oldest verified person in history

Jeanne Louise Calment (21 February 1875 – 4 August 1997) was a French supercentenarian who holds the record for the longest verified human lifespan: 122 years and 164 days. She lived her entire life in Arles, in the south of France.

Life

Calment was born into a prosperous family of millers and shopkeepers. In 1888, as a teenager working in her uncle's fabric shop, she met the young Vincent van Gogh, whom she later described as dirty, badly dressed, and disagreeable. In 1896 she married her double second cousin Fernand Calment, a wealthy shop owner, whose fortune allowed her a life of leisure devoted to tennis, cycling, swimming, piano, and opera. She outlived her husband (died 1942), her only daughter Yvonne (died 1934), and her grandson (died 1963).

Fame in old age

In 1965, at age 90 and with no heirs, Calment signed a viager contract selling her apartment to lawyer André-François Raffray, who agreed to pay her a monthly allowance until her death; she outlived him by two years, and his family continued payments exceeding the apartment's value. She took up fencing at 85, rode a bicycle until 100, and lived independently until nearly 110. Verified by exhaustive documentary research, her longevity attracted worldwide media attention; she attributed it to olive oil, port wine, chocolate, and a calm temperament.

Verification debate

Her age record was confirmed by demographers Jean-Marie Robine and Michel Allard through civil records. In 2018 a Russian researcher proposed that Jeanne had died in 1934 and that her daughter Yvonne had assumed her identity, but subsequent studies by French and international demographers re-examined the documents and rejected the hypothesis, reaffirming Calment's record.
