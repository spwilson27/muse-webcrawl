The Secret of Kells is a 2009 animated fantasy film directed by Tomm Moore and co-directed by Nora Twomey, produced by the Irish studio Cartoon Saloon. Set in 9th-century Ireland at the Abbey of Kells, the film follows the young monk Brendan as he helps complete the illuminated manuscript that became the real Book of Kells, under the shadow of impending Viking raids.

Plot

Brendan lives at the abbey under his stern uncle, Abbot Cellach, who has abandoned the scriptorium to build a great wall against the Northmen. When Brother Aidan of Iona arrives with the unfinished book and his cat Pangur Bán, Brendan is apprenticed to him and ventures into the forbidden forest to gather gall nuts for ink, befriending the fairy Aisling. He braves the darkness of the serpent-god Crom Cruach to obtain a crystal "eye" for the book's finest illumination. The Vikings breach the wall and sack the abbey; Cellach is wounded but survives, and years later the grown Brendan returns to show the completed Book of Kells to his aged uncle.

Style and themes

The film's hand-drawn animation deliberately imitates the art of Insular illuminated manuscripts — flat compositions, interlacing knotwork, and spiral motifs inspired by the Book of Kells itself and early Irish art. Its music is by Bruno Coulais, with contributions from the Irish band Kíla. The story explores the conflict between fear-driven protection and the preservation of art and culture.

Reception

The film premiered at the Gérardmer Film Festival and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature in 2010, a surprise nomination that brought international attention to Cartoon Saloon. It is the first film in Moore's "Irish folklore trilogy," followed by Song of the Sea (2014) and Wolfwalkers (2020).
