Melisma

In music, a melisma (from Greek μέλισμα, "song" or "melody") is the singing of a single syllable of text while moving between several different notes in succession. Music sung in this style is described as melismatic, in contrast to syllabic singing, in which each syllable is matched to a single note.

Usage

Melisma is a prominent feature of Gregorian chant — the long melismas of the Alleluia are famous examples — and of Byzantine, Syriac, and other liturgical traditions. It is central to Middle Eastern, South Asian and flamenco vocal styles, to Baroque coloratura arias, and to gospel, R&B and contemporary pop singing, where extended runs on a single syllable (for example, on the word "I" in many ballads) are a common expressive device. Handel's Messiah contains celebrated melismas, such as the setting of "and with His stripes we are healed".
