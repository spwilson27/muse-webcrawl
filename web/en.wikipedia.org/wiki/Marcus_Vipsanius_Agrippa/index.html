Marcus Vipsanius Agrippa (c. 63 BC – 12 BC) was a Roman general, statesman and architect, the closest friend, son-in-law and minister of the emperor Augustus. He was responsible for most of Octavian's military victories, most notably winning the naval battles of Naulochus (36 BC) against Sextus Pompey and Actium (31 BC) against Mark Antony and Cleopatra, which secured Augustus's supremacy over the Roman world.

Early life and rise

Of modest, possibly equestrian family origin, Agrippa was educated alongside Octavian (the future Augustus) and accompanied him in the campaigns that followed Julius Caesar's assassination in 44 BC. Though lacking aristocratic pedigree, his military talent and loyalty made him Octavian's indispensable right hand. He campaigned successfully in Gaul and Germany, crossing the Rhine, and served as consul in 37 BC.

Military achievements

Agrippa's greatest triumphs were naval. He built the harbour of Portus Julius near Baiae to train a fleet, invented the harpax grappling device, and defeated Sextus Pompey at Naulochus in 36 BC. At Actium on 2 September 31 BC, Agrippa's fleet blockaded and destroyed the forces of Antony and Cleopatra, decisively ending the civil wars.

Public works and administration

As aedile in 33 BC Agrippa undertook a vast programme of public works in Rome: repairing aqueducts, building the Aqua Julia, and later the Aqua Virgo (19 BC) and the Baths of Agrippa. He built the original Pantheon in Rome (27–25 BC); the surviving building, rebuilt by Hadrian, still bears Agrippa's name in its inscription. He also produced a geographical survey of the empire and a map displayed in the Porticus Vipsania. Augustus granted him extraordinary honours, including tribunician power, and married him to his daughter Julia in 21 BC; their children included Gaius and Lucius Caesar, Agrippina the Elder (mother of Caligula), and Agrippa Postumus.

Death

Agrippa died in Campania in 12 BC, aged about 51. Augustus delivered his funeral eulogy and buried him in the Mausoleum of Augustus. His organisational and military genius was widely credited as the foundation on which Augustus built the Principate.
