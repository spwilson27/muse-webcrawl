Seiko

Seiko Group Corporation, commonly known simply as Seiko (セイコー), is a Japanese manufacturer of watches, clocks, electronic devices, semiconductors, jewellery, and optical products. Founded in 1881 by Kintarō Hattori in Tokyo, Seiko is one of the most vertically integrated watch manufacturers in the world and is credited with producing the first commercial quartz wristwatch.

History

Kintarō Hattori opened the K. Hattori shop in Ginza in 1881, selling and repairing clocks and watches. In 1892 he established the Seikosha factory, whose name derived from seiko, meaning "exquisite" or "success". Seikosha produced Japan's first wristwatch, the Laurel, in 1913, and the first watch under the Seiko brand name appeared in 1924.

The quartz revolution

On 25 December 1969, Seiko launched the Astron 35SQ, the world's first production quartz wristwatch. Far more accurate than mechanical movements, quartz technology transformed the industry in the ensuing "quartz crisis", in which Seiko was a leading force. Seiko later developed the Spring Drive movement, a hybrid combining a mainspring with electronic quartz regulation, and Kinetic movements powered by the wearer's motion.

Brands and timing

The group's watch brands include Seiko, Grand Seiko, Credor, King Seiko, and the luxury and fashion labels it produces for others. Seiko also owns Orient Watch. Beyond horology, Seiko Epson — spun off from the group — became a major printer and electronics manufacturer. Seiko has served as official timekeeper for numerous Olympic Games and World Cups, and its precision timing equipment is widely used in sport.
