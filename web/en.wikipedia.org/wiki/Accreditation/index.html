Accreditation

Accreditation is the independent, third-party evaluation of a conformity assessment body (such as a certification body, inspection body, or testing laboratory) or of an institution or programme, against recognised standards, to confirm its competence to carry out specific tasks. Accreditation differs from certification: certification attests that a product, service, or person meets a standard, while accreditation attests that the certifier itself is competent.

Fields

In higher education, accreditation assures that institutions and programmes meet quality standards; in the United States it also gates access to federal student aid. Healthcare accreditation evaluates hospitals against patient-safety standards. Laboratories seek accreditation to standards such as ISO/IEC 17025 to demonstrate the technical validity of their results, and accreditation bodies themselves operate under ISO/IEC 17011.

Process

A typical process involves application, document review, an on-site assessment by peer evaluators, correction of nonconformities, and a decision by an accreditation committee, followed by periodic surveillance and full reassessment every few years. International mutual recognition arrangements, such as those of the International Accreditation Forum and ILAC, allow accredited results to be accepted across borders.
