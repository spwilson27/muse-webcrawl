Prescribed burn

A prescribed burn, also known as a controlled burn or hazard-reduction burn, is the planned application of fire to vegetation under specified conditions of fuel, weather, and topography to achieve management objectives. It is used in forestry, agriculture, and ecosystem restoration to reduce hazardous fuels, control pests and disease, recycle nutrients, and promote fire-adapted native species.

Purposes

Many ecosystems — including longleaf pine savannas, tallgrass prairies, and ponderosa pine forests — evolved with frequent low-intensity fire. Prescribed burning restores this process, limiting the fuel accumulation that contributes to severe wildfires near communities.

Planning and safety

Burns follow a written prescription covering wind, humidity, fuel moisture, crew, equipment, and containment lines; smoke management is a major consideration near populated areas. Despite precautions, escapes occasionally occur, most notoriously the 2000 Cerro Grande Fire in New Mexico, which began as a prescribed burn and destroyed hundreds of homes in Los Alamos.

Indigenous burning

Indigenous peoples in Australia, North America, and elsewhere used cultural burning for millennia, knowledge increasingly incorporated into modern fire management.
