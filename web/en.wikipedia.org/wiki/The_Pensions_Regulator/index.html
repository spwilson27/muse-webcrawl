The Pensions Regulator (TPR) is the United Kingdom's regulator of work-based pension schemes. A non-departmental public body sponsored by the Department for Work and Pensions, it was established under the Pensions Act 2004 and began operating on 6 April 2005, replacing the Occupational Pensions Regulatory Authority (Opra). Its statutory objectives include protecting the benefits of members of occupational pension schemes, protecting the Pension Protection Fund, promoting the good administration of work-based schemes, and (since the Pensions Act 2014 period reforms) maximising employer compliance with automatic enrolment duties.

Functions and powers

The regulator oversees defined benefit and defined contribution occupational schemes and public service pension schemes, and supervises the employers who sponsor them. It sets standards through codes of practice, collects scheme returns and levy data, and publishes guidance. The Pensions Act 2004 gave it a suite of enforcement tools, including improvement and third-party notices, fines, the power to appoint trustees, winding-up orders, and — for defined benefit schemes — "moral hazard" powers such as contribution notices and financial support directions aimed at employers or connected parties who seek to avoid their pension obligations. The Pension Schemes Act 2021 substantially expanded these powers, introducing new criminal offences (including avoidance of employer debt and conduct risking members' accrued benefits, punishable by up to seven years' imprisonment), new civil penalties of up to £1 million, and strengthened information-gathering and scheme-funding requirements.

Automatic enrolment

TPR is responsible for ensuring that employers comply with the automatic enrolment programme rolled out from 2012 under the Pensions Act 2008, under which employers must enrol eligible workers into a qualifying pension scheme and make minimum contributions. The regulator runs declaration-of-compliance processes and has issued compliance and penalty notices to large numbers of employers, mostly small businesses, that missed their duties.

Notable cases

The regulator attracted significant attention for its handling of the collapse of the retailer BHS in 2016, after which it reached a £363 million settlement with Sir Philip Green to support the BHS pension schemes, and for its work leading to the rescue settlement for the Toys "R" Us scheme and the restructuring of the Carillion and other major schemes. Its approach has at times been criticised by parliamentary committees as insufficiently proactive, prompting a shift, announced from 2018 onward, toward clearer, quicker and tougher enforcement, and a new supervisory model engaging proactively with the largest schemes and master trusts. The regulator also authorises and supervises defined contribution master trusts under an authorisation regime introduced in 2018.
