Macworld is a digital magazine and website dedicated to products and software of Apple Inc., previously published as a print magazine. Founded in 1984, the same year Apple launched the Macintosh computer, it was for decades one of the best-known publications covering the Mac platform, with editions in numerous countries.

History

The magazine was launched in 1984 by IDG, with David Bunnell as publisher and Andrew Fluegelman as its first editor. Early issues famously featured Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak. In 1997 the magazine absorbed its rival MacUser following a joint venture between IDG and Ziff-Davis. In September 2014 the US print edition ceased and much of the editorial staff was laid off; Macworld continued as a web-only publication under IDG and later Foundry.

Macworld Conference & Expo

The Macworld Conference & Expo (later Macworld/iWorld) was a trade show held annually in January in San Francisco, closely associated with the magazine though owned separately. Apple used its keynotes for major product launches, including the iPhone in 2007. Apple withdrew after 2009, and the event was discontinued after 2014.

Content

Macworld publishes news, reviews and benchmarking of Mac computers, iPhones, iPads and accessories, along with how-to features and buying guides. Its product ratings historically used a scale of mice icons.
