Jon Fosse

BornSeptember 29, 1959, Haugesund, Norway
OccupationNovelist, playwright, poet, essayist

Jon Olav Fosse (born 29 September 1959) is a Norwegian author, translator and playwright. One of the most widely performed contemporary dramatists in the world, his plays have been staged in more than a thousand productions internationally. In 2023 he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature "for his innovative plays and prose which give voice to the unsayable."

Life and work

Fosse was born in Haugesund and grew up in Strandebarm on the Hardangerfjord in western Norway. He writes in Nynorsk. He made his literary debut with the novel Raudt, svart ("Red, Black") in 1983 and had his breakthrough as a dramatist with Someone Is Going to Come (1996) and The Name (1995).

His plays — including Death Variations, A Summer's Day, Dream of Autumn and I Am the Wind — are characterised by spare, repetitive dialogue, long pauses and an intense, minimalist lyricism often compared to Samuel Beckett and Harold Pinter. His major prose works include the novels Melancholy I–II, Morning and Evening, Aliss at the Fire, Trilogy (winner of the Nordic Council Literature Prize in 2015) and the seven-volume Septology (2019–2021), written as a single flowing sentence.

Honours

Fosse lives in the Grotten, an honorary residence provided by the Norwegian state in Oslo. He converted to Catholicism in 2012, an influence reflected in his later work. In 2007 he was made a chevalier of the French Ordre national du Mérite.
