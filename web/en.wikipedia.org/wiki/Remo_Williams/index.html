Remo Williams

Remo Williams is a fictional character, the protagonist of The Destroyer series of men's adventure novels, created by Warren Murphy and Richard Sapir in 1963 and first published in 1971. Williams is a former Newark police officer and Marine Corps veteran who is framed for murder, "executed", and then recruited by a secret government agency, CURE, to work as an assassin against enemies the law cannot touch.

Character biography

Recruited by CURE director Dr Harold W. Smith, Williams is trained in the fictional Korean martial art of Sinanju by its elderly master, Chiun, who becomes a surrogate father to him. Sinanju is presented as the "sun source" of all martial arts, giving its masters superhuman speed, strength and control of the body. The stories blend action, satire and political comedy, with Chiun's disdain for Western culture — and his love of daytime soap operas and Barbra Streisand — providing much of the humour.

The Destroyer series

The series ran to more than 150 novels. Murphy and Sapir wrote the early books together; later volumes were written by Murphy alone and by a succession of ghost writers including Will Murray and James Mullaney. Spin-off series and reboots have appeared under the Legacy and New Destroyer imprints.

Adaptations

The character was played by Fred Ward in the 1985 film Remo Williams: The Adventure Begins, which received an Academy Award nomination for its make-up (Carl Fullerton) and has become a cult favourite. A 1988 television pilot, Remo Williams: The Prophecy, starred Jeffrey Meek. Director Shane Black was attached to a new film adaptation announced in 2014.
