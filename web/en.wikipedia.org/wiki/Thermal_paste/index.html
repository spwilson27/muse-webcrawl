Thermal paste

[Hero image placeholder: a pea-sized dot of thermal paste applied to a CPU integrated heat spreader.]

Thermal paste (also called thermal compound, thermal grease, heat paste, or thermal interface material, TIM) is a thermally conductive (but usually electrically insulating) chemical compound commonly used as an interface between heat sinks and heat sources such as high-power semiconductor devices.

Purpose

The main role of thermal paste is to eliminate air gaps or spaces (which act as thermal insulators) from the interface area, maximising heat transfer and dissipation. Even apparently smooth surfaces have microscopic irregularities that trap air; filling them with a compound of higher thermal conductivity than air greatly improves cooling.

Composition

Thermal pastes consist of a polymerisable liquid matrix (often silicone) filled with thermally conductive particles such as zinc oxide, aluminium oxide, aluminium nitride, boron nitride, or silver. Metal-based pastes have higher conductivity but are electrically conductive and capacitive, requiring care around exposed contacts. Liquid metal compounds based on gallium alloys offer the highest performance but can corrode aluminium.

Application

A thin layer is applied to the heat source before mounting the heat sink; too little leaves voids, while too much can impede transfer. In personal computing, thermal paste is routinely applied between a CPU or GPU and its cooler.
