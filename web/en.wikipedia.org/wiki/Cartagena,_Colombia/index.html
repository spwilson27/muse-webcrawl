Cartagena, Colombia

Cartagena, officially Cartagena de Indias, is a major port city on Colombia's Caribbean coast, capital of the Bolívar Department. Founded 1 June 1533 by Pedro de Heredia and named after Cartagena, Spain, it was a key port for trade between Spain and its overseas empire and a centre of early Spanish settlement in the Americas.

History

After repeated pirate attacks, notably by Francis Drake in 1586, Spain built extensive fortifications — the walled old town and the Castillo San Felipe de Barajas — among the most complete in South America and now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Cartagena declared independence from Spain on 11 November 1811 and endured a brutal siege in 1815 by Pablo Morillo.

Geography and economy

The city has a tropical savanna climate, average temperature around 28°C. Its economy rests on shipping, petrochemicals, and tourism drawn by the colonial old town, Bocagrande beach district and nearby Rosario Islands. Population is roughly one million.
