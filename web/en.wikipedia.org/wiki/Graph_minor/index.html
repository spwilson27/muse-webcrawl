Graph minor

In graph theory, an undirected graph H is a minor of a graph G if H can be formed from G by deleting edges and vertices and by contracting edges. Minors generalise the notion of a subgraph and are fundamental to the structural theory of graphs.

OperationsVertex deletion, edge deletion, edge contraction

Theory

Kuratowski's theorem characterises planar graphs as those containing neither K₅ nor K₃,₃ as a minor. The Robertson–Seymour theorem (proved over a series of twenty papers published between 1983 and 2004) states that graphs are well-quasi-ordered under the minor relation: every class of graphs closed under taking minors can be characterised by a finite set of forbidden minors. A major consequence is that membership in any such class can be tested in polynomial time.

Hadwiger's conjecture

One of the deepest open problems in the area, Hadwiger's conjecture, proposes that any graph with no Kt minor is (t−1)-colourable, generalising the four-colour theorem.
