County top

DefinitionHighest point of a county
Related pastimeHighpointing / peak bagging
Highest (UK)Ben Nevis (historic Inverness-shire), 1,345 m

A county top is the highest point in a county. The term is used particularly in the United Kingdom and Ireland for the summit or highest ground of each historic or ceremonial county, and collecting visits to all of them — sometimes called county topping — is a recognised branch of the hobby of highpointing, parallel to the American pursuit of state high points.

In the United Kingdom

British county tops vary wildly in character. The highest is Ben Nevis in the Scottish Highlands; England's is Scafell Pike in the Lake District; Wales's is Snowdon (Yr Wyddfa); and Northern Ireland's is Slieve Donard in the Mourne Mountains. At the other extreme, the historic county top of Huntingdonshire (Boring Field, 80 m) is an unremarkable field, and several lowland counties' highest points are modest hills or even urban ground. Lists differ depending on whether historic, ceremonial or administrative counties are used, boundary disputes occasionally arise, and some tops lie on private land or on the borders shared by two counties.

Highpointing

County topping belongs to the family of "peak bagging" lists such as the Munros, Wainwrights and Marilyns. Completing all the county tops of England, Scotland, Wales or Ireland is a long-term project recorded by enthusiasts' associations and websites devoted to hill lists. In the United States, the Highpointers Club tracks ascents of the highest point of each state, from Denali in Alaska to Florida's Britton Hill (105 m).
