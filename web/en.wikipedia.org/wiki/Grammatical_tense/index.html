Grammatical tense

In grammar, tense is a category that expresses time reference. Tenses are usually shown by changes in the form of the verb, and locate an event or state in the past, present, or future relative to the moment of speaking.

Grammatical tense
Basic tensesPast, present, future
Related categoriesAspect, mood
Not universalSome languages (e.g. Mandarin) lack tense

Overview

Many languages distinguish only two tenses — past and non-past, or future and non-future — while others mark finer distinctions such as remote and recent past. English is usually analysed as having two morphological tenses (past and present), with futurity expressed by auxiliaries such as will. Combinations of tense with aspect yield forms like the present perfect and past progressive.

Tense systems

Some languages, including Mandarin Chinese and many Austronesian languages, have no grammatical tense at all, relying on adverbs and context to locate events in time. In tense-promoting analyses, distinctions such as absolute versus relative tense describe whether the reference point is the speech moment or another event.
