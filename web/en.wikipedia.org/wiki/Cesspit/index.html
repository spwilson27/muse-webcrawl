Cesspit

A cesspit collects household wastewater in a sealed underground pit.

A cesspit (or cesspool) is a sealed underground pit or tank used for the temporary collection and storage of sewage and wastewater from a building not connected to a public sewer. Unlike a septic tank, a cesspit provides no treatment: it merely stores waste until it is emptied by a vacuum tanker, and in modern designs it must be watertight so that no effluent seeps into the surrounding soil.

History

Cesspits were standard in cities before sewerage; in London and Paris, night soil men emptied them by hand, and their overflow and leaching contributed to cholera and typhoid outbreaks in the 19th century. The introduction of water closets initially worsened the problem by greatly increasing the volume of waste, prompting the construction of municipal sewers.

Modern use and regulation

Today cesspits are used mainly where neither mains drainage nor a septic system is feasible. Because stored sewage can pollute groundwater, many jurisdictions restrict new cesspits, require sealed construction and regular emptying, and in some places (for example Hawaii) mandate conversion of older cesspools to septic or sewer systems.
