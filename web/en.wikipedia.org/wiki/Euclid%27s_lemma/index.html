Euclid's lemma

Euclid's lemma is a fundamental result in number theory, stated in Book VII of Euclid's Elements. In modern terms: if a prime number p divides a product ab of two integers, then p divides a or p divides b (or both).

Statement and uses

The lemma is the key step in proving the uniqueness of prime factorization, the fundamental theorem of arithmetic: every integer greater than 1 factors into primes in exactly one way, apart from order. It also underlies the theory of unique factorization domains in abstract algebra.

Proof

A standard proof uses Bézout's identity: if p divides ab but not a, then gcd(p, a) = 1, so there exist integers x and y with px + ay = 1. Multiplying by b gives pbx + aby = b; since p divides both terms on the left, p divides b.

Generalization

The property generalizes: in any integral domain, an element satisfying this divisibility property is called a prime element, distinguished from irreducible elements, and the two notions coincide in unique factorization domains such as the integers.
