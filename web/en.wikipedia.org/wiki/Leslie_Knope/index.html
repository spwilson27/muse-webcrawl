Leslie Barbara Knope is a fictional character in the NBC comedy series Parks and Recreation, portrayed by Amy Poehler. She is the main character of the show, a passionate and relentlessly optimistic bureaucrat in the Parks and Recreation Department of the fictional town of Pawnee, Indiana. Over the course of the series she rises from deputy director to director of the department, is elected to the Pawnee City Council, becomes a regional director of the National Park Service, and is eventually revealed to become Governor of Indiana.

Characterisation

Leslie is characterised by her boundless enthusiasm for local government, encyclopaedic preparation (famously expressed through colour-coded binders), fierce loyalty to her friends and colleagues, a prodigious sweet tooth — particularly for waffles and whipped cream — and an idolisation of powerful women politicians such as Hillary Clinton, Madeleine Albright and Nancy Pelosi. Poehler's performance won the Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Television Series – Musical or Comedy in 2014, along with multiple Emmy nominations.

Storylines

In the first season Leslie campaigns to convert a construction pit beside the home of nurse Ann Perkins into a park; Ann becomes her best friend and the project becomes a career-defining quest, fulfilled when Lot 48 becomes Pawnee Commons. Leslie runs for city council in season 4 against Bobby Newport, wins, survives a recall vote in season 5, and marries state auditor-turned-city-manager Ben Wyatt, with whom she has triplets. In season 6–7 she battles her former boss Ron Swanson over the Morningstar development, becomes Regional Director of the National Park Service's Midwest division, and launches a bid for governor of Indiana. The series finale flash-forwards show her as governor and hint at a possible future presidential run, while she and Ben are both courted as potential candidates.

Reception

The character evolved from a somewhat ditzy figure in the first season into one of television's most admired comic protagonists, frequently cited as an emblem of earnest, ambitious, civic-minded feminism. Critics praised the show's writing for softening Leslie's early abrasive optimism into warmth without losing her competence and drive.
