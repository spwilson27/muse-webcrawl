Land restoration

Land restoration, also called land reclamation in some contexts and closely related to ecological restoration, is the process of returning degraded land to a productive or ecologically functional state. Degradation may result from mining, overgrazing, deforestation, intensive agriculture, soil erosion, salinisation, industrial contamination or urban development. Restoration activities range from regrading and re-vegetating former mine sites to rebuilding soil organic matter on exhausted farmland and reconnecting fragmented habitats.

Methods

Common techniques include earthworks to stabilise slopes and restore drainage; spreading of stockpiled topsoil or manufactured soil substitutes; amelioration of acidity, salinity or contamination (including phytoremediation, using plants to extract or immobilise pollutants); direct seeding and planting of locally native species; control of invasive species and grazing pressure; and rewetting drained wetlands and peatlands. On agricultural land, restoration shades into regenerative agriculture — cover cropping, agroforestry and reduced tillage to rebuild soil carbon and structure. Successful projects define clear reference ecosystems or land-use goals, use genetic material suited to site conditions, and monitor outcomes over years to decades, adapting management as vegetation develops.

Scale and policy

Land degradation affects roughly a quarter of the Earth's land surface. International initiatives include the UN Convention to Combat Desertification's goal of land degradation neutrality, the Bonn Challenge (launched 2011) to restore 350 million hectares of deforested and degraded land by 2030, its regional offshoots such as AFR100 and Initiative 20x20, and the UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration (2021–2030). Many jurisdictions legally require restoration bonds and rehabilitation plans for mines, quarries and landfills; China's Sloping Land Conversion ("Grain for Green") programme is among the largest state restoration efforts, paying farmers to convert steep cropland to forest and grassland. Restoration is also promoted for its carbon sequestration, flood mitigation and rural employment benefits, though critics warn against monoculture plantations presented as restoration.
