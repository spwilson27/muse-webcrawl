Social media marketing

Social media marketing combines content, community management and paid advertising
TypeDigital marketing
Major platformsFacebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, LinkedIn, Pinterest
Key activitiesContent marketing, community management, influencer marketing, social advertising, social listening
Common metricsReach, engagement rate, click-through rate, conversions, follower growth

Social media marketing is the use of social media platforms and websites to promote a product, service or brand. It encompasses creating and sharing content (text, images, video and live streams) to achieve branding and marketing goals, managing an online community, running paid advertising campaigns targeted using platform data, and analysing audience behaviour. Because social media is interactive, marketing on it is typically two-way, in contrast to the one-way broadcast model of traditional advertising.

History

Brands began experimenting with social platforms in the mid-2000s as sites such as Facebook, YouTube and Twitter grew rapidly. Facebook introduced advertising in 2007 and business Pages soon after; over the following decade the platforms added sophisticated ad-targeting, analytics and e-commerce tools, and social media became one of the largest channels in digital advertising, rivalling search advertising in total spend. The rise of short-form video on TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts reshaped campaign formats in the early 2020s.

Strategies and tactics

Core tactics include content marketing (planning content calendars around audience interests), influencer marketing (paying creators to endorse products to their followers), social advertising (sponsored posts targeted by demographics, interests and behaviour), social listening (monitoring mentions and sentiment), and customer service through direct messages and comments. Campaigns aim variously at brand awareness, lead generation, website traffic, app installs or direct sales, and are measured against metrics such as engagement rate and return on ad spend.

Regulation and criticism

Social media marketing is subject to advertising-disclosure rules: regulators such as the US Federal Trade Commission and the UK Advertising Standards Authority require sponsored content and influencer endorsements to be clearly labelled. The field has been criticised for privacy-invasive targeting (highlighted by the Cambridge Analytica scandal), for astroturfing and fake followers, and for the mental-health and misinformation effects of engagement-optimised platforms.
