History of American football

The history of American football traces the sport's evolution from English rugby and association football in the colleges of the northeastern United States in the late 19th century to the modern professional game governed by the National Football League.

Origins

The first intercollegiate game is generally dated to 6 November 1869, when Rutgers defeated Princeton 6–4 under soccer-like rules. In the 1870s Harvard adopted rugby-style rules; a key turning point was the 1875 Harvard–Yale match and the adoption of rugby-derived rules at the Massasoit conventions. Walter Camp of Yale, the "Father of American Football", introduced decisive innovations in the 1880s: the line of scrimmage, the snap, the downs system, and the 11-man team.

Reform and professionalization

The brutality of mass-momentum plays caused a crisis: 18 deaths in 1905 prompted President Theodore Roosevelt to intervene, leading to the legalization of the forward pass in 1906 and other safety reforms. Professional football grew from club teams in Pennsylvania and Ohio; the National Football League was founded in 1920 in Canton, Ohio. The college game dominated popularity until televised pro football boomed after the celebrated 1958 NFL Championship Game.

Modern era

The NFL merged with the rival American Football League in the 1960s–70, creating the Super Bowl era. Rule changes favouring passing, improved protective equipment, free agency and vast television contracts made the sport the most popular in the United States, while recent decades have brought heightened attention to concussion and player safety.
