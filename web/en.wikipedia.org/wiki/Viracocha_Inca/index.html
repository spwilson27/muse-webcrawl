Viracocha Inca (Quechua: Wiraqucha Inka) was the eighth Sapa Inca of the Kingdom of Cusco, reigning from about 1410 until 1438. Born Hatun Túpac, he took the name of the creator god Viracocha after, according to tradition, receiving a vision of the deity promising victory in battle.

Reign

Viracocha extended Cusco's influence through campaigns and alliances, subjugating peoples around Lake Titicaca and in the surrounding highlands, and is remembered for administrative and building works. Late in his reign, the Chanka people attacked Cusco. According to the chroniclers, Viracocha and his designated heir Urco withdrew from the capital, leaving its defence to another son, Cusi Yupanqui, who defeated the Chanka and afterwards took power as Pachacuti — the ruler credited with transforming the kingdom into the Inca Empire. Viracocha died in exile or retirement at his estate of Caquia Xaquixaguana shortly after.

Sources

Accounts of his reign come from Spanish colonial chroniclers such as Juan de Betanzos and Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa, who drew on Inca oral tradition; details and dates vary between sources and remain debated by historians.
