Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps

Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps

Founded: 5 May 1979

Allegiance: Supreme Leader of Iran

Branches: Ground Forces, Aerospace Force, Navy, Quds Force, Basij

Persian name: Sepâh-e Pâsdârân

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC; Persian: سپاه پاسداران انقلاب اسلامی), also known as the Sepah or Pasdaran, is a multi-service primary branch of the Iranian Armed Forces. It was officially established by Ruhollah Khomeini as a military branch in May 1979, in the aftermath of the Iranian Revolution, to protect the new Islamic republican system and counterbalance the regular armed forces (Artesh).

Structure

The IRGC includes ground forces, an aerospace force operating Iran's ballistic missile program, a navy tasked with operations in the Persian Gulf, the Quds Force responsible for extraterritorial operations, and the Basij paramilitary militia. Its commander-in-chief is appointed by the Supreme Leader.

Role and influence

Beyond its military duties, the IRGC exerts major influence over Iran's economy and politics, with affiliated engineering and commercial conglomerates such as Khatam-al Anbiya. Several countries, including the United States since 2019, designate the IRGC as a terrorist organization, a characterization Iran rejects.
