Tây Sơn dynasty

The Tây Sơn dynasty (Vietnamese: Nhà Tây Sơn, 1778–1802) was a Vietnamese dynasty founded by three brothers from the village of Tây Sơn in Bình Định province. The Tây Sơn rebellion overthrew the ruling Nguyễn and Trịnh lords and briefly unified Vietnam after centuries of division. The dynasty is best known for the victories of its most famous leader, Nguyễn Huệ, crowned Emperor Quang Trung.

Origins

The rebellion began in 1771 under the brothers Nguyễn Nhạc, Nguyễn Huệ and Nguyễn Lữ, amid heavy taxation and corruption under the Nguyễn lord Nguyễn Phúc Thuần. The Tây Sơn movement drew support from peasants, merchants, and some ethnic minorities, and by 1777 had destroyed the Nguyễn regime in the south.

Unification and Quang Trung

In 1786 Tây Sơn forces marched north and overthrew the Trịnh lords, nominally restoring the Lê emperor. In 1788 Nguyễn Huệ proclaimed himself Emperor Quang Trung. When the Qing Empire invaded Vietnam to restore the Lê, Quang Trung destroyed the Qing army in the great victory of Ngọc Hồi–Đống Đa at Tết (Lunar New Year) 1789, securing Vietnamese independence.

Reforms and fall

Quang Trung promoted the chữ Nôm script for official use, reformed taxation and education, and encouraged trade. His sudden death in 1792, at about age 40, left the throne to the young Quang Toản, under whom the dynasty weakened. The surviving Nguyễn heir, Nguyễn Phúc Ánh, backed by French auxiliaries, captured Saigon and then the north, founding the Nguyễn dynasty in 1802 and ending the Tây Sơn.
