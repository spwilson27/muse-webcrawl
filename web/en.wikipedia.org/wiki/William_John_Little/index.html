William John Little (6 August 1810 – 7 July 1894) was an English surgeon regarded as the founder of orthopaedic surgery in Great Britain. He gave the first clinical description of spastic diplegia, a form of cerebral palsy long known as "Little's disease".

Early life and training

Little was born in London and himself suffered a childhood illness (thought to have been poliomyelitis) that left him with a club foot. After medical studies in London and Paris, he travelled to Berlin to study under Louis Stromeyer, who had pioneered subcutaneous tenotomy — the operation Little underwent on his own foot and then introduced to England.

Career

Appointed to the London Hospital in 1839, Little founded the Orthopaedic Institution in Bloomsbury in 1840 — the first specialist orthopaedic hospital in Britain, later the Royal National Orthopaedic Hospital. His 1853 work on deformities and later lectures described the condition of spastic rigidity in children associated with difficult birth, laying groundwork for the study of cerebral palsy.

Legacy

Little helped establish orthopaedics as a distinct specialty and influenced generations of surgeons, including his pupil William Adams. "Little's disease" remained the common term for spastic cerebral palsy well into the twentieth century.
