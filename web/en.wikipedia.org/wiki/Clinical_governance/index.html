Clinical governance

Clinical governance is a systematic approach to maintaining and improving the quality of patient care within a health system. The term was popularised in the United Kingdom with the 1997 white paper The New NHS: Modern, Dependable and the 1998 consultation document A First Class Service, which made clinical governance a statutory duty of National Health Service organisations and their chief executives. The classic definition, by Gabriel Scally and Liam Donaldson (1998), describes it as:

"A framework through which NHS organisations are accountable for continually improving the quality of their services and safeguarding high standards of care by creating an environment in which excellence in clinical care will flourish."

Components

Clinical governance is commonly described as resting on several pillars or components:

  • Clinical effectiveness — care based on evidence, using guidelines such as those of NICE;
  • Clinical audit — systematic review of care against explicit standards, with changes implemented and re-audited;
  • Risk management — systems for identifying, reporting, and learning from adverse incidents and near misses;
  • Education and training — continuing professional development and appraisal of staff;
  • Patient and public involvement — incorporating patient experience, complaints, and feedback into service improvement;
  • Information management — accurate records and use of data to monitor quality;
  • Staffing and staff management — safe staffing levels and a culture in which quality concerns can be raised.

Significance

Clinical governance shifted responsibility for quality from individual clinicians alone to the organisation as a whole, giving boards and chief executives explicit accountability for the standard of clinical care in the same way as for financial performance. The framework has been adapted, under the same or similar names, in health systems outside the UK, including Australia and New Zealand. High-profile failures of care, such as those investigated at Bristol Royal Infirmary and Mid Staffordshire, are frequently analysed in terms of breakdowns of clinical governance.
