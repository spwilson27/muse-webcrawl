Algebraic normal form

In Boolean algebra, the algebraic normal form (ANF), also known as the Zhegalkin normal form or Reed–Muller expansion, is a way of writing logical formulas in one of three subforms: as a single XOR (exclusive disjunction) of conjunctions of literals, possibly with an additional constant 1. In this representation the only operations are AND and XOR, and no complemented literals appear separately — negation is expressed using XOR with 1.

Properties

Every Boolean function has a unique algebraic normal form. The highest degree of any term (the largest number of variables multiplied together) is called the algebraic degree of the function. ANF can be computed from a truth table using the Möbius transform over the Boolean lattice, an algorithm analogous to the fast Fourier transform.

Applications

Algebraic normal form is fundamental in cryptography: the resistance of block ciphers and S-boxes to algebraic cryptanalysis and higher-order differential attacks depends on the algebraic degree and structure of their component functions. It is also used in coding theory in connection with Reed–Muller codes.
