Consumerism

Consumerism is a social and economic order that encourages the acquisition of goods and services in ever-increasing amounts. The term is used in several distinct senses: as the organised movement for consumer protection; as the ideology that personal well-being and happiness depend on consumption (sometimes called consumer culture or materialism); and, in economics, as the view that consumer choice should guide economic policy.

History

Historians often trace a "consumer revolution" to 18th-century Britain, where expanding markets, advertising and emulation of aristocratic fashions spread among the middle classes. Thorstein Veblen's The Theory of the Leisure Class (1899) introduced "conspicuous consumption" to describe status display through goods. Mass production, department stores, mail-order catalogues and new media in the late 19th and 20th centuries made consumption central to industrial economies; after the Second World War, suburbanisation, television advertising and consumer credit entrenched it in the United States and, subsequently, worldwide.

Criticism

Critics from John Kenneth Galbraith (The Affluent Society, 1958, with its "dependence effect") to the Frankfurt School argued that producers manufacture the wants they satisfy. Vance Packard's The Hidden Persuaders (1957) attacked manipulative advertising, and The Waste Makers (1960) criticised planned obsolescence. Environmental critics add that high-consumption lifestyles deplete resources and generate waste and emissions, giving rise to counter-movements such as voluntary simplicity, Buy Nothing Day and degrowth.

Consumer protection sense

In a second, positive sense, "consumerism" refers to advocacy for the rights and interests of consumers — product safety, honest labelling, redress for defective goods — associated with figures such as Ralph Nader and with legislation and consumer organisations from the mid-20th century onward. President John F. Kennedy's 1962 Consumer Bill of Rights articulated rights to safety, information, choice and to be heard.
