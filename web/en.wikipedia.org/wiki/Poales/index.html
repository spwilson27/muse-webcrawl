Poales

Wheat (Triticum aestivum), Poaceae

The Poales are a large order of flowering plants in the monocotyledons, and include families such as the grasses (Poaceae), sedges (Cyperaceae), rushes (Juncaceae), and bromeliads (Bromeliaceae). By species count and ecological dominance, Poales is one of the most important plant orders on Earth.

Composition

In the APG IV classification the order contains 14 families and roughly 18,000 species. The grass family alone has about 12,000 species and dominates savannas, steppes, and prairies; it supplies humanity's staple cereals – wheat, rice, maize, barley, sorghum, and oats – as well as sugarcane and bamboo. Other economically significant members include the pineapple (Bromeliaceae), the sedges (which include papyrus), and the cattails or bulrushes (Typhaceae).

Evolution and characteristics

Most Poales share features such as silica bodies in their leaves, small, wind-pollinated flowers in many lineages, and embryos with distinctive structure; the order is placed among the commelinid monocots. Molecular evidence dates the origin of the group to the Late Cretaceous, but the great ecological expansion of grasslands came much later, in the Cenozoic, transforming terrestrial ecosystems and the evolution of grazing animals.
