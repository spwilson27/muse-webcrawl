Setar
ClassificationString instrument (plucked lute)
OriginIran (Persia)

RelatedSitar, dutar, tanbur

The setar (Persian: سه‌تار, from se, "three", and tār, "string") is an Iranian musical instrument, a member of the lute family, used in Persian classical music and in the traditions of neighbouring regions. It has a small, deep, pear-shaped soundbox of mulberry wood, a long neck with movable gut or nylon frets (allowing the microtones of the Persian dastgah system), and — despite its name ("three strings") — four strings in its modern form, a fourth bass string having been added in the 19th century.

Playing and repertoire

The setar is played with the index fingernail of the right hand, producing a soft, intimate sound suited to solo performance and small gatherings; it is one of the principal instruments for the Persian classical repertoire (radif). Its sound is quieter and more delicate than the related tar. The Indian sitar is thought to derive its name and some features partly from the Persian instrument, though the instruments differ substantially.
