Applejack is a strong alcoholic drink produced from apples. Popular in the American colonial era, it was traditionally made by the process of freeze distillation — also called "jacking" — in which hard cider was left out in the winter cold and the ice that formed (mostly water) was repeatedly removed, concentrating the alcohol in the remaining liquid. The name derives from this process: jack referred to the method of raising the drink's strength by freezing. Modern commercial applejack in the United States is generally a blend of apple brandy (distilled in a still) and neutral grain spirits.

History

Applejack was first produced in colonial New Jersey, where the Scotsman William Laird settled in Monmouth County around 1698; his descendants founded Laird & Company in 1780, the oldest licensed distillery in the United States, which famously supplied applejack to George Washington, who had requested the recipe. In the colonial period applejack was used as currency to pay road construction crews, giving rise to the nickname "Jersey lightning" and to road names such as Applejack Road. Abraham Lincoln served it during his brief time as a tavern keeper in New Salem, Illinois.

Freeze distillation concentrates not only ethanol but also methanol and fusel oils, which contributed to applejack's reputation for severe hangovers — the "apple palsy" or "applejack rabbit" of nineteenth-century accounts. Modern regulation in the United States defines applejack as a blend containing at least 20% apple brandy aged in oak, combined with neutral spirits, coloured and bottled at no less than 40% alcohol by volume; pure pot-distilled versions are labelled apple brandy.

Production

Traditional jacking required no fuel or still: barrels of cider were exposed to freezing temperatures, and the ice was skimmed off over successive nights, potentially raising the proof dramatically. Because the process was cheap and easy, applejack became the archetypal frontier spirit. Contemporary craft producers have revived both freeze-distilled and pot-distilled apple spirits, though most large-scale "applejack" sold today is the blended style created by Laird's in the mid-twentieth century to compete with lighter spirits.

Cocktails

Applejack is the base of the Jack Rose cocktail (applejack, lemon or lime juice, and grenadine), popular in the 1920s and mentioned in Hemingway's The Sun Also Rises. It also appears in drinks such as the Applejack Rabbit (with maple syrup and citrus) and the Pink Lady in some early recipes.
