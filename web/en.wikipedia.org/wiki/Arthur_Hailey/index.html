Arthur Frederick Hailey (5 April 1920 – 24 November 2004) was a British-Canadian novelist whose meticulously researched, multi-strand novels set inside large institutions and industries sold more than 170 million copies worldwide. His books — including Hotel (1965), Airport (1968) and Wheels (1971) — were translated into some 40 languages and repeatedly adapted for film and television.


Hailey in his later years

Life and career

Born in Luton, Hailey served as a Royal Air Force pilot in the Second World War and settled in Canada in 1947. He began writing television drama, breaking through with Flight into Danger (1956), before turning to novels. For each book he spent roughly a year researching the milieu — airlines, hotels, the automobile industry, banking, pharmaceuticals, the news media — embedding fictional characters in realistically rendered settings.

Works and adaptations

Airport was adapted into the hit 1970 film that launched the disaster-movie cycle; Hotel became a long-running television series (1983–88), and The Moneychangers (1975) and Strong Medicine (1984) were also televised. Later novels included Evening News (1990) and Detective (1997). Critics sometimes faulted his prose as workmanlike, but his storytelling and insider detail commanded a vast readership.

Later life

Hailey moved to the Bahamas in the 1970s, where he lived at Lyford Cay. He died in Nassau in 2004, aged 84.
