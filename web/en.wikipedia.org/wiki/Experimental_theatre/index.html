Experimental theatre

Experimental theatre (also called avant-garde theatre) is theatre that challenges conventional forms of drama, staging, and the relationship between performers and audience. Emerging in the late 19th and early 20th centuries alongside modernism, it reacts against realism and the commercial stage.

Important currents include Alfred Jarry's Ubu Roi (1896), the work of Antonin Artaud and his Theatre of Cruelty, Bertolt Brecht's epic theatre, the Theatre of the Absurd of Samuel Beckett and Eugène Ionesco, and post-war groups such as the Living Theatre, Jerzy Grotowski's Laboratory Theatre, and the Wooster Group. Common techniques include improvisation, breaking the fourth wall, site-specific and immersive staging, multimedia, and devised rather than scripted work.

Experimental theatre overlaps with performance art and fringe theatre, and has strongly influenced mainstream practice.
