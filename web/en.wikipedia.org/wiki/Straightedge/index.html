Straightedge

A machinist's straightedge

A straightedge or straight edge is a tool with an accurately straight or flat edge, used for drawing straight lines, or for checking the straightness or flatness of surfaces. Unlike a ruler, a straightedge need not carry measurement markings; long precision straightedges are used in engineering metrology to test machine surfaces, often in combination with feeler gauges or engineers' blue.

Types

Straightedges range from simple wooden or plastic drawing edges to hardened steel or granite metrology instruments. Machinists' straightedges are typically made of steel with a precisely ground or lapped edge, sometimes with a bevelled working edge. In surface finishing, screeds act as straightedges for levelling concrete or plaster.

In geometry

In classical geometry, the "straightedge" of compass-and-straightedge constructions is an idealised tool: infinitely long, with a single edge, and without markings, used only to draw the line through two existing points. Several famous problems—trisecting an angle, doubling the cube, and squaring the circle—were eventually proved impossible with these idealised tools alone. Constructions performed with a straightedge alone, given a single circle and its centre, are the subject of the Poncelet–Steiner theorem.
