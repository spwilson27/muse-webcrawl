Contemporary classical music

Contemporary classical music is classical music composed in the late 20th and 21st centuries — broadly the period after 1945 or after 1975, depending on the definition — embracing a pluralism of styles after the dominance of modernism.

History

Post-war composition was dominated by avant-garde currents: serialism at Darmstadt, aleatoric and experimental music of John Cage, and musique concrète. From the 1970s, reactions against academic modernism produced minimalism, spectralism, new romanticism and postminimalism.

Styles and movements

  • Minimalism — Riley, Reich, Glass, Adams: pulse, repetition, gradual process.
  • Spectralism — Grisey, Murail: harmony derived from acoustic spectra.
  • New Complexity — Ferneyhough: extreme notational density.
  • Post-minimalism and "indie classical" — blending with rock and electronic music.

Notable composers

Prominent contemporary figures include Arvo Pärt, Kaija Saariaho, Thomas Adès, Unsuk Chin, George Benjamin and Caroline Shaw. Major venues include Donaueschingen, IRCAM and the Huddersfield Contemporary Music Festival.
