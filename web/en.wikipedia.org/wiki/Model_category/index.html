In mathematics, particularly in homotopy theory, a model category is a category with distinguished classes of morphisms — called weak equivalences, fibrations, and cofibrations — satisfying a set of axioms that abstract the essential features of topological spaces and continuous maps. The notion was introduced by Daniel Quillen in his 1967 work Homotopical Algebra as an axiomatic framework for doing homotopy theory in settings far removed from topology.

Definition

A model category is a category with finite limits and colimits, equipped with three classes of morphisms (weak equivalences, fibrations, cofibrations) such that: weak equivalences satisfy the two-out-of-three property; all three classes are closed under retracts; cofibrations have the left lifting property against trivial fibrations, and trivial cofibrations have the left lifting property against fibrations; and every morphism can be functorially (in Quillen's original formulation, not necessarily functorially) factored both as a cofibration followed by a trivial fibration, and as a trivial cofibration followed by a fibration.

Examples

The motivating example is the category of topological spaces, where weak equivalences are weak homotopy equivalences, fibrations are Serre fibrations, and cofibrations are retracts of relative cell complexes. Other fundamental examples include chain complexes of modules (giving rise to homological algebra via the projective and injective model structures), simplicial sets, categories of spectra, simplicial presheaves, and categories of diagrams. Model structures are generally not unique: the category of spaces also carries the Hurewicz (Strøm) model structure.

Homotopy category

From a model category one constructs its homotopy category by formally inverting the weak equivalences. Using cofibrant and fibrant replacement, mapping spaces and derived functors (such as left and right derived functors of Quillen adjunctions) can be defined systematically, unifying constructions that in topology give homology, cohomology, and homotopy groups.

Significance

Model categories underpin modern derived algebraic geometry, stable homotopy theory, and the theory of infinity-categories, to which they provide a concrete, computable presentation. Quillen's framework is regarded as one of the foundational developments of twentieth-century algebraic topology.
