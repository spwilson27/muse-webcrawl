Salt marsh

A salt marsh (also called a saltmarsh or tidal marsh) is a coastal wetland in the upper intertidal zone between land and open saltwater, regularly flooded by the tides. It is dominated by dense stands of salt-tolerant plants — grasses, herbs and low shrubs known as halophytes — and occurs worldwide, mainly at middle to high latitudes on sheltered, sediment-rich shores such as estuaries, lagoons and the landward side of barrier islands. Salt marshes are among the most productive ecosystems on Earth and provide major ecological services.

Formation and zonation

Marshes develop where fine sediments accumulate on tidal flats protected from strong wave action. Pioneer plants such as glasswort (Salicornia) and cordgrass (Spartina) slow the water, trap more sediment and raise the surface, allowing a sequence of plant communities — zones determined largely by how often each level is flooded and how salty it is. Creek networks drain the marsh, and salt pans and levees create further variety. Marshes can build upward as sea level rises, provided sediment supply is adequate and they are not blocked from migrating inland.

Ecology and services

High primary production by marsh plants supports detritus-based food webs, nurseries for commercially important fish and shellfish, and feeding grounds for wildfowl and waders. Marshes filter nutrients and pollutants from runoff, dissipate wave energy and reduce flood risk for coastal communities, and bury large amounts of "blue carbon" in their waterlogged, oxygen-poor soils, where it may remain for millennia. Many animal species, such as the saltmarsh sparrow and the diamondback terrapin, are marsh specialists.

Threats

Large areas have been lost to land reclamation ("inning") for agriculture, port and housing development, and pollution; remaining marshes are threatened by accelerating sea-level rise combined with coastal squeeze against hard defences. Restoration schemes increasingly use managed realignment — deliberately breaching sea walls to recreate marsh — to restore habitat and flood storage.
