Acoma Pueblo

Acoma Pueblo
Native name: Haak'u
Location: Cibola County, New Mexico
Elevation of mesa: approx. 2,100 m
Language: Western Keres

Acoma Pueblo (Western Keres: Haak'u) is a Native American pueblo and federally recognized tribe in western New Mexico, renowned for its historic village of Sky City, built atop a sheer sandstone mesa about 110 m (360 ft) above the surrounding valley. Acoma oral tradition and archaeology indicate occupation of the region for many centuries; Sky City is often described as one of the oldest continuously inhabited communities in the United States.

History

The Acoma people speak a Keresan language and trace their origins to earlier settlements including the White Rock and other ancestral sites. Coronado's expedition encountered Acoma in 1540. Relations with Spain turned violent after Juan de Oñate's colony arrived: in January 1599, following the killing of Juan de Zaldívar and other Spaniards, Oñate's forces stormed the mesa in the Acoma massacre; hundreds of Acoma were killed and survivors were sentenced to servitude and mutilation. The San Esteban del Rey Mission church, built in the 1620s–1640s with labor and materials hauled up the mesa, still stands in Sky City.

Acoma joined the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, and the pueblo was re-subdued by Diego de Vargas in the 1690s. In the 20th century the tribe defended its land in litigation culminating in confirmation of a large reservation; today the Pueblo of Acoma governs communities including Acomita, McCartys, and Anzac, and operates the Sky City Cultural Center and Haak'u Museum, which opened in 2006.

Culture

Acoma is celebrated for thin-walled polychrome pottery painted with fine geometric and parrot motifs, a tradition carried by noted potters such as Lucy M. Lewis and Marie Z. Chino. Access to Sky City is by guided tour; the mission church and the mesa-top village, with its adobe row houses and kivas, are listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
