Eight Deer Jaguar Claw

Eight Deer Jaguar Claw (Mixtec: Iya Nacuaa Teyusi Ñaña; 1063 – 1115) was a powerful Mixtec ruler of Tilantongo in the Oaxaca region of what is now Mexico. His life and campaigns are recorded in several Mixtec codices, including the Codex Zouche-Nuttall and the Codex Bodley, which present him as one of the most ambitious rulers of pre-Hispanic Mesoamerica.

Life

Named for his birth date in the Mixtec calendar, Eight Deer was born in 1063, the son of a high priest of Tilantongo. Through strategic marriages, alliances, and warfare he united much of the Mixtec region under his rule. Around 1083 he visited the Toltec-influenced court at Cholula, where he received a turquoise nose ornament in a ceremony that marked his elevation as a supreme lord (tecuhtli). His conquests are said to have encompassed dozens of towns; codices credit him with conquering over a hundred places.

Death and legacy

Eight Deer's power eventually provoked a coalition of enemies. In 1115, after the murder of members of a rival dynasty to whom he was tied by marriage, he was captured and sacrificed at the age of 52. Although his unified kingdom fragmented after his death, later Mixtec dynasties traced their legitimacy to him, and his biography — unusually well documented for a figure of his era — has made him one of the best-known individuals of pre-Columbian America.
