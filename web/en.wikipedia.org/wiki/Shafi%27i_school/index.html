Shafi'i school

The Shafi'i school is one of the four canonical schools of Sunni Islamic law, named after its eponym, Imam Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i (150-204 AH / 767-820 CE). This article summarises the school's history, legal methodology and geographical spread.

Methodology

Al-Shafi'i's al-Risala established a hierarchy of sources: the Qur'an, the Sunnah as recorded in hadith, the consensus of the community, and analogy. Later Shafi'i jurists such as al-Ghazali refined the school's theory, and figures like al-Nawawi and al-Rafi'i became its chief reference authorities in substantive law.

Spread

From Egypt the school spread along trade routes to Yemen, East Africa, India and Southeast Asia. It remains the majority school in Indonesia, Malaysia, Brunei, southern Thailand and the Philippines' Muslim regions, as well as in Somalia, Eritrea, coastal Kenya and Tanzania, and among Kurds.
