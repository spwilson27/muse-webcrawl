Territory of Hawaii

The Territory of Hawaii or Hawaii Territory was an organized incorporated territory of the United States that existed from April 30, 1900, until March 18, 1959, when most of its territory was admitted to the Union as the 50th U.S. state, the State of Hawaii. The Hawaii Admission Act specified that the new state did not include Palmyra Island, the Midway Islands, Johnston Island, Sand Island, and Kingman Reef.

History

The territory was created following the overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom in 1893, the short-lived Republic of Hawaii, and annexation by the United States through the Newlands Resolution in 1898 during the Spanish–American War. The Hawaiian Organic Act of 1900 established a territorial government with a governor appointed by the president of the United States and a bicameral territorial legislature. Sanford B. Dole, former president of the Republic of Hawaii, became the first territorial governor.

During World War II, following the attack on Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, Hawaii was placed under martial law, which remained in effect until 1944. The territory's plantation economy, dominated by sugarcane and pineapples and by the so-called "Big Five" corporations, shaped its politics for decades.

Statehood

Calls for statehood grew after the war. In a plebiscite held in June 1959, voters overwhelmingly approved statehood, and Hawaii was admitted on August 21, 1959, alongside Alaska, which had been admitted earlier the same year.
