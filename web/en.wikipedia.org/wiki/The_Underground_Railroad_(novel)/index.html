The Underground Railroad (novel)

The Underground Railroad is a historical fiction novel by American author Colson Whitehead, published in 2016. Reimagining the historical Underground Railroad as an actual subterranean railway, it won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction and the National Book Award in 2017.

Plot

Cora, a young enslaved woman on a Georgia cotton plantation, escapes with Caesar via a literal underground railroad — a secret network of tracks, tunnels and stations beneath the slave South — pursued relentlessly by the slave catcher Ridgeway. Each state she passes through presents a different facet of American racism: South Carolina's genteel, eugenicist paternalism; North Carolina's regime of public lynching and sundown terror; an attic hiding place in a white abolitionist's home; a utopian Black farming community in Indiana destroyed by white raiders. Cora survives, kills Ridgeway, and rides a handcar north into an unwritten future.

Reception

An Oprah's Book Club selection, the novel was hailed for fusing historical realism with speculative devices to survey the whole history of American racial violence, from slavery through forced sterilisation to the sundown town. Whitehead became one of few writers to win two Pulitzer Prizes in fiction (adding The Nickel Boys, 2020).

Adaptation

Barry Jenkins adapted the novel as a limited television series for Amazon Prime Video, released in 2021 to wide acclaim.
