House of Bonaparte

House of Bonaparte
Country: France and others
Founder: Carlo Buonaparte
Most famous member: Napoleon I
Reigns: First French Empire (1804–1814/15); Second French Empire (1852–1870)
Origin: Corsica (Italian roots in Tuscany/Liguria)

The House of Bonaparte is a former imperial and royal European dynasty of Italian origin, founded by Carlo Buonaparte (known in France as Charles Bonaparte) of Ajaccio, Corsica. It rose to prominence through his son Napoleon Bonaparte, who became First Consul of France in 1799 and Emperor of the French as Napoleon I in 1804.

Rise and thrones

Under Napoleon I, members of the family were placed on thrones across Europe: Joseph became King of Naples and then of Spain; Louis became King of Holland; Jérôme became King of Westphalia; Elisa ruled in Tuscany and Lucca; Pauline became Princess of Guastalla; and Caroline became Queen of Naples as the wife of Joachim Murat. After Napoleon's fall in 1814–1815, the family's European kingdoms collapsed, though the Bonapartes remained a focus of the Bonapartist political movement in France.

Second Empire and later history

Napoleon's nephew Louis-Napoléon was elected President of the French Second Republic in 1848 and proclaimed himself Emperor Napoleon III in 1852, ruling until his capture in the Franco-Prussian War in 1870. His only son, Napoléon, Prince Imperial, was killed in the Anglo-Zulu War in 1879. The headship of the house later passed through the descendants of Jérôme Bonaparte, and the family continues today as pretenders to the former French imperial throne.
