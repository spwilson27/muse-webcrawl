The Bilderberg Meeting (also known as the Bilderberg Group) is an annual private conference established in 1954 to foster dialogue between Europe and North America. Between 120 and 150 political leaders, experts from industry, finance, academia, and the media are invited each year to take part. Meetings are held under the Chatham House Rule, which permits participants to use information received but forbids revealing the identity or affiliation of speakers.

History

The first conference was held at the Hotel de Bilderberg in Oosterbeek, Netherlands, in May 1954, at the initiative of the Polish political adviser Józef Retinger, who was concerned about the growth of anti-Americanism in Western Europe. Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands agreed to sponsor the meeting and chaired the gatherings for more than two decades. The stated goal was to promote understanding between the cultures of the United States and Western Europe and to encourage cooperation on political, economic, and defence issues.

Structure and participation

A steering committee of roughly two dozen members organizes each meeting and selects the invitees; about two-thirds of participants come from Europe and the remainder from North America. Attendees include heads of government, finance ministers, central bankers, executives of multinational corporations, royalty, senior military officers, and journalists, all attending in a private capacity. No votes are taken, no resolutions are proposed, and no policy statements are issued; organizers describe the meeting as an informal discussion forum. Since the 2010s the group has published its agenda topics and participant lists in advance.

Criticism and conspiracy theories

The secrecy of the discussions has long attracted criticism and speculation. Critics on both the political left and right argue that the meetings allow unelected elites to coordinate policy outside democratic scrutiny. The group is also a prominent subject of conspiracy theories alleging that it operates as a shadow world government or "New World Order" — claims the organizers and most academic analysts reject, describing the meeting as an influential but non-decision-making networking forum. Protests regularly accompany the annual meetings, and security arrangements at the host venues are extensive.
