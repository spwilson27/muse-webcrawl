Aşık

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An aşık (Azerbaijani: aşıq; Turkish: aşık) is a traditional singer-poet and troubadour who accompanies his songs with the bağlama, a long-necked lute. The aşık tradition is central to the folk culture of Turkey, Azerbaijan, Iranian Azerbaijan and parts of the Caucasus and Central Asia. The word aşık derives from the Arabic ʿāshiq, meaning "lover".

Tradition

Aşıks improvise and perform songs and epic tales (hikâye) in coffeehouses, at weddings and festivals, often engaging in duelling exchanges of verse (atışma) with rival performers. Their repertoire ranges from love lyrics and religious hymns to heroic epics such as the Book of Dede Korkut and the Epic of Köroğlu. Training traditionally proceeded through an apprenticeship with a master, and many aşıks adopted pen names in the final verse of their poems.

Famous aşıks

Renowned figures include Karacaoğlan, Dadaloğlu, Pir Sultan Abdal, Âşık Veysel Şatıroğlu — the blind 20th-century bard whose recordings made the tradition famous nationwide — and Âşık Mahzuni Şerif. UNESCO inscribed the Âşıqlik tradition of Azerbaijan on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2009.
