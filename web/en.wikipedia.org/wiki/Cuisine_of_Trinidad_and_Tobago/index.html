Cuisine of Trinidad and Tobago

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The cuisine of Trinidad and Tobago is a distinctive fusion of African, East Indian, European (Spanish, British and French), Chinese, Amerindian, and Middle Eastern influences, reflecting the country's diverse population and history as a sugar colony populated by enslaved Africans and, after 1845, Indian indentured labourers.

Signature dishes

  • Doubles — the quintessential street food: two soft fried flatbreads (bara) filled with curried chickpeas (channa), topped with chutneys and pepper sauce.
  • Roti — flatbreads (paratha "buss up shut", dhalpuri, sada) wrapped around curried chicken, goat, beef or vegetables.
  • Pelau — a one-pot rice dish of caramelised chicken, pigeon peas and coconut milk.
  • Callaloo — a soup/stew of dasheen (taro) leaves cooked with coconut milk, okra and seasonings, often with crab; regarded as a national dish.
  • Bake and shark — fried shark in a fried bread, famously sold at Maracas Bay with an array of toppings.
  • Pastelles, black cake and sorrel — Christmas foods blending European and local traditions.

Flavours

Trinidadian cooking relies on "green seasoning" (a blended marinade of culantro/shadon beni, thyme, garlic and peppers), curry powders with turmeric and geera (cumin), coconut milk, and the fiery Scotch bonnet and locally bred scorpion peppers. Popular drinks include mauby, sorrel, ginger beer and peanut punch; the islands produce Angostura bitters and rums. Street snacks such as pholourie, aloo pie and saheena round out a celebrated food culture.
