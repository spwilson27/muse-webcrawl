Sleep apnea (also spelled sleep apnoea) is a sleep-related breathing disorder in which breathing repeatedly stops and starts during sleep. The main types are obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), in which the upper airway collapses despite continued breathing effort; central sleep apnea, in which the brain fails to signal the respiratory muscles; and complex (mixed) sleep apnea syndrome.

Signs and symptoms

Characteristic features include loud, disruptive snoring, pauses in breathing witnessed by a partner, gasping or choking awakenings, unrefreshing sleep, excessive daytime sleepiness, morning headaches, and impaired concentration. Untreated moderate-to-severe OSA is associated with hypertension, cardiovascular disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, and motor vehicle accidents.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by polysomnography (an overnight sleep study) or home sleep apnea testing, which measure the apnea–hypopnea index (AHI): the number of breathing interruptions per hour of sleep. First-line treatment for moderate-to-severe OSA is continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), which splints the airway open with pressurised air delivered by mask. Alternatives include mandibular advancement devices, weight loss, positional therapy, and surgery such as uvulopalatopharyngoplasty or hypoglossal nerve stimulation.

Epidemiology

OSA is very common: global estimates suggest nearly one billion adults aged 30–69 have at least mild OSA, though the majority remain undiagnosed.
