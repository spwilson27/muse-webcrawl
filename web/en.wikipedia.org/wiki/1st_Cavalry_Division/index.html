The 1st Cavalry Division ("First Team") is a combined arms division of the United States Army, headquartered at Fort Cavazos (formerly Fort Hood), Texas. Activated in 1921, it is one of the most famous and highly decorated divisions in the US Army, and today it is a heavy armoured division despite retaining "Cavalry" in its name for historical reasons.

History

The division was constituted in 1921 and spent the interwar years patrolling the Mexican border on horseback. In the Second World War it fought dismounted as infantry in the Pacific, taking part in the Admiralty Islands campaign and the invasions of Leyte and Luzon, and was the first American division to enter Manila; it later served on occupation duty in Japan, being the first division to enter Tokyo.

In the Korean War the division fought at Pusan, Pyongyang and Unsan. In 1965 it became the Army's first airmobile division and deployed to Vietnam, fighting in the Ia Drang Valley (the first major battle between US and North Vietnamese regular forces), the Tet Offensive and the 1970 Cambodian incursion. Reorganised as a heavy division, it fought in the 1991 Gulf War, and brigades of the division deployed repeatedly to Iraq and Afghanistan in the 2000s and 2010s.

Insignia

The division's shoulder patch — a yellow triangular shield with a black diagonal band and a black horse's head in the upper right corner — is one of the largest and most recognisable US Army patches.
