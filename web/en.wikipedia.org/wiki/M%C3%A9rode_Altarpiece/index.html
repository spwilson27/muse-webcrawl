Mérode Altarpiece

Mérode Altarpiece
Artist: Workshop of Robert Campin
Date: c. 1427–1432
Medium: Oil on oak panel
Location: The Cloisters, Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

The Mérode Altarpiece is an early Netherlandish triptych, attributed to Robert Campin and his workshop, depicting the Annunciation in a contemporary Flemish domestic interior. Dated to the late 1420s, it is one of the most famous works of Northern Renaissance painting and is held by The Cloisters in New York.

Panels

The central panel shows the Archangel Gabriel approaching the Virgin Mary, who reads by a fireplace in a well-furnished room rendered with meticulous detail. The left panel shows the donors kneeling in a garden before an open door; the right panel shows Saint Joseph at work in his carpentry shop, with mousetraps he has made — a visual reference to Saint Augustine's metaphor of the Incarnation as a mousetrap for the Devil.

Symbolism and scholarship

The painting is celebrated for its disguised symbolism: everyday objects — a laver, lilies, a mousetrap, newly extinguished candle — carry theological meanings. Art historians have long debated the respective hands of Campin and his assistants, possibly including the young Rogier van der Weyden.
