Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) is the ministry of foreign affairs and international development of the United Kingdom. It is responsible for British foreign policy, the diplomatic service, relations with the Commonwealth, overseas aid, and consular services for British nationals abroad. Its head is the Secretary of State for Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Affairs — the Foreign Secretary — one of the four Great Offices of State.

History

The department descends from the Foreign Office, created in 1782. The Foreign and Commonwealth Office (FCO) was formed in 1968 by merging the Foreign Office with the Commonwealth Office. In September 2020, under Prime Minister Boris Johnson, the FCO merged with the Department for International Development (DfID) to form the FCDO, integrating diplomacy and the aid budget in a single ministry — a move criticised by aid organisations and former prime ministers, who feared development would be subordinated to foreign-policy and trade goals.

Work and organisation

The FCDO runs a network of more than 270 embassies, high commissions and consulates, staffed by the Diplomatic Service. Executive agencies and associated bodies include Wilton Park (a conference centre), FCDO Services, and the British Council at arm's length. Its priorities have included supporting Ukraine, managing the post-Brexit relationship with Europe, development programmes and climate finance, and protecting British citizens abroad through consular assistance.

Headquarters

The department's London home is the grand Victorian Foreign Office building on King Charles Street, Whitehall, designed by George Gilbert Scott and completed in the 1870s, alongside offices at Abercromby House in East Kilbride, Scotland.
