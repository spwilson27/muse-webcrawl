Gerhard "Gerd" Müller (3 November 1945 – 15 August 2021) was a German professional footballer. A striker renowned for his clinical finishing, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest goalscorers of all time. Nicknamed Der Bomber, he scored 68 goals in 62 appearances for West Germany and won the 1974 FIFA World Cup and UEFA Euro 1972.

Club career

Müller joined Bayern Munich in 1964, when the club was in the second tier, and helped build it into one of Europe's dominant teams. With Bayern he won four Bundesliga titles, four DFB-Pokals, three consecutive European Cups (1974–1976), the European Cup Winners' Cup and the Intercontinental Cup. He was the Bundesliga's top scorer seven times and scored 365 goals in 427 Bundesliga matches, a record that still stands. He finished his playing career with the Fort Lauderdale Strikers in the North American Soccer League.

International career

Müller scored ten goals at the 1970 World Cup, winning the Golden Boot, and scored the winning goal in the 1974 World Cup final against the Netherlands. His tally of 14 World Cup goals was a tournament record for 32 years. He also scored four goals in West Germany's victorious Euro 1972 campaign, including two in the final.

Honours and legacy

Müller won the Ballon d'Or in 1970 and the European Golden Shoe twice. His scoring rate — nearly a goal per game across his career — and his records in German football have made him a benchmark for strikers; Bayern Munich's stadium museum and German football honour him as one of the sport's defining finishers. After retirement he battled alcoholism, recovered with the support of former teammates, worked as a coach of Bayern's reserve team, and was later cared for while suffering from Alzheimer's disease.
