The Bugatti Veyron EB 16.4 is a mid-engine sports car designed and developed by the Volkswagen Group and manufactured in Molsheim, France, by Bugatti. Named after racing driver Pierre Veyron, it was launched in 2005 as the fastest street-legal production car in the world, with an original version top speed of 407 km/h (253 mph). It was named Car of the Decade by the BBC television programme Top Gear.

Development

After Volkswagen acquired the Bugatti brand in 1998, Ferdinand Piech set the ambitious target of a production car with 1,000 horsepower and a top speed above 400 km/h that could nevertheless be driven comfortably every day. Meeting these goals required solving formidable problems of cooling, aerodynamics, tyres, and transmission durability, and the project was delayed several times before production began in 2005.

Specifications

The Veyron's 7,993 cc W16 engine — effectively two narrow-angle V8s joined at the crankcase — is fed by four turbochargers and produces 1,001 PS (736 kW; 987 hp) in the original version. A seven-speed dual-clutch transmission sends power to all four wheels. The car accelerates from 0-100 km/h in about 2.5 seconds; at top speed its specially developed Michelin tyres and active aerodynamics, including a rear wing that doubles as an air brake, keep it stable.

Versions

Variants included the Grand Sport roadster (2009), the Super Sport (2010) with 1,200 PS, and the Grand Sport Vitesse roadster (2012), combining the Super Sport's engine with the open-top body. Numerous special editions were produced across the 450-car run — 300 coupes and 150 roadsters — which concluded in 2015.

Records

In 2010 the Super Sport set a Guinness-recognised top speed record for production cars of 431.072 km/h (267.856 mph), averaged over runs in both directions at Ehra-Lessien. The Grand Sport Vitesse subsequently set a record for the fastest roadster at 408.84 km/h (254.04 mph).
