Sagittarius is one of the constellations of the zodiac and is located in the Southern celestial hemisphere. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. Its name is Latin for "archer", and it is commonly represented as a centaur drawing a bow. Sagittarius lies on the Milky Way, and the centre of the galaxy, with the radio source Sagittarius A*, is found in its western part.

The Teapot

The constellation's brighter stars form an easily recognised asterism known as the Teapot: γ, δ, ε, ζ, λ, τ, σ and φ Sagittarii outline a squat teapot, with the Milky Way rising from its spout like steam. The "milk" of the galactic centre pours from this region of sky.

Notable features

Because the galactic centre lies in Sagittarius, the constellation is rich in star clusters and nebulae. Charles Messier catalogued 15 objects here, including the Lagoon Nebula (M8), the Omega Nebula (M17), the Trifid Nebula (M20), and the globular cluster M22, one of the brightest in the sky. The Sagittarius Star Cloud (M24) is a dense patch of the Milky Way visible to the naked eye.

Mythology

In Babylonian astronomy the constellation was associated with the god Nergal and depicted as a winged centaur-like creature. The Greeks identified it with a centaur — sometimes Chiron, though Chiron is more often linked with Centaurus, and Sagittarius is sometimes said to be Crotus, a satyr and son of Pan. The archer's arrow points toward Antares, the heart of Scorpius.

Astrology

As a zodiacal sign, Sagittarius (♐) covers those born roughly from 22 November to 21 December in tropical astrology. The Sun actually passes through the constellation from mid-December to mid-January, spending part of that interval in Ophiuchus.
