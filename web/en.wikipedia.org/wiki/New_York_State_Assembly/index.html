New York State Assembly

The New York State Assembly is the lower house of the New York State Legislature, the state legislature of New York. It consists of 150 members, each elected from a single-member district for two-year terms with no term limits.

Composition and elections

Assembly districts are apportioned by population following each decennial census. Members must be United States citizens, residents of the state for five years, and residents of their district for one year before election. The Assembly has been controlled by the Democratic Party for decades.

Leadership

The Assembly is led by the Speaker, who controls committee assignments and the flow of legislation. Other officers include the majority leader, minority leader, and Speaker pro tempore. The Speaker is among the most powerful officials in New York government, traditionally negotiating the state budget with the governor and the Senate majority leader — the so-called “three men in a room.”

Powers

The Assembly shares legislative power with the New York State Senate. It originates revenue bills, can impeach state officials by majority vote, and passes bills that require the governor's signature to become law.

History

The Assembly traces its origin to the colonial General Assembly and was reconstituted under the state constitution of 1777. It meets in the State Capitol in Albany.
