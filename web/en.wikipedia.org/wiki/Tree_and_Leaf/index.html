Tree and Leaf

AuthorJ. R. R. Tolkien
Published1964
PublisherGeorge Allen & Unwin

Tree and Leaf is a small book published in 1964, containing two works by the English author and philologist J. R. R. Tolkien: his essay "On Fairy-Stories" and the short story "Leaf by Niggle".

Contents

"On Fairy-Stories" originated as Tolkien's Andrew Lang Lecture, delivered at the University of St Andrews in 1939. In it Tolkien defends fantasy as a serious literary form, coining or developing concepts such as sub-creation, recovery, escape, consolation, and the happy ending he called eucatastrophe.

"Leaf by Niggle", first published in 1945, is an allegorical short story about a painter, Niggle, who labours on a painting of a great tree but is constantly distracted by the needs of his neighbour, Parish, before being called away on a journey — widely read as an allegory of Tolkien's own creative life and of death and purgatory.

Reception

The pairing of essay and story has been influential in studies of Tolkien's thought: the essay states his theory of fairy-stories, while the story enacts it. Later editions have sometimes added Tolkien's poem "Mythopoeia". See J. R. R. Tolkien.
