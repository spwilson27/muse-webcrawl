Pereyaslav Agreement

Pereyaslav Agreement (Treaty of Pereyaslav)
Signed: 18 January 1654
Location: Pereyaslav, Cossack Hetmanate (now Ukraine)
Parties: Zaporozhian Host (Bohdan Khmelnytsky) and Tsardom of Russia (Alexis of Russia)

The Pereyaslav council (rada) of January 1654

The Pereyaslav Agreement, concluded on 18 January 1654 at the town of Pereyaslav, was an agreement between the Cossack Hetmanate, led by Hetman Bohdan Khmelnytsky, and emissaries of Tsar Alexis of Russia. It was reached during the Khmelnytsky Uprising against the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, when the Cossacks sought a powerful ally and protector.

Terms and interpretation

At a council (rada) in Pereyaslav, Khmelnytsky and the Cossack leadership swore an oath of allegiance to the tsar, and subsequent negotiations in Moscow produced the "March Articles" setting out conditions: the Hetmanate retained its own administration, army of 60,000 registered Cossacks, courts, and fiscal system, while the tsar became its sovereign and protector. The precise legal character of the agreement has been disputed ever since: Russian and Soviet historiography traditionally portrayed it as the "reunification of Ukraine with Russia", whereas Ukrainian historiography has variously read it as a military alliance, a personal union, or a protectorate that Moscow progressively hollowed out as it curtailed Cossack autonomy over the following century.

Consequences

The agreement brought Russia into war with Poland (the Russo-Polish War of 1654–1667), ended by the Truce of Andrusovo, which divided Ukraine along the Dnieper River between Poland and Russia. In 1954 the Soviet government staged lavish tercentenary celebrations of the agreement and transferred Crimea from the Russian SFSR to the Ukrainian SSR as a symbolic gesture. The meaning of Pereyaslav remains politically charged in Ukrainian–Russian relations.
