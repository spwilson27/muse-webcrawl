Community media

Community media are a form of media where a specific, often local, community participates in the production and dissemination of content. Community media are generally operated by non-profit organizations, community groups, or cooperatives, and include community radio, community television, and local participatory online media.

Characteristics

Community media are distinguished by community ownership and control, participation by non-professionals, coverage of local issues neglected by mainstream outlets, and service to a defined geographic or interest-based community. They often broadcast in local or minority languages.

Community radio

Community radio stations are typically small, low-power stations licensed to serve a locality. In the United Kingdom, community radio was formally licensed from 2005; in the United States, the Low Power FM service was created in 2000.

Community television

Public-access television channels in the United States and community channel services in Canada and elsewhere allow residents to produce and air their own programming.

Significance

Scholars and bodies such as UNESCO view community media as important for media pluralism, cultural diversity, and democratic participation.
