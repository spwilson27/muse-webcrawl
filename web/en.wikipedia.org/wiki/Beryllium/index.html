Beryllium is a chemical element with the symbol Be and atomic number 4. It is a steel-gray, strong, lightweight and brittle alkaline earth metal. It is a divalent element that occurs naturally only in combination with other elements to form minerals. Notable gemstones high in beryllium include beryl (aquamarine, emerald) and chrysoberyl.

Because of its low density and high stiffness, beryllium is used in aerospace components, military aircraft and precision instruments. It is transparent to X-rays and is used in X-ray window applications. However, beryllium and its compounds are toxic and carcinogenic; inhalation of beryllium dust can cause the chronic lung disease berylliosis.

History

Beryllium oxide was identified in 1798 by Louis Nicolas Vauquelin in beryl and emerald. The metal was isolated independently in 1828 by Friedrich Wöhler and Antoine Bussy. The name derives from the Greek beryllos, meaning beryl.

Properties

Beryllium has one of the highest melting points of the light metals and exceptional flexural rigidity. Its oxide, beryllium oxide, has high thermal conductivity and is an electrical insulator.
