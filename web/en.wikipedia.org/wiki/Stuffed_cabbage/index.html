Stuffed cabbage

Stuffed cabbage rolls
TypeDumpling / main course
RegionCentral, Northern, Eastern and Southeastern Europe; Middle East
Main ingredientsCabbage leaves, minced meat, rice
VariationsGołąbki, holubtsi, sarmale, sarma, holishkes

Stuffed cabbage rolls are a dish found in many cuisines of Central, Northern, Eastern, and Southeastern Europe, as well as in parts of the Middle East and Central Asia. The dish consists of blanched cabbage leaves wrapped around a filling, usually of seasoned minced meat (pork, beef, or veal) mixed with rice or other grains, onions, and spices. The rolls are then baked, simmered, or steamed in a covered pot and often served with a tomato-based sauce, broth, or sour cream.

Regional varieties

  • Poland — gołąbki ("little pigeons"): cabbage leaves filled with pork, beef, and rice, served in tomato sauce.
  • Ukraine — holubtsi: fillings vary by region, using rice, buckwheat, or corn grits; served with sour cream.
  • Romania and Moldova — sarmale: often made with pickled cabbage or vine leaves, traditionally served at holidays with mămăligă (polenta) and sour cream.
  • Balkans and Turkey — sarma: from the Turkish verb sarmak, "to wrap"; a member of the dolma family of stuffed dishes.
  • Jewish cuisine — holishkes: sweet-and-sour tomato sauce with raisins, traditionally eaten at Sukkot and Simchat Torah.
  • Sweden — kåldolmar: believed to have been introduced in the 18th century after Charles XII's return from the Ottoman Empire.
  • Hungary — töltött káposzta: made with sour cabbage and smoked meat, seasoned with paprika.

Preparation

Whole cabbage leaves are softened by blanching or freezing, the thick central rib is trimmed, and a spoonful of filling is rolled up in each leaf, tucking in the ends. The rolls are packed tightly in a pot, sometimes layered with sauerkraut or chopped cabbage, covered with liquid, and cooked slowly until tender.
