Hispania

Hispania was the Roman name for the Iberian Peninsula and its provinces. Under the Roman Republic, Hispania was divided into two provinces: Hispania Citerior ("Hither Spain") and Hispania Ulterior ("Further Spain"). Under Augustus it was reorganised into three provinces: Baetica, Lusitania, and Hispania Tarraconensis.

History

Rome acquired territory in Iberia during the Second Punic War against Carthage, beginning in 218 BCE, but the conquest of the whole peninsula took two centuries, concluding under Augustus in 19 BCE. Roman Hispania became a rich source of grain, olive oil, wine, metals, and soldiers, and produced emperors including Trajan, Hadrian, and Theodosius I.

Legacy

The name Hispania survives in "Spain" (España) and in terms such as "Hispanic". After the fall of the Western Roman Empire, the peninsula was ruled by the Visigoths and later partly conquered by Muslim forces from 711 CE.
