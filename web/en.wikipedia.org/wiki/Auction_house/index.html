TypeIntermediary business
ServicesValuation, cataloguing, marketing and conducting auctions

An auction house is a company that facilitates the buying and selling of assets, such as works of art and collectibles, through auctions. The auction house acts as an intermediary between sellers (consignors) and buyers, researching and cataloguing the goods, publishing catalogues, marketing the sale, conducting the auction itself, and handling payment and delivery.

Business model

Auction houses typically charge the seller a commission based on the hammer price and charge the successful buyer an additional buyer's premium, expressed as a percentage of the hammer price. They provide expert valuation and provenance research, set estimates and reserve prices with the consignor, and increasingly run sales simultaneously in the saleroom, by telephone and online.

History

The oldest surviving auction houses in the fine-art world are Sotheby's, founded in London in 1744 as a bookseller's auction business, and Christie's, founded by James Christie in 1766; together they dominate the international art market, with major salerooms in London, New York, Hong Kong and Paris. In the 20th and 21st centuries the model expanded to automobiles, wine, jewellery, real estate and general household goods, and to vast online platforms.
