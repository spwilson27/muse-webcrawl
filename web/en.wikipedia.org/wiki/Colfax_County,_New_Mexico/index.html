Colfax County is a county in the U.S. state of New Mexico, located in the north-eastern corner of the state along the Colorado border. As of the 2020 census, its population was 12,387. Its county seat and largest city is Raton. The county was created in 1869 and named for Schuyler Colfax, Vice President of the United States under Ulysses S. Grant.

Geography and history

The county spans the high plains in the east and rises into the Sangre de Cristo Mountains in the west. Much of the county formed part of the Maxwell Land Grant, one of the largest Mexican land grants in the Southwest; disputes over the grant culminated in the Colfax County War of the 1870s. Coal mining around Raton and Dawson drove the economy in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries; the ghost town of Dawson, abandoned after fatal mine explosions in 1913 and 1923, is a noted historic site.

The county is home to Philmont Scout Ranch, a large Boy Scouts of America backcountry camp near Cimarron, as well as Sugarite Canyon and Capulin Volcano National Monument lies just across the Union County line. The historic Santa Fe Trail crossed the county through Raton Pass.

Communities

Cities and towns include Raton, Springer, Cimarron, Angel Fire, Eagle Nest and Maxwell.
