Canon of the Mass

The Canon of the Mass (Latin: Canon Missae) is the central prayer of the Mass in the Roman Rite of the Catholic Church, containing the words of consecration of the bread and wine. In the post-conciliar liturgy it is known as the Roman Canon or Eucharistic Prayer I, one of four eucharistic prayers in the ordinary Missal.

Structure

The Canon begins after the Sanctus with the Te igitur and includes commemorations of the living (Memento), the saints (Communicantes), the offering of the gifts, the Quam oblationem, and the account of the institution of the Eucharist at the Last Supper, culminating in the words of consecration. There follow the anamnesis (Unde et memores), intercessions and the commemoration of the dead, the Nobis quoque, and the concluding doxology (Per ipsum) with its Great Amen.

History

The Canon's prayers took shape between late antiquity and the early Middle Ages, drawing on earlier Roman sacramentaries such as the Leonine and Gelasian. It was recited silently by the priest (in a low voice) in the Tridentine Mass, a practice retained as an option today. The revision after the Second Vatican Council introduced additional eucharistic prayers of Eastern and ancient inspiration, while the Roman Canon retained its traditional text with minor modifications.
