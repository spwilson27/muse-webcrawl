Khorramabad (Persian: خرم‌آباد) is a city in western Iran, the capital of Lorestan Province and of Khorramabad County. Situated in a valley of the Zagros Mountains on the Khorramabad River, it is the largest Luri-speaking city and a regional center for the Lori people.

History

The site has been inhabited since prehistoric times; nearby cave sites in the Khorramabad valley have yielded evidence of human occupation tens of thousands of years old, and the area formed part of ancient Elam and later of the Kassite and Persian worlds. The modern city's emblem is the great fortress of Falak-ol-Aflak ("the sky of skies"), whose foundations date to the Sasanian era, when the town of Shapurkhast stood here. The fortress, built on a hill above the river, served successive dynasties as treasury, seat of government, and prison; it now houses a museum of anthropology and archaeology.

The region was ruled for centuries by local dynasties, including the Hazaraspids (Atabegs of Luristan), and later came under Safavid, Zand, and Qajar control. Khorramabad grew substantially in the 20th century as a provincial capital and agricultural and administrative center.

Geography and climate

Khorramabad lies in a fertile valley ringed by limestone mountains, with a temperate climate of hot, dry summers and cold, wet winters. The river, fed by springs such as the Golestan spring beneath the castle rock, runs through the city.

Sights and culture

Besides Falak-ol-Aflak Castle, landmarks include the Sasanian-era brick minaret (Manara Ajori), the Shabpuri bridge, and the Keeyow lake. The city is known for Luri music, dress, and horsemanship, and for the Ashura mourning ritual in mud (gel-mali) practiced in Lorestan. The surrounding province holds major tourist attractions such as Bisheh waterfall and Gahar Lake.
