Dihydroxyphenylalanine

Dihydroxyphenylalanine refers to compounds derived from the amino acid phenylalanine bearing two hydroxyl groups on the aromatic ring. The best known is 3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine, universally known as DOPA: its levorotatory form, L-DOPA (levodopa), is the immediate precursor of the catecholamine neurotransmitters dopamine, norepinephrine and epinephrine.

Biosynthesis

In the body, L-DOPA is formed from the amino acid tyrosine by the enzyme tyrosine hydroxylase, the rate-limiting step in catecholamine synthesis. It is then decarboxylated to dopamine by aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase.

Medical use

Unlike dopamine itself, levodopa crosses the blood–brain barrier, so it is administered (usually with a peripheral decarboxylase inhibitor such as carbidopa) as the standard treatment for the motor symptoms of Parkinson's disease. Another isomer, 2,5-dihydroxyphenylalanine, occurs in some natural products.
