Mongoose

Mongooses are small feliform carnivoran mammals of the family Herpestidae, which comprises about 34 species in 14 genera, ranging in size from the dwarf mongoose at roughly 1 kg to the white-tailed mongoose at about 5 kg. They are native to southern Europe, Africa, the Middle East and Asia, and have been introduced to several other regions.

Anatomy and behaviour

Mongooses have long bodies and faces, small rounded ears, short legs and long, tapering tails. Many species are famous for attacking venomous snakes such as cobras; they are not truly immune to snake venom, but their speed and modified acetylcholine receptors reduce the venom's effect. Diet consists mainly of insects, crabs, rodents, birds and eggs.

Social life

Some species, such as the meerkat and banded mongoose, live in cooperative groups ("mobs") with sentinel behaviour and communal pup-rearing; others, such as the Egyptian mongoose, are largely solitary. Group-living mongooses are a model system for studying cooperative breeding and intergroup conflict.

Introductions and impact

The small Indian mongoose was introduced to islands in the Caribbean, Hawaii and elsewhere in the 19th century to control rats and snakes in sugarcane fields. It proved a destructive predator of native ground-nesting birds, reptiles and amphibians, and is now listed among the world's worst invasive alien species.
