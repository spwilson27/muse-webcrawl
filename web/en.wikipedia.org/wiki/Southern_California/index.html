Southern California

Southern California (commonly shortened to SoCal) is a geographic and cultural region in the southern portion of the U.S. state of California. It is generally defined as the state's ten southernmost counties and is home to more than 23 million people, anchored by the metropolitan areas of Los Angeles and San Diego.

Geography

The region stretches from the Pacific coast eastward to the deserts of the interior. It includes the coastal plains, the Transverse Ranges (including the San Gabriel and San Bernardino mountains), the Peninsular Ranges, and parts of the Mojave and Colorado deserts. Its Mediterranean climate features mild, wet winters and warm, dry summers, moderated near the coast by the marine layer.

Major counties and cities

The ten counties usually counted are Imperial, Kern, Los Angeles, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino, San Diego, San Luis Obispo, Santa Barbara and Ventura. Major cities include Los Angeles, San Diego, Long Beach, Anaheim, Santa Ana, Riverside, San Bernardino, Bakersfield (sometimes included), Palm Springs and Santa Barbara.

Economy and culture

Southern California is a global centre for entertainment, centred on Hollywood, as well as international trade through the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach, aerospace, technology and tourism. The region is famous for its beaches, surfing culture, freeways and car culture, and landmarks such as Disneyland, Griffith Observatory and the Santa Monica Pier.
