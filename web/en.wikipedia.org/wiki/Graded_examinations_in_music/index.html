Graded examinations in music

Graded examinations in music are a system of formal assessments of musical performance and theory, arranged in a ladder of grades of increasing difficulty — typically Grades 1 to 8 — offered by examination boards such as ABRSM (the Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music), Trinity College London, Rockschool and the London College of Music. They are taken by hundreds of thousands of students worldwide each year, mainly children learning instruments privately or at school.

Structure

A practical examination typically requires three prepared pieces, scales and arpeggios, sight-reading and aural tests. Marks are awarded out of 150 in ABRSM examinations, with 100 required to pass, 120 for a merit and 130 for a distinction. Separate written examinations assess music theory; ABRSM traditionally required a pass at Grade 5 theory before candidates could enter practical examinations above Grade 5. Above Grade 8, boards offer performance diplomas (such as DipABRSM, LRSM and FRSM).

Recognition

In the United Kingdom, higher grades carry UCAS tariff points for university admission (Grade 8 can be worth up to 30 points). Graded examinations provide structured goals and recognised certification for learners outside formal school music, and are used across the Commonwealth and in many other countries.
