Elm

Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Ulmaceae
Genus: Ulmus L.
Type species: Ulmus campestris (now Ulmus minor)

Elms are deciduous and semi-deciduous trees comprising the flowering plant genus Ulmus in the family Ulmaceae. The genus first appeared in the Miocene geological period about 20 million years ago and is native throughout the temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere.

Description

Elms are typically large trees with rough, furrowed bark and alternate, simple leaves with serrated margins and characteristically asymmetrical bases. The small, wind-pollinated flowers appear before the leaves in spring and are followed by winged fruits called samaras, which aid wind dispersal of the seed.

Dutch elm disease

Elm populations across North America and Europe were devastated in the twentieth century by Dutch elm disease, a fungal infection (caused by Ophiostoma ulmi and the more aggressive Ophiostoma novo-ulmi) spread by bark beetles. Millions of trees died, transforming landscapes and city streets. Breeding programmes have produced disease-resistant cultivars, and elms are gradually being replanted.

Uses

Elm wood is valued for its interlocking grain, which resists splitting; it was traditionally used for wheel hubs, water pipes, coffins and furniture. Elms were also widely planted as shade and street trees for their graceful, vase-shaped crowns, and they hold a prominent place in mythology and folklore.
