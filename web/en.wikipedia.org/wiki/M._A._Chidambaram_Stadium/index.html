M. A. Chidambaram Stadium, commonly known as the Chepauk Stadium, is a cricket stadium in Chennai, Tamil Nadu, India. Located in the Chepauk neighbourhood, close to Marina Beach, it is one of the oldest cricket stadiums in the country, having been established in 1916.

The stadium is named after M. A. Chidambaram, a former president of the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) and head of the Tamil Nadu Cricket Association. It serves as the home ground of the Tamil Nadu cricket team and the Indian Premier League franchise Chennai Super Kings.

History

Cricket has been played at Chepauk since the nineteenth century. The stadium hosted its first Test match in 1934, when India played England. It was also the venue for India's first-ever Test victory, achieved against England in 1952.

The ground is renowned for its knowledgeable and sporting crowds, who famously gave the Pakistan team a standing ovation after a close Test in 1999. The stadium underwent major redevelopment ahead of the 2011 Cricket World Cup, with new stands and facilities added.

Notable matches

Chepauk hosted the tied Test between India and Australia in 1986, only the second tied match in Test cricket history. It has also staged matches in multiple Cricket World Cups, including the 2023 edition.
