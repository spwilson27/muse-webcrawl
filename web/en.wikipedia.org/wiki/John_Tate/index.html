John Torrence Tate Jr. (13 March 1925 – 16 October 2019) was an American mathematician who made fundamental contributions to algebraic number theory, arithmetic geometry and related areas in algebraic geometry.

Contributions

Tate's 1950 Princeton doctoral thesis, supervised by Emil Artin, reformulated class field theory using Fourier analysis on the adeles — an approach known as Tate's thesis. Over a long career at Harvard (1954–1990) and then the University of Texas at Austin, he introduced numerous concepts that bear his name, including Tate cohomology, the Tate module, the Tate curve, the Tate conjecture on algebraic cycles, the Hodge–Tate decomposition, Tate duality, and the Néron–Tate height. With Jonathan Lubin he developed the theory of formal group laws used in local class field theory.

His conjectures, like those of his contemporaries, shaped the modern Langlands-era landscape of arithmetic geometry. Many of his results circulated for years in influential unpublished lecture notes.

Honours

Tate shared the Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 2002/03 and received the Abel Prize in 2010 "for his vast and lasting impact on the theory of numbers". He was a member of the National Academy of Sciences and the French Academy of Sciences.
