Subnormal series

In mathematics, especially group theory, a subnormal series (also called a subinvariant series) of a group G is a chain of subgroups

1 = A0 ⊲ A1 ⊲ ⋯ ⊲ An = G

in which each subgroup Ai is a normal subgroup of the next subgroup Ai+1. The quotients Ai+1/Ai are called the factor groups of the series.

Related notions

A subnormal series in which each Ai is also normal in the whole group G is called a normal series (or invariant series). A composition series is a subnormal series that cannot be refined further — equivalently, one whose factor groups are all simple groups; the Jordan–Hölder theorem states that any two composition series of a finite group have the same length and the same factor groups up to order. A group possessing a subnormal series with abelian factor groups is called a solvable group, and one with factor groups that are cyclic of prime order relates to the classical theory of solvability by radicals.
