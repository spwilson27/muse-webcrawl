The Ambassadors (Holbein)

The Ambassadors is a 1533 painting by Hans Holbein the Younger, held in the National Gallery, London. It is a double portrait of Jean de Dinteville, French ambassador to England, and Georges de Selve, Bishop of Lavaur, standing either side of a table laden with instruments and objects.

Description

The upper shelf holds celestial and astronomical instruments — a celestial globe, sundials and a quadrant — while the lower holds terrestrial objects: a globe, arithmetic book, lute with a broken string, and a hymn book. The objects allude to learning, exploration, religious division in Reformation Europe, and discord symbolised by the broken string.

The skull

In the foreground floats a distorted shape that resolves, when viewed from a low angle at the right, into a human skull — an anamorphic memento mori and a visual pun on Holbein's name (hohle Bein, "hollow bone"). A partly hidden crucifix in the upper left offers a counterpoint of resurrection and hope.

Interpretation

Painted during Henry VIII's break with Rome, the work is read as a commentary on political and religious schism and on the vanity of worldly diplomacy and learning in the face of death.
