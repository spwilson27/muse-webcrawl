Francia

Francia, also called the Kingdom of the Franks (Latin: Regnum Francorum), was the largest post-Roman barbarian kingdom in Western Europe. It was ruled by the Franks during late antiquity and the early Middle Ages, evolving from the Merovingian realm founded by Clovis I to the Carolingian Empire of Charlemagne.

History

Clovis united the Frankish tribes and conquered much of Gaul after 481, converting to Catholic Christianity. Under the Merovingians the realm was repeatedly divided among royal sons. The Carolingian mayors of the palace supplanted the dynasty in 751; Charlemagne, crowned emperor in 800, ruled most of western and central Europe. The Treaty of Verdun (843) divided the empire into West, Middle and East Francia — forerunners of France and Germany.

Society

Francia combined Roman law and institutions with Germanic custom (Salic law), a landed aristocracy, and close alliance with the Church, laying foundations for medieval European kingship.
