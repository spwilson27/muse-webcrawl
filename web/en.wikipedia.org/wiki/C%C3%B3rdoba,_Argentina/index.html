Córdoba is the capital city of the province of Córdoba, Argentina, and the second most populous city in the country after Buenos Aires. It is located in the geographical centre of Argentina, in the foothills of the Sierras Chicas on the Suquía River. Nicknamed La Docta ("the Learned One") for its many universities and scientific institutes, Córdoba is a major educational, cultural and industrial centre.

History

The city was founded on 6 July 1573 by Jerónimo Luis de Cabrera, who named it Córdoba de la Nueva Andalucía after the Spanish city. Its Jesuit Block (Manzana Jesuítica), including the National University of Córdoba — founded in 1613 and among the oldest universities in the Americas — is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Córdoba was a centre of the 1918 university reform movement that spread across Latin America, and in 1969 it was the scene of the Cordobazo, a major popular uprising against the military dictatorship.

Geography and economy

Córdoba enjoys a humid subtropical climate with mild, dry winters. The metropolitan area is home to roughly 1.9 million people. The economy is driven by automotive and aeronautical manufacturing, agriculture-related industry, software and services, and a large student population. Sights include the colonial cathedral, the Arco de Córdoba, the Paseo del Buen Pastor and the nearby Jesuit estancias and Sierras resorts such as Villa Carlos Paz.
