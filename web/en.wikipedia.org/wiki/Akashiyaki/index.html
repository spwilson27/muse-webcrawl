Akashiyaki (Japanese: 明石焼き) is a small round dumpling from the city of Akashi in Hyōgo Prefecture, Japan, made of an egg-rich batter containing a piece of octopus, cooked in a moulded pan similar to a takoyaki pan.

Akashiyaki served with a bowl of dashi

Description

Unlike the firmer takoyaki of Osaka, akashiyaki uses a much softer, more eggy batter (with plenty of egg and jinenjo yam flour in some recipes), giving a delicate, custardy texture. The dumplings are eaten by dipping them into a light bowl of hot dashi broth rather than with sauces and toppings.

History

Akashiyaki is considered the precursor of takoyaki: in the 1930s a street vendor in Osaka who had eaten akashiyaki adapted the idea using wheat-flour batter and sauce, creating takoyaki. Locally in Akashi the dish is also called tamagoyaki (玉子焼き, "grilled egg"), not to be confused with the rolled omelette of the same name elsewhere in Japan.
