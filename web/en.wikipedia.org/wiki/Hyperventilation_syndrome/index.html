Hyperventilation syndrome

Hyperventilation syndrome is a breathing pattern disorder characterised by episodes of breathing in excess of metabolic needs, lowering arterial carbon dioxide and causing respiratory alkalosis, with a wide range of distressing symptoms. It is frequently associated with anxiety and panic attacks, although organic causes of hyperventilation must be excluded.

Episodic over-breathing lowers CO2 and produces the syndrome's symptoms.

Symptoms

Typical symptoms include breathlessness disproportionate to exertion, chest tightness or pain, palpitations, dizziness, perioral and digital tingling (paraesthesiae), carpopedal spasm in severe cases, tremor and a sense of unreality or impending doom. The fall in CO2 causes cerebral vasoconstriction (contributing to dizziness) and reduces ionised calcium (contributing to tingling and spasm), while alkalosis shifts the oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is clinical and one of exclusion; questionnaires such as the Nijmegen questionnaire are used as screening tools, and a hyperventilation provocation test was historically used but is now considered unreliable. Management centres on explanation and reassurance, breathing retraining with physiotherapy, and treatment of underlying anxiety or panic disorder. The traditional remedy of rebreathing into a paper bag is no longer recommended because it can cause dangerous hypoxia if the diagnosis is wrong.
