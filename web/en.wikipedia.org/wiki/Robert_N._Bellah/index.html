Robert N. Bellah

Robert Neelly Bellah (February 23, 1927 – July 30, 2013) was an American sociologist whose best-known works are The Broken Covenant (1975), Habits of the Heart (1985) and Religion in Human Evolution (2011). He was Elliott Professor of Sociology Emeritus at the University of California, Berkeley. His 1967 essay "Civil Religion in America" made the concept of American civil religion central to debates about national identity.

Life and career

Bellah grew up in Oklahoma and Los Angeles, studied at Harvard under Talcott Parsons, and spent two years at McGill's Institute of Islamic Studies before joining Harvard's faculty. In 1967 he moved to Berkeley, where he remained for the rest of his career. His early work, Tokugawa Religion (1957), applied Weberian analysis to Japan.

Work

In "Civil Religion in America" (1967) Bellah argued that the United States possesses an elaborate, well-institutionalised religious dimension alongside its churches — expressed in inaugural addresses, Memorial Day rituals and sacred texts such as the Declaration of Independence. Habits of the Heart (1985, with Richard Madsen, William M. Sullivan, Ann Swidler and Steven M. Tipton), taking its title from Tocqueville, examined American individualism and commitment, popularising the phrase "Sheilaism" for purely private faith. His late magnum opus, Religion in Human Evolution (2011), traced religion from tribal societies to the axial age.

Honours

Bellah received the National Humanities Medal in 2000. He died in Oakland in 2013 following heart surgery.
