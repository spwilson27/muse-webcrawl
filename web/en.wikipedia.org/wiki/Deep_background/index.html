Deep background

In journalism, deep background is an arrangement under which a source provides information to a reporter that may be used to guide reporting and understanding, but which may not be quoted, attributed, or in some definitions published at all. The precise meaning of the term varies, and experienced journalists often insist on clarifying terms with a source before an interview begins.

Levels of attribution

Journalists commonly distinguish several degrees of attribution:

  • On the record — information may be published and attributed to the named source.
  • On background — information may be published but attributed only generally, for example to "a senior official".
  • On deep background — information may be used to inform the reporter's understanding but traditionally could not be published or attributed in any way; in modern United States usage it sometimes means the information may be published without any attribution whatsoever.
  • Off the record — information is provided in confidence and must not be published or used to confirm other reporting.

Usage notes

Because the terms are conventions rather than laws, and because American and British usage differ (British practice treats "background" somewhat differently), editors' guidance typically urges reporters and sources to agree explicitly on what the ground rules mean before confidential information is exchanged.
