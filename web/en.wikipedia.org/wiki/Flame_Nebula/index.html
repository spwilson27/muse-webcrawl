The Flame Nebula, designated NGC 2024 and Sh2-277, is an emission nebula in the constellation Orion. It is located near the easternmost star of Orion's Belt, Alnitak, and forms part of the Orion molecular cloud complex, the same vast star-forming region that includes the Horsehead Nebula and the Orion Nebula. The nebula lies roughly 1,350 light-years from Earth.

Structure

The nebula glows because ultraviolet light from hot young stars ionises its hydrogen gas. At its centre lies a dense cluster of recently formed stars, about 86% of which still retain circumstellar discs. A prominent dark lane of cool dust crosses the bright centre and hides much of the cluster in visible light, though infrared and X-ray observations — notably from the Chandra X-ray Observatory — reveal several hundred young stars within. The dark lane gives the nebula its flame-like appearance, as though a burning torch were silhouetted against the glow.

Observation

NGC 2024 was discovered by William Herschel in 1786. The glare of nearby Alnitak makes it a challenging but popular target for amateur astronomers; it is best seen with averted vision under dark skies, and long-exposure photographs show its orange-red colour vividly. It is one of the closest regions of massive star formation to the Sun.
