Quetiapine

Trade names: Seroquel, others
Class: Atypical antipsychotic
Routes: By mouth
Legal status: Prescription only
Elimination half-life: approx. 7 hours (parent); 9–12 hours (norquetiapine)

Quetiapine, sold under the brand name Seroquel among others, is an atypical antipsychotic medication used for the treatment of schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and major depressive disorder. It was developed by AstraZeneca and first approved in 1997. Owing to its sedative effects, it is also sometimes prescribed off-label for insomnia, a practice that is discouraged by guidelines because of metabolic and other risks.

Medical uses

Quetiapine is approved for schizophrenia in adults and adolescents, for manic and depressive episodes of bipolar disorder, and as adjunctive treatment of major depressive disorder. The extended-release formulation is used for maintenance treatment. Its active metabolite, norquetiapine, inhibits the norepinephrine transporter and acts at serotonin receptors, which is thought to contribute to antidepressant effects.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include sedation and somnolence, dry mouth, dizziness, constipation, and orthostatic hypotension. Quetiapine has a relatively high risk among atypical antipsychotics of metabolic effects, including weight gain, elevated blood lipids and glucose, and type 2 diabetes. Extrapyramidal symptoms are comparatively uncommon, though tardive dyskinesia and neuroleptic malignant syndrome can occur, and the drug carries warnings regarding increased mortality in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis. Abrupt discontinuation may produce withdrawal symptoms including insomnia and nausea.

Pharmacology

Quetiapine antagonises dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors, with relatively loose, transient D2 binding sometimes invoked to explain its low extrapyramidal profile. Its strong histamine H1 antagonism underlies sedation at low doses. It is metabolised mainly by CYP3A4 in the liver.

History and society

Quetiapine was discovered in the 1980s and launched in 1997. It became one of AstraZeneca's best-selling drugs; generic versions appeared after patent expiry in 2012. AstraZeneca paid a US$520 million settlement in 2010 over off-label marketing allegations in the United States. The drug is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines for schizophrenia and bipolar disorder.
