The Bible is a collection of religious texts or scriptures central to Christianity, and (in its Hebrew Bible/Old Testament portion) to Judaism. Christians regard the Bible as divinely inspired; it comprises the Old Testament and the New Testament (Gospels, Acts, Epistles and Revelation), with canons varying between Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant churches (Protestant Old Testaments exclude the deuterocanonical books).

Written in Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek over more than a millennium, the Bible has been translated into more languages than any other book and was the first major work printed with movable type in Europe (Gutenberg Bible, c. 1455). Its influence on Western (and global) literature, law, art, language and ethics is immeasurable; historical-critical scholarship studies its composition and contexts, while billions read it devotionally.
