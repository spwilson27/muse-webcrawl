Pietà

The Pietà (Italian for "pity") is a subject in Christian art depicting the Virgin Mary cradling the dead body of Jesus after his crucifixion. It is a specific form of the Lamentation of Christ.

Origins

The image arose in Germany around 1300 as the Vesperbild, an image for evening devotion, and spread through Europe in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries. Early examples emphasised grief and the wounds of Christ; Renaissance treatments became more serene.

Michelangelo's Pietà

The most famous example is Michelangelo's marble Pietà (1498–1499) in St. Peter's Basilica, Vatican City, the only work he signed. Completed when he was twenty-four, it shows a youthful Mary holding Christ's body with an expression of quiet resignation. The sculpture was damaged in 1972 by a hammer attack and is now displayed behind protective glass.
