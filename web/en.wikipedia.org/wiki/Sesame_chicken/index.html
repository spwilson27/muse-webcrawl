Sesame chicken is a dish commonly found in Chinese restaurants throughout the United States. The dish is similar to General Tso's chicken but the taste of the Chinese-based chicken is sweet rather than spicy.

Description

The dish involves chicken pieces, usually boneless thigh or breast meat, that are deboned, battered, and deep-fried, then dressed with a translucent, reddish-brown, sweet sauce made from corn starch, vinegar, wine or sake, chicken broth, and sugar. It is topped with sesame seeds (which may be toasted) and served with steamed, stir-fried, or blanched white or green vegetables such as broccoli, with a side of white rice. Another similar dish is "sesame shrimp," prepared in the same manner with shrimp instead of chicken.

Origins

Like many American Chinese dishes, sesame chicken was developed in the United States to suit American tastes, drawing on techniques of Chinese cooking — battered and fried meat finished in a sauce — while featuring a markedly sweeter flavour profile than traditional Chinese preparations of sesame-flavoured chicken.
