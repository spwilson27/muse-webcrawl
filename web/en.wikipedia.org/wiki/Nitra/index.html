Nitra

Nitra (German: Neutra; Hungarian: Nyitra) is a city in western Slovakia, situated at the foot of Zobor Mountain in the valley of the river Nitra. With a population of about 78,000, it is the fifth-largest city in Slovakia. Nitra is one of the oldest cities in the country, with a history reaching back to the 9th century as a seat of the Principality of Nitra within Great Moravia.

History

Nitra was an important centre of the Slavic principality ruled by Prince Pribina, who had the first known Christian church in Slovakia consecrated there around 828. Under Great Moravia it became a bishopric seat. After the Hungarian conquest it belonged to the Kingdom of Hungary for roughly a thousand years, before becoming part of Czechoslovakia in 1918 and of independent Slovakia in 1993.

Landmarks

The city's dominant feature is Nitra Castle, incorporating St Emmeram's Cathedral, parts of which date from the 11th century. Other sights include the Piarist church, the synagogue, and the Slovak Agricultural Museum. Nitra hosts the Constantine the Philosopher University and the Slovak University of Agriculture.

Economy

Nitra is an industrial and agricultural centre. A major employer is the Jaguar Land Rover plant opened in 2018, and the city hosts the Agrokomplex exhibition grounds, one of the largest fair venues in Slovakia.
