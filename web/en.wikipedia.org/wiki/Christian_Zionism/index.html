Christian Zionism

Christian Zionism is a political and religious ideology that, in a Christian context, espouses the return of the Jewish people to the Holy Land and their retention of the Land of Israel. It is a type of Zionism that holds Christian supporters of Zionism believe the gathering of the Jews in Israel is a prerequisite for the Second Coming of Jesus Christ. The term began to be used in the mid-20th century, in place of Christian restorationism, as proponents of the ideology rallied in support of Israel.

Support for the concept sometimes overlaps with the premillennialist and dispensationalist schools of Christian theology, particularly within American evangelicalism. Christian Zionists believe that the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948 was in accordance with Bible prophecy and signals the approach of the end times.

History

The roots of Christian Zionism lie in the Protestant Reformation and the spread of biblical literalism in England in the 17th century. Figures such as Thomas Brightman and Joseph Mede wrote of the restoration of the Jews to Palestine. In the 19th century, British figures including Lord Shaftesbury advocated for a Jewish homeland, and American preachers such as William Eugene Blackstone petitioned the United States government in support of the cause.

After the Six-Day War of 1967, Christian Zionism grew considerably in the United States, with organizations such as Christians United for Israel (founded in 2006 by pastor John Hagee) becoming significant political forces.

Criticism

Christian Zionism has been criticized by other Christian traditions, including Palestinian Christians and bodies such as the World Council of Churches, as well as by some Jewish commentators who question its theological motivations. The 2006 Jerusalem Declaration on Christian Zionism, issued by Palestinian church leaders, rejected the ideology as a false teaching.
