Cevian

In geometry, a cevian is a line segment in a triangle with one endpoint at a vertex and the other endpoint on the opposite side (or its extension). Familiar cevians include medians, angle bisectors, and altitudes. The term is generally attributed to Giovanni Ceva, whose theorem characterises when three cevians are concurrent.

Ceva's theorem

Ceva's theorem states that for a triangle ABC and points D, E, F on sides BC, CA, AB respectively, the cevians AD, BE, CF are concurrent if and only if (BD/DC)·(CE/EA)·(AF/FB) = 1. Its trigonometric form uses the sines of the split angles. Menelaus's theorem gives the corresponding condition for collinearity of points on the sides.

Lengths and related concepts

Stewart's theorem relates the length of a cevian to the sides of the triangle and the segments it creates on the opposite side. Special concurrent cevians define well-known triangle centres: medians meet at the centroid, bisectors at the incenter, and altitudes at the orthocenter. Splitters and symmedians are other studied families of cevians.
