Taupō Volcano

LocationNorth Island, New Zealand
TypeRhyolitic caldera supervolcano
Last major eruptionsOruanui (≈26,500 yrs ago); Hatepe (232 CE)

The Taupō Volcano is a large rhyolitic supervolcano in the centre of New Zealand's North Island, lying beneath Lake Taupō. It forms part of the Taupō Volcanic Zone, one of the most frequently active volcanic regions on Earth.

Eruptions

The Oruanui eruption of about 26,500 years ago ejected roughly 1,170 km³ of material and created much of the present caldera — one of the largest eruptions of the last 70,000 years. The later Hatepe eruption of 232 CE (± a few years) was the most violent of the last 5,000 years, producing pyroclastic flows that devastated the surrounding region and ash that reportedly turned skies red as far away as Rome and China.

Monitoring

Although currently dormant, the volcano is actively monitored by GeoNet; episodes of ground deformation and earthquake swarms, such as in 2022, reflect ongoing unrest in the caldera system.
