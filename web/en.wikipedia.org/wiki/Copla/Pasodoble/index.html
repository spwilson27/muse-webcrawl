Pasodoble

The pasodoble (Spanish: "double step") is a fast-paced Spanish military march used by infantry troops, with music in 2/4 time and a tempo of about 120 beats per minute — the pace of the regulation quick march. It is also the music traditionally played during the entry of bullfighters into the ring (paseíllo) and during the faena, and a dramatic ballroom dance of the International Latin programme, in which the man portrays the matador and the woman the cape or the bull.

Music

The pasodoble is built in a binary form with characteristic accentuated beats and dramatic pauses, usually in a minor key with strongly marked rhythms imitating the drama of the bullring. Famous examples include "España cañí" (composed by Pascual Marquina), "El gato montés" by Manuel Penella, and "Suspiros de España". Many Spanish towns have their own pasodoble, played by municipal bands at festivals and processions, and the form is a staple of bullfighting afternoons across Spain, southern France and Latin America.

Dance

As a social and competitive dance, the pasodoble developed in southern France and Spain and entered the International Style Latin American programme of ballroom dancing. The dance is theatrical and staccato: partners strike sculptural poses recalling the bullfight, with the man as torero and the woman variously as his cape, the bull, or a flamenco dancer, echoing the shapes of flamenco dance. It is danced almost exclusively in competition, rarely socially.
