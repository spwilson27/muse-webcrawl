Cuplé

The cuplé (from the French couplet) was a popular Spanish stage song of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, performed in variety theatres, cafés-concert and the género chico. Usually sung by a female performer, the cupletista, cuplés were light, witty, often risqué songs commenting on current events, fashions and politics, with catchy refrains the audience could join.

History

The genre arrived from France in the 1890s and flourished in Spain until the 1930s, declining with the Spanish Civil War and changing tastes. Famous cuplés include "La violetera", "El relicario", "La chica del 17" and "Tápame, tápame". Leading performers such as Raquel Meller achieved international fame; Meller's renditions of "La violetera" and "El relicario" became emblematic, and she was admired by audiences in Europe and the Americas. Another celebrated cupletista, La Fornarina, was among the first stars of the genre in Madrid.

Characteristics

A cuplé was typically short, strophic, and theatrical, relying as much on the singer's gesture, costume and comic or suggestive delivery as on the music. The double meanings of many lyrics allowed performers to skirt censorship, and the genre is regarded as a precursor of the later copla song tradition and of the Spanish popular song of the mid-20th century.
