Copla

The copla is a traditional form of Spanish song and poetic stanza. As a verse form, a copla is a stanza — commonly of four octosyllabic lines, often with assonant rhyme — used throughout Spanish popular poetry. As a musical genre, copla refers to the theatrical popular song that flourished in Spain in the early and mid twentieth century, also called canción española.

The musical genre

The copla genre emerged from the cuplé and tonadilla traditions of the music hall and became Spain's dominant popular song form from the 1930s to the 1950s, strongly associated with the Franco era. Its songs are dramatic miniature narratives — frequently about love, jealousy, honor, and fate — with lush orchestral arrangements drawing on flamenco and pasodoble rhythms. Celebrated interpreters include Concha Piquer, Imperio Argentina, Juanita Reina, Lola Flores, and Rocío Jurado; classic songs such as "Ojos verdes," "María de la O," and "Tatuaje" were written by teams including Rafael de León, Antonio Quintero, and Manuel Quiroga.

Later reception

After the transition to democracy, the genre was sometimes dismissed as a symbol of the old regime, but it has since been reassessed and revived by artists across genres, and regional variants of copla-like song traditions also thrive in Latin America.
