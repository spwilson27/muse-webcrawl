Cuplé

The cuplé is a genre of light, often risqué Spanish popular song that flourished on the variety and music-hall stages of Spain in the late 19th and especially the early 20th centuries. Derived from the French couplet, the cuplé was performed by a usually female singer — a cupletista — accompanied by piano or small orchestra, and featured witty, topical, and frequently double-entendre lyrics.

Characteristics

Cuplés were short, catchy songs commenting on fashion, politics, and social mores. The more suggestive numbers were known as cuplés sicalípticos, and their performers cultivated a playful, provocative stage persona within the limits of the era's censorship.

Famous performers

La Fornarina was an early star of the genre. Raquel Meller became its most famous exponent, achieving international fame in the 1920s with songs such as "La violetera" and "El relicario", and performing in Paris, London, and New York. Other celebrated cupletistas included Pastora Imperio and La Goya.

Legacy

The cuplé declined with changing tastes and the Spanish Civil War, but it influenced the later copla tradition and was revived in nostalgic stage shows and recordings in the later 20th century. Sara Montiel performed cuplés in her films, renewing interest in the repertoire.
