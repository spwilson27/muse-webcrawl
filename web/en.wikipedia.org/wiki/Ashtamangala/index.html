Ashtamangala
Sanskrit: aṣṭamaṅgala, "eight auspicious signs"
Traditions: Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism

The Ashtamangala are a sacred suite of eight auspicious signs or symbols endemic to a number of religions including Hinduism, Jainism and Buddhism. The symbols or "symbolic attributes" differ between traditions, but in Tibetan Buddhism the set comprises a parasol, a pair of golden fish, a treasure vase, a lotus, a white conch shell, an endless knot, a banner of victory, and a wheel.

Buddhist symbolism

In Buddhism the eight symbols represent the offerings made by the gods to Shakyamuni Buddha after his enlightenment, and each carries a specific spiritual meaning: the wheel symbolises the Dharma, the endless knot interdependence, the conch the fame and sound of the teaching, and the golden fish freedom from suffering.

Other traditions

Hindu lists include the lion, bull, elephant and fly-whisk among other signs, while the Jain sets for the Digambara and Shvetambara traditions include the swastika, srivatsa and the mirror (darpana).
