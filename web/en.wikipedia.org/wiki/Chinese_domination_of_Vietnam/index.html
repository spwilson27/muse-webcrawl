Chinese domination of Vietnam

Period111 BC – AD 40; 43–544; 602–905; 1407–1427
Vietnamese termBắc thuộc ("belonging to the north")
Key revoltsTrung sisters (40–43); Ngô Quyền's victory at Bạch Đằng (938)

Chinese domination of Vietnam refers to the roughly thousand years, divided into four periods, during which northern Vietnam was ruled as part of successive Chinese empires, from the Han conquest of Nanyue in 111 BC until independence was established in the 10th century.

Periods

The first domination (111 BC – AD 40) ended with the revolt of the Trưng sisters; after their defeat, a second period lasted until 544. A third domination followed from 602 to 905 amid the collapse of the Tang dynasty. A brief fourth period occurred under the Ming (1407–1427), ending with Lê Lợi's Lam Sơn uprising. Ngô Quyền's defeat of the Southern Han fleet at the Battle of Bạch Đằng in 938 is traditionally taken as the restoration of Vietnamese independence.

Legacy

Chinese rule deeply influenced Vietnamese administration, writing (chữ Hán), Confucianism, Buddhism and law, while resistance to it became central to Vietnamese national identity.
