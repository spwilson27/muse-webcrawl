Cantonese opera is one of the major forms of Chinese opera. It originated in Guangdong province and is performed in the Cantonese language, combining music, singing, martial arts, acrobatics and acting. It is popular in Guangdong, Guangxi, Hong Kong and Macau, and in Chinatowns worldwide. In 2009 it was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

History

The form developed from northern opera traditions brought south during the Ming dynasty, absorbing local Guangdong music and dialect by the late Qing. Troupes once performed on red boats plying the Pearl River Delta. Twentieth-century stars such as Ma Shizeng, Hong Xiannü, Yam Kim-fai and Bak Sheut-sin transformed the repertoire; playwright Tang Ti-sheng created classics such as The Flower Princess (1957).

Performance elements

Productions combine singing in stylised vocal modes, spoken dialogue, elaborate painted-face makeup and heavily embroidered costumes with headdresses and long "water sleeves". Role types follow Chinese opera convention — sheng (male), dan (female), jing (painted face) and chou (clown). The orchestra mixes Chinese instruments (gaohu, erxian, pipa) with percussion, and historically included Western instruments such as the violin and saxophone.
