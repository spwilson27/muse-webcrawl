Pinewood Derby

Pinewood Derby cars racing down the track

The Pinewood Derby is a gravity-powered model car racing event for Cub Scouts in the Boy Scouts of America. Scouts, with the help of a parent or guardian, build a small wooden car from an official kit — a block of pine wood, plastic wheels and metal axles — and race it against others on a sloped multi-lane track.

History

The event was created in 1953 by Cubmaster Don Murphy of Pack 280C in Manhattan Beach, California, whose son was too young to compete in the Soap Box Derby. Murphy designed miniature cars that could be built at home and raced indoors. The first race was held on 15 May 1953, and the idea spread rapidly through the Boy Scouts; it has since become one of the most familiar activities in Cub Scouting, with similar events run by other youth organisations such as Awana and Royal Rangers.

Rules

Under standard BSA rules a car may weigh no more than 5 ounces (141.7 g), must be built from the official kit for that season's race, and rolls on gravity alone — no motors, springs or starting devices. Cars race down a track, typically about 32 feet long, first rolling down an incline and then coasting along a flat section to the finish, where electronic timers or judges determine placing.

Design and physics

Competitive builders attend to weight placement (adding weights so the centre of mass sits just ahead of the rear axle), wheel and axle polishing to reduce friction, alignment, and aerodynamic shaping of the block. The event is often used to teach basic physics, craftsmanship and sportsmanship.
