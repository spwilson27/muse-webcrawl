Italo-Turkish War

The Italo-Turkish War (1911–1912) was fought between the Kingdom of Italy and the Ottoman Empire over control of the North African provinces of Tripolitania and Cyrenaica — modern-day Libya. Italy's victory stripped the Ottomans of their last North African holdings and foreshadowed the First Balkan War.

Causes

Italy, a latecomer to European colonialism, coveted Libya as its "Fourth Shore" and demanded Ottoman withdrawal. An ultimatum expired on 29 September 1911, and Italy declared war, landing troops at Tripoli, Benghazi, Tobruk, Derna, and Homs.

Course of the war

Although Ottoman regulars under officers like Mustafa Kemal and Enver Pasha, allied with Senussi forces, contained the Italians on the coast, Italy's naval superiority proved decisive. Italian forces occupied the Dodecanese islands in the Aegean in May 1912 and struck Ottoman positions in the Dardanelles and the Red Sea. The war also saw the first combat use of powered aircraft, by Italian aviators, and the first aerial bombing in history (1 November 1911).

Peace and consequences

The Treaty of Lausanne (signed at Ouchy, Switzerland, 18 October 1912) gave Italy de facto control of Libya; Italy retained the Dodecanese "temporarily," keeping them until 1947. The war exposed Ottoman weakness and encouraged the Balkan League to attack the empire days before the peace was even signed, triggering the First Balkan War.
