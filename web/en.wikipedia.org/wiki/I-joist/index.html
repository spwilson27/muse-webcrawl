I-joist

An I-joist is an engineered wood product shaped like the letter "I", widely used as a floor or roof joist in residential and light commercial construction. It consists of top and bottom flanges—usually laminated veneer lumber or solid sawn timber—joined by a vertical web of oriented strand board (OSB) or plywood. I-joists were introduced commercially in 1969 by the Trus Joist company.

Advantages

Compared with solid-sawn joists, I-joists are lighter, straighter and stronger for their weight, resist warping, shrinking and squeaky floors, and can span longer distances. Pre-cut or knock-out holes in the web allow plumbing and electrical services to pass through, though larger holes and cuts are strictly limited by manufacturers' specifications.

Fire performance and handling

Because the thin OSB web burns through faster than solid timber, I-joists have drawn criticism from firefighters, and modern building codes often require a protective membrane such as gypsum board when they are used in floor assemblies. They must also be braced during installation to prevent buckling before the deck is fitted.
