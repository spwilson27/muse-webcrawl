Butterfly Lovers Violin Concerto

The Butterfly Lovers Violin Concerto (梁祝小提琴协奏曲, Liáng Zhù xiǎotíqín xiézòuqǔ) is a concerto for violin and orchestra composed in 1959 by Chen Gang and He Zhanhao, based on the Chinese folktale of Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai, the "Butterfly Lovers". It is the most famous Chinese violin concerto and one of the most widely performed works of Chinese orchestral music.

Composition

He Zhanhao and Chen Gang, students at the Shanghai Conservatory of Music, wrote the concerto in 1959. Drawing on melodies from Yue opera and using the Chinese pentatonic scale within a Western sonata-form framework, they created a single-movement work of about 25 minutes. The solo violin represents Zhu Yingtai; the solo cello answers as Liang Shanbo.

Structure and story

The exposition portrays the lovers meeting at school; the development depicts their growing love, Zhu's forced betrothal to another man, and Liang's death; the recapitulation describes Zhu's suicide at Liang's grave and their transformation into butterflies, reprising the opening theme.

Reception

Premiered in Shanghai in 1959 with Yu Lina as soloist, the concerto survived suppression during the Cultural Revolution and, after the 1970s, became a staple of concert halls worldwide, recorded by violinists including Vanessa-Mae (in a popular crossover arrangement) and many Chinese soloists.
