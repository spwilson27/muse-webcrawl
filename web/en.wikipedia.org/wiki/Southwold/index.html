Southwold is a seaside town and civil parish on the North Sea coast in the East Suffolk district of Suffolk, England. It lies at the mouth of the River Blyth, within the Suffolk Coast and Heaths Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. The town is known for its pier, lighthouse, colourful beach huts, and the Adnams brewery.

History

Southwold was recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 as a fishing port. The Battle of Sole Bay, fought offshore against the Dutch in 1672, was the first battle of the Third Anglo-Dutch War. Much of the town was destroyed by a great fire in 1659, and the open greens that remain act as firebreaks. The parish church of St Edmund, built in the 15th century, is one of the finest in Suffolk.

Landmarks

Southwold Pier, originally built in 1900 and rebuilt in 2001, houses the eccentric "Under the Pier Show" of handmade arcade machines. The lighthouse, built in 1890, remains operational. A row of cannons on Gun Hill commemorates the Battle of Sole Bay. The town's old harbour lies across the river at Walberswick, reached by a foot ferry.

Culture

Adnams has brewed beer in the town since 1872 and operates a distillery and shops there. The town has literary associations: George Orwell lived there in the 1930s, and it appears as "Knype Hill" in his work.
