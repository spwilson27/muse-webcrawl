Arne Næss

Born27 January 1912, Oslo, Norway
Died12 January 2009 (aged 96), Oslo, Norway
OccupationPhilosopher, mountaineer
Known forDeep ecology, ecosophy

Arne Dekke Eide Næss (27 January 1912 – 12 January 2009) was a Norwegian philosopher and mountaineer who founded the deep ecology movement. He was a professor at the University of Oslo and one of Norway's most influential post-war intellectuals.

Philosophy

In a 1973 article, Næss distinguished between "shallow" and "deep" ecology movements, arguing that environmental concern should address the deeper philosophical causes of ecological crisis, including anthropocentrism. He developed his own philosophy, "ecosophy T", named after his mountain hut Tvergastein, emphasising self-realisation and the intrinsic value of all living beings.

Mountaineering

Næss was an accomplished climber who led the first ascent of Tirich Mir (7,708 m) in Pakistan in 1950. Mountaineering and time spent at his cabin in the Hallingskarvet mountains deeply shaped his philosophy of nature.

Later life

Næss remained active in environmental activism into old age, participating in nonviolent protests against dam construction in Norway. He died in Oslo in 2009, shortly before his 97th birthday.
