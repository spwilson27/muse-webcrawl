Michigan Stadium

Michigan Stadium, nicknamed "The Big House", is the football stadium for the University of Michigan in Ann Arbor, Michigan. It is the largest stadium in the United States and the Western Hemisphere, the third-largest stadium in the world, and the 34th-largest sports venue in the world. Its official capacity is 107,601, but it has hosted crowds in excess of 115,000, including the record for a college football game: 115,109 spectators on 7 September 2013 for Michigan's game against Notre Dame, the largest crowd to watch a football game in American history.

History

The stadium was built in 1927 at a cost of about $950,000 under athletic director Fielding H. Yost, whose vision of ever-expanding crowds led to foundations sized for a future upper deck. Originally seating 72,000, it replaced Ferry Field; Michigan defeated Ohio Wesleyan 33–0 in the first game and dedicated the stadium with a 21–0 win over Ohio State on 22 October 1927. Successive expansions — including the 2010 renovation that added luxury suites, club seating and new scoreboards — lifted capacity past 100,000 in 1975, where it has remained ever since; Michigan has drawn crowds above 100,000 for every home game since November 1975, an NCAA record streak.

Other events

Michigan Stadium has hosted ice hockey's "Big Chill at the Big House" (2010), which set a world hockey attendance record of 104,073, and the 2014 NHL Winter Classic between the Toronto Maple Leafs and Detroit Red Wings before 105,491 fans. It has also staged soccer matches, including a 2014 International Champions Cup game between Real Madrid and Manchester United that drew 109,318 — the largest soccer crowd in US history — as well as graduations and concerts.
