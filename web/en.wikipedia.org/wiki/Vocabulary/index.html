Vocabulary

A vocabulary (also known as a lexicon) is a set of words, typically ones that are well or somewhat known to a particular individual or in a particular language. A person's vocabulary is the collection of words within a language that they are familiar with. Vocabulary knowledge grows throughout life and is a fundamental component of language proficiency, reading comprehension and academic success.

Types

Researchers commonly distinguish a receptive (passive) vocabulary — words understood when heard or read — from a productive (active) vocabulary — words a person can use correctly in speech or writing. Receptive vocabulary is normally much larger. A distinction is also drawn between oral and literate vocabularies, the latter including words encountered mainly in print.

Size and growth

Children typically learn their first words around age one and acquire vocabulary at a remarkable rate during the school years, largely through incidental exposure in conversation and reading. Estimates of adult native-speaker vocabulary range from roughly 17,000 to 20,000 word families for English, depending on how words are counted. Estimates vary because of questions about compounds, proper names and derived forms.

Vocabulary acquisition

First-language vocabulary is acquired implicitly through interaction, while second-language vocabulary is studied both incidentally and deliberately with techniques such as spaced repetition, word lists and extensive reading. Depth of word knowledge — pronunciation, spelling, grammar, collocations and associations — develops alongside breadth (the number of words known). Scholars such as Paul Nation have systematised vocabulary teaching by frequency bands, prioritising the most common 2,000–3,000 word families, which cover the great majority of everyday text.

Word frequency and coverage

Word frequency in natural language follows a skewed distribution: a small core of very frequent words accounts for most running text, while thousands of rarer words make up the long tail. Corpus linguistics uses large text collections to measure these frequencies and to define the core vocabulary of a language, informing dictionary making, readability measures and language curricula.
