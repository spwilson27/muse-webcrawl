Rudraprayag district is a district of Uttarakhand state in northern India, created in 1997 from parts of Chamoli, Tehri Garhwal and Pauri Garhwal districts. Its administrative headquarters is the town of Rudraprayag.

Geography

The district lies in the Garhwal Himalaya at the confluence of the Alaknanda and Mandakini rivers — one of the Panch Prayag, the five sacred confluences of the Alaknanda. The Mandakini valley leads to the temple town of Kedarnath, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and a Char Dham pilgrimage site, making the district a major centre of Hindu pilgrimage.

2013 floods

In June 2013, catastrophic floods and landslides in the Mandakini valley devastated Kedarnath and settlements across the district, killing thousands of pilgrims and residents. Reconstruction and new pilgrim infrastructure followed. The district is also known for the rare musk deer and for the monal, Uttarakhand's state bird, in the Kedarnath Wildlife Sanctuary.
