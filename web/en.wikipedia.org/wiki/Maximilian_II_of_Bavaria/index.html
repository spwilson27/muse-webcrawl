Maximilian II of Bavaria

Maximilian II
King of Bavaria: 1848–1864
Born: November 28, 1811, Munich
Died: March 10, 1864, Munich
Spouse: Marie of Prussia
Successor: Ludwig II

Maximilian II (28 November 1811 – 10 March 1864) reigned as King of Bavaria from 1848, when his father Ludwig I abdicated during the revolutions of that year, until his death. He is remembered as a patron of science, the arts and Bavarian culture, and as a comparatively liberal and scholarly monarch.

Reign

As crown prince he rebuilt Hohenschwangau Castle in romantic style. As king he promoted education and scholarship, brought notable academics (the "Nordlichter") to Munich, supported Bavarian folk culture and history, and initiated projects such as the Maximilianeum foundation. His reign balanced constitutional government with cautious reform, while resisting both reaction and Prussian domination of German affairs; he supported the "Trias" idea of a bloc of middle German states.

Family and death

He married Princess Marie of Prussia in 1842; their sons became kings Ludwig II and Otto. Maximilian II died after a short illness in 1864, and Ludwig II succeeded him.
