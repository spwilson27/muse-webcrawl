Dolichocephaly (derived from the Ancient Greek δολιχός, "long", and κεφαλή, "head") is a condition where the head is longer than would be expected, relative to its width. In humans, the condition is defined by a cephalic index of less than 75. The term scaphocephaly is applied when the long-headed shape results from premature fusion of the sagittal suture.

Causes

Dolichocephaly can be a normal variant of head shape found in many healthy populations, and historically it was used — misleadingly — in attempts at racial classification of skull shapes. Pathological dolichocephaly may occur in syndromes such as Marfan syndrome and is common in premature infants, whose soft skulls are moulded by side-lying positioning in neonatal care; this "positional" form usually resolves with growth. When caused by sagittal craniosynostosis, the condition may require surgical treatment.

In animals

The term is also used in cynology to describe dog breeds with long, narrow heads, such as the Greyhound, Borzoi, and Collie, contrasted with brachycephalic breeds like the Pug and mesaticephalic breeds of intermediate proportions.
