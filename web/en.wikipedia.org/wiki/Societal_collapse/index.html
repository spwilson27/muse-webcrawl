Societal collapse

Societal collapse (also known as civilizational collapse) is the fall of a complex society characterized by the loss of cultural identity and of social complexity as an adaptive system, the fall of government, and the rise of violence. Possible causes of a societal collapse include natural catastrophe, war, pestilence, famine, economic meltdown, population decline or overshoot, and the interaction of several of these factors.

Sometimes a collapsed society can be recovered and re-established, while at other times a society itself is extinguished, as with the Classic Maya collapse or the disappearance of the Norse settlements in Greenland.

Definitions and theory

Joseph Tainter, in The Collapse of Complex Societies (1988), defines collapse as a rapid, significant loss of an established level of sociopolitical complexity, arguing that societies collapse when diminishing returns on investment in complexity make continued problem-solving unsustainable. Jared Diamond's Collapse (2005) identified five interacting factors: environmental damage, climate change, hostile neighbors, loss of essential trading partners, and a society's own maladaptive responses.

Examples

  • The Late Bronze Age collapse of the eastern Mediterranean (c. 1200 BC)
  • The fall of the Western Roman Empire
  • The Classic Maya collapse (8th–9th centuries)
  • The collapse of the Khmer Empire
  • The abandonment of Norse Greenland

Contemporary discussion

Modern debate considers whether industrial civilization faces collapse risks from climate change, resource depletion, and systemic fragility, a field sometimes called collapsology.
