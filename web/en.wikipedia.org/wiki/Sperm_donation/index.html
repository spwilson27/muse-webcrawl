Sperm donation

Sperm donation is the provision by a man of his sperm with the intention that it be used in the artificial insemination or other fertility treatment of one or more women who are not his sexual partners, in order that they may become pregnant. Where pregnancies go to full term, the sperm donor will be the biological father of every baby born from his donations. The man is known as a sperm donor and the sperm he provides is known as donor sperm.

Process

Donation usually takes place through a sperm bank, where donors are screened medically and genetically, give samples over an extended period, and the semen is quarantined and frozen before release. Recipients choose donors from catalogues listing physical characteristics, education, and sometimes photographs or essays. Donation may also occur privately by arrangement between parties, which can raise legal questions about parentage and child support depending on jurisdiction.

Anonymity and identity

Historically most donation was anonymous. Since Sweden abolished anonymity in 1985, a number of countries — including the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and parts of Australia — have moved to identity-release systems under which donor-conceived adults can obtain the donor's identity. Consumer DNA testing has in practice made anonymity difficult to guarantee, a development debated by ethicists, donors, and donor-conceived people.

Law and ethics

Laws govern who may access treatment, whether donors are paid, how many offspring one donor may have (to limit risks of accidental half-sibling relationships), and record-keeping. Ethical debates concern the rights of donor-conceived people to know their origins, the commodification of gametes, and screening standards.
