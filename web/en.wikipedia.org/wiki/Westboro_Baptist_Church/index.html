The Westboro Baptist Church (WBC) is an American unaffiliated Primitive Baptist church in Topeka, Kansas, founded in 1955 by Fred Phelps. It is widely described as a hate group by organisations including the Anti-Defamation League and the Southern Poverty Law Center, and is monitored as such. The church, composed largely of members of Phelps's extended family, became internationally notorious for its picketing campaigns, conducted under slogans condemning homosexuality and what it regards as the moral failings of the United States and other nations.

Activities

WBC members picket funerals — including those of American servicemen and servicewomen killed in action, victims of mass shootings and natural disasters, and public figures — holding signs with provocative slogans and attributing tragedies to divine judgement. The church says it has conducted tens of thousands of pickets since 1991. Its activities have prompted counter-protests, laws restricting funeral picketing in several US states, and the formation of groups such as the Patriot Guard Riders, who shield mourners from the pickets.

Snyder v. Phelps

In the landmark case Snyder v. Phelps (2011), the Supreme Court of the United States ruled 8–1 that the First Amendment protected the church's right to picket a military funeral on matters of public concern, overturning a damages award to the deceased serviceman's father. The ruling affirmed that even hurtful speech on public issues cannot be restricted simply because it is offensive.

Beliefs and membership

The church holds a hyper-Calvinist theology of predestination and teaches that God hates most of humanity, with only a small elect destined for salvation. Its membership has been reported at a few dozen, mostly relatives of the founder. Following Fred Phelps's death in 2014, leadership passed to a board of elders; several younger family members, most prominently Megan Phelps-Roper, have left the church and become critics of it.
