History of the Prophets and Kings

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The History of the Prophets and Kings (Arabic: تاريخ الرسل والملوك, Taʾrīkh al-rusul wa-l-mulūk), commonly known as the History of al-Tabari, is a monumental Arabic historical chronicle written by the Persian historian Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari (839–923). It is one of the most important primary sources for the early history of Islam and the pre-Islamic Near East.

Contents

The work begins with the Creation, retells biblical and legendary Persian history, narrates the life of the Prophet Muhammad, and continues year by year through the Rashidun, Umayyad, and Abbasid caliphates, ending in the year 915. Al-Tabari typically presents multiple reports for an event, each supported by a chain of transmission (isnad), often leaving the reader to judge among conflicting accounts — a method characteristic of early Islamic historiography.

Sources and significance

Al-Tabari drew on earlier historians, including Ibn Ishaq's biography of the Prophet (known largely through Ibn Hisham), and on transmitters such as Sayf ibn Umar, whose reliability later scholars debated. Although al-Tabari's chronicle was abridged and translated into Persian by Bal'ami in the 10th century, the full Arabic text became widely available only with the Leiden edition of 1879–1901, edited by M. J. de Goeje. A complete English translation, The History of al-Ṭabarī, was published by SUNY Press in 40 volumes (1985–2007).

Related works

Al-Tabari also wrote a celebrated Qur'an commentary, the Jāmiʿ al-bayān. Continuations of his History were composed by later authors to extend the annals beyond 915.
