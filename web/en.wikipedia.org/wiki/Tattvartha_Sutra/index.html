Tattvartha Sutra

The Tattvartha Sutra (also spelled Tattvarthadhigama Sutra) is the most authoritative text of Jain philosophy, accepted by both the Digambara and Svetambara traditions. It is traditionally attributed to the monk Umaswati (Umasvami), who lived around the 2nd to 5th century CE.

Content

Written in Sanskrit aphorisms, the text systematically summarises Jain doctrine in ten chapters. Its famous opening sutras state that right faith, right knowledge and right conduct together constitute the path to liberation. It expounds the seven categories of truth (tattvas): soul (jiva), non-soul (ajiva), influx of karma (asrava), bondage (bandha), stoppage (samvara), shedding (nirjara) and liberation (moksha).

Commentaries

Numerous commentaries have been written on the sutras, including the Sarvarthasiddhi and the Rajavartika. The text also contains early discussions of cosmology, ethics, and the vows observed by monks and laypeople.
