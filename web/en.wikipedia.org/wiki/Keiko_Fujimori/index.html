Keiko Fujimori

Keiko Sofía Fujimori Higuchi (born 25 May 1975) is a Peruvian politician, the daughter of former president Alberto Fujimori. She served as First Lady of Peru from 1994 to 2000 after her parents' separation, was a congresswoman from 2006 to 2011, and as leader of the right-wing party Fuerza Popular (formerly Fuerza 2011) ran for president three times, losing the runoffs of 2011 (to Ollanta Humala), 2016 (to Pedro Pablo Kuczynski) and 2021 (to Pedro Castillo) — the last two by extremely narrow margins.

Early life and First Lady

Fujimori studied business administration in the United States, at Stony Brook University and Boston University, later earning an MBA from Columbia. In August 1994 her father named the 19-year-old First Lady after separating from Susana Higuchi, who had denounced corruption in the regime; Keiko remained in the role until Alberto Fujimori's fall in 2000.

Political career

Elected to Congress in 2006 with the highest personal vote in the country, she built Fujimorismo into a disciplined national movement. Her 2016 Fuerza Popular won a commanding congressional majority, and her bloc's confrontations with President Kuczynski contributed to his resignation in 2018. In October 2018 she was arrested in the Odebrecht-related "Cocktails case" over alleged illegal campaign financing and money laundering, spending periods in preventive detention (2018–2019 and briefly in 2020) while denying the charges; the case has remained in Peru's courts for years. Her 2021 runoff loss to Castillo, by about 44,000 votes, was followed by fraud claims her campaign could not substantiate. She announced a fourth presidential candidacy for the 2026 general election.
