Brown Corpus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Full name: Brown University Standard Corpus of Present-Day American English
Compiled: 1963–1964
Size: ~1 million words

The Brown University Standard Corpus of Present-Day American English, better known simply as the Brown Corpus, is an electronic collection of text samples of American English, the first major corpus of varied English in machine-readable form. It was compiled at Brown University in the 1960s by W. Nelson Francis and Henry Kučera.

The corpus contains 500 samples of English-language text, each 2,000 words long, drawn from works first published in 1961 in the United States. The samples span fifteen genre categories, including press reportage, editorials, fiction, religion, learned writing and humour, in proportions designed to reflect the range of published prose of the time.

Tagging and influence

The corpus was tagged for part of speech using an original 87-tag set, and its word-frequency analyses—published as Computational Analysis of Present-Day American English (1967)—became foundational data for corpus linguistics and computational linguistics. The Brown Corpus provided a model for many later corpora, including the British LOB Corpus, which copied its sampling frame so that American and British English could be compared directly.

Legacy

Although small by modern standards, the Brown Corpus remains widely used in language teaching, lexicography and natural language processing, and its "Brown family" of corpora—including diachronic successors sampling 1901, 1931 and 2006—enables the study of language change over time.
