Point reflection

In geometry, a point reflection (or point symmetry) is a transformation in which every point of a figure is mapped to a point on the opposite side of a fixed centre, at the same distance from it. The centre is the midpoint of every segment joining a point to its image. In the plane, point reflection about a centre is equivalent to a rotation of 180 degrees about that centre.

In coordinates and higher dimensions

Reflecting a point (x, y) through the origin gives (−x, −y); through a centre (a, b) it gives (2a−x, 2b−y). In three dimensions, point reflection through a centre is equivalent to inversion through that point combined with scaling — it reverses orientation in odd dimensions but preserves it in even dimensions. A figure that maps onto itself under some point reflection is said to have point symmetry, or 2-fold rotational symmetry; parallelograms, circles, and regular hexagons have it in the plane, while triangles and semicircles do not.

Applications

Point symmetry appears in crystallography, where centrosymmetric crystals lack certain optical properties such as piezoelectricity in some classes, and in physics, where spatial inversion is the parity operation P of quantum mechanics.
