Unani medicine

Unani or Yunani medicine (Urdu: طب یونانی, tibb yūnānī) is the term for Perso-Arabic traditional medicine as practised in Mughal India and among South Asian Muslim cultures. The term derives from the Arabic word Yūnānī, meaning "Greek", because the Perso-Arabic system of medicine was based on the teachings of the Greek physicians Hippocrates and Galen.

Theory

Unani medicine is based on the classical four-humour theory: blood (dam), phlegm (balgham), yellow bile (safrā), and black bile (saudā). Each person is held to possess a unique temperament (mizāj) arising from the mixture of these humours, and illness results from their imbalance. Treatment aims to restore balance through regimental therapy, diet, and drugs derived from herbs, minerals, and animal products. Practitioners are called hakims.

History

The system was developed and refined by medieval physicians such as Avicenna, whose Canon of Medicine became its foundational text, and Al-Razi. It was carried to India by the 13th century and flourished under the Mughal Empire. In modern India, Unani is officially recognized under the AYUSH ministry alongside Ayurveda, Yoga, Naturopathy, Siddha, and Homoeopathy, with degree courses and licensed practitioners; it is also practised in Pakistan, Bangladesh, and parts of the Middle East. Its efficacy claims are not generally supported by modern scientific evidence, though it remains a widely used traditional system.
