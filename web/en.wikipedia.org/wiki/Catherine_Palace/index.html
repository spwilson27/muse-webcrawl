The Catherine Palace (Russian: Екатерининский дворец) is a Rococo palace in Tsarskoye Selo (now Pushkin), 30 km south of Saint Petersburg, Russia. It was the summer residence of the Russian tsars.

History

Peter the Great gave the estate to his wife Catherine in 1710; a modest palace was built from 1717. Empress Elizabeth commissioned Bartolomeo Rastrelli to rebuild it on a grand scale (1752–1756), producing the 325-metre blue-and-white façade adorned with gilded atlantes and caryatids. Catherine the Great later remodelled interiors in Neoclassical taste with architect Charles Cameron.

Interiors

The palace is famed for the Great Hall and the Amber Room — panels of amber and gold leaf backed by mirrors, looted by German forces in 1941 and never recovered; a painstaking reconstruction was completed in 2003. The palace and park were devastated in the Second World War and have been progressively restored.

Museum

Today the palace is a museum within the Tsarskoye Selo State Museum-Preserve and part of the UNESCO World Heritage site of Saint Petersburg.
