Outboard motor

An outboard motor is a self-contained propulsion system for boats, mounted externally on the stern (transom) and containing the engine, gearbox and propeller in a single portable unit. The motor pivots for steering, typically via a tiller arm or remote steering, and can be tilted up out of the water for beaching and transport.

The first practical outboard motor is credited to Ole Evinrude, who marketed his detachable motor in the United States from 1909; brands such as Evinrude, Johnson, Mercury and Yamaha became dominant. Traditional outboards used two-stroke petrol engines; modern models are predominantly four-stroke, with growing numbers of direct-injected two-strokes and electric outboards. Power ranges from under 2 horsepower for small dinghies and trolling motors to several hundred horsepower; some modern units exceed 600 hp, and multiple outboards are commonly fitted to large offshore boats.

Operation uses a throttle and gear lever for forward, neutral and reverse, and most motors include safety kill-switch lanyards. Because the engine is outside the hull, outboards free up interior space and can be removed for maintenance.
