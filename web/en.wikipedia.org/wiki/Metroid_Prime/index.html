Samus Aran in Metroid Prime

Metroid Prime is a 2002 action-adventure game developed by Retro Studios and published by Nintendo for the GameCube. It is the fifth main installment in the Metroid series and the first rendered in 3D, played from a first-person perspective—the first Metroid game released in eight years, following Super Metroid (1994). It was widely acclaimed and is frequently listed among the greatest video games ever made.

Plot

Bounty hunter Samus Aran answers a distress call from a Space Pirate frigate orbiting the planet Tallon IV, once home to the Chozo civilization. Pursuing her nemesis Ridley to the planet's surface, she discovers the Pirates experimenting with Phazon, a highly mutagenic substance introduced by a meteor impact that also devastated the Chozo. Samus explores the planet's regions—gathering the twelve Chozo Artifacts to unlock the Impact Crater—while acquiring upgrades to her Power Suit, and ultimately destroys the Phazon-spawned creature Metroid Prime at the core.

Gameplay

The game adapts the series' exploration-driven formula to first person: Samus navigates an interconnected world, unlocking areas with the Morph Ball, missiles, new beams, and visors. The Scan Visor lets players scan creatures, objects, and lore entries, which tell much of the story. Combat uses a lock-on system rather than free-aim shooting, emphasizing movement and puzzle-like boss fights.

Reception and legacy

Metroid Prime sold over two million copies and won numerous Game of the Year awards, praised for its atmosphere, graphics, and successful translation of the series into 3D. It spawned two direct sequels, Metroid Prime 2: Echoes (2004) and Metroid Prime 3: Corruption (2007), collected in Metroid Prime: Trilogy (2009), and a remaster for Nintendo Switch in 2023.
