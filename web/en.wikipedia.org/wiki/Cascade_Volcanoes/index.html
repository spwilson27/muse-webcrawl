Cascade Volcanoes

The Cascade Volcanoes (also known as the Cascade Volcanic Arc) are a number of volcanoes in a volcanic arc in western North America, extending from southwestern British Columbia through Washington and Oregon to Northern California, a distance of well over 700 miles (1,100 km).

Geology

The arc formed as a result of subduction of the Juan de Fuca Plate and other small oceanic plates beneath the North American Plate along the Cascadia subduction zone. The volcanoes are typically stratovolcanoes built of andesite and dacite lavas.

Major volcanoes

Major Cascade volcanoes include Mount St. Helens, Mount Rainier, Mount Hood, Mount Shasta, Mount Baker, Glacier Peak, Mount Adams, Mount Jefferson, the Three Sisters, Newberry Volcano, Mount Lassen (Lassen Peak), and Mount Garibaldi in British Columbia. The 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens was the most destructive volcanic event in United States history. Lassen Peak erupted between 1914 and 1917, and is, with Mount St. Helens, one of two Cascade volcanoes to erupt in the contiguous United States during the 20th century.
