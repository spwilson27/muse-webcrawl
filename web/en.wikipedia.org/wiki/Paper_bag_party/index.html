Paper bag party

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A paper bag party is a term used to describe a social gathering or admissions practice, alleged to have occurred historically in some African-American social circles and organizations in the United States, at which entry was restricted according to skin colour: only people whose skin was lighter than or comparable to the colour of a brown paper bag were admitted. The practice is an example of colourism — discrimination within a racial group based on skin tone.

History

Accounts of the "paper bag test" are associated with early 20th-century social life in cities such as New Orleans, where a Creole colour hierarchy distinguished lighter-skinned people of mixed ancestry, and with certain churches, social clubs, and historically Black fraternities, sororities, and colleges. In some retellings, a brown paper bag was hung at the door of a venue, and guests whose skin was darker than the bag were refused entry.

Historians note that while skin-tone stratification and colourism within Black communities are well documented, the literal paper bag test is difficult to verify as a formal, widespread institutional practice, and it may in part be oral tradition and folklore expressing real social hierarchies. The phrase nonetheless became a widely recognized shorthand for intra-racial colour prejudice.

In popular culture

The paper bag test appears in discussions of colourism in literature, film, and scholarship. Spike Lee's film School Daze (1988) dramatizes colour conflicts within a Black college community, and the theme recurs in works examining the legacy of slavery, in which lighter skin was historically associated with house labour, manumission, and privilege.

Related tests

Other folk "tests" described in African-American oral tradition include the "comb test" (hair texture judged by whether a fine comb could pass through it) and the "flashlight test" in some accounts. Similar colour hierarchies have been documented in other post-slavery societies in the Caribbean and Latin America.
