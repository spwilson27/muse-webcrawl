Qiandao Lake

Qiandao Lake (Chinese: 千岛湖, "Thousand Island Lake") is a human-made lake and reservoir located in Chun'an County, Zhejiang Province, China. It was formed after the completion of the Xin'an River hydroelectric station in 1959. The lake covers an area of about 573 square kilometres (221 sq mi) and holds roughly 17.8 cubic kilometres of water.

History

The lake was created in September 1959 when the valley of the Xin'an River was flooded to build the Xin'anjiang Dam, the first large hydroelectric station designed and built by China itself. Before flooding, some 290,000 people were relocated from the area. Beneath the lake lie the remains of two ancient cities: Hecheng, dating to the Han dynasty, and Shicheng ("Lion City"), founded in the Tang dynasty and once the political and cultural centre of the region. The submerged ruins, preserved in unusually good condition, have become a noted destination for technical divers and were designated a cultural relic protection unit.

Geography and ecology

Qiandao Lake contains 1,078 large islands and thousands of smaller ones, giving it its name. The water is exceptionally clear and is famous as the source of Nongfu Spring bottled water. The surrounding region is densely forested, with forest coverage above 90 percent, and the lake supports a rich fishery, including the bighead carp used in the local speciality dish of fish head soup. The lake is also known for its caviar production from farmed sturgeon.

Tourism

The lake is a national AAAAA-rated tourist area. Visitors tour island clusters such as Meifeng Island, from whose summit the classic panorama of the archipelago can be seen, as well as islands known for monkeys, ostriches, snakes and historic locks. The area has hosted international swimming, cycling and triathlon events.
