LGBT rights in Mexico

Lesbian, gay, bisexual, and transgender (LGBT) rights in Mexico have expanded significantly in the twenty-first century. Same-sex sexual activity has been legal since 1871, when the federal penal code adopted the Napoleonic Code and omitted sodomy laws. Mexico City was one of the first jurisdictions in Latin America to legalise same-sex marriage, in 2010, and marriage equality became available nationwide by the end of 2022.

Same-sex marriage

In December 2009 the Legislative Assembly of the Federal District passed a law redefining marriage as the union of two persons, and the first same-sex marriages were performed in Mexico City in March 2010. In 2015 the Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation ruled that state bans on same-sex marriage were unconstitutional, enabling couples to marry through amparo injunctions. One by one states reformed their civil codes; Tamaulipas, the last state, legislated marriage equality in October 2022.

Anti-discrimination protections

A 2003 federal law, the Federal Law to Prevent and Eliminate Discrimination, prohibits discrimination based on sexual orientation, and in 2011 discrimination on the grounds of "sexual preferences" was added to Article 1 of the Mexican Constitution. Mexico City has one of the most progressive legal frameworks, including legal gender recognition for transgender people by administrative procedure since 2015.

Social attitudes

Public opinion has shifted steadily toward acceptance, particularly in large cities, though violence against LGBT people—especially transgender women—remains a serious concern, and Mexico records one of the highest numbers of reported murders of transgender people in the world. Annual pride marches in Mexico City draw hundreds of thousands of participants.
