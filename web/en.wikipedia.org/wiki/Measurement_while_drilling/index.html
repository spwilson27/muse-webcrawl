Measurement while drilling

Measurement while drilling (MWD) is a service within the oil industry in which downhole measurements — typically of the wellbore's direction, inclination, and toolface orientation — are made during drilling and transmitted to the surface in real time. It is one component of the broader practice of logging while drilling (LWD), which adds formation-evaluation sensors such as gamma ray, resistivity, density, and neutron porosity measurements.

Technology

MWD systems convey data to surface most commonly by mud-pulse telemetry, in which a valve in the drill string generates pressure pulses in the drilling fluid that are decoded at the surface; other methods include electromagnetic telemetry and wired drill pipe. The downhole tool, housed in a non-magnetic drill collar within the bottom hole assembly, contains accelerometers and magnetometers to measure inclination and azimuth, and is powered by batteries or a mud-driven turbine.

Uses

Real-time directional data allow drillers to steer directional and horizontal wells along planned trajectories, position wellbores precisely within reservoirs, and practice "geosteering" using LWD formation data. MWD services are provided by major oilfield service companies and are standard on most directional wells worldwide.
