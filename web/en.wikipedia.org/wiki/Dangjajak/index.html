Dangjajak

Dangjajak (Korean: 당적) is a traditional Korean end-blown bamboo flute, and the term is also used for the ensemble genre named after it. More precisely, dangjajak ("ensemble music centred on the dangjeok") is one of the standard repertories of Korean court-derived chamber music within the yeongsan hoesang tradition.

The instrument

The dangjeok (당적, "Tang flute") is a medium-sized transverse — in some classifications end-blown-corrected — bamboo flute with six finger holes, introduced from Tang-dynasty China and used in Korean court music for over a millennium. It is smaller than the daegeum and larger than the junggeum, and its clear, bright tone carries the melodic line in chamber ensembles. Its name preserves its origin as a Chinese import, in contrast to native Korean instruments.

The genre

As a genre, dangjajak is one of the three instrumental suites derived historically from the Buddhist chant yeongsan hoesang: alongside samhyeon jajak (led by plucked strings) and pyojong jajak, it presents the same core material arranged for a wind-centred ensemble including dangjeok, piri, haegeum, janggu and buk. Court and literati musicians of the Joseon dynasty cultivated these suites as refined chamber music, and the repertoire is still performed by the National Gugak Center in Seoul.
