Overview

Leptis (or Lepcis) Magna was a prominent city of the Carthaginian and Roman empires, on the Mediterranean coast of present-day Libya near Khoms. Founded by the Phoenicians, it became one of the most beautiful cities of the Roman world, enriched by trade in olive oil, grain, ivory and wild animals for the games.

Severan heyday and decline

Leptis was the birthplace of the emperor Septimius Severus (r. 193–211), who lavished it with a new forum, basilica, harbour works and a triumphal arch. The city declined with Vandal and Byzantine-era disruption and Arab conquest, and was gradually buried by sand, preserving its marble and limestone ruins to an extraordinary degree. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and was placed on the danger list during Libya's civil war.
