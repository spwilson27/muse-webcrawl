Alexandru Ioan Cuza (20 March 1820 – 15 May 1873) was the first domnitor (ruler) of the United Principalities of Moldavia and Wallachia, the nucleus of the modern Romanian state. His election in both principalities in 1859 achieved the union of the two Danubian states, and his reign brought sweeping reforms.

Rise to power

Born into a Moldavian boyar family and educated in Paris, Cuza served as an army officer and official, took part in the 1848 revolutionary movement, and became a leading unionist. In January 1859 he was elected prince of Moldavia, and weeks later, exploiting a loophole in the Convention of Paris, was elected prince of Wallachia too, uniting the principalities in his person. The union was formally recognised by the great powers in 1861–62, with Bucharest as capital.

Reforms

Cuza's government secularised monastic estates, introduced a land reform in 1864 that abolished serfdom and redistributed land to peasants, adopted new civil and penal codes modelled on French law, made primary education free and compulsory, and founded the universities of Iași (1860) and Bucharest (1864). His increasingly authoritarian methods — including a coup and plebiscite in 1864 — alienated both conservatives and liberals.

Abdication and legacy

In February 1866 a coalition of opponents, the so-called "monstrous coalition", forced Cuza to abdicate and leave the country. He was succeeded by Carol of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen. Cuza died in exile at Heidelberg in 1873. He is honoured in Romania as the architect of the 1859 Union and of the modern Romanian state.
