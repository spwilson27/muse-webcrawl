Cuisine of the Southwestern United States


Enchiladas served "Christmas style" with both red and green chile

The cuisine of the Southwestern United States is food associated primarily with Arizona and New Mexico, with influences extending into southern Colorado and Utah, West Texas, and Southern California. It is a fusion of Spanish colonial, Mexican, and Native American (especially Pueblo, Navajo, and Apache) foodways, centred on the New Mexico chile, corn, beans, and squash — the "three sisters" of indigenous agriculture.

History

Spanish settlers arriving from Mexico in the late 16th and 17th centuries brought wheat, cattle, pigs, sheep, and chile peppers to the Rio Grande valley, where they encountered Pueblo peoples farming maize, beans, and squash in irrigated fields. Over centuries of contact, a distinctive regional cooking developed that diverges from the food of central Mexico: blue corn, stacked enchiladas, slow-simmered chile sauces, and breads baked in outdoor adobe ovens (hornos) are hallmarks.

Key ingredients and dishes

  • New Mexico chile — served as red sauce (from dried ripe pods) or green sauce (from roasted unripe pods); ordering both is called "Christmas". The Hatch chile of the Hatch Valley is especially prized.
  • Enchiladas — typically stacked flat rather than rolled, often topped with a fried egg.
  • Posole — a hominy stew with pork and red chile.
  • Carne adovada — pork marinated and braised in red chile.
  • Sopaipillas and Navajo tacos (on frybread) — puffed fried doughs served savoury or with honey.
  • Biscochitos — anise-and-cinnamon lard cookies, the official state cookie of New Mexico.
  • Green chile cheeseburger and breakfast burritos — modern staples of the region.

Related cuisines

Southwestern cuisine is closely related to, but distinct from, Tex-Mex cooking to the east and the Sonoran-influenced food of southern Arizona, including Tucson — designated a UNESCO City of Gastronomy in 2015. In the late 20th century, chefs such as Mark Miller popularised a refined "Southwestern cuisine" nationally, emphasising native ingredients and chiles.
