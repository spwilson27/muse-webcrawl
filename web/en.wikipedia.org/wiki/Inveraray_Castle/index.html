Inveraray Castle


Inveraray Castle on the shore of Loch Fyne.

Inveraray Castle (Scottish Gaelic: Caisteal Inbhir Aora) is a country house near Inveraray in the county of Argyll, in western Scotland, on the shore of Loch Fyne, Scotland's longest sea loch. It has been the seat of the Dukes of Argyll, chiefs of Clan Campbell, since the 18th century, and is a category A listed building open to the public.

History and design

The present castle was built in the Gothic Revival style from 1746 to 1789, to designs involved with William Adam and executed by his son Roger Morris and later Robert Mylne. It replaced an earlier 15th-century castle, and the planned town of Inveraray was rebuilt nearby to suit the new setting. Rectangular in plan with four conical-roofed corner towers and a central tower, it is considered one of the earliest and finest examples of the Gothic Revival in Britain.

Collections and fire

The interiors include an armoury hall displaying more than a thousand pattern-1853 rifles, swords and pikes, state apartments with Beauvais tapestries, and paintings and furniture collected by generations of dukes. A serious fire in 1975 gutted parts of the roof; restoration, funded by a worldwide appeal, took most of a decade.

Popular culture

The castle appears in film and television, including a 2012 Christmas special of Downton Abbey, in which it stood in as the fictional Duneagle Castle.
