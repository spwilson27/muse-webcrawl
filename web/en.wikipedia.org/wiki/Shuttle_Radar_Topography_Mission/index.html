Shuttle Radar Topography Mission

Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM)
MissionSTS-99, Space Shuttle Endeavour
Dates11–22 February 2000
Coverage80% of Earth's land surface, 56°S to 60°N
AgenciesNASA, NGA, DLR, ASI

The Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) was an international research effort that obtained digital elevation models on a near-global scale from 56°S to 60°N, creating the most complete high-resolution digital topographic database of Earth up to that time. The data were collected during the 11-day STS-99 mission of the Space Shuttle Endeavour in February 2000.

Method

SRTM used a technique called radar interferometry. Two radar antennas received reflections of C-band (and, for swath segments, X-band) radar signals: one antenna was in the shuttle's payload bay and the second at the end of a 60-metre (200 ft) mast — the longest rigid structure flown in space at the time — extended from the bay. By comparing the phase of the two signals, the system computed the elevation of the terrain below with posting spacings of 1 arc-second (about 30 m) and 3 arc-seconds (about 90 m).

Data and uses

The mission mapped about 80% of Earth's land surface. The resulting digital elevation models are distributed by the U.S. Geological Survey and are freely used in geographic information systems, hydrology and flood modelling, geology, aviation safety, telecommunications planning, virtual globes, and video games and simulations. Initially the finest 1-arc-second data were restricted to the United States, but in 2014–2015 global 1-arc-second SRTM data were released to the public. Known limitations include voids (data gaps in steep terrain and over water/sand shadows), which later "void-filled" products address using other sources.
