The Niños Héroes ("Boy Heroes" or "Heroic Cadets") were six teenage cadets of Mexico's Military Academy who died defending Chapultepec Castle in Mexico City against United States forces on 13 September 1847, during the Mexican–American War. They are among the most revered figures of Mexican national memory.

The cadets

The six were Juan de la Barrera (aged 19), Juan Escutia, Agustín Melgar, Vicente Suárez, Francisco Márquez (aged 13) and Fernando Montes de Oca. Ordered to withdraw as U.S. troops stormed the castle, they refused and fought to the death. Legend holds that the last survivor wrapped himself in the Mexican flag and leapt from the castle walls rather than surrender it — an episode historians treat as patriotic legend.

Commemoration

The cadets are honoured by the great Altar a la Patria monument at Chapultepec (1952), by the Niños Héroes metro station and streets across Mexico, and by an annual ceremony on 13 September.
