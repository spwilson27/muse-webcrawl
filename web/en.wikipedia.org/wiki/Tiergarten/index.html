Tiergarten

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tiergarten (German for "animal garden" or "zoo", literally "animal yard") may refer to several places in Germany and Austria, most famously the large urban park in Berlin.

Großer Tiergarten, Berlin

The Großer Tiergarten is Berlin's oldest and largest inner-city park, covering about 210 hectares in the locality of Tiergarten in the borough of Mitte. Originally a hunting ground for the electors of Brandenburg, it was laid out as a pleasure ground under Frederick the Great and transformed into a landscape park in the 19th century by Peter Joseph Lenné. The Straße des 17. Juni runs through it from the Brandenburg Gate to the Victory Column (Siegessäule), which stands at its centre on the Großer Stern.

Badly damaged in the Second World War — its trees were largely felled for fuel in the postwar winters — the park was replanted from 1949 onward. Today it is a protected garden monument and a popular recreation area, surrounded by landmarks including the Reichstag building, Potsdamer Platz, the Berlin Zoological Garden and the embassies of the diplomatic quarter.

Locality of Tiergarten

Tiergarten is also the name of a locality (Ortsteil) of Berlin within the borough of Mitte, created in its present form by the 2001 administrative reform. The former district of Tiergarten (1950–2000) included Moabit and Hansaviertel, which are now separate localities. The area includes the Kulturforum with the Berlin Philharmonie, the Bauhaus Archive, and Berlin's central station area at its northern edge (in Moabit).

Other uses

  • Tiergarten Schönbrunn — the zoo at Schönbrunn Palace in Vienna, founded 1752, the world's oldest continuously operating zoo (named "Tiergarten", animal garden)
  • Numerous zoos and neighbourhoods in German-speaking Europe bear the name
  • "Tiergarten" is also a surname and the name of various streets and stations (Berlin Tiergarten station on the S-Bahn)
