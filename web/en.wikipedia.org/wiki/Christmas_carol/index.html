Christmas carol

A Christmas carol is a carol (a song or hymn) on the theme of Christmas, traditionally sung at Christmas itself or during the surrounding Christmas and holiday season. Christmas carols may be regarded as a subset of the broader category of Christmas music, typically focused on the Nativity of Jesus and the Christian celebration of Christmas, though some have secular themes.

History

Carols were originally communal songs sung at festivals throughout the year, and only later became associated primarily with Christmas. Early Christmas hymns include the 4th-century Latin hymns of the Roman church. In the Middle Ages, St Francis of Assisi popularised Nativity plays with songs. Many famous carols date from the 18th and 19th centuries: "Silent Night" was composed in Austria in 1818, while Charles Wesley wrote "Hark! The Herald Angels Sing" in 1739.

Carol services

The Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols, first held at King's College, Cambridge, in 1918, has become one of the best-known carol services, broadcast worldwide. Carol singing door-to-door, sometimes called wassailing, remains a charitable tradition in many countries.

Popular carols

Widely sung carols include "O Come, All Ye Faithful" (Adeste Fideles), "The First Noel", "God Rest You Merry, Gentlemen", "O Holy Night", "Away in a Manger", and "We Wish You a Merry Christmas".
