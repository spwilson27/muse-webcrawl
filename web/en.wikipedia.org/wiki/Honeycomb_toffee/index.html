Honeycomb toffee
Alternative namesCinder toffee (UK), hokey pokey (NZ), sponge toffee (Canada), sea foam, golden crunchers
TypeToffee / confectionery
Main ingredientsBrown sugar or golden syrup, baking soda
TextureLight, rigid, sponge-like with many air bubbles

Honeycomb toffee, honeycomb candy, sponge toffee, cinder toffee, rock toffee, or hokey pokey is a sugary toffee with a light, rigid, sponge-like texture. Its main ingredients are typically brown sugar (or corn syrup, molasses or golden syrup) and baking soda, with an acid such as vinegar sometimes used. The baking soda and acid react to form carbon dioxide which is trapped in the highly viscous mixture, producing the characteristic honeycomb-like bubbles.

Production

Sugar syrup is heated to the hard-crack stage (around 150 °C / 300 °F). Bicarbonate of soda is then whisked in; it decomposes and reacts with any acid present, releasing carbon dioxide gas that foams the still-liquid sugar. The mixture is poured into a lined tray and left to set, hardening into an aerated slab that is broken into pieces. Despite the name, most commercial honeycomb toffee contains no honey.

Regional names and uses

  • United Kingdom — "cinder toffee", traditionally sold at fairs; coated in chocolate in the Cadbury Crunchie bar.
  • New Zealand — "hokey pokey", famously used as a flavour of ice cream with small toffee balls.
  • Canada — "sponge toffee".
  • Australia — the chocolate-coated Violet Crumble bar.
  • South Korea — dalgona (ppopgi), a similar candy pressed with stamped shapes, popularised globally by the television series Squid Game.
  • Japan — karumeyaki, a traditional street-vendor candy.
  • Hungary — törökméz ("Turkish honey").
