The Third Portuguese Republic is the current period of Portuguese history, designating the democratic regime established after the Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974 overthrew the authoritarian Estado Novo. Following a two-year transition (the PREC), the present order was consolidated by the Constitution of 1976.

Transition

The Armed Forces Movement (MFA) coup of April 1974 ended nearly five decades of authoritarian rule and Portugal's colonial wars in Africa. The turbulent revolutionary period that followed saw competing civilian and military projects, land occupations in the Alentejo, and nationalisations, before moderate forces prevailed in the crisis of 25 November 1975.

Consolidation and Europe

The 1976 Constitution — revised several times, notably in 1982 and 1989 to reduce its socialist economic provisions — established a semi-presidential system with an elected President and an Assembly of the Republic. Portugal joined the European Economic Community in 1986, adopted the euro in 1999, and completed decolonisation with the handover of Macau to China in 1999.

Party system

Politics in the Third Republic has been dominated by the centre-left Socialist Party (PS) and the centre-right Social Democratic Party (PSD), with smaller parties including the CDS – People's Party, the Left Bloc, and the Portuguese Communist Party; the right-wing Chega grew rapidly from 2019.
