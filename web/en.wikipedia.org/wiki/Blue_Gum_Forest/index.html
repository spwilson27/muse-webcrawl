Blue Gum Forest

LocationGrose Valley, Blue Mountains, New South Wales, Australia
Dominant speciesSydney blue gum (Eucalyptus saligna)
Protected1932; now in Blue Mountains National Park

The Blue Gum Forest is a stand of tall Sydney blue gum trees (Eucalyptus saligna) in the Grose Valley of the Blue Mountains, New South Wales, Australia, near Blackheath. It is famous in Australian conservation history as the focus of one of the country's first successful public campaigns to save a forest: in the early 1930s bushwalkers raised money to buy out the lease of a farmer who planned to clear the grove, leading to its reservation in 1932.

Description

The forest occupies a sheltered site where Govetts Creek meets the Grose River. Some of its blue gums rise over 60 metres, and the grove is reached only on foot by steep walking tracks such as those from Perrys Lookdown and Acacia Flat, making it a popular destination for overnight bushwalking and camping.

Conservation history

In 1931 bushwalker Alan Rigby learned that farmer Clarrie Hungerford held a lease over the area and intended to clear it, reportedly to grow walnuts. Members of Sydney bushwalking clubs, led by campaigners including Myles Dunphy, raised £130 to purchase the lease; the forest was dedicated as a reserve in November 1932. The campaign is regarded as a landmark in the Australian conservation movement and contributed momentum toward the eventual creation of the Blue Mountains National Park, into which the forest was later incorporated. The forest was badly affected by the 2019–20 Australian bushfire season, though blue gums are capable of resprouting from epicormic buds after fire.
