Kurfürstendamm

LocationBerlin, Germany
Length3.5 km (2.2 mi)

The Kurfürstendamm (colloquially Ku'damm) is one of the most famous avenues in Berlin, Germany. The wide, tree-lined boulevard in the western part of the city is lined with shops, hotels, cafés, and restaurants, and is considered the Champs-Élysées of Berlin.

History

The avenue originated as a causeway built around 1542 for the Elector Joachim II Hector of Brandenburg to ride to his hunting lodge in the Grunewald. In the 1880s, Chancellor Otto von Bismarck promoted its development into a grand boulevard modelled on Paris. During the Cold War division of the city, the Ku'damm became the commercial centre of West Berlin, dominated by the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church, whose ruined tower was kept as a memorial.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church, the KaDeWe department store at its eastern end near Wittenbergplatz, and numerous theatres and cinemas.
