Jacob Zuma

Jacob Gedleyihlekisa Zuma (born 12 April 1942) is a South African politician who served as the fourth President of South Africa from 2009 to 2018. He is a member and former leader of the African National Congress (ANC). Zuma previously served as Deputy President of South Africa from 1999 to 2005 under Thabo Mbeki.

Early life and anti-apartheid activity

Zuma was born in Nkandla, in rural Natal (now KwaZulu-Natal), into a Zulu family. He had no formal schooling. He joined the ANC in 1959 and became an active member of its armed wing, uMkhonto we Sizwe, in 1962. He was imprisoned for ten years on Robben Island for conspiring to overthrow the apartheid government, and later lived in exile in Mozambique and Zambia, rising to head the ANC's intelligence department.

Presidency

After becoming ANC president in 2007, defeating Mbeki, Zuma led the party to victory in the 2009 general election and became president. His administration oversaw South Africa's hosting of the 2010 FIFA World Cup and admission to the BRICS grouping. However, his presidency was marred by allegations of corruption, including the Nkandla homestead scandal, the influence of the Gupta family ("state capture"), and long-standing corruption charges relating to a 1999 arms deal.

Facing mounting pressure, Zuma resigned on 14 February 2018, shortly before a parliamentary vote of no confidence, and was succeeded by Cyril Ramaphosa.

Legal troubles

In 2021, Zuma was sentenced to 15 months' imprisonment for contempt of court after defying an order to testify before the Zondo Commission into state capture; his arrest sparked widespread unrest in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng. He was released on medical parole later that year. His corruption trial over the arms deal continued for many years.
