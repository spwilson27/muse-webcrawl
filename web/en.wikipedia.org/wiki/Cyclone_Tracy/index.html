Severe Tropical Cyclone Tracy was a tropical cyclone that devastated the city of Darwin in Australia's Northern Territory on Christmas Day, 25 December 1974. Although physically small — its gale-force winds extended only about 48 km from the centre — Tracy was exceptionally intense, and it remains one of the most destructive cyclones in Australian history, killing at least 66 people and destroying or badly damaging most of the city's buildings.

Impact

Tracy crossed the coast in the early hours of Christmas morning, striking Darwin almost directly. Wind gusts at Darwin Airport reached 217 km/h before the anemometer failed, and stronger gusts were likely. More than 70% of Darwin's buildings, and 80% of its houses, were destroyed or seriously damaged; the northern suburbs were nearly flattened. Sixteen people died at sea. Communications and services collapsed, and in the largest peacetime airlift in Australian history, over 30,000 people — most of the city's population of about 47,000 — were evacuated by air and road in the following days. The recovery effort, directed under Major-General Alan Stretton of the Natural Disasters Organisation, became a landmark in Australian emergency management.

Aftermath

Darwin was rebuilt to strict new cyclone-resistant building codes, which transformed construction standards across northern Australia. Tracy remains a defining event in Darwin's identity, commemorated in memorials and in the song "Santa Never Made It into Darwin" by Bill and Boyd, a charity hit of 1975.
