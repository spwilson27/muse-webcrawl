Hastings and Rye is a constituency in East Sussex represented in the House of Commons of the UK Parliament. Created for the 1983 general election from parts of the former Hastings and Rye seats, it covers the coastal borough of Hastings (including St Leonards-on-Sea) and the eastern part of the Rother district, centred on the historic Cinque Port town of Rye.

History

The constituency has a competitive electoral history between the Conservative Party and the Labour Party. It was held by the Conservatives from its creation until 1997, when Labour's Michael Foster gained it in the party's national landslide; he held the seat until 2010. Conservative Amber Rudd then won the seat, famously by only 346 votes in 2017, and served as Home Secretary under Theresa May. After Rudd stepped down, Sally-Ann Hart held the seat for the Conservatives from 2019. Labour regained the constituency at the 2024 general election with Helena Dollimore.

Profile

Hastings is a seaside town with a fishing fleet, a revived arts scene centred on the Hastings Contemporary gallery and the regenerated pier, but also some of the most deprived neighbourhoods in South East England. Rye is a smaller, affluent former port and tourist destination. The constituency combines tourism, fishing, public services, and commuting, and its politics have often turned on regeneration funding and transport links to London.

Members of Parliament

Members have included Kenneth Warren (1983–1992), Jacqui Lait (1992–1997), Michael Foster (1997–2010), Amber Rudd (2010–2019), Sally-Ann Hart (2019–2024), and Helena Dollimore (2024–).
