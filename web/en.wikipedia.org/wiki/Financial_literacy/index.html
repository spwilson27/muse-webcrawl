Financial literacy

Financial literacy is the possession of skills, knowledge, and behaviors that allow an individual to make informed decisions regarding money. Financial literacy, financial education, and financial knowledge are used interchangeably. Key concepts typically include budgeting, saving, compound interest, inflation, risk diversification, debt management, and retirement planning.

Measurement

Standard measures include the "Big Three" questions developed by Annamaria Lusardi and Olivia Mitchell, which test understanding of compound interest, inflation, and diversification; surveys using these questions find substantial gaps in financial knowledge in many countries, correlated with age, gender, and income. The OECD's International Network on Financial Education (INFE) coordinates measurement and policy across countries, and the PISA assessment has tested the financial literacy of 15-year-olds since 2012.

Education efforts

Governments and nonprofits promote financial literacy through school curricula, workplace programmes, and public campaigns. Research is divided on how far classroom education changes behaviour; meta-analyses find modest but real effects, and "just-in-time" education tied to specific decisions may be more effective than general instruction.
