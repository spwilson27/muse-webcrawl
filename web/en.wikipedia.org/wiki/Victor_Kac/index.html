Victor Gershevich Kac (born 19 December 1943) is a Soviet and American mathematician at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, known for his work in representation theory and mathematical physics. He independently discovered, along with Robert Moody, the class of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras now called Kac–Moody algebras, which generalise the finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras and underlie much of modern conformal field theory and string theory.

Work

Kac's research includes the representation theory of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras and Lie superalgebras, the Weyl–Kac character formula, the denominator identity (the "Macdonald identities" in affine form), and vertex operator algebras. His textbook Infinite-Dimensional Lie Algebras (3rd edition, 1990) is a standard reference. With his collaborators he developed the theory of Poisson vertex algebras and integrable systems, and he was a pioneer of supermathematics through his 1977 classification of simple Lie superalgebras.

Career and honours

Kac studied at Moscow State University, receiving his doctorate in 1968 under Ernest Vinberg. After being refused permission to emigrate for years, he left the Soviet Union in the 1970s and joined the MIT faculty in 1977. He is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and a fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, and received the 2015 Leroy P. Steele Prize for Lifetime Achievement from the American Mathematical Society.
