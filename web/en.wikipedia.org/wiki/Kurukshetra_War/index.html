Kurukshetra War

The Kurukshetra War, also called the Mahabharata War, is a war described in the Hindu epic Mahabharata as a conflict between two groups of cousins — the Kauravas and the Pandavas — over the throne of Hastinapura. The war is said to have been fought at Kurukshetra, in present-day Haryana, and to have lasted eighteen days, leaving only a handful of survivors.

Narrative

When diplomacy failed — Krishna's peace embassy was spurned by Duryodhana, who refused to yield even five villages — the two armies assembled at Kurukshetra. Before the first clash, Arjuna, surveying his kin arrayed against him, despaired; Krishna's counsel to him forms the Bhagavad Gita. The blind king Dhritarashtra followed the battle through the divine sight of his charioteer Sanjaya, whose narration frames the epic's account.

The Kaurava army was commanded successively by Bhishma (days 1–10), Drona (11–15), Karna (16–17), and Shalya (18). Famous episodes include the fall of Bhishma on a bed of arrows, the death of Arjuna's son Abhimanyu in the discus formation, Bhima's vow-fulfilling slaughter of the Kaurava brothers, and the night raid of Ashvatthama, which wiped out the remaining Pandava camp. On the eighteenth day Duryodhana fell to Bhima in a mace fight. Survivors were few: the five Pandavas and Krishna on one side, and Ashvatthama, Kripa, and Kritavarma on the other.

Historicity

Scholars debate whether the war preserves a memory of a real conflict, sometimes dated between roughly 1200 and 600 BCE and associated with the Painted Grey Ware culture of the western Ganges plain. Traditional Hindu chronology places it at the dawn of the Kali Yuga, over five thousand years ago; the epic text itself was composed and expanded over centuries, reaching near-final form in the early centuries CE.
