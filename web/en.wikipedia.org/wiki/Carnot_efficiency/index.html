Carnot efficiency

Carnot efficiency is the maximum theoretical thermal efficiency of a heat engine operating between two temperatures. It depends only on the absolute temperatures of the hot and cold reservoirs:

η = 1 − TC/TH

where TH is the absolute temperature (in kelvin) of the hot reservoir and TC that of the cold reservoir. The result follows from Carnot's theorem, proved by Sadi Carnot in 1824: no engine operating between two heat reservoirs can be more efficient than a reversible engine, and all reversible engines operating between the same reservoirs have the same efficiency, regardless of the working substance.

Significance

Carnot efficiency sets an upper bound that no real engine can exceed; practical engines fall short because of friction, turbulence and other irreversibilities. For a steam plant with steam at about 840 K rejecting heat at 300 K, the Carnot limit is about 64%, whereas real plants achieve roughly 35–45%. Raising efficiency therefore depends on increasing the temperature difference between source and sink, which motivates high-temperature materials research and combined-cycle designs.

The corresponding result for refrigerators and heat pumps is expressed as a coefficient of performance, also bounded by the reversible (Carnot) values.
