British Aerospace

British Aerospace plc (BAe) was a British aircraft, munitions and defence-systems manufacturer created in 1977 by the nationalisation and merger of the British Aircraft Corporation, Hawker Siddeley, and Scottish Aviation. Privatised during the 1980s, it became controversially involved in the Al-Yamamah arms deal with Saudi Arabia. In 1999 it merged with Marconi Electronic Systems to form BAE Systems, one of the world's largest defence contractors.

History

The Aircraft and Shipbuilding Industries Act 1977 vested the assets of BAC, Hawker Siddeley Aviation and Dynamics, and Scottish Aviation in the state-owned British Aerospace. The company was floated on the stock market in 1981, with full privatisation in 1985. Through the 1980s and 1990s BAe pursued joint ventures — notably Panavia (Tornado), Eurofighter, and a 20% stake in Airbus — while acquiring Royal Ordnance (1987) and briefly owning the Rover Group car maker (1988–1994).

Programmes and legacy

BAe products included the Hawk advanced trainer, the Harrier and Sea Harrier V/STOL fighters, the BAe 125 business jet, the BAe 146 regional airliner, and guided weapons such as Rapier and Sea Wolf missiles developed through its dynamics businesses. The 1999 merger with Marconi created BAE Systems, headquartered in Farnborough, ending the British Aerospace name.
