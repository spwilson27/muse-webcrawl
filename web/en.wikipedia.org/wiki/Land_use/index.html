Land use

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Land use is the human use of land: it represents the economic and cultural activities (for example agricultural, residential, industrial, mining, and recreational uses) that are practiced at a given place. Public and private lands are often used for quite different purposes. Land use differs from land cover, which describes the physical surface of the land (forest, water, cropland, built-up area); a single land cover type, such as grass, may support several uses, such as grazing, recreation, and wildlife habitat.

Categories

Common classifications of land use include:

  • Agricultural — cropland, pasture, orchards and plantations
  • Residential — housing of varying density
  • Commercial — retail, offices and services
  • Industrial — manufacturing, warehousing and extraction
  • Transportation — roads, railways, airports and ports
  • Recreational and open space — parks, sports grounds, protected areas
  • Forest and natural land — managed forest, wilderness, wetlands

Globally, agriculture occupies the largest share of habitable land — roughly half — while forests cover about a third of the ice-free land surface and built-up areas a small but rapidly growing fraction.

Regulation

Because uses can conflict — housing beside heavy industry, farming beside a wetland reserve — most countries regulate land use through planning law. Instruments include zoning ordinances (in North America), development plans and green belts (in much of Europe), building codes, and environmental impact assessment. Land-use planning seeks to balance economic development, housing supply, food production, infrastructure and environmental protection.

Land-use change

Conversion of land from one use to another — deforestation for pasture, farmland lost to urbanisation, wetland drained for development — is a major driver of biodiversity loss and greenhouse-gas emissions. Land use, land-use change and forestry (LULUCF) is a reporting category in international climate agreements. Remote sensing from satellites such as the Landsat series is the principal tool for monitoring land use and land cover change over time.
