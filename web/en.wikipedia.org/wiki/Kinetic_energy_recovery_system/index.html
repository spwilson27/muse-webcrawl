Kinetic energy recovery system

A kinetic energy recovery system (KERS) is a type of regenerative braking system used in motor vehicles and motorsport. It recovers kinetic energy that would otherwise be lost as heat during braking and stores it — electrically in a battery or supercapacitor, or mechanically in a flywheel — for later use under acceleration.

Formula One

KERS was introduced to Formula One in the 2009 season, allowing drivers an extra 60 kW (about 80 hp) for up to 6.67 seconds per lap at the press of a button. Only some teams raced it that year, and a gentlemen's agreement saw it dropped for 2010 before returning in 2011. From 2014, more powerful hybrid energy recovery systems (ERS) harvesting energy from both braking (MGU-K) and exhaust heat (MGU-H) became integral to the 1.6-litre turbocharged power units.

Other applications

Flywheel KERS units have been fitted to endurance racing cars such as the Audi R18 e-tron quattro and Porsche 911 GT3 R Hybrid, and the principle underlies the regenerative braking of hybrid and electric road cars. Mechanical systems using continuously variable transmissions and high-speed composite flywheels spinning in vacuum have also been developed for buses and trains.
