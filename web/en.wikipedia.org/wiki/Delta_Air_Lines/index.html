Delta Air Lines

Delta Air Lines, Inc. is a major American airline headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia. One of the "Big Four" U.S. carriers and a founding member of the SkyTeam alliance, it operates, with its Delta Connection partners, thousands of daily flights to destinations on six continents, and is among the world's largest airlines by revenue and fleet.

History

Delta began in 1924–25 as Huff Daland Dusters, a crop-dusting operation in Macon, Georgia and Monroe, Louisiana; renamed Delta Air Service in 1928 (for the Mississippi Delta), it began passenger flights in 1929 under C.E. Woolman. It grew through acquisitions—Chicago and Southern (1953), Northeast Airlines (1972), Western Airlines (1987), Pan Am's transatlantic routes (1991)—filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2005, and merged with Northwest Airlines in 2008–2010, creating then the world's largest airline.

Operations

Its Atlanta hub at Hartsfield–Jackson is the world's busiest airport by passengers. The fleet (around 1,000 mainline and regional aircraft historically) mixes Airbus and Boeing types, including A220, A321neo, A330neo and A350 aircraft. Subsidiaries include Delta Connection carriers and an oil refinery (Monroe Energy) purchased in 2012 to manage fuel costs. Delta is noted for operational reliability and premium revenue, and holds stakes/partnerships with Air France–KLM, Virgin Atlantic, Korean Air, Aeroméxico and LATAM.
