Port of Brunswick


Colonel's Island Terminal, Brunswick

The Port of Brunswick is a deep-water port in Brunswick, Georgia, United States, operated by the Georgia Ports Authority. Located on the Atlantic coast near the Florida border, it is one of the busiest ports in the United States for roll-on/roll-off cargo, especially automobiles, and is the largest U.S. port for new-vehicle imports and exports by volume in recent years, having surpassed the Port of Baltimore.

Terminals

The port comprises two principal terminals. The Colonel's Island Terminal handles roll-on/roll-off cargo — cars, trucks, and heavy machinery — for dozens of auto manufacturers and machinery makers. The Mayor's Point Terminal handles breakbulk cargoes such as forest products. The port is connected to the Brunswick River and the Atlantic via a maintained deep-water channel and is served by two Class I railroads.

History and incidents

Brunswick has been a port since the colonial era, exporting naval stores, timber, and cotton. In September 2019 the car carrier Golden Ray capsized in St. Simons Sound at the port's entrance with some 4,200 vehicles aboard; the wreck was removed in a lengthy cutting and salvage operation completed in 2021. Expansion projects, including new roll-on/roll-off berths and vehicle-processing capacity, have continued as volumes grow.
