Head Start (program)

Head Start is a program of the United States Department of Health and Human Services that provides comprehensive early childhood education, health, nutrition, and parent involvement services to low-income children and families. Created in 1965 as part of President Lyndon B. Johnson's War on Poverty, it is one of the longest-running programs addressing systemic poverty in the United States, serving more than a million children annually and over 37 million children since its inception.

History

Head Start began in the summer of 1965 as an eight-week demonstration project serving some 560,000 children, designed by a panel chaired by pediatrician Robert Cooke with developmental psychologist Urie Bronfenbrenner among its influential members. Its premise was that poverty's effects on child development could be countered by combining preschool education with health screening, nutrition, and family support. The program was authorized under the Economic Opportunity Act and repeatedly reauthorized, most comprehensively by the Improving Head Start for School Readiness Act of 2007. Early Head Start, for pregnant women and children under three, was added in 1994.

Services and structure

Federal funds flow directly to local grantees — school districts, non-profits, and community action agencies — which must meet detailed federal Performance Standards. Services cover early learning, health and dental screening, nutrition, mental health, and parent engagement, including Policy Councils that give parents formal roles in program governance. Children in foster care and families experiencing homelessness are categorically eligible.

Research and debate

Evaluation has been contentious. The federally commissioned Head Start Impact Study (2010) found initial cognitive gains that largely faded by the early elementary grades, while longer-term quasi-experimental studies have found lasting benefits in high-school completion, health, and reduced mortality. The debate over "fade-out" versus long-run effects continues to shape early-childhood policy.
