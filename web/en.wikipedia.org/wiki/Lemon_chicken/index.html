Lemon chicken

Chinese-style lemon chicken with a sweet lemon glaze
Lemon chicken
CourseMain course
Main ingredientsChicken, lemon sauce
VariationsChinese, Italian (pollo al limone), French

Lemon chicken is the name given to several dishes, found in cuisines around the world, in which chicken is served with a sauce flavoured with lemon. The best-known version is the sweet, battered dish of Chinese restaurants in the West, but Italian, French, and Greek preparations also bear the name.

Chinese-style lemon chicken

In the version served in Chinese restaurants in Britain, North America, Australia, and Hong Kong, pieces of chicken breast are coated in batter, deep-fried until crisp, sliced, and dressed with a thick, sweet lemon sauce made from lemon juice, sugar, and water or stock, sometimes garnished with lemon slices and sprinkled with sesame seeds. The dish is a product of the Chinese diaspora rather than of mainland tradition; a more delicate Cantonese relative steams or fries the chicken without batter and uses a lighter lemon sauce. It belongs to the family of sweet-and-sour-adjacent dishes popularised by overseas Chinese restaurants in the 20th century.

Other versions

Italian pollo al limone sautés chicken breasts or scaloppine in a pan sauce of lemon juice, white wine, butter, and parsley, sometimes enriched with cream. French poulet au citron similarly pairs roast or braised chicken with lemon, while Greek cooking combines lemon with egg in avgolemono sauce. Moroccan tagines pair preserved lemon with chicken and olives, a distinct but related tradition.

Preparation

The Western Chinese version typically involves marinating the chicken briefly in soy sauce, egg, and cornflour, deep-frying twice for crispness, and preparing the glaze separately so its bright, sweet-tart flavour coats rather than soaks the crust. Lemon zest is often preferred to juice in the sauce to provide aroma without excessive acidity.
