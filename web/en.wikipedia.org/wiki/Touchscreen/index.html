A touchscreen is an electronic visual display that can detect the presence and location of a touch within the display area, allowing the user to interact directly with what is displayed rather than through a separate pointing device. Touchscreens are ubiquitous in smartphones, tablets, laptops, point-of-sale systems, ticket machines, ATMs, museum kiosks, and vehicle infotainment systems.

Technologies

The dominant technology is projected capacitive sensing, in which a grid of electrodes under the glass detects the change in capacitance caused by a conductive fingertip; it supports multi-touch gestures such as pinch-to-zoom. Resistive touchscreens, consisting of two flexible conductive layers pressed together at the touch point, work with any stylus or gloved hand and were common in older PDAs and industrial equipment, but support only limited multi-touch. Other systems use infrared light grids, surface acoustic waves, or optical cameras, and active styli may add pressure sensitivity and hover detection.

History

Eric Johnson at the Royal Radar Establishment described a capacitive touchscreen in 1965, initially for air traffic control. The PLATO IV educational terminal (1972) used an infrared touch panel, and HP's HP-150 (1983) brought infrared touch to a personal computer. Resistive screens powered the PDAs of the 1990s, but the technology's mass adoption came with capacitive multi-touch: Apple's iPhone (2007) made the finger-operated multi-touch interface the default paradigm for mobile devices, and touch has since spread to nearly every category of consumer electronics.
