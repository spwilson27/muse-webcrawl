Duke of Norfolk

Dukedom of Norfolk
Created: 1397 (third creation 1483)
Family: Howard
Seat: Arundel Castle, West Sussex
Current holder: Edward Fitzalan-Howard, 18th Duke

Duke of Norfolk is a title in the peerage of England and the premier dukedom in England. The Dukes of Norfolk, of the Howard family, are the premier peers of the realm and hold the hereditary office of Earl Marshal, responsible for state ceremonial including coronations and state funerals.

History

The dukedom was created three times: for Thomas de Mowbray in 1397, for John de Mowbray in 1425, and in 1483 for John Howard, whose family has held it ever since, though with attainders and restorations. Thomas Howard, 2nd Duke, commanded the English victory over Scotland at Flodden in 1513. Thomas Howard, 3rd Duke, was a dominant and ruthless Tudor courtier, uncle of two queens, Anne Boleyn and Catherine Howard. Thomas Howard, 4th Duke, was executed in 1572 for the Ridolfi plot to free Mary, Queen of Scots. His son Philip Howard, Earl of Arundel, is venerated as a Catholic saint and martyr.

Modern role

The family, which remained Roman Catholic through penal times, has its seat at Arundel Castle. As Earl Marshal, the 18th Duke organised the state funeral of Elizabeth II and the coronation of Charles III in 2022–23.
