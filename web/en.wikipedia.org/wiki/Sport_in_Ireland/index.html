Sport in Ireland

Sport in Ireland plays an important cultural role on the island, encompassing both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland. Many sports are organised on an all-island basis, with a single team representing the whole island in rugby union, cricket, and hockey. The most popular sports by participation and attendance include Gaelic games, association football, and rugby union.

Gaelic games

The Gaelic games — Gaelic football, hurling, camogie, and handball — are administered by the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA), founded in 1884. Hurling, one of the world's fastest field sports, and Gaelic football draw huge crowds to Croke Park in Dublin for the All-Ireland championships each summer. The games are strictly amateur, yet county teams command intense local loyalty.

Association football

Football is governed separately in the Republic (FAI) and Northern Ireland (IFA), each fielding its own national team. Irish players feature prominently in the English and Scottish leagues.

Rugby union

The Irish Rugby Football Union organises the sport across the island. The national team competes in the Six Nations Championship, and the four provinces — Leinster, Munster, Ulster, and Connacht — play in the United Rugby Championship and European competitions.

Other sports

Horse racing, golf, boxing, athletics, and greyhound racing are also significant. Ireland has produced celebrated golfers such as Rory McIlroy and champion boxers including Katie Taylor.
