G minor

G minor is a minor scale based on G, consisting of the pitches G, A, B♭, C, D, E♭ and F. Its key signature has two flats. Its relative major is B-flat major and its parallel major is G major.

Forms of the scale

The natural minor scale of G is modified in the harmonic minor by raising the seventh degree, giving F♯, which creates the leading tone needed for a major dominant chord (D major). In the melodic minor, the sixth and seventh degrees are raised ascending (E♮ and F♯) and restored descending, smoothing the melodic line across the augmented second.

Notable compositions

G minor has long been associated in the Classical tradition with pathos and unrest. Mozart's two symphonies in the key — No. 25 ("Little G minor") and the great No. 40 — are among his most famous works, and his String Quintet No. 4 is also in G minor. Other well-known examples include Chopin's Ballade No. 1 in G minor for piano, Fauré's Piano Quartet No. 1 is in C minor but his Sicilienne is in G minor, and Queen's "Bohemian Rhapsody" makes extensive use of the key in popular music. Baroque composers such as Handel and Vivaldi wrote numerous concertos and arias in G minor.
