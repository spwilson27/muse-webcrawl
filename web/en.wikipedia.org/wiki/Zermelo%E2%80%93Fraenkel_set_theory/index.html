Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory

Image: Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory

Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory, commonly abbreviated ZF (or ZFC when including the axiom of choice), is the standard axiomatic system for set theory and the most common foundation of modern mathematics.

History

The axioms were first proposed by Ernst Zermelo in 1908 and were augmented by Abraham Fraenkel and Thoralf Skolem in the 1920s, including the axiom of replacement and the axiom of regularity.

Axioms

The system includes axioms of extensionality, pairing, union, power set, separation (as a schema), replacement (schema), infinity, regularity, and—in ZFC—the axiom of choice. ZFC is widely accepted as the foundational framework within which most mathematical theorems can be formalised.
