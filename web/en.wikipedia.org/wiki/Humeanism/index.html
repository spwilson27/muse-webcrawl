Humeanism

Humeanism refers to a family of philosophical views inspired by the Scottish philosopher David Hume (1711–1776). In contemporary metaphysics, the term most often denotes the doctrine associated with David Lewis: that the world is fundamentally a "mosaic" of local matters of particular fact, and that laws of nature merely describe regularities in that mosaic rather than governing it — a view often called Humean supervenience.

Humean theory of laws

On the Humean "best system" account, developed by John Stuart Mill, Frank Ramsey, and Lewis, the laws of nature are the axioms of the deductive system that best balances simplicity and strength in describing the world. Causation, chance, and dispositions are analysed in terms of patterns in the mosaic rather than irreducible necessities.

Other Humean views

Humeanism also encompasses views in metaethics and philosophy of action: the Humean theory of motivation holds that beliefs alone cannot motivate action — desires are required — and the Humean theory of reasons holds that an agent has a reason to act only if it serves their desires.
