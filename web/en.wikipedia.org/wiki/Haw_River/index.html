Haw River

The Haw River is a tributary of the Cape Fear River, approximately 110 miles (177 km) long, in north-central North Carolina in the United States. It rises in the Piedmont near Kernersville in Forsyth County, flows southeast through Guilford, Alamance and Chatham counties, and joins the Deep River near Moncure to form the Cape Fear River below Jordan Lake.

History

The river takes its name from the Sissipahaw Native Americans who lived along its banks. From the late nineteenth century the Haw powered a string of textile mills in towns such as Burlington, Graham, Swepsonville and Saxapahaw, and the textile village of Haw River in Alamance County. Mill activity brought both employment and severe industrial pollution to the river.

Environment and recreation

Much of the lower Haw flows into B. Everett Jordan Lake, a major reservoir and recreation area. Haw River State Park, established in 2003 near Browns Summit, protects land along the upper river. Paddling, fishing and the Haw River Trail — a developing land and paddle trail following the river's course — are popular uses, and conservation groups have worked for decades on improving the river's long-troubled water quality.
