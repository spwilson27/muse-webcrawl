Musical notation

Musical notation is any system used to visually represent aurally perceived music, played with instruments or sung by the human voice, through the use of written, printed, or otherwise-produced symbols. The dominant system in the Western world uses notes placed on a five-line staff to indicate pitch and duration, supplemented by symbols for rhythm, dynamics, articulation, and expression.

Modern staff notation: the vertical position of a notehead gives its pitch, and its shape gives its duration.

History

The earliest known notation appears on a Hurrian hymn tablet from Ugarit (c. 1400 BC), and ancient Greek notation used letters above lyrics. Byzantine and early medieval chant used neumes — gestural signs indicating melodic contour. Around the 11th century, Guido d'Arezzo is credited with the staff lines and solmisation syllables that made precise pitch teachable. Mensural notation in the 13th–16th centuries encoded rhythm; bar lines, key signatures, and printed scores standardised the system by the Baroque era.

Elements of modern notation

  • Staff and clefs: five lines; treble, bass, alto, and tenor clefs fix the pitch of the lines.
  • Notes and rests: values from whole notes to sixty-fourth notes and beyond; dots extend values by half.
  • Key and time signatures: indicate the key centre and the metre.
  • Accidentals: sharps, flats, and naturals alter pitches.
  • Expression marks: dynamics (piano, forte), tempo terms, slurs, and articulations guide performance.

Other systems

Many traditions use other notations: tablature specifies finger positions for lute and guitar; Chinese music historically used gongche characters and guqin tablature, and modern Chinese practice often uses numbered notation (jianpu); Indian music is written in sargam syllables; jazz and popular music use lead sheets and chord charts. Twentieth-century composers introduced graphic scores and other experimental notations.

Digital notation

Software such as Finale, Sibelius, Dorico, and the open-source MuseScore and LilyPond encodes scores digitally; formats including MusicXML and MIDI allow exchange between notation, playback, and production tools.
