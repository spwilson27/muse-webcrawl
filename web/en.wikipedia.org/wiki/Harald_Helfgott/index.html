Harald Helfgott

Harald Andrés Helfgott (born 25 November 1977) is a Peruvian mathematician working in analytic number theory. He is best known for his 2013 proof of the ternary Goldbach conjecture, also called the weak Goldbach conjecture, which states that every odd number greater than 5 can be written as the sum of three primes.

Early life and education

Helfgott was born in Lima, Peru. He studied mathematics at Brandeis University, graduating in 1998, and received his PhD from Princeton University in 2003 under the supervision of Henryk Iwaniec. He has held positions at the University of Bristol, the École Normale Supérieure in Paris, and the University of Göttingen.

Work on the ternary Goldbach conjecture

In 2013 Helfgott posted a proof that every odd integer greater than 1030 satisfies the weak Goldbach conjecture, using the Hardy–Littlewood circle method combined with extensive computer verification of the remaining cases up to that bound. The result resolved a problem that had been open since Christian Goldbach proposed it in 1742. See Goldbach's conjecture and Hardy–Littlewood circle method.

Other work

Helfgott has also worked on growth in groups, expander graphs, and the product theorem for SL(2, p) and SL(3, p), contributing to the theory of approximate groups alongside work by Breuillard, Green and Tao.
