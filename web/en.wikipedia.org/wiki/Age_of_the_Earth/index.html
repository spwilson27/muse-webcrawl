Age of the Earth
Value4.54 ± 0.05 billion years
MethodRadiometric dating of meteorites and lunar samples

The age of the Earth is estimated at 4.54 billion years (± 0.05 billion years). This age is based on evidence from radiometric dating of the oldest terrestrial minerals and of meteorites, which are assumed to have formed at the same time as the planets of the Solar System.

The Earth from space

History of estimates

Early scientific estimates varied enormously. In the 17th century, Archbishop James Ussher calculated from biblical chronology that the Earth was created in 4004 BC. In the 19th century, Lord Kelvin estimated 20–400 million years from the Earth's cooling rate, unaware of radioactive heating. Geologists such as Charles Lyell, studying erosion and sedimentation, argued for hundreds of millions of years.

Radiometric dating

The discovery of radioactive decay at the turn of the 20th century provided a reliable clock. Arthur Holmes pioneered uranium–lead dating of rocks. The currently accepted value comes from lead–lead isochron dating of the Canyon Diablo meteorite and lunar samples, performed by Clair Cameron Patterson in the 1950s.

Oldest materials

The oldest known minerals are zircon crystals from the Jack Hills of Western Australia, dated to about 4.4 billion years. The oldest dated rocks, the Acasta Gneiss of Canada, are about 4.03 billion years old.
