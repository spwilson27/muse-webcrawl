ITV, legally Channel 3, is a British free-to-air public broadcast television network. Launched in 1955 as Independent Television under the Independent Television Authority (ITA) to challenge the BBC, it was the first commercial television channel in the United Kingdom, funded by advertising.

The ITV logo introduced in the network's 2013 rebrand.

History

ITV was established by the Television Act 1954 and began broadcasting in London on 22 September 1955, with regional franchises rolling out across the country over the following decade. The network was long structured as a federation of regional companies — including Granada, Thames, ATV/Central, Yorkshire, and London Weekend Television — each holding a regional Channel 3 licence and contributing programmes to a shared national schedule through a networking system.

Deregulation under the Broadcasting Act 1990 triggered progressive consolidation, culminating in the 2004 merger of Granada and Carlton to form ITV plc, which today owns 13 of the 15 regional licences; the remaining two in Scotland are held by STV Group. ITV1 (now branded simply ITV) is the UK's most-watched commercial channel, known for programmes such as Coronation Street (the world's longest-running television soap), Emmerdale, Broadchurch, Downton Abbey, and entertainment formats including The X Factor and Britain's Got Talent. Sister channels include ITV2, ITV3, ITV4, and ITVBe; its streaming service is ITVX, launched in 2022.

Regional structure

The network retains regional news and some regional programming obligations under its public-service licences, regulated by Ofcom. Regions include London, Granada (North West), Meridian (South), Central, Yorkshire, Tyne Tees, Anglia, West Country, Border, UTV (Northern Ireland), and Channel Television (Channel Islands).
