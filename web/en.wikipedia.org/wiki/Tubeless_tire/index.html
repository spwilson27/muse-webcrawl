Tubeless tire

A tubeless tire is a pneumatic tire that does not require a separate inner tube to hold air. Instead, the tire and the rim of the wheel together form an airtight chamber, with the tire's beads sealing against the rim and an airtight inner liner within the tire.

Design

Tubeless tires have a smooth inner liner of low-permeability rubber, precision-moulded beads that seat on rim flanges, and a valve fitted directly to the rim. The design was patented in the 1940s and became standard on passenger cars from the 1950s onward; it is now also common on motorcycles and increasingly on bicycles, where liquid sealant is often added.

Advantages and disadvantages

Without a tube, there is no risk of pinch flats or friction between tube and tire, heat dissipation is better, and small punctures in the tread area often lose air slowly rather than deflating suddenly. Downsides include the need for well-matched rims, and more difficult roadside repair if the bead seal is broken.
