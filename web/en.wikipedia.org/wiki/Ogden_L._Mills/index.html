Ogden L. Mills

Ogden Livingston Mills, U.S. Secretary of the Treasury 1932–1933.

Ogden Livingston Mills (August 23, 1884 – October 11, 1937) was an American lawyer, businessman and politician. He served as United States Secretary of the Treasury under President Herbert Hoover from 1932 to 1933, during the Great Depression.

Career

Mills, heir to a wealthy banking family, served in the New York State Senate and in the U.S. House of Representatives (1921–1927). He ran unsuccessfully for Governor of New York in 1926 against Al Smith. From 1927 he served as Undersecretary of the Treasury under Andrew Mellon, whom he succeeded as Secretary in February 1932.

Treasury Secretary

As Secretary, Mills defended the gold standard and orthodox fiscal policy, supporting the Revenue Act of 1932. After leaving office he criticised the New Deal in print and remained a leading figure in the Republican Party until his death in 1937.
