Overview

William Ernest Powell Giles (1835–1897) was an Australian explorer who led five major expeditions into the deserts of central Australia in the 1870s. He named the Gibson Desert after Alfred Gibson, a member who died on the 1874 expedition — an ordeal in which Giles crossed the desert on foot after his horses died, famously surviving to rescue his companion.

Expeditions

Giles's journeys (1872–76), backed at first by Baron Ferdinand von Mueller, traversed country between the Overland Telegraph Line and Perth, reaching the Gibson and Great Victoria deserts, and in 1875–76 he crossed from South Australia to Perth and back by a northern route — the first European to cross the western deserts both ways. He sighted Kata Tjuta (the Olgas) and named Ayers Rock's neighbour Mount Olga. His account, Australia Twice Traversed (1889), remains a classic of exploration literature. Giles was later a minor figure of the Western Australian goldfields, dying at Coolgardie in 1897.
