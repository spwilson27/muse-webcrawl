David Lekuta Rudisha, MBS (born 17 December 1988) is a Kenyan middle-distance runner and the former world record holder in the 800 metres. He is a two-time Olympic champion (2012 and 2016) and two-time World champion over the distance, and is widely considered the greatest 800-metre runner in history.

Rudisha's front-running world record at London 2012 was called the greatest 800 m race ever.

Career

Rudisha, a Maasai from Kilgoris, came to prominence winning the 2006 World Junior Championships in Beijing. Coached by Brother Colm O'Connell, he broke Wilson Kipketer's long-standing world record twice in August 2010, reaching 1:41.01 in Rieti. At the 2011 World Championships in Daegu he won his first senior world title.

At the London 2012 Olympic final, Rudisha led from gun to tape and won in 1:40.91, becoming the first man to run under 1:41 for 800 metres; the race produced personal bests for nearly the entire field. He defended his Olympic title at Rio 2016, winning in 1:42.15, despite an intervening period disrupted by a knee injury sustained in 2013. A further injury ended his career after 2017. His world record still stands.

Personal life

Rudisha's father, Daniel Rudisha, was an Olympic silver medallist with the Kenyan 4×400 m relay team at the 1968 Mexico City Games. David Rudisha has worked as an athletics ambassador and coach since retiring.
