Rexford Tugwell

Rexford Guy Tugwell
Born: 10 July 1891, Sinclairville, New York
Died: 21 July 1979, Santa Barbara, California
Offices: Governor of Puerto Rico (1941–1946); Undersecretary of Agriculture

Rexford Guy Tugwell (July 10, 1891 – July 21, 1979) was an American economist and government official who became one of the best-known members of President Franklin D. Roosevelt's "Brain Trust". A professor at Columbia University, he advised Roosevelt during the 1932 campaign and went on to serve as Assistant Secretary and then Undersecretary of Agriculture.

New Deal career

Tugwell was an advocate of economic planning and of the Agricultural Adjustment Administration's efforts to raise farm prices by reducing production. From 1935 he headed the Resettlement Administration, which assisted poor farmers, promoted soil conservation and built planned "greenbelt" towns; its photographic section, later part of the Farm Security Administration, produced iconic Depression-era images by photographers such as Dorothea Lange. His prominence and forthright views made him a lightning rod for conservative critics, and he was sometimes branded "Rex the Red". He resigned from the administration at the end of 1936.

Puerto Rico and later life

After work in private industry and as chairman of the New York City Planning Commission, Tugwell was appointed Governor of Puerto Rico in 1941, serving until 1946 — the last non-Puerto Rican appointed governor. He supported the Popular Democratic Party's reforms and the eventual move to an elected governorship. He later taught at the University of Chicago and the University of California, Santa Barbara, and wrote extensively, including a biography of Roosevelt and works on constitutional reform.
