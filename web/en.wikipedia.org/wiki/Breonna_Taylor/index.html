Breonna Taylor

Breonna Taylor (June 5, 1993 – March 13, 2020) was a 26-year-old African-American emergency medical technician from Louisville, Kentucky, who was fatally shot by Louisville Metro Police Department officers during a botched raid on her apartment in the early hours of March 13, 2020. Her death, together with the murder of George Floyd weeks later, sparked widespread protests in the United States and internationally in 2020, and prompted legal reforms including Louisville's ban on no-knock warrants, known as "Breonna's Law."

The raid

Police obtained a no-knock search warrant connected to a drug investigation involving an acquaintance. Shortly after midnight, officers forced entry to Taylor's apartment. Her boyfriend, Kenneth Walker, believing intruders were breaking in, fired one shot, striking an officer in the leg. Officers returned fire with dozens of rounds; Taylor was struck multiple times and died at the scene. No drugs were found in the apartment.

Aftermath

The shooting drew intense scrutiny of no-knock warrants and police accountability. Louisville settled a wrongful-death lawsuit with Taylor's family for $12 million alongside policing reforms, and in June 2020 the city council unanimously banned no-knock warrants. State-level versions of "Breonna's Law" followed. Several officers were fired; federal civil rights charges were later brought against officers involved in obtaining the warrant, and in 2022 former detective Kelly Goodlett pleaded guilty to conspiring to falsify the warrant affidavit. Protests in Louisville and across the country carried the slogan "Say her name."
