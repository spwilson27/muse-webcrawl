Peleș Castle


Location: Sinaia, Prahova County, Romania
Style: German Renaissance Revival (Neo-Renaissance)
Construction: 1873–1914
Commissioned by: King Carol I of Romania
Architects: Johannes Schultz; Karel Liman

Peleș Castle (Romanian: Castelul Peleș) is a Neo-Renaissance palace in the Carpathian Mountains near Sinaia, Romania, on the historic route linking Transylvania and Wallachia. Built for King Carol I, it is considered one of the most beautiful castles in Europe and was among the most technologically advanced residences of its era.

History

Construction began in 1873 under the German architect Johannes Schultz, with the foundation stone laid in 1875 in the presence of the royal family. The castle was inaugurated in 1883 and works continued until 1914 under the Czech architect Karel Liman. Peleș was the summer residence of the Romanian royal family; King Michael I was born in the nearby Foișor Lodge in 1921. After the forced abdication of 1947, the communist regime seized the property, later opening it as a museum. Following the 1989 revolution, the castle was legally restored to the former royal family, which leases it to the Romanian state to continue operating as a public museum.

Architecture and interiors

The palace combines German Renaissance Revival exteriors with richly varied interiors in styles ranging from Florentine and Moorish to Turkish and Imperial. It contains about 160 rooms, including a theatre, a council hall, and a celebrated Hall of Honour with a movable glass ceiling. Notable features include extensive wood carving, stained glass from Switzerland and Germany, a large armoury collection, and central heating, electricity, and an internal lift installed remarkably early for the region.

Museum and grounds

The castle forms a complex with the smaller Pelișor Castle and the Foișor hunting lodge, set in landscaped terraces with statues and fountains. The museum's collections include European arms and armour, paintings, sculpture, tapestries, and decorative arts; Peleș is one of Romania's most visited tourist attractions.
