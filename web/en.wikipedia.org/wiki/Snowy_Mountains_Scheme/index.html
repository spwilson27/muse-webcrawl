The Snowy Mountains Scheme, also known as the Snowy Hydro, is a hydroelectricity and irrigation complex in south-east Australia. It consists of sixteen major dams; nine power stations; two pumping stations; and 225 kilometres of tunnels, pipelines and aqueducts that were constructed between 1949 and 1974. It is the largest engineering project undertaken in Australia.

Description

The scheme collects water from the Snowy River and its tributaries on the eastern slopes of the Snowy Mountains and diverts it westward through trans-mountain tunnels into the Murray and Murrumbidgee river systems, providing irrigation water to the Murray–Darling Basin and generating peaking hydroelectric power for New South Wales, Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory. Major structures include Blowering, Talbingo, Jindabyne and Eucumbene dams and the Tumut and Murray power stations, most of them underground.

Construction and people

More than 100,000 people from over 30 countries worked on the scheme, many of them post-war migrants; 121 workers died in industrial accidents. The project transformed Australian society, becoming a symbol of nation-building and multiculturalism. The Snowy Mountains Authority, created in 1949, ran company towns such as Cooma, Jindabyne and Cabramurra to house the workforce.

Snowy 2.0 and environmental issues

Diverting the Snowy River reduced its flow to about 1 percent of natural levels at Jindabyne, prompting environmental-flow releases agreed from 2000. A major pumped-hydro expansion, Snowy 2.0, linking the Tantangara and Talbingo reservoirs with a new underground station of about 2,000 MW, was announced in 2017 and has proceeded with delays and cost increases.
