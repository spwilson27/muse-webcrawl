Tinnitus

Tinnitus is the perception of sound when no corresponding external sound is present. It is usually described as a ringing, but can also sound like clicking, buzzing, hissing, humming, or roaring. The perceived sound may range from a quiet background noise to one so loud as to drown out external sounds, and may be heard in one or both ears, or in the head.

Types

Subjective tinnitus, heard only by the affected person, is by far the most common type and is often associated with hearing loss. Objective tinnitus can be detected by an examiner, for example with a stethoscope, and may arise from vascular or muscular causes.

Causes

Tinnitus is a symptom, not a disease in itself. Prolonged exposure to loud sound, which damages the hair cells of the cochlea, is the most common cause. Other causes include earwax blockage, age-related hearing loss, Ménière's disease, ototoxic drugs such as high-dose aspirin and certain antibiotics, head injury, and metabolic or cardiovascular disorders. Pulsatile tinnitus, beating in time with the heartbeat, may indicate a vascular abnormality.

Mechanism

The prevailing model holds that reduced auditory input leads the central auditory system to increase its gain, and the resulting neural hyperactivity is perceived as sound. Changes occur along the pathway from the cochlear nucleus through the inferior colliculus and auditory cortex.

Diagnosis and management

Assessment includes audiometry, a history of noise exposure and medication, and examination of the ear. There is no drug approved to eliminate tinnitus; management focuses on treating underlying causes where possible, hearing aids, sound therapy, and cognitive behavioural therapy, which reduces the distress associated with the perception. Prevention centres on hearing protection in noisy environments.

Prevalence

Roughly 10 to 15 percent of people experience some degree of tinnitus; in about 1 to 2 percent it is severe enough to affect quality of life significantly, causing sleep disturbance, anxiety, and difficulty concentrating.
