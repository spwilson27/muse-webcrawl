Origen of Alexandria (c. 185 – c. 253), also known as Origen Adamantius, was an early Christian scholar, ascetic, and theologian who was born and spent the first half of his career in Alexandria. He was a prolific writer who wrote roughly 2,000 treatises in multiple branches of theology, including textual criticism, biblical exegesis and hermeneutics, homiletics, and spirituality. He was one of the most influential and controversial figures in early Christian theology, apologetics, and asceticism. He has been described by some as "the greatest genius the early church ever produced".

Life

Origen's father Leonides was martyred in the persecution of 202 under Septimius Severus, and the young Origen supported his family by teaching grammar before succeeding Clement as head of the Catechetical School of Alexandria. In about 231, after a conflict with Bishop Demetrius — partly over Origen's ordination to the priesthood in Caesarea — he settled permanently in Caesarea Maritima in Palestine, where he founded a school and library. During the Decian persecution (c. 250) he was imprisoned and tortured, and he died a few years later at Tyre, reportedly from the injuries.

Works and thought

His greatest scholarly achievement was the Hexapla, a massive synopsis of the Old Testament in six parallel columns (Hebrew, a Greek transliteration, and four Greek versions), foundational for biblical textual criticism. De Principiis (On the First Principles) was the first systematic work of Christian theology. Contra Celsum, written late in life, is a point-by-point refutation of the pagan philosopher Celsus's attack on Christianity and a major work of early apologetics. Origen taught a threefold sense of Scripture (literal, moral, allegorical) and doctrines such as the pre-existence of souls and the universal restoration (apokatastasis) of all beings, which later brought his legacy under a cloud: teachings attributed to his followers ("Origenism") were condemned in the 6th century, and many of his works survive only in Latin translation by Rufinus or in fragments.
