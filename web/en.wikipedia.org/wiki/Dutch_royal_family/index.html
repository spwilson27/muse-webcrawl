Dutch royal family

The Dutch royal family comprises the members of the House of Orange-Nassau who are related to the reigning monarch of the Netherlands and who are members of the Royal House (Koninklijk Huis) or of the wider royal family as defined by Dutch law.

The distinction between the Royal House and the royal family is set out in the Membership of the Royal House Act of 2002. Members of the Royal House — the monarch, the former monarch, relatives within two degrees of kinship in line to the throne, and their spouses — are subject to ministerial responsibility; members of the wider royal family are not.

Current members

The head of the family is King Willem-Alexander, who acceded to the throne on 30 April 2013 following the abdication of his mother, Queen Beatrix. His wife is Queen Máxima. Their daughters — Catharina-Amalia, Princess of Orange, Princess Alexia, and Princess Ariane — are in the line of succession. Other members include Princess Beatrix, Prince Constantijn and Princess Laurentien, and Princess Margriet and Pieter van Vollenhoven.

The royal family's official residence functions centre on palaces including the Royal Palace of Amsterdam, Noordeinde Palace and Huis ten Bosch in The Hague, and Villa Eikenhorst at Wassenaar, where the King and his family live.
