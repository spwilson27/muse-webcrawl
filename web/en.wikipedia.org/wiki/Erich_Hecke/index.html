Erich Hecke (20 September 1887 – 13 February 1947) was a German mathematician who worked in number theory and the theory of modular forms. He is best known for the Hecke operators, a family of commuting operators acting on spaces of modular forms whose eigenvalues encode deep arithmetic information, and for the theory of Hecke characters and their L-functions.

Life and career

Hecke studied at Breslau, Berlin and Göttingen, receiving his doctorate under David Hilbert in 1910. After positions in Göttingen and Basel, he became a professor at the newly founded University of Hamburg in 1919, where he remained for the rest of his career. He died in Copenhagen in 1947.

Work

In his early work Hecke extended the theory of Dedekind zeta functions and Dirichlet L-functions, introducing the Größencharaktere (Hecke characters) of number fields. His 1936 work on modular forms introduced the operators now named after him, which later became central to the Langlands program linking number theory and representation theory. The Hecke algebra concept arising from his work plays a role across modern mathematics, including in the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem.
