Ludwig Alois Friedrich Ritter von Köchel (14 January 1800 – 3 June 1877) was an Austrian musicologist, writer, composer, botanist and publisher. He is best known for cataloguing the works of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart and originating the "K." numbers (Köchel-Verzeichnis) by which Mozart's compositions are identified.

Life

Köchel was born in Stein, Lower Austria, and studied law in Vienna, receiving his doctorate in 1827. He served as tutor to the sons of Archduke Charles of Austria, a post that brought him a knighthood (the title Ritter) and financial independence. He subsequently devoted himself to scholarship across several fields: he travelled widely for geological and botanical research, published studies of the flora of the Austrian Danube region, and worked as a school administrator in Salzburg and Vienna.

The Köchel catalogue

In 1862 Köchel published his Chronologisch-thematisches Verzeichnis sämtlicher Tonwerke Wolfgang Amadé Mozarts ("Chronological-thematic catalogue of the complete works of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart"), listing 626 compositions in the order he believed they were written. The catalogue has been repeatedly revised—notably a third edition by Alfred Einstein (1937) and a sixth edition of 1964—so many works carry renumbered designations, but the "K." numbers remain the universal reference system for Mozart's music.

Other work

Köchel also published a biography of the composer and theorist Johann Joseph Fux (1872), promoted performances of Mozart's works, and financed the early complete edition of Mozart's works (the Alte Mozart-Ausgabe). He died in Vienna in 1877.
