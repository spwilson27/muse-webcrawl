High-speed rail in China


Fuxing EMU, China Railway High-speed

The high-speed rail (HSR) network in China is the world's longest and most extensively used, operated under the China Railway High-speed (CRH) brand by China State Railway Group. With over 42,000 kilometres of lines in operation by the end of 2022 — roughly two-thirds of the world's total — the network connects virtually all major cities and carries billions of passengers annually.

History

China's first high-speed line, the Qinhuangdao–Shenyang passenger railway, opened in 2003. The network expanded rapidly after the 2004 Mid-to-Long Term Railway Network Plan and a massive stimulus programme. Landmark lines include the Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway (opened 2011, 1,318 km), the Beijing–Guangzhou line (the world's longest when completed in 2012), and the Harbin–Dalian line, the first high-speed railway in a frigid alpine region.

Technology and rolling stock

Early CRH trains derived from licensed foreign designs — Bombardier's Regina, Kawasaki's Shinkansen E2, Siemens' Velaro, and Alstom's Pendolino/New Pendolino — before China developed its own standardized Fuxing trains, which operate at up to 350 km/h in regular service, the world's fastest conventional commercial rail speed. In 2011, following the Wenzhou train collision and a corruption scandal involving railways minister Liu Zhijun, maximum speeds were reduced to 300 km/h before being restored on major lines in 2017.
