N-heterocyclic carbene

An N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) is a persistent carbene in which the divalent carbon is part of a nitrogen-containing heterocyclic ring — most commonly an imidazol-2-ylidene, in which the carbene carbon is flanked by two nitrogen atoms. The adjacent nitrogens stabilise the singlet carbene both inductively and by π-donation into the carbon's empty p-orbital, making many NHCs isolable, crystalline solids. Although Wanzlick explored their chemistry in the 1960s, the first free, stable NHC was isolated only in 1991 by Arduengo and co-workers.

Structure of a typical imidazol-2-ylidene NHC (IMes).

Synthesis and properties

NHCs are usually prepared by deprotonation of the corresponding azolium salt (imidazolium, thiazolium, triazolium) with a strong base such as potassium tert-butoxide or sodium hydride, or generated in situ. Common backbones include imidazol-2-ylidenes, saturated imidazolin-2-ylidenes, benzimidazolylidenes and 1,2,4-triazolylidenes. Bulky N-substituents such as mesityl (IMes) or 2,6-diisopropylphenyl (IPr) shield the carbene centre. NHCs are strong σ-donor ligands, generally stronger donors than most phosphines, a property quantified by the Tolman electronic parameter and related measures; their steric profile is described by the percent buried volume (%Vbur).

As ligands in catalysis

Replacing phosphines with NHCs transformed metal catalysis. Second-generation Grubbs and Hoveyda–Grubbs olefin-metathesis catalysts bear an NHC ligand and offer far greater activity and stability than their first-generation predecessors. NHC complexes of palladium, gold, copper and nickel are widely used in cross-coupling, hydrosilylation, and C–H activation chemistry.

Organocatalysis

Free NHCs are themselves nucleophilic catalysts: thiazolium and triazolium carbenes catalyse the benzoin condensation (the basis of Breslow's 1958 mechanism for vitamin B1 catalysis) and the Stetter reaction, umpolung processes in which the carbene converts an aldehyde carbonyl into an acyl-anion equivalent (the Breslow intermediate). NHC catalysis now encompasses homoenolate chemistry, transesterification, and ring-opening polymerisation.

Other applications

NHCs stabilise unusual main-group and low-valent species, form robust self-assembled monolayers on gold, and appear in ionic liquids, metal–organic frameworks and medicinal chemistry research.

References: Arduengo et al., J. Am. Chem. Soc. 1991 (first stable NHC); Hopkinson, Richter, Schedler & Glorius, Nature 2014, "An overview of N-heterocyclic carbenes"; Enders et al., Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2007 on NHC organocatalysis.
