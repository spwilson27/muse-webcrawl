Girsu (Sumerian ĝir2-su; modern Telloh, Iraq) was an ancient Sumerian city, the religious capital and principal shrine city of the city-state of Lagash. It was the sanctuary of the god Ningirsu, from whom it took its name. Girsu flourished in the Early Dynastic and Neo-Sumerian periods (roughly 2900–2000 BC).

History

Girsu was the sacred centre of the Lagash state, whose rulers — among them Ur-Nanshe, Eannatum, Urukagina and Gudea — built and lavishly endowed its temples, above all the Eninnu, the temple of Ningirsu. Although the political capital later shifted to the city of Lagash itself (al-Hiba), Girsu retained its religious primacy. The city declined after the fall of Ur III around 2000 BC.

Archaeology

Telloh was excavated by the French from 1877, first under Ernest de Sarzec, making it the first major Sumerian site excavated. The finds included the statues of Gudea, the Stele of the Vultures, and vast archives of cuneiform tablets — tens of thousands of administrative texts that underpin modern knowledge of the Sumerian temple economy. More recent Franco-Iraqi and British Museum-supported work (the Girsu Project, from 2016) has resumed research and site protection after years of looting damage.
