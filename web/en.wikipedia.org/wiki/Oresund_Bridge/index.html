Oresund Bridge

Øresund Bridge (Danish: Øresundsbroen)
Connects: Copenhagen, Denmark — Malmö, Sweden
Length: about 7.85 km (bridge section)
Opened: 1 July 2000
Carries: E20 motorway; Øresund railway line

The Oresund Bridge (Danish: Øresundsbroen; Swedish: Öresundsbron) is a combined railway and motorway cable-stayed bridge across the Øresund strait between Denmark and Sweden. Together with a tunnel on the Danish side and the artificial island of Peberholm in between, it forms the Øresund Link, opened in 2000, which created a fixed connection between Copenhagen and Malmö.

Construction

The bridge itself is about 7.8 km long, with a main cable-stayed span of 490 m carried on pylons 204 m tall — among the tallest bridge pylons in the world — allowing shipping to pass to the port of Copenhagen. On the Swedish side the link meets land at Lernacken; on the Danish side, traffic descends from the artificial island of Peberholm into the 4 km Drogden Tunnel, a solution chosen to avoid interfering with Copenhagen Airport's flight paths and to preserve water flow through the strait.

Impact

The link integrated the labour and housing markets of Zealand and Scania into a single Øresund Region, with tens of thousands of commuters crossing daily. It is also culturally prominent as the setting of the Scandinavian crime drama The Bridge (Bron/Broen).
