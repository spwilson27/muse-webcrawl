Minimum ground time
Abbreviation: MGT
Field: Airline operations
Also called: Turnaround time

Minimum ground time (MGT), also known as minimum turnaround time, is the shortest scheduled time an aircraft is planned to spend on the ground between an arrival and its next departure. It represents the minimum period needed to complete all turnaround processes — disembarking and boarding passengers, unloading and loading baggage and cargo, refuelling, catering, cleaning, and safety and technical checks — under normal conditions.

Typical values

Minimum ground times depend on aircraft size, route type and airport. A short-haul narrow-body aircraft such as a Boeing 737 or Airbus A320 may be scheduled with a ground time of about 25–45 minutes; low-cost carriers are known for targeting turnarounds near 25 minutes. Wide-body aircraft on long-haul services usually require 90 minutes to three hours or more.

Operational significance

Ground time is a central factor in aircraft utilisation and schedule planning: shorter turnarounds mean more flying hours per day per aircraft and lower unit costs, but schedules built at the minimum ground time have little buffer, so delays propagate through an airline's network. Airlines and airports use MGT standards by aircraft type and station in slot planning and schedule construction.
