Converse (logic)

In logic and mathematics, the converse of a conditional statement "if P then Q" is formed by swapping its two parts: "if Q then P". The converse of a true statement need not be true.

Reversing the arrow: the converse of P → Q is Q → P.

Definition

For implication P → Q, the converse is Q → P. Example: the converse of "if it is raining, the ground is wet" is "if the ground is wet, it is raining" — false in general (sprinklers exist).

Related forms

  • Inverse: ¬P → ¬Q
  • Contrapositive: ¬Q → ¬P — logically equivalent to the original.
  • Biconditional: P ↔ Q holds when a statement and its converse are both true.

Assuming a statement's converse without proof is the fallacy of affirming the consequent.

Converse relation

The converse relation of a binary relation R swaps each ordered pair: y R⁻¹ x exactly when x R y — e.g. "is parent of" converts to "is child of."
