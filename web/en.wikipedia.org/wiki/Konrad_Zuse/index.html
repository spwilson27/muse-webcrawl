Konrad Zuse (1910–1995) was a German civil engineer, inventor and computer pioneer. His greatest achievement is widely regarded as the world's first working programmable, fully automatic digital computer, the Z3, completed in 1941.

Early work

Trained as a civil engineer, Zuse tired of tedious structural calculations and began building mechanical calculating machines in his parents' Berlin apartment. His Z1 (1938) was a binary mechanical computer; the relay-based Z2 followed, then the Z3 in 1941.

Plankalkül

Between 1942 and 1945 Zuse designed Plankalkül ("plan calculus"), the first high-level programming language, including algorithms for chess. It was not implemented during his lifetime.

Later life

After the war he founded Zuse KG, which built dozens of computers before being absorbed by Siemens. His Z4 was delivered to ETH Zürich in 1950, then the only working computer in continental Europe.
