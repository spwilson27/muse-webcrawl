The Return of the King (1980 film)

Directed by: Jules Bass, Arthur Rankin Jr.
Released: 11 May 1980 (ABC)
Production: Rankin/Bass Productions

The Return of the King is a 1980 American animated musical fantasy television film produced by Rankin/Bass Productions and animated by Topcraft, adapting the third volume of J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings. It first aired on ABC on 11 May 1980.

Plot

Opening at Bilbo's 129th birthday party in Rivendell, the story is told in flashback by a minstrel (voiced by Glenn Yarbrough, who also sings the ballad "Frodo of the Nine Fingers"). The film covers Frodo and Sam's journey through Mordor, the siege of Minas Tirith, the destruction of the Ring, Aragorn's coronation, and the departure from the Grey Havens. Orson Bean voiced both Bilbo and Frodo; John Huston returned as Gandalf from Rankin/Bass's 1977 The Hobbit.

Relationship to other adaptations

The film functions as a sequel to the 1977 animated The Hobbit rather than to Ralph Bakshi's 1978 The Lord of the Rings, although it necessarily retells elements of The Fellowship of the Ring and The Two Towers in a brief prologue. Its songs and simplified narrative target a young audience.

Reception

The film received mixed reviews and is generally considered the weakest of the early Tolkien adaptations, though its folk-ballad soundtrack retains admirers.
