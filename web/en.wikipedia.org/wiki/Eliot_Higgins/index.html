Eliot Ward Higgins (born 1979) is a British citizen journalist and open-source investigator. He founded the investigative journalism website and collective Bellingcat in 2014. Higgins first gained attention for his blog Brown Moses, on which he analysed video footage from the Syrian civil war to identify the weapons in use.

Career

Working from his home in Leicester, Higgins taught himself to use open-source intelligence (OSINT) techniques — geolocating photographs and videos, and cross-referencing footage against satellite imagery and weapons catalogues. His analysis of footage from Syria demonstrated, among other findings, the use of cluster munitions and chemical weapons, and was cited by major news organisations and human rights groups.

In July 2014, days before the shootdown of Malaysia Airlines Flight 17 over Ukraine, Higgins launched Bellingcat, initially funded through Kickstarter. Bellingcat's volunteer investigators used open-source methods to trace the Buk missile launcher that destroyed MH17 to Russia's 53rd Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigade, findings later adopted by the official Dutch-led Joint Investigation Team. Bellingcat subsequently investigated the Skripal poisoning, identifying the suspects as officers of Russian military intelligence, and the poisoning of Alexei Navalny.

Recognition

Higgins and Bellingcat have received numerous journalism and human rights awards. His book We Are Bellingcat (2021) recounts the organisation's development and methods. Higgins has been widely credited with demonstrating that rigorous investigative work can be performed with publicly available data.
