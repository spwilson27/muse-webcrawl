Ankle–brachial pressure index

SynonymsAnkle–brachial index (ABI)
PurposeScreening for peripheral artery disease
Normal range~1.0–1.4

The ankle–brachial pressure index (ABPI), or ankle–brachial index (ABI), is the ratio of the systolic blood pressure at the ankle to the systolic blood pressure in the upper arm (brachium). It is a simple, non-invasive test used to assess peripheral artery disease.

Method

Blood pressures are measured at the brachial artery and at the dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial arteries of each foot, using a sphygmomanometer cuff and a Doppler ultrasound probe. The ABI for each leg is calculated by dividing the higher ankle pressure by the higher of the two arm pressures.

Interpretation

Values from about 1.0 to 1.4 are considered normal. An ABI of 0.9 or lower indicates peripheral artery disease, with lower values reflecting more severe disease; values above 1.4 suggest non-compressible, calcified arteries, as may occur in diabetes, and require alternative testing such as a toe–brachial index.
