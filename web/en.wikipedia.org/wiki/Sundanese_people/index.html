Sundanese people

The Sundanese (Sundanese: Urang Sunda) are a native Austronesian ethnic group of western Java, Indonesia, numbering roughly 40 million and forming the second-largest ethnic group in the country after the Javanese. Their traditional homeland, Tatar Pasundan, comprises the provinces of West Java and Banten, as well as the western part of Central Java and the capital region of Jakarta, whose native Betawi culture has strong Sundanese roots.

Language and religion

The Sundanese speak the Sundanese language, a Malayo-Polynesian language with a distinctive system of speech levels reflecting social status. The overwhelming majority are Sunni Muslims, Islam having spread through the region from the 16th century, although the Baduy people of the interior preserve the ancestral Sunda Wiwitan belief system and some communities retain pre-Islamic customs.

History

The Sundanese kingdoms of Tarumanagara and later the Sunda Kingdom dominated western Java for over a millennium. The Battle of Bubat in 1357, in which a Sundanese royal party was killed at the Majapahit court, remains a potent symbol in Sundanese–Javanese relations. The region later came under Banten, Cirebon, Mataram and ultimately Dutch colonial rule.

Culture

Sundanese culture is known for gamelan degung music, the bamboo angklung (inscribed by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage), wayang golek wooden puppet theatre, jaipongan dance, and a cuisine emphasizing fresh raw vegetables (lalab), sambal, and dishes such as nasi timbel and karedok.
