TOPGUN, officially the United States Navy Strike Fighter Tactics Instructor program (SFTI), is the US Navy's premier school for air combat tactics, teaching selected naval aviators and naval flight officers advanced strike-fighter tactics and techniques, and training them as instructors who return to their fleet squadrons to pass on what they have learned.

History

The school was established on 3 March 1969 at Naval Air Station Miramar, California, in response to the poor air-to-air kill ratios of US aircraft during the Vietnam War — the subject of the classified Ault Report. The first class trained with F-8 Crusaders and used dissimilar aircraft (such as the A-4 Skyhawk and F-5) to simulate enemy fighters. During the 1972 Operation Linebacker bombing campaign, Navy kill ratios improved dramatically compared with the Air Force, which had no equivalent school; the improvement is widely credited to TOPGUN training.

Modern school

In 1996 TOPGUN moved from Miramar to Naval Air Station Fallon, Nevada, where it became part of the Naval Strike and Air Warfare Center, now the Naval Aviation Warfighting Development Center. The course, lasting about 12–13 weeks, includes classroom instruction, one-versus-one and multi-aircraft engagements against adversary aircraft flown by instructors, and training in weapons employment. Graduates wear the TOPGUN patch and serve as strike-fighter tactics instructors in fleet units.

In popular culture

The school was immortalised by the 1986 film Top Gun starring Tom Cruise, and its 2022 sequel Top Gun: Maverick, both of which greatly raised its public profile; the real school reportedly fine-jokes that anyone quoting the film on the premises owes a small fine.
