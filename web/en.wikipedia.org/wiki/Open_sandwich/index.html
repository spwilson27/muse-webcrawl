Open sandwich

Open sandwich
Alternative namesOpen-face sandwich; smørrebrød (Denmark); smörgås (Sweden); belegtes Brot (Germany)
TypeSandwich
Main ingredientsA single slice of bread with savoury toppings
ServingTypically eaten with knife and fork

An open sandwich, also known as an open-face sandwich, is a sandwich consisting of a single slice of bread with one or more toppings, eaten with a knife and fork rather than by hand. Open sandwiches are a traditional staple across Scandinavia and northern and central Europe.

Regional varieties

In Denmark, smørrebrød — rye bread spread with butter and topped with combinations such as pickled herring, roast beef with remoulade and crispy onions, or shrimp with egg and mayonnaise — is a national institution, served in specialised restaurants with elaborate conventions about the order in which varieties are eaten. Swedish smörgås and the elaborate smörgåsbord follow similar principles, while in Germany belegtes Brot and in the Netherlands boterham met beleg are everyday fare.

The custom is said to descend from medieval trenchers — slices of stale bread used as edible plates — and became a vehicle for presenting costly ingredients attractively: unlike a closed sandwich, the topping of an open sandwich is fully visible, so the quality of the ingredients is on display.

Similar dishes

Related preparations include the French tartine, Italian bruschetta and crostini, and Welsh rarebit, in which a cheese sauce is poured over toast. In the United States, open-faced hot sandwiches — such as the hot turkey sandwich with gravy, or the Louisville Hot Brown — are served as plated meals.
