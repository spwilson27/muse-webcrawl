Minnehaha Creek

Minnehaha Creek is a 22-mile (35 km) stream in Hennepin County, Minnesota, United States, flowing from Lake Minnetonka eastward through the suburbs of Minneapolis to its mouth on the Mississippi River. It is best known for Minnehaha Falls, the 53-foot waterfall near its mouth made famous by Henry Wadsworth Longfellow's poem The Song of Hiawatha (1855).

Course

The creek begins at Gray's Bay Dam on Lake Minnetonka and flows through Minnetonka, Hopkins, St. Louis Park, Edina, and Minneapolis. Within Minneapolis it passes through the Chain of Lakes area near Lake Harriet and Lake Nokomis before dropping over Minnehaha Falls in Minnehaha Park and joining the Mississippi. Because its flow is controlled by the dam at Lake Minnetonka, water levels vary considerably, and the falls can slow to a trickle in dry summers.

Watershed and recreation

The Minnehaha Creek Watershed District manages water quality and flooding across the roughly 180-square-mile watershed. A popular paddling and cycling corridor follows much of the creek's length, and the creek is part of the Grand Rounds National Scenic Byway park system in Minneapolis. Urban runoff, stormwater management, and aquatic invasive species are the principal environmental concerns.
