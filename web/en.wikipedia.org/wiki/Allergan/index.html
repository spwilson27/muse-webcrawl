Allergan plc was an American-founded, Irish-domiciled pharmaceutical company known principally for Botox (botulinum toxin) and products in medical aesthetics, eye care, the central nervous system, and gastroenterology. In 2020 it was acquired by AbbVie in a transaction valued at about $63 billion; the Allergan name survives as a division, Allergan Aesthetics.

History

The original Allergan was founded in 1950 in Los Angeles by pharmacist Gavin S. Herbert, growing from eye-care products into ophthalmic pharmaceuticals, and later acquiring the rights to Botox, developed from botulinum toxin research and approved for cosmetic use in 2002. In 2015 Actavis — itself the product of a merger chain that included Watson Pharmaceuticals — acquired Allergan, Inc. for about $70 billion and took the Allergan name, domiciling the combined company in Ireland in a tax inversion. Under chief executive Brent Saunders the company divested its generics business to Teva in 2016 and bought Zeltiq (CoolSculpting) and LifeCell.

Products and controversies

Signature products included Botox and Botox Cosmetic, the dry-eye drug Restasis, Juvederm dermal fillers, and the Alzheimer's drug Namenda. Allergan drew criticism in 2017 for transferring Restasis patents to the Saint Regis Mohawk Tribe in an attempt to shield them from administrative patent challenge — a strategy courts rejected. Its sale to AbbVie, announced in June 2019 and completed in May 2020, created one of the world's largest pharmaceutical companies.
