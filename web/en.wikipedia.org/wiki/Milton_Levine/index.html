Milton Levine

Born: November 3, 1913, Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Died: January 16, 2011 (aged 97), Thousand Oaks, California, U.S.
Occupation: Entrepreneur, inventor
Known for: Co-inventing the ant farm

Milton M. Levine (November 3, 1913 – January 16, 2011) was an American entrepreneur best known as the co-inventor of the ant farm, the educational toy consisting of two panes of glass or plastic in a frame, filled with sand or gel, in which live ants tunnel visibly. With his brother-in-law E. J. Cossman, he founded the company that became Uncle Milton Industries in 1956 and sold the first ant farms by mail order.

Career

Levine said the idea came to him at a Fourth of July picnic in 1956, watching ants after remembering childhood fascination with collecting insects in jars. The original Ant Farm kit sold for $2.95 and included a coupon with which the buyer ordered live red harvester ants by mail. The toy became a durable success: millions were sold, the design was exhibited as a design classic, and ant farms were flown on a NASA Space Shuttle mission for classroom experiments. Levine expanded into other science and nature toys under the Uncle Milton brand.

Later life

Levine remained associated with the company for decades and was widely profiled as a quintessential American novelty entrepreneur. He died in Thousand Oaks, California, in 2011 at the age of 97.
