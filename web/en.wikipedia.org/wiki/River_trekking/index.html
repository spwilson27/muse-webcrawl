River trekking

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

River trekkers making their way upstream through a rocky gorge

River trekking, also known as river tracing or stream trekking, is an outdoor activity combining trekking, climbing, swimming and scrambling along a river or stream course, usually progressing upstream through gorges, waterfalls and pools. It is particularly popular in East and Southeast Asia — including Japan (where it is called sawanobori, "stream climbing"), Taiwan, and Hong Kong — as well as in parts of Europe and the Americas.

Description

Participants travel on foot along the bed of a river rather than on a bank-side path, wading through pools, swimming where the water is deep, scrambling over boulders and climbing beside or up waterfalls. Routes range from gentle walks up shallow streams to technically demanding ascents requiring ropes and climbing equipment. The activity is closely related to canyoning (canyoneering), which typically descends a canyon or gorge rather than ascending it.

Equipment

River trekkers typically wear felt-soled or specialised gripping shoes for traction on wet rock, helmets to protect against falling stones, and wetsuits in cold water. Ropes, harnesses and carabiners may be carried for waterfall climbs, and dry bags protect supplies and electronics. In Japan, traditional tabi-style felt-soled footwear is widely used for sawanobori.

Risks

Principal hazards include flash flooding after heavy rain upstream, slippery rock, hypothermia, drowning in deep pools or currents, and rockfall in narrow gorges. Checking weather forecasts, avoiding narrow gorges during or after rain, and travelling in groups with appropriate equipment are standard safety precautions. A number of fatal accidents in Hong Kong and Taiwan have prompted official warnings about river trekking during the rainy season.
