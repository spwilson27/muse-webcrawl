Modal shift

Policies for modal shift aim to move journeys from cars to public transport, cycling and walking

Modal shift (also modal transfer or mode shift) is a change in the share of journeys made by a particular mode of transport relative to others, for example from private cars to public transport, cycling or walking. It is a central objective of sustainable transport policy, because different modes have very different costs in emissions, road space, congestion and safety per passenger-kilometre.

Measures

Measures intended to produce modal shift include investment in public transport capacity and reliability, bus priority lanes, integrated ticketing, park-and-ride schemes, cycling infrastructure, congestion charging and low-emission zones, parking restraint, and land-use planning that shortens journeys. In freight, policy often aims to shift goods from road to rail or water.

Measurement

Modal share is measured by travel surveys, ticket data and traffic counts, and is usually expressed as a percentage of trips or of distance travelled. Because habits, income and geography strongly affect mode choice, achieved shifts are typically gradual and contested; critics note that new road capacity can induce traffic that erodes gains.
