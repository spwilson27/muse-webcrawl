Ziz

The Ziz (Hebrew: זיז) is a giant griffin-like bird in Jewish mythology, said to be large enough to be able to block out the sun with its wingspan. It is considered a primordial monster unafraid of anyone, parallel to the land beast Behemoth and the sea monster Leviathan, with the three forming a trio of enormous creatures of land, sea, and air.

In Jewish tradition

The Ziz appears in rabbinic literature. The Talmud (Bava Batra 73b) recounts tales of its immense size, including an egg that fell and flooded towns and broke cedars. Psalms 50:11 refers to the "ziz śāday" — "the ziz of the field" — known to God along with all the birds of the mountains. Later tradition held that at the messianic banquet, the righteous would feast on Behemoth, Leviathan, and Ziz.

Characteristics

According to legend, the Ziz was created to rule the birds, just as Leviathan rules the fish, and to protect smaller birds from predators; one account says that if it had not been created, the smaller birds would have no defence. Stories emphasise that despite its size it is a creature under God's authority, and some texts describe it standing ankle-deep in the ocean with its head reaching the sky.

In popular culture

The Ziz appears in modern fantasy games, novels, and bestiaries as a giant roc-like bird, often alongside Behemoth and Leviathan as a triad of legendary monsters.
