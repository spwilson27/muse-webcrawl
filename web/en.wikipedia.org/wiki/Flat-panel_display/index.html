Flat-panel display

Flat-panel display
Main technologiesLCD, LED-backlit LCD, OLED, plasma, e-paper
PredecessorCathode-ray tube (CRT)
ApplicationsTelevisions, monitors, smartphones, tablets, signage

A flat-panel display (FPD) is an electronic display used to display visual content such as text or images. It is present in consumer, medical, transportation and industrial equipment. Flat panels are far lighter and thinner than traditional cathode-ray tube (CRT) television sets and video displays, rarely being thicker than 10 cm (3.9 in).

History

The first proposal for a flat-panel display was made by Hungarian inventor Kálmán Tihanyi in 1928. Practical devices arrived with the plasma display panel, developed in 1964 at the University of Illinois, and early liquid-crystal displays (LCDs) in the 1970s. By the early 2000s LCDs had overtaken CRTs in computer monitors, and by the end of the decade in televisions as well.

Types

The most common technologies are liquid-crystal displays, which use a backlight and liquid crystals to modulate light; organic light-emitting diode (OLED) displays, in which each pixel emits its own light; and electronic paper (e-ink), which imitates the appearance of ordinary ink on paper and is used in e-readers. Plasma displays, once popular for large televisions, have largely been discontinued.

Characteristics

Flat panels are characterised by their resolution, refresh rate, contrast ratio, viewing angle and colour gamut. Unlike CRTs, they have a fixed native resolution, and images displayed at other resolutions must be scaled.
