Riemann–Hurwitz formula

In mathematics, the Riemann–Hurwitz formula, named after Bernhard Riemann and Adolf Hurwitz, describes the relationship between the Euler characteristics of two surfaces when one is a ramified covering of the other. It thereby relates the purely topological problem of covering surfaces to the algebraic problem of finding holomorphic maps between Riemann surfaces.

Statement

For a non-constant holomorphic map f : X → Y of degree N between compact Riemann surfaces, the formula reads:

2gX − 2 = N · (2gY − 2) + Σ (eP − 1),

where g denotes the genus of each surface and the sum runs over all points P of X, with eP the ramification index of f at P. Points where eP > 1 are ramification points, and their images in Y are branch points.

Consequences

For a map from a Riemann surface to the Riemann sphere (genus 0), the formula computes the genus from the degree and ramification data. It implies, for example, that there is no non-constant holomorphic map from a torus to the sphere of degree 1, and it is used to prove that a compact Riemann surface of genus greater than 1 has a finite automorphism group — Hurwitz's theorem bounding that group by 84(g − 1), the famous Hurwitz surfaces bound.

Generalisations

The formula is a special case of the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem and generalises to algebraic curves over other fields, where the different ideal supplies the ramification correction, and to orbifolds and stacks in modern geometry.
