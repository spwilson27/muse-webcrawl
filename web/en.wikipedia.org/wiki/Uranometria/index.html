Uranometria

Uranometria is a star atlas produced by the German astronomer Johann Bayer. Published in Augsburg in 1603 by Christoph Mang (Christophorus Mangus), its full title is Uranometria: omnium asterismorum continens schemata, nova methodo delineata, aereis laminis expressa — "Uranometria, containing charts of all the constellations, drawn by a new method and engraved on copper plates". It was the first atlas to cover the entire celestial sphere.

Contents

The atlas contains 51 copperplate charts engraved by Alexander Mair: 48 charts of the Ptolemaic constellations, one chart of the twelve southern constellations newly surveyed by Dutch navigators (based on observations by Pieter Dirkszoon Keyser and Frederick de Houtman), and two planispheres of the northern and southern hemispheres. Bayer's positions drew heavily on the catalogue of Tycho Brahe, supplemented for the far south by Dutch reports.

Bayer designations

Uranometria introduced the system of Bayer designations, in which stars within each constellation are labelled with Greek letters — usually, though not strictly, in order of brightness (alpha, beta, gamma…) — followed by the Latin genitive of the constellation name: Alpha Centauri, Beta Pictoris, and so on. The system remains in universal use for bright stars today.

Legacy

The atlas set new standards for celestial cartography in accuracy and artistic quality; its constellation figures were widely copied. Bayer's southern constellations — including Pavo, Triangulum Australe, and Hydrus — are among the 88 modern constellations recognised by the International Astronomical Union.
