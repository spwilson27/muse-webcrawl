Rallying is a wide-ranging form of motorsport with various competitive elements such as speed tests (also called rally racing), navigation tests, or driving tests for the reliability and economy of the car. They can be run on closed or open roads, using modified production cars or specially built competition vehicles. It is distinguished by its point-to-point format, in which participants and their co-drivers "rally" to a set of points, leaving as intervals, with the team that completes the course with least penalty or fastest aggregate time being the winner.

Rallying is run on any surface — asphalt, gravel, snow and ice, and sometimes a mixture — and in all weather. A modern rally consists of timed special stages on closed roads, connected by untimed liaison sections on open public roads, where crews must observe traffic laws. The co-driver reads pacenotes describing the road ahead in detail — bends, crests, hazards — allowing the driver to attack stages at speeds that would otherwise be impossible.

History

The term "rally" derives from the French rallier, to muster or gather together. The first event to bear the name was the Monte Carlo Rally of 1911, organised by Prince Albert I of Monaco as a motoring tour converging on Monte Carlo from different European starting points. Long-distance endurance events such as the Mille Miglia and the marathon rallies of the 1950s, including the East African Safari Rally, developed the sport's format. The World Rally Championship was inaugurated by the FIA in 1973.

The 1980s saw the spectacular but dangerous Group B era, in which supercharged and turbocharged cars of more than 500 horsepower attracted huge crowds; after a series of fatal accidents, Group B was banned at the end of 1986 and replaced by the more production-based Group A, which in turn gave rise to the World Rally Car formula in 1997. From 2022 the WRC's top class has used Rally1 cars with plug-in hybrid powertrains.

Types of rally

Stage rallies

The dominant modern form, decided by total time over closed-road special stages. Classes run from the top-tier Rally1 cars down through Rally2, Rally3, Rally4 and Rally5, and national championships admit a wide variety of machinery.

Road rallies

Run at night on public roads in some countries (notably the United Kingdom), emphasising navigation and regularity rather than outright speed.

Rally raids

Long-distance off-road events such as the Dakar Rally, run over days or weeks across deserts and wilderness, with navigation by roadbook and GPS.

Historic rallies

Events for classic rally cars, either in full competition or in regularity format.
