Acid–base titration

An acid–base titration is a quantitative analytical method for determining the concentration of an acid or a base by exactly neutralising it with a standard solution of base or acid of known concentration. A pH indicator or pH meter is used to monitor the reaction and detect the equivalence point.

Procedure

The titrant is added from a burette into a flask containing the analyte until the endpoint — the point at which the indicator changes colour — is reached. From the volume of titrant used and the stoichiometry of the neutralisation reaction, the unknown concentration is calculated.

Titration curves

A plot of pH against volume of titrant added shows a characteristic steep section near the equivalence point. The shape differs for strong acid–strong base, weak acid–strong base and polyprotic systems, and the choice of indicator (such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange) must match the pH jump at equivalence.
