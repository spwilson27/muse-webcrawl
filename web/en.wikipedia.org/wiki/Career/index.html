Career

Career
DefinitionThe sequence of work-related experiences over a person's life
Related fieldsCareer development, vocational psychology, human resources

A career is an individual's progression through learning, work and other aspects of life, particularly the sequence of occupations and roles a person holds over their working life. The word derives from the Latin carrus (wagon) via French carrière, a road or racetrack — reflecting the idea of a course run through life.

Concepts

Career studies distinguish the objective career (the observable sequence of jobs) from the subjective career (the individual's own sense-making of that sequence). Traditional models assumed a linear, upward progression within a single organisation — the "organisational career" — but late-20th-century scholarship described more mobile patterns: the boundaryless career, in which people move across employers, and the protean career, directed by the individual rather than the organisation.

Career development

Donald Super's life-span theory described stages of career development from growth and exploration through establishment, maintenance and disengagement. Career guidance and counselling help individuals choose occupations, plan education, change jobs and manage transitions; organisations invest in career management for recruitment, retention and succession planning. Earnings, status and occupational identity all accumulate around career trajectories, making careers a central subject in sociology and economics.
