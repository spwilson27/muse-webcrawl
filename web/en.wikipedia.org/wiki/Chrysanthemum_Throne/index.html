Chrysanthemum Throne

The Chrysanthemum Throne (Japanese: 皇位, Kōi, literally "Imperial position") is the term used to identify the throne of the Emperor of Japan. The term also can refer to the Takamikura, the very specific seat, such as the throne in the Shishin-den at Kyoto Imperial Palace used for enthronement ceremonies.

The chrysanthemum (菊, kiku) is the Imperial crest (kamon) of Japan: the sixteen-petal Imperial Seal of Japan appears on the emperor's flag, on Japanese passports, and on the Order of the Chrysanthemum, the country's highest order.

History

The Japanese monarchy is regarded as the oldest continuous hereditary monarchy in the world, traditionally traced to Emperor Jimmu in 660 BCE, though the historically verifiable line runs from around the 6th century CE. The physical Takamikura throne dates to the early 20th century, modelled on earlier designs, and is transported from Kyoto to the Imperial Palace in Tokyo for each enthronement ceremony (Sokui no Rei), most recently for Emperor Naruhito in 2019.

The seat

The Takamikura is an octagonal, canopied structure decorated with lacquer, gold, and images of phoenixes and the sun. The emperor sits on it during the enthronement ceremony, while the empress occupies the smaller companion throne, the Michōdai. When not in use, both are kept at the Kyoto Imperial Palace.

By extension, "the Chrysanthemum Throne" serves in English as a metonym for the Japanese imperial institution itself, in the same way "the Crown" is used for other monarchies.
