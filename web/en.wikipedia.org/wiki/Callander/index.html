Callander (Scottish Gaelic: Calasraid) is a small town in the council area of Stirling, Scotland, situated on the River Teith. The town is located in the historic county of Perthshire and is a popular tourist stop on the main route to the Scottish Highlands. It lies within the Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park and is often described as the "Gateway to the Highlands".

Geography and tourism

Callander sits where the lowland meets the Highland boundary fault, overlooked by Ben Ledi (879 m) and the crags of Callander Craig. The River Teith, formed nearby by the confluence of the Garbh Uisge and Eas Gobhain, flows through the town on its way to join the Forth. The town developed as a Victorian tourist resort and railway destination; though the railway closed in the 1960s, the former line now forms part of National Cycle Route 7, and the town remains a base for walking in the Trossachs and visits to the Falls of Leny and Bracklinn Falls.

History

The area has prehistoric and Roman associations, including a Roman camp nearby at Bochastle. Callander grew with the arrival of tourism in the 19th century following the popularity of Sir Walter Scott's poem The Lady of the Lake, set in the nearby Trossachs. The Rob Roy MacGregor story is closely associated with the surrounding countryside.
