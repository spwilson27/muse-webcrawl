Sexual slavery

Sexual slavery is a form of slavery involving the systematic sexual exploitation of a person, in which the enslaved person is limited in autonomy, freedom of movement and power to decide matters relating to their sexual activity. It encompasses forced prostitution, single-owner sexual slavery, ritual slavery, and slavery used primarily for non-commercial sexual purposes, and is recognised under international law as a crime against humanity and a war crime when committed in the relevant contexts.

Definitions and law

The Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court lists sexual slavery among crimes against humanity and war crimes. The 1926 Slavery Convention and later instruments address slavery generally, while the Palermo Protocol covers trafficking in persons for sexual exploitation. Forced marriage may include elements of sexual slavery and is treated as a related practice by several courts and UN bodies.

Forms

  • Commercial sexual exploitation and trafficking — people coerced or deceived into prostitution controlled by others.
  • Wartime sexual slavery — notably the Imperial Japanese Army's "comfort women" system before and during the Second World War, and sexual enslavement by armed groups in later conflicts, including by ISIL against Yazidi women and girls, prosecuted in some national courts as genocide and crimes against humanity.
  • Chattel and domestic settings — enslaved people sexually exploited by owners in historical systems of slavery.
  • Ritual slavery — practices such as trokosi in parts of West Africa.

Prevalence

The Global Slavery Index and ILO estimates of "modern slavery" include millions of people in forced commercial sexual exploitation and forced marriage, though prevalence figures are contested because the crimes are hidden.
