Society for the Protection of Nature in Israel

The Society for the Protection of Nature in Israel (SPNI; Hebrew: החברה להגנת הטבע) is Israel's oldest and largest environmental organisation. It was founded in 1953 by Azaria Alon, Amotz Zahavi and others, in reaction to plans to drain Lake Hula, and is a nonprofit membership organisation best known for nature education, hiking and conservation advocacy.

Activities

SPNI operates a network of field schools near nature reserves — including Ein Gedi, Har Meron and Eilat — that host schoolchildren, soldiers, hikers and birdwatchers. It helped establish and promote Israel's system of marked hiking trails and has campaigned to protect wildflowers (a famously successful 1960s campaign made picking them socially unacceptable), wetlands such as the Hula Valley, open spaces threatened by development, and migratory bird habitats along the Great Rift Valley flyway.

Influence

Through its membership, community branches in Israeli cities and participation in planning proceedings, SPNI has been a major voice in Israeli environmental policy for over seventy years, working on issues from urban nature and transport to the preservation of the Mediterranean coastline and the Jerusalem hills.
