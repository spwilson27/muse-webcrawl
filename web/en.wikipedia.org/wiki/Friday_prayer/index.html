Friday prayer

Friday prayer, or congregational prayer (Arabic: صَلَاة ٱلْجُمُعَة, Ṣalāt al-Jumuʿah), is a prayer (salah) that Muslims hold every Friday, just after noon instead of the regular Zuhr prayer. It is one of the most strongly emphasised duties in Islamic practice for adult Muslim men, and is mentioned in the Qur'an in Surah Al-Jumu'ah (62:9), which calls believers to leave off trade and hasten to the remembrance of God when the call to Friday prayer is proclaimed.

Form of the prayer

The Friday prayer is preceded by a sermon (khutbah) delivered by the imam, normally in two parts separated by a brief sitting. Attendance at the sermon is considered an integral part of the observance. The prayer itself consists of two rak'ahs (units) prayed aloud in congregation, in contrast to the four rak'ahs of the ordinary midday prayer, and is commonly preceded and followed by voluntary sunnah prayers. Worshippers typically bathe, wear clean clothes and perfume, and arrive early, in accordance with the Prophet Muhammad's example.

Obligation and attendance

According to the majority of jurists, Friday prayer is obligatory (fard) for free, adult, resident Muslim men who are able to attend; women, travellers, children and the sick are excused, though women may attend. A minimum congregation is required for validity, with the schools of law differing on the number. In many Muslim-majority countries Friday is part of the weekend, and businesses close or pause during the prayer; elsewhere, employers and schools may grant time for attendance.

Significance

Friday is regarded as the most excellent day of the week in Islam — the day on which Adam was created, according to tradition — and the congregational prayer serves as the weekly focal point of communal worship, preaching and social gathering for the local Muslim community.
