Paul Joseph Schrader (born 22 July 1946) is an American screenwriter, film director and critic. A leading figure of the New Hollywood generation, he wrote the screenplays for Martin Scorsese's Taxi Driver (1976), Raging Bull (1980, co-written), The Last Temptation of Christ (1988) and Bringing Out the Dead (1999), and has directed films including Blue Collar (1978), American Gigolo (1980), Mishima: A Life in Four Chapters (1985), Affliction (1997) and First Reformed (2017), the last of which earned him his first Academy Award nomination, for Best Original Screenplay, at the age of 71.

Early life and theory

Schrader was raised in Grand Rapids, Michigan, in a strict Christian Reformed household in which films were forbidden; he did not see a movie until he was seventeen. He studied theology at Calvin College, then film at UCLA, becoming a critic for the Los Angeles Free Press. His master's thesis became the influential 1972 book Transcendental Style in Film, analysing the austere spiritual cinema of Yasujirō Ozu, Robert Bresson and Carl Theodor Dreyer — a framework critics have applied to Schrader's own late work.

Career

Screenwriting

Schrader broke through with the script for The Yakuza (1974, with his brother Leonard) and Brian De Palma's Obsession (1976). Taxi Driver, written in a burst while he was living in his car and in pain from an ulcer, created the archetype of the alienated loner that recurs throughout his work — the "man in a room" writing in a diary, as in Light Sleeper (1992), The Walker (2007) and First Reformed.

Directing

As a director Schrader has moved between studio films (American Gigolo, Cat People, Patty Hearst), independent dramas (Mishima, Affliction, which won James Coburn an Oscar), and provocative late works. First Reformed (2017), starring Ethan Hawke as a tormented minister, was a major critical revival, followed by The Card Counter (2021), Master Gardener (2022) and Oh, Canada (2024).

Themes

Schrader's films repeatedly explore lonely men, religious guilt, redemption through violence, and the conflict between a Calvinist upbringing and worldly desire. His outspoken commentary on cinema and culture, including prolific social-media writing, has kept him a combative public figure into his late seventies.
