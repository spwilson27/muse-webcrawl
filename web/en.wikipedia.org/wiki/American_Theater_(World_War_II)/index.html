The American Theater was a theater of operations during the Second World War, comprising the Americas and the waters extending about 200 miles (320 km) offshore. Although fighting in the theater was comparatively limited, it included the German U-boat campaign against shipping in the Atlantic and Caribbean, attacks on North American soil, and the Aleutian Islands campaign.

U-boat operations

Following the U.S. declaration of war in December 1941, German submarines launched Operation Drumbeat (Paukenschlag) in January 1942, attacking poorly defended merchant shipping along the U.S. East Coast and later in the Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean, sinking hundreds of ships. Convoy systems, air patrols, and blackouts gradually reduced losses through 1942. Brazilian entry into the war followed U-boat sinkings of Brazilian ships in August 1942.

Attacks on North America

Axis forces carried out several small attacks on the American mainland: the Japanese submarine shelling of the Ellwood oil field near Santa Barbara (February 1942) and of Fort Stevens, Oregon (June 1942), the only attacks on U.S. military installations in the continental United States; Japanese occupation of the Aleutian islands of Attu and Kiska (1942–1943), retaken by American and Canadian forces; and the Fu-Go balloon bomb campaign of 1944–1945, which caused the only deaths from enemy action in the continental United States when a balloon bomb killed six people in Oregon. German agents were also landed by submarine in the failed Operation Pastorius sabotage mission of 1942.

Other operations

The theater included the defense of the Panama Canal, the occupation of Greenland and garrisoning of Iceland, the Battle of the St. Lawrence in Canada, and clashes such as the Battle of the Caribbean. The American Theater campaign medal, the American Campaign Medal, recognised service in the region.
