Arab socialism

Arab socialism is a political ideology combining Arab nationalism with socialism, distinct from Marxist-Leninist communism, developed notably by Michel Aflaq and the Ba'ath Party and by Gamal Abdel Nasser in Egypt.

Ideas

Its slogan in Ba'athism — "Unity, Freedom, Socialism" — sought Arab unity, liberation from colonialism, land reform, nationalisation and state-led development while respecting religion and private property more than Soviet communism did. Nasser's version after 1952 brought land redistribution, the Aswan High Dam, nationalisation of the Suez Canal (1956) and expanded education and health.

History and decline

Arab socialist regimes ruled Egypt, Syria, Iraq, Libya, Algeria and South Yemen at various times. Defeat in the 1967 Six-Day War, economic stagnation and Sadat's infitah liberalisation in Egypt eroded the model, though its legacy persists in state structures and rhetoric. Compare Nyerere's African socialism and ujamaa.
