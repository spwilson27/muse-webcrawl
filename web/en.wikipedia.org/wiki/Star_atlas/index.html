Star atlas

A star atlas (or star map/sky atlas) is a set of charts of the night sky, showing the positions of stars, constellations, and other celestial objects across the celestial sphere. Atlases divide the sky into regions, each chart covering a portion at a chosen scale and limiting magnitude.

Contents

A typical atlas plots stars down to a specified apparent magnitude, deep-sky objects such as galaxies, nebulae and star clusters, the Milky Way's outline, constellation boundaries, and a coordinate grid of right ascension and declination for a stated epoch.

Notable atlases

Historically important atlases include Bayer's Uranometria (1603), Flamsteed's Atlas Coelestis (1729), the Bonner Durchmusterung, and in modern amateur use, Sky Atlas 2000.0 and Uranometria 2000.0. Digital planetarium software has largely superseded printed atlases for many observers.
