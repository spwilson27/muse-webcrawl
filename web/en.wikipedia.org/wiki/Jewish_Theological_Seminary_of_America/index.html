The Jewish Theological Seminary (JTS) is a Conservative Jewish education institution in New York City, New York. It is one of the academic and spiritual centers of Conservative Judaism, training rabbis, cantors, educators, and scholars, and it houses one of the world's great Judaica libraries.

History

JTS was founded in 1886 by a group of rabbis and lay leaders, including Sabato Morais, who sought a seminary that would preserve traditional Jewish learning while engaging modern scholarship, in reaction to the Reform movement's Pittsburgh Platform. In 1902, the Cambridge scholar Solomon Schechter became its president and transformed it into a major research institution, recruiting leading scholars such as Louis Ginzberg, Alexander Marx, and Israel Davidson. Under Schechter, the Seminary became the intellectual anchor of the emerging Conservative movement, and he founded the United Synagogue of America in 1913.

Academics

JTS comprises several schools, including the Rabbinical School, the H. L. Miller Cantorial School, the William Davidson Graduate School of Jewish Education, the Gershon Kekst Graduate School, and List College, its undergraduate program run jointly with Columbia University. Its library holds the largest collection of Hebraica and Judaica in the Western Hemisphere, including rare manuscripts and incunabula. The Seminary has been led since 2020 by Chancellor Shuly Rubin Schwartz, the first woman to hold the position.
