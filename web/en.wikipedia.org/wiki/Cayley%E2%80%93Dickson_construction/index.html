Cayley–Dickson construction

In mathematics, the Cayley–Dickson construction, named after Arthur Cayley and Leonard Eugene Dickson, produces a sequence of algebras over the real numbers, each double the dimension of the previous one. Starting from the real numbers it generates the complex numbers, the quaternions, the octonions, the sedenions, and beyond.

The construction

Given an algebra A with an involution (conjugation), the doubled algebra consists of ordered pairs (a, b) of elements of A, with multiplication (a, b)(c, d) = (ac − d*b, da + bc*) and conjugation (a, b)* = (a*, −b). Each doubling sacrifices an algebraic property: the complex numbers lose self-conjugacy, the quaternions lose commutativity, the octonions lose associativity, and the sedenions lose alternativity and acquire zero divisors.

Properties

All algebras in the sequence are power-associative and carry a norm related to the conjugation. The first four (reals, complex numbers, quaternions, octonions) are normed division algebras, matching Hurwitz's theorem.
