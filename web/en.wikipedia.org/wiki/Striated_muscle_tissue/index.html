Striated muscle tissue

Striated muscle tissue
TypesSkeletal muscle, cardiac muscle
Distinguished fromSmooth muscle
Contractile unitSarcomere

Striated muscle tissue is a form of muscle tissue that features repeating functional units called sarcomeres, whose orderly arrangement of actin and myosin filaments gives the tissue a banded or "striated" appearance under the microscope. It contrasts with smooth muscle, which lacks this regular banding.

Types

There are two main types. Skeletal muscle attaches to bones, is under voluntary control, and consists of long, multinucleated fibres. Cardiac muscle, found only in the heart, is involuntary, with branched cells joined by intercalated discs that synchronise contraction.

Structure

Within each muscle fibre, myofibrils are divided into sarcomeres bounded by Z-lines. Alternating dark A bands (containing myosin thick filaments) and light I bands (containing actin thin filaments) produce the striations. The sliding filament theory explains contraction: myosin heads pull actin filaments toward the sarcomere centre, shortening the muscle without the filaments themselves changing length.
