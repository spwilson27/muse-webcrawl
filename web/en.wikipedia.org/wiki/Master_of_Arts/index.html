Master of Arts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Master of Arts (Latin: Magister Artium, abbreviated MA) is the holder of a master's degree awarded by universities in many countries. The degree is usually contrasted with the Master of Science (MSc): it is generally granted for studies in the humanities, social sciences and fine arts, though the boundary varies by institution and some science subjects lead to an MA at particular universities.

History

The title descends from the medieval universities, where the licence to teach in the faculty of arts — the licentia docendi — was conferred on those who had completed the trivium and quadrivium and subsequent study. In the hierarchy of medieval faculties, arts was the foundational faculty below theology, law and medicine, and the Master of Arts was its senior degree, marking the holder as a master qualified to teach.

Modern usage

In most countries today an MA is a postgraduate degree taken after a bachelor's degree, typically over one or two years of coursework, often with a dissertation or thesis. Programmes exist for nearly every arts, humanities and social-science subject, and professional variants (such as the Master of Fine Arts or Master of Arts in Teaching) are common. Admission normally requires an undergraduate degree in a related field, and the MA may be a terminal qualification or a step towards a doctorate.

Variations

At the universities of Oxford, Cambridge and Dublin, the MA is an honorary status conferred on graduates some years after the BA, without further study (see Master of Arts (Oxford, Cambridge, and Dublin)). In Scotland the ancient universities award the MA as a first degree in the humanities and social sciences, equivalent to a bachelor's degree elsewhere. In continental Europe the Bologna Process aligned many traditional long degrees with the master's level, and the MA designation is widely used in the Netherlands, Germany and elsewhere.
