Zillow

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zillow Group, Inc., or simply Zillow, is an American tech real-estate marketplace company that was founded in 2006 by Rich Barton and Lloyd Frink, former Microsoft executives and founders of the travel company Expedia. Zillow's website provides a database of homes in the United States with estimated market values, known as "Zestimates," along with listings of homes for sale and rent, mortgage services, and agent-finding tools.

History

The company launched its website in 2006, drawing large audiences with its automated home-value estimates. Zillow went public on the NASDAQ in 2011. It grew through acquisitions, most notably purchasing rival Trulia in 2015 for about $2.5 billion in stock. In 2018 the company entered the home-buying business with Zillow Offers, an iBuying program; the program was wound down in 2021 after incurring large losses, and the company refocused on its core marketplace, rentals, and mortgage offerings.

Services

Zillow's Zestimate combines public records, user-submitted data, and statistical modeling to estimate home values. The site also offers Premier Agent advertising for real-estate agents, a rentals marketplace, and Zillow Home Loans. The accuracy of Zestimates has been a recurring subject of public debate and litigation.
