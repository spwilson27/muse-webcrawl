The River Esk in Lothian, Scotland, is formed by the confluence of the North Esk and South Esk rivers near Dalkeith in Midlothian. From there it flows north-east for a short distance through Musselburgh in East Lothian to enter the Firth of Forth. It is one of several Scottish rivers named Esk, a name deriving from a Brittonic word for water.

Tributaries

The North Esk rises in the Pentland Hills near the Bavelaw reservoirs west of Penicuik and flows through Penicuik and Roslin Glen — a wooded gorge beneath Rosslyn Castle — before reaching Dalkeith. The South Esk rises in the Moorfoot Hills, flows through Temple and Gorebridge, and passes the grounds of Dalkeith Palace before joining the North Esk.

History

The Esk at Musselburgh is crossed by the Old Bridge, a three-arched stone bridge of medieval origin rebuilt in the 16th century and traditionally called the "Roman Bridge" after an earlier structure on the Roman road south from Inveresk, where a Roman fort stood. In September 1547 the banks of the Esk near Musselburgh saw the Battle of Pinkie Cleugh, the last pitched battle between England and Scotland before the Union of the Crowns, fought during the "Rough Wooing".

The river once powered numerous mills in the Dalkeith and Musselburgh area, including paper and corn mills. Musselburgh's harbour at Fisherrow lies beside the river mouth, and the estuary mudflats form part of an internationally important site for wading birds.

Wildlife

The Esk supports brown trout and a run of sea trout, and its clean upper reaches hold dippers and grey wagtails. The North Esk valley in Roslin Glen is protected as part of a country park and nature reserve.
