Four Weddings and a Funeral

Four Weddings and a Funeral is a 1994 British romantic comedy film directed by Mike Newell and written by Richard Curtis. Starring Hugh Grant and Andie MacDowell, it follows a group of friends through five social occasions — four weddings and a funeral — as the charming but commitment-shy Charles repeatedly encounters an American guest, Carrie.

Plot

Charles (Grant), a serial wedding guest perpetually late and awkward with speeches, meets Carrie (MacDowell) at the first wedding and spends the night with her, but she returns to America. Across successive weddings — including his friend Angus's, Bernard and Lydia's, and eventually Carrie's own to the older Hamish — Charles's circle (Scarlett, Matthew, Gareth, Fiona, Tom, and his brother David) navigates love and loss. After Gareth's sudden death — the funeral of the title — Charles reconsiders; at his own wedding to another woman, Carrie's reappearance leads him to call it off at the altar, and the film ends with the pair choosing a life together without marriage.

Reception and legacy

Made for under £3 million, the film grossed over $245 million worldwide, became the highest-grossing British film of its time, and launched Hugh Grant's international career. It was nominated for Academy Awards for Best Picture and Best Original Screenplay, won the BAFTA Award for Best Film, and revived the British romantic comedy genre, paving the way for Notting Hill and Curtis's later films. W. H. Auden's poem "Funeral Blues" ("Stop all the clocks") surged in popularity after its reading in the funeral scene.
