Pokhran-I

Pokhran-I
Country: India
Date: 18 May 1974
Location: Pokhran, Rajasthan
Code name: Smiling Buddha

Pokhran-I, codenamed Smiling Buddha, was India's first successful nuclear device test, conducted on 18 May 1974 at the Pokhran Test Range in Rajasthan. India described the detonation as a "peaceful nuclear explosion" and became, at the time, the sixth country to demonstrate a nuclear explosive capability (after the United States, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, France, and China).

The device

The device was an implosion-type fission weapon using plutonium produced by the CIRUS research reactor at the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC) in Trombay, supplied by Canada under peaceful-use understandings; heavy water for the reactor came from the United States. The project, authorised by Prime Minister Indira Gandhi in 1972, was led by scientists including Raja Ramanna, P. K. Iyengar, and Rajagopala Chidambaram, with the device assembled under conditions of great secrecy. India reported a yield of about 12 kilotons; outside estimates have generally ranged from roughly 2 to 20 kilotons.

International reaction

The test drew strong international criticism, particularly from Canada, which suspended nuclear cooperation with India, arguing that the plutonium had been produced in violation of the spirit of their agreements. The United States responded with legislation tightening controls on nuclear exports and, in 1975, the major suppliers formed the Nuclear Suppliers Group to prevent similar transfers. India maintained that the explosion was for peaceful purposes and declined to sign the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty, which it regarded as discriminatory.

Aftermath

Although India did not immediately weaponise the capability or conduct further tests for over two decades, Pokhran-I established the technical basis of its nuclear programme. India returned to Pokhran in May 1998 for Pokhran-II (Operation Shakti), a series of five tests that led it to declare itself a nuclear-weapon state.
