The 1st Maryland Regiment was an infantry regiment of the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War. Raised in 1776 as part of the Maryland Line, it was commanded for much of the war by officers including William Smallwood and Mordecai Gist, and earned a reputation as one of the most reliable units in the American army.

Battle of Long Island

The regiment is best remembered for its stand at the Battle of Long Island on 27 August 1776. A force of about 400 Maryland soldiers — remembered as the "Maryland 400" — repeatedly charged British positions near the Old Stone House to cover the retreat of George Washington's army to Brooklyn Heights. Most of the Marylanders were killed or captured, but their sacrifice allowed the bulk of the army to escape. Maryland's nickname, the "Old Line State", is traditionally attributed to Washington's praise of the Maryland Line.

Later service

Rebuilt after Long Island, the regiment later served in the southern campaigns, fighting at Camden, Cowpens, Guilford Court House, and in the Siege of Yorktown, before being disbanded in 1783.
