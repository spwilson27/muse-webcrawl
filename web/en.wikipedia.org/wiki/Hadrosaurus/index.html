Hadrosaurus is a genus of hadrosaurid ornithopod dinosaur that lived in what is now North America during the Late Cretaceous period, about 80 to 78 million years ago. Its fossil remains were found in 1858 in the Woodbury Formation of New Jersey — the first dinosaur species known from more than isolated teeth to be identified in North America.

Discovery

The bones were found in a marl pit in Haddonfield, New Jersey, by William Parker Foulke, and described by paleontologist Joseph Leidy, who named the species Hadrosaurus foulkii. In 1868 the skeleton, mounted in a lifelike pose at the Academy of Natural Sciences in Philadelphia, became the first mounted dinosaur skeleton displayed anywhere in the world.

Description

Hadrosaurus was a large herbivore, roughly 7–8 metres long, able to walk both bipedally and quadrupedally. Like other hadrosaurids it had a toothless beak and rows of grinding teeth suited to processing plant material. It is the state fossil of New Jersey.
