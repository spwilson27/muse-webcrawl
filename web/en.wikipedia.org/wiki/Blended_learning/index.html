Blended learning

Blended learning is an approach to education that combines online educational materials and opportunities for interaction online with physical place-based classroom methods. It generally requires the physical presence of both teacher and student, with some elements of student control over time, place, path or pace.

Blended learning
Also calledHybrid learning, mixed-mode learning
ComponentsFace-to-face instruction + computer-mediated activities
Common modelsRotation, flex, à la carte, enriched virtual; flipped classroom

Models

The Clayton Christensen Institute identified several widely used models. In rotation models students move on a fixed schedule between learning modalities, one of which is online; the flipped classroom is a rotation variant in which content is studied online at home and class time is used for active work. In flex models online learning is the backbone and teachers provide face-to-face support as needed. À la carte models let students take some courses entirely online alongside face-to-face classes, and enriched virtual models combine occasional required face-to-face sessions with mostly online coursework.

History

Technology-based training emerged in the 1960s with mainframe and television-based instruction, and the term "blended learning" came into common use in the late 1990s as web-based training spread. Adoption accelerated with learning management systems and, dramatically, during the COVID-19 pandemic, when schools and universities combined remote and in-person teaching at scale.

Advantages and criticism

Advocates cite flexibility, opportunities for personalised pacing, richer data on student progress, and cost savings. Critics note dependence on students' access to devices and connectivity, the risk of reduced social interaction, and that simply adding technology to unchanged teaching yields little benefit. Research reviews generally find blended approaches perform at least as well as, and often modestly better than, purely face-to-face or purely online instruction, though results depend heavily on design quality.
