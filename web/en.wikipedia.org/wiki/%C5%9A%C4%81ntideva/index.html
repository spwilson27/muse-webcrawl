Śāntideva

Śāntideva (Sanskrit: शान्तिदेव; c. 685 – c. 763 CE) was an 8th-century Indian Buddhist monk and scholar of the Madhyamaka tradition, associated with the monastic university of Nālandā.

Works

He is best known as the author of the Bodhicaryāvatāra ("A Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life"), a long poem describing the path of a bodhisattva from the awakening mind (bodhicitta) to Buddhahood, which remains one of the most studied texts in Mahāyāna Buddhism. He also wrote the Śikṣāsamuccaya, a compendium of Mahāyāna teachings.

Legend

Tibetan histories depict Śāntideva as a secretly accomplished yogi who appeared outwardly idle—known as "the one who eats, sleeps and defecates"—and recount that when challenged to recite scripture before the Nālandā assembly, he delivered the Bodhicaryāvatāra from memory and ascended into the sky upon reaching its ninth chapter.
