Duke of Edinburgh's Award

The Duke of Edinburgh's Award (commonly abbreviated to DofE) is a youth awards programme founded in the United Kingdom in 1956 by Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, in conjunction with the German educationalist Kurt Hahn. It has since expanded to more than 140 countries and territories, where it operates as The Duke of Edinburgh's International Award, and is aimed at young people aged 14 to 24.

The programme

The award has three progressive levels—Bronze, Silver and Gold. Participants complete activities in several sections: Volunteering, Physical, Skills, and an Expedition; at Gold level there is an additional Residential section. Activities must be sustained over a set period, encouraging persistence, self-reliance and service to the community. Gold awards in the United Kingdom have traditionally been presented at royal palaces.

History

Prince Philip was inspired by Hahn's Moray Badge scheme at Gordonstoun School, where Philip himself had been educated. The award was launched for boys in 1956 and extended to girls in 1958. Following the Duke's death in 2021, his youngest son Prince Edward, Duke of Edinburgh, became patron of the award.

International operation

National award authorities license the programme abroad, where it is run by schools, youth groups and uniformed organizations. Millions of young people have participated since its founding, and completion is widely recognized by universities and employers as evidence of commitment and teamwork.
