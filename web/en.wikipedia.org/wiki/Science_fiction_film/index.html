Science fiction (or sci-fi) is a film genre that uses speculative, fictional science-based depictions of phenomena that are not fully accepted by mainstream science, such as extraterrestrial lifeforms, spacecraft, robots, cyborgs, time travel and other technologies. Science fiction films have often been used to focus on political or social issues and to explore philosophical questions such as what it means to be human.

History

The genre is as old as cinema itself: Georges Méliès's A Trip to the Moon (1902) took audiences to the Moon, and Fritz Lang's Metropolis (1927) created the template for the cinematic future city. In the 1950s, films such as The Day the Earth Stood Still and Invasion of the Body Snatchers channelled Cold War anxieties, while in Japan Godzilla (1954) confronted the nuclear age. Stanley Kubrick's 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) raised the genre's artistic prestige, and Star Wars (1977) and Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977) made it the commercial engine of Hollywood.

Ridley Scott's Alien (1979) — written by Dan O'Bannon and starring Sigourney Weaver as Ellen Ripley — fused science fiction with horror, while Blade Runner (1982) defined cyberpunk aesthetics drawn from krautrock-era electronic culture and techno futures. Later landmarks include The Terminator, Back to the Future, The Matrix and the works of David Fincher's contemporaries such as Christopher Nolan and Denis Villeneuve.
