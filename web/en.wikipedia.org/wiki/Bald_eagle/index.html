Bald eagle

The bald eagle (Haliaeetus leucocephalus) is a bird of prey found in North America. A sea eagle, it has two known subspecies and forms a species pair with the white-tailed eagle of Eurasia. Its range includes most of Canada and Alaska, all of the contiguous United States, and northern Mexico. It is found near large bodies of open water with abundant food supply and old-growth trees for nesting. The bald eagle is the national bird of the United States and appears on its Great Seal.

Description

Despite its name, the bald eagle is not truly bald; the word derives from an older sense of "bald" meaning white-headed. Adults are unmistakable: dark brown body and wings contrasting with a pure white head and tail, and a bright yellow bill, eyes, and feet. Females are about 25 percent larger than males. Wingspan ranges from 1.8 to 2.3 metres and body mass from 3 to 6.3 kg. Juveniles are mostly brown and acquire the adult plumage in their fourth or fifth year. Bald eagles can live more than 30 years in the wild.

Behaviour and diet

The bald eagle is an opportunistic feeder, subsisting mainly on fish, which it snatches from the water with its talons, but also taking waterfowl, small mammals, carrion, and prey stolen from other birds (kleptoparasitism, famously criticised by Benjamin Franklin, who considered the bird of "bad moral character"). Eagles build enormous stick nests, called eyries, in tall trees near water; the largest recorded nest, in Florida, measured 2.9 m across and 6 m deep. Pairs mate for life and lay one to three eggs per year.

Conservation

Once abundant, the bald eagle was driven toward extinction in the contiguous United States by habitat loss, hunting, and the eggshell-thinning effects of the pesticide DDT. Protected from 1940 and listed under the Endangered Species Act, the population recovered dramatically after DDT was banned in 1972; the species was removed from the endangered list in 2007. It is now classed as Least Concern, with hundreds of thousands of individuals including tens of thousands of nesting pairs.

Cultural significance

Chosen for the Great Seal in 1782, the bald eagle symbolises the United States in seals, currency, and federal iconography; it was formally designated the national bird by law in 2024. It is also sacred in many Native American cultures, whose members may legally possess eagle feathers for religious use under federal permit.
