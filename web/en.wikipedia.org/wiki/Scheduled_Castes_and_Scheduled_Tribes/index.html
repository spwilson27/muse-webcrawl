The Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs) are officially recognised groups of historically disadvantaged communities in India, listed ("scheduled") in orders issued under Articles 341 and 342 of the Constitution of India. The Scheduled Castes largely comprise communities formerly regarded as "untouchable" within the Hindu caste order (many of whose members prefer the name Dalit); the Scheduled Tribes comprise indigenous and tribal communities, often called Adivasi.

Constitutional provisions

The Constitution provides for the protection and advancement of these groups through reservations of seats in the Lok Sabha and state legislatures, quotas in government employment and public education (15% for SCs and 7.5% for STs at the central level), and protective legislation such as the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989. Untouchability is abolished by Article 17.

Identification

The lists of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes are state-specific: a community may be scheduled in one state but not another, and Scheduled Caste status was originally restricted to Hindus, later extended to Sikhs and Buddhists. Two national commissions — the National Commission for Scheduled Castes and the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes — monitor safeguards.

History

The categories descend from the British colonial Government of India Act 1935, which first drew up schedules of "depressed classes" and tribal groups for electoral purposes. After independence the framers of the Constitution retained and expanded the framework as a tool of affirmative action against deep social and economic inequality.
