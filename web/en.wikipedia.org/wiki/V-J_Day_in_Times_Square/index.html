V-J Day in Times Square

V-J Day in Times Square is a photograph by Alfred Eisenstaedt that portrays an American sailor, George Mendonsa, grabbing and kissing a stranger—a dental assistant named Greta Zimmer Friedman—on Victory over Japan Day ("V-J Day") in New York City's Times Square on August 14, 1945. The photograph was published a week later in Life magazine and became one of the most famous images of the 20th century.

Background

The picture was taken on the day news broke of Japan's surrender, ending World War II. Eisenstaedt, walking through the celebrations in Times Square, saw the sailor grabbing women and kissing them, and captured four frames as Mendonsa embraced Friedman, who was wearing her dental assistant's uniform and was long misidentified as a nurse.

Identities and legacy

The identities of the couple were debated for decades, with several people claiming to be the subjects; photographic and forensic analyses eventually supported Mendonsa and Friedman. The kiss has been reenacted, commemorated by statues, and discussed in later years in light of changing views about consent, since Friedman said the kiss was not consensual but that she did not hold it against Mendonsa.
