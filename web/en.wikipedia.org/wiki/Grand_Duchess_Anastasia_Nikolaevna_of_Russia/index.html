Grand Duchess Anastasia Nikolaevna of Russia (18 June 1901 – 17 July 1918) was the youngest daughter of Tsar Nicholas II, the last sovereign of Imperial Russia, and his wife, Tsarina Alexandra Feodorovna.

Life

Anastasia was the younger sister of Grand Duchesses Olga, Tatiana and Maria, and the older sister of Tsarevich Alexei. Known within the family as "Nastya" or "Shvybzik" (roughly "little imp"), she was lively, mischievous and close to her brother, with whom she shared lessons. During the First World War she visited wounded soldiers in hospital with her sisters.

Death

After Nicholas II abdicated in the February Revolution of 1917, the family was held under house arrest at Tsarskoye Selo and then exiled to Tobolsk and finally to Yekaterinburg. On the night of 16–17 July 1918 Anastasia was murdered with her parents, siblings and several retainers by Bolshevik guards in the basement of the Ipatiev House.

Survival claims and identification

For decades, rumours that Anastasia had survived generated numerous impostors, most famously Anna Anderson, whose claim inspired books, plays and the 1956 film Anastasia. DNA testing in the 1990s disproved Anderson's claim, and the discovery in 2007 of the remaining two bodies near Yekaterinburg, confirmed by DNA analysis in 2009, established that no member of the imperial family survived. The Russian Orthodox Church canonised Anastasia and her family as passion bearers in 2000.
