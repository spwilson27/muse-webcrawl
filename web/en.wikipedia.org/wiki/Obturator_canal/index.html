Obturator canal

The obturator canal is a small passageway at the upper part of the obturator foramen of the pelvis. The obturator membrane, which closes most of the foramen, leaves this gap through which structures pass between the pelvic cavity and the medial compartment of the thigh.

Contents

From lateral to medial and above downwards, the canal transmits the obturator nerve, the obturator artery, and the obturator vein out of the pelvis. The nerve divides around the adductor brevis muscle into anterior and posterior divisions supplying the medial thigh muscles and a small area of skin.

Clinical significance

The canal is the site of the rare obturator hernia, in which abdominal contents protrude through the opening; it occurs most often in elderly, thin women and can cause bowel obstruction and pain radiating down the inner thigh (the Howship–Romberg sign), due to compression of the obturator nerve.
