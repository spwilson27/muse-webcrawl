Thyroglossal duct cyst

A thyroglossal duct cyst is a fibrous cyst that forms from a persistent thyroglossal duct, the embryonic structure along which the thyroid gland descends from the base of the tongue to its position in the neck. It is the most common congenital neck mass, present in about 7% of the population, though many never cause symptoms.

Presentation

The cyst typically appears as a painless, midline swelling in the neck, often below the hyoid bone, that moves upward on swallowing or protrusion of the tongue — a sign distinguishing it from other neck masses. It may become infected, producing pain and redness, or drain through a fistula.

Treatment

Definitive treatment is surgical excision by the Sistrunk procedure, which removes the cyst, the tract, and the central portion of the hyoid bone, greatly reducing recurrence compared with simple cyst excision. A small percentage of cysts are found to contain ectopic thyroid tissue, and rarely papillary thyroid carcinoma.
