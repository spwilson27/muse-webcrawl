Dental composite

Dental composite resins are tooth-coloured filling materials made of a synthetic resin matrix reinforced with glass or quartz filler particles. Introduced in the 1960s, they have largely replaced amalgam for visible restorations because they bond to tooth structure and match natural enamel.

Properties

Composites are placed in increments and hardened with a curing light. They allow conservative cavity preparation but are more technique-sensitive than amalgam and may wear or discolour over time. Modern nanohybrid formulations offer improved strength and polishability.
