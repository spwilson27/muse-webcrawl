List of Nazi concentration camps

Nazi Germany maintained concentration camps (Konzentrationslager, abbreviated KL or KZ) throughout the territories it controlled before and during the Second World War. The first concentration camps were established in 1933, shortly after Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party came to power, for holding political prisoners. The camp system expanded enormously during the war, and came to include forced-labor camps, transit camps, prisoner-of-war camps, and extermination camps (Vernichtungslager) built specifically for mass murder during the Holocaust. In total, historians have identified more than 1,000 camps and subcamps, alongside thousands of ghettos and other detention sites.

Subcamps and related lists

Most main camps administered networks of subcamps (Außenlager) supplying forced labor to factories, mines, and construction projects. Lists of subcamps exist for Auschwitz, Dachau, Buchenwald, Mauthausen, and many other main camps. Beyond the SS concentration camp system, the regime also ran Gestapo "work education camps," ghettos, and prisoner-of-war camps.
