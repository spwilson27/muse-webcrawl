Aero L-39 Albatros

The Aero L-39 Albatros is a high-performance jet trainer aircraft developed in Czechoslovakia by Aero Vodochody. It first flew on 4 November 1968 and became the standard advanced trainer of the Warsaw Pact air forces, succeeding the L-29 Delfín.

Design and service

The L-39 is a low-wing, tandem-seat aircraft powered by a single Ivchenko AI-25 turbofan, giving a maximum speed of about 750 km/h. More than 2,900 were built, including the armed L-39ZA light-attack variant. After the Cold War, hundreds of surplus L-39s entered civil hands, particularly in the United States, where they are popular warbird and air-racing aircraft, and the type serves with the Breitling Jet Team display team.

Development

An extensively modernised derivative, the L-159 ALCA, entered Czech service in the 2000s, and a new-generation version, the L-39NG with a Williams FJ44 engine and modern avionics, was unveiled in the 2010s.
