Protests against the Iraq War

Protests against the Iraq War were held in many cities worldwide beginning in 2002, before the invasion of Iraq in March 2003, and continued during the war. On 15 February 2003, a coordinated day of protests across 600 cities saw between six and ten million people demonstrate — described by Guinness World Records as the largest protest event in human history.

February 15, 2003

The largest single demonstrations took place in Rome (around three million people), London (about one million), Madrid, Barcelona, Berlin, Paris and New York City. In London the march, organised by the Stop the War Coalition, was the largest political demonstration in British history. Protesters argued that the case for war — based on alleged Iraqi weapons of mass destruction and links to al-Qaeda — was unproven and that an invasion would be illegal without a further UN Security Council resolution.

Scope and organisation

The protests were coordinated by networks including United for Peace and Justice in the United States, ANSWER, and international anti-war coalitions. Demonstrations continued after the invasion began, including large marches on 20 March 2003 and annual protests on the war's anniversary.

Impact

The protests did not prevent the invasion, which began on 20 March 2003, and their political effect remains debated. Commentators have noted that the scale of pre-war opposition contributed to the electoral defeat of some pro-war governments, including Spain's in 2004, and to the long-running controversy over the war's legitimacy.
