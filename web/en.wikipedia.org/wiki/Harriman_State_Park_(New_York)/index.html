Harriman State Park is a state park in Rockland and Orange counties, New York, in the United States. Located about 50 kilometres north of New York City, it is the second-largest state park in New York, with an area of 47,527 acres (19,235 hectares).

History

The park was created in 1910 with a gift of 10,000 acres and one million dollars from Mary Williamson Harriman, in memory of her husband, the railroad magnate Edward H. Harriman, conditional on the state abandoning plans to build a prison at Bear Mountain. The park grew rapidly through further donations and purchases under the Palisades Interstate Park Commission.

Features

Harriman contains more than 30 lakes and reservoirs, numerous streams, and over 300 kilometres of hiking trails, including sections of the Appalachian Trail and the Long Path. The Appalachian Trail's first completed section, opened in 1923, runs through the park. Popular destinations include Lake Welch, Lake Tiorati, and Lake Sebago, which offer beaches, picnic areas, and boat launches, as well as many group campsites built during the 20th century.
