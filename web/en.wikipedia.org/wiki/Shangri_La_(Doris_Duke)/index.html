Shangri La (Doris Duke)

Shangri La is the former Honolulu, Hawaii, home of the American philanthropist Doris Duke (1912–1993), now open to the public as the Shangri La Museum of Islamic Art, Culture & Design. Located at Kalauea Point near Diamond Head, it houses one of the finest collections of Islamic art in the United States, assembled by Duke over nearly sixty years.

History

Duke conceived the house during her 1935 honeymoon world tour with James Cromwell, which included an extended stay in India and travels through the Islamic world. Inspired by the Taj Mahal and Mughal and Persian architecture, she commissioned architect Marion Sims Wyeth to design a home that would blend modernist Hawaiian living with Islamic architectural forms. Construction took place from 1936 to 1938; Duke continued to acquire art and commission architectural features — carved plaster, tilework, carved and painted ceilings, and a Mughal-style garden — for the rest of her life.

Collection

The museum holds around 4,500 objects from across the Islamic world, including ceramics, metalwork, textiles, carved wood and glass from Iran, Turkey, Syria, Egypt, Morocco, Spain and Central Asia. Highlights include the Syrian Damascus Room interior, Iznik tiles, and the waterfront Playhouse modelled on a 17th-century Persian pavilion.

Museum

Under Duke's will, the estate is administered by the Doris Duke Foundation for Islamic Art, part of the Doris Duke Charitable Foundation. It opened to the public in 2002 and is accessible by tour through partnership with the Honolulu Museum of Art.
