The New York Review of Books (or NYREV or NYRB) is a semi-monthly magazine with articles on literature, culture, economics, science, and current affairs. Founded in New York City in 1963, it is known for long, essayistic book reviews by leading scholars and writers, and for its distinctive caricature illustrations by David Levine, who contributed drawings for more than four decades.

History

The Review was founded by Robert B. Silvers and Barbara Epstein, together with publisher A. Whitney Ellsworth, during the 1962–63 New York newspaper strike, when book reviewing in the city's papers had halted. Its first issue appeared on February 1, 1963. Silvers and Epstein co-edited the magazine until Epstein's death in 2006; Silvers continued as editor until his death in 2017, followed by Ian Buruma (2017–2018) and Emily Greenhouse (from 2019).

From the beginning the Review combined literary criticism with political engagement, publishing major essays on the Vietnam War, civil rights, and later conflicts. Contributors have included W. H. Auden, Susan Sontag, Joan Didion, Gore Vidal, Mary McCarthy, Hannah Arendt, Oliver Sacks, and many Nobel laureates. Its letters section is famous for extended, sometimes combative exchanges between critics and authors. The publishing house NYRB Classics, founded in 1999 under the same umbrella, reissues neglected works of fiction and nonfiction in translation and in English.
