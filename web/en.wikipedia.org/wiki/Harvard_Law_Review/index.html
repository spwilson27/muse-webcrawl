The Harvard Law Review is a law review published by an independent student group at Harvard Law School. First published in 1887, it is one of the oldest and most influential student-edited law journals in the United States and is among the most widely cited legal periodicals in the world.

History

The Review was founded with support from alumni including Louis Brandeis, and its first issue appeared in April 1887. It pioneered the modern format of the American law review, combining articles by leading scholars with student-written notes and case comments. Its citation manual, developed in the 1920s, became the basis of The Bluebook, the standard legal citation system in the United States.

Editors

Membership on the Review is a prestigious distinction at Harvard Law School, obtained through a competitive writing and editing exercise. Notable former editors include several US Supreme Court justices and presidents; Barack Obama served as the Review's first Black president in 1990–1991.

Contents

Each annual volume traditionally includes a Supreme Court issue reviewing the Court's previous term, along with articles, essays, book reviews, and the annual survey of developments in the law.
