The Vegreville egg is a giant sculpture of a pysanka, a Ukrainian-style Easter egg, located in Vegreville, Alberta, Canada. It is one of the best-known roadside attractions in Alberta and is often described as the largest pysanka in the world.

History

The egg was designed by Paul Maxym Sembaliuk, a Canadian artist of Ukrainian descent, and was commissioned by the town of Vegreville in 1973. It was built to commemorate the centennial of the Royal Canadian Mounted Police in 1974 and to honour the Ukrainian settlers who helped found the region. The sculpture was formally dedicated in 1975.

Design and construction

The egg stands about 9.4 metres (31 ft) tall and roughly 7.8 metres (25.5 ft) wide, and weighs around 2.5 tonnes. Its shell is made of aluminium and was assembled from more than 3,500 individual pieces. The geometric pattern of the traditional pysanka was translated into a mathematical model, making the sculpture an early and notable example of computer-aided design and fabrication: the surface was worked out using computer modelling at the University of Alberta.

Significance

The patterns on the egg carry traditional Ukrainian symbolism, including motifs representing life, good fortune and eternity. The Vegreville egg has become a symbol of the town and of Ukrainian heritage in east-central Alberta, and it appears on postcards and in tourism promotions across the province.
