Logroño
Country: Spain
Autonomous community: La Rioja
Population: approx. 150,000
Elevation: 384 m

Logroño (Spanish: [loˈɣɾoɲo]) is the capital city of the province of La Rioja and autonomous region of La Rioja in northern Spain, situated on the River Ebro. Its population in the early 2020s was around 150,000. The city is a centre of trade of Rioja wine, for which the area is noted, and manufacturing of wood, metal and textile products.

History

Logroño was an old settlement, first of the Romans, under the name of Vareia, a commercial port, and then of the Celts. From the 10th century, possession of Logroño was disputed between the kings of Navarre and those of Castile; the region was finally annexed to Castile in 1173. Alfonso VI of Castile granted it a charter of privileges (the Fuero de Logroño) in 1095, which served as a model for other Spanish towns.

In 1609–1610 Logroño was the site of the Basque witch trials, one of the largest witch trials in European history, conducted by the Spanish Inquisition.

The Camino de Santiago (Way of St James) passes through the city, entering across the Puente de Piedra over the Ebro.

Geography and climate

The city lies on the northern bank of the Ebro, at an elevation of about 384 m (1,260 ft), in the heart of the Rioja wine region. Logroño has a climate transitional between Mediterranean and oceanic influences, with hot, dry summers and cool winters.

Culture

The historic quarter contains the Co-cathedral of Santa María de la Redonda, the churches of San Bartolomé and Santa María de Palacio, and the Revellín walls. The Calle Laurel area is famous for its tapas bars, each specialising in a single dish. The city's main festival is San Bernabé (11 June), commemorating the 1521 victory over French besiegers, and the San Mateo wine harvest festival in September.
