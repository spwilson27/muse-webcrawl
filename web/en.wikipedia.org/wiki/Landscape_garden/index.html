Landscape garden

A landscape garden, also called a landscaped park or an English garden, is a style of parkland garden that emerged in England in the early eighteenth century and spread across Europe, replacing the more formal, symmetrical French baroque garden. It presents an idealised natural landscape, with rolling lawns, serpentine lakes, groves of trees and classical or Gothic ornaments.

Development

Pioneers included Charles Bridgeman and William Kent at estates such as Stowe and Rousham, followed by Lancelot "Capability" Brown, who remodelled more than 170 parks with smooth lawns, clumps of trees, ha-has and artificial lakes. Humphry Repton later softened Brown's style. Writers such as William Gilpin linked the style to the aesthetic of the picturesque.

Features

Typical features include a serpentine body of water, belts and clumps of trees, a ha-ha separating garden from pasture, and eye-catching structures — temples, grottoes, follies and mock ruins — placed to be seen from composed viewpoints. On the continent the style became known as the jardin anglais or jardin à l'anglaise.
