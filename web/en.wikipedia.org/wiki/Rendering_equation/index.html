Rendering equation

The rendering equation is an integral equation in computer graphics that describes the equilibrium distribution of light in a scene. Introduced by David Immel and colleagues and by James Kajiya in 1986, it is the central theoretical result of physically based rendering: the outgoing light from a point in a given direction equals the light emitted from that point plus the light reflected from all incoming directions, weighted by the surface's bidirectional reflectance distribution function (BRDF) and the cosine of the incidence angle.

Formulation

In one common form: Lo(x, ωo) = Le(x, ωo) + ∫Ω fr(x, ωi, ωo) Li(x, ωi) (ωi · n) dωi, where the integral is over the hemisphere of incoming directions at surface point x.

Solving the equation

The equation cannot be solved analytically for general scenes, because the incoming light at a point depends on the outgoing light of every other point. Numerical techniques include Monte Carlo path tracing, radiosity (which discretises surfaces into patches), photon mapping and Metropolis light transport. A render farm may be used to evaluate these methods over many frames.
