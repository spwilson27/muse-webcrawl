German mark

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Banknotes and coins of the Deutsche Mark.

The German mark (German: Deutsche Mark, abbreviated DM or D-Mark) was the official currency of West Germany from 1948 until 1990, and of the reunified Federal Republic of Germany from 1990 until it was replaced by the euro in 1999 (with cash changing over in 2002). It was subdivided into 100 Pfennige and issued by the Deutsche Bundesbank.

History

The Deutsche Mark was introduced on 20 June 1948 as part of the currency reform in the western occupation zones of Germany, replacing the Reichsmark. The reform wiped out most monetary savings but stabilized the economy and is credited with helping launch the West German economic miracle (Wirtschaftswunder). The Soviet zone responded with its own currency, the East German mark.

Following German reunification on 3 October 1990, the Deutsche Mark became the sole legal tender of the united Germany. The conversion of East German marks took place at rates ranging from 1:1 to 2:1 depending on the type and amount of the balance.

The euro changeover

Germany adopted the euro as its accounting currency on 1 January 1999 at the irrevocable conversion rate of 1 euro = 1.95583 DM. Euro banknotes and coins entered circulation on 1 January 2002, and the Mark ceased to be legal tender shortly afterwards. Uniquely among euro-area countries, the Bundesbank continues to exchange Deutsche Mark cash for euros at the fixed rate without any time limit.

Coins and banknotes

Coins were issued in denominations of 1, 2, 5, 10, and 50 Pfennig and 1, 2, and 5 Mark. Banknotes circulated in denominations from 5 to 1,000 Mark; the final series, introduced from 1989, depicted notable figures from German culture and science, including Clara Schumann, Paul Ehrlich, and the Brothers Grimm.

Reputation

The Deutsche Mark was one of the world's most stable and respected currencies, often serving as a reserve currency second only to the United States dollar. Its strength symbolized postwar German economic recovery, and its surrender in favour of the euro was a major political step in European integration.
