Thermosphere

The thermosphere is the layer of Earth's atmosphere directly above the mesosphere and below the exosphere. It extends from about 80–90 km (the mesopause) up to 500–1,000 km, with its upper boundary, the thermopause, varying with solar activity.

Properties

Within the thermosphere, temperature rises steeply with altitude because the sparse gas strongly absorbs high-energy ultraviolet and X-ray radiation from the Sun; temperatures can reach 500 °C to 2,000 °C or more, though the gas is so thin that a conventional thermometer would register very cold temperatures. The ionosphere lies largely within the thermosphere, and auroras occur there. The Kármán line, at 100 km, conventionally marks the boundary of space.

Human activity

The International Space Station orbits in the thermosphere at about 400 km altitude, as do many satellites; atmospheric drag there is small but still gradually decays orbits and is a key factor in tracking space debris.
