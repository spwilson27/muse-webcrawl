NIST Cybersecurity Framework

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) is a set of voluntary guidelines, standards and best practices published by the US National Institute of Standards and Technology to help organisations manage and reduce cybersecurity risk. First released in February 2014 in response to Executive Order 13636 on improving critical-infrastructure cybersecurity, it has been widely adopted by governments and private industry worldwide.

Structure

The framework is built from three components: the Core, a catalogue of activities, outcomes and references organised into Functions, Categories and Subcategories; Organizational Profiles, which describe an organisation's current and target cybersecurity posture; and (in version 1.x) Implementation Tiers describing the rigour of an organisation's risk-management practices.

Functions

Version 1.1 defined five concurrent functions: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover. Version 2.0, released in 2024, added a sixth function, Govern, emphasising organisational accountability, roles, and supply-chain risk management, and broadened the framework's intended audience beyond critical infrastructure to organisations of every size and sector.

Use and influence

Because it is expressed in outcomes rather than mandated controls, the CSF is commonly used as a translation layer between technical security teams and executives, and as a structure for self-assessment, audits and regulatory compliance. It complements — and cross-references — standards such as NIST SP 800-53 and ISO/IEC 27001. US federal policy has directed agencies to use the framework, and variants of it underpin cybersecurity regulations in several countries.
