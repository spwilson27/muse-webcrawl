Le Bon Marché

Le Bon Marché
Location: 7th arrondissement, Paris
Founded: 1838 (as Au Bon Marché)
Owner: LVMH

Le Bon Marché is a department store in Paris, widely considered the first modern department store in the world. Founded in 1838 and transformed after 1852 by Aristide Boucicaut, it pioneered fixed marked prices, free entry and browsing, seasonal sales, and mail-order catalogues.

Le Bon Marché, Rue de Sèvres, Paris.

History

Boucicaut's innovations turned a modest drapery shop into a vast emporium on the Left Bank, housed in buildings designed with the participation of Gustave Eiffel's engineering firm. The store famously inspired Émile Zola's 1883 novel Au Bonheur des Dames ("The Ladies' Paradise"). Since the 1980s it has belonged to the LVMH luxury group and operates as an upscale store, together with its food hall, La Grande Épicerie de Paris.
