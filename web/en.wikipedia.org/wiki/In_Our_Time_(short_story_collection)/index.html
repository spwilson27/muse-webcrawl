In Our Time is a short story collection by American writer Ernest Hemingway, published in 1925 as his first American book. An earlier, smaller Paris edition (in our time, 1924) had printed the vignettes alone. The collection established Hemingway's reputation and showcased his lean "iceberg" prose style.

Contents and structure

Fifteen stories alternate with short interchapters — stark vignettes of war, bullfighting, executions and violence drawn from Hemingway's journalism and his experience in the First World War and the Greco-Turkish War. Many stories feature the semi-autobiographical Nick Adams, following him from boyhood in Michigan ("Indian Camp", "The Doctor and the Doctor's Wife") through wounding at the front to the solitary fishing trip of "Big Two-Hearted River", the volume's closing story, widely read as a portrait of a traumatised veteran restoring himself in nature.

Reception

The collection was praised by critics and writers such as F. Scott Fitzgerald and Edmund Wilson for its economy and intensity, and it is now considered a landmark of modernist fiction and a foundation of Hemingway's later work, including The Sun Also Rises (1926).
