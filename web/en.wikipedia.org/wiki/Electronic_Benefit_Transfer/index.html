Electronic Benefit Transfer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Electronic Benefit Transfer (EBT) is an electronic system that allows state welfare departments in the United States to issue benefits via a magnetically encoded payment card used in the manner of a debit card. It replaced the paper food stamp coupons formerly used by the federal food assistance program.

Through EBT, recipients of programs such as the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) and Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) receive a plastic card, similar in appearance to a bank debit card, which is swiped at the point of sale. Benefits are deposited into an account linked to the card, and purchases are deducted electronically.

History

Pilot EBT systems began in the 1980s, with an early demonstration in Reading, Pennsylvania, in 1984. Congress moved toward nationwide adoption in the 1990s, and the 1996 welfare reform law required all states to implement EBT for food stamps by 2002. When the Food Stamp Program was renamed SNAP in 2008, paper coupons had essentially disappeared from the system.

Use

SNAP benefits on an EBT card may be used only for eligible food items at authorized retailers, while cash benefits from TANF and related state programs can be withdrawn at ATMs or used for purchases. Benefits are typically loaded onto cards on a staggered schedule during each month. The system is administered by state agencies, usually through contracts with private processing companies.
