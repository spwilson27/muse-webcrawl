Brigadier

Brigadier is a military rank used in many countries, senior to a colonel and subordinate to a major general. In armies following the British model, a brigadier commands a brigade and is classified as a one-star rank (NATO code OF-6), although in the British Army the rank is technically an appointment-grade for senior field officers rather than the lowest grade of general officer, a distinction reflected in the separate rank of brigadier general used in the United States and other armies.

History

The title derives from the French brigadier des armées du roi, introduced in the 17th century for officers commanding brigades. The British Army used "brigadier-general" from 1685 until 1922, when it was replaced by "colonel-commandant" and then, in 1928, by "brigadier." The Royal Marines use the rank, corresponding to a Royal Navy commodore and an RAF air commodore.

Use around the world

Armies including those of the United Kingdom, India, Pakistan, Australia, and Sri Lanka use brigadier as a one-star rank. In India and Pakistan the rank insignia include the national emblem over three stars. Some countries' police forces, including India's, also use brigadier-equivalent grades. The Argentine gendarmerie and several other services employ cognate titles.
