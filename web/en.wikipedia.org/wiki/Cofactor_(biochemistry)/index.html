Cofactor (biochemistry)

[Image placeholder: An enzyme holoenzyme with its cofactor]

A cofactor is a non-protein chemical compound or metallic ion that is required for an enzyme's role as a catalyst. Cofactors can be considered "helper molecules" that assist in biochemical transformations. An inactive enzyme without its cofactor is called an apoenzyme, while the complete, active enzyme is a holoenzyme.

Classification

Cofactors divide into two groups: inorganic ions (such as Mg2+, Zn2+, Fe2+/3+ and Mn2+) and organic compounds called coenzymes. Tightly and permanently bound organic cofactors are termed prosthetic groups (for example haem in cytochromes), while loosely bound coenzymes, such as NAD+ and coenzyme A, act as carriers of electrons or chemical groups.

Vitamins as precursors

Many coenzymes are derived from vitamins: NAD+ from niacin, FAD from riboflavin, coenzyme A from pantothenic acid, and thiamine pyrophosphate from vitamin B1. Deficiency of these vitamins impairs the enzymes that depend on them.
