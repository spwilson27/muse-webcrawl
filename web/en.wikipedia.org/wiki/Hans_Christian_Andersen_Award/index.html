Hans Christian Andersen Award

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hans Christian Andersen Award is the highest international recognition given to creators of children's books. It is awarded every two years by the International Board on Books for Young People (IBBY), and is often called the "Nobel Prize of children's literature." The award is named for the Danish author Hans Christian Andersen, whose fairy tales such as "The Little Mermaid" and "The Ugly Duckling" are read worldwide.

History

The award was founded in 1956 by IBBY, largely through the efforts of Jella Lepman, who established the organization to promote international understanding through children's books. Initially it recognized a single author; a separate award for illustrators was added in 1966. The Queen of Denmark has traditionally been patron of the award.

Selection and ceremony

National sections of IBBY nominate candidates, and an international jury selects the winners, considering the complete body of work of each nominee and its lasting contribution to children's literature. Winners receive a gold medal and a diploma at a ceremony held during the IBBY World Congress. Separate awards are given to an author and to an illustrator.

Notable recipients

Author winners have included Astrid Lindgren, Erich Kästner, Tove Jansson, Maurice Sendak, Katherine Paterson, and Jacqueline Woodson. Illustrator winners include Maurice Sendak (who won in that category before winning as an author), Quentin Blake, and Shaun Tan.
