A samarium–cobalt (SmCo) magnet is a type of rare-earth permanent magnet made of an alloy of the lanthanide samarium and the transition metal cobalt. Developed in the early 1970s, SmCo magnets were the strongest permanent magnets available before the introduction of neodymium magnets.

Types and properties

Two families are produced: SmCo5 and the higher-energy Sm2Co17, the latter alloyed with iron, copper and zirconium. Samarium–cobalt magnets offer excellent temperature stability, retaining their strength at temperatures up to about 300 °C, and good corrosion resistance, though they are brittle and expensive because of the cost of samarium and cobalt.

Uses

They are used where performance at high temperature matters: aerospace and military systems, motorsport motors, satellite components, medical devices and high-end electric motors and generators.
