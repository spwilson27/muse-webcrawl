Aniene

The Aniene (Italian: Aniene, pronounced [aˈnjɛːne]; Latin: Anio, formerly also the Teverone) is a 99-kilometre (62 mi) river in Lazio, Italy. It originates in the mountains of the region's eastern Apennines and flows westward past Subiaco and Tivoli before joining the Tiber river in Rome, north of the historic city centre.

Course

The river rises near Filettino in the Simbruini mountains. In its upper valley it passes Subiaco, where Saint Benedict founded his first monasteries and where the Roman emperor Nero built a villa with artificial lakes. At Tivoli the river plunges over the famous waterfalls, the Cascate, in a gorge overlooked by the town's Roman temples; the falls were diverted in the 19th century to protect Tivoli from flooding, creating the Villa Gregoriana park.

History

In Roman times the Anio supplied two major aqueducts to Rome: the Anio Vetus (built 272–269 BC) and the Anio Novus, completed under Claudius in AD 52, one of the longest and highest of the city's aqueducts. The valley was a favoured site for suburban villas, including Hadrian's Villa near Tivoli. The river was also the scene of legendary early Roman battles against the Aequi and Volscians.

Modern use

The Aniene's waters at Tivoli have powered mills and, since the late 19th century, hydroelectric plants—Tivoli supplied some of the first electric lighting to Rome. The valley today is followed by the Via Tiburtina and a regional railway.
