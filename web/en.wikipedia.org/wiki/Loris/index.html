Loris

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lorises are a group of strepsirrhine primates of the subfamily Lorinae, in the family Lorisidae. They comprise the slender lorises of India and Sri Lanka (genus Loris) and the slow lorises of Southeast Asia (genus Nycticebus). Lorises are nocturnal, arboreal animals characterised by large forward-facing eyes, a keen sense of smell, and slow, deliberate movements.

Characteristics

Lorises have a powerful grasp with thumbs that oppose the other fingers, allowing them to cling to branches for long periods. They move slowly and quietly through the canopy, stalking insects and other small prey. Slow lorises are unique among primates in producing a venom: a secretion from a gland on the inner elbow is mixed with saliva and delivered by a bite, which can cause painful wounds — an adaptation used in defence and in competition with other lorises.

Species and distribution

The slender lorises — the red slender loris and the gray slender loris — are found in the forests of southern India and Sri Lanka. The slow lorises range from northeastern India and Bangladesh through mainland and insular Southeast Asia, and include the Sunda slow loris, the Bengal slow loris and the tiny pygmy slow loris. Several species have been recognised only recently as studies of museum specimens and wild populations have progressed.

Conservation

All lorises are threatened by habitat loss and by capture for the pet trade, traditional medicine and the tourist photo-prop trade. Slow lorises are listed in CITES Appendix I, prohibiting international commercial trade, yet illegal trade persists, and most species are classified as Endangered or Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List.
