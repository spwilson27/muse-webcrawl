Kalinago language

Native toDominica (Kalinago Territory)
EthnicityKalinago (Island Carib) people
FamilyArawakan (Maipurean)
StatusExtinct as a first language; revival efforts under way

The Kalinago language, historically called Island Carib, is an Arawakan language formerly spoken by the Kalinago people of the Lesser Antilles, principally on Dominica. Despite the people's name "Carib", the language belongs to the Arawakan family rather than the Cariban family, a consequence of the historical conquest and intermarriage pattern in which Carib-speaking men married Arawak-speaking women; early observers also reported distinct men's and women's speech varieties.

History

French missionaries of the seventeenth century, notably Raymond Breton, compiled dictionaries and grammars of the language. As colonial pressure and language shift to French-based creole and English advanced, fluent first-language speakers declined, and the last fully fluent native speakers died in the twentieth century.

Revival

In the Kalinago Territory of Dominica, community projects, school materials and linguistic documentation based on historical sources support teaching Kalinago as a heritage language. A closely related mainland variety survives in Garifuna, spoken in Central America and itself descended from the language of St Vincent.
