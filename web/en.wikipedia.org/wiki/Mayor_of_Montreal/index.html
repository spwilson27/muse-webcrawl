The mayor of Montreal is the head of the executive branch of the municipal government of Montreal, Quebec, Canada. The mayor represents the city, presides over the Montreal City Council in an executive capacity through the executive committee, and is elected by the citizens of Montreal in municipal elections held every four years. The mayor is also, by virtue of the office, a member of the council of the Montreal agglomeration.

The office was created in 1833, when Montreal was first incorporated as a city. The first mayor was Jacques Viger, a journalist, antiquarian, and militia officer. In the early decades, mayors were chosen by the city council; direct election by property-owning male citizens began in the 1850s, and universal suffrage was extended over the following century.

History

Montreal's first charter of incorporation came in 1832, and the first city council, with Jacques Viger as mayor, took office in 1833. The charter was allowed to lapse in 1836 amid the political turmoil preceding the Lower Canada Rebellion, and the city was governed by appointed justices of the peace until a new charter was granted in 1840.

During the 19th and 20th centuries the mayoralty was dominated by figures who often served multiple terms, including Charles Wilson, the first mayor elected directly by the citizens, and Honoré Beaugrand, founder of the newspaper La Patrie. Jean Drapeau, mayor from 1954 to 1957 and again from 1960 to 1986, transformed the city, overseeing the Montreal Metro, Expo 67, and the 1976 Summer Olympics.

Election and term

Mayoral elections are held every four years on the first Sunday of November, at the same time as elections for city council and borough mayors. Any eligible voter in Montreal may run. The mayor sits as the councillor for the city as a whole and chairs the executive committee, which prepares the city budget and supervises the administration.
