Courtaulds was a British manufacturer of textiles, artificial fibres and chemicals, and one of the great industrial companies of the 20th century. The firm began in 1794 when George Courtauld, of a Huguenot family, established a silk business in Essex; his descendant Samuel Courtauld IV transformed it into a silk and crêpe empire.

Rayon empire

Courtaulds' fortune was made in artificial silk. In 1904 it bought the British rights to the viscose process, and under the leadership of Samuel Courtauld IV built factories at Coventry and elsewhere; by the 1920s and 1930s it dominated world rayon production through British Celanese rivals it later absorbed and its American subsidiary, the American Viscose Corporation. Profits from industry funded Samuel Courtauld's celebrated art collection, which formed the basis of the Courtauld Institute of Art and the Courtauld Gallery in London (opened 1932).

Later history

After the Second World War Courtaulds expanded into chemicals, paints, packaging and clothing brands, becoming one of Britain's largest manufacturing groups and the country's biggest textile producer. It survived a famous takeover battle with ICI in 1961–62. In 1990 the textiles businesses were demerged as Courtaulds Textiles. The remaining international fibres and chemicals company, Courtaulds plc, was acquired by the Dutch group AkzoNobel in 1998. Courtaulds' research legacy included the development of Tencel, the first lyocell fibre.
