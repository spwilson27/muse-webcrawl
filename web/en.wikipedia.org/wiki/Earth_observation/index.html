Earth observation

Earth observation is the gathering of information about the physical, chemical, and biological systems of the planet Earth via remote sensing technologies, usually involving satellite or airborne imagery, supplemented by in-situ measurements. It is used in weather forecasting, climate monitoring, agriculture, forestry, disaster response, and urban planning.

History

Earth observation began with aerial photography from balloons and aircraft, then with the first satellite photographs from TIROS-1 in 1960 for weather monitoring. The Landsat programme, begun in 1972, created the longest continuous record of Earth's land surface and pioneered open data sharing.

Instruments

Modern satellites carry multispectral and hyperspectral imagers, synthetic-aperture radar (which sees through cloud and darkness), altimeters measuring sea level, and sounders profiling atmospheric temperature and composition. Europe's Copernicus programme flies the Sentinel satellite families; NASA and NOAA operate fleets including Terra, Aqua, and the GOES weather satellites.

Uses

Data supports numerical weather prediction, monitoring of deforestation and ice-sheet loss, crop-yield estimation, detection of methane leaks, and rapid mapping after earthquakes and floods. The field increasingly combines satellite data with machine learning and citizen science.
