Formation (stratigraphy)

A geological formation, or simply formation, is a body of rock having a consistent set of physical characteristics (lithology) that distinguish it from adjacent bodies of rock, and which occupies a particular position in the layers of rock exposed in a geographical region (the stratigraphic column). It is the fundamental unit of lithostratigraphy, the study of strata or rock layers.

Definition and naming

A formation must be large enough that it can be mapped at the surface or traced in the subsurface. Formations are formally named according to the North American Stratigraphic Code and the International Stratigraphic Guide: the name combines a geographical locality with the dominant rock type or the word "Formation" itself, as in the Morrison Formation or Kaibab Limestone. Each formation has a designated type section where its characteristics are best displayed.

Hierarchy

Formations may be subdivided into members, and members into beds; conversely, related formations may be combined into groups, and groups into supergroups. The boundaries of a formation are chosen at positions of lithologic change, so a formation need not correspond to a single interval of geological time — its boundaries may be diachronous, crossing time lines.

Usefulness

Formations are the basic units used in making geological maps and in correlating rock sequences between regions, and they underpin the identification of aquifers, reservoirs, and other economically important rock bodies.
