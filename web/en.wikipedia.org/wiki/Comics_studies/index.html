Comics studies

Comics studies (also comic art studies, sequential art studies or graphic narrative studies) is an academic field that focuses on comics and graphic novels. Although comics and graphic novels have been generally dismissed as less relevant pop culture texts, scholars in fields such as semiotics, aesthetics, sociology, composition studies and cultural studies are now re-considering comics and graphic novels as complex texts similar to other kinds of literature and art forms.

Rather than simply segmenting comics studies as an offspring of literary studies or art history, scholars of the field typically hold that comics studies likely evolved from the intersection of, and interrelation between, various disciplines.

Terminology and theory

Key theoretical contributions include Scott McCloud's Understanding Comics (1993), which defined comics as "juxtaposed pictorial and other images in deliberate sequence" and introduced concepts such as closure and the picture plane. Will Eisner earlier promoted the term "sequential art" in Comics and Sequential Art (1985). Thierry Groensteen's The System of Comics (1999) offered a structuralist account of the medium's spatio-topical system, and scholars such as Hillary Chute have examined graphic narrative's treatment of history and trauma.

History of the field

Early academic work on comics appeared in the mid-20th century, but the field consolidated from the 1990s onward, with dedicated journals such as the International Journal of Comic Art (founded 1999), ImageTexT, and European Comic Art, specialist conferences, university presses' comics series, and graduate programs. Organizations such as the Comics Studies Society (founded 2014) support scholarly work in the area.
