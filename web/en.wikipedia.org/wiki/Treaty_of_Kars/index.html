The Treaty of Kars was a treaty signed on 13 October 1921 in the city of Kars between the Grand National Assembly of Turkey and representatives of the Russian, Armenian, Azerbaijani and Georgian Soviet republics. It supplemented the earlier Treaty of Moscow of March 1921 and definitively established the north-eastern border of Turkey — essentially the border that still divides Turkey from Georgia and Armenia today.

Terms

Turkey retained the districts of Kars, Ardahan and Iğdır (including Mount Ararat), while the port of Batum was returned to Georgia, with guarantees for its Muslim population and free transit for Turkish trade. The Nakhchivan region was constituted as an autonomous territory under the protection of Azerbaijan, on condition that Azerbaijan could not transfer it to a third state — the origin of Nakhchivan's status as an Azerbaijani exclave separated from the rest of Azerbaijan by Armenia. Turkey abandoned its claims arising from the 1918 Treaty of Brest-Litovsk settlement east of the new line, and capitulations were abolished.

Legacy

Because Armenia did not exist as an independent state in 1921, the treaty's validity has occasionally been questioned in Armenian politics, and after 1945 the Soviet Union briefly demanded revision of the Kars settlement — a claim dropped in 1953. Turkey regards the treaty as final; the Turkey–Armenia border drawn at Kars remains closed in practice but legally unchanged.
