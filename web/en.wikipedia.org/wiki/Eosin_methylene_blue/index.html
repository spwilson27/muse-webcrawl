Eosin methylene blue

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eosin methylene blue (EMB) is a selective and differential culture medium used in microbiology for the isolation and identification of Gram-negative bacteria, especially fecal coliforms such as Escherichia coli. It contains the dyes eosin Y and methylene blue, which inhibit the growth of most Gram-positive bacteria, together with the sugars lactose and sucrose as fermentable substrates.

E. coli on EMB agar, showing the characteristic green metallic sheen.

Composition and principle

EMB agar was developed in the early twentieth century, building on formulations by Holt-Harris and Teague (1916) and by Levine (1918), whose version — still widely used and known as Levine EMB agar — omitted sucrose and adjusted the dye ratio. A typical formulation contains peptone, lactose, dipotassium phosphate, eosin Y, methylene blue, and agar at a slightly acidic pH.

The dyes act both as selective agents and as pH indicators. Under acidic conditions they form a dark precipitate in the medium. Organisms that ferment lactose vigorously, producing abundant acid, take up the dyes and form dark colonies; E. coli, a rapid and strong lactose fermenter, produces so much acid that its colonies develop a distinctive green metallic sheen, a sign considered presumptive evidence of the organism. Weaker lactose fermenters, such as Enterobacter and Klebsiella species, form pink to purple mucoid colonies, while non-fermenters such as Salmonella and Shigella remain colorless or pale.

Uses

EMB agar is a standard medium in clinical, food, and water microbiology. It is used in the examination of drinking water and dairy products for coliform contamination, and in clinical laboratories for the initial differentiation of enteric bacteria from stool and other specimens. Colonies presumptively identified on EMB are normally confirmed with further biochemical tests.
