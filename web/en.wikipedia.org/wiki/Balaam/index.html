Balaam (Hebrew: בִּלְעָם, Bilʿām) is a diviner in the Torah whose story appears in the Book of Numbers (chapters 22–24). Hired by Balak, king of Moab, to curse the Israelites encamped on the plains of Moab, Balaam instead pronounced blessings over Israel three times. His journey is famous for the episode of Balaam's talking donkey, which saw an angel blocking the road that Balaam himself could not see.

The narrative in Numbers

Balak son of Zippor, alarmed by Israel's victories over the Amorites, sent messengers with the fees for divination to Balaam at Pethor near the Euphrates, asking him to curse Israel. God told Balaam not to go; after a second, more prestigious embassy, Balaam set out on his donkey, but God's angel stood in the road with a drawn sword. The donkey saw the angel and turned aside three times; after Balaam beat her, she spoke and reproached him, and Balaam's eyes were opened.

From three vantage points — Bamoth-Baal, Pisgah and Peor — Balaam built seven altars and offered sacrifices, but each time delivered oracles blessing Israel, including the famous line "How goodly are thy tents, O Jacob" and the prophecy "there shall come a Star out of Jacob."

Counsel and death

Numbers 31 attributes to Balaam the counsel that led Israel into sin at Baal-Peor through Moabite and Midianite women. Balaam was killed by the Israelites in the war against Midian.

In later tradition

Balaam is a negative exemplar in much of the New Testament (2 Peter, Jude, Revelation), where "the way of Balaam" signifies greed and corruption, yet the prophet Micah recalls his answer to Balak approvingly. The Deir Alla inscription, a plaster text from Iron Age Jordan, mentions "Balaam son of Beor," a "seer of the gods," suggesting a regional tradition broader than the biblical account.
