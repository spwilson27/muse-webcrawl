Choux pastry

Choux pastry (pâte à choux)
Origin: France / Italy
Main ingredients: Butter, water, flour, eggs
Uses: Profiteroles, éclairs, gougères, beignets

Choux pastry, or pâte à choux, is a delicate French pastry dough containing only butter, water, flour and eggs. Instead of a raising agent, its high moisture content creates steam during baking that puffs the pastry into a crisp shell with a hollow centre, ready to be filled with cream.

History

A precursor was brought to France by the cooks of Catherine de' Medici in the 16th century; the dough was refined by later pâtissiers including Marie-Antoine Carême. The name choux (cabbage) refers to the round cabbage-like shape of profiterole puffs.

Preparations

Choux forms the basis of profiteroles, éclairs, croquembouche, French crullers, churros-like beignets, and savoury cheese gougères. The panade is cooked on the stove, cooled slightly, then eggs are beaten in one by one before piping and baking at high heat.
