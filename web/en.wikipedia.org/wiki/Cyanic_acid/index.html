Cyanic acid


Cyanic acid structure (placeholder image)

Formula: CHNO (HOCN)
Molar mass: 43.03 g·mol−1

Cyanic acid is an inorganic compound with the formula HOCN, in which the hydrogen is attached to oxygen. It is a tautomer of isocyanic acid (HNCO), which is far more stable and much more common; cyanic acid itself is usually encountered only as a minor component in equilibrium mixtures and in matrix-isolation studies.

Properties and related chemistry

Cyanic acid is a weak acid; its salts are the cyanates, such as potassium cyanate (KOCN). The cyanate ion is ambidentate and isomeric with the fulminate ion. Cyanic acid held a historic place in chemistry: in 1828 Friedrich Wöhler prepared urea from ammonium cyanate, a landmark often cited as the first synthesis of an organic compound from inorganic starting materials. On standing, cyanic/isocyanic acid polymerizes, including to the trimer cyanuric acid.
