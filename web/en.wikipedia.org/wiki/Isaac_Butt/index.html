Isaac Butt

Isaac Butt QC MP (6 September 1813 – 5 May 1879) was an Irish barrister, editor, politician, Member of Parliament in the House of Commons of the United Kingdom, economist and the founder and first leader of a number of Irish nationalist parties and organisations, including the Home Government Association and the Home Rule League.

Early life and career

Butt was born in Glenfin, County Donegal, the son of a Church of Ireland rector. He was educated at Trinity College Dublin, where he became a professor of political economy at the age of 23, and was called to the Irish Bar in 1838. Early in his career he was a conservative and an Orangeman who opposed Daniel O'Connell's repeal movement in celebrated debates.

Nationalist leadership

Defending Fenian prisoners after the 1867 rising moved Butt towards nationalism. He founded the Home Government Association in 1870, campaigning for an Irish parliament for domestic affairs within the United Kingdom ("Home Rule"). The Home Rule League won a majority of Irish seats in 1874, but Butt's conciliatory parliamentary style was eclipsed by the more aggressive obstructionism of Charles Stewart Parnell, who displaced him as leader in 1877.

Later years

Butt died in Clonskeagh, Dublin, in 1879 and is buried at Stranorlar, County Donegal.
