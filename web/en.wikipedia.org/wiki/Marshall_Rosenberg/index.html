Marshall Rosenberg

Marshall Bertram Rosenberg
Born: October 6, 1934, Canton, Ohio, U.S.
Died: February 7, 2015 (aged 80), Albuquerque, New Mexico, U.S.
Occupation: Psychologist, author, mediator
Known for: Nonviolent Communication

Marshall Bertram Rosenberg (October 6, 1934 – February 7, 2015) was an American psychologist, mediator, author, and teacher. He is best known as the creator of Nonviolent Communication (NVC), a communication process intended to help people exchange the information necessary to resolve conflicts and differences peacefully.

Early life and education

Rosenberg was born in Canton, Ohio, and raised in Detroit, Michigan, where witnessing racial violence and anti-Semitism during the 1943 Detroit race riot influenced his lifelong interest in how people can remain compassionate in the face of conflict. He studied psychology at the University of Michigan and earned his PhD in clinical psychology from the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 1961, studying under Carl Rogers.

Nonviolent Communication

Beginning in the 1960s, Rosenberg developed Nonviolent Communication, a framework built around four components: observations, feelings, needs, and requests. The method emphasizes expressing oneself honestly without blame or judgment, and receiving others empathically. Rosenberg founded the Center for Nonviolent Communication in 1984 and worked as a peacemaker in conflict zones including Rwanda, Burundi, Nigeria, Malaysia, Indonesia, and the Middle East.

Works

His best-known book, Nonviolent Communication: A Language of Life (1999), has been translated into dozens of languages. Other works include Speak Peace in a World of Conflict and Raising Children Compassionately.
