Limbourg brothers

The Limbourg brothers (Dutch: Gebroeders van Limburg) — Herman, Paul (Pol), and Johan (Jean) — were Dutch miniature painters from Nijmegen in the service of the French court, active in the early 15th century. They are famous for the illuminated manuscripts they produced for John, Duke of Berry, above all the Très Riches Heures du Duc de Berry, often called the most beautiful book of the Middle Ages.

Life

The brothers were born around 1385–1387, sons of the woodcarver Arnold de Limburg and nephews of the court painter Jean Malouel. They were apprenticed to a Paris goldsmith, then captured and ransomed while travelling home during an epidemic. By about 1402 they were working for Philip the Bold, Duke of Burgundy, on a Bible; after his death in 1404 they entered the service of his brother John, Duke of Berry, the greatest art patron of the age. The duke gave Paul a house in Bourges and rich gifts, and the brothers enjoyed an unusually close relationship with their patron. All three brothers — and the duke himself — died in 1416, probably of plague.

Works

Their surviving masterpieces are the Belles Heures (c. 1405–1409, now at The Cloisters, New York) and the Très Riches Heures (c. 1412–1416, Musée Condé, Chantilly), whose calendar pages — showing peasants and courtiers through the seasons before the duke's castles — are among the most famous images of medieval life. Their painting marks the high point of the International Gothic style, combining Northern naturalism, observed landscape and light, with courtly elegance and brilliant color. The Très Riches Heures was left unfinished at their deaths and was completed decades later by other hands, including Jean Colombe.
