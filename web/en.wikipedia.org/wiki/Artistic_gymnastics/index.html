Artistic gymnastics is a discipline of gymnastics in which athletes perform short routines on different apparatus, combining strength, flexibility, balance, and acrobatic skill. It is one of the most popular sports at the Summer Olympic Games and is governed internationally by the Fédération Internationale de Gymnastique (FIG), founded in 1881.

Apparatus

Men compete on six apparatus: floor exercise, pommel horse, still rings, vault, parallel bars, and horizontal bar. Women compete on four: vault, uneven bars, balance beam, and floor exercise (performed to music). Each routine is judged for difficulty and execution.

Scoring

Since 2006, the FIG Code of Points has used an open-ended system combining a Difficulty score (D-score), which rewards the content of the routine without a fixed maximum, and an Execution score (E-score), which starts at 10.0 and deducts for errors. The system replaced the traditional "perfect 10" made famous by Nadia Comăneci at the 1976 Montreal Olympics, when she became the first gymnast to receive a perfect score.

History

Gymnastics developed from physical training systems of the early nineteenth century, notably those of Friedrich Ludwig Jahn in Germany. Men's artistic gymnastics was included in the first modern Olympics at Athens in 1896; women's events were added at Amsterdam in 1928. Dominant figures in the modern era include Larisa Latynina, who won 18 Olympic medals; Simone Biles, widely regarded as the greatest gymnast of all time, with numerous World and Olympic titles and several skills named after her; and Kohei Uchimura, the dominant men's all-around gymnast of the 2010s.

Competitions

Major competitions include the Olympic Games, the World Artistic Gymnastics Championships, continental championships, and the World Cup series. Competition formats include team finals, individual all-around finals, and apparatus finals.
