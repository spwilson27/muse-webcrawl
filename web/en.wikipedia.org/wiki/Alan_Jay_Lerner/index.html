Alan Jay Lerner

Alan Jay Lerner (31 August 1918 – 14 June 1986) was an American lyricist and librettist. In partnership with composer Frederick Loewe he created some of the most enduring works of the American musical theatre, including Brigadoon, Paint Your Wagon, My Fair Lady, Camelot, and the film musical Gigi.

Career

Lerner studied at Harvard, where he contributed to the Hasty Pudding shows, and lost the sight of one eye in a boxing accident, which kept him out of Second World War service. He met the Vienna-born Loewe at the Lambs Club in 1942. Their first Broadway success was Brigadoon (1947). My Fair Lady (1956), adapted from Shaw's Pygmalion and starring Rex Harrison and Julie Andrews, became one of the most successful musicals in history. Gigi (1958), written directly for the screen, won nine Academy Awards including Best Original Song for its title number. Camelot (1960) became closely associated with the Kennedy presidency.

Later work and legacy

Lerner also wrote the book and lyrics for On a Clear Day You Can See Forever (1965, with Burton Lane) and the screenplay of An American in Paris (1951), which won him an Academy Award. His later collaborations were less successful, and his autobiography The Street Where I Live (1978) chronicled his years with Loewe. He married eight times and died of lung cancer in 1986; he was posthumously inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame's ranks of greats (he had been inducted in 1971).
