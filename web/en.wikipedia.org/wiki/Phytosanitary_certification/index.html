Phytosanitary certification

Phytosanitary certification is the process by which a government plant protection authority attests that a consignment of plants, plant products or other regulated articles complies with the phytosanitary import requirements of the destination country — in particular that it is considered free from quarantine pests and practically free from other regulated pests.

An inspector examining produce before issuing a certificate.

The certificate

The phytosanitary certificate is issued by the National Plant Protection Organization (NPPO) of the exporting country, following inspection (and, where required, testing or treatment) of the consignment. The model certificate is set out in the International Plant Protection Convention (IPPC), a treaty administered by the FAO. It records the consignment's origin, contents, distinguishing marks and any treatments applied. Increasingly, certificates are exchanged electronically through the IPPC ePhyto system rather than on paper.

Purpose

Certification is a key defence against the international spread of plant pests and diseases through trade — organisms such as fruit flies, citrus greening bacteria and wood-boring beetles can cause severe agricultural and environmental damage when introduced to new regions. Importing countries may refuse, treat or destroy consignments arriving without valid certification or found infested on arrival inspection.
