Grand strategy

Grand strategy sits above military strategy, integrating all instruments of national power.

Grand strategy (also called high strategy) is the overarching strategy by which a state pursues its long-term security and interests, integrating military, diplomatic, economic, informational and other instruments of power. It operates above the level of military strategy, which concerns the conduct of campaigns, and above tactics, which concern individual engagements.

Definition and elements

The military historian B. H. Liddell Hart defined grand strategy as coordinating "all the resources of a nation, or band of nations, towards the attainment of the political object of the war". Later writers such as Paul Kennedy and Barry Posen extended the concept to peacetime, treating grand strategy as a state's theory of how it can best achieve security — linking ends (goals such as survival, territorial integrity and prosperity) with ways (concepts such as containment, engagement or offshore balancing) and means (military forces, alliances, trade and wealth).

Historical examples

Classic studies examine, among others, the grand strategies of imperial Rome and Byzantium, Britain's maritime strategy of the 18th and 19th centuries, Bismarck's alliance system, and the United States' Cold War strategy of containment. Comparative scholarship assesses how material resources, geography, regime type and ideas shape a state's choices.

In games

"Grand strategy" also names a genre of video and board wargames — exemplified by series such as Europa Universalis and Hearts of Iron — in which players manage a nation's warfare, diplomacy, economy and internal politics over long time spans on a map of the world or a continent.
