Arianrhod

Welsh mythology
Abode: Caer Arianrhod
Parent: Dôn
Children: Dylan ail Ton, Lleu Llaw Gyffes
Brother: Gwydion

Arianrhod is a figure in Welsh mythology, appearing principally in the Fourth Branch of the Mabinogi, Math fab Mathonwy. Her name is often interpreted as "silver wheel" or "silver circle", suggesting associations with the moon and stars.

In the Fourth Branch

Arianrhod is a daughter of Dôn and niece — in the tale, a prospective virgin footholder — of Math fab Mathonwy, lord of Gwynedd. When she steps over Math's magic staff to prove her virginity, she gives birth to Dylan ail Ton and to a small shapeless thing that her brother Gwydion hides and nurtures until it becomes a boy.

The three curses

Ashamed and angry, Arianrhod lays three tynged (destinies/curses) on the boy: he shall have no name unless she names him, no arms unless she arms him, and no wife of any race on earth. Gwydion tricks her into defeating the first two — she names him Lleu Llaw Gyffes ("the fair one with the skilful hand") after he shoots a wren, and arms him during a phantom invasion. To defeat the third, Gwydion and Math create a wife of flowers, Blodeuwedd.

Legacy

Caer Arianrhod, a reef off the coast near Dinas Dinlle in Gwynedd, is linked to her in folklore as her submerged palace. She remains a popular figure in modern paganism and fantasy literature.
