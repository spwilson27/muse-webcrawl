Halloween

Halloween
Date: 31 October
Also called: All Hallows' Eve, Allhalloween
Observances: Trick-or-treating, costume parties, jack-o'-lanterns, haunted attractions, horror films
Related to: Samhain, All Saints' Day, All Souls' Day, Day of the Dead

Halloween or Hallowe'en (less commonly Allhalloween, All Hallows' Eve and All Saints' Eve) is an international celebration observed on 31 October, the eve of the Western Christian feast of All Hallows' Day. It is at the beginning of the season of Allhallowtide, the time in the liturgical year dedicated to remembering the dead, including saints (hallows), martyrs and the faithful departed.

Origins

Halloween traditions are widely believed to have roots in the ancient Celtic festival of Samhain, marking the end of the harvest season and the beginning of winter, when the boundary between the worlds of the living and the dead was thought to blur. In the 8th century Pope Gregory III designated 1 November as a time to honour all saints, and the evening before became All Hallows' Eve. Over centuries, Christian observances blended with folk customs involving mumming, bonfires, divination games and offerings for the souls of the dead; turnips — later pumpkins in North America — were carved into lanterns to ward off or represent wandering spirits.

Customs

The best-known Halloween activity is trick-or-treating, in which costumed children go from house to house asking for treats, typically sweets, with the formula "trick or treat". Other customs include costume parties, carving jack-o'-lanterns, apple bobbing, haunted houses and horror attractions, watching horror films, telling scary stories, and decorating homes with symbols such as skeletons, witches, black cats and ghosts. Irish and Scottish immigrants carried many of these customs to North America in the 19th century, where Halloween developed into a major commercial holiday; in the United States it is among the largest celebrations of the year, with spending on costumes, confectionery and decorations running to billions of dollars.

Around the world

Halloween is most firmly established in Ireland, the United Kingdom, the United States and Canada, and its popularity has spread through American cultural influence to much of Europe, Latin America, Japan and elsewhere, where it sometimes blends with local observances of the dead such as Mexico's Día de los Muertos (Day of the Dead, 1–2 November). Some Christians abstain from the holiday or emphasise its religious origins, and alternative events such as Reformation Day observances or "light parties" are held on the same date.
