Mariel Margaret "Mia" Hamm (born 17 March 1972) is an American former professional soccer player. A forward for the United States women's national team from 1987 to 2004, she was the face of women's soccer during its rise in popularity and, at the time of her retirement, the world's all-time leading international goalscorer among both women and men.

Career

Hamm played college soccer for the North Carolina Tar Heels, winning four NCAA championships. She debuted for the national team at age 15 and was the youngest member of the U.S. squad that won the inaugural FIFA Women's World Cup in 1991. She won a second World Cup in 1999 — the tournament famous for the sold-out final at the Rose Bowl and Brandi Chastain's winning penalty — and Olympic gold medals at Atlanta 1996 and Athens 2004, with a silver at Sydney 2000.

In club play she was a founding player of the Washington Freedom in the Women's United Soccer Association (WUSA), the first professional women's league in the United States. She finished her international career with 158 goals and 144 assists, records that stood for years; her 276 caps were also a world record at the time.

Legacy

Hamm was named FIFA World Player of the Year in 2001 and 2002, the first two years the award was given to women, and was inducted into the National Soccer Hall of Fame in 2007. Through the Mia Hamm Foundation she raises awareness for bone marrow and cord blood transplants, inspired by her brother Garrett, who died of aplastic anemia in 1997. She is married to former baseball player Nomar Garciaparra and is a part-owner of Los Angeles FC.
