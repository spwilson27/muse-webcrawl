McDonnell Douglas F-15EX Eagle II

The Boeing F-15EX Eagle II is an American all-weather multirole strike fighter derived from the McDonnell Douglas F-15E Strike Eagle and the F-15QA developed for Qatar. The United States Air Force (USAF) procured the F-15EX to replace its ageing fleet of F-15C/D Eagle air superiority fighters and to supplement the F-35A fleet. The aircraft is a single-seat variant with fly-by-wire flight controls, a digital cockpit, the APG-82(V)1 AESA radar, the EPAWSS electronic warfare suite, and the ability to carry up to 12 air-to-air missiles.

Development and procurement

Boeing first proposed an advanced single-seat F-15 variant to the USAF in 2018 as a lower-cost way to refresh the F-15C/D fleet, whose average age exceeded 35 years. The fiscal 2020 budget requested eight aircraft, the first USAF order for new F-15s since 2004. The first F-15EX, serial 20-0052, flew on 2 February 2021 at Lambert–St. Louis International Airport and was delivered to Eglin Air Force Base in March 2021. In April 2021 the type was officially named Eagle II.

Operational use

The Oregon Air National Guard's 142nd Wing at Portland Air National Guard Base became the first operational F-15EX unit, receiving its first aircraft in June 2024. Other planned operators include the California, Louisiana and Massachusetts Air National Guard units. The USAF plans to acquire 104 F-15EXs as of the mid-2020s budget plans, reduced from earlier plans for 144.

Specifications

The F-15EX is powered by two General Electric F110-GE-129 turbofans, reaches Mach 2.5+, has a ferry range of about 2,100 nautical miles with conformal fuel tanks, and can carry a payload of 29,500 lb (13,400 kg) — more than any other U.S. fighter. Its AMBER (Advanced Missile and Bomb Ejector Rack) system allows carriage of up to 12 AIM-120 AMRAAMs in the air-to-air role.
