Nancy is a city in northeastern France, the prefecture of the Meurthe-et-Moselle department in the Grand Est region. It was the capital of the Duchy of Lorraine, which was annexed by France in 1766. The city lies on the left bank of the Meurthe River, about 10 km upstream from its confluence with the Moselle.

History

Nancy developed in the Middle Ages as the seat of the dukes of Lorraine. In 1477 the Battle of Nancy was fought outside its walls, in which Duke René II defeated and killed Charles the Bold of Burgundy. The city's golden age came in the 18th century under Stanisław Leszczyński, the exiled king of Poland and last duke of Lorraine, who commissioned the celebrated Place Stanislas and the adjoining squares, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

After the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71) and the annexation of nearby Alsace-Moselle by Germany, Nancy received many French optants and grew rapidly. Around 1900 it became a leading centre of Art Nouveau through the École de Nancy, including artists such as Émile Gallé and the Daum brothers.

Sights

The ensemble of Place Stanislas, Place de la Carrière and Place d'Alliance is regarded as one of the finest examples of 18th-century urban planning, noted for the gilded wrought-iron gates by Jean Lamour. The Musée de l'École de Nancy preserves Art Nouveau furniture, glass and objects, and the Fine Arts Museum on Place Stanislas holds major European paintings.

Education

Nancy is a significant university city; the University of Lorraine, formed by a merger including the historic Henri Poincaré University, enrols tens of thousands of students.
