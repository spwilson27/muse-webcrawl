George Ripley (3 October 1802 – 4 July 1880) was an American social reformer, Unitarian minister, journalist, and founder of the utopian community Brook Farm.

Life

Ripley graduated from Harvard and its divinity school and served as a Unitarian minister in Boston before resigning over his association with Transcendentalism. In 1841 he founded Brook Farm in West Roxbury, Massachusetts, an experimental community combining manual and intellectual labour; it later adopted Fourierism before dissolving after a fire in 1847. Nathaniel Hawthorne was among its early members and drew on the experience in The Blithedale Romance.

Journalism

Ripley became literary critic of Horace Greeley's New-York Tribune and, with Charles A. Dana, edited The New American Cyclopaedia (1858–1863), a major reference work of the era.
