Atlantic hurricane season

The Atlantic hurricane season is the annual period during which tropical cyclones — tropical storms and hurricanes — most commonly form in the North Atlantic Ocean, including the Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of Mexico. The season officially runs from 1 June to 30 November, encompassing the months when warm sea-surface temperatures, low wind shear and moist air favour tropical cyclogenesis. Activity peaks in late summer, climatologically around 10 September.

Climatology

An average season, based on the 1991–2020 climatology, produces about 14 named storms, of which around 7 become hurricanes and 3 become major hurricanes (Category 3 or higher on the Saffir–Simpson scale). Activity varies strongly from year to year, influenced by El Niño–La Niña conditions in the Pacific, Atlantic sea-surface temperatures, and the phase of the Atlantic Multidecadal Oscillation. The most active season on record was 2020, with 30 named storms; the hyperactive 2005 season produced Katrina, Rita and Wilma, the last of which remains the most intense Atlantic hurricane measured by central pressure.

Forecasting and monitoring

The US National Hurricane Center (NHC) in Miami issues advisories for the basin, naming storms from rotating six-year lists maintained by the World Meteorological Organization; names of exceptionally deadly or costly storms are retired. Preseason forecasts from NOAA, Colorado State University and other groups project seasonal activity. Hurricane reconnaissance aircraft, satellites and ocean buoys monitor developing systems, and storms occasionally form outside the official season — leading the NHC to begin issuing routine tropical outlooks in mid-May.

Impacts

Atlantic hurricanes threaten the Caribbean, Central America, Mexico's Yucatán and Gulf coasts, and the Atlantic and Gulf coasts of the United States and Canada. Damage comes from storm surge, extreme winds, inland flooding and tornadoes; among the costliest are Katrina (2005), Harvey (2017), Ian (2022) and Maria (2017), whose devastation of Puerto Rico was among the deadliest in US history.
