The Winter Olympic Games are a major international multi-sport event held once every four years for sports practised on snow and ice. The first edition, held in Chamonix, France, in 1924, was originally organised as an "International Winter Sports Week" and was only retrospectively recognised as the first Winter Olympic Games. The Winter Games were held in the same year as the Summer Olympic Games until 1992, after which the International Olympic Committee moved them to the even-numbered years between Summer editions, beginning with Lillehammer 1994.

The most recent Games were held in Beijing in 2022, making Beijing the first city to host both the Summer and Winter editions. The next Games will be hosted in 2026 by Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo in Italy, followed by the French Alps in 2030 and Salt Lake City in 2034.

History

Winter sports such as figure skating and ice hockey had appeared at the Summer Games of 1908 and 1920 before the IOC created a separate winter event. Chamonix 1924 drew about 250 athletes from 16 nations; the Norwegian and Finnish teams dominated the Nordic skiing events. St. Moritz, Lake Placid and Garmisch-Partenkirchen hosted the interwar editions, and the Games resumed after the Second World War at St. Moritz in 1948.

The Winter Games grew steadily with television: the 1980 Lake Placid Games featured the "Miracle on Ice", in which the United States men's ice hockey team of college players defeated the heavily favoured Soviet Union. The 1988 Calgary Games extended the schedule to 16 days, and professional NHL players competed in ice hockey from Nagano 1998. Snowboarding was added in 1998, and the programme has continued to expand with events such as ski jumping for women (2014) and big air snowboarding and freestyle skiing (2022).

Sports

The Beijing 2022 programme comprised seven sports and 109 medal events: alpine skiing, biathlon, bobsleigh, cross-country skiing, curling, figure skating, freestyle skiing, ice hockey, luge, Nordic combined, short track speed skating, skeleton, ski jumping, snowboarding and speed skating. Norway — whose cross-country skier Marit Bjørgen is the most decorated Winter Olympian with 15 medals — has topped the all-time Winter medal table for much of the Games' history, competing with Germany, Russia/the Soviet Union, Canada and the United States. Ice hockey's most famous Winter Games figure is Wayne Gretzky, who played at Nagano 1998.
