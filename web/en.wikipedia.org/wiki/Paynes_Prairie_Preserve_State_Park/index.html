Paynes Prairie Preserve State Park is a Florida state park encompassing a 21,000-acre (8,500 ha) savanna in Alachua County, south of Gainesville. It is designated a National Natural Landmark. The prairie is a large, shallow basin that periodically floods, and in wet years its centre forms Alachua Lake. It is notable as one of the few places in Florida where wild-roaming bison and wild horses can be seen.

History

The prairie takes its name from King Payne, a Seminole chief. In the 17th and 18th centuries it supported the LaChua ranch, the largest cattle ranch in Spanish Florida. Naturalist William Bartram visited in 1774 and described the "great Alachua savanna" in his Travels. In the 19th and early 20th centuries the basin was drained by the Alachua Sink for cattle ranching and farming; from 1871 to 1891 it filled as Alachua Lake, deep enough that steamboats crossed it. Florida bought the land in 1970, and it became the state's first state preserve in 1971.

Wildlife

A herd of plains bison was reintroduced in 1975 from the Wichita Mountains Wildlife Refuge, and wild horses descend from the Spanish horses of the ranching era and later cracker horses. The preserve hosts more than 270 bird species, including sandhill cranes in winter, as well as American alligators, white-tailed deer and bobcats. Migratory and resident birds use the prairie's marshes extensively.

Recreation

Eight trails provide hiking, cycling and horseback riding, including the Gainesville–Hawthorne State Trail, which passes the park. A 50-foot observation tower near the visitor centre overlooks the basin. The park offers camping near Lake Wauburg and ranger-guided walks and excursions.
