Urtica

Family: Urticaceae

Common name: Nettles

Notable species: Urtica dioica (stinging nettle)

Urtica is a genus of flowering plants in the family Urticaceae, commonly known as nettles. Most species bear stinging hairs (trichomes) on their leaves and stems that inject histamine and other chemicals on contact, producing a painful rash. The genus includes the widespread stinging nettle, Urtica dioica.

Description

Species range from small annual herbs to perennials spreading by rhizomes. The stinging hairs act like hypodermic needles, breaking off in the skin and delivering a cocktail that includes formic acid, histamine and acetylcholine.

Uses

Nettles have long been used as food — the sting disappears on cooking — as a fibre plant comparable to flax, and in traditional medicine. Nettle leaves are rich in vitamins and minerals. The plants are also important food sources for the caterpillars of many butterfly species, including the small tortoiseshell and peacock.

Distribution

The genus is found in temperate regions on most continents, typically in nutrient-rich, disturbed soils near human habitation and along riverbanks.
