UCAS (the Universities and Colleges Admissions Service) is a charity and private limited company based in Cheltenham, England, which provides educational support services. Its main role is to operate the application process for British universities and colleges, and it is the world's largest central admissions service for higher education.

History

UCAS was formed in 1992 from the merger of the Universities Central Council on Admissions (UCCA, founded in 1961, with origins in a 1950s clearing-house scheme) and the Polytechnics and Colleges Admissions System (PCAS). It processes applications for full-time undergraduate courses at UK universities and colleges, as well as postgraduate teacher training (UCAS Teacher Training), conservatoires (UCAS Conservatoires), and some postgraduate courses (UCAS Postgraduate).

Application process

Applicants submit a single online application with up to five course choices, predicted grades, a personal statement, and a reference. The standard deadline for most courses is in late January, with earlier (mid-October) deadlines for medicine, dentistry, veterinary science, and Oxford and Cambridge. Universities respond with conditional or unconditional offers; applicants select a firm and an insurance choice. On results day, Clearing matches unplaced applicants with courses that still have vacancies, a process supplemented by UCAS Adjustment and, more recently, self-release. The UCAS Tariff converts qualifications into points used by some institutions in offers.
