Eggs Benedict

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Eggs Benedict on a plate: toasted English muffin, ham, poached egg and hollandaise]
Eggs Benedict on a plate: toasted English muffin, ham, poached egg and hollandaise

Eggs Benedict is an American breakfast dish consisting of two halves of an English muffin, each topped with Canadian bacon (or ham), a poached egg and Hollandaise sauce. It is a fixture of brunch menus worldwide and has spawned countless variants — eggs Florentine (spinach), eggs royale (smoked salmon) and country Benedicts with sausage gravy.

Origins

Two New York institutions claim its invention in the 1890s: Delmonico's, for regulars Mr and Mrs LeGrand Benedict, and the Waldorf Hotel, where retired stockbroker Lemuel Benedict demanded the components as a hangover cure in 1894 and maître d' Oscar Tschirky refined them into the dish. Either way the dish crystallised in Gilded Age hotel dining and spread globally with the twentieth-century brunch.

Preparation

The dish tests three techniques at once: poaching eggs to runny yolks, toasting the muffin base crisp, and keeping the hollandaise emulsified and warm — plus timing their assembly, which is why it is ordered out more often than cooked at home.
