The PlayStation 4 (PS4) is a home video game console developed by Sony Interactive Entertainment. Announced as the successor to the PlayStation 3 in February 2013, it was launched in North America in November 2013 and in Japan in February 2014. It competed with Microsoft's Xbox One and Nintendo's Wii U and Switch as part of the eighth generation of consoles.

Hardware

Designed under lead architect Mark Cerny, the PS4 deliberately adopted a PC-like x86-64 architecture — an AMD Accelerated Processing Unit combining an eight-core Jaguar CPU and a Radeon-based GPU rated at 1.84 teraflops — with 8 GB of unified GDDR5 memory, to make development easier after the PlayStation 3's notoriously difficult Cell processor. The DualShock 4 controller added a touchpad, a light bar, and a Share button for capturing and streaming gameplay. In 2016 Sony released two hardware revisions: a slimmer PS4 and the more powerful PlayStation 4 Pro, aimed at 4K displays, along with the PlayStation VR headset.

Software and services

The PS4 runs a FreeBSD-derived operating system (Orbis OS) and supports the PlayStation Network for online play, the PlayStation Store for downloads, and streaming services. It is not natively backward compatible with PS3 discs, though many titles were remastered or streamed. Sony emphasised indie developers, "play while downloading", and social sharing at launch, and its $399 launch price undercut the Xbox One by $100 — factors credited with its decisive lead in the generation.

Sales and games

The PS4 became one of the best-selling consoles of all time, shipping more than 117 million units by 2022. Its best-selling and most acclaimed games include Marvel's Spider-Man, God of War (2018), The Last of Us Part II, Horizon Zero Dawn, Uncharted 4: A Thief's End, Bloodborne, Grand Theft Auto V, and Minecraft. Production of most PS4 models in Japan ended in 2021 as Sony transitioned to the PlayStation 5, though the console's large library remained supported for years afterward.
