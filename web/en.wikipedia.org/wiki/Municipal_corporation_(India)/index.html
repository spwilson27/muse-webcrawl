Municipal corporation (India)

A municipal corporation in India is a local government body that administers a large urban area, typically a city with a population of more than one million. Municipal corporations are established by state legislation under the framework of the 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992.

The corporation is headed by a mayor as ceremonial and political head, while executive authority rests with a municipal commissioner appointed by the state government. Elected councillors represent wards.

Functions

Functions include water supply, sanitation, solid waste management, roads, street lighting, public health, primary education, town planning and issuing birth and death certificates. Major examples include the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (Mumbai), Municipal Corporation of Delhi, and corporations in Chennai, Kolkata, Bengaluru and Hyderabad.

Finance

Revenue comes from property tax, professional tax, grants from state and central governments, and fees for services.
