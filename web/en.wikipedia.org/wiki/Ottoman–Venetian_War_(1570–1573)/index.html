Ottoman–Venetian War (1570–1573)

Fourth Ottoman–Venetian War
Date1570 – 7 March 1573
LocationCyprus, Adriatic Sea, Ionian Sea, Aegean Sea
BelligerentsOttoman Empire vs Republic of Venice and the Holy League (Spain, Papal States, Genoa, Savoy, Knights of Malta, others)
ResultOttoman victory; Cyprus ceded to the Ottomans (Treaty of Constantinople, 1573)

The Fourth Ottoman–Venetian War, also known as the War of Cyprus, was fought between 1570 and 1573 between the Ottoman Empire and the Republic of Venice, the latter joined by a Christian coalition known as the Holy League. Its most famous event was the naval Battle of Lepanto (7 October 1571), but despite that Christian victory the war ended with the Ottoman conquest of Cyprus.

Conquest of Cyprus

Demanding the cession of Cyprus, the great Ottoman expedition of 1570 under Lala Mustafa Pasha took Nicosia in September 1570 with enormous loss of civilian life, and after an eleven-month siege starved Famagusta into surrender in August 1571; the Venetian commander Marcantonio Bragadin was then flayed alive in violation of the surrender terms.

Lepanto and aftermath

The Holy League fleet under Don John of Austria destroyed the Ottoman fleet at Lepanto in October 1571 — one of the largest naval battles in history — but the allies failed to exploit the victory, and the Ottomans rebuilt a fleet within a year. By the Treaty of Constantinople (7 March 1573) Venice recognised the loss of Cyprus, paid an indemnity of 300,000 ducats, and raised its tribute for Zante.

Significance

Though Lepanto became a celebrated symbol of Christian resistance, the war confirmed Ottoman naval power in the eastern Mediterranean and the decline of Venetian influence there.
