Picard (endonym: picard) is a langue d'oïl, a Romance language of the northern part of France and parts of Belgium (Wallonia), closely related to French. It is spoken in the historic region of Picardy and, in varieties often called Chtimi or Ch'ti, in the Nord-Pas-de-Calais mining basin, as well as in the Belgian province of Hainaut.

History

Picard developed from the Vulgar Latin of northern Gaul and in the Middle Ages had a notable literary presence; Picard scribes were influential in the transmission of Old French texts. The variety written in medieval Picardy differed from the Francien of Paris in phonology — notably, Picard retained Latin /k/ before "a" where French palatalised it (compare Picard cat vs French chat, "cat").

Characteristics

Picard is a group of closely related dialects rather than a single standardised language; mutual intelligibility with standard French is partial. It has its own orthographic traditions and a modern literature of poetry, theatre and song. The popular 2008 film Bienvenue chez les Ch'tis (Welcome to the Sticks) brought its northern variety to national attention in France.

Status and revival

Picard has been in decline since the 19th century under pressure from standard French in schooling and media. Belgium's French Community recognised Picard as a regional endogenous language in 1990; France has not granted official status, though regional cultural bodies and festivals promote its use. Speaker numbers are uncertain, with estimates ranging from tens of thousands to several hundred thousand, mostly older speakers.
