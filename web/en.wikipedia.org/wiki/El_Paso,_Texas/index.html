El Paso is a city in and the county seat of El Paso County in far-west Texas, United States. It stands on the Rio Grande, across the border from Ciudad Juárez in the Mexican state of Chihuahua, with which it forms a major binational metropolitan area. It is one of the largest cities in Texas and a historic crossroads of the American Southwest.

Geography and climate

El Paso lies in the Chihuahuan Desert at the southern end of the Franklin Mountains, which divide the city. It has a hot desert climate with abundant sunshine — hence its nickname "The Sun City" — hot summers, mild winters, and an annual monsoon season. The Rio Grande forms the international boundary along its southern edge.

History

The area was a Spanish colonial outpost from 1659, when El Paso del Norte (present-day Juárez) was founded on the Camino Real. The northern bank settlements became part of the United States after the Mexican–American War. El Paso grew rapidly with the arrival of the railroads in the 1880s, becoming a centre for mining, ranching, and cross-border commerce, and a refuge during the Mexican Revolution.

Economy and culture

The economy rests on international trade, manufacturing, healthcare, education, and the military: Fort Bliss, one of the largest US Army installations, is headquartered there. The University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP) is a major institution. The city's population is predominantly Hispanic, and its culture is deeply bicultural and bilingual.
