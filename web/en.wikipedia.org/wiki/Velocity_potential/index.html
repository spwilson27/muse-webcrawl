Velocity potential

FieldFluid mechanics
Definitionv = ∇φ
Applicable flowsIrrotational (potential) flow

A velocity potential is a scalar potential used in potential flow theory. If a velocity potential exists for a flow, the flow is irrotational, and the velocity field can be written as the gradient of the potential: v = ∇φ.

Definition

If φ is defined such that v = ∇φ, then the irrotational condition ∇ × v = 0 is automatically satisfied, because the curl of a gradient is identically zero. Conversely, in a simply connected region, any irrotational velocity field can be expressed as the gradient of some scalar potential. The potential is defined up to an additive constant (and, in unsteady flow, may also depend on time).

Use in potential flow

If the fluid is also incompressible, the continuity equation requires ∇·v = 0, which reduces to Laplace's equation, ∇²φ = 0. Solving Laplace's equation with suitable boundary conditions — typically that the normal component of velocity vanish (or match a body's motion) at solid surfaces — therefore determines the entire flow field. This formulation underlies classical aerodynamics and the theory of water waves, and is closely related to the stream function in two-dimensional flow. Velocity potential theory cannot describe viscous effects such as boundary layers, so it is applied where viscosity is negligible outside thin regions near surfaces.
