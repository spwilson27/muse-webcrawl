Washoku (和食) is the traditional dietary culture of the Japanese, notable as an example of sustainable, well-balanced eating drawing on Japan's land and sea. The term literally means "Japanese food" and contrasts with yōshoku, Western-influenced Japanese dishes. In 2013, "Washoku, traditional dietary cultures of the Japanese, notably for the celebration of New Year" was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Principles

Washoku is organised around several core principles: respect for the natural flavours of fresh, diverse ingredients; nutritional balance; expression of the beauty of nature and the seasons in presentation; and close connection with annual festivals and ceremonies such as New Year celebrations. The customary meal structure is ichijū-sansai ("one soup, three dishes") — rice, a soup such as miso soup, and three accompanying dishes, typically combining raw, simmered, grilled, and pickled preparations — which is said to ensure a balance of nutrients, colours, flavours, and cooking methods.

Ingredients and techniques

Staples include rice, soy products such as tofu and natto, miso, soy sauce, seafood, mountain and sea vegetables, and seasonal produce. The broth dashi, made from kombu kelp and dried bonito flakes, provides the umami foundation of much of the cuisine, complemented by fermented seasonings. Presentation often uses dishes and garnishes evoking the season, such as maple leaves in autumn.

New Year and festivals

The UNESCO inscription emphasised washoku's role in celebrations, above all New Year, when families share special dishes such as osechi-ryōri — elaborately prepared foods packed in tiered lacquer boxes, each carrying a symbolic wish — and pounded rice cakes (mochi).
