Ovation

An ovation is a form of applause and recognition given to a performer, speaker or honouree, in which the audience expresses strong approval. In its best-known modern form, the standing ovation, spectators rise to their feet while applauding.

Roman origins

The word derives from the Latin ovatio, a lesser form of the Roman triumph awarded to a general whose victory did not meet the criteria for a full triumph — for example when the enemy was considered inferior, the war was undeclared, or the achievement fell short in scale. The general entered Rome on foot or on horseback rather than in a chariot, wearing a wreath of myrtle instead of laurel.

Modern use

Standing ovations are common at concerts, theatre performances, political speeches and film festival premieres. Their frequency varies by culture and venue; at some festivals nearly every premiere receives one, which has prompted commentary on the dilution of the gesture's meaning. Duration of ovations is sometimes reported as a measure of a performance's reception.
