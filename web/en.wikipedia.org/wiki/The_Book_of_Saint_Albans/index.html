The Book of Saint Albans

Published: 1486, St Albans, England
Attributed to: Juliana Berners (in part)
Subject: Hawking, hunting, heraldry

The Book of Saint Albans (or Boke of Seynt Albans) is the common title of a book printed in 1486 at the St Albans press in England. It is one of the earliest printed English books on field sports and is famous for containing the first printed treatise on fishing with a hook and line, as well as treatises on hawking, hunting, and heraldry (coat armour).

Contents

The volume comprises three essays: on hawking, on hunting, and on heraldry. The hunting treatise is traditionally attributed to Dame Juliana Berners, a prioress of Sopwell, and includes the celebrated list of collective nouns for animals — the "terms of venery" — such as "a pride of lions" and "a murmuration of starlings," many coined in this tradition. A second, enlarged edition printed by Wynkyn de Worde in 1496 added the Treatyse of Fysshynge wyth an Angle, the earliest known printed work on angling in English, which describes rods, lines, hooks, flies, and the ethics of the sport.

Significance

The book was aimed at a gentry audience and codified the language and etiquette of aristocratic pastimes. Its lists of terms of venery have influenced English idiom ever since, and the fishing treatise is a foundational text in angling literature, anticipating Izaak Walton's The Compleat Angler by more than 150 years.
