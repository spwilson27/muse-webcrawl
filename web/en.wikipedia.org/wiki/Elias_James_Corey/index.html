Elias James Corey

Born: July 12, 1928, Methuen, Massachusetts, U.S.
Fields: Organic chemistry
Institutions: Harvard University
Awards: Nobel Prize in Chemistry (1990)

Elias James Corey (born July 12, 1928) is an American organic chemist. In 1990, he won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry "for his development of the theory and methodology of organic synthesis", specifically retrosynthetic analysis.

Early life and education

Corey was born in Methuen, Massachusetts, to Lebanese immigrant parents. He entered the Massachusetts Institute of Technology at age 16 and received his bachelor's degree in 1948 and his PhD in 1951. After positions at the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign, he joined Harvard University in 1959, where he has remained.

Contributions

Corey formalized retrosynthetic analysis, a method for designing syntheses of complex molecules by working backward from the target to simple starting materials. His group completed total syntheses of many complex natural products, including prostaglandins, ginkgolide B, and (with others' methods disputed) oseltamivir. He also developed numerous reagents and reactions, including the Corey–Bakshi–Shibata reduction, the Corey–Fuchs reaction, and the Corey–Kim oxidation, as well as the protect-group chemistry and the use of computers in synthesis design through the LHASA program.
