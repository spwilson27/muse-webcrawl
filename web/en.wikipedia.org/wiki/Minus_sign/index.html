Minus sign (U+2212)

The minus sign (−) is a mathematical symbol that represents the operation of subtraction, as well as, together with the plus sign, the idea of negative and positive quantities. In Unicode it is U+2212 − MINUS SIGN, which is designed to have the same width and height as the plus sign and the equals sign. In most contexts the hyphen-minus character (-), available on keyboards, is used in its place in digital text.

Uses

In arithmetic, the minus sign denotes the binary operation of subtraction, as in 5 − 3 = 2. As a unary operator placed before a number, it indicates the additive inverse: −3 is the negative number three. Paired with the plus sign in the form ± it denotes a range of values in measurements and tolerances. Negative temperatures, electric charges and directions are also indicated with the minus sign.

Beyond mathematics, the sign appears in proofreading marks, in chemical notation for single bonds in structural shorthand, and in weather and finance reporting to show decreases. In computing, look-alike characters include the hyphen-minus, the en dash and the em dash, which differ in length and meaning.

History

The plus and minus signs came into general use in printed mathematical works of the late fifteenth century, notably in Johannes Widmann's commercial arithmetic of 1489, where they originally indicated surplus and deficit in warehouse accounts before being adopted for the operations themselves. Earlier manuscripts had used the letters p and m (for più and meno in Italian usage) or horizontal strokes to mark the same ideas.

Typography

The true minus sign is longer than a hyphen and aligns with the horizontal stroke of the plus sign, sitting at the height of the crossbar of the equals sign. Proper typesetting therefore distinguishes a raised or short hyphen, used in compound words, from the minus sign used in numerical expressions and ranges.
