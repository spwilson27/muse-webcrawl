The Siege of Famagusta (1570–1571) was the concluding episode of the Ottoman conquest of Cyprus, a Venetian colony. After Nicosia fell in September 1570, the fortified port of Famagusta, commanded by Marcantonio Bragadin, held out for nearly eleven months against the army of Lala Mustafa Pasha, despite overwhelming Ottoman superiority in numbers and artillery.

The siege

Some 8,000 defenders withstood repeated assaults, bombardment by dozens of cannon, and extensive mining, inflicting heavy casualties on the besiegers; a Venetian relief convoy briefly broke through in January 1571. By the summer of 1571, with powder and food exhausted and the walls breached, Bragadin negotiated surrender on terms guaranteeing the garrison's safe passage to Crete.

Aftermath

During the embarkation, a dispute escalated: Lala Mustafa, enraged by reports of murdered Muslim prisoners and by Venetian defiance, had the Venetian officers seized and executed, Bragadin being tortured and flayed alive on 17 August 1571; his skin, stuffed with straw, was carried back to Constantinople as a trophy (it was later recovered and interred in the church of Santi Giovanni e Paolo, Venice). News of the siege and Bragadin's fate helped spur the formation of the Holy League, whose fleet defeated the Ottomans at the Battle of Lepanto two months later — too late to save Cyprus, which remained Ottoman until 1878.
