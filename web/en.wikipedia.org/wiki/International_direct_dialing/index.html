International direct dialing

International direct dialing (IDD), or international subscriber dialling (ISD), is the ability of telephone subscribers to dial international telephone calls directly, without operator assistance. It replaced the older system in which all international calls had to be booked through an operator.

History

The first transatlantic direct-dialled call was made on 30 March 1963, when Queen Elizabeth II and the Lord Mayor of London inaugurated the service between London and Ottawa, Canada. Direct dialling spread through Europe and North America during the 1960s and 1970s as electromechanical and later electronic exchanges made automatic international switching practical.

How it works

To place an IDD call, the caller dials an international call prefix — most commonly 00, as recommended by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), though North America uses 011 — followed by the destination country code, the area code, and the local number. On mobile phones, the prefix may be replaced by a plus sign (+), which the network converts automatically.

Prefixes and country codes

Country calling codes are assigned by the ITU under recommendation E.164. Examples include +1 for the North American Numbering Plan, +44 for the United Kingdom, +81 for Japan, and +61 for Australia. Some countries retain alternative prefixes for carrier selection or discounted international services.
