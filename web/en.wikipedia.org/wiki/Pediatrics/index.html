Pediatrics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pediatrics (paediatrics in British English) is the branch of Medicine concerned with the health and medical care of infants, children and adolescents, from birth up to about 18 years of age. It differs from adult medicine because children's bodies are growing: their diseases, drug doses, nutrition and communication all differ by developmental stage.

Scope

Paediatricians provide preventive care — growth and development monitoring, immunisation and screening — and treat the infections, injuries and chronic conditions of childhood, from asthma and diabetes to congenital disorders. Subspecialties include neonatology (newborns, especially premature babies), paediatric cardiology and oncology, and adolescent medicine.

Development

The specialty emerged in the 19th century with the first children's hospitals, such as the Hôpital des Enfants Malades in Paris (1802) and Great Ormond Street Hospital in London (1852). Child health advanced dramatically with vaccination, antibiotics, nutrition and neonatal intensive care, cutting infant mortality worldwide.
