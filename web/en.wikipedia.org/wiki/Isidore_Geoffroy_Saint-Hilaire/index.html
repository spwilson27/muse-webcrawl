Isidore Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire (16 December 1805 – 10 November 1861) was a French zoologist, the son of the naturalist Étienne Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire. He continued and systematised his father's work on anatomical anomalies and comparative anatomy, and is credited with coining the term teratology for the study of developmental abnormalities and with early use of the word "ethology" for the study of animals in relation to their environment.

Career

Born in Paris, Isidore assisted his father at the Muséum national d'histoire naturelle and in 1829 began lecturing on zoology at the Athénée. He succeeded his father in museum posts, becoming professor of zoology at the Faculty of Sciences in Paris in 1850. His major work, the Histoire générale et particulière des anomalies de l'organisation chez l'homme et les animaux (1832–1837), classified congenital malformations and treated them as lawful variations of development — the foundation of teratology as a scientific discipline.

Acclimatisation and later work

Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire championed the acclimatisation of exotic animals for agriculture and founded the Société zoologique d'acclimatation in 1854 and the Jardin d'Acclimatation in the Bois de Boulogne, opened in 1860. He wrote on domestication, the classification of mammals and birds, and a biography of his father (1847). He also investigated reports of anomalous creatures for the Academy of Sciences, applying teratological reasoning to alleged monsters.
