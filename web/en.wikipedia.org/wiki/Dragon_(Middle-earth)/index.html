Dragons are powerful, intelligent, fire-breathing (and sometimes winged) creatures in J. R. R. Tolkien's fantasy world of Middle-earth. They were bred in the First Age by the Dark Lord Morgoth as weapons of war in his fortress of Angband. Tolkien's dragons draw on the great worms of Northern European legend — above all Fáfnir of the Völsunga saga and the dragon of Beowulf — and are characterised by immense strength, long life, cunning speech, a hypnotic gaze (the "dragon-spell"), and a consuming greed for gold and treasure.

Major dragons

  • Glaurung, the Father of Dragons — the first dragon, wingless, who destroyed the Elven kingdom of Nargothrond and wrought the tragedy of Túrin Turambar before being slain by him.
  • Ancalagon the Black — the greatest of the winged dragons, unleashed in the War of Wrath; his fall crushed the peaks of Thangorodrim. He was killed by Eärendil.
  • Scatha — a long-worm of the Grey Mountains, slain by Fram of the Éothéod; his hoard became a source of strife between Men and Dwarves.
  • Smaug — the last great dragon of the Third Age, who in TA 2770 destroyed the kingdom of Erebor and the town of Dale and lay on its treasure for nearly two centuries until, in The Hobbit, he was killed by Bard the Bowman after Bilbo Baggins discovered the weak spot in his jewelled underbelly.

Characteristics

Tolkien distinguishes winged dragons from wingless "long-worms", and fire-drakes from cold-drakes, which cannot breathe fire. Dragons are vulnerable at the underbelly, a weakness treasure-hoarding individuals remedy by lying on gems until they form a protective waistcoat. Their blood in legend is poisonous, and their speech is deceitful — Smaug's conversation with Bilbo is a masterclass in mutual cunning.
