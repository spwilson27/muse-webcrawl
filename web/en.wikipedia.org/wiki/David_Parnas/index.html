David Parnas

David Lorge Parnas (born 10 February 1941) is a Canadian early pioneer of software engineering, widely regarded as the developer of the concept of information hiding in modular programming, a foundational principle of object-oriented design.

Parnas's 1972 paper on modular decomposition reshaped how large programs are structured.

Career

Parnas earned his PhD in electrical engineering from Carnegie Mellon University, where he later taught. He held positions at the University of North Carolina, in Darmstadt, Germany, at the US Naval Research Laboratory — where he founded a long-running software engineering research programme — and at McMaster University in Canada, where he led the Software Engineering Programme. He later worked at the University of Limerick in Ireland.

Contributions

His 1972 paper "On the Criteria to Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules" argued that modules should be defined around design decisions likely to change, rather than around steps of a flowchart — the principle of information hiding. He also developed tabular specifications for software documentation, promoted rigorous inspection of safety-critical software, and wrote influential critiques of the US Strategic Defense Initiative's software feasibility, resigning from an advisory panel in 1985 on the grounds that its battle-management software could not be adequately tested.

Honours

Parnas is a Fellow of the ACM and the Royal Society of Canada, and received the ACM SIGSOFT Outstanding Research Award. He has campaigned for the professional licensing of software engineers.
