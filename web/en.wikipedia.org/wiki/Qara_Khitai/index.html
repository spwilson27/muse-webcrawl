The Qara Khitai (also Khara-Khitai or Qara-Khitay; Chinese historians call the state the Western Liao, 西遼) was a Khitan-led empire that ruled Central Asia from 1124 to 1218. It was founded by Yelü Dashi, a prince of the Liao dynasty of northern China, who led a remnant of his people west after the Jurchen Jin dynasty destroyed the Liao. In Central Asia he built a powerful, religiously tolerant, Chinese-style imperial state — ruled by Buddhist Khitans over a largely Muslim population — that for decades was the dominant power between Persia and China, and whose fame fed the European legend of Prester John.

Foundation

After the fall of the Liao (1125), Yelü Dashi gathered followers at Kedun and marched west, gaining the allegiance of steppe tribes and of the city of Balasagun in the Chu valley, which became his capital (with the Chinese name Husifusi). In 1134 he took the title Gurkhan ("universal khan") and the Chinese imperial style of Emperor Dezong. Campaigns in the 1130s–1140s subdued the Karakhanids of Transoxiana and Khwarazm. His crushing victory over the Seljuk sultan Ahmad Sanjar at the Battle of Qatwan (1141) — one of the great battles of the medieval steppe — made the Qara Khitai overlords of the region and echoed in the West as a victory of a Christian king, "Prester John", over Islam.

Government and society

The empire combined Chinese bureaucratic forms — era names, imperial titles, coinage with Chinese characters — with light-touch rule over subject peoples, often through local dynasties paying tribute. The Khitan court practised Buddhism while Islam, Nestorian Christianity and other faiths flourished among subjects; the state exacted a per-household tax but otherwise left cities and tribes largely self-governing. At its height its suzerainty extended from the Altai and Lake Balkhash to the Amu Darya (Oxus) and the approaches to Tibet.

Decline and fall

After 1170s the empire weakened amid succession disputes and pressure from Khwarazm and the rising Naiman. In 1211 the Naiman prince Kuchlug — a refugee from Chinggis Khan's unification of Mongolia — usurped the throne from the last Gurkhan, Yelü Zhilugu, and persecuted his Muslim subjects. In 1218 the Mongol general Jebe invaded; Kuchlug fled and was captured and executed, and the Qara Khitai lands passed peacefully, in many places by welcome, into the Mongol Empire. A loyalist offshoot, the Qutlugh-Khanid or Kirmanid dynasty founded by Buraq Hajib in Kirman (Persia), survived as a Mongol vassal until 1306.
