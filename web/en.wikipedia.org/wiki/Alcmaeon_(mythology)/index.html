Alcmaeon (mythology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Greek mythology, Alcmaeon (Ancient Greek: Ἀλκμαίων Alkmaiōn), was the son of Amphiaraus and Eriphyle. He was one of the Epigoni, the sons of the Argive heroes who had fought and been killed in the first Theban war, and who avenged their fathers by conquering Thebes a generation later. Alcmaeon is best known for killing his own mother, Eriphyle, in obedience to his father's dying command, and for his subsequent madness and wandering in search of purification.

Mythology

Amphiaraus, a seer, had foreseen that he would die in the expedition of the Seven against Thebes, but was compelled to go because his wife Eriphyle had been bribed with the necklace of Harmonia to persuade him. Before departing, Amphiaraus exacted an oath from his sons to avenge his death. Alcmaeon led the Epigoni — among them Aegialeus, Diomedes and Sthenelus — in the successful second assault on Thebes, in which the city was sacked.

On his return, reminded of his oath, Alcmaeon consulted the oracle of Apollo at Delphi and, with its sanction, killed Eriphyle. Pursued by the Erinyes (Furies) and driven mad, he wandered through Greece seeking purification. He was cleansed by King Phegeus of Psophis, whose daughter Arsinoe (or Alphesiboea) he married, giving her the necklace and robe of Harmonia. The land of Psophis then became barren while he remained there, and the oracle declared he must settle in a land that had not existed when he killed his mother. He finally found peace at the newly formed silt deposits at the mouth of the river Achelous, where he married Callirrhoe, daughter of the river god. Phegeus's sons, learning of the deception over the necklace, ambushed and killed Alcmaeon.

In literature

The story was treated in the lost epic Alcmaeonis and by several tragedians; Euripides wrote two plays on the subject, now lost. Alcmaeon's fate — the avenger who becomes a matricide — made him a standard example in Greek literature of inherited guilt and the curse on the house of Amphiaraus.
