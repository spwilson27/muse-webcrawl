Colmar

Colmar (French pronunciation: [kɔlmaʁ]; Alsatian: Colmer; German: Kolmar) is a city and commune in the Haut-Rhin department and Grand Est region of north-eastern France. It is the prefecture (capital) of the Haut-Rhin department and the seat of the largest arrondissement in the region. With a population of roughly 70,000, Colmar lies on the Alsatian Wine Route and is considered the "capital of Alsatian wine" (capitale des vins d'Alsace).

The "Little Venice" (Petite Venise) quarter of Colmar.

Geography

Colmar is located about 64 kilometres (40 miles) south of Strasbourg, between the Vosges mountains to the west and the River Rhine to the east. The River Lauch flows through the town. The climate is relatively dry and sunny by French standards — Colmar is among the driest cities in France — a microclimate favourable to viticulture, thanks to the rain shadow of the Vosges.

History

Colmar was first mentioned in 823 as Columbaria. It joined the Décapole, a league of ten imperial cities of Alsace, in 1354. During the Thirty Years' War the town was taken by Swedish troops in 1632. Under the Treaties of Nijmegen (1678–79) it passed to France, though it was annexed by Germany in 1871 following the Franco-Prussian War. Colmar alternated between French and German control, returning to France after the First World War, being annexed by Nazi Germany in 1940, and liberated in February 1945 after the Battle of the Colmar Pocket.

Main sights

The old town is noted for its well-preserved half-timbered houses, many dating from the Middle Ages and Renaissance. Notable buildings include the Maison Pfister (1537), the Maison des Têtes, and St Martin's Church (Collégiale Saint-Martin), a large Gothic church built in the 13th and 14th centuries. The Unterlinden Museum, housed in a former Dominican convent, holds the famous Isenheim Altarpiece by Matthias Grünewald. Colmar was the birthplace of Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi, sculptor of the Statue of Liberty; the Bartholdi Museum preserves his house and works. The "Little Venice" quarter, with its canalside houses, was once the district of market gardeners, fishermen, and tanners.

Economy and wine

Colmar sits at the heart of the Alsace wine region, known for white wines such as Riesling, Gewürztraminer, and Pinot gris. Wine-growing and wine trading are central to the local economy, alongside tourism and some industry.
