Dal Khalsa

Dal Khalsa was the collective army and governing confederacy of the Sikh Khalsa in 18th-century Punjab. Formed in 1748 by uniting the various Sikh fighting bands (jathas) into a single army under the authority of the Sarbat Khalsa assembly, it became the principal military force resisting Mughal and Afghan power in the region.

History

At a Sarbat Khalsa gathering at Amritsar in 1748, the Sikhs resolved to merge their eleven major jathas into one army, the Dal Khalsa, divided into two main camps: the Budha Dal (army of the elders) and the Taruna Dal (army of the young). Nawab Kapur Singh was honoured as its supreme religious leader, while Jassa Singh Ahluwalia served as commander-in-chief.

Through decades of guerrilla warfare against the Mughal governors of Lahore and the invasions of Ahmad Shah Durrani, the Dal Khalsa gradually took control of much of Punjab. By the 1760s its constituent misls (confederacies) held territory from the Indus to the Yamuna. The Dal Khalsa structure faded as Ranjit Singh unified the misls into the Sikh Empire from 1799 onwards.
