Toluene diisocyanate

Toluene diisocyanate (TDI) is an organic compound with the formula CH3C6H3(NCO)2. Two of the six possible isomers are commercially important: 2,4-TDI and 2,6-TDI, usually sold as an 80:20 mixture (TDI-80). It is a colourless to pale-yellow liquid and one of the most-produced diisocyanates in the world.

Production and uses

TDI is manufactured by nitrating toluene to dinitrotoluene, hydrogenating to toluenediamine, and phosgenating the diamine. Reacted with polyols it forms polyurethanes, and TDI-based flexible foams dominate the market for furniture cushioning, mattresses, automotive seats and carpet underlay.

Health hazards

TDI is a potent respiratory sensitiser: inhalation can cause occupational asthma, and it is one of the most common chemical causes of the condition. Handling therefore requires strict exposure controls; the compound also reacts vigorously with water, releasing carbon dioxide.
