Stretching

Stretching is a form of physical exercise in which a specific muscle or tendon (or muscle group) is deliberately expanded and flexed in order to improve the muscle's felt elasticity and achieve comfortable muscle tone. The result is a feeling of increased muscle control, flexibility, and range of motion. Stretching is also used therapeutically to alleviate cramps and to improve function in daily activities by increasing range of motion.

Types

  • Static stretching: holding a stretch in a challenging but comfortable position for a period of time, usually 10 to 30 seconds.
  • Dynamic stretching: moving parts of the body through a full range of motion in a controlled manner.
  • Ballistic stretching: using bouncing movements to push a joint beyond its normal range; generally discouraged because of injury risk.
  • PNF stretching: proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation, involving contraction and relaxation of the target muscle, often with a partner.
  • Passive and active stretching: distinguished by whether an external force or the opposing muscles produce the stretch.

Physiology and research

Stretching affects the muscle-tendon unit through neural and mechanical mechanisms, including reduced neural excitability and changes in the viscoelastic properties of tissue. Research on whether pre-exercise static stretching prevents injury or improves performance is mixed; prolonged static stretching immediately before activity can temporarily reduce strength and power, while regular flexibility training increases range of motion. Dynamic warm-ups are generally recommended before sport.
