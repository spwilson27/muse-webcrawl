Adams spectral sequence

In algebraic topology, the Adams spectral sequence is a spectral sequence introduced by J. Frank Adams (1958) which computes the stable homotopy groups of topological spaces. Like all spectral sequences, it is a computational tool; it relates homology theory to what is now known as stable homotopy theory. It is a reformulation using homological algebra, and an extension, of a technique called "killing homotopy groups" used by the French school of Henri Cartan and Jean-Pierre Serre.

Formulation

The E2-term of the Adams spectral sequence is given by the Ext groups

E2s,t = ExtAs,t (H*(X), H*(Y))

computed over the mod-p Steenrod algebra, and the sequence converges, under suitable finiteness hypotheses, to the p-completed stable homotopy classes of maps [X, Y] — more precisely to the groups πt−s of the function spectrum F(X, Y), completed at p. The filtration is by s, and the differentials have bidegree dr: (s, t) → (s + r, t + r − 1).

Classical Adams spectral sequence

The classical case takes Y to be the sphere spectrum, so that the spectral sequence computes the stable homotopy groups of spheres, among the most difficult invariants in algebraic topology. The computation of the E2-page can be aided by algebraic techniques such as the May spectral sequence, and the deduction of the actual homotopy groups from the spectral sequence requires resolving extension problems. Adams himself used the sequence to prove results about the Hopf invariant one problem, showing that Hopf invariant one elements exist only in dimensions 1, 2, 4, and 8 — a result later sharpened using K-theory in the Adams operations.

Adams–Novikov spectral sequence

A generalization, the Adams–Novikov spectral sequence, replaces ordinary cohomology with a generalized cohomology theory such as complex cobordism MU or Brown–Peterson cohomology. Its E2-term involves Ext over the Hopf algebroid MU*MU. Though harder to compute, it converges more directly to the stable homotopy groups of spheres and often converges faster.
