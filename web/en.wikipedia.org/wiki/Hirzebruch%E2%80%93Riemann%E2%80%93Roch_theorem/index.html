Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem

The Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem is a far-reaching generalization of the classical Riemann–Roch theorem from algebraic curves to all complex manifolds. It was proved by Friedrich Hirzebruch in his 1954 dissertation work, using the then-new theory of cobordism and characteristic classes, and is a special case of the later Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch and Atiyah–Singer index theorems.

Statement

For a holomorphic vector bundle E over a compact complex manifold X, the theorem equates the holomorphic Euler characteristic — the alternating sum of the dimensions of the sheaf cohomology groups of E — with an integral over X of characteristic classes: χ(X, E) = ∫_X ch(E) · td(X), where ch is the Chern character and td(X) the Todd class of the tangent bundle of X. For a Riemann surface and a line bundle it reduces to the classical formula involving the degree of the bundle and the genus of the curve.

Significance

The theorem linked algebraic geometry with topology and analysis, showed that arithmetic genera are topological invariants, and opened the way to Grothendieck's functorial reformulation and to the index theory of elliptic operators. Its special cases compute dimensions of spaces of sections (hence of modular and automorphic forms) that are essential throughout modern geometry.
