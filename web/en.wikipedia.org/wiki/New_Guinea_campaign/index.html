New Guinea campaign

The New Guinea campaign of the Pacific War lasted from January 1942 until the end of the war in August 1945. It began when Japanese forces landed at Rabaul and invaded the Australian-administered Territory of New Guinea and the Dutch colony of western New Guinea, and it continued through a long series of Allied counter-offensives fought in some of the most difficult terrain and climate encountered in the Second World War.

Course of the campaign

After seizing Rabaul and Lae in early 1942, Japanese forces attempted to take Port Moresby, first by sea — turned back at the Battle of the Coral Sea — and then overland along the Kokoda Track across the Owen Stanley Range. Australian forces, later joined by Americans, halted the advance and counterattacked. The battles of Milne Bay, Buna–Gona and Wau marked the first major land defeats of Japan.

From 1943, General Douglas MacArthur's South West Pacific Area forces advanced along New Guinea's north coast in a sequence of amphibious landings — Salamaua–Lae, the Huon Peninsula, Aitape and Hollandia, Biak and Morotai — bypassing and isolating the great Japanese base at Rabaul and the garrison at Wewak. Fighting continued in the interior until Japan's surrender.

Conditions

Disease, especially malaria, dense jungle, extreme rainfall and rudimentary logistics caused casualties rivalling those from combat, and the campaign relied heavily on Papuan carriers and labourers, remembered in Australia as the "Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels".
