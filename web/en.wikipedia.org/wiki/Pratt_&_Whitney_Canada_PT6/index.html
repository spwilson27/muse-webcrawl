Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6

The Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6 is a turboprop aircraft engine produced by Pratt & Whitney Canada. First run in 1960 and entering service in 1964, it is one of the most popular turboprop engines in history, with tens of thousands built and hundreds of millions of flight hours accumulated across more than 100 applications.

Design

The PT6 is a free-turbine turboshaft design in which the gas generator and the power turbine are mechanically independent. Air enters at the rear of the engine and flows forward — a reverse-flow layout that keeps the engine compact and simplifies installation. The power turbine drives the propeller through a reduction gearbox at the front. Power output across the family ranges from roughly 500 to over 2,000 shaft horsepower. Variants with a shaft output rather than a propeller drive, designated PT6T (Twin-Pac), power helicopters such as the Bell 212 and UH-1N.

Applications

The PT6 powers an exceptionally wide range of aircraft, including the Beechcraft King Air and Super King Air, the de Havilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter, the Pilatus PC-12, the Cessna Caravan, the Air Tractor agricultural aircraft, and the Beechcraft T-6 Texan II military trainer. Its reliability and tolerance of austere operating conditions have made it the standard engine for utility and commuter aviation. See also turboprop.
