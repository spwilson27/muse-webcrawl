Altos Hornos de México, S.A.B. de C.V. (AHMSA; Spanish for "Blast Furnaces of Mexico") is one of Mexico's largest steel companies, headquartered in Monclova, Coahuila. For decades it operated the country's biggest integrated steelworks, closely associated with its long-time chairman Alonso Ancira. The company entered severe financial difficulties in the 2010s–2020s, suspending most production and entering bankruptcy proceedings, while remaining a central actor in Mexican industrial politics.


The AHMSA steelworks at Monclova, Coahuila

History

AHMSA was founded in 1942, during Mexico's wartime industrialisation drive, and began operations in 1944 with Monclova chosen for its proximity to the iron ore of Cerro del Mercado and the coking coal of the Sabinas basin. The company expanded steadily through the post-war decades, privatising in 1991 when it was sold to the Grupo Acerero del Norte headed by Alonso Ancira and Xavier Autrey. In 1999 AHMSA suspended payments on roughly US$2.3 billion of debt, one of the largest defaults in Mexican corporate history; it emerged from the suspension of payments process only in 2016.

Operations

The Monclova complex included blast furnaces, a steel shop with continuous casting, hot-strip and plate mills, and cold-rolling and tinning lines; subsidiaries mined iron ore at Hércules (through Minera del Norte) and coal in the Muzquiz region. AHMSA produced flat and long products for the automotive, construction, energy and container industries and was for many years Mexico's largest steel producer alongside Ternium.

Agronitrogenados scandal and decline

In 2019 chairman Alonso Ancira was arrested in Spain in connection with the sale of the idle Agronitrogenados fertiliser plant to the state oil company Pemex at an allegedly inflated price during the Emilio Lozoya administration; he was extradited to Mexico in 2021 and agreed to a reparations settlement reportedly exceeding US$200 million. Burdened by debts to Pemex, the tax authorities and suppliers, AHMSA halted most steelmaking in late 2022–2023, and in 2024 a Mexican court declared the company bankrupt, with the federal government exploring options to preserve employment in Monclova.
