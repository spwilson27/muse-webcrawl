Thin Red Line (1854 battle)

The Thin Red Line
Conflict: Crimean War (Battle of Balaclava)
Date: 25 October 1854
Combatants: British 93rd Highlanders vs. Russian cavalry

The "thin red line" refers to an episode of the Battle of Balaclava during the Crimean War on 25 October 1854, in which the British 93rd (Sutherland Highlanders) Regiment of Foot, drawn up in a line two men deep rather than the conventional square, repelled a Russian cavalry charge near the village of Kadikoi.

The action

Under the command of Sir Colin Campbell, roughly 500 Highlanders — aided by Royal Marines and Turkish troops who largely fled — stood in line across the path of about 400 Russian cavalrymen advancing on the road to Balaclava. Refusing to form square, Campbell told his men, "There is no retreat from here, men. You must die where you stand." Their disciplined volley fire at long range checked the charge, and the Russians wheeled away.

The phrase

War correspondent William Howard Russell described the Highlanders as a "thin red streak tipped with a line of steel", which was condensed into the famous phrase "the thin red line". The expression came to symbolise steadfast defence by British infantry and later entered general usage for any small force holding a critical position; it also inspired Robert Gibb's 1881 painting The Thin Red Line.
