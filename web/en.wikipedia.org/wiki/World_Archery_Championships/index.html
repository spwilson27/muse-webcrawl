The World Archery Championships are a series of competitions in archery organised by World Archery (formerly FITA). The first championships were held in Lwów, Poland (then part of Poland, now Lviv, Ukraine) in 1931. The outdoor target championships are contested biennially in odd-numbered years and include individual, team and mixed team events in the recurve and compound bow divisions, for men and women.

History

Early championships were dominated by European archers. Following archery's return to the Olympic programme in 1972, the recurve division grew in prominence. The compound division was added in the 1990s, reflecting the bow's popularity in North America. South Korea has been the dominant nation in recurve competition, while the United States has been highly successful in compound events.

Other championships

World Archery also organises separate world championships for indoor archery (18 metres), field archery, 3D archery, para-archery, youth and masters categories. The outdoor championships also serve, in the year before the Olympic Games, as a primary quota qualification event for the Olympic archery tournament.
