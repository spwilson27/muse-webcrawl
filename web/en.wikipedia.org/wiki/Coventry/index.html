Coventry

Coventry
Region: West Midlands, England
Historic county: Warwickshire
Status: City and metropolitan borough
Population: approx. 345,000

Coventry is a city and metropolitan borough in the West Midlands of England. Historically part of Warwickshire, it is the second-largest city in the West Midlands region after Birmingham, from which it lies about 19 miles (31 km) to the east-south-east. Coventry is known historically for its cloth and ribbon trades, its watchmaking, and, in the twentieth century, its motor industry.

History

The settlement grew around a Saxon nunnery and the priory founded by Leofric, Earl of Mercia, and his wife Godgifu — remembered as Lady Godiva, who according to legend rode naked through the city to protest against her husband's taxes. Coventry became a major centre of the medieval cloth trade and one of England's largest cities. In the industrial era it became a centre of ribbon weaving, watchmaking, and then bicycle manufacture; firms such as Rover and the cycle companies of Coventry helped give birth to the British motor industry, and the city became a hub of car production.

On the night of 14 November 1940, Coventry suffered a devastating Luftwaffe air raid, the Coventry Blitz, in which the medieval St Michael's Cathedral was destroyed and much of the city centre laid waste. The ruined old cathedral was preserved beside Basil Spence's new cathedral, consecrated in 1962, as a symbol of reconciliation. The rebuilt post-war city centre was a landmark of modernist planning.

Culture and education

Coventry is home to the University of Warwick, on the city's southern edge, and Coventry University. The Herbert Art Gallery and Museum, the Belgrade Theatre, and the Coventry Transport Museum are among its cultural institutions. In the late 1970s the city was a centre of the 2 Tone ska revival, associated with bands such as The Specials. Coventry was the UK City of Culture for 2021.

Transport

Coventry railway station is on the West Coast Main Line, with services to London Euston and Birmingham. The city is served by the M6, M40, and M69 motorways and by the Coventry Canal.
