Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi (10 December 1804 – 18 February 1851) was a German mathematician who made fundamental contributions to elliptic functions, dynamics, differential equations, determinants and number theory. Along with Niels Henrik Abel, he founded the theory of elliptic functions, and he is regarded as one of the most inspiring teachers of his age.

Life

Born in Potsdam to a prosperous Jewish banker family, Jacobi was educated at the University of Berlin, obtaining his doctorate in 1825. He taught at the University of Königsberg from 1826 to 1844, where he created a famous seminar in mathematical research, before returning to Berlin under royal patronage. His health, weakened by overwork and diabetes, failed in 1851, and he died of smallpox at the age of 46.

Contributions

Jacobi's Fundamenta nova theoriae functionum ellipticarum (1829) transformed the theory of elliptic integrals. He introduced the Jacobian determinant of a transformation, the Jacobi symbol in number theory, and the Jacobi identity fundamental to Lie algebras. In mechanics, the Hamilton–Jacobi equation and his work on the last multiplier were foundational; his solution of the geodesic problem on an ellipsoid and of the dynamics of a rotating fluid mass (Jacobi ellipsoids) remain standard. His name attaches to Jacobi polynomials, the Jacobi method for eigenvalues, and the Jacobi triple product.
