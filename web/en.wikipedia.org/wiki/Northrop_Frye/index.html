Northrop Frye

Herman Northrop Frye (14 July 1912 – 23 January 1991) was a Canadian literary critic and theorist, considered one of the most influential of the 20th century.

Career

Born in Sherbrooke, Quebec, and raised in Moncton, Frye studied at Victoria College, University of Toronto, and at Oxford. He taught at Victoria College for his entire career. His first major work, Fearful Symmetry (1947), a study of William Blake, established his reputation. His Anatomy of Criticism (1957) proposed a systematic theory of literature based on archetypes, genres and modes, arguing criticism should be a coherent, scientific discipline.

Later work

The Great Code: The Bible and Literature (1982) and Words with Power (1990) explored the Bible's influence on Western literature and imagination. Frye received numerous honours and was made a Companion of the Order of Canada.
