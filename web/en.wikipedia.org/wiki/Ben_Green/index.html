Ben Green

Born: 27 February 1977, Bristol, England

Field: Mathematics (combinatorics, number theory)

Institution: University of Oxford

Known for: Green–Tao theorem

Ben Joseph Green FRS (born 27 February 1977) is a British mathematician specialising in combinatorics and analytic number theory. He is the Waynflete Professor of Pure Mathematics at the University of Oxford and a fellow of Magdalen College, Oxford.

Career and work

Green was educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, where he studied under Timothy Gowers and won the Smith's Prize. He represented the United Kingdom at the International Mathematical Olympiad as a schoolboy. After positions at Princeton, Cambridge (Trinity College), and McGill, he moved to Oxford in 2013 as Waynflete Professor.

His best-known result, proved with Terence Tao in 2004, is the Green–Tao theorem: the sequence of prime numbers contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. The work combined ideas from ergodic theory, additive combinatorics, and analytic number theory, and opened a broad programme in the area. Green has also made major contributions to additive combinatorics, including work on sum-free sets, Fourier analysis on finite groups, and the structure of approximate groups and arithmetic progressions in sets of integers.

Awards

Green has received the Clay Research Award (2004), the Salem Prize (2005), the Whitehead Prize, the Sylvester Medal (2014), and was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 2010.
