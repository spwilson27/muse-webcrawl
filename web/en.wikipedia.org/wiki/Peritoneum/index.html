Peritoneum

The peritoneum is the serous membrane forming the lining of the abdominal cavity and covering most of the intra-abdominal organs. It consists of a layer of mesothelium supported by connective tissue, and is divided into the parietal peritoneum, lining the abdominal wall, and the visceral peritoneum, covering the organs.

Structure

Folds of peritoneum form the mesenteries, which suspend organs and carry their blood vessels, nerves and lymphatics, and the greater and lesser omenta. The space between the layers is the peritoneal cavity, containing a small amount of lubricating serous fluid. Organs fully covered are called intraperitoneal, while organs behind the peritoneum, such as the kidneys, are retroperitoneal.

Clinical significance

Inflammation of the peritoneum, peritonitis, is a medical emergency usually caused by perforation or infection. Fluid accumulation is called ascites. The membrane's absorptive surface is exploited in peritoneal dialysis for kidney failure.
