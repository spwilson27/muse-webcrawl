Skirt

A skirt is the lower part of a dress or a separate outer garment that covers a person from the waist downwards. At its simplest, a skirt can be a draped garment made out of a single piece of material. Most skirts are fitted to the body at the waist or hips and fuller below, tapering to a hem of varying length.

History

Skirt-like garments have been worn by both men and women in many cultures since prehistory; a string skirt dating to about 20,000 years ago was found in Siberia. In ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome, draped and wrapped lower garments were universal. In Western Europe, skirts became predominantly female attire from the sixteenth century onward, evolving through farthingales, crinolines, and bustles in the nineteenth century. In contrast, men in some cultures continue to wear skirt-like garments such as the Scottish kilt, the Greek fustanella, and the Pacific lavalava.

Twentieth century

Hemlines rose dramatically in the 1920s, and the twentieth century saw rapid cycling of styles: the pencil skirt of the 1940s and 1950s, Mary Quant's miniskirt of the 1960s, the midi and maxi reactions of the 1970s, and denim and prairie skirts. Trousers for women became socially routine, making the skirt an optional rather than obligatory garment.

Styles

Common styles include the A-line, pencil, pleated, wrap, circle, tiered, and tube skirts, classified by length as micro, mini, knee-length, midi, tea-length, and maxi. Skirts appear in school and office uniforms, in kilts and ceremonial dress, and as part of dresses across the world.
