Charles Maurice de Talleyrand-Périgord

Born2 February 1754, Paris, France
Died17 May 1838, Paris, France
OfficesForeign Minister of France; Bishop of Autun; Ambassador to Britain

Charles Maurice de Talleyrand-Périgord, 1st Prince of Benevento (1754–1838), was a French statesman and diplomat who served under regimes from the French Revolution through the Napoleonic era to the Bourbon Restoration. Renowned for his political survival and negotiating skill, he represented France at the Congress of Vienna in 1814–1815.

Early career

Born into an aristocratic family and prevented by a foot injury from a military career, Talleyrand entered the Church and became Bishop of Autun. During the Revolution he supported the nationalization of Church property and served in the National Assembly.

Diplomacy

Talleyrand served as foreign minister under the Directory and under Napoleon, though he grew disillusioned with Napoleon's ambitions. After 1814 he negotiated favorable terms for defeated France at Vienna, helping secure the restoration of the Bourbon monarchy.

Legacy

Talleyrand remains a byword for diplomatic cunning, famously described as treating speech as a means of concealing thought.
