Locomotion No. 1 (originally named Active) is an early steam locomotive built by Robert Stephenson and Company in 1825 for the Stockton and Darlington Railway in north-east England — the world's first public railway to use steam locomotives. Designed under George Stephenson, it hauled the inaugural train on 27 September 1825, carrying coal and several hundred passengers in open wagons at speeds of up to about 24 km/h (15 mph), an event often taken to mark the birth of the railway age.

Design and service

The locomotive is a 0-4-0 with two vertical cylinders driving the wheels through connecting rods, a design derived from Stephenson's earlier Killingworth engines. It worked coal trains between the Durham coalfield and Darlington and Stockton for a number of years, though its flanged wheels and primitive boiler limited its career; after an explosion of its boiler in 1828 (which killed its driver) it was rebuilt and returned to service, later being relegated to stationary or minor duties as more advanced locomotives appeared.

Preservation

Withdrawn in the 1850s, Locomotion was preserved by the railway company — one of the earliest acts of industrial heritage preservation — and displayed for many years on a plinth at Darlington's Bank Top station. It steamed again for the railway centenary in 1875 anniversaries and the 1925 centenary celebrations. It is now displayed at the Head of Steam — Darlington Railway Museum, and a working replica (built in 1975 for the 150th anniversary) operates at the Beamish Museum. For the Railway 200 celebrations in 2025 the original was exhibited as a centrepiece of the bicentenary.
