Christianisation of Anglo-Saxon England

The Christianisation of Anglo-Saxon England was the process, mainly during the 7th century, by which the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms converted from Germanic paganism to Christianity. It began with the Gregorian mission of Augustine of Canterbury in 597 to Kent.

Missions

Pope Gregory the Great sent Augustine, who converted King Æthelberht of Kent. Irish missionaries from Iona, led by Aidan, evangelized Northumbria from Lindisfarne after 635. The Synod of Whitby (664) aligned Northumbria with Roman practice. Mercia, Wessex, Sussex and the Isle of Wight converted over following decades; Sussex was the last mainland kingdom, in the 680s.

Effects

Conversion brought literacy, Latin learning, stone church building, and closer ties to continental Europe, though pagan practices persisted for generations.
