David Harel (Hebrew: דוד הראל; born 12 April 1950) is an Israeli computer scientist, long a professor at the Weizmann Institute of Science in Rehovot. He is best known as the inventor of statecharts, a visual formalism for specifying the behaviour of complex reactive systems, which became a core part of the Unified Modeling Language (UML) and of industrial model-based design tools.

Education and career

Harel was born in London and raised in Israel. He earned degrees from Bar-Ilan University and Tel Aviv University and completed his PhD at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1978, at record speed (he is reputed to have finished in under two years). He joined the Weizmann Institute, serving as dean of the Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science, and has held visiting positions at Carnegie Mellon, Cornell and Edinburgh, among others.

Statecharts

In his 1987 paper "Statecharts: A Visual Formalism for Complex Systems", Harel extended classical state machines with hierarchy (states within states), orthogonality (concurrent regions) and broadcast communication, producing diagrams that engineers could actually use to model avionics, telecommunications and automotive systems. The language was commercialised in tools such as Statemate and Rhapsody — the company I-Logix, which Harel co-founded to develop them — and its ideas were adopted into UML state diagrams in the 1990s.

Other contributions

Harel's earlier work on dynamic logic (1979–1984) shaped the theory of reasoning about programs. With students and colleagues he developed live sequence charts, a scenario-based specification method, and the "play-in/play-out" approach to programming systems by demonstrating example behaviour. His textbook Algorithmics: The Spirit of Computing (1987, with Yishai Feldman in later editions) became a classic popular exposition of computer science, and his Computers Ltd.: What They Really Can't Do (2000) surveys the limits of computation. In the 2000s he turned to systems biology, modelling the development of the C. elegans vulva and other biological systems.

Honours

Harel received the Israel Prize in exact sciences in 2004 and the ACM Karl V. Karlstrom Outstanding Educator Award in 2007. He is a fellow of the ACM, the IEEE and several national academies, and has been active in Israeli public life, including as a vice-president of the Israel Academy of Sciences and Humanities.
