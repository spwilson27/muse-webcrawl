Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL) is one of the United States Department of Energy national laboratories, managed by the Department of Energy's Office of Science. PNNL research advances scientific frontiers in the environmental, biological, computational and physical sciences — including chemistry, Earth systems and data science — and provides national-security capabilities in areas such as nonproliferation and cybersecurity. The main campus is in Richland, Washington, adjacent to the Hanford Site, with a marine research laboratory at Sequim and offices in several other cities.

History

PNNL traces its origins to research at the Hanford Works, the World War II plutonium production complex on the Columbia River. In 1965 Battelle Memorial Institute won the contract to operate the Hanford laboratories as a multiprogram research organization, which grew into today's national laboratory; Battelle has operated PNNL for the DOE ever since. The laboratory played large roles in Hanford environmental cleanup science, in atmospheric and climate research, and in energy storage and grid research centered in Richland's Discovery Hall and related facilities.

Research areas and facilities

The laboratory's programs span fundamental chemistry and materials science, biological and environmental molecular science, climate and Earth-system modeling (including work with global aerosol and cloud models), electricity-grid modernization and energy-storage research relevant to the Pacific Northwest, nuclear chemistry, and national-security science such as radiation detection for border and port security. Its signature user facility, the Environmental Molecular Sciences Laboratory (EMSL), opened in 1997 and provides mass spectrometry, microscopy, NMR and supercomputing resources to thousands of visiting researchers. PNNL also manages the DOE Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) user facility's data and sites.

Impact

PNNL has consistently ranked among the DOE laboratories with the most patents and technology licenses, with technologies such as the first single-pass radio-frequency identification systems, advanced battery materials, and screening technologies used at airports worldwide.
