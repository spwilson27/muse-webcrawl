The Enzyme Commission number (EC number) is a numerical classification scheme for enzymes, based on the chemical reactions they catalyze. As a system of enzyme nomenclature, every EC number is associated with a recommended name for the corresponding enzyme-catalyzed reaction.

EC numbers do not specify enzymes as such, but enzyme-catalyzed reactions: two different enzymes that catalyze the same reaction receive the same EC number, while one enzyme catalyzing two different reactions receives two different EC numbers.

Format

Every enzyme code consists of the letters "EC" followed by four numbers separated by periods, representing a progressively finer classification. For example, the alcohol dehydrogenase reaction is EC 1.1.1.1. The first number defines one of seven top-level classes:

ClassNameReaction type
EC 1OxidoreductasesOxidation/reduction reactions
EC 2TransferasesTransfer of a functional group
EC 3HydrolasesHydrolysis of various bonds
EC 4LyasesCleavage of bonds other than by hydrolysis or oxidation
EC 5IsomerasesIsomerization changes within a molecule
EC 6LigasesJoining of two molecules with covalent bonds
EC 7TranslocasesMovement of ions or molecules across membranes

The seventh class, translocases, was added in 2018; before that the scheme had six classes.

History

The scheme was first published in 1961 by the Enzyme Commission of the International Union of Biochemistry, and is now maintained by the Nomenclature Committee of the International Union of Biochemistry and Molecular Biology (IUBMB), in consultation with IUPAC. The authoritative database of EC numbers is ExplorEnz.
