West Lothian question


The question concerns voting rights at Westminster after devolution.

The West Lothian question is a constitutional anomaly in the United Kingdom: why should Members of Parliament from Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland be able to vote at Westminster on matters that affect only England, when English MPs cannot vote on the same matters as they affect the devolved nations, because those powers are exercised by the devolved legislatures? The question is named after the former Scottish constituency of West Lothian, whose MP, Tam Dalyell, raised it repeatedly during the devolution debates of the 1970s; the phrase itself was coined by the Ulster Unionist MP Enoch Powell.

Background

The question became practical after the establishment of the Scottish Parliament, the Welsh Assembly (now Senedd), and the Northern Ireland Assembly in 1999. Matters such as health and education in Scotland were decided in Edinburgh, yet Scottish MPs at Westminster could still vote on health and education policy for England.

Proposed solutions

Proposals have included an English parliament, English regional assemblies, and "English votes for English laws" (EVEL). EVEL procedures were adopted in the House of Commons in 2015, requiring the consent of English (or English and Welsh) MPs for England-only legislation, but they were suspended in 2020 and abolished in 2021, leaving the question formally unresolved.
