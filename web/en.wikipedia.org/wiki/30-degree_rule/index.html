The 30-degree rule is a guideline in filmmaking stating that when editing together two shots of the same subject, the camera position for the second shot should differ from the first by at least 30 degrees (and, in many formulations, the framing should also change noticeably in size).

Purpose

If two successive shots of the same subject are taken from nearly the same angle, the cut appears as an awkward, unintentional jump — the subject seems to shift position slightly. Moving the camera at least 30 degrees makes the change of viewpoint read as deliberate, preserving the audience's spatial orientation and the flow of the action.

Relation to other conventions

The rule complements the 180-degree rule, which keeps the camera on one side of an imaginary axis between subjects. Deliberate violations — the jump cut — have been used stylistically since the French New Wave, notably in Jean-Luc Godard's Breathless (1960).
