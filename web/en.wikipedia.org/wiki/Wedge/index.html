Wedge

A wedge is a triangularly shaped tool, essentially a portable inclined plane, used to separate two objects or portions of an object, lift an object, or hold an object in place. It converts a force applied to its blunt end into forces perpendicular to its inclined surfaces.

Mechanical advantage

The mechanical advantage of a wedge is the ratio of its length to its width: a long, thin wedge multiplies force more than a short, thick one, though it must travel farther. Friction has a large effect on wedge behaviour — wedges such as nails and doorstops rely on friction to stay in place.

Examples

Axes, chisels, knives, and splitting mauls are wedges; so are shims, doorstops, and the wedge used in quarrying to split rock. The wedge is traditionally counted among the six classical simple machines.
