Ménière's disease

SpecialtyOtolaryngology
SymptomsVertigo, tinnitus, hearing loss, aural fullness
Named afterProsper Ménière

Ménière's disease is a disorder of the inner ear characterised by episodes of spontaneous vertigo, fluctuating sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus, and a feeling of fullness or pressure in the affected ear. It is named after the French physician Prosper Ménière, who in 1861 first reported that vertigo could originate from inner ear dysfunction.

Signs and symptoms

Attacks of vertigo typically last from 20 minutes to 12 hours and are often accompanied by nausea. Hearing loss is initially fluctuating and affects low frequencies, but over years it tends to become permanent and involve all frequencies. In most cases only one ear is affected, though the condition becomes bilateral in a substantial minority of patients.

Cause and diagnosis

The condition is associated with endolymphatic hydrops, an excess of fluid (endolymph) in the inner ear, though the underlying cause remains uncertain; genetics, viral infections, allergies, and vascular factors have all been proposed. Diagnosis is clinical, based on the characteristic pattern of two or more spontaneous vertigo episodes with documented hearing loss, and exclusion of other causes such as vestibular migraine.

Treatment

There is no cure. Management includes low-salt diet and lifestyle measures, betahistine, diuretics, intratympanic injections, and, for severe disabling cases, surgical procedures such as endolymphatic sac surgery or labyrinthectomy.
