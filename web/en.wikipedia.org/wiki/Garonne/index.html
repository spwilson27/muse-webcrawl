Garonne


The Garonne at Bordeaux.

The Garonne (French: Garonne [ɡaʁɔn]; Occitan and Catalan: Garona) is a river in southwest France and northern Spain. It rises in the central Pyrenees in the Val d'Aran, Spain, and flows for about 529 km (329 mi) before joining the Dordogne at the Bec d'Ambès to form the Gironde estuary, which empties into the Atlantic Ocean. Its main cities are Toulouse and Bordeaux.

Course

From its source near the Port de la Bonaigua, the Garonne flows north into France past Saint-Gaudens, then northeast through Toulouse, and northwest through Agen and Bordeaux. Below Bordeaux it merges with the Dordogne to form the Gironde, the largest estuary in Europe, subject to a notable tidal bore (the mascaret).

Navigation and canals

The river is navigable by ocean-going vessels as far as Bordeaux. The Canal de Garonne links the river at Toulouse to the Canal du Midi, together forming the Canal des Deux Mers connecting the Atlantic to the Mediterranean. The Garonne has a long history of severe flooding, notably the catastrophic flood of 1875 at Toulouse.

Ecology

The Garonne historically supported Atlantic salmon and the European sturgeon, now critically endangered; fish passes and restocking programmes aim to restore migratory species.
