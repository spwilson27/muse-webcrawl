Paleosiberian languages

The Paleosiberian languages (also spelled Paleo-Siberian, Palaeosiberian, or Paleoasiatic) are several language isolates and small language families spoken by ethnic groups in remote regions of Siberia. They are not known to be genealogically related to one another; the term is a geographical grouping of convenience, collecting the languages of the region that do not belong to the larger Tungusic, Turkic, Mongolic, Uralic, or Yeniseian families—though Yeniseian is sometimes included.

Groups

  • Chukotko-Kamchatkan languages of the Chukotka and Kamchatka peninsulas, including Chukchi, Koryak, Alutor, and the extinct Kerek, as well as Itelmen (sometimes placed in its own family).
  • Yukaghir languages of northeastern Siberia, of which Tundra Yukaghir and Kolyma Yukaghir survive with only a handful of speakers.
  • Nivkh, a language isolate spoken around the Amur estuary and on Sakhalin.
  • Yeniseian languages of central Siberia, of which only Ket survives; the family has attracted attention for the proposed Dene–Yeniseian link to the Na-Dene languages of North America.
  • Ainu, spoken historically in Hokkaido, Sakhalin, and the Kurils, is sometimes included as Paleoasiatic in a broader sense.

Status

Most Paleosiberian languages are severely endangered, having been displaced by Russian (and, earlier, by expanding Tungusic and Turkic languages) over the last several centuries. Many are now spoken only by small numbers of elderly speakers, though some, such as Chukchi and Koryak, retain larger speaker communities.
