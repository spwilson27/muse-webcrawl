Joseph François Dupleix (1697–1763) was governor-general of the French establishments in India and the chief architect of French imperial ambitions there during the mid-18th century. From his base at Pondicherry, he intervened skilfully in the succession disputes of Indian states, built alliances with local rulers, and raised sepoy forces to extend French influence across the Carnatic and the Deccan.

Downfall

His campaigns against the British East India Company, whose champion was Robert Clive, proved costly, and the French Compagnie des Indes, dissatisfied with the expense, recalled him in 1754. The concessions he had won were largely surrendered in negotiation, and Dupleix died in obscurity and debt in Paris, though later generations in France honoured him as a founder of empire.
