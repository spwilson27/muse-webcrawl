Aerial reconnaissance

DisciplineImagery intelligence (IMINT)
PlatformsManned reconnaissance aircraft, UAVs, satellites
SensorsPhotographic cameras, electro-optical/infrared, radar (SAR)

Aerial photography remains the foundation of imagery intelligence

Aerial reconnaissance is reconnaissance conducted by aircraft — historically using visual observation and photography, and today also electro-optical, infrared, and radar sensors — to gather imagery intelligence about enemy forces, terrain, and targets. It is one of the oldest military uses of aviation and remains a core element of intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR).

History

Observation from balloons began in the eighteenth century, and cameras were carried aloft within years of the invention of photography. In the First World War, systematic aerial photography transformed artillery targeting and trench mapping; purpose-built reconnaissance aircraft and photographic interpreters became fixtures of both armies. In the Second World War, fast, high-flying photo-reconnaissance aircraft such as the de Havilland Mosquito and photo variants of the Spitfire covered occupied Europe, and interpretation units — famously at RAF Medmenham — assessed bomb damage and hunted weapons such as the V-2.

Cold War and after

The Cold War produced specialised strategic platforms: the high-altitude Lockheed U-2 (whose 1960 shoot-down over the USSR became a diplomatic crisis) and the Mach-3 SR-71 Blackbird, while spy satellites progressively assumed the deepest-penetration missions. U-2 flights over Cuba in October 1962 produced the photographic evidence of Soviet missile sites that precipitated the Cuban Missile Crisis.

Modern practice

Today aerial reconnaissance is performed by manned aircraft (U-2, RC-135), long-endurance unmanned aircraft such as the RQ-4 Global Hawk and MQ-9 Reaper, tactical drones, and commercial satellite imagery, with synthetic-aperture radar allowing imaging through cloud and at night. The discipline overlaps with civilian remote sensing, aerial surveying, and aerial photography.
