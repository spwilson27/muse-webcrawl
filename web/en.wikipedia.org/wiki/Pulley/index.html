Pulley

A pulley is a wheel on an axle or shaft that is designed to support movement and change of direction of a taut cable or belt, and to transfer power between the shaft and the cable or belt. A pulley may have a groove or grooves between flanges around its circumference to guide the cable or belt. The drive element of a pulley system can be a rope, cable, belt, or chain.

Pulleys are one of the six classical simple machines identified by Renaissance scientists. A system of two or more pulleys with a rope or cable threaded between them, usually to lift or move a heavy load, is called a block and tackle.

History

The earliest evidence for pulleys comes from Mesopotamia in the early 2nd millennium BCE, and from ancient Egypt in the Twelfth Dynasty (1991–1802 BCE). The Greek scientist Archimedes is traditionally credited with developing compound pulley systems in the 3rd century BCE; Plutarch relates that Archimedes moved a fully laden ship single-handedly using such a system. Hero of Alexandria listed the pulley among the five simple machines for lifting heavy weights.

Mechanical advantage

A single fixed pulley changes only the direction of the force. A movable pulley, attached to the load, halves the force required, giving a mechanical advantage of two. Combining fixed and movable pulleys in a block and tackle multiplies the advantage in proportion to the number of rope segments supporting the load, at the cost of pulling a proportionally longer length of rope.

Types

  • Fixed pulley — the axle is anchored; it changes the direction of force only.
  • Movable pulley — the axle moves with the load; it provides mechanical advantage.
  • Compound pulley — a combination of fixed and movable pulleys.
  • Belt and pulley systems — used to transmit rotary power between shafts, as in belt drives.

Applications

Pulleys are used in cranes, elevators, flagpoles, window blinds, sailing rigging, clotheslines, garage doors, and exercise equipment. Belt-driven pulleys historically powered the machinery of factories from a central line shaft.
