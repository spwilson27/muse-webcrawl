Duck

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Duck is the common name for many species of the waterfowl family Anatidae, which also includes swans and geese. Ducks are mostly aquatic birds, smaller than swans and geese, found in fresh and salt water worldwide except Antarctica. The best-known is the mallard, ancestor of nearly all domestic ducks.

Characteristics

Ducks have broad bodies, webbed feet and flattened bills adapted for dabbling, grazing or diving for food — plants, seeds, insects, small fish and molluscs. Males (drakes) of northern species often have showy breeding plumage while females are mottled brown for camouflage; after breeding, drakes moult into a dull 'eclipse' plumage. Many species migrate long distances between breeding and wintering grounds.

Humans and ducks

Domestic ducks, bred from mallards (and separately from Muscovy ducks), are farmed for meat, eggs and feathers; breeds such as the Pekin and Indian Runner are prolific layers. Ducks are also hunted for sport and food, watched by birders, and kept as emblematic park birds. Famous fictional ducks aside, real ducks feature in conservation: several island species are endangered by introduced predators.
