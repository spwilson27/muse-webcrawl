The Most Noble Order of the Garter is an order of chivalry founded by Edward III of England in 1348. It is the most senior order of knighthood in the British honours system, and membership is limited to the sovereign, the Prince of Wales, and no more than 24 living "Companions" at a time, together with certain supernumerary members such as foreign monarchs ("Stranger Knights") and other royals. Appointments are in the personal gift of the sovereign and traditionally recognise public service or personal service to the monarch.

Origins and legend

Edward III founded the Order around 1348, at the height of his wars in France, modelling his court on the Arthurian Round Table and binding his leading captains — including the Black Prince, Henry of Grosmont and other veterans of Crécy — into a single chivalric fellowship dedicated to St George. The famous story that the Order commemorates a garter dropped by the Countess of Salisbury at a court ball, which Edward retrieved with the words Honi soit qui mal y pense, is first recorded much later and is generally treated as legend, though the motto is genuinely that of the Order.

Insignia and ceremonies

The insignia include the Garter itself — a dark blue velvet band bearing the motto in gold, worn by men below the left knee and by women on the left arm — the mantle and hood, the star, and the Lesser George, a pendant of St George slaying the dragon. The Order's chapel is St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle, where each Companion's stall displays their banner, helm, crest and stall plate, and where the annual Garter Day service is held each June, attended by the sovereign and Companions in full mantle and insignia. Knights who die are "degraded" only if convicted of treason; the banners of disgraced members, including foreign sovereigns at war with Britain, have on rare occasions been removed.

Women were appointed as Ladies of the Garter in the middle ages and again from 1901; in 1987 they became eligible for appointment as full Lady Companions on the same terms as knights.
