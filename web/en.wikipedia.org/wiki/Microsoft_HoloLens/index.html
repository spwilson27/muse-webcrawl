Microsoft HoloLens, known under development as Project Baraboo, is a pair of mixed reality smartglasses developed and manufactured by Microsoft. HoloLens was the first head-mounted display running the Windows Mixed Reality platform under the Windows 10 operating system. Its tracking technology was adapted from the Kinect sensor.

The pre-production version of HoloLens, the Development Edition, shipped on March 30, 2016, aimed at developers in the United States and Canada for a list price of US$3,000. A Commercial Suite followed later that year. On February 24, 2019, Microsoft announced HoloLens 2 at Mobile World Congress in Barcelona.

Design

HoloLens is a head-mounted display unit connected to an adjustable, cushioned inner headband, tilting up and down as well as forward and backward. The front houses the sensors and cameras and a pair of transparent combination lenses through which computer-generated imagery is projected into the wearer's field of view, appearing as "holograms" anchored to the real environment.

The device contains an inertial measurement unit, an ambient light sensor, four environment-understanding cameras, an energy-efficient depth camera, a 2-megapixel photographic video camera, a microphone array and a custom-built processing unit Microsoft calls the Holographic Processing Unit (HPU), which processes sensor data for spatial mapping and gesture recognition on-device.

Interaction

Users interact through gaze, voice commands via Cortana, and hand gestures including the "air tap" and "bloom". The headset is untethered, containing its own compute, battery and connectivity (Wi-Fi and Bluetooth), and runs Universal Windows Platform apps rendered as floating windows or three-dimensional objects.

Applications and reception

Early adopters included NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, which used HoloLens for Mars rover planning (OnSight), as well as partners in architecture, medicine, automotive design and education. Reviewers praised the spatial mapping and display concept while noting the limited field of view and developer-focused pricing. Microsoft later pivoted the product toward enterprise customers, and in 2018 the United States Army awarded Microsoft a contract to develop the Integrated Visual Augmentation System (IVAS) based on HoloLens technology.
