Galois cohomology

In mathematics, Galois cohomology is the study of the group cohomology of Galois modules — that is, the application of homological algebra to modules acted on by a Galois group. It is a central tool of modern algebraic number theory, providing the language for class field theory, the theory of algebraic groups over local and global fields, and much of the arithmetic of elliptic curves.

Definition

Let L/K be a Galois extension of fields with Galois group G = Gal(L/K), and let A be a G-module. The Galois cohomology groups Hi(G, A) are the derived functors of the functor of G-invariants, A ↦ AG. Passing to the limit over finite subextensions yields the cohomology of the absolute Galois group, a profinite group, using continuous cochains.

Low-degree interpretations

The group H0(G, A) is simply the invariants AG. The first cohomology group H1 classifies crossed homomorphisms modulo principal ones; for trivial action it is the group of homomorphisms G → A. Hilbert's theorem 90 states that H1(Gal(L/K), L×) = 0. The second cohomology group of the multiplicative group, H2(G, L×), is the (relative) Brauer group of the extension.

Applications

Galois cohomology underlies the cohomological formulation of local and global class field theory, Tate duality for local fields, the Poitou–Tate duality theorems for global fields, and the study of principal homogeneous spaces (torsors) under algebraic groups: H1 classifies twists of varieties, so vanishing theorems such as the Hasse principle can be phrased cohomologically. Kummer theory, the theory of quadratic forms (via the cohomology of μ2), and the Galois-symbol conjectures proved by Voevodsky all belong to this framework.
