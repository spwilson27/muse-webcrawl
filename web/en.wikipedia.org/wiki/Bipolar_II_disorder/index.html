Bipolar II disorder

Bipolar II involves hypomania alternating with major depression.

Bipolar II disorder is a mood disorder characterized by at least one hypomanic episode and at least one major depressive episode, without any manic episode. It is one of the bipolar spectrum disorders, alongside bipolar I disorder, in which full mania occurs. Although sometimes described as a milder form of bipolar disorder, bipolar II is associated with substantial disability, largely because depressive episodes tend to be frequent, prolonged and severe.

Diagnosis

Under the DSM-5, a hypomanic episode is a distinct period of abnormally and persistently elevated, expansive or irritable mood and increased energy lasting at least four consecutive days, present most of the day. Unlike mania, hypomania does not cause marked impairment, require hospitalization, or involve psychotic features; if psychosis occurs, the episode is by definition manic. Diagnosis is often difficult because hypomania may be remembered as a period of productivity or well-being, and bipolar II is frequently misdiagnosed as unipolar depression.

Course and treatment

The condition typically begins in late adolescence or early adulthood. People with bipolar II spend considerably more time in depressive than hypomanic states and face an elevated risk of suicide. Treatment usually centers on mood stabilizers such as lithium and lamotrigine and atypical antipsychotics such as quetiapine; antidepressants are used cautiously because they may trigger hypomania or rapid cycling. Psychotherapy, sleep regulation and psychoeducation are important adjuncts.
