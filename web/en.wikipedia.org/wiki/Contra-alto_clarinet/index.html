Contra-alto clarinet

The contra-alto clarinet is a large member of the clarinet family, pitched in E♭, one octave below the alto clarinet and a fifth below the bass clarinet. Its written range corresponds to sounding pitches a major sixth lower. The instrument is a regular, if uncommon, member of the clarinet choir and appears occasionally in wind ensembles, where it fills the tenor-bass register between the bass and contrabass clarinets.

ClassificationWoodwind, single-reed aerophone
KeyE♭
RangeSounds a major sixth below written pitch
Related instrumentsAlto clarinet, bass clarinet, contrabass clarinet

Design

Contra-alto clarinets are made in two shapes: a straight model resembling an elongated bass clarinet with an upturned metal bell, and a curved, compact "paperclip" style. Makers historically included Leblanc, whose metal and wood models are the most widespread, and Buffet Crampon. Most instruments descend to low E♭ or low C. Because of their size, they are played with the help of a floor peg or stand.

Use

The contra-alto doubles parts flexibly: it can reinforce bass lines, substitute for bassoon or bass clarinet, and anchor clarinet choirs. Its repertoire is largely shared with the bass clarinet and contrabass clarinet, and contemporary composers occasionally write for it specifically. Production has always been limited, and the instrument is far rarer than the B♭ contrabass clarinet.
