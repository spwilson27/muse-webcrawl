Obligor

An obligor is the party bound to perform under a legal or financial obligation.

An obligor, also known as a debtor, is an entity legally bound to provide a payment, service, or other benefit to another entity, the obligee. The term is used most commonly in bond markets, loan agreements, and structured finance to refer to the party that owes the debt — for example, the issuer of a bond or the borrower under a loan.

Usage in finance

In bond indentures and credit agreements, "obligor" is the collective term for the issuer and any guarantors or co-borrowers jointly responsible for repayment. Credit rating agencies assess an obligor's creditworthiness when assigning ratings to its debt instruments. In securitization, the obligors are the underlying borrowers whose payments service the securities issued by a special-purpose vehicle.

Legal obligations

Under contract and civil law, an obligor is the party upon whom an obligation rests. If the obligor fails to perform, the obligee may pursue remedies such as damages, specific performance, or enforcement against collateral. The corresponding term in some civil-law systems is derived from the Latin obligatus, "bound."

Related concepts

The obligor–obligee relationship underlies instruments such as bonds, promissory notes, leases, and surety arrangements. A surety or guarantor becomes a secondary obligor, liable if the principal obligor defaults.
