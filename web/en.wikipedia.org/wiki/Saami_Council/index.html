Saami Council

The Saami Council (Northern Sámi: Sámiráđđi; Norwegian: Samerådet) is a voluntary, non-governmental umbrella organisation of Sámi organisations in Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Russia, with a permanent secretariat. Founded in 1956, it is one of the oldest indigenous peoples' organisations in the world, and it works to promote Sámi rights and interests across the four states that divide Sápmi, the Sámi homeland.

Activities

The Council coordinates Sámi policy on cultural, political, and environmental questions, organises the Sámi Conference held every four years, and adopts declarations such as the Trondheim and Kiruna declarations. It holds special consultative status with the United Nations Economic and Social Council, participates as a Permanent Participant in the Arctic Council, and played a central role in the development of international indigenous-rights law, including ILO Convention 169 and the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples.

Structure

The Council has 15 members appointed by its nine member organisations, which include the Sámi associations of Norway, Sweden, and Finland, reindeer herders' associations, and the Sámi organisations of the Russian Kola Peninsula. The presidency rotates between countries. Not to be confused with the separate Sámi parliaments (elected bodies in Norway, Sweden, and Finland), or with the Saami Council's role in administering grants such as Nordic cultural funding for Sámi projects.
