Achar (Hindi: अचार, from Persian āchār) is a family of pickles originating in the Indian subcontinent, made from a wide variety of vegetables and fruits preserved in edible oils, brine, or vinegar with Indian spices. Intensely flavoured — sour, salty, hot, and aromatic — achar is eaten in small quantities as a condiment with rice, dal, breads, and curries, and a single spoonful can enliven an entire meal.

Preparation

The classic method uses raw mango, lime, green chillies, carrots, or mixed vegetables cut and partially sun-dried to reduce moisture, then mixed with salt and a spice blend (panch phoron or rai/mustard-based masalas) and submerged in mustard oil, which acts as a preservative seal. The pickle matures in the sun for days or weeks as fermentation and spice infusion develop its flavour. Regional styles vary enormously: Punjabi mango achar is fiery and oily, Gujarati versions may be sweetened, and South Indian avakaya (mustard-powder mango pickle) is famously pungent.

Spread and influence

Through trade and empire, achar travelled across Asia and beyond: it became acar in Malay and Indonesian cooking, atchara in the Philippines, and, via British officers returning from India, inspired the mustard pickle piccalilli. Commercial brands such as Mother's Recipe, Priya, and Patak's now export achar worldwide, and diaspora communities maintain home pickling as a seasonal ritual tied to the mango harvest.
