British Ghanaians

British Ghanaians are British people of Ghanaian descent or Ghanaians who have migrated to the United Kingdom. Ghana-born residents and their descendants form one of the largest sub-Saharan African communities in Britain, numbering well over 100,000 people.

Kente cloth is a prominent symbol of Ghanaian identity at community events.

History

Links between Britain and Ghana date to the colonial period, when the Gold Coast was a British colony; Ghanaian students, seamen and professionals travelled to Britain through the early 20th century. Migration increased after Ghana's independence in 1957, and grew substantially in the 1980s and 1990s as economic and political instability encouraged emigration. Many settled in London boroughs such as Tottenham, Peckham, Hackney and Croydon.

Culture and community

The community is strongly church-centred, with Ghanaian-led Pentecostal and charismatic congregations among the fastest-growing churches in London, alongside Methodist, Presbyterian and Catholic parishes founded in Ghana. Funerals, naming ceremonies and independence-day celebrations are major communal occasions, and remittances to Ghana form a significant economic link. Languages spoken include Twi, Ga, Ewe and English.

Notable people

British Ghanaians have achieved prominence in politics, sport, music and broadcasting; in the 2020s the community has been represented in the UK Parliament and in the Premier League, and Ghanaian-influenced Afrobeats and gospel scenes have shaped London's black music culture.
