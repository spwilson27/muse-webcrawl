Renminbi

Renminbi
Code: CNY (ISO 4217)
Symbol: ¥ (元)
Subunit: jiǎo (1/10), fēn (1/100)
Issued by: People's Bank of China
First issued: 1948

The renminbi (Chinese: 人民币; literally "people's currency"; symbol: ¥; ISO code: CNY) is the official currency of the People's Republic of China and one of the most traded currencies in the world. The yuan (元) is the basic unit of the renminbi, and the two terms are often used interchangeably internationally — much as "pound" relates to sterling. One yuan is divided into ten jiǎo and one hundred fēn.

History

The renminbi was first issued by the People's Bank of China in December 1948, shortly before the founding of the People's Republic. For much of the planned-economy era it was not convertible, and a parallel system of Foreign Exchange Certificates operated for visitors until 1995. China unified its dual exchange rates in 1994, maintained a strict peg to the US dollar at about 8.28 per dollar from 1997 to 2005, and has since managed the yuan's value against a basket of currencies within a daily trading band.

Internationalisation

From 2009 China promoted the cross-border use of the renminbi in trade and investment, aided by the offshore yuan market (CNH) centred on Hong Kong. In 2015 the International Monetary Fund added the renminbi to the basket of currencies making up the Special Drawing Right, effective 1 October 2016 — the first currency added since the euro. A central bank digital currency, the e-CNY, has been piloted since 2020.
