Ebenezer Scrooge is the protagonist of Charles Dickens's 1843 novella A Christmas Carol. A cold-hearted, miserly London money-lender who despises Christmas and all sentiment — his catchphrase is "Bah! Humbug!" — Scrooge is visited on Christmas Eve by the ghost of his late partner Jacob Marley and by the Ghosts of Christmas Past, Present and Yet to Come. The visions redeem him, and he awakes a generous and joyful man. His surname has entered the language: a "Scrooge" is a miser or a killjoy.

Character in the novella

Dickens introduces Scrooge as "a squeezing, wrenching, grasping, scraping, clutching, covetous, old sinner", solitary as an oyster, who keeps his clerk Bob Cratchit shivering in a chilly office and refuses his nephew Fred's Christmas invitation and all charitable appeals. Marley's Ghost, dragging chains "made of cash-boxes, keys, padlocks, ledgers, deeds, and heavy purses wrought in steel", warns him to change his ways. The Ghost of Christmas Past shows him his lonely childhood, his apprenticeship to the kindly Fezziwig, and his broken engagement to Belle, who released him as his love of money grew. The Ghost of Christmas Present reveals the Cratchits' humble but loving Christmas — and their frail child Tiny Tim, doomed to die if the future is unchanged — and the figures of Ignorance and Want. The Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come shows Scrooge his own unmourned death and Tiny Tim's grave. Scrooge begs for a second chance, awakes on Christmas morning, and famously buys the Cratchits a prize turkey, raises Bob's salary, and becomes "a second father" to Tiny Tim, who does not die.

Origins

Scholars have proposed several models, none conclusively: the miser John Elwes; the Edinburgh undertaker's surname "Scrougie"; and Dickens's own anxieties about money and his father's debts. The name also plays on the archaic verb "scrouge" (to squeeze or crowd). Scrooge's transformation anchors Dickens's social message: that wealth carries obligations to the poor, a theme given urgency by the poverty of industrial Britain in the "Hungry Forties".

Portrayals

Scrooge is one of the most frequently portrayed characters in adaptation. Notable interpreters include Seymour Hicks, Alastair Sim (1951 film, often judged definitive), Albert Finney (1970 musical Scrooge), George C. Scott (1984), Michael Caine (opposite the Muppets, 1992), Patrick Stewart (one-man stage version and 1999 film), Jim Carrey (2009 motion-capture film) and Christopher Plummer (2017). Walt Disney's Scrooge McDuck, created in 1947, is a direct parody-descendant, and the character continues to anchor countless stage versions each December.
