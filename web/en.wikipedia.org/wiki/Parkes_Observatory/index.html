Parkes Observatory

The Parkes Observatory, near Parkes in New South Wales, Australia, is a radio telescope observatory operated by the CSIRO. Its centrepiece is the 64-metre Parkes radio telescope, nicknamed "The Dish", completed in 1961 and still among the largest single-dish telescopes devoted to radio astronomy.

History and achievements

The telescope famously relayed television images of the Apollo 11 Moon landing in July 1969, a story dramatised in the film The Dish (2000). Parkes conducted the influential Parkes pulsar surveys, discovering many pulsars, and in 2007 data from Parkes revealed the first known fast radio burst, the Lorimer burst. Its sensitive receivers have also supported searches for extraterrestrial intelligence.

Instrumentation

The telescope has been continually upgraded, including a multibeam receiver that greatly accelerated sky surveys of neutral hydrogen. It remains a key element of the Australia Telescope National Facility.
