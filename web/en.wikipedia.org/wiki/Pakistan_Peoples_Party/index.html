The Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP; Urdu: پاکستان پیپلز پارٹی) is a centre-left political party in Pakistan, affiliated with the Socialist International. Founded in 1967 by Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, it has been one of the country's largest and most influential parties, forming the federal government several times and remaining the dominant party in the province of Sindh.

History

The PPP was founded in Lahore on 30 November 1967 after Bhutto, a former foreign minister, broke with the military regime of Ayub Khan. Campaigning on the slogan roti, kapra aur makaan ("bread, clothing, and housing") and a programme of "Islamic socialism", the party swept West Pakistan in the 1970 general election, Pakistan's first free vote. After the secession of East Pakistan (Bangladesh) in 1971, Bhutto became president and then prime minister under the 1973 constitution, nationalising industries, banks, and schools. His government was overthrown by General Zia-ul-Haq in 1977; Bhutto was hanged in 1979 after a controversial murder trial.

Under Bhutto's daughter Benazir Bhutto, the party led the Movement for the Restoration of Democracy against Zia's regime. After Zia's death in 1988, Benazir became prime minister — the first woman to head the government of a Muslim-majority country — serving two non-consecutive terms (1988–90 and 1993–96), both ended by presidential dismissal. Benazir Bhutto was assassinated at an election rally in Rawalpindi on 27 December 2007. The PPP nevertheless won the 2008 election and formed a coalition government under Yousaf Raza Gillani, with Benazir's widower Asif Ali Zardari as president (2008–2013); that parliament completed a full term, the first in Pakistan's history to do so.

Recent politics

After a heavy defeat in 2013, the PPP's national strength contracted, though it retained the government of Sindh. Bilawal Bhutto Zardari, son of Benazir and Zardari, became party chairman. Following the removal of Imran Khan's government in 2022, the PPP joined the Pakistan Democratic Movement coalition, with Bilawal serving as foreign minister; after the 2024 election the party supported the coalition government of Shehbaz Sharif while taking constitutional offices, including the presidency for Zardari once again.

Ideology

The party's founding manifesto combined parliamentary democracy with social justice and an egalitarian reading of Islam. In office, its governments have combined populist welfare measures with, in later decades, more market-oriented policies. The PPP draws support particularly from rural Sindh, southern Punjab, and among minorities and secular voters.
