The Chicago Cubs are an American professional baseball team based in Chicago, competing in Major League Baseball as a member of the National League Central Division. One of the oldest franchises in American sports, the Cubs play at Wrigley Field, famous for its ivy-covered brick outfield walls and hand-turned scoreboard.

History

Founded in 1876 as the Chicago White Stockings, the club dominated the early National League under Cap Anson and won consecutive World Series in 1907 and 1908. It then endured the longest championship drought in North American professional sports: 108 years without a title, accompanied by folklore such as the "Curse of the Billy Goat" (1945). The drought ended dramatically in 2016, when the Cubs defeated the Cleveland Indians in a ten-inning Game 7 to win the 2016 World Series.

Rivalries and culture

The Cubs' principal rivals are the St. Louis Cardinals and the crosstown Chicago White Sox. The team's fan base is famed for loyalty through decades of losing, immortalized in the song "Go, Cubs, Go" played after home wins.

Notable players

Hall of Famers associated with the Cubs include Ernie Banks ("Mr. Cub"), Ryne Sandberg, Billy Williams, Fergie Jenkins, and Ron Santo. The 2016 champions were led by Kris Bryant, Anthony Rizzo, and Javier Báez.
