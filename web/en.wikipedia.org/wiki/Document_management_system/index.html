Document management system

A document management system (DMS) is a system used to receive, track, manage and store documents and reduce paper. Most are capable of keeping a record of the various versions created and modified by different users (version control). In the case of the management of digital documents, such systems are based on computer programs. The term has some overlap with the concepts of content management systems and enterprise content management.

Components

Typical components include document capture (scanning and optical character recognition), metadata and indexing, storage and archiving, retrieval and full-text search, version control, access control and security, workflow and collaboration features, and records retention and disposition.

History

Electronic document management emerged in the 1980s as vendors built systems to manage the growing volume of digital files alongside scanned paper records. Standards such as the U.S. Department of Defense's DoD 5015.2 and ISO 15489 records management standards helped define compliance requirements.
