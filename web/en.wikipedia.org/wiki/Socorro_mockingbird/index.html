Socorro mockingbird


Scientific name: Mimus graysoni
Family: Mimidae
Status: Critically Endangered
Range: Socorro Island, Mexico

The Socorro mockingbird (Mimus graysoni) is an endangered mockingbird endemic to Socorro Island in the Revillagigedo Islands, Mexico. It is one of the rarest birds in North America, with a population estimated at only a few hundred individuals.

Description

The species is a large mockingbird, about 25 cm long, with plain brown upperparts, paler streaked underparts, a long tail, and a slightly downcurved bill. Its song is loud and varied, incorporating mimicry of other species, and males sing persistently during the breeding season.

Habitat and behaviour

It inhabits lowland scrub and prickly-pear thickets, mainly in the island's southern lowlands. It feeds on invertebrates, small vertebrates, fruit, and nectar, foraging both on the ground and in vegetation. Nests are built in thorny shrubs.

Conservation

The population declined severely in the twentieth century, largely through habitat degradation by introduced sheep and predation by feral cats. Sheep were removed from the island, and conservation measures including cat control and habitat restoration have been undertaken. The species is listed as Critically Endangered by the IUCN.
