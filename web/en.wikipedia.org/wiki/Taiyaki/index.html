Taiyaki

Taiyaki (鯛焼き, literally "baked sea bream") is a Japanese fish-shaped pastry. It is made using regular pancake or waffle batter poured into a two-sided fish-shaped mould, with a filling placed inside before the mould is closed and cooked on both sides. The most common filling is sweet red bean paste made from azuki beans; other fillings include custard, chocolate, cheese, and sweet potato.

History

Taiyaki is thought to have originated in Tokyo in the Meiji era around 1909, adapted from imagawayaki, a round filled cake. The sea bream (tai) was chosen because it is a symbol of good luck and celebration in Japan, and because the fish was expensive, the cake offered a festive treat at modest cost.

Variations

A Korean version, bungeo-ppang, is nearly identical. Modern variations include mini taiyaki, soft-serve ice cream served in an open-mouthed taiyaki cone, and savoury fillings such as sausage or okonomiyaki-style cabbage.
