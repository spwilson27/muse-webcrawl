Psychopomp

A psychopomp (from Greek psychopompós, "guide of souls") is a creature, spirit, deity or figure in many religions whose role is to escort newly deceased souls from Earth to the afterlife. Psychopomps do not judge the dead but provide safe passage between worlds; they are frequently associated with liminal animals such as horses, ravens, owls, dogs, deer and sparrows.

Examples

  • Greek religion — Hermes Psychopompos guides souls to Hades; Charon ferries them across the Styx or Acheron for a coin.
  • Egyptian religion — the jackal-headed Anubis presides over mummification and conducts the dead to judgement.
  • Norse religion — the valkyries choose the slain and escort warriors to Valhalla.
  • Christianity — the archangel Michael is traditionally depicted receiving and weighing souls.
  • Islam — Azrael is the angel of death who separates soul from body.
  • Hinduism — Yama, first mortal and king of the dead, is served by messengers (Yamadutas).
  • Japanese religion — shinigami are later folk figures associated with death's guidance.

In modern culture

The psychopomp survives in literature and psychology: Jungian thought treats the psychopomp as a mediator between conscious and unconscious, and contemporary death-doula and hospice movements sometimes invoke the figure for those who accompany the dying.
