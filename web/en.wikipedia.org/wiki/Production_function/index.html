Production function

Production function

In economics, a production function gives the technological relation between quantities of physical inputs and quantities of output of goods and services. The production function is one of the key concepts of mainstream neoclassical theories, used to define marginal product and to distinguish allocative efficiency, a key focus of economics.

Isoquant curves for combinations of capital and labour yielding equal output

Formulation

In general form, output Q is written as a function of inputs such as capital K and labour L: Q = f(K, L). The marginal product of an input is the additional output obtained by using one more unit of that input, holding the others constant. Along an isoquant, the marginal rate of technical substitution measures the rate at which one input can be substituted for another while keeping output unchanged.

Common forms

Cobb–Douglas

The Cobb–Douglas production function, Q = A Kα Lβ, introduced by Charles Cobb and Paul Douglas in 1928, exhibits constant returns to scale when α + β = 1, and its exponents correspond to factor income shares under competitive assumptions.

CES and Leontief

The constant elasticity of substitution (CES) function generalises Cobb–Douglas by allowing the elasticity of substitution between inputs to differ from one. The Leontief function, Q = min(aK, bL), is its limiting case of fixed proportions, with L-shaped isoquants and no substitutability.

Returns to scale and technical change

A production function exhibits increasing, constant, or decreasing returns to scale according to whether output rises more than, exactly, or less than proportionally when all inputs are scaled up. In growth economics, shifts of the function — often summarised by total factor productivity — represent technological progress, as in the Solow growth model.
