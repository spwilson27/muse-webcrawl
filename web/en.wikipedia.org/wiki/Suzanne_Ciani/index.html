Suzanne Ciani

Suzanne Ciani (born 4 June 1946) is an American musician, sound designer, composer, and record label executive who pioneered electronic music and sound design. She is best known for her work with the Buchla synthesizer, for creating iconic commercial sound logos — including the Coca-Cola "pop-and-pour" sound — and as one of the first female new-age recording artists, with several Grammy-nominated albums.

Career

Ciani studied composition and earned a master's degree in composition from the University of California, Berkeley, where she encountered Don Buchla and became devoted to his modular synthesis system. After a period of financial hardship supported by composing for advertising and pinball and video game sound — including the voice and effects for the Bally pinball machine Xenon (1980) — she founded Ciani/Musica to produce sound design for major brands. Her 1982 album Seven Waves, first released in Japan, launched a successful recording career; The Velocity of Love (1984) and its title track became staples of new-age radio.

Later work

After years of acoustic piano recordings, Ciani returned publicly to the Buchla in the 2010s amid revived interest in modular synthesis, performing widely, collaborating with Kaitlyn Aurelia Smith, and releasing the quadraphonic live album LIVE Quadraphonic (2018). She has received the Moog Innovation Award and numerous lifetime-achievement honours.
