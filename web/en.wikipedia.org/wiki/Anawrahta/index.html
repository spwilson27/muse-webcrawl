Anawrahta Minsaw (Burmese: အနော်ရထာ မင်းစော; 11 May 1014 – 23 March 1077) was the founder of the Pagan Empire and is considered the father of the Burmese nation. Over a reign of more than three decades, Anawrahta unified the regions that would later form modern Myanmar for the first time in history, transforming the small city-state of Pagan into the first Burmese empire.

Unification

Anawrahta came to the throne in 1044 after killing his half-brother Sokkate in single combat, according to chronicle tradition. From Pagan he conquered the Mon kingdom of Thaton in the south in 1057 — an event later tradition credits with bringing Theravada Buddhist scriptures and craftsmen to his capital — extended his authority northward toward the borders of the Nanzhao kingdom, and campaigned in the Shan hills and Arakan. He built extensive irrigation works in the dry zone, notably around Kyaukse, which made Pagan's granaries the economic engine of the empire.

Religious reforms

Inspired by the Mon monk Shin Arahan, Anawrahta adopted Theravada Buddhism and promoted it across his realm, suppressing the influence of the Ari monks, whose tantric practices had dominated earlier Pagan religion. He maintained relations with Sri Lanka, sending assistance to King Vijayabahu I and receiving in return a replica of the Buddha's tooth relic. Construction of great monuments at Pagan, begun under Anawrahta — including the Shwezigon Pagoda — laid the foundations of the city of thousands of temples built by his successors.

Death and legacy

Anawrahta died in 1077, reportedly killed by wild buffalo on a hunting expedition outside Pagan. He was succeeded by his son Saw Lu. Burmese chronicles celebrate him as one of the three great warrior-kings of Burmese history, alongside Bayinnaung and Alaungpaya, and his name has been given to roads, schools, and a corvette of the Myanmar Navy.
