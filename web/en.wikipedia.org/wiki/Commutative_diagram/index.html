Commutative diagram

In mathematics, especially category theory, a commutative diagram is a diagram of objects (also known as vertices) and morphisms (also known as arrows or edges) such that all directed paths with the same start and end points lead to the same result by composition.

Definition

A diagram commutes if, whenever two morphisms are formed by composing arrows along different paths between the same pair of objects, those morphisms are equal. A square of morphisms f, g, h, k commutes when the composition along one route equals the composition along the other.

Usage

Commutative diagrams play a central role in category theory and homological algebra, serving the role that equations play in elementary algebra. Famous examples include the five lemma diagram, snake lemma diagram, and diagrams expressing universal properties such as those of products and pullbacks.

Diagram chasing

"Diagram chasing" is a method of mathematical proof used especially in homological algebra, in which properties of morphisms are deduced by tracing elements through a commutative diagram.
