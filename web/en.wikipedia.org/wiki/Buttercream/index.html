Buttercream

Placeholder image: a cake decorated with buttercream.

Buttercream, also known as butter icing or butter frosting, is a type of icing used to fill, coat and decorate cakes. It is made by creaming butter with powdered sugar, usually with added milk or cream, vanilla and other flavourings, and is prized for its smooth texture and pipeability.

Types

  • American buttercream — butter beaten with powdered (icing) sugar and a little liquid; simple, sweet and slightly crusting.
  • Swiss meringue buttercream — egg whites and sugar heated over a bain-marie, whipped to a meringue, then butter beaten in; silky and less sweet.
  • Italian meringue buttercream — similar, but the sugar is added as a hot syrup poured into whipping egg whites; very stable.
  • French buttercream — made with egg yolks and hot sugar syrup, rich and custard-like.
  • German buttercream — butter beaten into pastry cream (custard).
  • Ermine (flour) buttercream — based on a cooked flour-and-milk roux.

Use

Buttercream is used as a filling between cake layers, as an outer coating, and piped into decorations such as roses, shells and borders. Because butter melts at low temperatures, buttercream cakes are best kept cool; the frosting firms in the refrigerator and softens at room temperature.
