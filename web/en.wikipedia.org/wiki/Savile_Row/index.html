Savile Row

Savile Row is a street in Mayfair, central London, famous as the traditional home of bespoke tailoring for men. The term "bespoke" is said to have originated here, from cloth that was "spoken for" by a customer. Since the nineteenth century the Row has dressed royalty, statesmen, military officers and film stars, and remains shorthand for the finest hand-made suits.

History

The street was laid out in the 1730s on the Burlington Estate and named after Dorothy Boyle, Countess of Burlington and Cork, née Savile. Tailors began moving in during the early nineteenth century, following their wealthy clients to the new Mayfair squares. Firms that became synonymous with the Row include Henry Poole & Co (credited with creating the dinner jacket, or tuxedo, in the 1860s), Huntsman, Kilgour, and Gieves & Hawkes at No. 1 Savile Row. The Beatles' Apple Corps headquarters stood at No. 3, where the band played its final rooftop concert in 1969.

Bespoke tailoring

A true Savile Row suit is cut from an individual paper pattern drafted for the client, hand-sewn over dozens of hours and refined through several fittings. The Savile Row Bespoke Association, founded in 2004, sets standards to protect the designation. Challenges from rents, changing fashions and ready-to-wear luxury have been met by new generations of tailors and fashion houses joining the street.
