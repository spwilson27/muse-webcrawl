In mathematics, particularly in algebra, a field extension is a pair of fields K ⊆ L such that the operations of K are those of L restricted to K. In this case L is called an extension field of K, and K is the base field of L. Field extensions are fundamental in algebra, for example in the study of polynomial roots and in Galois theory.

Definitions

The larger field L is a vector space over K; its dimension is called the degree of the extension, written [L : K]. An extension is finite if its degree is finite. An element α ∈ L is algebraic over K if it is a root of a non-zero polynomial with coefficients in K, and transcendental otherwise. An extension is algebraic if every element of L is algebraic over K.

Examples

  • The complex numbers ℂ are an extension of the real numbers ℝ of degree 2, obtained by adjoining i.
  • ℚ(√2) is an extension of the rationals of degree 2.
  • The rational function field K(x) is a transcendental extension of K.

Algebraic closure

Every field has an algebraic closure — an algebraically closed algebraic extension — which is unique up to isomorphism. The fundamental theorem of algebra states that the complex numbers are algebraically closed. Towers of extensions satisfy the degree formula [M : K] = [M : L][L : K].
