The Donmar Warehouse is a 251-seat, not-for-profit theatre in Covent Garden, London, England. Despite its small size, it is regarded as one of the most influential producing theatres in the English-speaking world, known for actor-led revivals of classics, new writing, and intimate productions of musicals. The building was originally a warehouse — used in the 19th century for storing bananas and later as a film studio and rehearsal space — before being converted into a theatre, first used by the Royal Shakespeare Company in 1977.

The Donmar's intimate auditorium seats just 251 people, placing audiences close to the stage.

History

The theatre takes its name from its founder, producer Donald Albery, and his wife Margaret ("Don-mar"). After a period as an RSC studio space, the Donmar became an independent producing house. Under artistic director Sam Mendes from 1992, it rose to international prominence with productions such as Cabaret, The Glass Menagerie with Zoë Wanamaker, and Stephen Sondheim's Assassins and Company. Michael Grandage's tenure brought further acclaim, including Guys and Dolls, Don Carlos, and Jude Law's Hamlet, which transferred to Broadway and Elsinore. Josie Rourke's productions included an all-female Shakespeare trilogy with Harriet Walter. The Donmar has won numerous Olivier and Tony Awards, and many of its productions transfer to the West End, Broadway, and film.
