Makar (poetry)

Makar is a Scots word for a poet or bard, literally a 'maker'. The term is most often applied to the Scots poets of the 15th and early 16th centuries — the golden age of Scottish literature under the Stewart court.

The Makars

Principal makars include Robert Henryson, William Dunbar, Gavin Douglas — whose Eneados (1513) was the first complete translation of Virgil's Aeneid into an Anglic language — and David Lyndsay, as well as Blind Harry and the king James I, author of The Kingis Quair. Writing in Middle Scots in forms influenced by Chaucer, they produced works like Henryson's Testament of Cresseid and Dunbar's Lament for the Makaris, itself a key source for the lesser-known makars' names.

Modern use

The title Makar has been revived for civic poets: Edinburgh appoints a Makar, and Scotland created the national post of The Scots Makar in 2004, first held by Edwin Morgan, followed by Liz Lochhead, Jackie Kay and Kathleen Jamie.
