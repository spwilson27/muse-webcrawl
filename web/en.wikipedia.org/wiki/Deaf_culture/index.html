Deaf culture

Deaf culture is the social beliefs, behaviours, art, traditions, history and institutions of communities that use sign languages as their main means of communication, often viewing deafness as cultural-linguistic identity rather than solely disability.

Terminology

Capital-D "Deaf" denotes cultural affiliation and fluency in a sign language such as American Sign Language; lowercase "deaf" refers to audiological hearing loss.

History

Communities formed around deaf schools in the 18th-19th centuries. The 1880 Milan Conference endorsed oralism and suppressed signing. The 1988 Deaf President Now movement at Gallaudet University was a landmark in Deaf civil rights.

Values

The culture values visual communication, signed storytelling and poetry, name signs, and norms like eye contact. Debates continue over cochlear implants and mainstreaming.
