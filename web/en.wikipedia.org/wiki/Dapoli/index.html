Dapoli

Dapoli (Marathi: दापोली) is a town in the Ratnagiri district of Maharashtra, India, on the Konkan coast. Situated on a plateau at an elevation of about 240 metres (800 feet) above sea level, some 8 kilometres inland from the Arabian Sea, it enjoys a cooler climate than the surrounding coastal plain and was developed by the British as a hill camp and sanatorium, earning it the nickname "Mini Mahabaleshwar".

History

Dapoli was a military camp during British rule and later a cantonment-like settlement. It is associated with several figures of the Indian freedom movement and social reform: Lokmanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak founded the Tilak Nagar area and supported education here, and Dr. B. R. Ambedkar's family had roots in the nearby village of Ambadave. Maharshi Dhondo Keshav Karve also spent part of his early career teaching in the region.

Geography and climate

The town lies amid the Sahyadri foothills, surrounded by jackfruit, mango (notably the Alphonso/Hapus variety), cashew and coconut plantations. It receives heavy monsoon rainfall typical of the Konkan. Nearby attractions include the beaches of Murud, Karde and Harnai, the Suvarnadurg and Kanakdurg sea forts, the ancient Panhalekaji rock-cut caves, and the hot springs at Unhavare.

Education

Dapoli is home to the Dr. Balasaheb Sawant Konkan Krishi Vidyapeeth, an agricultural university established in 1972, as well as numerous schools and colleges that draw students from across the Konkan region.

Transport

Dapoli is connected by state highways to Khed (on National Highway 66 and the Konkan Railway), Mandangad and Ratnagiri. The nearest railway station is Khed, about 30 km away.
