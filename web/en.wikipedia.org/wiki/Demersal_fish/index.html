Demersal fish

Flatfish are classic demersal fish, living on or near the seabed

Demersal fish, also known as groundfish, are fish that live on or near the bottom of seas or lakes — the demersal zone. They occupy the sea floors and lake beds, which usually consist of mud, sand, gravel or rocks, and are contrasted with pelagic fish, which live in the open water column.

Characteristics

Many demersal fish are negatively buoyant and rest directly on the bottom; others, termed benthopelagic, hover just above it with the aid of a swim bladder. Demersal species feed on benthic organisms such as crustaceans, mollusks and other bottom-dwelling animals, or on zooplankton just above the seabed.

Examples and fisheries

Well-known demersal fish include flatfish such as halibut and sole, cods, haddock, hake, groupers and many rays and skates. Demersal fisheries, often conducted with bottom trawls, are among the world's most valuable commercial fisheries, and the term "whitefish" in the fishing industry generally refers to demersal species.
