TiHKAL: The Continuation is a 1997 book by Alexander Shulgin and Ann Shulgin, whose title is an acronym for "Tryptamines I Have Known and Loved". It is the sequel to PiHKAL: A Chemical Love Story (1991), which treated phenethylamines; TiHKAL applies the same structure to the tryptamine family of psychedelic compounds.

Contents

Like its predecessor, the book is divided into two parts. The first is a partly fictionalised autobiographical narrative continuing the story of the characters Shura and Alice Borodin, the Shulgins' alter egos, together with essays on psychotherapy, the war on drugs and related topics. The second part catalogues 55 tryptamine compounds — including DMT, psilocin and 5-MeO-DMT — giving synthesis instructions, dosages, durations and the Shulgins' qualitative ratings and commentary on the subjective effects of each.

Reception

The publication of the Shulgins' books drew official attention: after PiHKAL appeared, the US Drug Enforcement Administration raided Alexander Shulgin's laboratory in 1994 and revoked his DEA licence, though no charges were brought. The detailed catalogues remain influential reference works in psychedelic research and harm-reduction communities, while their legality and safety have been debated; many of the compounds described are controlled substances in numerous jurisdictions.
