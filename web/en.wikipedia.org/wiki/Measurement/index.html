Measurement

Measurement is the quantification of attributes of a thing or event, which can be used to compare with other things or events. In other words, measurement is a process of determining how large or small a physical quantity is as compared to a basic reference quantity of the same kind. The scope and application of measurement depend on the context and discipline.

Units and standards

Every measurement is expressed as a number multiplied by a unit. The International System of Units (SI) defines seven base units — metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela — from which all other units are derived. Since the 2019 revision of the SI, all base units are defined in terms of fixed numerical values of fundamental physical constants, such as the speed of light and the Planck constant, rather than by physical artefacts like the former international prototype of the kilogram. The science of measurement itself is called metrology.

Uncertainty and error

No measurement is exact. Measurement uncertainty quantifies the range within which the true value plausibly lies, arising from instrument resolution, calibration, environmental effects and the object itself. Results are distinguished by accuracy (closeness to the true value) and precision (repeatability), and by the number of significant figures reported. In quantum mechanics, measurement has an additional foundational role: the uncertainty principle limits the joint precision of complementary quantities, and the "measurement problem" concerns how definite outcomes arise from quantum superpositions.

Levels of measurement

Statisticians classify scales of measurement, following S. S. Stevens, as nominal (labels), ordinal (rankings), interval (equal intervals but arbitrary zero, such as Celsius temperature) and ratio (true zero, such as mass) — a typology that determines which statistics are meaningful for a given variable. Measurement theory in psychology and the social sciences addresses how latent attributes such as intelligence or utility can be quantified at all.

History

Measurement is among the oldest technologies: cubit rods in Egypt, standard weights in the Indus Valley, and the surveying behind Roman aqueducts all attest to early systems, later unified through the metric system of revolutionary France and the Metre Convention of 1875.
