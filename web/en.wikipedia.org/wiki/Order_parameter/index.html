Order parameter

Order parameter
FieldCondensed matter physics, statistical mechanics
Associated withPhase transitions, spontaneous symmetry breaking

A typical order parameter is zero in the disordered phase and nonzero in the ordered phase.

An order parameter is a quantity characterizing the degree of order across a phase transition. It typically measures the extent to which some symmetry of the high-temperature (disordered) phase is broken in the low-temperature (ordered) phase: the order parameter is zero in the disordered phase and nonzero — though not necessarily saturated — in the ordered phase.

Examples

  • In a ferromagnet, the magnetization serves as the order parameter: it vanishes above the Curie temperature and grows below it.
  • In a liquid–gas transition, the difference in density between liquid and gas acts as an order parameter in the theory of the critical point.
  • In superconductors and superfluids, the order parameter is a complex wavefunction (as in Ginzburg–Landau theory), whose phase encodes broken gauge or phase symmetry.
  • In liquid crystals, the order parameter is a tensor describing the average alignment of the elongated molecules.
  • In alloys undergoing ordering (such as brass), the order parameter measures the preferential occupation of sublattices by the different atomic species.

Theory

Lev Landau's theory of second-order phase transitions is built around expanding the free energy in powers of the order parameter near the transition. Order parameters need not be simple numbers: they may be vectors, tensors, or fields, and their transformation properties encode the broken symmetry. Fluctuations of the order parameter diverge at critical points, described by critical exponents and universality classes.
