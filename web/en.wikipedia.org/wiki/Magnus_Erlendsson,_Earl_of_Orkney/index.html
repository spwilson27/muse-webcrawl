Magnus Erlendsson (c. 1075 – 16 April 1115), known as Saint Magnus, was Earl of Orkney from about 1106 until his death. He was killed on the island of Egilsay by his cousin and co-earl Haakon Paulsson, in an episode recounted by the Orkneyinga saga, and was subsequently venerated as a martyr and saint. His nephew Rögnvald Kali Kolsson built St Magnus Cathedral in Kirkwall in his honour.

Life

Magnus was the son of Erlend Thorfinnsson, joint earl of Orkney. As a young man he served in the following of King Magnus III of Norway ("Magnus Barelegs"), accompanying his expeditions in the Irish Sea; the sagas relate that he refused to fight in a raid on Anglesey, instead remaining aboard ship singing psalms, an early sign of the piety for which he became known. After time in Scotland and at the court of King Malcolm, he returned to claim his share of the earldom of Orkney, which he held jointly with his cousin Haakon.

Death

Relations between the two earls deteriorated, and a meeting on Egilsay was arranged at Easter to settle their differences, each to come with two ships and an equal number of men. Magnus arrived as agreed; Haakon came with eight ships full of armed men. Magnus spent the night in prayer in the island's church and, refusing to flee or fight, was captured the next day. He offered alternative expiations — pilgrimage, exile, or mutilation — which Haakon rejected; he was executed by Haakon's cook Lifolf, reportedly after Haakon's standard-bearer refused the task, and was buried where he fell. The traditional date of his death is 16 April 1115, though some sources give 1117.

Veneration

Miracles were soon reported at his grave, and Bishop William of Orkney recognised his sanctity around 1135. His relics were translated to Kirkwall, first to the church of St Olaf and then to the new St Magnus Cathedral, founded in 1137. In the 20th century, human remains found hidden in a pillar of the cathedral were identified as likely to be those of Magnus. His feast day, 16 April, is still marked in Orkney, and he is a patron saint of the Northern Isles.
