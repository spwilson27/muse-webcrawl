Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997

Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997

Official name: Republic Act No. 8371

Country: Philippines

Enacted: 29 October 1997

Administering body: National Commission on Indigenous Peoples

The Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act of 1997 (Republic Act No. 8371), commonly known as IPRA, is a Philippine law that recognizes and promotes the rights of indigenous cultural communities and indigenous peoples in the Philippines.

Provisions

The Act recognizes indigenous peoples' rights to their ancestral domains and lands, to self-governance and empowerment, to social justice and human rights, and to cultural integrity. It provides mechanisms for issuing Certificates of Ancestral Domain Title and requires free and prior informed consent for projects affecting ancestral domains.

Implementation

The law created the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) to implement its provisions. Its constitutionality was challenged but upheld by the Supreme Court of the Philippines in 2000.
