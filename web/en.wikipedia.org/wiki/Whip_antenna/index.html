Whip antenna

A whip antenna is an antenna consisting of a straight, flexible rod or wire, often tapered, mounted vertically. It is a type of monopole antenna, usually a quarter-wavelength long, fed at the base and used over a ground plane or counterpoise. Whip antennas are widely used on handheld and mobile radios, vehicles, boats and portable equipment at VHF, UHF and HF frequencies.

Design

The classic whip is a quarter-wavelength monopole: the rod forms one half of a dipole, with the ground plane (such as a car roof) acting as the other half. Because a full quarter-wave can be impractically long at lower frequencies, many whips are electrically shortened using a loading coil, often at the base or centre, which adds inductance to bring the antenna to resonance at the cost of bandwidth and efficiency. Flexible whips on handheld radios ("rubber ducky" antennas) are shortened helical windings of wire in a rubber sheath.

Materials and construction

Whips are made from spring steel, stainless steel, telescoping tubing, or fibreglass rods with an internal conductor, giving them the flexibility implied by the name: they bend under impact and spring back rather than breaking.

Uses

Whip antennas are standard on police and military manpack radios, taxis and emergency vehicles, CB and amateur radio installations, marine VHF sets, and older portable consumer electronics such as transistor radios and early mobile phones.
