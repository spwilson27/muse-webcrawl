Taizé Community

The Taizé Community is an ecumenical Christian monastic fraternity in Taizé, Saône-et-Loire, Burgundy, France. Founded in 1940 by Brother Roger Schütz, a Swiss Reformed Protestant, it has become one of the world's most important sites of Christian pilgrimage, especially for young people, welcoming well over 100,000 visitors a year from around the world.

History

Roger Schütz arrived in the village of Taizé in 1940 and sheltered refugees, including Jews fleeing the Nazi occupation. After the war he returned with companions and in 1949 the first brothers — drawn from several Protestant traditions — committed themselves to a life of celibacy, community of goods and simplicity. The community later came to include Roman Catholic brothers, making it a unique ecumenical order. In 1962 the Church of Reconciliation was built on the site. Brother Roger led the community until 16 August 2005, when he was stabbed to death during evening prayer by a mentally disturbed Romanian woman. He was succeeded by Brother Alois Löser, a German Catholic. In 2023 Brother Alois handed over leadership to Brother Matthew, an English Anglican.

Prayer and music

The community's worship, held three times daily, is famous for its meditative style: short, repetitive chants in many languages, long periods of silence, readings and candlelight. The "Songs of Taizé", developed with the composer Jacques Berthier, are sung by Christian congregations worldwide. The community describes its vocation as a "pilgrimage of trust on earth", organising meetings for young adults in cities across Europe and other continents, in which participants are hosted by local families and parishes.

The community today

Around one hundred brothers from some thirty countries live at Taizé, supporting themselves by their work rather than accepting donations for themselves. The community has been recognised by leaders of many churches: Pope John Paul II visited in 1986, and ecumenical patriarchs, archbishops of Canterbury and Orthodox and Protestant leaders have also come as pilgrims.
