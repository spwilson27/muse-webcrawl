Luis de Góngora y Argote (11 July 1561 – 24 May 1627) was a Spanish Baroque lyric poet of the Spanish Golden Age. His distinctive, densely metaphorical style, known as culteranismo or Gongorism, was controversial in his lifetime and profoundly influential thereafter.

Life

Born in Córdoba, Góngora studied at the University of Salamanca and took minor orders, eventually becoming a chaplain; he later served as honorary chaplain to King Philip III in Madrid. He travelled widely in Spain on church business and frequented the court, where he engaged in a celebrated literary rivalry with Francisco de Quevedo, whose plainer conceptismo contrasted with Góngora's ornateLatinate manner. Ill and in debt in his last years, he returned to Córdoba, where he died in 1627.

Works and style

Góngora's major poems include the Fábula de Polifemo y Galatea (1612), a retelling of the myth of Polyphemus, Acis, and Galatea in octava rima, and the unfinished Soledades (1613), a pastoral poem of extraordinary syntactic and metaphorical complexity. His poetry is marked by hyperbaton, classical allusion, neologisms, and elaborate metaphors that often make the surface meaning deliberately difficult. He also wrote many sonnets, romances, and letrillas in more accessible registers. Long dismissed as obscure, Góngora was championed in the twentieth century by the poets of the Generation of 1927, who took their name in part from the tercentenary of his death.
