The First Carlist War (Spanish: Primera Guerra Carlista) was a civil war in Spain from 1833 to 1840, fought between supporters of the regency of Maria Christina, acting for her daughter Isabella II, and supporters of the Infante Carlos María Isidro, brother of the deceased king Ferdinand VII, who claimed the throne under the Salic law introduced by the Bourbons and abolished by Ferdinand's Pragmatic Sanction of 1830.

Background and course

The succession dispute overlapped a deeper ideological struggle: the Carlists championed traditionalism, the Church, and the fueros (regional privileges) of the Basque provinces and Navarre under the slogan "God, Fatherland, Fueros, King", while the government side — the Liberals or Isabelines — moved toward constitutional monarchy and centralisation. The war was strongest in the north, where the Carlist general Tomás de Zumalacárregui built an effective guerrilla army from 1833 until his death at the siege of Bilbao in 1835. After his death the war became a grinding contest of sieges, columns, and reprisals; the Carlist "Royal Expedition" reached the gates of Madrid in 1837 but failed to take it.

Internationally, the Quadruple Alliance of 1834 aligned Britain, France, and Liberal Portugal with the Isabeline cause; Britain sent the British Auxiliary Legion under George de Lacy Evans, and France the Foreign Legion detachment that became famous at Huesca and Barbastro. Atrocities on both sides led to the Lord Eliot Convention of 1835 on the treatment of prisoners.

Conclusion

Increasingly divided and exhausted, the Carlist northern army split between supporters of compromise and hardliners. On 31 August 1839 the Liberal general Baldomero Espartero and the Carlist general Rafael Maroto signed the Convention of Vergara ("the Embrace of Vergara"), under which Carlist officers were integrated into the royal army and the government undertook to propose honoring the fueros — enacted in modified form in the law of 25 October 1839. The last Carlist forces under Ramón Cabrera in the Maestrazgo were defeated in 1840, and Carlos went into exile. The conflict seeded two further Carlist wars (1846–1849 and 1872–1876) and a century and a half of Carlist political tradition.
