Regina Caeli

Regina Caeli (Ecclesiastical Latin: [reˈdʒiːna ˈtʃɛːli]; Queen of Heaven) is a musical antiphon addressed to the Blessed Virgin Mary that is used in the liturgy of the Roman Rite of the Catholic Church during the Easter season, from Easter Sunday until Pentecost. During this season, it is the Marian antiphon that ends Compline (Night Prayer) and it takes the place of the Angelus at noon.

It is one of the four seasonal antiphons of the Blessed Virgin Mary, the others being Alma Redemptoris Mater (from the first Sunday of Advent until Candlemas), Ave Regina Caelorum (until Holy Saturday), and Salve Regina (from the day after Pentecost until the first Sunday of Advent).

Text

Regina caeli, laetare, alleluia;
Quia quem meruisti portare, alleluia,
Resurrexit, sicut dixit, alleluia:
Ora pro nobis Deum, alleluia.

In English translation:

Queen of heaven, rejoice, alleluia.
The Son you merited to bear, alleluia,
Has risen as he said, alleluia.
Pray to God for us, alleluia.

History

The authorship of the Regina Caeli is unknown. It is traced back to the 12th century and was found in an antiphonary of about 1200. A legend says that the antiphon was revealed by angels to Pope Gregory the Great during a procession in Rome. The Franciscans popularized its use in the office, and Pope Nicholas III (1277–1280) ordered it to be used in all churches of Rome during Eastertide.

Many composers have set the Regina Caeli to music, including movements within Easter masses and standalone motets.
