The Monarchy of Greece was the system of government of the Kingdom of Greece from 1832 until its abolition in 1974. After the Greek War of Independence, the protecting powers installed the Bavarian prince Otto as Greece's first king under the London Conference of 1832. The monarchy survived, with interruptions, through periods of constitutional rule, republic and military government for over 140 years.

History

Otto was deposed in 1862 and replaced by the Danish prince William, who reigned as George I for 50 years as a constitutional monarch. The monarchy was repeatedly destabilised in the 20th century: the National Schism during the First World War, the abolition of the monarchy in 1924 and the establishment of the Second Hellenic Republic, and its restoration in 1935. During the Metaxas dictatorship and the Second World War the king remained head of state in exile.

Abolition

Constantine II fled Greece after his failed counter-coup against the military junta in December 1967. The junta abolished the monarchy in 1973, and after the restoration of democracy in 1974 a referendum held on 8 December 1974 confirmed the republic, with about 69% voting against the monarchy. Greece has remained a parliamentary republic since then.
