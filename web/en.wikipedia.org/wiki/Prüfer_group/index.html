Prüfer group

In mathematics, specifically in group theory, the Prüfer p-group or quasicyclic group Z(p∞), for a prime number p, is the unique p-group in which every element has p different p-th roots. The group is named after Heinz Prüfer. It is a countably infinite abelian group which plays a fundamental role in the classification of infinite abelian groups.

Constructions

The Prüfer p-group may be defined as the subgroup of the circle group (or equivalently of the additive group Q/Z) consisting of all pn-th roots of unity for all n ≥ 0; that is, the elements are the complex numbers exp(2πi·m/pn) with m, n non-negative integers. Alternatively, it can be presented as the direct limit of the cyclic groups Z/pnZ under the maps sending 1 to p, and in terms of generators and relations it has the presentation ⟨g1, g2, … | g1p = 1, g2p = g1, g3p = g2, …⟩.

Properties

  • The Prüfer p-group is the unique infinite p-group in which every proper subgroup is cyclic; moreover, its subgroups are linearly ordered by inclusion, forming a chain of order type ω.
  • Every proper subgroup is finite and cyclic of order pn for some n, and for each n there is exactly one subgroup of order pn.
  • It is a divisible group, and appears as the p-primary component of the torsion subgroup in the structure theory of abelian groups.
  • It is not finitely generated, and it is isomorphic to the p-Sylow subgroup of Q/Z.

Role in classification

Together with the group of rational numbers Q, the Prüfer groups are the building blocks of divisible abelian groups: every divisible abelian group is a direct sum of copies of Q and Prüfer p-groups for various primes p.
