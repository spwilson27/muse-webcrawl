Amoebiasis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Amoebiasis, or amoebic dysentery, is an infection of the intestines caused by the parasitic amoeba Entamoeba histolytica. The infection is spread through ingestion of food or water contaminated with faeces containing the parasite's cysts, and is most common in tropical areas with poor sanitation.

Cause: Entamoeba histolytica
Symptoms: abdominal pain, bloody diarrhoea, fever
Complications: amoebic liver abscess, colitis
Treatment: metronidazole, tinidazole; diloxanide furoate for cysts

Signs and symptoms

Most infected people, about 90%, are asymptomatic, but the disease has the potential to become severe. Symptoms may include abdominal pain, loose or bloody stools, cramps, and fatigue. Invasive disease can cause flask-shaped ulcers in the colon, and trophozoites may travel through the blood to form abscesses in the liver, and rarely the lungs or brain.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention rests on safe drinking water, proper sewage disposal, and hand hygiene. Tissue-active agents such as metronidazole or tinidazole kill the invasive trophozoites, and a luminal agent such as paromomycin or diloxanide furoate is used afterwards to eradicate cysts from the gut and prevent relapse or spread.

Epidemiology

Amoebiasis is estimated to infect tens of millions of people worldwide each year and causes tens of thousands of deaths, mainly in Central and South America, Africa, and Asia.
