Osamu Dazai (太宰 治, Dazai Osamu), born Shūji Tsushima (津島 修治), was a Japanese novelist and author, one of the foremost fiction writers of 20th-century Japan. His best-known works, The Setting Sun and No Longer Human, remain modern classics in Japan, and his semi-autobiographical style exemplifies the I-novel.

Life

Dazai was born into a wealthy landowning family in Aomori Prefecture. At university in Tokyo he drifted into the illegal Communist Party, dropped out, and began a life marked by morphine addiction, illness and repeated suicide attempts. He found literary footing in the 1930s, survived the war years writing stories and retellings of folk tales, and achieved fame in the ruined postwar world with The Setting Sun (1947), whose aristocratic narrator voiced the decline of the old order.

No Longer Human and death

His final novel, No Longer Human (Ningen Shikkaku, 1948), narrated by the alienated Ōba Yōzō, is considered his masterpiece and one of the best-selling novels in Japanese history. In June 1948 Dazai and his lover Tomie Yamazaki drowned themselves in the Tamagawa Canal in Tokyo; their bodies were found on 19 June, his 39th birthday.

Legacy

Dazai's works have been repeatedly adapted to film, manga and anime, and his birthday is commemorated in Japan as Ōtōki ("Cherry and Death Day").
