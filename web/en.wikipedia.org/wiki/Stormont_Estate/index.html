The Stormont Estate is a public estate in the east of Belfast in Northern Ireland, the seat of the Northern Ireland Assembly and the Northern Ireland Executive. The name "Stormont" is commonly used as a metonym for the devolved government of Northern Ireland itself.

History

The estate takes its name from the townland of Stormount—from Scots, a "place for crossing the moor". The government of Northern Ireland purchased the roughly 224-acre estate in the 1920s for £21,000 as the site for its new parliament. The neoclassical Parliament Buildings, designed by Arnold Thornely and built of Portland stone, were opened by the Prince of Wales on 16 November 1932. The main approach, Prince of Wales Avenue, is lined with lime trees and leads to a statue of Lord Carson.

Buildings and grounds

Besides Parliament Buildings, the estate contains Stormont Castle (the seat of the Executive and the Northern Ireland Office), Stormont House, the Maynard Sinclair Pavilion and several departmental offices. Much of the grounds are public parkland, with woodland walks, the Stormont Parkrun route, a children's playground and sports pitches. The estate hosts public events and is protected as a designated landscape of historic interest.
