Tashilhunpo Monastery

Location: Shigatse, Tibet Autonomous Region, China

Founded: 1447

Founder: Gedun Drub, 1st Dalai Lama

Tradition: Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism

Seat of: The Panchen Lama

Tashilhunpo Monastery (Tibetan: བཀྲ་ཤིས་ལྷུན་པོ་, "Heap of Glory") is a historic and culturally important monastery in Shigatse, the second-largest city in Tibet. Founded in 1447 by Gedun Drub, the First Dalai Lama, it is the traditional seat of the Panchen Lamas, the second-highest lineage in the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism.

History

Gedun Drub, a disciple of Tsongkhapa, founded Tashilhunpo in 1447. The Fourth Panchen Lama, Lobsang Chökyi Gyaltsen — the first to hold the title in his lifetime — greatly expanded the monastery in the 17th century, and it became one of the great monastic universities of Tibet, at its height housing some 4,000–5,000 monks organized in four colleges (tratsangs) for the study of philosophy, tantra, and ritual.

The monastery suffered damage at various times, including during the Cultural Revolution, though its most important chapels survived better than many other Tibetan sites, and it has been extensively restored since the 1980s.

Features

The monastery's most famous monument is the Maitreya Chapel, built by the Ninth Panchen Lama in 1914, housing a 26-metre-high statue of Maitreya (the Future Buddha), among the largest gilded statues in the world. Other highlights include the assembly hall (Tsokchen), the tomb stupas of the Panchen Lamas — including the spectacular gilded tomb of the Fourth Panchen Lama — and a vast display wall (thangka wall) on which giant religious banners are unfurled during festivals.

Present day

Tashilhunpo remains an active monastery with several hundred resident monks and is a major pilgrimage and tourist destination in Shigatse. It also administers branch monasteries in the region.
