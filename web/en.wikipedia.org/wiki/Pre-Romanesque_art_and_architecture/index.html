Pre-Romanesque art and architecture

Pre-Romanesque art and architecture is a term applied to the art and building of Western and Central Europe from the late Roman period (and the Merovingian era) until the emergence of Romanesque art around the year 1000–1075. It encompasses a mosaic of regional and dynastic styles — Merovingian, Carolingian, Ottonian, Asturian, Visigothic, Lombard, Anglo-Saxon, and early Croatian among them — united by their position between late antiquity and the first pan-European medieval style.

Characteristics

Pre-Romanesque churches typically adapt the Roman basilica plan with modifications such as cruciform plans, multiple apses, towers (including the westwork), and crypts for relic cults. Builders reused Roman materials and techniques while experimenting with barrel vaults and new liturgical arrangements. In the arts, the period produced illuminated manuscripts of extraordinary quality, ivory carving, goldsmith work such as votive crowns, and monumental sculpture in embryo.

Major phases

Merovingian and early medieval

The successor kingdoms of the Franks, Visigoths, and Lombards maintained late antique traditions in baptisteries, crypts, and small churches, with notable Visigothic examples such as San Pedro de la Nave in Spain.

Carolingian

The Carolingian Renaissance under Charlemagne consciously revived Roman forms: the Palatine Chapel at Aachen (modelled on San Vitale in Ravenna), great abbey churches planned in documents like the Plan of Saint Gall, and a flowering of manuscript illumination in court schools.

Ottonian

Ottonian architecture in the German lands — St. Michael's at Hildesheim, Gernrode, Essen — developed alternating pier-and-column supports, double-ended choirs, and monumental westworks, paving the way for Romanesque.

Regional variants

Asturian pre-Romanesque in northern Spain (Santa María del Naranco, San Miguel de Lillo) and the Mozarabic style of Christian Iberia show distinctive vaulting and horseshoe arches, while Anglo-Saxon churches in England are characterised by tall, narrow proportions and long-and-short quoins.
