Perugia is a city in central Italy and the capital of the region of Umbria. An ancient Etruscan and Roman city perched on a hill overlooking the Tiber valley, it is known for its medieval old town, its university founded in 1308, and its chocolate industry, which gave its name to the famous Baci Perugina chocolates.

History

Perugia was one of the twelve cities of the Etruscan confederation. It fought Rome in the Perusine War of 41–40 BC, when Octavian besieged Lucius Antonius there. In the Middle Ages it was an independent commune, later a possession of the Papal States. The city is associated with the painter Pietro Vannucci, called Perugino, the teacher of Raphael, whose works adorn the Collegio del Cambio.

Sights and culture

Principal sights include the Palazzo dei Priori, housing the Galleria Nazionale dell'Umbria; the Fontana Maggiore, a 13th-century fountain sculpted by Nicola and Giovanni Pisano; the Etruscan Arch; and the Rocca Paolina fortress. Perugia hosts the Umbria Jazz Festival, one of Europe's leading jazz events, and the EuroChocolate festival each autumn. It is also home to the University for Foreigners, founded in 1921.
