The Eastern Front of the First World War was the theatre of combat in Central and Eastern Europe between the Central Powers — principally the German Empire and Austria-Hungary — and the Russian Empire, joined in 1916 by Romania. Unlike the largely static trench warfare of the Western Front, the Eastern Front ran for more than a thousand miles from the Baltic Sea to the Black Sea and remained a war of movement, with vast advances, retreats and encirclements across the plains of Poland and Ukraine.

1914: invasion and Tannenberg

Russia opened the war by invading German East Prussia and Austro-Hungarian Galicia. In East Prussia the German Eighth Army under Paul von Hindenburg and Erich Ludendorff destroyed Alexander Samsonov's Second Army at the Battle of Tannenberg (late August 1914) and drove the Russians out at the First Battle of the Masurian Lakes. In Galicia, by contrast, the Russians inflicted heavy defeats on Austria-Hungary, capturing Lemberg (Lviv) and besieging Przemyśl, at enormous cost to both sides.

1915: the Great Retreat

In May 1915 the Central Powers broke through at Gorlice–Tarnów, triggering Russia's Great Retreat: the Russian armies abandoned Poland, including Warsaw, and fell back hundreds of miles, with millions of soldiers killed, wounded or captured and vast numbers of civilian refugees displaced. Germany established the military administration of Ober Ost in the occupied east.

1916–1917: Brusilov and revolution

In June 1916 General Aleksei Brusilov launched the Brusilov Offensive against Austria-Hungary — the most successful Allied operation of the war to that date — shattering Austro-Hungarian armies and helping to draw Romania into the war on the Allied side; Romania was nevertheless overrun by the end of the year. Russia's sacrifices and failures fed the collapse of the Tsarist regime in the February Revolution of 1917. The Provisional Government's last offensive (the Kerensky Offensive, July 1917) disintegrated, and after the October Revolution the Bolshevik government sought an armistice.

1918: Brest-Litovsk

The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (March 3, 1918) took Soviet Russia out of the war at a huge territorial price: Ukraine, the Baltic provinces, Finland and other western territories passed under German domination, and German troops advanced deep into Ukraine and the Caucasus. The settlement freed dozens of German divisions for the final offensives in the West, but Germany's defeat in November 1918 annulled the treaty and left the east in chaos — the setting for the Russian Civil War and the border wars of the successor states.
