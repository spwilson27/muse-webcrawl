Seventeen or Bust

Seventeen or Bust was a distributed computing project that used volunteers' computers to attack the Sierpiński problem, which asks whether the number 78,557 is the smallest Sierpiński number. A Sierpiński number is an odd integer k such that k·2n + 1 is composite for every positive integer n. To prove 78,557 the answer, a prime had to be found for each of the seventeen values of k below it that remained unresolved in 2002 — hence the project's name.

History

The project was founded in March 2002 by Louis Helm, a graduate student at the University of Michigan, and David Norris. Participants ran a client (based on the PRP/Lucas–Lehmer–Riesel style tests implemented in programs such as PRP and later Prime95-compatible software) which searched for primes of the form k·2n + 1 for the remaining multipliers.

Progress

Between 2002 and 2007 the project eliminated eleven of the seventeen multipliers by finding very large primes, including 46157·2698207 + 1 (found 2002) and 65567·21013803 + 1. Only six multipliers remained: 10223, 21181, 22699, 24737, 55459 and 67607. In April 2016 Seventeen or Bust suspended its independent operation and its remaining work was folded into the PrimeGrid project, where the search continues as part of the Sierpiński problem effort. In October 2016 a PrimeGrid participant found the prime for k = 10223, leaving five candidates.
