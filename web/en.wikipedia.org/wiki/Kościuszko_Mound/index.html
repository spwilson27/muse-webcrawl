Kościuszko Mound

Kościuszko Mound (Polish: Kopiec Kościuszki) is an artificial mound in Kraków, Poland, erected by the citizens of Kraków in 1820–1823 to commemorate the Polish national hero Tadeusz Kościuszko. It stands on Sikornik Hill in the Zwierzyniec district, west of the old town, and is modelled on the legendary prehistoric mounds of Krakus and Wanda elsewhere in the city.

History

Construction began on 16 October 1820, the third anniversary of Kościuszko's death. Thousands of volunteers — veterans, peasants, schoolchildren and foreign visitors — carried earth in wheelbarrows and baskets; soil from Kościuszko's battlefields, including Racławice and Maciejowice, was incorporated into the mound, and later soil from American battlefields of the Revolutionary War in which he fought was also added. The mound was completed on 25 October 1823.

After the failed Kraków Uprising of 1846, the Austrian authorities ringed the mound with a fort (Citadel Fort 2 "Kościuszko") as part of the Kraków Fortress, though the mound itself remained a Polish patriotic symbol and a destination for pilgrimages under the partitions. The mound suffered serious rain damage in 1997 and was restored, reopening in 2002. A small museum and the Kościuszko chapel stand at its base.
