Epinephrine autoinjector

DrugEpinephrine (adrenaline)
IndicationAnaphylaxis
BrandsEpiPen, Auvi-Q, others

An epinephrine autoinjector (or adrenaline autoinjector) is a medical device for injecting a measured dose of epinephrine (adrenaline) by means of autoinjector technology. It is most often used for the treatment of anaphylaxis. The first epinephrine autoinjector was brought to market in 1983.

Use

The device is pressed against the mid-outer thigh, delivering a dose of epinephrine into the vastus lateralis muscle. Autoinjectors typically contain 0.3 mg for adults or 0.15 mg for children. After use, emergency medical care should still be sought, as the effects of epinephrine may wear off within minutes.

History

Autoinjectors were originally developed for military use with nerve agent antidotes. The EpiPen, derived from the ComboPen developed for the US military, became the best-known civilian device; pricing controversies in the 2010s brought widespread attention to the market.
