TRIUMF is Canada's national laboratory for particle and nuclear physics and accelerator-based science, located on the campus of the University of British Columbia in Vancouver, British Columbia. Founded in 1968 by the University of British Columbia, Simon Fraser University and the University of Victoria, it is now a consortium of Canadian universities and a major node in international experimental physics.

The cyclotron

TRIUMF's centrepiece is a 520 MeV cyclotron accelerating negative hydrogen (H⁻) ions, commissioned in 1974 and still the world's largest cyclotron of its kind. Because H⁻ ions can be stripped to protons by passing them through thin foils, the machine can deliver several beams of different energies simultaneously to multiple experiments. The magnet is some 18 m across and weighs about 4,000 tonnes.

Research programme

The laboratory's ISAC facility produces rare-isotope beams for nuclear physics and nuclear astrophysics, studying the structure of exotic nuclei and the stellar processes that forge the elements; the ARIEL project added a superconducting electron linac to expand isotope production. TRIUMF also produces medical isotopes, supplies proton therapy (it operated Canada's first proton treatment facility for ocular melanoma), and studies materials with muons and neutrons. Together with partners it contributed hardware to CERN's Large Hadron Collider and hosts Canada's Tier-1 data centre for the ATLAS experiment. In the 1990s–2000s TRIUMF scientists participated in the Sudbury Neutrino Observatory work that resolved the solar neutrino problem.

Name

The acronym originally stood for Tri-University Meson Facility, reflecting the three founding universities; as the consortium grew the name was retained simply as TRIUMF.
