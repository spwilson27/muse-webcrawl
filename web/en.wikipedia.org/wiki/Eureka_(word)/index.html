Eureka (word)

Eureka
Greek: εὕρηκα (héurēka), "I have found (it)"
Attributed to: Archimedes of Syracuse
Meaning: A cry of joy at a sudden discovery
Related: Eureka effect; state motto of California

"Eureka" is an interjection used to celebrate a sudden discovery or insight. It comes from the Ancient Greek word héurēka (εὕρηκα), meaning "I have found it", and is famously attributed to the polymath Archimedes of Syracuse.

The Archimedes story

According to a story recounted by the Roman author Vitruvius, Archimedes was asked by King Hiero II to determine whether a votive crown was made of pure gold. While stepping into a bath, Archimedes noticed the water level rise and realised that the volume of an irregular object could be measured by the water it displaces—allowing him to test the crown's density. He is said to have leapt out and run naked through the streets of Syracuse shouting "Eureka! Eureka!" ("I have found it!"). The tale may be apocryphal, but it has fixed the word in world culture.

Usage

The word gives its name to the "eureka effect", the sudden experience of insight (also called the aha! moment). "Eureka" is the motto of the US state of California, referring to the discovery of gold in 1848, and names numerous towns, including Eureka, California. Gauss is said to have written "ΕΥΡΗΚΑ!" in his diary upon proving that every positive integer is the sum of at most three triangular numbers.
