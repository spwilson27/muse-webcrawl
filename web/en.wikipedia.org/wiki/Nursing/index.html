Nursing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A nurse monitoring a patient.

Nursing is a health care profession within the health care sector focused on the care of individuals, families, and communities so they may attain, maintain, or recover optimal health and quality of life. Nurses may be differentiated from other health care providers by their approach to patient care, training, and scope of practice. Nurses practice in many specialties with differing levels of prescription authority, and comprise the largest component of most healthcare environments.

The International Council of Nurses defines nursing as encompassing "autonomous and collaborative care of individuals of all ages, families, groups and communities, sick or well and in all settings", including the promotion of health, prevention of illness, and the care of ill, disabled and dying people.

History

Care of the sick was historically provided in religious and military institutions; early Christian deaconesses, monastic orders, and nursing orders such as the Daughters of Charity delivered nursing care for centuries. Modern nursing is associated with Florence Nightingale, whose work during the Crimean War and whose 1859 Notes on Nursing and school at St Thomas' Hospital in London laid foundations for professional nursing education. Other pioneers include Mary Seacole, Clara Barton (founder of the American Red Cross), and Mary Eliza Mahoney, the first African American registered nurse in the United States.

Practice

Nursing practice generally follows the nursing process: assessment, diagnosis, planning, implementation, and evaluation. Roles range from direct bedside care, medication administration, wound care, patient monitoring and patient education, to specialised work in critical care, emergency, perioperative, oncology, psychiatric-mental health, maternity and community nursing. Advanced practice registered nurses, such as nurse practitioners and clinical nurse specialists, diagnose and manage health conditions, in some jurisdictions with full practice authority.

Education and regulation

Entry to practice as a registered nurse typically requires a diploma, an associate degree, or a Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BSN), followed by licensure — for example, passing the NCLEX-RN examination in the United States and Canada. Practical/vocational nurses (LPN/LVN) have a shorter training pathway and a more limited scope. Nursing is regulated by professional bodies in most countries, and nurses account for roughly half of the global health workforce; the World Health Organization has repeatedly highlighted global shortages and maldistribution of nurses.

Workforce and culture

Nursing has traditionally been a female-majority profession, though the proportion of men has grown. Nursing features prominently in public esteem surveys, frequently ranking among the most trusted professions. Nurses' work settings include hospitals, clinics, schools, long-term care, home health, public health agencies, the military and industry.
