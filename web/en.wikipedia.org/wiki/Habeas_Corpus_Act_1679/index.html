Habeas Corpus Act 1679

The Habeas Corpus Act 1679 is an Act of the Parliament of England (31 Cha. 2 c. 2), passed during the reign of Charles II. It strengthened the legal procedure of habeas corpus, requiring that a person under arrest be brought before a court so the legality of their detention could be examined, and imposing penalties on officials who evaded the writ.

Significance

Though habeas corpus existed at common law earlier, the 1679 Act closed loopholes — such as recommitment for the same offence or imprisonment overseas — and set strict deadlines for returns. It remains a landmark in the development of civil liberties in English law, though parts have since been repealed.
