Final stellation of the icosahedron

The final stellation of the icosahedron is the outermost stellation of the regular icosahedron, a polyhedron with 20 intersecting triangular spike-like faces. It is also known as the echidnahedron, after its resemblance to an echidna or sea urchin.

Geometry

The stellation diagram of the icosahedron yields 58 stellations in total; the final one encloses all the cells of the stellation solid. It has icosahedral symmetry and was described in H. S. M. Coxeter's work on stellations, including The Fifty-Nine Icosahedra (1938).
