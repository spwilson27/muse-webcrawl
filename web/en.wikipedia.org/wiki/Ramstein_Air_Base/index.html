Ramstein Air Base is a United States Air Force base in Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany, near the city of Kaiserslautern. It serves as the headquarters of United States Air Forces in Europe – Air Forces Africa (USAFE-AFAFRICA) and of NATO's Allied Air Command, and is the largest American military installation outside the United States.

History

The site was occupied by French forces after World War II and handed over to the US Air Force in the early 1950s. The base was formed in 1953 by merging the Landstuhl and Ramstein airfields, and was officially dedicated as Ramstein-Landstuhl Air Base before being renamed Ramstein Air Base in 1958.

During the Cold War it was one of the principal USAFE fighter bases, hosting the 86th Fighter-Interceptor Wing and later the 86th Tactical Fighter Wing flying F-4 Phantoms.

Current operations

Today the 86th Airlift Wing operates C-130J Super Hercules and C-21 aircraft from Ramstein, supporting airlift, aeromedical evacuation, and contingency operations across Europe, Africa, and the Middle East. The base is the hub of the Kaiserslautern Military Community, the largest American population outside the United States, and hosts the Landstuhl Regional Medical Center nearby.

Ramstein air show disaster

On 28 August 1988, during the Flugtag '88 air show at the base, three aircraft of the Italian Air Force display team Frecce Tricolori collided mid-air and crashed into the crowd, killing 70 people and injuring hundreds. The disaster led to a ban on air shows in West Germany and to major changes in air-show safety regulations.
