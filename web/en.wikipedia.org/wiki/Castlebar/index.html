Castlebar

Castlebar (Irish: Caisleán an Bharraigh, meaning "Barry's Castle") is the county town of County Mayo, Ireland, in the west of the country. It developed around a castle built by the de Barra (Barry) family in 1235, from which it takes its name.

History

The town is best known for the "Races of Castlebar" in 1798, when during the Irish Rebellion a combined French and Irish force under General Humbert routed a much larger British force, which retreated in such haste that the engagement was mockingly named a race. A short-lived Republic of Connacht was proclaimed before the rebellion's defeat.

Geography and amenities

Castlebar lies near Lough Lannagh and is served by a railway station on the Dublin–Westport line. It hosts a campus of the Atlantic Technological University and the Museum of Country Life, part of the National Museum of Ireland, at nearby Turlough. The town is twinned with several towns abroad and is a regional centre for retail, healthcare, and administration in Mayo.

Sport and culture

Gaelic football is the dominant sport, with the Castlebar Mitchels club representing the town. The town also has a notable tradition of theatre and music, and hosts the annual Blues and roots festivals in the region.
