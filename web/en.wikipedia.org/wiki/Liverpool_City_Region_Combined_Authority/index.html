The Liverpool City Region Combined Authority is the combined authority of the Liverpool City Region in North West England. Established on 1 April 2014, it brings together the six local authorities of Halton, Knowsley, Liverpool, Sefton, St Helens, and Wirral to exercise strategic functions across the region, notably transport, economic development, and skills.

History

The authority was created under the Local Democracy, Economic Development and Construction Act 2009. In November 2015 it agreed a devolution deal with the UK government, providing for a directly elected "metro mayor", control of a £900 million investment fund over 30 years, and devolved powers over transport, planning, housing, and employment support. The first mayoral election was held in May 2017 and won by Steve Rotheram, the Labour MP for Liverpool Walton, who was re-elected in 2021 and 2024.

Functions

The Combined Authority is responsible for the Merseytravel transport executive and oversees the Merseyrail network; it has moved toward bus franchising under the Bus Services Act powers. It also administers the adult education budget, strategic housing and land programmes, and the region's economic strategy, including the LCR Freeport established around the Port of Liverpool. Its cabinet consists of the mayor and the leaders of the six constituent councils.
