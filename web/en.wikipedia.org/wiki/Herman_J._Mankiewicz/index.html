Herman J. Mankiewicz

Herman Jacob Mankiewicz (November 7, 1897 – March 5, 1953) was an American screenwriter who, with Orson Welles, wrote the screenplay for Citizen Kane (1941), for which they shared the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay. A former journalist and member of the Algonquin Round Table, he was famed in Hollywood as much for his corrosive wit as for his writing.

Early life and journalism

Mankiewicz was born in New York City to German-Jewish immigrant parents and educated at Columbia University. After serving in the First World War he worked as a reporter and drama critic, writing for The New York Times and The New Yorker, and belonged to the celebrated Algonquin Round Table circle of wits.

Hollywood career

Recruited to Paramount in the mid-1920s — he famously wired his friend Ben Hecht: "Millions are to be grabbed out here and your only competition is idiots" — Mankiewicz wrote or contributed to dozens of films, including early Marx Brothers pictures and Dinner at Eight (1933). Heavy drinking and gambling repeatedly damaged his career. In 1939 RKO hired him to work with the young Orson Welles; convalescing from a car accident in Victorville, California, he drafted the script that became Citizen Kane, drawing on the life of newspaper magnate William Randolph Hearst.

Later life and legacy

Mankiewicz's later credits included The Pride of the Yankees (1942). He died in 1953 of kidney failure aggravated by alcoholism. His younger brother Joseph L. Mankiewicz became a celebrated director. The dispute over the authorship of Citizen Kane, inflamed by Pauline Kael's 1971 essay "Raising Kane", was dramatised in David Fincher's film Mank (2020), in which Gary Oldman played Mankiewicz.
