Phytosterol

Phytosterols (also called plant sterols and stanols) are a group of naturally occurring compounds found in plant cell membranes. Structurally similar to cholesterol, they are present in vegetable oils, nuts, seeds, legumes, and whole grains.

Types

More than 200 sterols and related compounds are known. The most common phytosterols are β-sitosterol, campesterol, and stigmasterol. Stanols, their saturated derivatives, occur in smaller amounts and are produced commercially by hydrogenation.

Health effects

Phytosterols compete with cholesterol for absorption in the intestine, and regular intakes of about 2 grams per day lower LDL cholesterol by roughly 8–10%. Sterol- and stanol-enriched margarines and other functional foods are marketed for this purpose and are endorsed in dietary guidelines for people with elevated cholesterol. Rare genetic disorders such as sitosterolemia cause excessive absorption of plant sterols.
