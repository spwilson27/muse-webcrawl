Felipe Carrillo Puerto

Felipe Carrillo Puerto
Born: 8 November 1874, Motul, Yucatán, Mexico
Died: 3 January 1924, Mérida, Yucatán
Office: Governor of Yucatán (1922–1924)

Felipe Carrillo Puerto (1874–1924) was a Mexican journalist, politician and revolutionary socialist who served as governor of Yucatán from 1922 until his assassination in 1924. Known for his advocacy of the Maya people, land reform and women's rights, he is remembered in Yucatán as a martyr of the Mexican Revolution.

Early life

Born in Motul, Yucatán, Carrillo Puerto worked as a railway conductor and journalist, founding newspapers that criticised the henequen plantation oligarchy (the "divine caste") that dominated the state under Porfirio Díaz. Fluent in Yucatec Maya, he organised workers and indigenous communities and was repeatedly imprisoned under the Porfiriato.

Political career

During the Revolution he joined the movement of Salvador Alvarado, whose 1915 entry into Yucatán began sweeping reforms, and in 1917 founded the Socialist Party of the Southeast (PSS). Elected to the federal Congress, he later won the governorship in 1921, taking office in February 1922.

As governor he pursued radical reforms: breaking up henequen estates and distributing land, building schools and roads with labour organised through the party's resistance leagues, promoting Maya language and culture — including founding an academy of the Maya language — and supporting feminism; his government backed women's education and political rights, and his sister Elvia Carrillo Puerto became one of the first women elected to a Mexican legislature. His government also controversially promoted birth control, influenced by his relationship with the American journalist Alma Reed.

Assassination

When the De la Huerta rebellion broke out in December 1923, Carrillo Puerto attempted to flee but was captured and, on 3 January 1924, executed by firing squad in Mérida along with three of his brothers and associates. He was later officially declared a martyr; the Yucatán city and municipality of Felipe Carrillo Puerto in neighbouring Quintana Roo also bear his name.
