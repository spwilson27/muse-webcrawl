Horned God

The Horned God is one of the two principal deities of the neopagan religion of Wicca, the consort of the Triple Goddess. He is associated with nature, wilderness, sexuality, hunting, and the life cycle, and is often depicted with horns or antlers.

Origins of the concept

The modern figure synthesizes a number of horned or antlered beings from ancient and folkloric sources: the Gaulish god Cernunnos (known from the Pillar of the Boatmen in Paris), the Greek Pan, the English folklore figure Herne the Hunter, and horned figures in the Palaeolithic cave art interpretation popularized in the early 20th century. Margaret Murray's controversial witch-cult hypothesis proposed that medieval witches had worshipped a horned god; although her historical claims were largely discredited, the image influenced Gerald Gardner and the founders of Wicca.

Theology

In Wiccan practice the Horned God embodies the male principle, the sun, and the wild, dying-and-rising cycle of the year: he is born at Yule, weds the Goddess at Beltane, and dies at Samhain to be reborn. He is frequently invoked in the forms of the Oak King and the Holly King, who rule the waxing and waning halves of the year.

Names and forms

Various traditions address him as Cernunnos, Pan, Herne, the Green Man, Tammuz, or simply the Lord, and he may be represented in ritual by the High Priest wearing an antlered or horned headdress.
