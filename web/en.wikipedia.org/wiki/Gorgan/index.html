Gorgan

Country: Iran
Province: Golestan
County: Gorgan
Population (2016): 350,676
Elevation: 155 m (509 ft)

Gorgan (Persian: گرگان) is a city in the Central District of Gorgan County, Golestan province, Iran, serving as capital of the province, county, and district. It lies approximately 400 km (250 mi) northeast of Tehran, some 30 km (19 mi) away from the Caspian Sea. In the 2016 census, its population was 350,676, in 111,099 households.

The city was known as Astarabad (also spelled Esterabad) until 1937. Its ancient name relates to Hyrcania, the classical name for the region southeast of the Caspian Sea; the word "Gorgan" derives from Old Persian Varkana, "land of the wolves."

History

The region around Gorgan was inhabited in prehistoric times, and the nearby Tureng Tepe and Shah Tepe are important archaeological sites. The Great Wall of Gorgan, a massive Sasanian-era defensive fortification stretching about 195 km from the Caspian coast into the Alborz mountains, protected the region from nomadic incursions from the north; it is the second-longest defensive wall in the world after the Great Wall of China.

In the Islamic period Astarabad was a significant regional centre, celebrated in the 11th century by figures such as the Ziyarid prince Kaykavus, author of the Qabus-nama, whose son's court was at Gorgan. The Gonbad-e Qabus tower, built in 1006–7, stands in the nearby city of the same name. The city suffered during the Mongol invasions and later earthquakes, including a destructive quake in 1721.

Geography and climate

Gorgan lies on the fertile Gorgan Plain between the Caspian Sea and the forested Alborz mountains. It has a humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and mild winters. The surrounding Hyrcanian forests, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, are remnants of ancient temperate broadleaf forest. The Nahar Khoran forest park south of the city is a popular recreation area.

Transport and economy

Gorgan is served by an international airport and lies on the railway and road network linking Tehran with Mashhad and Central Asia. The economy is based on agriculture (wheat, cotton, rice, and citrus), food processing, and trade with the Caspian region.
