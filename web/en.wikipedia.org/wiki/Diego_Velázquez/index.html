Diego Velázquez

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Self-portrait of Velázquez as the painter in Las Meninas (1656), brush and palette in hand, the cross of the Order of Santiago on his chest]

Diego Rodríguez de Silva y Velázquez (baptised 6 June 1599 – 6 August 1660) was a Spanish painter, the leading artist at the court of King Philip IV of Spain and Portugal, and one of the most important painters of the Spanish Golden Age. His mature work is noted for its illusionism, psychological depth and masterful handling of paint, and he was a hugely influential model for later realists and modernists, especially Édouard Manet.

Velázquez's most famous painting is Las Meninas (1656), a complex court scene in the Alcázar of Madrid that places the viewer in the position of the king and queen. His other celebrated works include the portrait of Pope Innocent X (c. 1650), The Surrender of Breda (1634–35) and the single full-length female nude of Spanish painting, the Rokeby Venus.

Early life and Seville period

Velázquez was born in Seville and apprenticed to the painter and theorist Francisco Pacheco, whose daughter Juana he married in 1618. His early works, known as bodegones, are kitchen and tavern scenes — such as Old Woman Frying Eggs (1618) — that combine everyday subjects with an intense, Caravaggesque naturalism. In 1623 he painted a successful portrait of Philip IV and was appointed court painter, a post he held for the rest of his life.

Madrid and the Italian journeys

Velázquez travelled twice to Italy (1629–31 and 1649–51), studying Raphael, Michelangelo and the Venetian colourists, especially Titian. His style loosened into the fluid, sketch-like brushwork of his last decade, in which forms are built from unblended touches of paint that resolve only at a distance. At court he also rose through administrative posts — usher, chamberlain, and eventually marshal of the palace — and was made a knight of the Order of Santiago in 1659.

Legacy

Forgotten for two centuries, Velázquez was rediscovered in the nineteenth century: Manet called him 'the painter of painters'. His influence runs through Goya, the Impressionists, Picasso — who repainted Las Meninas in a series of 58 canvases in 1957 — and Francis Bacon, who made dozens of studies after the portrait of Innocent X without ever seeing the original.
