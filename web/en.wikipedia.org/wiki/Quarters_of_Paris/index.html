Quarters of Paris

The 80 administrative quarters of Paris.

The quarters of Paris (French: quartiers de Paris) are the eighty administrative subdivisions of the French capital. Each of Paris's twenty municipal arrondissements is officially divided into four quarters, numbered 1 to 80 city-wide. The quarters serve administrative and statistical purposes — including the assemblies of local government and the organisation of the police — and are distinct from the informal neighbourhood names familiar to Parisians.

History

Paris had quarters as early as the Middle Ages; in 1702 the city was divided into twenty quarters for policing. When the present twenty arrondissements were created in 1860 under Baron Haussmann's expansion of the city, each was subdivided into four quarters, giving the modern system of eighty. Many quarters take their names from a prominent building, gate, or former village: the Quartier du Faubourg-Montmartre, the Quartier de la Madeleine, the Quartier des Invalides, and the Quartier du Jardin-des-Plantes among them.
