North Swedish Horse

The North Swedish Horse (Swedish: Nordsvensk brukshäst) is a Swedish breed of small heavy horse, developed in northern and central Sweden for forestry and agricultural draught work. It is hardy, long-lived, and noted for its energetic, ground-covering trot.

History

The breed derives from the old Scandinavian landrace horses, influenced in the 19th century by heavier draught breeds. A breeding programme was organised from the 1890s, centred on the stud at Wången, with the aim of producing a compact, strong horse suited to pulling timber in forest terrain. Two types emerged: a heavier draught type and a lighter “coldblood trotter” strain bred for harness racing, popular in Sweden and Norway.

Characteristics

North Swedish Horses have a deep body, muscular hindquarters, a relatively large head with a straight or convex profile, and abundant mane and tail. They are economical feeders with strong hooves and good resistance to disease, and they commonly remain sound and working into their twenties. Numbers are small, and the breed is considered vulnerable; it is conserved as part of Sweden's native livestock heritage.
