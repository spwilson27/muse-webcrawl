Supreme Court of Georgia (country)

The Supreme Court of Georgia (Georgian: საქართველოს უზენაესი სასამართლო) is the highest court in the court system of Georgia, the country's court of last resort for civil, criminal and administrative cases. It sits in Tbilisi and should not be confused with the Supreme Court of the U.S. state of Georgia, nor with Georgia's Constitutional Court, which is a separate body seated in Batumi.

Established: 1991 (current form)
Location: Tbilisi, Georgia
Composition: Justices elected by Parliament for life terms

Structure and powers

The Supreme Court exercises cassation review through three chambers — for civil, administrative and criminal cases — and its Grand Chamber resolves conflicts in case law and questions of fundamental importance. The court also supervises the unified practice of the lower courts and issues interpretive guidance. Justices are nominated through the High Council of Justice and elected by the Parliament of Georgia; since constitutional amendments in the 2010s they serve for life. The Chief Justice is elected by Parliament for a ten-year term.

History

Georgia's first Supreme Court was established under the 1921 constitution of the Democratic Republic of Georgia. In the Soviet period the Supreme Court of the Georgian SSR functioned within the USSR's judicial system. The modern court was reshaped by judicial reforms after independence, including the 2005 reforms that abolished the old appellate structure and made the Supreme Court a pure cassation instance. Its appointment process has been a subject of domestic controversy and international scrutiny, including reviews by the Venice Commission.
