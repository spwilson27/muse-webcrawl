Neonicotinoid

Neonicotinoids (sometimes shortened to neonics) are a class of neuro-active insecticides chemically similar to nicotine. Developed in the 1980s by Shell and in the 1990s by Bayer, they are the most widely used class of insecticides worldwide, applied as seed treatments, soil drenches and sprays. Common members include imidacloprid, clothianidin, thiamethoxam, acetamiprid, thiacloprid and dinotefuran.

Mode of action

Neonicotinoids act on nicotinic acetylcholine receptors in the insect central nervous system, causing excitation, paralysis and death. Because they are systemic, treated plants take them up and distribute the compound through tissues, pollen and nectar, which protects the plant but also exposes pollinators.

Environmental controversy

From the mid-2000s, studies linked neonicotinoids to declines in bees — including sublethal effects on navigation, learning and colony reproduction — and to harm to aquatic invertebrates and birds. The European Union banned the outdoor use of imidacloprid, clothianidin and thiamethoxam in 2018, while debates over the balance of evidence and agricultural necessity continue in the United States and elsewhere.
