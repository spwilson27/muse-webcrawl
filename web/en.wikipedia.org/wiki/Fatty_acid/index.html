A fatty acid is a long-chain carboxylic acid, typically with 4–28 carbons, forming the building blocks of fats and oils: three fatty acids esterified to glycerol make a triglyceride. Saturated fatty acids (no double bonds, e.g. stearic acid) pack tightly and are solid; unsaturated ones (oleic acid, with one cis double bond) kink the chain and stay liquid.

Fatty acids are energy stores (about 9 kcal/g, twice carbohydrate), membrane components (as phospholipids) and signalling molecules. Essential fatty acids — linoleic (omega-6) and alpha-linolenic (omega-3) — must come from the diet; industrial partial hydrogenation created trans fatty acids, now largely banned for their cardiovascular harm.

Naming

Fatty acids are described by chain length and unsaturation: C18:1 cis-9 is oleic acid. Omega (ω) numbering counts from the methyl end, hence 'omega-3' oils from fish and flaxseed.

Soaps and candles

Hydrolysing fats releases fatty acids and glycerol — saponification — historically yielding soap and candle stearin; the same chemistry underlies biodiesel production from vegetable oils.
