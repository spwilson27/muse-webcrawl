Belgian Grand Prix

Venue: Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps

First held: 1925

Most wins (driver): Michael Schumacher (6)

Laps: 44 (modern layout)

The Belgian Grand Prix (French: Grand Prix de Belgique; Dutch: Grote Prijs van België) is a motor racing event that forms part of the Formula One World Championship. The first national race of Belgium was held in 1925 at the Spa region's race course, an area which had been associated with motor sport since the very early years of racing. The race is held at the Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps, famous for its fast, sweeping corners such as Eau Rouge and Raidillon and for its unpredictable Ardennes weather.

History

The original Spa circuit, laid out on public roads in 1921, was a fearsome 14.9 km triangle. The Belgian Grand Prix became part of the inaugural Formula One World Championship in 1950, won by Juan Manuel Fangio. The old circuit's dangers led drivers to boycott the race in 1969, and after 1970 the Grand Prix moved to Nivelles and then Zolder before returning in 1983 to a shortened, safer Spa-Francorchamps.

The race has been the scene of many memorable moments: Ayrton Senna won five times, and Michael Schumacher — who made his Formula One debut at Spa in 1991 — won a record six Belgian Grands Prix. The 1998 race saw a huge first-lap pile-up in heavy rain, and the 2021 event was effectively abandoned after a few laps behind the safety car amid torrential rain.

Winners

Multiple winners include Michael Schumacher (6), Ayrton Senna (5), Kimi Räikkönen, Jim Clark and Lewis Hamilton (4 each). Ferrari is the most successful constructor at the event.
