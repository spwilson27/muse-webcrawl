Overview

Moxidectin is an anthelmintic and antiparasitic drug belonging to the milbemycin family of macrocyclic lactones, which also includes ivermectin. It is a semi-synthetic derivative of nemadectin, produced by fermentation of the soil bacterium Streptomyces cyaneogriseus. Like other drugs in its class, it acts on glutamate-gated chloride channels in invertebrate nerve and muscle cells, paralysing and killing parasites, while having relatively low affinity for the corresponding mammalian channels.

Veterinary use

Moxidectin is widely used in veterinary medicine to prevent and treat parasites in dogs (notably heartworm prevention), cats, horses, cattle and sheep. It is sold under brand names including ProHeart and Advantage Multi/Advocate, and in combination products with other agents. It is effective against many nematodes and some ectoparasites, and has a long half-life that makes it useful in sustained-release formulations.

Human use

In June 2018 the United States Food and Drug Administration approved moxidectin for the treatment of onchocerciasis (river blindness), caused by the filarial worm Onchocerca volvulus, in patients aged 12 and over. Trials showed that a single dose suppressed skin microfilariae for considerably longer than ivermectin — the long-standing standard treatment — because of its much longer half-life in humans (roughly 20–43 days). Development was led by the nonprofit Medicines Development for Global Health, and researchers have also investigated moxidectin for scabies, strongyloidiasis and head lice. Precautions in human use include the risk of serious reactions in people co-infected with heavy Loa loa burdens, a concern highlighted during African safety studies.
