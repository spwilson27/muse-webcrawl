Fokker was a Dutch aircraft manufacturer founded by aviation pioneer Anthony Fokker (1890–1939). It operated, in various forms, from 1912 to 1996 and was one of the world's most successful aircraft builders of the interwar period. The name survives in successor companies providing aircraft services and components.

First World War

Fokker began building aircraft in Germany in 1912. His factories supplied the German air service with famous fighters including the Fokker Eindecker — the first aircraft fitted with a synchronisation gear allowing a machine gun to fire through the propeller arc, which gave Germany air superiority in 1915–16 (the "Fokker Scourge") — the Dr.I triplane flown by Manfred von Richthofen (the Red Baron), and the D.VII, regarded by many as the best fighter of the war; the Treaty of Versailles singled out the surrender of D.VIIs.

Interwar and postwar years

Fokker smuggled production to the Netherlands in 1919 and re-founded the company in Amsterdam. Fokker trimotors (F.VII and the American-built F-10) became leading early airliners, flying for KLM and pioneering routes including the first transpacific flights and Roald Amundsen's and Richard Byrd's polar expeditions. In the United States, the Fokker Aircraft Corporation became part of General Motors before fading in the Depression.

After the Second World War, the rebuilt company produced the hugely successful F27 Friendship turboprop (first flight 1955; 586 built, plus licence production by Fairchild), the F28 Fellowship jet, and the later Fokker 70 and Fokker 100 jets. Financial difficulties, the costs of the 100 programme and mounting debts led to bankruptcy on 15 March 1996 after rescue talks with Daimler-Benz (DASA) failed; parts of the business were taken over by Stork (later Fokker Technologies, now part of GKN Aerospace), and hundreds of F27s, F28s, 70s and 100s remain in service worldwide.
