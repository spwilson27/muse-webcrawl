Georges St-Pierre (born 19 May 1981), often nicknamed GSP, is a Canadian retired mixed martial artist. Widely regarded as one of the greatest fighters in MMA history, he was a two-division UFC champion, holding the welterweight title in two reigns and the middleweight title once.

Early life and style

St-Pierre was born in Saint-Isidore, Quebec, and began Kyokushin karate at age seven to defend himself against bullies. He worked as a garbage collector while training, and built a game that combined karate striking, wrestling takedowns and Brazilian jiu-jitsu into one of the most complete skill sets of his era, training at Tristar Gym in Montreal under Firas Zahabi and with wrestling and boxing coaches across North America.

Career

St-Pierre debuted in the UFC in 2004. He lost his first title challenge to Matt Hughes, then a shocking upset loss to Matt Serra in 2007; he avenged both defeats, regaining the welterweight belt from Serra in 2008 and defending it nine consecutive times against fighters including B.J. Penn, Jon Fitch, Thiago Alves, Dan Hardy, Josh Koscheck, Jake Shields, Carlos Condit, Nick Diaz and Johny Hendricks.

Middleweight title and retirement

After a four-year hiatus, St-Pierre returned in November 2017 at UFC 217 and submitted Michael Bisping to win the middleweight championship, becoming only the fourth fighter to hold UFC titles in two divisions. He vacated the belt weeks later, citing illness (ulcerative colitis), and formally retired in February 2019. He was inducted into the UFC Hall of Fame in 2020.
