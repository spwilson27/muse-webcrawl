On War

AuthorCarl von Clausewitz
Original titleVom Kriege
Published1832 (posthumous)
SubjectMilitary strategy and theory of war

On War (German: Vom Kriege) is a book on war and military strategy by Prussian general Carl von Clausewitz (1780–1831), written mostly after the Napoleonic Wars, between 1816 and 1830, and published posthumously by his wife Marie von Brühl in 1832. It is one of the most important treatises on military theory ever written and remains required reading at many military academies.

Main ideas

Clausewitz famously defines war as "the continuation of policy by other means" and describes it as a "remarkable trinity" composed of primordial violence and passion (the people), the play of chance and probability (the commander and his army), and subordination to political purpose (the government). He introduced enduring concepts including the fog of war, friction — the accumulation of small difficulties that makes the simple difficult — and the culminating point of an attack, beyond which an offensive loses strength.

Although unfinished at his death, the work's eight books range across the nature of war, strategy, combat, defence (which Clausewitz considered the stronger form of fighting), and war plans. Its dialectical method contrasts the abstract ideal of "absolute war" with the limited wars actually fought in history.
