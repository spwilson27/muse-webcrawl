The Grand Prismatic Spring in Yellowstone National Park is the largest hot spring in the United States, and the third largest in the world, after Frying Pan Lake in New Zealand and Boiling Lake in Dominica. It is located in the Midway Geyser Basin.

History

The spring was noted by fur trapper Osborne Russell in 1839 and described by the Washburn Expedition of 1870. It gets its name from its striking optical colours, likened by early visitors to the dispersion of white light by an optical prism into the colours of the rainbow: red, orange, yellow, green and blue.

Colour

The vivid colours around the edge of the mineral-rich water are the result of microbial mats of pigmented bacteria, especially carotenoid-producing species, which thrive at different temperatures and produce orange, red, yellow and green hues that change with the seasons. The centre of the pool is too hot—around 87 °C (189 °F)—to support most life and is sterile, giving it a deep blue colour from the scattering of light in the pure water.

Physical structure

The spring is approximately 370 feet (113 m) in diameter and over 160 feet (49 m) deep. It discharges an estimated 560 US gallons (2,100 litres) of 87 °C water per minute.
