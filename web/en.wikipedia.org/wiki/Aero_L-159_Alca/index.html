The Aero L-159 ALCA (Advanced Light Combat Aircraft) is a Czech-built subsonic light combat aircraft and advanced trainer derived from the Aero L-59 Super Albatros and ultimately from the widely exported L-39 Albatros. It combines a modern glass cockpit, multi-mode radar, and weapons carriage with the low operating costs of a trainer-class airframe.

Development

Aero Vodochody developed the L-159 in the 1990s for the Czech Air Force, replacing the original Soviet equipment with Western systems: a Honeywell F124 turbofan, Rockwell Collins avionics, and the Italian FIAR Grifo-L multi-mode radar in the single-seat L-159A. The first prototype flew on 2 August 1997, and production aircraft entered Czech service from 2000. A two-seat conversion trainer, the L-159B and T variants, supports lead-in fighter training.

Service

The Czech Republic ordered 72 aircraft but, after defence cuts, kept only about 24 in service, storing or selling the remainder. Surplus aircraft were sold to Iraq, which used the L-159 in combat against Islamic State forces from 2015, and to private companies including Draken International, which flies them as aggressor aircraft supporting U.S. Air Force training. The type also serves in the Czech inventory for air policing, close air support training, and light attack.

Design

The L-159 has seven hardpoints for up to 2,340 kg of stores, including AIM-9 Sidewinder air-to-air missiles, AGM-65 Maverick air-to-surface missiles, bombs, rockets, and a 20 mm gun pod. Its airframe was strengthened for higher g-loads and fitted with armour, self-sealing fuel tanks, and radar-warning receivers for the combat role.
