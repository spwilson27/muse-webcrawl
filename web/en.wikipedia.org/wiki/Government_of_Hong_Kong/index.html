Government of Hong Kong

Official nameGovernment of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region
Head of governmentChief Executive
SeatTamar, Hong Kong
Constitutional basisBasic Law of Hong Kong

The Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, commonly known simply as the Hong Kong Government or HKSAR Government, is the executive authority of Hong Kong. It was formed on 1 July 1997 in accordance with the Sino-British Joint Declaration of 1984 and the Basic Law, which together established Hong Kong as a special administrative region of China following the end of British rule.

Structure

The government is headed by the Chief Executive, who is selected by an Election Committee and appointed by the State Council of China. The Chief Executive leads the Executive Council and appoints principal officials, including the Chief Secretary for Administration, the Financial Secretary, and the Secretary for Justice, who head policy bureaux and departments.

The principal organs of the HKSAR government are the Chief Executive's office, the Executive Council, the policy bureaux and departments, the Legislative Council (the legislature), and the judiciary, headed by the Court of Final Appeal.

Administration

Hong Kong's civil service is one of the largest per capita in the world, with roughly 170,000 civil servants. The government operates from the Central Government Complex at Tamar, completed in 2011. Eighteen district offices provide local administration.
