Canes Venatici is one of the 88 constellations designated by the International Astronomical Union. It is a small northern constellation that was created by Johannes Hevelius in the 17th century. Its name is Latin for "hunting dogs", and the constellation is usually depicted as two dogs held on a leash by Boötes, the herdsman.

History

The stars of Canes Venatici are not bright, and in classical times they were listed by Ptolemy as unfigured stars below the constellation Ursa Major. Hevelius formally separated them into a distinct constellation in his sky atlas of 1687–1690, naming the two dogs Asterion and Chara. The brighter of the two stars, Cor Caroli ("Charles's Heart"), is said to have been named by Edmond Halley in honour of King Charles II of England.

Notable features

Although the constellation contains no very bright stars, it is rich in galaxies visible in amateur telescopes, lying in a direction away from the Milky Way's plane. The most famous is the Whirlpool Galaxy (M51), a face-on spiral galaxy interacting with a smaller companion, discovered by Charles Messier in 1773 and the first galaxy recognised as having spiral structure. Other notable objects include the Sunflower Galaxy (M63), the spiral galaxy M94, M106, and the globular cluster M3, one of the brightest in the northern sky. The supergiant variable star La Superba (Y CVn) is one of the reddest stars visible to the naked eye.
