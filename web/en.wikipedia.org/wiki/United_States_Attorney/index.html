United States Attorney

A United States Attorney is the chief federal law enforcement officer in one of the 94 federal judicial districts of the United States, within the Department of Justice. US Attorneys prosecute federal crimes, and represent the United States in civil litigation, in their districts.

AppointerPresident, with Senate confirmation
Term4 years (serves at President's pleasure)
Districts94 (93 offices; one covers Guam and Northern Mariana Islands)

Duties and organisation

Created by the Judiciary Act of 1789, each office is staffed by Assistant US Attorneys (over 6,000 nationwide) handling criminal, civil, and appellate divisions. Prominent offices include the Southern District of New York, known for financial-crime and national-security prosecutions, and the District of Columbia.

Appointment and removal

US Attorneys are nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate, and traditionally resign when administrations change; interim appointments may be made by the Attorney General or district courts. Controversies include the 2006 dismissals investigated by Congress and the 2020 removal of the SDNY US Attorney. Oversight comes from the Executive Office for United States Attorneys and the Attorney General.
