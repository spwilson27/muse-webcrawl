Compartmentalization (fire protection)

In fire protection, compartmentalization is the subdivision of a building into fire compartments separated by fire-resistant walls, floors, and doors, in order to limit the spread of fire and smoke and to protect escape routes.

Principle

Each compartment is designed to contain a fire for a specified period (its fire-resistance rating, often 30–120 minutes), giving occupants time to evacuate and firefighters time to intervene. Openings in compartment boundaries must be protected by fire doors, dampers, or firestopping.

Regulation

Building codes in many jurisdictions prescribe maximum compartment sizes and required fire resistance, especially in hospitals, high-rise residential buildings, and other sleeping-risk premises where a "stay put" or phased evacuation strategy may apply.
