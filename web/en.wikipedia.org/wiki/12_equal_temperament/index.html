12 equal temperament

12 equal temperament (12-TET, 12-ET) is the musical system that divides the octave into twelve equal parts, all semitones of equal size. It is the predominant tuning system of Western music. Each semitone corresponds to a frequency ratio of the twelfth root of two, approximately 1.0594631, and the interval of 100 cents.

Properties

In 12 equal temperament, all intervals are slightly mistuned relative to their just intonation counterparts except the octave. The perfect fifth is narrowed by about 2 cents and the major third widened by about 14 cents. The equal division makes all keys equally usable and allows unrestricted modulation, at the cost of slightly beating thirds and sixths.

History

Approximations to equal temperament were described in China by Zhu Zaiyu in 1584 and in Europe by Simon Stevin around the same period. The system gradually displaced meantone temperament during the 18th and 19th centuries as fixed-pitch instruments, especially the piano, became standard. Johann Sebastian Bach's The Well-Tempered Clavier demonstrated music in all 24 major and minor keys, though it was written for a circulating well temperament rather than modern 12-ET.
