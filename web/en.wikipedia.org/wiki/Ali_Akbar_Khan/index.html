Ali Akbar Khan (14 April 1922 – 18 June 2009) was an Indian Hindustani classical musician of the Maihar gharana, celebrated as a virtuoso of the sarod and often honoured with the title Ustad. Trained rigorously by his father, the legendary multi-instrumentalist Allauddin Khan, he became one of the most influential ambassadors of Indian classical music in the West.

Life and career

Born in the village of Shibpur in present-day Bangladesh, Khan gave his first public performance at the age of thirteen in Allahabad. He became court musician to the Maharaja of Jodhpur and was named Ustad at twenty-two. In 1955, at violinist Yehudi Menuhin's invitation, he performed in New York and recorded Music of India: Morning and Evening Ragas, among the first long-playing records of Indian classical music released in the West. He founded music colleges in Calcutta (1956), Berkeley, California (1967), and later settled in San Rafael, California, teaching generations of students. He performed frequently with his brother-in-law Ravi Shankar, and with tabla players including Alla Rakha.

Legacy

Khan composed numerous ragas, scored films including Satyajit Ray's Devi and Merchant Ivory's The Householder, and received India's Padma Bhushan (1967) and Padma Vibhushan (1989), a MacArthur "genius" fellowship in 1991, and the United States National Heritage Fellowship in 1997.
