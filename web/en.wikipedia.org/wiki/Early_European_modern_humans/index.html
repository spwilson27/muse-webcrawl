Early European modern humans

Early European modern humans (EEMH), or Cro-Magnons, are the earliest populations of modern humans (Homo sapiens) known in Europe, arriving from roughly 56,000 years ago and persisting through the Upper Paleolithic until about 10,000 years ago, when their descendant populations became the ancestors of later Europeans.

Early modern humans spread across Ice Age Europe between about 56,000 and 40,000 years ago.

Arrival and spread

Anatomically modern humans originated in Africa and dispersed into western Eurasia in several waves. Early modern human fossils at sites such as Bacho Kiro Cave in Bulgaria (about 45,000 years old) and Zlatý kůň in the Czech Republic document some of the earliest arrivals. The initial Upper Paleolithic and later Aurignacian culture (about 43,000–26,000 years ago) are associated with these populations, followed by the Gravettian (about 33,000–21,000 years ago) and, after the Last Glacial Maximum, the Solutrean and Magdalenian cultures.

Interaction with Neanderthals

Early modern humans overlapped in Europe with Neanderthals for several thousand years. Genetic evidence shows interbreeding occurred: present-day non-African genomes carry roughly 1–4% Neanderthal-derived DNA. Some of the earliest European modern humans, such as the individual from Peștera cu Oase in Romania, had a recent Neanderthal ancestor. Neanderthals disappeared from the fossil record by about 40,000 years ago.

Culture

EEMH produced sophisticated stone and bone tools, personal ornaments of shell, ivory and teeth, and some of the earliest known figurative art, including the cave paintings of Chauvet and Lascaux and portable sculptures such as the Venus figurines and the Lion-man of Hohlenstein-Stadel. They buried their dead, sometimes with rich grave goods, as at Sungir in Russia.

Genetics and legacy

Ancient DNA studies show that early European populations were repeatedly reshaped by migrations and population turnovers, especially after the Last Glacial Maximum. Hunter-gatherer groups descended from EEMH—Western, Eastern and Scandinavian hunter-gatherers—later mixed with incoming Neolithic farmers from Anatolia and with Bronze Age pastoralists from the Pontic steppe to form the genetic profile of present-day Europeans.
