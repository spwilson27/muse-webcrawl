Elizabeth Ann Warren (née Herring; born 22 June 1949) is an American politician and former law professor serving as the senior United States senator from Massachusetts, a seat she has held since 2013. A member of the Democratic Party, Warren was a candidate in the 2020 Democratic Party presidential primaries, finishing third.

Warren is a prominent figure in American progressive politics and a leading scholar of bankruptcy and commercial law. While a professor at Harvard Law School, she became a well-known public intellectual for her work on behalf of the American middle class and her criticism of deregulated financial markets.

Early life and education

Warren was born in Oklahoma City, the fourth child of a middle-class family. After her father suffered a heart attack, the family faced financial difficulty, an experience she has frequently cited as formative. She won a debate scholarship to George Washington University at 16, later earning a degree in audiology and speech pathology from the University of Houston (1970) and a law degree from Rutgers Law School (1976).

Academic career

Warren taught law at several universities, including the University of Texas, the University of Pennsylvania, and from 1995 Harvard Law School, where she became the Leo Gottlieb Professor of Law. Her research on household bankruptcy, including the book The Two-Income Trap (2003), co-written with her daughter Amelia Warren Tyagi, argued that middle-class families were taking on greater financial risk.

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

Following the financial crisis of 2007–2008, Warren chaired the Congressional Oversight Panel for the Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP). She proposed and championed the creation of the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), established by the Dodd–Frank Act in 2010, and served as a special advisor to President Barack Obama in setting up the agency.

U.S. Senate

Warren was elected to the Senate in 2012, defeating incumbent Republican Scott Brown, and was re-elected in 2018 and 2024. She has served on the Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs Committee, the Finance Committee, the Armed Services Committee, and the Special Committee on Aging, advocating for financial regulation, student debt relief, and a wealth tax.

2020 presidential campaign

Warren announced her candidacy for the 2020 Democratic presidential nomination in February 2019, campaigning on a detailed series of policy plans — earning the slogan "Warren has a plan for that" — including an "ultra-millionaire tax", breaking up large technology companies, and Medicare for All. She suspended her campaign in March 2020 after Super Tuesday.
