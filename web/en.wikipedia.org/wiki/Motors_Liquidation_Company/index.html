Motors Liquidation Company (MLC), formerly General Motors Corporation, was the name taken by the "old" General Motors after its 2009 Chapter 11 bankruptcy and the sale of its viable assets to a newly formed company, General Motors Company ("new GM"). Motors Liquidation retained the unwanted assets and liabilities of the old corporation and was charged with liquidating them for the benefit of creditors.

Background

General Motors, once the world's largest automaker, filed for Chapter 11 protection on 1 June 2009 — one of the largest industrial bankruptcies in American history. Under a section 363 sale approved by the bankruptcy court on 5 July 2009, substantially all operating assets were sold to the new, government-backed General Motors Company, which emerged from the process on 10 July 2009. The old corporation was renamed Motors Liquidation Company the same day.

Liquidation

What remained in MLC included closed plants and their environmental remediation obligations, discontinued brands' residual assets, and a vast body of claims. A liquidation plan was confirmed in March 2011, and the Motors Liquidation Company GUC Trust was created to distribute value — principally shares and warrants in new GM — to general unsecured creditors. The case was complicated by the ignition-switch defect scandal, which produced litigation over whether claims arising from pre-bankruptcy vehicles had been discharged. The trust completed its distributions and was dissolved in 2021, bringing the old GM's legal existence effectively to an end.
