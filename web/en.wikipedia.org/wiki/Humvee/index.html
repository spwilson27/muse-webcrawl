Humvee

High Mobility Multipurpose Wheeled Vehicle (HMMWV)
Type: Light military truck / utility vehicle
Manufacturer: AM General
Produced: 1984–present
Crew: 1 driver + passengers/cargo by variant
Successor (partial): Joint Light Tactical Vehicle (JLTV)

The High Mobility Multipurpose Wheeled Vehicle (HMMWV; colloquially Humvee) is a family of light, four-wheel drive military trucks and utility vehicles produced by AM General. It has served as the cornerstone of U.S. military light-vehicle fleets since the 1980s, replacing the jeep, M151 and other light vehicles in a wide range of roles.

Design

The HMMWV was designed for high mobility over rough terrain, with a wide stance, independent suspension, portal-geared hubs giving 16 inches of ground clearance, and a central tyre-inflation system on later models. A 6.2- or 6.5-litre diesel V8 powers most variants. The modular aluminium body can be configured as a troop carrier, cargo truck, ambulance, shelter carrier or weapons platform, including TOW missile and grenade-launcher carriers.

Service

Humvees entered U.S. service in 1985 and saw extensive combat use from Panama and the Gulf War through Somalia, Iraq and Afghanistan. In Iraq and Afghanistan the lightly armoured vehicle proved vulnerable to improvised explosive devices, leading to hurried up-armouring programmes (M1114 and "frag kit" variants) and ultimately to MRAP vehicles and the JLTV replacement in front-line roles. Tens of thousands have been exported to allied militaries worldwide.

Civilian versions

AM General sold a civilian version as the Hummer H1 beginning in 1992, which spawned General Motors' Hummer brand (H2, H3) before its discontinuation in 2010.
