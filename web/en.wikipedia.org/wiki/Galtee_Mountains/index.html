The Galtee Mountains (Irish: Na Gaibhlí, also spelled Galtee or Galty) are a mountain range in Munster, Ireland, spanning parts of counties Tipperary and Limerick. It is Ireland's highest inland mountain range. The highest peak is Galtymore (917.9 metres, 3,011 ft), one of Ireland's thirteen Furths / Vandeleur-Lynams and a Marilyn.

Geography

The range runs roughly east–west for about 30 km between Cahir and Mitchelstown, north of the Ballyhoura Mountains and south of the Golden Vale, overlooking the Glen of Aherlow. Principal summits besides Galtymore include Galtybeg, Greenane, Slievecushnabinnia and Carrignabinnia. Numerous corrie lakes, such as Lough Curra, Lough Muskry and Lake Diheen, nestle beneath the steep northern escarpment of old red sandstone conglomerate crags.

Recreation and legends

The Galtees are popular hillwalking country, served by routes from the Glen of Aherlow and Kings Yard. Local folklore associates the lakes with enchanted herds and the wizard Earl of Desmond's cattle; arrows of "fairy" legend and the mythological figure Aibell are connected with the Diheen lake. Folktales say the lakes were formed by St Patrick banishing serpents from the mountains.
