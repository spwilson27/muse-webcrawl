Sacred Headwaters

LocationNorth-western British Columbia, Canada
Area~9,000 km² (Klappan area)
RiversStikine, Skeena, Nass headwaters
PeoplesTahltan Nation traditional territory

The Sacred Headwaters is a large mountainous basin in north-western British Columbia, Canada, where the headwaters of three of North America's great salmon rivers — the Stikine, Skeena and Nass — arise within close proximity of one another. The area lies in the traditional territory of the Tahltan Nation and is known to the Tahltan as the Klappan. Its name reflects its importance as a source of the region's rivers and wildlife.

Geography and ecology

The region is a high plateau and mountain landscape of tundra, wetlands, and river valleys that supports caribou, stone sheep, grizzly bears, moose, wolves and mountain goats, as well as spawning salmon in the rivers downstream. The three river systems drain to the Pacific and sustain major commercial, recreational and Indigenous fisheries.

Resource conflicts

The Sacred Headwaters became the focus of prominent conflicts over industrial development. In the 2000s, Royal Dutch Shell pursued coalbed methane extraction in the Klappan, opposed by Tahltan elders and community members, who blockaded access in 2005. In 2012 the British Columbia government announced that Shell would relinquish its tenure and that oil and gas development would be banned in the area. Separately, Fortune Minerals' proposed Arctos anthracite coal mine in the Klappan met sustained Tahltan opposition; in 2015 the company and the province reached an agreement under which the mining licences were bought back and the project ended.

Conservation

Conservation organisations and the Tahltan have promoted long-term protection of the Sacred Headwaters, and portions of the wider region are included in protected areas such as the Stikine River Provincial Park. The nearby Mount Edziza and Spectrum Range form part of the same volcanic plateau.
