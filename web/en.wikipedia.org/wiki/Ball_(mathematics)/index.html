In mathematics, a ball is the solid interior of a sphere. In a metric space, a ball is the set of points whose distance from a given centre point is less than (for an open ball) or less than or equal to (for a closed ball) a given radius.

Euclidean space

In three-dimensional Euclidean space, an open ball of radius r centred at a point p consists of all points x with |x − p| < r. In n dimensions the volume of a ball grows with the radius raised to the power n, with constants involving the gamma function; the volume of the unit ball reaches its maximum in five dimensions and then decreases toward zero as n increases.

General metric spaces

The definition of a ball makes sense in any metric space and underlies the definition of open sets in metric topology: a set is open if every point of it is the centre of some ball entirely contained in the set. In spaces with unusual metrics, balls may take unexpected shapes; in the discrete metric (where distinct points are distance 1 apart), a ball of radius 1/2 is just its centre point, while a ball of radius 2 is the whole space.
