The Battle of Dan-no-ura (壇ノ浦の戦い, Dan-no-ura no tatakai) was a major sea battle of the Genpei War, fought on 25 April 1185 at Dan-no-ura in the Shimonoseki Strait off the southern tip of Honshu. The fleet of the Minamoto clan, commanded by Minamoto no Yoshitsune, decisively defeated the fleet of the Taira clan, ending the war and paving the way for the Kamakura shogunate.

The climactic naval battle of the Genpei War, 1185.

The battle

The Taira, driven west along the Inland Sea, carried with them the child Emperor Antoku and the Imperial Regalia. At first the strong tidal current favoured the experienced Taira sailors, whose archers took a heavy toll. But the tide reversed in the afternoon, a Taira defector revealed the location of the emperor's ship, and Yoshitsune's men concentrated their attack, reportedly aiming at the enemy's oarsmen and helmsmen. As defeat became certain, many Taira nobles drowned themselves; the emperor's grandmother, Taira no Tokiko, took the six-year-old Antoku in her arms and leapt into the sea, along with the sacred sword of the regalia, which was never recovered.

Aftermath and legend

The annihilation of the Taira cleared the way for Minamoto no Yoritomo to establish the Kamakura shogunate in 1192, beginning nearly seven centuries of military government in Japan. The battle is retold in the epic The Tale of the Heike. Local legend holds that the heikegani ("samurai crabs") of the strait, whose shells bear patterns resembling a samurai's face, are the reincarnated spirits of the drowned Taira warriors.
