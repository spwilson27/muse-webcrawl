Pharmacogenomics

Pharmacogenomics is the study of how a person's genome affects their response to drugs. Combining pharmacology with genomics, the field seeks to tailor medication choice and dose to individual genetic makeup in order to maximize efficacy and minimize adverse drug reactions — a central goal of precision (personalized) medicine. The related term pharmacogenetics traditionally referred to single-gene effects on drug response, while pharmacogenomics addresses genome-wide influences.

Mechanisms

Genetic variants influence drug action through pharmacokinetics (absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion) and pharmacodynamics (drug targets). Key genes include the cytochrome P450 enzymes (such as CYP2D6, CYP2C9, and CYP2C19), which metabolize a large share of commonly prescribed drugs; TPMT, affecting thiopurine drugs; the vitamin K pathway gene VKORC1, affecting warfarin sensitivity; HLA alleles associated with severe hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., HLA-B*57:01 and abacavir; HLA-B*15:02 and carbamazepine); and DPYD, affecting fluoropyrimidine toxicity.

Clinical applications

Regulators such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration include pharmacogenomic biomarkers in the labelling of well over a hundred drugs. Well-established examples include clopidogrel activation by CYP2C19, codeine metabolism by CYP2D6 (ultrarapid metabolizers risk opioid toxicity), HER2 testing before trastuzumab, and EGFR mutation testing for certain lung-cancer therapies. Consortia such as the Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC) publish guidelines translating genotype results into prescribing recommendations, and preemptive pharmacogenomic testing panels have been implemented at a number of academic medical centres.

History

Observations date to the 1950s — primaquine-induced haemolysis in G6PD deficiency and variable succinylcholine metabolism — before Friedrich Vogel coined "pharmacogenetics" in 1959. Sequencing of the human genome in the early 2000s expanded the field to genome-wide association studies of drug response.
