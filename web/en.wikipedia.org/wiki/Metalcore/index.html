Metalcore

Metalcore
Stylistic originsExtreme metal, hardcore punk, crossover thrash
Cultural originsLate 1980s–early 1990s, United States
Derivative formsMelodic metalcore, deathcore, mathcore, nu metalcore
Typical instrumentsElectric guitar, bass, drums, vocals (screamed and sung)

Metalcore is a fusion music genre that combines elements of extreme metal and hardcore punk. As with other styles blending metal and hardcore, such as crust punk and grindcore, metalcore is noted for its use of breakdowns, which are slow, intense passages conducive to moshing. Other defining instrumentation includes heavy guitar riffs often incorporating dissonance, double bass drumming, and contrasting screamed and cleanly sung vocals.

History

The genre grew out of the crossover of hardcore and metal in the late 1980s, with bands such as Integrity, Earth Crisis, and Converge shaping its early form in the 1990s — Converge's Jane Doe (2001) is a landmark. In the 2000s, metalcore achieved mainstream success through melodic metalcore bands such as Killswitch Engage, Bullet for My Valentine, As I Lay Dying, and Trivium, and later through commercially prominent acts including Bring Me the Horizon, Architects, Parkway Drive, and Bad Omens.

Characteristics

Metalcore songs commonly alternate aggressive verses employing screamed or shouted vocals with melodic choruses sung cleanly. Guitarists use dropped tunings, palm-muted chugging, and harmonised leads influenced by Swedish melodic death metal, while drummers employ double-kick patterns and blast beats. Lyrics often deal with personal struggle, politics, and social issues inherited from hardcore.

Subgenres

Subgenres include melodic metalcore, deathcore (fused with death metal), mathcore (technically complex, pioneered by Botch and The Dillinger Escape Plan), electronicore, and nu metalcore.
