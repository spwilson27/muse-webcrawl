Model (person)

A model is a person employed to display, advertise and promote commercial products — notably fashion clothing in runway shows — or to serve as a visual aid for people who are creating works of art, or to pose for photography. Modelling is generally considered distinct from other performing arts such as acting or dancing, though models frequently appear in films and television.

Types

Fashion models

Editorial and runway models work for fashion designers, brands and magazines. The industry has historically imposed strict physical requirements — tall, slender builds for women's runway work — though standards have broadened with the growth of plus-size, petite and mature modelling categories. The most successful fashion models, often dubbed supermodels, became global celebrities in the 1990s; the era's defining names included Naomi Campbell, Cindy Crawford, Linda Evangelista, Christy Turlington and Claudia Schiffer.

Commercial and other models

Commercial models advertise products in print, broadcast and online media and span a wider range of ages and body types. Specialist categories include fit models, who try on garments during production; parts models, who model hands, legs or other features; glamour models; promotional models at trade shows and events; and artist's models, who pose for painters, sculptors and photographers.

History

Charles Frederick Worth, the 19th-century Paris couturier, is credited with pioneering live modelling by having his wife Marie Vernet present his designs. Photography made models central to 20th-century advertising; the rise of agencies such as Ford Models (founded 1946) professionalised the trade. The "British invasion" of the 1960s made Jean Shrimpton and Twiggy international faces, and the profession has since been transformed by social media, which allows models to build independent audiences as influencers.

Industry issues

The modelling industry has faced sustained criticism over body-image pressures and the health of models, leading several countries and fashion houses to adopt minimum age and body-mass rules for runway work. Concerns about working conditions, payment and the treatment of underage models have prompted unionisation efforts and regulatory changes in France, the United Kingdom and elsewhere.
