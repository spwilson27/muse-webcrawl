Landstuhl (German pronunciation: [ˈlantʃtuːl]) is a town in the district of Kaiserslautern, in Rhineland-Palatinate, western Germany. It lies at the northern edge of the Palatinate Forest, near the Sickingen Heights, about 11 km west of Kaiserslautern. The town's official title, Sickingenstadt Landstuhl, recalls the imperial knight Franz von Sickingen, whose seat was Nanstein Castle above the town.

History

The area was settled in Roman times, and the modern town grew up beneath Nanstein Castle, built in the 12th century. In 1523 the castle was besieged by the forces of the Archbishop of Trier, the Elector Palatine and the Landgrave of Hesse; Franz von Sickingen was mortally wounded in the defence and died there, an event often taken to mark the end of the era of the free imperial knights. Landstuhl received town rights and later became a local administrative and garrison centre.

American presence

Landstuhl is internationally known for the Landstuhl Regional Medical Center, the largest United States military hospital outside the continental United States, established in the 1950s on the Kirchberg heights above the town. Together with nearby Ramstein Air Base, it forms a key part of the Kaiserslautern Military Community, the largest American military community overseas, and treated tens of thousands of wounded service members evacuated from the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan.
