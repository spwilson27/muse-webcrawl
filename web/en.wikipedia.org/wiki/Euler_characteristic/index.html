Euler characteristic

In mathematics, and more specifically in algebraic topology and polyhedral combinatorics, the Euler characteristic (or Euler number, or Euler–Poincaré characteristic) is a topological invariant, a number that describes a topological space's shape or structure regardless of the way it is bent.

Definition

For a convex polyhedron, χ = V − E + F, where V is vertices, E edges and F faces, and the value is 2 — Euler's polyhedron formula, known from work by Leonhard Euler around 1750. For a triangulated surface, χ = V − E + F using any triangulation. More generally χ is the alternating sum of Betti numbers.

Examples

A sphere has χ = 2, a torus χ = 0, a double torus χ = −2, and a closed orientable surface of genus g has χ = 2 − 2g. For a non-orientable surface of genus k, χ = 2 − k.

Properties

The Euler characteristic is additive under disjoint union and behaves multiplicatively for products and covering spaces; it is a homotopy invariant.
