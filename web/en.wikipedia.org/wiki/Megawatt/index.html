Megawatt

The megawatt (symbol: MW) is a unit of power equal to one million watts (106 W), or one thousand kilowatts. It is commonly used to express the output of power stations, electric motors, and other large-scale electrical systems. Energy produced or consumed over time is often measured in megawatt-hours (MWh): one megawatt sustained for one hour.

Usage

Large power plants are rated in hundreds or thousands of megawatts (often expressed in gigawatts). A typical large wind turbine produces around 2–5 MW at rated output; a large nuclear reactor generates roughly 1,000 MW (1 GW) of electricity. In science fiction, the fictional flux capacitor in Back to the Future famously required "1.21 gigawatts" of power.

Conversions

  • 1 MW = 1,000 kW
  • 1 MW = 1,000,000 W
  • 1 GW = 1,000 MW
  • 1 MW ≈ 1,341 horsepower
