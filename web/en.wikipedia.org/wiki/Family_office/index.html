Family office

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A family office is a privately held company that handles investment management and wealth management for a wealthy family, generally one with at least $50–100 million in investable assets, with the goal of effectively growing and transferring wealth across generations. The company's financial capital is the family's own wealth.

Family offices also provide personal services such as managing household staff, travel arrangements, property management, day-to-day accounting and payroll, management of legal affairs, family governance, financial and investor education, coordination of philanthropy, and succession planning.

Types

A single-family office serves one family and is wholly owned and controlled by it. A multi-family office serves several families, pooling costs while offering similar services; many began as single-family offices that opened to others. An embedded family office operates inside a family business, where executives handle personal financial matters alongside corporate ones.

History

The concept has roots in the Roman domus and the medieval royal steward, but the modern family office is usually traced to the Rockefeller family, which established a dedicated office in 1882 to manage its fortune and affairs. The number of family offices grew sharply in the late 20th and early 21st centuries alongside global wealth creation; estimates of the worldwide total run into the thousands, managing several trillion dollars.

Regulation

In the United States, the Dodd-Frank Act of 2010 exempted qualifying single-family offices from registration as investment advisers under the Investment Advisers Act, subject to conditions set by the Securities and Exchange Commission; multi-family offices typically must register. Disclosure controversies, including those following the 2021 collapse of Archegos Capital Management (structured as a family office), prompted debate over whether large family offices should face greater oversight.
