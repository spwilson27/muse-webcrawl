Debt peonage

Debt peonage is a system in which a worker is bound to an employer through debt, common historically in Latin America and the post-Civil War American South.

Under peonage, workers — often sharecroppers or labourers on haciendas — were paid in scrip or advanced goods at inflated prices, keeping them perpetually indebted and unable to leave. In the United States, the Peonage Act of 1867 outlawed the practice, but it persisted through convict leasing and coercive contracts until federal enforcement in the 1940s.

Legacy

Debt peonage is closely related to debt bondage and is classified as a form of unfree labour.
