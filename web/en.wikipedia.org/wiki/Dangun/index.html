Dangun

Dangun (Korean: 단군; Hanja: 檀君), also known as Dangun Wanggeom (단군왕검), is the legendary founder of Gojoseon, the first Korean kingdom. According to the founding myth recorded in the 13th-century Samguk yusa, Dangun was the grandson of the Lord of Heaven and founded his kingdom in 2333 BCE. He is venerated as the progenitor of the Korean people, and the traditional date of his founding of Gojoseon is commemorated in South Korea as National Foundation Day (Gaecheonjeol, 3 October).

The myth

The myth relates that Hwanin, the Lord of Heaven, granted his son Hwanung's wish to descend to Earth. Hwanung came down with 3,000 followers to Mount Taebaek, where he governed with ministers of wind, rain, and clouds. A bear and a tiger prayed to become human; Hwanung gave them sacred mugwort and garlic and instructed them to avoid sunlight for one hundred days. The tiger gave up, but the bear endured twenty-one days and became a beautiful woman, Ungnyeo, who married Hwanung. Their son was Dangun Wanggeom, who founded the capital at Asadal and named his kingdom Joseon.

Historical and cultural significance

Historians treat Dangun as a mythic figure reflecting Bronze Age tribal federation and shamanic tradition rather than a verifiable individual, though he remains a powerful national symbol in both Koreas. North Korea announced in 1993 the excavation of the "Tomb of Dangun" near Pyongyang, which it presents as his burial place. The era name Dangi, counting years from 2333 BCE, was officially used in South Korea from 1948 to 1961.
