Lake Monroe (Florida)

Lake Monroe is one of the lakes that make up the St. Johns River system in Central Florida, United States. It lies on the border of Seminole and Volusia counties, with the city of Sanford on its southern shore and DeBary and Deltona to the north. The lake is situated northeast of Orlando and is one of the largest lakes on the St. Johns River.

Geography

Lake Monroe has a surface area of roughly 9,400 acres (about 38 square kilometres), making it the eighth largest lake in Florida by some counts and one of the largest on the St. Johns chain. The St. Johns River enters the lake at its southern end and exits at the northeast, continuing northward toward Lake George and ultimately to Jacksonville. The lake is shallow, with an average depth of only about 2 metres (6.5 feet).

The southern shore is dominated by the historic waterfront of Sanford, the county seat of Seminole County. U.S. Highway 17/92 crosses the lake's western outlet on the Senator St. Johns River Veterans Memorial Bridge at DeBary, and Interstate 4 passes near the lake's eastern end.

History

The area around the lake was long inhabited by Timucua peoples before European contact. In the 19th century, the lake served as a key steamboat landing: Sanford, founded as a port on the lake's south shore, became an important gateway for commerce into interior Florida, with steamboats carrying citrus, lumber and passengers up the St. Johns River from Jacksonville.

Recreation and ecology

Lake Monroe is popular for largemouth bass and black crappie fishing, boating, and manatee watching, particularly in the cooler months when manatees gather in the warmer waters near the lake's springs and power-plant outflows. Lake Monroe Park, on the lake's northwest shore in DeBary, provides boat ramps, camping and trails, and the lake is a stop on regional paddling and birding trails.
