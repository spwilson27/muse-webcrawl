Windham is a town in Greene County, New York, United States. The population was 1,708 at the 2020 census. Located in the northern Catskill Mountains along the Schoharie Creek watershed, Windham is best known as the home of Windham Mountain Resort, a popular ski area that has earned the town the nickname "Gem of the Catskills."

History

The region was first settled around 1780, and the town was formed from the town of Woodstock in 1798 under the name Batavia; it was renamed Windham in 1800. Early residents came largely from Connecticut and elsewhere in New England. The town developed as a farming and tanning community, and in the nineteenth century its mountain scenery began attracting summer visitors.

The opening of Windham Mountain (originally Cave Mountain) in 1960 transformed the local economy toward tourism and second homes. The town was heavily damaged by flooding from Hurricane Irene in 2011, when the Batavia Kill inundated Main Street, but it has since rebuilt.

Geography

Windham occupies the northwestern corner of Greene County, bordered by Albany and Schoharie counties. The town includes the hamlets of Windham, Hensonville, and Maplecrest. New York State Route 23 passes through the town, connecting it to Cairo and the Hudson Valley to the east.

Recreation

Windham Mountain Resort offers skiing and snowboarding in winter and mountain biking in summer. The Windham Path, a paved multi-use trail, links the hamlet to the resort. The town also hosts the World Cup mountain bike races and various festivals.
