Drag queen

Drag queen
MediumPerformance, theatre, nightlife
ElementsCostume, wig, make-up, lip-sync, comedy, dance

A drag queen is a person, usually male, who uses dress and make-up to imitate and often exaggerate female gender signifiers and gender roles for entertainment purposes. Drag queens typically perform in shows involving lip-syncing, live singing, dancing, comedy and audience interaction, adopting an exaggerated persona with a distinctive stage name. Drag is an art form and a subculture with roots in theatre — where female roles were historically played by men — and in the queer nightlife of the 20th century, including the ball culture of Black and Latino LGBTQ communities in New York City.

History

Female impersonation appeared in Elizabethan theatre, 19th-century minstrel and vaudeville acts (such as Julian Eltinge), and early-20th-century "pansy clubs". Drag became a cornerstone of gay bar culture; performers such as those at San Francisco's Finocchio's and later the queens of Harlem's drag balls developed its modern forms. The 1990 documentary Paris Is Burning documented the ball scene, and performers such as Divine and RuPaul brought drag toward mainstream audiences.

Mainstream popularity

The reality competition series RuPaul's Drag Race, launched in 2009, became a global franchise and made drag a worldwide entertainment phenomenon, spawning international editions, conventions such as DragCon, and mainstream careers for its contestants. Drag has also become a focus of political controversy in some jurisdictions, where proposed or enacted restrictions on public drag performance have drawn protests and legal challenges on free-expression grounds.
