Motorcycle trials

Motorcycle trials, also known as observed trials, is a non-speed motorcycle discipline in which riders navigate an obstacle course (sections) without putting a foot down or stopping. The sport emphasizes balance, throttle control, and precision at very low speeds rather than speed.

Competition

A trial consists of a series of marked sections over natural or artificial terrain — rocks, logs, streams, steep banks. An observer at each section scores penalty points: one point for each "dab" (foot touching the ground), up to three dabs, and five points for a failure such as falling, stalling while footing, or leaving the course. The rider with the lowest total score wins. World championship competition is governed by the FIM (TrialGP); indoor events are held as X-Trial. The Trial des Nations is the annual team competition. Spanish rider Toni Bou has dominated the modern era with an unprecedented run of consecutive world titles.

Machines

Trials motorcycles are extremely light (around 70 kg), with low gearing, soft suspension, high ground clearance, and engines of roughly 125–300 cc tuned for low-end torque. Major manufacturers include Montesa, Gas Gas, Beta, Sherco, and TRS; electric models are increasingly common.
