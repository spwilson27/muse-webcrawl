Jack Tramiel

Jack Tramiel (born Idek Trzmiel; December 13, 1928 – April 8, 2012) was a Polish-American businessman best known for founding Commodore International, whose Commodore 64 became the best-selling single computer model of all time, and for later leading Atari Corporation. A Holocaust survivor, his motto was "computers for the masses, not the classes."

Early life

Born to a Jewish family in Łódź, Tramiel survived the Łódź Ghetto and the Auschwitz and Ahlem concentration camps. Liberated in 1945, he emigrated to the United States in 1947, joined the U.S. Army (where he learned typewriter repair), and founded a typewriter business in the Bronx that became Commodore.

Commodore and Atari

Commodore moved from typewriters to calculators to computers; after acquiring MOS Technology, it launched the PET (1977), VIC-20 (1980), and Commodore 64 (1982). Tramiel's aggressive price wars ("business is war") drove rivals from the market but squeezed Commodore itself. Ousted in 1984, he bought Atari's consumer division from Warner Communications, forming Atari Corporation, and launched the Atari ST line against Commodore's Amiga. He retired in 1996 when Atari merged into JTS.
