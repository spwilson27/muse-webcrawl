British sovereign

The British sovereign, or monarch of the United Kingdom, is the head of state of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland and of the other Commonwealth realms. The monarchy is constitutional: the sovereign reigns but does not rule, exercising executive authority through ministers responsible to Parliament while retaining formal roles in the legislature, the judiciary, the armed forces and the established Church of England.

Constitutional role

The sovereign is the head of the executive, an integral part of the legislature (as one component of the King-in-Parliament), head of the judiciary, commander-in-chief of the British Armed Forces, and Supreme Governor of the Church of England. In practice, nearly all prerogative powers are exercised on the binding advice of ministers. The sovereign appoints the prime minister — conventionally the person able to command the confidence of the House of Commons — gives royal assent to legislation, opens Parliament each session, and grants honours.

Succession

Succession is governed by the Bill of Rights 1689 and the Act of Settlement 1701, which limited the crown to Protestant descendants of Sophia, Electress of Hanover, and by the Succession to the Crown Act 2013, which introduced absolute primogeniture for those born after 28 October 2011 and ended the bar on marrying a Roman Catholic. The sovereign must be in communion with the Church of England and swears to maintain the Church of Scotland.

History

The unified British monarchy dates from the Acts of Union 1707, which joined the crowns of England and Scotland, and took its present form with the union with Ireland in 1801 and Irish partition in 1922. The political power of the sovereign declined steadily through the nineteenth century; by the reign of Queen Victoria the modern constitutional settlement — in which, in Walter Bagehot's phrase, the monarch retains the rights "to be consulted, to encourage and to warn" — was firmly established.

Finances and residences

The monarchy is funded principally through the Sovereign Grant, calculated as a percentage of the profits of the Crown Estate. Official residences include Buckingham Palace in London and Windsor Castle, with the Palace of Holyroodhouse in Edinburgh and Hillsborough Castle in Northern Ireland.
