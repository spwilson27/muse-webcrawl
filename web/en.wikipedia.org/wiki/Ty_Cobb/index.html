Ty Cobb

Tyrus Raymond Cobb (December 18, 1886 – July 17, 1961), nicknamed "the Georgia Peach", was an American baseball center fielder. He spent 22 seasons with the Detroit Tigers, the last six as the team's player-manager, and finished his career with the Philadelphia Athletics.

Career

Cobb is widely credited with setting ninety records during his career, including the highest career batting average (.366) and twelve batting titles. He held the record for career hits (4,189 or 4,191 depending on the source) until it was broken by Pete Rose in 1985, and his record of stealing home 54 times still stands. Cobb's fierce competitiveness made him one of the most controversial figures in the sport. In 1936 he became one of the five inaugural members of the Baseball Hall of Fame, receiving more votes than any other player, including Babe Ruth. After baseball he became wealthy through investments in Coca-Cola and General Motors stock and funded scholarships and a hospital in his native Georgia.
