Ventura, California

Ventura, officially named City of San Buenaventura, is a city in and the county seat of Ventura County, California, United States. It is a coastal city on the Pacific Ocean northwest of Los Angeles, with a population of about 110,000 (2020).

History

The area was inhabited by the Chumash people for thousands of years; the village of Shisholop stood near the Ventura River mouth. Mission San Buenaventura was founded by Junípero Serra in 1782, the ninth and last mission he personally established. The city was incorporated in 1866 and grew with agriculture (citrus and lima beans) and the oil industry after the discovery of the Ventura Oil Fields in the early 20th century.

Geography and economy

Ventura lies between the Ventura and Santa Clara Rivers, backed by coastal hills. Its historic downtown centres on Main Street; the Ventura Pier dates to 1872. The outdoor clothing company Patagonia is headquartered in Ventura. The city is also a departure point for Channel Islands National Park, headquartered at Ventura Harbor.
