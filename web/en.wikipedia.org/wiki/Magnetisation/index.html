Magnetization

SymbolM
SI unitAmpere per metre (A/m)
DefinitionMagnetic dipole moment per unit volume
RelationB = μ₀(H + M)

Magnetization (American spelling; British magnetisation), denoted M, is a vector field that expresses the density of permanent or induced magnetic dipole moments in a material — that is, the magnetic moment per unit volume. Its SI unit is the ampere per metre (A/m), the same as the magnetic field strength H to which it is directly related.

Role in Maxwell's equations

Magnetization describes how a material responds to and modifies a magnetic field. The magnetic flux density inside matter is B = μ₀(H + M), where H accounts for free currents and M for the material's own aligned dipoles, which can be modelled as "bound currents". For linear materials M = χmH, with χm the magnetic susceptibility — negative for diamagnets, small and positive for paramagnets. In ferromagnets the relation is nonlinear and history-dependent, producing hysteresis: a permanent magnet retains a remanent magnetization after the applied field is removed.

Behaviour by material type

In diamagnetic materials, magnetization opposes the applied field weakly. In paramagnetic materials, pre-existing atomic moments align partially with the field, following Curie's law. In ferromagnetic materials such as iron, exchange coupling aligns moments in domains, and magnetization can approach a saturation value; above the Curie temperature the order collapses into paramagnetism. Magnetization dynamics — precession and damping of M described by the Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert equation — underpin technologies from magnetic recording and MRI contrast behaviour to spintronics.
