Inyo County is a county in the eastern central part of the U.S. state of California, located between the Sierra Nevada mountains and the state of Nevada. As of the 2020 census, the population was 19,016. The county seat is Independence, and the largest community is Bishop.

Geography

Inyo County is the second-largest county in California by area. It contains Mount Whitney, at 14,505 feet (4,421 m) the highest point in the contiguous United States, and Badwater Basin in Death Valley, at 282 feet (86 m) below sea level the lowest point in North America. Much of Death Valley National Park lies within the county, as does the Owens Valley, whose water history — the California water wars over the Los Angeles Aqueduct — is famously contentious.

History

The county was formed in 1866 from parts of Mono and Tulare counties. Its name, given by the explorer Joseph R. Walker party's predecessors, is traditionally described as a Paiute word meaning "dwelling place of the great spirit." The region was home to the Owens Valley Paiute and Shoshone peoples, and saw mining booms at Cerro Gordo and other sites. During the Second World War, the Manzanar camp near Independence held Japanese Americans interned under Executive Order 9066.

Communities

Communities include Bishop, Lone Pine, Independence, Big Pine, and Furnace Creek.
