Racing Rules of Sailing

The Racing Rules of Sailing (often abbreviated to RRS) govern the conduct of yacht racing, windsurfing, kitesurfing, model boat racing, dinghy racing and virtually any other form of racing around a course with more than one vessel while powered by the wind. A new revision is published every four years by World Sailing, the sport's world governing body.

The rules are organized in parts covering fundamental rules (including safety and fair sailing), right-of-way rules for boats meeting on the course, rules at marks and obstructions, conduct after incidents (protests, penalties and hearings), starting and finishing, race management, and technical appendices giving definitions and prescriptions.

Fundamental principles

The basic principles require competitors to follow and enforce the rules, to accept penalties when they break a rule, and to help others in danger. Key right-of-way rules include: a boat on port tack keeps clear of one on starboard; a leeward boat has right of way over a windward boat on the same tack; and a boat that is tacking keeps clear of others. Rule 18 governs mark-room, requiring boats to give each other space to round marks.

History

Uniform racing rules developed in the 19th century from the rules of individual yacht clubs; international codification proceeded through the Yacht Racing Association and, after 1946, the International Yacht Racing Union (now World Sailing). The rules went through a major simplification in 1997.
