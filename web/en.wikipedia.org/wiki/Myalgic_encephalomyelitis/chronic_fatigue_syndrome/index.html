Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome

Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is a debilitating chronic illness characterised by profound fatigue that is not improved by rest, post-exertional malaise (a worsening of symptoms after even minor physical or mental effort), unrefreshing sleep, and cognitive difficulties often called "brain fog". Many patients also experience orthostatic intolerance, pain, and sensitivity to light and sound. The illness can leave some people bedbound for years, and its severity ranges from mild to very severe.

ME/CFS affects multiple body systems; its hallmark is post-exertional malaise.

Signs and symptoms

The central feature is a substantial reduction in the ability to engage in pre-illness levels of activity, lasting more than six months, accompanied by fatigue that is often profound, is not the result of ongoing excessive exertion, and is not substantially relieved by rest. Post-exertional malaise — a delayed exacerbation of symptoms following exertion — is considered the hallmark of the condition. Sleep is typically unrefreshing, and cognitive impairment affects memory, concentration, and information processing speed.

Causes and mechanisms

The cause of ME/CFS is unknown. Onset often follows a viral infection, such as Epstein–Barr virus, and research has implicated immune dysfunction, autonomic nervous system abnormalities, metabolic and mitochondrial problems, and neuroinflammation. The condition is more common in women than in men. A substantial increase in cases has been associated with long COVID, and some long COVID patients meet ME/CFS diagnostic criteria.

Diagnosis

There is no single diagnostic test; diagnosis is clinical, based on criteria such as those issued by the US Institute of Medicine (now National Academy of Medicine) in 2015, after excluding other conditions that cause similar symptoms. The name "systemic exertion intolerance disease" (SEID) was proposed by the IOM but has not been widely adopted.

Management

No cure or approved drug treatment exists. Management focuses on symptom relief and "pacing" — carefully managing activity to stay within the patient's "energy envelope" and avoid post-exertional malaise. Earlier recommendations of graded exercise therapy and cognitive behavioural therapy as curative treatments have been controversial and were revised in guidelines such as NICE NG206 (2021).

History and controversy

Clusters of the illness were described from the 1930s onward; the term "myalgic encephalomyelitis" arose after a 1955 outbreak at the Royal Free Hospital in London, and "chronic fatigue syndrome" was coined in the 1980s. For decades the illness was frequently dismissed as psychological, which patients and researchers have criticised as harmful; biomedical research funding has historically been low relative to disease burden.
