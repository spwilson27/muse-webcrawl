The Gibson EDS-1275 is a double-neck electric guitar manufactured by the Gibson Guitar Corporation. It combines a 12-string guitar and a conventional six-string guitar on a single body, derived from the shape of the Gibson SG, allowing a guitarist to switch between the two instruments mid-performance without changing guitars.

History

Gibson produced double-neck instruments as special orders in the 1950s; the EDS-1275 entered regular production in 1963. It was made in limited numbers through the 1960s and 1970s, discontinued at times, and reissued repeatedly, including as a signature model for Jimmy Page.

Notable users

The guitar became iconic through its association with Jimmy Page of Led Zeppelin, who used a cherry-red EDS-1275 for live performances of songs such as "Stairway to Heaven" (using the 12-string neck for the opening sections and the 6-string neck for the solo), "The Song Remains the Same" and "The Rain Song". Don Felder of the Eagles used a white EDS-1275 for live performances of "Hotel California", and Alex Lifeson of Rush also played one. Felder's guitar is displayed at the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.

Design

The EDS-1275 has a solid mahogany body and two mahogany necks, each with its own set of humbucking pickups, volume and tone controls, and a toggle switch to select between necks. The six-string neck conventionally sits at the bottom and the twelve-string neck at the top. The instrument is notably heavy, weighing around 5.5–6 kg (12–13 lb).
