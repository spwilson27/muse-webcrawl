Barbara Kruger (born 26 January 1945) is an American conceptual artist and collagist associated with the Pictures Generation. She is known for large-scale works that layer declarative, often confrontational text — typically in Futura Bold Italic, white on red bands — over black-and-white photographs, directly addressing viewers about consumerism, power, gender and identity through pronouns such as "you", "I", "we" and "they".

Early life and design career

Born in Newark, New Jersey, Kruger studied at Syracuse University and Parsons School of Design in New York, briefly attending classes with photographer Diane Arbus and designer Marvin Israel. From 1966 she worked as a graphic designer at Condé Nast's Mademoiselle magazine, becoming its head designer at 22; a decade in commercial publishing gave her the direct, cropped, advertising-derived visual language of her mature work.

Artwork

From the late 1970s Kruger developed her signature paste-up collages combining found photographs with aphoristic texts: Untitled (I shop therefore I am) (1987), a play on Descartes that critiques consumer identity; Untitled (Your body is a battleground) (1989), produced for the Women's March on Washington in support of abortion rights; and Untitled (Your gaze hits the side of my face) (1981). She has expanded into room-size installations that wrap text across floors, walls and ceilings (at venues including the Hirshhorn Museum and Tate Modern's Turbine Hall commissions), billboards, buses, and public projects. Her work has also been widely imitated — notably in the streetwear brand Supreme's red-box logo, which Kruger has publicly commented on.

Kruger represented the United States at the Venice Biennale in 1982 and received its Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement in 2005. A major retrospective, Thinking of You. I Mean Me. I Mean You., opened at the Art Institute of Chicago in 2021 and travelled to LACMA and MoMA. She has taught at UCLA, where she is a Distinguished Professor.
