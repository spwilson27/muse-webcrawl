The Book of Sirach (Ecclesiasticus), also known as the Wisdom of Sirach or the Wisdom of Jesus, the Son of Sirach, is a Jewish work of ethical teachings, written in Hebrew around 200–175 BC by the scribe Jesus ben Sira of Jerusalem. It is one of the largest wisdom books in the tradition, consisting of 51 chapters of proverbs, hymns, prayers, and practical instruction.

Authorship and date

Ben Sira identifies himself in the text as "Jesus, the son of Sirach, of Jerusalem" (50:27). His grandson translated the work into Greek in Egypt after 132 BC, adding a prologue that is a key early witness to the existence of a three-part Hebrew Bible canon ("the Law, the Prophets, and the other books").

Contents

The book ranges widely: reverence for God as the beginning of wisdom, duties toward parents, humility, self-control, speech, friendship, money and lending, table manners, and the management of a household. Chapters 44–50 form the famous "Praise of the Ancestors," a hymn celebrating the heroes of Israel from Enoch to the high priest Simon son of Onias.

Canon status

Sirach is accepted as canonical scripture (deuterocanonical) by the Catholic Church and Eastern Orthodox Church. Protestants generally class it among the Apocrypha, while it was not included in the Hebrew Bible, though it was quoted in rabbinic literature.

Manuscripts

Long known mainly from the Greek Septuagint, substantial Hebrew fragments were recovered from the Cairo Geniza (1896) and among the Dead Sea Scrolls at Qumran and Masada, confirming the Hebrew original behind the Greek version.
