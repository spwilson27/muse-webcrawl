Hydrogen chloride

Hydrogen chloride
Formula: HCl
Molar mass: 36.46 g/mol
Appearance: colourless gas
Melting point: −114.2 °C
Boiling point: −85.1 °C

The compound hydrogen chloride has the chemical formula HCl and is composed of diatomic molecules of one hydrogen and one chlorine atom bonded covalently. At room temperature it is a colourless, corrosive gas with a pungent odour; it fumes in moist air, forming droplets of aqueous solution. The solution of hydrogen chloride in water is hydrochloric acid, a major industrial chemical and the acid of gastric juice in the stomach.

Production

Most hydrogen chloride is produced as a by-product of the chlorination of organic compounds (for example in making PVC intermediates). It is also made directly by burning hydrogen in chlorine, and on a small scale in laboratories by reacting sodium chloride with concentrated sulfuric acid. Historically, the Leblanc soda process released vast quantities of HCl gas, prompting the British Alkali Act of 1863, one of the first pieces of modern environmental legislation.

Uses

Hydrogen chloride and hydrochloric acid are used in producing chlorides and PVC, steel pickling, ore processing, food processing, oil-well acidising, and water treatment. The gas is also used in semiconductor manufacture for etching and cleaning. Exposure is hazardous: the gas is strongly irritating to the eyes and respiratory tract.
