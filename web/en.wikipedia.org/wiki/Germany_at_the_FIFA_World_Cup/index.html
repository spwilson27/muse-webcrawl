Germany at the FIFA World Cup

This is a record of Germany's results at the FIFA World Cup. Germany is one of the most successful national teams in the history of the tournament, having won the title four times (1954, 1974, 1990, and 2014), finished as runners-up four times, and reached the semi-finals more often than any other nation. For most of the post-war period the team competed as West Germany; following reunification in 1990, the unified Germany inherited the record of the West German team. East Germany appeared once, in 1974.

Notable matches

The Miracle of Bern in 1954 saw West Germany come back from 2–0 down to beat the heavily favoured Hungarian "Golden Team" 3–2, a victory of great symbolic importance in post-war Germany. In 1974 Germany hosted and won the tournament, defeating Johan Cruyff's Netherlands in the Munich final. The 2014 campaign in Brazil included the extraordinary 7–1 semi-final defeat of the hosts — the "Mineirazo" — before Götze's extra-time goal settled the final against Argentina.

Recent tournaments

After the triumph of 2014, Germany's fortunes declined sharply: the defending champions were eliminated in the group stage in both 2018 (in Russia, after defeats by Mexico and South Korea) and 2022 (in Qatar), a run that prompted a rebuilding of the squad under new coaching leadership.

Records

Miroslav Klose is the World Cup's all-time top scorer with 16 goals, scored across the 2002, 2006, 2010, and 2014 tournaments. Lothar Matthäus held the record for most World Cup matches played (25) for two decades. Germany has also supplied several Golden Boot winners and is the most frequent finalist in tournament history alongside Brazil.
