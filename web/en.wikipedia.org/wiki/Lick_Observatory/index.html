The Lick Observatory is an astronomical observatory owned and operated by the University of California. It is on the summit of Mount Hamilton, in the Diablo Range just east of San Jose, California. The observatory is managed by the University of California Observatories, headquartered at UC Santa Cruz. It was the first permanently occupied mountain-top observatory, built between 1876 and 1887 from a bequest of about $700,000 by the eccentric San Francisco millionaire James Lick, who is buried beneath the base of its great refractor.

History and instruments

When completed in 1888, the 36-inch (91 cm) Great Lick Refractor was the largest refracting telescope in the world; it was surpassed three years later by Yerkes Observatory's 40-inch instrument. In 1892 Edward Emerson Barnard used it to discover Amalthea, the fifth moon of Jupiter — the last planetary moon discovered visually. The Crossley reflector, donated in 1895, demonstrated the power of large silvered-mirror telescopes and was a forerunner of modern reflectors.

The C. Donald Shane 3-metre (120-inch) reflecting telescope, completed in 1959, became the observatory's workhorse and was a major instrument in the study of quasars and galactic structure. In 2013 the 2.4 m Automated Planet Finder, built to search for exoplanets around nearby stars, entered service. The observatory survived a serious threat in August 2020 when the SCU Lightning Complex fires burned up to its buildings; firefighters saved the historic domes, though several support structures were damaged.

Discoveries

Lick astronomers have contributed to the radial-velocity surveys that found many of the first exoplanets (notably around 47 Ursae Majoris), measured the expansion of the universe, studied active galactic nuclei, and carried out long-term supernova searches (the Lick Observatory Supernova Search used the Katzman Automatic Imaging Telescope). The observatory now functions largely as a research and training facility for UC astronomers and students, with public visitor programs in the summer.
