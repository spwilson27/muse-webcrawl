Metonic cycle

The Metonic cycle is a period of 19 solar years after which the phases of the Moon recur at the same time of year. It is used in lunisolar calendars.

Calculation

19 tropical years = 6,939.60 days; 235 synodic months = 6,939.69 days, a difference of about 2 hours. The cycle is named for Meton of Athens (5th century BCE), though known earlier in Babylon.

Applications

Basis for the Hebrew calendar, the Greek calendar, and the computus for Easter: the Golden Number in ecclesiastical calendars derives from a year's position in the Metonic cycle.
