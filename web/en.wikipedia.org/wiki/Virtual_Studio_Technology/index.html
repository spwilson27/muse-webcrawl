Virtual Studio Technology (VST) is an audio plug-in software interface that integrates software synthesizers and effects units into digital audio workstations (DAWs). Developed by Steinberg and released with Cubase in 1996, VST became one of the most widely supported plug-in formats, allowing thousands of third-party instruments and effects to run inside host software.

History

Steinberg introduced VST in 1996, initially for real-time audio effects processing. In 1999, VST2 added support for software instruments (VSTi), enabling virtual synthesizers and samplers to receive MIDI and output audio. VST3, released in 2008, brought improvements such as sample-accurate automation, dynamic I/O allocation, and multiple MIDI ports. Licensing terms changed over the years; Steinberg eventually discontinued VST2 licensing for new developers, prompting some open-source alternatives and competing formats.

Technology

A VST plug-in is a dynamically loaded library that a host application calls to process audio and MIDI in real time. Plug-ins typically provide a custom graphical editor window. Instruments respond to MIDI note and control data, while effects process audio buffers. VST plug-ins are distinct from related standards such as Audio Units (Apple), AAX (Avid), and the community-developed CLAP and LV2 formats.
