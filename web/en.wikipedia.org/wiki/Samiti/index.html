Samiti

Type: Assembly (tribal council) of Vedic India

Period: Vedic period, c. 1500–500 BCE

Related body: Sabha (council of elders)

The Samiti (Sanskrit: समिति, "meeting together") was a popular assembly in early Vedic India, one of the earliest institutions of collective governance in South Asia. Together with the sabhā, a smaller council of elders, the samiti formed a key political body of the Vedic tribes.

Functions

The samiti appears in the Rigveda and later Vedic texts as a gathering of the tribe or clan (the viś), attended by adult male members. It debated matters of common concern, and its approval appears to have been important — perhaps essential — for the election and legitimation of the king (rājan). Hymns pray for harmony between king and samiti, suggesting the assembly could deliberate on policy, warfare and disputes, and may have acted as a check on royal power.

Historical development

In the later Vedic period, as tribal kingdoms grew into larger territorial states, the influence of the samiti declined in favour of court officials and councils of nobles, though assemblies survived in some non-monarchical polities (gaṇa-saṅgha). Historians cite the samiti and sabha as early precedents of deliberative institutions in India.

Modern usage

The word samiti persists in modern Indian languages meaning "committee" or "association", as in the names of organisations such as village committees and cooperative societies.
