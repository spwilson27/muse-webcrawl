Bundesstraße

A Bundesstraße (plural Bundesstraßen, "federal road") is a class of trunk road in Germany administered by the federal government, below the Autobahn motorways in the road hierarchy. Bundesstraßen are identified by the letter "B" followed by a number — for example the B 1, which runs from the Dutch border near Aachen across Germany to the Polish border — and are signed with black numbers on yellow signs.

Network

The network extends to roughly 38,000 km. Unlike autobahns, Bundesstraßen pass through towns and villages, have at-grade junctions and are generally subject to the normal speed limits (100 km/h outside built-up areas, 50 km/h within them), unless upgraded to dual carriageway express-road standard (Gelber Autobahn-like Kraftfahrstraßen in some regions). Well-known routes include the B 2, Germany's longest Bundesstraße at over 800 km, the coastal B 96, and the alpine B 20.

Austria and Switzerland

In Austria the term was used for federal roads generally; since the transfer of most to the Länder in 2002, "Bundesstraße" survives mainly in road names and the B numbering retained for the former federal routes. Switzerland uses its own classification of main roads (Hauptstrassen) rather than the German term.

History

The numbering dates from the Reichsstraßen system of 1932, when the Weimar-era Fernverkehrsstraßen were renumbered; the Federal Republic retained and extended the system, and after reunification it was applied to the former East Germany, where trunk roads had been designated Fernverkehrsstraßen (F).
