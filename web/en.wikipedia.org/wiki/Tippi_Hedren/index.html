Nathalie Kay "Tippi" Hedren (born January 19, 1930) is an American actress. Initially a fashion model, she appeared in the Alfred Hitchcock films The Birds (1963), for which she won the Golden Globe Award for New Star of the Year, and the psychological drama Marnie (1964). She became one of the most famous of the "Hitchcock blondes", and her account of the director's obsessive and abusive behaviour toward her has become a defining part of his biography.

Career

Hedren was working as a model when Hitchcock saw her in a television commercial in 1961 and signed her to a contract, casting her as Melanie Daniels in The Birds. The production was harrowing — in the climactic attic scene she was subjected to live birds being thrown at and attached to her for days. She starred again for Hitchcock in Marnie (1964) opposite Sean Connery. Their relationship collapsed; Hedren later described a pattern of control and sexual harassment, and Hitchcock, holding her contract, effectively stalled her career by refusing to release or cast her. She subsequently worked in a variety of films and television, including A Countess from Hong Kong (1967) for Charlie Chaplin.

Animal activism

After filming Roar (1981) — a notoriously dangerous production involving dozens of untrained big cats, in which Hedren and many crew members were injured — she founded the Roar Foundation and established the Shambala Preserve in Acton, California, a sanctuary for abandoned and abused big cats. She became a prominent campaigner against the private ownership of exotic animals, work recognised with numerous humanitarian awards.

Personal life

Hedren is the mother of actress Melanie Griffith and the grandmother of Dakota Johnson. Her memoir Tippi was published in 2016.
