Trishula

The trishula (Sanskrit: त्रिशूल, triśūla) is a trident, a divine symbol deeply rooted in Hinduism and also found in Buddhism. In India and Thailand, the term also refers to a short-handled weapon which may be mounted on a danda or staff. Unlike the Okinawan sai, the trishula is often bladed, and its three prongs make it a distinctive sacred emblem as well as a weapon.

Symbolism

The trishula is most famously the weapon of the god Shiva, who uses it to destroy evil and ignorance. Its three points carry rich symbolic meaning, commonly interpreted as the three gunas (sattva, rajas and tamas), the three functions of creation, preservation and destruction embodied in the Trimurti, or the three aspects of time — past, present and future. The central prong is sometimes said to represent Shiva himself, with the side prongs representing his powers of will, action and wisdom.

The goddess Durga also wields a trishula among her many weapons when she battles demons such as Mahishasura, and the weapon appears in the iconography of other deities including Agni and Kartikeya in some traditions.

In Buddhism

In Buddhist symbolism, the trishula — particularly when combined with a wheel — crowns the imperial standard of the Chakravartin, the universal monarch. It is associated with the Three Jewels of Buddhism: the Buddha, the Dharma and the Sangha, and appears on ancient coins and inscriptions.

Other appearances

The trishula appears in the national emblem and flag of Nepal's iconography, in the hands of sadhus and ascetics (often as the similar trishul carried by Shaivite holy men), and as an emblem in ayurvedic and yogic traditions where it represents the three main energy channels (nadis): ida, pingala and sushumna.
