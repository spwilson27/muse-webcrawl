The Oireachtas (/ˈɛrəktəs/ ERR-ək-təs), sometimes called the Oireachtas Éireann, is the legislature of Ireland. Under Article 15 of the Constitution of Ireland, the Oireachtas consists of the President of Ireland and two houses: Dáil Éireann (the lower house) and Seanad Éireann (the Senate, or upper house). The houses of the Oireachtas sit in Leinster House in Dublin, an eighteenth-century ducal palace.

The houses

Dáil Éireann is directly elected by proportional representation (single transferable vote) and is by far the more powerful house: it alone can initiate money bills, and the government (led by the Taoiseach) is drawn from and accountable to it. Its members are called TDs (Teachtaí Dála).

Seanad Éireann has 60 members: 11 nominated by the Taoiseach, 6 elected by university graduates, and 43 elected by an electorate of politicians from vocational panels. The Seanad can delay and recommend amendments to legislation but cannot block bills passed by the Dáil for long — 90 days for most bills, 21 days for money bills. A referendum in 2013 to abolish the Seanad was narrowly rejected by voters.

Legislative process

Most bills are introduced in the Dáil, pass through committee and report stages in both houses, and are then signed into law by the President, who may — after consulting the Council of State — refer a bill to the Supreme Court to test its constitutionality. The Oireachtas also approves the budget, declares war, and can remove the President or judges for stated misbehaviour.

History

The Oireachtas of the Irish Free State (1922–1937) was the direct predecessor; the current body rests on the 1937 constitution drafted under Éamon de Valera. The Irish word oireachtas originally denoted a gathering or assembly, and the same word is used for the annual festival of Irish-language culture, Oireachtas na Gaeilge.
