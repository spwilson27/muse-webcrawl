Mercedes-Benz Group

The Mercedes-Benz Group is headquartered in Stuttgart, Baden-Württemberg.

The Mercedes-Benz Group AG (formerly Daimler-Benz, DaimlerChrysler and Daimler AG) is a German multinational automotive company headquartered in Stuttgart. It is one of the world's leading manufacturers of premium and luxury passenger cars and vans, selling vehicles under the Mercedes-Benz, Mercedes-AMG, Mercedes-Maybach and Smart brands, and is among the largest carmakers by revenue.

History

The company traces its origins to the 1926 merger of Benz & Cie. and Daimler Motoren Gesellschaft, whose founders, Karl Benz and Gottlieb Daimler, independently pioneered the automobile in the 1880s. The Mercedes-Benz name honours the Mercedes marque of DMG, named for the daughter of businessman Emil Jellinek. As Daimler-Benz, the company expanded through the twentieth century, acquiring brands and building trucks, buses and aero engines.

In 1998 Daimler-Benz merged with the American Chrysler Corporation in a "merger of equals" to form DaimlerChrysler; the union proved troubled and Chrysler was sold to Cerberus Capital Management in 2007, after which the company became Daimler AG. In December 2021 Daimler spun off its commercial-vehicle business as the independent Daimler Truck, and on 1 February 2022 Daimler AG renamed itself the Mercedes-Benz Group AG, focusing on passenger cars and vans.

Business

The group comprises Mercedes-Benz Cars (including AMG and Maybach), Mercedes-Benz Vans, and Mercedes-Benz Mobility (financial and mobility services). It has pursued an electrification strategy with its EQ-branded battery-electric models and the MMA and MB.EA platforms, and it fields the works Formula One team, Mercedes-AMG Petronas, through a subsidiary. Major shareholders have included the Chinese investors Geely (via Li Shufu) and BAIC.
