Rupa Goswami (1489–1564) was a devotional teacher, poet, and philosopher of the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition in India. He was one of the Six Goswamis of Vrindavan, the intimate disciples of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu who systematized the theology and devotional practice of the movement.

Life

Born into a scholarly family in Bengal, Rupa and his elder brother Sanatana Goswami served under the Sultan of Bengal before renouncing worldly life to join Chaitanya. Settling in Vrindavan, Rupa is credited with discovering the lost sacred sites associated with Krishna's pastimes and with overseeing the construction of temples there, including the Govindaji temple.

Works

Rupa Goswami's most influential work, the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu ("The Ocean of the Nectar of Devotion"), defines devotional service (bhakti) and its stages, and became a foundational text of Gaudiya theology. His Ujjvala-nilamani elaborates the doctrine of devotional mellows (rasa), and he also composed dramas such as Vidagdha-madhava and Lalita-madhava, as well as the short instructional poem Upadesamrita.
