Hud (prophet)


Ottoman miniature depicting the prophet Hud preaching to the tribe of ʿĀd.

Hud (Arabic: هُود, romanized: Hūd) was a prophet of ancient Arabia mentioned in the Qur'an. The eleventh chapter of the Qur'an, Surah Hud, is named after him, though most of the chapter concerns other prophets. Hud is believed to have been a descendant of Noah (Nuh) and was sent to preach to the tribe of ʿĀd, an ancient tribe of Arabia frequently mentioned in the Qur'an.

Narrative

According to the Qur'an, Hud preached to the rich and powerful people of ʿĀd, who lived in Iram, a legendary city of lofty pillars. He called them to worship God alone and to abandon their idolatry and mistreatment of weaker peoples. The people of ʿĀd rejected Hud's message, and for their disbelief they were destroyed by a violent wind that raged for seven nights and eight days; only Hud and his followers were saved.

Tomb

Several sites are claimed as the tomb of Hud, most famously a shrine in a barren valley in the Hadhramaut region of Yemen, which is a site of annual pilgrimage. Another claimed tomb is located in Damascus, Syria.
