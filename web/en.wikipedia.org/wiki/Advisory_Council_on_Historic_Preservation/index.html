Advisory Council on Historic Preservation

Formed1966
TypeIndependent U.S. federal agency
HeadquartersWashington, D.C.
BasisNational Historic Preservation Act

The Advisory Council on Historic Preservation (ACHP) is an independent agency of the United States government that promotes the preservation, enhancement, and sustainable use of the nation's historic resources, and advises the President and Congress on national historic preservation policy.

History and role

The ACHP was created by the National Historic Preservation Act of 1966. Its best-known function is administering the Section 106 review process: federal agencies must consider the effects of their undertakings — construction projects, licenses, permits, and funding — on properties listed in or eligible for the National Register of Historic Places, and must consult with the ACHP, state and tribal historic preservation officers, and the public to seek ways to avoid, minimize, or mitigate harm.

Composition

The council's membership includes heads of federal agencies, representatives of state and local governments, preservation experts, tribal representatives, and members of the public appointed by the President. Day-to-day work is carried out by a professional staff based in Washington, D.C. The ACHP also issues policy guidance, trains agency staff, and coordinates the Preserve America initiative encouraging heritage tourism and stewardship.
