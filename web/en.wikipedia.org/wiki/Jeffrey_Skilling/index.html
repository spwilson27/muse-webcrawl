Jeffrey Skilling

Born: November 25, 1953, Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Occupation: Business executive
Known for: CEO of Enron; role in the Enron scandal
Criminal status: Released from prison in 2019

Jeffrey Keith Skilling (born November 25, 1953) is an American businessman and convicted felon who was the chief executive officer of the Enron Corporation during its collapse in 2001, one of the largest corporate bankruptcies in United States history.

Early life and education

Skilling was born in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, and grew up in New Jersey and Aurora, Illinois. He earned a bachelor's degree in applied science from Southern Methodist University in 1975 and an MBA from Harvard Business School in 1979, graduating as a Baker Scholar. He worked as a consultant at McKinsey & Company, where Enron chairman Kenneth Lay was among his clients.

Enron

Skilling joined Enron in 1990 and championed its transformation from a natural-gas pipeline company into an energy trading giant. He promoted mark-to-market accounting for long-term contracts and an aggressive, performance-driven corporate culture. He became president and chief operating officer in 1997 and CEO in February 2001, resigning abruptly in August 2001, citing personal reasons. Enron declared bankruptcy that December after revelations of accounting fraud and hidden debt.

Trial and imprisonment

In 2006 Skilling was tried in Houston alongside Kenneth Lay. The jury convicted Skilling on 19 counts, including conspiracy, securities fraud, and insider trading, while acquitting him on nine insider-trading counts. He was sentenced to 24 years and 4 months in prison. Following appeals, including a Supreme Court ruling in Skilling v. United States (2010) narrowing the honest-services fraud statute, his sentence was reduced in 2013 to 14 years under an agreement with prosecutors. He was released from federal prison in February 2019. Skilling maintained throughout that he had not committed fraud.
