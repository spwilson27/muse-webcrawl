Steely Dan is an American rock band founded in 1971 in Los Angeles by Walter Becker (guitars, bass) and Donald Fagen (keyboards, lead vocals) — the group's core and only consistent members. The band took its name from "Steely Dan III from Yokohama," an appliance named in William S. Burroughs's novel Naked Lunch. Becker and Fagen met at Bard College in 1967 and worked as staff songwriters at ABC Records before forming the band with guitarists Denny Dias and Jeff "Skunk" Baxter, drummer Jim Hodder, and singer David Palmer.

Career

The debut album Can't Buy a Thrill (1972) produced the hits "Do It Again" and "Reelin' in the Years." Disliking touring, Becker and Fagen dissolved the touring band after Pretzel Logic (1974) — which included "Rikki Don't Lose That Number" — and became a studio-only project, hiring the finest session musicians in Los Angeles and New York for each track. The albums Katy Lied (1975), The Royal Scam (1976), and the platinum-selling Aja (1977), with its singles "Peg" and "Deacon Blues," are regarded as landmarks of studio craft, fusing jazz harmony, R&B rhythms, and sardonic, literate lyrics. Gaucho (1980) was followed by a twelve-year hiatus as the duo pursued solo work, including Fagen's The Nightfly (1982).

Steely Dan resumed touring in 1993 and returned to the studio with Two Against Nature (2000), which unexpectedly won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year, and Everything Must Go (2003). The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2001. Becker died on September 3, 2017; Fagen pledged to keep the music alive and has continued touring under the Steely Dan name with a large backing band.
