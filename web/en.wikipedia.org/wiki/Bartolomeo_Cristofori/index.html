Bartolomeo Cristofori


Cristofori piano of 1720, Metropolitan Museum of Art

Bartolomeo Cristofori (May 4, 1655 – January 27, 1731) was an Italian maker of musical instruments, generally regarded as the inventor of the piano. Born in Padua, he was engaged in 1688 by Ferdinando de' Medici, Grand Prince of Tuscany, to maintain the harpsichords and other instruments of the Medici court in Florence.

Invention of the piano

A Medici inventory of 1700 already lists an arpicimbalo "that can play soft and loud", built by Cristofori. His design solved the harpsichord's main limitation — its inability to vary dynamics by touch — by replacing the plucking mechanism with hammers that strike the strings and fall back immediately, escaping so the string can vibrate while still allowing rapid repetition and separate control of volume. In 1711 the Venetian journalist Scipione Maffei described the new instrument as a gravicembalo col piano e forte, whence "pianoforte" and ultimately "piano".

Surviving instruments

Three Cristofori pianos survive: one dated 1720 in the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, one of 1722 in Rome, and one of 1726 in Leipzig. Cristofori also made harpsichords, spinets and a novel oval spinet, and developed the basic layout — wrestplank, soundboard and case structure — that grand pianos retained throughout the 18th century. His assistant Giovanni Ferrini carried the craft on in Florence.
