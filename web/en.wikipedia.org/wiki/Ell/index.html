Ell

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An ell (from Proto-Germanic *alinō, cognate with the Latin ulna, the forearm bone) is a historical unit of length, originally the length of a man's arm or forearm (a cubit-like measure) and later standardised for use chiefly in measuring cloth.

Values

The ell varied considerably by place and period. In England the legal ell was standardised at 45 inches (114.3 cm), equal to a yard and a quarter, and was used for woollen cloth. The Scottish ell was about 37 inches (94 cm), and the Flemish ell about 27 inches (69 cm); other European regions had their own variants, such as the French aune.

Use

Ells were measured with graduated rods called ell wands kept by cloth merchants and town authorities. The unit survived in the English cloth trade into the nineteenth century before metrication and standardised imperial measure displaced it, though it lingers in idioms such as "give him an inch and he'll take an ell".
