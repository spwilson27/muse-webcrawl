Teatro La Fenice (pronounced [la feˈniːtʃe], "The Phoenix") is a historic opera house in Venice, Italy. It is one of the most famous and renowned landmarks in the history of Italian theatre, and in the history of opera as a whole. The name reflects the theatre's survival of fires and rebuildings: it opened in 1792, burned down and was rebuilt in 1837, and after being nearly destroyed by arson in 1996, it reopened in 2003 following a meticulous reconstruction.

History

The theatre was commissioned by the Noli Me Tangere society of box-holders after they lost their earlier theatre, and was designed by Gian Antonio Selva. It inaugurated on 16 May 1792 with Giovanni Paisiello's opera I giuochi d'Agrigento. In the 19th century La Fenice premiered major works by Rossini, Bellini, Donizetti, and Verdi, including Verdi's Rigoletto (1851) and La traviata (1853), as well as Ernani and Attila.

Fire and rebirth

On 29 January 1996, the theatre was destroyed by a fire set deliberately by two electricians facing contract penalties. After a lengthy legal process and reconstruction under the motto com'era, dov'era ("as it was, where it was"), La Fenice reopened on 14 December 2003 with an inaugural concert conducted by Riccardo Muti. It remains a principal venue of Venice's musical life and hosts the traditional New Year's Concert broadcast across Italy.
