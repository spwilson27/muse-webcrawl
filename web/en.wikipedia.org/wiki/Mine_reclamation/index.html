Mine reclamation

Mine reclamation is the process of restoring land that has been mined to an economically usable or natural state. It involves regrading spoil, replacing topsoil, controlling erosion and acid mine drainage, and replanting vegetation.

Regulation

In the United States the Surface Mining Control and Reclamation Act of 1977 requires operators to restore surface-mined land and funds the clean-up of abandoned mines through a fee on coal production. Modern practice often plans reclamation concurrently with mining, reshaping land progressively rather than at closure.

Outcomes

Reclaimed land may become pasture, woodland, wetlands, recreation areas or solar farms. Challenges include long-term water quality, especially where sulfide-bearing rock generates acid drainage.
