Chrétien de Troyes

Chrétien de Troyes (fl. c. 1160–1191) was a French poet of the late twelfth century, regarded as one of the founders of Arthurian romance and of the courtly romance genre in Western literature.

Works

His five surviving verse romances — Érec et Énide, Cligès, Yvain, the Knight of the Lion, Lancelot, the Knight of the Cart, and the unfinished Perceval, the Story of the Grail — introduced enduring elements of the Arthurian legend, including Lancelot's love for Guinevere and the quest for the Holy Grail.

Patronage

Chrétien wrote at the court of Marie de Champagne and later for Philip of Flanders, who commissioned Perceval. His work, blending Celtic material with the ideals of courtly love (fin'amor), shaped medieval narrative across Europe.
