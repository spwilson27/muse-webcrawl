Île-de-France is the most populous of the eighteen regions of France, located in the north-central part of the country. It consists of the city of Paris and its surrounding area, often referred to as the région parisienne. Created as the "District of the Paris Region" in 1961 and renamed Île-de-France in 1976, it is France's political, economic, and cultural centre.

Etymology

The name "Île-de-France" ("Island of France") dates to the Merovingian and Capetian era, when the domain of the kings of France was effectively an island bounded by the Seine, Marne, Oise, and Beuvronne rivers.

Geography

The region is a low-lying sedimentary basin centred on Paris. The Seine and its tributaries, the Marne and the Oise, cross it. Despite dense urbanisation in the centre, much of the outer region remains farmland and forest, including the forests of Fontainebleau and Rambouillet.

Demographics

With roughly 12 million inhabitants — about 18–19% of France's population on just 2% of its territory — Île-de-France is by far the most populous French region. It is also one of the most ethnically diverse regions in Europe.

Economy

The region generates roughly 30% of France's GDP and has one of the highest regional GDPs per capita in the world. It hosts the headquarters of most large French companies, the La Défense business district, and major financial, media, technology, and luxury-goods sectors.

Transport

Île-de-France has the densest transport network in France: the Paris Métro, RER regional express lines, Transilien suburban trains, tramways, and the airports of Charles de Gaulle and Orly. The Grand Paris Express project is adding some 200 km of automated metro lines around the capital.
