Port of Durban

The Port of Durban, commonly called Durban Harbour, is the largest and busiest shipping terminal in sub-Saharan Africa, situated on the east coast of South Africa in the city of Durban, KwaZulu-Natal. It handles up to 31.4 million tonnes of cargo each year and is the country's premier container port, accounting for roughly 60% of South Africa's container traffic.

Geography

The port occupies a sheltered natural bay of about 17 square kilometres, entered from the Indian Ocean through an artificial channel roughly 120 metres wide. The harbour serves the industrial heartland of Gauteng via the NATCOR rail corridor and the N3 highway, and also handles cargo for landlocked countries including Zimbabwe, Zambia, Botswana and Malawi.

History

European mariners knew the bay early: Vasco da Gama sighted the coast on Christmas Day 1497 and named the region Terra do Natal ("Christmas Land"). Commercial shipping developed after British settlement in the 1820s, but the treacherous sandbar at the entrance long limited vessel size. Harbour works, dredging and the construction of the Point and Bluff breakwaters gradually deepened the entrance. In the 2000s the entrance channel was widened and deepened in a major project completed around 2010, allowing larger post-Panamax vessels to call.

Terminals and facilities

  • Durban Container Terminal — the largest container facility in the southern hemisphere, split between Pier 1 and Pier 2.
  • Maydon Wharf — handles break-bulk, dry bulk and agricultural products.
  • Island View — liquid bulk and chemical storage precinct.
  • Point precinct — multi-purpose terminals, car terminal and cruise facilities.
  • Bayhead — ship repair yards and dry docks.

Significance

Durban is a principal gateway for South African imports of oil, vehicles and manufactured goods and exports of coal, ferrochrome, fruit, sugar and other agricultural produce. Congestion and equipment constraints at the port have periodically been cited as constraints on national economic growth, prompting ongoing expansion and concession plans.
