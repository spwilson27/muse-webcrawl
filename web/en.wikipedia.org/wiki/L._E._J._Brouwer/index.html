Luitzen Egbertus Jan Brouwer (1881–1966) was a Dutch mathematician and philosopher who founded intuitionism, a philosophy of mathematics holding that mathematical objects are mental constructions and that the law of excluded middle cannot be applied uncritically to infinite sets. He was also a founder of modern topology.

Topology

In early work (c. 1909–1913) Brouwer proved landmark results including the Brouwer fixed-point theorem, the invariance of domain, and the invariance of dimension, and introduced the concept of degree of a mapping.

Intuitionism

From his 1907 dissertation onward, Brouwer developed intuitionism, rejecting non-constructive existence proofs. His stance led to the Grundlagenstreit (foundations crisis) with David Hilbert, who famously vowed not to be expelled from "Cantor's paradise". Brouwer's ideas led, through his student Arend Heyting's formalisation, to intuitionistic logic and later to constructive mathematics and proof assistants.
