The Association to Advance Collegiate Schools of Business, known as AACSB International, is an American professional and accreditation organisation for business schools. Founded in 1916 by a group of American universities including Harvard, Columbia and the University of Pennsylvania, it is the oldest and one of the most prestigious accreditors of business education.

Accreditation

AACSB accredits business schools and, separately, accounting programmes. Its standards cover strategic management, learner success, thought leadership and societal impact; the 2020 standards reorganised requirements around competency goals, curriculum quality and engagement. Accreditation is voluntary and peer-reviewed, with continuous improvement reviews typically every five years. Schools holding AACSB, EQUIS and AMBA accreditation are described as "triple crown" accredited.

Membership

Membership in AACSB does not imply accreditation: the organisation has well over a thousand member institutions in more than 100 countries, while accredited schools number in the hundreds — a small minority of the world's business schools. AACSB also runs seminars, conferences and data services for deans and faculty.

History

The association was founded as the Association of Collegiate Schools of Business in 1916, began accrediting in 1919, and adopted its present name in 2001 to reflect international expansion beyond the United States and Canada.
