Changbai Mountains

The Changbai Mountains (Chinese: 长白山; Korean: 장백산맥) are a mountain range on the border between China and North Korea, forming the watershed between the Yalu, Tumen and Songhua river systems. The range's highest and most famous peak is Paektu Mountain (Changbai Shan proper), a sacred volcano crowned by the crater lake Tianchi ("Heaven Lake").

Geography

The range stretches some 800 kilometres through China's Jilin and Liaoning provinces and North Korea's Ryanggang and Chagang provinces. It is a region of mixed coniferous and broadleaf forest — one of the best-preserved temperate forest ecosystems in East Asia and a UNESCO biosphere reserve (Changbaishan, designated 1979). The forests are habitat for Siberian tigers, Amur leopards, Asiatic black bears and sable.

Paektu Mountain

The volcanic cone of Paektu erupted massively in 946 CE in one of the largest eruptions of the last two millennia, the "Millennium Eruption," which created the present caldera. The mountain is revered in Korean culture as the mythical birthplace of Dangun, legendary founder of the first Korean kingdom, and it occupies a central place in the national symbolism of both Korean states as well as among the Manchu, whose ruling Qing dynasty regarded the range as its ancestral homeland.

Tourism and conservation

The Chinese side, administered as the Changbaishan National Nature Reserve, draws large numbers of visitors to view Heaven Lake, hot springs and waterfalls. Cross-border access and territorial interpretation of the mountain remain sensitive topics in China–Korea relations.
