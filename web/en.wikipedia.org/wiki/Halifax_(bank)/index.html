Halifax is a British banking brand operating as a trading division of Bank of Scotland, itself a wholly owned subsidiary of Lloyds Banking Group. It is named after the town of Halifax, West Yorkshire, where it was founded as a building society in 1853. It developed into the UK's largest building society and, following demutualisation in 1997, became a bank.

In 2001 Halifax merged with the Bank of Scotland to form HBOS. In 2009, following the financial crisis, HBOS was acquired by Lloyds TSB and Halifax became part of Lloyds Banking Group. Halifax remains the UK's largest provider of residential mortgages and savings accounts, and is known for long-running advertising campaigns built around staff members, launched with the slogan and song parody "Who Gives You Extra?".

History

The Halifax Permanent Benefit Building Society was founded in 1853 in the Old Cock Inn, Halifax, funded by local businessmen to help residents buy their own homes. The society grew steadily through mergers — absorbing societies including the Halifax Equitable and, in 1995, the Leeds Permanent. When it demutualised and floated on the London Stock Exchange in 1997, over 7.5 million customers became shareholders, the largest extension of share ownership in British history to that date.

Products and services

Halifax offers current accounts, savings accounts and ISAs, credit cards, personal loans, insurance, and residential mortgages, distributed through a large branch network, telephone banking, and online and mobile banking. Its Reward Current Account and children's savings products have been prominent in its retail offer.
