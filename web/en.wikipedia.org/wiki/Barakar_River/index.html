Barakar River

SourceChota Nagpur Plateau, Jharkhand
MouthDamodar River
LengthAbout 225 km
StatesJharkhand, West Bengal
Major damMaithon Dam

The Barakar River is the principal tributary of the Damodar River in eastern India. It rises in the Chota Nagpur Plateau in the Hazaribagh district of Jharkhand, flows generally eastward for about 225 kilometres through Jharkhand and West Bengal, and joins the Damodar near Dishergarh.

Course

From its source near Padma in Hazaribagh district, the river passes through the coal-bearing country of the north Damodar valley, flowing past Barhi, Barakar, and the industrial areas around Dhanbad and Asansol. Its main tributaries include the Barsoti and Usri. The river forms part of the boundary between Jharkhand and West Bengal for a stretch of its lower course.

Projects

The Maithon Dam, built across the Barakar about 48 km from Dhanbad by the Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC) and completed in 1957, is one of the first dams of independent India, designed for flood control, irrigation, and hydroelectric generation; the reservoir behind it, Maithon Lake, is a popular recreation area. Another dam at Tilaiya, upstream, was completed in 1953 as DVC's first project.

Geology and surroundings

The Barakar gives its name to the Barakar Formation, an Early Permian coal-bearing sequence of the Gondwana Supergroup, and to the Barakar stage of Indian Gondwana stratigraphy—reflecting the river's exposure of rich seams that underpin the Jharia coalfield. The ancient town of Barakar, on its banks, preserves notable early Bengal temple architecture.
