The Copa Sudamericana (Portuguese: Copa Sul-Americana), known as the Copa Conmebol Sudamericana for sponsorship reasons, is an annual international club football competition organised by CONMEBOL since 2002. It is the second-most prestigious South American club competition after the Copa Libertadores, and is broadly comparable to the UEFA Europa League.

History

The competition replaced the Copa Merconorte and Copa Mercosur, which had split South American clubs along regional lines, and itself followed earlier secondary tournaments such as the Supercopa Masters and Copa CONMEBOL. San Lorenzo of Argentina won the inaugural edition in 2002. From 2005 to 2008, clubs from Mexico's CONCACAF were invited to participate, with Pachuca winning in 2006 — the only non-South American champion. From 2017 the tournament moved to a year-long calendar running parallel to the Copa Libertadores.

Format

Clubs qualify through their domestic league standings or cup results, with additional places going to teams eliminated from the Copa Libertadores. The modern format begins with a preliminary round and a group stage, followed by knockout play-offs, round of 16, quarter-finals and semi-finals. Since 2019 the final has been played as a single match at a neutral venue. The champion earns a place in the following season's Copa Libertadores group stage and contests the Recopa Sudamericana against the Libertadores champion.

Champions

No club has won the tournament more than twice. The two-time champions include Boca Juniors, Independiente, Athletico Paranaense, Independiente del Valle and LDU Quito. Argentine and Brazilian clubs have won the great majority of editions; Ecuador's Independiente del Valle and LDU Quito and Colombia's Santa Fe are among the few winners from other countries.
