Moog Music Inc. is an American manufacturer of electronic musical instruments based in Asheville, North Carolina. Founded by Robert Moog (1934–2005), the company pioneered the commercial synthesizer and its instruments have shaped popular and electronic music since the 1960s.

History

Robert Moog began building theremins in the 1950s and, with composer Herbert Deutsch, developed the first Moog modular synthesizer in 1964, introducing voltage-controlled oscillators, filters and envelope generators configured with patch cords. Wendy Carlos's album Switched-On Bach (1968), recorded on a Moog modular, demonstrated the instrument to a mass audience and won three Grammy Awards.

In 1970 the company launched the Minimoog, a portable, pre-wired monophonic synthesizer with a keyboard, which became the definitive analogue synth and a staple of progressive rock, funk and electronic music. Financial difficulties and changes of ownership followed; the company (renamed Moog Music after a period as part of Norlin) ceased production in the 1980s. Robert Moog regained the rights to his name in 2002 and revived the company in Asheville, reissuing instruments such as the Minimoog Voyager and, in 2016, the Minimoog Model D. The company was acquired by inMusic Brands in 2023.

Products

Notable Moog instruments include the Moog modular systems, Minimoog, Polymoog, Memorymoog, Taurus bass pedals, the Sub Phatty and Subsequent series, the semi-modular Mother-32, DFAM and Grandmother, and the Moog One polyphonic synthesizer (2018). The company also produces theremins, including the Etherwave and Claravox Centennial.
