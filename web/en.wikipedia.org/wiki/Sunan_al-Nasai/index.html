Sunan al-Nasa'i

Sunan al-Nasa'i (Arabic: سنن النسائي), also known as al-Sunan al-Sughra ("The Lesser Sunan") or al-Mujtaba ("The Selected"), is one of the six major hadith collections (Kutub al-Sittah) of Sunni Islam. It was compiled by the scholar Ahmad ibn Shu'ayb al-Nasa'i (829–915 CE) and contains roughly 5,700 hadith arranged in 52 books dealing predominantly with ritual practice and law.

Background

Al-Nasa'i, born in Nasa in Khurasan, first compiled a much larger collection, al-Sunan al-Kubra ("The Greater Sunan"). According to a well-known account, when he presented it to the governor of Ramla, he was asked whether every hadith in it was authentic; replying that it was not, he was asked to extract only the sound ones, producing the abridged al-Mujtaba that became famous as his Sunan. Al-Nasa'i was renowned for applying stricter conditions of acceptance than many of his contemporaries, and his collection is considered among the most reliable of the four Sunan works.

Contents and reception

The collection is organised by legal chapters beginning with ritual purification and prayer. Hadith commentators have praised its arrangement and the author's critical annotations on narrators, though, like all collections outside the Sahihayn, it includes some narrations graded weak by later scholars.
