Chuquisaca Department

Chuquisaca (Spanish pronunciation: [tʃukisaka]) is a department of Bolivia located in the south-central part of the country. Its capital is Sucre, which is also the constitutional capital of Bolivia and the seat of the national judiciary. With an area of about 51,524 km2 and a population of around 580,000 (2012 census), Chuquisaca is one of Bolivia's smaller and less populous departments.

Geography

The department is divided into 10 provinces and 29 municipalities. Its terrain descends from the high Andean ridges of the Cordillera Oriental in the west, through inter-Andean valleys around Sucre (at about 2,800 m), to the lowland Chaco plains along the Pilcomayo River in the southeast. It borders the departments of Cochabamba and Santa Cruz to the north, Tarija to the south, Potosí to the west and Paraguay to the east. The climate varies from cool highlands to subtropical heat in the Chaco.

History

The region corresponds to the territory of the pre-Inca Charcas peoples and was later part of the Inca Empire. The city of Sucre — founded in 1538 as La Plata — became the seat of the Real Audiencia of Charcas, the high court governing much of Spanish South America, and of the University of Saint Francis Xavier (1624), one of the oldest universities in the Americas. On 25 May 1809 the Chuquisaca Revolution in Sucre issued one of the first cries for independence in Spanish America. Bolivian independence was declared in Sucre on 6 August 1825, and the city became the capital of the new republic; it remains the constitutional capital even though the seat of government moved to La Paz at the end of the 19th century.

Economy and culture

Agriculture (maize, wheat, potatoes, fruit and wine grapes in the Cinti valley) and livestock are important, along with natural gas production in the Chaco fields and cement manufacture near Sucre. Quechua is widely spoken alongside Spanish, and Guaraní in the Chaco lowlands. Sucre's whitewashed colonial centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site; the department is also known for Tarabuco's indigenous market and textiles, the dinosaur tracks at Cal Orck'o near Sucre — one of the largest collections of theropod footprints in the world — and the Pujllay festival.
