The London Borough of Haringey (pronounced /ˈhærɪŋɡeɪ/) is a London borough in north London, classified by some definitions as part of Inner London. It was created in 1965 from the former municipal boroughs of Hornsey, Tottenham and Wood Green.

Alexandra Palace, the "People's Palace", sits on high ground in the west of the borough.

Geography and character

Haringey contrasts sharply between its leafy, affluent west — Highgate, Muswell Hill, Crouch End and Alexandra Park — and its denser east around Tottenham, Wood Green and Seven Sisters. Tottenham Hotspur Football Club's stadium, Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, opened in Tottenham in 2019. Alexandra Palace was the birthplace of regular BBC television broadcasting in 1936, and the surrounding high ground gives views across central London.

Population

The borough is ethnically diverse, with large Turkish and Kurdish, Polish, Caribbean, African and other communities, and more than a hundred languages spoken in its schools. Tottenham was the starting point of the August 2011 riots that spread across England.

Transport

Haringey is served by the Piccadilly and Victoria lines of the London Underground, the Northern line at Highgate, and London Overground and National Rail stations including Alexandra Palace, Harringay, Hornsey, Tottenham Hale and Seven Sisters.
