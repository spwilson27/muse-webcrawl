Kandariya Mahadeva Temple

LocationKhajuraho, Madhya Pradesh, India
DeityShiva (Mahadeva)
Builtc. 1025–1050 CE
PatronChandela king Vidyadhara (attributed)
StyleNagara architecture

The Kandariya Mahadeva Temple is the largest and most ornate of the surviving temples at Khajuraho, Madhya Pradesh, India. Dedicated to the Hindu god Shiva, it was built during the reign of the Chandela dynasty, around 1025–1050 CE, and is considered the finest example of the central Indian Nagara style. The temple is part of the Khajuraho Group of Monuments, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Architecture

The temple stands on a high platform (jagati) and rises in a sequence of halls — entrance porch, mandapa, great hall and vestibule — to the sanctum (garbhagriha), crowned by a soaring shikhara about 31 metres (102 ft) high, ringed by smaller turrets that evoke Mount Kailasa, Shiva's abode. The exterior walls carry some 872 statues (646 outside and 226 inside in the traditional count), including the celebrated erotic sculptures (mithuna) for which Khajuraho is famous, though these form only a small fraction of the carving.

History

Khajuraho's temples fell into obscurity after the decline of the Chandelas and were reintroduced to the outside world in the nineteenth century. The Kandariya temple, protected within the western group, remains in worship and is the centrepiece of the annual Khajuraho dance festival.
