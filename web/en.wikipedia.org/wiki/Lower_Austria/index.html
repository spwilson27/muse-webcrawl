Lower Austria (German: Niederösterreich, abbreviated NÖ) is the northeasternmost of the nine states of Austria. It is the country's largest state by area and second most populous. The state surrounds the national capital, Vienna, which is a separate state enclaved within it. The name "Lower" refers to its position downstream along the Danube, east of Upper Austria.

Geography

Lower Austria is traditionally divided into four quarters (Viertel): the Weinviertel (wine quarter) in the northeast, the Waldviertel (forest quarter) in the northwest, the Mostviertel (cider quarter) in the southwest, and the Industrieviertel (industrial quarter) in the southeast. The Danube flows through the state, including the celebrated Wachau valley between Melk and Krems, a UNESCO World Heritage landscape of terraced vineyards and abbeys. The Alps enter the south of the state, rising to the Schneeberg (2,076 m), the highest point in Lower Austria and a landmark visible from Vienna.

History

The region forms the historical core of Austria: the Ostmark margraviate established under the Babenbergs in 976, with residences at Melk and later Klosterneuburg and Vienna, grew into the Archduchy of Austria. For centuries "Austria below the Enns" (Österreich unter der Enns) was governed from Vienna. After 1945 the state lay in the Soviet occupation zone until the Austrian State Treaty of 1955. In 1986 the state capital was moved from Vienna to Sankt Pölten, which had become a chartered city only in that decade; the new government quarter opened in the 1990s.

Economy and culture

Agriculture, wine production (notably Grüner Veltliner from the Wachau and Weinviertel), industry and commuting into Vienna dominate the economy. Major attractions include Melk Abbey, Göttweig Abbey, the castle of Schönbühel, Carnuntum archaeological park, and the Semmering railway, another UNESCO site.
