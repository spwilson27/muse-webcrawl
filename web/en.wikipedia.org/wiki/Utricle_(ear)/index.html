Utricle (ear)

The utricle is one of the two otolith organs in the vertebrate inner ear, the other being the saccule. Located within the vestibule of the bony labyrinth, the utricle detects linear accelerations and head tilts in the horizontal plane, contributing to the sense of balance and spatial orientation.

Structure

The utricle is a small membranous sac filled with endolymph. On its inner surface lies the macula of utricle, a patch of sensory epithelium containing hair cells whose stereocilia are embedded in a gelatinous layer covered with otoconia — crystals of calcium carbonate that give the otolith organs their name. The utricle connects with the saccule and the semicircular ducts via the utriculosaccular duct, from which the endolymphatic duct arises.

Function

When the head moves linearly or tilts, gravity and inertia shift the otoconial membrane relative to the hair cells, bending their bundles and changing the firing rate of vestibular nerve fibers. Because the macula of the utricle lies roughly horizontally when the head is upright, the utricle is especially sensitive to horizontal linear acceleration and side-to-side tilt. Its signals drive reflexes such as the vestibulo-ocular reflex and inform the brain's perception of orientation.
