Intermediate cartridge

An intermediate cartridge is a rifle/carbine cartridge intermediate in power between a full-power rifle cartridge and a pistol cartridge. It typically features a reduced powder charge and lighter bullet than traditional military rifle rounds, giving controllable automatic fire and effective range out to roughly 300–600 metres.

History

The concept emerged during the Second World War. Combat experience showed most infantry engagements occurred at ranges far shorter than the 1,000-plus metres full-power cartridges were designed for. Germany pioneered the class with the 7.92×33mm Kurz, chambered in the StG 44, generally considered the first assault rifle. The Soviet Union followed with the 7.62×39mm, used in the SKS and AK-47.

Postwar adoption

NATO initially standardized the full-power 7.62×51mm, but the United States adopted the smaller 5.56×45mm for the M16 during the Vietnam War, and NATO followed. The Soviet Union countered with the 5.45×39mm for the AK-74. Intermediate cartridges remain the standard for modern assault rifles, balancing range, controllability, weight of ammunition carried, and terminal effect.

Characteristics

Typical intermediate cartridges produce muzzle energies between about 1,300 and 2,000 joules. Their reduced recoil makes automatic fire practical from a shoulder-fired individual weapon, and soldiers can carry more rounds for the same weight.
