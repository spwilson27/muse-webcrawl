Amstrad

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Amstrad was a British consumer electronics company, founded in 1968 by Alan Sugar. Its name is a contraction of Alan Michael Sugar Trading. The company became famous in the 1980s for affordable home computers, hi-fi systems, and word processors, and later for set-top boxes.

Founded: 1968
Founder: Alan Sugar
Headquarters: Brentwood, Essex, England
Fate: Acquired by Sky (BSkyB) in 2007

History

Amstrad began trading in electrical goods and made its name selling inexpensive hi-fi separates. Its breakthrough came in 1984 with the CPC 464 home computer, which, sold complete with monitor and built-in tape deck, captured a large share of the European market. The PCW series of dedicated word processors, launched in 1985, sold in the millions, and in 1986 Amstrad bought Sinclair Research's computer business, continuing the ZX Spectrum line. Its IBM-compatible PCs were among the first aimed at mass-market prices.

Later years

During the 1990s and 2000s Amstrad focused on telecommunications and set-top boxes for satellite television. In 2007, British Sky Broadcasting acquired the company for about £125 million, and Alan Sugar moved on to other ventures, remaining a prominent media figure.
