Toruń

Toruń (German: Thorn) is a city in north-central Poland, on the Vistula River, in the Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship. It is one of the oldest cities in Poland and is famed as the birthplace of the astronomer Nicolaus Copernicus (1473). Its medieval Old Town, largely preserved, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Toruń was founded by the Teutonic Knights, who built a castle there in the 1230s, and the town received city rights in 1233. As a member of the Hanseatic League it grew rich on the Vistula grain and timber trade. The Second Peace of Thorn (1466) transferred the city from the Teutonic Order to the Kingdom of Poland. Copernicus was born in a house on what is now St. Anne Street in 1473. The city later passed to Prussia in the Partitions of Poland and returned to Poland after the First World War.

Sights and culture

Landmarks include the Old Town Hall, the leaning tower, the ruins of the Teutonic castle, Copernicus's house (now a museum), and the Gothic churches of St. John and St. James. Toruń is renowned for its gingerbread (pierniki), baked there since the Middle Ages, and is home to Nicolaus Copernicus University, founded in 1945.
