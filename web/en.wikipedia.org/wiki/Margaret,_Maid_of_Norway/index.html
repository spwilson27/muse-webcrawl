Margaret, Maid of Norway

Margaret (March or April 1283 – 26 September 1290), known as the Maid of Norway, was the queen-designate of Scotland from 1286 until her death. She was the daughter of King Eric II of Norway and Margaret of Scotland, daughter of Alexander III of Scotland.

Succession

When Alexander III died in a riding accident in March 1286, leaving no surviving sons, the infant Margaret—his granddaughter—was recognized by the Scottish Guardians as his heir. By the Treaty of Birgham (1290), negotiated under the oversight of Edward I of England, Margaret was to marry Edward's son, the future Edward II, while Scotland was to remain a separate kingdom.

Death

In September 1290 Margaret sailed from Bergen for Scotland, but fell ill during the voyage and died in Orkney, aged seven, before ever setting foot in her kingdom. Her death left Scotland without an undisputed heir and triggered the succession crisis known as the Great Cause, in which Edward I adjudicated among thirteen competitors—a process that led to the accession of John Balliol and ultimately to the Wars of Scottish Independence.
