Satchō Alliance

The Satchō Alliance (薩長同盟, Satchō dōmei), also known as the Satsuma–Chōshū Alliance, was a military alliance between the feudal domains of Satsuma and Chōshū formed in 1866 to combine their efforts to overthrow the Tokugawa shogunate of Japan.

The name Satchō (薩長) is an abbreviation combining the names of the provinces Satsuma (薩摩) and Chōshū (長州), two of the strongest Imperialist domains in Edo-period Japan.

History

In the 1860s, Satsuma tended to take a moderate position, aiming at the maintenance of peace, while Chōshū had become a bastion of rebels aiming at the overthrow of the government. With the mediation of Sakamoto Ryōma of Tosa Domain, the military leaders of Satsuma — Saigō Takamori and Ōkubo Toshimichi — were brought together with Katsura Kogorō (Kido Takayoshi) of Chōshū.

The two domains were traditional rivals, and earlier mistrust ran deep: Satsuma forces had helped the shogunate suppress Chōshū rebels at the Forbidden Gate Incident of 1864, and Satsuma samurai had fought Chōshū troops. Nevertheless, the alliance was concluded in Kyoto on 7 March 1866, in part facilitated by arms purchases arranged through the Scottish merchant Thomas Blake Glover — Satsuma agreed to help Chōshū obtain modern weapons, which the shogunate had forbidden it to buy.

Consequences

Though not quite an agreement to combine forces militarily, the alliance proved decisive. When the shogunate launched the Second Chōshū expedition in the summer of 1866, Satsuma refused to send its troops, depriving the bakufu of a key contingent; Chōshū's modernized forces defeated the shogunate army, badly damaging the prestige of the Tokugawa regime. In 1867, the two domains, together with Tosa, issued a joint memorial calling for the restoration of Imperial rule; following Tokugawa Yoshinobu's resignation, the alliance's forces fought and won the Boshin War (1868–1869), paving the way for the Meiji Restoration. Men from Satsuma and Chōshū dominated the new Meiji government for decades afterward.
