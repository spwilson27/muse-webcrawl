Horatio Walpole, 4th Earl of Orford (24 September 1717 – 2 March 1797), better known as Horace Walpole, was an English writer, art historian, man of letters, antiquarian, and Whig politician. He is best remembered for his Gothic novel The Castle of Otranto (1764), for his voluminous correspondence, and for Strawberry Hill House, the villa he built in Twickenham, south-west London, where he revived the Gothic style some decades before his Victorian successors.

Early life

Walpole was born in London, the youngest son of the first British Prime Minister, Sir Robert Walpole, and his wife Catherine. He was educated at Eton College and at King's College, Cambridge. From 1739 to 1741 he travelled in France and Italy with his friend the poet Thomas Gray, an experience recorded in their letters.

Political career

In 1741 Walpole entered Parliament as a Whig member, first for Callington in Cornwall, then for Castle Rising, and finally for King's Lynn. He never rose to high office but remained a Member of Parliament until 1768, and became known as a sharp observer of political life, which he recorded in his memoirs.

Strawberry Hill

In 1747 Walpole leased a small house at Twickenham and over the following decades transformed it into Strawberry Hill House, a playful "little Gothic castle" with battlements, cloisters, and stained glass. The house became one of the most influential examples of the Gothic Revival in architecture and design, inspiring the style sometimes called "Strawberry Hill Gothic".

The Castle of Otranto

Published in 1764, The Castle of Otranto was presented in its first edition as a translation of a medieval Italian manuscript. Its tale of a usurping prince, a gigantic helmet, and supernatural portents founded the Gothic novel genre and influenced writers from Ann Radcliffe and Matthew Lewis to Bram Stoker and beyond.

Letters and memoirs

Walpole's letters, particularly those to Horace Mann, George Montagu, and the Countess of Ossory, are among the most celebrated in English literature, offering a vivid, witty chronicle of 18th-century society, politics, and gossip. His Memoirs of the reigns of George II and George III were published posthumously.

Death and legacy

Walpole succeeded his nephew as 4th Earl of Orford in 1791. He died unmarried at his London home in Berkeley Square in 1797. His collections at Strawberry Hill were sold in a famous 24-day sale in 1842. He is also credited with coining the word "serendipity", in a 1754 letter, from the Persian fairy tale The Three Princes of Serendip.
