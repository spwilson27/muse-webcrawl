Man of Marble

Man of Marble (Polish: Człowiek z marmuru) is a 1977 Polish film directed by Andrzej Wajda. It is one of the landmark works of Polish cinema, a political film that exposed the mechanisms of Stalinist propaganda in 1950s Poland and, by implication, the dishonesty of the communist system of the 1970s. The screenplay by Aleksander Ścibor-Rylski had waited some thirteen years for approval.

Plot

Agnieszka (Krystyna Janda), a young television filmmaker, sets out to make a documentary about Mateusz Birkut (Jerzy Radziwiłowicz), a bricklayer who in 1952 became a national hero by laying a record 30,000 bricks in a single shift during the building of Nowa Huta. Through interviews and archive newsreels she uncovers how Birkut was manufactured as a model worker, how he fell from favour after a show trial of his friend, was imprisoned, and was then erased from official memory — his marble statue stored away in a museum basement. Her own film is shut down by her superiors, mirroring the suppression she is investigating.

Reception and sequel

The film was a sensation in Poland and won the FIPRESCI prize at Cannes in 1978. Its sequel, Man of Iron (1981), made during the Solidarity strikes and incorporating documentary footage of them, won the Palme d'Or at Cannes and follows Birkut's son, a shipyard activist.
