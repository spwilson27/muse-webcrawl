Voroneț Monastery

Voroneț Monastery
Location: Voroneț, Suceava County, Romania
Founded: 1488
Founder: Stephen the Great

The Voroneț Monastery is a medieval monastery in the Romanian village of Voroneț, in Suceava County, part of the famous painted churches of Moldavia in southern Bukovina. It is renowned worldwide for its exterior frescoes, especially a vivid depiction of the Last Judgment, and for the intense blue pigment known as "Voroneț blue". The monastery is often called the "Sistine Chapel of the East".

History

The church of Saint George within the monastery was built in 1488 by Stephen the Great of Moldavia, reportedly in under four months, to commemorate his victory at the Battle of Vaslui. The exterior paintings were added in 1547 under Metropolitan Grigore Roșca, when the church's outer walls were covered in frescoes intended to teach biblical stories to villagers and soldiers.

The Last Judgment

The western wall bears a complete and celebrated fresco of the Last Judgment, unusual for depicting the entirety of the scene on a single wall. The vivid blue background of the frescoes, obtained from lapis lazuli, has become emblematic of the monastery.

World Heritage

The monastery is included in UNESCO's World Heritage List as part of the "Churches of Moldavia" site, inscribed in 1993 and extended in 2010.
