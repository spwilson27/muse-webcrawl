Tropical fish

Tropical fish is a term for fish originating from tropical environments around the world, most familiarly in the aquarium trade. It includes both freshwater species — from the Amazon Basin, West Africa, and Southeast Asia — and saltwater species from tropical oceans and coral reefs. Tropical fish are generally kept in heated aquaria, because they cannot tolerate the cool water temperatures of temperate regions; "tropical" in fishkeeping usually means water maintained around 24–27 °C (75–81 °F).

Freshwater tropical fish

Freshwater species dominate the aquarium hobby because they are generally hardier and cheaper than marine fish. Popular groups include:

  • Tetras (neon tetra, cardinal tetra) — small South American characins kept in shoals.
  • Livebearers (guppies, mollies, platies, swordtails) — hardy Central American fish that give birth to free-swimming young.
  • Cichlids — a large family from Africa and the Americas, including the freshwater angelfish, discus, and the rift-lake cichlids of Lakes Malawi and Tanganyika.
  • Anabantoids — labyrinth fish such as the Siamese fighting fish (betta) and gouramis, which can breathe air at the surface.
  • Catfish and loaches — bottom-dwelling species such as Corydoras and plecos, often kept as scavengers.

Saltwater tropical fish

Marine tropical fish are typically reef species with bright colours: clownfish, damselfish, tangs, angelfish, butterflyfish, wrasses and gobies. Because many marine ornamentals are collected from the wild rather than bred, and because reef aquaria demand stable, high-quality water, marine fishkeeping is generally regarded as more difficult and expensive than the freshwater hobby. Captive breeding of popular species such as clownfish has increased, reducing pressure on reef populations.

The trade

The ornamental fish trade is a global industry worth billions of dollars, centred on breeding farms in Florida, Singapore and other parts of Asia, and on wild collection in Amazonia, Indonesia and the Philippines. Environmental concerns include collection methods, the introduction of invasive species through aquarium releases (a major origin of alien fish in Florida waterways), and the welfare of fish in transport, where mortality can be high.
