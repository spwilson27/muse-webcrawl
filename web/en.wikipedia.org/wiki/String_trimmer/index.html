String trimmer

A string trimmer, also known by the portmanteau strimmer and the trademarked names Weedwacker, Weed Eater and Whipper Snipper, is a garden tool for cutting grass, groundcover, and small weeds. A string trimmer uses a flexible monofilament line instead of a blade, which spins at high speed to cut vegetation. It is commonly used to trim grass in areas a lawn mower cannot reach, such as along fences, walls, and garden edges.

Design

A typical string trimmer consists of a cutting head at the end of a long shaft with a handle, powered by petrol (gasoline), mains electricity, or a rechargeable battery. The cutting head contains a spool of nylon monofilament line; as the head spins at several thousand revolutions per minute, centrifugal force extends the line outward, and its speed allows it to slice through grass and weeds. Line is advanced by a bump feed, automatic feed, or fixed-line head. Some models accept metal or plastic blades for heavier brush.

History

The string trimmer was invented in the early 1970s by George Ballas of Houston, Texas, who reportedly got the idea while watching the rotating brushes of an automatic car wash. He founded Weed Eaters, Inc. to market the device, and the tool became a standard piece of lawn-care equipment worldwide.

Safety

String trimmers can throw debris at high speed; eye protection and long trousers are recommended. Nylon line fragments are also a source of microplastic litter in gardens.
