Operation Drumbeat

Operation Drumbeat (German: Unternehmen Paukenschlag) was the code name for the initial wave of German U-boat attacks against shipping along the East Coast of the United States and Canada in early 1942, during the Second World War. It formed the opening phase of what became known as the "Second Happy Time" for the U-boat arm.

Background

Following Germany's declaration of war on the United States on 11 December 1941, Admiral Karl Dönitz, commander of the U-boat force, ordered long-range Type IX U-boats to the American east coast to attack merchant shipping. Five boats — U-66, U-109, U-123, U-125, and U-130 — formed the first Drumbeat group, reaching their patrol areas in mid-January 1942.

The attacks

The U-boats found American shipping unprepared: merchant ships sailed alone, unescorted, with lights burning, silhouetted against coastal cities that had not been blacked out. U-123 under Reinhard Hardegen opened the offensive by sinking the British steamer Cyclops on 12 January 1942, and sank the tanker Norness off Long Island two days later. During January and February the Drumbeat boats sank dozens of ships with almost no losses. Over the following months, successive waves of U-boats extended operations into the Gulf of Mexico and the Caribbean, sinking hundreds of vessels before effective convoy and air patrol systems were established.
