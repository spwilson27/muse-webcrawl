The history of Myanmar (Burma) covers early Pyu and Mon kingdoms, the Burmese empires centred on Bagan, Ava and Mandalay, British colonial rule, and the troubled politics of independence, military rule and civil conflict.

Early kingdoms

The Pyu city-states flourished in the Irrawaddy valley in the first millennium CE. In 1044 Anawrahta founded the Pagan Empire, unifying the valley and promoting Theravada Buddhism; thousands of temples were built at Bagan before Mongol invasions ended the empire in 1287.

Taungoo and Konbaung dynasties

The Taungoo dynasty reunified Burma in the 16th century, briefly ruling the largest empire in Southeast Asian history under Bayinnaung. The Konbaung dynasty (1752–1885) fought wars with Siam and Qing China before clashing with Britain.

British Burma

Three Anglo-Burmese Wars (1824–26, 1852, 1885) brought the whole country under British rule, administered as part of India until 1937. Burma became a major rice-exporting colony.

Independence and military rule

Burma gained independence on 4 January 1948 under U Nu. General Ne Win's 1962 coup began decades of military rule and socialist isolation. The 1988 uprising was crushed; elections in 1990 won by Aung San Suu Kyi's NLD were annulled. A partial transition to civilian rule began in 2011, but the military seized power again in the February 2021 coup, sparking civil war.
