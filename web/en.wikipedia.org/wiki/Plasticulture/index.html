Plasticulture
DefinitionUse of plastics in agriculture and horticulture
Key productsMulch film, drip irrigation, greenhouses, tunnels, silage wrap
PioneerEmery M. Emmert (University of Kentucky, 1940s–50s)

Plasticulture is the practice of using plastic materials in agricultural and horticultural applications. Its major components are plastic mulch films spread over soil, drip-irrigation tubing laid on or under the mulch, plastic-covered greenhouses and high and low tunnels (polytunnels), row covers, silage and bale wrapping, nursery pots and plastic fencing and netting.

Benefits

Plastic mulch warms the soil, conserves moisture, suppresses weeds, reduces soil splash and some diseases, and can advance harvests by days or weeks and raise marketable yields; black, clear and coloured films have different warming and weed effects. Combined with drip irrigation, plasticulture sharply improves water-use efficiency and allows precise fertigation. Tunnels and greenhouses extend the growing season and protect high-value crops such as strawberries, melons, tomatoes, peppers and cut flowers. The field is often credited to Emery M. Emmert of the University of Kentucky, who experimented with plastic greenhouses and mulches from the late 1940s.

Scale and environmental concerns

Plasticulture is used worldwide on millions of hectares — China is by far the largest user of mulch film. Concerns focus on disposal: thin films fragment in soil, are hard to recycle when contaminated, and contribute to microplastic accumulation; burning or landfilling used film is common. Responses include thicker, recoverable films, biodegradable mulch films, collection schemes, and research into soil impacts.
