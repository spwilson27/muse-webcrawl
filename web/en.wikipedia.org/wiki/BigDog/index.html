BigDog, Boston Dynamics' quadruped robot

BigDog was a dynamically stable quadruped military robot created in 2005 by Boston Dynamics in collaboration with Foster-Miller, the NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory, and the Harvard University Concord Field Station. Funded by DARPA, it was intended as a robotic pack mule able to accompany dismounted soldiers over terrain too rough for vehicles.

Design and capabilities

BigDog stood about 0.76 m tall, was roughly 0.91 m long, and weighed about 110 kg. It was powered by a single-cylinder two-stroke engine driving a hydraulic system, with four legs articulated like those of an animal, equipped with sensors for joint position, force, ground contact, and a laser gyroscope and stereo vision system for balance and navigation. It could walk at about 6.4 km/h, climb slopes up to 35 degrees, cross rubble and streams, recover from slips and kicks without falling, and carry loads of up to roughly 150 kg. Videos of BigDog moving through snow and ice — and regaining its footing when kicked — became some of the most widely viewed robot demonstrations of the era.

Legacy

An enlarged follow-on, the Legged Squad Support System (LS3), was field-tested with the US Marine Corps, but in 2015 the programme was shelved, largely because the petrol-engine robot was too noisy for use alongside troops. BigDog's control technology and public impact, however, paved the way for Boston Dynamics' later electric quadruped Spot, released commercially in 2019–2020.
