Edinger–Westphal nucleus

Part ofMidbrain; oculomotor nuclear complex
SystemParasympathetic nervous system
FunctionPupillary constriction and lens accommodation via the ciliary ganglion

The Edinger–Westphal nucleus is the parasympathetic preganglionic nucleus associated with the oculomotor nerve (cranial nerve III), located in the midbrain at the level of the superior colliculus, just dorsal to the main motor nucleus of the oculomotor complex. It is named after the German anatomists Ludwig Edinger, who described it in a fetus in 1885, and Karl Friedrich Otto Westphal, who described it in an adult in 1887.

Function

Preganglionic parasympathetic fibres from the Edinger–Westphal nucleus travel with the oculomotor nerve to the ciliary ganglion in the orbit, where they synapse. Postganglionic fibres (short ciliary nerves) then supply two smooth muscles of the eye: the sphincter pupillae, whose contraction constricts the pupil (miosis), and the ciliary muscle, whose contraction allows the lens to thicken for near vision (accommodation). The nucleus is therefore the efferent arm of both the pupillary light reflex — receiving input relayed from the retina via the pretectal olivary nucleus, bilaterally, which produces the consensual light reflex — and the near reflex.

Subdivisions

Research in primates distinguishes the preganglionic Edinger–Westphal nucleus (EWpg), containing the classically described cholinergic neurons projecting to the ciliary ganglion, from a centrally projecting population (EWcp) whose neurons express the peptide urocortin-1 and project within the brain. The EWcp has been implicated in responses to stress, feeding behaviour and alcohol consumption, and in rodents it is this population that dominates the anatomical region bearing the nucleus's name.

Clinical significance

Lesions affecting the nucleus or its fibres produce a dilated pupil unreactive to light (internal ophthalmoplegia), often as part of an oculomotor nerve palsy with ptosis and eye-movement deficits. Compression of the third nerve by a herniating uncus characteristically dilates the pupil early ("blown pupil") because the parasympathetic fibres run superficially in the nerve.
