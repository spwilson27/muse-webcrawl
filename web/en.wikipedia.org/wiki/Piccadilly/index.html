Piccadilly is a road in the City of Westminster, London, running from Hyde Park Corner in the west to Piccadilly Circus in the east. It forms part of the A4 road. The street is one of London's most famous thoroughfares, known for its grand hotels, clubs, shops and institutions.

Name and early history

The name derives from "piccadill", a fashionable broad laced collar of the early 17th century. Robert Baker, a tailor who grew rich selling piccadills, built a mansion nearby around 1612 that was nicknamed "Piccadilly Hall"; the name transferred to the road, which had previously been known as Portugal Street. By the later 17th century Piccadilly was lined with grand town houses of the aristocracy.

Notable buildings

Surviving great houses include Burlington House, home of the Royal Academy of Arts; Apsley House, the Duke of Wellington's residence at Hyde Park Corner; and Albany, the exclusive bachelor apartment block. The Ritz Hotel (1906) and the Wolseley restaurant occupy grand premises on the south side, while the department store Fortnum & Mason, founded in nearby Duke Street in 1707, has stood on Piccadilly since the 18th century. Hatchards, London's oldest bookshop (founded 1797), is also on the street. St James's Church, Piccadilly, designed by Christopher Wren, was consecrated in 1684. Green Park borders the street's western half on the south side.

Transport

The Piccadilly line of the London Underground is named after the street and serves stations at Hyde Park Corner, Green Park and Piccadilly Circus. The street is also a major bus corridor and part of London's strategic road network.
