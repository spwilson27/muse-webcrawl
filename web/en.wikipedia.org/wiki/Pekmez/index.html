Pekmez

Pekmez (Turkish: pekmez; Greek: petimezi; in the Balkans also pekmez, džem-related names vary by region) is a thick molasses-like syrup produced by condensing the juice of fruits, most often grapes, figs or mulberries, and in some regions pomegranate, carob, plum or sugar beet. It is a traditional product across Turkey, Greece, Cyprus, the Balkans, the Levant and the Caucasus, with roots in Roman defrutum and Byzantine cooking.

Production

Fruit is crushed and pressed, and the juice is briefly boiled, traditionally with a special whitish soil or clay (pekmez earth, rich in calcium carbonate) or wood ash that reduces acidity and clarifies the must. The juice is then strained and simmered for hours until it thickens to a dark, sweet syrup. No sugar is added; the sweetness comes entirely from concentrating the fruit's own sugars.

Uses

Pekmez is eaten at breakfast, classically mixed with tahini as a spread, drizzled over yoghurt or porridge, or used as a sweetener in desserts and sherbets. It has a long folk reputation as a remedy for anaemia and winter colds because of its iron and mineral content. Grape pekmez may also be used in making traditional sweets such as Turkish delight variants, churchkhela (in Georgia, where a similar product coats strings of nuts) and bastık. Pomegranate pekmez, sour rather than sweet, is used as a dressing in salads and dishes in eastern Turkey and the Levant, resembling pomegranate molasses.
