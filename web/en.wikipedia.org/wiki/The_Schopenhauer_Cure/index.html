Overview

The Schopenhauer Cure is a 2005 novel by American psychiatrist and author Irvin D. Yalom. The book interweaves two narratives: the story of Julius Hertzfeld, a prominent psychotherapist diagnosed with terminal cancer who re-evaluates his life and work, and the biography of the nineteenth-century German philosopher Arthur Schopenhauer, whose pessimistic philosophy runs through the novel as a counterpoint to the therapy group's human connections.

Plot

After learning he has perhaps a year of healthy life remaining, Julius contacts Philip Slate, a former patient he considers one of his failures — a man who had been addicted to sex and whom Julius could not help. Philip has since studied philosophy, cured himself through Schopenhauer's writings, and now wishes to become a therapist himself, asking Julius to supervise him. Julius agrees on the condition that Philip first spend six months as a member of his therapy group.

The resulting clashes between Philip's cold Schopenhauerian detachment and the group's emotional openness form the heart of the novel, alternating with chapters recounting Schopenhauer's life and thought. Yalom, himself a noted existential psychotherapist, uses the story to explore death, meaning, and interpersonal connection.
