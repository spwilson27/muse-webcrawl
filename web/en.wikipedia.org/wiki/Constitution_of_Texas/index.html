Constitution of Texas

The Constitution of Texas is the governing document of the U.S. state of Texas, adopted in 1876. It is the second-longest state constitution in the United States, after that of Alabama, owing to its highly detailed and restrictive character: unlike the federal Constitution, which grants broad powers, the Texas Constitution limits state government by spelling out what it may and may not do.

History

Texas has had several constitutions: the constitution of Coahuila y Tejas (1827), the constitution of the Republic of Texas (1836), the state constitution of 1845 upon annexation by the United States, the Confederate constitution of 1861, the constitution of 1866, and the Reconstruction constitution of 1869, which centralised power in the governorship under Edmund J. Davis.

The current constitution was written by the Constitutional Convention of 1875, dominated by members of the Grange and by Democrats determined to undo Reconstruction-era centralisation. Voters ratified it on 15 February 1876. It decentralised government, limited the governor's powers, shortened terms of office, made many offices elective (including judges), and strictly limited state debt and taxation.

Structure

The constitution contains 17 articles:

  • Article I — Bill of Rights
  • Article II — Separation of powers
  • Article III — Legislative department (the Texas Legislature)
  • Article IV — Executive department (a "plural executive" of separately elected officials)
  • Article V — Judicial department
  • Article VII — Education, including the Permanent University Fund
  • Article VIII — Taxation and revenue
  • Article XVI — General provisions
  • Article XVII — Mode of amending the constitution

Amendments

Because the constitution is so detailed, it must be amended frequently to accommodate ordinary policy changes. Amendments are proposed by a two-thirds vote of both houses of the legislature and ratified by a simple majority of voters in a statewide election. By the 2020s, voters had approved more than 500 amendments. A comprehensive revision attempt in 1974–75 failed, and the constitution remains fundamentally the 1876 document.
