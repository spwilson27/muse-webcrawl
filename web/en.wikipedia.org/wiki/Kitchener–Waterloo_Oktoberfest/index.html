Kitchener–Waterloo Oktoberfest

Kitchener–Waterloo Oktoberfest Location: Kitchener and Waterloo, Ontario, Canada
First held: 1969
Duration: 9 days, ending the Saturday before Canadian Thanksgiving
Attendance: about 700,000
Claim to fame: Largest Bavarian festival in North America

Kitchener–Waterloo Oktoberfest is an annual nine-day festival held in the twin cities of Kitchener and Waterloo, Ontario, Canada, widely billed as the largest Bavarian festival in North America and the second-largest Oktoberfest in the world after Munich's. It celebrates the region's deep German heritage — Kitchener was known as Berlin until 1916 — and culminates on Canadian Thanksgiving weekend.

Events

The festival centres on beer halls (Festhallen) operated by local German clubs, featuring German food, beer, and oompah and polka music presided over by the festival's mascot, Onkel Hans. Its signature event is the Thanksgiving Day Parade, the only major parade held on that holiday in Canada, broadcast nationally. Other fixtures include a keg-tapping ceremony, Miss Oktoberfest pageant, a pancake breakfast, barrel races and charity events.

Founded in 1969 by local German-Canadian clubs building on earlier celebrations, the festival now attracts roughly 700,000 visitors and hundreds of events across the Waterloo Region, and contributes tens of millions of dollars to the local economy.
