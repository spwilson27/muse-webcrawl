Names and titles of Jesus in the New Testament

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Two names and a variety of titles are used to refer to Jesus in the New Testament. In Christianity, the two names Jesus and Emmanuel, which stem from contexts in the Old Testament, both refer to Jesus of Nazareth and his salvific role. The title "Christ" (from the Greek for "anointed one") eventually became used almost as a surname and is integral to Christian confessions of faith.

Names

Jesus

The name Jesus derives from the Greek Ἰησοῦς (Iēsous), a rendition of the Hebrew Yeshua (ישוע), itself a form of Yehoshua (Joshua), meaning "Yahweh is salvation". Matthew 1:21 explains the name with a wordplay: "you shall call his name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins."

Emmanuel

Matthew 1:23 also applies to Jesus the name Emmanuel ("God with us"), quoting Isaiah 7:14. Emmanuel appears only three times in the Bible, but functions in Christian theology as a declaration of the Incarnation.

Titles

The New Testament applies numerous titles and images to Jesus, among them:

  • Christ and Messiah — "the anointed one"
  • Son of God
  • Son of Man — Jesus' distinctive self-designation in the Gospels
  • Son of David
  • Lamb of God (John 1:29)
  • King of the Jews and King of Israel
  • Rabbi and Teacher; Prophet
  • Light of the World (John 8:12)
  • Bread of Life, the True Vine, the Good Shepherd, the Way, the Truth, and the Life — the "I am" sayings of John's Gospel
  • Logos (the Word) — in the Prologue of John (John 1:1–14)
  • Lord (Kyrios)
  • Alpha and Omega, the First and the Last (Revelation)
  • High Priest (Hebrews), Mediator, Savior

Study

The study of the names and titles of Jesus forms part of Christology, the branch of theology concerned with his person and work; the titles have often been read as evidence for how the earliest Christian communities understood Jesus' identity and mission.
