Quo Vadis (1951 film)

Quo Vadis is a 1951 American religious epic film directed by Mervyn LeRoy from a screenplay by S. N. Behrman, Sonya Levien, and John Lee Mahin, adapted from the classic 1896 novel Quo Vadis by Polish author Henryk Sienkiewicz. Produced by Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer and filmed at Cinecittà Studios in Rome, it stars Robert Taylor as the Roman commander Marcus Vinicius and Deborah Kerr as the Christian hostage Lygia, with Peter Ustinov acclaimed as the emperor Nero.

Plot

Returning to Rome after years campaigning in Britain, Marcus Vinicius falls in love with Lygia, a Christian convert held as a hostage. As Nero's megalomania grows — culminating in the Great Fire of Rome, which Nero blames on the Christians to deflect suspicion from himself — Vinicius is drawn toward the persecuted faith and toward rescuing Lygia, who is condemned to the arena. The apostle Peter, who earlier asked the risen Christ "Quo vadis, Domine?" ("Where are you going, Lord?"), returns to Rome and martyrdom.

Production and reception

One of the most expensive films of its time, the production employed tens of thousands of extras and hundreds of sets at Cinecittà, helping to establish Rome as a location for Hollywood epics. The film was a major box-office success and was credited with helping to rescue MGM from financial difficulty; it received eight Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture, though it won none. Miklós Rózsa's score and Ustinov's Nero are frequently singled out for praise.
