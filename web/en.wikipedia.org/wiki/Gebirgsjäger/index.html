Gebirgsjäger (German pronunciation: [ɡəˈbɪʁksˌjɛːɡɐ]) are the light infantry part of the alpine or mountain troops (Gebirgstruppe) of Germany and Austria. The word Jäger (hunter) is a characteristic term for light infantry in German-speaking military contexts.

History

The German mountain troops trace their origins to the Alpenkorps raised during the First World War, which fought on the Italian front in the Dolomites and later in the Balkans and on the Western Front. During the Second World War, the Wehrmacht fielded several mountain divisions (Gebirgsdivisionen), which served in Norway, Finland, the Eastern Front, the Balkans, Italy and the Caucasus.

After 1955, the newly founded Bundeswehr re-established mountain troops, headquartered in Bavaria. The 1st Mountain Division existed until 2001; today the Gebirgsjägerbrigade 23 "Bayern", based in Bad Reichenhall, continues the tradition. In Austria, mountain infantry form part of the Austrian Armed Forces' territorial forces.

Equipment and training

Gebirgsjäger are trained in alpine movement, high-altitude warfare, rock and ice climbing, skiing, and survival in mountainous terrain. They work closely with pack animals, notably Haflinger horses and mules, which remain in service for supplying remote mountain positions. Their traditional insignia is the edelweiss, also worn by Austrian and other allied mountain troops.
