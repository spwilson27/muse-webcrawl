1,4-Benzoquinone

IUPAC nameCyclohexa-2,5-diene-1,4-dione
FormulaC6H4O2
Molar mass108.10 g/mol
AppearanceYellow crystalline solid
Melting point115 °C
Other namesp-Benzoquinone, quinone

1,4-Benzoquinone, commonly known as para-quinone or simply quinone, is a chemical compound with the formula C6H4O2. It is one of two isomers of benzoquinone, the other being 1,2-benzoquinone (ortho-quinone). In a pure state it forms bright-yellow crystals with a characteristic irritating odour resembling that of chlorine or formaldehyde. This cyclic, conjugated diketone is an oxidised derivative of hydroquinone, to which it is readily interconverted in redox reactions.

Properties and redox chemistry

1,4-Benzoquinone is a planar molecule and a strong oxidising agent. It undergoes a reversible two-electron, two-proton reduction to hydroquinone; this quinone–hydroquinone couple was historically important in electrochemistry, forming the basis of the quinhydrone electrode once used to measure pH. Quinone also participates readily in Diels–Alder reactions as a dienophile and in Michael additions, and it photochemically dimerises. It forms a dark green 1:1 charge-transfer complex with hydroquinone called quinhydrone.

Preparation and uses

Industrially, benzoquinone is produced mainly by oxidation of hydroquinone or directly from benzene via diisopropylbenzene routes, and through oxidation of aniline with manganese dioxide or other oxidants; the classic laboratory preparation oxidises hydroquinone with sodium chlorate and vanadium pentoxide, or with chromic acid. Benzoquinone is used as a hydrogen acceptor and oxidant in organic synthesis, as a polymerisation inhibitor, in the manufacture of hydroquinone for photographic developers and antioxidants, and as an intermediate in dye and agrochemical production.

Biological relevance and safety

Quinone motifs occur throughout biology: ubiquinone (coenzyme Q) and plastoquinone shuttle electrons in respiration and photosynthesis, and vitamin K is a naphthoquinone. 1,4-Benzoquinone itself is an irritant to skin, eyes and the respiratory tract; it can cause dermatitis and conjunctivitis, and prolonged exposure may discolour the skin and cornea. It is also a metabolite of benzene and contributes to benzene's toxicity in the bone marrow.
