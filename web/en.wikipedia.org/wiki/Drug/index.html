Drug

A drug is any chemical substance other than a nutrient that, when consumed, affects the structure or function of the body and mind of an organism. Drugs are typically distinguished from food and substances that provide nutritional support. Consumption can be via inhalation, injection, smoking, ingestion, absorption via a patch on the skin, suppository, or dissolution under the tongue.

Medication

In pharmacology, a drug (or medicine) is used to treat, cure, prevent or diagnose a disease or to promote well-being. Medications are classified in many ways — by their origin (natural or synthetic), their target (for example antibiotics against bacteria), their mechanism of action, or their legal status and risk of abuse. Drug development, from discovery through clinical trials to regulatory approval, typically takes more than a decade.

Recreational and psychoactive drugs

Psychoactive drugs alter consciousness, mood and perception. They include legal substances such as caffeine, alcohol and nicotine; prescription drugs used non-medically; and controlled substances such as cannabis, cocaine, opioids and hallucinogens. Their production and sale are regulated under national laws and international treaties, and misuse can cause addiction, tolerance and withdrawal, prompting debates between prohibition, decriminalisation and harm-reduction policies.

Pharmacology

The study of drugs is divided into pharmacodynamics (what a drug does to the body) and pharmacokinetics (what the body does to the drug: absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion). Effects depend on dose, route and the individual's physiology; adverse effects range from mild side effects to life-threatening interactions and overdoses.
