J. L. Austin

John Langshaw Austin (26 March 1911 – 8 February 1960) was a British philosopher of language and a leading proponent of ordinary language philosophy. He is best known for developing speech act theory in his posthumously published book How to Do Things with Words (1962).

Life and work

Austin studied at Balliol College, Oxford, and served in British military intelligence during the Second World War. After the war he became White's Professor of Moral Philosophy at Oxford. His Saturday morning discussion group became a centre of postwar British philosophy.

Speech acts

In his William James Lectures at Harvard (1955), Austin argued that many utterances do not merely describe the world but perform actions — promises, verdicts, christenings — which he called performatives. He developed the distinction between locutionary, illocutionary, and perlocutionary acts, founding the study of speech acts later extended by John Searle. His papers, collected in Philosophical Papers and Sense and Sensibilia, display a meticulous attention to the distinctions of ordinary usage.
