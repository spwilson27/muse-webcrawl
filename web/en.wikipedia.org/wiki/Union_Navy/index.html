Union Navy

The Union Navy is the formal name applied to the United States Navy during the American Civil War, to contrast it with the Confederate States Navy. The term is sometimes used carelessly to include warships that served under the Union Army, as when operating in rivers, but they were not part of the Navy.

Role in the Civil War

The Union Navy's primary missions were to enforce the blockade of Confederate ports under the Anaconda Plan, to support army operations on the western rivers and along the coast, and to protect Union commerce. Under Secretary of the Navy Gideon Welles, the fleet grew from a few dozen vessels in 1861 to more than 600 ships by 1865.

Notable engagements

Important actions included the Battle of Hampton Roads (1862), the first clash between ironclads, USS Monitor and CSS Virginia; the capture of New Orleans by David Farragut; operations on the Mississippi River culminating at Vicksburg; and the Battle of Mobile Bay (1864). The navy also fielded pioneering submarines and torpedo (mine) warfare defences.
