HDMI

HDMI (High-Definition Multimedia Interface) is a proprietary audio/video interface for transmitting uncompressed video data and compressed or uncompressed digital audio data from an HDMI-compliant source device to a compatible display, projector or audio device. It is the dominant digital connection standard for consumer electronics.

History and versions

HDMI was developed by a consortium of manufacturers (Hitachi, Panasonic, Philips, Silicon Image, Sony, Thomson and Toshiba) and the first specification, HDMI 1.0, was released in December 2002; the first products appeared in 2003. HDMI builds on the DVI interface, adding audio and control signals and a smaller connector, and incorporates HDCP content protection. Successive versions raised bandwidth and added features: HDMI 1.3 deep colour; 1.4 3D support, an audio return channel (ARC) and 4K at 30 Hz; 2.0 4K at 60 Hz and 18 Gbit/s; and 2.1 48 Gbit/s, supporting 8K and dynamic HDR, variable refresh rate and enhanced ARC (eARC).

Connectors and cables

Five connector types exist, of which Type A (standard), Type C (mini) and Type D (micro) are common in consumer devices. Cables are certified in categories such as High Speed and Ultra High Speed. HDMI Licensing Administrator, Inc. administers licensing of the specification and trademark.
