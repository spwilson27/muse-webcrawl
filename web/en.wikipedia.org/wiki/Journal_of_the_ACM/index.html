The Journal of the ACM (JACM) is a peer-reviewed scientific journal covering computer science, especially theoretical aspects, published by the Association for Computing Machinery since 1954. It is the ACM's flagship journal and one of the most prestigious venues in theoretical computer science.

Scope

JACM publishes significant contributions in areas including algorithms and data structures, computational complexity, programming languages, artificial intelligence, databases, logic in computer science, and systems. Many landmark papers have appeared in it, among them work associated with Edsger Dijkstra, Michael Rabin and other Turing Award laureates.

History

The journal began in 1954 as computing emerged as an academic discipline. For decades papers were selected partly through the ACM's special interest groups, before centralising its editorial process. It is indexed in major bibliographic databases and distributed through the ACM Digital Library.
