Achilles tendinitis, also known as Achilles tendinopathy, is a condition of the Achilles tendon — the large tendon connecting the calf muscles to the heel bone — characterised by pain, stiffness and sometimes swelling at the back of the ankle. It commonly affects runners and other athletes and typically results from overuse rather than a single injury. The broader term tendinopathy is now preferred by many clinicians, since examination often shows degenerative change (tendinosis) rather than pure inflammation.

Signs and symptoms

The characteristic symptom is pain along the tendon, worst at the start of activity and after rest, especially first thing in the morning, which may ease as the tendon "warms up". Pain may occur in the mid-portion of the tendon (2–6 cm above the heel) or at its insertion into the calcaneus. Running, jumping and climbing stairs typically aggravate it. A sudden severe pain with a snapping sensation suggests tendon rupture, a distinct and more serious injury.

Causes and diagnosis

Tendinopathy arises when repetitive loading exceeds the tendon's capacity to adapt — for example after abrupt increases in training distance or intensity. Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on the history and localised tenderness; ultrasound or MRI can show tendon thickening and structural change and help exclude other causes such as retrocalcaneal bursitis.

Treatment

First-line management is conservative: relative rest from aggravating loads, progressive strengthening programmes (classically eccentric heel-drop exercises), footwear or heel lifts, and analgesia. Most cases improve over weeks to months. Refractory cases may be offered shockwave therapy, injections or surgical debridement.
