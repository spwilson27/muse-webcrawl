Dashain

Dashain (Nepali: दशैं), also known as Vijaya Dashami, is the longest and most important festival in Nepal, celebrated by Hindus and many others over fifteen days in the month of Ashvin (September–October). It is also observed in parts of India, particularly in Sikkim, Darjeeling and Assam, and by the Nepali diaspora.

Significance

Dashain celebrates the victory of good over evil: the goddess Durga's triumph over the buffalo demon Mahishasura, and the god Rama's victory over the demon king Ravana. Families reunite, often travelling long distances, and elders place tika (a mixture of rice, yoghurt and vermilion) on the foreheads of younger relatives together with jamara — sprouted barley or maize shoots grown from the first day, Ghatasthapana.

Celebrations

Key days include Fulpati (the seventh day), Maha Ashtami and Maha Navami (the eighth and ninth days, marked by animal sacrifices to Durga and the worship of tools, vehicles and machinery), and Vijaya Dashami (the tenth day), when tika is received. Government offices and schools close for the festival, new clothes are worn, swings (ping) are erected, and feasts are held. Kite flying and card games are popular pastimes during the holidays.
