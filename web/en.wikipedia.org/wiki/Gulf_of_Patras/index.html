Gulf of Patras

Gulf of Patras (Greek: Πατραϊκός Κόλπος)
Part of: Ionian Sea
Location: Western Greece
Length: about 50 km

The Gulf of Patras (Greek: Πατραϊκός Κόλπος, Patraïkós Kólpos) is a branch of the Ionian Sea in western Greece. It stretches about 40–50 kilometres from the Strait of Rion in the east, which connects it to the Gulf of Corinth, to Cape Araxos and the island of Oxeia in the west. Its width ranges from roughly 10 to 20 kilometres.

Geography

The northern shore belongs to Aetolia-Acarnania and the southern shore to Achaea in the Peloponnese. The city of Patras, Greece's third-largest city and a major ferry port for Italy, lies on the gulf's southeastern shore. Other settlements include Messolonghi, known for its lagoons and salt pans, Rio, and Antirrio.

The Rio-Antirrio Bridge

Since 2004 the gulf's eastern entrance has been spanned by the Rio-Antirrio Bridge, one of the world's longest multi-span cable-stayed bridges, linking the Peloponnese with mainland Greece across the Strait of Rion.

History

The gulf's waters were the setting of the Third Battle of Lepanto in 1571, fought near the entrance of the Gulf of Corinth just east of the gulf, where the fleet of the Holy League defeated the Ottoman navy. In 1948 the wreck of the ocean liner Normandie-era steamship traffic reflected Patras's role as the emigrant gateway to the Americas; today ferries connect Patras with Ancona, Bari, Brindisi, and Venice.
