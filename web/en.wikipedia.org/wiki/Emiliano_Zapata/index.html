Emiliano Zapata Salazar (8 August 1879 – 10 April 1919) was a Mexican revolutionary and the principal leader of the Liberation Army of the South during the Mexican Revolution. A champion of agrarian reform, he fought for the return of communal village lands seized by hacienda owners, and his slogan "Tierra y Libertad" ("Land and Freedom") became a rallying cry of the revolution.

Early life

Zapata was born in Anenecuilco, Morelos, to a family of small landowners and horse trainers. He witnessed the encroachment of sugar haciendas on village lands under the dictatorship of Porfirio Díaz and in 1909 was elected to lead his village's defence committee.

Revolution

When Francisco Madero called for revolt in 1910, Zapata rose in arms in the south. Dissatisfied with Madero's failure to enact land reform, Zapata repudiated him in the Plan of Ayala (November 1911), which demanded the immediate return of stolen lands and expropriation of one third of the great estates. Zapata fought successively against Madero, the usurper Victoriano Huerta, and Venustiano Carranza, whose government he refused to recognise because it would not implement agrarian reform. In 1914 his forces joined Pancho Villa's at the Convention of Aguascalientes and briefly occupied Mexico City.

Death and legacy

On 10 April 1919 Zapata was lured to the Hacienda de Chinameca by Colonel Jesús Guajardo, who had feigned defection to the revolutionary cause, and was shot dead in an ambush. His movement, Zapatismo, survived him: Article 27 of the Mexican Constitution of 1917 embodied much of his agrarian programme, and his name was revived in 1994 by the Zapatista Army of National Liberation (EZLN) in Chiapas. Zapata endures as one of the most celebrated heroes of Mexico, famously summarised in the saying attributed to him: "It is better to die on your feet than to live on your knees."
