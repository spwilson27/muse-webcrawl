Weekend

The weekend is a period of the week set aside for rest from regular work or study. In most of the world, the weekend consists of Saturday and Sunday, following a working week of Monday to Friday. In many Muslim-majority countries the weekend is Friday and Saturday, and Israel observes Friday afternoon and Saturday. The precise days and length of the weekend vary by country and have changed considerably over history.

History

The concept of a weekly day of rest has ancient roots, notably the Jewish Sabbath (Saturday) and the Christian Lord's Day (Sunday). A two-day weekend developed much later: in 1908 a New England mill became one of the first American factories to close on both Saturday and Sunday so that Jewish workers could observe the Sabbath. Henry Ford standardised the five-day, 40-hour week at the Ford Motor Company in 1926, and the practice spread through the United States and internationally over the following decades, supported by trade union campaigns for shorter working hours.

Variations around the world

  • In most of Europe, the Americas, China and Japan, the weekend is Saturday and Sunday.
  • Many countries in the Middle East and North Africa observe Friday and Saturday; the United Arab Emirates moved its public-sector weekend to Saturday and Sunday in 2022, with a half day on Friday.
  • Nepal observes a one-day weekend on Saturday.
  • Some countries and companies have experimented with a four-day working week, effectively creating a three-day weekend.

Cultural significance

The weekend is associated with leisure, family time, religious observance, sport and entertainment, and it strongly shapes patterns of retail trade, travel and broadcasting. "Weekend" activities such as tourism, markets and sporting fixtures are major parts of many economies.
