Fletcher's checksum

Fletcher's checksum is an error-detection code invented by John G. Fletcher of Lawrence Livermore Laboratory in the late 1970s. It is a checksum algorithm whose properties are similar to a cyclic redundancy check but with a lower computational cost.

Algorithm

The Fletcher checksum divides the input into blocks and maintains two running sums. For the common 16-bit variant, the data is processed in bytes, and two 8-bit sums, sum1 and sum2, are accumulated modulo 255: sum1 is the simple sum of the data bytes, and sum2 is the sum of the running values of sum1. The two sums are concatenated to form the 16-bit check value. 32-bit and 64-bit variants process data in larger words for efficiency.

Properties

Because the second sum depends on the order of the data, Fletcher's checksum can detect errors such as swapped words that a simple additive checksum would miss. It is used in protocols and formats including OSI transport protocols and the Adler-32 variant is a simplified relative used in the zlib compression format.
