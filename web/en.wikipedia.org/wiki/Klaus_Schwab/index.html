Klaus Schwab

Klaus Schwab Born: 30 March 1938, Ravensburg, Germany
Occupation: Economist, engineer
Known for: Founder of the World Economic Forum (1971)
Offices: Executive Chairman of the WEF (1971–2025)
Notable works: The Fourth Industrial Revolution (2016); COVID-19: The Great Reset (2020)

Klaus Martin Schwab (born 30 March 1938) is a German economist and engineer best known as the founder of the World Economic Forum (WEF), the organisation that convenes the annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland. He led the Forum from its foundation in 1971 until stepping down as chairman in 2025.

Life and career

Schwab was born in Ravensburg to a Swiss mother and a German father who managed a factory. He studied mechanical engineering at ETH Zurich, economics at the University of Fribourg, and public administration at Harvard, where he was influenced by Henry Kissinger. In 1971, while a professor of business policy at the University of Geneva, he founded the European Management Forum, renamed the World Economic Forum in 1987.

Ideas

Schwab popularised the concept of "stakeholder capitalism," arguing that companies should serve customers, employees, communities and the environment as well as shareholders. His 2016 book The Fourth Industrial Revolution framed advances in AI, robotics and biotechnology as a new industrial era and became a Davos catchphrase. In 2020 he co-authored COVID-19: The Great Reset, proposing that the pandemic offered an opportunity to rebuild economies more sustainably; the phrase "Great Reset" subsequently became the focus of widespread conspiracy theories.

Criticism and succession

Critics have attacked the Davos meeting as an elitist gathering of the global rich, and Schwab personally over the WEF's finances and governance. In 2024–25, following internal investigations into workplace culture allegations (which Schwab denied), he resigned as executive chairman; former Norwegian foreign minister Børge Brende served as interim chair before former Nestlé chairman Peter Brabeck-Letmathe was announced in the role, while Schwab moved to chair the Forum's board of trustees and then stepped back entirely.
