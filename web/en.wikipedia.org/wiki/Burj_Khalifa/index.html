Burj Khalifa

The Burj Khalifa is a skyscraper in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. With a total height of 829.8 m (2,722 ft), it has been the world's tallest structure and building since its topping out in 2009. Construction began in 2004 and the building opened on 4 January 2010 as the centerpiece of Downtown Dubai.

Design

Architect Adrian Smith of Skidmore, Owings & Merrill designed the tower with a triple-lobed, Y-shaped floor plan inspired by the desert flower Hymenocallis and Islamic geometric patterns. The "buttressed core" structural system — a hexagonal central core reinforced by three wings — resists torsional wind forces as the tower spirals upward in a series of setbacks.

Construction

More than 330,000 m³ of concrete and 39,000 tonnes of steel rebar went into the building; at peak, some 12,000 workers were on site. Originally named Burj Dubai, it was renamed at its opening in honor of Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan, ruler of Abu Dhabi, whose government had supported Dubai through the 2008–09 financial crisis.

Use

The tower contains the Armani Hotel, luxury residences, corporate offices, and the At.mosphere restaurant. Observation decks include At The Top on levels 124–125 and a premium lounge at level 148, among the highest outdoor observation decks in the world.

Records

Besides tallest building, the Burj Khalifa holds records for the most stories, the highest occupied floor, and the longest elevator travel distance.
