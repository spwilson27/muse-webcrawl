Raqqa (Arabic: الرقة, romanised ar-Raqqa) is a city in north-central Syria on the north bank of the Euphrates River, about 160 kilometres east of Aleppo. It is the capital of Raqqa Governorate. Historically known as Kallinikos and, in the early Islamic period, a residence of the Abbasid caliph Harun al-Rashid, the city became known worldwide as the de facto capital of the Islamic State from 2014 to 2017.

History

The Hellenistic city was founded by Seleucus II Callinicus (as Kallinikos). Under the Abbasids, Harun al-Rashid made Raqqa his summer residence and imperial capital from 796 to 808, building palaces northeast of the old city. Raqqa also became known for glazed ceramics ("Raqqa ware") produced in the medieval period.

Syrian civil war

Rebel forces captured Raqqa in March 2013; the Islamic State seized full control in January 2014 and declared it the capital of its caliphate. The US-backed Syrian Democratic Forces captured the city in October 2017 after a months-long battle that left much of Raqqa destroyed. Reconstruction has since proceeded slowly amid continuing instability.
