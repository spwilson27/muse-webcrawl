Academy (English school)

TypeState-funded independent school
CountryEngland
Introduced2000 (as city academies)
Governing bodyAcademy trusts

An academy is a type of state-funded school in England that is independent of local authority control, funded directly by the Department for Education. Academies are self-governing non-profit trusts and have greater freedom over the curriculum, teachers' pay, and the length of the school day and term than maintained schools.

History

The programme began under the Labour government of Tony Blair with the first "city academies" opening in 2002, modelled partly on charter schools in the United States and intended to raise standards in disadvantaged areas. The Academies Act 2010, passed by the coalition government, greatly expanded the programme by allowing all schools to convert to academy status and enabling the creation of "free schools".

Structure

Most academies are run by multi-academy trusts (MATs), organisations that operate several schools under a single board of trustees. Academies are inspected by Ofsted and their pupils take the same national examinations (GCSEs and A-levels) as other state schools.

Criticism and debate

Supporters argue academies raise standards through autonomy and strong leadership. Critics question the evidence for improvement, the lack of local democratic accountability, and the salaries of some trust executives.
