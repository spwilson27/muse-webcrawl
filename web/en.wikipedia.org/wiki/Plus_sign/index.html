Plus sign

Plus sign
+
Unicode: U+002B + PLUS SIGN

The plus sign (+) is a binary operator that indicates addition, as in 2 + 3 = 5. It can also serve as a unary operator that leaves a value unchanged (i.e. +x is the same as x), or it may indicate positivity, especially when contrasted with the negative value represented by the minus sign (−).

History

The signs + and − came into general use in printed works in the late 15th century, notably in the arithmetic of Johannes Widmann (1489), where they originally indicated surplus and deficit in commercial contexts. The plus sign is a simplification of the Latin word et, meaning "and", written in ligature; a competing derivation traces it to a shorthand for the letter t.

Other uses

Beyond arithmetic, the plus sign denotes positive charge in physics, blood types such as A+ and O+, grades (A+), the intersection of sets in some notations reversed as disjoint union, concatenation in regular expressions (where it means "one or more"), and the Red Cross emblem in medical contexts. In computing, it is used in programming languages for addition and string concatenation, and in URLs it often encodes a space.
