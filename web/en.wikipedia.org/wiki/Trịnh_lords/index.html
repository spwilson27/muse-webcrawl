Trịnh lords

The Trịnh lords (Vietnamese: Chúa Trịnh) were the de facto rulers of northern Vietnam (Đàng Ngoài, or Tonkin) from 1545 to 1787, governing in the name of the restored Lê emperors, who reigned as figureheads. After helping to restore the Lê dynasty against the Mạc, Trịnh Kiểm and his descendants monopolised power as hereditary "lords" while the rival Nguyễn lords ruled the south — a partition that lasted for over two centuries.

History

The Trịnh–Nguyễn War (1627–1672) ended in stalemate, and the two regimes settled into an uneasy partition along the Gianh River. The Trịnh maintained a formal court in Thăng Long beside the Lê palace, fielded armies against periodic unrest, and received Jesuit missionaries such as Alexandre de Rhodes. Weakened by fiscal crisis and peasant rebellion, the Trịnh regime collapsed in 1786–87 when the Tây Sơn brothers marched north and overthrew it.
