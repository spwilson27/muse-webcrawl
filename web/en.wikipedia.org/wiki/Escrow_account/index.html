Escrow

Escrow is a contractual arrangement in which a third party (the escrow agent) receives and disburses money or documents for the primary transacting parties, with the disbursement dependent on conditions agreed to by those parties. An escrow account is the account in which such funds are held. Escrow is commonly used in real estate transactions, mergers and acquisitions, software licensing (source code escrow), and online commerce.

Real estate

In a property sale, the buyer typically deposits earnest money into escrow, where it is held until closing conditions — inspections, title searches, financing — are satisfied. In the United States, mortgage lenders commonly require an escrow account (also called an impound account) into which the borrower pays a portion of property taxes and homeowner's insurance premiums with each monthly mortgage payment; the servicer then pays those obligations when due. The Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA) regulates such accounts, limiting the cushion a servicer may require.

Software escrow

In source code escrow, a software vendor deposits the source code of a critical application with a neutral agent, to be released to the licensee if the vendor goes out of business or fails to maintain the software. This protects the licensee's investment in business-critical systems.

Internet escrow

Online escrow services hold a buyer's payment until the buyer confirms receipt of goods in acceptable condition, reducing fraud in high-value transactions between strangers, such as domain name and vehicle sales.
