The Twenty-seventh Dynasty of Egypt designates the first period of Achaemenid Persian rule over Egypt, from 525 to 404 BC, during which Egypt was a satrapy of the Achaemenid Empire. In Manetho's scheme the dynasty comprises the Persian Great Kings from Cambyses II through Darius II, who ruled Egypt as pharaohs.

History

The dynasty began when Cambyses II defeated Pharaoh Psamtik III at the Battle of Pelusium in 525 BC and took Memphis. Cambyses adopted pharaonic titulary and, after an unsuccessful expedition toward Nubia and the Siwa Oasis, returned to Persia, dying in 522 BC. His successor, Darius I, is remembered in Egyptian tradition as a lawgiver: he completed the codification of Egyptian law, finished a canal from the Nile to the Red Sea begun under Necho II, and built the Temple of Hibis in the Kharga Oasis.

Egypt revolted at the end of Darius's reign and again under Xerxes I (486 BC), and joined the revolt of Inaros in the 460s BC with Athenian support, which was crushed by 454 BC. The dynasty ended in 404 BC, when Amyrtaeus of Sais expelled the Persians and founded the native Twenty-eighth Dynasty; Persian rule returned in 343 BC as the so-called Thirty-first Dynasty.
