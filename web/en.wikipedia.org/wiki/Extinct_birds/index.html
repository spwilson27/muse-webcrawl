Extinct birds

Since 1500, over 190 species of birds have become extinct, and this rate of extinction seems to be increasing. Island species are particularly vulnerable, as are flightless birds. Human activity is the principal cause: habitat destruction, hunting, and the introduction of invasive predators such as rats, cats, and pigs have devastated bird populations worldwide.

Well-known extinct birds include the dodo of Mauritius, the great auk of the North Atlantic, the passenger pigeon of North America, the moa of New Zealand, and the elephant birds of Madagascar. Many species are known only from subfossil remains or from brief historical accounts.

Causes of extinction

The most common cause is habitat loss due to deforestation, wetland drainage, and agriculture. Introduced species prey on eggs and adults and compete for food. Hunting for meat, feathers, and the cage-bird trade eliminated several species, and diseases spread by introduced mosquitoes, such as avian malaria, have driven many Hawaiian birds to extinction.

Recently extinct species

Recent losses include the poʻouli of Hawaii (2004), the Alagoas foliage-gleaner (2011), and Spix's macaw in the wild (declared extinct in the wild in 2019, though captive-bred birds have since been reintroduced). Conservation programs aim to prevent further losses through captive breeding, habitat protection, and predator eradication on islands.
