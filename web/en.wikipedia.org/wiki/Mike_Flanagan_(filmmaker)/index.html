Mike Flanagan (born May 20, 1978) is an American filmmaker best known for his work in the horror genre. He frequently writes, directs, and edits his own projects, and is noted for emotionally driven horror centred on grief, trauma, addiction, and family. His frequent collaborators include his wife, actress and writer Kate Siegel, and actors such as Henry Thomas, Carla Gugino, and Rahul Kohli.

Early life

Flanagan was born in Salem, Massachusetts, and grew up partly in Bowie, Maryland, where his father served in the United States Coast Guard. He studied at Towson University in Maryland, majoring in Electronic Media and Film with a minor in theatre, and worked in reality television and documentary production before turning to feature filmmaking.

Career

Flanagan's breakthrough came with the low-budget independent film Absentia (2011), which he expanded into the studio feature Oculus (2013). He followed it with the home-invasion thriller Hush (2016), co-written with Siegel, the Ouija prequel Ouija: Origin of Evil (2016), and the Stephen King adaptation Gerald's Game (2017).

For Netflix he created the limited series The Haunting of Hill House (2018), a loose adaptation of Shirley Jackson's novel, and its follow-up The Haunting of Bly Manor (2020). He directed Doctor Sleep (2019), the sequel to The Shining, and created the acclaimed limited series Midnight Mass (2021), the teen horror series The Midnight Club (2022), and The Fall of the House of Usher (2023), after Edgar Allan Poe. His film The Life of Chuck, adapted from a Stephen King novella, premiered in 2024 to strong reviews.

Style and themes

Flanagan's work is characterized by long takes, extended dialogue and monologues, parallel timelines, and supernatural threats that mirror psychological or emotional wounds. He typically edits his own films and works with a recurring ensemble of actors. Recovery from addiction is a recurring theme; Flanagan has spoken openly about his own sobriety.
