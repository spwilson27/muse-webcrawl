The Internet Governance Forum (IGF) is a multistakeholder governance group for policy dialogue on issues of Internet governance. It brings together all stakeholders in the Internet governance debate, whether they represent governments, the private sector or civil society, including the technical and academic community, on an equal basis and through an open and inclusive process. The establishment of the IGF was formally announced by the United Nations Secretary-General in July 2006, and it was first convened in October–November 2006 in Athens, Greece.

The IGF's annual meeting rotates among host countries on several continents.

Background

The IGF emerged from the World Summit on the Information Society (WSIS), held in Geneva (2003) and Tunis (2005). The Tunis Agenda called on the UN Secretary-General to convene a forum for multi-stakeholder policy dialogue, deliberately without decision-making or oversight powers. The UN General Assembly renewed the IGF's mandate for ten years in 2015 and again in connection with the WSIS+20 review.

Structure and meetings

The annual IGF meeting, held over several days, is organized with the help of a Multistakeholder Advisory Group (MAG) appointed by the UN Secretary-General. Preparatory work occurs in Best Practice Forums, Dynamic Coalitions, Policy Networks (on topics such as AI, environment and internet fragmentation), and more than 170 national and regional IGF initiatives (NRIs). Annual meetings have been held in Rio de Janeiro, Hyderabad, Sharm el-Sheikh, Vilnius, Nairobi, Baku, Bali, Istanbul, João Pessoa, Guadalajara, Geneva, Paris, Berlin, Katowice (online/hybrid), Addis Ababa, Kyoto, Riyadh, and Oslo/Lillestrøm.

Criticism

Because the IGF produces no binding outcomes, some governments and commentators have criticized it as a "talk shop", while supporters argue that its non-binding, open format enables dialogue impossible in treaty negotiations, and that its outputs inform policy elsewhere.
