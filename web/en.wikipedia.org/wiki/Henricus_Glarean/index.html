Henricus Glarean

Henricus Glarean (28 February or 3 June 1488 – 28 March 1563), born Heinrich Loriti in Mollis, canton of Glarus (from which his Latinised name derives), was a Swiss humanist, poet, geographer, and music theorist of the Renaissance.

Life

Glarean studied at Cologne, where he was crowned poet laureate by Emperor Maximilian I in 1512. He later taught in Basel, where he was close to Erasmus, and in Paris, before settling in Freiburg im Breisgau in 1529 after the Reformation reached Basel. He published editions of classical authors including Livy and Boethius, a geography (De Geographia, 1527), and a widely used description of Switzerland.

Music theory

His most famous work is the Dodecachordon (1547), one of the most influential music treatises of the sixteenth century. In it Glarean expanded the traditional eight church modes to twelve, adding the Aeolian and Ionian modes (corresponding to the natural minor and major scales) and their plagal forms — a reform that anticipated the major–minor system of later Western music.
