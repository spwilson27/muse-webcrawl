Overview

Chert is a hard, fine-grained sedimentary rock composed of microcrystalline or cryptocrystalline quartz, the mineral form of silicon dioxide (SiO2). The term also applies to occurrences of the material in limestone and dolomite. Flint is a variety of chert that occurs in chalk and marly limestone formations, usually dark in colour.

Formation

Chert forms where silica-rich waters precipitate quartz within sediments or replace carbonate rock. Much of its silica derives from the dissolved skeletons of marine organisms such as radiolarians, diatoms and sponges. Banded iron formations of Precambrian age contain great thicknesses of chert, and bedded cherts accumulate in deep marine environments.

Uses

Because it fractures with a sharp, glassy conchoidal fracture, chert was one of the most important tool stones of prehistory, knapped into arrowheads, spear points, scrapers and blades. In historical times flint from chert deposits was used in gunflints and as a building stone. Today chert is used as road aggregate, though its poor adhesion to bitumen and its tendency to polish limit some applications.
