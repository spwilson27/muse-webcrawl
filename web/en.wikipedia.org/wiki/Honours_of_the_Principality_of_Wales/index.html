Honours of the Principality of Wales

The Honours of the Principality of Wales are the regalia used at the investiture of Princes of Wales. The set comprises a coronet, a ring, a rod, a sword, a belt, and a mantle, and is analogous to the Honours of Scotland and the Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom. The current pieces date from the investiture of Prince Edward (later Edward VIII) at Caernarfon Castle in 1911, with a replacement coronet made for Prince Charles's investiture in 1969.

History

Welsh princes such as Llywelyn ap Gruffudd had their own regalia, but the original Welsh crown jewels were surrendered to the English Crown after the Edwardian conquest of Wales in the 1280s and are lost. For centuries, English heirs apparent were created Prince of Wales by letters patent with at most a coronet and ring. The modern investiture ceremony at Caernarfon was devised for 1911, largely by David Lloyd George, and a new set of honours was commissioned for it, incorporating Welsh gold.

The pieces

The 1911 coronet follows the statutory design for the heir apparent's coronet, with a single arch topped by a monde and cross. The 1969 coronet, designed by Louis Osman for Prince Charles, is strikingly modern: made of resin-set gold with a purple velvet cap, its arch carries a golden orb engraved with the Prince's insignia and studded with diamonds and emeralds—its futuristic appearance divided opinion at the time. The gold rod, ring set with an amethyst, sword, and belt complete the set.

Display and custody

The honours are part of the Royal Collection. After being displayed for many years at the National Museum Cardiff, the 1911 and 1969 regalia were moved in 2020 to a permanent display at Llanelly House and related venues, and the 1969 coronet and other pieces have since been exhibited in Wales. Prince William, created Prince of Wales in 2022, has not held a formal investiture ceremony.
