Taste of Cherry

Taste of Cherry
Persian title: Ta'm-e gīlās
Director: Abbas Kiarostami
Released: 1997
Award: Palme d'Or, Cannes Film Festival

Taste of Cherry (Persian: طعم گيلاس, Ta'm-e gīlās) is a 1997 Iranian minimalist drama film written, produced, edited and directed by Abbas Kiarostami. It tells the story of a middle-aged man, Mr. Badii, who drives through the outskirts of Tehran searching for someone willing to help him end his life by burying him after he takes a fatal overdose.

Plot

Badii offers a large sum to whoever will come to a grave he has dug in the hills at dawn and either help him out — if he survives — or cover him with earth if he is dead. He approaches a young Kurdish soldier and an Afghan seminary student, both of whom refuse, before an elderly Azerbaijani taxidermist agrees, while trying to persuade Badii to reconsider by describing the simple pleasures of life, including the taste of cherries. The film ends ambiguously, followed by a video-shot coda showing the film crew at work.

Production

The film was made in Kiarostami's characteristic style, using largely non-professional actors, long takes and conversations filmed almost entirely within and around Badii's Range Rover as it traverses dusty roads outside Tehran.

Reception

Taste of Cherry won the Palme d'Or at the 1997 Cannes Film Festival, shared with Shohei Imamura's The Eel. Although a few critics found it forbiddingly slow, it has since been widely ranked among the greatest films of the 1990s and of Iranian cinema.
