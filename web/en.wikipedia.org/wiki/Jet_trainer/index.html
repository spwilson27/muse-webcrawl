Jet trainer

A jet trainer is a jet aircraft used to train military pilots, forming the bridge between basic flying training (usually on turboprop aircraft) and operational fast-jet or large-aircraft flying. Trainers are typically two-seat aircraft with dual controls, docile handling and, in advanced models, simulated weapons and sensor systems.

Roles

Training syllabuses usually divide into basic jet training, advanced jet training and lead-in fighter training (LIFT). Early jet trainers such as the Lockheed T-33 — the most widely used of all, with more than 6,500 built — were derived from fighter designs. Later purpose-built trainers include the BAE Systems Hawk, the Aero L-39 Albatros, the Aermacchi/Alenia M-346 Master, the KAI T-50 Golden Eagle and the Boeing–Saab T-7 Red Hawk, selected to replace the long-serving Northrop T-38 Talon in US Air Force service.

Light attack variants

Many trainers are adaptable to a light attack or close air support role, and armed versions serve in numerous air forces. Display teams also favour trainers: the RAF Red Arrows fly the Hawk, and the US Navy's Blue Angels historically used training-capable aircraft types early in their history.
