Citroën DS

Citroën DS

Manufacturer: Citroën
Production: 1955–1975
Designer: Flaminio Bertoni; André Lefèbvre
Class: Executive car

The Citroën DS (French pronunciation: [si.tʁɔ.ɛn de.ɛs]) is a front mid-engined, front-wheel-drive executive car manufactured and marketed by the French company Citroën from 1955 to 1975, in sedan/saloon, wagon/estate, and convertible body configurations, across three series of one generation.

Marketed with a less expensive variant, the Citroën ID, the DS was known throughout its production for its aerodynamic, futuristic body design; unorthodox, quirky, and innovative technology; and set new standards in ride quality, handling, and braking. The DS won the Monte Carlo Rally in 1959 and 1966, the 1000 Lakes Rally in 1962, and the first World Rally Championship for Manufacturers in 1973. In 1999, the DS ranked third in the Car of the Century competition, behind the Ford Model T and the BMC Mini.

History

After 18 years of secret development as the successor to the Traction Avant, the DS 19 was introduced on 6 October 1955 at the Paris Motor Show. In the first 15 minutes of the show, 743 orders were taken, and orders for the first day totalled 12,000. During the 10 days of the show, the DS took in 80,000 deposits; this was a record that stood for over 60 years.

The DS placed third in the 1999 Car of the Century competition, and fifth in Automobile Magazine's 2005 list of “100 Coolest Cars”. It was also named the most beautiful car of all time by Classic & Sports Car magazine after a poll of 20 world-renowned car designers.

Technology

The DS featured a hydropneumatic self-levelling suspension system, which gave an exceptionally smooth ride and allowed the car to be raised or lowered for rough ground. Power steering, power brakes, and a semi-automatic transmission were driven by the same high-pressure hydraulic system. Later models (from 1967) added swivelling directional headlamps that turned with the steering. The car's structure used a steel platform chassis with fibreglass roof panels to lower the centre of gravity.

The name DS is a pun in French: pronounced déesse, it means 'goddess'. The cheaper ID model likewise punned on idée ('idea').

Motorsport

The DS achieved notable rally success, winning the Monte Carlo Rally in 1959 (ID 19) and 1966 (DS 21), and long-distance events such as the East African Safari in marathon class and the 1974 London–Sahara–Munich World Cup Rally.
