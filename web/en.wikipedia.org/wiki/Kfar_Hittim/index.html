Kfar Hittim

Kfar Hittim (Hebrew: כְּפַר חִטִּים) is a moshav in northern Israel, located near Tiberias and the Sea of Galilee. Founded in 1936, it is recognised as the first "Tower and Stockade" settlement and the first moshav shitufi in Mandatory Palestine. It falls under the jurisdiction of the Lower Galilee Regional Council.

History

The lands of Kfar Hittim were purchased by the Jewish National Fund in the early 1900s near the Arab village of Hittin, built on the site of the ancient village of Kfar Hittaya. Settlement attempts began in 1913–1914 under the sponsorship of the Odessa Committee and continued intermittently, hampered by water shortages and security problems. The village takes its name from its proximity to the Horns of Hattin, where Saladin defeated the Crusader army in the Battle of Hattin in 1187.

Tower and stockade

On the night of December 6–7, 1936, at the height of the Arab revolt, settlers re-established Kfar Hittim overnight, erecting a prefabricated watchtower and a surrounding stockade wall filled with gravel — the first application of the "Tower and Stockade" (Homa u-Migdal) method, which was subsequently used to found dozens of settlements across Palestine. The settlers organised as a moshav shitufi, an intermediate form between a kibbutz and a cooperative moshav, combining collective production with private households.

Economy and present day

The moshav's economy historically rested on agriculture — orchards, bananas, field crops and dairy — and it later developed rural tourism, aided by its location above the Sea of Galilee and near the Arbel cliffs and the Horns of Hattin. In the 2010s the village had a population of several hundred residents.
