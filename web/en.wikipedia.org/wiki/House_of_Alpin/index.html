House of Alpin

The House of Alpin, also known as the Alpínid dynasty or Clann Chinaeda, is the name given to the kin-group that ruled the Pictish kingdom and then the kingdom of Alba from the advent of Kenneth MacAlpin (Cináed mac Ailpín) in the 840s until the death of Malcolm II in 1034. The dynasty takes its name from Kenneth's father, Alpín mac Echdach.

Origins and rule

Kenneth MacAlpin is traditionally credited with uniting the Picts and the Scots of Dál Riata into the kingdom of Alba, although historians debate how sudden and complete this union was. The Alpínid kings ruled from centres such as Forteviot, Scone and Dunkeld, contending with Viking raids, internal succession disputes governed by tanistry-like customs, and pressure from the kings of Wessex and England to the south.

Kings

Kings of the house included Kenneth I, Donald I, Constantine I, Áed, Giric (whose membership is disputed), Constantine II, Malcolm I, Indulf, Dub, Cuilén, Kenneth II, Constantine III, Kenneth III and Malcolm II. Constantine II's long reign (900–943) is associated with the consolidation of Alba and the establishment of the Church of the Céli Dé at St Andrews. The dynasty ended when Malcolm II, having no surviving sons, was succeeded by his grandson through the female line, Duncan I of the House of Dunkeld.
