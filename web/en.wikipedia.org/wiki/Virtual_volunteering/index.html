Virtual volunteering

Virtual volunteering, also known as online volunteering or e-volunteering, refers to volunteer activities completed, in whole or in part, at a distance from the organisation supported, using the Internet and a computer, tablet or phone. Tasks that can be performed remotely include research, writing and editing, translation, graphic design, mentoring and tutoring, web development, social media management and data entry.

History

Online service has roots in the early days of networked computing, when volunteers provided technical support on bulletin boards and early online communities. The practice expanded in the 1990s with organisations such as Impact Online (later VolunteerMatch), founded in 1998, and the United Nations Volunteers programme's Online Volunteering service, launched in 2000, which connected development organisations with volunteers worldwide. The term "virtual volunteering" was popularised by the Virtual Volunteering Project at the University of Texas at Austin.

Micro-volunteering

A related form, micro-volunteering, involves small, bite-sized tasks that can be completed in minutes, often from a smartphone — for example tagging photographs, transcribing short texts, or contributing to citizen-science projects such as Galaxy Zoo. Such tasks lower the barrier to participation and are often crowdsourced.

Benefits and challenges

Virtual volunteering lets people contribute regardless of geography, mobility or schedule, and allows organisations to draw on a global skills pool. Challenges include volunteer retention and engagement at a distance, digital exclusion, supervision, and verifying work quality. The COVID-19 pandemic greatly accelerated adoption as in-person programmes were suspended.
