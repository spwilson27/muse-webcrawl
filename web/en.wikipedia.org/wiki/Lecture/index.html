Lecture

A lecture (from Latin lectura, "reading") is an oral presentation intended to present information or teach people about a particular subject, for example by a university or college teacher. Lectures are used to convey critical information, history, background, theories, and equations.

History

The lecture dates from the medieval university, where, books being scarce, a master literally read aloud (legere) from an authoritative text and commented on it. Despite the invention of printing and, later, digital media, the lecture has remained the most common teaching method in higher education, supplemented by seminars, tutorials, and laboratory work.

Criticism and alternatives

Research on learning has long questioned the effectiveness of passive lectures for retaining information and developing skills, and many institutions have adopted active learning, flipped classrooms, and problem-based learning. Defenders argue that a good lecture can model expert thinking, organise a field for novices, and inspire interest in a subject. Public lectures, such as the Royal Institution Christmas Lectures founded by Michael Faraday, also serve to communicate science to general audiences.

Etymology and related terms

Related words include lecturer, the person delivering the lecture (a formal academic rank in British universities), and lectern, the stand holding the lecturer's notes. A short, admonishing talk by a parent is also jokingly called a "lecture".
