Toulon (French pronunciation: [tu.lɔ̃]; Occitan: Tolon) is a city on the French Mediterranean coast and a large military harbour. It is the capital of the Var department in the Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region, and lies about 52 km east of Marseille.

The naval base

Toulon's natural harbour — one of the finest in Europe — is home to the French Navy's Mediterranean fleet, including the aircraft carrier Charles de Gaulle and the fleet of nuclear attack submarines. The arsenal at Toulon, developed from the reign of Henri IV and greatly expanded under Louis XIV by Vauban and Colbert, has been France's principal naval base for centuries.

History

Settled in antiquity as a dye-works port (Telo Martius, named for the sea goddess and known for its purple dye), Toulon was fortified in the Middle Ages and incorporated into France with Provence in 1481. Its most famous moments came in 1793, when royalists handed the port to a British-Spanish fleet; a young artillery officer, Napoleon Bonaparte, won his first fame by directing the republican guns that retook the heights dominating the harbour, forcing the allies to evacuate — burning much of the French fleet as they left.

On 27 November 1942, as German troops moved to seize the fleet after the Allied landings in North Africa, the French scuttled 77 warships at Toulon rather than surrender them. The city was liberated in August 1944 during Operation Dragoon, in bitter fighting.

Sport and culture

Toulon is famous for its rugby union club, RC Toulon, three-time European champions (2013–2015) playing at the Stade Mayol. The nearby Tour Royale and old town, the cable car to Mont Faron, and the Mourillon beaches are leading sights.
