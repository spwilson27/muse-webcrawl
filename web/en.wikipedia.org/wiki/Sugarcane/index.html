Sugarcane
Scientific nameSaccharum officinarum and hybrids
FamilyPoaceae (grasses)
OriginNew Guinea / Southeast Asia
Top producersBrazil, India, Thailand, China

Sugarcane is a group of tall, perennial grasses of the genus Saccharum grown in tropical and subtropical regions for their thick stalks, which are rich in sucrose. It is the world's largest crop by production volume and the source of roughly 70–80% of the world's sugar; it is also used to produce ethanol fuel, rum, molasses, and bagasse-based products.

History

Sugarcane was domesticated in New Guinea several thousand years ago and spread westward to India, where crystallised sugar was first produced, and then to the Middle East and Mediterranean. European colonisation carried it to the Caribbean and the Americas, where it became the basis of plantation economies built on enslaved labour — a history central to the Atlantic slave trade.

Cultivation and processing

Cane is propagated vegetatively from stem cuttings (setts) and harvested by hand or machine after 12–24 months. At the mill, stalks are crushed to extract juice, which is clarified, evaporated, and crystallised into raw sugar. The fibrous residue, bagasse, is commonly burned to power the mill; in Brazil, a large share of the harvest is fermented into ethanol for fuel.
