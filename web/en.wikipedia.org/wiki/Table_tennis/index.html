Table tennis, also known as ping-pong, is a racket sport derived from lawn tennis, in which two or four players hit a lightweight ball back and forth across a table using small rackets. Play is fast and demands exceptional reflexes and spin control; games are played to 11 points, matches typically best of five or seven.

The game began in Victorian England as an after-dinner parlour pastime improvised with books and a cigar-box lid. The International Table Tennis Federation was founded in 1926, and the first World Championships were held in London that year. China has dominated the sport since the 1960s and overwhelmingly since the 1990s: Ma Long, a two-time Olympic singles champion and three-time world champion, is considered its greatest player. Table tennis also holds a famous place in diplomacy — the 1971 "ping-pong diplomacy" exchanges helped open US–China relations.
