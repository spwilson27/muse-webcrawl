The Penn State Nittany Lions football team represents Pennsylvania State University in college football at the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision level. The programme began in 1887 and has played its home games at Beaver Stadium in University Park, Pennsylvania, since 1960; with a capacity of over 106,000, it is among the largest stadiums in the world.

History

Penn State competed as an independent for most of its history before joining the Big Ten Conference in 1993. The programme's defining era came under head coach Joe Paterno, who led the team from 1966 to 2011 and won national championships in the 1982 and 1986 seasons, the latter capped by a victory over Miami in the 1987 Fiesta Bowl. Paterno became the winningest coach in major college football history, though many of his wins were vacated by the NCAA in 2012 following the child sex abuse scandal involving former assistant Jerry Sandusky; the wins were later restored in 2015 under a settlement.

Penn State is known for its plain uniforms — blue and white with no names on the jerseys — and for the "White Out" games in which the entire crowd wears white. The Nittany Lions have produced numerous NFL players and Heisman Trophy winner John Cappelletti (1973). The Lions' traditional rivals include Ohio State, Michigan, and Pittsburgh.
