Wuxi (Chinese: 无锡; pinyin: Wúxī) is a prefecture-level city in southern Jiangsu province, China. It lies on the northern shore of Lake Tai, about 135 kilometres northwest of Shanghai, in the Yangtze River Delta. One of the birthplaces of China's modern industry and commerce, Wuxi is today a major manufacturing and technology centre and a popular tourist destination.

History

Wuxi has a history of more than 3,000 years, tracing its origins to the state of Wu in the Zhou dynasty. Its name is traditionally said to mean "without tin", referring to the exhaustion of tin deposits at nearby Tin Hill (Xishan) during the Han dynasty. The Grand Canal passes through the city, historically making it a hub for the rice and textile trades. In the early twentieth century Wuxi became one of China's most important industrial cities, known for silk, flour and textile mills founded by local entrepreneurs.

Economy

Wuxi's economy is based on manufacturing, including textiles, electronics, precision machinery, solar photovoltaics and, increasingly, the Internet of Things — the city hosts the World Internet of Things Exposition. Its GDP per capita is among the highest of Chinese cities.

Tourism

Attractions include the Lingshan Grand Buddha, an 88-metre bronze statue of the Buddha completed in 1996; the classical gardens Jichang and Liyuan; Turtle Head Isle (Yuantouzhu) on Lake Tai, famous for cherry blossoms; and the film studios of CCTV Wuxi Film and Television Base. Local specialities include Wuxi spare ribs and soup dumplings (xiaolongbao).

Transport

The city is served by Sunan Shuofang International Airport, the Shanghai–Nanjing intercity railway and the Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway, as well as a growing metro system that opened in 2014.
