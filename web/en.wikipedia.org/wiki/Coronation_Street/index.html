Coronation Street (colloquially known as Corrie) is a British television soap opera created by Tony Warren and produced by Granada Television (now ITV Studios) for ITV. First broadcast on 9 December 1960, it centres on a cobbled terraced street in the fictional town of Weatherfield, modelled on Salford in Greater Manchester, and on the working-class residents who live there and gather at the Rovers Return pub. Guinness World Records recognised it in 2010 as the world's longest-running television soap opera still in production.

History

Warren, a young Granada scriptwriter, drew on his Salford childhood for the series, originally titled Florizel Street before being renamed. Commissioned initially for only 13 episodes, it quickly became a national institution despite early critical snobbery, with early stars including Violet Carson as the formidable Ena Sharples, Doris Hare as Minnie Caldwell and Margot Bryant as Martha Longhurst. Ken Barlow, played by William Roache since the first episode, holds the record as the longest-serving actor in a soap opera; Roache is the only remaining original cast member.

Setting and production

The programme is filmed in Manchester. An outdoor set of the street was built at Granada Studios in 1982 and expanded to include Rosamund Street and Viaduct Street; in 2014 production moved to a larger purpose-built set at MediaCityUK in Trafford, which has since been extended with a Victoria Street precinct. Episodes are typically broadcast in half-hour instalments several times a week, and landmark storylines — from the 1986 Rovers fire to the 2010 tram crash, broadcast live for the 50th anniversary — have drawn audiences in the tens of millions, with peak ratings historically exceeding 20 million viewers.

Characters and themes

The serial is known for its blend of kitchen-sink realism and dry northern humour. Long-running families include the Barlows, the Platts, the Websters and the Connors; iconic figures such as Hilda Ogden, Bet Lynch, Deirdre Barlow and Roy Cropper have become fixtures of British popular culture. Storylines have addressed issues including domestic abuse, mental illness, sexuality and miscarriage of justice — most famously the "Free the Weatherfield One" campaign for the wrongly imprisoned Deirdre Rachid in 1998, which was debated in Parliament.

Cultural impact

Coronation Street has won numerous British Soap Awards and BAFTAs, been exported around the world (with an especially devoted Canadian following), and inspired the enduring public tour of its former set. It remains one of ITV's flagship programmes and a benchmark against which British continuing drama is measured.
