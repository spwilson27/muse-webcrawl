Cantar de gesta

A cantar de gesta is a medieval Spanish epic poem, sung or recited by minstrels (juglares), celebrating the deeds (gesta, from Latin, "deeds") of a hero. The term corresponds to the French chanson de geste. These poems form the earliest substantial body of literature in Castilian Spanish.

Characteristics

The cantares are composed in long, irregular lines of roughly fourteen to sixteen syllables, divided into hemistichs, with assonant rhyme in long stanzas (tiradas) of varying length. They are anonymous, realistic in tone compared with French epic, and preoccupied with frontier warfare between Christians and Muslims, feudal honour, exile and loyalty.

Surviving works

Very few cantares survive complete, because the tradition was oral and manuscripts were little valued. The supreme example is the Cantar de mio Cid (c. 1200), recounting the exile and campaigns of Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar, preserved in a single manuscript signed by Per Abbat. Others survive fragmentarily or in prose versions embedded in chronicles: the Cantar de los Siete Infantes de Lara, the Mocedades de Rodrigo (about the young Cid), and the lost poems on Fernán González and Bernardo del Carpio, reconstructed from chronicle paraphrases.

Legacy

Episodes from the cantares fed the later Spanish ballad tradition (the romancero) and Golden Age theatre. The scholar Ramón Menéndez Pidal argued for a rich lost epic tradition behind the chronicles, an influential view that shaped modern study of Spanish epic.
