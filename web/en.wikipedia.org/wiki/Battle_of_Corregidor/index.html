Battle of Corregidor
Date5–6 May 1942 (first battle); 16–26 February 1945 (recapture)
LocationCorregidor Island, Philippines
ResultJapanese victory (1942); American victory (1945)

The Battle of Corregidor was the assault by Imperial Japanese forces on the fortified island of Corregidor in Manila Bay, the last American-Filipino stronghold in the Philippines, culminating in its surrender on 6 May 1942. The name is also applied to the American recapture of the island in February 1945. Corregidor, nicknamed "the Rock", guarded the entrance to Manila Bay with heavy coastal artillery, a network of tunnels centred on Malinta Tunnel, and a garrison of some 11,000 men.

The 1942 siege

After the fall of Bataan on 9 April 1942, Japanese artillery on the Bataan shore and aircraft pounded Corregidor for weeks; by early May the island's defences were shattered, water and ammunition were short, and casualties mounted. On the night of 5–6 May, Japanese troops of the 4th Division landed on the island's tail and fought their way toward Malinta Hill. Fearing a massacre in the crowded tunnels, where thousands of wounded lay, Lieutenant General Jonathan Wainwright surrendered at noon on 6 May. The capitulation covered all US forces in the Philippines; the prisoners faced years of harsh captivity.

The 1945 recapture

Returning in February 1945, American forces combined a parachute drop by the 503rd Parachute Regimental Combat Team onto the island's "Topside" with an amphibious landing by the 24th Infantry Division at "Bottomside" on 16 February. Fighting over the cratered island was fierce; the Japanese garrison of about 6,700 was almost annihilated, many dying in a huge explosion when they detonated themselves inside Malinta Tunnel on 23 February. The island was declared secure on 26 February, and General MacArthur formally returned to the Rock on 2 March 1945.

Legacy

Corregidor is preserved as a national shrine of the Philippines, with the Pacific War Memorial, the ruined barracks and batteries, and Malinta Tunnel open to visitors.
