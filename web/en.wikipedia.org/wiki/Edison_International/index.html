Edison International is a publicly traded electric utility holding company headquartered in Rosemead, California. Through its principal subsidiary, Southern California Edison (SCE), it generates, transmits and distributes electricity across a service territory of about 50,000 square miles in central, coastal and southern California, serving roughly 15 million people in some 5 million customer accounts. It is one of the largest electric utilities in the United States.

History

The company's roots go back to the Holt & Knupps lighting partnership of 1886 in Visalia and to the Southern California Edison Company incorporated in 1909 through the consolidation of regional utilities built around Los Angeles's growth, hydroelectric plants in the Sierra Nevada and early long-distance transmission lines. The modern holding company took the name Edison International in 1988. Its unregulated generating arm, Edison Mission Energy, which owned coal and gas plants in the Midwest and elsewhere, filed for bankruptcy in 2012 amid low power prices, and its assets were subsequently sold — a restructuring that refocused Edison International on its regulated California utility.

Operations and issues

SCE operates major transmission and distribution networks, hydroelectric facilities, and a share of the Palo Verde nuclear plant in Arizona; its San Onofre nuclear station was permanently retired in 2013 and is being decommissioned. The company has faced major scrutiny and liabilities connected with California wildfires ignited by electrical equipment, including the 2017 Thomas Fire and the 2018 Woolsey Fire, and has invested heavily in grid hardening, covered conductors, vegetation management and public-safety power shutoffs. Edison International also pursues California's clean-energy targets through large-scale procurement of solar, wind, geothermal and battery storage, and through its Edison Energy advisory business for large energy users.
