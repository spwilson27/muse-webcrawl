Seborrhoeic dermatitis

Seborrhoeic dermatitis is a common, chronic inflammatory skin condition that mainly affects areas rich in sebaceous (oil) glands, such as the scalp, face and upper trunk. It causes flaky, yellowish, greasy scales over reddened skin and itching. On the scalp, its mildest form is dandruff; in infants it commonly appears as cradle cap. The condition is not contagious and is not caused by poor hygiene.

Signs and symptoms

Lesions are typically symmetrical and favour the scalp, eyebrows, eyelid margins, nasolabial folds, behind the ears, the presternal chest and the back. Symptoms wax and wane, often worsening in winter, during stress, and in some illnesses. The condition is considerably more common and more severe in people with HIV/AIDS and in neurological diseases such as Parkinson's disease.

Causes

The exact cause is incompletely understood, but the condition is associated with an abnormal inflammatory response to lipophilic yeasts of the genus Malassezia, which are normal inhabitants of human skin. Sebum production, individual susceptibility, immune status and environmental factors all play roles.

Treatment

There is no permanent cure, but symptoms can be controlled. First-line treatments include antifungal agents such as ketoconazole shampoo or cream, selenium sulfide and zinc pyrithione; short courses of topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors (pimecrolimus, tacrolimus) for inflammation; and keratolytics such as salicylic acid for thick scale. Maintenance therapy is often needed because relapse is common.

Epidemiology

Seborrhoeic dermatitis affects roughly 1–5% of the general population, with peaks in infancy, adolescence and later middle age, and is more common in males.
