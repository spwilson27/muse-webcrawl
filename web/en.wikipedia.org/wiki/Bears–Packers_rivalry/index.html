The Bears–Packers rivalry is a National Football League (NFL) rivalry between the Chicago Bears and the Green Bay Packers. It is the oldest and most-played rivalry in the NFL, dating to 1921, the league's second season of play. The two franchises are separated by roughly 200 miles (320 km) along the western shore of Lake Michigan, and their fan bases in Illinois and Wisconsin have clashed for more than a century.

The rivalry is notable for the sustained success of both franchises: the Bears and Packers rank among the winningest teams in league history, and their combined championship totals include numerous NFL titles and Super Bowl victories — the Packers won Super Bowl I and II under Vince Lombardi, while the Bears won Super Bowl XX under Mike Ditka.

History

Early years (1921–1959)

The teams first met on November 27, 1921, when the Chicago Staleys (renamed the Bears the following year) defeated Green Bay 20–0. The early decades were marked by intense, sometimes violent, competition. In 1924 a game produced the first documented player ejections for fighting in NFL history. George Halas, the Bears' founder and longtime coach, and Curly Lambeau, his Packers counterpart, built the rivalry's foundation. Chicago dominated much of the 1930s and 1940s behind players such as Sid Luckman, while Green Bay fielded stars including Johnny "Blood" McNally and Don Hutson.

Lombardi and Ditka eras

Under Vince Lombardi in the 1960s, the Packers won five NFL championships in seven years, including the first two Super Bowls, and generally held the upper hand. The Bears' lone title of the era came in 1963. In the 1980s, Mike Ditka — a former Bears tight end — coached Chicago to its Super Bowl XX triumph, and the Bears enjoyed a run of dominance in the rivalry during that decade.

Favre and Rodgers eras

The arrival of quarterback Brett Favre in Green Bay in 1992 began a long stretch of Packers ascendancy. Favre, and later Aaron Rodgers, led Green Bay to consistent success, and the Packers gradually overtook the Bears in the all-time series standings during the 2010s — a notable reversal, since Chicago had led the series for most of its history. The teams met in the postseason for only the second time in the 2010 NFC Championship Game, which Green Bay won 21–14 at Soldier Field en route to winning Super Bowl XLV.

Notable games

Memorable meetings include the 1980 "blocked field goal" game, the 1985 regular-season contests during the Bears' Super Bowl run — including the game in which defensive lineman William "The Refrigerator" Perry scored a rushing touchdown — the 1986 game marred by Charles Martin's late hit on Bears quarterback Jim McMahon, and the 2010 NFC Championship Game.
