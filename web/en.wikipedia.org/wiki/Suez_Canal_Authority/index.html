Suez Canal Authority

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Abbreviation: SCA
Formation: 26 July 1956
Type: Egyptian state authority
Headquarters: Ismailia, Egypt
Role: Owns, operates, and maintains the Suez Canal

The Suez Canal Authority (SCA) is an Egyptian state-owned authority which owns, operates, and maintains the Suez Canal. It was established on 26 July 1956 by President Gamal Abdel Nasser, when he nationalised the Suez Canal Company, the Franco-British firm that had operated the canal since its opening in 1869 — an act that precipitated the Suez Crisis later that year.

Functions

The Authority manages navigation through the canal, sets and collects transit tolls, maintains and deepens the channel, operates pilotage and signalling services, and runs associated shipyards and companies. It regulates traffic in the canal's convoy system and issues the rules of navigation with which transiting vessels must comply.

Development projects

Under the SCA, the canal has been repeatedly widened and deepened, and bypasses added at the Great Bitter Lake and elsewhere. In 2015 Egypt inaugurated the "New Suez Canal", a parallel channel along part of the route intended to allow two-way traffic and reduce waiting times. Canal revenues, collected largely in hard currency, are a major source of income for the Egyptian economy; transit receipts reached record levels of around ten billion US dollars annually in the early 2020s before falling amid Red Sea shipping disruptions.

Incidents

The Authority co-ordinates responses to groundings and blockages, most famously the March 2021 obstruction by the container ship Ever Given, which halted traffic for six days and led Egypt to claim compensation for the salvage operation and lost revenue.
