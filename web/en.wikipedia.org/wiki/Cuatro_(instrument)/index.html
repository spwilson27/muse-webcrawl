Cuatro (instrument)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The cuatro is a family of Latin American stringed instruments of the guitar family, named for their four courses (or strings) — cuatro means "four" in Spanish. The best-known forms are the Puerto Rican cuatro, the national instrument of Puerto Rico, and the Venezuelan cuatro, central to the folk music of Venezuela and Colombia.

Puerto Rican cuatro

The modern Puerto Rican cuatro has ten strings in five courses, tuned B–E–A–D–G, with the lowest two courses using octave pairs. It evolved from earlier four- and six-string forms and from the Spanish vihuela and tiple traditions. Built with a distinctive figure-eight body, sometimes with a pointed lower bout, it is the lead melodic instrument in música jíbara, the traditional music of the Puerto Rican countryside, and appears in ensembles with the guitar and güiro at Christmas parrandas.

Venezuelan cuatro

The Venezuelan cuatro is a small four-string instrument, roughly ukulele-sized, with nylon strings and a characteristic "re-entrant" tuning (A–D–F♯–B, with the first string tuned an octave lower than expected). It provides the rhythmic and harmonic foundation of joropo and other Venezuelan genres, typically played with rapid strumming patterns. A similar instrument, also called cuatro, is played in Colombia.
