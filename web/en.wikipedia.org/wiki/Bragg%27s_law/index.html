Bragg's law

[Image placeholder: Bragg's law]

In physics, Bragg's law (also the Bragg–Wulff condition) gives the angles at which coherent scattering of waves from a crystal lattice produces constructive interference. It was proposed by William Henry Bragg and William Lawrence Bragg in 1913 in connection with X-ray diffraction.

Formulation

When waves of wavelength λ strike parallel lattice planes separated by distance d at angle θ, constructive interference occurs when the path difference is an integer number of wavelengths: nλ = 2d sin θ. Bragg diffraction underlies X-ray crystallography, which has been used to determine the structures of crystals, proteins and DNA, and Bragg and his father shared the 1915 Nobel Prize in Physics for their work.
