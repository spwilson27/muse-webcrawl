William Pitt the Younger (28 May 1759 – 23 January 1806) was a British statesman who served as Prime Minister from 1783 to 1801 and again from 1804 until his death. Taking office at the age of 24, he remains the youngest person ever to become British prime minister. The son of William Pitt the Elder, he dominated British politics for a generation, leading the country through the French Revolutionary Wars and the early Napoleonic Wars.

Early life

Pitt was educated at home, largely by his father, and entered Pembroke College, Cambridge, at the age of fourteen. He was called to the bar in 1780 and entered Parliament in 1781 as member for Appleby. A brilliant debater, he became Chancellor of the Exchequer in 1782 under Lord Shelburne.

First ministry

In December 1783 King George III appointed Pitt prime minister after the fall of the Fox–North coalition. Pitt reformed public finances, introduced a sinking fund to reduce the national debt, negotiated the Eden Treaty with France, and passed the India Act of 1784 to tighten control of the East India Company. He attempted, but failed, to carry parliamentary reform and Catholic emancipation. After war broke out with revolutionary France in 1793, he built successive coalitions against France and suppressed radical movements at home. He resigned in 1801 over the king's refusal to grant Catholic emancipation following the Union with Ireland.

Second ministry and death

Pitt returned to office in 1804 as Britain faced invasion by Napoleon. He formed the Third Coalition against France, which won the naval triumph of Trafalgar in October 1805 but was shattered at Austerlitz that December. Worn out by overwork and ill health, Pitt died in January 1806, aged 46. His last words are reputedly, "Oh, my country! How I leave my country!"

Legacy

Pitt is remembered as one of Britain's greatest wartime leaders and finance ministers; he founded the modern Conservative tradition within the Tory party, and his administration laid foundations for the eventual defeat of Napoleonic France under his successors.
