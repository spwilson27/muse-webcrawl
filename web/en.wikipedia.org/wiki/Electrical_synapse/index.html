Electrical synapse

Electrical synapse
StructureGap junction
Key proteinsConnexins (vertebrates); innexins (invertebrates)

An electrical synapse is a mechanical and electrically conductive synapse, a functional junction between two neighboring neurons. The synapse forms a gap junction at a narrow (about 3.5 nm) gap between the pre- and postsynaptic neurons, much smaller than the roughly 20–40 nm distance of a chemical synapse. At electrical synapses, the neurons are physically connected by channel proteins called connexons, which allow ions and small molecules to pass directly from one cell to the next.

Structure

Each gap junction contains numerous channels that cross the membranes of both cells. Each channel is composed of two connexons (hemichannels), one in each cell's membrane, and each connexon is made of six connexin protein subunits. In invertebrates, the equivalent channels are formed by innexins. The pore of the channel is large enough to permit ions and even small signalling molecules such as ATP, cyclic AMP and inositol trisphosphate to pass between cells.

Transmission

Because current flows directly through the channels, transmission at electrical synapses is extremely fast and is usually bidirectional, although some junctions rectify, passing current more easily in one direction. Unlike chemical synapses, there is no neurotransmitter release and essentially no synaptic delay. The postsynaptic response is typically smaller than the presynaptic signal, since much of the current leaks away or is shunted.

Occurrence and function

Electrical synapses are found throughout the nervous systems of most animals, including the retina, the thalamus, the inferior olive and many brainstem and cortical interneuron networks. They are especially valuable where fast, synchronized activity of a population of neurons is required — for example, in escape circuits of crayfish and fish, and in the generation of rhythmic oscillations. In the developing brain, electrical coupling is widespread and helps coordinate early network activity before chemical synapses mature.

Comparison with chemical synapses

Compared with chemical synapses, electrical synapses are faster but lack gain: the postsynaptic signal cannot exceed the presynaptic one, and transmission cannot be as flexibly modified. However, gap junctions are themselves regulated — by voltage, pH, calcium concentration and phosphorylation — so electrical synapses are more plastic than once assumed.
