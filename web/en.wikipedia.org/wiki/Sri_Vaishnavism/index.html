Sri Vaishnavism

The Sri Ranganathaswamy Temple, Srirangam, principal seat of the Sri Vaishnava tradition.

Sri Vaishnavism (also transliterated Shri Vaishnavism) is a denomination within the Vaishnavism tradition of Hinduism. The name refers to goddess Lakshmi (Sri), who is regarded as an inseparable mediatrix alongside Vishnu (Narayana). The tradition traces its roots to the devotional hymns of the Alvars, the Tamil poet-saints of the first millennium CE, and was systematised by theologians including Nathamuni, Yamunacharya, and above all Ramanuja (1017–1137), whose Vishishtadvaita ("qualified non-dualism") philosophy became the school's doctrinal foundation.

Doctrine

Sri Vaishnavism holds that Narayana, eternally accompanied by Sri, is the supreme reality, and that individual selves and the material world are real and form the "body" of God, inseparable from him though distinct. Salvation (moksha) is attained through devotion (bhakti) matured into complete self-surrender (prapatti) to God's grace. The tradition venerates the Sanskrit Vedas together with the Tamil Divya Prabandham hymns of the Alvars, treating both as revealed scripture — the "dual Vedanta" (Ubhaya Vedanta).

Sub-schools

After Ramanuja, the community gradually divided into two sub-schools: the Vadakalai ("northern") school, centred at Kanchipuram and emphasising Sanskrit texts and active cooperation with grace, and the Tenkalai ("southern") school, centred at Srirangam and emphasising the Tamil hymns and the sufficiency of unmerited grace. The two differ in some rituals, temple customs, and the form of the Urdhva Pundra forehead mark, and their disputes have historically been adjudicated in temple litigation.

Practices

Devotees receive initiation (pancha-samskara) with the conch and discus marks of Vishnu, wear the white clay Urdhva Pundra, and centre worship on temple icons regarded as God's accessible forms (archa). Major centres include Srirangam, Tirupati, Kanchipuram, and Melkote.
