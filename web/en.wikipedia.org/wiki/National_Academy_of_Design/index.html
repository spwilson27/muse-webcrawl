National Academy of Design

The National Academy of Design is an honorary association of American artists, founded in New York City in 1825 by Samuel Morse, Asher Durand, Thomas Cole and others. Modelled on London's Royal Academy, it was created after artists' grievances with the older American Academy of the Fine Arts, and it combines three functions: an honorary membership society of artists (National Academicians), a museum, and a school of fine arts.

History

The Academy's founders seceded from the American Academy in protest against its control by non-artist patrons. It established its own teaching programme, ran annual exhibitions that became central events in American art life, and required each elected Academician to donate a representative work — initially including a self-portrait — building an important collection of American art that now numbers thousands of works, particularly strong in 19th-century painting such as the Hudson River School.

Today

Membership is capped and held by leading American artists and architects. The Academy has occupied several Manhattan homes, including a long stay on Fifth Avenue at 89th Street; in recent years it reorganised, selling historic property and relocating its programmes while maintaining its museum collection and annual exhibitions.
