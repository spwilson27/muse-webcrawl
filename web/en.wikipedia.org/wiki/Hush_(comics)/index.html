Hush (comics)

PublisherDC Comics
Publication dateOctober 2002 – September 2003
WriterJeph Loeb
ArtistJim Lee (pencils), Scott Williams (inks), Alex Sinclair (colors)

"Hush" is a 2002–2003 comic book storyline published by DC Comics in the monthly series Batman, running from issue #608 to #619. Written by Jeph Loeb with art by Jim Lee, Scott Williams, and Alex Sinclair, it follows Batman as a mysterious bandaged adversary — the eponymous Hush — manipulates much of his rogues' gallery and his allies against him. The story was a major commercial success, helped relaunch Lee's popularity at DC, and has been reprinted in numerous collected editions, including an oversized Absolute Edition.

Plot

Batman is drawn into a sprawling conspiracy: Poison Ivy controls Superman, Catwoman becomes both ally and love interest, and Killer Croc, Harley Quinn, the Joker, Ra's al Ghul, and the Scarecrow all play roles orchestrated from the shadows. The mastermind is ultimately revealed to be Thomas Elliot, Bruce Wayne's childhood friend, a brilliant surgeon who had resented the Wayne family since boyhood — Thomas's father had survived a car crash thanks to Dr. Thomas Wayne's intervention, thwarting young Elliot's plan to inherit early. Elliot, his face wrapped in bandages, adopts the name Hush and works in league with the Riddler, who had deduced Batman's secret identity and used a Lazarus Pit cure for his cancer, setting the plot in motion.

Reception and legacy

"Hush" was praised chiefly for Jim Lee's artwork and its showcase of Batman's extended cast, though some critics found the mystery's resolution convoluted. The storyline introduced a fan-favourite romantic development between Batman and Catwoman and led to follow-ups including Batman: Hush Returns and Paul Dini's Heart of Hush, which deepened Elliot's backstory. An animated film adaptation, Batman: Hush, was released in 2019 as part of the DC Animated Movie Universe, with a significantly altered ending regarding the mastermind's identity.
