Netlist

In electronic design, a netlist is a description of the connectivity of an electronic circuit. In its simplest form, it consists of a list of the electronic components in a circuit and a list of the nodes they are connected to. A network (net) is a collection of two or more interconnected components.

A netlist describes the components of a circuit and how they connect.

Types

Netlists can be structural, describing a circuit in terms of component instances and their interconnections, or behavioral, describing what a circuit does rather than how it is built. Netlists are typically classified by abstraction level: a transistor-level (or device-level) netlist lists individual transistors and passive components, as used by SPICE simulators, while a gate-level netlist is made of logic gates and is the typical output of logic synthesis.

Example

* Example SPICE netlist
Vcc 1 0 DC 5
R1 1 2 1k
Q1 3 2 0 QN2222
C1 3 0 10n
.model QN2222 NPN
.end

Uses

Netlists are the interchange format between schematic capture, simulation, and layout tools in electronic design automation (EDA). They are used for circuit simulation (e.g., SPICE netlists), for printed circuit board layout, where the netlist defines which component pins must be wired together, and for layout-versus-schematic (LVS) verification in integrated circuit design. Common formats include SPICE, EDIF, and various vendor-specific text formats; gate-level netlists are commonly written in Verilog or VHDL.
