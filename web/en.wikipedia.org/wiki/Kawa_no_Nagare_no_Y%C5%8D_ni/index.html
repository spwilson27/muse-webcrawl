"Kawa no Nagare no Yō ni" (川の流れのように, "Like the Flow of the River") is a Japanese song recorded by enka singer Hibari Misora, released in 1989 shortly before her death. With lyrics by Yasushi Akimoto and music by Akira Mitake, it was the final single of Misora's career and became her signature song, frequently described as one of the greatest Japanese songs of all time.

Background

Misora, known as the "Queen of Enka", recorded the song while in declining health. Its lyric — reflecting on a life lived like a river flowing gently toward the sea — was widely read as her farewell. She died in June 1989, aged 52, and the song anchored her posthumous reputation.

Legacy

In a national NHK poll conducted in 1997, "Kawa no Nagare no Yō ni" was voted the greatest Japanese song of all time. It is a staple of karaoke and ceremonial occasions, has been covered by many artists in Japan and abroad — including by The Three Tenors and by Teresa Teng in Chinese versions — and is heard daily as the departure melody at railway stations in Japan. A monument to the song stands in Misora's memory.
