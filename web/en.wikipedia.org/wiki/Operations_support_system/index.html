Operations support system

Operations support systems (OSS), also referred to as operational support systems in British usage, are computer systems used by telecommunications service providers to manage their networks. They support management functions such as network inventory, service provisioning, network configuration and fault management.

Together with business support systems (BSS), OSS are used to support various end-to-end telecommunications services. BSS deals with customers and products — order taking, billing, revenue management — while OSS deals with the network and service delivery itself. The two are often referred to jointly as OSS/BSS.

Functions

  • Service provisioning and activation: configuring network elements to deliver services ordered by customers.
  • Fault management: monitoring alarms, detecting and diagnosing network failures.
  • Performance management: collecting and analysing network performance data.
  • Inventory management: tracking network resources, equipment and capacity.
  • Network planning: supporting design and expansion of the network.

Frameworks

The TM Forum's Frameworx suite, including the Business Process Framework (eTOM) and the Information Framework (SID), provides widely used reference models for OSS/BSS architecture. The ITU-T's Telecommunications Management Network (TMN) model was an earlier influential framework.
