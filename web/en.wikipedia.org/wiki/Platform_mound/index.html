Platform mound


Monks Mound at Cahokia, Illinois

A platform mound is any earthwork or mound intended to support a structure or activity. Based on interpretation of the various uses of the platform mounds, the platform mounds are generally believed to have been used for various ritual and secular functions. Many Indigenous cultures of the Americas built platform mounds, including the Mississippian culture of the southeastern and midwestern United States and the civilizations of Mesoamerica, where they supported temples and are often called pyramids.

Mississippian platform mounds

In the Mississippian world (c. 800–1600 CE), flat-topped mounds supported temples, council houses, charnel houses, and the residences of chiefs. They were built in stages, often with each new chief adding a layer of earth. The largest, Monks Mound at Cahokia in Illinois, rises about 30 metres and is the largest prehistoric earthwork in the Americas north of Mexico. Other notable sites include Etowah and Moundville.

Mesoamerica

In Mesoamerica, stepped pyramidal platforms of stone supported temples dedicated to deities; famous examples include the pyramids of Teotihuacan, the Maya temples of Tikal and Palenque, and the Mexica Templo Mayor in Tenochtitlan. Ritual activity, including sacrifice and astronomical observation, was centred on the structures atop these platforms.
