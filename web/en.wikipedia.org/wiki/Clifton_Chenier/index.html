Clifton Chenier (25 June 1925 – 12 December 1987) was an American musician known as the "King of Zydeco." A Louisiana Creole singer and accordionist, he fused traditional Creole la-la music with blues, rhythm and blues, and rock and roll to create the modern sound of zydeco.

Career

Born into a sharecropping family near Opelousas, Louisiana, Chenier learned the accordion from his father. He began recording in 1954 and scored a regional hit with "Ay-Tete Fi" in 1955. His career took off in the 1960s with recordings for Chris Strachwitz's Arhoolie Records, including the album Bon Ton Roulet. Fronting the Red Hot Louisiana Band, with his brother Cleveland on washboard (frottoir), Chenier toured relentlessly, often wearing a crown on stage.

In 1984 he won a Grammy Award for Best Ethnic or Traditional Folk Recording for I'm Here. He was inducted posthumously into the Blues Hall of Fame and the Louisiana Music Hall of Fame, and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2014. His son C.J. Chenier continues to lead the Red Hot Louisiana Band.
