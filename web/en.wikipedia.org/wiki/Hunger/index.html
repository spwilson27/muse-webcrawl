Hunger

Hunger is a sensation of unease or pain caused by lack of food, and in social science it denotes the condition of not having enough food to eat. The Food and Agriculture Organization defines hunger as food deprivation, or undernourishment, in which dietary energy intake falls below the minimum needed for a healthy, active life.

Physiology

The sensation of hunger is regulated by hormones including ghrelin, which stimulates appetite, and leptin, which signals satiety, acting on the hypothalamus. Short-term hunger pangs arise partly from contractions of the empty stomach; prolonged deprivation leads to malnutrition, wasting and, in extreme form, starvation.

Global hunger

The UN's State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World reports estimate that roughly 700–800 million people — about one in eleven of the world's population — faced chronic hunger in recent years, concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. Causes include poverty, conflict, climate extremes and food-price shocks. The Sustainable Development Goal 2 ("Zero Hunger") targets ending hunger by 2030, and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) is used to declare famines.
