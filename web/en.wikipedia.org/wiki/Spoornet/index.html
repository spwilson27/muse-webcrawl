Spoornet

Spoornet was the largest division of Transnet, the state-owned South African transport company, responsible for operating the country's freight and passenger railway network. In 2009 it was renamed Transnet Freight Rail.

History

Railways in South Africa developed from colonial-era lines built to serve the ports of Cape Town and Durban and the diamond and gold fields of Kimberley and the Witwatersrand. The network was unified under South African Railways, later part of the transport parastatal that became Transnet in 1990. Spoornet — from the Afrikaans spoorweg, meaning railway — became the rail operating division.

Operations

Spoornet operated one of the largest narrow-gauge (1,067 mm Cape gauge) networks in the world, moving bulk commodities such as coal, iron ore, and containers between mines, industrial centres, and ports including Richards Bay and Saldanha. Dedicated heavy-haul lines, such as the Sishen–Saldanha iron ore line and the Mpumalanga–Richards Bay coal line, ranked among the most productive freight railways in the world.

Passenger services

Long-distance passenger trains, including the famous Blue Train luxury service, were operated under the Shosholoza Meyl brand before passenger services were transferred to the Passenger Rail Agency of South Africa (PRASA) in 2009.
