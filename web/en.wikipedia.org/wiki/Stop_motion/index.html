Stop motion

Stop motion (also called stop frame animation) is an animated filmmaking technique in which objects are physically manipulated in small increments between individually photographed frames, so that they exhibit independent motion or change when the series of frames is played back.

Techniques

Variants include puppet animation (using jointed puppets with armatures, as in the films of Ray Harryhausen, whose process he called "Dynamation"), clay animation or "claymation" (figures modelled in plasticine, pioneered by Art Clokey's Gumby and popularised by Aardman's Wallace and Gromit), cutout animation, object animation and pixilation (animating live actors).

History

Early examples include J. Stuart Blackton and Albert E. Smith's The Humpty Dumpty Circus (1898). Willis O'Brien's work on The Lost World (1925) and King Kong (1933) established stop motion for special effects. Features such as The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993), Coraline (2009) and the films of Laika studio and Aardman (Chicken Run, 2000) brought the technique to wide modern audiences, often at 24 frames per second—hours of work for each second of screen time.
