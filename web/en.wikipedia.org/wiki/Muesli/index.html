Muesli (Swiss German: Müesli, "little mush") is a cold oatmeal dish based on rolled oats mixed with nuts, seeds, and dried or fresh fruit, typically served with milk, yogurt, or fruit juice. It is eaten mainly at breakfast and is popular across Europe and Australasia.

History

Muesli was developed around 1900 by the Swiss physician Maximilian Bircher-Benner for patients in his sanatorium above Zurich, where a diet of raw vegetables and fruit was part of the therapy. His original "apple diet dish" combined raw grated apple with a small amount of oats soaked in water, lemon juice, condensed milk, and ground nuts. The modern dry mix — emphasising oats with nuts, seeds, and dried fruit — evolved from this recipe; the freshly prepared, soaked version is known in Switzerland as Birchermüesli.

Packaged muesli

Commercial dry muesli, produced by brands such as Alpen (introduced in the UK in 1971) and Dorset Cereals, became widely popular from the 1970s. Soaking the mix overnight in milk or juice — "overnight oats" — softens the raw oats. Muesli is usually unsweetened or lightly sweetened, distinguishing it from granola, which is baked with oil and sweeteners.
