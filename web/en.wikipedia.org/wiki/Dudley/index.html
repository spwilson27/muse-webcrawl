Dudley is a market town in the West Midlands, England, about 9 miles (14 km) north-west of Birmingham. It is the administrative centre of the Metropolitan Borough of Dudley and the largest town of the Black Country. Historically part of Worcestershire — for centuries an exclave of that county surrounded by Staffordshire — Dudley became a centre of coal mining, ironworking, limestone quarrying and chain- and nail-making during the Industrial Revolution.

History

The town grew around Dudley Castle, a Norman motte-and-bailey rebuilt in stone and partly ruined by fire and by slighting after the Civil War. Below the castle hill, medieval Dudley prospered as a market town; in the 18th and 19th centuries it was transformed by industry. The Dudley Canal and its tunnel, among the earliest canal tunnels, carried limestone and coal, and the town's fossil-rich limestone — including the trilobite nicknamed the "Dudley Bug" — made it famous among geologists. Abraham Darby I, pioneer of coke smelting, was born nearby. The Black Country Living Museum, opened in 1978, recreates the town's industrial era.

Modern Dudley

Dudley Zoo, opened in 1937 within the castle grounds, is noted for its modernist Tecton concrete structures. The town centre declined with the loss of heavy industry and competition from the Merry Hill shopping centre, but regeneration schemes and a new Midland Metro tram extension linking Dudley to Wolverhampton and Birmingham have been pursued in the 2020s. The borough is also home to the Himley Hall estate and the Stourbridge glass quarter.
