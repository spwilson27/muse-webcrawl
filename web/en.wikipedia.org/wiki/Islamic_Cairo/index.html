Islamic Cairo

Islamic Cairo (Arabic: القاهرة الإسلامية) is the historic core of Cairo, Egypt, encompassing the medieval districts built between the 7th and 19th centuries. It contains one of the world's largest concentrations of historic Islamic architecture — mosques, madrasas, mausolea, hammams, caravanserais and fountains — and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979 under the name "Historic Cairo".

The skyline of minarets in Islamic Cairo.

History

The area's foundation dates to the founding of Fustat after the Muslim conquest of Egypt in 641 CE, and to the founding of Cairo itself (al-Qahira) by the Fatimids in 969 CE. The city flourished under the Ayyubids, who built the Cairo Citadel under Saladin, and reached its architectural zenith under the Mamluk Sultanate (1250–1517). It remained an important centre under Ottoman rule.

Major monuments

Notable monuments include the Mosque of Ibn Tulun (9th century), Al-Azhar Mosque and university (founded 972), the Mosque of Sultan al-Mu'ayyad, the Sultan Hasan Mosque-Madrasa (14th century), the Qaitbey complex in the Northern Cemetery, the gates of Bab al-Futuh and Bab Zuweila, and the market district of Khan el-Khalili.
