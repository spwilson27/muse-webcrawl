A mantra (Sanskrit: मन्त्र, mantra) is a sacred utterance — a syllable, word, or sequence of words — believed by practitioners to have religious, magical, or spiritual powers. Mantras originated in the Vedic tradition of India and remain central to Hinduism, and they are also widely used in Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. The Sanskrit word is traditionally analysed as deriving from the root man- ("to think") with the suffix -tra ("instrument"), hence "an instrument of thought."

History

The earliest mantras are the hymns of the Rigveda, composed in Vedic Sanskrit more than three thousand years ago. In Vedic ritual, correct recitation of mantras was held to be essential to the efficacy of sacrifice. The most revered mantras include the Gayatri Mantra from the Rigveda and the syllable Om (Aum), considered the primordial sound of the universe. In the Upanishads and later Hindu thought, mantra recitation (japa) became a devotional and meditative practice, often performed with a rosary of 108 beads (mala).

In other traditions

In Buddhism, mantras were incorporated early and became especially prominent in Mahayana and Vajrayana practice. The best-known Buddhist mantra is Om mani padme hum, associated with the bodhisattva Avalokiteshvara. Jain monks and laity recite the Navkar Mantra, and in Sikhism the mool mantra opens the Guru Granth Sahib. Through the spread of yoga and meditation in the twentieth century, mantra-based practices such as Transcendental Meditation reached a global audience.

Function and understanding

Within the traditions that use them, mantras are understood in varied ways: as invocations of deities, as condensed embodiments of a deity's power (in some tantric systems a mantra is the deity in sound form), as aids to concentration and meditation, and as protective formulas. Some mantras are considered effective only when received by initiation from a teacher (guru) and kept secret.

Modern usage

In contemporary English, "mantra" has acquired a figurative sense: any oft-repeated phrase, slogan, or guiding principle — a sense derived from the repetitive character of mantra recitation.
