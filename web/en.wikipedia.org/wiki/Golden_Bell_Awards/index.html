Golden Bell Awards

The Golden Bell Awards (Chinese: 金鐘獎; pinyin: Jīnzhōng Jiǎng) is an annual Taiwanese television production award, presented by Taiwan's Bureau of Audiovisual and Music Industry Development, Ministry of Culture. It was founded in 1965 and is Taiwan's equivalent to the Emmy Awards in the United States.

History

The awards were first presented in 1965, initially honouring radio broadcasting before expanding to television in 1970. The trophy depicts a bell, symbolising the role of broadcasting in sounding a warning and reminding media workers of their responsibilities. Over the decades the categories have grown to cover drama, variety, children's programming, hosting, directing and technical achievement, as well as radio categories.

Ceremony

The ceremony is held annually, typically in the autumn, in Taipei. In recent years separate ceremonies or segments have been used for programming and radio awards as the number of categories has expanded. The Golden Bell Awards are regarded, together with the Golden Horse Awards for film and the Golden Melody Awards for music, as one of the three major annual entertainment and cultural awards presented in Taiwan.
