Primus pilus

The primus pilus ("first spear") was the senior centurion of a Roman legion and the highest-ranking officer attainable by a common soldier in the Roman army. The position was a step below that of the legion's tribunes and prefects, but carried great prestige and substantial rewards.

Role

Each legion had sixty centurions arranged in a hierarchy, with the centurions of the first cohort at the top. The primus pilus commanded the first century of the first cohort — the most elite troops of the legion — and sat on the legion's war council alongside the legate and tribunes. He was also responsible for the care of the legion's eagle standard (aquila), the most sacred symbol of the unit.

Career

A soldier typically reached the post only after some 30 years of service, rising through the ranks of the centurionate across different legions. The tenure was usually one year. On leaving office, the primus pilus (then termed primipilaris) received a very large discharge bonus and was automatically enrolled in the equestrian order, opening the way to senior administrative and military posts such as camp prefect (praefectus castrorum), tribune of the cohorts at Rome, and eventually procuratorships.
