Newar people

The Newar people (also spelled Newa) are the historical inhabitants of the Kathmandu Valley of Nepal and its surrounding areas. They speak Nepal Bhasa (Newari), a Tibeto-Burman language written in several scripts including Ranjana and Devanagari. The Newars built the valley's celebrated cities — Kathmandu, Lalitpur (Patan), and Bhaktapur — and are renowned for their art, architecture, festivals, and cuisine.

Culture and society

Newar society is traditionally organized into a complex system of castes and occupational groups, encompassing both Hindu and Buddhist communities; the coexistence of the two religions, often worshipping at shared shrines, is a hallmark of Newar religious life. The valley's pagoda-style temples, carved wooden windows, and brick-and-timber craftsmanship strongly influenced architecture across the Himalayas and beyond. Newar festivals, such as Indra Jatra and Bisket Jatra, and institutions like the living goddess Kumari, remain central to the valley's cultural identity.

History

The Newar kingdoms of the valley flourished under the Malla dynasty before being conquered by Prithvi Narayan Shah in 1768–69 during the unification of Nepal. Under later regimes the Nepal Bhasa language faced periods of suppression, and Newar activists have continued to campaign for linguistic and cultural recognition. Today Newars make up a significant share of the population of the Kathmandu Valley and are active in Nepal's trade, civil service, and arts.
