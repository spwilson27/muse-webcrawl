Dafydd ap Gwilym (c. 1315/1320 – c. 1350/1370) is regarded as one of the leading Welsh poets and among the great poets of Europe in the Middle Ages. He is known for his love poetry and nature poetry written in the cywydd metre, of which he was a master and possibly an originator.

Life

Dafydd was born at Brogynin near Penrhyn-coch in Cardiganshire, into a noble family; his uncle Llywelyn ap Wilym was constable of Newcastle Emlyn. Tradition says he was buried at Strata Florida Abbey (Ystrad Fflur). Roughly 150 poems attributed to him survive, though the canon is debated; he probably died around the time of the Black Death.

Poetry

Dafydd's work breaks with the older praise-poetry of the court bards (the Gogynfeirdd): he wrote in a more personal voice, often as the frustrated lover of Morfudd, a married woman of Llanbadarn, and of Dyddgu. Famous poems include "Morfudd fel yr Haul" (Morfudd like the Sun), "Y Rhugl Groen" (The Rattle Bag), "Trafferth mewn Tafarn" (Trouble at an Inn), and "Yr Wylan" (The Seagull), in which a gull is sent as a love-messenger (llatai) to his beloved. His nature imagery and the persona of the poet in the woodland (his "green chapel") are distinctive, drawing on Welsh tradition and wider European influences including the troubadours and Ovid.
