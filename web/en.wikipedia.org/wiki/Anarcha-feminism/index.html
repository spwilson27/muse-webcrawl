Anarcha-feminism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anarcha-feminism, also referred to as anarchist feminism, is a philosophy which synthesises radical feminism and anarchism, regarding patriarchy and traditional gender roles as manifestations of involuntary, coercive hierarchy that should be abolished. The school of thought emerged in the 1960s, though it draws on earlier figures of the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

History

Precursors include Emma Goldman, Voltairine de Cleyre, and Lucy Parsons, who argued that women's liberation could not be achieved within marriage, capitalism, or the state, and advocated of the Spanish organisation Mujeres Libres during the Spanish Civil War. The term "anarcha-feminism" itself gained currency in the 1970s, for example in Peggy Kornegger's essay "Anarchism: The Feminist Connection" (1975). Its symbol combines the circled A of anarchism with the Venus symbol of feminism, and its colours are purple and black.

Ideas

Anarcha-feminists argue that the struggle against patriarchy is an essential part of the class struggle and the anarchist struggle against the state, since the family, gender hierarchy, and state authority reinforce one another. They criticise liberal feminism for seeking women's advancement within existing hierarchical institutions, and have influenced movements such as riot grrrl, queer anarchism, and contemporary intersectional activism.
