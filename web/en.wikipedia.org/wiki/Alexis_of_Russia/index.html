Alexis of Russia

Alexis Mikhailovich (Russian: Алексей Михайлович; 19 March 1629 – 8 February 1676), known in tradition as "Alexei the Quietest" (Тишайший), was Tsar of all Russia from 1645 until his death in 1676. The second Romanov tsar, he was the father of Peter the Great and the ruler under whom Russia fought major wars with Poland and Sweden, absorbed Left-bank Ukraine, and experienced the Raskol (Old Believer schism) in the Russian Orthodox Church.

Reign

Alexis came to the throne at sixteen, initially under the influence of his tutor Boris Morozov. Popular anger at Morozov's administration sparked the Salt Riot of 1648, followed by plague riots and the Copper Riot of 1662. In 1649 the Sobornoye Ulozheniye, a comprehensive law code, was adopted; it completed the legal enserfment of the peasantry.

In foreign affairs, the Pereyaslav Agreement of 1654 with the Zaporozhian Cossacks of Bohdan Khmelnytsky brought war with the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth (1654–1667), ending with the Truce of Andrusovo, which gave Russia Smolensk and Left-bank Ukraine including Kyiv. Russia also fought Sweden in 1656–1661 without major gains.

Domestically, Patriarch Nikon's liturgical reforms, backed by Alexis before the two fell out, provoked the schism of the Old Believers. The great Cossack-peasant revolt of Stepan Razin (1670–1671) shook the Volga region before being suppressed. Alexis died in 1676 and was succeeded by his son Feodor III; two other sons, Ivan V and Peter I, later reigned jointly.
