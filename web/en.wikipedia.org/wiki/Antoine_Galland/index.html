Antoine Galland (4 April 1646 – 17 February 1715) was a French orientalist and archaeologist best known as the first European translator of One Thousand and One Nights (Les mille et une nuits, 1704–1717), the work that introduced Aladdin, Ali Baba, and Sinbad to Western readers and ignited Europe's fascination with the literature of the Middle East.

Galland, professor of Arabic at the Collège de France.

Life and career

Born to a poor family in Picardy, Galland mastered Latin, Greek, and Hebrew before travelling to Constantinople in 1670 with the French embassy, where he learned Turkish, Persian, and Arabic and began collecting coins and manuscripts. He later travelled in Syria and the Levant cataloguing antiquities for the French crown and assisted Barthélemy d'Herbelot with the Bibliothèque orientale (1697). He became professor of Arabic at the Collège de France in 1709 and a member of the Académie des Inscriptions.

The Nights

Galland's translation, published in twelve volumes between 1704 and 1717 (the last two posthumously), was based principally on a 14th- or 15th-century Syrian manuscript. When his manuscript ran out, he drew on tales told to him in 1709 by Hanna Diyab, a Maronite Christian from Aleppo — the source of the famous "orphan tales" including Aladdin and the Magic Lamp and Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves, which appear in no earlier Arabic manuscript. Galland polished the tales for French taste, omitting poetry and much earthy detail. His elegant version was translated across Europe, shaped the Western image of the Orient, and remains a landmark in translation history.
