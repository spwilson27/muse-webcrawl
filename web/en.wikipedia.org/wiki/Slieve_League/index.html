Slieve League

Elevation596 m (1,955 ft)
LocationCounty Donegal, Ireland
Irish nameSliabh Liag

Slieve League (Irish: Sliabh Liag, "mountain of the flagstones") is a mountain on the Atlantic coast of County Donegal, Ireland. Its cliffs, rising 596 metres (1,955 ft) above the ocean, are among the highest sea cliffs in Ireland and in Europe — nearly three times the height of the more famous Cliffs of Moher. The name refers to the flat flagstones of the summit plateau.

Features

The cliffs form part of the Blue Stack Mountains and overlook Donegal Bay. A viewing platform at Bunglass, reached by a narrow road from Carrick and Teelin, gives access to paths along the cliff edge, including the dramatic "One Man's Pass" ridge leading toward the summit. Below the cliffs lie the remains of a Napoleonic-era watchtower, one of a chain built along the coast to guard against French invasion.

Heritage

The mountain was a site of Christian pilgrimage, with the ruins of an early monastic settlement and St. Hugh's (Aodh Mac Bric's) chapel and holy well on its slopes. The cliffs are a Special Area of Conservation and support colonies of seabirds, including razorbills and fulmars. Slieve League is a signature point on Ireland's Wild Atlantic Way tourist route, though it remains less visited than the Cliffs of Moher.
