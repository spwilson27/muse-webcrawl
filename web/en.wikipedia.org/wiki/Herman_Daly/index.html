Herman Edward Daly (21 July 1938 – 28 October 2022) was an American ecological economist and professor at the School of Public Policy of the University of Maryland, College Park. He was a leading critic of conventional economic growth, a co-founder of the field of ecological economics, and the foremost advocate of the steady-state economy.

Life and career

Daly earned his PhD from Vanderbilt University, where he was influenced by Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen, the founder of bioeconomics. He taught at Louisiana State University for two decades, served as a senior economist in the Environment Department of the World Bank (1988–1994), and then joined the University of Maryland. With Robert Costanza and others he founded the International Society for Ecological Economics and its journal Ecological Economics (1989).

Ideas

Daly argued that the economy is a subsystem of the finite, non-growing ecosphere, subject to the laws of thermodynamics; therefore unlimited material growth is impossible. He distinguished growth (quantitative increase in throughput) from development (qualitative improvement). His policy framework for a steady-state economy rested on three goals — sustainable scale, fair distribution, and efficient allocation — and institutions such as cap-and-trade on resource throughput, ecological tax reform, and limits on income inequality. With theologian John B. Cobb Jr. he co-authored For the Common Good (1989) and helped develop the Index of Sustainable Economic Welfare (ISEW).

Recognition

Daly received the Right Livelihood Award (1996), sometimes called the "Alternative Nobel Prize", and the Blue Planet Prize (2014) for his contributions to ecological economics.
