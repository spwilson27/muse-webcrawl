Bois de Vincennes

Bois de Vincennes
Location: 12th arrondissement, Paris, France
Area: 995 hectares (2,458 acres)
Type: Urban park / woodland
Created: 1860s, under Napoleon III

The Bois de Vincennes is a large public park located in the 12th arrondissement of Paris, France, on the eastern edge of the city. At roughly 995 hectares, it is the largest green space in Paris, about three times the size of New York's Central Park and nearly three times larger than its western counterpart, the Bois de Boulogne.

History

The area was originally a royal hunting ground, used from the time of Hugh Capet in the 10th century. Louis VII built a hunting lodge there, and Philip Augustus enclosed the forest with a wall. A castle, the Château de Vincennes, was gradually built on its northern edge from the 14th century onward. After the French Revolution the grounds were used for military exercises, before Napoleon III had them landscaped in the 1860s under the direction of Baron Haussmann and the engineer Jean-Charles Adolphe Alphand, in the style of an English landscape garden with lakes, cascades, and grottoes.

Features

The park contains four lakes, the largest being Lac Daumesnil, as well as the Parc floral de Paris, the Paris Zoological Park (Zoo de Vincennes), a Buddhist temple in the Daumesnil pagoda, the Lac des Minimes, a racecourse (Hippodrome de Vincennes), the Vélodrome de Vincennes, and the INSEP sports institute. Part of the park was annexed to Paris in 1929; the rest remains a popular destination for cycling, boating, jogging, and picnics.
