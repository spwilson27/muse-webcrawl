Carrie Rachel Brownstein (born September 27, 1974) is an American musician, actress, writer, and comedian. She is a guitarist and vocalist of the rock band Sleater-Kinney, and co-creator and co-star, with Fred Armisen, of the IFC sketch comedy series Portlandia (2011–2018).

Music career

Born in Seattle and raised in Redmond, Washington, Brownstein began playing guitar at 15. At Evergreen State College in Olympia she formed the queercore band Excuse 17. In 1994 she co-founded Sleater-Kinney with Corin Tucker; with drummer Janet Weiss from 1996, the trio became one of the most acclaimed indie rock bands of their era, releasing albums such as Dig Me Out (1997) and The Woods (2005). Rolling Stone named Brownstein one of the greatest guitarists of all time. After a hiatus beginning in 2006, Sleater-Kinney reunited in 2014 with No Cities to Love. Brownstein also played in the supergroup Wild Flag.

Television and writing

With Armisen, Brownstein developed the web series ThunderAnt, which became Portlandia, a Peabody Award-winning satire of Portland, Oregon hipster culture; Brownstein played numerous characters including one half of the feminist bookstore duo. She had a recurring role in the series Transparent. Her memoir, Hunger Makes Me a Modern Girl (2015), was a bestseller.
