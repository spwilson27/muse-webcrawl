Abbas Ibn Firnas (Arabic: عباس بن فرناس; c. 810–887), also Latinized as Armen Firman, was an Andalusi polymath of Berber descent in the Emirate of Córdoba: inventor, engineer, aviator, physician, poet, and musician. He is best known for an early attempt at gliding flight and for contributions to astronomy, horology, and glass manufacture at the court of the Umayyad emirs Abd al-Rahman II and Muhammad I.

Life and works

Ibn Firnas was born in Izn-Rand Onda (Ronda) and educated in Córdoba, where he became a court poet and a maker of ingenious devices. He constructed a water clock known as al-Maqata, designed a process for cutting rock crystal that freed Córdoba from sending quartz to Egypt to be cut, and is credited with producing colourless glass from sand. His home reportedly contained a room simulating the heavens, with stars, clouds, thunder, and lightning produced by hidden mechanisms — an early planetarium. He also devised an early metronome-like device and chain-free astronomical rings (an armillary sphere), and is sometimes credited with an early form of magnifying lens or reading stone.

The flight attempt

According to the seventeenth-century historian al-Maqqari, drawing on earlier sources, Ibn Firnas constructed wings covered with feathers and, around the year 875, launched himself from a height near Córdoba (accounts differ between the Rusafa palace hill and the Sierra Morena). He reportedly glided a considerable distance before landing badly and injuring his back — an injury al-Maqqari's source attributes to his omission of a tail for landing. If the account is accurate, it is among the earliest recorded attempts at controlled heavier-than-air flight, more than six centuries before Leonardo da Vinci's designs. Earlier, in 852, a daredevil named Armen Firman had reportedly leapt from a minaret in Córdoba using a voluminous cloak as a parachute; some scholars consider "Armen Firman" to be Ibn Firnas himself under a Latinized name, others a separate figure.

Legacy

The lunar crater Ibn Firnas is named for him, as are a bridge over the Guadalquivir in Córdoba and a statue outside Baghdad's airport; Libya issued a postage stamp honouring his flight. Ibn Firnas is celebrated in Spain and the Arab world as a symbol of early experimental science.
