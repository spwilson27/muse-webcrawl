Chemise

A chemise is a loose-fitting garment that has historically been worn as an undergarment or nightshirt. Chemises are traditionally made from lightweight materials like cotton, linen, silk, or synthetic fibers such as nylon.

History

In medieval Europe, the chemise was the primary undergarment worn by both men and women beneath their outer clothing. For women it was a simple, knee-length or longer sleeveless or short-sleeved dress worn under a kirtle or gown. Men wore a similar garment, sometimes called a shirt, under their tunics and doublets.

During the 18th and 19th centuries, the chemise was a standard part of women's underwear, worn under corsets, drawers, and petticoats. It protected outer clothing from perspiration and body oils, and protected the skin from the stiff fabrics of corsetry.

Modern usage

Today, the term is most often applied to women's lingerie and nightwear: a short, loose, often lacy slip-like garment, sometimes worn with a matching robe. Chemises also appear in historical reenactment and in folk dance costuming.
