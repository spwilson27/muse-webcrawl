Ralph Bakshi (born 29 October 1938) is an American animator, filmmaker and painter. He was a pioneer of adult-oriented animation, achieving breakthrough success with the X-rated Fritz the Cat (1972), the first animated feature to receive an X rating in the United States, and went on to direct the animated features Heavy Traffic (1973), Coonskin (1975), Wizards (1977), The Lord of the Rings (1978), American Pop (1981), Fire and Ice (1983) and Cool World (1992).

Early life and career

Bakshi was born in Haifa to a Krymchak Jewish family and emigrated as an infant to Brooklyn, New York. He began work at 18 as a cel polisher at Terrytoons, rising to director of series such as The Mighty Heroes within a decade. In 1967 he moved to Paramount's animation division, then founded Bakshi Productions, producing the controversial Fritz the Cat, adapted from Robert Crumb's underground comic, on a budget of under $1 million; it grossed over $90 million worldwide.

Feature films

Bakshi's 1970s films combined rotoscoping, live-action backgrounds, scratchboard and photostat techniques with urban, satirical and often profane subject matter. Wizards (1977) was an allegorical post-apocalyptic fantasy; his two-part adaptation of The Lord of the Rings (1978) used extensive rotoscoping and covered roughly the first half of Tolkien's story. American Pop (1981) traced four generations of a family through American popular music, and Fire and Ice (1983), made with Frank Frazetta, was a sword-and-sorcery adventure.

His later career included the television series The New Adventures of Mighty Mouse (1987), influential for its creator-driven approach, and the live-action/animation hybrid Cool World (1992), a troubled production whose failure led him to step back from feature filmmaking; he subsequently concentrated on painting and independent shorts, including the crowdfunded Last Days of Coney Island (2015).

Legacy

Bakshi is widely credited with proving that animation could address adult themes, influencing animators who later worked on shows such as The Simpsons and Ren & Stimpy; John Kricfalusi and other Mighty Mouse staff were among his protégés. In 2003 he received a Winsor McCay Award at the Annie Awards for his lifetime contribution to animation.
