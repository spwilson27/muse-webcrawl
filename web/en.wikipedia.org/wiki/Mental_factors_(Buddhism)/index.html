Mental factors (Buddhism)

Mental factors arise together with each moment of mind

Mental factors (Sanskrit: caitasika; Pali: cetasika; Tibetan: sems byung) are formations concurrent with mind (citta) in Buddhist Abhidharma psychology. They can be described as aspects of the mind that apprehend the quality of an object, and that have the ability to color the mind. Within the Abhidharma schools, the number and classification of mental factors vary: the Theravada tradition identifies 52 cetasikas, while Mahayana Abhidharma lists 51.

Function

According to Abhidharma analysis, a moment of consciousness never occurs alone: the mind (citta) is always accompanied by a cluster of mental factors that give the experience its character. Universal factors present in every mind-moment include contact, feeling, perception, volition, and attention. Other factors occur only in wholesome or unwholesome states, determining the ethical quality of the consciousness.

Classification

In the Theravada Abhidhammattha Sangaha, the 52 cetasikas are divided into: seven universals, common to all consciousness; six occasionals, present in some wholesome and unwholesome minds; fourteen unwholesome factors, including delusion, shamelessness, and restlessness; and twenty-five beautiful factors, including faith, mindfulness, loving-kindness-related qualities, and wisdom. The Sarvastivada and Yogacara traditions arrange their lists differently, with the Yogacara fifty-one including five universals, five object-determining factors, eleven virtuous factors, six root afflictions, twenty secondary afflictions, and four changeable factors.

Significance

Because liberation in Buddhism depends on transforming the mind, identifying which mental factors are present — and cultivating wholesome factors such as mindfulness and equanimity while abandoning unwholesome ones such as craving and aversion — is a central concern of Buddhist practice and meditation manuals.
