Black Panther Party

The Black Panther Party (originally the Black Panther Party for Self-Defense) was an American Black power political organisation founded by Huey P. Newton and Bobby Seale in Oakland, California, in October 1966. Combining Black nationalism, socialism, and armed self-defence against police brutality, the Panthers grew into a national organisation with chapters in dozens of cities and thousands of members at their peak around 1970.

Ten-Point Program

The Panthers' platform demanded full employment, decent housing, an end to police brutality, exemption from the draft, and ultimately a United Nations-supervised plebiscite for Black Americans. Early activities included legally armed patrols observing police arrests in Oakland, which prompted California's Mulford Act of 1967 banning open carry.

Survival programs

From 1969 the party emphasised community "survival programs," most famously the Free Breakfast for Children program, which fed tens of thousands of children and influenced the expansion of US federal school breakfast provision. Other programs included free health clinics, sickle-cell anaemia testing, liberation schools, and clothing distribution.

Repression and decline

The FBI's COINTELPRO targeted the Panthers with surveillance, raids, and disinformation; Chicago leader Fred Hampton was killed in a police raid in 1969. Internal splits — notably between Newton and Eldridge Cleaver in 1971 — trials, and Newton's later troubles weakened the party, which formally dissolved in 1982. Figures such as Elaine Brown and Kathleen Cleaver later became prominent writers, and Panther imagery and politics continue to influence activism and culture.
