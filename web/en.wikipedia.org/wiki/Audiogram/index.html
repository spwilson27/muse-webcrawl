Audiogram

An audiogram is a graph that shows the audible threshold for standardized frequencies as measured by an audiometer. The Y axis represents intensity measured in decibels hearing level (dB HL) and the X axis represents frequency measured in hertz (Hz). The audiogram is a standard output of pure tone audiometry and is the primary clinical record of a person's hearing sensitivity for each ear.

Symbols and conventions

By international convention, red symbols denote the right ear and blue symbols the left ear. Air-conduction thresholds are typically plotted with circles (right) and crosses or X marks (left); bone-conduction thresholds use bracket-like symbols. Thresholds are usually measured at octave intervals from 125 Hz to 8000 Hz.

Interpretation

Comparing air- and bone-conduction thresholds helps classify hearing loss: if both are equally depressed the loss is sensorineural; if bone conduction is normal while air conduction is depressed, the loss is conductive and the difference is called an air–bone gap. The configuration of the audiogram can suggest causes — for example, a notch at 4 kHz in noise-induced hearing loss, or the sloping high-frequency loss of presbycusis. Severity is graded from normal (≤20 dB HL average) through mild, moderate, severe, and profound.

Limitations

The audiogram measures detection thresholds for pure tones in quiet and does not fully predict speech understanding, especially in noise; speech audiometry and other tests supplement it. Results depend on calibrated equipment, test environment, and the patient's responses.
