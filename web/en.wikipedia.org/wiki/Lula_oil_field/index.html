The Lula oil field is a large oil field located in the Santos Basin, about 230 kilometres (140 mi) off the coast of Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Originally named the Tupi field, it was the first supergiant discovery in Brazil's pre-salt layer and one of the largest oil discoveries in the world in the 2000s.

Discovery and geology

The field was discovered in 2006 by a consortium of Petrobras (operator), BG Group (later Shell), and Galp Energia. The oil lies in pre-salt microbial carbonate reservoirs roughly 6,000–7,000 metres below sea level, beneath some 2,000 metres of water and a thick layer of salt. Recoverable reserves were estimated at 5 to 8 billion barrels of oil equivalent, of light crude (around 28–30° API).

Development

Production began in 2010 with a pilot floating production storage and offloading (FPSO) vessel, followed by a series of large FPSOs. Lula grew to become Brazil's largest producing field and, together with the neighboring Búzios field, helped make the pre-salt province responsible for the majority of Brazil's total oil output. The field's name was changed from Tupi to Lula in 2010 — officially in honor of the giant squid (lula in Portuguese) found in the region, though it coincided with the surname of then-president Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva.
