iHeartMedia

iHeartMedia, Inc., formerly Clear Channel Communications, is an American mass media corporation based in San Antonio, Texas. It is the largest owner of radio stations in the United States, operating more than 850 full-power AM and FM stations, and runs the iHeartRadio digital audio platform. The company was founded by Lowry Mays and B. J. "Red" McCombs in 1972.

History

Clear Channel grew from a single San Antonio radio station into the dominant US radio broadcaster, expanding rapidly after the Telecommunications Act of 1996 removed national ownership caps. In 2008 the company was taken private in a leveraged buyout led by Bain Capital and Thomas H. Lee Partners, a deal that left it heavily indebted. It rebranded as iHeartMedia in 2014 to emphasize its digital platform, and filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in March 2018, emerging in 2019 with reduced debt.

Operations

iHeartMedia's broadcast network reaches roughly nine out of ten Americans monthly. The iHeartRadio app offers live radio streams, custom stations, podcasts — the company is one of the world's largest podcast publishers — and on-demand music. It also promotes live events such as the iHeartRadio Music Festival and the iHeartRadio Music Awards.

Criticism

The company has been criticized for homogenizing radio programming, heavy use of voice-tracking and syndicated content, and large-scale layoffs of local staff.
