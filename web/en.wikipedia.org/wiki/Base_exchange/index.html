A base exchange (BX) — called a post exchange (PX) on Army installations, a Navy Exchange (NEX) at naval stations, and a Marine Corps Exchange (MCX) — is a retail department store operated on military bases of the United States Armed Forces. Exchanges sell merchandise and services at discounted, tax-free prices to authorised patrons, chiefly active-duty service members, their dependents, military retirees and, since 2020, many disabled and honorably discharged veterans.

History

The exchange system grew out of the sutlers and post traders who supplied frontier garrisons, and from the post canteens established from 1889. In 1895 the War Department created the Army Exchange Service to standardise the system and direct its profits to soldiers' welfare; its successor, the Army and Air Force Exchange Service (AAFES), founded in 1941 as the Army Exchange Service, now serves both the Army and the Air Force (and the Space Force), and is one of the largest retail organisations in the United States. The Navy Exchange system is administered by NEXCOM, established in 1946.

Operations

A main exchange resembles a civilian department store, selling clothing, electronics, sporting goods and household items, and typically anchors a complex of concessions — barbershops, food courts, movie theatres, pharmacies and brand-name outlets. Exchanges are non-appropriated fund instrumentalities: they operate without direct taxpayer funding for their retail mission, and a majority of their earnings are paid as dividends to Morale, Welfare and Recreation programmes that fund gyms, libraries, recreation and family services. Because sales are generally exempt from state and local sales tax and prices exclude profit margins charged commercially, exchanges are a tangible component of military compensation.
