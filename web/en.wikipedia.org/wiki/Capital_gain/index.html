Capital gain

A capital gain is the increase in the value of a capital asset — such as stocks, bonds, real estate, or businesses — that gives it a higher worth than the purchase price. The gain is not realised until the asset is sold, at which point it may become subject to capital gains tax. A loss incurred when the sale price is below the purchase price is a capital loss.

Calculation

The gain is generally computed as the difference between the sale proceeds and the asset's cost basis, which includes the purchase price plus certain costs such as commissions and improvements. Assets held for longer periods ("long-term" gains) are taxed at lower rates than short-term gains in many jurisdictions, including the United States.

Taxation

Most countries tax realised capital gains, though the scope varies: many exempt a taxpayer's principal residence, and some, such as Singapore and ( historically) Belgium, levy no general capital gains tax. Preferential treatment of gains is debated by economists, who note both the incentive for investment and the benefit to higher-income households that own most assets.

Unrealised gains

Appreciation that has not been converted into a sale is an unrealised or "paper" gain; proposals to tax such gains on very wealthy individuals have been debated in the United States and elsewhere.
