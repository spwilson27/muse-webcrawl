Anaheim, California

Anaheim, home of the Disneyland Resort in Orange County, California.

Anaheim is a city in northern Orange County, California, United States, part of the Greater Los Angeles area. With a population of about 346,000 at the 2020 census, it is the most populous city in Orange County and the 10th-most populous in California. It is best known as the home of the Disneyland Resort.

History

Anaheim was founded in 1857 by German immigrants as an agricultural colony; its name combines "Ana" for the Santa Ana River and the German "-heim" (home). The settlers planted vineyards, making it a wine-growing center until a blight in the 1880s, after which citrus — especially Valencia oranges — took over. The city was incorporated in 1876. In the 1920s it was briefly notorious as a center of Ku Klux Klan political influence.

Tourism and sports

The opening of Disneyland in 1955 transformed Anaheim into a resort city, joined later by Disney California Adventure, the Anaheim Convention Center, and the Anaheim Resort district of hotels. The city hosts the Los Angeles Angels of Major League Baseball at Angel Stadium and the Anaheim Ducks of the NHL at the Honda Center.

Geography and demographics

Anaheim spans about 50 square miles from flatlands in the west to the Anaheim Hills in the east. It is a majority-minority city with large Hispanic and Asian communities, and its economy rests on tourism, manufacturing, and distribution.
