Position-specific scoring matrix

A position-specific scoring matrix (PSSM), also called a position weight matrix (PWM) or position-specific weight matrix (PSWM), is a commonly used representation of motifs (patterns) in biological sequences. A PSSM is derived from a set of aligned sequences, typically created by tools such as PSI-BLAST or by motif-discovery algorithms applied to DNA or protein sequences.

Construction

The matrix has one row for each symbol of the alphabet (4 nucleotides for DNA sequences, or 20 amino acids for protein sequences) and one column for each position in the pattern. For each cell, a score is computed from the observed frequency of that symbol at that position, usually expressed as a log-odds ratio comparing the observed frequency with a background frequency. Pseudocounts are added to avoid zero frequencies for symbols not observed in the training alignment.

Uses

PSSMs are used to scan sequences for occurrences of a motif by summing the matrix scores over a window of the sequence and comparing the total with a threshold. They underlie protein family databases such as PROSITE and Pfam, transcription factor binding site models in databases such as JASPAR and TRANSFAC, and sequence logos, which are a visual representation of the information content of each column of the matrix.

Limitations

A basic PSSM assumes independence between positions, so it cannot capture correlations between residues, such as compensatory mutations in RNA stems. More general models, including profile hidden Markov models, relax this assumption.
