Sierra Club

Founded: 28 May 1892
Founder: John Muir
Type: Environmental organisation
Headquarters: Oakland, California, US

The Sierra Club is an American environmental organisation founded in 1892 in San Francisco by the conservationist John Muir, who served as its first president. One of the oldest and largest grassroots environmental organisations in the United States, it works to protect wilderness, promote clean energy, and address climate change.

History

The club's first campaigns defended Yosemite and the Sierra Nevada. An early defeat was the damming of Hetch Hetchy Valley (1913), but under executive director David Brower in the 1950s and 1960s the club grew into a national force, helping to stop dams in Dinosaur National Monument and the Grand Canyon and lobbying for the Wilderness Act of 1964 and the creation of national parks.

Activities

The Sierra Club organises outings and conservation campaigns through state and local chapters, publishes Sierra magazine, and engages in electoral politics through its political action committee. Its "Beyond Coal" campaign, launched in the 2000s, has worked to retire coal-fired power plants across the United States, and the organisation now focuses heavily on a transition to clean energy and environmental justice.
