Beaufort (pronounced "BYOO-furt") is a city and the county seat of Beaufort County, South Carolina, United States. Located on Port Royal Island in the heart of the Sea Islands and Lowcountry, it is known for its well-preserved antebellum architecture and its history as a centre of the Gullah-Geechee culture.

History

Founded in 1711, Beaufort is the second-oldest city in South Carolina after Charleston. Its early wealth came from indigo, rice and Sea Island cotton grown on surrounding plantations. Early in the Civil War, after the Battle of Port Royal in November 1861, Beaufort came under Union occupation, which spared much of its historic building stock from destruction; its entire central district is a National Historic Landmark.

Geography and culture

The city sits on the Beaufort River, part of the Intracoastal Waterway. Henry C. Chambers Waterfront Park hosts festivals including the Beaufort Water Festival and a shrimp festival. Nearby military installations include Marine Corps Air Station Beaufort and the recruit depot at Parris Island. Novelist Pat Conroy, who lived in the area, set several books in the Lowcountry, and films such as The Big Chill and Forrest Gump used Beaufort locations.
