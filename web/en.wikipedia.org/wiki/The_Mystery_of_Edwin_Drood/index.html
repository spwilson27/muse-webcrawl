The Mystery of Edwin Drood

Author: Charles Dickens
Published: 1870 (unfinished)
Genre: Novel, mystery

The Mystery of Edwin Drood is the final novel by Charles Dickens, left unfinished at his death on 9 June 1870. Only six of the planned twelve monthly instalments were completed, and the intended resolution of its central mystery has been the subject of speculation, continuations, and adaptations ever since.

Plot

In the cathedral town of Cloisterham, the choirmaster John Jasper — opium-addicted and obsessed with his pupil Rosa Bud — seethes with jealousy of her fiancé, his own nephew Edwin Drood. On a stormy Christmas Eve, Drood disappears after a reconciliation dinner with his rival Neville Landless. Suspicion falls on Landless, while the mysterious detective figure Dick Datchery arrives in town to investigate.

The "Drood problem"

Evidence from Dickens's letters, his friend John Forster's biography, and illustrator Luke Fildes's recollections suggests Jasper murdered Drood and was to confess in a final prison scene. Numerous authors have completed the novel; Rupert Holmes's musical Drood (1985) turned the puzzle into an audience vote on the murderer. The fragment also influenced later detective fiction and scholarship on Dickens's darkest themes.
