The Yemeni Socialist Party (YSP; Arabic: الحزب الاشتراكي اليمني) is a political party in Yemen. It was the ruling party of South Yemen (the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen) from its founding in 1978 until Yemeni unification in 1990, and remains a significant party in the unified Republic of Yemen.

History

The party was founded in October 1978 through the unification of the National Liberation Front — which had led South Yemen to independence from Britain in 1967 — with smaller Marxist parties. As the governing party of the only avowedly Marxist state in the Arab world, the YSP implemented land reform, nationalization, and extensive state planning, while maintaining close ties to the Soviet Union.

The party was torn by factional violence, most severely in January 1986, when a power struggle between supporters of President Ali Nasir Muhammad and the faction of Abdul Fattah Ismail erupted into a brief civil war that killed thousands, including many senior party leaders. The disaster weakened the state and pushed the party toward reform and, ultimately, reunification with North Yemen.

After unification

At unification in 1990 the YSP entered government alongside the northern General People's Congress, with YSP leader Ali Salem al-Beidh becoming vice-president. Disputes over power-sharing led to the 1994 civil war, in which southern separatist forces led in part by al-Beidh were defeated; the party's assets were seized and many leaders were exiled. The YSP subsequently reorganized, participated in parliamentary politics as an opposition party, joined the Joint Meeting Parties alliance, and took part in the National Dialogue Conference of 2013–2014. Since the Yemeni civil war began in 2015, the party has been divided between factions in Sanaa, Aden, and exile.
