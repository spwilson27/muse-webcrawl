Felix of Urgell

Felix of Urgell (died 818) was Bishop of Urgell in Catalonia from about 782 to 799 and, together with Elipandus of Toledo, a leading proponent of the Christological doctrine known as Spanish Adoptionism, which was condemned as heretical by the Carolingian church.

Adoptionism controversy

Felix taught that Christ in his human nature was the adoptive Son of God — God by grace rather than by nature — while in his divine nature he was Son by nature. The doctrine, intended partly to answer Islam's denial of Christ's divinity in the divided religious landscape of Iberia, drew sharp attack from the monk Beatus of Liébana and from Alcuin of York.

Summoned north, Felix was condemned at the Council of Regensburg (792), where he recanted before Pope Hadrian I in Rome, again at Frankfurt (794), and debated Alcuin at Aachen in 799. After the disputation at Aachen he was deposed from his see and placed under supervision in Lyon, where he died in 818. Alcuin's treatises against him are a principal source for his thought, which survives mainly in fragments and in a letter to the faithful of Urgell preserved among Alcuin's works.

Legacy

The Adoptionist controversy helped bind the Spanish March ecclesiastically to Francia rather than to Toledo, then under Muslim rule. Felix's former diocese later preserved his memory ambivalently; some of his writings were rediscovered in the early modern period when manuscripts preserving his letter and a confession of faith came to light.
