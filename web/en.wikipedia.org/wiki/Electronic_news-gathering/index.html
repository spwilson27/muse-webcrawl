Electronic news-gathering

Electronic news-gathering (ENG) is a term used in the television broadcast industry for the collection of news material using portable electronic cameras and recording equipment, as opposed to film. ENG replaced 16 mm film for television news during the 1970s, allowing stories to be shot, edited on videotape, and broadcast far more quickly.

History

The development of portable video cameras and videocassette recorders — notably Sony's U-matic format and later Betacam — freed news crews from film processing delays. By the early 1980s, ENG crews with a camera operator and a reporter could transmit material back to the studio by microwave link from specially equipped vans, enabling live reporting from the field. Satellite news-gathering (SNG), using transportable satellite uplinks, extended this capability to locations beyond microwave range.

Modern practice

Today ENG relies on digital camcorders, solid-state recording media, laptop editing, and transmission over bonded cellular networks and the internet. The term is often contrasted with electronic field production (EFP), which uses similar technology for non-news programming, and with the work of multimedia journalists who shoot and edit alone. Drones are increasingly used for aerial ENG coverage.
