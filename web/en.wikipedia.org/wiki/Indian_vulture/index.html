Indian vulture

The Indian vulture or long-billed vulture (Gyps indicus) is a bird of prey native to the Indian subcontinent. It is an Old World vulture in the family Accipitridae. It is closely related to the slender-billed vulture, with which it was formerly treated as conspecific.

Description

It is a medium-sized, bulky vulture with a pale bill and collar, sandy-brown body feathers and darker flight feathers. It breeds in south-eastern Pakistan and peninsular India south of the Ganges, nesting mainly on cliffs.

Conservation

The species suffered a catastrophic decline of over 97% (in common with other Gyps vultures in Asia) caused by poisoning from the veterinary drug diclofenac in livestock carcasses. It is listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List, and captive breeding programmes have been established.
