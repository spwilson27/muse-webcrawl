Between the World and Me is a 2015 book by American author Ta-Nehisi Coates. Written as a letter to his teenage son, it explores the feelings, symbolism and realities associated with being Black in the United States.

Content

Its title derives from a Richard Wright poem. Coates recounts his childhood in Baltimore, his education at Howard University ("the Mecca"), the killing of his friend Prince Jones by police, and meditates on the vulnerability of the Black body, the American Dream and the legacy of slavery and segregation.

Reception

Endorsed by Toni Morrison, who compared Coates to James Baldwin, the book won the National Book Award for Nonfiction, was a Pulitzer finalist and became a major bestseller and a staple of discussions of race in America. It was adapted for the stage and as an HBO special in 2020.
