Nevers

Country: France
Region: Bourgogne-Franche-Comté
Department: Nièvre (prefecture)
Population: about 32,000 (municipality)
River: Loire, at the confluence with the Nièvre

Nevers (French pronunciation: [nəvɛʁ]) is a commune in the Nièvre department in the Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region of east-central France, of which it is the prefecture. It lies on the banks of the Loire River, at its confluence with the Nièvre, about 230 km (140 mi) south-southeast of Paris. Nevers is the historic capital of the former province of Nivernais.

History

The site was an Aedui settlement known as Noviodunum, later Nevirnum. It became a bishopric in the 5th or 6th century, and the Counts of Nevers were important feudal lords from the 10th century. The county passed by marriage and inheritance through several houses, including the Gonzaga of Mantua, who became dukes of Nevers in the 16th–17th centuries; Cardinal Mazarin bought the duchy in 1659, and it later passed to the Mancini family.

In the Middle Ages and early modern period Nevers was renowned for its faience — tin-glazed earthenware introduced by Italian potters in the late 16th century — which remains a signature product. The city was also known for glassmaking and enamel work. Saint Bernadette Soubirous, the visionary of Lourdes, spent the last years of her life in the convent of Saint-Gildard in Nevers, where her body is displayed.

Sights

  • Nevers Cathedral (Cathédrale Saint-Cyr-et-Sainte-Julitte) — unusual in having two apses, one Romanesque and one Gothic, at opposite ends; rebuilt after heavy bombing in 1944.
  • Ducal Palace — the late-15th/16th-century palace of the counts and dukes of Nevers, considered one of the first Loire châteaux.
  • Church of Saint-Étienne — an 11th–12th-century Romanesque church.
  • Espace Bernadette — the convent of Saint-Gildard with the incorrupt body of Saint Bernadette.
  • Faience museum (Musée de la Faïence Frédéric Blandin) — collections of Nevers pottery.

Geography and transport

Nevers stands where the Nièvre joins the Loire; the Canal latéral à la Loire passes nearby. The city is served by the A77 autoroute and by rail services toward Paris, Clermont-Ferrand, and Dijon. The Circuit de Nevers Magny-Cours racing circuit, long home of the French Grand Prix in Formula One (1991–2008), lies about 12 km south of the city.

Economy

Traditional industries include faience pottery, metallurgy, and railway engineering; the Technicentre of Nevers maintains SNCF rolling stock. The city is the administrative and commercial centre of the Nièvre department.
