Luoyang

Luoyang (simplified Chinese: 洛阳; traditional Chinese: 洛陽; pinyin: Luòyáng) is a city located in the confluence area of the Luo River and the Yellow River in the west of Henan province, China. Governed as a prefecture-level city, it had a population of about 7.06 million at the 2020 census, of whom roughly 2.86 million lived in the built-up urban area.

Situated on the central plain of China, Luoyang is among the oldest cities in the country and one of the cradles of Chinese civilisation. It served as the capital of thirteen dynasties and kingdoms, including the Eastern Han, Cao Wei, Western Jin, Northern Wei and Sui dynasties, and is commonly counted among the Four Great Ancient Capitals of China alongside Xi'an, Beijing and Nanjing.

History

The area around Luoyang was considered sacred geography from early times, lying at the centre of the realm as understood by ancient Chinese cosmography. Chengzhou, near modern Luoyang, was built in the 11th century BC as a secondary capital of the Western Zhou. In AD 25, Liu Xiu, the Guangwu Emperor, made Luoyang the capital of the restored Eastern Han dynasty, a status it held for nearly two centuries. During this period the city hosted the first imperial university and the White Horse Temple, traditionally regarded as the first Buddhist temple in China, founded around AD 68.

Luoyang was devastated during the warlord conflicts at the end of Han and again during the fall of Western Jin, when it was sacked in AD 311. It flourished again under the Northern Wei after Emperor Xiaowen moved his capital there in AD 494, and the nearby Longmen Grottoes were carved from the late 5th century onward. The Sui and Tang dynasties treated Luoyang as an eastern capital; Wu Zetian, the only female emperor in Chinese history, ruled from Luoyang, which she renamed Shendu, "Divine Capital." The city declined after the An Lushan rebellion and the Tang capital's shift back to Chang'an.

Sites and culture

The Longmen Grottoes, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, contain tens of thousands of Buddhist statues carved into limestone cliffs along the Yi River. The White Horse Temple, Guanlin (the burial site of Guan Yu's head), and the Luoyang Museum are major attractions. Luoyang is famed for its peonies, celebrated each April at the Luoyang Peony Festival, and for its "water banquet" (shuixi), a traditional set of soup-based dishes.

Economy and transport

Modern Luoyang is an industrial centre producing tractors (the YTO Group), mining machinery, glass, petrochemicals and aluminium products. Luoyang Beijiao Airport and the Longmen railway station on the Zhengzhou–Xi'an high-speed railway connect the city nationally.
