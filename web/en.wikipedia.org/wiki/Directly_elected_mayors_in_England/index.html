Directly elected mayors in England

Directly elected mayors in England are local government executive leaders who are chosen directly by voters, rather than indirectly by fellow councillors. The office was introduced by the Local Government Act 2000, which allowed local authorities in England (and Wales, initially) to adopt a "mayor and cabinet" executive model, normally after a referendum. The Mayor of London, created under separate legislation in 2000 as head of the Greater London Authority, was the first and remains the most powerful such mayor. A separate category, the mayors of combined authorities ("metro mayors"), was created by later devolution legislation from 2016 onwards.

Local authority mayors

Under the 2000 Act, councils could propose an elected mayor, and in many cases local voters were asked in a referendum. Early adopters included Hartlepool, which elected Stuart Drummond (who had campaigned as the town's football mascot, H'Angus the Monkey), Middlesbrough, Doncaster, Watford, Hackney, Lewisham, Newham and North Tyneside. In a series of referendums in major cities in May 2012, most cities — including Birmingham, Leeds, Manchester and Sheffield — voted against adopting a mayor, while Bristol voted in favour. Some places have since abolished the office after further referendums, including Hartlepool and Stoke-on-Trent.

Powers

A directly elected mayor leads the council executive: appointing a cabinet from among councillors, proposing the budget and major policies (which the council may amend by a two-thirds majority), and serving as the area's political figurehead. The mayor is not the same as the ceremonial or civic mayor, a largely honorary role that continues to exist alongside the elected office in many places.

Combined authority mayors

Under the Cities and Local Government Devolution Act 2016, groups of councils forming combined authorities could agree devolution deals with central government headed by an elected mayor. The first "metro mayor" elections took place in 2017, including for Greater Manchester, the West Midlands, the Liverpool City Region, the West of England, Tees Valley and the Sheffield City Region (South Yorkshire). These mayors hold devolved powers over areas such as transport, skills and housing.
