Sercquiais


Sark, where Sercquiais is spoken (placeholder image)

Sercquiais (in the language itself serkyee; also known as Sarkese or Sark-French) is the Norman dialect traditionally spoken on the island of Sark in the Channel Islands. It is a variety of Norman French descended from the Jèrriais brought by settlers from Jersey who colonised Sark under Helier de Carteret from 1565 onward, subsequently developing in relative isolation.

Characteristics

Sercquiais preserves features of sixteenth-century Jèrriais while developing its own distinctive phonology, including pronounced palatalisation and its treatment of consonant groups. Because Sark's small population was drawn from forty Jersey families (the "forty tenements"), the dialect shows comparatively little internal variation, though individual family pronunciations were once noted.

Endangerment

The language is severely endangered. By the early twenty-first century the number of fluent native speakers was estimated at only a handful of elderly islanders — commonly reported as fewer than ten — as English became the universal language of the island and Norman speech receded from schools and public life. Sercquiais has been documented by linguists, notably Anthony J. Liddicoat, whose grammar remains a principal record of the dialect.
