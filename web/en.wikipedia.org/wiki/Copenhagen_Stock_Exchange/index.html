Copenhagen Stock Exchange

The Copenhagen Stock Exchange or CSE (Danish: Københavns Fondsbørs) is an international marketplace for Danish securities, including shares, bonds, treasury bills, and financial futures and options. It is part of Nasdaq Nordic and operates as Nasdaq Copenhagen.

History

Securities trading in Copenhagen dates to the early 17th century, when Christian IV built the Børsen building (1625–1640), famous for its spire of intertwined dragon tails. The exchange was officially organized in the 19th century. In 2005 the Copenhagen exchange merged with the Stockholm exchange into OMX, which was acquired by Nasdaq in 2008.

Market

The exchange lists major Danish companies such as Novo Nordisk, Maersk, and Danske Bank. The benchmark C25 index tracks the 25 most traded shares. A fire in April 2024 severely damaged the historic Børsen building, toppling its spire; the trading functions had long since moved to modern premises.

Operations

Trading is electronic through the shared INET platform used by Nasdaq's Nordic exchanges. The market includes main-market listings as well as Nasdaq First North Growth Market for smaller companies.
