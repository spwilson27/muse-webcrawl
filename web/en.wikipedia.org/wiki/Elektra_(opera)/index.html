Elektra (opera)

Elektra, Op. 58
Composer: Richard Strauss
Librettist: Hugo von Hofmannsthal
Premiere: 25 January 1909, Semperoper, Dresden

Elektra, Op. 58, is a one-act opera by Richard Strauss, to a German-language libretto by Hugo von Hofmannsthal, which he adapted from his 1903 drama of the same title. The opera was premiered at the Königliches Opernhaus in Dresden on 25 January 1909. It was the first of Strauss's many collaborations with Hofmannsthal.

Plot

The opera is based on the ancient Greek tragedy Electra by Sophocles. In the palace of Mycenae, Elektra mourns her father Agamemnon, murdered by her mother Klytämnestra and her lover Aegisth. Obsessed with vengeance, Elektra confronts her sister Chrysothemis, who longs only for ordinary life, and later Klytämnestra herself, who comes seeking relief from her nightmares. When a stranger arrives reporting the death of Elektra's exiled brother Orest, Elektra despairs—until the stranger reveals himself as Orest, returned to carry out the killings. After Klytämnestra and Aegisth are murdered within the palace, Elektra dances in ecstatic triumph until she collapses, dead.

Music

Elektra is scored for an enormous orchestra of over 110 players and is among the most dissonant works in the standard repertoire, featuring extended passages of polytonality and grinding chromaticism that mirror the heroine's psychological extremity—most famously the clash of chords in the "Agamemnon" motif and the Recognition Scene. Strauss's next opera, Der Rosenkavalier (1911), adopted a markedly more lyrical style, and he never returned to Elektra's level of dissonance.

Reception

The premiere was a success with audiences, though critics were divided by the score's violence and the subject's morbidity. The opera quickly entered the international repertoire and remains a star vehicle for dramatic sopranos; celebrated Elektras have included Astrid Varnay, Birgit Nilsson, and Hildegard Behrens.
