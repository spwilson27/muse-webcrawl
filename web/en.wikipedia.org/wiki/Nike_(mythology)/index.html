Nike (mythology)

Abode Mount Olympus

Symbols Wings, wreath, palm branch

Parents Pallas and Styx

Roman equivalent Victoria

Nike (Ancient Greek: Νίκη, "Victory") is the Greek goddess who personifies victory in any field, including art, music, war, and athletics. She is often depicted in Greek art as a winged female figure, alighting to crown a victor with a laurel wreath or to present a palm branch. Her Roman equivalent is Victoria.

Mythology

According to Hesiod's Theogony, Nike is the daughter of the Titan Pallas and the river goddess Styx, and the sister of Kratos (Strength), Bia (Force), and Zelus (Zeal). Nike and her siblings sided with Zeus in the Titanomachy, and in reward they were allowed to dwell with him on Mount Olympus forever; Nike became the divine charioteer and constant companion of Zeus and Athena.

Worship and art

Nike was worshipped together with Athena on the Acropolis of Athens, where the small Temple of Athena Nike stands at the entrance. The most famous representation of her is the Winged Victory of Samothrace (c. 200–190 BC), a Hellenistic marble now in the Louvre, depicting the goddess descending onto the prow of a ship. Nike figures also appeared on Greek coins and on the hand of the cult statue of Zeus at Olympia. Her image endures in modern culture, most familiarly as the namesake of the sportswear company Nike, Inc., whose "swoosh" logo is said to evoke her wing, and in the design of Olympic medals.
