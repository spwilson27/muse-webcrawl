Environmental justice

Environmental justice
FocusFair distribution of environmental benefits and burdens
Key eventsWarren County protests (1982); Executive Order 12898 (1994)
Key figuresRobert Bullard, Benjamin Chavis

Environmental justice is the principle that all people are entitled to equal protection from environmental hazards and equal access to environmental benefits, regardless of race, income or nationality. As a social movement it seeks fair treatment and meaningful involvement of all communities in environmental decision-making.

Origins

The movement is usually dated to the 1982 protests in Warren County, North Carolina, where a predominantly Black community resisted a landfill for PCB-contaminated soil. Sociologist Robert Bullard's Dumping in Dixie (1990) documented the disproportionate siting of waste facilities in communities of colour in the United States.

Policy

In 1994 US President Bill Clinton signed Executive Order 12898, directing federal agencies to address environmental justice in minority and low-income populations. The concept has since informed climate policy debates about the uneven burdens of pollution and global warming, and parallels movements for climate justice worldwide.
