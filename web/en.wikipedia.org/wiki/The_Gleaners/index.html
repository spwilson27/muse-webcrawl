The Gleaners

ArtistJean-François Millet
Year1857
MediumOil on canvas
Dimensions83.5 cm × 111.8 cm
LocationMusée d'Orsay, Paris

The Gleaners (French: Des glaneuses) is an oil painting by Jean-François Millet completed in 1857. It depicts three peasant women gleaning — gathering stray grains of wheat left behind after a harvest — in a field. It is regarded as one of the masterpieces of nineteenth-century French Realism.

Description

In the foreground, three women bend under the harsh sun, searching the stubble for ears of wheat missed by the harvesters. Behind them, in a luminous haze, a loaded harvest cart, stacked sheaves and a mounted overseer suggest the landowner's abundance, in pointed contrast to the women's poverty. Millet worked on the subject for a decade, producing studies and an etching before the final canvas.

Reception

Exhibited at the Salon of 1857, the painting provoked hostile reactions from upper-class critics, who saw in its monumental treatment of labourers a dangerous glorification of the rural poor in the aftermath of the revolutions of 1848. Later generations came to see it as a dignified, even monumental image of labour and an emblem of Millet's empathy for the peasantry.

Legacy

The painting is now housed in the Musée d'Orsay in Paris. It remains one of the most reproduced images of rural labour in Western art and has inspired works in many media, including Agnès Varda's documentary film The Gleaners and I (2000).
