Vincenzo Bellini

Vincenzo Salvatore Carmelo Francesco Bellini (3 November 1801 – 23 September 1835) was an Italian opera composer, best remembered for his long, flowing melodic lines, for which he was named "the Swan of Catania". His most celebrated works are La sonnambula (1831), Norma (1831) and I puritani (1835). He is one of the central figures of the bel canto style, alongside Rossini and Donizetti.

Life

Bellini was born in Catania, Sicily, into a musical family; his grandfather and father were composers and organists. A municipal scholarship sent him to the Real Collegio di Musica in Naples in 1819, where he studied under Nicola Zingarelli. His first opera, Adelson e Salvini, was performed by students in 1825, leading to a commission from the Teatro San Carlo for Bianca e Gernando (1826). Il pirata (1827), his first collaboration with the librettist Felice Romani for La Scala, Milan, made his reputation.

Major works

With Romani he produced a string of successes: La straniera (1829), Zaira (1829), I Capuleti e i Montecchi (1830), La sonnambula (1831) and Norma (1831), the latter famous for the aria "Casta Diva" and regarded as his masterpiece despite a lukewarm premiere. After a rift with Romani, Bellini wrote Beatrice di Tenda (1833) with less success. He moved to Paris, where his final opera, I puritani (1835), written for the Théâtre-Italien with a libretto by Carlo Pepoli, was a triumph.

Death

Bellini died in Puteaux, near Paris, in September 1835, aged only 33, of an intestinal inflammation. He was initially buried in Père Lachaise Cemetery; his remains were transferred to Catania Cathedral in 1876.

Style and influence

Bellini's art is distinguished by intensely expressive, long-breathed melody closely wedded to the text, restrained orchestration, and dramatic pacing that influenced Chopin's melodic style and Verdi's early operas. Wagner praised his cantilena. His operas remain tests of bel canto singing, with roles such as Norma and Amina central to the soprano repertoire.
