New Jerusalem

In the Bible, the New Jerusalem is the holy city prophesied to descend from heaven at the culmination of history, presented most fully in chapters 21 and 22 of the Book of Revelation. John of Patmos sees "the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband", in the context of "a new heaven and a new earth" after the Last Judgment. The vision draws on earlier prophetic imagery in Ezekiel, Isaiah, and Zechariah of a restored and glorified Jerusalem.

Description in Revelation

The city is described in striking geometrical and jewelled detail. It lies foursquare, 12,000 stadia in each dimension, forming a vast cube — echoing the Holy of Holies — with walls of jasper and foundations adorned with twelve precious stones corresponding to the twelve apostles. It has twelve gates, three on each side, named for the twelve tribes of Israel and guarded by twelve angels. Its streets are of pure gold "as it were transparent glass". There is no temple, "for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it", and no need of sun or moon, for the glory of God illuminates it; nothing impure may enter, and the river of the water of life and the tree of life stand within it.

Interpretations and influence

Interpretations differ among Christian traditions: many read the city literally as a future dwelling of the redeemed, while others read it symbolically as the Church, the people of God, or the renewed creation. The motif profoundly influenced hymnody ("Jerusalem the golden"), medieval and Renaissance art, and ideals of the perfect city. Distinct movements have also taken the name: the followers of Emanuel Swedenborg call their church the New Jerusalem Church, and the Latter Day Saint movement identifies a New Jerusalem to be built in America. Not to be confused with the New Jerusalem Monastery outside Moscow, a 17th-century Russian monastery built as a replica of the Holy Land.
