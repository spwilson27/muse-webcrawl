Hadhramaut (Arabic: حضرموت, Ḥaḍramawt) is a historic region situated in the southeast of the Arabian Peninsula, in modern-day eastern Yemen. It comprises a coastal plain along the Gulf of Aden, an arid plateau, and the deep canyon of Wadi Hadhramaut, one of the largest wadi systems in Arabia, whose towns — Shibam, Seiyun and Tarim — are famed for their mud-brick tower houses.

History

In antiquity the Kingdom of Hadhramaut was one of the South Arabian kingdoms enriched by the frankincense trade. The region later came under Himyarite, Persian, and then Islamic rule. In the 19th and 20th centuries the Qu'aiti and Kathiri sultanates dominated the valley under British protection as part of the Aden Protectorate, before joining South Yemen in 1967.

Culture and diaspora

Hadhrami merchants built a vast diaspora across the Indian Ocean, in East Africa, India, and Southeast Asia, spreading Islam and leaving a strong imprint on the cultures of Indonesia and Malaysia. Shibam's old walled city is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
