James Waddell Alexander II (September 19, 1888 – September 23, 1971) was an American mathematician and topologist of the pre-World War II era. A member of Princeton University's mathematics faculty and later of the Institute for Advanced Study, he was one of the founders of modern topology.

Contributions

Alexander introduced the Alexander invariant of knots and links, proved Alexander duality relating the homology of a subset of a sphere to that of its complement, and constructed the Alexander horned sphere, a pathological embedding of the 2-sphere in three-space that showed the need for care in generalising the Jordan curve theorem. With Oswald Veblen and others he helped make Princeton a world centre of topology. He also contributed to cohomology theory through the Alexander–Spanier cohomology.

Life

Born into a prominent family — his grandfather James Waddell Alexander was president of the Equitable Life Assurance Society — Alexander studied at Princeton and Göttingen, served as an officer in the First World War, and was an accomplished mountaineer. He retired from the Institute for Advanced Study in 1951 and spent his later years largely out of public life.
