Edward Michael "Bear" Grylls OBE (born 7 June 1974) is a British adventurer, author, television presenter and motivational speaker. He is best known for the television series Man vs. Wild (also known as Born Survivor in the UK), in which he demonstrates survival techniques in remote and hostile environments around the world.

Early life and military service

Grylls was educated at Eton College and the University of the West of England and Birkbeck, University of London. From 1994 to 1997 he served in the British Army Reserve with 21 SAS, specialising in combat survival, parachuting and climbing. In 1996 he broke three vertebrae in a freefall parachuting accident in Zambia when his parachute failed to open fully; he spent 18 months in rehabilitation.

Everest

On 16 May 1998, aged 23, Grylls reached the summit of Mount Everest, becoming one of the youngest Britons to do so. He had earlier spent months climbing Ama Dablam in the Himalayas as preparation. Other expeditions include crossing the North Atlantic in an inflatable boat, paramotoring over Angel Falls and the Himalayas, and circumnavigating the British Isles on a jet ski.

Television career

Following early work on Escape to the Legion (2005), Grylls fronted Man vs. Wild from 2006 to 2011, which was broadcast in over 200 countries. Later series include Running Wild with Bear Grylls, in which celebrities accompany him on adventures, The Island with Bear Grylls, Bear Grylls: Mission Survive and the interactive Netflix series You vs. Wild. In July 2009 he became the youngest ever Chief Scout of the UK Scout Association.
