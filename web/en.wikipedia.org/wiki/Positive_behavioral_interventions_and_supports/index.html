Positive behavioral interventions and supports

Positive behavioral interventions and supports (PBIS) is a school-wide framework for improving student behaviour and school climate through the explicit teaching of behavioural expectations, systematic acknowledgement of positive behaviour, and data-based decision-making. It organises support in a multi-tiered model and is used in tens of thousands of schools in the United States and internationally.

PBIS organises support into three tiers: universal (all students), targeted (some students) and intensive (few students).

The three tiers

  • Tier 1 — Universal: schools define three to five positively stated expectations (often summarised in an acronym), teach them explicitly, and acknowledge students for following them. Office discipline referral data are reviewed to guide practice.
  • Tier 2 — Targeted: students not responding to Tier 1 receive structured interventions such as Check-In/Check-Out, in which a mentor gives daily feedback on behaviour goals.
  • Tier 3 — Intensive: individualised, function-based behaviour support plans developed after a functional behavioural assessment, sometimes involving wraparound services.

Origins

PBIS emerged from applied behaviour analysis and the inclusion movement for students with disabilities. The term was written into the US Individuals with Disabilities Education Act in its 1997 reauthorisation, which required schools to consider positive behavioural interventions when a student's behaviour impedes learning. A national Center on PBIS, funded by the Office of Special Education Programs and led by researchers including George Sugai and Robert Horner, was established in 1998 to support implementation.

Evidence

Randomised controlled trials of school-wide PBIS have found reductions in office discipline referrals and suspensions and improvements in school climate and, in some studies, reading outcomes. Critics note variation in implementation fidelity and debate over the use of tangible rewards and behavioural approaches in education.
