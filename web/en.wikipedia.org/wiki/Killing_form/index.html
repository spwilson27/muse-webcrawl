Killing form

In mathematics, the Killing form, named after Wilhelm Killing, is a symmetric bilinear form that plays a basic role in the theories of Lie groups and Lie algebras. It can be defined for a Lie algebra 𝔤 over a field as the trace of the composition of two adjoint representation maps.

Definition

Let 𝔤 be a Lie algebra over a field k. The Killing form is the bilinear form B(x, y) = tr(ad(x) ∘ ad(y)), where ad(x) denotes the adjoint action of x, sending an element z to the bracket [x, z]. In terms of structure constants, B can be computed as a sum of products of those constants.

Properties

The Killing form is invariant under automorphisms of the Lie algebra and satisfies B([x, y], z) = B(x, [y, z]). A fundamental result, Cartan's criterion, states that a Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero is semisimple if and only if its Killing form is nondegenerate. For a simple Lie algebra, the Killing form is unique up to a scalar multiple and is closely related to the Casimir element and to the classification of simple Lie algebras via root systems and Dynkin diagrams.

Applications

The Killing form is used to define an invariant inner product on semisimple Lie algebras, which underlies much of representation theory and is also used in physics, where it provides the metric on the Lie algebra of a gauge group in Yang–Mills theory.
