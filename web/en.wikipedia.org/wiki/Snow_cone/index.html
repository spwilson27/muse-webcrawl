A snow cone (or sno-cone) is a variation of shaved ice dessert made with ground-up ice crystals and flavored sugar syrup, served in a paper cone or cup. Unlike Hawaiian shave ice, whose ice is finely shaved, snow cone ice is more coarsely crushed, giving a crunchier texture.

History

Snow cones became popular in the United States in the early 20th century. Samuel Bert of Dallas is often credited with inventing a snow cone machine in 1919 and selling cones at the State Fair of Texas. In Baltimore, "snowballs" made with crushed ice and simple syrups became a summertime staple during the Industrial Revolution, when ice wagons transported ice from New York to the South and children asked for shavings.

Variations

Regional relatives include Hawaiian shave ice (often topped with condensed milk and mochi), the Italian granita, and the Filipino halo-halo. Flavors range from cherry, blue raspberry, and tiger's blood to pickle juice in parts of Texas. Sugar-free versions use syrups sweetened with sucralose or sugar substitutes.

Culture

Snow cones are fixtures of fairs, carnivals, beaches, and food trucks in warm weather. They are eaten with a spoon-straw, and the melting syrup collects at the bottom of the cone.
