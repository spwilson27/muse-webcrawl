The Red Bull Air Race World Championship was an international series of air races in which competitors navigated a low-level aerial track of inflatable pylons in the fastest time while executing prescribed aerobatic manoeuvres. Established by the energy drink company Red Bull in 2003, it was sanctioned in its later years as an official world championship by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale.

Format

Pilots flew high-performance aerobatic aircraft — types such as the Edge 540, MXS-R and Extra 300 — at speeds up to about 370 km/h through pairs of 25-metre inflatable pylons ("Air Gates"), enduring forces of up to 10 g in turns. Qualifying determined the order for knockout rounds (Round of 14, Round of 8), culminating in a Final 4. Time penalties were imposed for incorrect gate passage, exceeding g limits or hitting a pylon. Alongside the Master Class, the Challenger Class served as a development category for rising pilots.

History

The concept grew out of Red Bull's earlier Flugtag and Air Race events; the first races were held in 2003 at Zeltweg, Austria, and Budapest, Hungary. The series expanded to venues worldwide, including spectacular settings over the Danube, Sydney Harbour, Rio de Janeiro, Abu Dhabi and Ascot Racecourse. The championship was suspended from 2011 to 2013 for restructuring and safety improvements, resumed in 2014, and was discontinued after the 2019 season, with Red Bull citing insufficient outside commercial interest.
