Apple IIe

The Apple IIe (styled as ][e) is the third model in the Apple II series of personal computers produced by Apple Computer. The "e" stood for enhanced, reflecting built-in features that previously required add-on cards, including 64 KB of RAM, an 80-column text display capability, and lowercase letter display. Introduced in January 1983, it became the longest-lived model in the Apple II family, remaining in production until November 1993.

Features

The IIe used custom chips to reduce the component count and cost of earlier models. Its keyboard supported both upper- and lowercase characters and full ASCII input. With an Extended 80-Column Text Card, memory rose to 128 KB and the machine could display 80 columns of text, a key requirement for business and education software such as AppleWorks, which became one of the platform's flagship applications. The IIe retained seven expansion slots, preserving compatibility with the large library of Apple II peripheral cards and software.

Variants

Later revisions included the Enhanced IIe (1985), which adopted the 65C02 processor and character-ROM improvements matching the Apple IIc, and the Platinum IIe (1987), with a light-grey case, numeric keypad, and reduced electromagnetic interference. The IIe Card allowed early Macintosh LC models to run Apple IIe software, extending the platform's life in schools well into the 1990s.

Legacy

Ubiquitous in North American classrooms, the IIe anchored Apple's dominance in education computing for a decade, and its combination of durability and software compatibility made it one of the most successful 8-bit computers ever sold. See also Apple IIc and Apple IIGS.
