Limit order

Limit order
TypeSecurities trading order
MeaningBuy or sell at a specified price or better
Contrasted withMarket order, stop order

A limit order is an order to buy or sell a security at a specific price or better. A buy limit order can only be executed at the limit price or lower, and a sell limit order can only be executed at the limit price or higher. It is a way for traders to control the price at which they trade, at the cost of not being guaranteed execution — the order rests in the market's order book until the market reaches the specified price, the order is cancelled, or it expires.

Limit orders are fundamental to the operation of modern exchanges: the collection of outstanding limit orders forms the limit order book, and the interaction of limit orders with market orders determines the bid–ask spread and the depth of the market.

Execution

When a buy limit order's price equals or exceeds the best available selling price, it is matched immediately against resting sell orders. Otherwise it joins the queue on the bid side, where price–time priority generally applies: orders at better prices execute first, and among orders at the same price, those placed earlier execute first. If the market never trades at the limit price, the order may never fill, fully or partially.

Comparison with market orders

A market order trades immediately at the best available price, guaranteeing execution but not price; a limit order guarantees price but not execution. Traders supplying limit orders are said to provide liquidity, and on many exchanges they receive maker rebates, while market-order traders pay fees.

Variations

Common instructions include day orders and good-til-cancelled orders; immediate-or-cancel and fill-or-kill orders; stop-limit orders, which become limit orders once a stop price is reached; and iceberg orders, which display only part of their quantity. The limit price may also be set aggressively, "crossing the spread" to obtain an immediate fill at a protected worst price.
