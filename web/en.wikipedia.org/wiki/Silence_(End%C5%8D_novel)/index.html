Silence (Endō novel)

AuthorShūsaku Endō
Original titleChinmoku (沈黙)
Published1966

Silence (Japanese: 沈黙, Chinmoku) is a 1966 historical novel by Japanese author Shūsaku Endō, generally considered his masterpiece. It won the Tanizaki Prize.

Plot

In the 1630s, the young Portuguese Jesuit Sebastião Rodrigues travels to Japan with a companion to find his former teacher, Father Ferreira, who is rumoured to have apostatised under persecution, and to minister to the hidden Christians (Kakure Kirishitan) suffering under the Tokugawa shogunate's suppression of Christianity. Captured by the authorities, Rodrigues is forced to watch Christians tortured and ultimately confronts the silence of God in the face of suffering — culminating in his decision about whether to trample the fumie, an image of Christ.

Adaptations

The novel was filmed by Masahiro Shinoda in 1971 and by Martin Scorsese in 2016, starring Andrew Garfield and Adam Driver.
