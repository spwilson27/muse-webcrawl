Competition (economics)

[Image: rival coffee shops side by side on a high street competing for customers]

Competition in economics is the rivalry among sellers (or buyers) in a Market to attract customers — by lower prices, better quality, innovation or service. It is the engine that, in Adam Smith's account, disciplines self-interest: firms that serve consumers badly lose out to rivals.

Forms and effects

The benchmark of perfect competition — many small firms, homogeneous products, free entry and full information — yields efficient outcomes in theory, and anchors antitrust thinking; real industries range from this pole through monopolistic competition and Oligopoly to Monopoly. Competition policy (antitrust in the US) prohibits cartels and abuse of dominance and reviews mergers, administered by bodies such as the Federal Trade Commission, the European Commission and the UK Competition and Markets Authority. Economists debate how competition affects innovation — Schumpeter argued monopoly rents fund research; Kenneth Arrow that competitive firms gain more from inventing — and how the rise of dominant digital platforms changes the analysis.
