Mobile virtual network operator

A mobile virtual network operator (MVNO) is a wireless communications services provider that does not own the wireless network infrastructure over which it provides services to its customers. An MVNO enters into a business agreement with a mobile network operator (MNO) to obtain bulk access to network services at wholesale rates, then sets retail prices independently for its own subscribers.

Business model

MVNOs range from "thin" or branded resellers, which rely almost entirely on the host operator's systems and provide mainly marketing, sales and customer care, to "full" MVNOs, which operate their own core network elements — such as HLR/HSS, billing and value-added services platforms — and issue their own SIM cards and mobile network codes, leasing only the radio access network. Between these sit service providers and "light" MVNOs. Intermediary firms called mobile virtual network enablers (MVNEs) supply the technical platform for companies that want to launch an MVNO without building infrastructure.

History

The concept emerged in Europe in the late 1990s as regulators sought to increase competition without new spectrum. Denmark and the United Kingdom were early markets; Virgin Mobile, launched in the UK in 1999, became the emblematic discount MVNO brand. The model spread worldwide, with notable examples including Tracfone in the United States, Tesco Mobile, giffgaff, Consumer Cellular, Google Fi and countless niche operators serving ethnic, youth, business or machine-to-machine (IoT) segments.

Regulation

Whether MVNOs can flourish depends heavily on regulation and wholesale terms. Some regulators mandate wholesale access or set reference offers; in other markets access is purely commercial, and some operators have historically resisted hosting MVNOs. MVNO market share varies from negligible to more than a tenth of subscribers in mature markets such as Germany and the Netherlands.
