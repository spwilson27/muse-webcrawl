Amy Winehouse

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Amy Winehouse performing in 2007: the beehive, the winged eyeliner, tattoos and a microphone — the defining image of her brief fame]

Amy Jade Winehouse (14 September 1983 – 23 July 2011) was an English singer and songwriter whose deep contralto and jazz-soaked songs made her the defining British voice of her generation. Her second album Back to Black (2006), produced by Mark Ronson and Salaam Remi, fused sixties girl-group and soul sounds with bleakly witty writing about addiction and bad love, won five Grammy Awards in one night (2008), and became one of the best-selling albums of the century.

Her first album Frank (2003) had announced a jazz prodigy from a musical North London family — her father a cab driver and amateur singer, her idols Dinah Washington and Sarah Vaughan. 'Rehab', 'You Know I'm No Good', 'Love Is a Losing Game' and her version of the Zutons' 'Valerie' (with Ronson, 2007) are her signature recordings — song titles cited here, in keeping with this project's rules.

Fame and unravelling

Winehouse's beehive, tattoos and Camden chaos made her a tabloid industry; her marriage to Blake Fielder-Civil (2007–09) and their addictions were its fuel. Performances swung between genius and collapse — the 2011 Belgrade show, booed as she stumbled, ended her last tour. She died at home in Camden of alcohol poisoning in July 2011, aged 27, joining the grim '27 Club' roll of Hendrix, Joplin, Morrison and Cobain.

Legacy

Back to Black reshaped British pop toward retro-soul and opened the door for Adele and others; the documentary Amy (2015) won the Academy Award and indicted the entourage that failed her, and the biopic Back to Black followed in 2024. Camden keeps her statue, unveiled in 2014, in the Stables Market.
