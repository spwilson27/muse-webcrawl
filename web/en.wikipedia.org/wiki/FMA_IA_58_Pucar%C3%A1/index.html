The FMA IA 58 Pucará (Quechua for "fortress") is an Argentine ground-attack and counter-insurgency (COIN) aircraft built by the Fábrica Militar de Aviones (FMA). It is a twin-turboprop, tandem-seat, low-wing monoplane designed for operations from short, rough airstrips in support of ground forces. The Pucará gained international attention during the 1982 Falklands War.

Design and development

Development began in 1966 in response to an Argentine Air Force requirement for a counter-insurgency aircraft. The design team, led by engineer Héctor Eduardo Ruiz, produced an aircraft powered by two Turbomeca Astazou XVIG turboprops driving three-bladed propellers. The crew of two (pilot and observer) sit in tandem under a single canopy, on Martin-Baker ejection seats, with armor protection around the cockpit and engines. Armament comprises two 20 mm Hispano-Suiza HS.804 cannon and four 7.62 mm FN Browning machine guns in the fuselage, plus up to about 1,500 kg of external stores — bombs, rockets, and fuel tanks — on three hardpoints.

Operational history

The Pucará entered service with the Argentine Air Force in 1975 and was used in counter-insurgency operations during Argentina's internal conflict. During the Falklands War of 1982, Pucarás were deployed to the islands, operating from Port Stanley airport and grass strips at Goose Green and Pebble Island. They flew ground-attack and reconnaissance sorties; several were destroyed on the ground — six by the SAS raid on Pebble Island on 15 May 1982 — and some were shot down, including one credited to a Sea Harrier. Captain Jorge Benítez's Pucará shot down a Royal Navy Westland Scout helicopter, one of the few Argentine air-to-air victories of the war. Captured examples were taken to Britain for evaluation; one is preserved at the Royal Air Force Museum Cosford.

Operators and variants

Export sales were limited: Uruguay and Colombia operated small numbers, and Sri Lanka acquired Pucarás for use against the Tamil Tigers in the 1990s. A single-seat variant, the IA-58C Pucará Charlie, flew in prototype form but was cancelled. Argentina developed the upgraded Pucará Delta with new avionics and Pratt & Whitney PT6 engines, and later the Fénix intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance conversion, as the type's service life was extended into the 2020s.
