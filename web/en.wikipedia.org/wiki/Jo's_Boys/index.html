Jo's Boys, and How They Turned Out: A Sequel to "Little Men" is a novel by American author Louisa May Alcott, published in 1886. It is the third and final book in the Little Women series, following Little Women (1868) and Little Men (1871). The novel revisits Josephine "Jo" Bhaer, now a famous writer and co-founder with her husband Professor Bhaer of the experimental Plumfield school, and follows the fates of the boys raised there ten years on.

Plot

The story interweaves vignettes of the former pupils: Franz and Emil at sea, Nat pursuing music in Germany, Dan seeking his fortune in the West — where his wild nature leads to tragedy and a brief imprisonment — and Tom in medical school. Jo's own sons and the March sisters' families frame the narrative, which closes with several marriages and a harvest festival at Plumfield. Alcott also gives Jo passages reflecting on fame, women's suffrage and the lives of independent women, which many readers take as partly autobiographical.

Reception

Alcott wrote the sequel under pressure from readers eager to know "how they turned out". It was her last March family novel, published two years before her death. Although less famous than Little Women, Jo's Boys completes the saga and is notable for its treatment of Dan, the most morally complex of the boys, whose story ends with hinted redemption rather than full triumph.
