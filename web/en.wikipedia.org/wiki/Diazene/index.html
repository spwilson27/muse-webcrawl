Diazene

Diazene, also called diimide, is a compound with the formula HN=NH. It exists as two geometric isomers, cis and trans. Diazene is a transient species, generated in situ and best known as a reagent for the hydrogenation of alkenes and alkynes.

Reactivity

Diimide reduces carbon–carbon multiple bonds by delivering hydrogen through a concerted, cyclic transition state, releasing nitrogen gas. Because the reduction is stereospecifically syn and tolerates many other functional groups, and proceeds under metal-free conditions, it is valuable where catalytic hydrogenation with H2 and a metal catalyst would be incompatible — for example with sulfur-containing substrates that poison catalysts.

Generation

Diazene can be generated by oxidation of hydrazine, by decomposition of sulfonyl hydrazides, or from azodicarboxylates such as potassium azodicarboxylate under acidic conditions. It readily disproportionates to hydrazine and nitrogen.
