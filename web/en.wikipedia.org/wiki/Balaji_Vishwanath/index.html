Balaji Vishwanath

Balaji Vishwanath Bhatt (1662 – 2 April 1720) was the sixth Peshwa (prime minister) of the Maratha Confederacy, appointed in 1713. He is credited with consolidating the power of Chhatrapati Shahu I and establishing the Peshwa's office as a hereditary position, laying the foundation for the Peshwa-dominated phase of Maratha history.

Early life

Balaji Vishwanath was a Chitpavan Brahmin from the western coast of Maharashtra. He began his career as a revenue official and clerk, serving variously under the Marathas and, for a time, in local administrations in the Deccan. His abilities as a diplomat and financier brought him to the attention of Shahu after Shahu's release from Mughal captivity in 1707.

Rise to Peshwa

Balaji played a key role in Shahu's victory in the civil war against his aunt Tarabai. He negotiated alliances, secured defections from the rival court at Kolhapur, and raised funds for Shahu's cause. In November 1713, Shahu appointed him Peshwa. In 1719 Balaji led a Maratha force to Delhi alongside the Sayyid brothers and obtained imperial grants (sanads) recognising Shahu's right to collect chauth and sardeshmukhi in the six Mughal provinces of the Deccan — a landmark legitimisation of Maratha power.

Legacy

Balaji died in 1720 and was succeeded by his son, the celebrated Baji Rao I, beginning a century in which the Peshwaship remained in his family. Historians credit Balaji Vishwanath with creating the confederate structure of the Maratha state and converting the office of Peshwa into its effective executive centre.
