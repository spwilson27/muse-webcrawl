Euler–Maclaurin formula

Euler–Maclaurin formula
Field: Mathematical analysis
Developed by: Leonhard Euler, Colin Maclaurin (c. 1735)
Relates: Finite sums and integrals, via Bernoulli numbers

The Euler–Maclaurin formula is a formula for the difference between an integral and a closely related sum. It can be used to approximate integrals by finite sums, or conversely to evaluate sums using integrals and a machine that can compute integrals. The formula expresses a sum of a function over integers in terms of the corresponding integral, half the sum of endpoint values, and correction terms involving Bernoulli numbers multiplied by derivatives of the function at the endpoints.

Uses

The formula underlies asymptotic expansions such as Stirling's approximation for factorials, provides error estimates for the trapezoidal rule in numerical integration, and is used in analytic number theory, for example in estimating sums of powers and the harmonic numbers. The remainder can be expressed using periodic Bernoulli functions, which form the basis of Euler–Maclaurin summation in numerical analysis.
