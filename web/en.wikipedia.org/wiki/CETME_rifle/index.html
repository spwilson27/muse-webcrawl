CETME rifle

CETME
Type: Battle rifle / assault rifle
Origin: Spain
Designer: Ludwig Vorgrimler
Designed: 1949–1957
Cartridge: 7.62×51mm NATO (Model C)

The CETME is a Spanish selective-fire battle rifle developed at the Centro de Estudios Técnicos de Materiales Especiales (CETME) in the 1950s, principally by German engineer Ludwig Vorgrimler. It uses a roller-delayed blowback action derived from wartime German designs.

History

The CETME Model C, chambered for 7.62×51mm NATO, became the standard rifle of the Spanish armed forces from 1957 and was widely exported. Its design rights and tooling influenced the German Heckler & Koch G3, which shares the roller-delayed mechanism. Later models included the 5.56mm CETME Model L of the 1980s.

Design

The rifle features a fluted chamber to ease extraction, a stamped-steel receiver, and wooden (later polymer) furniture. It feeds from 20-round box magazines and is effective to about 600 metres.
