La Haine

La Haine (French pronunciation: [la ɛn], "Hate") is a 1995 French social drama film written, co-edited, and directed by Mathieu Kassovitz. Shot in black and white, it follows three young friends in a poor immigrant district of Paris over 24 hours following a riot sparked by police brutality. The film is regarded as a landmark of modern French cinema.

Plot

The film opens after a night of rioting in a banlieue housing estate, during which a young resident, Abdel, lies in a coma after being beaten in police custody, and an officer has lost a revolver. Vinz (Vincent Cassel), a Jewish youth burning with resentment, has found the gun and vows to kill a policeman if Abdel dies. Saïd (Saïd Taghmaoui), of North African descent, and Hubert (Hubert Koundé), an Afro-French boxer whose gym was burned in the riots, try to restrain him. After a trip into central Paris goes badly wrong, the trio return to the estate; the film ends in a sudden, shocking confrontation.

A recurring motif is the story of a man falling from a skyscraper, reassuring himself on each floor: "jusqu'ici tout va bien" — "so far, so good." "The important thing is not the fall, but the landing."

Reception

Kassovitz won Best Director at the 1995 Cannes Film Festival, and the film won the César Award for Best Film. It made Vincent Cassel a star and is frequently ranked among the greatest films ever made; in 2022 it entered the Sight and Sound critics' poll of the 250 greatest films.

Context

The film was partly inspired by the 1993 death of Makomé M'Bowolé, a Zairian teenager shot in police custody in Paris, and addressed tensions between police and marginalized youth in the banlieues that later erupted in the 2005 French riots.
