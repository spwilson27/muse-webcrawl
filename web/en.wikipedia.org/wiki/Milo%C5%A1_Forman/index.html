Jan Tomáš "Miloš" Forman (18 February 1932 – 13 April 2018) was a Czech-American film director, screenwriter, and professor, regarded as one of the most important filmmakers of his generation. He won Academy Awards for Best Director for One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest (1975) and Amadeus (1984).

Early life and Czechoslovak career

Forman lost both parents in Nazi concentration camps and was raised by relatives. A leading figure of the Czechoslovak New Wave, he made Black Peter (1964), Loves of a Blonde (1965), and The Firemen's Ball (1967), satires of everyday life whose gentle mockery of authority drew official censure; The Firemen's Ball was banned after the 1968 Warsaw Pact invasion, during which Forman was abroad.

American career

Settling in the United States, Forman directed Taking Off (1971) and then One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest, which won all five major Academy Awards. His version of the musical Hair (1979), the period drama Ragtime (1981), and Amadeus — his portrait of Mozart and Salieri, filmed partly in Prague — followed. Later films include Valmont, The People vs. Larry Flynt (1996), Man on the Moon (1999), and Goya's Ghosts (2006).

Legacy

Forman's films characteristically pit free-spirited individuals against rigid institutions, a theme shaped by his experience of both Nazism and communism. He served as a professor of film at Columbia University and died in Connecticut in 2018.
