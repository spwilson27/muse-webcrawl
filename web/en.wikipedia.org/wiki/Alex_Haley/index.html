Overview

Alexander Murray Palmer Haley (1921–1992) was an American writer, best known for Roots: The Saga of an American Family (1976), which traced his ancestry from Kunta Kinte, a Mandinka youth captured in the Gambia in 1767 and enslaved in Virginia, through generations to Haley himself. The book — which Haley called "faction", a blend of fact and fiction — won a special Pulitzer citation, and its 1977 ABC miniseries adaptation was watched by an estimated 130 million Americans, igniting mass interest in genealogy and African-American history.

Career and controversies

Haley served in the US Coast Guard and became a journalist; his interviews for Playboy (with Miles Davis, Martin Luther King Jr. and others) were a landmark of the form, and he co-authored The Autobiography of Malcolm X (1965). Roots later faced a plagiarism settlement with Harold Courlander (whose novel The African contained passages Haley acknowledged had reached his book) and scholarly challenges to details of its Gambian research — debates that continue alongside its cultural impact.
