Liao River

The Liao River (simplified Chinese: 辽河; traditional Chinese: 遼河) is the principal river in southern Northeast China, and one of the seven main river systems in China. It flows through Hebei, Inner Mongolia, Jilin and Liaoning provinces for about 1,345 kilometres (836 mi) before emptying into Liaodong Bay of the Bohai Sea.

Course

The river is formed by the confluence of the Xiliao River (west Liao) and Dongliao River (east Liao) near Changtu in Liaoning. Its main tributaries include the Hun River, Taizi River and Liu River. The Liao River plain is a major agricultural and industrial region, with cities such as Shenyang located in its basin via tributaries.

History and name

The Liao gave its name to Liaoning Province and to the Liao dynasty (907–1125) founded by the Khitan people. The river historically changed course and carried heavy sediment loads; flood control and dam projects in the 20th century reshaped its lower reaches, and since 1958 the Liao's main course has flowed via the Shuangtaizi River.
