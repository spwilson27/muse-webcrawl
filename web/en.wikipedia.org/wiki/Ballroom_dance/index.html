Ballroom dance is a set of partner dances that are enjoyed socially and competitively around the world, largely because of their entertainment and performance appeal. The term derives from the Latin ballare, "to dance", and originally denoted dancing performed in a large room or ballroom at formal gatherings. In its competitive form, ballroom is known as DanceSport and is regulated internationally by the World Dance Council and the World DanceSport Federation.

Styles

International style

The International Standard dances — modern waltz, tango, Viennese waltz, slow foxtrot and quickstep — are danced in closed hold, with couples maintaining body contact. The International Latin dances — cha-cha-cha, samba, rumba, paso doble and jive — use more open positions, hip action and expressive arm styling.

American style

The American Smooth repertoire (waltz, tango, foxtrot, Viennese waltz) permits partners to separate and dance apart, while American Rhythm (cha-cha, rumba, East Coast swing, bolero, mambo) parallels the Latin dances.

History

Social partner dancing in European courts evolved from folk and country dances into the minuet-dominated balls of the 17th–18th centuries. The waltz, arriving around 1800, scandalised society with its close embrace before conquering the ballroom. In the early 20th century, English teachers — notably through the Imperial Society of Teachers of Dancing (founded 1904) — codified technique: a 1920s conference standardised the English (International) style, and Victor Silvester and others popularised strict-tempo ballroom music. Latin American and American dances (tango, rumba, foxtrot, swing) flowed into the ballroom from the Americas in the same decades. The most prestigious competitions include the Blackpool Dance Festival (begun 1920) in England.

Competition and teaching

DanceSport couples progress through syllabus levels (bronze, silver, gold) and graded classes to open amateur and professional ranks, judged on timing, posture, technique, partnership and presentation. DanceSport was recognised by the International Olympic Committee in 1997, though it has not been included in the Olympic programme; related styles such as breaking have since appeared at the Games. Ballroom dancing is also taught widely as a social skill, and television shows such as Strictly Come Dancing and Dancing with the Stars have driven surges in enrolment.
