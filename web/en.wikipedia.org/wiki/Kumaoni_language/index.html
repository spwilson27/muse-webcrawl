Kumaoni language

Kumaoni (कुमाऊँनी, Kumāūnnī) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken by over two million people in the Kumaon region of the Indian state of Uttarakhand and by communities in neighbouring areas of Nepal. It is one of the Central Pahari languages and is closely related to Garhwali, spoken in the adjoining Garhwal region.

Distribution and dialects

Kumaoni is spoken in the districts of Almora, Bageshwar, Champawat, Nainital, Pithoragarh, and Udham Singh Nagar in Uttarakhand, as well as parts of Himachal Pradesh. Grierson's Linguistic Survey of India identified numerous local varieties, including Askoti, Bhabari, Chaugarkhiya, Danpuriya, Gangoli, Johari, Khasparjiya, Kumaiyya, Pachhai, Phaldakotiya, Rau-Chaubyansi, and Sirali, broadly grouped into eastern, western, northern, southern, and central dialects.

Script and literature

The language is written in the Devanagari script. Kumaoni has a rich oral tradition of folk tales, songs, and ballads, and a growing body of modern literature, including the work of poets such as Shailesh Matiyani and Sher Singh Bisht. The 2011 Census of India recorded about 2.08 million speakers.

Status

UNESCO lists Kumaoni as a "definitely endangered" language, reflecting declining intergenerational transmission as younger speakers shift to Hindi. Advocates have sought its inclusion in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution of India, and it is taught and studied at institutions such as Kumaon University in Nainital.
