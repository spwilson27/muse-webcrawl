Russian Alaska

Russian Alaska, formally Russian America, was the period of the colonial possessions of the Russian Empire in North America, from the 1780s (with Russian activity dating to the 1740s) until the Alaska Purchase of 1867, when the territory was sold to the United States.

Colonisation

Following Vitus Bering's voyages of 1728 and 1741, Russian promyshlenniki (fur traders) crossed to the Aleutian Islands and Alaska Peninsula in pursuit of sea otter pelts, often brutally exploiting the Indigenous Aleut (Unangax) population. Grigory Shelikhov founded the first permanent Russian settlement at Three Saints Bay on Kodiak Island in 1784. In 1799 Tsar Paul I chartered the Russian-American Company, which administered the colony under chief manager Alexander Baranov and moved the capital to New Archangel (Sitka), with its distinctive Russian Orthodox Cathedral of St. Michael.

Sale to the United States

By the 1860s the colony was unprofitable, sea otters were depleted, and Russia feared losing Alaska to Britain in a future war without compensation. Negotiations with Secretary of State William H. Seward produced the Treaty of Cession, signed on 30 March 1867, selling Alaska for US$7.2 million. The formal transfer took place at Sitka on 18 October 1867, a date now celebrated as Alaska Day.
