Companies Act 2006

The Companies Act 2006 (c 46) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom which forms the primary source of UK company law. It is one of the longest pieces of legislation ever passed by Parliament, containing 1,300 sections and 16 schedules, and received royal assent on 8 November 2006. The Act was brought into force in stages, with full implementation completed on 1 October 2009, replacing the Companies Act 1985 and related legislation.

Background

The Act was the culmination of the Company Law Review, a review begun in 1998 by the Department of Trade and Industry under the principle of "think small first" — designing the law primarily with small private companies in mind. It consolidated and reformed the law governing the formation, management, and dissolution of companies in England and Wales, Scotland, and (with variations) Northern Ireland.

Key provisions

  • Formation: a company may be formed by a single person; the memorandum of association became a short formation document, and the articles of association the key constitutional document. A statement of capital replaced the authorised share capital.
  • Directors' duties: the Act codified for the first time the general duties of directors in sections 171–177, including the duty in section 172 to promote the success of the company for the benefit of its members as a whole, having regard to employees, the community, and the environment.
  • Derivative claims: a new statutory procedure allowed shareholders to bring claims on behalf of the company for wrongs committed by directors.
  • Private companies: requirements were simplified — private companies no longer required a company secretary or annual general meetings by default, and written resolutions were made easier.
  • Accounts and audit: filing deadlines were shortened and small-company exemptions extended.
  • Shareholder meetings and communications: electronic communication with shareholders and web-based publication of documents were facilitated.
  • Takeovers: the Act placed the Panel on Takeovers and Mergers on a statutory footing.

Directors' duties

Sections 171 to 177 set out seven general duties: to act within powers; to promote the success of the company; to exercise independent judgment; to exercise reasonable care, skill and diligence; to avoid conflicts of interest; not to accept benefits from third parties; and to declare interests in proposed transactions. These duties are owed to the company and are enforceable as fiduciary and common-law duties, drawing on the pre-existing case law which the Act largely codified.

Commencement and amendments

Provisions were commenced in tranches between January 2007 and October 2009. Subsequent reforms, including the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023, have amended parts of the 2006 Act, particularly on identity verification and the powers of Companies House. The Act nevertheless remains the foundation of UK company law.
