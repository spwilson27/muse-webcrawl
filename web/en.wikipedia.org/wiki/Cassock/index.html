Cassock

The cassock, also called a soutane, is a Christian clerical garment: a long, close-fitting, ankle-length robe fastened with buttons down the front, traditionally worn by clergy of the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican and some Reformed churches.

History and forms

Derived from the ancient tunic and standardised in the early modern period, the Roman cassock is worn with a sash (fascia) and may be piped in colours denoting rank: black for priests, purple piping for monsignors, scarlet for cardinals, and white for the Pope. Eastern riassa is a looser outer cassock worn over an inner cassock.

Use

Once everyday dress for clergy, it is now chiefly liturgical and formal wear in the West, though it remains daily dress for many Orthodox and traditional Catholic clergy. See Vestment.
