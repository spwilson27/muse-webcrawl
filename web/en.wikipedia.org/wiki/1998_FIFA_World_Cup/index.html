1998 FIFA World Cup

The 1998 FIFA World Cup was the 16th FIFA World Cup, held in France from 10 June to 12 July 1998. It was the first World Cup expanded to 32 teams. Hosts France won their first title, defeating defending champions Brazil 3–0 in the final.


Host: France
Dates: 10 June – 12 July 1998
Teams: 32
Champions: France
Runners-up: Brazil
Top scorer: Davor Šuker (6 goals)

Final

At the Stade de France in Saint-Denis, Zinedine Zidane scored two headed goals from corners in the first half and Emmanuel Petit added a third in stoppage time. Ronaldo, Brazil's star striker, had suffered a convulsive fit before the match and played below his best.

Notable events

Croatia, in their first World Cup as an independent nation, finished third, with Davor Šuker winning the Golden Boot. England were eliminated by Argentina on penalties after David Beckham was sent off for kicking Diego Simeone; Michael Owen scored a celebrated solo goal in that match. The tournament introduced the golden goal in the knockout stage, first used by Laurent Blanc for France against Paraguay.

Venues

Matches were played in ten stadiums including Paris, Marseille, Lyon, Bordeaux and the new Stade de France.
