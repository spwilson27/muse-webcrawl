European hornet

The European hornet (Vespa crabro) is the largest eusocial wasp native to Europe. It is also the only true hornet (genus Vespa) found in North America, having been introduced there by European settlers in the 19th century. Queens reach about 35 mm in length, with workers typically 18–25 mm.

Description

The species has a reddish-brown thorax and head, and an abdomen banded in brown and yellow. Colonies are usually founded in hollow trees, chimneys, or wall cavities, where the queen builds a paper nest from chewed wood fibre. A mature colony may contain several hundred workers.

Behaviour

European hornets are largely nocturnal foragers and are attracted to lights at night. They prey on other insects, including grasshoppers, flies, and honey bees, and also feed on sap and fallen fruit. Despite their size, they are generally not aggressive unless a nest is disturbed; their sting is comparable to that of other large wasps. In Germany the species is legally protected.

Distribution

Native across temperate Europe and Asia as far east as Japan, the hornet has established populations in eastern North America, where it is widespread but considered only a minor pest.
