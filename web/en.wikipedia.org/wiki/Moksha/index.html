Moksha (Sanskrit: 'release, liberation') is the final release from samsara, the cycle of birth, death and rebirth, in Hinduism, Jainism and Sikhism. In Hindu thought it is the fourth and highest aim of life (after dharma, artha and kama), attained through self-knowledge, devotion or disciplined action, when the soul recognises its true nature and is no longer bound by Karma.

Paths

The Bhagavad Gita integrates three paths: knowledge (jnana), devotion (bhakti) and selfless action (karma yoga). Vedanta schools dispute the result's nature — for Advaita, total identity of self and Brahman; for Dvaita, eternal loving distinction. Jain moksha is the soul's ascent to the top of the universe after all karma is shed; Sikh mukti is union with God through devotion and grace while living righteously in the world.
