Laura Alicia Esquivel Valdés (born 30 September 1950) is a Mexican novelist, screenwriter, and politician who first came to international attention for the novel Como agua para chocolate (Like Water for Chocolate) (1989). The novel of magical realism, structured around monthly instalments of recipes from the kitchen of its heroine Tita, became a best seller in Mexico and abroad and was translated into dozens of languages. The film adaptation, directed by her then-husband Alfonso Arau with a screenplay by Esquivel, became the highest-grossing foreign-language film ever released in the United States at the time.

Life and career

Esquivel was born in Mexico City, the third of four children of a telegraph operator and a homemaker. She trained and worked for eight years as a kindergarten teacher before co-founding a children's theatre workshop and writing for children's television. She has described her grandmother's kitchen as the formative space of her fiction, and food, domestic labour, and female experience run throughout her work.

Works

After Like Water for Chocolate, Esquivel published the novels The Law of Love (La ley del amor, 1995), an early multimedia novel issued with a CD; Swift as Desire (Tan veloz como el deseo, 2001); Malinche (2006), a fictional biography of the interpreter of Hernán Cortés; and Piercing the Darkness (A Lupita le gustaba planchar, 2014). She also wrote the screenplay for Chido One and other film and television projects, and published two sequels continuing the story of Like Water for Chocolate — El diario de Tita (2016) and Mi negro pasado (2017).

Politics

Esquivel entered politics with the National Regeneration Movement (Morena) and served as a federal deputy in the Mexican Chamber of Deputies for the LXIII Legislature (2015–2018), representing a district of Mexico City and working on cultural affairs.
