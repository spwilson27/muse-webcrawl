Gymnotiformes

The Gymnotiformes are an order of teleost bony fishes commonly known as Neotropical knifefish or South American knifefish. They have long bodies and swim using undulations of their elongated anal fin. Found predominantly in fresh water, most species are nocturnal and are capable of generating weak electric fields, which they use for navigation, object detection (electrolocation) and communication.

Taxonomy and distribution

The order contains about 250 valid species divided among roughly 34 genera and five families: Apteronotidae (ghost knifefishes), Gymnotidae (banded knifefishes, including the electric eel), Hypopomidae (bluntnose knifefishes), Rhamphichthyidae (sand knifefishes) and Sternopygidae (glass knifefishes). They are distributed throughout the humid Neotropics, from Guatemala and northern Argentina to the Río de la Plata. Fossil gymnotiforms are known from the late Miocene of Bolivia.

Electric organs

Gymnotiforms possess electric organs derived from modified muscle or nerve cells that produce an electric organ discharge (EOD). Most species are weakly electric, but the electric eel (Electrophorus electricus, now classified within the Gymnotidae) can generate discharges of several hundred volts used in predation and defence. The EOD varies in waveform and frequency between species and is used in social signalling and in jamming avoidance responses.
