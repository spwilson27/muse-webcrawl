Southdale Center

Southdale Center is a shopping mall in Edina, Minnesota, a suburb of Minneapolis. Opened on 8 October 1956, it is widely regarded as the first fully enclosed, climate-controlled shopping mall in the United States, and it defined the template for the American regional mall.

Design

Southdale was designed by Austrian-born architect Victor Gruen for the Dayton Company. Gruen arranged two department store anchors around a central air-conditioned "garden court" with trees, fountains and cafes, intending the mall to serve as a civic and social centre for the suburbs, not merely a retail machine. The original complex cost about $20 million and drew some 75,000 visitors on its opening day.

History

The mall was expanded and remodelled repeatedly, adding further anchors and wings. Like most American malls it faced the loss of department stores in the 2010s, responding with redevelopment that added apartments, a hotel, fitness and medical uses, and new retail formats on its large site. Southdale's influence was enormous: Gruen's concept was copied across North America, though Gruen himself later disavowed what the suburban mall became.
