Berkshire is a ceremonial and historic county in South East England, lying largely in the Thames Valley west of London. It is officially the Royal County of Berkshire, a title granted because Windsor Castle, the largest inhabited castle in the world and a principal residence of the British monarch, stands within it. The county town is Reading, the county's largest settlement.

Geography

The River Thames forms most of Berkshire's northern boundary, with the Kennet flowing through its south and centre. In the west the chalk Berkshire Downs — part of the North Wessex Downs Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty — contain Walbury Hill (297 m), the county's highest point, and the prehistoric Ridgeway path and the Uffington White Horse lie near its border. East Berkshire, closer to London, is heavily urbanised and forms part of the capital's commuter belt and the "Silicon Corridor" of technology companies along the M4 motorway.

History and government

Berkshire was one of the oldest Saxon counties, named from a large birch wood (Bearroc). Alfred the Great was born at Wantage in 849, then in Berkshire. The county was the scene of the Battle of Ashdown (871) and, in the Civil War, the two Battles of Newbury (1643, 1644). Boundary changes in 1974 transferred the Vale of White Horse, including Abingdon and Wantage, to Oxfordshire and moved Slough and Eton from Buckinghamshire into Berkshire. Since 1998 the county council has been abolished and Berkshire is administered by six unitary authorities — Reading, Slough, Windsor and Maidenhead, Wokingham, Bracknell Forest and West Berkshire — with the ceremonial county retained for lieutenancy purposes.

Economy and culture

Reading and Slough host the UK headquarters of many technology and pharmaceutical companies; Newbury is the home of Vodafone and of Newbury Racecourse. Royal Ascot takes place at Ascot Racecourse each June, and Eton College, founded 1440, stands across the Thames from Windsor. The Berkshire pig breed takes its name from the county.
