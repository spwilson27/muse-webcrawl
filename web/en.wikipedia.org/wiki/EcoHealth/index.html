EcoHealth

EcoHealth is a non-profit organisation based in New York City, United States, dedicated to research on the relationships between human, animal and environmental health. Founded in 1971 (originally as Wildlife Trust), the organisation is formally known as EcoHealth Alliance, the name it adopted in 2010. It studies emerging infectious diseases, particularly zoonotic diseases that spill over from wildlife into people, and promotes the "One Health" approach linking veterinary medicine, ecology and public health.

Work

EcoHealth Alliance scientists have studied the wildlife origins of diseases including SARS, Nipah virus, Ebola and MERS, sampling bats and other animals in Asia, Africa and Latin America, and running programmes such as USAID's PREDICT project, which sought to identify viruses with pandemic potential before they emerge. The organisation publishes the journal EcoHealth and convenes research on how deforestation, wildlife trade and climate change drive disease emergence.

Controversy

EcoHealth Alliance's funding of coronavirus research at the Wuhan Institute of Virology in China, through grants from the U.S. National Institutes of Health, brought it intense scrutiny during debates over the origins of COVID-19. Its NIH grants were suspended in 2020, conditionally reinstated, and the organisation faced congressional inquiries and debarment proceedings in the United States in the mid-2020s, which the organisation disputed. Its president, Peter Daszak, became a prominent and controversial figure in the pandemic-origins debate.
