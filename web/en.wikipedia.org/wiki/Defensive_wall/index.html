Defensive wall

[Image: The Great Wall of China]
The Great Wall of China, the largest defensive wall system ever built

A defensive wall is a fortification used to protect a city, settlement or territory from potential aggressors. Defensive walls range from simple earthworks and palisades around villages to enormous frontier systems such as the Great Wall of China and Hadrian's Wall, and the elaborate circuit walls of medieval and early modern cities. From the earliest cities of Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley until the advent of gunpowder artillery, the wall was the central element of urban defence.

Features

Typical elements of a mature defensive wall include a thick curtain wall of stone, brick or rammed earth; a parapet and wall walk for defenders; crenellations and machicolations for dropping missiles on attackers; flanking towers and bastions that allow enfilading fire; fortified gatehouses at road entrances; and an outer ditch or moat. Many cities had concentric rings of walls — famously Constantinople's Theodosian Walls, whose triple line of moat, outer wall and great inner wall resisted sieges for a thousand years.

History and decline

Linear frontier walls such as the Roman limes controlled movement as much as they blocked invasion. In China, walls were built from the Warring States period and unified and extended under the Qin and Ming dynasties. The spread of effective siege artillery in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries made high medieval walls vulnerable, leading to the low, thick, angular bastion fortifications of the trace italienne; by the nineteenth century most European cities demolished their walls for ring roads and parks, as in Vienna and Paris. Modern defensive walls, such as border barriers, serve chiefly to control movement rather than to withstand armies.
