The Eocene is a geological epoch that lasted from about 56 to 33.9 million years ago. It is the second epoch of the Paleogene Period in the modern Cenozoic Era. The name Eocene comes from the Ancient Greek ēṓs ("dawn") and kainós ("new"), referring to the "dawn" of modern fauna that appeared during the epoch.

Climate

The Eocene began with the Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum, a sharp global warming event, and its early part — the Eocene Climatic Optimum — was the warmest sustained interval of the Cenozoic: tropical forests reached high latitudes, palms grew in Alaska, and alligators lived on Ellesmere Island. The later Eocene cooled gradually, and at the Eocene–Oligocene boundary (the Grande Coupure in Europe) falling temperatures and the first Antarctic ice sheets drove significant faunal turnover.

Life

During the Eocene the first recognisable members of many modern mammal orders appeared and diversified, including early perissodactyls and artiodactyls, primates, bats, and the earliest whales, which evolved from land mammals into fully marine forms through intermediates such as Pakicetus and Basilosaurus. Birds radiated widely, including giant flightless forms and the huge pelagornithids. Rich fossil assemblages are known from sites such as the Green River Formation and Germany's Messel Pit.
