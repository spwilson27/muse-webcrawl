Waterline

The waterline is the line where the hull of a ship meets the surface of the water. It marks the boundary between the submerged and exposed parts of the hull and varies with the vessel's load and the density of the water.

Significance

Waterline length strongly influences a hull's theoretical maximum ("hull") speed in displacement mode, and the shape of the hull at the waterline determines much of its resistance and stability. Load lines — including the Plimsoll mark — indicate the maximum legal waterline for a given water type and season, ensuring adequate freeboard. Anti-fouling coatings and boot-top paint bands are applied around the waterline, where fouling growth and wave action are most aggressive.

Other uses

The term also denotes the water level in boilers and tanks, and, in geology and speleology, the level of standing water in a cave or mine.
