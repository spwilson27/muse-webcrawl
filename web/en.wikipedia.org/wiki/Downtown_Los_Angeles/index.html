Downtown Los Angeles (DTLA) is the central business district of Los Angeles, California, and the historic heart of the city. Centred on the area around Pershing Square and the skyscrapers of Bunker Hill and the Financial District, it contains the city's government centre, its oldest neighbourhoods, and a concentration of early-20th-century commercial architecture unmatched elsewhere in Los Angeles.

History

Downtown grew from the Spanish pueblo founded in 1781 near present-day Olvera Street and El Pueblo de Los Ángeles Historical Monument. The business district shifted south along Spring and Main streets in the late 19th century, then west to Broadway and Bunker Hill, producing the Beaux-Arts and Art Deco towers of the Historic Core and landmarks such as the Bradbury Building (1893), the Los Angeles City Hall (1928) and Union Station (1939). After the Second World War, suburbanisation and freeway building drained downtown of retail and residents; Bunker Hill was controversially cleared and redeveloped with office towers from the 1960s.

Revival

From the late 1990s, an Adaptive Reuse Ordinance (1999) enabled the conversion of vacant office buildings into lofts, bringing a substantial residential population back downtown. Investment produced venues including the Walt Disney Concert Hall (2003), Crypto.com Arena and the L.A. Live complex, the Broad museum, and Grand Central Market's revival, alongside persistent challenges including the homelessness crisis concentrated in Skid Row and post-2020 office vacancies.

Districts and transport

Downtown comprises districts including the Financial District, Historic Core, Jewelry, Fashion and Toy districts, Little Tokyo, Chinatown and the Arts District. It is the hub of the LA Metro rail system, with Union Station serving Amtrak, Metrolink and Metro lines, and it is ringed by the 101, 110, 10 and 5 freeways.
