Kings of Israel

The Kings of Israel were the monarchs of the northern Kingdom of Israel (Samaria), which, according to the Hebrew Bible, split from the united monarchy of Israel after the death of Solomon around 930 BCE and endured until the Assyrian conquest of Samaria in 722 BCE.

History

Jeroboam I, the first northern king, established sanctuaries at Bethel and Dan. The kingdom saw frequent dynastic change: the house of Omri produced Ahab, whose reign and conflicts with the prophets Elijah and Elisha feature prominently in the biblical narrative. Later kings such as Jeroboam II presided over prosperity, but Assyrian expansion under Tiglath-Pileser III and Shalmaneser V ended with Samaria's fall and the deportation of much of its population — the origin of the legend of the Ten Lost Tribes.

Sources

The principal source is the biblical Books of Kings, supplemented by Assyrian inscriptions such as the Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III, which depicts Jehu paying tribute.
