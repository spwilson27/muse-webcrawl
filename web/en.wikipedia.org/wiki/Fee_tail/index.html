Fee tail

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In common law, a fee tail or estate tail is a form of trust or estate in land that restricts the transmission of the land to the lineal descendants of the original grantee, keeping the property within a family line. The term derives from the Latin feodum talliatum, a "cut" or restricted fee — cut down from the fee simple so that it can pass only to heirs of the body.

Creation and types

A fee tail was traditionally created by a grant "to A and the heirs of his body." In a fee tail male, only male descendants could inherit; in a fee tail female, only females; a fee tail special limited inheritance to the issue of a particular spouse. When the direct line failed, the land reverted to the grantor or passed under a remainder. Entails protected landed dynasties from the improvidence of individual heirs, who held only a life interest in practice and could not sell the inheritance outright.

Barring the entail

Landowners and courts long sought ways around entails, which locked land out of commerce. From the fifteenth century, the collusive court action known as a common recovery allowed a tenant in tail to "bar" the entail and convert his estate into a fee simple; fines served a similar purpose earlier. Later statutes replaced these fictions with enrolled disentailing deeds.

Abolition and survival

Most jurisdictions have abolished or severely restricted the fee tail. In England and Wales, the Law of Property Act 1925 ended the creation of new legal entails (they can exist only in equity, and the Trusts of Land and Appointment of Trustees Act 1996 barred new ones entirely). In the United States, most states abolished fee tail early in their history, often converting it into a fee simple or a life estate with remainder; a small number of states, such as Delaware, Massachusetts, Maine, and Rhode Island, retain it in modified form. The device survives in literary imagination through novels such as Jane Austen's Pride and Prejudice, whose plot turns on an entail away from the Bennet daughters.
