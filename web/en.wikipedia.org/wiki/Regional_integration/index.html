Regional integration

Regional integration is the process by which neighbouring states enter into agreements to cooperate politically and economically, typically through regional institutions and rules. Integration ranges from loose trade preferences to deep political union.

IMAGE: World map coloured by major regional integration organisations including the EU, ASEAN, African Union and Mercosur

Stages of integration

Economist Béla Balassa described a ladder of deepening integration:

  1. Preferential trading area
  2. Free trade area
  3. Customs union
  4. Common (single) market
  5. Economic and monetary union
  6. Complete political integration

Examples

  • European Union — the deepest example, with a single market and shared currency for many members
  • ASEAN, African Union, Mercosur, USMCA
  • Historical East African cooperation, including the short-lived University of East Africa

Theory and debate

Theories of integration include federalism, functionalism and neofunctionalism (emphasising "spillover" from economic to political cooperation). Critics raise concerns about democratic deficits, loss of sovereignty, and asymmetric benefits among members — debates sharpened by Brexit.
