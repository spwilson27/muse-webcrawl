The Ken River is a river in central India, flowing through the states of Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh. It is one of the major tributaries of the Yamuna River and a principal river of the Bundelkhand region. The river is about 427 kilometres (265 mi) long.

Course

The Ken rises in the Kaimur Range near the village of Ahirgawan in Katni district, Madhya Pradesh, close to the Bandhavgarh region. It flows generally north-eastward through Panna district — passing through the gorges and raneh falls of the Panna Tiger Reserve area — then through Banda district in Uttar Pradesh, before joining the Yamuna near the village of Chilla in Banda district.

Features

The Ken is noted for its scenic gorges, the Raneh Falls (often called the "Grand Canyon of India" for their multicoloured crystalline rock formations), and the Ken Gharial Sanctuary. Its waters support irrigation in the drought-prone Bundelkhand region. The proposed Ken–Betwa river link project, intended to transfer surplus Ken water to the Betwa basin for irrigation and drinking water, has been a major and controversial water-resources project in India.

Tributaries

Major tributaries include the Sonar, Bearma, Mirhasan, Shyameri, and Banne rivers.
