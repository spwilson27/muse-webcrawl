Rating scale

A rating scale is a set of categories designed to elicit information about a quantitative or qualitative attribute. In the social sciences and in survey research, respondents are asked to select the category that best represents their judgement — for example, rating a product from one to five stars, or indicating agreement with a statement on a scale from "strongly disagree" to "strongly agree".

Types of rating scale

The best-known rating scale is the Likert scale, introduced by Rensis Likert in 1932, in which respondents specify their level of agreement with a statement across (typically) five ordered points. Other common formats include:

  • Semantic differential scales, which place a concept between pairs of opposite adjectives (e.g. "good–bad");
  • Visual analogue scales, on which respondents mark a point on a continuous line, widely used in measuring pain;
  • Numerical rating scales, such as the 0–10 numeric pain rating scale used clinically;
  • Behaviourally anchored rating scales, where each point is defined by a concrete behavioural description.

Measurement properties

Rating scale responses are usually treated as ordinal data, though summed scores across multiple items are often analysed as if interval-level. Design choices — the number of points, whether a neutral midpoint is offered, and how endpoints are labelled — measurably affect response distributions. Common biases include central tendency bias, acquiescence bias, and extreme response style.
