Agricultural extension

Agricultural extension is the application of scientific research and knowledge to agricultural practices through farmer education. Generally, agricultural extension can be defined as the delivery of information inputs to farmers. The role of extension services has expanded over time from transferring technology from research laboratories to farmers, to facilitating innovation, advising on farm management, organising farmer groups, and addressing rural development more broadly.

History

The term "extension" originated in 19th-century England, where universities such as Cambridge and Oxford extended lectures to communities beyond their walls. In the United States, the Morrill Acts established land-grant universities, and the Smith–Lever Act of 1914 created the Cooperative Extension Service, a partnership between the U.S. Department of Agriculture, land-grant universities, and county governments. County agricultural agents became the local face of the system, and youth programmes such as 4-H grew out of extension work.

Methods

Extension methods include farm visits, demonstration plots and field days, farmer field schools, training courses, printed and digital advisory materials, radio and mobile-phone services. Modern approaches emphasise participatory methods, in which farmers help set the agenda and evaluate technologies, rather than simply receiving recommendations.

Extension systems worldwide

Many countries operate public extension services, often alongside private and NGO providers. China's system employs large numbers of village-level technicians; India's network includes state agricultural universities and Krishi Vigyan Kendras (farm science centres). Pluralistic systems combine public funding with commercial advisory services.
