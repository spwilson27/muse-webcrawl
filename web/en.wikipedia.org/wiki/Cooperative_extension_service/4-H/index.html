4-H is a youth development programme in the United States administered by the National Institute of Food and Agriculture of the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA), with the guidance and support of the Cooperative Extension System of the nation's land-grant universities. The name represents four personal development areas of focus: head, heart, hands, and health. The organisation serves millions of young people aged 5 to 19 through clubs, camps, school programmes and after-school activities.

History

4-H grew out of the boys' and girls' agricultural clubs of the early 1900s, such as corn-growing and tomato clubs organised by extension workers to introduce improved farming methods to rural youth, who would in turn influence their parents. The Smith–Lever Act of 1914 formalised the Cooperative Extension System and gave the clubs a national framework. The four-leaf clover emblem was adopted in the 1910s, and the 4-H pledge — "I pledge my head to clearer thinking, my heart to greater loyalty, my hands to larger service, and my health to better living" — became a hallmark of the programme.

Programmes

Members complete hands-on projects in areas such as agriculture and livestock, science and engineering, healthy living, citizenship and leadership. County fairs, where members exhibit livestock and project work, remain a central feature. The programme also runs through the agricultural extension networks of other countries in adapted forms.
