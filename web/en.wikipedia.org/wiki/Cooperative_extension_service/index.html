Cooperative extension service

A cooperative extension service is an educational network in the United States that provides research-based information from land-grant universities to farmers, families, businesses, and communities. It is formally known as the Cooperative Extension System, administered through the National Institute of Food and Agriculture of the U.S. Department of Agriculture.

History

The system was created by the Smith–Lever Act of 1914, which funded cooperative agricultural extension work between the USDA and the land-grant colleges established under the Morrill Acts. It built on earlier demonstration work by Seaman Knapp and the appointment of county agricultural agents. The name reflects the cooperative funding and administration by federal, state, and county governments.

Programs

Extension services operate offices in nearly every U.S. county, staffed by extension agents (educators) who deliver programs in agriculture and natural resources, family and consumer sciences, community development, and youth development. Its best-known youth program is 4-H. Master Gardener programs train volunteers to assist home gardeners. Services range from soil testing and pest management advice to nutrition education and small-business support.
