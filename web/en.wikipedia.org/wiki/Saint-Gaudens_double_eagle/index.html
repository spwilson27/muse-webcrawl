Saint-Gaudens double eagle

The Saint-Gaudens double eagle is a twenty-dollar gold coin, or double eagle, produced by the United States Mint from 1907 to 1933. The coin is named for its designer, the sculptor Augustus Saint-Gaudens, and is widely considered the most beautiful coin ever struck by the United States.

The Saint-Gaudens double eagle, often called the most beautiful American coin.

Origins

President Theodore Roosevelt, seeking to beautify American coinage, personally commissioned his friend Saint-Gaudens in 1905 to redesign the cent and the gold coins. Saint-Gaudens produced a design showing Liberty striding forward, torch in one hand and an olive branch in the other, with the Capitol visible below; the reverse depicts an eagle flying over the sun. The sculptor died in 1907, shortly before the coins entered production.

Production and varieties

The first issues of 1907 were struck in extremely high relief, following Saint-Gaudens's artistic vision, but proved impractical for mass production and were soon lowered in relief. The motto "In God We Trust", absent on early issues at Roosevelt's preference, was added by Congress in 1908. Coins were struck until 1933, when the United States left the gold standard; most 1933 double eagles were melted, and the few survivors are among the most famous rarities in numismatics — one example sold at auction in 2021 for a record US$18.9 million.

Legacy

The obverse design was revived in 1986 for the American Gold Eagle bullion coin programme and remains in use.
