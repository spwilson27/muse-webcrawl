Kagome lattice

The kagome lattice is a two-dimensional pattern of interlaced triangles and hexagons, named after kagome, a traditional Japanese woven bamboo pattern in which the holes (me) of the basket (kago) form this arrangement. In mathematics it is known as the trihexagonal tiling, one of the Archimedean tilings, with vertex configuration 3.6.3.6.

Structure

The lattice consists of corner-sharing triangles arranged so that each site has four neighbours, and the pattern contains both triangles and hexagons. It can be viewed as a triangular lattice with one quarter of its sites removed, or as a hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice decorated at bond midpoints.

Physics

Kagome lattices are of intense interest in condensed matter physics because antiferromagnetic interactions on their triangular motifs produce strong geometrical frustration: neighbouring spins cannot all be simultaneously satisfied, suppressing magnetic ordering. The kagome antiferromagnet is a leading candidate host for a quantum spin liquid state. The mineral herbertsmithite, ZnCu3(OH)6Cl2, realises nearly ideal kagome layers of copper ions, as does the jarosite family of minerals.

Tight-binding electrons on a kagome lattice have a band structure containing Dirac points and a perfectly flat band, features that have made the lattice a model system for studying topological and strongly correlated phases, including in intermetallic compounds such as FeSn and in metal-organic frameworks.
