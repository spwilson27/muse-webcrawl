Alcohol laws of Canada

The alcohol laws of Canada are governed primarily by the provinces and territories, a division of responsibility dating to the end of Canada's brief experiment with national prohibition in the early twentieth century. Each province sets its own legal drinking age: 19 in most provinces and 18 in Alberta, Manitoba and Quebec.

Retail sale is commonly controlled by provincial liquor boards and crown corporations such as the Liquor Control Board of Ontario (LCBO), though Alberta privatised retail sales in 1993 and grocery and convenience store sales have expanded elsewhere, including Ontario in 2024.

Federal law governs production licensing, taxation and criminal offences such as impaired driving under the Criminal Code, where the prohibited blood-alcohol concentration is 80 milligrams per 100 millilitres of blood, supplemented by provincial administrative penalties.
