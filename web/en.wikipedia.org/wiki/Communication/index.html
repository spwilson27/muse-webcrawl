Communication

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Communication is the act of conveying information or meaning from one entity or group to another through the use of mutually understood signs, symbols, and semiotic rules. The main steps inherent to all communication are the formation of a communicative intent, the composition and encoding of a message, its transmission through a channel, the receiver's perception and decoding of the message, and an interpretation of its meaning.

Forms

Human communication is usually divided into verbal communication (spoken and written language, including sign languages) and nonverbal communication — body language, gesture, facial expression, eye contact, touch, posture, and paralanguage such as tone of voice. Communication also occurs within an individual (intrapersonal), between individuals (interpersonal), within groups and organisations, and to mass audiences through media.

Models

The influential Shannon–Weaver model (1948) describes a source encoding a message, transmitted over a noisy channel to a receiver, originally for telephony but widely applied to human exchange. Later models (Schramm, Barnlund) emphasise feedback, shared fields of experience, and the transactional, simultaneous nature of sending and receiving. Scholars distinguish functions of communication including informing, persuading, expressing identity and emotion, and building relationships.

Communication studies

The academic field of communication studies examines how messages are created, transmitted, received, and interpreted, and their effects — spanning rhetoric, media studies, journalism, public relations, and organisational and health communication. Barriers to effective communication include noise, differing codes and cultures, selective perception, and information overload.

Animal and plant communication

Communication is not uniquely human: animals signal through calls, displays, pheromones, and bioluminescence, and plants exchange chemical signals with each other and with insects. The scientific study of such systems falls under biosemiotics and zoosemiotics.
