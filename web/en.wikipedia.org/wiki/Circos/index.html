Circos is a software package for visualizing data in a circular layout, written in Perl by Martin Krzywinski at Canada's Michael Smith Genome Sciences Centre in Vancouver. It is widely used in genomics to display relationships between genomic positions — such as chromosomal rearrangements, synteny between species, and gene fusions — but the circular "Circos plot" format has spread to many other fields.

Description

In a Circos plot, data are arranged around a circle: ideograms (often chromosomes) form the outer ring, data tracks such as histograms, scatterplots, heat maps and tiles are drawn in concentric rings, and curved ribbons or links connect related positions across the layout. The design exploits the fact that relationships are easy to trace along chords, and that circular layouts make genome-wide comparisons compact and visually striking.

History and use

The software was described in a 2009 paper in Genome Research ("Circos: an information aesthetic for comparative genomics"). Circos figures have appeared on the covers and in the pages of leading journals and in the New York Times, and the style has been imitated by libraries in other languages, including circlize in R, BioCircos in JavaScript, and the chord diagram in D3.js. Outside genomics, Circos-style diagrams have been used to visualize migration flows, trade, telecommunications traffic and social networks.
