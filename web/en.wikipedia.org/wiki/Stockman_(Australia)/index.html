Stockman (Australia)

In Australia, a stockman (plural stockmen) is a person who looks after the livestock on a large property known as a station, which may be a cattle or sheep station. Stockmen traditionally worked on horseback, mustering, branding, and moving herds over vast distances, and they occupy an iconic place in Australian folklore comparable to the cowboy in the United States. Women who performed the same work were historically called stockwomen, and the modern term is often gender-neutral.

Work

The stockman's duties include mustering cattle or sheep from open paddocks, droving herds along stock routes (the famous "long paddock") between stations and markets, branding and castrating calves, breaking in horses, maintaining fences and bore drains, and general station repairs. On the great cattle drives of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, drovers such as Harry Redford ("Captain Starlight") and the Durack family moved thousands of head over thousands of kilometres to open up the Kimberley and the Channel Country.

Equipment and culture

The stockman's traditional outfit includes the Akubra felt hat, moleskin trousers, elastic-sided boots, and a stockwhip, and his mount is the Australian Stock Horse, bred for endurance and agility. The Australian Stockman's Hall of Fame in Longreach, Queensland, commemorates the occupation. Stockmen figure prominently in bush ballads and literature — Banjo Paterson's "The Man from Snowy River" celebrates a legendary ride — and Aboriginal stockmen were for decades the backbone of the northern cattle industry, often working for rations rather than wages until the equal-pay decisions and walk-offs of the 1960s, most famously the Wave Hill walk-off of 1966.

Modern era

Today much mustering is done by motorbike, quad bike, and helicopter, and the term "ringer" is common in the north for a station hand. Nonetheless, horsemanship remains essential in rough country, and stock work continues to attract young workers to remote stations. The role's skills are tested at campdrafting and rodeo competitions across rural Australia.
