The regions of Greece (Greek: περιφέρειες, periféries) are the country's thirteen first-level administrative divisions. Until the Kallikratis reform which entered into force on 1 January 2011, they were purely decentralised divisions of the central government with limited powers; since then each region has had an elected governor and regional council.

In addition, the monastic community of Mount Athos is an autonomous self-governing polity under Greek sovereignty. The regions are further subdivided into regional units and municipalities. Attica, containing the capital Athens, is by far the most populous region, while the Ionian Islands is the least populous.

History

The regions were first established in 1987 as administrative units for regional development planning. The Kallikratis programme of 2010 substantially expanded their competences and introduced direct elections, first held in November 2010.
