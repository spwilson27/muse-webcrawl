Tottenham

Tottenham is an area of north London, England, within the London Borough of Haringey, about six miles (10 km) north-east of Charing Cross. Centred on Tottenham High Road, the historic Roman road of Ermine Street, it is one of the most ethnically diverse districts in the capital and is best known internationally as the home of Tottenham Hotspur Football Club.

History

Tottenham is recorded in the Domesday Book as Toteham, a farming settlement that grew along the Roman road and the River Lea. It remained a rural village popular with wealthy Londoners until the railway arrived in the nineteenth century, when cheap workmen's fares spurred rapid working-class housing development. The district became part of the County of London's suburbs and then of Haringey in 1965. The Broadwater Farm estate riots of 1985 and the disturbances that began in Tottenham in August 2011, following the police shooting of Mark Duggan, drew national attention; a major regeneration programme has since centred on the area around the football stadium and Tottenham Hale.

Sport and transport

Tottenham Hotspur, founded in 1882, moved into the new Tottenham Hotspur Stadium on the site of White Hart Lane in 2019. The area is served by the Victoria line at Seven Sisters and Tottenham Hale, and by London Overground stations including Bruce Grove and White Hart Lane.
