Uisang

Uisang (Korean: 의상; 625–702) was one of the most eminent scholar-monks of the Korean kingdom of Silla and the founder of the Korean Hwaeom (Huayan) school of Buddhism. Along with his contemporary Wonhyo, he is regarded as one of the twin pillars of Silla Buddhist thought.

Life

Born into an aristocratic family, Uisang traveled in 661 to Tang China to study, enduring shipwreck and delay before reaching China, where he studied the Avatamsaka (Flower Garland) Sutra under the Huayan patriarch Zhiyan alongside the young Fazang, who would become the school's third patriarch. According to tradition, Uisang returned to Silla in 671 bearing a warning of an impending Tang invasion, and was rewarded by King Munmu, who supported his mission to establish Hwaeom teaching in Korea.

Teaching and works

Uisang founded Buseoksa Temple in 676 and ordained ten principal disciples, through whom Hwaeom spread across Silla. His most famous work is the Hwaeom ilsung beopgyedo ("Diagram of the Dharma Realm of the One Vehicle"), a condensed statement of Huayan philosophy arranged as a 210-character poem inscribed in a spiral, seal-like diagram of 54 turns. Its most celebrated line teaches that "one is all, all is one" — the interpenetration of all phenomena, visualized in the metaphor of Indra's net. Uisang's accessible teaching style is credited with popularizing Buddhism among commoners in Silla, in contrast to more courtly rivals.

Legacy

The Korean Hwaeom school Uisang founded remained one of the principal doctrinal traditions of Korean Buddhism, and his diagram continues to be studied and reproduced in Korean temples today.
