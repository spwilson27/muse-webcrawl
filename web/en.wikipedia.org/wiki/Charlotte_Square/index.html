Charlotte Square is a city square in Edinburgh, Scotland. It is part of the New Town, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The square was designed by James Craig in his 1767 New Town plan, with unified palace-front elevations designed by Robert Adam in 1791 — his last major architectural work. It is considered a masterpiece of Georgian architecture and one of the finest squares in Britain.

Design

Charlotte Square was intended as the mirror image of St Andrew Square at the eastern end of George Street, the New Town's principal axis. Adam's elevations treat each side as a single classical palace: a central block with Corinthian columns and a pediment, flanked by lower wings. The north side, the most celebrated, was built first (from 1791); the remaining sides were completed by other hands, broadly following Adam's scheme, by about 1820.

Notable buildings and residents

No. 6 Charlotte Square is Bute House, the official residence of the First Minister of Scotland. No. 5, The Georgian House, is a restored late-18th-century townhouse museum run by the National Trust for Scotland. The central garden contains the Prince Albert Memorial, an equestrian statue by John Steell unveiled in 1876. Residents have included the pioneer of antiseptic surgery Joseph Lister and, for a time, Alexander Graham Bell was born nearby. The square also hosts venues of the Edinburgh International Book Festival, the world's largest public celebration of the written word, founded in the square's gardens in 1983.
