Farkas Bolyai (Hungarian: Bolyai Farkas; 9 February 1775 – 20 November 1856), known in German as Wolfgang Bolyai, was a Hungarian mathematician and polymath, best known as the father and teacher of János Bolyai, one of the founders of non-Euclidean geometry.

Life and work

Bolyai studied at the University of Göttingen, where he became a close friend of Carl Friedrich Gauss. From 1804 he taught mathematics, physics and chemistry at the Reformed College in Marosvásárhely (now Târgu Mureș, Romania) for nearly half a century. His major work, the Tentamen juventutem studiosam in elementa matheseos purae introducendi (1832–33), attempted a rigorous foundation for arithmetic, geometry and analysis; its appendix contained his son János's revolutionary treatise on non-Euclidean geometry.

The parallel postulate

Farkas devoted much of his life to attempts to prove Euclid's parallel postulate, corresponding with Gauss on the subject. Knowing the cost of the obsession, he famously warned his son in a letter not to waste an hour on the problem — "You must not attempt this approach to parallels. I know this way to the very end." János disregarded the advice and succeeded in creating a consistent geometry in which the postulate does not hold. Farkas also gave an early proof that two polygons of equal area are equidecomposable in his work on what became the Bolyai–Gerwien theorem.
