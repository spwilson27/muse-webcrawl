St Matthew Passion

The St Matthew Passion (German: Matthäus-Passion), BWV 244, is a sacred oratorio by Johann Sebastian Bach, first performed on Good Friday, 11 April 1727, at the Thomaskirche in Leipzig. Setting chapters 26 and 27 of the Gospel of Matthew to music for solo voices, double choir and double orchestra, with a libretto by Picander (Christian Friedrich Henrici), it is widely regarded as one of the supreme masterpieces of Western sacred music.

Structure and forces

The work is scored for two choirs and two orchestras that converse antiphonally throughout, joined by a third choir of boys singing the chorale melody in the opening and closing choruses of Part One. In the vast opening chorus, "Kommt, ihr Töchter, helft mir klagen" ("Come, ye daughters, help me lament"), the two choirs dialogue while a ripieno soprano line soars above. The Passion story is narrated by a tenor Evangelist; Jesus' words are accompanied by a "halo" of strings, withdrawn only at his final cry, "Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani."

Music

Reflective arias meditate on the drama: "Erbarme dich" ("Have mercy, Lord"), sung after Peter's denial with a lamenting solo violin, and "Aus Liebe will mein Heiland sterben" ("Out of love my Saviour is willing to die"), whose flute obbligato floats without continuo support. The final chorus, "Wir setzen uns mit Tränen nieder" ("We sit down in tears"), brings the work to a close of spacious grief.

Revival

After Bach's death the Passion fell into obscurity until the 20-year-old Felix Mendelssohn conducted an abridged performance in Berlin in 1829 with the Sing-Akademie — an event often credited with launching the modern Bach revival. Bach's autograph score, written in part with distinctive red ink for the biblical text, survives in the Berlin State Library.
