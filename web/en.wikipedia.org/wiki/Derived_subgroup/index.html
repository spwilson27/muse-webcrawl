Derived subgroup

In mathematics, more specifically in abstract algebra, the derived subgroup (or commutator subgroup) of a group G is the subgroup generated by all the commutators of G. It is usually denoted G′, [G, G], or G(1).

Definition

For elements a and b of G, the commutator is [a, b] = a−1b−1ab. The subgroup generated by all commutators is the derived subgroup. It is a characteristic subgroup of G, hence normal, and the quotient G/[G,G] is abelian — indeed it is the largest abelian quotient of G, called the abelianisation of G.

Derived series

Iterating the construction gives the derived series; a group for which the series reaches the trivial subgroup is called a solvable group.
