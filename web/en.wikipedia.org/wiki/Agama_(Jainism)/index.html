In Jainism, Agamas are the canonical scriptures that contain the teachings of the Tirthankara Mahavira (599–527 BCE) as compiled by his chief disciples, the Ganadharas. The word agama denotes a traditional doctrine or precept received from tradition. The existing Agamas are regarded by Svetambara Jains as the authoritative canon; Digambara Jains hold that the original texts were lost and revere later works instead.

Composition

The Svetambara canon traditionally comprises 45 texts: 11 Angas, 12 Upangas, 6 Chedasutras, 4 Mulasutras, 10 Prakirnakas and 2 Culikasutras. The best-known Anga, the Acaranga Sutra, deals with the conduct of monks. The texts were transmitted orally for centuries before being written down, traditionally at a council at Valabhi in the 5th century CE under Devardhigani.

Content

The Agamas expound the central Jain doctrines: ahimsa (non-violence), anekantavada (many-sidedness of reality), aparigraha (non-possession), the nature of the soul (jiva), karma, and the path to liberation (moksha). They also include cosmology, biographies of the Tirthankaras, and rules of monastic discipline.

Digambara view

Digambaras maintain that the original Purva texts were progressively forgotten and that no authentic Agama survives. Their principal scriptures include the Shatkhandagama and the works of Kundakunda. This disagreement over scriptural authority is one of the defining differences between the two major Jain traditions.
