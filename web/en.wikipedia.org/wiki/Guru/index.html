Guru

Origin: Sanskrit guru ("weighty", revered teacher)

Traditions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism

A guru (Sanskrit: गुरु) is a spiritual teacher and guide in Indian religions. More than an instructor, the guru is regarded as a revered counsellor who dispels ignorance and leads the disciple (shishya) toward self-realisation and liberation. The Sanskrit syllables are popularly interpreted as gu (darkness) and ru (remover).

In the traditions

In Hinduism the guru–disciple relationship is ancient, central to yoga, Vedanta and bhakti lineages, and celebrated on the festival of Guru Purnima. In Tibetan Buddhism the lama (guru) is essential to tantric practice; in Jainism the guru is one of the three jewels of refuge. In Sikhism, "Guru" names the ten human Gurus from Guru Nanak onward and, ultimately, the scripture Guru Granth Sahib, the eternal Guru.

Modern usage

In modern English "guru" is used loosely for any recognised expert or mentor, for example in business or technology.
