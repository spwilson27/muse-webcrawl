Antikythera

Antikythera (Greek: Αντικύθηρα, "opposite Kythera") is a small Greek island in the Ionian Sea lying between Kythera and Crete, at the edge of the Aegean Sea. It is part of the municipality of Kythera and has a small permanent population, concentrated in the port village of Potamos.

History

The island was settled in antiquity (its ancient name was Aegilia) and was a haunt of Cilician pirates until Pompey cleared the Mediterranean of piracy. It later passed through Byzantine, Venetian and Ottoman hands before joining Greece.

The shipwreck

Antikythera is most famous for the Antikythera wreck, a Roman-era shipwreck discovered by sponge divers in 1900. Salvaged artefacts included the Antikythera Ephebe bronze statue and the Antikythera mechanism, an ancient geared astronomical calculator dated to roughly the 2nd–1st century BC, often described as the earliest known analogue computer. Renewed expeditions since 2014 have recovered further artefacts, including human skeletal remains and elements of a large bronze statue.

Geography

The island covers about 20 km² of rugged limestone terrain. It is an important stopover for migratory birds and hosts a bird observatory run by the Hellenic Ornithological Society; its feral goat population and remoteness have drawn attention in depopulation discussions.
