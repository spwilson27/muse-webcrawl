AB Volvo (the Volvo Group) is a Swedish multinational manufacturing corporation headquartered in Gothenburg. Founded in 1927 by Assar Gabrielsson and Gustaf Larson, it is one of the world's largest manufacturers of heavy trucks, and also produces buses, construction equipment, marine and industrial drive systems, and financial and other services.

History

Volvo began as a subsidiary of the ball-bearing maker SKF; its first car, the ÖV 4, left the Gothenburg factory in April 1927. The name Volvo is Latin for "I roll," a reference to bearings. The company grew into cars, trucks, and engines over the twentieth century. In 1999 Volvo sold its car division, Volvo Cars, to Ford Motor Company (it was later acquired by China's Geely in 2010), so that AB Volvo and Volvo Cars are today separate companies sharing the Volvo brand.

Business

The group's truck operations include Volvo Trucks, Mack Trucks (United States), and Renault Trucks (France), and it has a major truck joint venture in Japan (UD Trucks, sold to Isuzu in 2021) and China. Volvo Construction Equipment is a leading maker of excavators, wheel loaders, and haulers, and Volvo Penta supplies marine and industrial engines. The group has a longstanding reputation for emphasising safety and, more recently, electrification of commercial vehicles.
