Antonov An-70

Role: Military transport aircraft
Manufacturer: Antonov
First flight: 16 December 1994
Status: In service (limited)

The Antonov An-70 is a four-engine medium-distance transport aircraft, and the first aircraft to take off powered only by propfan engines. It was developed in the late 1980s by the Antonov Design Bureau in Ukraine as a replacement for the ageing An-12 military transport and as a counterpart to types such as the Ilyushin Il-76.

Design

The An-70 is powered by four Progress D-27 propfan engines, each driving SV-27 contra-rotating propellers. The propfan configuration gives the aircraft a cruising speed of up to 750 km/h combined with short take-off and landing performance: the An-70 can operate from unpaved runways of about 600–700 metres. It can carry up to 47 tonnes of cargo, or 300 troops, and its cargo hold can accommodate most types of Ukrainian and (formerly) Russian military equipment.

Development history

The first prototype flew on 16 December 1994 but was lost in a mid-air collision in February 1995; a second prototype followed in 1997. The programme was pursued for many years as a joint Ukrainian–Russian project, but Russia withdrew in the mid-2000s in favour of its own Il-76 variants. Ukraine completed state testing and accepted the type into service in 2015 in small numbers, and a turboshaft-fan derivative, the An-188, has been promoted for export.
