A monster truck is a specialised off-road vehicle, typically styled after a pickup truck, modified with extremely large wheels and off-road suspension. Monster trucks are used in competition and popular sports entertainment, where they race, perform freestyle routines, and crush rows of cars. The modern trucks are purpose-built tube-frame machines with fibreglass bodies, tyres about 66 inches (1.68 m) in diameter, and engines producing around 1,500 horsepower.

History

Monster trucks developed in the late 1970s in the United States, when owners of lifted pickups began competing to drive over cars. Bob Chandler's Bigfoot, built from a 1974 Ford F-250, is regarded as the first monster truck; its car-crushing videos in the early 1980s launched the phenomenon.

Competitions were organised by bodies such as the USHRA, and events became fixtures of arena and stadium shows. The sport's best-known trucks include Bigfoot, Grave Digger (created by Dennis Anderson in 1982), and Maximum Destruction.

Modern trucks and events

Today's trucks sit on custom tubular chassis with four-link suspension offering several feet of travel, four-wheel steering, and methanol-fuelled supercharged V8 engines. Drivers compete in racing and freestyle in series such as Monster Jam, judged on stunts including jumps, wheelies, donuts, and backflips.

Safety has improved with remote engine shut-offs, roll cages, and multi-point harnesses after serious accidents in the sport's early decades.
