Stavanger

Stavanger is a city and municipality in Rogaland county on the south-western coast of Norway. It is the country's fourth most populous city, with roughly 150,000 inhabitants in the municipality (following a 2020 merger with the municipalities of Finnøy and Rennesøy), and is widely regarded as the centre of the Norwegian petroleum industry. The city is home to the headquarters of Equinor, Norway's largest energy company.

History

Stavanger is traditionally dated to 1125, when the building of Stavanger Cathedral — Norway's oldest cathedral still in use, built under Bishop Reinald — began and the town became a diocesan seat. For centuries it remained a modest fishing and shipping town, growing in the nineteenth century on herring fisheries and sardine canning, which made it known as Norway's "canning capital". Many of the white wooden houses of Old Stavanger (Gamle Stavanger), the largest surviving wooden-house settlement in northern Europe, date from this era.

The oil age

The discovery of the Ekofisk field in the North Sea in 1969 transformed Stavanger into the "oil capital of Norway". The Norwegian Petroleum Directorate and the state oil company Statoil (now Equinor) established their headquarters there, and multinational service companies followed, bringing rapid population growth and high incomes.

Tourism and culture

Nearby natural attractions include Preikestolen (Pulpit Rock) overlooking the Lysefjord and the Kjerag boulder. The city hosts the Norwegian Petroleum Museum, the Stavanger Museum, and an annual jazz festival (MaiJazz) and food festival; it was a European Capital of Culture in 2008 together with Liverpool. Stavanger Airport, Sola, serves the region, and the Ryfast tunnel system links the city northwards under the fjords.
