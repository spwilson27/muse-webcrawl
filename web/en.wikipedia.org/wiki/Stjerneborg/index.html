Stjerneborg

Stjerneborg ("Star Castle") was an underground observatory built by the Danish astronomer Tycho Brahe near his better-known observatory Uraniborg on the island of Hven in the Øresund, then part of Denmark. Constructed beginning in 1584, it was designed to overcome the limitations Tycho perceived in Uraniborg, where large instruments mounted high in the building were disturbed by wind and vibration and exposed to the weather.

Design

Stjerneborg was built largely below ground. Five circular crypts, sunk into the earth, held fixed instruments — including large equatorial armillary spheres, quadrants, and sextants — mounted on massive stone pillars isolated from building motion and sheltered from wind. Only small domes or opening covers projected above the surface. A central heated chamber gave astronomers workspace and access to the crypts. The layout was arranged symmetrically, reflecting Tycho's taste for cosmological symbolism as well as practical stability.

Use and fate

The instruments at Stjerneborg formed part of the most ambitious naked-eye observing programme in history, which produced the star and planetary data later used by Johannes Kepler to formulate his laws of planetary motion. After Tycho fell from royal favour and left Hven in 1597, the observatories were abandoned; the buildings were demolished and their materials reused, leaving little above ground.

Excavation

The site of Stjerneborg was excavated in the 1950s, revealing the foundations of the crypts. The observatory has been partially reconstructed, with the underground instrument chambers visible to visitors, and replicas of Tycho's instruments have been installed. Together with the surviving foundations of Uraniborg and Tycho's gardens, Stjerneborg is a centrepiece of the Tycho Brahe museum on present-day Ven, Sweden.
