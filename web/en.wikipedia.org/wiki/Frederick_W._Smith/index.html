Frederick Wallace Smith (August 11, 1944 – June 21, 2025) was an American business magnate and investor, best known as the founder of FedEx Corporation, the world's first overnight delivery company and one of the largest transportation and logistics firms in the world. He served as the company's chairman and chief executive officer for nearly five decades, pioneering the hub-and-spoke distribution model that transformed global shipping.

Early life and education

Smith was born in Marks, Mississippi, and raised in Memphis, Tennessee. His father, founder of a regional bus line and restaurant chain, died when Smith was four. Despite a childhood bone disease, Smith became a keen athlete and pilot. At Yale University he wrote an economics paper in 1965 outlining an overnight delivery system built around a central hub — a paper that later became famous as the blueprint for FedEx, though Smith himself played down the legend of the grade it received. He graduated in 1966.

Military service and founding FedEx

Smith served two tours in Vietnam with the U.S. Marine Corps, as a platoon leader and forward air controller, and was decorated with the Silver Star, Bronze Star, and two Purple Hearts. Observing military logistics reinforced his ideas about integrated air-ground delivery. In 1971 he founded Federal Express in Little Rock, Arkansas, moving operations to Memphis in 1973 for its central location and reliable weather. The company began overnight operations on 17 April 1973, delivering 186 packages with a fleet of Dassault Falcon jets. After near-bankruptcy in its early years, FedEx grew into a global giant, and Smith became one of America's most influential business figures and a prominent advocate on trade and tax policy.

Later life

Smith stepped down as CEO in 2022, becoming executive chairman, and the company rebranded its operating units under the FedEx name in a major restructuring. He was a part-owner of the Washington Commanders NFL franchise and a noted philanthropist, supporting the Marine Corps Scholarship Foundation and conservation causes. Smith died on 21 June 2025, at the age of 80.
