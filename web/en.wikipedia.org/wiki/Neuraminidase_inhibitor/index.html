Neuraminidase inhibitor

Neuraminidase inhibitors (NAIs) are a class of antiviral drugs that block the neuraminidase enzyme found on the surface of influenza viruses. By preventing neuraminidase from cleaving sialic acid, they stop newly formed virions from being released from infected cells and from penetrating mucus, limiting viral spread within the respiratory tract.

Drugs in the class

The principal agents are oseltamivir (Tamiflu, oral), zanamivir (Relenza, inhaled), peramivir (intravenous) and laninamivir (used in Japan). They are active against influenza A and B and are used for treatment and post-exposure prophylaxis, ideally started within 48 hours of symptom onset.

Resistance and debate

Resistance — notably the H275Y mutation in seasonal H1N1, which became widespread in 2008–09 — can erode effectiveness. Systematic reviews have debated the size of clinical benefit in otherwise healthy adults and the adequacy of trial data publication, leading to the release of full clinical study reports by manufacturers.
