Flavin-containing monooxygenase 3

Flavin-containing monooxygenase 3 (FMO3), also known as dimethylaniline monooxygenase [N-oxide-forming] 3, is an enzyme that in humans is encoded by the FMO3 gene on chromosome 1. It is the major flavin-containing monooxygenase expressed in adult human liver and plays a central role in the oxidative metabolism of many drugs, xenobiotics, and dietary compounds.

Function

FMO3 uses NADPH and molecular oxygen to oxygenate soft nucleophilic substrates, particularly nitrogen- and sulfur-containing compounds. Its best-known substrate is trimethylamine (TMA), a product of gut microbial metabolism of choline and carnitine, which FMO3 converts to odorless trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO). The enzyme also metabolizes drugs such as nicotine, ranitidine, tamoxifen, and the antipsychotic clozapine.

Clinical significance

Loss-of-function mutations in FMO3 cause trimethylaminuria, also called fish odor syndrome, in which undegraded trimethylamine accumulates and is excreted in sweat, urine, and breath, producing a strong fishy body odor. FMO3-mediated production of TMAO has also been investigated for a possible role in cardiovascular disease.
