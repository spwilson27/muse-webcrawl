Iron-carbon phase diagram

The iron-carbon phase diagram is a graphical representation of the phases present in alloys of iron and carbon at different temperatures and compositions. It is the foundation of the metallurgy of steels and cast irons. The diagram is usually drawn up to 6.67% carbon by weight, the composition of cementite (Fe3C); because of this, the system is often treated as the metastable iron–cementite diagram rather than the true equilibrium iron–graphite diagram.

Phases

  • Ferrite (α) – a body-centred cubic solid solution of carbon in iron, soft and ductile, dissolving at most 0.02% carbon.
  • Austenite (γ) – a face-centred cubic solid solution, stable at high temperature, dissolving up to 2.14% carbon at 1147 °C.
  • Cementite (Fe3C) – a hard, brittle intermetallic compound containing 6.67% carbon.
  • δ-ferrite – the high-temperature body-centred cubic phase stable near the melting point.
  • Pearlite – not a phase but a lamellar mixture of ferrite and cementite formed at the eutectoid.

Invariant reactions

The eutectoid reaction occurs at 727 °C and 0.76% carbon, where austenite decomposes into pearlite on cooling. The eutectic occurs at 1147 °C and 4.3% carbon, producing ledeburite, a mixture of austenite and cementite characteristic of cast irons. A peritectic reaction occurs at 1495 °C.

Classification of alloys

Alloys with less than 2.14% carbon are classified as steels; those with more are cast irons. Steels below the eutectoid composition are hypoeutectoid, and those above it (but below 2.14%) are hypereutectoid.
