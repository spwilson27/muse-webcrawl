Acehnese (also spelled Achinese; in the language itself Basa Acèh) is an Austronesian language native to Aceh, on the northern tip of Sumatra in Indonesia. Unusually for western Indonesia, it belongs to the Chamic branch, whose other languages are spoken in Vietnam and Cambodia, reflecting early maritime links across the South China Sea.

Features

Acehnese is notable among Austronesian languages for its large vowel inventory, with numerous diphthongs and a contrast in vowel length, and for having partially monosyllabic word structure unusual in the region. Its vocabulary includes many loanwords from Malay, Arabic, Persian, Sanskrit and Dutch, reflecting Aceh's history as a trading sultanate and centre of Islamic scholarship.

Status

Acehnese is the dominant regional language of Aceh and is used in local media and education, though Indonesian is the language of government and formal schooling. The language has a rich oral and written literature, including epic poems (hikayat) traditionally written in the Jawi (Arabic-based) script.
