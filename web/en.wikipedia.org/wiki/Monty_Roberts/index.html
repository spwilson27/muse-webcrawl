Monty Roberts

Marvin Earl "Monty" Roberts (born May 14, 1935) is an American horse trainer and author, known for his non-violent horse training methods based on his system of communication he calls "Equus". He popularised the technique of "join-up" — working an unhandled horse in a round pen until it chooses to approach and follow the trainer — in his bestselling 1996 autobiography The Man Who Listens to Horses.

Life and career

Roberts grew up in Salinas, California, where his father ran a horse-training and rodeo centre. A child riding competitor and stunt double, he later studied the behaviour of wild mustangs in Nevada, from which he says he developed his understanding of equine body language. He rejected the traditional practice of "breaking" horses by force and built an international career demonstrating gentle starting of unbroken horses before audiences, including demonstrations for Queen Elizabeth II, whose patronage brought him wide attention in Britain.

Books and reception

The Man Who Listens to Horses spent over a year on bestseller lists and has been translated into many languages; he followed it with Shy Boy, Horse Sense for People, and other works. His claims and methods are influential in natural horsemanship, though some details of his biography and the originality of his techniques have been contested by family members and other trainers.
