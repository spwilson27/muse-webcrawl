Bond energy

In chemistry, bond energy (E) or bond enthalpy (H) is the measure of bond strength in a chemical bond. It is the amount of energy required to break one mole of bonds in gaseous molecules, or the energy released when a bond is formed.

Definition

Bond dissociation energy refers to a specific bond in a specific molecule, while mean bond energy is averaged over similar bonds. For example, breaking O–H in water requires different energy for the first and second bonds.

Values

Typical values: H–H 436 kJ/mol, C–H 413 kJ/mol, C–C 348 kJ/mol, C=C 614 kJ/mol, C≡C 839 kJ/mol, O=O 498 kJ/mol. Stronger bonds have higher energies and shorter bond lengths.

Applications

Bond energies estimate enthalpy changes of reactions using Hess's law: ΔH ≈ sum of bonds broken minus sum of bonds formed.
