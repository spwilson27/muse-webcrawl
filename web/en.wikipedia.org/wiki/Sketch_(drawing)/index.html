Sketch (drawing)

A sketch (ultimately from Greek σχέδιος – schedios, "done extempore") is a rapidly executed freehand drawing that is not usually intended as a finished work. A sketch may serve several purposes: it might record something the artist sees, develop an idea for later use, or serve as a quick way of graphically demonstrating an image, idea, or principle.

Sketches can be made in any drawing medium. The term is most often applied to graphic work executed in dry media such as graphite pencil, pen and ink, inked brushes, or charcoal. Watercolor, oil paint, and pastels may also be used for sketching. Artists' sketchbooks preserve these works; famous examples include the sketchbooks of Leonardo da Vinci and Edgar Degas, whose exploratory drawings became highly valued works in their own right.

Uses

Sketching is used as a tool for creative thinking in art, architecture, industrial design, and courtroom illustration. A courtroom sketch records proceedings where cameras are barred. In design, thumbnail sketches rapidly explore composition and ideas. "Sketching" also names the preliminary underdrawing of a painting.

Techniques

Common sketching techniques include line drawing, hatching and cross-hatching, scribbling, stippling, and blending (smudging). Sketch artists value speed and economy of line over finish, often leaving construction lines visible.
