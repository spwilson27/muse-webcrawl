Dilated cardiomyopathy

Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is a condition in which the heart becomes enlarged and cannot pump blood effectively. Symptoms vary from none to feeling tired, leg swelling and shortness of breath, and it may also result in chest pain or fainting.

Dilated cardiomyopathy
SpecialtyCardiology
SymptomsShortness of breath, fatigue, leg swelling
CausesGenetics, alcohol, coronary artery disease, viral infection, often unknown
TreatmentHeart-failure medicines, ICD, transplant

Causes

Many cases are idiopathic. Known causes include genetic mutations (often in titin, TTN), excessive alcohol use, certain chemotherapy agents, myocarditis, peripartum cardiomyopathy, and haemochromatosis. The dilated, thin-walled ventricle ejects a reduced fraction of its volume.

Diagnosis

Echocardiography shows chamber enlargement and reduced ejection fraction. ECG, blood tests, cardiac MRI and genetic testing help establish cause and prognosis.

Treatment

Standard heart-failure therapy — ACE inhibitors/ARNI, beta blockers, mineralocorticoid antagonists and SGLT2 inhibitors — improves outcomes. Implantable defibrillators prevent sudden death in selected patients; severe cases may require ventricular assist devices or heart transplantation. Complications include heart failure, arrhythmias and blood clots.
