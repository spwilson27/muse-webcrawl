Sanctuary of Monte Sant'Angelo

The Sanctuary of Monte Sant'Angelo (Italian: Santuario di San Michele Arcangelo) is a Roman Catholic shrine on Mount Gargano in Apulia, Italy, in the town of Monte Sant'Angelo. It is the oldest shrine in Western Europe dedicated to the Archangel Michael and has been a major pilgrimage site since the early Middle Ages. It forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Longobards in Italy: Places of the Power".

Legend

According to tradition, the Archangel Michael appeared at the site in 490, 492 and 493. In the most famous episode, a stray bull was found kneeling in a cave; when an arrow shot at it miraculously turned back on the archer, the local bishop sought Michael's guidance, and the archangel declared the cave sacred to him and forbade its pagan use.

The shrine

The sanctuary centres on the sacred cave (the Sacra Grotta), reached by descending staircases from the church above. The complex includes the octagonal bell tower built under Frederick II, the church with its bronze doors cast in Constantinople in 1076, and a museum. An inscription traditionally invites visitors to leave their sins at the entrance, since the cave itself grants pardon.

Pilgrimage

The shrine lies on the Via Francigena's southern routes toward the Holy Land and was visited by numerous popes, saints (including Francis of Assisi) and crusaders. Its feast days are 8 May and 29 September.
