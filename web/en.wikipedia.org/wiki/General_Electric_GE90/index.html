General Electric GE90

The General Electric GE90 is a family of high-bypass turbofan aircraft engines built by GE Aerospace (formerly GE Aviation) for the Boeing 777, with thrust ratings from 81,000 to 115,000 lbf (360 to 510 kN). It entered service with British Airways in November 1995. The GE90-115B variant is recognized by Guinness World Records as the world's most powerful commercial jet engine, and the GE90 held the title of largest fan diameter until surpassed by its successor, the GE9X.

Design and development

GE developed the GE90 in the early 1990s, drawing on work from the NASA Energy Efficient Engine program. It was the first commercial engine to use curved composite fan blades, swept wide-chord blades made of carbon-fiber composite, which give the engine its distinctive appearance. The core features a 10-stage high-pressure compressor driven by a 2-stage turbine and a 6-stage low-pressure turbine driving the fan.

The later "growth" versions — the GE90-110B1 and GE90-115B — power the 777-200LR and 777-300ER exclusively, with a 128-inch fan and increased thrust. The GE90-115B set a world record of 127,900 lbf of thrust during ground testing in 2002. The engine also enabled the 777's long-range twin-engine operations (ETOPS) records, including the longest commercial flight by distance flown at the time.

Applications

All Boeing 777-200LR, 777-300ER, and 777F freighter aircraft are powered exclusively by GE90 variants, while earlier 777-200/-200ER/-300 models offered GE90, Pratt & Whitney PW4000, and Rolls-Royce Trent 800 options.
