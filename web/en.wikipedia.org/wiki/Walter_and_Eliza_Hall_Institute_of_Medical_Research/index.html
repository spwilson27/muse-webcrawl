Walter and Eliza Hall Institute of Medical Research

Founded: 1915
Location: Parkville, Melbourne, Australia
Focus: Immunology, cancer, infectious diseases, developmental biology
Also known as: WEHI

The Walter and Eliza Hall Institute of Medical Research, known as WEHI, is Australia's oldest medical research institute, located in Parkville, Melbourne. Founded in 1915 with a bequest from the trust of Walter and Eliza Hall — Eliza Hall having directed funds from her husband's pastoral and mining fortune to charitable purposes — the institute is renowned internationally for research in immunology, cancer, blood diseases, and infectious disease.

History

The institute grew out of the Melbourne Hospital pathology laboratories, with Henry Newton as its first director. Under Sir Frank Macfarlane Burnet, director from 1944 to 1965, WEHI became a world centre of virology and immunology; Burnet developed the clonal selection theory of antibody formation and shared the 1960 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the discovery of acquired immunological tolerance. Later directors included Sir Gustav Nossal, who expanded the institute's immunology programme, and Suzanne Cory, under whom cancer research grew strongly.

Research achievements

WEHI scientists discovered colony stimulating factors (CSF) that regulate white blood cell production — work led by Donald Metcalf that translated into drugs used by millions of cancer patients. Institute researchers identified the Bcl-2 cancer gene family and championed BH3-mimetic drugs, leading to venetoclax, a breakthrough therapy for certain leukaemias. WEHI also maintains major programmes on malaria — Eliza Hall's trust and the institute have long supported anti-malarial drug and vaccine research — and houses structural biology and drug discovery facilities in the Parkville precinct alongside the University of Melbourne.
