The Battle of Baekgang, also known as the Battle of Baekgang-gu or, in Japanese, the Battle of Hakusukinoe (白村江の戦い), was a naval battle fought in August 663 AD at the mouth of the Baekgang River in Korea. A Tang Chinese and Silla fleet decisively defeated a much larger Yamato Japanese fleet sent to support the restoration of the fallen kingdom of Baekje.

The naval battle at the Baekgang estuary, 663 AD.

Background

In 660 a Tang–Silla alliance conquered Baekje, one of the Three Kingdoms of Korea. Baekje loyalists rose in revolt and appealed to Yamato Japan — with which Baekje had long-standing ties — for help, requesting the return of Prince Buyeo Pung, who had lived in Japan as a hostage-guest. Japan sent a large expeditionary force under Abe no Hirafu and others to restore him.

The battle

Despite outnumbering the Tang fleet more than four to one in ships, the Japanese fleet attacked in disorder and was defeated in two days of fighting; Japanese chronicles record that 400 of their ships were burned. The Baekje restoration collapsed, its stronghold at Juryu fell shortly afterwards, and Buyeo Pung fled to Goguryeo.

Consequences

The defeat ended Japanese military involvement on the Korean peninsula for centuries. Fearing a Tang–Silla invasion, Yamato Japan built extensive defences in Kyushu, including the Mizuki earthwork and mountain fortresses, and reorganised its military system. Within a few years Tang and Silla destroyed Goguryeo (668), unifying most of the peninsula under Silla.
