Novelty song

A novelty song is a comical or nonsensical song, performed principally for humour or topicality rather than musical merit. Novelty songs depend on unusual effects—sped-up voices, sound effects, silly lyrics, parody, or a topical hook—and were a staple of music hall, vaudeville, and comic radio.

History

Comic songs were common in 19th-century popular music and on Tin Pan Alley. In the recording era, hits included Spike Jones's satirical arrangements in the 1940s, Stan Freberg's parodies in the 1950s, and chart-toppers such as “The Purple People Eater” (1958) and “Monster Mash” (1962). Allan Sherman's My Son, the Folk Singer (1962) made parody albums a mass phenomenon.

From the 1970s, radio presenter Dr. Demento popularised the genre, championing artists such as “Weird Al” Yankovic, whose song parodies became a durable mainstream career. Novelty records frequently tie into films, television, dances, or news events, and many are one-hit wonders whose appeal fades with their moment. In the internet era, viral novelty songs spread through video platforms, continuing the tradition in new media.

A novelty record: comedy songs have been chart hits since the early recording era.
