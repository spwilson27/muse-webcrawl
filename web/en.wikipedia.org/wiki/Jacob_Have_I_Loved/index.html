Jacob Have I Loved is a coming-of-age novel by American author Katherine Paterson, published in 1980 and winner of the Newbery Medal in 1981. Its title quotes the Book of Romans: "Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated."

Plot

The novel is narrated by Sara Louise Bradshaw, who grows up on the fictional Rass Island in Chesapeake Bay during the Second World War, working the water alongside her waterman father. Louise has always felt eclipsed by her beautiful, gifted twin sister Caroline, who is sent off the island for music training while Louise stays behind. Through her friendship with the returned islander Captain Hiram Wallace and the devastation of a hurricane, Louise gradually claims an identity of her own, eventually leaving to study medicine and becoming a midwife in Appalachia.

Themes and reception

The book is noted for its portrait of sibling rivalry, gender expectations in a fishing community, and faith. It was adapted as a television film in 1989. Paterson drew on her knowledge of Chesapeake Bay island communities for the setting.
