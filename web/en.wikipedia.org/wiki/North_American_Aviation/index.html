North American Aviation (NAA) was an American aerospace manufacturer founded in 1928. It produced some of the most famous aircraft of the Second World War and the Cold War, including the T-6 Texan trainer, the P-51 Mustang fighter, the B-25 Mitchell bomber and the F-86 Sabre jet fighter. In the 1960s it built the X-15 rocket plane, the XB-70 Valkyrie bomber and, as prime contractor, the Apollo command and service module and the second stage of the Saturn V rocket. In 1967 it merged with Rockwell-Standard to form North American Rockwell, later Rockwell International; its aircraft divisions ultimately became part of Boeing.

History

North American began as a holding company for several airlines and manufacturers. Under president James "Dutch" Kindelberger from 1934, it concentrated on aircraft production in California. The Mustang, designed in 1940 in response to a British requirement and transformed by the Rolls-Royce Merlin engine, became one of the war's outstanding fighters. Post-war designs included the F-86 Sabre of Korean War fame, the F-100 Super Sabre and the A-5 Vigilante. NAA's Los Angeles and Columbus divisions, its Rocketdyne engine subsidiary and its Atomics International unit made it a major defence and space contractor until the merger with Rockwell.
