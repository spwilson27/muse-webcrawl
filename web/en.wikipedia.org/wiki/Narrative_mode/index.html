Narrative mode

The narrative mode is the set of methods the author of a literary work, film, or other narrative uses to convey the story to the audience. It comprises the choice of narrator, grammatical person, tense, and the techniques — description, dialogue, exposition, and interior monologue, among others — by which events and information are presented. Narrative mode answers the question of how a story is told, complementing plot (what happens) and theme (what it means).

Narrative mode is the toolkit by which a story is presented to its audience.

Elements of narrative mode

Point of view

The narrator may be a participant (first person), address the protagonist as "you" (second person), or stand outside the action (third person). Third-person narration subdivides into omniscient, limited, and objective varieties according to how much interior knowledge is granted. Genette's concept of focalization separates who speaks from who sees, allowing finer distinctions such as a heterodiegetic narrator with internal focalization through one character.

Modes of presentation

Since Plato's Republic, critics have contrasted diegesis (telling: the story mediated by a narrator) with mimesis (showing: events presented directly, as in drama or pure dialogue). Percy Lubbock formalised the pair as "telling" and "showing" in The Craft of Fiction (1921), and modernists prized showing; later critics note the two form a continuum, since even scenic presentation is selected and arranged. Related measures include scene versus summary — whether story time and discourse time roughly coincide — and the handling of speech and thought, from direct quotation through reported speech to free indirect discourse, in which a character's voice colours third-person narration.

Tense and order

Narration is usually in the past tense, which gives events the shape of completed history, but present-tense narration creates immediacy and uncertainty. Order may be chronological or exploit anachrony: analepsis (flashback) and prolepsis (flashforward), in Genette's terms.

Rhetorical modes

School rhetoric traditionally divides prose discourse into four modes of discourse — narration, description, exposition, and argumentation (or persuasion). Within narrative works these modes interleave: exposition supplies background, description builds setting, dialogue enacts conflict, and narration proper advances events in time.
