San Francisco International Airport (IATA: SFO) is the primary international airport serving the San Francisco Bay Area, located about 13 miles south of downtown San Francisco in unincorporated San Mateo County. It is the largest airport in Northern California and a major gateway for trans-Pacific travel.

Facilities

SFO has four terminals arranged in a ring, connected by the AirTrain people mover, and four runways built on reclaimed land along San Francisco Bay. Its closely spaced parallel runways limit simultaneous arrivals in fog, a long-standing operational constraint and one reason the airport is known for weather delays. The International Terminal, opened in 2000, was the largest international terminal in North America at the time; Terminal 1 is named for the late supervisor Harvey Milk.

Traffic and airlines

United Airlines maintains its largest Pacific hub at SFO, with extensive service to Asia, Oceania and Europe, alongside Alaska Airlines and most major US and international carriers. Before the COVID-19 pandemic the airport handled over 57 million passengers in a year (2019); traffic has since recovered toward those levels, ranking SFO among the busiest airports in the United States.

Ground transport

The airport is served directly by the BART rapid transit system, Caltrain via a shuttle/bus connection at Millbrae, SamTrans buses, taxis and ride-hailing, and by US Highway 101. The SFO station opened in 2003, making SFO one of the few major US airports with a direct heavy-rail link into the city.

Environment

SFO has pursued carbon-neutrality goals, sustainable aviation fuel use, and one of the first airport-wide bans on the sale of plastic water bottles (2019).
