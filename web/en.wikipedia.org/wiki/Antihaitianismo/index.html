Antihaitianismo

Antihaitianismo (Spanish for "anti-Haitianism") is a form of prejudice, discrimination, and xenophobia directed against Haitians and people of Haitian descent, most prominently in the Dominican Republic, where anti-Haitian sentiment has been a recurring feature of national politics and identity since the nineteenth century. The term is also applied to discrimination against Haitian migrants in other countries, including the Bahamas, the United States, and Chile.

The island of Hispaniola, shared by Haiti and the Dominican Republic

Historical background

Haiti and the Dominican Republic share the island of Hispaniola. Haiti, the first independent black republic, was founded in 1804 after a successful slave revolt against French colonial rule. In 1822, Haitian forces under President Jean-Pierre Boyer occupied the Spanish-speaking eastern part of the island, unifying it under Haitian rule until Dominican independence was declared in 1844. The memory of this 22-year unification period has been invoked in Dominican nationalist discourse to portray Haiti as a historic enemy and to define Dominican identity in opposition to Haitian culture, language, and African heritage.

The Trujillo era

Anti-Haitian ideology was institutionalized under the dictatorship of Rafael Leónidas Trujillo (1930–1961). In October 1937, Dominican forces carried out the Parsley massacre, in which thousands of ethnic Haitians and dark-skinned Dominicans living near the border were killed. Estimates of the death toll range from several thousand to more than 20,000. Trujillo's regime promoted hispanidad—an ideology emphasizing the Dominican Republic's Spanish and Catholic heritage—while portraying Haitian culture, Vodou religion, and the French-based Haitian Creole language as threats to the nation.

Contemporary issues

Hundreds of thousands of Haitian migrants and their descendants live in the Dominican Republic, many working in agriculture, construction, and the sugar industry, historically including in bateyes (sugar workers' settlements) with poor living conditions. In September 2013, the Dominican Constitutional Court issued ruling TC/0168/13, which retroactively stripped Dominican citizenship from tens of thousands of people born in the country to undocumented foreign parents, overwhelmingly of Haitian descent, reaching back to 1929. The ruling drew widespread international criticism; a 2014 law (169-14) partially restored documentation to some affected people, though advocates argued it left many stateless or at risk of statelessness.

Anti-Haitian sentiment has also been documented in periodic immigration crackdowns, mass deportations, and political campaigns in the Dominican Republic, as well as in discrimination against Haitian communities in the Bahamas and elsewhere in the Caribbean and the Americas.
