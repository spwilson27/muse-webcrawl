Base dissociation constant

The base dissociation constant, denoted Kb, is a measure of the strength of a base in solution. It is the equilibrium constant for the reaction of a base B with water, in which the base accepts a proton to form its conjugate acid BH+ and hydroxide ions:

B + H2O ⇌ BH+ + OH−

Kb = [BH+][OH−] / [B]

pKb

As with the acid dissociation constant Ka, a logarithmic form is often used: pKb = −log10 Kb. A larger Kb (and hence a smaller pKb) indicates a stronger base, which dissociates more completely and produces a higher hydroxide concentration.

Relation to Ka

For a conjugate acid–base pair in water at 25 °C, the constants are related through the self-ionization constant of water: Ka × Kb = Kw = 1.0 × 10−14, equivalently pKa + pKb = 14. Aqueous ammonia, for example, has Kb ≈ 1.8 × 10−5 (pKb ≈ 4.74).
