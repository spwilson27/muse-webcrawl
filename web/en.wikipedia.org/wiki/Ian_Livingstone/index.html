Ian Livingstone

Sir Ian Livingstone CBE (born 29 December 1949) is an English fantasy author and entrepreneur. With Steve Jackson he co-founded Games Workshop in 1975, which grew from a small mail-order business selling role-playing games into the retailer and publisher behind Warhammer. With Jackson he also created the Fighting Fantasy series of interactive gamebooks, beginning with The Warlock of Firetop Mountain in 1982.

Career

Livingstone and Jackson launched Games Workshop in London, initially importing Dungeons & Dragons into the UK before developing the Games Workshop retail chain and the magazine White Dwarf. The Fighting Fantasy books sold in the millions and helped popularise interactive fiction for children. In 1995 Livingstone became involved with computer game publisher Eidos Interactive, serving as president and overseeing franchises including Tomb Raider and Deus Ex.

Later work

Livingstone has championed computer science and games education in the UK, co-authoring the 2011 Nesta report Next Gen, which led to the inclusion of computing in the national curriculum. He co-founded the Livingstone Academy school in Bournemouth and the games investment fund Hiro Capital. He was knighted in 2022 for services to the online gaming industry.
