In Verrem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Verrem ("Against Verres") is a series of speeches by the Roman orator and statesman Marcus Tullius Cicero, delivered in 70 BC in the prosecution of Gaius Verres, the former governor of Sicily, on charges of extortion and corruption during his three-year governorship (73–71 BC). The case made Cicero's reputation as Rome's leading advocate.

The trial

The Sicilians, whom Verres had systematically plundered — seizing grain, money, and works of art — appealed to Cicero, who had served as quaestor in western Sicily in 75 BC and was remembered there for his honesty. Verres was defended by Quintus Hortensius, then considered Rome's foremost orator, and hoped to delay the trial into the following year, when friendly officials would preside. Cicero frustrated the strategy by gathering evidence in Sicily in fifty days and delivering a short, devastating first action (Actio Prima) that relied heavily on witness testimony. Verres abandoned his defence and fled into exile at Massilia (modern Marseille), where he lived for the rest of his life.

The speeches

Only the first action and the preliminary Divinatio in Caecilium were delivered. Cicero published the undelivered second action (Actio Secunda) in five books, treating Verres' career, his administration of justice, the grain supply, his looting of artworks, and his illegal executions of Roman citizens. The speeches remain a prime source for Roman provincial administration and for the provenance of lost Greek art.
