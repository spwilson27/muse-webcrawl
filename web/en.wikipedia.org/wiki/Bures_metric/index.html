Bures metric

In mathematics and quantum information theory, the Bures metric is a metric on the space of density matrices (density operators), closely related to the notion of fidelity between quantum states. It is named after Donald Bures, who introduced it in 1969 as a generalisation of the statistical distance to non-commuting density matrices.

Definition

The Bures distance between two density matrices ρ and σ is defined through the fidelity F(ρ, σ) = (tr √(√ρ σ √ρ))². The Bures angle and Bures distance are obtained from this fidelity, and the infinitesimal form of the distance defines a Riemannian metric on the space of states.

Properties

The Bures metric is the smallest among the monotone metrics on quantum states that reduce to the Fisher–Rao metric on commuting (classical) states. For pure states it coincides with the Fubini–Study metric. It is intimately connected with quantum Fisher information, and it plays a role in quantum metrology, state discrimination and the geometry of mixed states.
