From Here to Eternity

From Here to Eternity is a 1953 American romantic war drama film directed by Fred Zinnemann and based on the 1951 novel of the same name by James Jones. Set at Schofield Barracks in Hawaii in the months before the attack on Pearl Harbor, it stars Burt Lancaster, Montgomery Clift, Deborah Kerr, Donna Reed, and Frank Sinatra. The film won eight Academy Awards, including Best Picture.

Plot

Private Robert E. Lee Prewitt (Clift), a former boxer who quit after blinding an opponent, refuses to fight for his company team and is subjected to "the Treatment" — relentless hazing by his captain and fellow soldiers. Sergeant Milton Warden (Lancaster) begins an affair with the captain's neglected wife Karen (Kerr), whose beach embrace with Warden became one of cinema's most iconic scenes. Prewitt's friend Angelo Maggio (Sinatra) is brutally beaten by the stockade sergeant "Fatso" Judson and dies; Prewitt kills Judson in a knife fight and is wounded. When the Japanese attack Pearl Harbor, Prewitt tries to rejoin his unit despite his wounds and is shot dead by a military patrol.

Production and legacy

The novel's profanity and content were toned down for the screen, and the production received U.S. Army cooperation after changes were made. Sinatra, whose career was then in decline, campaigned hard for the role of Maggio and won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor, relaunching his career; Reed also won Best Supporting Actress. The film was selected for the National Film Registry in 2002 and remains a classic of postwar American cinema.
