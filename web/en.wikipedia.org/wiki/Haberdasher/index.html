Haberdasher

A haberdasher is a merchant who sells small articles for sewing, such as buttons, ribbons, zips, needles and thread. The term originally referred to a peddler or dealer in such wares, and later to the owner of a shop selling them. In American English, "haberdasher" came to mean a retailer of men's clothing and accessories instead.

Etymology

The word is attested from the Middle Ages. Its origin is uncertain; it may derive from the Anglo-French hapertas, a kind of cloth, or from an Old Norse phrase meaning "haversack", the bag in which a travelling haberdasher carried his wares. The Worshipful Company of Haberdashers is one of the livery companies of the City of London.

History

Haberdashers were among the traders who supplied the small notions needed by tailors, dressmakers and households. Haberdashery departments became a standard feature of department stores, selling sewing supplies, trimmings and accessories.
