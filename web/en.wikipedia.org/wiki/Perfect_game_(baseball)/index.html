Perfect game (baseball)

Perfect game

Definition: A complete game in which no opposing batter reaches base

Minimum batters faced: 27

MLB occurrences: 24 (1876–present)

First: Lee Richmond, 1880

Most recent (MLB): Domingo Germán, 2023

In baseball, a perfect game is a game in which a pitcher (or combination of pitchers under some definitions) completes at least nine innings without allowing any opposing player to reach base — no hits, no walks, no hit batters, no errors. The pitcher faces the minimum of 27 batters, each retired in turn. It is one of the rarest achievements in the sport.

History

Major League Baseball officially recognises 24 perfect games. The first two were thrown within a week of each other in 1880 by Lee Richmond and John Montgomery Ward. Don Larsen pitched the only perfect game in World Series history, in 1956 for the New York Yankees against the Brooklyn Dodgers. The modern era brought a cluster of them, including three in the 2012 season (by Philip Humber, Matt Cain and Félix Hernández). Domingo Germán threw the most recent in June 2023.

Near misses

Several pitchers have lost perfect games with two outs in the ninth inning, most famously Armando Galarraga in 2010, whose 28-out "imperfect game" was spoiled by an incorrect safe call at first base, an error the umpire, Jim Joyce, immediately acknowledged.
