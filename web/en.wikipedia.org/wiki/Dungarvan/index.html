Dungarvan

Dungarvan (Irish: Dún Garbhán, meaning "Garbhann's fort") is a coastal town and harbour in County Waterford, on the southeast coast of Ireland. It lies at the mouth of the Colligan River where it enters Dungarvan Bay, between the Comeragh Mountains and the sea. The town had a population of around 10,000 in the 2022 census and serves as the administrative centre of the western part of the county.

History

Dungarvan was founded by the Normans in the late twelfth century, and its castle — King John's Castle — was built around 1209 to guard the harbour entrance. The town developed as a fishing and trading port, and for many years returned members to the Irish Parliament and later to Westminster. In the nineteenth century it was a major port for the export of agricultural produce, and the arrival of the railway in 1878 connected it to Waterford and Cork. The town suffered during the Great Famine, when workhouses and mass graves marked its outskirts.

Today Dungarvan is a popular tourist destination known for its seafood restaurants, its position on the Waterford Greenway cycling route to Waterford city, and nearby attractions including Clonea Strand and the Comeragh Mountains. The town also hosts the Sean Kelly Tour of Waterford cycling event and is associated with the cyclist Sean Kelly, who was born nearby.
