The greylag goose or graylag goose (Anser anser) is a species of large goose in the waterfowl family Anatidae and the type species of the genus Anser. It is the ancestor of most breeds of domestic goose.

Description

The greylag is the largest and bulkiest of the grey geese of Europe, measuring 74–91 cm in length with a wingspan of 147–180 cm and weighing around 2.2–4.5 kg. Its plumage is grey-brown with a pale fringing to the feathers, a whiter belly with variable black barring, and a stout orange-pink bill and pink legs. The eastern subspecies, A. a. rubrirostris, is paler with a pink bill.

Distribution and habitat

The species breeds across northern and central Europe and Asia, in marshes, moorlands, lakesides and coastal wetlands. Northern populations migrate south to winter around the Mediterranean and in southern Asia; feral and re-established populations are widespread elsewhere, including in Britain, where the species was reintroduced in many areas.

Behaviour and domestication

Greylags are grazing birds, feeding on grass, roots, leaves and grain. They form long-lasting pair bonds and family groups, and are known for their loud honking calls. The species was domesticated in Europe at least 3,000 years ago; the domestic goose (Anser anser domesticus) descends from it. Greylags were famously used by ethologist Konrad Lorenz in his pioneering studies of imprinting.
