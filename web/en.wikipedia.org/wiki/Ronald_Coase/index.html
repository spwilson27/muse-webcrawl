Ronald Harry Coase (29 December 1910 – 2 September 2013) was a British economist at the University of Chicago Law School, a founder of law and economics and of the new institutional economics. He received the 1991 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for discovering the significance of transaction costs and property rights for economic structure.

Two famous papers

'The Nature of the Firm' (1937), written when Coase was in his twenties, asked why firms exist at all if markets are efficient: because using the price mechanism is costly, transactions are organised inside firms up to the point where internal organisation costs as much. 'The Problem of Social Cost' (1960) — the most cited economics article of its era — argued that with well-defined property rights and negligible transaction costs, parties will bargain to the efficient allocation of resources whatever the initial rights assignment: the Coase theorem, central to the analysis of externalities. Coase himself stressed the real world of positive transaction costs, where institutions, law and firms determine outcomes — the research programme carried on by Oliver Williamson and Douglass North.
