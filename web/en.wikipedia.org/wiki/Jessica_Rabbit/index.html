Jessica Rabbit

Jessica Rabbit
First appearance: Who Censored Roger Rabbit? (novel, 1981); Who Framed Roger Rabbit (film, 1988)
Voiced by: Kathleen Turner (speaking), Amy Irving (singing)
Spouse: Roger Rabbit

Jessica Rabbit is a fictional character from Gary K. Wolf's 1981 novel Who Censored Roger Rabbit? and its 1988 film adaptation Who Framed Roger Rabbit. In the film she is the glamorous cartoon femme fatale wife of Roger Rabbit, a nightclub singer at the Ink and Paint Club, and one of the most recognisable animated characters of the era.

Character

Drawn as an exaggerated caricature of the 1940s femme fatale, Jessica famously protests, "I'm not bad — I'm just drawn that way." Despite her appearance, she is devoted to Roger ("He makes me laugh") and proves courageous and loyal, helping detective Eddie Valiant uncover the plot against Toontown. Kathleen Turner provided her speaking voice, uncredited, and Amy Irving sang her performance of "Why Don't You Do Right?".

Legacy

Jessica Rabbit became an enduring pop-culture figure and an icon of the film's blend of live action and animation. She appears in Roger Rabbit short films, Disney theme park attractions and extensive merchandise, and is regularly cited in lists of the greatest animated characters.
