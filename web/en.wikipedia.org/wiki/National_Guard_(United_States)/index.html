National Guard (United States)

Founded1636 (militia origins); modern form 1903
ComponentsArmy National Guard; Air National Guard
SizeAbout 430,000 personnel
OversightNational Guard Bureau; state governors

The National Guard is a military reserve component of the United States Armed Forces, made up of the Army National Guard and the Air National Guard, with units in all fifty states, three territories and the District of Columbia. It is unique among U.S. forces for its dual state–federal mission: in peacetime, Guard members serve under their governor; when federalised, they serve under the President as part of the Army or Air Force.

Organisation and status

Guard soldiers and airmen are mostly part-time — traditionally "one weekend a month, two weeks a year" — holding civilian jobs while training with their units. They can be called up in three statuses: State Active Duty, funded and controlled by the governor for emergencies such as floods and hurricanes; Title 32 status, federally funded but under state control; and Title 10, full federal active duty for overseas deployments and war. The Chief of the National Guard Bureau, a four-star general, has been a member of the Joint Chiefs of Staff since 2012.

History

The Guard dates its birthday to 13 December 1636, when the Massachusetts Bay Colony organised its militia into regiments. The Militia Act of 1903 gave the modern Guard its name and federal standards; it fought as complete divisions in both World Wars, was used — controversially — for domestic order in the civil-rights era (both to enforce and to resist integration), and after 2001 became an operational reserve with repeated combat deployments to Iraq and Afghanistan. Domestically it is the first military responder to natural disasters, civil unrest and, in 2020–21, pandemic logistics and vaccine distribution.
