Historic house museum
Type: Museum in a house of historic significance
Examples: Mount Vernon; Sir John Soane's Museum; Anne Frank House

A historic house museum is a house of historic significance that has been converted into a museum. Historic furnishings may be displayed in a way designed to reflect their original placement and usage, and such museums are often presented as a window into everyday life of a past period or the life of a notable resident.

History of the type

The earliest projects to preserve historic homes began in the late 18th and 19th centuries, driven by nationalism and antiquarian interest. In the United States, the Mount Vernon Ladies' Association (founded 1853) saved George Washington's home and became a model for preservation organisations.

Interpretation

House museums range from grand country houses and palaces to modest workers' cottages. Modern interpretation increasingly addresses the lives of servants, enslaved people and other residents once omitted from tours, and uses restored period rooms, costumed guides and digital media.
