Orsini family

Coat of arms of the Orsini, bearing the family's rose emblem.

The Orsini are an Italian noble family that was one of the most influential princely families in medieval Italy and Renaissance Rome. Originating in Rome around the 11th–12th centuries, the family claimed descent from the Roman Julio-Claudian dynasty and, more colourfully, from a child nursed by a she-bear (orsa), which supplied their name and their emblem of a rose. For centuries the Orsini were the great rivals of the Colonna family, and the two houses' feud — Orsini generally leading the Guelph (papal) faction, Colonna the Ghibelline — dominated Roman politics.

History

The family rose to prominence with Giacinto Bobone, who became Pope Celestine III (1191–1198). His nephew Giovanni Gaetano Orsini became Pope Nicholas III (1277–1280), who enriched his kin and made them hereditary senators of Rome. Matteo Rosso Orsini "the Great" (d. 1246) established the family's power base in Rome, locking the cardinals into the first conclave in 1241. Thereafter an unbroken succession of Orsini cardinals, condottieri, and feudal lords controlled castles and territories across Lazio, Abruzzo, and the Kingdom of Naples.

Branches and members

The family divided into several lines, including the Orsini of Bracciano, of Gravina (which survives to the present day and heads the family), of Monterotondo, and of Pitigliano. Notable members include Clarice Orsini, wife of Lorenzo de' Medici; Virginio Orsini, duke of Bracciano; Paolo Giordano Orsini; and numerous commanders who served as condottieri for the papacy, Naples, Venice, and Florence. Pope Benedict XIII (1724–1730), born Pietro Francesco Orsini of the Gravina line, was the family's third pope. The Orsini held the hereditary office of assistant prince to the papal throne from 1735 until the reforms of 1968.

Legacy

Orsini castles survive at Bracciano — where the Castello Orsini-Odescalchi remains a famous landmark and event venue — Sorano, Pitigliano, and elsewhere. The family name persists in Roman street names, the Palazzo Orsini, and the rose of the Orsini shield.
