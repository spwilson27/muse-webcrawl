The Dagda

The Dagda
Irish mythology; Tuatha Dé Danann
Also: Eochaid Ollathair, Ruad Rofhessa — "the Good God"
Attributes: Magic cauldron, club, harp
Consort/kin: The Morrígan; father of Brigid, Aengus

The Dagda is a god in Irish mythology, a chief of the Tuatha Dé Danann. His name means "the Good God" — good at everything. He is a father-figure associated with fertility, agriculture, strength, magic, druidry and wisdom.

Attributes

He possesses an inexhaustible cauldron (coire ansic) from which no company goes unsatisfied, a huge club that kills with one end and revives with the other, and a magic harp, Uaithne, that orders the seasons and plays sorrow, joy and sleep.

Myths

Before the Second Battle of Mag Tuired he mates with the Morrígan; he is father of Brigid and Aengus, and his exploits are told in the Mythological Cycle.
