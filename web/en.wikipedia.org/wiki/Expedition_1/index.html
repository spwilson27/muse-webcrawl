ISS Expedition 1

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

ISS Expedition 1
CrewWilliam Shepherd, Yuri Gidzenko, Sergei Krikalev
Arrival2 November 2000
Duration136 days
SignificanceStart of continuous human presence in orbit
The first residents.

Expedition 1 was the first long-duration crew of the International Space Station, arriving on 2 November 2000 aboard Soyuz TM-31 — commander William Shepherd (US), and cosmonauts Yuri Gidzenko and Sergei Krikalev. Their arrival began the continuous human occupation of space that has now lasted over a quarter of a century: since that date, there has never been a moment when no human was in orbit.

The station they moved into was three modules — Zarya, Unity and the newly arrived Zvezda — with no working toilet redundancy, sporadic communications, and a construction schedule ahead of it. Shepherd insisted on the call sign 'Station' and on being addressed as commander of an integrated crew; the trio activated systems, received the first shuttle assembly flight (bringing the giant US solar arrays), and handed over to Expedition 2 in March 2001.

Milestone status

Expedition 1's anniversary each November marks the running clock of permanent occupation — a record of continuity that spans wars, accidents and programme cancellations on the ground. Krikalev, who had been aboard Mir when the Soviet Union dissolved, bookended the era: the last Soviet cosmonaut became the first station resident of the international age.
