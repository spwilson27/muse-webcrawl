The Hobbit, or There and Back Again is a children's fantasy novel by the English author J. R. R. Tolkien, published in 1937 to wide critical acclaim. It is nominally set in Tolkien's fictional universe of Middle-earth and follows the home-loving hobbit Bilbo Baggins, the wizard Gandalf, and a company of thirteen dwarves on a quest to reclaim the Lonely Mountain and its treasure from the dragon Smaug.

Plot

Bilbo Baggins, a respectable hobbit of Bag End, is swept from his comfortable hobbit-hole by Gandalf and Thorin Oakenshield's company of dwarves, who engage him as their "burglar". Travelling east, the party is captured by trolls, sheltered by the elf-lord Elrond at Rivendell, and captured again by goblins in the Misty Mountains, where Bilbo, lost in the tunnels, finds a magic ring and outwits the creature Gollum in a riddle contest. Escaping, the company survives giant spiders and imprisonment by the Wood-elves of Mirkwood, reaches Lake-town, and enters the Lonely Mountain, where Bilbo confronts Smaug. After the dragon destroys Lake-town and is killed by the archer Bard, dwarves, elves, and men dispute the treasure, and the conflict erupts into the Battle of the Five Armies when an army of goblins and wargs arrives. Thorin is mortally wounded, and Bilbo returns home with a modest share of treasure and the ring.

Publication and reception

Tolkien wrote the story in the early 1930s, famously beginning with the sentence "In a hole in the ground there lived a hobbit" written on a blank exam page. Published by George Allen & Unwin in 1937, the book sold out quickly and has never been out of print. Its success prompted the publisher to request a sequel, which became the much darker and larger The Lord of the Rings (1954–55). Tolkien later revised The Hobbit, notably the Gollum chapter, to align the ring's nature with the sequel.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted into radio, stage, and animated versions, and into Peter Jackson's three-part live-action film series The Hobbit (2012–2014).
