Epitrachelion

The epitrachelion (Greek: ἐπιτραχήλιον, "around the neck"; Slavonic: Епитрахиль, Yepitrakhil) is the stole worn by priests and bishops of the Eastern Orthodox Church and Eastern Catholic Churches as the symbol of their priesthood. It is essentially the orarion adapted for priests and bishops, consisting of a long band of brocade joined at the front, worn around the neck so that it hangs down in two parallel strips.

Description and use

The epitrachelion is decorated with embroidery, often depicting the twelve apostles or other saints, and fringed at the bottom. Seven embroidered crosses may mark points touched by the priest when hearing confessions. A priest must wear the epitrachelion to perform virtually any service; when serving the Divine Liturgy, it is worn beneath the phelonion. When a priest is vested in the epitrachelion alone with the epimanikia, he may hear confessions or perform certain brief services and blessings.

Symbolism

The vestment symbolises the grace of the Holy Spirit flowing down upon the priest, and the yoke of the priesthood taken up in service. The accompanying vesting prayer speaks of the grace poured out "like the precious oil upon the head, that ran down unto the beard, the beard of Aaron."
