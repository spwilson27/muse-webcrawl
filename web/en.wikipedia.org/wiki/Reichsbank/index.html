The Reichsbank (German: [ˈʁaɪçsˌbaŋk]) was the central bank of the German Reich from 1876 until 1945. It was founded on 1 January 1876, shortly after the establishment of the German Empire in 1871, taking over from the Prussian Bank.

History

The Reichsbank issued the German gold mark and enjoyed a monopoly of note issue, although state banks retained limited rights for a time. After the First World War, under president Rudolf Havenstein, the bank printed money to meet reparations and budget deficits, contributing to the hyperinflation of 1923. Under Hjalmar Schacht the currency was stabilised with the introduction of the Rentenmark and then the Reichsmark in 1924.

In the Nazi era the bank, again led by Schacht (1933–1939) and then by Walther Funk, financed rearmament, in part through the covert Mefo bills. The Reichsbank was dissolved after Germany's defeat in 1945; in the west its functions passed to the Bank deutscher Länder (1948), predecessor of the Deutsche Bundesbank, while in the east the Deutsche Notenbank was established.
