NCAA Division I FCS football

NCAA Division I FCS football is the lower of the two subdivisions of Division I college football in the United States, governed by the National Collegiate Athletic Association. FCS stands for Football Championship Subdivision, named for the post-season tournament that decides its national champion — in contrast to the Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS), which historically relied on bowl games and polls.

History

Division I football split into I-A and I-AA in 1978. The I-AA classification was renamed the Football Championship Subdivision in 2006. FCS programmes may award the equivalent of up to 63 full scholarships, which can be divided among more players, compared with 85 full scholarships in the FBS. Several FCS schools have produced notable upsets of FBS opponents, most famously Appalachian State's 2007 victory over Michigan.

Playoffs

The FCS championship is decided by a single-elimination tournament, expanded over the years to a 24-team bracket. Conference champions from participating leagues receive automatic bids, with the remaining places filled by at-large selections; the top eight teams receive byes and seeds. The championship game has been held in Frisco, Texas, since the 2010 season.

Conferences and programmes

More than 120 schools compete in FCS conferences including the Missouri Valley Football Conference, the Big Sky Conference, the Colonial Athletic Association (CAA Football), the Southern Conference and the Ivy League — whose members historically declined to participate in the playoffs. North Dakota State became the subdivision's dominant dynasty, winning nine national titles between 2011 and 2021; Georgia Southern and Appalachian State won multiple titles before moving up to the FBS.

Differences from the FBS

Beyond scholarships, FCS programmes typically operate on smaller budgets and in smaller stadiums, and their players are less frequently selected in the early rounds of the NFL Draft — though notable exceptions include quarterbacks Carson Wentz and Trey Lance of North Dakota State and wide receiver Cooper Kupp of Eastern Washington. FCS champions are recognised by the NCAA, whereas the FBS championship is organised separately by the College Football Playoff.
