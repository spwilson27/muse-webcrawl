Concussion

A concussion, also known as a mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI), is a head injury that temporarily affects brain functioning. It is caused by a bump, blow, or jolt to the head or body that causes the brain to move rapidly back and forth inside the skull, resulting in chemical changes and sometimes stretching and damage to brain cells.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms may include headache, confusion, dizziness, nausea, balance problems, sensitivity to light and noise, difficulty concentrating, and feeling "foggy". Loss of consciousness occurs in only a minority of cases. Symptoms usually resolve within days to weeks, although some people experience persistent post-concussive symptoms. Diagnosis is clinical, based on the mechanism of injury and symptoms; CT or MRI scans are used to rule out more serious injuries such as bleeding in the brain when indicated by decision rules.

Causes and prevention

Common causes include falls, motor vehicle collisions, assaults, and sports injuries. Concussions are a major concern in contact sports such as American football, ice hockey, and boxing, and in military service. Prevention involves protective equipment, rule changes, tackling technique, and neck strengthening, though helmets do not eliminate risk. Repeated concussions before full recovery are dangerous, and cumulative exposure has been linked to chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE).

Management

Initial management includes removal from play or activity, brief physical and cognitive rest (typically 24–48 hours), followed by a gradual, stepwise return to normal activities and sport. Most people recover within 10–14 days, though recovery is often longer in children and adolescents.
