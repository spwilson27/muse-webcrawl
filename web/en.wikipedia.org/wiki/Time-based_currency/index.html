Time-based currency

A time-based currency is a complementary currency or exchange system in which the unit of account is the person-hour, so that one unit represents one hour of one person's labour. Because every participant's hour is valued equally, such systems embody an egalitarian theory of value rooted in the classical labour theory of value. Modern implementations are usually called time banks or service exchanges.

History

The idea dates to the early nineteenth century. The Welsh social reformer Robert Owen issued "labour notes" at his Equitable Labour Exchange in London in 1832, denominated in hours. In the United States, Josiah Warren opened the Cincinnati Time Store in 1827, where goods were priced by the labour time required to produce them, and customers paid with labour notes. The Japanese philosopher and economist Yoshikazu Sakamoto and others revived interest in the twentieth century, and Teruko Mizushima founded an early volunteer labour bank in Japan in 1973.

Modern timebanking

Contemporary timebanking was systematised by the American law professor Edgar S. Cahn in the 1980s. In a time bank, a member who provides an hour of service — gardening, tutoring, elder care, home repair — earns one time credit, which can be spent on an hour of any other member's service, regardless of the market value of the skills exchanged. Software platforms record credits and debits. Hundreds of time banks operate in the United Kingdom, the United States, and elsewhere, often supported by local authorities as tools for community cohesion and co-production of public services. The best-known time-based paper currency was Ithaca Hours, launched in Ithaca, New York, in 1991.

Criticism

Critics note that valuing all labour equally can deter highly skilled participants, that time credits cannot pay for goods, rent, or taxes, and that most schemes remain small and dependent on grant funding. Supporters respond that the purpose is social rather than purely economic: building reciprocity and valuing unpaid community work.
