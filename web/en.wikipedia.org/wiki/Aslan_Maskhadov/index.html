Aslan Maskhadov

Born: September 21, 1951, Karaganda, Kazakh SSR
Died: March 8, 2005, Tolstoy-Yurt, Chechnya
Office: President of the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria (1997–2005)

Maskhadov, third president of the separatist Chechen Republic of Ichkeria.

Aslan (Khalid) Aliyevich Maskhadov (1951–2005) was a Chechen separatist leader who served as the third president of the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria. A former Soviet artillery colonel, he became chief of staff of the Chechen armed forces during the First Chechen War and negotiated the 1996 Khasavyurt Accord that ended that conflict.

Early life and Soviet career

Maskhadov was born in Kazakhstan, whither his family had been deported in 1944 during the mass deportation of the Chechen people ordered by Stalin. He returned to Chechnya as a teenager and joined the Soviet Army, rising to the rank of colonel in the artillery with postings in Hungary and the Baltic states. He retired in 1992 and returned to Chechnya, joining the government of Dzhokhar Dudayev.

First Chechen War and presidency

As chief of staff, Maskhadov directed the defence of Grozny against Russian forces in 1994–95 and the recapture of the city in August 1996. The ensuing Khasavyurt agreement secured a Russian withdrawal. In January 1997 he won internationally monitored presidential elections in Chechnya with about 60 per cent of the vote.

His presidency was undermined by warlordism, kidnapping and the growing influence of radical Islamist commanders such as Shamil Basayev, over whom Maskhadov exercised diminishing control. In 1999, following the incursion into Dagestan by Basayev's fighters and a series of apartment bombings in Russia, Moscow launched the Second Chechen War and declared Maskhadov's government illegitimate.

Second war and death

During the second war Maskhadov led guerrilla resistance while periodically calling for negotiations and condemning attacks on civilians, including the 2004 Beslan school siege. On March 8, 2005, he was killed by Russian special forces in the village of Tolstoy-Yurt; Russian authorities said he died when troops blew open the bunker in which he was hiding. He was succeeded by Abdul-Khalim Sadulayev.
