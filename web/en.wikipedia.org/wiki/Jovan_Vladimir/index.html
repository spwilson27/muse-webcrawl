Jovan Vladimir (Serbian Cyrillic: Јован Владимир; c. 990 – 22 May 1016) was the ruler of Duklja, the most powerful Serbian principality of the time, from around 1000 to 1016. During his reign he was drawn into the long war between the Byzantine Empire and the Bulgarian Empire of Tsar Samuel. Captured by Samuel, he was — according to tradition — released after the tsar's daughter Theodora Kosara fell in love with him, and he ruled Duklja as a Bulgarian vassal until he was murdered by Samuel's successor, Ivan Vladislav. He is venerated as a saint and martyr by the Eastern Orthodox Church and is the patron saint of the city of Bar in Montenegro.

Reign and war with Samuel

Vladimir succeeded his father Petrislav as ruler of Duklja, a coastal principality centered near modern Bar. Around 1009–1010, as Tsar Samuel consolidated Bulgarian control over the western Balkans, Vladimir withdrew before the Bulgarian advance to Mount Oblik near his capital. The Byzantine historian John Skylitzes and the later Chronicle of the Priest of Duklja recount that Samuel's forces besieged him — legend adds that Vladimir miraculously dealt with venomous snakes on the mountain — and that he surrendered to save his people from slaughter.

Captivity, marriage, and return

Taken prisoner to Samuel's capital at Prespa, Vladimir was, by tradition, visited in prison by the tsar's daughter Kosara, who washed the feet of prisoners as an act of charity. Struck by his piety and nobility, she asked her father for Vladimir's hand; Samuel consented, released Vladimir, and restored him to Duklja as his son-in-law and vassal. The marriage allied Duklja peacefully to the Bulgarian Empire for the rest of Samuel's life.

Death and cult

After Samuel's death in 1014 and the brief reign of Gavril Radomir, the throne passed to Ivan Vladislav, who feared Vladimir as a potential rival with Byzantine support. Luring him to Prespa under a guarantee of safe conduct sealed by a wooden cross, Vladislav had him beheaded on 22 May 1016 — tradition says Vladimir clutched the cross, and that when a gold cross was later substituted, its falseness was taken as a sign of the broken oath. Kosara brought his body back and buried it at the church of the Most Pure Mother of God Krajinska near Lake Skadar. His relics were later transferred to Elbasan in Albania, where the Monastery of Saint Jovan Vladimir preserves them; icons commonly depict him holding his severed head and a cross. His feast is celebrated on 22 May, and an annual procession carries his cross to the summit of Mount Rumija above Bar.
