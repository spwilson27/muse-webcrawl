Jean-François Champollion

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Portrait engraving of Jean-François Champollion in profile]

Jean-François Champollion (23 December 1790 – 4 March 1832) was a French philologist and orientalist, known chiefly as the decipherer of Egyptian hieroglyphs. His work, announced in 1822 and expanded in publications of the 1820s, used the Rosetta Stone and his knowledge of Coptic to unlock the writing system of ancient Egypt.

Decipherment

Champollion built on work by Thomas Young and others, correctly recognising that hieroglyphs combined phonetic and logographic signs, and that the names of rulers in cartouches could be read phonetically. His 1822 Lettre à M. Dacier announced the breakthrough, and his later grammar and dictionary founded modern Egyptology.

Life

A linguistic prodigy who learned numerous ancient languages as a youth, Champollion became a professor at Grenoble and later curator of Egyptian collections at the Louvre; he led a Franco-Tuscan expedition to Egypt in 1828–29 and died aged 41, shortly after taking a chair at the Collège de France.
