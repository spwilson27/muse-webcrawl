Blue and white pottery

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Blue and white pottery covers a wide range of white pottery decorated under the glaze with a blue pigment, generally cobalt oxide. It was developed to its highest level in China, from where it was exported around the world and widely imitated in Europe, Japan, and the Islamic world.

Origins in China

Precursors appeared in Tang dynasty China, but true blue and white porcelain flourished in the Yuan dynasty at Jingdezhen, using imported Persian cobalt. It reached an artistic peak in the early Ming dynasty under the Xuande and Chenghua emperors, and vast quantities were traded to Europe by the Dutch East India Company.

Influence abroad

Chinese export porcelain shaped Delftware in the Netherlands, English "willow pattern" china, Japanese Imari ware and Persian fritware. The style remains strongly associated with Chinese ceramics and with European chinoiserie. See also Porcelain and Blue–white screen (unrelated topic).
