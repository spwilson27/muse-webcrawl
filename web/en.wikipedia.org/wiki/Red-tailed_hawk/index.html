The red-tailed hawk (Buteo jamaicensis) is a bird of prey that breeds throughout most of North America, from the interior of Alaska and northern Canada to as far south as Panama and the West Indies. It is one of the most common members of the genus Buteo.

Description

Adults are recognizable by their brick-red tail; plumage varies considerably across the many subspecies, from pale (Krider's) to very dark (Harlan's) forms. The species has a wingspan of 110–141 cm and weighs roughly 690–1,600 g.

Behaviour

Red-tailed hawks hunt from perches and from the air, feeding mainly on rodents and other small mammals, as well as birds, reptiles, and carrion. Their descending kee-eeeee-arr call is famously used in films as a generic raptor sound. They are monogamous and may mate for life.
