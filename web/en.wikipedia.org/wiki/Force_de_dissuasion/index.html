Force de dissuasion

The Force de dissuasion ('Deterrence Force'), formerly known as the Force de frappe ('Strike Force'), is the French nuclear deterrent. It comprises the country's nuclear weapons capability, operated by the French Air and Space Force and the French Navy, and is controlled directly by the President of the Republic, who is commander-in-chief of the armed forces.

History

France began an independent nuclear weapons programme after the Second World War, accelerated under Charles de Gaulle following his return to power in 1958. The first French atomic bomb was tested on 13 February 1960 in the Sahara (the Gerboise bleue test). De Gaulle's doctrine held that France must be able to defend itself independently, and that its deterrent should be capable of inflicting unacceptable damage on any aggressor — the strategy of deterring the strong by the weak (dissuasion du faible au fort).

Components

Originally a triad of land-based missiles, Mirage IV bombers, and submarine-launched ballistic missiles, the deterrent was reduced to a dyad after the Cold War: ballistic missile submarines (the Triomphant class, based at Île Longue near Brest) provide the sea-based leg, while Rafale fighter-bombers armed with ASMP-A supersonic cruise missiles provide the airborne component. France maintains an arsenal of roughly 290 nuclear warheads.
