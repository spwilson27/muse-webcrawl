Correspondence analysis

Correspondence analysis (CA) is a multivariate statistical technique for analysing and visualising the relationships in a two-way contingency table. Developed principally by Jean-Paul Benzécri and colleagues in France in the 1960s and 1970s, it decomposes the chi-squared statistic of the table and represents row and column categories as points in a low-dimensional space — usually a two-dimensional map — in which proximity indicates association.

Method

Starting from a table of counts, CA computes row and column profiles (conditional proportions) and measures distance between them with the chi-squared distance, which weights differences relative to the average profile. A singular value decomposition of the standardized residuals yields principal coordinates for rows and columns and a set of principal inertias that sum to the total inertia, equal to the table's chi-squared statistic divided by the grand total. The first two dimensions typically capture most of the association and are plotted as a biplot.

Applications and extensions

Correspondence analysis is widely used in market research, ecology (for species-by-site data), archaeology (for seriation of assemblages), sociology and linguistics. Extensions include multiple correspondence analysis (MCA) for several categorical variables, detrended correspondence analysis used in community ecology, and joint correspondence analysis.
