Gravity dam

[Image: Gravity dam]

A gravity dam is a dam constructed from concrete or stone masonry and designed to hold back water by using only the weight of the material and its resistance against the foundation to oppose the horizontal pressure of water pushing against it.

Design

Gravity dams are designed so that each section is independently stable. Their cross-section is roughly triangular, thick at the base and narrowing toward the crest. Stability analysis must consider sliding, overturning, and uplift pressures from water seeping beneath the structure.

Examples

Famous gravity dams include the Grand Coulee Dam and Hoover Dam (an arch-gravity design) in the United States, and the Bhakra Dam in India. The Itaipu and Three Gorges dams also employ gravity sections. Because they consume enormous amounts of concrete, gravity dams are economical mainly where suitable foundations and construction materials are available.
