Kathak

Kathak
Origin: North India
Type: Indian classical dance
Features: storytelling, intricate footwork, spins, facial expression

Kathak is one of the eight major forms of Indian classical dance. Its name derives from the Sanskrit word kathā, "story", and its earliest practitioners were travelling bards, the Kathakas, who narrated epics such as the Mahabharata and Ramayana through dance, song and mime in temple courtyards of northern India.

History

Under Mughal patronage from the 16th century, Kathak moved into royal courts, absorbing Persian and Central Asian influences: straight-legged postures, spinning, and an emphasis on rhythmic improvisation and Urdu poetry. It later flourished in the courts of Lucknow, Jaipur and Banaras, which gave rise to the three major schools or gharanas.

Technique

Kathak is distinguished by upright posture, bare feet, ankle bells (ghungroo), dazzlingly fast footwork set against the rhythmic cycles (tala) of the tabla or pakhawaj, rapid pirouettes, and expressive mime (abhinaya) used for storytelling. Performances typically move from slow devotional pieces to virtuosic displays of rhythm, with a characteristic interplay between dancer and drummer.

Gharanas

The Lucknow gharana emphasises grace and expression; the Jaipur gharana, rhythmic complexity and footwork; and the Banaras gharana, a blend developed in the courts of Varanasi. Noted exponents include Birju Maharaj, Sitara Devi and Shambhu Maharaj.
