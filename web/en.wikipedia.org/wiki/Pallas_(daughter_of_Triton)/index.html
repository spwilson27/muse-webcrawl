Pallas (daughter of Triton)

In Greek mythology, Pallas (Ancient Greek: Παλλάς) was the daughter of Triton, the messenger of the sea, and the childhood playmate and sparring companion of the goddess Athena. The two were raised together in Libya, at Lake Tritonis, where they practised the arts of war together.

Myth

According to the account preserved in the Bibliotheca of Apollodorus, during a friendly sparring match between the two goddesses, Zeus became alarmed for his daughter and intervened by holding up the aegis. Pallas, startled, glanced upward and was struck down by Athena, who had not seen the danger in time. In grief and remorse, Athena fashioned a wooden image of her friend—the Palladium—and wrapped the aegis around it, honouring it beside Zeus. Some versions connect Pallas's skin to the making of the aegis itself, and Athena took the name Pallas Athena in her memory.

The Palladium

The Palladium became a sacred talisman: an image later said to have fallen from heaven at Troy, whose possession guaranteed the safety of the city. Its theft by Odysseus and Diomedes was a famous episode of the Trojan War cycle.
