Cabbage roll

Cabbage roll
TypeStuffed vegetable dish
RegionCentral, Northern, Eastern Europe; Middle East
Main ingredientsCabbage leaves, minced meat, rice
VariationsGołąbki, sarma, holubtsi, töltött káposzta, kåldolmar

A cabbage roll is a dish consisting of cooked cabbage leaves wrapped around a filling, usually seasoned minced meat (pork, beef, or a mixture) combined with rice or other grains. Cabbage rolls are a staple of the cuisines of Central, Northern, and Eastern Europe, the Balkans, and parts of the Middle East and Central Asia, where related dishes are made with grape or other leaves.

Cabbage rolls served in a tomato-based sauce.

Regional varieties

In Poland they are gołąbki ("little pigeons"); in Ukraine holubtsi; in Romania and the Balkans sarma (often made with pickled cabbage); in Hungary töltött káposzta, traditionally served at Christmas and New Year; in Sweden kåldolmar, said to have been introduced after Charles XII's exile in the Ottoman Empire; and in Germany Kohlrouladen. Jewish cuisine has a long tradition of cabbage rolls (holishkes) eaten at Sukkot and Simchat Torah. Fillings in some traditions are meatless, using mushrooms, grains, or lentils for fasting periods.

Preparation

Cabbage leaves are blanched or fermented to make them pliable, wrapped around the filling, and then baked, simmered, or steamed, often in a tomato or sour-cream sauce. The dish is widely regarded as comfort food and is commonly prepared in large batches for festivals and family gatherings.
