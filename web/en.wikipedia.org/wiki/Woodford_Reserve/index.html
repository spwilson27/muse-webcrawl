Woodford Reserve

Woodford Reserve is a brand of Kentucky straight bourbon whiskey produced at the Woodford Reserve Distillery in Woodford County, Kentucky, by the Brown-Forman Corporation. Launched in 1996, it is known for pot-still distillation blended with column-still whiskey and a high rye mash bill.

History

Distilling on the site near Versailles, Kentucky, began in 1812, and the stone distillery — one of the oldest in Kentucky — dates to the 19th century, operating historically as the Labrot & Graham Distillery. Brown-Forman repurchased the property in 1993, restored it, and relaunched the brand in 1996 under master distiller Chris Morris.

Production

Woodford Reserve uses a mash bill of 72% corn, 18% rye and 10% malted barley. Production combines triple distillation in copper pot stills with column-distilled spirit, aged in new charred oak barrels in heat-cycled warehouses. The distillery is unusual in using limestone water and barrel entry at a relatively low proof.

Products and recognition

The core range includes the standard bourbon, Double Oaked, a rye whiskey, and limited Master's Collection releases. The brand is the official bourbon of the Kentucky Derby and sponsors the "Best of Kentucky" culinary programme.

Site

The distillery, a National Historic Landmark, is a stop on the Kentucky Bourbon Trail and welcomes visitors for tours and tastings.
