Shooter game

Shooter games are a genre of video games centred on combat with ranged weapons, usually firearms or projectile weapons. The player typically controls a character who must aim and fire at enemies while managing ammunition, health and positioning. It is one of the most commercially successful genres in the industry.

Subgenres

Major subgenres include the first-person shooter (FPS), viewed through the character's eyes — pioneered by Wolfenstein 3D (1992) and Doom (1993) and extended by series such as Half-Life, Halo and Call of Duty; the third-person shooter, viewed over the shoulder (e.g., Gears of War); rail and light-gun shooters; shoot 'em ups ("shmups"); hero shooters with character abilities; looter shooters; and the battle royale format popularised by Fortnite and PUBG. Tactical shooters emphasise realism and teamwork, while arena shooters favour speed and movement.

Design elements

Core mechanics include hit-scan or projectile weapons, heads-up displays showing health and ammunition, cover systems, regenerating health or health packs, and multiplayer modes from deathmatch to objective-based team play. Shooter games are a major focus of esports and of public debate about violence in media.
