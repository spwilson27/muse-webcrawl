Isotype (picture language)

Isotype (International System of Typographic Picture Education) is a method of displaying social, technological, biological and historical information in pictorial form. It was developed in Vienna in the 1920s and 1930s by the Austrian philosopher and curator Otto Neurath, his wife Marie Neurath, and the artist Gerd Arntz, building on the "Vienna Method of Pictorial Statistics" at the Gesellschafts- und Wirtschaftsmuseum.

Principles

Isotype charts use standardised pictograms, designed so that each symbol represents a fixed quantity; larger quantities are shown by repeating the symbol rather than by enlarging it, so that amounts can be counted and compared at a glance. Quantities are never represented by the height of a single symbol alone. Rules of consistency, simplification and a small set of "transformer" guidelines — Marie Neurath's term for the person mediating between data and designer — governed production.

Legacy

Isotype influenced modern information design, infographics and pictogram systems worldwide, from road signage to public-health charts. The Isotype collection is held at the University of Reading. Otto Neurath's book International Picture Language (1936) set out the basic English vocabulary of the system.
