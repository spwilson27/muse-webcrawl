Motorboat

A motorboat, also called a powerboat or speedboat, is a boat that is exclusively powered by an engine. Some motorboats are fitted with inboard engines, others have an outboard motor installed at the rear, containing the internal combustion engine, the gearbox and the propeller in one portable unit. Hybrid configurations include inboard–outboard (sterndrive) installations and jet drives, which have no external propeller.

Types of engine

  • Outboard motors — self-contained units mounted on the transom; dominant on small boats, easily removed for storage and service.
  • Inboard motors — engines mounted inside the hull, driving a shaft and fixed propeller, with a separate rudder; typical of larger cruisers.
  • Sterndrives — an inboard engine connected to a steerable drive leg outside the hull.
  • Jet boats — an internal impeller draws in water and expels it in a jet, allowing operation in very shallow water but with less precise low-speed handling.

Hull forms

Motorboat hulls are broadly displacement hulls, which push through the water at modest speed, or planing hulls, shaped so that at speed the boat rises and skims across the surface, greatly reducing drag. Deep-V hulls handle rough offshore water; flat-bottomed and modified-V hulls suit sheltered waters.

Uses

Motorboats serve for recreation (fishing, waterskiing, cruising), racing — including offshore powerboat racing and hydroplane classes — and work: patrol, pilotage, rescue, and military roles. The first practical motorboats appeared in the late 19th century, following experiments such as Daimler's 1886 test boat on the Neckar, and mass production of outboards began with Ole Evinrude's detachable motor of 1909.
