Incentive

An incentive is something that motivates or encourages someone to do something. Economists treat incentives as central to the study of human behaviour: people respond to rewards and penalties, whether monetary (wages, bonuses, fines, taxes) or non-monetary (status, praise, punishment, moral satisfaction).

Types

Incentives are often divided into intrinsic incentives, arising from the inherent satisfaction of an activity, and extrinsic incentives, provided by others. Remunerative incentives offer material reward; moral incentives appeal to conscience or social approval; coercive incentives involve the threat of force or penalty. Economists also study perverse incentives, where a policy produces unintended and undesirable outcomes — the classic example being a bounty on snakes that led people to breed snakes for the reward (the "cobra effect").

Applications

Incentive design underlies contract theory, mechanism design, employee compensation, tax policy and public programme design. Principal–agent problems arise when one party's incentives diverge from another's, and economists study how contracts can align them.
