William Hornby (governor)

Born: 1723
Died: 1803
Office: Governor of Bombay (1771–1784)
Known for: Hornby Vellard sea wall, Bombay

William Hornby (c. 1723 – 1803) was a British colonial administrator who served as Governor of Bombay from 1771 to 1784. He is best remembered for the great sea wall known as the Hornby Vellard, which began the reclamation that joined Bombay's islands into one landmass.

Career in India

Hornby joined the East India Company as a writer and rose through its service in Bombay, becoming governor in 1771. His long administration covered the period of the First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–1782), during which Bombay played a central role in the Company's western India strategy.

Hornby Vellard

In 1782 Hornby obtained permission to build a vellard (embankment) across the Great Breach at Worli, closing the inlet that separated the islands of Bombay and Worli and flooding at low tide large areas between them. Completed in 1784, the Hornby Vellard was the first major step in transforming the seven islands of Bombay into a single city, and its name survives in Mumbai today.

Later life

Hornby returned to England in 1784 and settled in Hampshire, where he built the mansion Hook House near Titchfield. He died in 1803.
