Wide-bandgap semiconductor

Wide-bandgap semiconductors (WBG, WBGS) are materials with an electronic bandgap significantly larger than silicon's 1.1 eV, conventionally above about 2 eV. Leading examples are silicon carbide (SiC, ~3.2 eV) and gallium nitride (GaN, ~3.4 eV), with gallium oxide and diamond under research.

Properties

A larger bandgap gives a higher critical breakdown field, permitting thinner, more heavily doped drift regions, lower on-resistance and faster switching. WBG devices tolerate higher temperatures and voltages and switch with lower losses than silicon.

Applications

SiC MOSFETs and diodes are used in electric-vehicle traction inverters, rail traction, solar inverters and industrial drives; GaN HEMTs dominate fast chargers, RF power and compact power supplies. Blue and white LEDs and laser diodes are also wide-bandgap (GaN/InGaN) devices.

Challenges

Costs, substrate defects, gate-oxide reliability in SiC, and packaging for high switching speeds remain active engineering issues.
