The Wooden Churches of Maramureș are a group of almost one hundred Orthodox and Greek-Catholic churches of different architectural styles from different periods and areas in the historic Maramureș region of northern Romania. Eight of them were inscribed by UNESCO on the World Heritage List in 1999 for their outstanding significance as vernacular religious architecture.

The churches are high timber constructions with characteristic tall, slim bell towers at the western end of the building, single or double roofs, and interiors painted with biblical scenes by local artists. They are a particular vernacular expression of the cultural landscape of this mountainous area of northern Romania.

The eight inscribed churches

  • Bârsana — Church of the Presentation of the Virgin at the Temple, built 1720.
  • Budești Josani — Church of Saint Nicholas, dated 1643.
  • Desești — Church of the Holy Paraskeva, built 1770, with interior paintings by Radu Munteanu.
  • Ieud Deal — Church of the Nativity of the Virgin, dated by dendrochronology to the early 17th century, among the oldest in Maramureș.
  • Plopiș — Church of the Archangels Michael and Gabriel, built 1796–1798.
  • Poienile Izei — Church of the Holy Paraskeva, begun 1604, noted for its vivid mural of hell.
  • Rogoz — Church of the Holy Archangels, built 1663.
  • Șurdești — Church of the Holy Archangels, built 1721; its tower, at about 54 metres, made it for centuries the tallest wooden church in the world.

Architecture and construction

The churches are built of massive oak beams laid horizontally and joined at the corners, resting on stone foundations that protect the timber from ground moisture. Roofs are steeply pitched and covered with wooden shingles, often in two tiers with an overhanging eaves gallery. The plan is typically a small rectangular nave with a polygonal apse; interiors are low and dark, their walls covered with tempera paintings executed by itinerant local painters such as Alexandru Ponehalschi and Radu Munteanu. The tradition of building in wood reflected both the abundance of oak forests and restrictions imposed in the Habsburg era on Orthodox communities building in stone.

Preservation

Many churches survived because villages maintained them as parish churches for centuries. Restoration campaigns before and after UNESCO inscription repaired roofs, consolidated beams, and conserved wall paintings. The related Church of the Holy Archangels in Șurdești and the monastery church at Bârsana (rebuilt in the 1990s with a tower of about 57 m) remain landmarks of the region's wooden architecture.
