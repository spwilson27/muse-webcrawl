Animal slaughter

[Image: Livestock being moved through handling pens at a slaughterhouse]

Animal slaughter is the killing of animals, usually for food production. Modern industrial slaughter takes place in slaughterhouses (abattoirs) where animals are typically stunned into unconsciousness — by captive bolt, electrical current or carbon dioxide — before being bled, to reduce suffering and comply with humane-slaughter law.

Scale

Globally, tens of billions of land animals are slaughtered each year, the great majority chickens, followed by pigs, sheep, goats, cattle and ducks. Numbers are tracked by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations.

Regulation

Most developed countries require pre-slaughter stunning, with exemptions for religious slaughter such as Jewish shechita and Islamic halal practice — an ongoing source of ethical and political debate. Inspection regimes govern hygiene, animal handling and worker safety.

Criticism

Animal rights and welfare advocates criticise slaughter on ethical grounds, while environmental assessments note the contribution of meat production to greenhouse-gas emissions and land use.
