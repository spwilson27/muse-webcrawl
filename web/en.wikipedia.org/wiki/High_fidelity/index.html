High fidelity


A hi-fi home audio system

High fidelity (often abbreviated as hi-fi or hifi) is the high-quality reproduction of sound. It is popular with audiophiles and home audio enthusiasts. Ideally, high-fidelity equipment has inaudible noise and distortion, and a flat (neutral) frequency response within the human hearing range.

History

The term began to be popularized in the 1950s as the LP record, FM stereo broadcasting, and improved amplifiers and loudspeakers made accurate home reproduction feasible. Standards such as the German DIN 45500 gave the term a technical definition for consumer equipment. Through the 1960s and 1970s, component-based stereo systems — turntable or tape source, preamplifier and power amplifier, and loudspeakers — became the standard enthusiast setup, and specialist magazines reviewed equipment by measurement as well as listening.

Modern practice

The compact disc (1982) shifted hi-fi toward digital sources; today streaming services, DACs, and high-resolution audio formats are central, alongside a revival of vinyl records. The pursuit of fidelity continues to generate debate over issues such as loudspeaker-room interaction, amplifier distortion, cable claims, and the limits of human perception, with double-blind testing used by some reviewers to evaluate audible differences.
