Relative dating

Relative dating is the science of determining the relative order of past events — that is, the age of an object compared to another — without necessarily determining their absolute age in years. It is used in geology, archaeology and palaeontology, and contrasts with absolute dating methods such as radiometric dating.

Principles in geology

Geological relative dating relies on principles formulated from the 17th to 19th centuries: the law of superposition (younger strata lie above older ones), the principle of original horizontality, the principle of cross-cutting relationships, and the principle of faunal succession, by which fossil assemblages succeed one another in a definite order.

Methods in archaeology

Archaeologists use stratigraphy, typology and seriation to order artefacts and features relative to one another. Relative sequences can then be anchored to calendar dates where absolute methods or historical records are available.
