Brake fluid

Brake fluid is a hydraulic fluid used in the braking systems of cars, motorcycles and some bicycles.

Brake fluid is a type of hydraulic fluid used in hydraulic brake and hydraulic clutch applications in automobiles, motorcycles, light trucks, and some bicycles. It is used to transfer force into pressure, and to amplify braking force. It works because liquids are not appreciably compressible.

Most brake fluids used today are glycol-ether based, but mineral oil (Citroën Hydraulic Mineral Fluid, LHM) and silicone-based (DOT 5) fluids are also available. Brake fluids must meet certain requirements as defined by various standards set by organizations such as SAE International and the United States Department of Transportation (DOT).

Standards and grades

The United States Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 116 defines grades DOT 3, DOT 4, DOT 5 and DOT 5.1, distinguished mainly by their dry and wet boiling points. "Dry" boiling point refers to fresh fluid from a sealed container; "wet" boiling point refers to fluid that has absorbed about 3.7% water, approximating fluid after roughly two years of service. Typical minimum dry boiling points are 205 °C for DOT 3, 230 °C for DOT 4, and 260 °C for DOT 5 and DOT 5.1. Higher grades are required because braking generates considerable heat; if the fluid boils, vapour forms and, being compressible, causes a spongy pedal and loss of braking.

Characteristics

Brake fluids must have a low freezing point, remain stable at high temperatures, lubricate seals and moving parts, and not corrode metals or swell rubber components. Glycol-based fluids are hygroscopic — they absorb moisture from the air over time, which lowers their boiling point and promotes internal corrosion. For this reason, manufacturers recommend periodic replacement, commonly every two years.

Incompatibility

Silicone-based DOT 5 fluid must never be mixed with glycol-based fluids, and systems designed for one type may suffer seal damage if filled with another. Mineral-oil systems, such as Citroën's hydropneumatic suspension and braking circuits, likewise require their specific fluid and are incompatible with glycol fluids.

Safety

Brake fluid is toxic and can damage paintwork. Spills should be cleaned promptly with water. Because of its hygroscopic nature, containers should be kept sealed.
