Third Council of the Lateran

Date: March 1179
Location: Lateran Palace, Rome
Convoked by: Pope Alexander III
Documents: 27 canons
Topic: Papal elections, heresy, church reform

The Lateran Palace, Rome

The Third Council of the Lateran met in March 1179 in Rome under Pope Alexander III. It is counted by the Roman Catholic Church as the eleventh ecumenical council. About 302 bishops attended, together with abbots and representatives of the chapters, following the Peace of Venice (1177) which had ended the schism between Alexander III and the Emperor Frederick Barbarossa.

Decrees

The council issued 27 canons. Its most enduring measure concerned papal elections:

  • Two-thirds majority — Canon 1 required that a pope be elected by a two-thirds majority of the cardinals, a rule (with later modifications) that still governs papal conclaves today.
  • Minimum ages — Candidates for bishoprics had to be at least thirty years old, and legitimately born.
  • Simony and fees — The council forbade charging fees for burials, blessings, and the administration of the sacraments.
  • Heresy — Canon 27 condemned the Cathars and other heretics of southern France and authorized action against them and against mercenary bands such as the Routiers.
  • Jews and Muslims — Provisions restricted the employment of Christian servants and nurses by Jews and Saracens and the bearing of arms.
  • Tournaments — Knights killed in tournaments were to be denied ecclesiastical burial.

Significance

The council consolidated the restoration of unity after nearly two decades of schism, regularized papal elections, and advanced the legal reform of the Church that would culminate in the Fourth Council of the Lateran in 1215.
