Sierra (supercomputer)

Sierra, also known as ATS-2, is a supercomputer built by IBM for the Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) in California for use by the United States National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA). Delivered in 2017–2018, it is primarily dedicated to predictive simulations for nuclear stockpile stewardship — modelling the behaviour and ageing of US nuclear weapons without underground testing. Sierra is a close sibling of the Summit supercomputer at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, sharing its architecture.

Architecture

Sierra consists of 4,320 nodes. Each node contains two IBM POWER9 processors (44 cores) and four NVIDIA Volta V100 GPU accelerators connected by NVLink, giving the system 17,280 GPUs in total. The machine has more than 1.5 petabytes of memory and uses a fat-tree Mellanox EDR InfiniBand interconnect. Its non-volatile storage tier uses node-local NVMe devices aggregated by a burst-buffer layer. The system consumes roughly 11 megawatts of power (commonly cited at about 11–13 MW).

Performance and use

On the TOP500 list of November 2018, Sierra debuted at number two behind its sister machine Summit, with a High Performance LINPACK result of about 94.6 petaFLOPS and a theoretical peak near 125 petaFLOPS. Unlike Summit, most of Sierra's capacity is classified and devoted to weapons simulation under the NNSA's Advanced Simulation and Computing program, although a portion of its time supports unclassified science. In 2020, for example, Sierra capacity was used in open research connected with COVID-19 modelling alongside other national laboratory systems. Sierra was succeeded in the Livermore computing line-up by the exascale machine El Capitan, which entered service in 2024.
