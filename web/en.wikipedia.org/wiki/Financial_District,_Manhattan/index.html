The Financial District of Lower Manhattan, also known as FiDi, is a neighbourhood located on the southern tip of Manhattan Island in New York City. It is bounded roughly by the West Side Highway on the west, Chambers Street and City Hall Park on the north, Brooklyn Bridge on the northeast, the East River to the southeast, and the Hudson River to the southwest.

History

The district occupies the site of New Amsterdam, founded in 1624; its name recalls the protective wall built across the island in 1653 that gave Wall Street its name. Federal Hall on Wall Street was the site of George Washington's inauguration in 1789 and the first US Capitol. The New York Stock Exchange, formally organised in 1817, anchored the district's rise as the capital of American finance, joined by the headquarters of major banks and the Federal Reserve Bank of New York.

Landmarks and economy

The district includes the New York Stock Exchange Building, the Charging Bull statue, Trinity Church, the Stone Street historic district, and the rebuilt World Trade Center complex, including One World Trade Center (opened 2014). Once almost purely an office district emptying after business hours, FiDi has gained a large residential population since the 1990s through office-to-residential conversions and new towers.
