Popliteal artery

The popliteal artery is a deeply placed continuation of the femoral artery that runs behind the knee through the popliteal fossa. It supplies blood to the knee joint and the muscles of the thigh, calf and sole of the foot.

Course

The artery begins at the adductor hiatus in the adductor magnus, descends through the popliteal fossa where it is the deepest structure, lying on the floor of the fossa formed by the femur, joint capsule and popliteus muscle, and ends at the lower border of the popliteus, where it divides into the anterior tibial artery and the tibioperoneal trunk, which gives rise to the posterior tibial and fibular arteries.

Branches

It gives off superior and inferior lateral and medial genicular arteries and the middle genicular artery, which form an anastomosis around the knee, along with sural branches to the gastrocnemius and cutaneous branches.

Clinical significance

The popliteal pulse can be felt deep in the fossa with the knee flexed. The artery is vulnerable in knee dislocations and distal femur fractures, and is a recognised site for aneurysm — popliteal artery aneurysm is the most common peripheral arterial aneurysm — as well as for the rare popliteal artery entrapment syndrome.
