The Four Seasons (Italian: Le quattro stagioni) is a group of four violin concertos by Italian composer Antonio Vivaldi, each giving musical expression to a season of the year. They were written around 1718–1720 and published in Amsterdam in 1725 as the first four works of his Opus 8 collection, Il cimento dell'armonia e dell'inventione ("The Contest Between Harmony and Invention").

Structure

Each concerto follows the fast–slow–fast three-movement pattern: Spring (E major, RV 269), Summer (G minor, RV 315), Autumn (F major, RV 293), and Winter (F minor, RV 297). Vivaldi published the concertos with accompanying sonnets, possibly written by himself, describing the scenes the music depicts — birdsong and thunderstorms in Spring, a languid heat and violent storm in Summer, harvest dances and the hunt in Autumn, and shivering cold and slipping ice in Winter.

Significance

The concertos are among the earliest and most detailed examples of programme music and remain among the most popular works in the classical repertoire, extensively recorded and widely used in film and advertising.
