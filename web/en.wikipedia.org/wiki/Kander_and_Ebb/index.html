Kander and Ebb

Kander and Ebb were an American songwriting team consisting of composer John Kander (born 1927) and lyricist Fred Ebb (1928–2004), one of the most successful partnerships in Broadway history, active from the early 1960s until Ebb's death.

Works

Introduced in 1962, they wrote the score for Flora the Red Menace (1965), which launched Liza Minnelli, a frequent collaborator. Their breakthrough was Cabaret (1966), which won the Tony Award for Best Musical and became the 1972 film directed by Bob Fosse. A second landmark, Chicago (1975), initially overshadowed, was revived in 1996 to become Broadway's longest-running American musical, and its 2002 film won the Academy Award for Best Picture.

Other shows include Zorba, Woman of the Year, Kiss of the Spider Woman (Tony for Best Musical, 1993), Steel Pier, and posthumous works Curtains and The Scottsboro Boys, completed by Kander. For film they wrote the title song of Martin Scorsese's New York, New York (1977), introduced by Minnelli and made famous by Frank Sinatra.

Style

Their work is noted for vaudeville- and ragtime-inflected scores, Brechtian framing devices, and a dark, ironic edge beneath show-business glitter — pairing entertainment with moral unease in stories of Weimar Berlin decadence and Jazz Age murder.
