Whitney Biennial

The Whitney Biennial is an exhibition of contemporary American art held every two years at the Whitney Museum of American Art in New York City. Founded in 1932 as an annual show and held biennially since 1973, it is the longest-running survey of American art and one of the most closely watched events in the art world.

History and format

Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney established the exhibition to showcase living American artists at a time when American art was overshadowed by European modernism. Each edition is organised by one or more curators, who select a few dozen artists working in painting, sculpture, photography, film, video, performance and new media. Inclusion is a major career milestone for emerging artists, and the show is famous both for launching reputations and for provoking controversy.

Reception

Biennials are routinely debated: the politically charged 1993 edition, the sprawling 2006 "Day for Night", and the 2017 edition — marked by protests over a painting of Emmett Till by a white artist — each generated national discussion about race, identity and the role of museums. Critics variously hail the Biennial as a vital barometer of American art or attack it as trend-driven, but few deny its influence.
