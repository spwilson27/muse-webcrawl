Susceptance

In electrical engineering, susceptance (B) is the imaginary part of admittance (Y), where the real part is conductance (G): Y = G + jB. It is measured in siemens (formerly mhos). The concept was introduced by Oliver Heaviside and Charles Proteus Steinmetz in the development of phasor analysis of AC circuits.

Relations

Susceptance is related to reactance (X), the imaginary part of impedance, by B = −X / (R² + X²), which simplifies to B = −1/X for a purely reactive element. For a capacitor, susceptance is positive and equals B = ωC; for an inductor it is negative, B = −1/(ωL), where ω is angular frequency.

Interpretation

Physically, susceptance describes the ease with which a circuit element passes the reactive (energy-storing) component of alternating current. A positive susceptance is capacitive and a negative susceptance is inductive, by the sign convention of admittance.
