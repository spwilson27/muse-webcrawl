Depth of field

In optics, the depth of field (DOF) is the distance between the nearest and the farthest objects that are in acceptably sharp focus in an image captured with a camera. For cameras, depth of field is measured for a particular focal length, aperture (f-number), focus distance and acceptable circle of confusion. Depth of field can be used for artistic effect: a shallow depth isolates a subject from its background, while a deep depth keeps an entire landscape sharp.

Factors

  • Aperture: wider apertures (lower f-numbers) give shallower depth of field; stopping down increases it.
  • Focal length: longer lenses yield shallower depth of field at the same subject framing and aperture (though at equal magnification the effect is largely due to aperture diameter).
  • Distance: depth of field increases with focus distance and decreases sharply at close range, which is why macro photography has extremely shallow focus.
  • Sensor/format size: smaller formats yield deeper depth of field for equivalent framing and f-number.

Hyperfocal distance

The hyperfocal distance is the focus distance at which depth of field extends from half that distance to infinity — the basis of zone focusing used in landscape and street photography. The visual quality of out-of-focus areas is termed bokeh.
