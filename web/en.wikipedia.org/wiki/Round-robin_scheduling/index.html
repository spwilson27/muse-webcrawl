Round-robin scheduling

Round-robin (RR) scheduling is a preemptive scheduling algorithm used in operating systems and networks. Each process in the ready queue is given a fixed time slice, or quantum, in cyclic order.

Operation

Processes are held in a FIFO queue. The scheduler runs the first process for up to one quantum; if it does not finish, it is preempted and returned to the end of the queue. The choice of quantum is critical: too small increases context-switch overhead, too large degenerates to first-come, first-served.

Applications

Round-robin is simple, fair and starvation-free, making it common in time-sharing systems. Variants such as weighted round robin are used in packet switching and load balancing.
