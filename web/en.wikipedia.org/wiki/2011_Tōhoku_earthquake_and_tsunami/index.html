The 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami occurred at 14:46 JST on Friday, 11 March 2011. The undersea megathrust earthquake, with a magnitude of 9.0–9.1, was the most powerful earthquake ever recorded in Japan and the fourth most powerful in the world since modern record-keeping began in 1900. It triggered powerful tsunami waves that devastated the Pacific coast of the Tōhoku region and caused the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster.

Earthquake

The earthquake occurred where the Pacific Plate subducts beneath the plate carrying northern Honshu, at a depth of about 29 km. The rupture shifted the seabed by tens of metres; the main island of Honshu moved roughly 2.4 m eastward, and the Earth's axis shifted by an estimated 10–25 cm. Hundreds of aftershocks followed, several exceeding magnitude 7.

Tsunami

Waves reached heights of up to 40.5 metres in Miyako, Iwate Prefecture, and travelled up to 10 km inland around Sendai. Entire towns — including Rikuzentakata, Minamisanriku and much of Ishinomaki — were destroyed. Approximately 19,700 people were confirmed dead and more than 2,500 listed as missing, overwhelmingly by drowning. Over 120,000 buildings were totally destroyed.

Fukushima nuclear accident

The tsunami flooded the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant, disabling cooling systems and causing meltdowns in three reactors, hydrogen explosions and substantial releases of radioactive material. The accident was rated Level 7 on the International Nuclear Event Scale, the only such rating besides Chernobyl. Around 150,000 residents were evacuated from the surrounding area.

Impact and recovery

The World Bank estimated the economic cost at US$235 billion, making it the costliest natural disaster in history. Japan's response included a massive Self-Defense Forces relief operation and international assistance. Reconstruction of the Tōhoku coast has continued for more than a decade, alongside long-term decommissioning work at Fukushima Daiichi.
