Durendal

Durendal
Owner: Roland
Source: The Song of Roland
Type: Legendary sword

Durendal, also spelled Durandal or Durandana, is the legendary sword of Roland, the paladin of Charlemagne in the Matter of France. According to The Song of Roland, the sword was given to Charlemagne by an angel and contained in its golden hilt a tooth of Saint Peter, blood of Saint Basil, hair of Saint Denis, and a piece of the raiment of the Virgin Mary.

Legend

Durendal was said to be the sharpest sword in existence, capable of cutting through giant boulders of marble. At the Battle of Roncevaux Pass (778), the mortally wounded Roland attempted to destroy the sword against the rocks so it would not fall into enemy hands, but the blade proved indestructible — instead, legend says, it carved the Roland's Breach in the Pyrenees. In some traditions Roland threw it into a poisoned stream; in others it was taken to Rocamadour.

Rocamadour

A sword embedded in the cliff wall above the sanctuary of Rocamadour in France is traditionally identified as Durendal; a replica remains on display after the original was stolen in 2024 and later recovered. The name may derive from the French dur, "hard" or "enduring".
