EDGE

Enhanced Data rates for GSM Evolution (EDGE), also known as Enhanced GPRS (EGPRS), is a digital mobile phone technology that allows improved data transmission rates as a backward-compatible extension of GSM. EDGE is considered a pre-3G ("2.75G") radio technology and has been part of the ITU's 3G definition. EDGE was deployed on GSM networks beginning in 2003 — initially by Cingular (now AT&T) in the United States.

Technology

EDGE introduces a new modulation scheme, 8-PSK (eight-phase shift keying), in addition to the GMSK used by GSM, tripling the data carried per symbol. With nine modulation and coding schemes (MCS-1 through MCS-9) chosen adaptively according to channel quality, EDGE delivers theoretical peak rates of up to 473.6 kbit/s using all eight timeslots, with typical real-world user rates around 100–200 kbit/s and latency around 200 ms — roughly three times the throughput of GPRS. Deploying EDGE required changes to base-station hardware and software but reused GSM's core network and spectrum.

Evolved EDGE

The 3GPP Release 7 enhancement "Evolved EDGE" doubled potential rates (up to about 1 Mbit/s peak) through dual-carrier operation, higher-order 16/32-QAM modulation, and improved receivers, and halved latency. It saw little commercial deployment as networks moved to HSPA and LTE.

Deployment and legacy

By the late 2000s EDGE was available on most GSM networks worldwide — over 300 networks in more than 150 countries — and served as the fallback data bearer for early smartphones, including the first iPhone (2007), which famously launched with EDGE only. As 2G networks are progressively shut down in many countries, EDGE coverage is disappearing, though it persists on numerous networks in developing markets.
