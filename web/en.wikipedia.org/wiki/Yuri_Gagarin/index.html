Yuri Gagarin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Yuri Gagarin in his flight suit and helmet smiling before the Vostok 1 mission, 1961 photograph]
Gagarin before his flight.

Yuri Alekseyevich Gagarin (1934–1968) was a Soviet pilot and cosmonaut who became the first human to journey into outer space. On 12 April 1961 his Vostok 1 capsule completed one orbit of Earth in 108 minutes, and his reported words — \'Poyekhali!\' (\'Let\'s go!\') at launch and remarks on Earth\'s beauty — made him a global hero. Gagarin never flew in space again (his superiors feared losing him) and died in a jet training crash in 1968.

Early life and selection

Born near Gzhatsk, Gagarin trained as a foundry worker and then as a fighter pilot. In 1960 he was chosen among the first twenty cosmonauts; his small stature (1.57 m), calm temperament and peasant background suited both the cramped capsule and Soviet publicity.

The flight

Vostok 1 launched from Baikonur, flew one orbit, and Gagarin ejected at about 7 km altitude to land by parachute separately from the capsule, as planned — a fact long downplayed because landing rules for aviation records then required pilot and craft to land together. He was promoted and celebrated worldwide, touring dozens of countries.

Death and legacy

On 27 March 1968 Gagarin died when his MiG-15UTI trainer crashed; the cause remains debated (a nearby jet\'s wake turbulence is a leading explanation). 12 April is celebrated as Cosmonautics Day in Russia and the International Day of Human Space Flight; his ashes rest in the Kremlin Wall Necropolis.
