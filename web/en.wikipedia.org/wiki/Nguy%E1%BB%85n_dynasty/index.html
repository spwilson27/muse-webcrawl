The Nguyễn dynasty was the last Vietnamese dynasty, ruling a unified Vietnam from 1802 to 1945. It was founded by Nguyễn Phúc Ánh, who took the reign name Gia Long after defeating the Tây Sơn dynasty with French assistance.

Early period

Gia Long unified the country under the name Việt Nam and established the capital at Huế. His successor Minh Mệnh (r. 1820–1841) centralised administration on the Chinese Confucian model, reorganised the provinces, and pursued isolationist policies toward the West, including persecution of Christian missionaries.

French colonisation

French military intervention from 1858 onward forced the court to cede territory in stages. By the treaties of 1883–1884 Vietnam became a French protectorate (Annam and Tonkin) while Cochinchina was a direct colony, forming part of French Indochina. The emperors retained a ceremonial role under French residents.

End of the dynasty

During the Second World War the Japanese displaced the French administration. In August 1945, amid the August Revolution led by the Viet Minh, Emperor Bảo Đại abdicated, ending 143 years of Nguyễn rule. The dynasty left major cultural monuments in Huế, including the Imperial City and the imperial tombs, now UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
