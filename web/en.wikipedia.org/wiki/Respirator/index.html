Respirator

A respirator is a device designed to protect the wearer from inhaling hazardous atmospheres including lead fumes, vapours, gases, dusts, and airborne microorganisms. There are two main categories of respirators: the air-purifying respirator, in which respirable air is obtained by filtering a contaminated atmosphere, and the air-supplied respirator, in which an alternate supply of breathable air is delivered. Within each category, there are various techniques employed to either reduce or eliminate noxious airborne contaminants.

History

The history of protective respiratory equipment can be traced back as far as the first century, when Pliny the Elder described the use of animal bladder skins to protect workers in Roman mines from red lead oxide. In the 16th century, Leonardo da Vinci suggested that a finely woven cloth dipped in water could protect sailors from a toxic powder weapon he had designed. Modern respirator development was greatly stimulated by the chemical warfare of the First World War.

Types

Air-purifying respirators range from relatively inexpensive single-use disposable face masks — sometimes referred to as dust masks — to more robust reusable models with replaceable cartridges, often called gas masks. Air-supplied respirators deliver clean air from a stationary source or a portable tank; supplied-air respirators (SARs) and self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA) are examples.

Standards

In the United States, the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) certifies respirators under 42 CFR Part 84, which classifies particulate filters by efficiency (95%, 99%, or 99.97%) and resistance to oil degradation (N, R, or P series). The Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) requires fit testing to ensure an adequate seal between the respirator facepiece and the wearer's face.
