Praseodymium

Symbol: Pr

Atomic number: 59

Atomic mass: 140.908 u

Group: Lanthanide

Melting point: 931 °C

Praseodymium is a chemical element with the symbol Pr and atomic number 59. It is the third member of the lanthanide series and is traditionally considered one of the rare-earth metals. Praseodymium is a soft, silvery, malleable and ductile metal, valued for its magnetic, electrical, chemical, optical and other properties too specific for most consumer applications.

History

In 1885, the Austrian chemist Carl Auer von Welsbach separated the substance then called didymium into two elements at Vienna: neodymium and praseodymium, named from the Greek prasios didymos, "green twin," because its salts are green. Relatively pure metal was first produced in 1931.

Properties

Praseodymium is paramagnetic at all temperatures above 1 K. Unlike many rare-earth metals it is not ferromagnetic. It tarnishes in air, forming a green oxide layer that, unlike the passivating layer on aluminium, spalls off and exposes fresh metal. Its only stable isotope is 141Pr.

Applications

Praseodymium is used in high-strength magnesium alloys for aircraft components, in mischmetal for lighter flints, in permanent magnets alloyed with neodymium and iron-boron, in didymium glass for glassblowers' goggles, and as a yellow colorant in ceramics and glass. Praseodymium oxide nanoparticles have uses in catalysis.
