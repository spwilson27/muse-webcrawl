Gerstmann syndrome

The angular gyrus of the dominant parietal lobe

Gerstmann syndrome is a neurological disorder characterised by a tetrad of symptoms: difficulty with writing (agraphia or dysgraphia), difficulty learning or comprehending mathematics (acalculia), inability to distinguish one's own fingers (finger agnosia), and left–right disorientation. It is named after the Austrian-American neurologist Josef Gerstmann, who described it in the 1920s–30s.

Causes

The syndrome is typically caused by damage to the angular gyrus of the dominant (usually left) parietal lobe, for example from stroke, tumour or trauma. Its existence as a unitary syndrome is debated, since the four symptoms occur together less often than early reports suggested, and pure cases are rare.

Developmental form

A developmental Gerstmann syndrome has been described in children, presenting with poor handwriting, difficulty with spelling and arithmetic, and finger-recognition problems, without an acquired lesion.
