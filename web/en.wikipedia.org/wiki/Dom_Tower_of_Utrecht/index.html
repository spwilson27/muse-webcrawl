The Dom Tower (Dutch: Domtoren) of Utrecht is the tallest church tower in the Netherlands, at 112.5 metres. It is the symbol of the city of Utrecht and was the tower of St Martin's Cathedral, the city's former cathedral.

History

The Gothic tower was built between 1321 and 1382 to a design associated with Jan van Henegouwen, standing on the spot where Utrecht was founded as a Roman fort. The cathedral's nave, never properly completed, collapsed in a great storm in 1674, leaving the tower freestanding across the Domplein square from the surviving choir and transept. The tower houses a carillon and fourteen bells, including the great Salvator bell of 1505.

Today

Visitors can climb the tower's 465 steps for panoramic views; by long-standing tradition no building in Utrecht may exceed its height. A major restoration completed in 2024 returned its spire's gilded weathercock and stonework to view after years of scaffolding.
