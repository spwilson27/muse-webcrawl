Mercer University

Private university in Macon, Georgia, US

Motto: Scientiae Censeas ("Let knowledge be valued")

Established: 1833

Endowment: ~$500 million

Students: ~9,000

Campus: Macon (main), Atlanta, Savannah, Columbus

Nickname: Bears

Mercer University is a private research university with its main campus in Macon, Georgia, United States. Founded in 1833 as Mercer Institute by Georgia Baptists — and named for Jesse Mercer, an early Baptist leader — it is the oldest private university in Georgia. Mercer moved to Macon in 1871.

Academics

Mercer enrolls more than 9,000 students across twelve schools and colleges, including liberal arts, law (Mercer University School of Law, founded 1873, one of the oldest law schools in the US), medicine (founded 1982, focused on training physicians for rural Georgia), pharmacy, nursing, engineering, business, education, theology, and professional advancement. Its undergraduate programme is built on a liberal-arts core, and the university is noted for service learning through the Mercer On Mission programme.

Athletics

The Mercer Bears compete in NCAA Division I as members of the Southern Conference. The men's basketball team became nationally famous in 2014 when, as a No. 14 seed, it upset Duke in the NCAA tournament. Mercer reinstated football in 2013 after a 72-year absence.

People

Alumni include Georgia governors, the journalist and author Steve Forbes attended its law school briefly, and notable graduates include former US Senator Sam Nunn (who attended but graduated from Emory) — alumni in law and politics are prominent, as are civil-rights era figures; Macon native Little Richard briefly studied there.
