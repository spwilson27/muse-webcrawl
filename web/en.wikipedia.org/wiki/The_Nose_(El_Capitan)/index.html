The Nose (El Capitan)

The Nose is a big wall rock climbing route on El Capitan in Yosemite National Park, California. Rising about 2,900 feet (880 m) up the central prow where the wall's southeast and southwest faces meet, it is one of the most famous rock climbs in the world.

History

The Nose was first climbed in 1958 by Warren Harding, Wayne Merry, and George Whitmore in 47 days of siege-style climbing spread over 18 months. Lynn Hill made the first free ascent in 1993 and in 1994 climbed it free in under 24 hours. Speed records have since driven the time below two hours: in 2018 Alex Honnold and Tommy Caldwell climbed it in 1 hour 58 minutes. Earlier milestones include the first one-day ascent by Jim Bridwell, John Long, and Billy Westbay in 1975.

Route

The route follows crack systems and features such as the Stoveleg Cracks, the King Swing pendulum, the Great Roof, and Camp VI before topping out. It remains a benchmark test piece for big-wall climbers.
