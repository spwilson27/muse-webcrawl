Typhoon Meranti, known in the Philippines as Super Typhoon Ferdie, was the strongest tropical cyclone of 2016 and one of the most intense ever recorded, with a minimum central pressure of 890 hPa. In September 2016 it passed through the Luzon Strait, striking Taiwan's southern tip and the Philippine island of Itbayat, before making landfall in Fujian, China — the strongest typhoon on record to strike that province.

Meranti at peak intensity in the Luzon Strait, September 2016.

Meteorological history

Meranti formed from a disturbance southeast of Guam on 8 September 2016 and tracked west-northwestward, intensifying into a typhoon on 11 September. Under nearly ideal conditions it explosively deepened, reaching peak intensity on 13 September with one-minute sustained winds estimated at 315 km/h (195 mph) and a pressure of 890 hPa — comparable to Typhoon Haiyan of 2013. Its tiny, well-defined eye passed very near Itbayat in the Batanes Islands of the Philippines, where gusts were estimated above 300 km/h and instruments failed.

Impact

In Taiwan, Meranti's outer core battered the Hengchun Peninsula and Kaohsiung: over a million households lost power at some point, ports and shipping were disrupted, and flooding and landslides damaged agriculture. The storm then made landfall near Xiamen, Fujian, in the early hours of 15 September as a strong typhoon, causing severe flooding, power outages affecting millions, and the destruction of a historic Song-dynasty bridge in Yongchun County. Total damage was estimated at about $4.8 billion, with 47 deaths reported across the affected countries. The name Meranti was retired after the season and replaced with Nyatoh.
