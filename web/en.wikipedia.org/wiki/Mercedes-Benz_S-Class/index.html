Mercedes-Benz S-Class

Mercedes-Benz S-Class
Manufacturer: Mercedes-Benz (Daimler AG)
Production: 1954–present
Class: Full-size luxury car (F-segment)

The Mercedes-Benz S-Class, formerly known as Sonderklasse (German for "special class", abbreviated as "S-Klasse"), is a series of full-size luxury cars, limousines and armored sedans produced by the German automaker Mercedes-Benz. The S-Class is the flagship vehicle for Mercedes-Benz, serving as the company's top model and a showcase for new technology. The name was officially introduced with the W116 series in 1972, though earlier "S" models date to the mid-1950s.

History and generations

The line traces its origins to the "Ponton" models (1954) and the "Fintail" (W111) series. Formal S-Class generations include the W116 (1972–1980), W126 (1979–1992), W140 (1991–1998), W220 (1998–2005), W221 (2005–2013), W222 (2013–2020), and W223 (2020–present).

Innovations

The S-Class debuted many automotive safety and comfort technologies later adopted industry-wide, including anti-lock brakes (ABS) on the W116, airbags and seatbelt pretensioners on the W126, electronic stability control, adaptive cruise control (Distronic), night-view assist, and advanced driver-assistance and semi-autonomous driving features in recent generations.

Variants

The range includes short- and long-wheelbase sedans, the ultra-luxury Mercedes-Maybach S-Class, high-performance Mercedes-AMG S 63 models, plug-in hybrids, and armored "Guard" versions used by heads of state. A coupé and cabriolet were offered as S-Class variants until replaced by separate model lines.
