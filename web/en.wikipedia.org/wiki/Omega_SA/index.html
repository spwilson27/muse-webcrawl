Omega SA

Omega SA is a Swiss luxury watchmaker based in Biel/Bienne, Switzerland. Founded by Louis Brandt in La Chaux-de-Fonds in 1848, the company took the name Omega after its landmark 1894 movement, the 19-ligne Omega calibre. Today Omega is a subsidiary of the Swatch Group and one of the world's best-known watch brands.

History

Omega gained a reputation for precision, winning numerous observatory chronometer trials, and was chosen as the official timekeeper of the Olympic Games beginning in 1932—a role it has held at most Games since. The company industrialised watch production early, and its movements set records for accuracy in the early 20th century.

Notable models

Omega's best-known watches include the Speedmaster, flight-qualified by NASA in 1965 and worn by Buzz Aldrin on the Moon in 1969 (earning it the name "Moonwatch"); the Seamaster line of dive watches, associated with the James Bond films since 1995; the Constellation; and the De Ville. In 1999 Omega introduced the co-axial escapement, developed by George Daniels, into serial production.
