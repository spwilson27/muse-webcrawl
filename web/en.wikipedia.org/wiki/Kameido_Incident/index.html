Kameido Incident

The Kameido Incident (亀戸事件, Kameido jiken) was the murder of labour and leftist activists by Japanese military police in the aftermath of the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake. In September 1923, soldiers of the Imperial Japanese Army detained members of the Nankatsu Labour Union in the Kameido district of Tokyo and summarily executed ten men, including the activist Hirasawa Keishichi.

Background

Following the earthquake of 1 September 1923, martial law was declared and rumours that Koreans and socialists were poisoning wells and plotting revolt led to widespread violence: thousands of Koreans were massacred by vigilantes, and authorities used the chaos to eliminate political opponents, as in the contemporary Amakasu Incident, in which the anarchist Ōsugi Sakae was murdered.

The incident

On 3–4 September 1923, union members assisting with relief were arrested and taken to Kameido police station, then bayoneted and shot by troops of the 13th Cavalry Regiment; their bodies were disposed of along the Arakawa canal. The killings were covered up as deaths in the unrest, and no one was prosecuted.
