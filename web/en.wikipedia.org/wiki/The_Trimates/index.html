Overview

The Trimates, sometimes called 'Leakey's Angels', is the name given to three women primatologists recruited by the paleoanthropologist Louis Leakey to conduct long-term field studies of great apes in their natural habitats: Jane Goodall, who began studying chimpanzees at Gombe, Tanzania, in 1960; Dian Fossey, who studied mountain gorillas in Rwanda from 1967; and Biruté Galdikas, who began studying orangutans in Borneo in 1971.

Significance

Leakey believed women might prove more patient and observant field researchers, and the three women's painstaking, decades-long studies transformed understanding of ape behavior, social structure, and intelligence, as well as public attitudes toward conservation. Goodall documented tool use and complex social bonds among chimpanzees; Fossey's work with gorillas and anti-poaching activism ended with her murder in 1985; Galdikas's research illuminated the solitary lives of orangutans and she became a leading advocate for Indonesian rainforest conservation. Their work helped establish modern primatology and inspired generations of women in science.
