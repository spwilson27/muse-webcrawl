The Palazzina di caccia of Stupinigi ("little hunting palace of Stupinigi") is a royal hunting lodge in Stupinigi, a suburb of Nichelino about 10 km southwest of Turin, Italy. Designed by Filippo Juvarra for Victor Amadeus II of Savoy, it is one of the Residences of the Royal House of Savoy, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997.

History

The site belonged to the Order of Saints Maurice and Lazarus, and Victor Amadeus II, as Grand Master of the Order, commissioned Juvarra in 1729 to build a pavilion for the royal hunts in the surrounding oak woods. The cross-shaped central salon — with its great frescoed oval hall painted by the Valeriani brothers — was crowned by a dome surmounted by a bronze stag. The palace was enlarged under Charles Emmanuel III and used for court festivities and weddings; Napoleon stayed there in 1805. In 1832 ownership returned to the crown, and in the twentieth century the building became a museum of art and furnishings.

Architecture and collections

From the elliptical central hall, diagonal wings extend towards the forecourt and gardens, forming a St Andrew's cross plan. The interiors preserve original furniture, paintings and stucco work in the Rococo taste, and the lodge is surrounded by a formal park laid out in the French style.
