Stress–energy tensor

The stress–energy tensor, sometimes called the stress–energy–momentum tensor or the energy–momentum tensor, is a tensor physical quantity that describes the density and flux of energy and momentum in spacetime, generalising the stress tensor of Newtonian physics. It is an attribute of matter, radiation, and non-gravitational force fields. This density and flux of energy and momentum are the sources of the gravitational field in the Einstein field equations of general relativity, just as mass density is the source of such a field in Newtonian gravity.

Definition

The stress–energy tensor involves the use of superscripted variables (not exponents). If Cartesian coordinates in SI units are used, then the components of the position of a particle in spacetime are given by: x0 = t, x1 = x, x2 = y, and x3 = z, where t is time in seconds, and x, y, and z are distances in metres. The tensor Tαβ is a symmetric contravariant tensor of order 2 (rank 2) with 16 components, of which 10 are independent:

  • T00 is the density of relativistic mass, i.e. the energy density divided by c².
  • T0i (i = 1, 2, 3) is the flux of energy/momentum density in the i-th direction.
  • Tij (i, j = 1, 2, 3) comprises the spatial stress components: the diagonal terms are pressure (or tensile stress) and the off-diagonal terms are shear stress.

Role in general relativity

In the Einstein field equations, Gμν + Λgμν = (8πG/c⁴) Tμν, the stress–energy tensor appears on the right-hand side as the source of spacetime curvature. For special cases it has simple forms: for a perfect fluid it depends only on the energy density, pressure, and four-velocity; for the electromagnetic field in vacuum it is given by the electromagnetic stress–energy tensor; for a scalar field it is built from the field's gradients and potential.

Conservation

In flat spacetime (special relativity), the local conservation of energy and momentum is expressed by the vanishing divergence of the tensor, ∂βTαβ = 0. In curved spacetime the corresponding statement uses the covariant derivative, ∇βTαβ = 0, which follows from the Bianchi identity applied to the Einstein field equations.
