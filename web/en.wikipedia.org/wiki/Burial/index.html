Burial

Burial, also known as interment or inhumation, is a method of final disposition whereby a dead body is placed into the ground, sometimes with objects. It is usually accomplished by excavating a pit or trench, placing the deceased and objects in it, and covering it over. Burial has been practised for at least 100,000 years — deliberate burials attributed to early modern humans and Neanderthals in the Middle Palaeolithic are among the earliest detectable forms of religious practice.

Practices

Burial customs vary widely: bodies may be interred in coffins, caskets, shrouds or vaults, in single graves, family plots, catacombs, crypts or ossuaries; grave goods accompanied the dead in many ancient cultures, from Egyptian tombs to ship burials such as Sutton Hoo. Major religions prescribe their own rites — prompt burial in Islam and Judaism, burial or cremation in Christianity and Hinduism respectively. Burial at sea, sky burial and tree burial are regional alternatives.

Modern issues

In densely populated regions, grave space is scarce and plots may be leased for fixed terms. Environmental concerns have prompted growth in "green" or natural burial, using biodegradable containers in woodland cemeteries, as an alternative to embalming and concrete vaults; other emerging alternatives include resomation (alkaline hydrolysis) and human composting. Burial law typically regulates depth, location, exhumation and the treatment of ancient human remains.
