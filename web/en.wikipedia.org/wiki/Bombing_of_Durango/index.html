Bombing of Durango

Bombing of Durango
Date: 31 March 1937
Location: Durango, Biscay, Spain
Context: Spanish Civil War
Attackers: Condor Legion and Aviazione Legionaria aircraft
Deaths: about 250–300 civilians

The bombing of Durango was an aerial attack carried out on 31 March 1937 against the Basque town of Durango in northern Spain during the Spanish Civil War. German Condor Legion and Italian aircraft supporting General Franco's Nationalist forces bombed the town, which had no significant military defences and no air-raid warning system.

The attack

The raid came at the start of the Nationalist offensive against Biscay. Waves of bombers attacked in the morning and again in the afternoon, striking the town centre, the church of Santa María de Uribarri during Mass, the Jesuit chapel and convent, and fleeing crowds in the streets and market. Estimates of the dead generally range from about 250 to around 300 people, among them priests and nuns.

Significance

Durango is often cited as one of the first indiscriminate bombings of a defenceless civilian population in Europe, preceding the more famous bombing of Guernica, which took place less than a month later, on 26 April 1937, and drew far greater international attention. Annual commemorations are held in Durango in memory of the victims.
