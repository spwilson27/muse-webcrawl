Catoptrics

Catoptrics (from Greek katoptron, mirror) is the branch of optics dealing with the reflection of light from mirrors and the formation of images by reflecting surfaces. Together with dioptrics (refraction) it formed the classical division of geometrical optics.

History

Euclid's Catoptrics (c. 300 BC) stated the law of reflection — that the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection — and treated concave and convex mirrors. Hero of Alexandria showed the law follows from a shortest-path principle. Ptolemy, Alhazen (Ibn al-Haytham), and later Kepler and Descartes developed the theory, including focal properties of conic mirrors.

Applications

Catoptric systems use only mirrors, avoiding chromatic aberration; they include reflecting telescopes such as the Newtonian and Cassegrain designs, searchlights, and solar concentrators. Combined catadioptric systems use both mirrors and lenses.
