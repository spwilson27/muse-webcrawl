Aggregate (composite)

Aggregate is the component of a composite material that provides bulk, volume, and resistance to compressive stress while the matrix binds it together. In construction, aggregate consists of granular mineral materials—sand, gravel, crushed stone, slag, and recycled concrete—used with a cementing medium to form concrete or mortar, or with bitumen to form asphalt concrete. Aggregate typically makes up 60–80% of the volume of concrete and strongly influences its strength, durability, workability, and cost.

Classification

Aggregates are classified by size into fine aggregate (sand and crushed fines below about 4.75 mm) and coarse aggregate (gravel and crushed stone above that size). They are also classified by source: natural aggregates are mined from pits, quarries, and river or marine deposits; manufactured aggregates are produced by crushing rock or processing industrial by-products such as blast-furnace slag; and recycled aggregates come from crushed construction and demolition waste, especially old concrete.

Properties

Key properties include particle size distribution (grading), shape and surface texture, specific gravity, water absorption, resistance to abrasion (e.g., the Los Angeles test), and soundness under weathering. Well-graded aggregate packs densely, reducing the cement paste needed. Certain aggregates can react harmfully with cement alkalis—the alkali–silica reaction ("concrete cancer")—causing expansion and cracking, so aggregates are tested for reactivity before use in major structures.

Production and use

Crushed stone is produced in quarries by blasting, crushing, and screening rock such as limestone, granite, and basalt; sand and gravel are dug or dredged and washed. Construction aggregate is among the most extracted solid materials in the world, with global consumption measured in tens of billions of tonnes annually. Outside concrete and asphalt, aggregate is used as railway ballast, road base and sub-base, drainage and filtration media, and armour stone for erosion control.
