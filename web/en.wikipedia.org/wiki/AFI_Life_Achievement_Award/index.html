AFI Life Achievement Award

The AFI Life Achievement Award was established by the board of directors of the American Film Institute in 1973 to celebrate an individual whose career in motion pictures or television has greatly contributed to the enrichment of American culture. It is regarded as the highest honor for a career in film in the United States.

History

The first recipient was director John Ford in 1973. Subsequent honorees have included James Cagney, Orson Welles, Bette Davis, Alfred Hitchcock, Fred Astaire, James Stewart, Steven Spielberg, Clint Eastwood, Martin Scorsese, Tom Hanks, Meryl Streep, and Denzel Washington. The award ceremony is a televised gala tribute in Los Angeles featuring clips and remarks from colleagues. The AFI trustees set criteria including that the recipient's work "stand the test of time." In 2025 the honoree was Eddie Murphy.

Notable facts

John Ford was the award's first recipient; the youngest recipient was Tom Hanks at age 46 in 2002. Several years had no ceremony, including postponements during the COVID-19 pandemic.
