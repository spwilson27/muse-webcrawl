Holacracy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Holacracy is a method of decentralized management and organizational governance in which authority and decision-making are distributed through a "holarchy" of self-organizing teams ("circles") rather than vested in a traditional management hierarchy. It was developed by Brian Robertson, building on practices at his software company Ternary Software, and formalized in the Holacracy Constitution; Robertson co-founded HolacracyOne in 2007 to promote the system.

Structure

Work is organized into roles, which people fill — often several at once — rather than static job descriptions. Roles are grouped into nested circles, each with an explicit purpose and accountabilities. Two meeting types structure the system: tactical meetings, for coordinating work, and governance meetings, which use an "integrative decision-making" process to amend roles and policies in response to "tensions" raised by members. The Holacracy Constitution replaces managers' authority with a rulebook binding on everyone, including the CEO.

Adoption and criticism

Several hundred organizations have adopted Holacracy, most famously online retailer Zappos, which converted its roughly 1,500 employees in 2013–2015; about 14% of staff left when offered severance rather than work without bosses. Medium adopted and later abandoned it. Supporters praise its clarity and agility; critics find its rules bureaucratic, meetings burdensome, and informal power unaccountable. The approach draws on sociocracy, from which it borrowed the circle structure and consent-based decision-making.
