Universal indicator

A universal indicator is a pH indicator made of a solution of several compounds that display a spectrum of smooth colour changes over a wide pH range, indicating the acidity or alkalinity of a solution. Although pre-mixed commercial universal indicators were marketed earlier, the formulation most widely used is Yamada's indicator, first described by Yamada in 1933.

Composition and colours

A typical formulation contains thymol blue, methyl red, bromothymol blue, and phenolphthalein in ethanol with sodium hydroxide to adjust the starting colour. Because each component changes colour at a different pH, their mixture produces a continuous gradient: red at pH 2 and below, orange and yellow through the acidic range, green at neutral pH 7, and blue through violet as the solution becomes strongly alkaline up to pH 14.

Use

Universal indicator is supplied as a solution or impregnated into test papers, allowing a rough pH estimate (to within one or two units) by comparison with a printed colour chart. It is widely used in school laboratories and field testing; for accurate measurement a pH meter is preferred. Wide-range papers covering pH 1–14 and narrow-range papers for finer readings are both common.
