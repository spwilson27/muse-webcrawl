Courts of England and Wales

The courts of England and Wales are the civil and criminal courts responsible for administering justice in England and Wales; they constitute the judiciary of England and Wales, one of the three legal jurisdictions of the United Kingdom (alongside Scotland and Northern Ireland). The courts are administered by His Majesty's Courts and Tribunals Service.

Hierarchy

The Supreme Court of the United Kingdom, created in 2009, is the final court of appeal for civil cases from England and Wales and for criminal cases from England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Below it stands the Court of Appeal, divided into Civil and Criminal Divisions. The High Court of Justice hears major civil cases through its King's Bench, Chancery, and Family Divisions, while the Crown Court hears serious criminal cases before a judge and jury. The County Court handles most civil litigation, and magistrates' courts deal with summary criminal offences and preliminary hearings.

Tribunals

Outside the main hierarchy, a system of tribunals — reorganized under the Tribunals, Courts and Enforcement Act 2007 into the First-tier Tribunal and Upper Tribunal — handles disputes in areas such as immigration, tax, social security and employment.

History

The modern system descends from the royal courts established after the Norman Conquest and was largely unified by the Judicature Acts of 1873–75, which fused the administration of common law and equity and created the Court of Appeal and the High Court in near-modern form.
