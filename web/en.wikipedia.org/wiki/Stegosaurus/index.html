Stegosaurus

Temporal rangeLate Jurassic, ~155–145 million years ago
LengthUp to about 9 m
DietHerbivore

Stegosaurus is a genus of herbivorous, four-legged, armored dinosaur from the Late Jurassic, characterised by the distinctive kite-shaped upright plates along its back and spikes on its tail. Fossils of the genus have been found in the western United States and in Portugal, in strata dating from about 155 to 145 million years ago.

Description

Stegosaurus was a large, heavily built quadruped with a small head held low, short forelimbs, and a stiff tail held high in the air. Two rows of bony plates — modified osteoderms — ran along its back; their function is debated, with proposals including display, species recognition and thermoregulation. The tail bore two pairs of long spikes, the "thagomizer", likely used for defence against predators such as Allosaurus. Despite its size, Stegosaurus had one of the smallest brains relative to body mass among dinosaurs.

Discovery and species

Stegosaurus was named by Othniel Charles Marsh in 1877 from fossils found in the Morrison Formation of Colorado; it became famous during the "Bone Wars". The best-known species is Stegosaurus stenops, represented by well-preserved specimens, including the nearly complete specimen nicknamed "Sophie" at the Natural History Museum in London. Stegosaurus is the state dinosaur of Colorado and one of the most recognisable of all dinosaurs.
