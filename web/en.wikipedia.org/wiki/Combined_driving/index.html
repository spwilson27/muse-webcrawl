Combined driving

Combined driving
TypeEquestrian sport
PhasesDressage, marathon, cones
Governing bodyFEI

Combined driving is an equestrian sport in which a driver, assisted by grooms known as navigators, guides a carriage pulled by a single horse or pony, a pair, or a team of four through three phases modelled on ridden three-day eventing.

Phases

The competition comprises driven dressage, in which prescribed movements are judged on precision and the horses' way of going; a marathon phase, a cross-country drive over natural and artificial obstacles against the clock; and a cones phase, requiring the carriage to pass through narrow pairs of cones without dislodging balls balanced on them.

History

The sport was formalised in the 1970s under the Fédération Équestre Internationale (FEI), with Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, a prominent early competitor and administrator. World championships for four-in-hand teams have been held since 1972.
