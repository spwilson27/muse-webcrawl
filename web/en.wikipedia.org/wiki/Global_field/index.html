Global field

In mathematics, a global field is a field of one of two types: an algebraic number field, a finite extension of the rational numbers ℚ; or a global function field, a finite extension of 𝔽q(t), the field of rational functions over a finite field. Global fields are the central objects of study in algebraic number theory, and the two cases exhibit a deep and fruitful analogy.

Places and adeles

A global field has infinitely many absolute values ("places"), finitely many of which are archimedean. Completing the field at each place and assembling the results yields the ring of adeles, in which the field sits discretely. The product formula — stating that for any nonzero element, the product of its normalized absolute values over all places equals 1 — ties the local pieces together into a single global statement.

Arithmetic

The ring of integers of a number field and its function-field analogue admit class groups measuring the failure of unique factorization, Dirichlet-type unit theorems, Dedekind zeta functions, and Chebotarev density theorems that parallel the theory of algebraic curves over finite fields. Tate's thesis recast the analytic theory of L-functions using harmonic analysis on the adeles, and class field theory describes the abelian extensions of global fields in terms of the fields themselves — the great unifying achievement of early twentieth-century number theory, later generalized by the Langlands program.
