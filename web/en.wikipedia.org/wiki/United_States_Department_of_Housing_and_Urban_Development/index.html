United States Department of Housing and Urban Development

The United States Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) is one of the executive departments of the U.S. federal government. It administers federal housing and urban development laws. The department was created as part of President Lyndon B. Johnson's Great Society program with the founding principles of equal housing opportunity.

History

The department was established on September 9, 1965, when President Lyndon B. Johnson signed the Department of Housing and Urban Development Act into law. It allowed the president to appoint a secretary to head the department. Its roots lay in earlier bodies such as the Housing and Home Finance Agency (HHFA), created in 1947, and the Federal Housing Administration (FHA), founded in 1934.

Robert C. Weaver became the first secretary of HUD in 1966, the first African American to hold a Cabinet position in the United States. The department's headquarters building was later renamed in his honour.

Mission and programs

HUD's mission is to create strong, sustainable, inclusive communities and quality affordable homes for all. Its major programs include:

  • Community Development Block Grants (CDBG) — funding for local community development activities.
  • HOME Investment Partnerships Program — grants to states and localities for affordable housing.
  • Section 8 housing choice vouchers — rental assistance for low-income families, the elderly, and people with disabilities.
  • Public housing — administered with local public housing agencies.
  • Federal Housing Administration — mortgage insurance that helps first-time and low-income buyers purchase homes.
  • Fair housing enforcement — implementing the Fair Housing Act of 1968 and combating housing discrimination.

Secretary

The secretary of housing and urban development is a member of the president's Cabinet and is thirteenth in the presidential line of succession. The secretary is assisted by a deputy secretary and several assistant secretaries overseeing areas such as housing, community planning and development, and fair housing and equal opportunity.
