The Pride of the Yankees is a 1942 American biographical film directed by Sam Wood and starring Gary Cooper as the New York Yankees first baseman Lou Gehrig, who died in 1941 from amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, now commonly known as "Lou Gehrig's disease". The film was produced by Samuel Goldwyn and released a little over a year after Gehrig's death.

Plot

The film traces Gehrig's life from his childhood in New York, the son of German immigrants, through his years at Columbia University, his signing with the Yankees, and his rise as the "Iron Horse" — a model of consistency who played in 2,130 consecutive games alongside Babe Ruth in the famous "Murderers' Row" line-up. It follows his marriage to Eleanor Twitchell, the diagnosis that ended his career in 1939, and his farewell ceremony at Yankee Stadium on 4 July 1939, recreating his famous speech in which he declared himself "the luckiest man on the face of the earth".

Reception and legacy

The film was both a critical and commercial success, receiving eleven Academy Award nominations and winning for Best Film Editing. Several of Gehrig's real teammates, including Babe Ruth, played themselves. The closing speech scene is frequently ranked among the most memorable moments in American cinema, and the film is regularly cited as one of the greatest sports biographies ever made.
