Personal knowledge management

Personal knowledge management (PKM) is the process by which an individual collects, classifies, stores, searches, retrieves, and shares knowledge for their own work and learning. It emerged in the 1990s as knowledge workers faced growing volumes of digital information.

Methods and tools

PKM practices range from traditional note-taking and filing to digital systems built around linked notes and personal wikis. Methods include Niklas Luhmann's Zettelkasten slip-box, Tiago Forte's "second brain" approach (CODE: capture, organise, distil, express), and the commonplace book tradition. Popular tools have included Evernote, Notion, Roam Research, Obsidian, and personal wiki software.

Skills

Frameworks for PKM emphasise competencies in retrieving, evaluating, organising, analysing, and presenting information, and in collaborating around it. Critics note the risk of spending more time organising notes than using them.
