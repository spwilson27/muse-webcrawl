International Master

The International Master (IM) is a title awarded to chess players by the world chess federation FIDE. Below the title of Grandmaster, it is the second-highest title a chess player can achieve. Once awarded, the title is held for life, though it can be revoked for cheating.

Requirements

To qualify, a player must ordinarily achieve an Elo rating of at least 2400 and earn three IM "norms" — performances at a prescribed level in tournaments meeting conditions on length, strength of opposition, and international representation. Titles can also be awarded directly for specified results, such as winning the World Junior Championship or the Women's World Championship runner-up position.

History

FIDE introduced the International Master title in 1950, initially awarding it to 94 players, including past greats recognized for earlier achievements. Several thousand players now hold the title; a parallel women's title, Woman International Master (WIM), has lower requirements.

Distinction from other titles

The IM title is distinct from the FIDE Master (FM) and Candidate Master (CM) below it, and from "master" titles awarded by national federations, which require no international norms.
