Typhoon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A typhoon is a mature tropical cyclone that develops in the western part of the North Pacific Ocean between 180° and 100°E longitude. The same kind of storm is called a hurricane in the Atlantic and north-eastern Pacific and a cyclone in the South Pacific and Indian Ocean.

Classification

The Japan Meteorological Agency grades systems in the basin from tropical depression up to typhoon, reached when maximum sustained winds are at least 64 knots (119 km/h). The Joint Typhoon Warning Center designates storms with one-minute sustained winds of at least 130 knots (240 km/h) as "super typhoons".

Formation and season

Typhoons form over warm ocean waters with low wind shear and abundant moisture. The Northwest Pacific is the most active tropical cyclone basin on Earth, generating roughly a third of all tropical cyclones; activity peaks in late summer, though storms can form in any month.

Impacts

Typhoons regularly strike the Philippines, Taiwan, Japan, China, Vietnam and Korea, bringing destructive winds, flooding rainfall and storm surges. Notable storms include Typhoon Haiyan (2013), one of the strongest landfalling cyclones ever recorded.
