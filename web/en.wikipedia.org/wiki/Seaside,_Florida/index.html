Seaside is an unincorporated master-planned community on the Florida Panhandle in Walton County, between Panama City Beach and Destin. Founded in 1981 by developer Robert S. Davis on land inherited from his grandfather, Seaside is regarded as a founding example of the New Urbanism movement in town planning.

Design

Designed by architects Andrés Duany and Elizabeth Plater-Zyberk of Duany Plater-Zyberk & Company, Seaside was laid out on about 80 acres with a compact, walkable street grid, a central square with shops and an amphitheater, and narrow brick streets. A strict design code required features such as front porches, picket fences, metal roofs, and pastel-colored cottages, while discouraging private lawns in favor of native landscaping. Eight beach pavilions mark the ends of streets leading over the dunes to the Gulf of Mexico shoreline.

Influence and reception

Seaside became the most famous early New Urbanist community, influencing later developments in Florida and beyond, and helped popularize Traditional Neighborhood Design. In 1998 it served as the filming location and set for the film The Truman Show, whose fictional town of Seahaven was portrayed as a domed studio replica modeled on Seaside itself. The community has also been criticized for high property values and an exclusive, resort-like character. A mixed-use town center, a charter school (the Seaside School), and the Seaside Repertory Theatre are among its institutions.

Time magazine named Seaside among its "Designs of the Decade" for the 1980s, and the community won an Award for Excellence from the Urban Land Institute.
