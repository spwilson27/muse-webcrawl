Antiochus IV Epiphanes ("God Manifest"; c. 215 – 164 BC) was a Greek king of the Seleucid Empire who reigned from 175 BC until his death. A son of Antiochus III, he had spent years as a hostage in Rome under the Treaty of Apamea before being released and seizing the throne on the murder of his brother Seleucus IV.

Wars with Egypt

Antiochus twice invaded Ptolemaic Egypt (170–168 BC) and briefly controlled much of the country, but during the Sixth Syrian War a Roman envoy, Gaius Popillius Laenas, famously drew a circle around him in the sand at Eleusis and demanded he withdraw before stepping out of it — a humiliation he accepted.

Judea and the Maccabean Revolt

Antiochus's decrees suppressing Jewish religious practice — including the desecration of the Temple in Jerusalem in 167 BC, associated with the "abomination of desolation" — provoked the Maccabean Revolt under Judas Maccabeus, commemorated by the festival of Hanukkah. He died in 164 BC while campaigning in Persia.
