Billiards is a term for a family of cue sports played on a cloth-covered table with cushioned rails, in which players use a cue stick to strike balls. The term is used in several different senses: in North America it often refers specifically to carom billiards, played on a pocketless table, while in British usage it refers to English billiards. The broader family also includes pool games (such as eight-ball and nine-ball) and snooker.

History

Cue sports evolved from outdoor lawn games similar to croquet and ground billiards played in medieval Europe. By the 15th century the game had moved indoors onto tables. Mary, Queen of Scots complained in 1586 that her billiard table had been taken away before her execution. The game spread through Europe and to America, where pool halls became fixtures of social life in the 19th and 20th centuries. The invention of vulcanised rubber cushions, slate beds, and celluloid and later phenolic resin balls progressively standardised equipment.

Major forms

Carom billiards is played on a pocketless table with three balls; points are scored by caroming the cue ball off both object balls. Three-cushion billiards is its most popular international form. Pool is played on a pocketed table, usually with sixteen balls, and includes eight-ball, nine-ball, ten-ball, and straight pool. Snooker is played on a larger table with 22 balls and a distinctive scoring system based on colour sequences. English billiards, a hybrid of carom and pocket play, was historically dominant in Britain and the Commonwealth.

Equipment

A billiard table typically has a slate bed covered in woollen cloth, rubber cushions, and, except in carom, six pockets. Balls are traditionally made of phenolic resin and are precisely machined. Cues are usually wooden or composite shafts tipped with leather, and chalk is applied to the tip to increase friction with the cue ball.
