The Oxford–AstraZeneca COVID-19 vaccine, sold under the brand names Covishield and Vaxzevria among others, is a viral vector vaccine for the prevention of COVID-19. It was developed in the United Kingdom by Oxford University and British-Swedish company AstraZeneca, using the modified chimpanzee adenovirus ChAdOx1 as a vector. The vaccine is given by intramuscular injection.

Development and authorisation

Work began in early 2020; the vaccine (AZD1222) entered trials in April 2020. The United Kingdom authorised it for emergency use on 30 December 2020, and it was widely deployed, particularly in low- and middle-income countries, because it can be stored at normal refrigerator temperatures and was supplied at cost during the pandemic. Billions of doses were delivered worldwide, including through the COVAX programme.

Efficacy and safety

Trials showed efficacy of roughly 70–80% against symptomatic disease after two doses, with strong protection against severe disease and death. In 2021 a rare side effect, thrombosis with thrombocytopenia syndrome (vaccine-induced immune thrombotic thrombocytopenia), was identified, leading many countries to restrict its use by age. AstraZeneca withdrew the vaccine from markets in 2024, citing the availability of updated vaccines and surplus supply.
