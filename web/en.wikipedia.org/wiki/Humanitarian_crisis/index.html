Humanitarian crisis

A humanitarian crisis (or humanitarian disaster) is a singular event or series of events that threatens the health, safety or well-being of a large group of people, typically through armed conflict, epidemic, famine, natural disaster or the collapse of basic services. Humanitarian aid is the short-term response intended to relieve it.

Causes

Crises arise from natural disasters (earthquakes, floods, droughts), man-made disasters (war, industrial accidents) and structural emergencies (economic collapse, state failure). Modern crises are most often "complex emergencies" in which conflict, displacement and food insecurity reinforce one another.

Response

The international response is coordinated chiefly through the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), specialised agencies such as UNHCR, the World Food Programme and UNICEF, and non-governmental organisations including the Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and Médecins Sans Frontières.

Principles

Humanitarian action is governed by the principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence, codified in international humanitarian law and UN General Assembly resolution 46/182.

Major crises

Recent large-scale crises include the wars in Yemen, Syria, Sudan and Ukraine, and famines in the Horn of Africa. In the mid-2020s the UN estimated that well over 300 million people required humanitarian assistance worldwide.
