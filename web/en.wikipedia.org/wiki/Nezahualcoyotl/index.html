Nezahualcoyotl (Nahuatl for "hungry coyote", 1402–1472) was the ruler (tlatoani) of the city-state of Texcoco in pre-Columbian Mexico, and one of the most celebrated figures of the Nahua world — a warrior, lawgiver, engineer and the most famous poet of the Aztec era.

Life

Driven into exile as a youth after the Tepanecs killed his father, Nezahualcoyotl returned with allies to reclaim Texcoco in 1431 and co-founded the Aztec Triple Alliance with Tenochtitlan and Tlacopan. He reorganised Texcoco's laws and government and directed major works, including the great dike that separated the brackish waters of Lake Texcoco from the fresh water around Tenochtitlan.

Poetry

His surviving Nahuatl poems meditate on the brevity of life and the search for a divine creator, earning him the title of the "poet-king". A major Mexico City suburb and municipality, Ciudad Nezahualcóyotl, is named for him.
