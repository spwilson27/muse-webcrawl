Furlough

A furlough (from Dutch verlof, "leave of absence") is a temporary leave of employees due to special needs of a company or employer, which may be due to economic conditions at the specific employer or in the economy as a whole. These furloughs may be short or long term and in some cases mandatory or voluntary.

United States

In the United States, furloughs are commonly imposed on federal government employees during government shutdowns, when a lapse in appropriations forces agencies to send non-essential workers home without pay until funding is restored. The Antideficiency Act prohibits federal agencies from spending money that has not been appropriated. In the private sector, furloughs became widely used during the Great Recession and the COVID-19 pandemic as an alternative to layoffs, allowing employers to reduce payroll costs while keeping employees on staff and often retaining their benefits.

Military and other uses

In military usage, a furlough is a leave of absence granted to a soldier. The term is also used for missionaries returning home for a period of rest, and in some contexts for prisoners granted temporary release.
