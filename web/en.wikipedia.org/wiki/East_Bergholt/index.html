East Bergholt is a village in the Babergh district of Suffolk, England, close to the Essex border and the River Stour. It lies in the heart of "Constable Country", the landscape associated with the painter John Constable, who was born in the village in 1776. Flatford Mill and Willy Lott's Cottage, immortalised in Constable's paintings, lie within the parish.

St Mary's Church

The parish church of St Mary is notable for having no bell tower. Construction of a tower was halted in the 16th century — local tradition blames the fall of Cardinal Wolsey or the Devil — and since 1531 the church's five bells have hung in a wooden bell cage in the churchyard, where they are rung by hand rather than by ropes and wheels, making them among the heaviest bells rung in this manner in England.

History

The village was once a prosperous centre of the woollen cloth trade. East Bergholt was also the birthplace of the naturalist and illustrator of fungi, and it retains many timber-framed and Georgian buildings. The painter John Constable attended school briefly in the village and painted the surrounding Dedham Vale throughout his career.

Notable people

  • John Constable (1776–1837), landscape painter, born here.
