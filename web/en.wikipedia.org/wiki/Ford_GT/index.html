The Ford GT is a mid-engine two-seater sports car produced by the Ford Motor Company in two generations, as a modern homage to the Ford GT40 race cars that won the 24 Hours of Le Mans four consecutive times from 1966 to 1969, defeating Ferrari.

First generation (2005–2006)

Unveiled as a concept at the 2002 North American International Auto Show, the first-generation GT entered production for model years 2005 and 2006. It closely resembled the original GT40 in appearance but was larger and entirely new mechanically. Its supercharged 5.4-litre V8 produced 550 hp, giving a top speed of about 205 mph (330 km/h). A total of 4,038 cars were built at Ford's Wixom, Michigan plant, with final assembly by Saleen.

Second generation (2017–2022)

The second-generation GT was revealed at the 2015 Detroit auto show as the centrepiece of Ford's centenary-era performance programme. Built around a carbon-fibre monocoque by Multimatic in Canada, it is powered by a 3.5-litre twin-turbocharged EcoBoost V6 producing 647 hp (later 660 hp), with a top speed of 216 mph (347 km/h). Production was limited to about 1,350 cars, allocated by application.

In motorsport, the racing Ford GT run by Chip Ganassi Racing won the GTE Pro class at the 2016 24 Hours of Le Mans — fifty years after the GT40's first victory — and competed successfully in the FIA World Endurance Championship and IMSA series until the programme ended in 2019. Track-only derivatives included the Ford GT Mk II and the final GT Mk IV.
