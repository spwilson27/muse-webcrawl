Siege of Isfahan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Isfahan (March – October 1722) was the climax of the Afghan invasion of Persia, in which Mahmud Hotak and his Ghilzai Afghan army starved the Safavid capital into surrender, bringing the Safavid dynasty to an effective end.

Background

The Safavid Empire under Shah Sultan Husayn had declined badly. After the Afghans of Kandahar under Mirwais Hotak won independence, his son Mahmud invaded Persia in 1721, defeated the Safavid army at the Battle of Gulnabad on 8 March 1722, and advanced on Isfahan.

The siege

Rather than assault the city, Mahmud blockaded it completely. Inside, a population of perhaps 600,000 faced catastrophic famine; contemporary accounts describe corpses in the streets and desperate consumption of animals and leather. Relief armies were defeated or dispersed. After some seven months, Sultan Husayn came out of the citadel and formally surrendered his crown to Mahmud on 23 October 1722, abdicating in his favour.

Aftermath

Afghan rule over Persia proved short and brutal; famine and disease during the siege may have killed 80,000 or more of Isfahan's inhabitants. The Afghan interregnum ended in 1729 when Nader Qoli Beg (later Nader Shah) defeated the Afghans and restored nominal Safavid rule before founding the Afsharid dynasty.
