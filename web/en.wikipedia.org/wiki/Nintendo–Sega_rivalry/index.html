Nintendo–Sega rivalry

The Nintendo–Sega rivalry, popularly known as the console war of the late 1980s and 1990s, was a period of intense competition between Japanese video game companies Nintendo and Sega for dominance of the home video game market. Fought through successive generations of hardware, aggressive advertising, and mascot characters — Nintendo's Mario and Sega's Sonic the Hedgehog — it is regarded as the first and most famous "console war".

Background

After the video game crash of 1983, Nintendo rebuilt the North American market with the Nintendo Entertainment System (1985), which Sega's Master System failed to dislodge. With the launch of the 16-bit Mega Drive (Genesis) in 1988–89, Sega seized an early technical lead and marketed aggressively to older players, encapsulated by the slogan "Genesis does what Nintendon't."

The 16-bit war

Nintendo answered with the Super Nintendo Entertainment System in 1990–91. Sega introduced Sonic the Hedgehog in 1991 as a faster, edgier mascot, and games such as Mortal Kombat — whose uncensored Genesis version outsold the sanitised SNES release — helped Sega briefly outsell Nintendo in North America. The rivalry fuelled debates in playgrounds and magazines, a ratings controversy that led to the creation of the ESRB, and competing add-ons such as the Sega CD and 32X.

Decline and end

The arrival of Sony's PlayStation in 1994–95 shifted the market to 3D and CD-based games. Sega's Saturn stumbled, Nintendo's Nintendo 64 retained second place, and after the commercial failure of the Dreamcast, Sega exited the console hardware business in 2001 to become a third-party developer — thereafter publishing games, including Sonic titles, on Nintendo platforms. The rivals' mascots later appeared together in crossover games such as the Mario & Sonic at the Olympic Games series and Super Smash Bros.

Legacy

The rivalry shaped modern video game marketing and the very idea of a "console war", later echoed by PlayStation–Xbox competition. It is the subject of the book Console Wars (2014) by Blake J. Harris.
