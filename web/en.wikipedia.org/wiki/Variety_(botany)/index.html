Variety (botany)

In botanical nomenclature, a variety (Latin: varietas; abbreviated var.) is a taxonomic rank below species and subspecies and above form (forma), governed by the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants (ICN). A variety is a population of plants within a species that consistently differs in minor, heritable characters — such as flower colour, leaf shape or hairiness — and typically occupies a distinct geographical area or habitat.

Nomenclature

A variety name is written as a trinomial: genus, species epithet, the abbreviation "var.", and the varietal epithet, the latter two in italics, e.g. Brassica oleracea var. sabellica (curly kale). When a variety is named, the remaining plants of the species automatically form an autonym variety repeating the species epithet (e.g. var. oleracea). To be validly published, a variety name must be accompanied by a description or diagnosis.

Distinctions

Variety is not to be confused with the cultivar, a rank of cultivated plants governed by a separate code (the ICNCP) and denoted by a name in single quotation marks, such as Malus domestica 'Granny Smith'. A cultivar is a human-selected assemblage maintained in cultivation, whereas a botanical variety arises in nature. In everyday and wine-industry usage, "variety" is loosely applied to grape cultivars (e.g. Pinot noir) and other crops, a practice botanists consider imprecise. The rank also differs from subspecies, which denotes more strongly differentiated, geographically defined populations, and from form, used for sporadic minor variants such as a white-flowered individual of a normally coloured species.
