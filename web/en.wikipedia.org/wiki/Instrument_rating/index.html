Instrument rating

An instrument rating is a rating in aviation that qualifies a pilot to fly under instrument flight rules (IFR), allowing flight in weather conditions with low visibility, such as heavy rain or low clouds, using only cockpit instruments for navigation.

Requirements

In the United States, an instrument rating requires at least 50 hours of cross-country flight time as pilot in command, 40 hours of actual or simulated instrument time, and passing written and practical examinations administered by the Federal Aviation Administration. Pilots must also maintain currency by flying a minimum number of instrument approaches within a set period.

Training

Training covers attitude instrument flying, navigation using VOR, GPS and other systems, holding patterns, instrument approaches, and emergency procedures. Much of the training may be completed in flight simulators or flight training devices.

Other countries

Equivalent qualifications exist worldwide, such as the EASA instrument rating in Europe, though specific requirements and privileges vary by jurisdiction.
