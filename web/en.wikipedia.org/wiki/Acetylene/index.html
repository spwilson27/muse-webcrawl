Acetylene

Acetylene (systematic name ethyne) is the chemical compound with the formula C2H2. It is the simplest alkyne and a colourless gas that is widely used as a fuel and as a chemical building block. Pure acetylene is odourless, but commercial grades usually have a garlic-like smell from impurities.

Acetylene burns with a very hot, luminous flame; the oxy-acetylene torch, reaching temperatures above 3,000 °C, was for decades the standard tool for welding and cutting metal. Because the gas is unstable and explosive under pressure, it is stored dissolved in acetone within cylinders packed with porous material.

Historically produced by reacting calcium carbide with water, acetylene was once a major feedstock for vinyl chloride, acetaldehyde and synthetic rubber; much of this large-scale chemistry was later displaced by ethylene. It is also formed in the atmospheres of gas giants and is the molecule whose polymerisation gives polyacetylene.
