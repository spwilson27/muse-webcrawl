Viktor Mihály Orbán (born 31 May 1963) is a Hungarian lawyer and politician who has served as Prime Minister of Hungary since 2010, having previously held the office from 1998 to 2002. He is the leader of the national conservative Fidesz party and is Hungary's longest-serving prime minister.

Early career

Orbán came to prominence in 1989 with a speech at the reburial of Imre Nagy demanding the withdrawal of Soviet troops and free elections. A founding member of Fidesz in 1988, he was first elected to the National Assembly in 1990. After Fidesz shifted from liberalism toward national conservatism in the 1990s, Orbán became prime minister in 1998 at the head of a coalition government, at 35 the country's youngest head of government.

Second premiership

Fidesz won a two-thirds parliamentary supermajority in 2010, enabling Orbán's government to adopt a new constitution (the Fundamental Law, effective 2012) and extensive institutional changes. His governments won further supermajorities in 2014, 2018 and 2022. Orbán has promoted what he calls "illiberal democracy", emphasising national sovereignty, restrictive immigration policy and conservative social positions, and has frequently clashed with the European Union over rule-of-law concerns, media freedom and judicial independence — disputes that led to EU funding being partially frozen. His tenure is praised by supporters for economic stability, family policies and border control, and criticised by opponents and international observers for democratic backsliding and corruption.
