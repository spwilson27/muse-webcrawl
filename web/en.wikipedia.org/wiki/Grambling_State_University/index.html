Grambling State University (GSU) is a public historically black university in Grambling, Louisiana. Founded in 1901 as the Colored Industrial and Agricultural School, it developed from efforts by African-American farmers in northern Louisiana who sought a school for their community's children.

History

The school was established with the help of the North Louisiana Colored Agriculture Relief Association and opened under the leadership of Charles P. Adams. Through the 20th century it grew from an elementary and industrial school into a teachers' college and then a full university, becoming Grambling State University in 1974.

Athletics and band

Grambling is internationally known for its football program under legendary coach Eddie Robinson, who became one of the winningest coaches in college football history, and for the Tiger Marching Band, one of the most famous marching bands in the United States, which has performed at Super Bowls and presidential inaugurations.

Academics

The university offers undergraduate and graduate programs across colleges of arts and sciences, business, education, and professional studies, and is a member of the University of Louisiana System.
