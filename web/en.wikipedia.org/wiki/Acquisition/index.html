Acquisition

An acquisition is a corporate transaction in which one company purchases most or all of another company's shares or assets to take control of it. Acquisitions are commonly made as part of a growth strategy, to gain market share, enter new markets, obtain technology or achieve synergies. The term is closely related to, and often combined with, merger in the phrase "mergers and acquisitions" (M&A).

Forms

In a share purchase the buyer acquires the target's stock from its shareholders; in an asset purchase it buys selected assets and liabilities. Acquisitions may be friendly, negotiated with the target's board, or hostile, taken directly to shareholders through a tender offer against the board's wishes. A takeover of a majority stake that grants control is sometimes called a buyout, particularly when financed with debt (a leveraged buyout).

Process and regulation

A typical deal involves valuation, due diligence, negotiation of a purchase agreement, financing and regulatory approval, including antitrust review by competition authorities. Payment may be in cash, stock, or a mixture of both.
