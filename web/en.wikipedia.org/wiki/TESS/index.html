Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite
OperatorNASA / MIT
Launch18 April 2018 (Falcon 9)
MethodTransit photometry, all-sky survey
Orbit2:1 lunar resonance, 13.7 days
TESS (artist's concept).

TESS, the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite, is a NASA space telescope launched in 2018 to find exoplanets around the nearest and brightest stars — the ones whose planets can be followed up in detail by ground telescopes and the James Webb Space Telescope. Where Kepler stared deep at one field, TESS scans nearly the whole sky in strips, dwelling 27 days on each sector.

Flying in a novel 13.7-day orbit resonant with the Moon — high, stable, and never used before — TESS has catalogued thousands of planet candidates and confirmed hundreds of planets, including nearby rocky worlds in habitable zones such as TOI 700 d and the planets of LHS 1140, prioritised targets for atmospheric study.

Extended mission

After completing its two-year primary survey of both celestial hemispheres, TESS entered an extended mission revisiting sectors at faster cadences, also serving as a general astrophysics observatory — catching supernovae, comets and tidal disruption events. Its candidate list, vetted by a global follow-up network of mostly small telescopes, feeds directly into the target lists of the atmospheric-characterisation era.
