FONATUR

Cancún, the first FONATUR integrally planned resort

FONATUR (Spanish: Fondo Nacional de Fomento al Turismo, "National Fund for Tourism Development") is a Mexican federal government agency responsible for planning and developing tourism infrastructure in Mexico. It operates under the Secretariat of Tourism (SECTUR) and is headquartered in Mexico City.

FONATUR was created in 1974 by merging earlier tourism promotion funds, INFRATUR and the Fondo de Garantía y Fomento de Turismo. Its best-known work is the creation of the Centros Integralmente Planeados (CIPs, "integrally planned centres"): purpose-built beach resorts developed from scratch on previously undeveloped coastlines.

Planned resorts

The agency's landmark projects include:

  • Cancún (Quintana Roo) — begun in 1970 as a pilot project and FONATUR's greatest success, growing from a small fishing settlement into one of the world's major beach destinations.
  • Ixtapa (Guerrero), near Zihuatanejo.
  • Los Cabos (Baja California Sur), including San José del Cabo and Cabo San Lucas.
  • Loreto (Baja California Sur).
  • Huatulco (Oaxaca), developed from the 1980s around a series of bays.

Functions

FONATUR plans land use, builds basic infrastructure such as roads, water treatment and airports, sells serviced lots to hotel developers, and provides financing for tourism projects. In the 2010s and 2020s the agency was also tasked with major federal tourism projects such as the Maya Train (Tren Maya) in the Yucatán Peninsula.
