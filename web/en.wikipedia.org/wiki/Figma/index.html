Figma is a collaborative web application for interface design, with additional offline features enabled by desktop applications for macOS and Windows. It is used primarily for user interface and user experience design, and is known for real-time multiplayer editing in the browser. The company, Figma, Inc., was founded in 2012 by Dylan Field and Evan Wallace and is headquartered in San Francisco.

History

Field and Wallace began working on the concept while studying at Brown University; Figma emerged from the Thiel Fellowship and early venture funding. The product launched publicly in September 2016 after a long preview period. Its browser-based, multiplayer approach — built on WebGL — differentiated it from desktop tools such as Adobe Photoshop and Sketch, and it rapidly became a standard tool for product design. In April 2021 Figma launched FigJam, an online whiteboard, and in 2023 introduced Dev Mode for developers.

Adobe acquisition attempt

In September 2022 Adobe announced an agreement to acquire Figma for approximately $20 billion in cash and stock, the largest-ever acquisition of a private software company at the time. The deal drew regulatory scrutiny in the United States, the United Kingdom and the European Union over competition concerns. In December 2023 the companies mutually terminated the agreement, and Adobe paid Figma a $1 billion breakup fee. Figma subsequently continued as an independent company and completed an initial public offering in 2025, listing on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker FIG.

Features

Figma's editor supports vector graphics editing, prototyping, design systems with components and variables, auto layout, and a plugin ecosystem. Files are shared via links with version history, commenting and branching. Companions include FigJam (whiteboarding), Figma Slides (presentations) and AI-assisted design generation under Figma Make.
