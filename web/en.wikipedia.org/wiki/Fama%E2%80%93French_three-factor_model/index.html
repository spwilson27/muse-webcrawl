Fama–French three-factor model

Developed byEugene Fama, Kenneth French
Published1992–1993
FieldFinancial economics

The Fama–French three-factor model is a statistical model designed by Eugene Fama and Kenneth French to describe stock returns. It expands on the capital asset pricing model (CAPM) by adding two factors to the market risk factor: a size factor (SMB, "small minus big"), capturing the tendency of small-cap stocks to outperform large-cap stocks, and a value factor (HML, "high minus low"), capturing the tendency of value stocks with high book-to-market ratios to outperform growth stocks.

r = Rf + β1(Rm − Rf) + β2·SMB + β3·HML + α

Development

Fama and French presented the model in papers in 1992 and 1993, showing that the three factors explained much of the cross-sectional variation in average stock returns that CAPM's single market beta could not. The model became a standard benchmark in empirical asset pricing and performance evaluation.

Extensions

Later work extended the framework: the Carhart four-factor model added a momentum factor, and Fama and French themselves proposed a five-factor model in 2015 adding profitability and investment factors.
