Tuamotu

Tuamotu Archipelago
Country: France (French Polynesia)
Number of atolls: 78
Largest atoll: Rangiroa

The Tuamotu Archipelago or Tuamotu Islands (French: Îles Tuamotu) are a French Polynesian chain of almost 80 low atolls in the Pacific Ocean, forming the largest chain of atolls in the world. They stretch over an area comparable to that of Western Europe, northeast of Tahiti.

Geography

The atolls are low coral islands rising only a few metres above sea level, making them highly vulnerable to cyclones and sea-level rise. The largest atolls include Rangiroa, Fakarava, Makemo and Hao. Several atolls, including Fakarava, are designated UNESCO biosphere reserves.

History

The islands were settled by Polynesian voyagers and first reached by Europeans in the early 16th century. France annexed the archipelago in the 19th century. The atolls of Moruroa and Fangataufa became notorious as the site of French nuclear weapons tests between 1966 and 1996. The local economy relies on black pearl farming, copra, fishing and tourism.
