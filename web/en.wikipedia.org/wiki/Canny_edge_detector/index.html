Canny edge detector

The Canny edge detector is an edge detection operator that uses a multi-stage algorithm to detect a wide range of edges in images. It was developed by John F. Canny in 1986. Canny also produced a computational theory of edge detection explaining why the technique works.

The algorithm

The Canny algorithm proceeds in five stages:

  1. Noise reduction — the image is smoothed with a Gaussian filter to remove noise.
  2. Gradient calculation — intensity gradients are computed to find areas of sharp change.
  3. Non-maximum suppression — spurious responses are removed, thinning edges to single-pixel lines.
  4. Double threshold — pixels are classified as strong edges, weak edges, or non-edges using high and low thresholds.
  5. Edge tracking by hysteresis — weak edge pixels connected to strong edges are kept; isolated weak pixels are discarded.

Design criteria

Canny formulated edge detection as an optimisation problem based on three criteria: good detection (low error rate), good localisation (detected edges close to true edges), and minimal response (one detector response per true edge). The resulting operator approximates the first derivative of a Gaussian and remains one of the most widely used edge detectors in computer vision, implemented in libraries such as OpenCV.
