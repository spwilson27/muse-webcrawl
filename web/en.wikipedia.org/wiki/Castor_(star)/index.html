Castor (star)

Castor (α Geminorum)
ConstellationGemini
Apparent magnitude1.58 (combined)
Distanceabout 51 light-years
Components6 stars in 3 binary pairs

Castor is the second-brightest star in the constellation Gemini, designated Alpha Geminorum even though it is slightly fainter than Pollux. It represents the head of the mortal twin of the Dioscuri. Castor appears as a single star to the naked eye but is actually a sextuple system of six stars organised into three binary pairs.

System

Telescopically Castor splits into two white A-type stars, Castor A and B, orbiting each other in about 445 years; each is itself a spectroscopic binary. A third, distant pair — Castor C, or YY Geminorum — consists of two red dwarfs in an eclipsing binary with a period under a day, orbiting the inner four stars. The system lies about 51 light-years from the Sun and is a member of the Castor moving group of young stars.

Names and mythology

Castor and Pollux are the twin sons of Leda in Greek mythology; Castor was the mortal twin, famed as a tamer of horses. The pair mark the heads of the Gemini twins and have been used in navigation since antiquity.
