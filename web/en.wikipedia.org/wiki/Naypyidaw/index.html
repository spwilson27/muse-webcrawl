Naypyidaw (Burmese: နေပြည်တော်), officially spelled Nay Pyi Taw, is the capital and third-largest city of Myanmar (Burma). The city is located at the centre of the Naypyidaw Union Territory. It is unusual among Myanmar's cities in that it is an entirely planned city built from scratch, replacing Yangon as the administrative capital.

History

Construction began in 2002 on a greenfield site near Pyinmana, some 320 kilometres north of Yangon. On 6 November 2005 the ruling military junta began moving government ministries to the new site, and in March 2006 the city was formally named Naypyidaw, meaning "Abode of Kings". The reasons given included Yangon's congestion and the new capital's more central, strategically defensible location, though the move was widely regarded as abrupt and was carried out with little public explanation.

Layout

Naypyidaw is organised into colour-coded zones for ministries, hotels, military facilities, residences and recreation, spread over an area of more than 7,000 square kilometres — several times the size of Yangon. Its avenues are famously wide; the highway in front of the parliament complex has up to twenty lanes, yet traffic remains sparse. The Uppatasanti Pagoda, a replica of Yangon's Shwedagon Pagoda completed in 2009, is a prominent landmark, and the city contains large government-built housing estates, a zoo, a safari park, and the Myanmar International Convention Centre. Naypyidaw hosted events of the 2013 Southeast Asian Games and, in 2014, ASEAN summits.

Transport

The city is served by Naypyidaw International Airport and by the Yangon–Mandalay railway and expressway. Despite substantial investment, the city has often been described in international reporting as sparsely populated relative to its vast scale, with population estimates concentrated largely among government employees and military personnel.
