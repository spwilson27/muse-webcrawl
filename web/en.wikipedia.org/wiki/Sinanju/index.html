Sinanju

Sinanju is a fictional Korean martial art and village at the heart of The Destroyer, the long-running series of men's adventure novels created by Warren Murphy and Richard Sapir, first published in 1971. In the series' mythology, Sinanju is a poor fishing village on the west coast of Korea whose inhabitants, centuries ago, hired out one of their number as an assassin to foreign rulers in order to feed the village's children — a practice summarised in the maxim of sending the master abroad so that "the babies of the village" would not starve or be drowned.

The martial art

Within the fiction, Sinanju is the "sun source" of all martial arts: every other discipline — karate, kung fu, ninjutsu and the rest — is portrayed as a diluted fragment of its techniques. Its masters command superhuman abilities through total control of the body: regulated breathing, heightened senses, the strength to tear steel, the speed to dodge bullets, and techniques of unarmed combat and pressure-point strikes taught only to the reigning Master of Sinanju. The art is passed from master to a single disciple, traditionally a boy of the village taken in childhood.

Characters and stories

The novels follow Remo Williams, a former Newark policeman framed for murder and recruited by the secret agency CURE, who is trained in Sinanju by the elderly, acerbic master Chiun — the reigning Master of Sinanju, who regards Remo, an outsider and the first non-Korean heir in the art's history, with a mixture of pride and comic disdain. The series ran to more than 150 novels, mixing action with satire of contemporary politics and culture, and inspired the 1985 film Remo Williams: The Adventure Begins and a comic-book adaptation.

Legacy

Sinanju and Chiun became durable fixtures of pulp-adventure culture; the Destroyer books are among the best-selling men's adventure series ever published, and the village's name has been borrowed in gaming and fiction as a byword for the ultimate assassin tradition.
