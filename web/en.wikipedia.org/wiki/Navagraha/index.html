Navagraha

The Navagraha are nine heavenly bodies and deities in Hindu astrology and Hinduism that are believed to influence human life and destiny. The word derives from nava (nine) and graha (that which seizes or holds). Seven of the grahas correspond to the visible "planets" of classical astronomy, including the Sun and Moon; the remaining two are the lunar nodes.

The nine grahas

  • Surya — the Sun
  • Chandra — the Moon
  • Mangala — Mars
  • Budha — Mercury
  • Brihaspati (Guru) — Jupiter
  • Shukra — Venus
  • Shani — Saturn
  • Rahu — the ascending (north) lunar node, associated with eclipses
  • Ketu — the descending (south) lunar node

Worship

Many Hindu temples, especially in South India, contain a Navagraha shrine in which the nine deities are placed in a square arrangement with Surya at the centre; none faces another directly, so that their influences are balanced. Devotees circumambulate the shrine, often nine times, and perform specific offerings — particular grains, fabrics and flowers are associated with each graha. Worship is especially connected with Vedic astrology (Jyotisha), in which the positions of the grahas at birth are held to shape a person's horoscope and periods of life (dashas). The Navagraha temples of Tamil Nadu, a cluster of nine Shiva temples around Kumbakonam each associated with one graha, are famous pilgrimage sites.

Rahu and Ketu

Rahu and Ketu originate in the myth of the churning of the ocean of milk, in which the demon Svarbhanu drank the nectar of immortality and was cut in two by Vishnu; his immortal head became Rahu and his body Ketu. They are called shadow planets (chhaya graha) because they mark the eclipse points rather than physical bodies.
