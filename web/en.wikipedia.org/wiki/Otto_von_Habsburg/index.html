Otto von Habsburg (20 November 1912 – 4 July 2011), also known in Austria as Otto Habsburg-Lothringen, was the last Crown Prince of Austria-Hungary, the eldest son of the last emperor, Charles I. Head of the House of Habsburg from 1922 until 2007, he became a German politician, writer and advocate of European integration, serving twenty years in the European Parliament.

Early life and exile

Otto was six when the Habsburg monarchy collapsed in 1918; the family was exiled, eventually settling in Belgium, where he studied at Louvain. Austria's Habsburg Law barred his return unless he renounced his claims. During the 1930s he campaigned against the Nazi annexation of Austria and had to flee to the United States in 1940, where he lobbied the Roosevelt administration; the Nazis condemned him to death in absentia.

Political career

After renouncing his claim to the Austrian throne in 1961, Otto was allowed to visit Austria in 1966. Based in Pöcking, Bavaria, he became president of the International Paneuropean Union and, from 1979 to 1999, a Member of the European Parliament for the Christian Social Union. A prominent anti-communist, he co-organised the 1989 Paneuropean Picnic on the Austro-Hungarian border, which enabled hundreds of East Germans to escape to the West and helped precipitate the fall of the Iron Curtain.

Death

Otto died at Pöcking on 4 July 2011, aged 98. His body was interred in the Imperial Crypt in Vienna after a requiem at St Stephen's Cathedral, while his heart was buried, according to family tradition, at Pannonhalma Archabbey in Hungary.
