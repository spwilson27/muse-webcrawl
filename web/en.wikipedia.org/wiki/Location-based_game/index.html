Location-based game

A location-based game (or location-enabled game) is a type of pervasive game in which gameplay evolves and progresses through the player's real-world location, typically using GPS or other positioning technology on a mobile device. Gameplay elements — objectives, collectibles, opponents, or events — are mapped onto physical places, so players must move through the real world to play.

History

Early examples include the 2000 GPS game Geocaching, in which players hunt containers hidden at published coordinates, and BotFighters (2000), often cited as an early mobile location-based game. Ingress (2012) by Niantic built a worldwide player community around capturing virtual portals at real landmarks, and its successor Pokémon Go (2016) became a global phenomenon, bringing location-based gaming to a mass audience.

Design and technology

Location-based games rely on satellite positioning, map data, and mobile data connections, and often overlay virtual content on the physical world via augmented reality. Designers must manage issues such as GPS inaccuracy, safety, trespassing, and uneven distributions of game content between urban and rural areas.

Related forms

The genre overlaps with pervasive games, alternate reality games, exercise games (exergames), and location-based services. Titles such as Zombies, Run!, Harry Potter: Wizards Unite, and Pikmin Bloom combine location mechanics with fitness or exploration themes.
