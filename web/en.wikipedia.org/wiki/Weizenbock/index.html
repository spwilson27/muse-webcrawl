Weizenbock


Weizenbock is a strong German wheat beer.

Weizenbock (German: "wheat bock") is a style of strong beer originating in Bavaria, Germany. It combines the strength and malt richness of a bock with the wheat character and yeast-derived flavours of a weissbier (wheat beer). The style is typically brewed with a significant proportion of wheat malt — often 40 to 60 percent of the grist — and fermented with traditional Bavarian wheat beer yeast, producing characteristic notes of banana and clove over a rich, bready malt base.

Characteristics

Weizenbocks range in colour from pale gold to deep mahogany brown, depending on the malts used, and are usually cloudy from yeast and wheat proteins. Alcohol content typically ranges from about 6.5% to 9% by volume. Flavours commonly include dark fruit, caramel, and toasted bread in darker versions, while paler examples emphasise fresh bread dough and lighter fruit esters. Hop bitterness is low, serving mainly to balance the malt sweetness.

History

The best-known example is Aventinus, first brewed in 1907 by the Schneider Weisse brewery in Munich; it is often described as the original weizenbock and is technically a weizendoppelbock. The style grew out of the long Bavarian traditions of both strong bock beers, brewed for Lent and winter festivals, and top-fermented wheat beers that the Wittelsbach dukes of Bavaria once reserved as a royal monopoly.
