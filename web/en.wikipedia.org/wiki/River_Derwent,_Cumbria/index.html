The River Derwent is a river in the county of Cumbria in north-west England. It rises in the Lake District fells above Borrowdale, flows through Derwentwater and Bassenthwaite Lake — the only two bodies of water in the Lake District formally called "lake" rather than mere, water or tarn, both lying on its course — and continues through Cockermouth and Workington to the Irish Sea.

Course

The Derwent's headwaters gather on the slopes around Sty Head and Sprinkling Tarn beneath Great End and Scafell Pike. It flows north through the Borrowdale valley, past the villages of Seathwaite, Rosthwaite and Grange, and enters Derwentwater near Keswick. Leaving the lake at its northern end, the river re-forms and flows into Bassenthwaite Lake. Below Bassenthwaite it turns west, is joined by the River Cocker at Cockermouth, and continues through Papcastle and Workington, where it enters the Irish Sea. The catchment is among the wettest in England, Borrowdale receiving some of the highest rainfall in the country.

Flooding

The Derwent catchment is prone to severe flooding. Storm Desmond in December 2015 brought record river levels, inundating parts of Cockermouth and Keswick, while earlier floods in November 2009 devastated Workington, where the collapse and closure of bridges divided the town and a police officer, PC Bill Barker, was killed when a bridge at Workington gave way. Substantial flood defence works have since been built at both towns.

Ecology

The river and its lakes form part of the River Derwent and Bassenthwaite Lake Special Area of Conservation. The system supports Atlantic salmon, sea trout, and the rare vendace, a glacial-relict fish found in Derwentwater and Bassenthwaite, among the species' only English locations. Otters are also present along the river.
