Clostridium botulinum is a Gram-positive, rod-shaped, anaerobic, spore-forming bacterium that produces botulinum toxin, the most potent neurotoxin known. Under low-oxygen conditions — classically in improperly preserved foods — its spores germinate and the growing bacteria release toxin, causing the paralytic illness botulism in humans and other animals.

Characteristics

The organism is found in soils and aquatic sediments worldwide. Its spores are highly heat-resistant, which is why pressure canning is required for low-acid foods. Strains are grouped by the antigenic type of toxin they produce (types A through G); human botulism is usually caused by types A, B, E, and rarely F.

Botulism

Botulinum toxin blocks the release of acetylcholine at neuromuscular junctions, producing a descending flaccid paralysis that can be fatal without antitoxin and ventilatory support. Forms of disease include foodborne botulism, infant botulism (associated with intestinal colonisation, sometimes after ingestion of honey), and wound botulism. Purified toxin type A is used medically and cosmetically as botulinum toxin injections ("Botox").
