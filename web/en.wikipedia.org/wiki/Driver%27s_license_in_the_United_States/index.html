Driver's license in the United States

In the United States, driver's licenses are issued by each individual state, territory and the District of Columbia rather than by the federal government. Drivers are normally required to obtain a license from their state of residence, and all states recognize each other's licenses for temporary driving under the principle of reciprocity.

Licensing process

Most states use graduated licensing for teenagers: a learner's permit, followed by a restricted intermediate license, then a full license, typically available from age 16 to 18 depending on the state. Applicants generally pass vision screening, a written knowledge test and a road skills test. Commercial driver's licenses (CDLs), by contrast, follow federal standards set by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration.

Identification and REAL ID

Because the United States has no national identity card, the driver's license is the de facto standard photo identification. The REAL ID Act of 2005 set federal standards for state licenses used for purposes such as boarding domestic flights; enforcement for air travel began in May 2025. Some states also issue licenses to residents regardless of immigration status, while others restrict eligibility.

Suspension and points

States operate demerit point systems under which traffic violations accumulate points that can lead to suspension or revocation of the license.
