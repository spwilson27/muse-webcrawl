San José del Cabo (Spanish pronunciation: [saŋ xoˈse ðel ˈkaβo], "Saint Joseph of the Cape") is a coastal city on the Gulf of California coast of Baja California Sur, Mexico. It is the municipal seat of Los Cabos Municipality and lies at the southern tip of the Baja California Peninsula, about 32 kilometres (20 mi) northeast of its sister city, Cabo San Lucas. Together the two cities form the resort corridor known as Los Cabos.

History

The bay was long used by Spanish galleons on the Manila–Acapulco route as a source of fresh water. A Jesuit mission, Misión San José del Cabo Añuití, was founded in 1730 by the Jesuit padre Nicolás Tamaral. The mission was moved several times and suppressed in the 1840s; the present church dates to 1940. During the Mexican–American War, the town was the site of fighting in 1847, when U.S. Marines held the mission against Mexican forces.

Geography and tourism

San José del Cabo is noted for its quieter, more traditional character compared with Cabo San Lucas, with a historic centre around Plaza Mijares, an art district with galleries, and an estuary (Estero San José) that forms an important freshwater wetland and bird sanctuary near the coast. Tourism expanded rapidly after the completion of the Transpeninsular Highway in 1973 and the opening of Los Cabos International Airport, which serves the city. The coastline east of town, the East Cape, is known for surfing breaks such as Zippers and Acapulquito.

Climate

The city has a tropical desert climate, with warm, dry winters and hot summers moderated by the sea. It lies in a hurricane-prone zone; Hurricane Odile struck the region in September 2014, causing severe damage across Los Cabos.
