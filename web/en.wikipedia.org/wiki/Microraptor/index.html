Microraptor (Greek for "small thief") is a genus of small, four-winged dromaeosaurid dinosaur that lived about 120 million years ago, during the early Cretaceous, in what is now Liaoning, China. Roughly the size of a crow, it is one of the smallest known non-avian dinosaurs and one of the most abundant theropods in the Jehol Biota, with hundreds of specimens known.

Description

Microraptor measured about 77 centimetres in length and weighed around 1 kilogram. Unusually, it bore long pennaceous (flight) feathers on both its arms and its legs, forming a second pair of "hind wings", as well as a fan of feathers at the tip of its long bony tail. Analysis of melanosomes in a fossil specimen published in 2012 indicated that its plumage was glossy black with a blue iridescence, similar to that of a modern starling.

Palaeobiology

Whether Microraptor could fly under its own power or only glide has been debated; wind-tunnel and modelling studies suggest it could glide efficiently between trees and may have been capable of some powered flight. Gut contents show a varied diet including birds, fish, lizards and mammals. Its combination of teeth, claws and small size suggests an arboreal or semi-arboreal predator. Microraptor is central to debates about the origin of flight in dinosaurs and the evolution of birds, since its four-winged condition may echo an early stage in the development of the avian flight stroke.

Species

The type species M. zhaoianus was named in 2000 by Xu Xing and colleagues. M. gui, described in 2003, was once controversially proposed as a separate genus but is now usually treated as a species or synonym within Microraptor, and M. hanqingi was named in 2010 from a larger specimen.
