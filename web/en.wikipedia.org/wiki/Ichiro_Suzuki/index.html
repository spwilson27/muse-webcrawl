Ichiro Suzuki

Ichiro Suzuki (Japanese: 鈴木 一朗, born 22 October 1973), usually known simply as Ichiro, is a Japanese former professional baseball outfielder widely regarded as one of the greatest hitters and defensive outfielders in the history of the sport. He played nine seasons with the Orix BlueWave of Nippon Professional Baseball (NPB) before joining the Seattle Mariners of Major League Baseball (MLB) in 2001, later playing for the New York Yankees and Miami Marlins before finishing his career with Seattle in 2019.

Career in Japan

With Orix, Ichiro won seven consecutive batting titles and three Pacific League MVP awards, revolutionizing Japanese hitting with his distinctive leg-kick swing and slap-hitting style. His record 210-hit season in 1994 made him a national celebrity, and he began using only his first name, "Ichiro," on his uniform.

Major League career

In his first MLB season (2001), Ichiro won both the American League Rookie of the Year and Most Valuable Player awards — only the second player ever to win both in the same year — as well as the batting title and stolen base crown. He set the MLB single-season hits record with 262 in 2004, breaking George Sisler's 84-year-old mark, and recorded ten consecutive 200-hit seasons, an MLB record. A 10-time All-Star and 10-time Gold Glove winner, he collected his 3,000th MLB hit in 2016; counting his Japanese totals, his 4,367 professional hits exceed any player in organized baseball history, surpassing Pete Rose's MLB record of 4,256.

Retirement and legacy

Ichiro retired after two games played in Tokyo in March 2019, and joined the Mariners' front office as a special assistant. In 2025 he was elected to both the Japanese Baseball Hall of Fame and the National Baseball Hall of Fame in Cooperstown, where he fell one vote short of unanimous election in his first year of eligibility.
