Mendele Mocher Sforim (Yiddish: מענדעלע מוכר ספֿרים, "Mendele the Book Peddler"), the pen name of Sholem Yankev Abramovitsh (1836–1917), was a Jewish author from the Russian Empire regarded as the "grandfather of modern Yiddish literature" and one of the founders of modern Hebrew prose fiction. Writing in both languages, he created a body of satirical fiction depicting life in the shtetls of the Pale of Settlement.

Life

Abramovitsh was born in Kapyl, near Minsk, and received a traditional yeshiva education before encountering the ideas of the Jewish Enlightenment (Haskalah). After years of wandering through Ukraine — an experience of poverty and itinerancy that furnished material for his fiction — he settled in Berdichev, then Zhytomyr, and from 1881 in Odessa, where he directed a Jewish school and remained until his death.

Works

Under his persona of Mendele, a folksy travelling book peddler who narrates and comments on the tales, Abramovitsh published his first Yiddish story in 1864. His major works include Dos kleyne mentshele ("The Little Man"), Fishke der krumer ("Fishke the Lame"), Dos vintshfingerl ("The Wishing Ring") and the picaresque novel Masoes Binyomin hashlishi ("The Travels of Benjamin III", 1878), a Jewish Don Quixote figure journeying beyond the shtetl. He also translated and adapted works of natural science into Hebrew, coining much modern Hebrew scientific terminology, and reworked many of his Yiddish writings into Hebrew versions.

Style and influence

Mendele's satire exposes the poverty, superstition and corrupt communal institutions of shtetl life while retaining affection for its people and a rich, idiomatic command of Yiddish. His balanced Hebrew prose style — the nusach — set the standard for modern Hebrew literature. He influenced both of the other classic Yiddish writers, Sholem Aleichem and I. L. Peretz, and is conventionally honoured as the first of the three.
