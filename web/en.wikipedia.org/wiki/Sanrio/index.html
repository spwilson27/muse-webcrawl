Sanrio Company, Ltd. (株式会社サンリオ, Kabushiki-gaisha Sanrio) is a Japanese entertainment and licensing company best known for creating and licensing kawaii ("cute") characters, most famously Hello Kitty. Founded by Shintaro Tsuji in 1960, the company designs characters and licenses them for use on a vast range of products — stationery, school supplies, clothing, accessories, gifts and greeting cards — rather than manufacturing most goods itself.

History

Tsuji founded the Yamanashi Silk Company in 1960 and discovered that adding small cute designs, such as a strawberry motif, to products greatly increased sales. Renaming the firm Sanrio in 1973, he built a business around the philosophy of nakayoku — friendship and mutual regard — expressed in its gift-giving culture: small gifts, it holds, can build friendship and happiness. In 1974 designer Yuko Shimizu created Hello Kitty, a white bobtail cat with a red bow whose simple, mouthless design became a global phenomenon and the company's mainstay.

Sanrio expanded into the United States in 1976 and Europe thereafter. It operates theme parks in Japan — Sanrio Puroland in Tama, Tokyo (opened 1990) and Harmonyland in Ōita — and produces animated films and series featuring its characters. The company also held the Japanese licensing rights to Peanuts for a period and has developed entertainment ventures including the Strawberry News fan magazine, published since 1975.

Characters

Sanrio has created hundreds of characters. The most successful after Hello Kitty include:

  • My Melody (1975) — a white rabbit in a pink hood;
  • Little Twin Stars (1975) — Kiki and Lala;
  • Keroppi (1988) — a frog;
  • Bad Badtz-maru (1993) — a mischievous penguin;
  • Cinnamoroll (2001) — a white flying puppy;
  • Kuromi (2005) — My Melody's rival;
  • Gudetama (2013) — a lazy egg yolk;
  • Aggretsuko (2015) — a red panda office worker, star of a Netflix series.

The annual Sanrio Character Ranking, a popularity poll voted by fans since 1986, is a major marketing event.

Business

Sanrio's model is character licensing: licensees pay royalties to apply characters to products, supplemented by direct retail stores, theme parks and media. Hello Kitty alone has appeared on tens of thousands of products and generated billions of dollars in retail sales over the decades, appealing both to children and to adults through nostalgia and collaborations with fashion and luxury brands.
