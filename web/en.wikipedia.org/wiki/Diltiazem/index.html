Diltiazem

Class: Calcium channel blocker (benzothiazepine)
Trade names: Cardizem, Tiazac, others
Routes: Oral, intravenous
Legal status: Prescription only

Diltiazem, sold under the brand name Cardizem among others, is a nondihydropyridine calcium channel blocker used in the treatment of hypertension, angina pectoris, and some types of arrhythmia. It is also an effective preventive medication for migraine. Diltiazem was approved for medical use in the United States in 1982 and is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Diltiazem is used for:

  • Stable angina and Prinzmetal (variant) angina
  • Hypertension, alone or in combination
  • Rate control in atrial fibrillation and atrial flutter, and acute treatment of supraventricular tachycardia (intravenous form)
  • Off-label: anal fissure (topical), migraine prophylaxis, and Raynaud's phenomenon

Mechanism of action

Diltiazem blocks L-type calcium channels in cardiac and vascular smooth muscle. This relaxes arterial smooth muscle (causing vasodilation and reduced blood pressure) and slows conduction through the atrioventricular node while modestly reducing heart rate and contractility. It is intermediate in profile between verapamil (more cardiac) and the dihydropyridines such as amlodipine (more vascular).

Adverse effects

Common side effects include headache, dizziness, flushing, peripheral edema, and constipation. More serious risks include bradycardia, heart block, and worsening of systolic heart failure. Diltiazem is a moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor and is involved in many drug interactions, including with statins and some immunosuppressants; grapefruit juice increases its levels.
