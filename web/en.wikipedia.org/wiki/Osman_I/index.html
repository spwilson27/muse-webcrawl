Osman I or Osman Gazi (Ottoman Turkish: عثمان غازى, ʿOsmān Ġāzī; Turkish: I. Osman or Osman Gazi; died 1323/4) was the founder of the Ottoman Empire (first known as the Ottoman Beylik or Emirate). While initially a small Turkoman principality during Osman's reign, his beylik transformed into a vast empire in the years following his death.

Due to the scarcity of historical sources dating from his lifetime, very little factual information about Osman has survived. Not a single written source survives from Osman's reign, and the Ottomans did not record the history of his life until the fifteenth century, more than a hundred years after his death. Because of this, historians find it very challenging to differentiate between fact and myth in the many stories told about him.

Rise

Osman succeeded his father Ertuğrul as bey of the Kayı tribe at Söğüt on the Byzantine frontier in northwestern Anatolia, around 1280/81. The declining Seljuk Sultanate of Rum had granted the family their marcher lordship (uç). Exploiting Byzantine weakness, Osman raided and captured fortresses such as Karacahisar (where tradition says he first had the Friday prayer read in his own name, a mark of sovereignty, c. 1299 — the conventional founding date of the Ottoman state) and defeated a Byzantine force at the Battle of Bapheus in 1302. His followers included Turkish ghazi warriors, converts, and Byzantine renegades and allies; his marriage to Malhun Hatun, daughter of the influential Sheikh Edebali, is a famous foundation legend. By his death his principality controlled much of Bithynia and stood before the walls of Bursa, which fell to his son Orhan in 1326; Osman was buried there as he had wished.

Legacy

The empire and dynasty took their name from Osman (hence "Ottoman", from the Arabic form ʿUthmān). The Sword of Osman became the symbol of accession, girded on each new sultan by the Sharif of Konya. Later Ottoman tradition credited Osman with a prophetic dream — a great tree growing from his navel to shade the world — interpreted as a prophecy of his dynasty's dominion.
