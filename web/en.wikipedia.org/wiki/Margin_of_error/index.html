Margin of error

FieldStatistics, survey methodology
Related conceptsConfidence interval, sampling error, standard error

The margin of error is a statistic expressing the amount of random sampling error in the results of a survey. The larger the margin of error, the less confidence one should have that a poll result reflects the result of a census of the entire population. It is usually half the width of a confidence interval for a particular statistic.

Calculation

For a simple random sample, the margin of error for a percentage is approximately z × √(p(1 − p)/n), where p is the sample proportion, n the sample size, and z the z-score for the desired confidence level (about 1.96 for 95% confidence). Because the error is largest when p = 0.5, polls often report the maximum margin of error; for a sample of about 1,000 respondents this is roughly ±3.1 percentage points at the 95% level. Quadrupling the sample size only halves the margin.

Interpretation and limitations

The margin of error quantifies only random sampling error. It does not account for systematic errors such as biased question wording, non-response, coverage problems, or likely-voter modelling errors, all of which can be much larger in practice. Results for subgroups have larger margins of error than the full sample, and the margin for a difference between two candidates is larger than either candidate's individual margin — common sources of misreporting in election coverage.

See also Mahalanobis distance for a related measure in multivariate statistics.
