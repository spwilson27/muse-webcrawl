The 2011 Cricket World Cup Final was played on 2 April 2011 at the Wankhede Stadium in Mumbai, India, between co-hosts India and Sri Lanka. It was the culmination of the tenth edition of the Cricket World Cup, and was notable as the first final contested by two Asian teams and the first in which the winning side was playing on home soil. India won the match by six wickets, chasing down Sri Lanka's total of 274 for 6 with ten balls to spare, to lift the World Cup for the second time, 28 years after their first triumph in 1983.

Background

The 2011 tournament, co-hosted by India, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh, featured 14 teams. India topped their group and defeated Australia in the quarter-finals and Pakistan in a celebrated semi-final at Mohali. Sri Lanka, winners in 1996, beat England in the quarter-finals and New Zealand in the semi-finals. Both finalists had appeared in a final before: India's victory in 1983 and defeat in 2003, Sri Lanka's win in 1996 and runners-up finish in 2007.

Match summary

Sri Lanka captain Kumar Sangakkara won the toss and elected to bat. Openers Tillakaratne Dilshan and Upul Tharanga were dismissed cheaply, but a century from Mahela Jayawardene (103 not out from 88 balls), supported by Sangakkara (48) and a late surge from Thisara Perera, carried Sri Lanka to 274 for 6. Zaheer Khan was India's most economical bowler.

India's chase began disastrously with the dismissals of Virender Sehwag (lbw to Lasith Malinga for a duck) and Sachin Tendulkar (18). Gautam Gambhir anchored the innings with 97, sharing stands with Virat Kohli and then captain MS Dhoni. Dhoni, promoting himself ahead of the in-form Yuvraj Singh, struck an unbeaten 91 from 79 balls. He sealed victory with a six over long-on off Nuwan Kulasekara, one of the most replayed shots in cricket history.

Aftermath

Yuvraj Singh was named player of the tournament for his all-round performances. The win sparked celebrations across India, and the final was watched by an enormous global television audience. Tendulkar, playing in his sixth and final World Cup, described the triumph as the highlight of his career. The match also marked the end of Muttiah Muralitharan's international career; the Sri Lankan off-spinner, the most prolific wicket-taker in the history of the game, retired after the final.
