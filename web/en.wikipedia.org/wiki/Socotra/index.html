Socotra

Socotra (Arabic: سُقُطْرَىٰ) is an island of Yemen in the Indian Ocean, lying between the Guardafui Channel and the Arabian Sea, near the Horn of Africa. It is the largest of the four islands of the Socotra Archipelago and a UNESCO World Heritage site famed for its extraordinary endemic flora.

Biodiversity

Long isolation made Socotra a "Galápagos of the Indian Ocean": about 37% of its plant species occur nowhere else, including the dragon blood tree (Dracaena cinnabari) with its umbrella canopy and red resin, the bottle tree (desert rose), and the cucumber tree. Many endemic birds, reptiles and invertebrates likewise evolved there. The archipelago was inscribed by UNESCO in 2008.

History and people

Socotra was known to ancient Greeks and Romans as Dioscorida, traded for frankincense and aloes, ruled successively by South Arabian kingdoms, the Mahra sultanate, Portuguese (briefly, 1507), and British-protected sultans, joining South Yemen in 1967. Socotris speak Soqotri, a Modern South Arabian language. Since Yemen's civil war, the island has been administered with UAE-backed and Southern Transitional Council involvement, largely spared direct fighting but politically contested. Cyclones in 2015 and 2018 caused severe damage.
