Biliverdin is a green tetrapyrrolic bile pigment, and a product of heme catabolism. It is the pigment responsible for the green color sometimes seen in bruises and for the greenish tint of the shells of some birds' eggs. Biliverdin is formed when the heme group of hemoglobin is cleaved and is subsequently reduced to the yellow pigment bilirubin.

Metabolism

Biliverdin is produced when macrophages of the reticuloendothelial system break down senescent red blood cells. The enzyme heme oxygenase cleaves the porphyrin ring of heme at its alpha-methene bridge, releasing carbon monoxide and iron and producing biliverdin. In mammals, biliverdin is then almost entirely reduced to bilirubin by the cytosolic enzyme biliverdin reductase; this is why mammalian bile and bruises are dominated by the yellow-orange bilirubin rather than the green pigment.

In many birds, reptiles, amphibians, and fish, however, biliverdin is the principal bile pigment excreted, because these animals have much lower biliverdin reductase activity. The green blood of certain skinks (Prasinohaema) of New Guinea contains exceptionally high concentrations of biliverdin, which would be toxic to most animals and is thought to deter predators or parasites.

Role in disease and physiology

The shifting colors of a healing bruise — from red-purple deoxyhemoglobin, through green biliverdin, to yellow-brown bilirubin and hemosiderin — reflect the sequential steps of heme breakdown. In severe liver disease or obstructive jaundice, accumulation of biliverdin can give the skin a greenish cast. Research has also explored biliverdin and biliverdin reductase as antioxidant and cytoprotective agents; biliverdin is itself an antioxidant, and the biliverdin–bilirubin redox cycle is thought to help protect cells against oxidative stress.

In nature

Biliverdin colors the eggshells of many bird species, including the striking blue-green eggs of the American robin, where it acts together with protoporphyrin in shell pigmentation. It also occurs in the integument of some insects and in the shells of certain mollusks.
