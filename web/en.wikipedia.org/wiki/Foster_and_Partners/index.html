Foster and Partners

Foster and Partners (stylized Foster + Partners) is a British international architecture and design practice founded by Norman Foster in London in 1967 as Foster Associates. It is one of the largest architectural firms in the United Kingdom, with offices worldwide and notable works on every continent.

History

Norman Foster established the practice after Team 4, his earlier partnership with Richard Rogers and others. Early landmark projects included the Willis Faber and Dumas headquarters in Ipswich (1975) and the Sainsbury Centre for Visual Arts (1978), exemplars of high-tech architecture. The Hongkong and Shanghai Bank headquarters in Hong Kong (1986) brought global fame.

Notable works

The firm's portfolio includes the Reichstag dome in Berlin (1999), the Great Court of the British Museum (2000), 30 St Mary Axe ("the Gherkin," 2004), the Millau Viaduct in France, Hearst Tower in New York, Wembley Stadium (2007), the Apple Park campus in California (2017), and airports including Hong Kong International, Beijing Capital Terminal 3, and Queen Alia International in Jordan. Foster won the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 1999 and the practice has twice won the Stirling Prize.

Organization

Foster and Partners employs more than a thousand staff. Its design approach emphasizes sustainability, integrated engineering, and technology-driven construction.
