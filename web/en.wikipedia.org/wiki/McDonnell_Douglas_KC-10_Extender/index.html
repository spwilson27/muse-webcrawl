McDonnell Douglas KC-10 Extender

KC-10 Extender
Role: Aerial refuelling tanker / transport
Manufacturer: McDonnell Douglas
First flight: 12 July 1980
Introduction: 1981
Retired: 2024 (USAF)
Number built: 62 (60 KC-10A, 2 KDC-10)
Developed from: McDonnell Douglas DC-10

The McDonnell Douglas KC-10 Extender was an aerial refuelling tanker and military transport aircraft operated by the United States Air Force. Derived from the commercial DC-10-30 airliner, it was the largest tanker in the USAF inventory and could also carry cargo and passengers on the same missions in which it refuelled other aircraft.

Design

The KC-10 retained about 88 percent commonality with the DC-10. Military additions included a flying boom operated from a station beneath the rear fuselage, a hose-and-drogue system allowing it to refuel probe-equipped Navy, Marine Corps and allied aircraft, additional fuel tanks in the lower fuselage that raised its fuel capacity to about 356,000 lb (161,500 kg), and a powered cargo compartment able to carry up to 27 pallets. Unlike most tankers it could itself be refuelled in flight, greatly extending its range.

Service

Sixty KC-10As were delivered between 1981 and 1988. The type supported operations from the 1986 strikes on Libya — when US-based F-111s flew a long circuit around denied European airspace, refuelled repeatedly by KC-10s — through Desert Storm, in which KC-10s flew some 380 missions delivering over 100 million pounds of fuel, to campaigns in Afghanistan, Iraq and Syria. Two converted KDC-10s served with the Royal Netherlands Air Force.

The USAF retired its last KC-10 in September 2024, its role passing to the KC-46 Pegasus and KC-135 Stratotanker.
