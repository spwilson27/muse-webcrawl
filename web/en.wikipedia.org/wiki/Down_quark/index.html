Down quark

The down quark (symbol: d) is, together with the up quark, one of the two lightest quarks and a constituent of ordinary matter. It is a first-generation elementary particle in the Standard Model of particle physics, with electric charge −1/3 e, spin 1/2, and a mass of about 4.7 MeV/c2.

Properties

Like all quarks, the down quark is a fermion subject to all four fundamental interactions, and carries colour charge, so it is bound by the strong interaction into hadrons. A proton consists of two up quarks and one down quark (uud), while a neutron consists of one up quark and two down quarks (udd); the greater mass of the down quark compared with the up quark is part of why the neutron is slightly heavier than the proton.

History

The down quark was proposed in 1964 by Murray Gell-Mann and, independently, George Zweig as part of the quark model explaining the pattern of known hadrons. Evidence for quarks emerged from deep inelastic scattering experiments at SLAC in the late 1960s, which revealed point-like constituents inside the proton and neutron.

Weak interaction

Through the weak interaction, a down quark can change into an up quark by emitting a W boson. This transformation underlies beta decay: in a neutron, one down quark converts to an up quark, turning the neutron into a proton while emitting an electron and an electron antineutrino. Down quarks can also transform into the heavier strange and bottom quarks, with probabilities described by the CKM matrix.
