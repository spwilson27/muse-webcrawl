Eucalyptus urophylla

Eucalyptus urophylla S.T.Blake
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Myrtales
Family: Myrtaceae
Genus: Eucalyptus
Common names: Timor white gum, Timor mountain gum
Native range: Lesser Sunda Islands (Wetar, Flores, Timor and neighbouring islands)

Eucalyptus urophylla, commonly known as the Timor white gum or Timor mountain gum, is a species of eucalypt tree native to the Lesser Sunda Islands of Indonesia and Timor-Leste, including Wetar, Flores, Alor and Timor. Unusually for a eucalypt, its natural distribution lies entirely outside Australia.

Description

It is a straight-trunked evergreen tree that can reach 25–45 metres in height, with smooth, peeling whitish to orange-tinted bark. The species name urophylla refers to the leaves, which taper to a long, tail-like tip. It grows naturally in mountain forests, often at elevations between about 400 and 3,000 metres.

Uses

Eucalyptus urophylla is one of the most important plantation eucalypts in the humid tropics. It is grown extensively in Indonesia, Vietnam, China and Brazil for pulpwood and timber, and is widely used in hybrid breeding—most notably in crosses with Eucalyptus grandis ("urograndis" hybrids), which combine fast growth with adaptability to tropical conditions and are a mainstay of Brazilian pulp production.
