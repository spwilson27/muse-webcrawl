In fixed-wing aircraft, autorotation is a phenomenon in which a stalled wing continues to roll without pilot input, because the two wings stall unevenly. When an aircraft approaches the stall, a slight yaw or disturbance causes one wing to drop; the descending wing experiences an increased angle of attack, stalling more deeply and losing lift, while the rising wing, at a lower angle of attack, regains lift. The resulting asymmetry sustains the rolling motion, and the aircraft enters the fully developed regime known as a spin, in which it descends in a steep, corkscrewing path.

Mechanism

Beyond the stalling angle, lift decreases with further increases in angle of attack while drag rises sharply. The downgoing wing therefore produces less lift and more drag, which both rolls and yaws the aircraft further in the same direction — a self-reinforcing, or "pro-spin", state. In a steady spin the aircraft settles at a high angle of attack (often 40–60 degrees), a high rate of descent and a constant rotation rate, with the forces in equilibrium.

Spin entry and recovery

Spins typically develop from a stall combined with yaw, for example from a skidding turn or a mishandled stall; aerobatic pilots enter them deliberately. Standard recovery technique — commonly taught by the mnemonic PARE (Power idle, Ailerons neutral, Rudder opposite the rotation, Elevator to break the stall) — stops the yaw, reduces the angle of attack below the stall, and then recovers from the resulting dive. Some aircraft types are prohibited from intentional spinning because of unfavourable recovery characteristics, and light aircraft certification (such as under FAR/CS 23) requires demonstrated spin recovery or spin resistance.

Distinction from rotorcraft autorotation

The term has an unrelated meaning for helicopters and autogyros, where autorotation describes the free-spinning of the rotor driven upward by the airflow during descent, allowing a controlled landing after engine failure. Samara seeds of maples and other trees exploit the same fixed-wing autorotation principle to disperse by spinning slowly to the ground.
