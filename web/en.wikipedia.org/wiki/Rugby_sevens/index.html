Rugby sevens

Rugby sevens (commonly known simply as sevens) is a variant of rugby union in which teams are made up of seven players playing seven-minute halves, instead of the usual fifteen players playing forty-minute halves. It is played on a full-size field under broadly the same laws as the fifteen-a-side game, but the reduced numbers create far more space, emphasising speed, handling and open running. Rugby sevens is administered by World Rugby.

History

Sevens was invented in 1883 in Melrose, Scotland, by butcher Ned Haig as a fundraising idea for his club, Melrose RFC. The short format spread through the Scottish Borders, where an annual circuit of tournaments persists, and later worldwide. The first international tournament was held at Murrayfield in 1973 for the SRU centenary, and the Hong Kong Sevens, founded in 1976, popularised the game in Asia and beyond.

Competitions

World Rugby runs the annual SVNS (formerly the World Rugby Sevens Series) for national teams, a Rugby World Cup Sevens every four years (first held in Edinburgh in 1993), and sevens is part of the Commonwealth Games. Sevens joined the Olympic Games at Rio de Janeiro in 2016, with Fiji winning the first men's gold medal and Australia the women's—an inclusion that greatly raised the sport's profile and funding. Unlike the fifteen-a-side game, smaller nations such as Fiji, Kenya and Samoa have excelled at sevens.

Play

Games consist of two halves of seven minutes (ten in finals), with conversions taken by drop-kick and set pieces involving only three forwards. Because tournaments are usually completed over a single weekend, squads of twelve rotate through several matches a day. The format rewards pace and evasive running, and scores are typically frequent, making sevens an attractive spectator and festival sport.
