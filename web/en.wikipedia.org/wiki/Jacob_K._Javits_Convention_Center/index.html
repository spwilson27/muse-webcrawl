Jacob K. Javits Convention Center

The Jacob K. Javits Convention Center, commonly the Javits Center, is a major convention centre on Manhattan's West Side in New York City, between 34th and 40th Streets along Eleventh Avenue, overlooking the Hudson River. It is named for Senator Jacob Javits, who served New York in the U.S. Senate from 1957 to 1981.

Building

The original centre, designed by I. M. Pei's firm and opened in 1986, is a glass-and-space-frame structure with about 760,000 square feet of exhibition space. A major expansion, Javits North, completed in 2021, added some 1.2 million square feet, including a rooftop farm and event pavilion, making the complex one of the largest convention centres in the United States.

Events

The Javits Center hosts the New York International Auto Show, New York Comic Con, and hundreds of trade shows and conventions annually. In March 2020 it was temporarily converted by the Army Corps of Engineers into a field hospital during the COVID-19 pandemic, and later served as a mass vaccination site.
