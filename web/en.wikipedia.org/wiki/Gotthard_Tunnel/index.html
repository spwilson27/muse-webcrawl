Gotthard Tunnel

The Gotthard Tunnel (German: Gotthardtunnel) is a 15-kilometre (9.3 mi) railway tunnel through the Saint-Gotthard Massif in the Swiss Alps. Opened in 1882, it connects Göschenen in the canton of Uri with Airolo in Ticino, forming the summit tunnel of the Gotthard railway between northern and southern Europe.

Built between 1872 and 1882 under the engineer Louis Favre, the tunnel was one of the great engineering feats of the 19th century. Construction cost the lives of nearly 200 workers, including Favre himself, who died of a heart attack during an inspection in 1879. When it opened it was the longest tunnel in the world.

The name "Gotthard Tunnel" may also refer to two later bores through the same massif: the Gotthard Road Tunnel, opened in 1980 for motor traffic, and the Gotthard Base Tunnel, opened in 2016, which at 57 km is the world's longest railway tunnel and now carries most high-speed and freight trains on the axis.
