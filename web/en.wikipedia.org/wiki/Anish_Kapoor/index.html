Sir Anish Mikhail Kapoor CBE (born 12 March 1954) is a British-Indian sculptor specialising in installation art and conceptual art. One of the most influential sculptors of his generation, he is known for large-scale works exploring voids, mirrors, pigment and the sublime.

Life and career

Kapoor was born in Bombay to an Indian Hindu father and an Iraqi Jewish mother. He moved to London in the early 1970s, studying at Hornsey College of Art and Chelsea School of Art. He came to prominence in the 1980s with geometric sculptures coated in pure powdered pigment, whose intense colours and fragile surfaces suggested ritual objects.

Major works

From the 1990s Kapoor explored polished stainless steel and cavernous voids: Sky Mirror (2001), the monumental Marsyas (2002) in Tate Modern's Turbine Hall, and Cloud Gate (2006) in Chicago — "the Bean" — one of the world's most popular public artworks. He designed the ArcelorMittal Orbit tower for the London 2012 Olympics. His later work includes pieces coated in Vantablack, a super-black material whose exclusive licence for artistic use he obtained in 2016, provoking controversy.

Recognition

Kapoor represented Britain at the 1990 Venice Biennale (winning the Premio Duemila), won the Turner Prize in 1991, and was knighted in 2013. A major Royal Academy retrospective in 2009 drew record crowds.
