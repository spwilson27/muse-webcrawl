Bragança (Portuguese pronunciation: [bɾɐˈɣɐ̃sɐ]; Mirandese: Bergáncia) is a city and municipality in north-eastern Portugal, capital of the district of the same name, in the traditional province of Trás-os-Montes near the border with Spain. It is one of Portugal's most remote district capitals, set on a plateau at the edge of the Montesinho Natural Park.

History

The site was settled in Celtic and Roman times, but the modern town grew around its castle, built in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries as part of the defence of the young Kingdom of Portugal's north-eastern frontier. Bragança gave its name to the House of Braganza, founded when Afonso, an illegitimate son of King John I, became the first Duke of Braganza in 1442. The house supplied the kings of Portugal from 1640, when the Duke was acclaimed as John IV at the restoration of independence from Spain, until the fall of the monarchy in 1910, and emperors of Brazil in the nineteenth century.

Sights

The walled citadel contains the castle keep, the Romanesque church of Santa Maria, the Domus Municipalis — a rare Romanesque civic building — and the pillory. The Iberian Museum of Mask and Costume reflects the region's winter-solstice festivals, in which masked figures called caretos parade through the villages.

Geography and economy

The municipality is mountainous and sparsely populated, with an economy based on agriculture — chestnuts, olives, wine and livestock — small industry, services and the Polytechnic Institute of Bragança, which draws many international students. The surrounding Montesinho park is one of the few Portuguese refuges of the Iberian wolf.
