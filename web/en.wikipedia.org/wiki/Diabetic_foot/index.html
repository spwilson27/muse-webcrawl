Diabetic foot

A diabetic foot disease is any condition that results directly from peripheral artery disease (PAD) or sensory neuropathy affecting the feet of people with diabetes mellitus. Common presentations include foot ulcers, infection, Charcot neuro-osteoarthropathy and gangrene. Diabetic foot complications are a leading cause of hospitalisation in diabetes and the most common cause of non-traumatic lower-limb amputation.

Pathophysiology

Peripheral sensory neuropathy removes protective sensation, so minor trauma goes unnoticed; motor neuropathy deforms the foot and concentrates pressure; autonomic neuropathy dries the skin. Combined with peripheral artery disease and impaired immunity, small wounds readily ulcerate and become infected, sometimes progressing to osteomyelitis.

Management

Care is multidisciplinary: glycaemic control, wound debridement and dressings, off-loading pressure (e.g. total contact casts), antibiotics for infection, revascularisation where feasible, and surgery as a last resort. The Wagner and University of Texas systems grade ulcer severity.

Prevention

Daily foot inspection, properly fitting shoes, regular professional foot examinations with monofilament testing for loss of protective sensation, and early treatment of callus and minor injuries substantially reduce ulceration and amputation rates. A person with diabetes has a lifetime risk of a foot ulcer of roughly 15–25%.
