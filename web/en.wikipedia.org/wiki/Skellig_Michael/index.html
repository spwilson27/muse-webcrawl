Skellig Michael

Skellig Michael (Irish: Sceilg Mhichíl, "Michael's rock"), also called Great Skellig, is a steep, rocky island in the Atlantic Ocean about 11.6 km (7.2 miles) off the Iveragh Peninsula in County Kerry, Ireland. It is the larger of the two Skellig Islands, the other being Little Skellig. A Christian monastery founded between the 6th and 8th centuries stands near its summit and is remarkably well preserved; it was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996.

Monastery

The monastery is reached by a steep flight of some 618 stone steps. It consists of a cluster of dry-stone beehive huts, the church of St Michael, a high altar, a monks' burial ground and oratories, all enclosed within terraces cut into the rock at about 180 metres above sea level. The monks endured several Viking raids in the 9th century. The community eventually relocated to the mainland at Ballinskelligs in the 12th century, though the island remained a place of pilgrimage.

A second, extremely precarious hermitage occupies ledges on the island's south peak; its construction and use remain remarkable feats of endurance.

Wildlife

Skellig Michael is internationally important for its seabirds. Together with Little Skellig it hosts one of the world's largest colonies of northern gannets (on Little Skellig) and large breeding populations of Manx shearwaters and European storm petrels. Puffins breed in burrows across the island during the summer months. The islands are a national nature reserve and a Special Protection Area for birds.

Access and popular culture

Visitor numbers are strictly limited to protect the fragile archaeology and wildlife; landings depend on weather and sea conditions, with boats running from Portmagee, Ballinskelligs and Cahersiveen when conditions permit. The island gained renewed global fame as the location of Luke Skywalker's hermitage in the films Star Wars: The Force Awakens (2015) and Star Wars: The Last Jedi (2017).
