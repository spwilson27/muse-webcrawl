Fighting game

A fighting game is a genre of video game that involves combat between characters, often in one-on-one matches within a confined arena. Fighting games typically feature hand-to-hand fighting in which players use a combination of button presses and directional inputs to attack, block, throw and perform special moves. The genre is distinguished from beat 'em ups, in which the player fights waves of weaker enemies.

History

Early examples include Sega's Heavyweight Champ (1976) and Capcom's Street Fighter (1987), but the genre was defined by Street Fighter II (1991), whose cast of distinct characters, special moves and competitive two-player play sparked an arcade boom. Midway's Mortal Kombat (1992) added digitized graphics and graphic finishing moves; SNK's The King of Fighters series popularized team play. The genre moved into 3D with Sega's Virtua Fighter (1993), followed by Tekken and Soulcalibur. Later crossover platform fighters such as Nintendo's Super Smash Bros. broadened the format.

Gameplay elements

Common systems include health bars, round timers, light/medium/heavy attacks, special and super moves performed with motion inputs, combos, blocking and throws, and resource meters. Subgenres include 2D "anime fighters", 3D arena fighters, and team-based "versus" games. Fighting games sustain a dedicated competitive community centred on tournaments such as EVO, and netcode quality — increasingly rollback-based — is a key modern concern for online play.

Terminology

The genre has a rich vocabulary: footsies for spacing-based neutral play, DP for an invincible rising uppercut (from "Dragon Punch"), oki for pressure on a rising opponent, and frame data for the start-up, active and recovery timings that govern interactions.
