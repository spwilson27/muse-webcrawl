The Second Bank of the United States was the second federally authorized Hamiltonian national bank in the United States, chartered for twenty years in 1816 by President James Madison. Located in Philadelphia, it functioned as a central bank, holding federal funds and regulating state banks.

History

Financial disorder during the War of 1812 prompted rechartering after the First Bank's charter lapsed in 1811. The Supreme Court upheld its constitutionality in McCulloch v. Maryland (1819). Under Langdon Cheves and then Nicholas Biddle, the Bank stabilized currency but its tight credit contributed to controversy after the Panic of 1819.

Bank War

President Andrew Jackson, distrusting the Bank as an unconstitutional monopoly favouring elites, vetoed its recharter bill in 1832 and removed federal deposits in 1833 — the "Bank War", a defining fight of the Second Party System. The charter expired in 1836; Biddle's successor state-chartered institution failed in the Panic of 1841.

Legacy

Its Greek Revival building, designed by William Strickland, survives as part of Independence National Historical Park and houses a portrait gallery.
