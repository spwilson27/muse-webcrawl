Dungeness is a headland on the coast of Kent, England, formed largely of a shingle beach in the form of a cuspate foreland. It shelters a large area of low-lying land, Romney Marsh. The headland is one of the largest expanses of shingle in Europe and is of international conservation importance for its geomorphology, plant and invertebrate communities and birdlife.

Geography and ecology

The ness is built from flint pebbles deposited by longshore drift over thousands of years. Its ridges and hollows support a distinctive shingle flora, and the surrounding gravel pits and wetlands make it a noted birdwatching area; the RSPB maintains a reserve here. Because of its unusual landscape, Dungeness has sometimes been described as Britain's only desert, though the Met Office has not classified it as such.

Settlements and structures

The small community consists largely of scattered houses, many converted from old railway carriages and fishing huts. The headland hosts two lighthouses (the old lighthouse of 1904, open to visitors, and its modern replacement), a lifeboat station, and Prospect Cottage, the former home of the artist and film-maker Derek Jarman, famous for its shingle garden.

Power stations

Dungeness is the site of two nuclear power stations: Dungeness A, a Magnox station that closed in 2006, and Dungeness B, an advanced gas-cooled reactor station that ended generation in 2021 and moved into defuelling. The headland is also a landfall for electricity interconnectors.
