The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (Māori: Te Pūtea Matua) is the central bank of New Zealand. Established in 1934, it is responsible for monetary policy, issuing currency, and promoting a sound and efficient financial system.

Monetary policy

Under the Reserve Bank of New Zealand Act 1989, the Bank was given operational independence to target price stability — a pioneering arrangement that made New Zealand the first country to adopt formal inflation targeting. The Governor sets the Official Cash Rate (OCR) to keep consumer price inflation near the target band agreed with the Minister of Finance. A 2021 remit added maximum sustainable employment as a consideration alongside price stability.

Other functions

The Bank issues New Zealand's banknotes and coins, registers and supervises banks and other financial institutions, operates payment systems, and manages the country's foreign reserves.
