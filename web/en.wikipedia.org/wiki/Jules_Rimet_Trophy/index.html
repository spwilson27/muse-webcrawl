Jules Rimet Trophy

The Jules Rimet Trophy was the original prize for winning the FIFA World Cup, awarded from 1930 to 1970. Named after FIFA president Jules Rimet, it depicted Nike, the Greek goddess of victory, holding an octagonal cup. Designed by French sculptor Abel Lafleur, it was made of gold-plated sterling silver on a lapis lazuli base.

History

FIFA decided the trophy would be permanently awarded to the first team to win three World Cups; Brazil achieved this in 1970. The trophy survived the Second World War hidden in a shoebox under the bed of FIFA vice-president Ottorino Barassi. It was stolen in London in 1966 ahead of the World Cup and found a week later by a dog named Pickles. In 1983 the trophy was stolen again in Rio de Janeiro and never recovered; it is believed to have been melted down. Brazil received a replica.
