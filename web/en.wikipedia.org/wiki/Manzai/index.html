Manzai

Manzai (Japanese: 漫才) is a traditional style of Japanese stand-up comedy performed by a duo. It features two performers — the boke (funny man, who makes foolish statements and mistakes) and the tsukkomi (straight man, who corrects and reacts, traditionally with a sharp slap of a paper fan or hand) — trading rapid-fire jokes at high speed.

History

Manzai derives from Heian-period New Year entertainments in which paired performers brought blessings to households. It developed in the Osaka region during the Edo and Meiji periods and was transformed in the 1930s by the Yoshimoto Kogyo entertainment company, which removed music and dance elements and centred the act on pure comic dialogue, creating the modern shabekuri manzai (talking manzai).

Performance

Duos typically stand before a single microphone, the boke delivering a stream of misunderstandings, puns, and absurd claims which the tsukkomi punctures with incredulous corrections. The humour depends on the Kansai dialect, timing, and the interplay between the two roles. Successful acts often graduate to television hosting and variety work.

M-1 Grand Prix

The M-1 Grand Prix, founded in 2001, is Japan's most prominent manzai competition, launching the careers of famous duos such as Sandwichman and drawing huge television audiences each December.
