The Odeon Luxe Leicester Square is a cinema in Leicester Square in the West End of London. Opened in 1937 as the flagship of Oscar Deutsch's Odeon chain, its black granite Art Deco tower is a landmark of the square, and the cinema is one of the principal venues for film premieres in the United Kingdom, hosting numerous Royal Film Performances.

History

The cinema was built on the site of the Alhambra Theatre and was designed by Harry Weedon and Andrew Mather in the Art Deco style, with a distinctive tower faced in black polished granite rising about 120 feet. It was among the largest cinemas in Britain, originally seating over 2,000. The building was damaged by bombing during the Second World War but was restored.

Refurbishment

After a major refurbishment completed in 2018 under the Odeon Luxe brand, seating was reduced in favour of reclining seats, and the main auditorium was equipped with Dolby Cinema technology, combining Dolby Vision projection with Dolby Atmos sound. The cinema retains a Compton organ, played on special occasions.

Premieres

Leicester Square's cinemas, with the Odeon chief among them, host most major London film premieres, with red-carpet barriers erected around the square's gardens. The Odeon hosted the first Royal Film Performance in its current era and numerous world premieres of major franchises.
