Bisection

Bisection
MeaningDivision into two equal or congruent parts
ApplicationsGeometry, numerical root-finding, computer science

Bisection is the division of something into two equal or congruent parts, usually by a line, which is then called a bisector. The most frequently described bisections are the segment bisector (a line or ray that passes through the midpoint of a segment) and the angle bisector (a ray that divides an angle into two equal angles).

In geometry

Classical constructions with compass and straightedge can bisect any segment or angle. The perpendicular bisector of a segment is the locus of points equidistant from its endpoints, a fact used in defining Voronoi diagrams and circumcenters of triangles.

In numerical methods

The bisection method is a root-finding algorithm that repeatedly halves an interval in which a continuous function changes sign, guaranteed by the intermediate value theorem to contain a root. It converges linearly but reliably, halving the error with each iteration. A similar halving strategy underlies binary search in sorted data and the debugging technique of bisecting a revision history to locate the change that introduced a defect.
