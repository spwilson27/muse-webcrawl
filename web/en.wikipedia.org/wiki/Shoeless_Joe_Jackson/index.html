Shoeless Joe Jackson

Joseph Jefferson Jackson (July 16, 1887 – December 5, 1951), nicknamed "Shoeless Joe", was an American professional baseball outfielder who played in Major League Baseball in the early 20th century. His .356 career batting average is the fourth highest in major league history. Jackson is remembered for his performance on the field and for his association with the Black Sox Scandal, in which members of the 1919 Chicago White Sox participated in a conspiracy to fix the World Series.

Career

Jackson, who grew up in mill towns in South Carolina and was functionally illiterate, debuted with the Philadelphia Athletics in 1908 and starred with the Cleveland Naps/Indians from 1910 to 1915, batting .408 in 1911. Traded to Chicago in 1915, he helped the White Sox win the 1917 World Series.

Black Sox Scandal

Jackson accepted $5,000 from gamblers to throw the 1919 World Series against Cincinnati, though he batted .375 in the Series and hit its only home run. A jury acquitted him and seven teammates in 1921, but Commissioner Kenesaw Mountain Landis banned all eight from professional baseball for life the next day. Jackson maintained his innocence for the rest of his life. The ban also made him ineligible for the Baseball Hall of Fame for decades; in 2025, Commissioner Rob Manfred lifted the permanent ineligibility of deceased players, making Jackson eligible for Hall of Fame consideration.

Nickname and legacy

The nickname arose in his minor league days in Greenville, South Carolina, when blisters from new spikes led him to bat in his stocking feet during one game. Jackson's natural swing was said to be the model for Babe Ruth's. He is a central figure in the film Field of Dreams (1989) and the novel Shoeless Joe.
