iWork

iWork is an office suite of applications created by Apple Inc. for its macOS, iOS, and iPadOS operating systems, and also available cross-platform through the iCloud website. It includes the word processor Pages, the spreadsheet application Numbers, and the presentation program Keynote.

History

The suite began with Keynote, first released in 2003 as a presentation tool developed at Steve Jobs's request for his own keynote addresses. Pages followed in 2005 with the launch of iWork '05, and Numbers was added in iWork '08 in 2007, giving the suite a spreadsheet component to complement the word processor and presentation software.

Apple positioned iWork as a lighter, more design-focused alternative to Microsoft Office, emphasizing templates, visual polish, and ease of use over exhaustive feature lists. In 2013, redesigned versions of the applications were released alongside OS X Mavericks, and the apps were made free with new Mac and iOS devices. Real-time collaboration features, including simultaneous editing through iCloud, were progressively added.

Applications

Pages is a word processor with page-layout capabilities, offering templates for documents, newsletters, resumes, and reports. Numbers is a spreadsheet application organized around free-form canvases on which tables, charts, and images can be placed, rather than a single infinite grid. Keynote is a presentation application known for its transitions and animations, including the "Magic Move" transition.

File formats and compatibility

The iWork applications use their own file formats (.pages, .numbers, and .key files) but can import and export Microsoft Office formats and PDF. Documents can also be shared via iCloud links for collaborative editing in a web browser.
