The Yugo is a subcompact hatchback built by Zastava Automobiles in Kragujevac, Yugoslavia (later Serbia), from 1980 to 2008. Based on Fiat-derived mechanicals, it became internationally famous — and infamous — as the cheapest new car sold in the United States in the mid-1980s.

History

The Yugo (sold at home as the Zastava Koral and in some markets as the Innocenti Koral) was derived from the Fiat 127 platform with Fiat 128 engines. Exported to the US from 1985 by Malcolm Bricklin's Yugo America, it was launched at $3,990 — the lowest price for any new car — and initially sold strongly before chronic quality and reliability problems destroyed its reputation.

Reception and legacy

The Yugo became a cultural byword for poor build quality and the butt of countless jokes, though later assessments note it was unremarkable rather than uniquely bad for its era and price. Production in Serbia, renamed Zastava Koral, survived the Yugoslav wars and sanctions and continued until 2008, with total production in the hundreds of thousands.
