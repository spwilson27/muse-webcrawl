Eskimo–Aleut languages
Region: Alaska, Canadian Arctic, Greenland, Chukotka
Branches: Aleut; Eskimo (Yupik, Inuit)
Speakers: c. 150,000

The Eskimo–Aleut languages (also called Eskaleut) are a language family native to Alaska, the Canadian Arctic, Greenland and the Chukotka Peninsula of Russia. The family comprises the Aleut language of the Aleutian Islands and the Eskimo branch, which divides into the Yupik languages of Alaska and Siberia and the Inuit language continuum stretching from Alaska across northern Canada to Greenland.

Characteristics

These languages are highly polysynthetic: complex words are built from a root plus many suffixes, expressing in a single word what English would say in a full sentence. They are ergative in alignment. Many varieties are endangered, though Greenlandic (Kalaallisut) is official in Greenland and Inuktitut has official status in parts of Canada.
