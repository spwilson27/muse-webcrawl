Mahout

Mahout
OccupationElephant keeper, trainer and rider
RegionsSouth and Southeast Asia

A mahout is an elephant rider, trainer, or keeper. The word derives from the Hindi mahāut and ultimately from the Sanskrit mahāmātra, meaning "great measure" or high official. Mahouts have worked with elephants in South and Southeast Asia for thousands of years, in warfare, logging, ceremony, and transport.

The relationship

Traditionally, a young mahout is assigned a young elephant early in both of their lives, and the pair may remain together for decades, forming a close working bond. The mahout feeds, bathes, and cares for his elephant and directs it with a combination of verbal commands, pressure from the legs and feet behind the ears, and a guiding tool such as the ankus (goad).

Terminology

In Thailand a mahout is called a kwan chang; in Myanmar, u zi; in Sri Lanka the handlers are often termed kurawanayaka. Historically, mahout families and communities formed hereditary occupational groups in India and elsewhere.

Modern practice

As working elephants have declined in number with mechanisation and logging bans, many mahouts have shifted to tourism, conservation, and sanctuary work. The training and handling methods of traditional mahouts — particularly the breaking-in process known in Thailand as phajaan — have attracted sustained animal-welfare criticism, prompting some camps and sanctuaries to adopt positive-reinforcement methods instead.
