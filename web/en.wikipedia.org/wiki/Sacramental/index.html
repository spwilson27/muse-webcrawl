Sacramental

Sacramentals
TraditionsRoman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican, Lutheran and others
ExamplesHoly water, blessings, rosaries, ashes, palms, medals, exorcisms
Distinguished fromSacraments

Sacramentals are sacred signs, objects, actions and blessings instituted by the Church which resemble the sacraments and signify spiritual effects obtained through the Church's intercession. In Roman Catholic teaching they are distinguished from the seven sacraments, which were instituted by Christ and confer grace in themselves; sacramentals prepare people to receive grace and dispose them to cooperate with it.

Holy water is among the most familiar sacramentals.

Types

Sacramentals include blessings of persons, places and objects (such as the blessing of homes, fields, throats on the feast of St Blaise, and religious articles); consecrations and dedications; exorcisms; and blessed objects used devotionally, such as holy water, blessed ashes and palms, rosaries, scapulars, medals and candles. The Catechism of the Catholic Church treats them as part of the Church's liturgical life, drawing the faithful into prayer through material signs.

Other traditions

Conceptually similar practices exist in Eastern Orthodoxy (such as the Great Blessing of Waters), and many Anglican and Lutheran communities retain blessings and blessed objects, although Reformed traditions generally restrict or reject the category. The keeping and use of sacramentals has been a longstanding feature of popular Catholic devotion.
