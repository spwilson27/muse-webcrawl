Airbus A320

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: An Airbus A320 in generic airline livery climbing after takeoff]
An A320 climbing out.

The Airbus A320 family is a series of narrow-body jet airliners developed and produced by Airbus, comprising the A318, A319, A320, A321 and the re-engined A320neo variants (including the long-range A321XLR). Launched in 1984 and first flown in 1987, the A320 pioneered digital fly-by-wire controls and side-stick controllers in airliners, and its family has rivalled — and by deliveries in the mid-2020s, surpassed — the Boeing 737 as the best-selling jet airliner line.

Innovation

Airbus bet on fly-by-wire with envelope protection, a common cockpit across types, and containerised cargo holds. The 1988 Habsheim airshow crash of an early A320 (Air France 296) became a famous controversy over automation, though the type matured into one of the safest and most popular airliners ever, with family commonality later extended to the A330/A350.

neo generation

The A320neo (new engine option — CFM LEAP or Pratt & Whitney GTF) cut fuel burn by about 15–20% and forced Boeing\'s 737 MAX response. The stretched A321neo dominates new orders; its XLR variant (extra fuel tank, range about 4,700 nmi) entered service in 2024, opening thin long-haul routes to single-aisle economics.

Scale

Final assembly lines run in Toulouse, Hamburg, Mobile (Alabama) and Tianjin; monthly production rates in the 60s are among the industry\'s highest. The family passed 12,000 deliveries in the mid-2020s.
