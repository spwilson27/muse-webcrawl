Yunus (surah)

Chapter: 10 of the Quran
Verses: 109
Revelation: Meccan

Yunus (Arabic: يونس, Yūnus), also known in English as Jonah, is the tenth chapter (surah) of the Quran, containing 109 verses (ayat). It is named after the prophet Yunus (Jonah), whose story is retold in the chapter, including the repentance of the people of Nineveh, presented as a rare example of a community that believed in time and was spared punishment.

Summary

The surah is generally classified as Meccan, revealed before the Hijra. Its main themes are the oneness of God, the truth of revelation, and the fate of earlier peoples who rejected their prophets. It recounts episodes from the stories of Noah, Moses and Jonah, argues against the association of partners with God, and addresses the Prophet Muhammad's critics, affirming that the Quran could not have been invented by him. The chapter contains the well-known verse that there is no compulsion in the matter of belief posed as a rhetorical question: "Had your Lord willed, all those on earth would have believed together — will you then compel people to become believers?"

The story of Jonah

Unlike the biblical account's focus on the great fish, the Quranic telling emphasises the faith of Jonah's people, who believed and were granted respite. Jonah's own prayer from darkness, "There is no god but You; glory be to You; indeed I was among the wrongdoers", is a widely used supplication in Muslim devotion.
