Facilitation (business)

In business, and in education generally, facilitation is the process of designing and running meetings, workshops, and group processes so that a group can achieve its objectives effectively. A facilitator is a neutral process guide who manages how the group works — agenda, participation, time, and decision methods — without contributing content or taking sides in the outcome.

Role

The facilitator helps the group clarify goals, establishes ground rules, encourages balanced participation, surfaces and manages conflict, keeps discussion on track, and records agreements and action items. This distinguishes the facilitator from the chair, who owns the meeting's content, and from a trainer, whose role is to teach.

Techniques

Common methods include brainstorming, nominal group technique, World Café, Open Space Technology, affinity grouping, dot voting, and structured consensus-building. Facilitation is applied to strategic planning, team building, retrospectives, design sprints, stakeholder consultations, and community engagement.

Professional bodies

The International Association of Facilitators (IAF), founded in 1994, certifies professional facilitators and promotes competencies and ethics for the field.
