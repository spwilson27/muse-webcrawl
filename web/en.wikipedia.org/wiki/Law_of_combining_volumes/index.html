Law of combining volumes

The law of combining volumes, also called Gay-Lussac's law of combining volumes, states that when gases react together at constant temperature and pressure, the volumes of the reacting gases and of any gaseous products bear a simple whole-number ratio to one another.

Two volumes of hydrogen combine with one volume of oxygen to give two volumes of water vapour.

History

Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac announced the law in 1808, based on his experiments with gases such as hydrogen and oxygen, which combine in a 2:1 volume ratio to form water. The result initially appeared to conflict with Dalton's atomic theory, since it implied that equal volumes of gases under the same conditions contain equal numbers of particles — a conclusion formalised by Amedeo Avogadro in 1811 as Avogadro's law, using the hypothesis that elementary gases such as oxygen consist of diatomic molecules.

Significance

The law was a crucial step in establishing relative atomic weights and molecular formulas, and it remains a foundation of gas stoichiometry in chemistry.
