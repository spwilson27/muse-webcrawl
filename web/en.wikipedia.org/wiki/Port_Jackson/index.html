Port Jackson, containing Sydney Harbour, is an inlet of the Tasman Sea (part of the South Pacific Ocean) and the natural harbour of Sydney, New South Wales, Australia. It is a ria — a drowned river valley — extending some 19 kilometres westward from its entrance between North Head and South Head to its meeting with the Parramatta River near Gladesville. Port Jackson comprises the waters of Sydney Harbour proper together with Middle Harbour and North Harbour (branches to the north), Lane Cove River, and the Parramatta River system. It is the site of the Sydney Opera House and the Sydney Harbour Bridge, two of Australia's most recognisable landmarks.

History

The harbour's shores were inhabited for thousands of years by Aboriginal peoples, including the Gadigal of the Eora Nation; the region preserves numerous rock engravings, shell middens, and significant sites. Lieutenant James Cook sighted and named Port Jackson in 1770, honouring Sir George Jackson, a Judge Advocate of the Fleet, though he did not enter it. In January 1788 Captain Arthur Phillip, arriving with the First Fleet, entered the harbour after finding Botany Bay unsuitable and chose Sydney Cove — with its deep water close to shore and a freshwater stream — as the site of the first European settlement in Australia, founded on 26 January 1788.

Geography and ecology

The harbour's convoluted shoreline, carved into Hawkesbury sandstone, totals roughly 317 kilometres and encloses about 55 square kilometres of water, with some 21,000 megalitres flowing through it at average tides. Numerous bays, coves, beaches, and islands — including Fort Denison, Goat Island, Cockatoo Island, and Shark Island — dot the waterway. Despite more than two centuries of urbanisation, the harbour supports surprisingly rich marine life, with more than 600 recorded marine species, seagrass meadows, and resident populations of penguins and seals in places. National parklands protect substantial stretches of the foreshore, including Sydney Harbour National Park.

Use and landmarks

Port Jackson is a working port and the principal waterway of Australia's largest city. Ferries of the Sydney Ferries network converge on Circular Quay, cruise ships berth at the Overseas Passenger Terminal, and the Royal Australian Navy maintains its main eastern base at Garden Island. The harbour hosts the start of the Sydney to Hobart Yacht Race each Boxing Day and the city's New Year's Eve fireworks. Two harbour crossings dominate the skyline: the Sydney Harbour Bridge (opened 1932) and, at the harbour's mouth, the Heads marking the Pacific beyond.
