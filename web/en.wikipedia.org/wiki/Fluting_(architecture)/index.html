Fluting (architecture)

Fluting in architecture and decorative arts is a series of shallow vertical grooves, most commonly carved into the shaft of a classical column or pilaster.

Classical orders

Doric columns characteristically have 20 flutes meeting at sharp arrises, while Ionic and Corinthian columns usually have 24 flutes separated by flat fillets.

Fluting emphasises verticality, plays with light and shadow, and may derive from the appearance of bundled reeds or wooden posts in early construction.

Other uses

Flutes also appear on entablatures, pottery, silverware, and furniture, and horizontal fluting occurs in some modern architecture.
