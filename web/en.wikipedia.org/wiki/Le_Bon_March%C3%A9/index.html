Le Bon Marché

Founded1838, Paris, France
FounderAristide Boucicaut (developer of the modern store)
Location7th arrondissement, Paris
OwnerLVMH

Le Bon Marché is a department store in Paris, France, generally regarded as the first modern department store in the world. Founded in 1838, it was transformed from the 1850s under Aristide Boucicaut into a new kind of retail institution.

Innovations

Boucicaut introduced practices that became standard in retail: fixed prices with price tags instead of haggling, free entry and browsing without obligation to buy, low margins with high turnover, seasonal sales, returns and refunds, and extensive advertising and catalogues. The store's expanding building on the Rue de Sèvres, partly engineered with the involvement of Gustave Eiffel's firm, pioneered the grand department-store interior.

Cultural legacy

Le Bon Marché inspired Émile Zola's novel Au Bonheur des Dames (1883), a celebrated depiction of the new consumer culture. The store is now owned by LVMH and trades as a luxury department store, accompanied by its food hall, La Grande Épicerie de Paris.
