Vladimiro Lenin Ilich Montesinos Torres (born 20 May 1945) is a Peruvian former intelligence officer who served as the long-standing head of Peru's National Intelligence Service (SIN) and as the closest adviser and de facto power broker of President Alberto Fujimori (1990–2000). His secret videotapes of bribery — the "Vladivideos" — triggered the collapse of the Fujimori government. He is serving multiple prison sentences for corruption, arms trafficking, embezzlement, and human rights abuses.

Early life and army career

Montesinos was born in Arequipa into a left-leaning family; his given names honor Vladimir Lenin. He graduated as an artillery officer from the Chorrillos Military School in 1966 and served as an aide to senior generals. In 1976 he was accused of espionage — traveling to the United States without authorization and passing documents — and was expelled from the army and briefly imprisoned. He then studied law at the National University of San Marcos and built a practice defending drug traffickers and officials accused of corruption, earning the nickname "the Rasputin of the Andes" in later accounts.

Rise under Fujimori

Montesinos met Alberto Fujimori during the 1990 presidential campaign, reportedly helping him with legal vulnerabilities. After Fujimori's victory he became the president's chief adviser and the operational head of the SIN, technically serving as an adviser while directing intelligence, military promotions, judicial appointments, and media manipulation. From the SIN headquarters he built a vast network of control, secretly recording thousands of meetings in which he bribed congressmen, judges, generals, and media owners with cash.

Downfall

On 14 September 2000, a leaked tape showing Montesinos bribing opposition congressman Alberto Kouri to switch parties was broadcast on television, detonating a national scandal. Montesinos fled to Panama and then Venezuela, where he was arrested in June 2001 and extradited to Peru. Fujimori resigned by fax from Japan in November 2000.

Trials and imprisonment

Montesinos has faced dozens of trials and holds the record for the most prosecutions of a former Peruvian official; he has been convicted in numerous cases, receiving sentences of up to 25 years, including for the illegal sale of 10,000 AK-47 rifles to Colombian FARC rebels and for his role in death-squad operations attributed to the Grupo Colina. He is imprisoned at the maximum-security prison at the Callao Naval Base.
