Mains electricity by country

Mains electricity by country surveys the voltage, frequency, and plug types used for general-purpose alternating current power supply around the world. Globally, most countries use either a nominal voltage in the range of 220–240 volts at 50 hertz, or 100–127 volts at 60 hertz.

Overview

The two broad standards reflect early electrification history: North America standardized on roughly 120 V at 60 Hz, while Europe adopted 220–240 V at 50 Hz, and much of the world followed one of these patterns through trade and colonization. Higher voltages reduce transmission losses in household wiring for a given power, while the North American system historically benefited from early standardization by the Edison and Westinghouse companies.

Examples: the United States and Canada use 120 V / 60 Hz with Type A and B plugs; the United Kingdom uses 230 V / 50 Hz with Type G plugs; most of continental Europe uses 230 V / 50 Hz with Type C, E, or F plugs; Japan uses 100 V, uniquely split between 50 Hz in the east and 60 Hz in the west; Australia and New Zealand use 230 V / 50 Hz with Type I plugs; India uses 230 V / 50 Hz; China uses 220 V / 50 Hz.

Travel considerations

Travelers may require plug adapters to connect devices abroad, and voltage converters when a device is not rated for the local supply. Most modern portable electronics use switching power supplies that accept 100–240 V at 50/60 Hz and need only a physical adapter.
