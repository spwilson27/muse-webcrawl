Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long

The Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long (Vietnamese: Hoàng thành Thăng Long) is a complex of historic buildings in the centre of Hanoi, Vietnam. It was the political centre of Vietnamese dynasties for roughly thirteen centuries, built on the remains of a Chinese fortress dating from the 7th century, on drained land reclaimed from the Red River Delta.

History

The site was first fortified during the period of Chinese domination. In 1010, King Lý Thái Tổ moved the capital of Đại Việt to the site, naming it Thăng Long ("Ascending Dragon"), and successive Lý, Trần, Lê, and Nguyễn dynasty rulers built palaces there. Much of the complex was demolished during the French colonial period, when new military buildings were erected on the grounds.

Surviving structures

Remaining structures include the Flag Tower of Hanoi, the Đoan Môn (Main Gate), the steps of Kính Thiên Palace, the Hậu Lâu (Princess Pavilion), and the Bắc Môn (North Gate). Excavations since 2002 have uncovered extensive foundations and artefacts. The central sector was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2010, the millennial anniversary of Hanoi's founding.
