MINUSCA (French: Mission multidimensionnelle intégrée des Nations unies pour la stabilisation en Centrafrique) is the United Nations peacekeeping mission in the Central African Republic. It was established by Security Council Resolution 2149 on 10 April 2014, and formally took over authority from the African Union-led MISCA force on 15 September 2014.

Mandate

The mission's priorities are the protection of civilians, support for the political process and peace agreements, facilitation of humanitarian assistance, promotion of human rights, and support for the extension of state authority, disarmament and security-sector reform. Its mandate has been renewed annually by the Security Council, with authorised strength rising to more than 14,000 military and 3,000 police personnel, in addition to a substantial civilian component.

Context

MINUSCA deployed amid the civil conflict that began in 2012–2013 with the Séléka rebellion and subsequent anti-Balaka violence. It supported the 2015–2016 elections and the 2019 Khartoum peace agreement between the government and armed groups. The security situation deteriorated again after the disputed 2020 elections, when a coalition of armed groups advanced on Bangui and the government was supported by bilateral forces from Rwanda and Russian instructors.

Operations and controversies

The mission has conducted operations alongside the Central African armed forces and runs programmes in disarmament, demobilisation and reintegration. Like several UN missions, MINUSCA has faced allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse by peacekeepers, prompting investigations and repatriations, as well as criticism from the host government, which at times has pressed for the mission's reduction.
