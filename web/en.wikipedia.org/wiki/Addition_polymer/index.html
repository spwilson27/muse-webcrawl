Addition polymer

An addition polymer is a polymer formed by an addition reaction between monomers containing double or triple bonds, without the loss of any small molecules. This contrasts with condensation polymers, whose formation releases water or other by-products. Common addition polymers include polyethylene, polypropylene, polystyrene, and polyvinyl chloride (PVC), all formed from alkene monomers.

Mechanism

Addition polymerization proceeds by chain growth: an active center — a free radical, cation, or anion — adds monomer units one at a time, regenerating the active site at the growing chain end. The process consists of initiation, propagation, and termination steps. Because no atoms are lost, the repeat unit of an addition polymer has the same composition as the monomer.

Examples

Polyethylene from ethene, poly(methyl methacrylate) (acrylic glass), polytetrafluoroethylene (Teflon), and synthetic rubbers such as polybutadiene are all addition polymers of major industrial importance.
