The Siege of Osaka (大坂の陣, Ōsaka no jin) was a series of battles undertaken by the Tokugawa shogunate against the Toyotomi clan, ending in the clan's destruction, the fall of Osaka Castle and the suicide of Toyotomi Hideyori. Divided into two campaigns—the Winter Campaign of 1614–15 and the Summer Campaign of 1615—it was the last major armed opposition to the shogunate and confirmed the Tokugawa grip on Japan for more than two and a half centuries.

Background

After the death of Toyotomi Hideyoshi in 1598 and the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600, power passed to Tokugawa Ieyasu, who became shōgun in 1603 but left Hideyoshi's heir Hideyori in Osaka Castle with a vast treasury. The castle became a rallying point for masterless samurai (rōnin) disaffected by the new order. In 1614 a pretext arose over an inscription on a temple bell at Hōkō-ji in Kyoto, which the shogunate construed as a curse on Ieyasu. Ieyasu demanded concessions; negotiations failed, and in November 1614 Tokugawa armies converged on Osaka.

Winter campaign

The Toyotomi forces, swollen by rōnin, declined battle in the open and defended the strongest castle in Japan. Tokugawa attacks in December and January were repulsed, notably at the Sanada-maru, an outwork commanded by Sanada Yukimura, whose men twice defeated shogunate troops. Artillery bombardment—among the cannon cast for the shogunate by the English pilot William Adams's circle—annoyed but could not break the defences. A truce in January 1615 ended the winter campaign; its terms, perfidiously enforced, allowed the Tokugawa to fill in the castle's outer moats, gravely weakening it.

Summer campaign

Fighting resumed in April 1615. With the moats gone, the Toyotomi gambled on open battle. Their armies were defeated in a series of engagements, culminating in the Battle of Tennōji on 3 June 1615, the last major samurai battle in Japanese history. Sanada Yukimura charged through to Ieyasu's headquarters before being cut down, exhausted; Gotō Mototsugu died at Dōmyōji. Osaka Castle was stormed and burned; Hideyori and his mother Yodo-dono committed suicide in a burning storehouse. Hideyori's young son Kunimatsu was captured and executed, extinguishing the Toyotomi line.

Aftermath

The shogunate rebuilt Osaka Castle on a far grander scale and tightened its control over the imperial court and the daimyō, issuing the laws for military households (buke shohatto) in 1615. No comparable challenge to Tokugawa rule arose until the 1860s.
