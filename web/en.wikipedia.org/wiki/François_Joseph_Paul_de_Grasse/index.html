François Joseph Paul, marquis de Grasse Tilly, comte de Grasse (13 September 1722 – 11 January 1788), known as Admiral de Grasse, was a French naval commander whose victory over the British fleet at the Battle of the Chesapeake in September 1781 made possible the Franco-American triumph at the Siege of Yorktown and thereby secured the independence of the United States. The following year he was defeated and captured by Admiral Rodney at the Battle of the Saintes.

Early career

De Grasse was born at Le Bar-sur-Loup in Provence. At eleven he entered the service of the Order of Saint John (the Knights of Malta) and in 1734 joined the French Navy as a midshipman. He fought in the War of the Austrian Succession and the Seven Years' War, gaining experience in convoy and fleet operations in the Atlantic and the Mediterranean.

American Revolutionary War

Promoted to chef d'escadre, de Grasse commanded a squadron at the Battle of Ushant in 1778 and served under the comte d'Estaing in the Caribbean and at the Siege of Savannah. In 1781 he was given command of the main French fleet in the West Indies. Sailing north with 28 ships of the line, he defeated a British fleet under Admiral Graves at the mouth of the Chesapeake Bay on 5 September 1781, forcing it to withdraw to New York. This left Cornwallis's army at Yorktown cut off from relief or evacuation by sea, while de Grasse's ships landed French troops and siege guns. Cornwallis surrendered on 19 October 1781 — the decisive event of the war.

Battle of the Saintes and later life

Returning to the Caribbean, de Grasse planned a joint Franco-Spanish invasion of Jamaica. On 12 April 1782 his fleet was intercepted by Admiral George Rodney near the Îles des Saintes. The British broke the French line, captured five ships including de Grasse's flagship Ville de Paris, and took de Grasse prisoner. Released after the peace of 1783, he was tried by a court martial in France and acquitted of serious blame, though his reputation suffered. He died in Paris in 1788. Several ships of the United States Navy have been named USS De Grasse in his honour.
