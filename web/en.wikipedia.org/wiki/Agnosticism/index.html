Agnosticism

Agnosticism is the view that the existence of God, or of the divine and supernatural generally, is unknown or unknowable. The word was coined by the biologist Thomas Henry Huxley in 1869 for a method as much as a creed: follow reason as far as it goes and do not pretend conclusions are certain when they are not demonstrated. In contemporary taxonomy (popularised by Dawkins) belief and knowledge are separated: one may be an agnostic atheist (no belief, no claim to know) or an agnostic theist.

Forms

Weak agnosticism suspends judgement for want of evidence; strong agnosticism holds the question unanswerable in principle; agnostic theism and 'apatheism' mark further positions. As a self-description in surveys, agnosticism has grown steadily in Western countries alongside the religiously unaffiliated 'nones'.
