Ghats in Varanasi

LocationVaranasi, Uttar Pradesh, India
RiverGanges
Number of ghatsc. 84

The ghats in Varanasi are a series of some 84 stepped embankments along the crescent-shaped bank of the river Ganges in Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh, India. Most were built or rebuilt after 1700 under Maratha patronage, and serve for ritual bathing and Hindu ceremonies; two are devoted to cremation.

Notable ghats

Dashashwamedh Ghat, near the Kashi Vishwanath Temple, is the most famous and the site of the nightly Ganga Aarti fire ceremony. Manikarnika Ghat and Harishchandra Ghat are the two burning ghats, where cremations proceed around the clock; Hindu tradition holds that dying in Varanasi grants liberation (moksha) from rebirth. Other significant ghats include Assi, Panchganga, Man Mandir, Lalita and Scindia.

Religious significance

For pilgrims, a dawn boat ride along the ghats and ritual bathing in the Ganges are central acts of worship. The ghats also host major festivals such as Dev Deepawali, when the steps are lit with thousands of earthen lamps in honour of the gods' descent to earth.

Conservation

Pollution of the Ganges at Varanasi has long been a concern; government programmes and local trusts have worked on sewage interception and ghat restoration, including walkway projects linking the ghats into a continuous riverfront promenade.
