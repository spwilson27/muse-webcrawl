NGC 4874

Constellation: Coma Berenices
Type: cD; E0
Distance: ~350 million light-years
Group: Coma Cluster
Discoverer: William Herschel, 1785

NGC 4874 is a supergiant elliptical galaxy in the constellation Coma Berenices. Discovered by William Herschel in 1785, it is one of the two dominant central galaxies of the Coma Cluster of galaxies, along with NGC 4889, and lies about 350 million light-years from Earth.

Characteristics

As a cD galaxy, NGC 4874 possesses an enormous diffuse stellar halo extending far beyond its visible core, formed by the accretion of smaller galaxies. It is surrounded by an exceptionally rich system of globular clusters — some 30,000 of them — far more than the Milky Way. Hubble Space Telescope observations have shown that some of these "clusters" are actually ultra-compact dwarf galaxies, the stripped cores of galaxies absorbed by NGC 4874.

Central black hole

Like other giant ellipticals, NGC 4874 hosts a supermassive black hole at its centre, with a mass measured in the billions of Suns. The galaxy is also a source of radio emission and X-rays from hot gas in its halo.
