UDP-glucose 4-epimerase
EC number5.1.3.2
Gene (human)GALE (chromosome 1)
Systematic nameUDP-glucose 4-epimerase
ReactionUDP-glucose ⇌ UDP-galactose

UDP-glucose 4-epimerase (EC 5.1.3.2), also known as UDP-galactose 4-epimerase or GALE, is a homodimeric epimerase found in many species. It is responsible for the reversible conversion of UDP-galactose to UDP-glucose, the final step of the Leloir pathway of galactose metabolism. The enzyme also acts on UDP-N-acetylglucosamine and UDP-N-acetylgalactosamine.

Function

The enzyme catalyzes the interconversion of UDP-glucose and UDP-galactose by transiently oxidizing the 4-hydroxyl group of the sugar using a tightly bound NAD+ cofactor, forming a 4-ketose intermediate, and then reducing it from the opposite face to invert the stereochemistry at carbon 4. Because the reaction is freely reversible, it allows cells to produce UDP-galactose for the synthesis of glycoproteins, glycolipids, and lactose even when no dietary galactose is available.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the human GALE gene cause epimerase deficiency galactosemia (galactosemia type III), an autosomal recessive disorder. The disease exists on a spectrum from a benign peripheral form, confined to red and white blood cells, to a severe generalized form whose symptoms — including jaundice, vomiting, failure to thrive, liver disease, and developmental delay — resemble those of classic galactosemia. Management involves dietary restriction of galactose.

Structure

The enzyme belongs to the short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase (SDR) superfamily. Each subunit binds one molecule of NAD+ and contains an N-terminal Rossmann-fold domain and a C-terminal substrate-binding domain. Bacterial homologs, such as the well-studied enzyme from Escherichia coli, have served as model systems for understanding the catalytic mechanism.
