Scene (drama)

A scene in performance

In drama, a scene is a unit of action within a play, traditionally defined as a continuous portion of the action occurring in a single place and period of time, usually marked by the entrance or exit of a character. Scenes are grouped into larger structural units called acts.

Definitions

Two main conventions define the scene. In the French convention (the scène of classical French theatre), a new scene begins whenever a character enters or leaves the stage, so a single act may contain many short scenes. In the convention common in English-language theatre, a scene is a longer unit defined by a change of location or a significant lapse of time.

History

The division of plays into acts and scenes was systematised by editors of classical texts and Renaissance commentators, influenced by Horace's dictum that a play should have five acts. Shakespeare's plays, for example, were divided into acts and scenes largely by later editors rather than by the playwright himself.

In film

The term "scene" is also used in cinema: a scene is a series of shots unified by location and time, as opposed to a sequence, which spans multiple locations.
