Roundup (herbicide)

Roundup
ManufacturerBayer (formerly Monsanto)
Introduced1974
Active ingredientGlyphosate (isopropylamine salt)
TypeSystemic, broad-spectrum herbicide

Roundup is a brand of systemic, broad-spectrum herbicides based on the chemical glyphosate, originally produced by Monsanto and introduced to the market in 1974. Glyphosate kills plants by inhibiting EPSP synthase, an enzyme in the shikimate pathway needed to produce aromatic amino acids. Roundup became the world's most widely used herbicide, especially after the introduction in 1996 of genetically modified "Roundup Ready" crops resistant to it. Monsanto was acquired by Bayer in 2018, which retains the Roundup brand for consumer products.

Use

Glyphosate herbicides are used in agriculture, forestry, lawns, gardens and for clearing vegetation from railways and pavements. Because glyphosate is absorbed through foliage and translocated to roots, it controls perennial weeds; it breaks down in soil through microbial action.

Controversy and litigation

The safety of glyphosate has been intensely debated. In 2015 the International Agency for Research on Cancer classified glyphosate as "probably carcinogenic to humans" (Group 2A), while the U.S. EPA, EFSA and other regulators have concluded it is unlikely to be carcinogenic at expected exposure levels. From 2018, tens of thousands of lawsuits in the United States alleged that Roundup caused non-Hodgkin lymphoma; early jury verdicts awarded large damages to plaintiffs, and in 2020 Bayer agreed to pay up to $10.9 billion to settle most current and future U.S. claims, while continuing to sell the product and denying liability. Several countries and municipalities have restricted or banned glyphosate use, though it remains authorised in the EU (renewed in 2023 for ten years) and many other markets. Formulations vary by country; in the 2020s many consumer Roundup products in the U.S. were reformulated with other active ingredients.
