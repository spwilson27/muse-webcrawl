Harry Potter is a series of seven fantasy novels written by British author J. K. Rowling. The novels chronicle the life of a young wizard, Harry Potter, and his friends Hermione Granger and Ron Weasley, all of whom are students at Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry. The main story arc concerns Harry's conflict with Lord Voldemort, a dark wizard who intends to become immortal, overthrow the wizard governing body known as the Ministry of Magic, and subjugate all wizards and Muggles (non-magical people).

The books

The series comprises Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone (1997), Chamber of Secrets (1998), Prisoner of Azkaban (1999), Goblet of Fire (2000), Order of the Phoenix (2003), Half-Blood Prince (2005), and Deathly Hallows (2007). Each novel covers one school year, and the tone grows darker as the characters mature.

Success and impact

With more than 600 million copies sold worldwide and translations into over 80 languages, Harry Potter is the best-selling book series in history. It revived children's publishing, made Rowling the world's first billionaire author (a status later lost through charitable giving and taxes), and generated enormous shared cultural phenomena around midnight book launches. Critics and scholars have debated the books' themes of death, prejudice, love, and the abuse of power, as well as their place in the canon of children's literature.

Media franchise

The eight Warner Bros. film adaptations (2001–2011) grossed over $7.7 billion, at the time making it the highest-grossing film series ever. The wider Wizarding World franchise includes the Fantastic Beasts prequel films, the stage play Harry Potter and the Cursed Child (2016), theme-park lands at Universal parks, and the video game Hogwarts Legacy (2023).
