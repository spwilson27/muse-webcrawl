Gil Eanes

Gil Eanes (Portuguese pronunciation: [ˈʒiɫ iˈɐnɨʃ]; fl. 1433–1445) was a 15th-century Portuguese navigator and explorer associated with the early voyages of discovery sponsored by Prince Henry the Navigator. He is remembered for rounding Cape Bojador in 1434, a landmark in the European exploration of the West African coast.

Cape Bojador

Cape Bojador, on the coast of modern Western Sahara, was long regarded by Europeans as impassable: shallow reefs, fog, contrary currents and legends of monsters and boiling seas fed its reputation as the edge of the navigable world. After earlier failed attempts — including one by Eanes himself in 1433, when he reached only the Canary Islands — Eanes sailed again in 1434 and succeeded in rounding the cape, returning with roses of Santa Maria (flowers gathered ashore) as proof of landfall. The feat opened the way for successive voyages down the African coast by Nuno Tristão, Antão Gonçalves and others, towards the Guinea coast and eventually the sea route to India.

Later voyages

In 1435 Eanes sailed again with Afonso Gonçalves Baldaia, reaching the Rio de Oro region (which they named Angra dos Ruivos). Little is known of his life beyond the chronicles of Gomes Eanes de Zurara, and the dates of his birth and death are unrecorded. He is commemorated in his home port of Lagos, Portugal, where a statue stands near the waterfront.
