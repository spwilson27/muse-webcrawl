The Directors Guild of America Awards are issued annually by the Directors Guild of America (DGA) to honour outstanding directorial achievement in feature film, television, commercials, and documentary. The first DGA Award for Outstanding Directorial Achievement in Motion Pictures was awarded to Joseph L. Mankiewicz for A Letter to Three Wives (1948).

Feature film award and the Oscars

The DGA feature film award is one of the most reliable predictors of the Academy Award for Best Director: since the award's inception, its winner has gone on to win the Oscar in all but a handful of years. Notable exceptions include 1972 (John Schlesinger won the DGA Award, Bob Fosse the Oscar), 1985 (Steven Spielberg vs. Sydney Pollack), 1995 (Ron Howard vs. Mel Gibson), 2000 (Ang Lee vs. Steven Soderbergh), 2002 (Rob Marshall vs. Roman Polanski), 2012 (Ben Affleck, who was not Oscar-nominated, vs. Ang Lee), and 2019 (Sam Mendes vs. Bong Joon-ho).

Categories

Categories include Outstanding Directorial Achievement in Feature Film, Dramatic Series, Comedy Series, Movies for Television and Limited Series, Documentary, Reality Programs, Commercials, and Children's Programs, as well as lifetime achievement honours.
