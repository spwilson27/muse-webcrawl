The Book of Esther (Hebrew: מְגִלַּת אֶסְתֵּר, Megillat Esther), also known in Hebrew as "the Scroll" (The Megillah), is a book in the third section (Ketuvim, "Writings") of the Jewish Tanakh (the Hebrew Bible) and in the Christian Old Testament. It relates the story of a Hebrew woman in Persia, born as Hadassah but known as Esther, who becomes queen of Persia and thwarts a genocide of her people.

Summary

King Ahasuerus (generally identified with Xerxes I), deposing Queen Vashti, chooses the Jewish orphan Esther — raised by her cousin Mordecai — as his new queen. The king's chief minister, Haman the Agagite, enraged that Mordecai refuses to bow to him, casts lots (purim) to choose a day to exterminate all the Jews of the empire, and obtains the king's sealed decree. At great personal risk — approaching the king unsummoned was punishable by death — Esther reveals her Jewish identity at a banquet and denounces Haman, who is hanged on the gallows he built for Mordecai. A second decree lets the Jews defend themselves, and their deliverance is commemorated by the festival of Purim, with feasting, gifts of food, and charity.

Historicity and text

The book is generally regarded by scholars as a historical novella rather than a chronicle; no Persian records confirm its events. Uniquely among biblical books, the Hebrew text never names God directly. The Greek Septuagint version contains six additions (107 verses) that supply explicit religious content; these are canonical in Catholic and Orthodox churches and placed in the Apocrypha in Protestant Bibles.
