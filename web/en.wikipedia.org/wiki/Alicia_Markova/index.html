Alicia Markova

Dame Alicia Markova (1910–2004), born Lilian Alicia Marks in London, was one of the greatest English ballerinas of the twentieth century. A child prodigy, she was taken to Paris at fourteen to join Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes, where Diaghilev gave her the Russian stage name Markova.

Career

After Diaghilev's death in 1929, Markova returned to England and became the founding ballerina of the companies that grew into The Royal Ballet. With Anton Dolin she formed the Markova-Dolin Ballet in 1935, touring widely. During the Second World War she danced in the United States with Ballet Theatre (now American Ballet Theatre), and she became especially celebrated for her interpretation of the title role in Giselle, the role with which her artistry is most identified.

English National Ballet

In 1950 Markova and Dolin founded Festival Ballet in London, the company now known as English National Ballet. Markova retired from dancing in 1963, was made a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire, and later worked as a teacher and director, including a period directing ballet at the Metropolitan Opera in New York.

Legacy

Markova was one of the first ballerinas to reach a mass audience through television appearances, and her partnership with Dolin helped establish ballet as a popular art in Britain and America.
