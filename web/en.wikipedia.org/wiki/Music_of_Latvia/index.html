Music of Latvia

Latvian music is deeply rooted in folk traditions, particularly the dainas—short folk songs, of which more than 1.2 million texts have been archived—and a powerful choral culture that became central to Latvian national identity.

Folk music

Dainas are typically quatrains on themes of nature, mythology, work, and family life; Krišjānis Barons compiled the monumental Latvju dainas collection (1894–1915). Traditional instruments include the kokles, a plucked zither that is a national symbol, the stabule flute, and the dūdas bagpipe. Folk singing plays a central role in the midsummer Jāņi festival.

Song festivals

The Latvian Song and Dance Festival, first held in Riga in 1873, gathers tens of thousands of singers and dancers; together with the Estonian and Lithuanian festivals it is listed by UNESCO as intangible heritage. Choral singing also powered the "Singing Revolution" of 1987–1991 that led to restored independence.

Classical music

Jāzeps Vītols founded the Latvian national school of composition. Prominent figures include conductor Mariss Jansons, violinist Gidon Kremer, composer Pēteris Vasks, mezzo-soprano Elīna Garanča, and soprano Kristīne Opolais. The Latvian Radio Choir and the State Choir Latvija are internationally acclaimed.

Popular music

Composer Raimonds Pauls became a star across the Soviet Union. Popular acts include the band Brainstorm (Prāta Vētra), Eurovision winner Marie N (2002), the a cappella group Latvian Voices, and folk-metal band Skyforger.
