Dominant strategy

In game theory, a dominant strategy is a strategy that is optimal for a player, no matter what the player's opponents do. A strategy is strictly dominant if choosing it always yields a strictly higher payoff than any other strategy; it is weakly dominant if it never does worse and sometimes does better.

Dominance and equilibrium

If every player has a strictly dominant strategy, the resulting profile is the unique Nash equilibrium of the game, called a dominant-strategy equilibrium. Rational players can find it without predicting opponents' behaviour: strategies that are strictly dominated can be eliminated iteratively (iterated elimination of dominated strategies). The classic example is the prisoner's dilemma, in which defecting dominates cooperating for both players, yet mutual defection is worse for both than mutual cooperation — the "dilemma".

Applications

Dominant-strategy reasoning is central to mechanism design: the Vickrey auction, for example, makes truthful bidding a weakly dominant strategy, simplifying the analysis of bidder behaviour.
