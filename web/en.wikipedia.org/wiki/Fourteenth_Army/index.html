Fourteenth Army

The British Fourteenth Army was a multi-national force comprising units from Commonwealth countries during the Second World War. Many of its units were from the Indian Army as well as British and African formations. It was often called the "Forgotten Army" because its operations in the Burma Campaign were overlooked by the contemporary press.

History

The Fourteenth Army was formed in India in late 1943 under Lieutenant-General William Slim, as part of South East Asia Command. After halting the Japanese offensive at the battles of Imphal and Kohima in 1944 — among the largest defeats suffered by the Imperial Japanese Army — it went on to reconquer Burma, advancing through Mandalay and Meiktila to Rangoon in 1945, the longest land campaign fought by British forces in the war.

Composition

At its height the army was one of the largest Commonwealth formations of the war, with over a million men under its control in its corps and lines-of-communication troops drawn from Britain, India, Nepal (Gurkhas), and East and West Africa.

Legacy

Slim's memoir Defeat into Victory is regarded as a classic of military literature. The army's veterans are commemorated by the Burma Star Association and by the Kohima epitaph: "When you go home, tell them of us and say, for your tomorrow, we gave our today."
