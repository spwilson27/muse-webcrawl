Canterbury Rugby Football Union

The Canterbury Rugby Football Union (CRFU) is the governing body of rugby union in the Canterbury region of New Zealand's South Island. Founded in 1879, it was the first provincial rugby union established in New Zealand, and its representative team — playing in red and black hoops — is one of the most successful in the country's domestic game, competing in the National Provincial Championship (Bunnings NPC).

History

The CRFU was formed in 1879, nine years after the first game of rugby in New Zealand was played in Nelson, and it played a leading role in the creation of the New Zealand Rugby Football Union in 1892. Canterbury teams have won the NPC championship on numerous occasions — including a dominant run of titles in the 2010s — and have held the Ranfurly Shield, New Zealand rugby's most prized provincial trophy, many times.

Relationship with the Crusaders

Canterbury is the principal union within the Crusaders Super Rugby franchise, founded in 1996, whose catchment also includes Tasman, West Coast, Buller, Mid Canterbury, and South Canterbury. The Crusaders are the most successful team in Super Rugby history, and Lancaster Park — the union's historic home — was their base until the stadium was damaged beyond repair in the February 2011 Christchurch earthquake. Provincial and franchise teams subsequently played at the temporary stadium at Rugby League Park in Addington.

Club rugby and development

The union administers senior and junior club competitions across Christchurch and mid-Canterbury, runs age-grade representative programmes, and has been a leading producer of All Blacks, including such figures as Dan Carter, who played provincial rugby for Canterbury before his record-breaking international career.
