Kalākaua

Born16 November 1836, Honolulu, Oahu
Died20 January 1891 (aged 54), San Francisco, California
Reign12 February 1874 – 20 January 1891
HouseKalākaua
SuccessorLiliʻuokalani

Kalākaua (David Laʻamea Kamananakapu Mahinulani Naloiaehuokalani Lumialani Kalākaua; 16 November 1836 – 20 January 1891) was the last king and penultimate monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaii, reigning from 1874 until his death in 1891. Nicknamed "the Merrie Monarch" for his love of music and celebration, he worked to revive Hawaiian culture — restoring public hula performance and native traditions — while also pursuing an ambitious programme of international diplomacy and modernisation.

Reign

Elected king by the legislature after the death of Lunalilo in 1874, Kalākaua negotiated the Reciprocity Treaty of 1875 with the United States, which boosted Hawaii's sugar economy. In 1881 he embarked on a world tour — the first by a reigning monarch — visiting Japan, China, Siam, Egypt and the courts of Europe, and meeting Queen Victoria and President Chester A. Arthur. He built the opulent ʻIolani Palace in Honolulu, completed in 1882, and was crowned in a lavish coronation ceremony in 1883.

In 1887, under threat of force from armed business interests, Kalākaua signed the so-called "Bayonet Constitution", which sharply reduced royal power and disenfranchised many native and Asian voters. His sister and heir, Liliʻuokalani, succeeded him after he died in San Francisco in January 1891 during a journey for medical treatment.

Legacy

Kalākaua composed the song "Hawaiʻi Ponoʻī", now the state anthem, and his cultural revival is honoured by the annual Merrie Monarch Festival of hula in Hilo.
