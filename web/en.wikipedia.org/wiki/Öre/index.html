Öre

Swedish öre coins; the 50-öre piece was withdrawn in 2010

The öre (Swedish: [ˈøːrɛ] (listen); plural öre or ören) is the centesimal subdivision of the Swedish krona, equal to one hundredth of a krona. The corresponding subdivisions of the Norwegian and Danish currencies are called øre, and the subdivision of the Icelandic króna is the eyrir; all derive from the Latin aureus, a gold coin.

History

The öre was introduced in Sweden in the 11th century as a unit of weight and account, originally denoting a measure of silver. When Sweden adopted a decimal currency system with the krona in 1873, as part of the Scandinavian Monetary Union, the öre became its hundredth part.

Coins

Swedish öre coins were historically issued in denominations of 1, 2, 5, 10, 25 and 50 öre. The smallest denominations were progressively withdrawn as inflation reduced their value: the 1- and 2-öre coins disappeared in 1971, the 5- and 25-öre in 1984–85, and the 10-öre in 1991. The last, the 50-öre coin, ceased to be legal tender on 30 September 2010, leaving Sweden with no subdivision coin in circulation; cash payments are rounded to the nearest krona, although the öre remains a unit of account for electronic transactions and pricing.
