Solfège


Solfège assigns syllables — do, re, mi, fa, sol, la, ti — to the degrees of a scale.

Solfège (French: solfège; Italian: solfeggio) is a music education method used to teach pitch and sight-reading, in which each note of a scale is assigned a syllable: do, re, mi, fa, sol, la, and ti (or si).

History

The syllables derive from the Latin hymn Ut queant laxis, whose phrases begin on successive scale degrees; the system is associated with the 11th-century monk Guido d'Arezzo, who also gave his name to the Guidonian hand used to teach the syllables. Ut was later replaced by the more singable do, and ti was added for the seventh degree.

Fixed and movable do

In the fixed-do system, used in much of Europe, the syllables correspond to absolute pitches (do is always C). In the movable-do system, used in the Kodály method and much Anglophone teaching, do is the tonic of whatever key is in use, training relative pitch.
