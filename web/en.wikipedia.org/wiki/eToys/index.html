eToys was an American online retailer of toys that operated during the dot-com boom. Founded in 1997 by Toby Lenk, a former Walt Disney Company executive, it became one of the most prominent — and most famously doomed — internet startups of the era, reaching a market valuation of several billion dollars before filing for bankruptcy in March 2001, less than two years after its initial public offering.

History

eToys raised substantial venture capital and went public on the NASDAQ in May 1999; its shares soared on the first day of trading, briefly valuing the young company at around $8 billion — more than the established toy retailer Toys "R" Us. The company invested heavily in warehousing, advertising, and acquisitions, including the purchase of rival BabyCenter.

Despite strong holiday sales in 1999, eToys burned through cash rapidly, and the collapse of the dot-com bubble cut off further financing. It laid off most of its workforce in early 2001 and filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in March 2001. Its assets, including the brand and website, were sold to KB Toys.

Aftermath and brand revivals

The eToys name passed through a succession of owners. KB Toys operated the site until its own bankruptcy; the brand was later acquired by Toys "R" Us, relaunched by the investment firm D. E. Shaw, and in 2017 was operated as an online storefront by Fingerhut's parent company. The rise and fall of eToys is frequently cited as a case study in the excesses of the dot-com bubble, illustrating how rapid growth, heavy spending, and an unsustainable cost structure destroyed even category-leading internet retailers.
