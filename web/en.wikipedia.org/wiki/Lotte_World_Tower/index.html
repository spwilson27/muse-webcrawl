Lotte World Tower

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lotte World Tower Location: Jamsil, Songpa District, Seoul, South Korea
Height: 555 m (1,821 ft), 123 floors
Completed: 2016; opened 3 April 2017
Architect: Kohn Pedersen Fox

The Lotte World Tower (Korean: 롯데월드타워) is a 123-storey supertall skyscraper in the Jamsil area of Seoul, South Korea. At 555 metres (1,821 ft) it is the tallest building in South Korea and among the tallest in the world. Developed by the Lotte Group, it houses offices, a luxury hotel (Signiel Seoul), residences ("officetels"), retail space and the Seoul Sky observation deck.

Design and construction

Designed by the American firm Kohn Pedersen Fox, the tower's gently tapered silhouette is inspired by traditional Korean ceramics and calligraphy brushes, with a seam of glass running up one side. Plans for a tall tower on the site dated to the 1990s, but government approval was delayed for years, in part over concerns about the nearby Seoul Air Base. Construction began in 2011; the tower was topped out in 2015 and completed at the end of 2016, opening to the public on 3 April 2017.

Features

Floors 117–123 hold the Seoul Sky observatory, which includes a glass-floor skywalk and one of the world's highest glass-bottom decks. The building was designed to withstand earthquakes and high winds, and received a LEED Gold rating for sustainable design. It stands beside the Lotte World amusement complex and Lotte World Mall, facing the Han River. Fireworks displays from the tower, first staged for its opening, have become a signature New Year spectacle in Seoul.
