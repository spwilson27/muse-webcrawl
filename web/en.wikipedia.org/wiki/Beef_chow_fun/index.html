Beef chow fun

Beef chow fun (Chinese: 乾炒牛河; pinyin: gān chǎo niú hé; Cantonese: gōn cháau ngàuh hó, "dry-fried beef ho fun") is a staple Cantonese dish made of stir-frying beef, wide flat rice noodles known as hor fun (河粉, also romanized ho fun), and bean sprouts. It is commonly found in yum cha restaurants and in cha chaan tengs across Hong Kong and in Cantonese restaurants abroad.

Preparation

The dish is prepared by stir-frying slices of marinated beef with flat rice noodles in a very hot wok, with bean sprouts, chives (often Chinese garlic chives), ginger, scallions, and a sauce of light and dark soy sauce with sugar. The dark soy lends the noodles their characteristic amber color. A defining quality standard is wok hei — the smoky "breath of the wok" imparted by searing the ingredients over intense heat without excessive oil, so that the dish arrives "dry" rather than greasy despite the name of the dry-fried style.

In Chinese culinary culture, a plate of properly made beef chow fun is often regarded as a test of a chef's basic wok skills: the noodles must not break into pieces, must be evenly coated with sauce, must not stick to one another, and must carry a distinct smoky aroma.

Variations

A closely related "wet" style (濕炒, shī chǎo) is served with a thickened sauce poured over crisped noodles. Other variations substitute char siu pork, chicken, or seafood for the beef. In overseas Chinatowns, beef chow fun is a standard item on Cantonese menus, and the noodles are sometimes prepared with bell peppers and onions, which are not part of the traditional Hong Kong version.
