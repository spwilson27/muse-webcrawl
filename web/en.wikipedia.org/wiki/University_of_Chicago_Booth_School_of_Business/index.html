University of Chicago Booth School of Business

Business school
Founded: 1898
Location: Hyde Park, Chicago, Illinois
Students: about 3,500
Named for: David G. Booth

The University of Chicago Booth School of Business (Chicago Booth) is the graduate business school of the University of Chicago. Founded in 1898, it is the second-oldest business school in the United States and is renowned for its influence on finance and economics.

History

The school was founded as the College of Commerce and Politics and awarded the first business PhD in 1921 and the first executive MBA in 1943. Its faculty developed foundational ideas of modern finance: Eugene Fama's efficient-market hypothesis, Merton Miller's and Harry Markowitz's portfolio work, and the capital asset pricing model; the "Chicago school of economics" reshaped antitrust and regulation. More faculty and alumni have won the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences than at any other business school.

Programs and campuses

Booth offers full-time, evening, weekend, and executive MBA programs, plus PhD and specialized master's programs. Its flexible curriculum lets students choose nearly all courses. Campuses in Chicago (Harper Center and Gleacher Center), London, and Hong Kong serve students worldwide.

Naming

In 2008 alumnus David Booth donated $300 million — then the largest gift to any business school — and the school was renamed in his honor.
