A gonad is a mixed gland and sex organ that produces the gametes — sperm in males and ova (egg cells) in females — and sex hormones of an organism. In vertebrates the male gonad is the testicle and the female gonad is the ovary. Because gonads both produce reproductive cells and secrete hormones such as testosterone, estrogen and progesterone, they function simultaneously as exocrine and endocrine glands.

Development

Gonads develop from the genital ridge of the intermediate mesoderm. Early in development they are bipotential ("indifferent"): in mammals, expression of the SRY gene on the Y chromosome directs development into testes, while its absence leads to ovaries. The testes secrete anti-Müllerian hormone and testosterone, shaping male internal and external anatomy; ovaries begin follicular development later. The gonads then descend — testes through the inguinal canal into the scrotum, ovaries into the pelvis.

Function

Gametogenesis occurs within specialized structures: seminiferous tubules in the testis support continuous sperm production from puberty, while ovarian follicles mature ova in cycles. Gonadal hormones, regulated by pituitary gonadotropins (LH and FSH), drive sexual maturation, secondary sexual characteristics, the menstrual cycle and fertility.

Disorders

Gonadal disorders include hypogonadism, gonadal dysgenesis, ovarian cysts and torsion, testicular torsion, and gonadal cancers. Disorders of sex development may involve gonads whose tissue or function differs from typical male or female patterns.
