Federal Witness Protection Program

The United States Federal Witness Protection Program (WITSEC) is a witness protection programme administered by the United States Marshals Service, established under the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970. It provides witnesses who testify against dangerous criminals — particularly organised crime figures — with relocation, new identities and assistance in starting new lives.

Operation

The programme was devised by Department of Justice attorney Gerald Shur. Witnesses — many of them criminals themselves — agree to testify and to abide by programme rules. The Marshals Service moves them to undisclosed locations, issues new identity documents, and provides stipends, housing help and job assistance. Most participants are resettled in ordinary communities under assumed names. According to the Marshals Service, no participant who followed the rules has been killed while in the programme, and protected testimony has contributed to a high conviction rate in cases involving WITSEC witnesses.

Notable participants

Famous protected witnesses include Mafia turncoats such as Joseph Valachi (whose testimony preceded the formal programme), Henry Hill — whose life inspired the book Wiseguy and the film Goodfellas — and Sammy "the Bull" Gravano, whose testimony helped convict Gambino boss John Gotti. Several US states run their own smaller witness protection programmes.
