Jonathan Dickinson State Park

Location: Martin County, Florida, United States
Area: 11,500 acres (47 km²)
Established: 1950
Named for: Jonathan Dickinson, shipwrecked Quaker merchant
Governing body: Florida State Parks

Jonathan Dickinson State Park is a Florida State Park and historic site located in Martin County, Florida, between Hobe Sound and Tequesta. The park covers about 11,500 acres of diverse habitats, including sand pine scrub, pine flatwoods, mangrove swamps, and the tidal Loxahatchee River, one of Florida's two federally designated Wild and Scenic Rivers.

History

The park is named after Jonathan Dickinson, a Quaker merchant whose ship was wrecked on the nearby coast in 1696 and who wrote a famous journal of his party's captivity and journey to St. Augustine. During World War II the land formed part of Camp Murphy, a top-secret Army radar training school; the park preserves remnants of the camp, and the site of the home and jungle park of the eccentric self-styled "Wild Man of the Loxahatchee," Trapper Nelson, is a popular river destination within the park.

Recreation

Visitors can camp, hike, bicycle (including an extensive off-road mountain bike trail system), horseback ride, fish, and paddle on the Loxahatchee River. The Elsa Kimbell Environmental Education and Research Center interprets the park's ecosystems, which support wildlife including Florida scrub jays, gopher tortoises, alligators, manatees, and bobcats. Hobe Mountain, an ancient sand dune within the park rising about 86 feet, is one of the highest natural points in South Florida.
