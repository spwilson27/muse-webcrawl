Gay pride (also known as LGBT pride) is the promotion of the self-affirmation, dignity, equality and increased visibility of lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender (LGBT) people as a social group. Pride, as opposed to shame and social stigma, is the predominant outlook that underpins most LGBT rights movements and their public celebrations.

Origins

The modern pride movement is generally traced to the Stonewall riots of June 1969 in New York City, when patrons of the Stonewall Inn resisted a police raid. On June 28, 1970, the first anniversary was marked by the Christopher Street Liberation Day march in New York and parallel events in Los Angeles, Chicago and San Francisco — among the first pride marches.

Activist Brenda Howard, known as the "Mother of Pride", was instrumental in organising the 1970 march and in popularising the idea of a week of events around it. The word "pride" itself was championed by activists including L. Craig Schoonmaker.

Celebrations

Pride events are held in cities worldwide, typically during June (designated Pride Month in many countries), though dates vary. They range from solemn commemorations of victims of hate crime and the AIDS epidemic to large carnival-like festivals and parades. Major events such as those in São Paulo, Madrid, New York, San Francisco, Toronto and Sydney draw millions of participants, and the largest, São Paulo's pride parade, has been among the biggest parades in the world.

Symbols

The rainbow flag, designed by artist Gilbert Baker in 1978, is the best-known symbol of LGBT pride. Other reclaimed symbols include the pink triangle, once used to mark homosexual prisoners in Nazi concentration camps, and the Greek letter lambda.

Criticism and debate

Pride events have periodically faced criticism from within and outside the LGBT community, including debates over corporate sponsorship ("rainbow capitalism"), the inclusion of police contingents, and whether parades have lost their protest character. Counter-events and alternative "anti-pride" marches have been organised in various places, and pride remains illegal or heavily restricted in many countries.
