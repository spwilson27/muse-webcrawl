The Supporters' Shield is an annual award in Major League Soccer (MLS) given to the team with the best regular-season record, as determined by the league's points system. Unusually for North American sports, the trophy was conceived and is maintained by the league's fans: it was proposed by supporter Sam Pierron and created through donations from supporters' groups, and is administered by the Independent Supporters Council. Winners were formally recognised from 1999, with the 1996–1998 seasons recognised retroactively.

History and significance

MLS determines its champion through the MLS Cup playoffs, but the Shield rewards consistency across the full season, analogous to a league title in most world football. Winning the Shield carries tangible benefits: qualification for the CONCACAF Champions Cup and, historically, home-field advantage through the playoffs. The LA Galaxy and D.C. United have been among its most frequent winners; other notable winners include the Columbus Crew, New York Red Bulls, Toronto FC (the first Canadian winner, in 2017) and Los Angeles FC, whose 2019 team set a then-record points total. The original trophy was replaced by a new shield in 2013, funded by supporters.

Shield winners and the double

Teams that win both the Supporters' Shield and MLS Cup in the same season are said to have won "the double"; D.C. United (1997, 1999), LA Galaxy (2002, 2011), Columbus Crew (2008), Toronto FC (2017) and Los Angeles FC (2022) are among those to have achieved it. Because MLS schedules are unbalanced between conferences, the fairness of cross-conference comparison is periodically debated among supporters.
