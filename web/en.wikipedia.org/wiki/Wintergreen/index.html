Wintergreen

Gaultheria procumbens, the eastern teaberry, with red berries

Wintergreen is a group of aromatic plants. The term "wintergreen" originally referred to plants that remain green (continue photosynthesis) throughout the winter. The term "evergreen" became preferred for this characteristic, and wintergreen now refers more specifically to plants that produce the characteristic "wintergreen" scent from their foliage.

The species of the genus Gaultheria, particularly the eastern North American Gaultheria procumbens (the teaberry, checkerberry, or American wintergreen), and the South American Gaultheria insipida and Gaultheria leucocarpa, are classic sources of the flavour. The Gaultheria species share the common characteristic of producing oil of wintergreen, whose principal component is methyl salicylate.

Uses

Wintergreen berries, from Gaultheria procumbens, are used medicinally. Native Americans brewed a tea from the leaves to alleviate rheumatic symptoms, headache, fever, sore throat, and various inflammations. The plant also lends its name to the flavour of wintergreen, used in candies, chewing gum, mints, and root beer in North America. In Britain, formulations with a similar flavour profile have historically been associated with medicinal products.

Oil of wintergreen

Many Gaultheria species produce methyl salicylate (oil of wintergreen) as a secondary metabolite. The compound is also produced synthetically on a large scale. It is used as a flavouring in very low concentrations, as a fragrance, and in topical analgesic preparations (liniments) because it produces a sensation of warmth and acts as a counterirritant. Methyl salicylate is, however, toxic in concentrated form: a teaspoon of the oil contains roughly as much salicylate as many adult aspirin tablets, and ingestion of relatively small amounts can be fatal, especially to children. Preparations are therefore required to carry safety warnings.

Other "wintergreens"

The name is also applied to other plants that are green in winter, including members of the genus Chimaphila and some members of the Ericaceae and the orchid family. In the heath family, the subfamily of "wintergreens" (Pyroleae) includes Chimaphila, Moneses, Orthilia, and Pyrola. Additionally, the birch trees Betula lenta (sweet birch) and Betula alleghaniensis produce wintergreen-scented oils rich in methyl salicylate and have been commercial sources of it.
