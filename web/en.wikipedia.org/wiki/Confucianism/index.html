Confucianism is an ethical and philosophical tradition originating with Confucius (551–479 BC) that shaped the cultures of China, Korea, Japan and Vietnam for two millennia. It teaches that social harmony flows from cultivated character: benevolence (ren), ritual propriety (li), filial piety toward parents and ancestors, and rulers who govern by moral example rather than force.

Development

Mencius (4th century BC) argued human nature is innately good; Xunzi held it needs shaping by ritual. Under the Han dynasty Confucianism became state orthodoxy, with civil-service examinations on its classics lasting into 1905. Neo-Confucianism (Zhu Xi, 12th century) absorbed Buddhist and Taoist metaphysics and dominated East Asian thought until modern reforms — and the Cultural Revolution's campaigns — displaced it; interest has revived since the 1980s. Whether Confucianism is a religion (it includes ancestor veneration and state rites to Heaven) is long debated.
