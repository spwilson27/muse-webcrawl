Railcar
TypeSelf-propelled railway vehicle
PropulsionDiesel, electric, petrol, steam (historical)
UsePassenger service on light-traffic lines

A railcar (not to be confused with the North American use of the term for any railroad car) is a self-propelled railway vehicle designed to transport passengers. The term usually refers to a train consisting of a single coach (carriage or car) with a driver's cab at one or both ends.

Characteristics

Railcars are economical to operate on lines with light traffic, since they avoid the cost of running a full locomotive-hauled train. Early examples were steam railcars; later types used petrol and then diesel engines, giving rise to the diesel multiple unit. Single-car diesel railcars were widely used on branch lines in Britain, Germany (as Schienenbus), and elsewhere.

Related vehicles

Modern lightweight railcars include diesel and battery-electric single cars for regional service. Vehicles coupled permanently in fixed sets are generally described as multiple units rather than railcars.
