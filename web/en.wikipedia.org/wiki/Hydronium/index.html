Hydronium

Hydronium (oxonium)
Formula: H3O+
Geometry: trigonal pyramidal
Conjugate base: water

In chemistry, hydronium (hydroxonium in traditional British English) is the cation [H3O]+, a type of oxonium ion produced by protonation of water. It is often viewed as the positive ion present when an Arrhenius acid is dissolved in water, and aqueous acidity is conventionally expressed in terms of its concentration through the pH scale.

Structure and solvation

The hydronium ion is trigonal pyramidal, isoelectronic with ammonia. In liquid water no single H3O+ exists in isolation: the excess proton is delocalised in larger hydrogen-bonded clusters. Two limiting structures are the Eigen cation (H9O4+, a hydronium core solvated by three waters) and the Zundel cation (H5O2+, a proton shared equally between two water molecules). The rapid interconversion of such clusters underlies the Grotthuss mechanism for the exceptionally fast diffusion of protons through water.

Acidity

Pure water auto-ionises slightly, giving equal hydronium and hydroxide concentrations of 10−7 mol/L at 25 °C (pH 7). Crystalline hydronium salts of very strong acids, such as hydronium perchlorate, can be isolated. Hydronium is the strongest acid that can exist in aqueous solution; any stronger acid protonates water completely to form it (the levelling effect).
