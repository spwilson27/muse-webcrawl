Robert James Shiller (born March 29, 1946) is an American economist, Sterling Professor of Economics at Yale University, and a pioneer of behavioural finance. He is best known for his warnings of the dot-com and housing bubbles, his book Irrational Exuberance (2000), and the Case–Shiller housing price indexes he developed with Karl Case. In 2013 he shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Eugene Fama and Lars Peter Hansen for their empirical analysis of asset prices.

Research

Shiller's early work challenged the efficient-market hypothesis: his 1981 paper showed that stock prices fluctuate far more than subsequent dividends could justify under present-value models — the "excess volatility" puzzle. He argued that markets are driven substantially by psychology, social dynamics and narratives. With Karl Case he designed repeat-sales house price indexes that became the S&P CoreLogic Case–Shiller indexes, the standard measure of U.S. home prices, and the cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio (CAPE or Shiller P/E) for valuing equity markets.

Bubble warnings and books

Irrational Exuberance, published at the peak of the dot-com boom in March 2000, argued that the stock market was overvalued; its second edition (2005) warned that housing was in a bubble, and its third (2015) extended the analysis to bond markets. With George Akerlof he wrote Animal Spirits (2009), on how human psychology drives the economy, and Phishing for Phools (2015). His later work, Narrative Economics (2019), examines how popular stories spread contagiously and move markets. Shiller also advocated "macro markets" — markets for claims on incomes and house prices — to let households hedge major economic risks, developed in The New Financial Order (2003).
