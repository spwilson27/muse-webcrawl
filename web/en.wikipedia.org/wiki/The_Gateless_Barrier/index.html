The Gateless Barrier (Chinese: 無門關 Wumenguan; Japanese: Mumonkan), sometimes translated as The Gateless Gate, is a collection of 48 Chan (Zen) koans compiled in 1228 by the Chinese Zen master Wumen Huikai (1183–1260). Together with the Blue Cliff Record and the oral tradition of Hakuin Ekaku, it is one of the central texts of koan study, particularly in the Rinzai school of Japanese Zen.

Contents

Each of the 48 cases consists of a short anecdote, dialogue or saying drawn from the recorded encounters of earlier Chan masters, followed by Wumen's prose commentary and a verse. The first and most famous case asks whether a dog has Buddha-nature; Zhaozhou answers "Mu" (無, "no" / "not"). Other celebrated cases include "Nansen kills the cat", "Zhaozhou's oak tree in the garden", and the encounter between Bodhidharma and Emperor Wu of Liang. A 49th case by Anwan, and additional verses, were appended shortly after Wumen's death.

Title and approach

The title plays on Wumen's name ("No-Gate") and on his preface's claim that the gate of Zen is gateless: the barrier is the student's own mind. Wumen urges readers to swallow the koan like a red-hot iron ball that can neither be swallowed nor spat out, until conceptual thinking is exhausted. Well-known English translations include those of Nyogen Senzaki and Paul Reps (in Zen Flesh, Zen Bones), Katsuki Sekida, and Robert Aitken.
