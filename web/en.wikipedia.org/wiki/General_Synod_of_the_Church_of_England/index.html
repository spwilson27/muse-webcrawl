General Synod of the Church of England

The General Synod is the tricameral deliberative and legislative organ of the Church of England. It was inaugurated in 1970, replacing the Church Assembly, and is the culmination of a process of rediscovering self-government for the Church.

General Synod
Type: Tricameral synod
Houses: Bishops, Clergy, Laity
Established: 1970
Meeting place: Church House, Westminster; York

Composition

The Synod consists of the House of Bishops (diocesan bishops plus suffragan representatives), the House of Clergy, and the House of Laity. Members are elected for five-year terms (quinquennia). It normally meets twice a year, in London and in York.

Functions

The Synod legislates by Measures, which after approval by Parliament and Royal Assent have the force of Acts of Parliament, and by Canons. It regulates doctrine and worship — including authorising liturgy such as Common Worship — budgets, and relations with other churches. Major decisions (for example, the ordination of women as priests in 1992 and as bishops in 2014) required two-thirds majorities in all three Houses.
