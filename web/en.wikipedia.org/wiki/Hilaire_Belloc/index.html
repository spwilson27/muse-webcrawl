Joseph Hilaire Pierre René Belloc (1870–1953) was a Franco-English writer, historian and politician, one of the most prolific and combative men of letters of the early 20th century. Born in France to a French father and English mother, he served in the French artillery, studied at Balliol College, Oxford, and became a naturalised British subject. He was Liberal MP for Salford South from 1906 to 1910.

Work

Belloc wrote in almost every genre: the beloved travel narrative The Path to Rome (1902); biographies of Danton, Robespierre, Napoleon and Marie Antoinette; the satirical Cautionary Tales for Children ("Matilda, who told lies, and was burned to death"); nonsense verse such as "Tarantella"; and, with his friend G. K. Chesterton — the pair caricatured by Shaw as the "Chesterbelloc" — defences of Roman Catholicism and of the economic philosophy of distributism, set out in The Servile State (1912). His historical writing, including Europe and the Faith (1920), presented Catholicism as the soul of European civilisation; some of his views, notably his later apologetics regarding authoritarian regimes, remain controversial.
