Structural alignment

Structural alignment attempts to establish residue-residue correspondence between two or more polymer structures based on their three-dimensional shapes and conformations, rather than on their amino acid sequences. It is used in molecular biology to compare proteins whose sequences have diverged beyond the point where sequence alignment is informative, exploiting the fact that protein structure is more conserved in evolution than sequence.

Methods

Alignment quality is usually measured by the root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of superimposed atom positions, or by dedicated scores such as the TM-score and the DALI Z-score. Widely used algorithms and servers include DALI (distance matrix alignment), CE (combinatorial extension), SSM, TM-align, and FATCAT, which allows flexibility and twists between domains.

Applications

Structural alignments underpin protein structure classification databases such as SCOP and CATH, remote homology detection, function prediction for newly solved structures, and the construction of structural phylogenies. Multiple structural alignment extends the approach to families of related folds.
