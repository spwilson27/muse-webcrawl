Passive electronically scanned array

A passive electronically scanned array (PESA), also known as a passive phased array, is an antenna in which the beam of radio waves can be electronically steered to point in different directions without moving the antenna, and in which all the radiating elements are connected to a single transmitter and/or receiver. This contrasts with an active electronically scanned array (AESA), in which each radiating element has its own transmitter and/or receiver module.

A PESA radar array on a naval vessel.

Operation

In a PESA, a single high-power source — historically a travelling-wave tube or klystron — feeds a network of phase shifters, one per radiating element. By adjusting the relative phases of the signals sent to each element, constructive and destructive interference steers the combined beam almost instantaneously. On reception, the element signals are combined in the same network and passed to a single receiver.

Advantages and disadvantages

Compared with mechanically scanned antennas, PESAs steer their beams nearly instantaneously, can track many targets, and have no moving parts in the scanning mechanism. Compared with AESAs, a PESA is simpler and cheaper and uses mature high-power tube technology, but the single transmitter is a single point of failure, the feed network incurs losses, and the system cannot form multiple simultaneous independent beams as flexibly, nor degrade as gracefully when elements fail. The widely deployed AN/SPY-1 radar of the Aegis Combat System is a prominent PESA example, as are many Cold War-era Soviet and early European fighter and air-defence radars.

History

Phased array research accelerated during the Second World War and the Cold War. Large PESA systems entered service in the 1960s and 1970s for ballistic missile early warning and naval air defence, and fighter-sized PESAs such as the Soviet Zaslon radar of the MiG-31 appeared in the early 1980s. From the 1990s, falling costs of solid-state transmit/receive modules led to AESAs progressively displacing PESAs in new designs.
