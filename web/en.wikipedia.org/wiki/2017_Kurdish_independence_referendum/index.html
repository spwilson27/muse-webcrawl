2017 Kurdish independence referendum

An independence referendum for the Kurdistan Region of Iraq was held on 25 September 2017, asking voters: "Do you want the Kurdistan Region and the Kurdistani areas outside the Region to become an independent state?" Despite strong opposition from the Iraqi federal government, Turkey, Iran and most of the international community, the vote went ahead, with approximately 92% of voters supporting independence on a turnout of about 72%.

Background

The Kurdistan Region had enjoyed autonomy within federal Iraq since 2005, and its Peshmerga forces had expanded into disputed territories, including oil-rich Kirkuk, while fighting the Islamic State. President Masoud Barzani announced the referendum in June 2017, arguing that the partnership with Baghdad had failed. Baghdad insisted the vote was unconstitutional; Iraq's Supreme Court ordered its suspension, and neighbouring states feared it would inflame their own Kurdish minorities.

Result and aftermath

The overwhelming "yes" vote triggered a swift backlash. Iraq imposed a flight ban on the region's airports and demanded control of border crossings and oil exports. In mid-October 2017, Iraqi federal forces and allied militias retook Kirkuk and most disputed territories from the Peshmerga with little resistance. Barzani announced he would step down as president on 1 November 2017, and the Kurdistan Regional Government offered to "freeze" the referendum result and enter dialogue with Baghdad. Iraq's Federal Supreme Court ruled in November that the referendum was unconstitutional and its results void.
