Laurin & Klement

Laurin & Klement Voiturette A, the company's first automobile (1905)

Laurin & Klement was a Czech manufacturer of bicycles, motorcycles, and automobiles founded in 1895 in Mladá Boleslav, then in the Kingdom of Bohemia, by mechanic Václav Laurin and bookseller Václav Klement. The company became one of the largest car manufacturers in Austria-Hungary and is the direct predecessor of today's Škoda Auto; the Laurin & Klement name is still used for the highest trim level of Škoda cars.

History

The partnership began when Klement, frustrated with the repair service for his bicycle, wrote to a manufacturer and received a dismissive reply in German; he resolved to open his own repair shop with Laurin. Trading first under the name Slavia, they built bicycles, then from 1899 motorcycles, which achieved notable racing success. Their first automobile, the Voiturette A, appeared in 1905 and was a commercial success, propelling the firm to become the largest car maker in Austria-Hungary before the First World War.

Merger with Škoda

After the war, production diversified into trucks, buses, and agricultural machinery, but the company struggled and, following a factory fire, merged in 1925 with the Škoda Works of Plzeň. Cars thereafter carried the Škoda name, with Laurin & Klement surviving as a badge for luxury versions. Václav Klement died in 1938 and Václav Laurin in 1930.
