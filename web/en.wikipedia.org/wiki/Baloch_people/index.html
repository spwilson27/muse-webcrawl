The Baloch or Baluch are an Iranian people who speak the Balochi language and inhabit the Balochistan region of the Iranian plateau, divided between Pakistan (the province of Balochistan), southeastern Iran (Sistan and Baluchestan province), and southern Afghanistan. Significant Baloch diaspora communities exist in the Gulf states and elsewhere.

Culture and society

Baloch society is traditionally tribal and pastoral-nomadic, organised under sardars (chiefs) and governed by a code of honour known as Balochmayar, which emphasises hospitality, protection of guests and refugees, and revenge for insults. Balochi culture is known for epic poetry, storytelling, distinctive embroidery, and music.

History

The Baloch migrated from northwestern Iran toward the southeast over many centuries, reaching their present homeland by around the early medieval period. In the 17th century the Khanate of Kalat unified many Baloch tribes. The region was divided by British-imposed boundaries in the 19th century (the Goldsmid Line and Durand Line). Baloch nationalism has fuelled several insurgencies in Pakistan and tensions in Iran.
