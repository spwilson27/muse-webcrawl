Summability

In mathematics, summability concerns whether and how a value can be assigned to a series (or family of numbers) as its "sum". A series is summable in the ordinary sense if its partial sums converge; various summability methods extend this notion to some divergent series.

Classical notions

A family of numbers is absolutely summable if the sum of absolute values is finite, which for series of real or complex numbers guarantees unconditional convergence. Cesàro summability assigns to a series the limit of the average of its partial sums, summing, for example, Grandi's series 1 − 1 + 1 − 1 + … to 1/2. Abel summation uses the limit of a power series at the boundary of its disc of convergence.

General theory

Summability methods are classically described by matrix transformations of sequences (Toeplitz matrices), and a method is called regular if it agrees with ordinary convergence whenever the latter holds. Modern functional analysis treats summability of families in topological vector spaces, where a family is summable if the net of finite partial sums converges.
