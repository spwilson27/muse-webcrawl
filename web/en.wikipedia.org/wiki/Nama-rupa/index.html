Nama-rupa

Nāmarūpa (Sanskrit: नामरूप; Pali: nāmarūpa) is a term used in Buddhism and Hinduism to refer to the constituents of a living being: nāma ("name"), the mental or spiritual component, and rūpa ("form"), the physical component. In Buddhist thought, nāmarūpa is the fourth of the twelve nidānas (links) in the chain of dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), conditioned by consciousness (vijñāna) and conditioning the six sense bases (ṣaḷāyatana).

In Buddhism

In the Pali Canon, nāmarūpa is analysed in the Nidana Samyutta and related texts as the combination of feeling (vedanā), perception (saññā), volition (cetanā), contact (phassa) and attention (manasikāra) on the mental side, and the four great elements (mahābhūta) — earth, water, fire and air — on the material side. The Buddha compared nāma and rūpa to two sheaves of reeds leaning against each other: when one is removed, the other falls. The relationship between consciousness and nāmarūpa is described as reciprocal, neither being prior to the other.

In later Abhidharma analysis, nāmarūpa came to denote the totality of the five aggregates (skandhas) other than consciousness, or more broadly the psychophysical organism as a whole. Commentators such as Buddhaghosa treated it as the basis for personal identity without an underlying self (ātman).

In Hinduism

In Hindu usage, nāmarūpa refers to name and form: the differentiated manifestations of the unmanifest absolute (Brahman). In Vedānta, the world of name and form is superimposed (adhyāsa) on the attributeless reality through māyā. Liberation involves seeing through name and form to the underlying unity.
