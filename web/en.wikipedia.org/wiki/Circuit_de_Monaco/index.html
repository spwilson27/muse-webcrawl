The Circuit de Monaco is a motor racing street circuit laid out on the city streets of Monte Carlo and La Condamine around the harbour of the Principality of Monaco. It hosts the Monaco Grand Prix, first run in 1929 and a round of the Formula One World Championship since 1950. Nelson Piquet likened driving it to "riding a bicycle around your living room"; along with the Indianapolis 500 and the 24 Hours of Le Mans, it forms the informal Triple Crown of Motorsport, and it is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious races in the world.

Layout

The 3.337 km circuit has changed only slightly since 1929. From the start-finish straight cars climb the hill through Sainte-Dévote to Casino Square, descend past the Mirabeau and the Grand Hotel Hairpin — the slowest corner in Formula One — run through the famous tunnel, then tackle the harbour-front chicane, the Tabac corner, the Swimming Pool complex, La Rascasse, and the final Anthony Noghes corner. Barriers sit millimetres from the racing line, and the tight layout, with elevation changes and a dark tunnel, makes it exceptionally demanding: Ayrton Senna won here a record six times.

Characteristics

Overtaking is notoriously difficult, making qualifying position crucial; the circuit has one of the lowest average speeds on the Formula One calendar. Because it uses public roads, the track is assembled each year from armco barriers, grandstands, and temporary facilities; practice traditionally took place on the Thursday before the race (with Friday, a saint's day in Monaco, as a rest day) until the schedule was revised in 2022. The circuit also hosts Formula E (on a shortened or full layout), the Historic Grand Prix, and support races.
