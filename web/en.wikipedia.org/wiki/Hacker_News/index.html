Hacker News (HN) is a social news website focusing on computer science and entrepreneurship. It is run by the investment fund and startup incubator Y Combinator. In general, content that can be submitted is defined as "anything that gratifies one's intellectual curiosity."

History

The site was created by Paul Graham in February 2007, initially under the name Startup News; it adopted its current name later that year. Graham wrote the site in Arc, a Lisp dialect he co-developed, partly as a demonstration of the language and partly to create a community modelled on the early years of Reddit but filtered for the interests of programmers and startup founders. The site is maintained by Y Combinator staff, with moderation led by Daniel Gackle ("dang" on the site).

Mechanics

Users submit links or text posts ("Ask HN", "Show HN"), which other users upvote; a ranking algorithm combining votes, age, and various penalties determines front-page placement. Users accumulate "karma" from upvotes on their submissions and comments. Comments can be downvoted, flagged, or "dead" (hidden by default). Accounts that violate guidelines can be shadow-banned or subjected to rate limits ("hellbanning" in the site's early years).

Community and criticism

Hacker News is known for its minimalist design, largely unchanged since launch, and for a comment culture that prizes substantive, civil technical discussion. It is a major distribution channel in the technology industry: posts reaching the front page can drive large traffic spikes (the "Hug of Death"). Criticism has included complaints about moderation opacity, groupthink, and the prevalence of startup-industry perspectives.
