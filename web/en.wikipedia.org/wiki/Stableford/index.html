Stableford

Stableford is a scoring system used in golf. Unlike traditional stroke play, where the aim is to have the lowest score, Stableford awards points based on the number of strokes taken at each hole relative to par, and the winner is the player with the highest point total. The system is very common in amateur and club golf because a disastrous hole costs only points rather than destroying a round's scorecard, and players may pick up their ball once they can no longer score.

History

The system was devised by Dr. Frank Barney Gorton Stableford, a member of the Wallasey and Royal Liverpool golf clubs, who first tried it in 1898 at Wallasey. It was first used in competition on 16 May 1932 at Wallasey Golf Club in Cheshire, England, and the club still hosts the Stableford Open. Stableford intended the system to stop golfers giving up after a poor start on the opening holes.

Scoring

Points are awarded on each hole according to the player's score relative to the hole's fixed target score (usually par, adjusted by handicap strokes). The standard scale is:

Strokes relative to parPoints
More than 1 over par (or no score)0
One over par (bogey)1
Par2
One under par (birdie)3
Two under par (eagle)4
Three under par (albatross)5
Four under par6

In handicap competitions, a player's net score on each hole is computed using strokes received according to the hole's stroke index, so a bogey may earn two points and a par three for a player receiving a shot.

Professional use

Professional events using Stableford include the Barracuda Championship on the PGA Tour, which employs a Modified Stableford system that rewards aggressive play more heavily (an eagle earns 5 points and a double bogey costs −3). The International, a former PGA Tour event, also used the modified system.
