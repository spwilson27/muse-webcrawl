The Grand Slam of Thoroughbred racing is an informal term for winning four major American Thoroughbred horse races in the same year: the three races of the American Triple Crown — the Kentucky Derby, Preakness Stakes, and Belmont Stakes — plus the Breeders' Cup Classic in the autumn.

History

The concept became possible only after the Breeders' Cup was inaugurated in 1984, and it entered popular usage in 2015 when American Pharoah won the Triple Crown and then the Breeders' Cup Classic at Keeneland in his final start — the first horse to complete the "Grand Slam". No other horse has done so; Justify, the 2018 Triple Crown winner, was retired before the Breeders' Cup. Triple Crown winners of earlier eras such as Secretariat and Seattle Slew never had the opportunity, since the Breeders' Cup did not yet exist.

Challenges

The feat requires a three-year-old to defeat older horses in the Classic, typically at 1¼ miles, months after the demanding five-week Triple Crown campaign — a test of both class and durability that underscores the achievement's rarity.
