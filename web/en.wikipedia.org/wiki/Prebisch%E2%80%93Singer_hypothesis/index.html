Prebisch–Singer hypothesis


Commodity price trends chart

The Prebisch–Singer hypothesis, also known as the Prebisch–Singer thesis, argues that the price of primary commodities declines relative to the price of manufactured goods over the long term, causing the terms of trade of commodity-exporting developing countries to deteriorate.

Origins

The hypothesis was proposed independently around 1950 by the Argentine economist Raúl Prebisch, in a study for the UN Economic Commission for Latin America, and by Hans Singer. It became a foundation of dependency theory and of calls for a New International Economic Order, import-substitution industrialisation, and commodity cartels such as OPEC.

Evidence

Long-run price series broadly support a relative decline for many commodities, though economists debate its magnitude, causes and policy implications.
