Mont Blanc Tunnel

Mont Blanc Tunnel
Location: Beneath Mont Blanc, France–Italy
Length: 11.611 km (7.215 mi)
Opened: 19 July 1965
Traffic: Automotive
Operator: ATMB (France), SITMB (Italy)

The Mont Blanc Tunnel (French: Tunnel du Mont-Blanc; Italian: Traforo del Monte Bianco) is a highway tunnel between France and Italy, under the Mont Blanc mountain in the Alps. It links Chamonix, Haute-Savoie, France with Courmayeur, Aosta Valley, Italy, via the French Route Nationale 205 and the Italian Traforo T1 motorway, forming part of European route E25.

The French entrance of the Mont Blanc Tunnel near Chamonix

Construction

Agreement between France and Italy to build the tunnel was signed in 1949, and construction began in 1959, with Italian and French drilling teams working from opposite ends. The two teams met in August 1962, with a misalignment of less than 13 centimetres. The tunnel is 11.611 kilometres (7.215 mi) long, 8.6 metres wide, and consists of a single gallery with a two-lane, two-way roadway. It passes beneath the Aiguille du Midi and remains one of the major trans-Alpine road routes.

Opening and traffic

The tunnel was inaugurated on 16 July 1965 by Presidents Giuseppe Saragat of Italy and Charles de Gaulle of France, and opened to traffic on 19 July 1965. It dramatically shortened the journey between France and Italy compared with winding mountain passes, and carries large volumes of cars and heavy goods vehicles. Its operation is shared between the French company Autoroutes et tunnel du Mont-Blanc (ATMB) and the Italian Società italiana per azioni per il Traforo del Monte Bianco (SITMB).

1999 fire

On 24 March 1999, a Belgian transport truck carrying flour and margarine caught fire in the tunnel. The fire spread to other vehicles and burned for 53 hours, reaching temperatures of about 1,000 °C. Thirty-nine people were killed. The disaster led to a three-year closure, major safety renovations, and criminal proceedings. The tunnel reopened on 9 March 2002 with new safety measures including a dedicated rescue team, additional shelters, improved ventilation and fire detection, and remote traffic control.
