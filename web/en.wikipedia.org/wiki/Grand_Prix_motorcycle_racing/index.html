Grand Prix motorcycle racing

Grand Prix motorcycle racing is the highest class of motorcycle road racing, contested on purpose-built road circuits with prototype machines unavailable to the general public. The championship is sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM) and commercially run by Dorna Sports. First held in 1949, it is the oldest established motorsport world championship.

Classes

The championship is divided into three classes. MotoGP, the premier class, uses four-stroke prototype motorcycles with engines up to 1000 cc (reduced to 850 cc from 2027), produced by manufacturers including Ducati, Yamaha, Honda, Aprilia, and KTM. Moto2 uses identical 765 cc Triumph three-cylinder engines in prototype chassis, and Moto3 uses 250 cc single-cylinder production-based engines. The electric MotoE World Cup ran alongside the series from 2019 to 2025.

History

The FIM inaugurated the world championship in 1949 with classes from 125 cc to 500 cc. The 500 cc class, dominated in succession by riders such as Giacomo Agostini — still the most successful rider in history with 15 world titles — gave way in 2002 to MotoGP, initially for 990 cc four-strokes. Famous champions include Valentino Rossi, who won nine titles across classes, Mick Doohan, Marc Márquez, and Jorge Lorenzo.

Race weekend

A Grand Prix weekend consists of practice and qualifying sessions on Friday and Saturday, a shortened sprint race on Saturday afternoon in the MotoGP class, and the main races on Sunday. Championship points are awarded to the top fifteen finishers, with 25 points for a win. The calendar spans around twenty rounds across Europe, Asia, the Americas, and Oceania.
