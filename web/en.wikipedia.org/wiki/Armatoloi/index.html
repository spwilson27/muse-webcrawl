RolePolicing mountain passes and districts (armatoliks)

The armatoloi (Greek: ἀρματολοί, "bearers of arms"; singular armatolos) were Christian Greek irregular soldiers, or militia, commissioned by the Ottoman authorities to enforce their authority in certain districts of Ottoman Greece known as armatoliks. Distinguished from the klephts, who were outlawed brigands, the armatoloi were formally recognized by the Sultan and paid to maintain order, though in practice the roles blurred and individuals frequently moved between the two.

Origins and organization

The institution emerged in the late Byzantine and early Ottoman period, when Ottoman rulers, unable to police the mountainous interior directly, granted local Christian captains (kapetanioi) the right to arm their followers in exchange for guarding passes and suppressing brigandage. Major armatoliks existed in Thessaly (around Agrafa, the first known armatolik), Macedonia, Roumeli and the Peloponnese.

Role in the Greek War of Independence

When the Greek War of Independence began in 1821, the armatoloi and klephts formed the core of the Greek irregular forces. Many famous independence leaders — including Theodoros Kolokotronis, Georgios Karaiskakis, Odysseas Androutsos, Athanasios Diakos and Markos Botsaris — came from armatolos or klepht families. Their guerrilla tactics were central to Greek resistance against Ottoman armies.
