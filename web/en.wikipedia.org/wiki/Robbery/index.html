Robbery

Robbery is the crime of taking or attempting to take anything of value by force, threat of force, or by use of fear. According to common law, robbery is defined in many jurisdictions as taking with intent to deprive, from a person or in their presence, by violence or intimidation. Unlike theft or burglary, robbery requires a victim present and force directed at a person, making it a violent crime.

Elements and degrees

Elements typically are: (1) a taking and carrying away (asportation), (2) of tangible personal property of another, (3) from the person or presence of the victim, (4) by force, violence or intimidation, (5) with intent to permanently deprive. Armed robbery (with a weapon), aggravated robbery and carjacking carry higher penalties. In England and Wales, robbery under section 8 of the Theft Act 1968 carries a maximum of life imprisonment.

Types

  • Street / mugging, bank robbery, commercial robbery, home-invasion robbery
  • Highway robbery (historical), train and armoured-car robbery
  • Piracy is sometimes described as robbery at sea

Statistics and law

Robbery rates are tracked by the FBI Uniform Crime Reports in the U.S. Robbery is a predicate act under RICO and, federally, bank robbery and Hobbs Act robbery are federal offences. It is distinct from blackmail / extortion, where the threat is future or reputational rather than immediate force.
