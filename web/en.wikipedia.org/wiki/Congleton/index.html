Congleton is a market town and civil parish in Cheshire East, Cheshire, England, on the River Dane, about 34 kilometres south of Manchester and 13 kilometres north of Stoke-on-Trent. It lies on the edge of the Cheshire Plain, below the Pennine foothills.

History

Congleton is recorded in the Domesday Book as Cogeltone. It received a market charter in the 1270s and later became known for textile manufacture, particularly silk throwing and ribbon weaving, and for glove-making. The Macclesfield Canal, opened in 1831, and the railway in the nineteenth century served its mills. The town was historically nicknamed "Beartown" after a legend, dating to the seventeenth century, that the town's bear-baiting fund was used to buy a Bible; a bear remains part of the town's civic identity and a local folk song preserves the story.

Landmarks and culture

Notable buildings include Congleton Town Hall (1866), St Peter's Church, and the Tudor Black Lion inn. Nearby stands Little Moreton Hall, a celebrated moated half-timbered manor house managed by the National Trust, and Biddulph Grange Garden. The town is also home to Congleton Park, laid out in Victorian times along the Dane.
