General James Murray (21 January 1721 – 18 June 1794) was a British Army officer and colonial administrator who served as the first British Governor of Quebec. A veteran of the Seven Years' War in North America, he was one of James Wolfe's three brigade commanders at the capture of Quebec in 1759.

Early career and the conquest of Canada

The fifth son of Alexander Murray, 4th Lord Elibank, Murray first served with Dutch and Scots forces in the Netherlands before obtaining a British commission. He fought at Cartagena and in Flanders, then in Canada: he commanded a brigade at the siege of Louisbourg in 1758, and at Quebec in 1759 his troops held the centre at the Battle of the Plains of Abraham. After Wolfe's death Murray, as senior surviving commander, took possession of Quebec City and defended it through a harsh winter until the French victory at the Battle of Sainte-Foy (April 1760) forced him back inside the walls; the arrival of British ships in May decided the campaign, and Murray led the advance that received the capitulation of Montreal in September 1760.

Governor of Quebec

Murray was military governor of the Quebec district from 1760 and civil Governor of the Province of Quebec from 1763 (formally appointed 1764) to 1768. He favoured conciliation with the French Canadian majority: he resisted pressure from British merchants to impose an exclusively Protestant English legal order, retained much French civil law and seigneurial custom, and protected the Roman Catholic Church — policies that made him enemies among the merchants, who lobbied successfully for his recall. He was exonerated by the Privy Council, and his approach broadly anticipated the Quebec Act of 1774.

Minorca and later life

As Governor of Minorca (1778–1782), Murray defended Fort St Philip through a seven-month Franco-Spanish siege during the American War of Independence, surrendering only when scurvy had incapacitated his garrison; he was acquitted of charges brought by his deputy and promoted to full general. He died at Beauport House, near Battle in Sussex, in 1794.
