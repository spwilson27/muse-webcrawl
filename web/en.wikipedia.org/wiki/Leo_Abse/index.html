Leo Abse (22 April 1917 – 19 August 2008) was a Welsh Labour politician and solicitor who served as a Member of Parliament for nearly thirty years and was a leading parliamentary campaigner for social and legal reform in Britain, most notably the decriminalisation of homosexuality.

Career

Born in Cardiff to a Jewish family of Polish and German descent, Abse studied law at the London School of Economics, served in the RAF during the Second World War, and built a successful solicitors' practice in Cardiff. He was elected MP for Pontypool in a 1958 by-election and held the seat (later Torfaen) until retiring in 1987.

Reform campaigns

Drawing on his legal practice and an interest in psychoanalysis, Abse championed a series of private member's bills: reform of the divorce laws, and above all the Sexual Offences Bill, which — after years of attempts — passed in 1967 as the Sexual Offences Act, partially decriminalising homosexual acts between consenting adults in England and Wales. He remained a flamboyant Commons figure, noted for his colourful dress and psychobiographical writings on politicians, including studies of Margaret Thatcher and Tony Blair.
