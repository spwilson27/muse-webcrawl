Booker T. and the M.G.'s

Booker T. and the M.G.'s are an American instrumental R&B/funk band that was influential in shaping the sound of Southern soul and Memphis soul. The band is best known for their 1962 hit single "Green Onions" and for serving as the house band of Stax Records, backing artists including Otis Redding, Sam & Dave, Carla Thomas, and Albert King.

Members and history

The original lineup consisted of Booker T. Jones (organ, piano), Steve Cropper (guitar), Lewis Steinberg and later Donald "Duck" Dunn (bass), and Al Jackson Jr. (drums). The initials "M.G.'s" are commonly said to stand for "Memphis Group." Formed in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1962, the racially integrated group—two Black and two white musicians—was unusual for its time and place.

Beyond their own instrumental hits such as "Hip Hug-Her," "Soul Limbo," and "Time Is Tight," the band members were prolific session musicians and songwriters. Booker T. and the M.G.'s were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1992 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2007.
