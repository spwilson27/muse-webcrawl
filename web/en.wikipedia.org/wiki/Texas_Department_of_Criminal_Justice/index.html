The Texas Department of Criminal Justice (TDCJ) is a department of the government of the U.S. state of Texas. It is responsible for statewide criminal justice for adult offenders, including operating state prisons and state jails, supervising parole and mandatory supervision releasees, and administering community supervision (probation) funding and standards. Its headquarters are in Huntsville, which has been the centre of the Texas prison system since the first penitentiary opened there in 1849.

History

The modern department was created in 1989, when the Texas Department of Corrections, the Board of Pardons and Paroles, and the Adult Probation Commission were consolidated. The system traces its origins to the Huntsville Unit ("Walls Unit"), opened in 1849. In the 20th century the Texas system was notable for its agricultural "prison farm" model and for the landmark Ruiz v. Estelle litigation (1980), in which federal courts found conditions unconstitutional and imposed sweeping reforms.

Operations

TDCJ's Correctional Institutions Division operates more than 100 facilities across Texas, from maximum-security units to state jails and transfer facilities, housing well over 100,000 incarcerated people — one of the largest prison populations of any U.S. state. The department administers the state's death row at the Polunsky Unit and carries out executions at the Huntsville Unit; Texas has carried out more executions than any other U.S. state since 1976. The Parole Division supervises offenders released to parole and mandatory supervision, while the Community Justice Assistance Division oversees local probation departments.

Organisation and issues

The nine-member Texas Board of Criminal Justice, appointed by the governor, governs the agency and appoints its executive director. Recurring policy issues include staffing shortages among correctional officers, extreme heat in largely non-air-conditioned facilities (the subject of litigation), and the cost and scale of incarceration in Texas.
