Entry/European Travel Information and Authorisation System

The European Travel Information and Authorisation System (ETIAS) is a planned electronic authorisation system of the European Union for visa-exempt visitors travelling to the Schengen Area and other European countries. Modelled in part on systems such as the United States ESTA, it is intended to strengthen security checks on travellers who do not currently require a visa for short stays.

How it works

Before travelling, visa-exempt visitors will complete an online application giving personal information, travel document details and answers to questions on background, criminal convictions and travel to conflict zones. Applications are automatically checked against EU databases including SIS, VIS, Europol data and Interpol databases. Most authorisations are expected to be issued within minutes; applications that trigger a hit undergo manual processing by the ETIAS Central Unit and national units.

Scope and timetable

ETIAS will apply to nationals of around 60 visa-exempt countries and territories entering 30 European countries for short stays of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. An ETIAS authorisation is not a visa and does not guarantee entry; border guards retain the final decision. The system's launch has been postponed several times and is linked to the prior start of the Entry/Exit System, with start dates progressively moved into the mid-2020s.
