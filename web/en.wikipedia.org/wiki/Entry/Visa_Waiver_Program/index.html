Visa Waiver Program

The Visa Waiver Program (VWP) is a program of the United States federal government that allows citizens of specific countries to travel to the United States for tourism, business or transit for stays of 90 days or less without obtaining a visa. It is administered by the Department of Homeland Security in consultation with the Department of State.

Requirements

Travellers must hold an e-passport with an electronic chip, obtain advance authorisation through the Electronic System for Travel Authorization (ESTA) before departure, and carry a return or onward ticket. Entry under the VWP waives the traveller's right to appeal an immigration officer's decision or, with limited exceptions, to change status or extend stay. Participating countries must in turn offer reciprocal visa-free travel to U.S. citizens, meet passport security standards, share security information and maintain low visa refusal and overstay rates.

Participating countries

More than forty countries take part, including most European Union member states, the United Kingdom, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Chile, Israel and Qatar. Poland joined in 2019 and Croatia in 2021, following years of effort to meet the statutory refusal-rate thresholds.

Security provisions

Following the November 2015 Paris attacks, the Visa Waiver Program Improvement and Terrorist Travel Prevention Act restricted VWP use by nationals of participating countries who had also travelled to certain states such as Iran, Iraq, Syria or Sudan, or who hold dual nationality of those states, requiring them to obtain regular visas instead.
