European migrant crisis

The European migrant crisis, also known as the refugee crisis, was a period beginning in 2015 characterised by a sharp rise in the number of people arriving in the European Union, travelling across the Mediterranean Sea or overland through Southeast Europe. Most arrivals were asylum seekers fleeing war and persecution, principally from Syria, Afghanistan and Iraq, alongside migrants from Africa and South Asia seeking safety or better lives.

Scale and routes

More than a million people crossed into Europe by sea in 2015 alone, according to the UN refugee agency, and thousands died attempting the journey — the photograph of the drowned Syrian toddler Alan Kurdi in September 2015 became an emblem of the crisis. The principal routes were the Eastern Mediterranean route from Turkey to the Greek islands and the Central Mediterranean route from Libya to Italy; many then moved north via the Western Balkan route toward Germany and Sweden.

Responses

Germany, under Chancellor Angela Merkel, suspended Dublin Regulation returns for Syrians in August 2015 and ultimately received over a million asylum applications in 2015–2016. The EU struggled to agree on relocation quotas, several states erected border fences, and in March 2016 the EU concluded a statement with Turkey under which irregular arrivals to Greece could be returned in exchange for financial aid and resettlement places — after which Aegean crossings fell dramatically.

Consequences

The crisis reshaped European politics, energising populist and anti-immigration parties, contributing to the debate around the United Kingdom's Brexit referendum, and prompting long-running reforms of the Common European Asylum System.
