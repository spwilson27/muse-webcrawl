Schengen Agreement

Signed14 June 1985
LocationNear Schengen, Luxembourg
Original partiesBelgium, France, Luxembourg, Netherlands, West Germany

The Schengen Agreement is a treaty which led to the creation of Europe's Schengen Area, in which internal border checks have largely been abolished. It was signed on 14 June 1985, near the town of Schengen, Luxembourg, by five of the ten member states of the then European Economic Community.

History

The agreement proposed the gradual abolition of border checks at the signatories' common borders. The 1990 Schengen Convention supplemented it with provisions on police cooperation, a common visa policy, and the Schengen Information System. The rules were later incorporated into European Union law by the Treaty of Amsterdam.

Schengen Area

The Schengen Area now comprises most EU member states plus Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, and Liechtenstein, allowing over 400 million people to travel without internal border controls, subject to temporary reintroductions in exceptional circumstances.
