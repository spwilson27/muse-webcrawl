Entry/Schengen Area

Entry to the Schengen Area by third-country nationals is governed by the Schengen Borders Code, which sets uniform conditions for crossing the external borders of the 29-country Schengen Area, within which internal border controls have been abolished. Travellers entering for a short stay must hold a valid travel document, hold a visa if required, justify the purpose and conditions of their stay, have sufficient means of subsistence, and not be the subject of an alert in the Schengen Information System or considered a threat to public policy or security.

Entry conditions and checks

At border crossing points, third-country nationals undergo a thorough check: verification of the travel document, consultation of the Visa Information System where applicable, and checks against databases for stolen documents and alerts. Until the Entry/Exit System becomes fully operational, entry and exit are recorded by stamps placed in passports; the EES will replace stamping with an electronic record including biometric identifiers, and will calculate automatically whether a traveller has exceeded the authorised stay.

Refusal of entry

A traveller who does not fulfil the entry conditions is refused entry by a substantiated decision stating the precise reasons, issued on a standard form, with a right of appeal under national law. Refusals are recorded, and carriers transporting passengers without proper documentation may be sanctioned under carrier-liability rules.
