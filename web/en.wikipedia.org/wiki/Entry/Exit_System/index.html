Entry/Exit System

AbbreviationEES
TypeBorder management database
Operatoreu-LISA
Applies toThird-country nationals entering the Schengen Area

The Entry/Exit System (EES) is a European Union information system that automatically registers the entry and exit of third-country nationals travelling to the Schengen Area for short stays, replacing the manual stamping of passports. It records the traveller's name, travel document, date and place of entry and exit, and biometric data (a facial image and fingerprints), in order to calculate the duration of authorised stay and identify overstayers.

Background and roll-out

The EES was established by Regulation (EU) 2017/2226 as part of the EU's "smart borders" package, together with the European Travel Information and Authorisation System (ETIAS). It is operated by the EU agency eu-LISA, which also runs the Visa Information System and Eurodac. After several postponements from an originally planned 2022 launch, the system was scheduled for a progressive roll-out beginning in October 2025, with border crossing points phasing in registration over about six months before full operation.

Operation

On first entry, a traveller's file is created and biometrics are captured; on later crossings the system verifies identity and checks the remaining authorised stay (normally 90 days in any 180-day period for visa-exempt travellers). Data are retained for three years (longer for overstayers) and are accessible to border, visa and law-enforcement authorities under defined conditions. The system has been criticised by privacy advocates and caused concern about queues at busy crossing points such as the Port of Dover.
