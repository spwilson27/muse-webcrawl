Google DeepMind

Google DeepMind is a British-American artificial intelligence research laboratory which serves as a subsidiary of Alphabet Inc. It was founded in London in 2010 as DeepMind Technologies by Demis Hassabis, Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman, acquired by Google in 2014, and merged with Google's Brain team in April 2023 to form Google DeepMind. The company is known for creating neural networks that learn to play video games and board games at superhuman levels, for the protein-structure prediction system AlphaFold, and for the Gemini family of large language models.

History

DeepMind's early work on deep reinforcement learning produced agents that learned Atari games from raw pixels (2013). Google acquired the company in January 2014, in a deal reported at over £400 million, and an ethics board was established as part of the terms. In October 2015 its program AlphaGo became the first computer program to defeat a professional human Go player, and in March 2016 it beat Lee Sedol, one of the world's strongest players, 4–1 in a widely watched match in Seoul. Successors AlphaGo Zero (2017) and AlphaZero mastered Go, chess and shogi through self-play alone.

Research achievements

AlphaFold 2 effectively solved the protein structure prediction problem at CASP14 in 2020, and DeepMind subsequently released predicted structures for more than 200 million proteins; Demis Hassabis and John Jumper shared part of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for this work. Other notable systems include AlphaStar (StarCraft II), MuZero, WaveNet for speech synthesis, AlphaCode for programming, Gato, and the Gemini multimodal models, which became Google's flagship AI models from December 2023.

Organisation

Headquartered at King's Cross, London, with research centres in the United States, Canada and elsewhere, Google DeepMind is led by co-founder Demis Hassabis as CEO. The lab also works on AI safety, AI for science (including weather forecasting with GraphCast, materials discovery with GNoME, and fusion plasma control), and reducing the energy use of Google's data centres.
