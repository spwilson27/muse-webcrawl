Antonine Wall

Antonine Wall
Built: from AD 142, under Antoninus Pius
Length: 63 km (39 mi), Forth to Clyde
UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2008

The Antonine Wall was a turf and timber fortification on stone foundations built by the Romans across the Central Belt of Scotland, between the Firth of Forth and the Firth of Clyde. At about 63 km, it was the empire's most northerly frontier barrier and its most complex.

Construction

Ordered by Emperor Antoninus Pius in AD 142 after campaigns by governor Quintus Lollius Urbicus, the wall took about 12 years to build. Unlike stone Hadrian's Wall to the south, it was a turf rampart about 3 m high fronted by a great ditch, with 16 known forts roughly every 3 km — a tighter spacing than Hadrian's — connected by a Military Way. Distance slabs recorded legionary construction lengths.

Abandonment

The frontier was occupied only briefly: after about 20 years (around AD 162) the Romans withdrew to Hadrian's Wall, for reasons debated — local hostility, overstretch, or imperial politics. A brief reoccupation may have occurred under Septimius Severus.
