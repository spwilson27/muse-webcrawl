Amagasaki rail crash

Date: 25 April 2005
Location: Amagasaki, Hyōgo, Japan
Deaths: 107 (including the driver)
Injured: 562

The Amagasaki rail crash was a fatal train derailment that occurred on 25 April 2005 on the West Japan Railway Company (JR West) Fukuchiyama Line near Amagasaki, Hyōgo Prefecture, Japan. A seven-car commuter train travelling at about 116 km/h on a curve limited to 70 km/h derailed and slammed into a car park and an apartment building, killing 107 people including the driver and injuring 562. It was Japan's most serious rail accident since the 1963 Tsurumi rail crash.

Causes

The official investigation found that the 23-year-old driver, fearing punishment for having overshot a platform at the previous station, accelerated to make up lost time and entered the curve far above its speed limit. The report sharply criticised JR West's culture: a punitive retraining regime known as nikkin kyōiku ("day-shift education"), which humiliated drivers for minor errors, discouraged reporting of mistakes and encouraged drivers to take risks to avoid delay. The line lacked the newer automatic train stop system that could have enforced the curve's speed.

Aftermath

JR West's president resigned, and the accident prompted reforms to driver training, timetable margins and safety investment, as well as the installation of advanced ATS-P automatic braking on the line. Several JR West executives were prosecuted; the company's former president was acquitted in 2012. Annual memorial services are held at the site, part of which has been preserved as a memorial.
