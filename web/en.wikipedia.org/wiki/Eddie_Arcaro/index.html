Eddie Arcaro

BornGeorge Edward Arcaro, February 19, 1916, Cincinnati, Ohio, U.S.
DiedNovember 14, 1997 (aged 81), Miami, Florida, U.S.
OccupationJockey
Major winsKentucky Derby (5), Preakness Stakes (6), Belmont Stakes (6); Triple Crown on Whirlaway (1941) and Citation (1948)

George Edward Arcaro (February 19, 1916 – November 14, 1997), known professionally as Eddie Arcaro, was an American Thoroughbred horse racing jockey who became one of the most successful riders in the sport's history. Nicknamed "the Master," he is the only jockey to have won the American Triple Crown twice.

Career

Arcaro began riding in the early 1930s and won his first race in 1932. He won the Kentucky Derby a record-tying five times, aboard Lawrin (1938), Whirlaway (1941), Hoop Jr. (1945), Citation (1948) and Hill Gail (1952). With Whirlaway and Citation, both trained by Ben Jones for Calumet Farm, he swept the Kentucky Derby, Preakness Stakes and Belmont Stakes to win the Triple Crown in 1941 and 1948.

His six Preakness and six Belmont victories were also records. Over a career of roughly three decades he rode in more than 24,000 races, winning about 4,800 of them, and his mounts earned over $30 million in purses. Fiercely competitive, he was suspended for a year in 1942 for rough riding after attempting to push a rival jockey over the rail.

Later life

Arcaro retired from riding in 1961 and worked as a television commentator and in public relations for a racetrack. He was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1958. He died in Miami in 1997.
