Captain Arthur J. M. Hastings, OBE, is a fictional character created by Agatha Christie as the companion and chronicler of the detective Hercule Poirot. Hastings narrates many of the Poirot novels and short stories in the first person, in the tradition of Dr. Watson in the Sherlock Holmes stories.

Character

Hastings is a former British Army officer who served in the First World War and was invalided out after being wounded. He first meets Poirot again at Styles Court in The Mysterious Affair at Styles (1920), Christie's first novel, having known the Belgian briefly during the war. Good-natured, straightforward and occasionally impulsive, Hastings frequently falls for red-haired women and is often a step behind Poirot's reasoning, providing a foil for the detective's deductions.

Appearances

Hastings appears in eight Poirot novels and several short-story collections, including The Murder on the Links (1923), The Big Four (1927), Peril at End House (1932), The A.B.C. Murders (1936), Lord Edgware Dies (1933), Dumb Witness (1937) and Curtain (1975), in which he returns to Styles for Poirot's final case. After marrying the music-hall performer Dulcie Duveen in The Murder on the Links, he settles in Argentina, returning periodically to assist Poirot.

Portrayals

Hastings was played by Hugh Fraser in the long-running television series Agatha Christie's Poirot (1989–2013) opposite David Suchet.
