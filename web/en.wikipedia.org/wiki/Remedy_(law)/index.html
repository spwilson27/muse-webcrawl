Remedy (law)

In law, a remedy is a court-ordered means of redress for a legal wrong — a breach of a right or a failure to perform a legal obligation. Remedies are generally divided into legal remedies (principally damages) and equitable remedies (such as injunctions and specific performance), reflecting the historical division between the common law courts and the courts of equity.

Legal remedies

The principal legal remedy is an award of damages: a sum of money intended to compensate the claimant for loss suffered. Categories include compensatory damages, punitive (exemplary) damages, nominal damages, and liquidated damages fixed in advance by contract. Restitutionary awards, which strip a defendant of gains made by wrongdoing, are also recognised in some circumstances.

Equitable remedies

Equitable remedies are discretionary and are generally available only where damages would be inadequate. They include injunctions (orders to do or refrain from doing something), specific performance (compelling performance of a contract), rescission of a contract, rectification of a document, and declaratory relief, in which the court states the parties' rights without ordering further relief.
