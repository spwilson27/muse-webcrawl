Barchetta

A barchetta (Italian for "little boat") is a style of open, two-seat sports car body, originally applied to lightweight Italian racing cars of the late 1940s and 1950s. Barchettas typically have no roof, a minimal or absent windscreen, and cut-down doors, prioritising low weight and aerodynamic simplicity over comfort.

Body styleOpen two-seat roadster
OriginItaly, 1940s
Notable examplesFerrari 166 MM, Fiat Barchetta

History

The term was popularised by the Ferrari 166 MM of 1948, whose Touring-built open body journalist Giovanni Canestrini likened to a small boat. Maserati, Alfa Romeo, and OSCA built similar racing barchettas for events such as the Mille Miglia and Targa Florio.

Modern usage

Fiat revived the name for its 1995–2005 front-wheel-drive roadster, the Fiat Barchetta. More recently, manufacturers have used "barchetta" for limited-run, screenless speedster-style specials, such as Ferrari's Monza SP1 and SP2.
