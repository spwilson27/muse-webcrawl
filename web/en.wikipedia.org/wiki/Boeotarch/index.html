Boeotarch

Boeotarch (Ancient Greek: βοιωτάρχης, boiōtarchēs) was the title of the chief magistrates of the Boeotian Confederacy in ancient Greece. The Boeotarchs, elected annually by the federal districts of Boeotia, held both civil and supreme military authority for the league, presiding over the federal council and commanding the confederate army in the field. The office is best known through famous holders such as Epaminondas and Pelopidas, who led Thebes to hegemony over Greece in the 4th century BC.

Constitutional role

The Hellenica Oxyrhynchia describes the Boeotian constitution of the late 5th century BC: Boeotia was divided into eleven districts (merē), each electing one Boeotarch and contributing a fixed contingent of hoplites and cavalry. The Boeotarchs together formed the executive of the federation and advised a federal council of 660 members. After Thebes destroyed Plataea and absorbed its districts, Thebes supplied four of the eleven Boeotarchs, giving it dominance of the league. Following the Theban reorganisation after 379 BC, the number became seven.

Famous Boeotarchs

Epaminondas held the office repeatedly, leading Boeotian forces to victory over Sparta at Leuctra (371 BC) and Mantinea (362 BC), where he fell in battle — having once been fined for retaining his command beyond his term to complete a campaign. Pelopidas, commander of the Sacred Band, served alongside him. The Boeotarch Issidas and others are mentioned in connection with the defence of Boeotia during the Third Sacred War.

Later history

The federation and its magistrates continued in reduced form through the Hellenistic period under Macedonian and later Roman domination, with Boeotarchs attested in inscriptions into the imperial era.
