Aspergillum

Type: Liturgical implement

Used for: Sprinkling holy water

Traditions: Roman Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, Orthodox

An aspergillum (plural aspergilla or aspergillums) is a liturgical implement used to sprinkle holy water. It comes in several forms: a brush dipped in the water stoup and shaken; a silver ball on a stick containing a sponge; or a perforated ball or brush attached to a handle, often used together with a bucket of holy water called a situla.

Use

The aspergillum is used in Roman Catholic, Anglo-Catholic, Lutheran and some other services for blessings, at funerals, at the Easter Vigil, and when blessing objects, buildings or congregations with holy water. The rite of sprinkling is called asperges, recalling the antiphon from Psalm 51, "Asperges me, Domine" ("You shall sprinkle me, Lord"). In the Eastern Orthodox tradition a brush (often called a kropilo) is used similarly, especially at Theophany (see asphyxia for an unrelated topic).

Etymology

The name comes from the Latin aspergere, "to sprinkle". The fungus genus Aspergillus was named by Pier Antonio Micheli in 1729 because its spore-bearing head resembles an aspergillum.
