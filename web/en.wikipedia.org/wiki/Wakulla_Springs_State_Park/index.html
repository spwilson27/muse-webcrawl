Edward Ball Wakulla Springs State Park

Edward Ball Wakulla Springs State Park is a Florida State Park located near Wakulla, south of Tallahassee. Its centrepiece, Wakulla Springs, is one of the largest and deepest freshwater springs in the world, discharging enormous volumes of water from the Floridan aquifer into the Wakulla River.

The spring

The spring vent lies roughly 55 metres (180 feet) below the surface, and cave divers have explored an extensive network of flooded tunnels connected to the spring. Water emerges at a constant temperature of about 21 °C (70 °F) year-round, attracting manatees in winter. Glass-bottom boat tours and river cruises have operated at the springs for generations, showcasing fish, alligators, birds and other wildlife.

History

The area was inhabited by Paleo-Indians thousands of years ago; mastodon bones have been recovered from the spring basin. In the 1930s financier Edward Ball purchased the property and built the Wakulla Springs Lodge, opened in 1937, which still operates as a hotel and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The springs served as a filming location for movies including Creature from the Black Lagoon (1954) and several Tarzan films. Florida acquired the property in the 1980s, and it became a state park in 1986.
