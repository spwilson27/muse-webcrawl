The Archdiocese of Uppsala (Swedish: Uppsala ärkestift) is a diocese of the Church of Sweden, and historically the archdiocese of the Catholic Church in Sweden. Its seat is Uppsala Cathedral, and the Archbishop of Uppsala is the primate of the Church of Sweden.

History

Uppsala was an early centre of Christianity in Sweden; tradition places a bishopric at (Old) Uppsala in the 11th century, and the see was established as a diocese in the 1130s, subject at first to the Archbishop of Lund. In 1164, Pope Alexander III elevated Uppsala to an archdiocese, with Stefan, a Cistercian monk from Alvastra Abbey, as its first archbishop. The medieval archbishops of Uppsala were leading figures in the kingdom, and the archbishop at times held the office of regent.

The cathedral was moved from Old Uppsala to its present site in Östra Aros (modern Uppsala) in the 1270s. With the Swedish Reformation under Gustav Vasa in the 1520s–1530s, the archdiocese became Lutheran; Laurentius Petri, consecrated archbishop in 1531, is considered the first Lutheran Archbishop of Uppsala. Since then the archbishop has remained the senior bishop of the Swedish church, though the archdiocese is today one of thirteen dioceses in the Church of Sweden, and the archbishop no longer exercises jurisdiction over the others.

Extent

The modern diocese covers Uppsala County, much of Stockholm County (outside the Diocese of Stockholm), and parts of surrounding counties, organised into deaneries and parishes. The archbishop also serves as diocesan bishop of the Uppsala diocese itself.
