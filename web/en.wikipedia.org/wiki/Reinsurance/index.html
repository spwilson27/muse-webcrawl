Reinsurance

Reinsurance is insurance that an insurance company purchases from another insurance company to insulate itself against the risk of a major claims event. With reinsurance, the insurer passes on ("cedes") some part of or all of its liabilities to the reinsurer, diversifying its portfolio risk and stabilising its results.

Types

Reinsurance contracts are broadly either facultative, covering a specific individual risk, or treaty, covering a defined portfolio of policies automatically. Treaties may be proportional (quota share or surplus share, in which premiums and losses are shared in a fixed proportion) or non-proportional, the most common form being excess-of-loss cover, under which the reinsurer pays claims above an agreed retention up to a limit. Major global reinsurers include Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re, and Berkshire Hathaway's reinsurance operations; the Lloyd's market in London is also a major centre.

Functions

Reinsurance increases insurers' underwriting capacity, protects against catastrophic losses such as hurricanes and earthquakes, stabilises earnings, and can provide regulatory capital relief.
