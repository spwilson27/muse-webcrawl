Foreign internal defense

Foreign internal defense (FID) is a term used by the United States armed forces for programmes in which US military and civilian agencies assist a foreign (host) nation government in defending itself against insurgency, subversion, lawlessness and terrorism, primarily by building the host nation's own capacity rather than fighting directly.

Activities

FID encompasses training, advising, equipping and assisting host-nation military and security forces; intelligence sharing; civil affairs and psychological operations in support of the host government; and support to governance and development intended to address the causes of instability. In US doctrine it is a core mission of special operations forces, particularly the Army Special Forces ("Green Berets"), though conventional forces and civilian agencies also participate.

Doctrine

US doctrine emphasises that internal defence is fundamentally the host nation's responsibility; external support should be indirect where possible, coordinated through the US ambassador's country team, and long-term in character. FID differs from direct-action counterinsurgency by US combat forces, though programmes can evolve toward combat support roles, as seen during the Vietnam War (where advisory efforts under programmes such as MAAG preceded full-scale intervention) and in El Salvador in the 1980s.
