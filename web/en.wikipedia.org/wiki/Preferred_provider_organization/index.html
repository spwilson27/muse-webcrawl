Preferred provider organization

A preferred provider organization (PPO) is a type of managed-care health insurance plan in the United States. PPOs contract with networks of physicians, hospitals and other providers who agree to discounted rates; plan members pay less when they use in-network ("preferred") providers but may also use out-of-network providers at higher cost.

Features

Unlike a health maintenance organization (HMO), a PPO normally does not require members to select a primary-care physician or to obtain referrals before seeing specialists. Cost sharing typically combines an annual deductible, copayments or coinsurance, and an out-of-pocket maximum, with separate, higher limits for out-of-network care. For these reasons PPOs offer greater flexibility at generally higher premiums than HMOs.

History and prevalence

PPOs emerged in the early 1980s as a looser form of managed care, encouraged by state enabling laws. They became the most common type of employer-sponsored health plan in the United States in the 2000s and 2010s, enrolling a majority of covered workers, though high-deductible plans paired with health savings accounts have grown at their expense.
