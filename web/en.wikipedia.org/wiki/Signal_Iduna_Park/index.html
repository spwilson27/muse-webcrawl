Signal Iduna Park

Signal Iduna Park, officially named through a sponsorship agreement and historically known as the Westfalenstadion, is a football stadium in Dortmund, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. It is the home ground of Bundesliga club Borussia Dortmund and, with a league capacity of 81,365, is the largest football stadium in Germany.

History

The stadium was built for the 1974 FIFA World Cup and expanded in several phases, most notably for the 2006 World Cup, when its distinctive yellow pylons and a glass front were added. Its south stand, the Südtribüne, holds about 24,450 standing spectators and is the largest free-standing grandstand of its kind in Europe; known as the "Yellow Wall" (Gelbe Wand), it is famous for the choreographed displays of Dortmund supporters.

Naming

In December 2005 the naming rights were sold to the insurance company Signal Iduna, and the stadium formally became Signal Iduna Park; many fans and some media continue to call it the Westfalenstadion. For UEFA competitions it is known as the BVB Stadion Dortmund.
