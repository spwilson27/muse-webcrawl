Takahashi Chikuzan

Born1910, Kanagi (now Goshogawara), Aomori, Japan
DiedFebruary 5, 1998, Hiraka, Aomori, Japan
OccupationTsugaru-jamisen musician
InstrumentShamisen

Takahashi Chikuzan (高橋 竹山, 1910 – February 5, 1998) was a Japanese musician renowned as a master of the Tsugaru-jamisen style of shamisen playing. Blind from early childhood, he spent much of his life as an itinerant bosama — a blind street and door-to-door performer in the Tsugaru region of Aomori Prefecture — before achieving national fame late in life and becoming the best-known exponent of Tsugaru shamisen in Japan.

Life

Born Takahashi Sadazō in Kanagi, Aomori, he lost his sight at about the age of two following measles. His mother, concerned for his future, apprenticed him as a teenager to the shamisen player and singer Todoroki, a bosama, from whom he took the professional name Chikuzan. For decades he travelled the Tsugaru countryside, performing at farmhouse doorways in exchange for food and small payments, enduring the harsh winters of northern Honshu.

Fame came in the 1960s and 1970s as folk culture was revalued in Japan. His rough, improvisational playing — quite different from the polished competition style that had developed in his absence — attracted wide attention. He performed in concert halls, appeared on television, and toured internationally. His autobiography, Tsugaru-jamisen Journey (Tsugaru jamisen ichidai), became well known.

Style and legacy

Chikuzan's playing is noted for its powerful attack, improvisation within traditional pieces such as "Tsugaru Jongara Bushi", and a raw, weathered tone shaped by his years of outdoor performance. He is credited with restoring public attention to the folk roots of Tsugaru-jamisen and influencing generations of players, from traditional performers to rock- and jazz-influenced shamisen musicians.
