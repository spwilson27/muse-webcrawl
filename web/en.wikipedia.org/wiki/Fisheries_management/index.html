Fisheries management

Fisheries management is the activity of protecting fishery resources so sustainable exploitation is possible, drawing on fisheries science and possibly including precautionary principles. Modern fisheries management is often referred to as a system of appropriate environmental management rules based on a series of data gathering and management objectives, implemented through a system of management rules and monitored and enforced through a system of control and surveillance.

According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), there are "no clear and generally accepted definitions of fisheries management". Management aims typically include maintaining fish stocks at levels capable of producing the maximum sustainable yield, protecting marine ecosystems, and sustaining the livelihoods of fishing communities.

Mechanisms

Many countries have set up Ministries or agencies charged with fisheries management. Common management measures include:

  • Catch quotas — limits such as a total allowable catch (TAC) for a stock, sometimes allocated as individual fishing quotas.
  • Effort controls — restrictions on vessel numbers, days at sea, or gear.
  • Technical measures — minimum mesh sizes, minimum landing sizes, and closed areas or seasons.
  • Marine protected areas — zones where fishing is restricted or prohibited.

Challenges

Fisheries management faces persistent problems including overfishing, illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing, habitat degradation, and the effects of climate change on stock distribution. The shared nature of many stocks, which migrate across national exclusive economic zones, requires international cooperation through regional fisheries management organisations.
