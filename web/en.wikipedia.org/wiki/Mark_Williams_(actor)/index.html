Mark Williams (born 22 August 1959) is an English actor, comedian, presenter and screenwriter. He first came to prominence as one of the stars of the BBC sketch show The Fast Show, reached a worldwide audience as Arthur Weasley in the Harry Potter film series, and has starred as the title character in the BBC One crime drama Father Brown since 2013.

Career

Williams was born in Bromsgrove and studied English at Brasenose College, Oxford. He worked in theatre and as a stand-up comedian before television success. On The Fast Show (1994–1997) he created characters such as the unlucky Jesse ("This week, I 'ave been mostly eating...") as part of the ensemble.

His film roles include 101 Dalmatians (1996), Shakespeare in Love (1998) and Stardust (2007). From Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets (2002) onwards he played the kindly, Muggle-obsessed Arthur Weasley in all but one of the films in the series.

Father Brown and presenting

Since 2013 Williams has played G. K. Chesterton's priest-detective in Father Brown, one of BBC One's most successful daytime dramas, exported around the world. He has also presented numerous documentary series, notably about Britain's industrial history — including Mark Williams' Big Bangs on explosives and series on the railways and canals — reflecting his lifelong interest in industrial heritage.

Personal life

Williams lives in Brighton and is a supporter of Brighton & Hove Albion football club. He has written about his passion for industrial archaeology and vintage machinery.
