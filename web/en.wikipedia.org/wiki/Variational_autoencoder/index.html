Variational autoencoder

In machine learning, a variational autoencoder (VAE) is an artificial neural network architecture introduced by Diederik P. Kingma and Max Welling in 2013. It belongs to the families of probabilistic graphical models and variational Bayesian methods. Unlike a standard autoencoder, which encodes each input to a fixed point in latent space, a VAE encodes it to a probability distribution — usually a Gaussian parameterised by a mean and variance — and is trained by maximising a lower bound on the data likelihood, so that sampling from the latent distribution and decoding produces new, plausible data.

Architecture of a VAE: an encoder, a probabilistic latent space, and a decoder.

Training

Training optimises the evidence lower bound (ELBO), which combines a reconstruction term (how well samples decoded from the encoded distribution reproduce the input) with a Kullback–Leibler divergence term that regularises the encoded distributions toward a prior, usually a standard normal. The reparameterisation trick — writing a sample as the mean plus the standard deviation times noise drawn from a fixed distribution — lets gradients flow through the sampling step so the network can be trained with backpropagation.

Applications and variants

VAEs are used for generative modelling of images, text and molecules, for representation learning, anomaly detection and data imputation. Well-known variants include the β-VAE, which weights the KL term to encourage disentangled latent factors; conditional VAEs, which condition generation on labels; and vector-quantised VAEs (VQ-VAE), which use a discrete codebook of latent embeddings.
