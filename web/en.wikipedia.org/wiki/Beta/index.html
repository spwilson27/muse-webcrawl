Beta (UK: /ˈbiːtə/, US: /ˈbeɪtə/; uppercase Β, lowercase β, cursive ϐ; Greek: βήτα) is the second letter of the Greek alphabet. In the system of Greek numerals it has a value of 2. Beta is derived from the Phoenician letter beth, and it gave rise to the Latin letter B and the Cyrillic letters В and Б.

Pronunciation and history

In Ancient Greek, beta represented the voiced bilabial plosive /b/. In Modern Greek it is pronounced as the voiced labiodental fricative /v/, a change reflected in the alternative name "vita". Words beginning with beta are commonly transliterated with b in English, giving words such as "bibliography" and "biology" elements derived from Greek roots, while the letter itself anchors the word "alphabet" (alpha + beta).

Uses

Beta is used widely as a symbol in science and finance. In physics, β denotes a beta particle or velocity as a fraction of the speed of light (v/c). In statistics, β is a regression coefficient and the probability of a Type II error. In finance, beta measures an asset's volatility relative to the market. Software nearing release but still in testing is called a "beta version", following "alpha". In the International Phonetic Alphabet, [β] represents the voiced bilabial fricative.
