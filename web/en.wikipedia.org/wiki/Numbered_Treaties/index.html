Numbered Treaties

The Numbered Treaties (or Post-Confederation Treaties) are a series of eleven treaties signed between the First Nations peoples in Canada and the reigning monarch of Canada (the Crown) from 1871 to 1921. They were created to allow the Government of Canada to pursue settlement, agriculture, mining and resource development of the Canadian West and North, in exchange for reserve lands, annuities, and certain rights and goods promised to the signatory First Nations. The treaties cover territory from northwestern Ontario across the Prairies to the Rocky Mountains, and north into the Northwest Territories and northeastern British Columbia.

The treaties

Treaties 1 and 2 (1871) covered Manitoba and the North-West Territories; Treaty 3 (1873, the North-West Angle Treaty) secured the route west from Ontario; Treaty 4 (1874) covered southern Saskatchewan; Treaty 5 (1875) the Lake Winnipeg region; Treaty 6 (1876) central Saskatchewan and Alberta; Treaty 7 (1877) southern Alberta; Treaty 8 (1899), the largest, covered northern Alberta, northeastern British Columbia and the southern Northwest Territories; Treaty 9 (1905, the James Bay Treaty) covered northern Ontario; Treaty 10 (1906) covered northern Saskatchewan; and Treaty 11 (1921) covered much of the Northwest Territories.

Interpretation and legacy

The meaning of the treaties is contested: the written texts record land surrenders, while First Nations oral histories maintain that their ancestors agreed to share the land, not to cede it outright, and did not surrender their governance or rights to hunt and fish. Treaty rights are protected by section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982, and the Numbered Treaties remain living agreements at the centre of Crown–Indigenous relations, land claims, and litigation in Canada.
