Mess hall

A mess hall, or dining facility (DFAC in United States military usage), is a room or building where military personnel, and members of similar uniformed or residential organisations, eat meals together. The term derives from "mess", an old word for a group of people who regularly eat together and for a portion of food, ultimately from the Latin missus, a course at a meal.

A military dining facility

Usage and organisation

Mess halls range from the formal officers' mess, with traditions such as formal mess dinners, toasting, and prescribed etiquette, to large cafeteria-style facilities serving enlisted personnel on bases and aboard ships. In the British and Commonwealth armed forces, the mess is an institution as much as a place: officers, warrant officers and senior non-commissioned officers, and junior ranks each have their own messes, which serve as dining rooms, bars, and social clubs governed by mess committees. Aboard warships, dining spaces are traditionally called messdecks, and at sea sailors traditionally ate in small groups ("messes") organised around a single table.

Beyond the military, the term is used for dining halls at boarding schools, summer camps (particularly in North America), prisons, and some workplaces and expeditions. Modern military dining facilities typically operate as cafeterias with a serving line, salad and hot bars, and set mealtimes, and are staffed by service cooks or civilian contractors.
