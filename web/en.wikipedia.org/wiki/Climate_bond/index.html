Climate bond

Climate bond
Type: Fixed-income instrument (green bond)
First issue: 2007 (European Investment Bank "Climate Awareness Bond")
Standard: Climate Bonds Standard (Climate Bonds Initiative)
Use of proceeds: Mitigation and adaptation projects

A climate bond is a bond issued by a government, bank, corporation or other entity to raise finance for climate change solutions — projects in mitigation (reducing greenhouse gas emissions) or adaptation (building resilience to climate impacts). Climate bonds are a subset of green bonds, restricted to climate-relevant assets and projects.

History

The European Investment Bank issued the first climate bond, its "Climate Awareness Bond", in 2007, followed by the World Bank's first "green bond" in 2008. The market grew rapidly in the 2010s as investors sought environmentally screened assets, with annual issuance of labelled green and climate bonds reaching hundreds of billions of dollars.

Certification

The Climate Bonds Initiative, an investor-focused non-profit, maintains the Climate Bonds Standard and sector criteria (for energy, transport, water, buildings and other areas) against which bonds can be certified. Certification requires that proceeds be earmarked for eligible low-carbon assets and that issuers report on their use. Common financed projects include renewable energy, energy efficiency, clean transport, and water and waste infrastructure.

Market and criticism

Issuers range from sovereign states and development banks to municipalities and corporations. Critics have raised concerns about "greenwashing" — bonds whose proceeds fund marginal or business-as-usual activities — which has driven efforts toward regulation, taxonomies such as the EU Taxonomy, and third-party verification.
