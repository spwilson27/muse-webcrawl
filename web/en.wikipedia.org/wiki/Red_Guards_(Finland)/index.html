Red Guards (Finland)

The Red Guards (Finnish: Punakaarti) were the paramilitary units of the Finnish labour movement in 1917–1918, forming the military force of the socialist Finnish People's Delegation during the Finnish Civil War of 1918. Opposed to them were the White Guards, who fought for the government of the Senate of Finland.

Origins

Workers' order guards were first formed during the general strike of 1905 and were revived in 1917 amid the collapse of Russian imperial authority in the Grand Duchy of Finland. Through 1917, local Red Guards multiplied as political polarisation deepened and Russian troops stationed in Finland became unreliable; many guards received weapons and training from sympathetic Russian soldiers.

Civil War

When civil war broke out in January 1918, the Reds seized Helsinki and much of industrial southern Finland. At their peak the Red Guards numbered perhaps 80,000–100,000 men and some women, but they lacked experienced officers, artillery and logistical capacity, and depended on supplies from Soviet Russia. Commanders included Kullervo Manner, Eero Haapalainen and Ali Aaltonen.

The Whites under General C. G. E. Mannerheim, supported by German troops who landed in April 1918, defeated the Reds by mid-May. In the war's aftermath, thousands of Red Guards died in battle and in reprisals; over 70,000 Reds were imprisoned in camps, where roughly 12,000 perished from disease, malnutrition and executions in the summer of 1918. Many surviving leaders fled to Soviet Russia, where some were later purged under Stalin.

Legacy

The Red Guards' defeat shaped Finnish politics for decades; commemoration of the Red dead remained divisive, and only gradually were Red graves and memorials publicly acknowledged in the post-war decades.
