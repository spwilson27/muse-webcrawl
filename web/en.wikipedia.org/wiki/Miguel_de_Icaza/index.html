Miguel de Icaza

Miguel de Icaza (born 1972) is a Mexican-American programmer known for starting the GNOME and Mono projects. Born in Mexico City, he studied at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) but did not graduate, beginning his career in free software in the early 1990s.

Career

De Icaza first gained prominence for the Midnight Commander file manager and contributions to the Linux kernel and the GIMP. In 1997, together with Federico Mena, he co-founded the GNOME project to create a fully free desktop environment for Unix-like systems after concerns about the licensing of KDE's Qt toolkit.

In 1999 he co-founded Helix Code (later Ximian) with Nat Friedman. Ximian developed the Evolution mail client and launched the Mono project in 2001, an open-source implementation of Microsoft's .NET platform. Ximian was acquired by Novell in 2003; de Icaza became Novell's vice-president of developer platforms. After Attachmate acquired Novell in 2011, he founded Xamarin to continue Mono-based mobile development products. Xamarin was acquired by Microsoft in 2016, and de Icaza joined Microsoft, working on mobile developer tools. He left Microsoft in 2022.

Recognition

He received the Free Software Foundation Award for the Advancement of Free Software in 1999 and was named a TR35 innovator by MIT Technology Review. He appeared as himself in the 2001 film Antitrust and was interviewed in the documentary Revolution OS.
