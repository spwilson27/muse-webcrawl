Necronomicon

The Necronomicon, also referred to as the Book of the Dead, or by its supposed Arabic title Kitab al-Azif, is a fictional grimoire (textbook of magic) appearing in stories by the American horror writer H. P. Lovecraft and his followers. It was first mentioned in Lovecraft's 1924 short story "The Hound", written in 1922.

The fictional grimoire has inspired numerous real-world hoax editions.

Fictional history

Within Lovecraft's fiction, the book was written in the 8th century by the "mad Arab" Abdul Alhazred of Damascus under the title al-Azif — azif being a word for the nocturnal sound of insects, imagined as the howling of demons. It was translated into Greek in 950 AD by Theodorus Philetas, who gave it the title Necronomicon, and into Latin in 1228 by Olaus Wormius. Copies were supposedly kept under lock and key in a handful of libraries, including that of fictional Miskatonic University in Arkham, Massachusetts. Reading it is portrayed as dangerous, driving readers mad by revealing humanity's insignificance in an indifferent cosmos.

The title

Lovecraft derived the title from Greek roots — nekros (corpse), nomos (law) and eikon (image) — glossing it as "an image of the law of the dead", though it is often popularly interpreted as "Book of the Dead".

Reality and hoaxes

Despite its fictional origin, the Necronomicon has repeatedly been mistaken for a real ancient text, and pranksters have planted fake catalogue entries for it in real libraries. Several published "Necronomicons" have appeared since the 1970s, most famously the Simon Necronomicon (1977), which blends Sumerian mythology with Lovecraftian names. The hoaxes have made the book one of the most famous fictional books in popular culture; it features in films such as the Evil Dead series and in countless games and novels.
