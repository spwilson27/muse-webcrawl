The Recovered Territories (Polish: Ziemie Odzyskane) is the term used in post-war Poland for the territories east of the Oder–Neisse line that were transferred from Germany to Poland after the Second World War. They comprised most of Silesia, Pomerania, East Brandenburg, the southern part of East Prussia (Warmia and Masuria), and the formerly Free City of Danzig (Gdańsk), together with adjacent areas.

Background

At the Potsdam Conference of July–August 1945, the Allied powers placed the German territories east of the Oder and Lusatian Neisse rivers under Polish administration, pending a final peace settlement. Poland, which had lost its eastern territories (the Kresy) to the Soviet Union, was thereby shifted westward as a whole. The transfer was not formally recognised by West Germany until the Treaty of Warsaw in 1970, and the border was confirmed definitively by the German–Polish Border Treaty of 1990 following German reunification.

Population transfer and resettlement

Almost the entire German population of the territories — several million people — fled or was expelled between 1944 and 1950, in the largest forced migration of the post-war settlement. The emptied lands were resettled by Poles, including roughly one and a half million people themselves displaced from the former eastern Polish territories annexed by the USSR, alongside settlers from central Poland and returning emigrants. Cities were given Polish names: Breslau became Wrocław, Stettin became Szczecin, and Danzig became Gdańsk.

Use of the term

The name "Recovered Territories" rested on the argument, promoted by the Polish communist authorities and the "Piast concept" of Polish history, that the lands had belonged to the medieval Polish kingdom of the Piast dynasty and were therefore being recovered rather than annexed. The term was used extensively in official propaganda of the Polish People's Republic and in the work of a dedicated Ministry of the Recovered Territories, first headed by Władysław Gomułka. In present-day Poland the phrase survives mainly as a historical designation; the regions are usually described simply as the western and northern territories (Ziemie Zachodnie i Północne).
