Griffon vulture

Scientific nameGyps fulvus
FamilyAccipitridae
Wingspan2.3–2.8 m (7.5–9.2 ft)
RangeSouthern Europe, North Africa, Middle East, Central Asia
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The griffon vulture (Gyps fulvus) is a large Old World vulture in the bird of prey family Accipitridae. It is also known as the Eurasian griffon. It is not to be confused with the Rüppell's griffon vulture, a different species with similar colors.

Description

The griffon vulture is 93–122 cm (37–48 in) long with a 2.3–2.8 m (7.5–9.2 ft) wingspan, weighing 6–11 kg. It has a white head and neck ruff, a buff-brown body, and dark flight feathers. Like other vultures, it is a scavenger, feeding mostly from carcasses of dead animals which it finds by soaring over open areas.

Behaviour

Griffons breed colonially on cliff ledges, laying a single egg. They are highly social at carcasses and roosts, and rely on rising air thermals to travel long distances with minimal flapping. In Spain and France, reintroduction and supplementary feeding have rebuilt populations after declines caused by poisoning and reduced food availability.

Conservation

Although listed as Least Concern globally, the species is protected across much of Europe and is a flagship for vulture conservation, including protection from veterinary drugs such as diclofenac that devastated Asian Gyps species.
