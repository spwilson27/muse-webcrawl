Shetland Bus

The Shetland Bus was the nickname of a clandestine special operations group that made crossings between German-occupied Norway and the Shetland Islands during the Second World War. Operating initially from Lunna and later Scalloway, its crews ferried agents, weapons and supplies to the Norwegian resistance and brought back refugees and intelligence.

Operations

From 1941 the service used Norwegian fishing boats, crewed largely by Norwegian volunteers, to make the roughly 200-nautical-mile crossing of the North Sea, mostly under cover of the winter darkness. Operations were organised in Britain by the Special Operations Executive (SOE) in cooperation with Norwegian authorities in exile.

Later war years

After heavy losses of fishing boats in the winter of 1942–43, the Shetland Bus received three American submarine chasers — Hitra, Vigra and Hessa — which made the crossings faster and safer. In total the operation carried hundreds of agents and tons of supplies, and evacuated many Norwegians fleeing the Gestapo. The base at Scalloway is commemorated by a memorial, and a museum in the town preserves the story of the operation.
