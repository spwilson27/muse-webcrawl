The Nederlander Organization, founded in 1912 by David T. Nederlander in Detroit, Michigan, is one of the largest operators of theatres and music venues in the United States. Together with the Shubert Organization and Jujamcyn Theaters, it is one of the three major owners of Broadway venues, and it operates theatres and presents touring productions across North America and in London's West End.

History

David Tobias Nederlander began by leasing the Detroit Opera House in 1912 and went on to acquire and operate theatres throughout the American Midwest. His son James M. Nederlander (1922–2016) expanded the business dramatically from the 1960s, purchasing or leasing a string of Broadway houses and becoming a leading producer of plays and musicals; he was widely known in the industry as "Jimmy". Under his leadership the family also gained control of major venues including the Hollywood Pantages Theatre in Los Angeles and theatres in Chicago, San Diego and other cities, and presented national tours and concerts in arenas and amphitheatres.

James M. Nederlander's son, James L. Nederlander, serves as president of the organisation and continues its producing activity on Broadway and in the West End, where the company has operated the Aldwych Theatre and the Dominion Theatre, among others.

Venues

On Broadway the organisation operates nine theatres, including the Brooks Atkinson, Gershwin, Lunt-Fontanne, Marquis, Minskoff, Nederlander, Neil Simon, Palace and Richard Rodgers theatres. Beyond New York it manages or owns major houses such as the Cadillac Palace, CIBC Theatre and James M. Nederlander Theatre in Chicago (the latter renamed for the company's chairman in 2019), the Hollywood Pantages and the Greek Theatre in Los Angeles.
