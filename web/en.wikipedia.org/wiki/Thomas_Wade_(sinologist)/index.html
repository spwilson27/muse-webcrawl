Sir Thomas Francis Wade (25 August 1818 – 31 May 1895) was a British diplomat and sinologist. He was the creator of the Wade romanization system for Mandarin Chinese, later modified by Herbert Giles into the Wade–Giles system, which remained the dominant transcription system for Chinese in the English-speaking world for much of the twentieth century.

Career in China

Wade went to China in 1842 as an interpreter with British forces during the First Opium War. He spent much of his career in the British consular and diplomatic service in China, becoming fluent in Mandarin. He served as British minister to China (envoy to Beijing) from 1871 to 1883, the longest-serving British minister in that post in the nineteenth century. He played a role in negotiations following the Margary Affair, which produced the Chefoo Convention of 1876 with the Qing government.

Sinology

Wade published influential works on the Chinese language, including The Peking Syllabary (1859), a collection of Mandarin syllables in his romanization, and the language course Yü-yen Tzŭ-êrh Chi (語言自邇集, 1867), which became the standard textbook for British learners of Mandarin. His system of transcription, using aspiration marks (e.g., p vs. p') rather than voiced consonant letters, was adopted in Herbert Giles's Chinese-English Dictionary (1892) and thereafter known as Wade–Giles.

Later life

After retiring from diplomacy in 1883, Wade became the first Professor of Chinese at the University of Cambridge in 1888, a chair he held until his death in 1895. He donated his extensive library of Chinese books—some 4,300 volumes—to Cambridge University Library, forming the core of its Chinese collection.
