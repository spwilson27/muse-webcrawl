Kissing number

In geometry, the kissing number in n dimensions is the maximum number of non-overlapping unit spheres that can simultaneously touch a central unit sphere. In one dimension it is 2, in two dimensions 6, in three dimensions 12, and in four dimensions 24. Exact values are known only for certain dimensions: 1, 2, 3, 4, 8 (240) and 24 (196,560), the latter two linked to the E8 and Leech lattices.

The 3D case was debated by Isaac Newton, who said 12, and David Gregory, who thought 13 possible; a rigorous proof that 12 is maximal came in 1953. Kissing numbers connect to sphere packing, coding theory and lattice theory.
