The foreign relations of Palestine have been conducted since the establishment of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) in 1964. Since the Palestinian Declaration of Independence in 1988, the State of Palestine's diplomatic campaign has centred on securing international recognition of Palestinian statehood on the basis of the 1967 borders, with East Jerusalem as its capital.

Recognition

Following the 1988 declaration, dozens of states recognised the State of Palestine; recognitions grew in further waves, particularly after 2010. As of the mid-2020s, a substantial majority of United Nations member states — more than 140 — recognise Palestine, although many Western states historically withheld recognition pending a negotiated settlement of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.

International organisations

In November 2012 the UN General Assembly upgraded Palestine to "non-member observer state" status (Resolution 67/19). Palestine is a full member of the Arab League, the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, UNESCO, the Group of 77, and Interpol, and in 2015 acceded to the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court. Palestinian diplomatic missions range from embassies in states that recognise Palestine to delegations and missions elsewhere.

Bilateral relations

Palestine maintains particularly close ties with Arab and Muslim-majority states and with members of the Non-Aligned Movement. Relations with Israel are governed by the framework of the Oslo Accords and the Palestinian Authority established in 1994, while relations with the United States and the European Union — major donors to the Palestinian Authority — have fluctuated with the state of the peace process.
