Marion County is a county in the U.S. state of Indiana. As of the 2020 census its population was 977,203, making it the most populous county in the state. The county seat is Indianapolis, the state capital, with which the county shares a consolidated city–county government known as Unigov, established in 1970.

History

Marion County was created in 1822 and named for General Francis Marion, the "Swamp Fox" of the American Revolutionary War. Indianapolis, founded in 1821 as the planned state capital, sits at its centre. Unigov merged most county and city functions in 1970, though a few excluded cities and towns — including Beech Grove, Lawrence, Southport and Speedway — retain separate governments.

Geography

The county covers about 403 square miles in central Indiana; the White River flows through it. It is the core of the Indianapolis metropolitan area.
