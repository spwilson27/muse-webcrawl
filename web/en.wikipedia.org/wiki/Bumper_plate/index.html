Bumper plate

A bumper plate is a weight plate designed for use with an Olympic barbell in weightlifting and CrossFit-style training. Unlike cast-iron plates, bumper plates are made largely of dense rubber with a steel hub, have a uniform diameter regardless of weight, and are built to be dropped from overhead without damaging the plate, the bar, or the floor.

Design

All bumper plates share the same 450 mm diameter, so the bar rests at a consistent height whether loaded lightly or heavily — essential for lifts starting from the floor, such as the deadlift, clean, and snatch. Lighter plates are thinner and can be fragile when dropped alone, so thin "technique" loads are often supplemented with rigid training plates.

Color coding

Competition plates follow the International Weightlifting Federation color code: red 25 kg, blue 20 kg, yellow 15 kg, green 10 kg, and white 5 kg. Pound-denominated training plates often use black rubber with colored lettering.

Types

Training bumpers are typically wide crumb-rubber plates, inexpensive and durable but imprecise. Competition bumpers are thinner, with steel disc hubs, calibrated to within grams, allowing more weight on the bar. Urethane plates offer durability and colorfastness at higher cost.

Use

Bumper plates made modern Olympic lifting and functional-fitness training practical in ordinary gyms, since failed lifts can simply be dropped onto a lifting platform instead of being lowered under control.
