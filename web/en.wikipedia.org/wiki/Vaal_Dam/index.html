The Vaal Dam is a concrete gravity dam on the Vaal River in South Africa, situated on the border between Gauteng and the Free State, about 70 km south of Johannesburg. It is one of the country's largest dams by surface area and is the principal water-storage facility supplying the Gauteng economic heartland, including Johannesburg and Pretoria.

History

Construction began in 1934 during the Great Depression, partly as a public-works employment scheme, and the dam was completed in 1938. The wall was raised in 1956 and again in 1985, bringing its height to roughly 54 metres and the reservoir's full supply capacity to about 2.6 billion cubic metres, with a surface area of more than 300 km². The reservoir receives additional water through inter-basin transfers, notably from the Lesotho Highlands Water Project via the Ash River.

Use

Besides bulk water supply managed in the integrated Vaal River System, the dam and its shoreline support extensive recreation: yachting (the annual Round the Island race on the reservoir is among the largest inland yacht races in the world), angling and holiday resorts around towns such as Deneysville and Oranjeville. The island in the reservoir was once used as a secret meeting place by the apartheid government and later for a large music festival.
