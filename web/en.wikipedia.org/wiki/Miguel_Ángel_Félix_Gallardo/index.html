Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo

Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo (born 8 January 1946), often called El Padrino ("The Godfather") or El Jefe de Jefes ("The Boss of Bosses"), is a convicted Mexican drug lord who founded the Guadalajara Cartel in the 1970s. He unified Mexico's regional drug trafficking organisations into a single federation that controlled most trafficking routes into the United States.

Biography

Born in Culiacán, Sinaloa, Félix Gallardo worked as a Mexico Federal Judicial Police agent and as a bodyguard for Sinaloa political figures, connections that protected his organisation. With partners Rafael Caro Quintero and Ernesto Fonseca Carrillo, he built a marijuana and heroin empire and, in the 1980s, became a major conduit for Colombian cocaine moving north.

Downfall

After the 1985 kidnapping and murder of DEA agent Enrique "Kiki" Camarena, intense pressure from the United States led to the arrests of his partners. Félix Gallardo was arrested in April 1989 in Guadalajara. Before and after his capture he arranged the division of the trade among plaza bosses — a fragmentation that seeded Mexico's modern cartels, including the Tijuana, Sinaloa, Juárez and Gulf organisations.

He was convicted in 1993 and sentenced to a long prison term (37 years, later extended); he has been held in the Altiplano maximum-security prison. In 2021 a judge granted him house arrest on health grounds, a decision contested by prosecutors. From prison he has maintained his innocence in the Camarena case.

In popular culture

He is portrayed by Diego Luna as the central character of the Netflix series Narcos: Mexico (2018–2021).
