WrestleMania is an annual professional wrestling event produced by WWE each spring, and is the company's flagship and longest-running pay-per-view (and livestreaming) event. First held in 1985, it is widely considered the most successful and influential event in professional wrestling history — routinely described as the Super Bowl of sports entertainment — and has played a central role in bringing wrestling into mainstream popular culture.

History

WrestleMania was conceived by WWF chairman Vince McMahon as a gamble on pay-per-view and closed-circuit television. The first event, at Madison Square Garden on 31 March 1985, headlined by Hulk Hogan and Mr. T against Roddy Piper and Paul Orndorff, with celebrities such as Muhammad Ali, Cyndi Lauper and Liberace appearing, succeeded and launched the "Rock 'n' Wrestling Connection" era. WrestleMania III (1987) at the Pontiac Silverdome, headlined by Hogan versus André the Giant, drew a claimed crowd of 93,173 and set the template of stadium spectaculars. The event grew through the Attitude Era of the late 1990s and the 2000s; from WrestleMania 36 in 2020 onward it has been held over two nights. WrestleMania XL (40) took place in Philadelphia in 2024 and WrestleMania 41 in Las Vegas in 2025.

Signature elements and records

WrestleMania traditionally headlines with matches for WWE's world championships and features celebrity involvement, from boxers and NFL players to musicians. For over two decades its central myth was The Streak: The Undertaker's run of 21 consecutive WrestleMania victories, ended by Brock Lesnar at WrestleMania XXX in 2014 in one of the most shocking results in wrestling history. Shawn Michaels earned the nickname "Mr. WrestleMania" for his celebrated performances, including his two matches with Undertaker at WrestleManias XXV and XXVI. The first women's main event closed WrestleMania 35 in 2019, when Becky Lynch defeated Ronda Rousey and Charlotte Flair. The largest announced attendance is 101,763 for WrestleMania 32 (2016) at AT&T Stadium in Texas.

Impact

WrestleMania week generates hundreds of millions of dollars in economic impact for host cities, which bid for the event much as for a Super Bowl, and anchors a festival including the WWE Hall of Fame ceremony, fan conventions and independent wrestling shows. It remains the centrepiece of WWE's calendar and the stage on which the company's biggest storylines conclude.
