Jewish–Christian relations

The relationship between Judaism and Christianity has a complex history spanning two thousand years, from Christianity's origins as a movement within Second Temple Judaism, through centuries of separation, persecution, and mutual misunderstanding, to modern efforts at reconciliation and dialogue.

Origins and separation

Jesus and his earliest followers were Jews, but Christianity and rabbinic Judaism gradually diverged in the first centuries CE over theology, including the Christian claims of Jesus's divinity and messiahship and differing interpretations of the Hebrew Bible and Jewish law. By late antiquity the two faiths were clearly distinct, and Christian teaching of contempt toward Jews — including the charge of deicide — contributed to widespread anti-Jewish discrimination in medieval Europe.

Modern reconciliation

After the Holocaust, many Christian churches re-examined their teachings. The Second Vatican Council's declaration Nostra aetate (1965) rejected the deicide charge and condemned antisemitism, transforming Catholic–Jewish relations. Interfaith bodies and statements such as the Jewish document Dabru Emet (2000) have since promoted dialogue, cooperation on social issues, and mutual theological respect, while disagreements remain over evangelism directed at Jews and over political questions concerning Israel.
