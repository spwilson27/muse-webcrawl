Bethel (Hebrew: בֵּית אֵל, Beit El, "House of El" or "House of God") was an ancient Israelite city and sanctuary mentioned frequently in the Hebrew Bible. It was situated in the hill country of Ephraim, about 16 km (10 miles) north of Jerusalem, near the modern Palestinian village of Beitin.

Biblical accounts

In the Book of Genesis, the patriarch Jacob, fleeing his brother Esau, dreams at Bethel of a ladder reaching to heaven and names the place "house of God" (Genesis 28). He later returns and builds an altar there (Genesis 35). After the division of the monarchy, Jeroboam I, first king of the northern kingdom of Israel, set up golden calves at Bethel and Dan as rival shrines to Jerusalem (1 Kings 12), an act condemned by the prophets Amos and Hosea, who punningly called it Beth-aven, "house of wickedness".

History and archaeology

The site, excavated in the 1930s and 1950s, shows occupation from the Bronze Age through the Byzantine period. Bethel remained an important settlement in the Persian and Hellenistic eras and is mentioned by Josephus. Its sanctuary's destruction is associated with the reforms of King Josiah of Judah (2 Kings 23).

Legacy

The name Bethel has been given to numerous towns, churches, and chapels worldwide — in the Methodist and Congregational traditions, a "bethel" became a common term for a place of worship, especially one for seamen.
