Southwick, Northamptonshire

Southwick is a village and civil parish in Northamptonshire, England, in the valley of the River Nene near the market town of Oundle. It is notable as an estate village of stone houses built largely in the 19th century, and for Southwick Hall, a manor house with medieval origins.

History

The village appears in the Domesday Book of 1086. The Lynn family, later Lynne, held the manor for centuries, and Southwick Hall preserves fabric from the 14th century onwards, remodelled over successive generations. The parish church of St Mary the Virgin contains medieval work and monuments to the Lynn family.

The village today

Southwick retains a strongly uniform character, with many cottages of local limestone under Collyweston slate or thatch, reflecting its past as a tied estate village. It has a village hall and a pub, and lies within countryside popular for walking along the Nene valley. Its population is small, numbering only a few hundred residents.
