Brotli is a lossless data compression algorithm developed by Google. It uses a combination of the LZ77 algorithm, Huffman coding and second-order context modelling, achieving compression ratios comparable to or better than the best general-purpose compressors of its era. It is widely used for compressing web content delivered over HTTP.

History

Brotli was initially developed in 2013 to compress offline transmission of web fonts, and a general-purpose version was released in September 2015. The name derives from Brötli, Swiss German for a small bread roll. In 2016 it was standardised by the Internet Engineering Task Force as RFC 7932.

Technology

Brotli compresses streams with a sliding window of up to about 16 MB and uses a built-in static dictionary of common strings, which makes it especially effective for small files such as HTML, CSS and JavaScript. In HTTP it is signalled by the br content encoding. Supported natively by all major web browsers, Brotli typically produces files roughly 15–25 per cent smaller than gzip at comparable settings, albeit at higher compression cost at its maximum quality levels.
