NASCAR Cup Series

The NASCAR Cup Series is the top racing series of the National Association for Stock Car Auto Racing (NASCAR) in the United States. It is contested over a season of 36 points races plus exhibition events, mostly on oval tracks, and is widely regarded as the premier form of stock car racing in the world.

History

The series began in 1949 as the Strictly Stock division under NASCAR founder Bill France Sr. It became the Grand National Series in 1950, and from 1971 to 2003 it carried the name of title sponsor Winston (the Winston Cup). It then became the Nextel Cup, Sprint Cup and Monster Energy NASCAR Cup Series before settling on the current multi-sponsor "premier partner" model and the name NASCAR Cup Series in 2020.

Format

Since 2014 the championship has been decided by a playoff system: sixteen drivers qualify for the NASCAR Playoffs on wins and points, and are progressively eliminated over ten races until four drivers contest the season finale, where the highest finisher wins the title. Races are divided into three stages, with championship points awarded at each stage end — a format introduced in 2017.

The calendar opens with the Daytona 500, "The Great American Race", and includes crown-jewel events such as the Coca-Cola 600 at Charlotte, the Brickyard 400 at Indianapolis and the Southern 500 at Darlington. Although most tracks are ovals of between half a mile and 2.66 miles, the schedule also includes road courses and, since 2023, a street race in Chicago.

Cars

Cup cars are purpose-built stock cars with silhouette bodies resembling production models from Chevrolet, Ford and Toyota. The "Next Gen" car, introduced in 2022, standardised many components — including a single-supplier chassis, independent rear suspension, a sequential gearbox and larger wheels — to cut costs and improve road-course performance. Engines are pushrod V8s of about 5.86 litres, producing roughly 670 horsepower in most configurations.

Champions

Richard Petty, Dale Earnhardt and Jimmie Johnson share the record of seven championships each. Petty also holds the record for race wins with 200. Other multiple champions include Jeff Gordon (four) and David Pearson and Darrell Waltrip (three each).
