Kingdom of León

The Kingdom of León was an independent kingdom situated in the northwest region of the Iberian Peninsula. It was founded in 910 when the Christian princes of Asturias along the northern coast of the peninsula shifted their capital from Oviedo to the city of León. Its kings fought in the Reconquista against the Moors and for a time formed the dominant Christian power in Iberia.

History

The kingdom emerged after García I became the first king of León in 910. In 924 it was joined with the Kingdom of Asturias, and in 931 the counties of Castile began to assert independence. León was united with Castile in 1037 under Ferdinand I, separated again in 1157, and definitively reunited with the Crown of Castile in 1230 under Ferdinand III, after which León retained a largely honorific status within the Spanish monarchy.

Legacy

The Leonese monarchy issued some of the earliest recognisable charters in western Europe, and the Cortes of León of 1188 are often cited among the earliest parliamentary bodies on the continent. The historic region corresponds roughly to the modern provinces of León, Zamora and Salamanca.
