Army Group North

Army Group North (German: Heeresgruppe Nord) was a German army group formation of the Second World War. It was first formed in 1939 for the invasion of Poland and re-formed for Operation Barbarossa in 1941.

Eastern Front

Commanded by Field Marshal Wilhelm Ritter von Leeb, Army Group North advanced through the Baltic states toward Leningrad, beginning the 900-day Siege of Leningrad in September 1941. It failed to capture the city and was gradually pushed back in 1943–44.

Courland Pocket

In October 1944 the army group was cut off in the Courland Peninsula of Latvia and renamed Army Group Courland in January 1945. Its roughly 200,000 men held out there, supplied by sea, until surrendering at the end of the war in May 1945.
