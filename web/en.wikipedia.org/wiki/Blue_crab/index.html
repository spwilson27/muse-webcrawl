The blue crab (Callinectes sapidus) is a species of swimming crab native to the waters of the western Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf of Mexico. Its scientific name means "savory beautiful swimmer", a nod both to its striking blue claws and to its value as seafood.

Description

The carapace can reach about 23 cm (9 in) across, with a distinctive pair of long lateral spines. Males have bright blue claws and a narrow, T-shaped abdomen, while females have red-tipped claws and a broad, rounded abdomen. The last pair of legs is flattened into paddles for swimming.

Ecology and fishery

Blue crabs inhabit estuaries such as Chesapeake Bay, where they feed on bivalves, fish, plants and detritus. They support one of the most valuable fisheries on the U.S. East Coast; the Chesapeake Bay harvest is a cultural and economic mainstay of Maryland and Virginia, and the crab is Maryland's state crustacean. Females migrate to the salty mouth of the bay to release their larvae.
