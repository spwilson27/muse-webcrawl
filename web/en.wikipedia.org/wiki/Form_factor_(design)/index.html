Form factor (design)

In design and engineering, a form factor is a specification of the size, shape, and physical interfaces of a component or device, describing how it fits and connects within a larger system. The term is used most extensively in electronics and computing, but also in other fields.

In computing

Motherboard form factors — such as ATX, microATX, and Mini-ITX — define board dimensions, mounting-hole positions, and power-supply requirements so that cases, boards, and supplies from different manufacturers interoperate. Storage devices follow form factors such as 3.5-inch and 2.5-inch drives and M.2 modules; mobile phones are described by form factors such as slab ("candybar"), flip, slider, and foldable.

Other uses

In physics, "form factor" also denotes quantities describing the distribution of charge or matter in scattering experiments, and the term is used in fields such as industrial design and packaging for the overall physical configuration of a product. A well-chosen form factor balances miniaturization, cooling, manufacturability, and usability.
