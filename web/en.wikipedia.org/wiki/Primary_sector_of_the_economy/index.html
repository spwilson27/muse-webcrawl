Primary sector of the economy

The primary sector of the economy includes any industry involved in the extraction and production of raw materials, such as farming, logging, fishing, forestry and mining.

The primary sector tends to make up a larger portion of the economy in developing countries than it does in developed countries. For example, in 2018, agriculture, forestry, and fishing comprised more than 15% of GDP in sub-Saharan Africa but less than 1% in North America.

By contrast, developed countries have a lower primary sector because further technological development allows for a less labour-intensive extraction of resources. Mining, quarrying, and extraction industries are often singled out as distinct from agriculture and forestry, but all involve extracting natural resources from the earth.

Development

Once a predominantly primary-sector economy, the Industrial Revolution in Britain and the United States saw a shift toward secondary (manufacturing) and later tertiary (service) sectors. This progression is described by the three-sector model of the economy, developed by economists Allan Fisher, Colin Clark, and Jean Fourastié.

In economies that have become post-industrial, primary industries are often heavily subsidised and mechanised. For instance, in the United States, corn farming supports a vast ethanol and food industry, while in the European Union agricultural subsidies form a large part of the budget.

List of primary sector activities

  • Agriculture and animal husbandry
  • Fishing and aquaculture
  • Forestry and logging
  • Mining and quarrying
  • Oil and gas extraction
  • Hunting and trapping
