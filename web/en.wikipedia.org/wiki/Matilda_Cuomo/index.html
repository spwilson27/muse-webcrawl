Matilda Cuomo (born Matilda Raffa; 16 September 1931) is an American educator and advocate who served as First Lady of New York from 1983 to 1994 during the governorship of her husband, Mario Cuomo. She is best known for founding Mentoring USA, a non-profit organisation that recruits and trains volunteer mentors for young people.

Early life and education

Raffa was born in New York City to Italian immigrant parents and grew up in Brooklyn. She trained as a teacher, graduating from St. John's University, and worked in New York City public schools. She married Mario Cuomo in 1954, and the couple had five children, including Andrew Cuomo, who also became governor of New York, and journalist Chris Cuomo.

First Lady of New York

As First Lady she championed literacy, child welfare and organ donation, and chaired the Governor's Commission on Child Care. In 1987 she established the New York State Mentoring Program, regarded as the first statewide school-based mentoring programme of its kind in the United States; after leaving Albany she refounded the initiative nationally as Mentoring USA in 1995.

Later work

Cuomo co-edited the book The Person Who Changed My Life, a collection of essays about mentors, and has continued to promote structured mentoring as a tool against school dropout. She has received numerous honours for her public service.
