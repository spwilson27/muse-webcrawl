Draper

A draper was originally a retailer or wholesaler of cloth used mainly for clothing. The term derives from the French drap, meaning cloth. In Britain, drapers were an important part of the retail landscape from the Middle Ages into the 20th century, selling fabrics, linens and related goods.

History

Drapers were organised in guilds in medieval towns; the Worshipful Company of Drapers, founded in London in the 14th century, was one of the great livery companies of the City of London and received its royal charter in 1364. Wool drapers dealt in woollen cloth while linen drapers specialised in linen goods.

Draper's shops and department stores

In the 18th and 19th centuries, high-street draper's shops sold cloth by the yard along with haberdashery, hosiery and ready-made garments. Many famous department stores grew out of drapery businesses, as drapers expanded into general retail. The traditional draper largely disappeared in the later 20th century as clothing retail moved to ready-to-wear outlets.

Other uses

"Draper" also survives as a common English surname, borne by many notable people, and as the name of several places, including Draper, Utah and Draper, Virginia in the United States.
