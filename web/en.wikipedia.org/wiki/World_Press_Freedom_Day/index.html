World Press Freedom Day

Observed3 May, annually
Proclaimed1993, by the UN General Assembly
OrganizerUNESCO
SignificanceCommemorates the Windhoek Declaration (1991)

World Press Freedom Day is an annual observance held on 3 May to celebrate the fundamental principles of press freedom, evaluate the state of press freedom around the world, defend the media from attacks on their independence, and pay tribute to journalists who have lost their lives in the exercise of their profession.

History

The day was proclaimed by the United Nations General Assembly in December 1993, following a recommendation of UNESCO's General Conference. The date marks the anniversary of the Windhoek Declaration, a statement of free-press principles adopted by African newspaper journalists in Windhoek, Namibia, on 3 May 1991.

Activities

Each year UNESCO organizes a global conference hosted by a different country, bringing together journalists, civil society, and public authorities to assess the state of press freedom and discuss solutions. A theme is chosen annually — recent themes have addressed journalism in the face of the environmental crisis, disinformation, and the safety of journalists.

Guillermo Cano Prize

The UNESCO/Guillermo Cano World Press Freedom Prize, created in 1997, is awarded each year on the occasion of the day to a person, organization, or institution that has made an outstanding contribution to the defence of press freedom, especially in the face of danger. The prize is named for Colombian journalist Guillermo Cano Isaza, assassinated in 1986.
