The advanced boiling water reactor (ABWR) is a Generation III+ boiling water reactor designed by General Electric in collaboration with Hitachi and Toshiba. It was the first Generation III design to enter commercial operation, offering improved safety, higher output (about 1,350 MWe), shorter construction times, and lower operating costs compared with earlier BWRs.

Design features

The ABWR uses internal recirculation pumps mounted inside the reactor pressure vessel, eliminating the large external recirculation piping of earlier BWRs and reducing the potential for large pipe breaks. Control rods are driven by fine-motion electro-hydraulic drives, allowing precise positioning. The containment is a reinforced-concrete cylindrical vessel with a steel liner, and the plant employs three independent divisions of safety systems, digital control systems, and a design intended to reduce occupational radiation exposure during refuelling and maintenance.

Operating units and projects

The first ABWRs were units 6 and 7 of Japan's Kashiwazaki-Kariwa plant, which entered service in 1996 and 1997; two further units followed at Hamaoka and Shika. Two units at Lungmen in Taiwan were largely constructed but never entered commercial service. In the United Kingdom, Hitachi's Horizon Nuclear Power planned ABWRs at Wylfa and Oldbury before suspending the projects in 2019. A derivative design, the ESBWR, simplified the concept further using natural circulation.

Certification

The ABWR design was certified by the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission in 1997, the first such certification of a Generation III design in the United States, and was approved by UK regulators through the Generic Design Assessment in 2017.
