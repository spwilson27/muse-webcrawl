Overview

Mpande kaSenzangakhona (c. 1798 – 1872) was king of the Zulu Kingdom from 1840 until his death in 1872, making him the longest-reigning Zulu king. He was a half-brother of the legendary Shaka and of Dingane, whom he displaced with Boer assistance.

Rise to power

Mpande was considered comparatively unambitious and was spared in the purges that killed other royal relatives. Granted a large fiefdom in south-eastern Zululand, he built his own following. In 1839, fearing Dingane intended to kill him, Mpande fled across the Thukela River into the Boer-held territory of Natal with thousands of followers and cattle. In January 1840 his forces, supported by Andries Pretorius and a Boer commando, defeated Dingane at the Battle of Maqongqo; Dingane fled and was assassinated, and Mpande was proclaimed king.

Reign

Mpande ruled for 32 years, pursuing a pragmatic policy of accommodation with the British in Natal and the Boer republics while retaining Zulu independence. He maintained the military system but avoided major wars, although a raid into Swaziland in 1852 caused friction with the British. His greatest difficulty was succession: in 1856 his sons Cetshwayo and Mbuyazi fought the bloody Battle of Ndondakusuka for the right to succeed, leaving Cetshwayo the effective heir. Mpande admitted Christian missionaries to the kingdom, though he did not convert. He died in 1872 and was succeeded by Cetshwayo, the last king of an independent Zululand.
