Eduard Čech (29 June 1893 – 15 March 1960) was a Czech mathematician known for his research in projective differential geometry and topology. His name is attached to several fundamental concepts of modern topology, including Čech cohomology and the Stone–Čech compactification.

Life

Čech was born in Stračov, Bohemia, then part of Austria-Hungary. He studied mathematics at Charles University in Prague, served in the Austro-Hungarian army during the First World War, and after the war completed a doctorate and a research stay in Turin, where he worked on differential geometry with Guido Fubini; together they produced a monograph on projective differential geometry. He became a professor at Masaryk University in Brno, where he built a leading topological school and founded a famous seminar. After the Second World War he moved to Charles University in Prague and helped found the Mathematical Institute of the Czechoslovak Academy of Sciences.

Work

In the 1930s Čech introduced the cohomology theory that bears his name, defining what are now called Čech cohomology groups based on the nerve of an open covering; his 1932 paper also anticipated ideas of higher homotopy groups. In 1937 he described the maximal compactification of a completely regular space, independently obtained by Marshall Stone — the Stone–Čech compactification βX, a cornerstone of general topology. He also worked on dimension theory and wrote an influential textbook, Point Sets (published posthumously in English), and devoted much effort to improving mathematics education in Czechoslovak schools.
