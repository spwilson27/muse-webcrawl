Channel Islands (California)

Coastline of Santa Cruz Island

The Channel Islands are a chain of eight islands located in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of Southern California, stretching from Santa Barbara to San Diego. The northern group comprises San Miguel, Santa Rosa, Santa Cruz, and Anacapa; the southern group Santa Barbara, San Nicolas, Santa Catalina, and San Clemente.

History

The islands were inhabited for thousands of years by the Chumash and Tongva peoples. European contact began with the expedition of Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo in 1542. Santa Catalina Island became a popular resort in the 20th century, while San Nicolas and San Clemente are used by the U.S. Navy.

National park and wildlife

Five of the islands form Channel Islands National Park, established in 1980. Isolation has produced endemic species such as the island fox and island scrub-jay, found nowhere else on Earth. The surrounding waters are protected as part of the Channel Islands National Marine Sanctuary and are rich in kelp forests, seals, and sea lions.
