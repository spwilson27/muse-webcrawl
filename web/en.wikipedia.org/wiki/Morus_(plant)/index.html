Morus (plant)

Morus, a genus of flowering plants in the family Moraceae, consists of diverse species of deciduous trees commonly known as mulberries, growing wild and under cultivation in many temperate world regions. Generally, the genus has 64 subordinate taxa, three of which are well-known and are ostensibly named for the fruit colour of the best-known cultivar: white, red, and black mulberry (Morus alba, M. rubra, and M. nigra, respectively), with numerous cultivars and some taxa currently unchecked and awaiting taxonomic review.

Description

Mulberries are fast-growing when young, rarely exceeding 15 m in height. The leaves are alternate, simple, often lobed — more often on young shoots than mature trees — with serrated margins. The fruit is a multiple fruit, 2–3 cm long, composed of many small drupes, ripening from white or pink to red, purple or black, and sweet when ripe.

Uses

The leaves of the white mulberry are the sole food source of the silkworm (Bombyx mori), making Morus alba central to sericulture. The fruits are eaten fresh and used in jams, wines and desserts; black mulberry is prized for its tart flavour. Mulberry wood and bark fibre have traditional uses, and various species are planted as shade and street trees, for which fruitless male clones are preferred in cities because ripe fruit can stain pavements and the pollen is allergenic.
