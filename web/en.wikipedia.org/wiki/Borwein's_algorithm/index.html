Borwein's algorithm

Borwein's algorithm is a family of algorithms devised by Jonathan and Peter Borwein for computing the value of π (pi) and related constants. The best known is their 1987 quartic algorithm, which converges with fourth-order speed: the number of correct digits roughly quadruples with each iteration, making it practical for very high-precision computations.

The quartic algorithm

The algorithm starts with initial values derived from √2 and proceeds by iterating a pair of coupled recurrences involving square roots. Each step updates two sequences, conventionally called an and yn, so that 1/an approaches π. Because convergence is quartic, only a handful of iterations are needed to reach hundreds of digits, and about a dozen iterations suffice for billions of digits when combined with fast multiplication methods such as the Schönhage–Strassen algorithm.

Related algorithms

The Borwein brothers also developed a cubic algorithm and a quintic algorithm for π, as well as iterative algorithms for the arithmetic–geometric mean and for computing 1/π. Their work builds on the Gauss–Legendre algorithm and the theory of elliptic integrals, and is closely related to the Ramanujan–Sato series used in the Chudnovsky algorithm, which long held records for digits of π computed.

In 2019, π was computed to 31.4 trillion digits using the Chudnovsky algorithm, while Borwein-type iterations remain important for verification computations because of their different arithmetic structure.
