Christopher Walken


Placeholder image: Christopher Walken

Christopher Walken (born Ronald Walken; March 31, 1943) is an American actor, dancer and comedian. Known for his distinctive voice, deadpan delivery and frequent portrayals of eccentric or menacing characters, he has appeared in more than a hundred films. He won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for his role as the traumatised prisoner of war Nick Chevotarevich in The Deer Hunter (1978).

Early life

Walken was born in Astoria, Queens, New York City, to a German immigrant father who owned a bakery and a Scottish immigrant mother. He and his brothers worked as child actors in early television; he trained as a dancer and appeared in musical theatre before moving into dramatic roles in the late 1960s.

Career

After small roles in films such as The Anderson Tapes (1971) and a memorable scene in Annie Hall (1977), Walken's performance in The Deer Hunter brought him the Oscar and international recognition. Subsequent notable roles include The Dead Zone (1983), A View to a Kill (1985, as the villain Max Zorin), King of New York (1990), Batman Returns (1992), Pulp Fiction (1994, the celebrated gold-watch monologue), Catch Me If You Can (2002, earning a second Oscar nomination), Hairspray (2007) and Seven Psychopaths (2012). His dance training was showcased in the music video for Fatboy Slim's "Weapon of Choice" (2001), which won multiple MTV awards. He has hosted Saturday Night Live seven times, creating the much-quoted "More cowbell" sketch (2000). In the 2020s he appeared in the series Severance and as Emperor Shaddam IV in Dune: Part Two (2024).

Personal life

Walken has been married to casting director Georgianne Thon since 1969. He was aboard the yacht from which actress Natalie Wood drowned in 1981; he cooperated with investigators and was never considered a suspect. He famously does not own a mobile phone or computer.
