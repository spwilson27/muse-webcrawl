Despotism

Despotism is a form of government in which a single entity rules with absolute power: a despot. In its classical usage, a despot was a ruler who wielded power arbitrarily, in the manner of a master over slaves in a household.

History of the concept

The Greek word despotēs originally denoted the master of a household. Aristotle contrasted despotic rule over slaves with political rule over free people. Montesquieu, in The Spirit of Law, treated despotism as one of three basic regime types, governed by fear. Enlightened despotism described reforming absolute monarchs such as Frederick the Great.
