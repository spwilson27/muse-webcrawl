Egypt–Israel peace treaty

The Egypt–Israel peace treaty was signed in Washington, D.C., United States, on 26 March 1979, following the Camp David Accords of 1978. It was the first peace treaty between Israel and an Arab country.

Egypt–Israel Treaty
Signed: 26 March 1979, White House
Parties: Anwar Sadat (Egypt), Menachem Begin (Israel), Jimmy Carter (witness)
Result: Israel returned Sinai; mutual recognition

Terms

Israel withdrew from the Sinai Peninsula (completed 1982, except Taba, resolved 1989), Egypt recognized Israel and opened the Suez Canal to Israeli shipping, diplomatic relations were established, and demilitarized zones with multinational observers were created.

Background and aftermath

After the 1973 Yom Kippur War and Sadat's 1977 visit to Jerusalem, US President Jimmy Carter mediated at Camp David. Sadat and Begin shared the 1978 Nobel Peace Prize. Egypt was suspended from the Arab League for a decade, and Sadat was assassinated in 1981, but the treaty endured — a "cold peace" that survived later crises.
