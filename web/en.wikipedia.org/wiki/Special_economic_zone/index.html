Special economic zone

A special economic zone (SEZ) is an area in which the business and trade laws are different from the rest of the country. SEZs are located within a country's national borders, and their aims include increasing trade balance, employment, increased investment, job creation and effective administration. Companies are offered tax holidays and other incentives to set up in the zone; the zones are also used as laboratories for economic reform.

Shenzhen, designated an SEZ in 1980, grew from a market town into a megacity.

History

Modern SEZs descend from free ports and free-trade zones such as Shannon Airport in Ireland (1959), often cited as the first modern free zone, and Taiwan's Kaohsiung export processing zone (1966). The model was transformed by China: in 1980, as part of Deng Xiaoping's reform and opening up, China established SEZs at Shenzhen, Zhuhai and Shantou in Guangdong, and Xiamen in Fujian, followed by Hainan island in 1988. Shenzhen's explosive growth made the SEZ a symbol of Chinese economic reform.

Types

The World Bank and UNIDO distinguish free trade zones, export processing zones, enterprise zones, free ports, single-factory zones, and comprehensive SEZs that combine multiple functions in a mini-city format. By 2019 there were nearly 5,400 SEZs across 147 economies, according to UNCTAD.

India

India was among the first in Asia to recognise export processing zones, setting up Kandla EPZ in 1965. The SEZ Act 2005 created a unified framework; zones such as those in Noida, Chennai and Hyderabad host much of India's IT-services exports, though land-acquisition controversies, notably at Nandigram (2006–07), made SEZs politically contentious.

Criticism

Critics cite tax revenue losses, land expropriation, weak labour protections, "race to the bottom" competition between jurisdictions, and the poaching of existing investment rather than the creation of new activity. Evaluations find SEZs succeed where they complement sound national policy and infrastructure, and fail where they substitute for it.
