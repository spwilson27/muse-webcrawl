Hirohito

Hirohito
Reign: 25 December 1926 – 7 January 1989
Born: 29 April 1901, Tokyo
Died: 7 January 1989, Tokyo
Era name: Shōwa

Hirohito (29 April 1901 – 7 January 1989), posthumously honoured in Japan as Emperor Shōwa, was the 124th emperor of Japan according to the traditional order of succession. His reign of more than 62 years was the longest of any historical Japanese emperor and spanned Japan's militarist expansion, the Second World War, the Allied occupation and the post-war economic miracle.

Reign and war

Hirohito became regent for his ill father, the Taishō Emperor, in 1921 and succeeded him in 1926. During the 1930s Japan's armed forces seized Manchuria, invaded China and increasingly dominated the government. Historians continue to debate the degree of Hirohito's personal responsibility for the war that followed: traditionally presented as a powerless constitutional monarch and marine biologist by inclination, later scholarship — notably Herbert Bix's biography — argued that he took an active, informed role in military decisions, though the debate remains unresolved.

Surrender and occupation

In August 1945 Hirohito intervened twice in cabinet deliberations to impose acceptance of the Potsdam terms, and on 15 August his recorded surrender broadcast — the first time most Japanese had heard the imperial voice — announced the end of the war. Unlike other Axis leaders he was neither tried nor deposed: General MacArthur's occupation authorities retained him as an instrument of stability. On 1 January 1946 the emperor issued the so-called Humanity Declaration, renouncing the concept of his own divinity, and under the 1947 constitution he became "the symbol of the State and of the unity of the people".

Later years

For the remaining four decades of his reign Hirohito travelled widely (including a state visit to the United States in 1975), pursued research in marine biology — he published monographs on hydrozoans — and presided over Japan's transformation into an economic superpower. He died of cancer on 7 January 1989 and was succeeded by his son Akihito.
