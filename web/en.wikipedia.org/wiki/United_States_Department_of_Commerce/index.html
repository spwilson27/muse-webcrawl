United States Department of Commerce

The United States Department of Commerce is an executive department of the US federal government concerned with promoting economic growth, trade and technological competitiveness. Its mission is to create the conditions for economic growth and opportunity for all communities. The department is headed by the Secretary of Commerce, a member of the President's Cabinet, and is headquartered in the Herbert C. Hoover Building in Washington, D.C. It employs roughly 47,000 people, the number swelling greatly in census years.

History

The department was created on 14 February 1903 as the Department of Commerce and Labor. In 1913 its labour functions were split off into a separate Department of Labor, leaving the renamed Department of Commerce. Herbert Hoover, Secretary from 1921 to 1928, greatly expanded its role in promoting business and standardisation. During the twentieth century it accumulated a disparate portfolio, prompting occasional proposals — including by President Ronald Reagan — to abolish or restructure it.

Bureaus and functions

Major components include the Census Bureau (which conducts the decennial census), the Bureau of Economic Analysis, the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) — by far the largest component, including the National Weather Service and the US fisheries service — the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), the US Patent and Trademark Office, the International Trade Administration, the Bureau of Industry and Security (which administers export controls), the National Telecommunications and Information Administration, the Minority Business Development Agency and the Economic Development Administration.

The department is central to US trade policy implementation, export-control enforcement on strategic technologies, weather and climate services, spectrum management and economic statistics used worldwide.
