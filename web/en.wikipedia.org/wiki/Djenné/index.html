Djenné is a town and urban commune in the Inland Niger Delta region of central Mali. It is famous for its distinctive Sudano-Sahelian mud-brick architecture, above all the Great Mosque of Djenné, the largest mud-brick building in the world, and for its historic role as a centre of trans-Saharan trade and Islamic scholarship. The old towns of Djenné were designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1988.

History

The nearby site of Djenné-Djenno was inhabited from around 250 BC and was one of the oldest urbanised settlements in sub-Saharan Africa, prospering through trade in gold, salt and grain along the Niger River. The present town was founded around 800 AD and grew rich as a southern terminus of trans-Saharan commerce, rivalling Timbuktu. It came under the Mali Empire, then the Songhai Empire, and was conquered by Morocco in 1591. French forces occupied it in 1893.

The Great Mosque and market

The current Great Mosque was built in 1907 on the site of an earlier mosque, using mud brick coated in banco plaster, with ostrich-egg finials on its spires. The whole community participates in an annual festival, the Crépissage, to replaster the mosque after the rains. Djenné's Monday market, held in the square before the mosque, is one of the most renowned in West Africa.
