Glenn Andreotta

Glenn Andreotta (October 30, 1947 – April 8, 1968) was an American helicopter crew chief in the Vietnam War noted for being one of three men who intervened to halt the My Lai massacre, in which American soldiers killed hundreds of unarmed Vietnamese civilians. He received the Soldier's Medal in 1998 (posthumously) and the Bronze Star Medal.

Andreotta served with the 123rd Aviation Battalion. On March 16, 1968, he was the crew chief of a scout helicopter piloted by Warrant Officer Hugh Thompson Jr., with Lawrence Colburn as door gunner. From the air, the crew observed the killing of civilians at My Lai and landed to intervene, positioning their helicopter between American troops and fleeing villagers and threatening to open fire if the killing continued. They also rescued civilians, including a young boy found alive in an irrigation ditch among the dead.

Andreotta was killed in action three weeks later, on April 8, 1968, when his helicopter was shot down near Quảng Ngãi; he was 20 years old. Because he had died before the massacre became public, his role was recognized later than those of Thompson and Colburn; in 1998 all three men were awarded the Soldier's Medal, the U.S. Army's highest award for heroism not involving direct contact with the enemy.
