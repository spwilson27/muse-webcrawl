Freshman's dream

The freshman's dream is a name given to the incorrect equation (x + y)p = xp + yp, where p is a real number. It is a common error made by beginning students of algebra, who may wrongly assume that powers distribute over addition. The name reflects the fact that the error is typically made by first-year ("freshman") students.

When it holds

The identity is false in ordinary real arithmetic — for example, (1 + 1)2 = 4, whereas 12 + 12 = 2. However, the equation becomes a genuine theorem in algebraic structures of prime characteristic: if p is a prime number and x and y are elements of a commutative ring of characteristic p, then (x + y)p = xp + yp. This follows from the binomial theorem, since the binomial coefficients C(p, k) for 0 < k < p are all divisible by p, and hence vanish in characteristic p. The resulting map x → xp is the Frobenius endomorphism.

Related results

The "freshman's dream" has analogues in other settings: the "sophomore's dream" names a surprising identity involving integrals, and in characteristic two, the squaring map is additive, which is used in finite field arithmetic. The erroneous identity also holds trivially when p = 1 or when one of the terms is zero.
