Diclofenac

ClassNonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)
RoutesOral, topical, rectal, intramuscular, ophthalmic
Brand namesVoltaren, Cataflam, others
Patented1965 (Ciba-Geigy); in medical use from 1973

Diclofenac, sold under the brand name Voltaren among others, is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used to treat pain and inflammatory diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, and acute migraine. It is applied topically as gels, given by mouth, rectally, by injection, or as eye drops.

Mechanism and adverse effects

Like other NSAIDs, diclofenac inhibits the cyclooxygenase enzymes COX-1 and COX-2, reducing prostaglandin synthesis. Common side effects include abdominal pain, nausea, headache, and dizziness; serious risks include gastrointestinal bleeding, kidney failure, and increased cardiovascular events, which have led regulators to restrict its use in people with established heart disease.

Environmental impact

Veterinary use of diclofenac in cattle in South Asia caused catastrophic declines of vulture populations — exceeding 95% for some species — because vultures feeding on treated carcasses suffered fatal kidney failure. India, Nepal, and Pakistan banned veterinary diclofenac in 2006, and meloxicam is promoted as a safer alternative.
