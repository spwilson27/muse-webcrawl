The Lord Chamberlain's Men was a company of actors, or playing company, for which William Shakespeare worked as actor and playwright for most of his professional career. Formed in 1594 under the patronage of Henry Carey, 1st Baron Hunsdon, then Lord Chamberlain, it became the leading company of Elizabethan London.

Organisation

Unlike most rival troupes, the company was owned by a small group of actor-shareholders — including Richard Burbage, John Heminges, Augustine Phillips, Thomas Pope, Will Kempe and Shakespeare himself — who shared profits and, from 1599, owned their own playhouse, the Globe Theatre on Bankside. The company performed first at The Theatre and the Curtain in Shoreditch before moving to the Globe.

Repertory

The company premiered most of Shakespeare's plays, including Richard III, Romeo and Juliet, A Midsummer Night's Dream, Hamlet and King Lear, alongside works by Ben Jonson, John Fletcher and others. In 1603, on the accession of James I, the company received a royal patent and became the King's Men, the most prestigious company of the Jacobean court.
