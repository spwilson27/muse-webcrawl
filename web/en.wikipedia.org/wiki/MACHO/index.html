MACHO

A massive astrophysical compact halo object (MACHO) is a kind of astronomical body that could explain the apparent presence of dark matter in galaxy halos. A MACHO is a body that emits little or no radiation and drifts through interstellar space unassociated with any solar system; examples include black holes, neutron stars, and brown dwarfs.

MACHO
Field: Astronomy / cosmology
Candidate bodies: Black holes, neutron stars, brown dwarfs
Detection method: Gravitational microlensing
Rival hypothesis: WIMPs

Detection

Because MACHOs are nearly invisible, astronomers searched for them through gravitational microlensing: when a MACHO passes in front of a distant star, its gravity briefly brightens the star's image. Surveys monitoring stars in the Magellanic Clouds during the 1990s detected microlensing events but concluded that MACHOs make up only a small fraction of the dark matter halo.
