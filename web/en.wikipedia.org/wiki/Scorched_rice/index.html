Scorched rice

Scorched rice
TypeRice dish
Main ingredientsRice, water
VariationsNurungji (Korea), tahdig (Iran), socarrat (Spain), okoge (Japan), guoba (China), kerak nasi (Indonesia)

Scorched rice, also known as burnt rice or crispy rice, refers to the thin crust of slightly browned, toasted rice that forms on the bottom of a cooking pot during rice cooking. Rather than being discarded, in many cuisines it is prized as a delicacy in its own right for its crunchy texture and nutty, toasted flavour, and it features in numerous named traditional dishes.

Regional varieties

  • Korea — nurungji: the crisp crust left after cooking rice in a pot (traditionally an iron gamasot). It is eaten as a snack, or simmered in water to make sungnyung, a warm toasted-rice tea served after meals.
  • Iran — tahdig: a prized golden crust, often made from rice mixed with yoghurt and saffron, or from bread or potato slices layered at the bottom of the pot. Tahdig is served inverted so the crust sits on top.
  • Spain — socarrat: the caramelised crust that forms on the bottom of a properly cooked paella in the Valencian tradition.
  • Japan — okoge: the scorched rice from the bottom of the pot or rice cooker, eaten plain, as a snack, or in soups.
  • China — guoba: crispy rice crust, eaten as a snack or used in dishes such as sizzling rice soup, where hot broth poured over it makes it crackle.
  • Indonesia — kerak nasi: the crust formed when rice is steamed in a bamboo or clay vessel.
  • Philippines — tutong: the toasted rice at the bottom of the pot, often eaten with sugar, coffee or hot chocolate.
  • Dominican Republic and Puerto Rico — concón / pegao: the crunchy layer at the bottom of the caldero.

Formation

Scorched rice forms through drying and the Maillard reaction at the pot surface, where the rice in direct contact with the hot base loses moisture and toasts. Traditional heavy pots of cast iron, clay or stone are especially effective at producing an even crust. In the modern era, electric rice cookers with crust settings can reproduce the effect deliberately.
