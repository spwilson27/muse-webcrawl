Cumulonimbus cloud

A mature cumulonimbus showing the characteristic anvil-shaped top.

GenusCumulonimbus (heaped, rainy)
AltitudeBase 200–4,000 m; tops to ~21,000 m in the tropics
PrecipitationHeavy rain, hail; virga possible

A cumulonimbus (from Latin cumulus, "heaped," and nimbus, "rainstorm") is a dense, towering vertical cloud associated with thunderstorms and unstable atmospheric conditions. It is the only cloud genus that produces hail, thunder and lightning.

Formation and life cycle

Cumulonimbus form when warm, moist air rises vigorously through a conditionally unstable atmosphere. A cumulus congestus grows until its top reaches the tropopause, where it spreads sideways into a flattened anvil (incus). A mature cell contains powerful updrafts and downdrafts side by side; precipitation loading and evaporative cooling eventually strengthen the downdraft until it chokes off the updraft and the storm dissipates.

Weather and hazards

These clouds bring intense, short-lived rainfall, lightning, gust fronts and sometimes large hail, downbursts and tornadoes. Organized clusters can grow into mesoscale convective systems and tropical cyclones. Cumulonimbus are a serious hazard to aviation because of extreme turbulence, icing and wind shear, and pilots avoid them by wide margins.

Species and features

The two species are calvus (smooth-topped) and capillatus (with fibrous, anvil-bearing tops). Supplementary features include mamma (pouch-like undersides), arcus (shelf and roll clouds), tuba and the overshooting top that penetrates the lower stratosphere.
