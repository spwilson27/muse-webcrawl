FracFocus is an online national hydraulic fracturing chemical registry in the United States, operated by the Ground Water Protection Council (GWPC) and the Interstate Oil and Gas Compact Commission (IOGCC). It was launched in April 2011 as a voluntary disclosure platform on which oil and gas companies report the chemical constituents of hydraulic fracturing fluids used at individual well sites.

Background

The registry was created in response to public concern about the chemicals injected underground during hydraulic fracturing ("fracking") and demands for disclosure by landowners, regulators, and environmental groups. Numerous states subsequently adopted FracFocus as the official means of chemical disclosure for wells hydraulically fractured within their jurisdictions, either as a mandatory reporting tool or as the designated repository.

Operation

For each reported well, operators disclose the trade name, supplier, and purpose of additives used, along with the chemical abstract service (CAS) number, maximum concentration in the additive, and maximum concentration in the total fracturing fluid for each ingredient. Companies may withhold the identity of chemicals they claim as trade secrets, as permitted by state rules. Disclosures are publicly searchable by well location, operator, and date.

Criticism

Academic studies and government reviews have criticized the registry for data quality problems, inconsistent reporting, difficulty in extracting machine-readable data in its early years, and the breadth of trade secret claims that can leave ingredients undisclosed. In response, the site was redesigned (FracFocus 3.0) to support system-to-system reporting and downloadable datasets.
