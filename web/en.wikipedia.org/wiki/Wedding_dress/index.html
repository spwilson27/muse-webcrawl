Wedding dress

A wedding dress or bridal gown is the dress worn by the bride during a wedding ceremony. Its colour, style and significance vary by culture and religion, though in much of the Western world the archetype is a white, floor-length gown with a veil.

Western traditions

Before the Victorian era, brides typically wore their best dress in any colour, and red was popular in many cultures. White became fashionable after Queen Victoria wore a white lace gown for her marriage to Prince Albert in 1840; photographs and press coverage spread the style among wealthy brides, and by the mid-twentieth century white was the dominant colour in the West, associated with purity and innocence. Common silhouettes include A-line, ballgown, mermaid and sheath, with fabrics such as satin, tulle, chiffon and lace.

Other cultures

In China, brides traditionally wear red — a symbol of luck and prosperity — as in the qun kua, while the white Western gown is now also common. Indian brides typically wear a richly embroidered sari or lehenga, often red or maroon. Japanese brides may wear a white shiromuku kimono for the Shinto ceremony and change into coloured garments for the reception. Korean brides wear the hanbok.

Industry

The bridal industry encompasses designer gowns, mass-market dresses, alterations and accessories such as veils and trains; dresses are frequently kept as heirlooms or preserved after the wedding.
