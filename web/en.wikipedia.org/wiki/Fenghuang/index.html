Fenghuang

The fenghuang (Chinese: 鳳凰) is a mythological bird in Chinese tradition that is said to reign over all other birds. In early usage, the male was called feng and the female huang, but the distinction faded and the fenghuang came to be regarded as a feminine entity, paired with the masculine Chinese dragon as a symbol of marital harmony and imperial authority.

Description

Classical descriptions give the fenghuang a composite body assembled from parts of many creatures: the head of a pheasant, the beak of a rooster, the neck of a snake, the tail of a fish and brilliant plumage in the five fundamental colours. It was said to appear only in places blessed by peace and prosperity, and to hide in times of trouble.

Symbolism

The fenghuang is one of the Four Auspicious Beasts and a symbol of virtue, grace and the union of yin and yang. It adorned imperial robes and architecture, representing the empress just as the dragon represented the emperor. Related birds appear in the mythologies of Japan (hōō), Korea (bonghwang) and Vietnam (phụng hoàng).
