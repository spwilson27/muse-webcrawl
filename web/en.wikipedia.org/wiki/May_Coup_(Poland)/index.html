The May Coup (Polish: Przewrót majowy or Zamach majowy) was a coup d'état carried out in Poland by Marshal Józef Piłsudski between 12 and 14 May 1926. The coup overthrew the government of President Stanisław Wojciechowski and Prime Minister Wincenty Witos and installed the authoritarian Sanacja regime, which ruled Poland until the outbreak of the Second World War in 1939.

Background

Piłsudski, the dominant figure of Polish independence, had retired from politics in 1923, disillusioned with parliamentary instability: Poland had cycled through numerous short-lived governments amid economic crisis, hyperinflation's aftermath and political polarisation. In May 1926 yet another Witos coalition was formed, which Piłsudski denounced. When the government moved to cut military budgets and marginalise his supporters in the army, Piłsudski decided to act, backed by loyal units and by striking railway workers who blocked government troop movements.

The coup

On 12 May 1926 Piłsudski's troops marched on Warsaw. Piłsudski met President Wojciechowski on the Poniatowski Bridge in a famous last attempt at negotiation; the president refused to yield. Fighting in the capital lasted three days. Government forces under General Władysław Anders and others were hampered by the railway strike and by defections. On 14 May Wojciechowski and Witos resigned rather than prolong a civil war.

Aftermath

Piłsudski declined the presidency himself; his candidate Ignacy Mościcki was elected president, while Piłsudski became Minister of Military Affairs and the regime's real ruler. The August Novelisation amended the constitution to strengthen the executive. The Sanacja ("healing") movement promised moral renewal of public life, but the regime grew increasingly authoritarian, imprisoning opposition leaders at Brest in 1930 and, after Piłsudski's death in 1935, adopting the authoritarian April Constitution.
