Plzeň

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Plzeň (Czech pronunciation: [ˈpl̩zɛɲ]; German: Pilsen) is a city in the western part of the Czech Republic, in Bohemia. It is the capital of the Plzeň Region and the fourth most populous city in the country, with about 175,000 inhabitants. The city is known worldwide as the birthplace of Pilsner beer and as the home of the Škoda Works engineering conglomerate.

History

Plzeň was founded in 1295 by King Wenceslaus II at the confluence of four rivers (Mže, Radbuza, Úhlava and Úslava). It grew as a trading town on the route from Prague to Nuremberg, and its Gothic St Bartholomew's Cathedral — whose 102.3 m tower is the tallest church tower in the Czech Republic — dominates the main square. The city was a Catholic stronghold during the Hussite Wars and was besieged during the Thirty Years' War, when it was stormed by Mansfeld's forces in 1618. In May 1945 Plzeň was liberated by the US Third Army under General Patton, an event still commemorated by the annual Liberation Festival.

Beer and industry

In 1842 the citizens' brewery produced the first batch of pale lager brewed with soft local water, Moravian malt and Saaz hops by the Bavarian brewer Josef Groll — the original Pilsner Urquell, which gave its name to the pilsner style copied worldwide. The Pilsner Urquell and Gambrinus breweries remain major employers, alongside the Škoda Works (founded 1859 by Emil Škoda), a producer of transport equipment and machinery.

Culture

Plzeň was, together with Mons in Belgium, a European Capital of Culture in 2015. Landmarks include the Great Synagogue — one of the largest in Europe — the Renaissance town hall, historic underground tunnels, and the Techmania Science Center.
