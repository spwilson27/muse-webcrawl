Schutzstaffel

The SS sig runes insignia

The Schutzstaffel (German: [ˈʃʊtsˌʃtafl̩]; SS; also stylised with Armanen runes) was a major paramilitary organisation under Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party in Nazi Germany, and later throughout German-occupied Europe during World War II. It began with a small guard unit known as the Saal-Schutz, made up of NSDAP volunteers to provide security at party meetings in Munich.

Growth under Himmler

In 1929 Heinrich Himmler became Reichsführer-SS and expanded the organisation from a few hundred men into the central instrument of Nazi terror and racial policy. After the Night of the Long Knives in 1934, in which the SS helped purge the SA leadership, it became an independent organisation, answerable only to Hitler. The SS controlled the Gestapo and the concentration camp system, and its Race and Settlement Main Office enforced the regime's racial selection criteria.

Branches and crimes

The SS comprised several main branches: the Allgemeine SS (General SS), the Waffen-SS (its armed military wing, which grew into a parallel army of some 38 divisions), and the SS-Totenkopfverbände, which ran the concentration and extermination camps. Through the Einsatzgruppen mobile killing squads, the camp system, and the SS leadership of the police apparatus, the organisation was a principal perpetrator of the Holocaust, in which six million Jews and millions of other victims were murdered, as well as of the Porajmos and widespread war crimes against civilians and prisoners of war.

Aftermath

After Germany's defeat in 1945, the SS was declared a criminal organisation by the International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg, and its leaders were tried for crimes against humanity. Himmler died by suicide after capture in May 1945.
