Metalorganic compound

Metalorganic compounds (also organometallic compounds) are chemical compounds containing at least one bond between a carbon atom of an organic molecule and a metal, including alkaline, alkaline earth, transition metals and, in broader usage, metalloids such as boron and silicon. The field of organometallic chemistry combines aspects of traditional inorganic and organic chemistry.

Scope and terminology

Usage varies: some chemists reserve "metalorganic" for compounds with metal–carbon bonds of significant covalent character, while metal carboxylates, alkoxides and similar species are sometimes called metalorganics even in the absence of direct metal–carbon bonding — a usage common in materials chemistry, where metalorganic precursors feed processes such as metalorganic vapour-phase epitaxy (MOVPE) and metal-organic chemical vapour deposition (MOCVD). Related cage-like assemblies of metal ions and organic linkers form metal–organic frameworks (MOFs).

Examples and uses

Classic examples include Grignard reagents, ferrocene, tetracarbonyl nickel and Ziegler–Natta catalysts. Metalorganic compounds are indispensable as homogeneous catalysts, stoichiometric reagents in synthesis, and semiconductor precursors.
