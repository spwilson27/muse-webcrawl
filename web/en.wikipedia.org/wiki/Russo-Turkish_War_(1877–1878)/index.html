Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)

The Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878 was a conflict between the Ottoman Empire and a coalition led by the Russian Empire — including Romania, Serbia, Montenegro and Bulgarian volunteers. Fought in the Balkans and the Caucasus after the Ottomans' bloody suppression of the Bulgarian April Uprising (1876), it ended with the Treaty of San Stefano and then the Congress of Berlin (1878), which recognised the independence of Romania, Serbia and Montenegro and created an autonomous Bulgaria — redrawing the Balkans for a generation.

Course

Russia declared war in April 1877 and crossed the Danube, but Osman Pasha's defence of Plevna stopped the advance for five months — three bloody assaults failed before siege and starvation forced Plevna's surrender in December, an episode watched by war correspondents and military observers worldwide. General Skobelev's winter crossing of the Balkans through the Shipka Pass then opened the road south: Philippopolis fell, and Russian forces reached San Stefano, within sight of Constantinople, by early 1878. In the Caucasus, Russian forces took Kars.

Settlement

San Stefano's "Big Bulgaria", a large Russian client state, alarmed Britain and Austria; Disraeli sent the fleet to the Dardanelles and Bismarck convened the Congress of Berlin (June–July 1878). Berlin split Bulgaria into a principality and the autonomous Ottoman province of Eastern Rumelia (united by the Bulgarians themselves in 1885), returned southern Bessarabia to Russia, gave Austria-Hungary occupation of Bosnia-Herzegovina and Britain Cyprus — leaving grievances that fed the Balkan Wars of 1912–13.
