Munakata Taisha

Munakata Taisha (宗像大社) is a collection of three Shinto shrines in Munakata, Fukuoka Prefecture, Japan, dedicated to the three Munakata goddesses (Munakata Sanjoshin): Ichikishima-hime-no-Kami, Tagitsu-hime-no-Kami, and Tagiri-hime-no-Kami, daughters of the deities Amaterasu and Susanoo. The shrines are the head shrines of the roughly six thousand Munakata shrines across Japan and have long been associated with prayers for maritime safety along the sea route between Japan and the Asian continent.

The three shrines

  • Hetsu-gū, on the Kyushu mainland at the mouth of the Tsurikawa River.
  • Nakatsu-gū, on the island of Ōshima.
  • Okitsu-gū, on the island of Okinoshima, about 60 kilometers offshore, where access is strictly limited.

Okinoshima and treasures

Okinoshima served as a site of rituals for safe passage between the fourth and ninth centuries. Excavations recovered some 80,000 votive offerings — including mirrors, beads, gold rings, and a gilt-bronze weaving implement — now designated National Treasures of Japan and housed in the shrine's treasure hall, the Shinpōkan.

World Heritage status

In 2017 the "Sacred Island of Okinoshima and Associated Sites in the Munakata Region" was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, recognizing the island's uniquely preserved record of ancient ritual practice.
