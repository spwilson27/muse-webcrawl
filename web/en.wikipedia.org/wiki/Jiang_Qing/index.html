Jiang Qing

Jiang Qing
Born: 19 March 1914, Zhucheng, Shandong, China
Died: 14 May 1991 (aged 77), Beijing, China
Known for: Member of the Gang of Four; wife of Mao Zedong

Jiang Qing (19 March 1914 – 14 May 1991), also known by the stage name Lan Ping, was a Chinese communist revolutionary, actress, and political figure. She was the fourth wife of Mao Zedong, the Chairman of the Chinese Communist Party, and rose to become one of the most powerful figures in China during the Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) as a leading member of the radical faction later denounced as the Gang of Four.

Early life and acting career

Born Li Shumeng in Zhucheng, Shandong, she had a difficult childhood and left home in her teens to join theatre troupes. In the 1930s she worked as an actress in Shanghai, performing in stage plays and films under the name Lan Ping. In 1937, as war with Japan began, she travelled to Yan'an, the Communist base area, where she met Mao Zedong; they married in 1938.

Rise to power

For much of the 1940s and 1950s Jiang played a limited political role, but in the early 1960s she became involved in efforts to reform Chinese opera and other performing arts along revolutionary lines, promoting "model works" (yangbanxi) — a small canon of revolutionary operas and ballets that came to dominate Chinese stages during the Cultural Revolution. With the launch of the Cultural Revolution in 1966, she emerged as a leading voice of the radicals, addressing mass rallies of Red Guards and attacking senior officials and cultural figures.

Gang of Four and fall

Together with Zhang Chunqiao, Yao Wenyuan, and Wang Hongwen, she formed the faction later called the Gang of Four. The group wielded enormous influence in the media, culture, and ideology in Mao's final years. Within a month of Mao's death in September 1976, the four were arrested — an event that marked the end of the Cultural Revolution.

In the televised trial of 1980–81, Jiang was the most defiant of the defendants, arguing that she had merely carried out Mao's orders. She was sentenced to death with a two-year reprieve, later commuted to life imprisonment. She died in 1991 while on medical parole; official reports stated that she took her own life.

Legacy

Jiang Qing remains one of the most controversial figures of modern Chinese history, widely blamed within China for the excesses and persecutions of the Cultural Revolution, particularly against artists and intellectuals.
