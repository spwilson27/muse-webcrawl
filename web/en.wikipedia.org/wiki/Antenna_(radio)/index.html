Antenna (radio)

In radio engineering, an antenna is the interface between radio waves propagating through space and electric currents moving in metal conductors, used with a transmitter or receiver. A transmitting antenna converts electric current into electromagnetic waves; a receiving antenna does the reverse.

TypeElectrical / electronic device
InventorsHeinrich Hertz (1888); Guglielmo Marconi and others
TypesDipole, monopole, loop, Yagi–Uda, parabolic dish, phased array

Principles

Alternating current in an antenna radiates because accelerated charges emit electromagnetic radiation. Key parameters include gain, directivity, radiation pattern, polarization, bandwidth, impedance and efficiency; a resonant antenna is typically comparable in size to a fraction of the wavelength, such as a half-wave dipole. Reciprocity means an antenna's properties are the same in transmission and reception.

Types and applications

Antennas range from AM broadcast masts and FM whips to satellite dishes, radar arrays, mobile-phone and Wi-Fi antennas, and radio telescopes. Arrays combine elements to steer beams electronically.

History

Heinrich Hertz first demonstrated antennas in 1888; Marconi's long-distance experiments after 1895 launched practical radio.
