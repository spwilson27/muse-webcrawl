Belle Isle Park (Michigan)

Belle Isle Park, known simply as Belle Isle, is a 982-acre (397 ha) island park in the Detroit River, in Detroit, Michigan. Connected to the mainland by the MacArthur Bridge, it is the largest city-owned island park in the United States and the third-largest island in the Detroit River.

LocationDetroit River, Detroit, Michigan
Area982 acres (397 ha)
Established1845 (city purchase)
Governing bodyMichigan Department of Natural Resources (state park lease since 2013)

History

The island was used by Native Americans and later by French settlers, who called it Île aux Cochons. The city of Detroit purchased it in 1845. Landscape architect Frederick Law Olmsted contributed to its design in the 1880s.

Attractions

Features include the Belle Isle Aquarium (opened 1904), Anna Scripps Whitcomb Conservatory, Dossin Great Lakes Museum, Belle Isle Nature Center, a giant slide, beaches, athletic fields, and the James Scott Memorial Fountain.

Events

The island hosted the Detroit Grand Prix for many years and is a popular site for picnics, kayaking, and viewing freighters on the river between the United States and Canada.
