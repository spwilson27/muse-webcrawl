The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) is a ministerial department of the Government of the United Kingdom. It is responsible for welfare, pensions, and child maintenance policy, and is the government's largest public-service department, administering the State Pension and a range of working-age, disability, and ill-health benefits to around 20 million claimants.

The department was created on 8 June 2001 from the former Department of Social Security and parts of the Department for Education and Employment, following the 2001 general election.

Responsibilities

The DWP's stated priorities include running an effective welfare system, promoting financial planning for retirement, helping people into work, reducing poverty, and tackling fraud and error. It spends roughly £230 billion a year — the largest single block of UK government expenditure — the majority on pensions and benefits.

Benefits administered

Major payments include the State Pension, Universal Credit, Jobseeker's Allowance, Employment and Support Allowance, Personal Independence Payment, Attendance Allowance, Carer's Allowance, Pension Credit, Housing Benefit (for pension-age claimants), and Statutory Sick Pay policy. Universal Credit, introduced from 2013, merged six working-age benefits into a single payment.

Agencies and services

Services are delivered through networks including Jobcentre Plus, the Pension Service, and the Child Maintenance Service. The Health and Safety Executive and The Pensions Regulator are among the department's associated bodies.

History

The DWP's institutional lineage runs through the Ministry of Pensions (1916), the Ministry of National Insurance (1944), and the Department of Health and Social Security (1968–1988), whose split produced the Department of Social Security. The 2001 merger brought employment services and social security together in one department.

Criticism

The department has faced sustained criticism over benefit sanctions, the rollout and administration of Universal Credit, disability assessment processes, and delays in payments, as well as praise for maintaining payment systems at scale, including during the COVID-19 pandemic surge in claims.
