Glen of Aherlow

The Glen of Aherlow (Irish: Gleann Eatharlaí) is a valley in County Tipperary, Ireland, lying between the Galtee Mountains (Galty Mountains) to the south and the wooded ridge of Slievenamuck to the north. It stretches roughly from Tipperary town in the west toward Bansha in the east, and the River Aherlow flows through it to join the River Suir. The glen forms part of the Golden Vale, one of Ireland's most fertile farming regions.

Geography and tourism

The valley is known for its scenery and for views from the Slievenamuck road, including a well-known viewpoint marked by the statue known as Christ the King, erected in 1950, overlooking the glen toward the Galtees and Galtymore (919 m). The area offers hill-walking on the Galtees, forest trails in Slievenamuck, and heritage sites including the ruins of medieval churches and St. Pecaun's holy well near Cahir. The Glen of Aherlow Fáilte Society promotes walking festivals and tourism in the valley.

History

The glen and its surroundings saw conflict during the Irish Civil War, and the nearby village of Bansha and the Knockmealdown and Galtee hills sheltered flying columns. In later years the valley became associated in popular song and local identity with emigration and its pastoral landscape.
