Plot twist

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A plot twist is a literary technique that introduces a radical change in the direction or expected outcome of the plot of a work of fiction. When it happens near the end of a story, it is often referred to as a twist ending. A plot twist may change the audience's perception of the preceding events, cast earlier scenes in a new light, or reveal information that recontextualises the whole narrative. Spoilers of major twists are typically guarded carefully, and revealing them is widely considered bad etiquette among audiences.

Techniques

  • Anagnorisis — a character's sudden recognition of his or her own or another's true identity or situation, as in Sophocles' Oedipus Rex.
  • Unreliable narrator — the storyteller's credibility is undermined, forcing the audience to reinterpret events.
  • Peripeteia — a sudden reversal of fortune for the protagonist, the classical term analysed by Aristotle in the Poetics.
  • Deus ex machina — an unexpected, contrived resolution introduced abruptly; often criticised as poor plotting.
  • False protagonist — a character presented as the hero is killed off or displaced, shifting the story's centre.
  • Chekhov's gun — a seemingly minor detail becomes decisive, rewarding attentive audiences.

Examples

Famous twists include the revelation of Darth Vader's identity in The Empire Strikes Back (1980), the ending of The Sixth Sense (1999), the identity of "Verbal" Kint in The Usual Suspects (1995), and the solution of Agatha Christie's The Murder of Roger Ackroyd (1926), which destabilised the conventions of detective fiction. O. Henry became so associated with surprise short-story endings that they are sometimes called "O. Henry endings".

Criticism

Critics distinguish twists that are fairly foreshadowed — surprising yet inevitable in retrospect — from arbitrary reversals used for shock value. Overuse of twist endings in thrillers and television has led some writers to subvert or deliberately avoid them.
