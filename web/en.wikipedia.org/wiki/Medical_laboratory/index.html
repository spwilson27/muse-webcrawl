Medical laboratory

A medical laboratory or clinical laboratory is a laboratory where tests are carried out on patient specimens — blood, urine, tissue and other samples — in order to obtain information about a patient's health to aid in the diagnosis, treatment and prevention of disease.

Departments

Hospital laboratories are typically divided into clinical chemistry, haematology, microbiology, immunology, transfusion services (blood bank) and anatomical pathology, including histology and cytology. Most routine testing is automated, and results flow to clinicians through laboratory information systems. An estimated majority of medical decisions draw on laboratory data.

Staff and regulation

Laboratories are directed by pathologists or clinical scientists and staffed by medical laboratory scientists and technicians. In the United States they are regulated under CLIA; quality management, accreditation and point-of-care testing are continuing themes, as is the growth of molecular diagnostics and genetic testing.
