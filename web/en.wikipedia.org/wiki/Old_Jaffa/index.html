Old Jaffa is the historical core of Jaffa, the ancient port city now part of Tel Aviv-Yafo, Israel. Built on a hill of kurkar sandstone overlooking the Mediterranean Sea, it is one of the oldest port areas in the world, with settlement reaching back some 4,000 years.

History

Jaffa served as the port of Jerusalem through the biblical, Roman, Crusader and Ottoman eras; tradition associates it with Jonah's embarkation and with the cedar wood landed for Solomon's Temple. The old city was badly damaged across the centuries and much of it was demolished under the British Mandate and during the 1948 war. From the 1960s the Old Jaffa Development Corporation restored its stone lanes — named for the signs of the zodiac — as an artists' quarter with galleries, theatres, restaurants and a flea market nearby.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the Jaffa Clock Tower (1900), St. Peter's Church with its tall bell tower, the Libyan Synagogue, the Ilana Goor Museum, the Wishing Bridge, Kikar Kedumim square with its visitors' centre beneath, and the lighthouse and harbour below the hill. The skyline of Old Jaffa, seen from the sea and from Tel Aviv's promenade, is a symbol of the city.
