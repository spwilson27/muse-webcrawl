Dolgellau is a market town and community in Gwynedd, north-west Wales, lying on the River Wnion below the mountain Cadair Idris at the southern edge of Snowdonia (Eryri) National Park. It was the county town of the historic county of Merionethshire.

History

Dolgellau grew as a centre of the Welsh woollen industry and, in the late 18th and 19th centuries, of tanning. The town was associated with the Quaker community — its meeting house tradition connects to Welsh Quaker emigration to Pennsylvania — and with Owain Glyndŵr, whose last parliament is associated with the town in 1404. A short-lived gold rush followed the discovery of gold in the surrounding hills in the 19th century; the nearby Gwynfynydd and Clogau mines supplied gold still used for royal wedding rings.

Today

The town is a base for walking and mountain biking, including the Mawddach Trail along the former railway to Barmouth, and hosts the Sesiwn Fawr folk festival. It is largely Welsh-speaking.
