Sign of the cross

The sign of the cross (Latin: signum crucis) is a ritual blessing made by members of many branches of Christianity. It is typically made with the hand tracing the shape of a cross over the body or in the air, often accompanied by the Trinitarian formula "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit" (or Holy Ghost).

Usage

The sign is used in Catholic, Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Anglican and some Lutheran practice, at prayer, Mass, blessings, and as a devotional gesture in moments of danger or reverence. In the Latin tradition the cross is traced from forehead to breast, then from left shoulder to right; in the Byzantine tradition the direction across the shoulders is reversed and the fingers are arranged to evoke the initials of Christ or the Trinity.

History

Tracing a small cross on the forehead is attested in early Christian writers such as Tertullian; the larger sign over the body developed in the medieval period.
