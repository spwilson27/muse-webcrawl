Triquetra

The triquetra (from the Latin tri-, "three", and quetrus, "cornered") is a triangular figure composed of three interlaced arcs, or equivalently a shape formed from three overlapping vesica piscis lenses. It is used as an ornamental design in architecture, and as a religious symbol in modern Pagan practices such as Wicca, where it represents the triple aspect of the goddess. The symbol appears on a wide variety of objects from ancient Europe and Asia.

History

The triquetra appears on carved stones and in insular illuminated manuscripts such as the Book of Kells, where it is used decoratively alongside other knotwork. Similar tri-lobed designs occur in Scandinavian art, on runestones, and in early coinage. In Christian usage the figure was adopted to symbolise the Holy Trinity — the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit — especially when combined with a circle representing unity and eternity.

Modern use

In contemporary Neopagan and Wiccan practice, the triquetra is commonly read as a symbol of the Maiden, Mother and Crone, or of the three realms of land, sea and sky. It has also become familiar in popular culture through jewellery, tattoos and television, notably as the emblem printed on the cover of the "Book of Shadows" in the series Charmed.
