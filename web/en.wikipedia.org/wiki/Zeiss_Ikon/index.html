Zeiss Ikon

Zeiss Ikon was a German camera manufacturer formed in 1926 by the merger of four camera companies — Contessa-Nettel, Ernemann, Goerz and Ica — under the leadership of the Carl Zeiss foundation. Based in Dresden, it became one of the largest camera makers in the world, famous for the Contax rangefinder system, the Contaflex single-lens reflex, and a vast range of folding, box, and miniature cameras.

History

The merger that created Zeiss Ikon was arranged by Zeiss to rationalise the German camera industry and secure an outlet for Zeiss lenses. The company's most prestigious product was the Contax line of 35mm rangefinder cameras, launched in 1932 to compete with Leica. The Contax II (1936) was widely regarded as one of the finest cameras of its era, and after the Second World War the design was continued both in West Germany (Contax IIa/IIIa) and, using seized tooling and plans, as the Kiev cameras in the Soviet Union.

Dresden lay in the Soviet occupation zone after 1945; the company was split, with a West German Zeiss Ikon re-established at Stuttgart. The 1950s and 1960s saw celebrated models including the Contarex professional SLR system, the Contaflex, the Ikoflex and Ikonta folders, and the simple Tenax and Box-Tengor cameras. However, Zeiss Ikon struggled against Japanese competition and internal complexity — it produced too many incompatible systems — and in 1971 it merged camera production with Voigtländer before camera manufacture under the name ceased in 1972. The Contax name was later licensed to Yashica/Kyocera for a new line of cameras (1974–2005).

Revival

In 2004 Carl Zeiss revived the Zeiss Ikon name for a new 35mm rangefinder camera (the Zeiss Ikon ZM), built by Cosina in Japan and compatible with Leica M-mount lenses, accompanied by a series of ZM lenses. The camera was produced until about 2012.

Notable cameras

Contax I–III rangefinders; Contarex ("Bullseye") SLR; Super Ikonta folding rangefinders; Ikoflex medium-format TLRs; Box-Tengor; Werra (from the Jena works, marketed under the marque in some periods); Zeiss Ikon ZM (2004).
