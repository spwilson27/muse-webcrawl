Peckham Rye

Peckham Rye
LocationLondon Borough of Southwark, London, England
TypePublic common and park
AreaAbout 46 hectares (113 acres) combined

Peckham Rye is an open space and road in Peckham, in the London Borough of Southwark, south London. It consists of Peckham Rye Common to the north and Peckham Rye Park to the south, together forming a large triangular green space bordered by housing. The name "Rye" is thought to derive from the Old English rith, a small stream — the River Peck, after which Peckham is named, once flowed here.

History

The common was historically grazing land for the manor of Peckham. In the 1860s the vestry of Camberwell purchased the common to preserve it from development, and the adjoining park was laid out and opened in 1894 on land formerly occupied by a large house and grounds. The park contains ornamental gardens, a lake, a Japanese garden, and sports facilities.

William Blake

Peckham Rye is celebrated in literary tradition as the place where the poet and artist William Blake, as a boy of eight or ten, reported seeing visions: a tree filled with angels, and on another occasion the prophet Ezekiel — experiences he recounted as early evidence of his visionary imagination.

The neighbourhood

The name also applies to the surrounding district, served by Peckham Rye railway station, which opened in 1865 and provides services toward London Bridge, Victoria, and (on the London Overground) Dalston and Clapham Junction. The area's southern end, near East Dulwich and Nunhead, is sometimes called Peckham Rye Village.
