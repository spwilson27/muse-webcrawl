Columbia Records is an American record label owned by Sony Music Entertainment, a subsidiary of Sony. Founded in 1889, it is among the oldest surviving record labels, originating with the Columbia Phonograph Company, which sold Edison phonographs and cylinders in Washington, D.C. The label's name derives from the District of Columbia.

History

Columbia was a pioneer of recorded sound: it marketed early disc records, adopted electrical recording in the 1920s, and in 1948 introduced the 33⅓ rpm long-playing (LP) record, developed under Peter Goldmark at CBS Laboratories, which became the standard album format. Under CBS ownership, Columbia was home to artists across classical, jazz, and popular music, including Miles Davis, Dave Brubeck, Bob Dylan, Simon & Garfunkel, Bruce Springsteen, and Barbra Streisand. The company's 30th Street Studio in New York was famed for recordings such as Kind of Blue.

Ownership changes

CBS sold its records division to Sony in 1988, which renamed it Sony Music Entertainment in 1991. Outside North America, the historic Columbia name and "Magic Notes" trademark had passed through EMI's lineage, complicating the label's branding abroad for decades. Columbia continues as a flagship front-line label of Sony Music, alongside RCA and Epic.
