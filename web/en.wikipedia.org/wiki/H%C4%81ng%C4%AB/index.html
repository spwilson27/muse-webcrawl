Hāngī

Hāngī is a traditional New Zealand Māori method of cooking food in a pit oven using stones heated by fire. It is used for feeding large gatherings and remains an important part of Māori hospitality (manaakitanga).

Method

A pit is dug in the earth and stones are heated in it with a fire. Baskets of food — traditionally root vegetables such as kūmara (sweet potato), potato, and pumpkin, along with meats such as pork, lamb, and chicken — are set on the hot stones, sprinkled with water, and covered with wet cloths or leaves and then earth, so the food steam-cooks slowly over several hours.

Modern practice

Contemporary hāngī may use steel baskets and damp sacks instead of leaves, and adapted barrel or above-ground cookers replicate the method for commercial catering. The cooking imparts a distinctive smoky, earthy flavour.
