Mandukya Upanishad

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Mandukya Upanishad (Sanskrit: Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad) is the shortest of the principal Upanishads, consisting of only twelve verses in prose. It is associated with the Atharvaveda and is ranked among the most important Upanishads; the Muktika canon declares that the Mandukya alone would suffice for liberation.

Content

The text expounds the sacred syllable Om as comprising three elements — a, u, and m — corresponding to the three states of consciousness: waking (jāgrat), dreaming (svapna), and deep sleep (suṣupti). Beyond these lies the "fourth" (turīya), the pure consciousness that is the Self (Ātman), identical with Brahman — "this Self is Brahman" (ayam ātmā brahma), one of the great sayings (mahāvākya) of Hindu philosophy.

Commentaries

Gaudapada's Kārikā, a commentary in verse on the Mandukya, is the earliest systematic exposition of Advaita Vedanta and was in turn commented on by Adi Shankara.
