Caspian Sea

The Caspian Sea is the world's largest inland body of water, often described as the world's largest lake. It lies between Europe and Asia, east of the Caucasus and west of the Central Asian steppe, and is bounded by Russia, Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan, Iran and Azerbaijan. Its surface is about 371,000 square kilometres and its waters are brackish, about a third as salty as seawater.

The Caspian lies about 28 metres below sea level and has no outflow. It is fed principally by the Volga. The sea is rich in oil and natural gas and was historically famous for sturgeon and caviar, though sturgeon populations have collapsed from overfishing and poaching.
