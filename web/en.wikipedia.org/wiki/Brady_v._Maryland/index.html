Brady v. Maryland

Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963)
Court: US Supreme Court
Decided: 13 May 1963
Holding: Prosecution must disclose exculpatory evidence

Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963), is a landmark United States Supreme Court case holding that the prosecution must turn over to the defence all exculpatory evidence — evidence favourable to the accused and material to guilt or punishment. Suppression violates due process.

Facts

John Leo Brady and a companion were convicted of murder in Maryland. The prosecution withheld the companion's extrajudicial statement admitting he alone did the killing.

Legacy

"Brady material" and "Brady violations" became central to criminal procedure; later cases (Giglio, Agurs, Bagley, Kyles) extended the rule to impeachment evidence and evidence known to police. Prosecutors maintain "Brady lists" of officers with credibility issues. See Due process, Exculpatory evidence, US Supreme Court.
