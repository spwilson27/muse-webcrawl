The Montreal Biosphere (French: Biosphère de Montréal) is a museum dedicated to the environment in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. It is housed in the former United States pavilion of Expo 67, a geodesic dome designed by architect Buckminster Fuller and Shoji Sadao, located on Saint Helen's Island in the Saint Lawrence River.

History

The steel-and-acrylic dome, a Class 1 geodesic sphere three-quarters the size of Fuller's complete sphere, was built as the American pavilion for the 1967 World's Fair. Its original exhibition, designed with a monorail passing through it, drew millions of visitors. After Expo 67 the site continued as part of the Man and His World exhibition. In May 1976, during renovations, a welding fire ignited the acrylic skin; the transparent bubble burned away in minutes, leaving the steel truss standing — a famous image in Montreal's history. The structure remained closed and empty for years.

The museum

In 1990 Environment Canada purchased the site, and the Biosphere reopened in 1995 as an environment museum focused on water, climate change, air, and sustainable development, with exhibitions and an "Ecolab". It is the only museum of its kind in Canada. The remaining structure, now open-air steel latticework containing museum buildings, is one of the best-known landmarks of Montreal and a celebrated example of Fuller's geodesic design.
