Hampstead

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hampstead is an affluent area in north-west London, England, in the London Borough of Camden, approximately four miles (6.4 km) north-west of Charing Cross. It is known for its intellectual, liberal, artistic, musical and literary associations, and for the expansive wild parkland of Hampstead Heath.

History

The name Hampstead derives from the Anglo-Saxon words ham and stede, meaning farmstead or homestead. The settlement is recorded in a charter of King Edgar in 959 AD, granting land to Westminster Abbey. For centuries it was a small rural village on the road north from London.

Hampstead grew rapidly after the discovery of chalybeate springs in the late seventeenth century turned it into a fashionable spa town. The opening of Hampstead station on the North London line in the nineteenth century, and later the deep-level tube station in 1907, made it an accessible residential suburb while it retained much of its village character.

Culture

Hampstead has long been home to writers, artists and thinkers. The poet John Keats lived at Wentworth Place, now Keats House museum; Sigmund Freud spent his final year at 20 Maresfield Gardens, now the Freud Museum. The village has numerous blue plaques, independent bookshops, cinemas and the Hampstead Theatre.

Geography

Hampstead village sits on a hill, one of the highest points in London, adjoining Hampstead Heath to the north-east. Its streets are noted for well-preserved Georgian and Victorian architecture, and much of the area is protected as a conservation area. The area is served by Hampstead tube station on the Northern line, the deepest station on the London Underground network.
