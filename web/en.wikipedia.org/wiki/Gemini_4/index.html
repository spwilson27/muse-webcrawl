Gemini 4

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gemini 4
CrewJames McDivitt, Edward White
Launch3 June 1965
Duration4 days
MilestoneFirst American spacewalk
Gemini 4 lifts off.

Gemini 4 was the second crewed flight of NASA's Project Gemini, launched on 3 June 1965 with James McDivitt and Edward White aboard. It is remembered for White's spacewalk — the first by an American — in which he floated outside the capsule for 23 minutes, manoeuvring with a zip-gun thruster and photographing the Earth, reluctant to come back in.

The four-day mission was also America's first long-duration flight, proving astronauts and systems could work for the length of a lunar voyage's outbound leg. An attempted rendezvous with the spent Titan booster failed instructively, teaching NASA that orbital mechanics is counter-intuitive — a lesson that shaped the rendezvous techniques Gemini then mastered.

Legacy

Gemini 4's ground control, for the first time, ran from the new Mission Control Center in Houston, with Christopher Kraft's teams working in shifts — the operational template for Apollo. White's gold-visored image became the icon of the programme; he died less than two years later in the Apollo 1 fire.
