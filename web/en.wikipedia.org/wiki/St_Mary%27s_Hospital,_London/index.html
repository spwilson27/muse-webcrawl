St Mary's Hospital is a teaching hospital in Paddington, in the City of Westminster, London, England. Founded in 1845, it is famous as the place where Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928, a breakthrough that revolutionised medicine.

History

The hospital opened in 1851 and quickly became a centre of medical teaching. In 1928, Alexander Fleming, working in a laboratory at St Mary's, observed that a mould of Penicillium had killed surrounding bacteria, leading to the development of penicillin. The hospital's medical school merged with Imperial College London in 1988.

Facilities

St Mary's is a major acute hospital providing accident and emergency services, maternity care through the Lindo Wing, and specialist services. It forms part of Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust.

Notable births

The Lindo Wing is known as the birthplace of several members of the British royal family, including Prince William and Prince George.
