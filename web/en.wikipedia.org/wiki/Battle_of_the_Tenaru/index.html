The Battle of the Tenaru, sometimes called the Battle of the Ilu River or the Battle of Alligator Creek, was a land battle between the Imperial Japanese Army and Allied ground forces that took place on 21 August 1942 on the island of Guadalcanal during the Pacific campaign of the Second World War. It was the first major Japanese land offensive against the Allied perimeter around Henderson Field.

Background

After the Allied landings on 7 August 1942, the Japanese rushed reinforcements to Guadalcanal. The first echelon of the Ichiki Detachment, about 900 men under Colonel Kiyonao Ichiki, landed at Taivu Point on 18–19 August. Badly underestimating American strength — Ichiki believed only a few thousand Allied troops were ashore, when in fact about 11,000 US Marines defended the perimeter — he marched west to attack immediately.

The battle

In the early hours of 21 August, Ichiki's men assaulted Marine positions dug in on the west bank of the Ilu River (mistakenly called the Tenaru on US maps) in repeated frontal charges against barbed wire, machine guns, canister-firing 37 mm guns and artillery, with US tanks attacking their flank. By the afternoon the detachment was effectively annihilated; Ichiki died, reportedly by suicide or killed in action, and fewer than 130 of his men escaped to Taivu.

Aftermath

The defeat did not deter the Japanese, who continued landing reinforcements and launched larger attacks in September at the Battle of Edson's Ridge and in October for Henderson Field. The Tenaru action demonstrated to both sides that the campaign would be one of attrition.
