Nitrobenzene is an organic compound with the chemical formula C6H5NO2. It is a pale yellow, oily liquid with an odour reminiscent of bitter almonds, and it is the simplest of the nitroarenes. Nitrobenzene is produced on a very large scale — millions of tonnes per year — almost entirely as a precursor to aniline.

Production

Nitrobenzene is manufactured by nitrating benzene with mixed acid (concentrated nitric and sulfuric acids) at about 50–60 °C. The strongly exothermic reaction is run in continuous processes with careful heat removal. In the laboratory it was first prepared in 1834 by Eilhard Mitscherlich, who also named it.

Uses

Roughly 95% or more of nitrobenzene output is hydrogenated to aniline, which is used mainly to make methylene diphenyl diisocyanate (MDI) for polyurethane foams. Minor uses include as a solvent, as a precursor to other chemicals such as benzidine historically, and as a masking scent in soaps and polishes under the name "oil of mirbane" — a practice abandoned because of toxicity.

Toxicity

Nitrobenzene is readily absorbed through the skin and lungs and is converted in the body to intermediates that oxidize haemoglobin to methemoglobin, impairing oxygen transport and causing cyanosis; it is also toxic to the liver, kidneys, and central nervous system, and is classified as a possible human carcinogen. Exposure in the workplace is tightly regulated. Environmental releases are controlled because the compound is persistent and harmful to aquatic life, although it is biodegradable under some conditions.
