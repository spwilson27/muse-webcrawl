Aaron Hillel Swartz (November 8, 1986 – January 11, 2013) was an American computer programmer, entrepreneur, writer, political organizer, and internet activist. A prodigy who helped create the web feed format RSS at age 14, he contributed to the technical architecture of Creative Commons, co-founded the social news site Reddit, and became a leading advocate for open access to scholarly and public information.

Early work

Swartz was born in Highland Park, Illinois. As a teenager he won the ArsDigita Prize for building educational websites, and in 2000 he joined the RDF Core working group that produced RSS 1.0. He later worked with Lawrence Lessig on Creative Commons, developed the web.py web framework, and contributed to the Open Library project at the Internet Archive. In 2005 he joined the founders of Y Combinator-backed Reddit through the merger of his company Infogami; Reddit was sold to Condé Nast in 2006, and Swartz left shortly afterward.

Activism

Swartz campaigned for open access to information. In 2008 he downloaded about 2.7 million federal court records from the PACER system — then charging per page — and made them freely available. In his "Guerilla Open Access Manifesto" (2008) he called for civil disobedience against paywalls on scholarly literature. In 2010 he co-founded Demand Progress, which organized the online protest that helped defeat the SOPA and PIPA internet legislation in 2012.

JSTOR case and death

In late 2010 and early 2011, Swartz connected a laptop to the MIT network and systematically downloaded several million articles from the academic archive JSTOR. He was arrested and charged under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act; a superseding indictment brought thirteen felony counts and the prospect of decades in prison, though JSTOR itself declined to pursue civil action and asked that the matter be dropped. On January 11, 2013, facing trial, Swartz hanged himself in his Brooklyn apartment. He was 26.

Legacy

Swartz's death prompted widespread criticism of the prosecution, a congressional inquiry, and proposals to amend the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act ("Aaron's Law"). MIT commissioned a review of its role in the case. He was posthumously inducted into the Internet Hall of Fame in 2013, and his life is the subject of the documentary The Internet's Own Boy (2014). An annual Aaron Swartz Day is observed on his birthday.
