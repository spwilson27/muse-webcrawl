Gustav Ludwig Hertz

Gustav Ludwig Hertz (22 July 1887 – 30 October 1975) was a German physicist who shared the 1925 Nobel Prize in Physics with James Franck for the Franck–Hertz experiment (1913–14), which demonstrated the quantised energy levels of atoms by bombarding mercury vapour with electrons — key early evidence for Bohr's model of the atom.

Hertz was the nephew of Heinrich Hertz, the discoverer of electromagnetic waves. After being barred from his Berlin professorship under Nazi racial laws, he worked in industry at Siemens. After the Second World War he was taken to the Soviet Union, where he worked on the Soviet atomic bomb project at Sukhumi until 1955, when he returned to East Germany as a professor in Leipzig. He is the only Nobel laureate whose nephew (Heinrich) is also honoured by a famous experiment and unit — the hertz.
