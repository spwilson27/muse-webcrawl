Cost of living

[Image: Cost of living]

The cost of living is the cost of maintaining a certain standard of living for an individual or a household. Changes in the cost of living over time are often operationalized in a cost-of-living index. Cost of living calculations are also used to compare the cost of maintaining a given standard of living in different geographic areas.

Components

The principal components of the cost of living are housing, food, transportation, healthcare, education, energy, clothing, and taxes. Housing is typically the largest single expense; in high-cost metropolitan areas such as New York, London, Hong Kong and Zurich, rents and house prices far exceed national averages, while some goods and services cost roughly the same everywhere.

Measurement

Statistical agencies measure changes in the cost of living using consumer price indices that track the prices of a basket of goods and services. Private surveys compare absolute living costs between cities; the Economist Intelligence Unit's Worldwide Cost of Living survey and Mercer's Cost of Living ranking are widely cited, frequently placing Singapore, Hong Kong, Zurich and New York among the most expensive cities in the world.

Adjustments

Employers may pay cost-of-living allowances to staff working in expensive locations, and many pension systems and welfare programmes include automatic cost-of-living adjustments (COLA) tied to inflation. In the United States, Social Security benefits are adjusted annually based on the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W).
