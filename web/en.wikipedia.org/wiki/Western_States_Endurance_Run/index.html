Western States Endurance Run

The Western States Endurance Run, known commonly as the Western States 100, is a 100.2-mile (161 km) ultramarathon that takes place on the trails of California's Sierra Nevada mountains each year on the last full weekend of June. The race starts at the base of the Squaw Valley ski resort and finishes at the Placer High School track in Auburn, California.

History

The course follows the Western States Trail, originally used for the Tevis Cup horse ride. In 1974 Gordy Ainsleigh ran the trail on foot alongside the horses, finishing in 23 hours 42 minutes, and is credited with founding the 100-mile trail run. The first official run was held in 1977 with 14 starters.

Course

Runners climb about 18,090 feet (5,510 m) and descend about 22,970 feet (7,000 m), crossing the Granite Chief Wilderness, deep canyons including El Dorado and Volcano creeks, and the American River at Rucky Chucky. Finishers within 30 hours earn a bronze belt buckle; those under 24 hours earn a silver buckle.

Notable records

The race is one of the oldest and most prestigious 100-mile races in the world. Jim Walmsley set the men's course record of 14:09:28 in 2019, and Courtney Dauwalter set the women's record of 15:29:33 in 2023.
