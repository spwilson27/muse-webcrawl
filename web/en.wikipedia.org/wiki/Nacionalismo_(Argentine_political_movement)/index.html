Nacionalismo (Argentine political movement)

Nacionalismo was a far-right nationalist political movement in Argentina that emerged in the 1920s and 1930s, drawing inspiration from European authoritarian currents such as Italian Fascism and Spanish Falangism. It advocated a corporatist, Catholic, anti-liberal and anti-communist vision of Argentine society.

History

The movement arose in reaction to the liberal-democratic order established after 1880 and to the social changes brought by mass immigration. Intellectuals such as Leopoldo Lugones, Manuel Gálvez, Carlos Ibarguren and the Irazusta brothers (Rodolfo and Julio) gave it ideological form. Groups including the Legión Cívica Argentina and later the Alianza de la Juventud Nacionalista mobilised students and military officers.

Nacionalistas supported the 1930 coup of José Félix Uriburu against President Hipólito Yrigoyen and hoped to impose a corporatist regime, though Uriburu ultimately opted for a more limited conservative restoration. The movement remained influential in the armed forces and the Catholic press through the 1940s, and elements of its thought fed into later Peronism and into the anti-Peronist nationalism of subsequent decades.

Ideology

Key tenets included Hispanidad (cultural and spiritual unity with Spain), Catholic integralism, rejection of both liberal capitalism and Marxism, admiration for strong executive authority, and hostility toward British economic influence in Argentina. The movement also contained strongly antisemitic currents, influenced by European fascist propaganda.

Legacy

Although nacionalismo never governed Argentina directly, its ideas shaped military nationalism, the 1943–46 regime, and later far-right organisations such as Tacuara in the 1960s.
