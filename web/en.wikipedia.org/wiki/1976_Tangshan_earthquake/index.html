1976 Tangshan earthquake

The 1976 Tangshan earthquake was a magnitude 7.6 earthquake that struck the industrial city of Tangshan in Hebei Province, China, in the early morning of 28 July 1976. Striking at 3:42 a.m. while most residents slept, it virtually levelled the city in a matter of seconds and is regarded as the deadliest earthquake of the 20th century, and possibly the deadliest in recorded history.

The disaster

Tangshan's buildings, largely of unreinforced brick and adobe construction, offered almost no resistance; some 85 per cent of structures collapsed or were rendered unusable. A major aftershock of magnitude 7.1 struck later that afternoon, trapping rescue efforts and survivors alike. The official Chinese death toll, released only in 1979, was 242,769, with some 164,000 severely injured; unofficial estimates are far higher. Tremors were felt as far away as Beijing, where buildings swayed and residents fled into the streets.

Response and politics

The Chinese government under the "Gang of Four"-era leadership declined all offers of foreign aid, mounting a vast domestic relief operation led by the People's Liberation Army. The earthquake, occurring weeks before the death of Mao Zedong and in a year already marked by the death of Premier Zhou Enlai and a rare meteor shower, was popularly interpreted in China as an omen of dynastic change, recalling the traditional Mandate of Heaven. Tangshan was rebuilt over the following decade as a modern, earthquake-resistant city.

Legacy

The disaster prompted major reforms in Chinese seismology, building codes, and disaster management. A memorial and museum in Tangshan commemorate the victims, and the quake remains a touchstone in Chinese popular memory, notably in Feng Xiaogang's 2010 film Aftershock.
