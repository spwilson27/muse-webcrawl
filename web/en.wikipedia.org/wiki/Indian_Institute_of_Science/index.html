The Indian Institute of Science (IISc) is a public research university in Bengaluru, India, widely regarded as the country's premier institution for advanced scientific and technological research. It was conceived by industrialist Jamsetji Tata in the late nineteenth century, established in 1909 with land granted by the Maharaja of Mysore, and began with its first students in 1911.

History and organization

The institute's first Indian director was the Nobel laureate C. V. Raman. IISc is organized into divisions spanning biological, chemical, electrical, interdisciplinary, mechanical, and physical and mathematical sciences, with more than forty departments and centres. It is primarily a postgraduate institution, admitting students for research degrees and, since 2011, a four-year undergraduate programme in science.

Research and standing

IISc consistently ranks at or near the top of Indian university rankings and among the world's leading institutions for research output per faculty. Notable alumni and faculty include C. V. Raman, Satish Dhawan, G. N. Ramachandran, Vikram Sarabhai, and C. N. R. Rao. The leafy 400-acre campus in northern Bengaluru is also known for its rich biodiversity.
