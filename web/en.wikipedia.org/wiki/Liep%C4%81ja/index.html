Liepāja (German: Libau) is a state city in western Latvia, on the Baltic Sea coast in the historical region of Courland. It is Latvia's third-largest city and is notable for its ice-free port, its nineteenth-century naval base at Karosta, and its reputation as a centre of Latvian rock music, earning it the nickname "the city where the wind is born".

History

The settlement originated as a Curonian fishing village at the mouth of the Līva River. It was held by the Livonian Order and later the Duchy of Courland, receiving town rights in 1625. As Libau in the Russian Empire, it grew into a major commercial and naval port; in 1890 Tsar Alexander III ordered construction of the Karosta naval fortress north of the city. Liepāja was briefly the seat of Latvia's provisional government in 1919.

Karosta and the Soviet era

Under Soviet occupation, Karosta became a closed military town and one of the largest Soviet naval bases in the Baltic. After Latvian independence its prison and fortress structures became tourist attractions.

Economy and culture

The modern port handles cargo year-round, and the city hosts the Liepāja Special Economic Zone. Landmarks include St. Nicholas Orthodox Naval Cathedral, the concert hall Great Amber (Lielais dzintars, opened 2015), and wide Baltic beaches.
