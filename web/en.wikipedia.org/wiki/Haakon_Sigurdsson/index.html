Haakon Sigurdsson

Haakon Sigurdsson (c. 935 – 995), known as Haakon Jarl (Old Norse: Hákon jarl), was the de facto ruler of Norway from about 975 to 995 as Earl of Lade. The son of Sigurd Haakonsson, earl of Lade in Trøndelag, he succeeded his father after Sigurd was murdered by King Harald Greycloak.

Rise to power

After years maneuvering between the Norwegian kings and Denmark, Haakon allied with Harald Bluetooth of Denmark, helped defeat Harald Greycloak, and was rewarded with authority over much of Norway as a Danish vassal. He later broke with Harald over the Danish king's attempts to impose Christianity.

Rule and death

Haakon was a staunch pagan and is remembered for restoring temples and sacrifices after the forced Christianisation attempts of the Danish kings. In 986 he defeated the Jomsvikings at the Battle of Hjörungavágr. His rule grew tyrannical, and in 995 the Trøndelag farmers rose against him; Haakon was killed by his own slave, Tormod Kark, while hiding in a pigsty at Rimul, clearing the way for the return of Olaf Tryggvason.
