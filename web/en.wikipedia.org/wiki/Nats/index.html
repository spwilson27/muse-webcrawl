Nats

Region: Myanmar (Burma)
Tradition: Burmese folk religion
Canonical number: 37 Great Nats
Major festival: Taungbyon Festival

Nats (Burmese: နတ်) are spirits worshipped in Myanmar (Burma) alongside, and intertwined with, Theravada Buddhism. Nat worship predates the arrival of Buddhism and survives as a major element of Burmese folk religion, involving shrines, offerings, spirit mediums and festivals.

The Thirty-Seven Nats

Tradition recognises an official pantheon of 37 Great Nats, most of them humans — kings, queens, heroes and wronged figures — who died violent or untimely deaths and became spirits. King Anawrahta of Bagan (11th century) is said to have tried to suppress nat worship and then to have systematised it, placing the Buddhist guardian Thagyamin (Indra) at its head. Famous nats include the Taungbyon brothers, executed servants of Anawrahta, and Popa Medaw, the "Queen Mother of Popa", associated with Mount Popa, an extinct volcano crowned with shrines that is the centre of nat worship.

Practice

Nats are propitiated with offerings of food, flowers, alcohol and money, often through a nat kadaw — a spirit medium, frequently a woman or a transgender person — who becomes possessed by the nat during ceremonies (nat pwè) accompanied by music and dancing. The largest festival, at Taungbyon near Mandalay, draws huge crowds each year. Many Burmese Buddhists see no contradiction between devotion to the Buddha and honouring nats for worldly protection.
