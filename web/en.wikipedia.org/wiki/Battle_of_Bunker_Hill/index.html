Battle of Bunker Hill

The Battle of Bunker Hill was fought on 17 June 1775 during the Siege of Boston in the first stage of the American Revolutionary War. Although named for Bunker Hill in Charlestown, Massachusetts, most of the fighting took place on nearby Breed's Hill. British forces under General William Howe launched frontal assaults on American colonial troops entrenched on the Charlestown peninsula; the British ultimately carried the position, but at such heavy cost — over 1,000 casualties, including many officers — that the battle is often described as a Pyrrhic victory and a major moral boost for the American cause.

The Bunker Hill Monument, completed in 1843, stands on Breed's Hill.

The battle

On the night of 16–17 June, some 1,200 colonial troops under Colonel William Prescott fortified Breed's Hill with a redoubt and breastworks. The next afternoon Howe landed troops and attacked. Twice the British advanced and were thrown back by disciplined musket fire — the occasion of the famous, possibly apocryphal, order "Don't fire until you see the whites of their eyes", attributed to Prescott or Israel Putnam and intended to conserve scarce powder. On the third assault, with the defenders' ammunition exhausted, the British overwhelmed the redoubt in hand-to-hand fighting. American losses were about 450 killed and wounded, including the popular leader Joseph Warren; British losses were over 1,000, roughly a third of those engaged.

Significance

The battle demonstrated that colonial militia could stand against British regulars, hardening opinion in the colonies and making a negotiated reconciliation less likely; in Britain the cost shocked the government and made Howe cautious in later campaigns. The 221-foot (67 m) granite obelisk of the Bunker Hill Monument, completed in 1843 with addresses by Daniel Webster, commemorates the battle on the site.
