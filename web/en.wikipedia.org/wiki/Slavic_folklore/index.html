Slavic folklore

Slavic folklore encompasses the traditions, tales, beliefs and customs of the Slavic peoples of Central, Eastern and Southeastern Europe. Developed over many centuries, it blends remnants of pre-Christian Slavic religion with Christian influences and local variation across Russian, Ukrainian, Polish, Czech, Serbian, Bulgarian and other Slavic cultures.

Household and nature spirits

Traditional belief populated the home and landscape with spirits: the domovoy guarded the household, the leshy ruled the forest, the vodyanoy haunted rivers and mills, and rusalki were spirits of the waters. The kikimora and bannik were associated with the house and bathhouse respectively. Offerings and rituals kept these beings benevolent.

Folk tales and heroes

Russian skazki collected by Alexander Afanasyev feature heroes such as Ivan Tsarevich, the wise Vasilisa, and the witch Baba Yaga, who lives in a hut on chicken legs and flies in a mortar. South Slavic epic poetry celebrates heroes like Prince Marko, while the Serbian and Bulgarian traditions preserve songs of haiduks (outlaw heroes). Recurring structures — threefold repetitions, quests, magical helpers — were foundational material for Vladimir Propp's Morphology of the Folktale (1928).

Calendar customs

Seasonal festivals such as Maslenitsa (the pre-Lenten butter festival), Ivan Kupala night with its fire-jumping and flower wreaths, and winter Koliada carolling mark the agricultural year and survive in modern celebrations.

Influence

Slavic folklore has inspired works from Rimsky-Korsakov's operas and Stravinsky's The Firebird to modern fantasy literature and video games featuring Baba Yaga and the leshy.
