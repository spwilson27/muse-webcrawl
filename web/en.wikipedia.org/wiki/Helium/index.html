Helium is a chemical element with symbol He and atomic number 2 — a colourless, odourless, inert noble gas, the second lightest and second most abundant element in the observable universe (after hydrogen), produced mainly by stellar fusion. It was first detected in the Sun's spectrum in 1868 (hence its name, from Greek helios) before being found on Earth in 1895.

Helium has the lowest boiling point of any element (4.2 K), so liquid helium is the indispensable coolant for superconducting magnets (MRI scanners, particle accelerators), cryogenics and spaceflight; it is also used in balloons and airships (non-flammable, unlike hydrogen), deep-sea diving gas mixtures, and welding. Earthly helium escapes to space and comes mainly from natural-gas fields (alpha decay underground replenishes pockets) — supply shortages are a recurring concern because it is effectively non-renewable once released.
