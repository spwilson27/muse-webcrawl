Oswald Boelcke

Born19 May 1891, Giebichenstein, German Empire
Died28 October 1916 (aged 25), near Bapaume, France
ServiceImperial German Army Air Service

Oswald Boelcke (19 May 1891 – 28 October 1916) was a German flying ace of the First World War and one of the fathers of air combat tactics. Credited with 40 aerial victories, he organised and led Jagdstaffel 2 (Jasta 2), the model German fighter squadron, and codified his doctrine in the famous Dicta Boelcke.

Early service

Boelcke joined the army as a cadet before the war and transferred to the air service in 1914. Flying two-seat reconnaissance aircraft, he won the Iron Cross and moved to single-seat Fokker fighters in 1915, becoming one of the first German aces alongside Max Immelmann. In January 1916 he was among the first aviators awarded the Pour le Mérite, Germany's highest decoration.

Jasta 2 and the Dicta Boelcke

After the Somme offensive exposed Germany's air weakness, Boelcke was recalled from a tour of the Eastern Front and given command of the newly formed Jasta 2 in September 1916. He hand-picked pilots — among them a young Manfred von Richthofen — and drilled them in his tactical rules: secure advantages before attacking, attack from close range, keep the sun behind you, never lose sight of the enemy, and always know how to break off combat.

Death

On 28 October 1916, during a dogfight over the Somme, Boelcke's Albatros collided in mid-air with the aircraft of his own wingman, Erwin Böhme. Boelcke crashed and was killed instantly; he was 25. He received a state funeral and was mourned nationally; Jasta 2 was renamed "Jasta Boelcke" in his honour.
