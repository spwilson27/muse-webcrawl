Sexy prime

In number theory, sexy primes are prime numbers that differ from each other by 6. For example, the numbers 5 and 11 are both prime and differ by 6, so they form a pair of sexy primes. The name is a pun on the Latin word for six, sex; it follows the pattern of twin primes (differing by 2) and cousin primes (differing by 4).

Groupings

Sexy primes can occur in pairs, triplets, quadruplets and quintuplets. A sexy prime triplet has the form p, p + 6, p + 12, with all three prime — for example (7, 13, 19). A quadruplet extends this to p + 18, such as (5, 11, 17, 23). There is only one possible quintuplet pattern, p, p + 6, p + 12, p + 18, p + 24, because among any five numbers spaced by 6 one must be divisible by 5; the unique example begins with 5 itself: (5, 11, 17, 23, 29).

Properties

With the exception of the pair (5, 11), sexy primes always end in the digit 1, 3, 7 or 9, and the larger member of a pair ends in 1 or 7 when the smaller ends in 1 or 7 accordingly. As with twin primes, it is unknown whether there are infinitely many sexy prime pairs, although the prime k-tuple conjecture implies that there are. The largest known sexy prime pairs are found through distributed prime-search projects and involve numbers with tens of thousands of digits.
