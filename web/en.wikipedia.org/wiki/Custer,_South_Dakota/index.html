Custer is a city in Custer County, South Dakota, United States, and the county seat of Custer County. It is the oldest town established by Euro-Americans in the Black Hills, founded in 1875 during the Black Hills gold rush, and named for Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer, whose 1874 expedition had confirmed gold in the region.

History

Gold was discovered near present-day Custer in 1874 by the Black Hills Expedition led by Custer. Prospectors soon flooded in despite the land being reserved to the Lakota Sioux under the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868). The settlement was initially called Stonewall, then renamed Custer City, and later simply Custer. Although richer strikes at Deadwood drew many miners away, Custer survived as a supply and civic centre, becoming the county seat.

Geography and tourism

Custer sits in the southern Black Hills at an elevation of roughly 5,300 feet. It serves as a gateway community for Custer State Park, famous for its free-roaming bison herd and the Needles Highway, and is a short drive from Mount Rushmore, the Crazy Horse Memorial, Wind Cave National Park, and Jewel Cave National Monument. Tourism is the mainstay of the local economy.
