New Order

New Order (German: Neuordnung) is principally known as the political and economic order which Nazi Germany sought to impose on the territories it conquered in Europe during the Second World War. The term was used by Adolf Hitler from 1941 to describe the planned reordering of the continent under German hegemony: a racially stratified system in which Germanic peoples would rule, vast areas of Eastern Europe would be cleared of Slavic and Jewish populations and resettled with German colonists, and the economies of subjugated states would be subordinated to the Reich.

The Nazi Neuordnung

The concept built on the ideology of Lebensraum ("living space") set out in Mein Kampf and was operationalised in documents such as the Generalplan Ost, the master plan developed under Heinrich Himmler's SS for the Germanisation of the East, which envisaged the expulsion, enslavement, or extermination of tens of millions of people. In Western Europe the New Order took the form of occupation administrations, annexations, and the economic exploitation of countries such as France, Belgium, and the Netherlands. Japan promoted a parallel concept in Asia, the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere.

Other uses

The phrase "new order" is generic and has been used by many movements and regimes, before and since: it appears in the Latin motto novus ordo seclorum ("a new order of the ages") on the reverse of the Great Seal of the United States (1782), commemorating the founding of the republic. In Indonesia, "New Order" (Orde Baru) is the standard name for the authoritarian regime of President Suharto (1966–1998), which followed Sukarno's "Old Order". In popular culture, New Order is also the name of the English rock band formed in 1980 from the members of Joy Division.
