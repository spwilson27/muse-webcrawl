Anton Karas (7 July 1906 – 10 January 1985) was an Austrian zither player and composer, best known for the music of the 1949 film The Third Man, whose theme—often called "The Third Man Theme" or "The Harry Lime Theme"—became an international hit and made Karas famous.

Karas with his zither

Life and career

Karas was born in Vienna to a working-class family of Hungarian and Czech origin. Fascinated by the zither from childhood, he studied music while working in an automobile factory. After the Second World War he earned a modest living performing in Viennese wine gardens (Heurigen).

In 1948 the British film director Carol Reed, in Vienna shooting The Third Man, heard Karas playing in a wine garden and engaged him to compose and perform the film's entire score on solo zither. The jauntily melancholic music became inseparable from the film's atmosphere. Released as a single, Karas's recording of the theme topped the American charts for weeks in 1950 and sold in the millions, making Karas briefly one of the most famous instrumentalists in the world.

Fame brought tours and recording contracts, and with his earnings Karas opened his own wine garden, Zum Dritten Mann ("At the Sign of the Third Man"), in Vienna's Sievering district, a popular attraction for tourists. Despite numerous offers, he remained homesick on tour and eventually limited his performances to Austria. He died in Vienna in 1985 and is buried in the Sievering cemetery.

Legacy

Karas's zither music for The Third Man is consistently ranked among the most celebrated film scores of all time, notable for relying on a single folk instrument rather than an orchestra. The theme was later covered by many artists, including Guy Lombardo, whose version also reached number one in the United States.
