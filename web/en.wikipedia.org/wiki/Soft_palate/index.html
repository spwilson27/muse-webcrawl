Soft palate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The soft palate (also known as the velum or muscular palate) is the soft tissue constituting the back of the roof of the mouth. It is distinguished from the hard palate at the front of the mouth in that it contains no bone, being composed of muscle and connective tissue covered by mucous membrane.

Structure

The soft palate consists of five muscles — the tensor veli palatini, palatoglossus, palatopharyngeus, levator veli palatini and musculus uvulae — which move it during swallowing and speech. Hanging from its posterior free border is the uvula.

Function

During swallowing the soft palate elevates to close off the nasopharynx, preventing food from entering the nasal cavity. It is also essential in speech, sealing the oral cavity from the nose for most consonants; incomplete closure (velopharyngeal insufficiency), as in cleft palate, produces hypernasal speech. Palatal vibration is involved in snoring.
