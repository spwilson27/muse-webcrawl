Recurrence relation

In mathematics, a recurrence relation is an equation according to which the nth term of a sequence of numbers is defined as a function of the previous terms in the sequence. The term difference equation sometimes refers to a specific type of recurrence relation. An example usage is a Fibbionaci recurrence, and, generally, the solutions to such equations can be approached with generating functions.

Recurrence relations are useful in combinatorics, number theory, the analysis of algorithms, and many other areas of discrete mathematics. The complexity of many algorithms is naturally expressed as a recurrence, such as the divide-and-conquer recurrence T(n) = 2T(n/2) + O(n), solved by the master theorem.

Definition

A recurrence relation for a sequence (an) is an equation that expresses an in terms of one or more of the previous terms an−1, an−2, ..., an−k. The integer k is called the order of the recurrence. Together with a set of initial conditions (values for the first k terms), the recurrence determines every term of the sequence.

Examples

Fibonacci numbers

The most famous recurrence defines the Fibonacci sequence: Fn = Fn−1 + Fn−2, with F0 = 0 and F1 = 1. This gives 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, ...

Factorial

The factorial function can be defined recursively by n! = n·(n−1)!, with 0! = 1.

Logistic map

The logistic map xn+1 = r xn(1 − xn) is a nonlinear recurrence famous in chaos theory for its period-doubling route to chaos as r increases.

Solving

A linear recurrence with constant coefficients, such as an = c1an−1 + ... + ckan−k, can be solved by the method of the characteristic equation: one forms the polynomial xk = c1xk−1 + ... + ck and expresses the general solution as a combination of powers of its roots. For the Fibonacci recurrence, this method yields Binet's formula, Fn = (φn − ψn)/√5, where φ is the golden ratio.

Applications

Recurrences appear in the enumeration of combinatorial objects (the Catalan numbers satisfy a convolution recurrence), in economics (the Solow and cobweb models), in population dynamics, and in numerical methods for differential equations.
