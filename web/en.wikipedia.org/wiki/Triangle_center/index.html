Triangle center

In geometry, a triangle center (or triangle centre) is a point in the plane that is in some sense in the middle of a triangle. This sense is made precise by a formal definition: a triangle center is a point whose trilinear coordinates are given by a well-defined function of the three side lengths (or angles), symmetric in a specified way, so that the point is defined independently of how the triangle's vertices are labelled.

Classical centers

  • Centroid — the intersection of the three medians; the triangle's centre of mass.
  • Circumcenter — the centre of the circumscribed circle, the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors.
  • Incenter — the centre of the inscribed circle, the intersection of the angle bisectors.
  • Orthocenter — the intersection of the three altitudes.
  • Nine-point center — the centre of the nine-point circle.

Cataloguing

Thousands of triangle centers have been catalogued in Clark Kimberling's Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers, each assigned a number X(n): the incenter is X(1), the centroid X(2), the circumcenter X(3), the orthocenter X(4), and the nine-point center X(5). Many centers are related by constructions such as isogonal conjugation, and the classical centers lie on notable lines such as the Euler line.
