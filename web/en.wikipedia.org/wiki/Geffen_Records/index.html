Geffen Records (formerly the Geffen Label) is an American record label, founded in 1980 by music executive David Geffen. It operates through Interscope Geffen A&M Records, a division of Universal Music Group.

History

Geffen, who had previously founded Asylum Records, launched the label with backing from Warner Bros. Records. Early signings included Donna Summer, Elton John, John Lennon — whose album Double Fantasy (1980) was the label's landmark early release — and Cher. Through the 1980s Geffen became a major force in rock, signing or distributing Asia, Guns N' Roses, Aerosmith, Peter Gabriel and Whitesnake.

In 1990 Geffen sold the label to MCA (later Universal) for about $550 million in stock. The label's 1990s roster defined alternative rock: Nirvana's Nevermind (1991) was released through its DGC imprint, alongside Sonic Youth, Beck, Weezer and Hole. After the 1998–99 PolyGram merger, Geffen was folded into Interscope Geffen A&M, absorbing A&M and later acts; it was revived as a full imprint in the 2010s and 2020s, signing artists including Olivia Rodrigo (in partnership with Interscope).
