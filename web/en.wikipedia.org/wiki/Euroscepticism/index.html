Euroscepticism

Euroscepticism, also spelled Euroskepticism, is a political position involving criticism of the European Union (EU) and European integration. It ranges from those who oppose some EU institutions and policies and seek reform ("soft Euroscepticism" or Eurorealism), to those who oppose EU membership outright and seek withdrawal ("hard Euroscepticism").

History

The term arose in Britain, where debate over membership of the European Communities shaped politics from the 1975 referendum onward. Eurosceptic parties have included the UK Independence Party, France's National Rally, Italy's Lega, Germany's AfD and movements across Central and Eastern Europe. The 2016 Brexit referendum, in which the United Kingdom voted to leave, was its most consequential expression; the UK withdrew in 2020.

Causes and arguments

Critics cite democratic deficit, loss of national sovereignty, immigration, economic regulation and budget contributions; supporters of integration counter with trade, peace and collective bargaining benefits. Eurobarometer surveys track wide variation in public trust in the EU by country and over time.
