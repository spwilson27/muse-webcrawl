Night game

A night game is a sporting event that takes place, either wholly or in part, after the local sunset. The term is most commonly used in baseball. Night games became possible with the wide implementation of floodlights in stadiums, and they transformed the economics and scheduling of professional sports by allowing working fans to attend games in the evening.

Baseball

Although exhibition and minor league games under lights were played earlier, the first night game in Major League Baseball history occurred on May 24, 1935, when the Cincinnati Reds defeated the Philadelphia Phillies 2–1 at Crosley Field in Cincinnati. President Franklin D. Roosevelt pressed a button in the White House that symbolically switched on the lights. Initially each team was allowed only a handful of night games per season, subject to league approval.

Night games gradually became the norm: the last major league holdout was the Chicago Cubs, whose Wrigley Field had no lights until 1988; the first official night game there was played on August 9, 1988, against the New York Mets (an attempt the previous evening was rained out). The final World Series game played entirely in the daytime was Game 4 of the 1987 World Series; since 1988 all World Series games have started in prime time.

Other sports

Night games are routine in American football, basketball, and hockey, and floodlit evening fixtures are standard in association football, cricket (including day/night one-day and Twenty20 matches), and tennis.
