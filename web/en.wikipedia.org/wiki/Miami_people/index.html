The Miami people (Miami–Illinois: Myaamia) are a Native American nation originally inhabiting the region around the southern shore of Lake Michigan and the Wabash and Maumee river valleys, in present-day Indiana, Ohio, Illinois and Michigan. They speak an Algonquian language shared historically with the Illinois Confederation peoples.

History

The Miami were prominent in the fur trade and in the wars of the 18th century; their war chief Little Turtle led the Western Confederacy to victories over U.S. forces in 1790–1791 before its defeat at Fallen Timbers (1794) and the Treaty of Greenville (1795). Through 19th-century treaties most Miami were removed westward, many to Kansas and then to Indian Territory (Oklahoma), though a significant group remained in Indiana. Notable leaders included Jean Baptiste Richardville, one of the wealthiest men of his era on the frontier.

Present day

The federally recognised Miami Tribe of Oklahoma is headquartered in Miami, Oklahoma. The Miami Nation of Indiana, based in Peru, Indiana, is state-recognised but has been denied federal recognition. The Myaamia Project at Miami University in Ohio works with the tribe on language and cultural revitalisation, and the once-dormant Miami–Illinois language has been revived by tribal linguists such as Daryl Baldwin.
