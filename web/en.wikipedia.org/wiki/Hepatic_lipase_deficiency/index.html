Hepatic lipase deficiency

Hepatic lipase deficiency is a rare autosomal recessive disorder of lipoprotein metabolism caused by mutations in the LIPC gene, which encodes hepatic lipase, an enzyme produced in the liver that hydrolyses triglycerides and phospholipids in circulating lipoproteins.

Features

Deficiency of the enzyme leads to the accumulation of triglyceride-rich remnant particles and abnormal, lipid-laden HDL and LDL particles. Affected individuals typically show elevated total cholesterol and triglycerides; some develop xanthomas and there may be an increased risk of premature atherosclerosis, though the condition can also be clinically mild. It is distinct from lipoprotein lipase deficiency, in which chylomicrons accumulate severely.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis rests on lipid profiling, post-heparin plasma lipase activity assays, and genetic testing. Management focuses on cardiovascular risk reduction, diet, and treatment of dyslipidaemia; only a small number of families have been described in the literature.
