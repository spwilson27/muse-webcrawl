Citroën is a French automobile brand founded in 1919 by André Citroën, known for innovative engineering and distinctive styling. Its double-chevron logo derives from the herringbone gears Citroën manufactured before turning to cars.

Notable models

The Traction Avant (1934) pioneered front-wheel drive and unibody construction in mass production. The 2CV (1948) became an icon of basic French motoring, and the DS (1955) introduced hydropneumatic self-levelling suspension and striking aerodynamic design. The SM, CX and later C-series cars continued the brand's reputation for comfort and technical daring.

History

After bankruptcy in 1934 the company was taken over by Michelin, and in 1976 it merged with Peugeot to form PSA. Since 2021 Citroën has been part of the Stellantis group. Its premium DS line was spun off as a separate brand, DS Automobiles, in 2014.
