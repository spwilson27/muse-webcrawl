Histamine

Histamine is a biogenic amine involved in local immune responses, gut acid secretion and wakefulness. It is stored mainly in mast cells and basophils and released during allergic reactions — the target of antihistamine drugs.

Roles

Through H1 receptors histamine dilates blood vessels, increases permeability, contracts airways and causes itching — producing the wheal, flare and sneezing of Allergy and the life-threatening picture of Anaphylaxis. H2 receptors drive stomach acid (blocked by drugs such as ranitidine for reflux and ulcers).

In the brain, histamine from the tuberomammillary nucleus promotes wakefulness; first-generation antihistamines that cross into the brain cause drowsiness.

Antihistamines

Modern second-generation H1 blockers (cetirizine, loratadine) relieve hay fever and hives with little sedation. Histamine intolerance and its food triggers remain a debated diagnosis.
