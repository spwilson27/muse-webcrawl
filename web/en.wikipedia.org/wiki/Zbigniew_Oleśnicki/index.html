Zbigniew Oleśnicki

Zbigniew Oleśnicki (5 December 1389 – 1 April 1455) was a Polish cardinal, Bishop of Kraków from 1423 to 1455, and one of the most powerful statesmen of the Kingdom of Poland in the fifteenth century. For three decades he was effectively the leading political figure of the realm, advising Kings Władysław II Jagiełło and Władysław III.

Early life

Oleśnicki came to prominence at the Battle of Grunwald (1410), where, serving as a royal secretary, he is said to have saved the life of King Władysław Jagiełło by felling a Teutonic knight who had charged the king. He studied at the University of Kraków and rose through the royal chancery before receiving the bishopric of Kraków in 1423.

Political career

As bishop and later cardinal (created in 1439 by Antipope Felix V and confirmed by Pope Nicholas V in 1449), Oleśnicki dominated Polish politics. He pursued a policy of strengthening the Catholic Church and the Polish crown, opposed the Hussite movement in Bohemia, and promoted the union of Poland with Hungary under Władysław III, who died at the Battle of Varna in 1444. He was the patron of the chronicler Jan Długosz, who served as his secretary and tutor to the royal princes.

Legacy

Oleśnicki founded churches, supported the University of Kraków, and corresponded with leading European humanists including Aeneas Sylvius Piccolomini (the future Pope Pius II). Historians regard him as the archetype of the medieval Polish churchman-statesman.
