Desertification

Desertification is a type of gradual land degradation in drylands where fertile areas become increasingly arid — losing their bodies of water, vegetation and wildlife. It is caused by factors such as climate change and human activities, including deforestation and overgrazing. It is a significant global ecological and environmental problem.

Definition and extent

The United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD) defines desertification as "land degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid areas resulting from various factors, including climatic variations and human activities". Drylands cover roughly 40% of Earth's land surface and are home to more than two billion people. The UNCCD estimates that up to 40% of the world's land is already degraded.

Causes

Processes driving desertification include soil erosion by wind and water, salinisation from poor irrigation practice, loss of vegetation cover from overgrazing and fuelwood collection, and shifts in rainfall patterns linked to climate change. The popular image of marching sand dunes engulfing farmland is largely a misconception: degradation usually happens in patches, often far from natural deserts.

Countermeasures

Responses include sustainable land management, reforestation and afforestation — such as Africa's Great Green Wall initiative across the Sahel, and China's shelterbelt programmes against the expansion of the Gobi — improved irrigation efficiency, and rotational grazing.
