Indus Fan

The Indus Fan is one of the largest submarine sediment fans in the world, located in the Arabian Sea offshore Pakistan and India. It is the deep-sea depositional system of the Indus River, which transports sediment eroded from the Himalaya and Karakoram ranges to the ocean.

Extent

The fan extends roughly 1,100 kilometres from the continental margin of Pakistan into the Arabian Sea, covering an area of about 1.1 million square kilometres — second in size only to the Bengal Fan. Sediment thicknesses reach many kilometres near the shelf and thin toward the abyssal plain.

Formation

Fan growth is closely linked to the uplift of the Himalayas and the intensity of the South Asian monsoon, which controls weathering and river discharge. Turbidity currents travelling down the Indus Canyon and along channel-levee systems have built the fan since at least the Eocene. Scientific drilling by the Ocean Drilling Program (Leg 116) and IODP Expedition 355 sampled the fan to reconstruct the history of Himalayan erosion and monsoon climate.
