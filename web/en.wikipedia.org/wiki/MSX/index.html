MSX

MSX is a standardized home computer architecture, announced by ASCII Corporation and Microsoft on June 16, 1983. It was initially conceived by Microsoft as a product similar to video cassette standards like VHS, allowing multiple manufacturers to produce compatible machines. Kazuhiko Nishi, then vice president of Microsoft Japan, was a driving force behind the standard.

Overview

MSX computers were popular in Japan, South Korea, the Netherlands, Spain, Brazil, and parts of the Soviet sphere, though they achieved little market penetration in the United States and the United Kingdom. Manufacturers included Sony, Philips, Panasonic, Yamaha, Canon, Sharp, and Toshiba. The standard specified a Zilog Z80 CPU at 3.58 MHz, a Texas Instruments TMS9918-family video display processor, and the MSX-BASIC interpreter in ROM.

Generations

Four generations were produced: MSX (1983), MSX2 (1985), MSX2+ (1988), and MSX TurboR (1990). The MSX2 added improved graphics and up to 512 KB of RAM. More than five million MSX units are estimated to have been sold worldwide. The architecture is also notable as the platform for early Konami and Compile titles, and the first home releases of Hideo Kojima's Metal Gear series.
