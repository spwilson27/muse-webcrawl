Los Zetas

Los Zetas is a Mexican criminal organisation and drug cartel founded in the late 1990s by deserters from the Mexican Army's special forces (GAFE). Originally recruited as the armed enforcement wing of the Gulf Cartel, Los Zetas broke away around 2010 and became an independent cartel notorious for extreme violence, paramilitary tactics, and diversification into extortion, kidnapping, human smuggling and oil theft.

Origins

Gulf Cartel leader Osiel Cárdenas Guillén recruited some thirty ex-commandos, led by Arturo Guzmán Decena ("Z1"), whose radio call signs gave the group its name. The Zetas professionalised cartel violence, applying military training, communications and intelligence methods. After Cárdenas's arrest in 2003 and extradition to the United States, tensions with the Gulf Cartel grew until an open split in 2010.

Activities and violence

Based in Nuevo Laredo, Tamaulipas, Los Zetas controlled plazas across north-eastern and Gulf-coast Mexico and routes into Guatemala. The group has been held responsible for atrocities including the 2010 San Fernando massacre of 72 migrants, the 2011 San Fernando mass graves, the Cadereyta massacre and the Casino Royale arson in Monterrey. Under leaders Heriberto Lazcano ("El Lazca", killed 2012) and Miguel Ángel Treviño Morales ("Z40", captured 2013), violence peaked before arrests and military pressure fragmented the organisation.

Fragmentation

From the mid-2010s Los Zetas splintered into rival factions, principally the Cartel del Noreste (CDN) and Zetas Vieja Escuela, and lost territory to rivals such as the CJNG. Authorities in Mexico and the United States continue to treat its remnants as a significant organised-crime threat, and the group has been designated under the U.S. Kingpin Act.
