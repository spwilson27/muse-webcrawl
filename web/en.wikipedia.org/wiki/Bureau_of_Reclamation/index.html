The Bureau of Reclamation, formerly the United States Reclamation Service, is a federal agency under the U.S. Department of the Interior, established by the Reclamation Act of 1902. It oversees water resource management, specifically the construction and operation of diversion, delivery, and storage projects in the western United States for irrigation, water supply, and hydroelectric power.

History

The Reclamation Act, signed by President Theodore Roosevelt, dedicated receipts from the sale of federal lands in the 17 western states to irrigation works that would "reclaim" arid land for farming and settlement. Early projects included the Salt River Project in Arizona and the Truckee–Carson project in Nevada. During the Great Depression the bureau built its most famous works, including Hoover Dam (1931–36) on the Colorado River and the Grand Coulee Dam on the Columbia.

Modern mission

Reclamation is the largest wholesaler of water in the United States, delivering water to more than 31 million people and irrigating some 10 million acres, and is the second-largest producer of hydroelectric power in the country. Since the 1970s its emphasis has shifted from building new dams to operating existing facilities, water conservation, recycling, environmental restoration and resolving water conflicts, including during the long drought in the Colorado River basin.
