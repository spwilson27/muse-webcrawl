In Defense of Food: An Eater's Manifesto is a 2008 book by American journalist Michael Pollan. The book grew out of Pollan's 2007 essay "Unhappy Meals" in The New York Times Magazine and became a number-one New York Times bestseller. It argues that the modern Western diet of processed foods is responsible for widespread chronic disease, and that the reductionist science of nutrition — which Pollan calls "nutritionism" — has failed eaters.

Summary

The book opens with Pollan's famous seven-word summary of his advice: "Eat food. Not too much. Mostly plants." Each phrase is unpacked in turn. "Eat food" means choosing real, recognisable foods over what Pollan calls "edible foodlike substances" — highly processed products with long lists of ingredients and health claims. He criticises the food industry and nutrition science for reducing food to nutrients, arguing that this ideology has paradoxically made populations less healthy even as consumption of officially condemned nutrients fell.

Pollan surveys alternatives: traditional food cultures, such as the French and Italian ways of eating, which combine pleasure and moderation; the wisdom of grandmothers' rules of thumb; and practices such as eating slowly, eating together, growing some of one's own food, and avoiding food marketed with health claims.

Reception and adaptation

The book was widely praised for its accessible critique of industrial food and won the James Beard Foundation Award for writing and literature. Some nutrition scientists criticised Pollan for oversimplifying nutritional research and romanticising traditional diets. In 2015 the book was adapted into a PBS television documentary of the same name, hosted by Pollan. His follow-up, Food Rules: An Eater's Manual (2009), distilled the advice into short rules.
