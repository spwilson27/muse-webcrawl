Medical coding

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Medical coding is the process of transforming healthcare diagnoses, procedures, medical services, and equipment into universal alphanumeric codes. The diagnoses and procedure codes are taken from medical record documentation, such as transcription of physician's notes, laboratory results, and radiologic results, by trained medical coders.

Code sets

Several standardized systems are used internationally. The ICD (International Classification of Diseases), maintained by the World Health Organization, codes diagnoses; the United States adopted ICD-10-CM for diagnoses in 2015. CPT (Current Procedural Terminology), maintained by the American Medical Association, codes procedures and services in outpatient settings, while ICD-10-PCS is used for inpatient procedures in the US. HCPCS codes cover products, supplies, and services not in CPT. Other systems include SNOMED CT for clinical terminology and LOINC for laboratory observations.

Purpose

Accurate coding supports billing and reimbursement, since insurers determine payment from submitted codes; it also underpins epidemiology, health statistics, quality measurement, and research. Errors can cause claim denials, compliance problems, or distorted statistics, so coding is subject to audit and is governed by official coding guidelines.

The profession

Medical coders work in hospitals, clinics, insurers, and remote settings. Professional certification is offered by bodies such as AAPC (e.g., the CPC credential) and AHIMA (e.g., CCS). Computer-assisted coding and natural-language-processing tools increasingly support, but do not replace, human coders.
