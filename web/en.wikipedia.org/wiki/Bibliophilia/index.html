Bibliophilia

A bibliophile's library of antiquarian books

Bibliophilia is the love of books. A bibliophile is an individual who loves and frequently reads or collects books, often forming a large and specialized collection. Bibliophiles may value fine or unusual editions, first editions, signed or association copies, beautiful bindings, typography, paper, and the physical qualities of books, in addition to their contents. The term should be distinguished from bibliomania, an obsessive-compulsive disorder involving excessive collecting, and from simple book ownership.

History

Book collecting is ancient, but bibliophilia as a self-conscious pursuit flourished after the printing press made books more available. The 14th-century English bishop Richard de Bury wrote the Philobiblon (1345), one of the earliest treatises celebrating the love of books. During the 19th century, book-collecting clubs such as the Roxburghe Club (founded 1812) and later the Grolier Club of New York (1884) institutionalised the pursuit, and antiquarian bookselling became a distinct trade with its own catalogues, auctions and bibliographies.

The bibliophile's collection

Bibliophiles typically focus their collections by author, subject, period, illustrator, binder or printer. Points of interest include edition and issue, provenance, condition, and rarity. Famous bibliophiles include Samuel Pepys, whose library survives intact at Magdalene College, Cambridge, and the collectors whose sales — such as the Britwell and Hoe sales — set records in the antiquarian market. Modern bibliophilia coexists with digital reading; for many collectors the physical object remains irreplaceable.
