Speaking in tongues

Speaking in tongues, also known as glossolalia, is a religious practice in which people utter speech-like syllables that lack readily comprehensible meaning. It is most prominently associated with Pentecostal and charismatic Christianity, where practitioners believe it to be a gift of the Holy Spirit (in Greek, glōssais lalein).

A Pentecostal congregation at worship; glossolalia is a hallmark of the movement.

Biblical accounts

The New Testament describes speaking in tongues at Pentecost (Acts 2), where the apostles are depicted as speaking in languages understood by foreign listeners — sometimes distinguished as xenoglossy — and in 1 Corinthians 12–14, where Paul discusses tongues as one of the spiritual gifts, insisting that public utterances be interpreted and regulating their use in assembly. Mark 16:17 also lists speaking in new tongues among signs of believers.

Modern Pentecostalism

The modern practice traces to the Holiness movement and events such as the 1901 Topeka outpouring under Charles Parham and the Azusa Street Revival (1906) in Los Angeles led by William J. Seymour. Classical Pentecostal denominations such as the Assemblies of God traditionally teach tongues as the initial evidence of baptism in the Holy Spirit; the charismatic movement later carried the practice into mainline Protestant and Catholic churches.

Cessationism and continuationism

Cessationists hold that tongues and other miraculous gifts ceased with the apostolic age; continuationists hold they remain available. This theological divide shapes denominational practice worldwide.

Linguistic and psychological study

Linguists such as William Samarin concluded that glossolalia is not a natural language: its syllable structure mirrors the speaker's native phonology and lacks consistent semantics or syntax. Neuroimaging studies have reported decreased frontal-lobe activity during glossolalia, consistent with practitioners' reports of diminished voluntary control. Anthropologists note similar phenomena in shamanic and other religious traditions worldwide.
