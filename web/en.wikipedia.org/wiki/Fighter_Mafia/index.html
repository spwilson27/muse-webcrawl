Fighter Mafia

The Fighter Mafia was a group of United States Air Force officers and civilian defence analysts, active in the 1960s and 1970s, who advocated for lightweight, manoeuvrable fighter aircraft and opposed the Air Force's trend toward large, heavy, technology-laden jets. The group's core included fighter pilot John Boyd, mathematician Thomas Christie, test pilot Everest Riccioni, and analyst Pierre Sprey.

Energy–manoeuvrability theory

Boyd and Christie developed energy–manoeuvrability (E-M) theory, a mathematical framework comparing aircraft performance across flight envelopes. Using E-M charts, they argued that smaller, lighter fighters with high thrust-to-weight ratios would outperform larger designs in air combat, and that excess weight and complexity degraded combat effectiveness.

Influence

The Fighter Mafia's advocacy contributed to the Lightweight Fighter programme, which produced the General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon, and influenced the design of the Fairchild Republic A-10 Thunderbolt II ground-attack aircraft. The group also played a role in the broader defence reform movement of the late 1970s and 1980s, which pressed for simpler, more reliable and less costly weapons systems.
