Parade

A parade is a public procession, usually organized along a street, in which people march or ride in celebration, commemoration, protest, or display. Parades commonly feature marching bands, floats, dancers, giant balloons, military units, and community groups, and draw crowds of spectators along the route.

Types of parades

  • Holiday parades: Events such as the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York (begun 1924), Saint Patrick's Day parades, and Christmas parades mark seasonal celebrations.
  • Carnival parades: During Mardi Gras and Carnival season, krewes and samba schools parade with elaborate floats and costumes.
  • Military parades: States display armed forces on national days — for example France's Bastille Day parade on the Champs-Élysées, among Europe's oldest and largest.
  • Victory and ticker-tape parades: Held to honour returning soldiers, astronauts, and championship sports teams.
  • Pride parades: Annual celebrations of LGBTQ+ identity originating in the commemorations of the 1969 Stonewall uprising.
  • Protest marches: Processions organized to demonstrate publicly for a cause share much of the parade's form.

History

Processional displays are ancient: Roman triumphs honoured victorious generals, and religious processions have long marked festivals in many cultures. The modern civic parade developed in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries alongside mass urban celebrations, national holidays, and commercial sponsorship. Parades also figure in military drill, where "parade" denotes a formal assembly of troops for inspection or ceremony.

Elements

Typical elements include a grand marshal, colour guards, marching bands and drum corps, decorated floats, vintage vehicles, equestrian units, and street performers. Routes are usually closed to traffic, and organization requires municipal permits, policing, and crowd management.
