Prospecting

Prospecting is the first stage of the geological analysis (followed by exploration) of a territory. It is the search for minerals, fossils, precious metals, or mineral specimens, and is also known as fossicking in some regions. Traditionally prospecting relied on direct observation of mineralization in outcrops, sediments, and waterways by individual prospectors; modern prospecting also uses geological, geophysical, and geochemical tools.

Methods

Traditional methods include panning for gold and other dense minerals in stream gravels, examining rock outcrops and "float" (loose rock fragments) for signs of ore, and following mineralized material uphill or upstream to its source. Prospectors look for indicator minerals and alteration patterns associated with ore deposits.

Modern prospecting incorporates geological mapping, soil and stream-sediment geochemistry, airborne and ground geophysics (magnetics, gravity, electromagnetics, radiometrics), satellite imagery, and statistical targeting. Successful prospecting leads to the staking of claims and to detailed exploration, including trenching and drilling, to define a deposit.

History

Individual prospectors were central to the great gold rushes of the 19th century — in California, Australia, the Klondike, and South Africa — and to the discovery of many major mining districts. Today most prospecting is carried out by exploration companies and geological surveys, though recreational prospecting and fossicking remain popular hobbies.
