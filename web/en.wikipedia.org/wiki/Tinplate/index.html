Tinplate

Tinplate consists of sheets of steel coated with a thin layer of tin. Before the advent of cheap mild steel, the backing metal was wrought iron. Tinplate is used principally for making tin cans, as well as caps, closures and toys.

Manufacture

Modern tinplate is made by cold-rolling steel to thickness, then coating it electrolytically with tin, followed by passivation and oiling. The tin layer protects the steel from corrosion, is non-toxic, and gives a surface suitable for lacquering and printing.

History

Tinning of iron sheets was practised in Bohemia and Saxony in the 14th century, and the industry spread to South Wales in the 18th–19th centuries, where Pontypool and later Swansea and Llanelli became world centres. The invention of canning by Nicolas Appert and Peter Durand (1810) created mass demand. The United States built a large industry after the McKinley Tariff of 1890.
