Ox (zodiac)

The Ox (牛, niú) is the second of the twelve animals in the Chinese zodiac cycle, appearing in the Chinese calendar and in related East and Southeast Asian traditions. Years of the Ox recur every twelve years; people born in such years are said to share the animal's traits of diligence, dependability, patience and quiet strength.

The zodiac race

In the popular legend of the Great Race, by which the Jade Emperor fixed the order of the zodiac animals, the Ox was the strongest swimmer and set off first across the river, but the Rat had hitched a ride in the Ox's ear and leapt ashore at the far bank to claim first place, leaving the Ox second. The story is used to explain both the Ox's position and the reputed cunning of the Rat.

Years and the five elements

Each Ox year is also associated with one of the five elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) in a repeating sixty-year cycle. The most recent cycles include the Wood Ox (1985), Fire Ox (1997), Earth Ox (2009) and Metal Ox (2021); the next Ox year, 2033, will be a Water Ox year. The Ox year begins at Chinese New Year (or, in some astrological traditions, at Li Chun, the start of spring), not on 1 January.

Compatibility and personality

Traditional Chinese astrology holds that those born in the Year of the Ox are honest, industrious and cautious, with strong determination and a dislike of unnecessary risk. The Ox is said to be most compatible with the Rat, Snake and Rooster, and least compatible with the Goat, Horse and Dog.

In other cultures

In the Vietnamese zodiac, the Ox is replaced by the water buffalo, an animal similarly central to wet-rice agriculture. The Japanese zodiac retains the ox (ushi), and New Year imagery of the ox is common across East Asia.
