Zener cards

Zener cards are cards used to conduct experiments for extrasensory perception (ESP) or clairvoyance. They were designed in the early 1930s by perceptual psychologist Karl Zener for use in experiments carried out by his colleague, parapsychologist J. B. Rhine, at Duke University.

Design and use

A deck contains 25 cards, five each bearing one of five symbols: a hollow circle, a plus sign (cross), three vertical wavy lines, a hollow square, and a hollow five-pointed star. In a typical test, the experimenter draws a card at random while the subject attempts to name the symbol without seeing it. By chance alone, the subject should average five correct guesses per run of 25; consistent scoring above that level was interpreted by Rhine as evidence of ESP.

Criticism

Rhine's early positive results were not reliably replicated under tighter controls. Critics noted flaws including sensory leakage (early cards were printed on stock through which the symbols could sometimes be seen) and inadequate shuffling. Zener card experiments remain an iconic, if scientifically contested, method in the history of parapsychology, and the cards have become a staple of popular culture, notably in the opening of the film Ghostbusters.
