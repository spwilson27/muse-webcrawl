Carlo Bugatti (16 February 1856 – 31 March 1940) was an Italian decorative artist and furniture designer, famed for his fantastical, orientalist furniture, and the patriarch of the Bugatti family: his son Ettore founded the Bugatti automobile company, and his son Rembrandt became a celebrated sculptor of animals.

Work

Trained at the Brera Academy in Milan and in Paris, Bugatti established a workshop in Milan producing furniture unlike anything else of its era: asymmetric forms clad in parchment (vellum), inlaid with copper, pewter and ivory, painted with insects, serpents and Japanese-inspired motifs, and hung with silk tassels. His "Snail Room" caused a sensation at the 1902 Turin International Exhibition of Modern Decorative Art, a landmark of Art Nouveau. He later designed silverware, musical instruments and jewellery, and in 1904 moved to Paris.
