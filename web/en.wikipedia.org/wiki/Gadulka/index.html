Gadulka

ClassificationBowed string instrument
OriginBulgaria
RelatedGusle, rebec, kemenche

The gadulka (Bulgarian: гъдулка) is a traditional Bulgarian bowed string instrument, central to the folk music of Bulgaria. It has a pear-shaped body carved from a single piece of wood, a short neck without frets, and is held vertically when played.

Construction and playing

A typical gadulka has three (occasionally four) main strings that are stopped with the fingernails of the left hand — the strings are touched lightly rather than pressed to a fingerboard — plus a set of sympathetic resonating strings underneath that give the instrument its characteristic shimmering sound.

Regional styles

The gadulka is found in distinct regional variants across Bulgaria, including the Dobrudzha, Thracian, and north Bulgarian styles, and it is a lead melody instrument in folk dance ensembles, accompanying the asymmetric rhythms of Bulgarian dances.
