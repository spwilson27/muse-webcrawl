Bruno of Cologne

Saint Bruno, founder of the Carthusian Order.

Bruno of Cologne (c. 1030 – 6 October 1101), venerated as Saint Bruno, was a German monk and the founder of the Carthusian Order. Born in Cologne to a prominent family, he studied at Reims, where he later became head of the cathedral school and chancellor of the archdiocese, teaching many notable pupils including the future Pope Urban II.

Founding of the Grande Chartreuse

Disillusioned by ecclesiastical corruption — he had accused his archbishop of simony — Bruno renounced his career and, with six companions, sought a life of solitude. In 1084 Bishop Hugh of Grenoble gave them a remote site in the Chartreuse Mountains near Grenoble, where they built the hermitage that became the Grande Chartreuse, motherhouse of the Carthusian Order, combining eremitic solitude with a communal framework.

In 1090 Urban II summoned Bruno to Rome as an adviser; he later obtained permission to found a second hermitage, La Torre (later the Certosa di Serra San Bruno) in Calabria, where he died in 1101. Bruno was never formally canonized by process, but his cult was approved and extended to the universal Church in the 16th and 17th centuries; his feast day is 6 October.
