The Presidential Medal of Freedom

CountryUnited States
Presented byPresident of the United States

The Presidential Medal of Freedom is the highest civilian award of the United States, alongside the Congressional Gold Medal. It is awarded by the president of the United States to recognize "an especially meritorious contribution to the security or national interests of the United States, world peace, cultural or other significant public or private endeavors."

History

The award in its modern form was established by President John F. Kennedy in 1963 through Executive Order 11085, replacing the Medal of Freedom created by President Harry S. Truman in 1945 to honor civilian service during World War II. Kennedy revived and redesigned the medal and renamed it the Presidential Medal of Freedom; the first recipients were announced shortly before his assassination, and the awards were presented by President Lyndon B. Johnson in December 1963.

Description

The medal consists of a white enamel star superimposed on a red enamel pentagonal background, surrounded by five golden eagles, suspended from a blue ribbon with white edge stripes. It is worn around the neck. A special grade, the Presidential Medal of Freedom with Distinction, features a larger star worn with a sash and has been awarded only rarely, to a small number of recipients including presidents and other heads of state.

Recipients

Recipients have included civil rights leaders, scientists, artists, athletes, humanitarian workers, military leaders, and former presidents. Notable recipients include Martin Luther King Jr. (posthumously), Mother Teresa, Pope John Paul II, Nelson Mandela, Stephen Hawking, and Maya Angelou. The medal may be awarded posthumously and to non-citizens. In total, the medal has been awarded to more than 600 individuals since 1963.
