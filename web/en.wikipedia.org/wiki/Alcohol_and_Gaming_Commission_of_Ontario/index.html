The Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario (AGCO) is a Crown agency of the Government of Ontario that regulates the alcohol, gaming, horse racing and cannabis sectors in the Canadian province of Ontario. It reports to the Ministry of the Attorney General.

History

The AGCO was established on 23 February 1998 under the Alcohol and Gaming Regulation and Public Protection Act, 1996, through the merger of the Liquor Licence Board of Ontario and the Gaming Control Commission. Its mandate expanded over time: in 2016 it assumed responsibility for regulating horse racing in Ontario, in 2018 it became the regulator for the private retail sale of recreational cannabis, and in 2022 it began overseeing Ontario's open, regulated internet gaming (iGaming) market administered through iGaming Ontario.

Responsibilities

The Commission is responsible for:

  • Issuing liquor licences and permits, and regulating the sale, service and consumption of alcohol;
  • Regulating casinos, slot machine facilities, charitable gaming, lotteries and internet gaming in the province;
  • Licensing and regulating privately operated cannabis retail stores;
  • Regulating horse racing, including licensing participants and oversight of racing integrity;
  • Enforcement and compliance inspections across all regulated sectors, using an approach centred on compliance rather than purely on enforcement.

The AGCO is governed by a board of directors appointed by the Lieutenant Governor in Council, and its Registrar serves as its chief executive for day-to-day regulatory decisions.
