The FIM-43 Redeye was a man-portable surface-to-air missile system used by the United States military. It was the first generation of American MANPADS, using infrared homing to track aircraft by their engine exhaust, and could be fired from a soldier's shoulder.

History

Development began in the 1950s at Convair/Pomona as the "Redeye" concept for a lightweight infantry air-defense weapon. After a prolonged development, the system entered service in 1967–1968. It was widely exported to US allies and was used by the Afghan mujahideen during the Soviet–Afghan War before being supplied in larger numbers with its successor.

Description

The weapon consists of a reusable launcher tube with a grip stock, an optical sight, and electronics, plus a disposable launch tube containing the missile. The gunner tracks the target through the sight until the infrared seeker locks onto the heat signature, indicated by an audible tone, then fires. The early seeker used uncooled lead sulfide detectors and could only engage aircraft from behind, after they had passed, and was easily decoyed by flares and confused by ground heat and the sun.

Successor

Its limitations led directly to the development of the FIM-92 Stinger, which replaced the Redeye in US service during the 1980s, with Redeye units phased out by the mid-1990s.

Operators

Operators included the United States, West Germany, Denmark, Greece, Turkey, Australia, and numerous other allied and partner forces, as well as non-state groups supplied during the Cold War.
