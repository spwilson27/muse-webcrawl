Connor Bedard

Connor Bedard (born 17 July 2005) is a Canadian professional ice hockey centre for the Chicago Blackhawks of the National Hockey League (NHL). Regarded as a generational talent from his early teens, he was the first player granted exceptional player status by the Western Hockey League (WHL), was selected first overall by the Regina Pats in the 2020 WHL bantam draft and first overall by Chicago in the 2023 NHL entry draft, and won the Calder Memorial Trophy as the NHL's rookie of the year in 2024.

Early life and junior career

Bedard was born in North Vancouver, British Columbia, and emerged as a prodigy in minor hockey, gaining national media attention while still in elementary school. Granted exceptional status in 2020 — the first such designation in WHL history — he joined the Regina Pats as a 15-year-old. In the 2022–23 season he produced one of the most remarkable junior campaigns ever recorded, scoring 71 goals and 143 points in 57 games, leading the WHL and the entire Canadian Hockey League in scoring and winning the CHL Player of the Year award. His shot release in particular was widely compared to the best shooters in the NHL.

International play

Bedard starred for Canada at the World Junior Championships, breaking several national records. At the 2023 tournament in Halifax he set Canadian records for career points and goals at the World Juniors, surpassing marks associated with Eric Lindros and Jordan Eberle, scored a celebrated overtime winner against Slovakia in the quarter-finals, and was named tournament MVP as Canada won gold. He also won gold at the 2021 IIHF World U18 Championships.

NHL career

Bedard signed with Chicago as the face of the franchise's rebuild and scored his first NHL goal in October 2023. Despite missing time with a fractured jaw suffered in January 2024, he led the Blackhawks and all NHL rookies in scoring in 2023–24 and won the Calder Trophy. He continued as Chicago's leading scorer in his second season and is regarded as one of the league's central young stars.
