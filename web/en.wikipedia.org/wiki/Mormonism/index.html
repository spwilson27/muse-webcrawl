Mormonism

Mormonism is the theology and religious tradition of the Latter Day Saint movement, founded by Joseph Smith in Western New York in the 1820s and 1830s. As a religious tradition, it is considered a Restorationist form of Christianity. The name derives from the Book of Mormon, a text Smith said he translated from golden plates revealed by an angel named Moroni.

Beliefs

Mormons believe in Jesus Christ as Saviour and in a restoration of the original Christian church through Smith as prophet. Scripture includes the Bible, the Book of Mormon, the Doctrine and Covenants, and the Pearl of Great Price. Distinctive doctrines include continuing revelation through a living prophet, temple ordinances, baptism for the dead, eternal marriage, a pre-mortal existence, and a non-Trinitarian conception of the Godhead as three distinct beings. Historically the movement practised plural marriage, officially discontinued by the largest church in 1890.

Denominations

The largest denomination is the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS Church), headquartered in Salt Lake City, Utah, with over 17 million members claimed worldwide. Other branches include the Community of Christ (formerly the Reorganized Church), and various fundamentalist groups that separated over the abandonment of polygamy.

Culture

Mormon culture emphasises family, missionary service (typically undertaken by young adults), abstinence from alcohol, tobacco, coffee and tea (the Word of Wisdom), tithing, and welfare and humanitarian programmes. The term "Mormon" remains in common use, though the LDS Church has since 2018 asked that its full name be preferred.
