Siege of Boston

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Boston (19 April 1775 – 17 March 1776) was the opening phase of the American Revolutionary War, in which New England militiamen, and later the newly formed Continental Army under George Washington, surrounded the city of Boston and confined the British forces garrisoned there under General Thomas Gage and later William Howe.

Background

After the Battles of Lexington and Concord on 19 April 1775, provincial militias converged on Boston and invested the city, which was connected to the mainland only by the narrow Boston Neck. Thousands of Loyalists fled into the city while many Patriots left it.

Course of the siege

On 17 June 1775 the British captured the Charlestown peninsula in the Battle of Bunker Hill, but at a cost of over a thousand casualties, and failed to break the siege. Washington took command of the Continental Army in July 1775, facing chronic shortages of gunpowder and artillery. The deadlock was broken when Colonel Henry Knox transported captured cannon from Fort Ticonderoga across snowy New England in the "noble train of artillery." On the night of 4–5 March 1776 Washington fortified Dorchester Heights overlooking the city and harbour, making the British position untenable.

Evacuation

On 17 March 1776 — thereafter celebrated in Boston as Evacuation Day — Howe evacuated the city by sea, carrying more than 11,000 soldiers and Loyalists to Halifax, Nova Scotia. Boston remained free of British occupation for the rest of the war.
