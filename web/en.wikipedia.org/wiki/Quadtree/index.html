Quadtree

A quadtree is a tree data structure in which each internal node has exactly four children. Quadtrees are the two-dimensional analog of octrees and are most often used to partition a two-dimensional space by recursively subdividing it into four quadrants or regions. The data associated with a leaf cell varies by application, but the cell represents a unit of interesting spatial information.

Types

Common variants include the region quadtree, which represents a partition of space into regions (used for image representation and compression), the point quadtree, which stores points and is an adaptation of a binary search tree to two dimensions, and the point-region (PR) quadtree, which subdivides cells containing more than one point. Edge and polygon map quadtrees store lines and boundaries.

Uses

Quadtrees are used in image processing, spatial indexing, collision detection in two dimensions, terrain rendering, geographic information systems, mesh generation for finite-element analysis, and in efficient sparse-matrix representations. Their three-dimensional counterpart, the octree, is widely used in 3D graphics and simulation.
