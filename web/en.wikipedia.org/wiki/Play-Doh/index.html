Play-Doh

Play-Doh modelling compound in assorted colours

Play-Doh is a modelling compound for children, manufactured by Hasbro, used for arts and crafts projects at home and in school. It is composed mainly of water, salt and flour, and is marketed as non-toxic.

History

The product began life not as a toy but as a wallpaper cleaner. Kutol Products, a Cincinnati soap company, developed a soft, pliable putty in the 1930s that could remove coal soot from wallpaper. After the Second World War, as homes switched from coal heating to oil and gas and washable vinyl wallpaper spread, demand collapsed. Joseph McVicker, whose brother-in-law ran Kutol, learned that schoolchildren had been using the putty for art projects, and the compound was relaunched as a toy in 1956 under the name Play-Doh, initially in off-white and then in red, blue and yellow. The brand passed through several owners before Hasbro acquired it in 1991.

Composition and use

The exact recipe is proprietary, but patents describe a water-based binder of starch and other ingredients that keeps the compound soft in its sealed tub while allowing it to harden when left exposed to air. Play-Doh is sold in dozens of colours and with moulds, presses and themed playsets. More than three billion tubs are estimated to have been sold since 1956, and the compound's distinctive smell has itself been trademarked.
