Uruguayan Clásico

Teams: Nacional and Peñarol

Location: Montevideo, Uruguay

First meeting: 15 July 1900

The Uruguayan Clásico (Spanish: Clásico uruguayo) is the football rivalry between Nacional and Peñarol, the two dominant clubs of Montevideo, Uruguay. Contested since 1900, it is one of the oldest football derbies in the world outside Britain and the centrepiece of Uruguayan sporting culture.

History

Peñarol traces its origins to the Central Uruguay Railway Cricket Club, founded by British railway workers in 1891, while Nacional was founded in 1899 by Uruguayan students as a club for native-born players. The first match between the clubs was played on 15 July 1900, with CURCC winning 2–0.

Dominance

Together the two clubs have won the overwhelming majority of Uruguayan Primera División championships and numerous international titles, including eight Copa Libertadores between them. Most Clásicos are played at the Estadio Centenario, the national stadium that hosted the 1930 World Cup final.

Culture

The rivalry divides families and workplaces across Uruguay, and the matches are famed for enormous choreographed displays (tifos) by the two supporter groups, including some of the largest flags ever unfurled in a stadium.
