Chemical symbol

A chemical symbol is a one- or two-letter abbreviation used to represent a chemical element. Chemical symbols are used worldwide in chemical formulas and equations. Most derive from the element's English name, while some come from Latin or other languages — for example, Fe for iron (from Latin ferrum), Na for sodium (natrium), K for potassium (kalium), and W for tungsten (wolfram).

Notation

By convention, the first letter of a symbol is capitalised and the second, if any, is lowercase: H (hydrogen), He (helium), Li (lithium). In chemical formulas, subscripts indicate the number of atoms (as in H₂O), and symbols are used in the periodic table to organise the elements. Temporary systematic symbols of three letters are assigned to elements that have not yet been formally named.

The modern system of symbols was introduced by the Swedish chemist Jöns Jacob Berzelius in the early 19th century, replacing earlier alchemical symbols and the circle-based notation of John Dalton.
