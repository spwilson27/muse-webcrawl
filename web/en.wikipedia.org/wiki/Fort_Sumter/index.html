Fort Sumter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Sumter is a sea fort built on an artificial island protecting Charleston, South Carolina, from naval invasion. Construction began in 1829 as part of the United States' "Third System" of coastal fortifications and was still incomplete in 1861, when it became the scene of the Battle of Fort Sumter — the bombardment that began the American Civil War.

Battle of Fort Sumter

After South Carolina seceded in December 1860, Major Robert Anderson moved his small Union garrison to Fort Sumter. On 12 April 1861, Confederate batteries under P. G. T. Beauregard opened fire on the fort. After some 34 hours of bombardment, Anderson surrendered on 13 April; remarkably, no one was killed during the battle itself. The attack galvanised the North and prompted President Lincoln's call for volunteers.

Later history

Confederate forces held the fort for most of the war, withstanding repeated Union bombardments that reduced it to rubble before Charleston was evacuated in 1865. Today the fort is administered by the National Park Service as part of Fort Sumter and Fort Moultrie National Historical Park, accessible by ferry from Charleston.
