Sovereign democracy (Russian: суверенная демократия) is a political concept formulated in the mid-2000s by Vladislav Surkov, deputy chief of the Russian presidential administration, and adopted as a guiding ideology of Vladimir Putin's Russia and the United Russia party. It asserts that Russia is a democracy whose specific institutional forms must be determined by Russia itself, according to its own traditions and circumstances, and that foreign criticism or pressure regarding those forms is an infringement of sovereignty.

Content and usage

Surkov introduced the term in speeches and articles in 2005–2006, arguing against "managed democracy" imposed from outside and for a strong state, national competitiveness and a political system insulated from foreign interference. The concept was written into United Russia's programme in 2006. It accompanied measures such as the tightening of NGO laws, the reorganisation of elections, and pushback against Western promotion of "colour revolutions" in the post-Soviet space.

Reception

Supporters presented it as a normal claim that democratic models vary by country. Critics — including then First Deputy Prime Minister Dmitry Medvedev, who preferred the plain word "democracy" — argued the qualifier implied a diluted, authoritarian variant and served to deflect scrutiny of elections, media freedom and the courts. After 2008 the term faded from official rhetoric, though its underlying premises about sovereignty and non-interference remained central to Kremlin discourse.
