Epimerase

Epimerases are enzymes that catalyse the stereochemical inversion of the configuration of a single stereogenic centre in a biological molecule that has more than one such centre. They are a subcategory of the isomerases (EC 5.1), alongside the racemases, which invert the configuration of molecules with a single stereocentre. The two substrates interconverted by an epimerase are epimers of one another — stereoisomers that differ at only one of several stereogenic centres.

Examples

  • UDP-glucose 4-epimerase (galactose epimerase, GALE) interconverts UDP-galactose and UDP-glucose in the Leloir pathway of galactose metabolism. Deficiency of this enzyme causes a form of galactosaemia.
  • UDP-glucuronate 4-epimerase and UDP-xylose 4-epimerase generate the nucleotide sugars used in glycosaminoglycan and cell-wall polysaccharide synthesis.
  • Methylmalonyl-CoA epimerase acts in the catabolism of odd-chain fatty acids and certain amino acids; its deficiency causes methylmalonic aciduria.
  • Alpha-methylacyl-CoA racemase/epimerase functions in bile acid synthesis and branched-chain fatty acid oxidation.

Mechanism

Epimerases commonly work by transiently removing and replacing a substituent at the target carbon — for example by oxidation to a keto intermediate and stereochemically opposite reduction (often using a tightly bound NAD+ cofactor that is regenerated in the reaction), or by deprotonation to a planar intermediate followed by reprotonation from the other face.
