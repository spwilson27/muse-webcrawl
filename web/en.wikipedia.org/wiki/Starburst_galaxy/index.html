Starburst galaxy

A starburst galaxy is a galaxy undergoing an exceptionally high rate of star formation, so rapid that it would consume all of the galaxy's available gas in far less than the galaxy's age if sustained. The starburst is usually triggered by a gravitational interaction or merger with another galaxy.

Characteristics

Starbursts are luminous in the infrared because dust absorbs ultraviolet light from young massive stars and re-radiates it. They drive powerful galactic winds of outflowing gas powered by supernovae. Well-known examples include M82 (the Cigar Galaxy), the Antennae Galaxies, and Arp 220.

Significance

Starburst episodes are thought to be a key phase in galaxy evolution, building stellar mass quickly and enriching the intergalactic medium with heavy elements. Nearby starbursts serve as local analogues of the vigorous star formation common in the early universe.
