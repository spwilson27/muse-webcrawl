Ferdinand I of Romania

Reign: 10 October 1914 – 20 July 1927

Born: 24 August 1865, Sigmaringen

Spouse: Marie of Edinburgh

Ferdinand I (Ferdinand Viktor Albert Meinrad; 24 August 1865 – 20 July 1927), nicknamed the Unifier, was King of Romania from 1914 until his death in 1927. Born a German prince of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen, he succeeded his uncle Carol I on the Romanian throne.

First World War

Although a member of the German Hohenzollern dynasty, Ferdinand honoured Romania's commitments and brought the country into the First World War on the side of the Allies in 1916. After defeats forced the government to retreat to Iași, Romania's position recovered, and the war ended with the creation of Greater Romania through the union of Transylvania, Bessarabia and Bukovina with the kingdom.

Later reign

Ferdinand and Queen Marie were crowned in Alba Iulia in 1922 as sovereigns of the enlarged state. His reign saw land reform and the adoption of the 1923 constitution. He died in 1927 and was succeeded by his grandson Michael I, under a regency, his son Carol having renounced his rights.
