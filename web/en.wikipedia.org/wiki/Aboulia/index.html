Aboulia or abulia (from the Greek a-, "without", and boulē, "will") is a disorder of diminished motivation characterised by an inability to make decisions or set goals. It is considered a middle ground between apathy (a milder failure of motivation) and akinetic mutism, a more severe state in which a patient fails to move or speak.

Symptoms and signs

Aboulia has been associated with dysfunction of the frontal lobes, basal ganglia and frontal cortical circuits, including the anterior cingulate cortex. Symptoms include a loss of spontaneity and initiative, slowed thinking, poverty of speech, emotional blunting and increased response latency. Patients may sit for long periods doing nothing, answer questions after long delays with short monosyllabic replies, and fail to initiate activities even though they retain the physical and intellectual capacity to perform them.

Causes

Aboulia may follow stroke, traumatic brain injury, or neurodegenerative disease affecting the frontal–subcortical circuits. Lesions of the medial frontal lobe, the caudate nucleus, or the globus pallidus are commonly reported. It is also seen in conditions such as Parkinson's disease, schizophrenia, and normal pressure hydrocephalus.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause. Dopaminergic agents such as bromocriptine, levodopa and stimulants have been reported to help in some cases, consistent with the role of dopamine pathways in motivation. Behavioural strategies, structured routines and environmental cueing are also used in rehabilitation.
