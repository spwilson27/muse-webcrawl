Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act

The Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act is a United States federal law enacted on October 28, 2009, as a rider to the National Defense Authorization Act for 2010. It expanded the 1969 federal hate-crime law to include crimes motivated by a victim's actual or perceived gender, sexual orientation, gender identity, or disability, and removed the requirement that the victim be engaged in a federally protected activity.

Background

The act is named for Matthew Shepard, a gay university student murdered in Wyoming in 1998, and James Byrd Jr., an African American man murdered by white supremacists in Texas in 1998. Versions of the bill had been introduced in Congress repeatedly since 1999.

Provisions

The law permits federal prosecution of hate crimes when local authorities cannot or will not act, provides funding and training for state and local enforcement, and authorizes the FBI to assist in investigations. It also amended record-keeping so that hate-crime statistics include crimes based on gender and gender identity. President Barack Obama signed the act into law on October 28, 2009.
