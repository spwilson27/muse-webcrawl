Queer Nation

Queer Nation is an LGBTQ activist organization founded in March 1990 in New York City by activists from ACT UP. The organization employed direct action, confrontational protest, and "zap" actions to fight homophobia, anti-gay violence, and discrimination, and it popularized the reclaiming of the word "queer" as a term of pride.

Founding

Queer Nation grew out of frustration among ACT UP members that the broader gay movement's focus on AIDS, while essential, did not address rising anti-gay violence and everyday homophobia. Founded by activists including members of ACT UP New York, the group held its first meetings in the spring of 1990. Its founding manifesto, "Queers Read This", was distributed anonymously at the New York Pride march in June 1990 and became a foundational text of the queer movement, arguing for pride in difference and anger at oppression.

Tactics and actions

The group became famous for its chant "We're here! We're queer! Get used to it!", and for actions such as kiss-ins in public places, "Queer Nights Out" visits to suburban shopping malls and straight bars, and demonstrations against media stereotyping and institutions such as the Catholic Church. Chapters quickly formed in cities across the United States and internationally. In San Francisco, members disrupted the 1991 Academy Awards broadcast protests over Hollywood's portrayal of LGBT people and protested the film Basic Instinct (1992) during production.

Queer Nation also confronted anti-gay violence through street patrols in some cities and campaigned against discriminatory ballot measures, such as Colorado's Amendment 2.

Legacy

Although most chapters faded by the mid-1990s, Queer Nation's impact was lasting: it helped shift the movement's language and politics, accelerated the adoption of "queer" as an umbrella term and academic field (queer theory), and influenced later activist groups and mainstream LGBT organizations toward more assertive visibility.
