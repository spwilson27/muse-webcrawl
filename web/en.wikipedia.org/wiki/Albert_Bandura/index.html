Albert Bandura (4 December 1925 – 26 July 2021) was a Canadian-American psychologist known as the originator of social learning theory and social cognitive theory, and for developing the concept of self-efficacy. A 2002 survey ranked Bandura as the fourth most frequently cited psychologist of all time, behind B. F. Skinner, Sigmund Freud and Jean Piaget, and the most cited living one.

Early life and education

Bandura was born in Mundare, a small town in Alberta, Canada, the youngest of six children of Polish and Ukrainian immigrant parents. He attended the University of British Columbia, where a chance assignment to an early-morning class led him to take a psychology course to fill his schedule; he graduated in 1949. He earned his master's degree and, in 1952, his PhD in clinical psychology from the University of Iowa.

Career

Bandura joined the faculty of Stanford University in 1953 and remained there for the rest of his career, holding the David Starr Jordan Professorship of Social Science in Psychology. He served as president of the American Psychological Association in 1974.

Bobo doll experiment

In 1961 Bandura conducted the famous Bobo doll experiment, in which children who observed an adult model behaving aggressively toward an inflatable Bobo doll later imitated the aggression, demonstrating that behavior can be acquired through observation and imitation without direct reinforcement. Follow-up studies showed that children were more likely to imitate models who were rewarded for aggression and less likely to imitate models who were punished.

Social cognitive theory and self-efficacy

Bandura later expanded his framework into social cognitive theory, which emphasizes triadic reciprocal causation among behavior, personal factors and the environment. In 1977 he introduced the concept of self-efficacy — a person's belief in their capacity to execute the behaviors necessary to produce specific outcomes — which proved influential in education, health psychology, psychotherapy and organizational research. His concept of human agency holds that people are self-organizing, proactive and self-regulating rather than merely reactive to their environment.

Honors and death

Bandura received numerous honors, including the National Medal of Science in 2016. He died of congestive heart failure at his home in Stanford, California, on 26 July 2021, at the age of 95.
