Die schöne Müllerin

Die schöne Müllerin ("The Fair Maid of the Mill", Op. 25, D. 795) is a song cycle by Franz Schubert, composed in 1823 to poems by Wilhelm Müller. Written for voice and piano, it is one of the earliest extended song cycles and one of the most frequently performed works in the Lied repertoire.

Narrative

The cycle's twenty songs follow a young miller who follows a brook to a mill, falls in love with the miller's daughter, briefly believes his love returned, and loses her to a huntsman dressed in green. Consumed by jealousy and despair, he drowns himself in the brook, which sings him a final lullaby in the closing song, "Des Baches Wiegenlied". The brook itself is a continuous presence in the piano accompaniment and in the protagonist's narration.

Music

Schubert's settings trace the emotional arc from the eager optimism of "Das Wandern" through the joyful "Mein!" to the darkening final songs, using key, mode, and piano figuration to chart the miller's psychological decline. Together with Winterreise (1827), also to Müller's poems, Die schöne Müllerin defined the Romantic song cycle as a vehicle for sustained narrative and inner drama.
