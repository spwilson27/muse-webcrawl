Pons Cestius

The Pons Cestius (Italian: Ponte Cestio) is an ancient Roman stone bridge connecting Tiber Island to the right bank of the Tiber (the Trastevere side) in Rome, Italy. It is the western of the two bridges serving the island, the other being the Pons Fabricius, which links the island to the left bank. The bridge was first built in the first century BCE and largely rebuilt in late antiquity.

History

The original bridge was probably built around 46–44 BCE, in the same period as the Pons Fabricius (62 BCE), and is associated with a member of the Cestius family — possibly Lucius Cestius, praetor in 44 BCE, or the family of Gaius Cestius, builder of the Pyramid of Cestius. The bridge was rebuilt in 370 CE under the emperors Valentinian I, Valens, and Gratian, using material that included spolia from the nearby demolished Theatre of Balbus; an inscription recording the work survives.

In 1888–1892, when the Tiber embankments (the muraglioni) were constructed to protect Rome from flooding, the western channel of the river was widened and the bridge was substantially demolished and rebuilt. Only the central arch of the ancient structure was retained; the two smaller side arches were replaced by two large arches, lengthening the bridge from about 48 metres to about 81 metres. Despite this, the bridge preserves its ancient name and much of its antique core in the central span.

Description

The present bridge has three arches and carries pedestrians and vehicles between the Lungotevere degli Anguillara on the Trastevere side and Tiber Island, near the Fatebenefratelli Hospital and the church of San Bartolomeo all'Isola. Together with the Pons Fabricius, it forms part of the oldest river crossing still in use in Rome along an ancient route.
