Chariots of Fire is a 1981 British historical sports drama film directed by Hugh Hudson, produced by David Puttnam, and written by Colin Welland. It is based on the true story of two British athletes in the 1924 Paris Olympics: Eric Liddell, a devout Scottish Christian missionary's son who runs for the glory of God, and Harold Abrahams, an English Jew who runs to overcome prejudice. The title is inspired by the line "Bring me my Chariot of fire!" from William Blake's poem adapted into the hymn "Jerusalem".

Plot

The film contrasts the two runners' motivations as they compete at Cambridge and prepare for the Paris Games. Liddell refuses to run his 100 m heat on a Sunday on religious grounds and switches to the 400 m, which he wins, while Abrahams wins the 100 m with the help of professional coach Sam Mussabini. Ian Charleson played Liddell and Ben Cross played Abrahams, with Ian Holm as Mussabini.

Awards and legacy

The film won four Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Original Screenplay, and Vangelis's synthesiser score — its title theme topping charts — became iconic. The opening and closing beach-running sequence, shot on the West Sands at St Andrews, is one of the most famous scenes in British cinema. The film was re-released and adapted for the stage for the London 2012 Olympics.
