Reverse Polish notation

Reverse Polish notation (RPN), also called postfix notation, is a mathematical notation in which operators follow their operands. The expression 3 4 + means add 3 and 4, while the equivalent infix expression is 3 + 4. Because the order of evaluation is encoded by position, parentheses are not needed to remove ambiguity.

Evaluation

An RPN expression is processed from left to right using a stack. Numbers are pushed onto the stack. When an operator is encountered, it removes the required number of operands, applies the operation and pushes the result back. For example, 5 1 2 + 4 * + 3 - evaluates the parenthesized infix expression 5 + ((1 + 2) * 4) - 3, giving 14.

History

The notation derives from the prefix system developed by Polish logician Jan Łukasiewicz in the 1920s. Reversing that order produced postfix notation, which proved convenient for compilers and stack machines. It became widely familiar through Hewlett-Packard scientific calculators beginning in the late 1960s and 1970s, and it is central to the programming language Forth and to PostScript.

Advantages and disadvantages

RPN eliminates parentheses, reduces ambiguity and maps naturally onto stack-based hardware. Intermediate results remain visible on the stack, which experienced users find efficient. Its principal disadvantage is unfamiliarity: long expressions can be difficult for new readers to parse without mentally simulating the stack.
