Traffic noise

Traffic noise or roadway noise is the energy that is harvested from vehicular traffic and consists of engine noise, exhaust noise, and tyre–road interaction noise. Roadway noise is the principal source of environmental noise exposure in most urban areas, and its level depends on traffic volume, vehicle speed, the proportion of heavy vehicles, and the road surface.

Sources

At low speeds, powertrain noise (engine, exhaust, and cooling fans) dominates, particularly for heavy trucks and buses. Above roughly 40–50 km/h for cars, tyre/road noise becomes the dominant source: air pumping in the tread pattern, vibration of the tyre carcass, and texture-induced vibration of the road surface. Aerodynamic noise also grows at motorway speeds.

Health and environmental effects

Long-term exposure to traffic noise is associated with annoyance, sleep disturbance, and elevated risk of cardiovascular disease. The World Health Organization's environmental noise guidelines recommend limiting average road-traffic noise exposure. Noise also affects wildlife, interfering with animal communication and altering behaviour near roads.

Mitigation

Mitigation measures include noise barriers and earth berms, low-noise ("quiet") road surfaces such as thin-layer and porous asphalt, traffic management (speed reduction, restrictions on heavy vehicles at night), quieter tyres and vehicles — electric vehicles reduce powertrain noise though tyre noise remains — and sound insulation of buildings.
