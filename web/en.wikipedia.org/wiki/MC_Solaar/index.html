MC Solaar

MC Solaar (born Claude Honoré M'Barali, 5 March 1969) is a French rapper of Senegalese and Chadian origin. One of the pioneers of French hip hop, he achieved international fame in the early 1990s with a style distinguished by literate, playful and philosophically inflected lyrics delivered over jazzy, melodic production — a marked contrast to the harder image of much contemporary rap. He is one of the most acclaimed and commercially successful francophone rappers of all time.

Early life

M'Barali was born in Dakar, Senegal, to parents from Chad, and moved with his family to the Paris region as an infant, growing up in the suburbs of Saint-Denis and Villeneuve-Saint-Georges. He studied languages and philosophy at university while beginning to write rhymes. His stage name combines "MC" (master of ceremonies) with "Solaar", after the solar wind.

Career

His breakthrough single "Bouge de là" (1990), built on a sample of Cymande's "The Message", was one of the first major rap hits in France. His debut album Qui sème le vent récolte le tempo (1991) sold widely and included "Victime de la mode" and "Caroline". The follow-up Prose Combat (1994) is widely regarded as a landmark of French rap, featuring "Nouveau Western", which samples Serge Gainsbourg's "Bonnie and Clyde", and the sombre "Obsolète". Paradisiaque (1997) and MC Solaar (1998) followed, produced in part with Boom Bass and others.

A legal dispute with his record label Polydor blocked his recordings for several years around the turn of the century; he returned with Cinquième As (2001), which included the hit "Solaar pleure" and the international single "La Belle et le Bad Boy". Mach 6 (2003) was recorded in part with survivors of Gainsbourg's studio circle. After Chapitre 7 (2007), Solaar took a long break from recording, returning a decade later with Géopoétique (2017), which reached number one in France, followed by the three-part project Triptyque in 2024.

Style and legacy

Solaar's lyrics are noted for dense wordplay, internal rhyme, literary and philosophical references, and a generally reflective, non-violent outlook. He brought French-language rap to audiences well beyond the hip hop scene, including in the United States, where he collaborated with Guru of Gang Starr on the Jazzmatazz project. He is regularly ranked among the greatest French rappers and is credited with proving that rap in French could be both commercially popular and lyrically sophisticated.
