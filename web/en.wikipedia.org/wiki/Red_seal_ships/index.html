Red seal ships

Red seal ships (Japanese: 朱印船, shuinsen) were Japanese armed merchant sailing ships bound for Southeast Asian ports, carrying a red-sealed patent issued by the early Tokugawa shogunate in the first half of the 17th century. Between 1600 and 1635, more than 350 Japanese ships went overseas under this permit system.

History

The system, formalized under Tokugawa Ieyasu, licensed merchants such as the Chaya, Suminokura, and Araki families to trade abroad and offered them a measure of protection. Red seal ships traded in ports in Vietnam, Siam, Cambodia, the Philippines, and elsewhere, exchanging silver and crafts for silk, spices, and other goods.

End of the system

The trade ended abruptly with the Sakoku isolation policy: between 1633 and 1636 the shogunate banned Japanese travel abroad and recalled Japanese residents overseas, closing the red seal system and destroying or abandoning most of the ships.
