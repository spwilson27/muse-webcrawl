Zard Kuh (Persian: زردکوه, "Yellow Mountain") is a mountain massif in the central Zagros Mountains of western Iran, in Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari province. Its highest summit reaches about 4,221 metres (13,848 ft) above sea level, making it one of the highest peaks in the Zagros system. The mountain takes its name from the yellowish hue of its exposed rock in afternoon light.

Geography

The Zard Kuh range trends northwest–southeast along the great Zagros fold belt, formed by the collision of the Arabian and Eurasian plates. Heavy winter snowfall feeds the headwaters of two of Iran's most important rivers: the Karun, which flows south-west toward the Persian Gulf, and the Zayanderud, which flows east toward Isfahan. Glaciers and permanent snowfields persist in the highest cirques, unusual for Iran's generally arid climate.

Hydrology and tunnels

Because central Iran is chronically short of water, several tunnels — the Kuhrang tunnels — have been driven beneath the massif since the 1950s to divert Karun headwaters into the Zayanderud basin, supplying Isfahan and Yazd. The transfers have been controversial, affecting downstream Khuzestan and the water available to local Bakhtiari communities.

People and recreation

The slopes are summer pasture for Bakhtiari nomads, whose seasonal migrations (the kooch) cross the high passes. The Chelgerd area on the mountain's flanks is a centre for skiing and mountaineering, and the summit is a popular, non-technical climb in summer for Iranian mountaineers.
