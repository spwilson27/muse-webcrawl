The Battle of Jengland (also spelled Beslé or Jengland-Beslé) was fought on 22 August 851 between the Breton forces of Erispoe and a West Frankish army led by Charles the Bald. The Breton victory led directly to the Treaty of Angers.

Background

Following the Battle of Ballon in 845, Erispoe's father Nominoe had established Breton supremacy in eastern Brittany. After Nominoe's death, Charles the Bald invaded to reimpose Frankish authority on the young Erispoe.

The battle

The two armies clashed near the Vilaine river over two or three days of skirmishing and combat. Breton light cavalry tactics harassed the heavier Frankish forces; Charles eventually retreated by night, abandoning his camp, baggage and royal insignia.

Aftermath

By the Treaty of Angers (851) Charles recognised Erispoe as king of Brittany and ceded the counties of Rennes and Nantes and the Pays de Retz, greatly enlarging the Breton kingdom.
