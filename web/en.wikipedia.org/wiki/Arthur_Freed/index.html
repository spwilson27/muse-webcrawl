Arthur Freed (born Arthur Grossman; 9 September 1894 – 12 April 1973) was an American film producer and lyricist. As head of the celebrated "Freed Unit" at Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, he produced many of the greatest Hollywood musicals of the 1940s and 1950s, including An American in Paris (1951), Singin' in the Rain (1952) and Gigi (1958), the first and third of which won the Academy Award for Best Picture.

Career

Freed began in vaudeville and song-plugging, then became a successful Tin Pan Alley lyricist, usually partnered with composer Nacio Herb Brown. Their songs — "Singin' in the Rain", "You Are My Lucky Star", "Broadway Melody", "You Were Meant for Me" — were introduced in early MGM musicals after Louis B. Mayer hired Freed in 1929. He worked as an uncredited associate producer on The Wizard of Oz (1939) (and is credited with insisting "Over the Rainbow" be kept in the film) before being given his own unit.

The Freed Unit

Freed gathered an unmatched stable of talent — directors Vincente Minnelli and Stanley Donen; choreographer-director Gene Kelly; performers Fred Astaire, Judy Garland, Mickey Rooney, Cyd Charisse and Leslie Caron; and writers Betty Comden and Adolph Green — and gave them unusual creative freedom. Freed Unit landmarks include Meet Me in St. Louis (1944), Easter Parade (1948), On the Town (1949), The Band Wagon (1953) and Silk Stockings (1957). Freed won two Best Picture Oscars and received the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award in 1951.

Later life

After the studio system declined, Freed served as president of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (1963–1966) and produced the Academy Awards ceremonies. He was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 1972.
