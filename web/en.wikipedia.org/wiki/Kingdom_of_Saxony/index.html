The Kingdom of Saxony (German: Königreich Sachsen) was a German state that existed from 1806 to 1918. It was created when the Electorate of Saxony was raised to a kingdom as the Holy Roman Empire dissolved, and Frederick Augustus I became its first king. It was a member first of the Confederation of the Rhine, then of the German Confederation, the North German Confederation, and from 1871 of the German Empire.

History

As Napoleon's ally, Saxony fought against Prussia in the War of the Sixth Coalition; after the Battle of Leipzig in 1813 the king was taken prisoner, and at the Congress of Vienna Saxony lost some two-fifths of its territory, including Wittenberg and Lower Lusatia, to Prussia. The reduced kingdom nevertheless industrialised rapidly: Leipzig became a centre of publishing and trade fairs, and Chemnitz of machine building and textiles.

Saxony sided with Austria in the Austro-Prussian War of 1866 but, unlike Hanover, escaped annexation and joined the North German Confederation. A constitution of 1831 had made it a constitutional monarchy, and by 1914 it was one of the most densely populated and industrialised states in Germany. In the German Revolution of 1918–19, King Frederick Augustus III abdicated and Saxony became a free state within the Weimar Republic.
