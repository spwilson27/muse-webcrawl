Albert Nelson (25 April 1923 – 21 December 1992), known as Albert King, was an American blues guitarist and singer, one of the "Three Kings of the Blues Guitar" alongside B.B. King and Freddie King. A towering figure — he stood about 6 feet 4 inches (1.93 m) — he was famous for playing a Gibson Flying V guitar left-handed with the strings upside down, producing deep string bends that influenced generations of rock guitarists.

Career

Born on a cotton plantation in Indianola, Mississippi, King worked as a bulldozer driver before turning to music full-time. After early recordings for small labels, his breakthrough came with Stax Records in Memphis, where, backed by Booker T. & the M.G.'s, he recorded the classic album Born Under a Bad Sign (1967), whose title track and songs such as "Crosscut Saw" and "The Hunter" became blues standards covered by Cream, Led Zeppelin and many others.

Influence and legacy

King's economical, stinging single-note solos directly influenced Eric Clapton, Jimi Hendrix and especially Stevie Ray Vaughan, who called him a mentor. He was inducted into the Blues Hall of Fame in 1983 and posthumously into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2013. He died of a heart attack in Memphis in 1992.
