Shrek is a 2001 American animated fantasy comedy film produced by DreamWorks Animation and directed by Andrew Adamson and Vicky Jenson. Loosely based on William Steig's 1990 picture book Shrek!, it stars Mike Myers as the voice of Shrek, a solitary ogre whose swamp is overrun by fairy-tale creatures exiled by Lord Farquaad (John Lithgow). To reclaim his home, Shrek — accompanied by a wisecracking Donkey (Eddie Murphy) — agrees to rescue Princess Fiona (Cameron Diaz) for Farquaad.

Plot

Shrek and Donkey rescue Fiona from a dragon-guarded castle, but on the journey home Shrek and Fiona fall in love. Fiona conceals a curse: by night she transforms into an ogre, and only "love's true kiss" can break the spell. After a misunderstanding separates the pair, Shrek interrupts Fiona's wedding to Farquaad; the spell is broken by their kiss, Fiona takes the ogre form permanently, and the two marry in the swamp.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing about $488 million worldwide. It won the first-ever Academy Award for Best Animated Feature and was nominated for Best Adapted Screenplay; it also competed for the Palme d'Or at Cannes, the first animated film so honoured since 1974. Its irreverent parody of Disney fairy tales and its pop soundtrack (including Smash Mouth's "All Star") made it a cultural phenomenon. In 2020 it was selected for the U.S. National Film Registry.

Franchise

Shrek launched one of the most successful animation franchises: sequels Shrek 2 (2004), Shrek the Third (2007), and Shrek Forever After (2010), the Puss in Boots spin-offs, television specials, and a Broadway musical. A fifth film was announced for 2026.
