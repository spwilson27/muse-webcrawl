The Australia Council for the Arts — since 2023 operating as Creative Australia — is the Australian Government's principal arts funding and advisory body. Established in 1973 by the Whitlam government and given statutory authority by the Australia Council Act 1975, it invests in artists and arts organisations across theatre, dance, music, visual arts, literature, and First Nations arts through peer-assessed grants.

History

The Council grew out of earlier Commonwealth arts funding bodies, including the Commonwealth Literary Fund and the Australian Elizabethan Theatre Trust. Its creation in 1973 marked the first comprehensive national framework for federal arts support, operating at arm's length from government through peer assessment panels.

Funding controversies

In 2015, Arts Minister George Brandis redirected about $105 million from the Council to a new National Programme for Excellence in the Arts (later the Catalyst fund), a move widely criticised by the arts sector as undermining arm's-length peer review. Following the change of government and the 2016 Senate inquiry, most of the funding was restored to the Council by 2017.

Creative Australia

Under the Albanese government's National Cultural Policy, Revive (launched January 2023), the Australia Council was rebranded and expanded as Creative Australia, with increased funding and new responsibilities, including a dedicated First Nations-led board and bodies for music (Music Australia) and writers (Writers Australia).
