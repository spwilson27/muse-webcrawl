American Gods is a fantasy novel by English author Neil Gaiman, first published in 2001. The novel is a blend of Americana, fantasy, and various strands of ancient and modern mythology, all centering on the mysterious protagonist Shadow Moon. It won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 2002, along with the Nebula, Locus, and Bram Stoker awards. A slightly expanded tenth-anniversary edition was published in 2011, and the novel was adapted into a television series for Starz that premiered in 2017.

Plot summary

Shadow Moon is released from prison a few days early after his wife Laura dies in a car accident. On his journey home he meets the enigmatic Mr. Wednesday, who offers him work as a bodyguard and driver. Wednesday is revealed to be an aspect of the Norse god Odin, recruiting the "Old Gods" — deities brought to America by immigrants and sustained by belief — for a coming war against the "New Gods" of media, technology, and modern life. Shadow travels across the American Midwest, encounters figures from many mythologies, and discovers that the conflict is part of a two-man con devised by Wednesday and his associate. Laura, returned from death, follows Shadow as he unravels his own origins, including his parentage.

Themes

The novel explores America's identity as a nation of immigrants, the power and fragility of belief, and the displacement of traditional faiths by modern distractions. Roadside attractions and small towns feature prominently as places of residual power.

Reception and adaptation

American Gods was a commercial and critical success. The Starz television adaptation, developed by Bryan Fuller and Michael Green, ran for three seasons (2017–2021) starring Ricky Whittle as Shadow and Ian McShane as Mr. Wednesday. Gaiman's novella The Monarch of the Glen continues Shadow's story, and the companion novel Anansi Boys (2005) features the trickster god Mr. Nancy.
