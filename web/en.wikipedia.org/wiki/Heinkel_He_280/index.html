Heinkel He 280

Role: Fighter aircraft
Manufacturer: Heinkel
First flight: 22 September 1940 (glide); 30 March 1941 (powered)
Status: Cancelled
Number built: 9

The Heinkel He 280 was the first turbojet-powered fighter aircraft in the world. It was designed and produced by the German aircraft manufacturer Heinkel and flew for the first time in 1940. Despite its pioneering status, the He 280 never entered production or operational service, losing out to the rival Messerschmitt Me 262.

Development

Heinkel had flown the world's first jet aircraft, the He 178, in August 1939, powered by Hans von Ohain's turbojet. The He 280 was designed around two Heinkel HeS 8 engines mounted under the wings. Because the engines were not ready, the prototype first flew as a glider on 22 September 1940, towed aloft by a Heinkel He 111. On 30 March 1941, test pilot Fritz Schäfer made the first powered flight, making the He 280 the first purpose-built jet fighter to fly under jet power.

The aircraft featured a tricycle undercarriage and was the first aircraft to be fitted with an ejection seat; in January 1943 pilot Helmut Schenk used a compressed-air ejection seat to escape from the aircraft after icing made it uncontrollable, the first emergency use of an ejection seat in history.

Cancellation

Nine prototypes were built, but engine development problems and political preference for the Me 262 led the Reich Air Ministry to cancel the programme in March 1943. The Heinkel HeS 8 engine never matured, and later prototypes flew with Junkers Jumo 004 or BMW 003 engines. None of the nine airframes survives.

Specifications

The He 280 V5 was powered by two Heinkel HeS 8A turbojets of 5.9 kN (1,320 lbf) thrust each, had a maximum speed of about 820 km/h (510 mph), a wingspan of 12.2 m and a planned armament of three 20 mm MG 151/20 cannon.
