Asclepius

Asclepius is a hero and god of medicine in ancient Greek religion and mythology. He is the son of Apollo and Coronis, or Arsinoe, or of Apollo alone.

SymbolRod of Asclepius (serpent-entwined staff)
ParentsApollo and Coronis

Myth

Raised by the centaur Chiron, Asclepius learned healing so well he could resurrect the dead. Zeus, fearing disruption of the natural order (at Hades' complaint), killed him with a thunderbolt; Apollo in grief placed him among the stars as Ophiuchus. He was later deified.

Cult

Sanctuaries called Asclepieia functioned as healing temples, most famously at Epidaurus, Kos and Pergamon. Patients practised incubation — sleeping in the temple to receive dream cures. His daughters include Hygieia and Panacea. The Rod of Asclepius remains a symbol of medicine, often confused with Hermes' caduceus.
