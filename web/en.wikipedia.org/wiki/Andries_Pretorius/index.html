Andries Pretorius

Andries Wilhelmus Jacobus Pretorius (November 27, 1798 – July 23, 1853) was a leader of the Voortrekkers during the Great Trek in Southern Africa. He is best known for commanding the Voortrekker forces that defeated the Zulu at the Battle of Blood River in 1838, and for founding the short-lived Natalia Republic and later serving as a leading figure in the South African Republic (Transvaal). The city of Pretoria, South Africa's administrative capital, is named after him.

Early life

Pretorius was born in Graaff-Reinet in the Cape Colony, a descendant of Dutch settlers. A prosperous farmer and an experienced frontier commando leader, he joined the Great Trek in 1838, emigrating north-east from the Cape Colony after tensions with the British colonial authorities over frontier policy, land and the abolition of slavery.

Battle of Blood River

Arriving in Natal after the Zulu king Dingane had killed the Voortrekker leader Piet Retief and his party, and after Zulu attacks on trekker encampments at Bloukrans and Weenen, Pretorius was chosen as commandant-general. On December 16, 1838, his commando of about 460 men, laagered at the Ncome River, defeated a Zulu army of some 10,000–15,000 warriors; thousands of Zulu were killed while no Voortrekkers died in the fighting. The Voortrekkers attributed the victory to a vow made before the battle, commemorated as the Day of the Vow (later the Day of Reconciliation). The river was renamed Blood River (Ncome).

Natalia and the Transvaal

Pretorius became the leading political figure of the Natalia Republic, negotiating with the British, whose annexation of Natal in 1843 pushed him back across the Drakensberg. In 1848 he resisted Sir Harry Smith's annexation of the Orange River Sovereignty, was defeated at Boomplaats, and moved to the Transvaal. There he negotiated the Sand River Convention of 1852, by which Britain recognized the independence of the emigrant farmers north of the Vaal River — the basis of the South African Republic. Pretorius died in 1853 at his farm in the Magaliesberg; his son Marthinus Wessel Pretorius later founded Pretoria (1855) and named it in his honor.
