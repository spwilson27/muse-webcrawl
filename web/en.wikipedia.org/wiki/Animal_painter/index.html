Animal painter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An animal painter is an artist who specialises in the depiction of animals. In earlier usage the term, in French peintre animalier, often implied a painter or sculptor whose work centred on animals as the principal subject rather than as accessories to landscape or history painting. The specialism rose to prominence in the seventeenth through nineteenth centuries, when works portraying horses, cattle, hunting scenes, and domestic animals were in high demand.

History

Animals had featured in art since prehistory, but the specialist animal painter emerged as a distinct professional in the Dutch Golden Age, when artists such as Paulus Potter and Aelbert Cuyp painted cattle and farm scenes for a prosperous market. In France, Jean-Baptiste Oudry became court painter of the royal hunt under Louis XV. British sporting art of the eighteenth century centred on the horse: George Stubbs combined anatomical study — published in his Anatomy of the Horse (1766) — with elegant portraits of racehorses and their owners.

In the nineteenth century, Edwin Landseer became Britain's most celebrated animal painter, known for stags, dogs, and sentimental narratives, while in France Rosa Bonheur achieved international fame with large, naturalistic works such as The Horse Fair. The French sculptor Antoine-Louis Barye led the animaliers, a group of sculptors devoted to realistic animal subjects.

Practice

Successful animal painting required close study of anatomy, movement, and behaviour, often involving dissection, menagerie visits, and long observation. Though academic hierarchies ranked animal painting below history painting, specialists could command high prices from aristocratic patrons interested in hunting, racing, and livestock breeding.
