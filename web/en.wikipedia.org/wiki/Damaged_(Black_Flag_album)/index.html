Damaged is the debut studio album by the American hardcore punk band Black Flag, released on 5 December 1981 through guitarist Greg Ginn's label SST Records. It was recorded with singer Henry Rollins, who had joined the band months earlier, and is widely regarded as one of the foundational records of hardcore punk.

Content

The album's fifteen songs channel alienation, paranoia and rage at suburban conformity. "Rise Above" became an anthem of the genre; other tracks include "Six Pack", "TV Party", "Gimmie Gimmie Gimmie" and the harrowing closing title suite "Damaged I" and "Damaged II". The cover photograph by Ed Colver shows Rollins's fist smashing a mirror, an image that became iconic in punk iconography.

Reception and legacy

Initially overlooked by mainstream media and hampered by a legal dispute with Unicorn Records (a sticker on early copies declared it an "anti-parent record"), Damaged was later acclaimed: Rolling Stone placed it among the greatest albums of all time, and it was included in the book 1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die. Its ferocious tempo, Ginn's dissonant guitar and Rollins's intensity influenced generations of punk, metal and alternative bands. See also Hardcore punk and SST Records.
