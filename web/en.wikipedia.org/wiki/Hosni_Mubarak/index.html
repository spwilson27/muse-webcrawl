Muhammad Hosni El Sayed Mubarak (4 May 1928 – 25 February 2020) was an Egyptian politician and military officer who served as the fourth president of Egypt from 1981 to 2011. Before entering politics he was a career officer in the Egyptian Air Force, which he commanded during the Yom Kippur War of 1973, and he served as vice president under Anwar Sadat from 1975.

Presidency

Mubarak became president on 14 October 1981, eight days after Sadat's assassination, and ruled for almost 30 years. His presidency was marked by political stability, a state of emergency in force for nearly his entire tenure, alignment with the United States, maintenance of the peace treaty with Israel, and economic liberalisation. He survived several assassination attempts, most notably in Addis Ababa in 1995. His National Democratic Party dominated Egyptian politics, and elections under his rule were widely criticised as neither free nor fair.

2011 revolution and fall

Mass protests beginning on 25 January 2011, part of the Arab Spring, demanded Mubarak's resignation. After 18 days of demonstrations centred on Cairo's Tahrir Square, Mubarak stepped down on 11 February 2011 and power passed to the Supreme Council of the Armed Forces.

Trial and later life

Mubarak was tried for his role in the killing of protesters and for corruption. In 2012 he was sentenced to life imprisonment, but the verdict was overturned on appeal; in a 2015 retrial he was sentenced to three years in a corruption case, and in 2017 Egypt's highest court acquitted him of charges over the deaths of protesters. He was released in March 2017 and died in Cairo in February 2020, receiving a military funeral.
