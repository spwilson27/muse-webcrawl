Laughing owl

Scientific nameNinox albifacies
FamilyStrigidae
StatusExtinct (last confirmed record 1914)
RangeNew Zealand

The laughing owl (Ninox albifacies), also known by its Māori name whēkau, was an owl endemic to New Zealand that became extinct in the early twentieth century. It was named for its distinctive call, described as a loud, eerie laugh-like series of shrieks.

Description

The laughing owl was a medium-sized owl with yellowish-brown plumage streaked with darker brown, a white facial disc, and relatively long legs adapted to a partly terrestrial life. It fed largely on the ground, taking birds, lizards, bats, frogs, and large insects, and it is thought to have preyed on the introduced Pacific rat as well.

Extinction

The species was still reasonably common when Europeans arrived but declined rapidly with the spread of introduced predators such as stoats, cats, and dogs, together with habitat change. The last confirmed specimen was found dead in South Canterbury in July 1914, and unconfirmed reports persisted for some decades afterwards.
