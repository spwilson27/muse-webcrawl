Khan el-Khalili

Location: Islamic Cairo, Egypt
Established: 1382–1389

Khan el-Khalili (Arabic: خان الخليلي) is a famous bazaar and souq in the historic centre of Cairo, Egypt. Founded in the late 14th century as a caravanserai by the Mamluk emir Dajaharks al-Khalili, it remains one of Cairo's major attractions and a hub for artisans and merchants.

History

The site was originally the location of a Fatimid-era cemetery and palace complex. Emir al-Khalili demolished it to build a large trading khan in 1382. The complex was rebuilt in the early 16th century by Sultan al-Ghuri, and its lanes gradually filled with workshops and stalls selling gold, spices, brassware, textiles and perfumes.

The bazaar today

Alongside tourist goods, the market retains workshops serving local customers. Its old coffeehouses — notably El Fishawy, said to have operated continuously for centuries and frequented by novelist Naguib Mahfouz — are landmarks of literary Cairo.
