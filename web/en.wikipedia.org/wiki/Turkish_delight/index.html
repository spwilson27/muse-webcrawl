Turkish delight

Turkish delight, or lokum, is a family of confections based on a gel of starch and sugar, traditionally flavoured with rosewater, lemon or mastic and often containing chopped nuts such as pistachios, hazelnuts or walnuts. The pieces are usually cut into small cubes and dusted with icing sugar or desiccated coconut.

History

Lokum is said to have been created in Istanbul in the late 18th century, most famously by confectioner Hacı Bekir, whose shop, founded in 1777, still operates. The sweet became popular in the Ottoman palace and spread across the empire and into Europe in the 19th century, where it acquired the name "Turkish delight".

In culture

Turkish delight is famously the enchanted sweet offered to Edmund Pevensie by the White Witch in C. S. Lewis's The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe (1950), which greatly increased its recognition in the English-speaking world.
