Itai-itai disease

The Jinzu River basin, Toyama Prefecture, where itai-itai disease was identified.

Itai-itai disease (Japanese: イタイイタイ病, literally "it hurts, it hurts disease") is a form of chronic cadmium poisoning first identified in Toyama Prefecture, Japan. It is one of the Four Big Pollution Diseases of Japan. The name comes from the cries of pain — "itai, itai" — uttered by sufferers because of severe bone and joint pain.

Cause

The disease was caused by cadmium released into the Jinzu River by mining operations at the Kamioka mine, operated by the Mitsui Mining and Smelting Company. The cadmium-contaminated river water was used to irrigate rice paddies, and the metal accumulated in the rice and in the bodies of local residents over decades.

Symptoms and mechanism

Cadmium damages the kidneys, impairing their ability to reabsorb calcium and phosphate, and interferes with vitamin D metabolism. The result is osteomalacia — softening of the bones — combined with osteoporosis, causing excruciating pain, skeletal deformities, loss of height, and fractures from trivial stress. The disease disproportionately affected middle-aged and elderly women, particularly those who had borne several children and had nutritional deficiencies. Kidney failure could follow in advanced cases.

Recognition and litigation

Local physician Noboru Hagino campaigned to identify cadmium as the cause, and in 1968 Japan's Ministry of Health and Welfare officially acknowledged that itai-itai disease was caused by cadmium poisoning from the mine. In 1971 a court ruled in favour of the victims against Mitsui, and the company accepted responsibility, agreeing to pay compensation and the costs of soil remediation.
