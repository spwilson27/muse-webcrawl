The Rocks, New South Wales

[Hero image placeholder: historic sandstone buildings and laneways of The Rocks beneath the Sydney Harbour Bridge.]

The Rocks is a suburb, tourist precinct and historic area of Sydney's city centre, in the state of New South Wales, Australia. It is located on the southern shore of Sydney Harbour, immediately north-west of the Sydney central business district.

History

The Rocks was established shortly after Australia's first European settlement in 1788, being located near the spot where the First Fleet arrived. It has a long history stretching back to the birthplace of modern-day Australia. The name derives from the rocky coast on the western side of Sydney Cove, where convicts pitched their tents.

Traditionally, the area was home to the Gadigal people of the Eora Nation before European settlement. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries the district developed a reputation as a slum, often associated with visiting sailors and gangs; much of it was demolished following the outbreak of bubonic plague in 1900.

Heritage and tourism

In the 1970s, residents and the Builders Labourers Federation, under Jack Mundey, imposed "green bans" to prevent the wholesale demolition of the area's historic buildings, leading to the preservation of much of its heritage streetscape. Today The Rocks is a popular tourist destination known for its weekend markets, pubs, cafes, museums, and its proximity to the Sydney Harbour Bridge and the Sydney Opera House.
