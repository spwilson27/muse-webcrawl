Damodar Valley Corporation

The Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC) is a statutory corporation of the Government of India, established in 1948 to manage the Damodar River valley in the states of Jharkhand and West Bengal. Modelled partly on the Tennessee Valley Authority of the United States, it was the first multipurpose river valley project of independent India, combining flood control, irrigation, hydropower and later large-scale thermal power generation.

History

The Damodar River was notorious for devastating floods — it was called the "Sorrow of Bengal." Devastating flooding in 1943 prompted plans for a unified valley authority. The DVC Act of 1948 created the corporation with participation from the central government and the governments of Bihar (now Jharkhand) and West Bengal. In its first phase DVC built four major dams — Tilaiya (1953), Konar (1955), Maithon (1957) and Panchet (1959) — along with the Durgapur barrage and an extensive canal network.

Power generation

Over time, electricity generation became DVC's dominant activity. It operates thermal power stations including Mejia, Chandrapura, Koderma and Raghunathpur, with an installed capacity of several thousand megawatts, supplying industry and railways across eastern India, alongside its original hydel stations.
