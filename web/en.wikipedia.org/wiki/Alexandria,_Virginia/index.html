Alexandria, Virginia

Alexandria is an independent city in the Commonwealth of Virginia, United States, located along the western bank of the Potomac River, approximately seven miles (11 km) south of downtown Washington, D.C. Its population was about 159,000 at the 2020 census. The city is known for its historic Old Town district, with brick sidewalks, preserved 18th- and 19th-century buildings, and the King Street commercial corridor leading to a working waterfront.

History

Alexandria was founded in 1749 by Scottish merchants and named for John Alexander, whose land formed part of the site. A young George Washington, whose Mount Vernon estate lay nearby, was involved in its early surveying. From 1791 to 1846 the city formed part of the District of Columbia before being retroceded to Virginia. It was an important port and, in the 19th century, a major centre of the domestic slave trade; the Franklin and Armfield office on Duke Street is preserved as the Freedom House Museum.

During the American Civil War Alexandria was occupied by Union forces from May 1861 until the war's end. In the 20th century it became closely tied to the federal government, and the arrival of Metrorail in 1983 spurred redevelopment. The United States Patent and Trademark Office campus and numerous defence and nonprofit headquarters are located in the city.

Geography and neighbourhoods

Neighbourhoods include Old Town, Del Ray, the West End, Arlandria, Rosemont, and Carlyle. Alexandria is served by the King Street–Old Town, Braddock Road, Van Dorn Street, and Potomac Yard stations on the Washington Metro.
