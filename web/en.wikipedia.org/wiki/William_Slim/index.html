William Slim

Field Marshal William Joseph Slim, 1st Viscount Slim (6 August 1891 – 14 December 1970) was a British military commander and the 13th Governor-General of Australia. He commanded the Fourteenth Army in the Burma campaign of the Second World War.

Field Marshal William Joseph Slim, 1st Viscount Slim, KG, GCB, GCMG, GCVO, GBE, DSO, MC, KStJ (6 August 1891 – 14 December 1970) was a British military commander and the 13th Governor-General of Australia. He is best known for commanding the British Fourteenth Army — the "Forgotten Army" — in the Burma campaign, turning one of Britain's worst defeats into final victory over Imperial Japan.

Early life and First World War

Slim was born in Bristol and brought up in Birmingham. Unable to afford a university or Sandhurst, he worked as a schoolteacher and clerk, joining the territorial forces. In the First World War he was severely wounded at Gallipoli, recovered, and won the Military Cross in Mesopotamia, later transferring to the Indian Army and serving with Gurkha regiments.

Second World War

Slim commanded the 10th Indian Division in Iraq and Persia in 1941. In March 1942 he took command of Burma Corps during the longest retreat in British military history, the 900-mile withdrawal from Burma to India. In October 1943 he became commander of the newly formed Fourteenth Army. He rebuilt its morale and logistics around air supply and "battle boxes", defeated the Japanese offensive at Imphal and Kohima in 1944, and then conducted the reconquest of Burma, culminating in the capture of Mandalay and Rangoon in 1945.

Post-war

Slim became Chief of the Imperial General Staff (1948–1952), the first Indian Army officer to hold the post, and was promoted field marshal. From 1953 to 1959 he served as Governor-General of Australia, where he was widely popular. His memoir of the Burma campaign, Defeat into Victory (1956), is regarded as one of the finest military memoirs in English. He died in London on 14 December 1970.
