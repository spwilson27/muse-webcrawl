Czech Philharmonic
Founded: 1896
Location: Prague, Czech Republic
Concert hall: Rudolfinum
Principal conductor: Semyon Bychkov (from 2018)

The Czech Philharmonic (Czech: Česká filharmonie) is a Czech symphony orchestra based in Prague, widely regarded as one of the foremost orchestras in Europe. It gave its first concert in 1896 under Antonín Dvořák, who conducted his own works.

History

The orchestra grew out of the orchestra of the Prague National Theatre. Its debut on 4 January 1896 featured works by Dvořák, who became closely associated with the ensemble. Rafael Kubelík, Václav Talich, Karel Ančerl, Jiří Bělohlávek and Václav Neumann are among its celebrated chief conductors.

Home and recordings

Its home is the Rudolfinum, on the bank of the Vltava. The orchestra has recorded extensively, notably Czech repertoire including Dvořák, Smetana, Janáček and Martinů, and regularly opens the Prague Spring festival with Smetana's Má vlast.
