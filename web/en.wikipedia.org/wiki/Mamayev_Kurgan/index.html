Mamayev Kurgan (Russian: Мамаев курган) is a dominant height overlooking the city of Volgograd (formerly Stalingrad) in southern Russia. The name supposedly derives from Mamai, a commander of the Golden Horde. During the Battle of Stalingrad (1942–1943) the hill — designated Height 102.0 on military maps — was fiercely contested, changing hands many times, because whoever controlled it overlooked the city and the Volga River crossings.

Battle of Stalingrad

From September 1942 to January 1943, Soviet and German forces fought for the hill with extraordinary intensity; the slopes were churned by shellfire and littered with remains and metal fragments long after the war. The Soviet 62nd Army held the eastern slopes, maintaining a precarious lifeline to the Volga.

Memorial complex

After the war the hill became the site of a vast memorial complex, "To the Heroes of the Battle of Stalingrad," designed under sculptor Yevgeny Vuchetich and completed in 1967. Its centerpiece is The Motherland Calls, an 85-metre statue of a woman striding forward with a raised sword; it was the tallest statue in the world when completed and remains among the largest. The complex includes the Hall of Military Glory with an eternal flame and the names of fallen defenders.

Vasily Chuikov, commander of the 62nd Army, is buried on the hill, as is the sniper Vasily Zaitsev, in accordance with their wishes.
