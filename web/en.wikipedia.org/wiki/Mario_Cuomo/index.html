Mario Cuomo

Mario Matthew Cuomo (June 15, 1932 – January 1, 2015) was an American lawyer and politician who served as the 52nd governor of New York for three terms, from 1983 to 1994. A leading voice of American liberalism, he was famed for his oratory, notably his keynote address at the 1984 Democratic National Convention.

Career

Born in Queens to Italian immigrant parents, Cuomo worked his way through St. John’s University and its law school, clerking and later teaching law. After serving as secretary of state of New York and then lieutenant governor under Hugh Carey, he won the 1982 Democratic primary and the governorship, defeating Ed Koch and Lew Lehrman respectively.

National profile

Cuomo’s 1984 convention speech, presenting his “Tale of Two Cities” as a rebuttal to President Ronald Reagan’s “shining city upon a hill”, made him a national figure. He was repeatedly urged to run for president but declined to enter the 1988 and 1992 races — indecision that earned him the nickname “Hamlet on the Hudson”. He lost his bid for a fourth term to George Pataki in the Republican wave of 1994. His son Andrew Cuomo later also served as governor of New York. The new Tappan Zee Bridge over the Hudson River was named the Governor Mario M. Cuomo Bridge in his honour.
