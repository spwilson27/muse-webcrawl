LG Corporation (Korean: 엘지), formerly Lucky-Goldstar, is a South Korean multinational conglomerate founded by Koo In-hwoi in 1947. It is one of the largest chaebol in South Korea, with businesses spanning electronics, chemicals, household goods, and telecommunications services.

The name LG derives from the merger of two Korean companies, Lucky (Lak-Hui Chemical Industrial Corp., founded 1947) and GoldStar Co. Ltd. (founded 1958). The combined group traded as Lucky-Goldstar before shortening its name to LG in 1995, adopting the slogan "Life's Good."

History

Koo In-hwoi established Lak-Hui Chemical in 1947, producing cosmetics and later plastics. GoldStar, founded in 1958, produced South Korea's first radios, televisions, refrigerators, washing machines, and air conditioners. The group expanded into petrochemicals, energy, and telecommunications during the 1970s and 1980s, and restructured as a holding-company system in the 2000s, with LG Corp. as the parent.

Subsidiaries

Major affiliates include LG Electronics, LG Chem, LG Household & Health Care, LG Uplus, LG Energy Solution, LG Display, and LG Innotek. LG Chem is among the world's largest chemical companies, and its battery spin-off LG Energy Solution is a leading manufacturer of lithium-ion batteries for electric vehicles.
