Hinamatsuri
Observed byJapan
Date3 March
Also calledDoll's Day, Girls' Day, Momo no Sekku

Hinamatsuri (雛祭り), also called Doll's Day or Girls' Day, is a Shinto festival in Japan celebrated on 3 March each year. Families pray for the health, happiness and prosperity of young girls, and display a set of ornamental dolls (hina-ningyō) representing the Emperor, Empress and attendants of the Heian-period imperial court on a tiered platform called a hinadan, covered with a red carpet.

Customs

The doll display typically has five or seven tiers. The top tier holds the imperial pair (dairi-bina); lower tiers hold court ladies, musicians, ministers and attendants, along with miniature furniture, carriages and offerings. Families usually put the dolls away promptly after the festival, as superstition holds that leaving them out too long may delay a daughter's marriage.

Festival foods include hina-arare (sweet rice crackers), hishimochi (diamond-shaped layered rice cakes in pink, white and green), chirashizushi, and shirozake, a sweet white sake. The celebration is also called Momo no Sekku, the Peach Festival, because peach trees bloom around this time in parts of Japan.

History

Hinamatsuri derives from ancient Chinese purification customs transmitted to Japan, in which paper dolls (hitogata) were floated down rivers to carry away misfortune — a rite surviving in the nagashibina ceremony still held in some regions. During the Edo period the festival became an official court holiday, and elaborate doll displays spread among wealthy townspeople.
