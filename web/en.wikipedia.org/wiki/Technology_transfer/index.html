Technology transfer

[Image: Technology transfer moves research results into practical use]

Technology transfer (or transfer of technology, TOT) is the process of transferring technology — knowledge, skills, methods, designs and inventions — from the person or organisation that owns or holds it to others, so that scientific and technological developments become accessible to a wider range of users who can develop them into products, processes and services.

Channels

Transfer occurs through licensing of patents and other intellectual property, spin-off and start-up companies, joint ventures, franchising, technical assistance, publication, and the movement of trained personnel. Universities and public laboratories maintain technology transfer offices to evaluate disclosures, file patents and negotiate licences; industry-sponsored research and "science parks" provide further routes.

Policy and history

In the United States, the Bayh–Dole Act of 1980 allowed universities to own patents arising from federally funded research, greatly expanding academic licensing; similar reforms followed in Europe and Asia. Internationally, technology transfer is a contentious element of trade and climate policy: the WTO's TRIPS Agreement and the UN climate regime both contain provisions intended to help developing countries acquire technology, balanced against the intellectual-property rights of its owners. Critics note the "valley of death" between laboratory proof-of-concept and commercial viability as a persistent obstacle that transfer offices and proof-of-concept funds aim to bridge.
