Neutering

Neutering, from the Latin neuter ("of neither sex"), is the removal of an animal's reproductive organ, either all of it or a considerably large part. The male-specific term is castration, and the female-specific term is spaying. In common usage, "neutering" often refers to males. The procedure is commonly performed on companion animals such as dogs and cats to control population, modify behaviour, and obtain health benefits, and on livestock for management purposes.

Methods

Females: spaying

Spaying usually means an ovariohysterectomy — removal of the ovaries and uterus — or, less commonly, an ovariectomy (ovaries only), performed under general anaesthesia through an abdominal incision. Removal of the ovaries eliminates heat cycles and the risk of pyometra (uterine infection) and greatly reduces the risk of mammary tumours when done early.

Males: castration

Castration is an orchidectomy: removal of the testes through a small incision. In large animals and livestock, alternatives include the Burdizzo clamp (bloodless crushing of the spermatic cord) and elastic banding, which cause the testes to atrophy. Chemical methods, such as intratesticular injections or hormone-based implants (e.g., deslorelin), exist for some species.

Health and behaviour effects

Neutering prevents unwanted litters and reduces roaming, urine marking, and some forms of aggression in males. Documented benefits include elimination of testicular cancer risk and reduced prostate disease in dogs, and prevention of pyometra in females. Possible disadvantages include surgical risk, a tendency toward obesity, and — depending on species, breed, and age at neutering — increased risks of certain orthopaedic problems and some cancers; the balance of evidence has led veterinary bodies to recommend case-by-case decisions for dogs, while generally supporting neutering of pet cats and shelter animals.

Population control

Neutering is central to animal shelter policy and to trap–neuter–return (TNR) programmes for feral cats, in which cats are surgically neutered, ear-tipped, and returned to their territory. Early-age (paediatric) neutering, from about 6–8 weeks in shelter settings, is endorsed by many veterinary associations.

Terminology

A neutered male cat is sometimes called a gib; a castrated male horse is a gelding; castrated cattle are steers or oxen depending on use and region. "Desexing" is the common term in Australia and New Zealand.
