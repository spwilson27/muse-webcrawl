Vermilion

Vermilion is a brilliant red or scarlet pigment originally made from the powdered mineral cinnabar, and also the name of the resulting vivid red-orange colour. Chemically it is mercury sulfide (HgS).

History

Cinnabar was mined at Almadén in Spain from Roman times, and vermilion was the principal red pigment of European painting from antiquity through the Renaissance, used by artists including Titian. In China, cinnabar lacquer and vermilion inks were prized for millennia; the pigment was also used in Maya burials in Mesoamerica.

Production

From the 17th century a synthetic "Dutch process" produced vermilion by combining mercury and sulfur and subliming the resulting black sulfide into the red form. Synthetic production in China is documented much earlier, possibly from the 4th century BC.

Toxicity and replacement

Vermilion is toxic because of its mercury content, and it can darken with age as the red alpha form converts to black metacinnabar. It has largely been replaced in artists' paints by cadmium reds and modern organic pigments, though the name "vermilion" survives for the hue.
