Montpellier

Montpellier is a city in southern France, prefecture of the Hérault department in the Occitanie region, located on the Mediterranean coast between Nîmes and Béziers. The city's metropolitan area had a population of roughly 800,000 in recent estimates, and Montpellier is one of France's fastest-growing cities, with a notably young population owing to its large student body.

History

Montpellier was founded in the late 10th century and grew into a major trading centre under the Crown of Aragon, famous for spices, wine, and textiles. Its medical school, founded in the 12th century and formalised by papal bull in 1220, is among the oldest in continuous operation; Rabelais and Nostradamus studied there. The city suffered severely in the Wars of Religion and the siege of 1622. Under mayor Georges Frêche (1977–2004) the city expanded with grand modern districts such as Antigone, designed by Ricardo Bofill, and Port Marianne.

Geography and life

The historic centre, the Écusson, is one of Europe's largest pedestrian areas, centred on the Place de la Comédie with its Fountain of the Three Graces and opera house. The Lez river flows through the city toward the Mediterranean lagoons at Palavas-les-Flots. Montpellier is known for its tramway lines designed by prominent artists and architects, its botanical garden (founded 1593), the Fabre Museum, and the annual Montpellier Danse festival.
