Turgo turbine

The Turgo turbine is an impulse water turbine designed for medium head hydroelectric applications. It was developed in 1919 by Eric Crewdson of the British firm Gilkes, as an improvement on the Pelton wheel.

Design

In a Turgo turbine, the water jet strikes the runner at an angle of about 20 degrees to the plane of the wheel, entering on one side of the cup-shaped blades and exiting on the other. Because the jet does not interfere with the discharge, the runner can be smaller and run faster than a Pelton wheel of the same capacity, and it handles higher flow rates. Turgo runners typically operate under heads of roughly 15 to 300 metres.

Applications

Turgo turbines are popular for small and medium hydroelectric schemes, including run-of-river and mini-hydro installations, where their simple construction, wide operating range and good efficiency at part load are advantageous.
