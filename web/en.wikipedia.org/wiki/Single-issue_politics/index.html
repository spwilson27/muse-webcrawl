Single-issue politics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Single-issue politics involves political campaigning or political support based on one essential policy area or idea. A single-issue voter is a voter who makes their electoral decision on the basis of a candidate's or party's position on one question alone, regardless of the candidate's other views. Likewise, a single-issue party is a political party whose platform and campaigning concentrate overwhelmingly on a single topic.

Characteristics

Single-issue politics is often highly emotional and values-driven. Because the issue is treated as overriding, single-issue voters may support candidates from parties they otherwise oppose, and single-issue movements may build coalitions across the usual left–right spectrum. Political scientists note that such voters can nonetheless be decisive in close elections, because they are unlikely to abstain and highly likely to turn out.

Critics argue that single-issue campaigning simplifies complex policy trade-offs and can distort party platforms, since parties chasing a mobilised bloc may adopt positions out of proportion to an issue's salience among the wider electorate. Supporters reply that intense minorities have a legitimate place in democratic deliberation and that single-issue campaigns have historically driven major reforms.

Examples of single issues

  • Abortion — pro-life and pro-choice movements are frequently cited as archetypal single-issue causes, especially in the United States.
  • Gun rights and gun control — organisations such as the National Rifle Association rate candidates mainly on firearm policy.
  • Prohibition and temperance — nineteenth-century movements that spawned dedicated parties in several countries.
  • Brexit — the UK Independence Party and later the Brexit Party campaigned almost exclusively on leaving the European Union.
  • Animal welfare — parties such as the Dutch Party for the Animals and animal-rights campaigns.
  • Drug policy reform, euthanasia, nuclear disarmament and tax resistance have also generated single-issue organisations and parties.

Single-issue parties

Pure single-issue parties rarely win power on their own, but in proportional-representation systems they can enter legislatures and influence coalition negotiations, while in plurality systems they more often act as pressure vehicles or spoilers. Successful examples sometimes broaden into general parties once their core demand is met or absorbed by larger parties — a process sometimes described as the movement's goal being "stolen" by the mainstream.
