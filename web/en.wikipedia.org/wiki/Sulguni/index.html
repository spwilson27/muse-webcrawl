Sulguni (Georgian: სულგუნი, სულუგუნი sulguni, suluguni) is a brined Georgian cheese from the Samegrelo region. It has a sour, mildly salty taste, a dimpled texture and an elastic consistency; its colour ranges from white to pale yellow. Sulguni is traditionally formed into flat discs about 2.5 to 3.5 centimetres thick and is one of the most popular cheeses in Georgia, where it is an essential ingredient of khachapuri.

Production

Sulguni is made from pasteurised cow's or buffalo's milk, or a mixture of the two. The curd is first ripened to develop acidity, then kneaded in hot water or whey — a "pasta filata" technique similar to that used for mozzarella — giving the cheese its layered, stringy texture. The formed discs are brined in a salt solution, which gives sulguni a long shelf life. A smoked version is also produced, with a firmer texture and amber rind.

Uses

Sulguni is eaten fresh as a table cheese, fried, or melted into dishes. In Mingrelian khachapuri it is used both inside the dough and grated over the top. It melts well and stretches, making it a common filling for breads and pastries across the Caucasus. In 2011 Georgia obtained a protected designation for sulguni, recognising it as part of the country's culinary heritage.
