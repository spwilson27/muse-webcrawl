Val d'Aran

Val d'Aran (Aranese: Vall d'Aran)
Country: Spain (Catalonia)
Capital: Vielha e Mijaran
Languages: Aranese (Occitan), Catalan, Spanish

The Val d'Aran (Occitan: Vall d'Aran, Catalan: Vall d'Aran, Spanish: Valle de Arán) is a valley and comarca in the Pyrenees, in the province of Lleida in Catalonia, Spain. Unusually for Spain, it lies on the northern side of the Pyrenean watershed, and the Garonne River rises here before flowing north through France. Its capital is Vielha e Mijaran.

Geography and climate

The valley, about 620 km², is surrounded by peaks above 3,000 metres and opens only toward France in the north — until the opening of the Vielha tunnel in 1948, winter access from the rest of Spain was impossible for months at a time. The Atlantic-influenced climate gives the valley greener, wetter conditions than most of the Spanish Pyrenees.

Language and status

The native language is Aranese, a dialect of Gascon Occitan, spoken alongside Catalan and Spanish. Aranese enjoys co-official status throughout Catalonia, and the valley has its own autonomous institution, the Conselh Generau d'Aran, with devolved powers under Catalonia's Statute of Autonomy. The valley preserved strong local privileges for centuries under agreements with the Crown of Aragon dating from 1313.

Economy

Traditionally based on livestock, forestry and smuggling across the French border, the economy today rests on tourism: the ski resort of Baqueira-Beret is among the largest in Spain and a favourite of the Spanish royal family. The valley's Romanesque churches and stone villages attract summer visitors as well.
