Comédie-Française
Founded: 1680
Location: Paris, France
Home venue: Salle Richelieu
Nickname: La Maison de Molière

The Comédie-Française is France's national theatre company, founded in 1680 by decree of Louis XIV through the merger of Parisian troupes, including the company that had been led by Molière. It is one of the few state theatres in France and is often called La Maison de Molière, "the House of Molière", after its most famous playwright.

History

The company was created by joining the Hôtel de Bourgogne troupe with Molière's former company at the Hôtel Guénégaud, giving it a monopoly on French-language spoken drama in Paris. Through the Revolution it was dissolved and refounded, and in 1799 it took up residence in the Salle Richelieu near the Palais-Royal, its home ever since. Talma and Rachel were among its celebrated performers of the nineteenth century.

Organisation

The company is structured as a society of actors. Its sociétaires—permanent members, among whom the doyen or doyenne is senior—share in governance and elect pensionnaires to membership. The troupe maintains a large repertory, especially of French classical drama by Molière, Racine, Corneille, and Marivaux, alongside modern and foreign works.

Venues

The Comédie-Française performs principally at the Salle Richelieu and also at the Théâtre du Vieux-Colombier and Studio-Théâtre in Paris. It tours nationally and internationally and is subsidised by the French state as a public institution under the Ministry of Culture.
