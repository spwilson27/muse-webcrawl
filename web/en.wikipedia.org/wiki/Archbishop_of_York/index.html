The Archbishop of York is a senior bishop in the Church of England, second only to the Archbishop of Canterbury. Styled Primate of England, the archbishop is diocesan bishop of the Diocese of York and metropolitan archbishop of the Province of York, which covers the northern dioceses of England. The archbishop sits, ex officio, among the Lords Spiritual in the House of Lords.

History

The see traces its origins to the Gregorian mission: Paulinus, consecrated bishop of York in 625, baptised King Edwin of Northumbria at York in 627. Pope Gregory the Great had planned for two English metropolitan sees, at London and York, each with twelve suffragans — a scheme realised only in part, as Canterbury, not London, became the southern metropolitan. Archbishops of York received the pallium from Rome and for centuries contested precedence with Canterbury, a rivalry settled only gradually: Canterbury's archbishop is "Primate of All England," York's "Primate of England." Notable holders include St William of York, Walter de Gray, William Melton, Cardinal Thomas Wolsey (who held York from 1514 while Lord Chancellor), and John Tillotson.

Role today

The archbishop leads the northern province, ordains bishops, and shares national responsibilities with Canterbury, including roles in coronations — the Archbishop of York traditionally anoints and crowns the queen consort. The archbishop's official residence is Bishopthorpe Palace, just outside York, and the cathedral is York Minster, one of the largest Gothic cathedrals in Northern Europe. From 2020 to 2025 the office was held by Stephen Cottrell; following his announced translation/retirement, the see passed through a vacancy process in 2025.
