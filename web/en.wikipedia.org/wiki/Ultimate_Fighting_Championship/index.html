Ultimate Fighting Championship

The Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) is an American mixed martial arts (MMA) promotion company based in Las Vegas, Nevada. It is the largest MMA promotion in the world, featuring many of the top-ranked fighters in the sport, and produces events worldwide that showcase twelve weight divisions (eight men's and four women's) governed by the Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts. The UFC is owned and operated by TKO Group Holdings, a subsidiary of Endeavor Group Holdings.

History

The first UFC event was held on 12 November 1993 at the McNichols Sports Arena in Denver, Colorado, as an eight-man single-elimination tournament intended to identify the most effective martial art in a contest with minimal rules. Royce Gracie, representing Brazilian jiu-jitsu, won the tournament, and the sport grew despite political opposition, notably from Senator John McCain, who campaigned against it as "human cockfighting".

In 2001 the Fertitta brothers' Zuffa LLC purchased the UFC for $2 million and installed Dana White as president. The adoption of the Unified Rules, state athletic commission sanctioning, and the success of the reality series The Ultimate Fighter (2005) drove mainstream growth. A landmark broadcast deal with Fox followed in 2011, and in 2016 Zuffa sold the company to Endeavor (then WME-IMG) for $4.025 billion, then the largest sale in sports history. In 2023 the UFC and WWE merged under TKO Group Holdings.

Rules and competition

Bouts take place in an eight-sided enclosure known as the Octagon. Championship fights last five five-minute rounds; other bouts typically last three rounds. Fighters can win by knockout, submission, judges' decision, or stoppage. The UFC maintains weight classes from strawweight to heavyweight and publishes pound-for-pound rankings. Notable champions have included Anderson Silva, Georges St-Pierre, Jon Jones, Ronda Rousey, Amanda Nunes, and Conor McGregor.
