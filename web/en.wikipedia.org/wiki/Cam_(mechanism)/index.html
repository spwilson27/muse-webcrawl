Cam (mechanism)

A cam is a rotating or sliding piece in a mechanical linkage used especially in transforming rotary motion into linear motion, or vice versa. It is often a part of a rotating wheel (the eccentric cam) or shaft (the cylinder cam) that strikes a lever at one or more points on its circular path. The cam can be a simple tooth, as is used to deliver pulses of power to a steam hammer, for example, or an eccentric disc or other shape that produces a smooth reciprocating motion in a follower.

Types

Common types include plate or disc cams, in which the follower moves in a plane perpendicular to the camshaft axis; cylindrical or barrel cams, in which the follower follows a groove cut into a cylinder; face cams; wedge cams; and conjugate or dual cams. Followers may be knife-edge, roller, or flat-faced, and may be held in contact by gravity, springs, or a positive-drive groove.

Applications

Cams are best known for their role in internal combustion engines, where a camshaft controls the opening and closing of intake and exhaust valves via cam lobes, tappets, pushrods and rocker arms. They are also used in automata, sewing machines, machine tools, printing presses, locks, and mechanically programmed controllers.

Design

The displacement diagram plots follower motion against cam rotation angle; profiles are chosen to limit acceleration and jerk, using motions such as simple harmonic, cycloidal, or modified trapezoidal curves.
