Widow

A widow is a woman whose spouse has died and who has not remarried; the male equivalent is a widower. The state is widowhood. Historically the term also carried legal and social meanings concerning property, mourning customs and remarriage.

Demographics and health

Because women typically live longer and often marry older men, widows greatly outnumber widowers. Bereavement is associated with elevated mortality and health risks for the surviving spouse — the "widowhood effect" — especially shortly after the loss.

Customs and law

Cultures prescribe mourning dress and periods, historically including black clothing in Europe. Practices such as sati in India and restrictions on inheritance have been subjects of reform; in many legal systems widows historically gained dower or inheritance rights. Levirate marriage, in which a widow marries her husband's brother, appears in several traditions.

Economic issues

Widows in many countries face poverty risks from lost income and discriminatory property rules; survivor pensions and social security benefits are common policy responses.
