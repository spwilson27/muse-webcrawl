Mojmir II (Latin: Moymirus, Czech: Mojmír II.; born after 871 – died after 901) was a member of the House of Mojmír and the last known ruler of Great Moravia from 894 to at least 901, and possibly until the state's destruction by the Hungarians around 906. He succeeded his father, Svatopluk I, whose death split Moravia between Mojmir and his brother Svatopluk II; their civil war, combined with East Frankish intervention and the arrival of the Magyars in the Carpathian Basin, fatally weakened the realm.

Reign

Before Svatopluk I's death in 894, Mojmir had governed a portion of the realm; contemporaries mention his participation in negotiations with King Arnulf of East Francia, with whom Svatopluk made peace in 894, surrendering claims over Pannonia. Soon after his accession, Mojmir II quarrelled with his brother Svatopluk II, who held the Principality of Nitra; the fraternal war (898–899) drew in Arnulf's Bavarian troops, who devastated Moravia. Moravian church affairs also mark the reign: in 899/900 Mojmir successfully petitioned Pope John IX to restore an independent Moravian archbishopric with three suffragan bishops, rebuffing the claims of the Bavarian church of Passau and Salzburg — a mission of papal legates arrived in Moravia in 900.

Downfall

The last secure references to Mojmir II date to 901, when Moravians and Bavarians again negotiated peace at Regensburg; a charter of 901 also records his forces fighting the Hungarians. Thereafter the sources fall silent. Most historians assume Great Moravia was destroyed in the Hungarian conquest culminating around 906/907 (the Battle of Pressburg, 907, in which Bavarian forces were annihilated, is sometimes connected with the Moravian collapse), and that Mojmir II perished in these events, though no contemporary source records his death or the exact date of Moravia's fall.
