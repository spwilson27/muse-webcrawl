Overview

Aden is a port city in Yemen, located by the eastern approach to the Red Sea (the Gulf of Aden), some 170 km east of the Bab el-Mandeb strait. The city is built around a natural harbour formed in the crater of an extinct volcano; the peninsula is dominated by Mount Shamsan. Aden has been a trading port since antiquity, exporting frankincense and myrrh.

History

Aden was a British colony and protectorate from 1839 to 1967, valued as a coaling station on the route to India; the Steamer Point and Crater districts date from this era. It was the capital of South Yemen (the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen) until unification in 1990. Since 2015 the city has been caught in the Yemeni Civil War, serving at times as the seat of the internationally recognised government and later of the Southern Transitional Council.

Geography

The city includes the historic Crater district, Maalla, Tawahi (Steamer Point) and the Little Aden peninsula across the harbour, where an oil refinery was built in the 1950s.
