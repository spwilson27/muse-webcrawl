The Funj Sultanate, also known as the Sultanate of Sennar or the Blue Sultanate, was a monarchy in what is now Sudan, northwestern Eritrea and western Ethiopia, founded in 1504 by the Funj people under Amara Dunqas. It was the dominant state of the middle Nile valley for over three centuries and played a central role in the Islamisation and Arabisation of Sudan.

According to tradition, Amara Dunqas defeated the last ruler of the Christian kingdom of Alodia and established his capital at Sennar on the Blue Nile. The Funj state expanded northward to the Third Cataract of the Nile, and for a time contended with the Ottoman Empire, which occupied northern Nubia, and with Ethiopia to the east.

Government and society

The sultanate was ruled by a sultan (mek) of the Unsab dynasty, surrounded by a court that combined Funj, Arab and Islamic institutions. Islam became the state religion, and the Funj sultans patronised Sufi holy men and Islamic learning, though local customs remained strong. The economy rested on agriculture along the Nile, rain-fed farming, pastoralism, and trade — including a profitable trade in gold, ivory and slaves with Egypt and the Red Sea ports.

Decline and fall

From the mid-18th century, real power passed to the Hamaj regents (viziers), who ruled through puppet sultans from the dynasty. Weakened by civil war and succession disputes, the last sultan, Badi VI, submitted to the invading forces of Muhammad Ali's Egypt in 1821, ending the sultanate and beginning the period of Egyptian rule known as the Turkiyya.
