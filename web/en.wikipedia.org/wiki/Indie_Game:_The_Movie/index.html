Indie Game: The Movie

Indie Game: The Movie

Released2012 (Sundance premiere January 20, 2012)

CountryCanada

Indie Game: The Movie is a 2012 documentary film by Canadian filmmakers James Swirsky and Lisanne Pajot. The film follows independent video game developers Edmund McMillen and Tommy Refenes during the development and troubled Xbox Live Arcade launch of Super Meat Boy, Phil Fish during the long development of Fez, and Jonathan Blow reflecting on the success of Braid.

Production

The film was financed through two successful Kickstarter crowdfunding campaigns. Swirsky and Pajot shot more than 300 hours of footage, following their subjects through the emotional highs and lows of finishing and releasing games as tiny teams working outside the major studio system. The film premiered at the 2012 Sundance Film Festival, where it won the World Cinema Documentary Editing Award, and was distributed digitally through platforms including Steam and iTunes — a novelty at the time.

Reception

The film was widely praised for capturing the personal sacrifice behind independent game development, holding a high approval rating on review aggregators, though some critics felt it romanticised its subjects. It is frequently credited with bringing wider public attention to indie games and to the then-fragile business of downloadable console titles.
