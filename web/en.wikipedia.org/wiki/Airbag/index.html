Airbag

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An airbag is a vehicle occupant-restraint system using a flexible bag designed to inflate extremely rapidly during a collision and then quickly deflate. It consists of an airbag cushion, a flexible fabric bag, an inflation module, and impact sensors. The purpose of an airbag is to provide a soft cushioning and restraint for the occupants of a vehicle during a crash, reducing injuries from contact with the steering wheel, dashboard, windows, and other interior structures. Airbags are supplemental restraints and are designed to work together with seat belts, not replace them.

Operation

Crash sensors — typically accelerometers — detect a rapid deceleration characteristic of a collision and signal the control unit. If deployment criteria are met, an igniter triggers a rapid chemical reaction in the inflator, historically using sodium azide and in modern designs other propellants or stored-gas hybrid systems, generating gas (mainly nitrogen) that fills the nylon bag in 20–40 milliseconds. Vents in the bag then allow it to deflate in a controlled manner as the occupant sinks into it, spreading deceleration forces over a longer time and a larger area of the body.

Types

  • Frontal airbags — mounted in the steering wheel and dashboard, mandatory in many jurisdictions since the 1990s.
  • Side airbags — seat- or door-mounted bags protecting the torso, and curtain airbags that drop from the roof rail to protect the head in side impacts and rollovers.
  • Knee airbags, seat-cushion airbags, rear airbags, and pedestrian-protection external airbags on some vehicles.
  • Motorcycle airbags and airbag vests and jackets worn by riders.

History

The basic concept dates to patents of the 1950s, including work by Walter Linderer in Germany and John Hetrick in the United States. General Motors offered airbags on some models in the mid-1970s, but widespread adoption came in the late 1980s and 1990s, driven by regulation — in the United States, dual frontal airbags became mandatory on new cars in 1998. The Takata airbag recalls, beginning in 2013, were the largest automotive recalls in history, after ammonium-nitrate inflators were found to rupture and spray metal fragments.

Safety

Frontal airbags are estimated to reduce driver fatalities in frontal crashes by roughly a third when used with seat belts. However, the force of deployment can injure occupants who sit too close to the module; children in rear-facing seats in front of an active airbag are at particular risk, which is why children are advised to ride in the rear seat.
