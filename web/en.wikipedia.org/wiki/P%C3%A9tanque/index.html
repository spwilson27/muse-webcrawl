Pétanque

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pétanque (French pronunciation: [petɑ̃k]; Occitan: petanca) is a sport that falls into the category of boules sports (along with raffa, bocce, boule lyonnaise, lawn bowls and crown green bowling). Players throw hollow metal balls (boules) as close as possible to a small wooden target ball called the cochonnet (literally "piglet") while standing inside a circle with both feet on the ground.

History

The modern game was invented in 1907 in La Ciotat, Provence, France. Unlike the older game of jeu provençal, in which players run before throwing, pétanque was adapted for a player suffering from rheumatism who could not run: the length of the pitch was reduced and the throw is made from a stationary position — pieds tanqués, "feet anchored", giving the game its name.

Play

Pétanque is played in singles, doubles or triples. Players score points for each of their boules closer to the cochonnet than the opponent's best boule; the first team to reach 13 points wins. Skilled throwing techniques include pointing (pointer) and shooting (tirer) to displace opponents' boules. The sport is governed internationally by the FIPJP and is played in dozens of countries.
