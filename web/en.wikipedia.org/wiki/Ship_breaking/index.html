Ship breaking

Ship breaking (also known as ship demolition, ship dismantling, or ship recycling) is a type of ship disposal involving the breaking up of ships either as a source of parts, which may be sold for re-use, or for the extraction of raw materials, chiefly scrap steel. Modern ships have a lifespan of 25 to 30 years before corrosion, metal fatigue and a lack of parts render them uneconomical to operate, at which point they are sold for dismantling.

Industry

The great majority of the world's ship breaking takes place in South Asia, principally at Alang in India, Chittagong in Bangladesh, and Gadani in Pakistan, where ships are driven onto tidal beaches at high tide ("beaching") and dismantled largely by hand as the tide recedes. China and Turkey also host substantial ship recycling industries using piers and dry docks. The economics of the industry are driven by the price of scrap steel and by much lower labour and regulatory costs than in developed countries.

Environmental and safety concerns

Ship breaking on open beaches has been widely criticised for worker deaths and injuries and for pollution. Older ships contain hazardous materials including asbestos, polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs), lead, heavy metals, and residual oils, which can be released into coastal environments during dismantling. Workers often labour with minimal protective equipment, and falls, fires, explosions and falling steel plates are common causes of accidents.

Regulation

The Basel Convention restricts the transboundary movement of hazardous waste, including end-of-life ships. The Hong Kong International Convention for the Safe and Environmentally Sound Recycling of Ships, adopted by the International Maritime Organization in 2009, sets global standards for ship recycling, including inventories of hazardous materials and authorised recycling facilities; it entered into force in June 2025. The European Union's Ship Recycling Regulation requires EU-flagged ships to be recycled only in approved facilities meeting specified standards.
