Elan Valley Reservoirs

The Elan Valley Reservoirs (Welsh: Cronfeydd Cwm Elan) are a chain of man-made lakes and dams in the Elan Valley in Mid Wales, built in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries to supply drinking water to the city of Birmingham, England. The reservoirs are Craig Goch, Pen y Garreg, Garreg Ddu, and Caban Coch, together with the later Claerwen reservoir in the neighbouring Claerwen valley.

Construction

Parliament authorised the scheme in 1892, and the Birmingham Corporation Water Department, under engineer James Mansergh, dammed the Rivers Elan and Claerwen in the Cambrian Mountains. The first four dams were completed in 1904, when King Edward VII opened the scheme. Water travels some 118 kilometres (73 miles) entirely by gravity along the Elan aqueduct to Frankley Reservoir near Birmingham — a gradient so gentle the journey takes about a day and a half. The Claerwen dam, much larger, was opened by Queen Elizabeth II in 1952.

The valley today

The catchment, around 45,000 acres, is managed for water quality and conservation and is rich in wildlife, including red kites. The Foel Tower and visitor centre interpret the scheme's history, which included the flooding of farms, a church, and a manor house associated with the poet Shelley. During the Second World War, the nearby Nant-y-Gro dam was used by Barnes Wallis in early experiments for the bouncing bomb later used by the Dambusters.
