Galega officinalis


Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Fabales
Family: Fabaceae
Genus: Galega
Species: G. officinalis

Galega officinalis, commonly known as galega, goat's rue, French lilac, Italian fitch, or professor-weed, is a herbaceous plant in the legume family Fabaceae. It is native to the Middle East and Europe and has been widely introduced elsewhere as a forage crop and ornamental.

Description

It is a vigorous perennial growing to around 1–1.5 m tall, with pinnate leaves and racemes of white, lilac, or pink pea-like flowers produced in summer, followed by seed pods. The plant fixes nitrogen and thrives in moist, fertile soils, where it can become invasive.

Uses and toxicity

Galega was historically used in traditional medicine for a wide range of complaints, including as a galactagogue to promote lactation — the source of the genus name, from Greek gala (milk). It is toxic to livestock in quantity, containing the alkaloid galegine, which can cause respiratory distress and death in grazing animals.

Medical significance

Galegine, a guanidine derivative isolated from the plant, was found to lower blood glucose. Although galegine itself proved too toxic for clinical use, its study led to the development of the biguanide class of antidiabetic drugs, most notably metformin, which became a first-line treatment for type 2 diabetes.
