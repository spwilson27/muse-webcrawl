Kampfgruppe Peiper was an armoured battle group of the 1st SS Panzer Division Leibstandarte SS Adolf Hitler, commanded by SS-Obersturmbannführer Joachim Peiper. It formed the spearhead of the Sixth Panzer Army's attack in the northern sector of the Battle of the Bulge in December 1944, tasked with racing through the Ardennes to seize crossings over the Meuse River and advance on Antwerp. The unit is infamous for its responsibility for war crimes, most notably the Malmedy massacre.

The offensive

Peiper's group attacked on 16 December 1944, following narrow, poor roads assigned to it through the Losheim Gap. Frustrated by delays and traffic jams, the column advanced through Honsfeld and Büllingen, where it captured American fuel stocks. On 17 December, near the Baugnez crossroads outside Malmedy, Peiper's troops murdered 84 American prisoners of war — the Malmedy massacre — one of a series of killings of prisoners and Belgian civilians along the group's route, totalling several hundred victims.

Destruction

The advance stalled at Stavelot, Trois-Ponts and La Gleize: American engineers blew key bridges, fuel ran out, and US counter-attacks cut the group's supply lines. By 23–24 December, with his remaining vehicles immobilised at La Gleize and surrounded, Peiper abandoned his equipment and led roughly 800 men out on foot through the woods back to German lines.

Aftermath

After the war Peiper and dozens of his men were tried by a US military tribunal at Dachau in 1946 for the Malmedy massacre; Peiper was sentenced to death, though the sentence was commuted, and he was released in 1956. The fairness of the trial became a subject of controversy in the United States. Peiper was killed in France in 1976 when his house was set on fire by unknown attackers.
