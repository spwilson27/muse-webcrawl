Quattro (four wheel drive system)

quattro
Manufacturer: Audi AG
Introduced: 1980, Geneva Motor Show
Layout: Front-engine, all-wheel drive

Quattro is the name of the four-wheel-drive system developed by Audi and fitted to its cars since 1980. The word is Italian for "four", and Audi writes it in lower case — quattro — as a registered trademark. The system made its debut in the Audi Quattro coupé, whose success in rallying in the early 1980s established all-wheel drive as a performance technology for road cars, not merely a rough-terrain feature.

Motorsport impact

Audi's works team dominated the World Rally Championship in the Group B era. Michele Mouton became the first woman to win a world championship rally at Sanremo in 1981, and Audi took manufacturers' championships in 1982 and 1984. Success at the Pikes Peak hill climb and later in touring car racing — which led several series to ban all-wheel drive — reinforced the system's reputation.

How it works

In the classic longitudinal layout, the engine sits ahead of the front axle and drives all four wheels through a centre differential. Early cars used vacuum-operated manual differential locks. From the late 1980s a Torsen centre differential provided automatic, purely mechanical torque distribution, biased 50:50 in normal running. Later systems added electronic brake-based differential locking, a crown-gear centre differential, and an optional sport differential able to overdrive the outside rear wheel in corners.

Other applications

Volkswagen Group brands sharing Audi platforms have used closely related systems under other names, such as 4motion. Smaller transverse-engined models use an electronically controlled multi-plate clutch coupling (Haldex-based) that is primarily front-wheel drive until slip is detected.
