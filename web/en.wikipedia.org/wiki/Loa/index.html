Loa

Loa (also spelled lwa) are the spirits of Haitian Vodou and Louisiana Voodoo. They are also referred to as "mystères" and "invisibles". They act as intermediaries between Bondye — the supreme creator god, who is distant from the world — and humanity. In ritual, the loa are served (not worshipped as gods) and are believed to mount, or possess, devotees known as "horses" (chwal), through whom they speak and act.

Nanchons (nations)

The loa are grouped into families or nations (nanchon), each with characteristic temperaments, rites, drum rhythms and offerings:

Major loa

  • Papa Legba — guardian of the crossroads and gatekeeper between worlds; every ceremony begins by saluting him.
  • Damballa — the ancient serpent loa, father figure of the pantheon.
  • Ogou (Ogoun) — warrior loa of iron, fire and politics.
  • Erzulie Freda (Rada) — loa of love, beauty and luxury; her fierce counterpart is Ezili Dantor (Petro).
  • Agwé — loa of the sea, served with offerings floated on rafts.
  • Baron Samedi — master of the cemetery and the dead, depicted in top hat and tails.
  • Azaka — loa of agriculture and the peasantry.

Ritual service

Ceremonies are led by a priest (oungan) or priestess (manbo) in a temple (ounfò). Symbols called vèvè are drawn on the ground in flour or ash to summon the loa; each loa has distinctive songs, preferred colours, foods, drinks and Catholic saint counterparts with whom they are syncretised — for example, Legba with Saint Peter or Saint Lazarus, and Damballa with Saint Patrick.
