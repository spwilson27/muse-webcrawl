Soga no Umako (蘇我 馬子, c. 551 – June 19, 626) was the son of Soga no Iname and a member of the powerful Soga clan of Japan. Umako conducted political reforms with Prince Shōtoku during the rules of Emperor Bidatsu and Empress Suiko, and established the Soga clan's strong grip over the imperial court.

Career

Umako succeeded his father as Ōomi and continued the Soga policy of promoting Buddhism. In 587, after the deaths of Emperor Yōmei, a succession dispute turned to battle; Umako's forces defeated Mononobe no Moriya at the Battle of Shigisan, eliminating the leading anti-Buddhist clan. He then placed Emperor Sushun on the throne, but when Sushun chafed at Soga dominance, Umako arranged his assassination in 592 by the assassin Yamato no Aya no Ataikoma — an act recorded as shocking even by the standards of the time.

Partnership with Prince Shōtoku

Umako installed his niece as Empress Suiko and governed with her regent, Prince Shōtoku (Umayado). Together they are credited with the Twelve Level Cap and Rank System (603), the Seventeen-article constitution (604), renewed embassies to Sui China, and the compilation of early national histories. Umako built Asuka-dera, the first full-scale Buddhist temple in Japan, and his Ishibutai Kofun — one of the largest stone tombs of the period — is traditionally identified as his burial place.
