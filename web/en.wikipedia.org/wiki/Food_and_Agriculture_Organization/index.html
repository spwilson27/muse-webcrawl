Food and Agriculture Organization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) is a specialized agency of the United Nations that leads international efforts to defeat hunger and improve nutrition and food security. Founded on 16 October 1945 in Quebec City (the date is now World Food Day) and headquartered in Rome since 1951, its Latin motto, fiat panis, means "let there be bread".

Work

The FAO is the system's knowledge centre for food and agriculture: it maintains the standard statistics (FAOSTAT) and assessments (the annual State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World, with sister agencies), sets standards (the Codex Alimentarius jointly with WHO governs food safety and trade), runs technical cooperation and emergencies (desert-locust surveillance, avian influenza, seed and tool distributions), and convenes standard-setting and treaty processes (plant genetic resources, fisheries agreements, the Rotterdam and pesticide codes). Alongside the World Food Programme (operations) and IFAD (financing), it forms the Rome-based food trio — with the chronic coordination questions of the UN system, sharpened by the 2007–08 food-price crisis and the 2020s' overlapping shocks (war in Ukraine's grain markets, climate extremes), which pushed reported hunger numbers up after decades of decline and put the FAO's mandate back at the top of the security agenda.
