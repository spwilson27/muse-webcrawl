Fur farming

Fur farming is the practice of breeding or raising certain types of animals for their fur. Most of the world's farm-produced fur is produced by European farmers, principally of mink and fox. Other species farmed include the chinchilla, raccoon dog, and rabbit. Fur farming is banned in several countries, including the United Kingdom, Austria, and the Netherlands, largely on animal welfare and ethical grounds.

The industry grew in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as wild fur stocks declined. Critics cite confinement conditions, killing methods, and disease risks — mink farms were notably affected by SARS-CoV-2 outbreaks in 2020, leading Denmark to cull its entire farmed mink herd. Supporters argue farming is regulated and provides rural livelihoods.
