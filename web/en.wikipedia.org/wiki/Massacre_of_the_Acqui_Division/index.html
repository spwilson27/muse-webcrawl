The Massacre of the Acqui Division, also known as the Cephalonia massacre, was a mass execution of Italian soldiers by German forces on the Greek island of Cephalonia in September 1943, following the announcement of the Italian armistice with the Allies. The 33rd Infantry Division "Acqui", under General Antonio Gandin, refused a German ultimatum to surrender its weapons and chose to resist; after about a week of fighting, the division was overwhelmed, and the Germans shot thousands of its officers and men in one of the largest massacres of prisoners of war of the Second World War.

Background

Italy had occupied Cephalonia and other Ionian islands since 1941. When the armistice between Italy and the Allies was announced on 8 September 1943, German commanders moved to disarm Italian forces throughout the Mediterranean. On Cephalonia, the German commander, Lieutenant-Colonel Hans Barge, demanded the Acqui Division hand over its heavy weapons. Gandin consulted his men — famously, an informal referendum among the troops favoured resistance — and refused.

Battle and massacre

Fighting began on 13 September. Reinforced by Stuka dive-bombers and the 1st Mountain Division under General Hubert Lanz, German forces crushed the Italian defence by 22 September; Gandin surrendered to save his remaining men. Despite the surrender, German troops, acting on orders from Hitler that no prisoners be taken, carried out systematic executions of officers — Gandin among them — and thousands of soldiers over the following days. Estimates of those shot range from about 1,200 to over 5,000; in addition, more than 3,000 Italian prisoners drowned when the ships transporting them to the mainland struck mines or were sunk.

Aftermath and memory

General Lanz was convicted for the Cephalonia killings at the Hostages Trial in Nuremberg and sentenced to 12 years, of which he served about three — an outcome widely criticized in Italy and Greece. The massacre was largely forgotten internationally until it became the subject of Louis de Bernières' 1994 novel Captain Corelli's Mandolin and its 2001 film adaptation. In 2001, German President Johannes Rau formally apologized on Cephalonia. Italy commemorates the Acqui Division as a symbol of resistance to Nazism.
