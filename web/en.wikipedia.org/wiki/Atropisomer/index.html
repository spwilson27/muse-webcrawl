Atropisomer

Atropisomers are stereoisomers arising from hindered rotation about a single bond, where steric strain or other barriers to rotation are high enough that the conformers can be isolated as distinct species. The term derives from the Greek a- (not) + tropos (turn).

Occurrence

Atropisomerism is most commonly observed in biaryl compounds such as substituted biphenyls, where bulky ortho substituents prevent free rotation about the aryl–aryl bond. The classic example is 6,6′-dinitro-2,2′-diphenic acid, resolved in 1922. By the empirical Ōki criterion, atropisomers have a rotational half-life of at least 1000 seconds at a given temperature, corresponding to a barrier of roughly 93 kJ/mol at 300 K.

Importance

Achiral-looking pharmaceuticals and natural products may be atropisomeric: vancomycin, knipholone and many kinase inhibitors display axial chirality. Ligands such as BINAP and BINOL owe their usefulness in asymmetric catalysis to stable atropisomerism.

Classification

LaPlante's classification groups pharmaceutical atropisomers into three classes by rotational barrier: class 1 (rapidly interconverting), class 2 (intermediate) and class 3 (configurationally stable).
