Fritz the Cat (film)

Fritz the Cat is a 1972 American adult animated black comedy film written and directed by Ralph Bakshi, in his directorial debut. Based on the comic strip of the same name by Robert Crumb, it follows a hedonistic anthropomorphic cat in a satirical tour of 1960s American college life, race relations, and radical politics. It was the first animated feature to receive an X rating in the United States and became the most successful independent animated film of its era.

Plot

Fritz, a cynical, skirt-chasing New York University student, drops out and drifts through a series of episodes: seducing co-eds, inciting a riot during a Harlem excursion, fleeing the police, and falling in with revolutionaries whose bombing plot leaves him hospitalized. In the final scene, recovering in hospital, he returns cheerfully to his old pursuits. The film opens with construction workers discussing the era over lunch and ends by mocking the counterculture it depicts.

Production and reception

Bakshi, an animator at Terrytoons, sought to make animation for adults. Producer Steve Krantz acquired the rights from Crumb, who later disowned the film and killed the character in the comics. Made on a shoestring budget with recorded real conversations used as dialogue in places, the film premiered in 1972 to controversy and commercial success, grossing around $90 million worldwide. Reviews were mixed but it is now regarded as a landmark of adult animation; a sequel, The Nine Lives of Fritz the Cat (1974), was made without Bakshi or Crumb.
