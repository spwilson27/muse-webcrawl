Palearctic realm

The Palearctic (also spelled Palaearctic) is the largest of Earth's eight biogeographic realms. It stretches across all of Europe, Asia north of the Himalayas, North Africa, and parts of the Arabian Peninsula. The realm covers about 54.1 million square kilometres (20.9 million square miles) and includes terrestrial ecoregions from arctic tundra to Mediterranean scrub and Central Asian deserts.

Boundaries

The Palearctic is bounded by the Arctic Ocean to the north, the Atlantic and Pacific oceans to the west and east, and by the Indomalayan realm to the south, with the boundary running along the Himalaya foothills and through southern China. In Africa, the Saharan and Arabian deserts form a transition to the Afrotropical realm. Together with the Nearctic realm of North America, the Palearctic is sometimes combined into the Holarctic realm, reflecting the many taxa shared across Beringia during the Pleistocene.

Biomes and fauna

Major biomes include tundra, boreal forest (taiga), temperate coniferous and broadleaf forests, Mediterranean forests and scrub, steppes, and deserts. Characteristic fauna include reindeer, brown bears, wolves, wolverines, saiga antelope, Siberian tigers in the far east, and many bird families such as accentors and buntings. The Palearctic shares many families with the Nearctic (such as deer, beavers, and loons), a legacy of faunal exchange across the Bering land bridge, but has its own endemic groups as well.

Ecoregions

The World Wildlife Fund divides the Palearctic into hundreds of terrestrial ecoregions, among them the Scandinavian and Russian taiga, the Western Siberian broadleaf and mixed forests, the Central Asian steppes, and the Mediterranean Basin ecoregions, the last being one of the world's biodiversity hotspots.
