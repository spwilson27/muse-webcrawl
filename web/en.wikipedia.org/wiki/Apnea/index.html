Apnea
Also spelledApnoea (British English)
SpecialtyRespiratory medicine, sleep medicine
DefinitionTemporary cessation of breathing

Apnea (also spelled apnoea) is the temporary cessation of breathing. During an apneic episode there is no movement of the respiratory muscles and lung volume initially remains unchanged; depending on the cause and duration, blood oxygen falls (hypoxemia) and carbon dioxide rises, eventually triggering arousal or a reflex resumption of breathing.

Types and contexts

Apnea is classified by mechanism as obstructive (the airway collapses despite continued respiratory effort, as in obstructive sleep apnea), central (the brainstem temporarily fails to signal breathing, as in Cheyne–Stokes respiration or opioid toxicity), or mixed. Apnea of prematurity is common in preterm infants. Voluntary apnea is the basis of breath-hold diving and the sport of freediving, where trained athletes can hold their breath for many minutes.

Consequences and treatment

Brief apneas are harmless, but repeated episodes in sleep fragment rest and are associated with daytime sleepiness, hypertension and cardiovascular disease. Diagnosis relies on polysomnography; treatment of obstructive sleep apnea centres on continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), weight loss and oral appliances, while central apnea management addresses the underlying cause.
