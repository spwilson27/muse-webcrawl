Humanoid robot

A humanoid robot is a robot resembling the human body in shape. The design may be for functional purposes, such as interacting with human tools and environments, for experimental purposes, such as the study of bipedal locomotion, or for other purposes.

History

Early humanoid machines included Leonardo da Vinci's mechanical knight (c. 1495) and the Japanese karakuri automata. Modern research milestones include Honda's ASIMO (2000), which could walk, climb stairs, and recognize gestures; Boston Dynamics' Atlas, known for dynamic acrobatic movement; and SoftBank's Pepper, designed for social interaction.

Locomotion and control

Bipedal walking is controlled using concepts such as the zero moment point to maintain balance. Humanoid robots use sensors including cameras, gyroscopes, force sensors, and increasingly lidar, together with actuators and AI-based planning to perceive and act in human environments.
