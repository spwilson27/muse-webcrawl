Sir Demis Hassabis (born 27 July 1976) is a British artificial intelligence researcher, neuroscientist and entrepreneur. He is the co-founder and chief executive officer of Google DeepMind, the AI company responsible for AlphaGo and AlphaFold. In 2024 he shared one half of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with John Jumper for their work on protein structure prediction, the other half going to David Baker.

Early life and games career

Hassabis was born in London to a Greek Cypriot father and a Chinese Singaporean mother. A chess prodigy, he reached master standard as a child and captained England's junior chess teams. After completing his A-levels early, he joined Bullfrog Productions and was lead programmer and co-designer of the hit simulation game Theme Park (1994) at the age of 17. He studied computer science at Queens' College, Cambridge, graduating with a double first, and in 1998 founded the video game developer Elixir Studios, which produced Republic: The Revolution and Evil Genius. He also won the multi-discipline Mind Sports Olympiad pentamind world championship a record five times.

Neuroscience and DeepMind

After Elixir, Hassabis completed a PhD in cognitive neuroscience at University College London, publishing influential work on the hippocampus, memory and imagination, including a 2007 study named among Science magazine's breakthroughs of the year. In 2010 he co-founded DeepMind Technologies with Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman, with the mission of "solving intelligence". Google acquired the company in 2014 for a reported sum of over £400 million.

DeepMind's systems achieved a series of landmarks: deep reinforcement learning agents that mastered Atari games from raw pixels (2013–2015); AlphaGo, which defeated world champion Lee Sedol at Go in 2016; AlphaZero; and AlphaFold 2, which in 2020 effectively solved the protein folding problem at the CASP14 assessment, leading to a public database of over 200 million predicted protein structures. Following the 2023 merger of DeepMind with Google Brain, Hassabis became CEO of the combined Google DeepMind, overseeing the Gemini family of large language models. He also co-founded Isomorphic Labs, an Alphabet company applying AI to drug discovery.

Honours

Hassabis was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 2018, appointed CBE in 2017, and knighted in 2024 for services to artificial intelligence. He received the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry jointly with John Jumper "for protein structure prediction". He has repeatedly advocated for careful governance of increasingly powerful AI systems while arguing that AI, properly directed, can accelerate scientific discovery.
