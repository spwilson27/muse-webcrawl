FUS (gene)

FUS (fused in sarcoma) is a human gene encoding the RNA-binding protein FUS, a member of the FET family of proteins along with EWSR1 and TAF15. The gene is located on chromosome 16p11.2.

Function

FUS is a predominantly nuclear protein involved in transcription regulation, RNA splicing, mRNA transport, and the DNA damage response. It contains an N-terminal low-complexity (prion-like) domain, an RNA-recognition motif, and zinc finger and RGG domains. Through its low-complexity domain it participates in liquid–liquid phase separation that forms ribonucleoprotein granules.

Cancer

The gene was first identified through chromosomal translocations in myxoid liposarcoma, where FUS fuses with the transcription factor DDIT3 (CHOP), producing the FUS–DDIT3 fusion oncoprotein. FUS rearrangements with CREB3L-family genes also occur in other sarcomas.

Neurological disease

Missense and other mutations in FUS cause a form of familial amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS20), often with early onset and rapid progression. Cytoplasmic mislocalisation and aggregation of mutant FUS in motor neurons is a hallmark; FUS-positive inclusions also characterise a subset of frontotemporal lobar degeneration (FTLD-FUS). Many disease mutations cluster in the C-terminal nuclear localisation signal.
