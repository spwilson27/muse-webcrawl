Trial film

A trial film, also known as a courtroom drama, is a film genre (a subgenre of the legal drama) centred on a court trial — typically a criminal trial — and the lawyers, witnesses, defendants, and jurors involved in it.

Characteristics

Trial films build suspense through testimony, cross-examination, revelations of evidence, and closing arguments, culminating in a verdict. The courtroom setting lets the films explore themes of justice, prejudice, and truth.

Notable examples

  • 12 Angry Men (1957) – jury deliberations after a murder trial
  • To Kill a Mockingbird (1962)
  • Anatomy of a Murder (1959)
  • A Few Good Men (1992) – a military court-martial
  • Judgment at Nuremberg (1961) – the postwar judges' trial

See also legal drama. For a specific real trial depicted on screen, see Trial of George Zimmerman.
