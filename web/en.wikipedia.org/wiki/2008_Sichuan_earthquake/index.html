The 2008 Sichuan earthquake, also known as the Great Sichuan earthquake or the Wenchuan earthquake, occurred at 14:28 China Standard Time on 12 May 2008. Measuring 7.9 on the moment magnitude scale, it struck Wenchuan County in Sichuan province, about 80 km west-northwest of Chengdu, where it was strongly felt.

The earthquake killed more than 69,000 people by confirmed count, with over 87,000 dead or missing in total, and injured some 375,000. Whole towns such as Beichuan and Yingxiu were destroyed, thousands of schools collapsed, and millions were left homeless. It was the deadliest earthquake in China since the 1976 Tangshan earthquake.

Response

China mounted a massive rescue and relief operation, deploying more than 100,000 troops, and accepted international assistance; three days of national mourning were declared. The collapse of school buildings prompted public anger and investigations into construction standards. Reconstruction in the affected region was largely completed within a few years, and the ruins of Beichuan were preserved as a memorial.
