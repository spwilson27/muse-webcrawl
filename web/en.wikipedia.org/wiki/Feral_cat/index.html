Feral cat

A feral cat is an unowned domestic cat (Felis catus) that lives outdoors and has had little or no contact with humans, or whose behaviour has become wild after being lost or abandoned. Feral cats are distinguished from stray cats, which were formerly pets and may be socialised to people. They are found on every continent except Antarctica, often in colonies around food sources.

Ecology and impact

Feral and free-roaming cats are efficient predators of birds, mammals and reptiles. Studies have estimated that cats kill billions of wild animals annually in the United States alone, and on islands feral cats have contributed to the extinction of endemic species, making them one of the world's most problematic invasive species. They can also transmit diseases such as rabies and toxoplasmosis.

Management

Common management approaches include trap–neuter–return (TNR), in which cats are sterilised, vaccinated and released, and adoption of socialisable kittens. TNR is advocated by animal-welfare groups but its effectiveness at reducing populations is debated by wildlife biologists, who often favour removal in ecologically sensitive areas. Laws governing feral cats vary widely between jurisdictions.
