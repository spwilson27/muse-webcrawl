The Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders (Princess Louise's) was an infantry regiment of the British Army, formed in 1881 by the amalgamation of the 91st (Argyllshire Highlanders) Regiment of Foot and the 93rd (Sutherland Highlanders) Regiment of Foot under the Childers Reforms. In 2006 it became the 5th Battalion of the Royal Regiment of Scotland.

Heritage

The 93rd Highlanders won fame at the Battle of Balaclava in 1854, where their two-deep line repelled Russian cavalry — an episode immortalized as the "Thin Red Line." The regiment's home was Stirling Castle, and its soldiers wore the Government tartan with a distinctive red-and-white dicing on their headgear, combining the traditions of both predecessor regiments.

Service

Battalions of the regiment served in the Boer War, in both World Wars — including rearguard actions in France in 1940 and Malaya in 1941–42, and fighting in North Africa, Italy, and northwest Europe — and in the Korean War, where the 1st Battalion fought at the Imjin River period operations. In 1967, under Lieutenant Colonel Colin Mitchell ("Mad Mitch"), the battalion famously reoccupied the Crater district of Aden. The regiment also served in Northern Ireland, the Falklands follow-up, the Balkans, Iraq, and Afghanistan in its later battalion form.

Royal Regiment of Scotland

As part of Army restructuring, the Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders amalgamated with the other Scottish infantry regiments on 28 March 2006 to form the Royal Regiment of Scotland, continuing as its 5th Battalion. In 2012 the battalion was reduced to a single company, Balaklava Company, for ceremonial duties in Scotland.
