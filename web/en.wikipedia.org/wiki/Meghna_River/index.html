Meghna River

The Meghna River is one of the major rivers of Bangladesh, and one of the three rivers that form the Ganges Delta, the largest river delta in the world. It is formed inside Bangladesh by the joining of the Surma and Kushiyara rivers, and flows south to meet the Padma near Chandpur before emptying into the Bay of Bengal.

Meghna River

The Meghna in Bangladesh

Country: Bangladesh
Source: Surma–Kushiyara confluence
Mouth: Bay of Bengal
Length: ~930 km (with Surma)

Course

The Surma and Kushiyara are both distributaries of the Barak River, which rises in India. After their confluence near Bhairab Bazar, the combined river is known as the Meghna. It flows through Kishoreganj, Brahmanbaria and Comilla districts, gathering the Gumti and other tributaries, and near Chandpur it is joined by the Padma. Below this point the river is sometimes called the Lower Meghna; it is extremely wide — in places several kilometres across — and braided with shifting channels and islands (chars).

Importance

The Meghna is a vital transport artery: ferries and cargo boats link Dhaka with the southern districts, and the river's hilsa fishery is nationally important — the hilsa (ilish) is Bangladesh's national fish, and the Meghna estuary is one of its main breeding grounds. The river also deposits the fertile silt that sustains the delta's agriculture, but its floods and bank erosion regularly displace riverside communities.
