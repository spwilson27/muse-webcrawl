Musketeers of the Guard

The Musketeers of the Guard (French: Mousquetaires de la garde) were a company of the military branch of the Maison du Roi, the royal household of the French king. Founded in 1622 by Louis XIII, they served as the king's mounted bodyguard and as an elite fighting unit and school for young gentlemen aspiring to become officers. They are world-famous as the corps of the heroes of Alexandre Dumas's 1844 novel The Three Musketeers.

History

Louis XIII created the company in 1622 from his light cavalry (carabins), arming them with muskets. They fought at the sieges of La Rochelle (1627–28), in the campaigns of the Franco-Spanish War, and across the battlefields of Louis XIV, including at Maastricht (1673), where the real d'Artagnan — Charles de Batz de Castelmore — was killed. Richelieu, jealous of the king's musketeers, obtained his own company in 1626; after the cardinal's death, Mazarin inherited them, and in 1660 they were merged into the king's corps as a second company. The two companies were distinguished by the colour of their horses: the Grey Musketeers and the Black Musketeers.

Membership was restricted to gentlemen who could equip themselves; the musketeers wore a blue tabard or cassock emblazoned with a silver cross with fleurs-de-lis. Their headquarters, the Hôtel des Mousquetaires, stood in the Faubourg Saint-Germain in Paris.

Disbandment and revivals

By the 18th century the corps had become more ceremonial and ruinously expensive; Louis XVI disbanded it on 1 January 1776 as an economy measure. It was re-formed in 1789, dissolved again in the Revolution, revived under the Bourbon Restoration in 1814, and finally disbanded in 1816.

In literature

Dumas's The Three Musketeers, based loosely on the memoirs of the real musketeer Gatien de Courtilz de Sandras, fixed the corps in popular imagination: d'Artagnan and his friends Athos, Porthos, and Aramis, serving under Captain de Tréville against Richelieu's guards. The novel's many adaptations have kept the Musketeers among the most recognisable soldiers in fiction.
