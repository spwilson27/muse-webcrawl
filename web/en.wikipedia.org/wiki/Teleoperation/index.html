Teleoperation

Teleoperation (or remote operation) indicates operation of a system or machine at a distance. It usually involves a human operator controlling a remote device—often a robot—through a communication link, receiving feedback by video, telemetry or haptic signals. Teleoperation is used where direct human presence is dangerous, impractical or impossible.

Applications

Applications include remotely operated underwater vehicles (ROVs) for offshore oil and gas work and oceanography, unmanned aerial vehicles, bomb-disposal robots, remote-controlled cranes and loaders in mining, and robotic surgery systems such as the da Vinci Surgical System. Space exploration uses teleoperation for planetary rovers, although signal delay forces increasing autonomy.

Challenges

Latency is a central problem: round-trip signal delays degrade the operator's sense of presence and control stability. Designers compensate with predictive displays, shared autonomy and supervisory control, in which the remote device executes higher-level commands autonomously.
