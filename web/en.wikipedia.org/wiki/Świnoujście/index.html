Świnoujście

Świnoujście German name: Swinemünde
Country: Poland
Voivodeship: West Pomeranian
Founded: 1743 (as a Prussian port)
Population: about 41,000
The Stawa Młyny beacon, symbol of Świnoujście.

Świnoujście (German: Swinemünde) is a city and Baltic seaport in north-western Poland, situated on the islands of Usedom (Uznam), Wolin and Karsibór at the mouth of the Świna river where the Szczecin Lagoon meets the Baltic Sea. It is uniquely placed: the westernmost part of the city lies on Usedom island, most of which belongs to Germany.

The town developed after Prussia dredged the Świna channel in the 1740s, making it the outport of Stettin (Szczecin). In the nineteenth century Swinemünde also became a fashionable seaside resort. Its lighthouse, completed in 1857, is among the tallest on the Baltic coast.

Modern role

Świnoujście was transferred to Poland in 1945. It remains a major ferry terminal with services to Sweden (Ystad and Trelleborg), a naval base, and the site of an LNG import terminal opened in 2015 that diversifies Poland's gas supplies. In 2023 a road tunnel under the Świna finally gave the Usedom side of the city a fixed link to the rest of Poland, replacing the ferries that had been the only connection.
