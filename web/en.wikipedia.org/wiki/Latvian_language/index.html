Latvian language

Native name: latviešu valoda
Family: Indo-European, Baltic
Official in: Latvia, European Union
Speakers: about 1.5–2 million

Latvian (latviešu valoda) is an Eastern Baltic language belonging to the Indo-European family, spoken by roughly 1.5 million people primarily in Latvia, where it is the sole official language. Together with Lithuanian it is one of only two surviving Baltic languages.

History

Latvian emerged from Proto-Baltic dialects in the early second millennium, influenced by Livonian (Finnic) and later German, Swedish, Polish and Russian. Written Latvian began in the 16th century with religious texts; the modern standard orthography, using Latin letters with diacritics (ā, č, ē, ģ, ī, ķ, ļ, ņ, š, ū, ž), was adopted in the early 20th century. Language policy after independence in 1991 strengthened Latvian following decades of Russification in the Soviet period.

Features

Latvian has fixed stress on the first syllable, three tones in some dialects, seven noun cases, and two genders. Its dialect groups are Central, Livonian and High Latvian (including Latgalian, sometimes treated separately).
