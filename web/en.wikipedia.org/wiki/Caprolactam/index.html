Caprolactam
FormulaC6H11NO
AppearanceWhite hygroscopic solid
Melting point69.2 °C
Main useMonomer for nylon 6

Caprolactam (ε-caprolactam) is an organic compound, a cyclic amide (lactam) of caproic acid, with the formula C6H11NO. This colourless, hygroscopic solid is produced on a scale of several million tonnes per year, almost entirely as the precursor to the synthetic polymer nylon 6.

Production

Caprolactam is most commonly synthesised from cyclohexanone: the ketone is converted to its oxime with hydroxylamine, which then undergoes a Beckmann rearrangement in oleum to give the lactam. A drawback of the classical process is the co-production of large quantities of ammonium sulphate. Alternative routes via cyclohexane photonitrosation, toluene, or phenol, and newer ammoximation processes, aim to reduce by-products.

Uses

Roughly 90% of caprolactam output goes into nylon 6 fibres, engineering resins and films, used in carpets, textiles, tyre cord and moulded parts. Ring-opening polymerisation — initiated by water at high temperature — opens the lactam ring to form the polyamide chain.

Safety

Caprolactam is an irritant to skin, eyes and the respiratory tract, and is classified by IARC as probably not carcinogenic to humans (Group 4, later re-evaluation Group 3); occupational exposure limits are set in many countries.
