Åland

Åland (Finnish: Ahvenanmaa) is an autonomous, demilitarised, and Swedish-speaking region of Finland, consisting of an archipelago of some 6,700 islands in the Baltic Sea at the entrance to the Gulf of Bothnia. Its capital is Mariehamn. Åland's autonomy and demilitarised status are guaranteed by international agreements dating to the 19th and 20th centuries.

Geography

The Åland archipelago lies between Finland and Sweden, roughly midway between Turku and Stockholm. Only about 60 of its islands are inhabited. The main island, Fasta Åland, hosts the majority of the population and the capital Mariehamn, on its southern coast.

History

Åland was part of the Swedish realm for centuries and was ceded, together with Finland, to the Russian Empire in 1809. After the Crimean War, the Treaty of Paris (1856) demilitarised the islands. Following Finnish independence in 1917, the islanders sought union with Sweden, but the League of Nations confirmed Finnish sovereignty in 1921 while guaranteeing Åland's autonomy, Swedish language, and culture. The Autonomy Act has since been revised, notably in 1951 and 1993.

Politics and economy

Åland has its own parliament (Lagting) and government, and issues its own postage stamps. It is part of the European Union via Finland but with derogations, including a tax border that sustains duty-free sales on ferries calling at Åland ports. Shipping, tourism, and trade are central to the economy.
