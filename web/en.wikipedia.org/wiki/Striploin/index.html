Striploin

Striploin
Also known asStrip steak, New York strip, Kansas City strip, club steak
Primal cutShort loin
MuscleLongissimus dorsi
TypeBeef steak

The striploin, cut into steaks, is commonly called the strip steak — or, in the United States, the New York strip. It is a cut of beef steaks taken from the short loin subprimal, consisting of the longissimus muscle, which does relatively little work and is therefore tender. It is recognisable by the strip of fat running along one edge.

Characteristics

Strip steaks have a fine grain, firm texture, moderate marbling, and a pronounced beefy flavour — firmer and chewier than the tenderloin, but less fatty than the ribeye. When left on the bone with the tenderloin attached, the cut forms the large T-bone and porterhouse steaks; the striploin is the larger side of the T-bone.

Names

According to a popular account, the cut took the name "New York strip" from Delmonico's Restaurant in New York City, which popularised it in the 19th century. In Australia and New Zealand it is usually sold as striploin or porterhouse; in Britain, strip steaks cut from the sirloin are often called sirloin steaks, a source of confusion with American usage.

Cooking

Strip steaks are suited to fast, high-heat methods — grilling, broiling, and pan-searing — and are usually served rare to medium-rare. The external fat strip bastes the meat as it cooks and is often left on during cooking, then trimmed at serving.
