Tigranes II, more commonly known as Tigranes the Great (c. 140 – 55 BC), was king of Armenia from 95 to 55 BC. Under him, Armenia briefly became the strongest state in the Roman East, expanding from the Caspian Sea to the Mediterranean and earning Tigranes the traditional title "King of Kings". He remains a towering figure in Armenian national memory.

Rise and expansion

Tigranes spent much of his youth as a hostage at the court of Mithridates II of Parthia, buying his freedom by ceding territory. On taking the throne he allied with Mithridates VI of Pontus — marrying his daughter Cleopatra — and embarked on rapid conquest, annexing Sophene, Cappadocian and Syrian territories, Phoenicia and parts of Mesopotamia from the declining Seleucid Empire and Parthia. Around 83 BC the Syrians invited him to take their crown. He founded a new capital, Tigranocerta, populating it by deporting peoples from across his realm, including Greeks from Cappadocia and Cilicia.

War with Rome and decline

When Rome's war with Mithridates VI reached Armenia, Tigranes refused to surrender his father-in-law. The Roman general Lucullus invaded and destroyed Tigranocerta at the Battle of Tigranocerta in 69 BC, despite Tigranes' much larger army. After further campaigning and internal revolts, Tigranes submitted to Pompey in 66 BC, retaining Armenia proper but losing his conquests and paying an indemnity. He ruled as a Roman client for another decade and died around 55 BC, succeeded by his son Artavasdes II.
