SkyMapper

LocationSiding Spring Observatory, NSW, Australia
OrganisationAustralian National University
Telescope1.35 m modified Cassegrain
First light2008

SkyMapper is a fully automated 1.35-metre (53-inch) wide-angle optical telescope at Siding Spring Observatory in northern New South Wales, Australia. It is owned and operated by the Research School of Astronomy and Astrophysics of the Australian National University (ANU). The telescope has the largest field of view of its class, imaging 5.7 square degrees at once with a 268-megapixel camera.

Southern Sky Survey

SkyMapper's principal project is the Southern Sky Survey, which is mapping the entire southern sky in six optical filters, including a unique violet filter sensitive to the heavy-element content of stars. The survey creates a deep digital atlas of hundreds of millions of stars and galaxies, with repeated visits enabling the discovery of variable objects and transients such as supernovae.

Discoveries

The survey found one of the oldest known stars in the universe, SMSS J031300.36−670839.3, whose extremely low iron content suggests it formed from gas enriched by a single primordial supernova. SkyMapper data are also used to search for distant quasars, extremely metal-poor stars, and near-Earth asteroids, and to follow up gravitational-wave events in the southern sky. The original telescope was destroyed in the 2003 Canberra bushfires before completion; the present instrument was rebuilt and commissioned in 2008–2009.
