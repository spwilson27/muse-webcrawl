Compressor stall

A compressor stall is a local disruption of airflow in the compressor of a gas turbine or turbocharger. It occurs when the compressor blades exceed their critical angle of attack, causing flow separation and a loss of compression. A stall affecting the entire compressor, with a complete breakdown and possible reversal of flow, is known as a compressor surge.

Causes

Stalls can be triggered by distorted or turbulent inlet flow, bird strikes and foreign object damage, worn or damaged blades, rapid throttle movements, high angles of attack of the aircraft, and ingestion of hot exhaust gases during crosswind or thrust-reverser operation. Rotating stall involves cells of stalled blades that propagate around the annulus, causing vibration and reduced performance.

Effects and recovery

Symptoms include loud bangs, engine vibration, fluctuating exhaust gas temperature, and loss of thrust; a full surge may produce flames from the intake or exhaust. Modern engines use variable stator vanes, bleed valves, and full-authority digital engine controls (FADEC) to keep the compressor away from its surge line. Recovery generally requires reducing the fuel flow or throttle setting to allow airflow to reattach.
