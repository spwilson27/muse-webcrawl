Backdoor pilot

A backdoor pilot is an episode of an existing television series that serves as a pilot for a proposed spin-off series. The episode introduces the characters, setting and tone of the new show while airing under the banner of the established parent programme, letting the network test audience reaction without paying for a standalone pilot.

Purpose and mechanics

Backdoor pilots are attractive because they guarantee an existing audience, use the parent show's production infrastructure, and carry less financial risk than an untested standalone pilot. If viewers respond well, the network can order the spin-off to series; if not, the episode simply functions as an ordinary instalment of the parent show. A variant is the "planted spin-off", in which new characters appear in recurring roles before being given their own series.

Examples

Successful backdoor pilots include the NCIS episodes "Ice Queen" and "Meltdown" on JAG, which launched one of television's biggest franchises; the Grey's Anatomy episode that introduced Private Practice; the The Vampire Diaries episode that launched The Originals; and episodes of Arrow that set up The Flash. Earlier examples include episodes of The Andy Griffith Show introducing Gomer Pyle, U.S.M.C. Many other backdoor pilots — such as the Supernatural episode "Bloodlines" — were not picked up.
