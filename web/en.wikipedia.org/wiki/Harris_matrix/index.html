Harris matrix

The Harris matrix is a tool used to depict the temporal succession of archaeological contexts on a dry-land archaeological site, using a schematic diagram of boxes and lines. It was developed in 1973 by the archaeologist Edward C. Harris during excavations in Winchester, England.

Principles

The matrix is built on the law of superposition: in an undisturbed sequence of layers, the upper layers are younger and the lower layers older. Each context — a layer, cut, fill or feature — is drawn as a box, and stratigraphic relationships between contexts are shown by connecting lines, producing a flowchart of the site's formation over time.

Use in excavation

The Harris matrix is the standard method for analysing stratigraphy in single-context recording, allowing excavators to test and display the sequence in which deposits accumulated and were cut into. It has been widely adopted internationally and adapted for standing buildings and digital recording.
