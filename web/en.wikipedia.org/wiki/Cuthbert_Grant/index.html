Cuthbert Grant

Born: 1793, Fort Tremblante, North-West Territory
Died: 15 July 1854, White Horse Plain, Red River Colony
Known for: Métis leader; Battle of Seven Oaks

Cuthbert Grant (1793 – 15 July 1854) was a prominent Métis leader in the early 19th century, known for his role in the fur trade conflicts of the Red River Colony and in the Battle of Seven Oaks in 1816. He later became a magistrate and the first Warden of the Plains, and is regarded as one of the founders of the Métis nation in western Canada.

Early life

Grant was born at Fort Tremblante in present-day Saskatchewan, the son of a Scottish fur trader, also named Cuthbert Grant, and a Métis woman. After his father's death he was sent to be educated, possibly in Scotland, and returned to the northwest around 1812 in the service of the North West Company.

Battle of Seven Oaks

In 1816 Grant led a party of Métis and North West Company men transporting pemmican who encountered Robert Semple, governor of the Red River Colony, and a group of Hudson's Bay Company men at Seven Oaks. In the ensuing confrontation Semple and about twenty of his men were killed, while Grant's party lost one man. The event became a defining episode in Métis history and in the rivalry between the two fur trade companies.

Later life

After the merger of the companies in 1821, Grant settled at White Horse Plain (present-day St. François Xavier, Manitoba), where he built a mill and became a leading figure in the community. In 1828 the Hudson's Bay Company appointed him "Warden of the Plains", responsible for maintaining order and organising the buffalo hunt's defences. He served as a magistrate and member of the Council of Assiniboia, and led Métis forces in support of the colony on several occasions, including during flooding and in the 1851 Battle of Grand Coteau.

Grant died on 15 July 1854 after falling from his horse. The rural municipality and village of Grantown in Manitoba were named for the community he founded.

Legacy

Grant is remembered as a nation-builder of the Métis people; his leadership at Seven Oaks has been commemorated as an assertion of Métis rights, and he remains an important figure in Manitoba history.
