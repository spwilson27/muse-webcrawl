Hardiness (plants)

Hardiness of plants describes their ability to survive adverse growing conditions, especially cold temperatures. Plants are usually described as hardy, half-hardy or tender according to the minimum temperatures they can withstand.

Terminology

A fully hardy plant tolerates the coldest winters in a given region; half-hardy plants need protection from severe frost; tender plants are killed by any frost. Hardiness also depends on factors such as soil drainage, exposure and the duration of cold.

Rating systems

Gardeners use systems such as the USDA hardiness zones in North America and the Royal Horticultural Society ratings (H1–H7) in the United Kingdom to match plants to local climates.
