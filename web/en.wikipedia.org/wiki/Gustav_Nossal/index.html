Sir Gustav Victor Joseph Nossal (born 4 June 1931) is an Austrian-born Australian research biologist, immunologist and science administrator. He was director of the Walter and Eliza Hall Institute of Medical Research (WEHI) in Melbourne from 1965 to 1996 and is one of Australia's most celebrated scientists.

Nossal at the Walter and Eliza Hall Institute

Career

Born in Austria, Nossal emigrated to Australia with his family in 1939, fleeing the Nazi annexation. He studied medicine at the University of Sydney and completed a PhD at the University of Melbourne under Sir Frank Macfarlane Burnet. His doctoral work provided key experimental support for Burnet's clonal selection theory of antibody formation, demonstrating that individual antibody-producing cells make a single type of antibody.

As director of WEHI for three decades, Nossal built it into one of the world's leading immunology institutes. He served as president of the Australian Academy of Science and of the International Union of Immunological Societies, and later became a prominent public advocate for science, medical research, global health and Indigenous health, including work connected with the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation's vaccine programmes. He was named Australian of the Year in 2000 and was knighted in 1977.
