Ajatashatru

Relief traditionally identified as Ajatashatru visiting the Buddha

Ajatashatru (c. 492 – c. 460 BCE) was a king of the Haryanka dynasty of Magadha in eastern India. The son of King Bimbisara, he is remembered in Buddhist and Jain texts for imprisoning and causing the death of his father in order to seize the throne, an act that later filled him with remorse and led him to seek out the Buddha. His reign saw the expansion of Magadha and the fortification of Pataligrama, the village that grew into Pataliputra, the great imperial capital of later centuries.

Accession and wars

According to the Buddhist canon, Ajatashatru was encouraged by the scheming monk Devadatta to overthrow Bimbisara, whom he starved to death in prison. As king he waged war against Kosala, ruled by his uncle Pasenadi, and against the Licchavi republic of Vaishali — a long conflict in which, the texts say, he deployed innovative war machines such as a great catapult and a chariot with whirling maces. His victory over the Vajjian confederacy consolidated Magadhan dominance over the middle Ganges valley.

Religion

The Samaññaphala Sutta describes Ajatashatru visiting the Buddha at Rajagriha, confessing his crime and taking refuge; the Buddha remarked that had he not killed his father he might have attained enlightenment that very night. Jain sources also claim his patronage. Tradition credits him with sponsoring the First Buddhist Council at Rajagriha after the Buddha's death.
