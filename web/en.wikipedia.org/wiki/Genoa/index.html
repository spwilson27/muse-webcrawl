Genoa (Italian: Genova [ˈdʒɛːnova]; Ligurian: Zêna) is the capital of the Italian region of Liguria and the sixth-largest city in Italy. Set on the Gulf of Genoa in the Ligurian Sea, it is one of the country's major economic centres and its principal seaport. The Port of Genoa, with its natural harbour, has been in use since ancient times and remains Italy's busiest port by cargo tonnage.

History

Genoa's harbour was used by the Etruscans and Greeks, and the city was a Roman municipium. Its golden age began in the early Middle Ages: the maritime Republic of Genoa (1099–1797), one of the Italian repubbliche marinare, grew rich on trade and crusading, rivalling Venice for dominion of Mediterranean commerce. Genoese colonies and counting houses stretched from the Black Sea to the Atlantic, the Bank of Saint George (founded 1407) pioneered public debt and banking practices, and the city supplied explorers to foreign crowns — most famously Christopher Columbus, born in Genoa around 1451.

Under the admiral Andrea Doria, Genoa became a key ally of Habsburg Spain in the sixteenth century, its bankers financing the Spanish monarchy. The republic was dissolved in the Napoleonic era and the city was assigned to the Kingdom of Sardinia in 1815. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries Genoa industrialised — steelworks, shipyards, and engineering — and became a great port of emigration to the Americas.

Landmarks

The old town, one of the largest medieval historic centres in Europe, is a maze of narrow alleys (caruggi). The Palazzi dei Rolli — Renaissance and Baroque palaces of the Strade Nuove — are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Other landmarks include the Lanterna, the city's ancient lighthouse and symbol; Genoa Cathedral (San Lorenzo); the Palazzo Ducale; and the Aquarium of Genoa, one of Europe's largest, built in the redeveloped old harbour for the 1992 Columbus celebrations. The city made international headlines in 2018 with the collapse of the Morandi Bridge, replaced in 2020 by the San Giorgio bridge designed by Renzo Piano.

Economy and culture

Shipping, shipbuilding, finance, and tourism anchor the modern economy. Genoa is home to two top-flight football clubs, Genoa C.F.C. — Italy's oldest football club, founded 1893 — and U.C. Sampdoria, who share the Stadio Luigi Ferraris. Its cuisine gave the world pesto alla genovese and focaccia.
