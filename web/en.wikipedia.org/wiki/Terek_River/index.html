Terek River

Length: 623 km (387 mi)

Source: Greater Caucasus, Georgia

Mouth: Caspian Sea

Basin size: 43,200 km²

The Terek River is a major river in the Northern Caucasus, flowing through Georgia and Russia into the Caspian Sea. It rises in the glaciers of the Greater Caucasus near Mount Kazbek, flows north across the Darial Gorge, then eastward along the northern foothills of the Caucasus range.

Course

The Terek flows through the Russian republics of North Ossetia–Alania, Kabardino-Balkaria, Chechnya and Dagestan. Important cities on its banks include Vladikavkaz, Mozdok and Kizlyar. Near its mouth the river splits into a wide delta before emptying into the Caspian Sea southwest of the Volga delta.

Tributaries and use

Major tributaries include the Sunzha, Malka, Baksan, Ardon and Gizeldon rivers. The Terek is used for irrigation in its lower reaches and for hydroelectric power generation; several hydroelectric stations operate on the river and its tributaries. The river is known for its swift current and heavy sediment load, and has historically flooded, prompting extensive levee construction in the lower course.

History

The Terek formed part of the frontier of the Russian Empire's expansion into the Caucasus. In the 16th century, Terek Cossacks settled along the river, and the Terek Cossack Host became a significant military force. During the Soviet period, the Terek Oblast was an administrative unit of the empire and later of the RSFSR. The river and its valley feature in Russian literature, including works by Lermontov and Tolstoy, who served in the Caucasus.
