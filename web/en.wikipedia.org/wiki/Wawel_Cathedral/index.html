The Wawel Cathedral (Polish: Katedra Wawelska), formally the Cathedral Basilica of St Stanislaus and St Wenceslaus, is a Roman Catholic church on Wawel Hill in Kraków, Poland. For centuries it was the coronation church of Polish monarchs and remains their principal burial place, as well as the mausoleum of Poland's national heroes.

History

The present Gothic cathedral, built between 1320 and 1364 under Casimir III the Great, is the third church on the site; the earliest was founded around the year 1000 after the establishment of the Kraków bishopric, and Romanesque fragments survive, including the Crypt of St Leonard. Nearly every Polish king from Władysław the Elbow-high onward was crowned here, the last in 1734.

Features

The cathedral is ringed by eighteen side chapels, pre-eminently the Renaissance Sigismund Chapel (1515–33), Bartolomeo Berrecci's domed masterpiece, called "the pearl of the Renaissance north of the Alps". In the Sigismund Tower hangs the Sigismund Bell (1520), one of Poland's largest historic bells, rung only on great occasions. The crypts contain the tombs of kings, Tadeusz Kościuszko, Józef Piłsudski, the poets Adam Mickiewicz and Juliusz Słowacki, and President Lech Kaczyński and his wife (buried 2010). Relics of St Stanislaus, Poland's patron martyr-bishop, rest in a silver coffin at the centre of the nave.
