Attica (Greek: Αττική, Attikí) is a historical region and modern administrative region of Greece centred on the metropolitan area of Athens. The Attica peninsula projects into the Aegean Sea, bordered by Boeotia to the north and the Gulf of Corinth to the west.

History

In antiquity Attica was the territory of the polis of Athens. Its unification under Athens was attributed in myth to Theseus (the synoikismos); historically it was accomplished by the archaic period. The region contained the silver mines of Laurion, which financed the Athenian fleet, the marble quarries of Mount Pentelicus, and the sanctuary of Demeter at Eleusis, home of the Eleusinian Mysteries. The demes (villages) of Attica formed the basis of Cleisthenes' democratic tribal system from 508 BCE. Attica was devastated by Spartan invasions during the Peloponnesian War and by later campaigns, but Athens's cultural primacy persisted under Macedonian and Roman rule.

Geography

The region comprises a triangular peninsula with a dry Mediterranean climate. Mountains ring the Athenian plain: Hymettus, Pentelicus, Parnes and Aegaleus. Modern Attica includes the port of Piraeus, the Saronic islands, and the Athens conurbation, home to more than a third of Greece's population, as well as ancient sites such as Marathon, Sounion (with the Temple of Poseidon) and Eleusis.

Modern administration

Attica is one of Greece's thirteen administrative regions, subdivided into eight regional units. It hosts the national government, the country's busiest port and airport, and remains Greece's economic and cultural centre.
