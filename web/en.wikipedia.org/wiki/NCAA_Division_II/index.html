NCAA Division II (D-II) is an intermediate-level division of competition in the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA). It offers an alternative to both the larger and better-funded Division I and the scholarship-free environment offered in Division III.

Division II schools tend to be smaller public universities and many private institutions. Athletics programmes at D-II schools are funded through a traditional model in which universities absorb the costs of running the programme, in contrast to the largely self-sustaining budgets of major Division I athletic departments. Division II operates on the partial-scholarship model: most athletes receive some athletically related financial aid, but few receive a full "free ride"; scholarships are awarded as equivalencies that coaches divide among team members.

Division II emphasises regional competition and a balance between athletics and academics.

Philosophy

Division II's membership adopted the platform "Life in the Balance", emphasising a comprehensive programme of learning and development in a personal setting: high-level competition, academic achievement, community engagement, and balanced student-athlete experience. The division requires a minimum number of sports (at least ten men's and women's teams combined, with specified offerings) but caps financial aid, squad sizes, and recruiting activity at levels below Division I.

Membership and conferences

About 300 schools compete in Division II, organised in conferences such as the Great Lakes Valley Conference, the Lone Star Conference, the Pennsylvania State Athletic Conference, the Gulf South Conference, and the Northern Sun Intercollegiate Conference. Most competition is regional, reducing travel costs and missed class time.

Championships

Division II conducts national championships in more than 25 sports. Its football championship dates to 1973, and its men's basketball championship game is traditionally held in conjunction with the Division II "Elite Eight". The division also pioneered championship festivals, in which several sports hold their national finals at one site in an Olympic-style format.

History

The NCAA split into University and College Divisions in 1956–57, largely along lines of size and scholarship policy. When the association reorganised into three numbered divisions in 1973, the College Division's scholarship-granting schools became Division II. Several prominent programmes have moved between divisions; a number of current Division I schools began in D-II, and the division has periodically admitted members transitioning from the NAIA or Division III.
