Slovak Republic (1939–1945)

Slovak Republic (Slovenská republika)
Capital: Bratislava
Government: One-party clerical-authoritarian republic, client state of Nazi Germany
President: Jozef Tiso
Established: 14 March 1939
Disestablished: 1945

The Slovak Republic (Slovak: Slovenská republika), also known as the First Slovak Republic, was a partially recognized clerical fascist client state of Nazi Germany that existed from 14 March 1939 to 1945. It was proclaimed when, under heavy German pressure following the dismemberment of Czechoslovakia after the Munich Agreement, the Slovak Diet declared independence one day before Germany occupied Bohemia and Moravia and created the Protectorate.

The state was ruled by the Slovak People's Party under President Jozef Tiso, a Catholic priest. Slovakia joined the Axis war against the Soviet Union in 1941, sending divisions to the Eastern Front. The regime adopted anti-Jewish racial laws (the 1941 Jewish Code) and in 1942 deported some 58,000 Slovak Jews to German-occupied Poland, paying Germany 500 Reichsmarks per deportee; most were murdered in the Holocaust. A further wave of deportations followed the German occupation of Slovakia in 1944, bringing the total to roughly 70,000 of the country's approximately 89,000 Jews.

End of the state

In August 1944 the Slovak National Uprising broke out against the regime and German forces. German troops occupied the country and crushed the uprising by October, though partisan warfare continued. The Red Army and Czechoslovak units liberated Slovakia in spring 1945, culminating in the liberation of Bratislava on 4 April 1945; the republic ceased to exist and Slovakia was reunited with the restored Czechoslovakia. Tiso was tried and executed in 1947. The wartime state remains a subject of controversy in modern Slovakia: it provided the first Slovak statehood in history, but did so as a collaborationist regime complicit in the Holocaust.
