Val-de-Grâce

The Val-de-Grâce is a Roman Catholic church and former royal abbey in the 5th arrondissement of Paris, France. Built by order of Anne of Austria, queen consort of Louis XIII, in thanks for the birth of her son, the future Louis XIV, it is one of the masterpieces of French Baroque (classical) architecture. The complex now houses a military hospital, the Hôpital d'instruction des armées du Val-de-Grâce, and the museum of the French army health service.

History

Anne of Austria, long childless, vowed to build a magnificent church if God granted her a son. After the birth of Louis-Dieudonné in 1638, she commissioned the abbey church for the Benedictine nuns of Val-de-Grâce; the five-year-old king laid the first stone on 1 April 1645. François Mansart produced the original design, though he was replaced before completion; Jacques Lemercier and others carried the work forward, and the church was finished in 1667.

Architecture

The church is famous for its dome, decorated inside with a vast fresco by Pierre Mignard (1663–1665) depicting more than two hundred figures of saints in glory — one of the largest frescoes of its era. The baldachin over the high altar, supported on twisted columns recalling St Peter's in Rome, is another celebrated feature. During the French Revolution the abbey was converted to a military hospital, a role the site retains today.
