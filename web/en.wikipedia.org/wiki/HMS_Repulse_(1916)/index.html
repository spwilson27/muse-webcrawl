HMS Repulse (1916)

ClassRenown-class battlecruiser
Launched8 January 1916
Commissioned18 August 1916
FateSunk by Japanese aircraft, 10 December 1941

HMS Repulse was one of two Renown-class battlecruisers built for the Royal Navy during the First World War. Armed with six 15-inch guns but lightly armoured by capital-ship standards, she was among the fastest large warships of her era.

Service history

Repulse was too late for the Battle of Jutland but saw action in the Second Battle of Heligoland Bight in November 1917. Between the wars she served with the Atlantic Fleet and made numerous goodwill voyages, including accompanying the Prince of Wales on tours, and she underwent two major reconstructions that improved her armour and anti-aircraft armament. In the Second World War she took part in the hunt for the German battleship Bismarck in May 1941 and escorted troop convoys.

Loss

In late 1941 Repulse was sent to Singapore as part of Force Z, alongside the new battleship HMS Prince of Wales, to deter Japanese expansion. On 10 December 1941, three days after the attack on Pearl Harbor, both ships were sunk off Kuantan, Malaya, by Japanese land-based torpedo bombers while operating without air cover. Repulse capsized and sank after at least five torpedo hits; 513 of her crew were lost, while her captain, John Leach, survived. The sinkings demonstrated decisively the vulnerability of capital ships to air power and shocked British opinion. Her wreck, lying in the South China Sea, is a designated war grave.
