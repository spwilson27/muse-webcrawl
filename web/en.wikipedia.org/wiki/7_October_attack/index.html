On 7 October 2023, Hamas and other Palestinian militant groups launched a large-scale surprise attack on Israel from the Gaza Strip, coinciding with the Jewish holiday of Simchat Torah. Under cover of thousands of rockets, around 3,000 militants breached the Gaza–Israel barrier and attacked military bases and civilian communities in southern Israel.

The attacks

Gunmen assaulted kibbutzim including Be'eri, Kfar Aza and Nir Oz, the city of Sderot, and an outdoor music festival near Re'im, where 364 people were killed. In all, about 1,195 people in Israel were killed, including 815 civilians, and 251 people — civilians and soldiers, Israelis and foreign nationals — were abducted to Gaza as hostages. The attack was the deadliest day for Jews since the Holocaust and the deadliest in Israel's history. Atrocities including mass killings and sexual violence were documented by investigators and journalists.

Aftermath

Israel formally declared war the following day and began a massive air and ground campaign in Gaza aimed at destroying Hamas and freeing the hostages — the start of the Gaza war. The conflict drew in regional actors, with Hezbollah opening a front in Lebanon and Iran and Israel exchanging direct strikes. Hostage releases occurred through ceasefire agreements in November 2023 and January 2025, alongside military rescues; many hostages died in captivity.
