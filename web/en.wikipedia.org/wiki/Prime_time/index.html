Prime time

Prime time, or the peak time, is the block of broadcast programming taking place during the middle of the evening for a television show. It is mostly targeted towards adults (and sometimes families). It is used by the major television networks to broadcast their season's nightly programming.

North America

In North America, prime time traditionally runs from 8:00 p.m. to 11:00 p.m. Eastern and Pacific Time, Monday through Saturday, and 7:00 p.m. to 11:00 p.m. on Sundays. Because of time zone differences, in the Central and Mountain time zones prime time begins an hour earlier. Networks schedule their most popular and prestigious programming during these hours, and advertising rates are highest because audiences are largest.

History

The concept developed with radio broadcasting in the early 20th century, when evening hours attracted the largest family audiences. Television inherited the pattern, and prime time became the centrepiece of network schedules, with genres such as drama series, sitcoms, news magazines, and reality competitions dominating.

Around the world

Prime time hours vary by country according to viewing habits. In the United Kingdom, peak time is generally considered to be from about 7:00 p.m. to 10:30 p.m.; in India, prime time runs roughly from 7:00 p.m. to 11:00 p.m.; and in many Latin American countries the evening telenovela slots anchor prime time. In some countries, such as Spain, prime time begins later in the evening because of later working and dining hours.

Streaming era

The rise of streaming services and time-shifted viewing has weakened the traditional boundaries of prime time, as viewers increasingly watch programmes on demand. Nevertheless, live events and major network broadcasts continue to draw their largest audiences in the evening hours.
