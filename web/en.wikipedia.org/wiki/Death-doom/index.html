Death-doom

Death-doom, also known as death-doom metal, is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal music that combines the slow tempos and pessimistic, melancholic atmosphere of doom metal with the deep growling vocals and double-kick drumming of death metal. It emerged in the late 1980s and gained popularity in the early 1990s.

History

The style began to take shape in the late 1980s with bands such as Dream Death and early work by Paradise Lost and Autopsy. In the early 1990s, the British "Peaceville Three" — Paradise Lost, My Dying Bride, and Anathema — defined the genre with albums combining mournful melodies, orchestral touches, and death growls. In Finland, bands such as Amorphis (in their early work) and later Swallow the Sun, and in the Netherlands Asphyx and The Gathering, further developed the sound.

Characteristics

Death-doom songs are typically slow, heavy, and lengthy, with downtuned guitars, themes of loss, despair, and death in the lyrics, and a mix of growled and occasionally clean or female vocals. When keyboards, violins, and other atmospheric elements dominate, the music often shades into gothic metal; an even slower, more minimal offshoot is funeral doom.
