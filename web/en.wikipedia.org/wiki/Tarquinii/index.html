Tarquinii

Tarquinii (Etruscan: Tarchna or Tarchuna) was one of the most ancient and important cities of the Etruscans, and a leading member of the Etruscan league of twelve cities. It stood on the Civita plateau near the modern town of Tarquinia in the province of Viterbo, Lazio, Italy, not far from the Tyrrhenian coast. Tradition made Tarquinii the home of the Tarquin dynasty of Roman kings and of the hero Tarchon, its legendary founder.

History

The site was occupied from the Villanovan period (9th–8th centuries BC) and grew into a wealthy city through trade and the exploitation of metal resources. Tarquinii flourished in the 7th and 6th centuries BC; Roman tradition credited its kings, Lucius Tarquinius Priscus and Tarquinius Superbus, with major building works in Rome. Relations with Rome alternated between trade and war. After long conflicts Tarquinii was defeated in the 4th century BC, and by the 3rd century it had come under Roman control, declining in importance as a Roman municipium. The medieval town shifted to the nearby hill of Corneto, and the ancient city was abandoned.

Necropolis

Tarquinii is most famous for the Monterozzi necropolis, containing some 6,000 tombs, of which about 200 are painted — the largest surviving corpus of Etruscan painting. Celebrated chambers include the Tomb of the Leopards, the Tomb of the Augurs, the Tomb of the Lionesses, the Tomb of Hunting and Fishing and the Tomb of the Ship. The paintings depict banquets, dancers, musicians, athletes and scenes from daily life and myth, offering an unmatched window into Etruscan society. The necropolis, together with that of Cerveteri (ancient Caere), was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004.

Archaeology

On the Civita plateau excavations have revealed the podium of the 4th-century BC "Ara della Regina" temple, from which came the celebrated terracotta relief of winged horses now in Tarquinia's National Museum, housed in the Palazzo Vitelleschi in the modern town.
