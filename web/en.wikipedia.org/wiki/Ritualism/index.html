Ritualism

Ritualism, in the context of the Church of England, was a movement of the mid-to-late 19th century advocating the adoption of elaborate ceremonial practices associated historically with Roman Catholic worship — such as vestments, incense, candles, and eucharistic adoration — within Anglican services.

Development

Ritualism grew out of the Oxford Movement (Tractarianism), which from the 1830s stressed the Catholic heritage of Anglicanism. The second generation of the movement, led by figures in London parishes such as Alexander Heriot Mackonochie at St Alban's, Holborn, carried its theology into liturgical practice, often in poor urban parishes.

Controversy

The movement provoked strong evangelical opposition, a series of prosecutions under the Church Discipline Act, and eventually the Public Worship Regulation Act 1874, under which several priests were imprisoned. A Royal Commission on Ritual (1904–1906) and the growth of Anglo-Catholicism gradually normalized many of the practices, which remain influential in the worldwide Anglican Communion.
