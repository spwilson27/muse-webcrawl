National Technical University of Athens

The National Technical University of Athens (NTUA; Greek: Εθνικό Μετσόβιο Πολυτεχνείο, Ethnikó Metsóvio Polytechneío), sometimes known as Athens Polytechnic, is among the oldest and most prestigious higher education institutions in Greece. Founded in 1837 as the Royal School of Arts, it was reorganised in 1862 and took its present form later in the 19th century.

History

The institution was named "Metsovio" in honour of its benefactors from Metsovo in Epirus. It moved to its Patission Street campus in central Athens in the 1870s–1900s. The university is historically significant as the site of the Athens Polytechnic uprising of November 1973, when students occupied the campus in protest against the military junta; the uprising was crushed by the army on 17 November 1973, an event commemorated annually in Greece.

Organisation

NTUA comprises nine engineering schools, including civil, mechanical, electrical and computer, chemical, architecture, mining and metallurgical, naval architecture and marine engineering, applied mathematical and physical sciences, and rural, surveying and geoinformatics engineering. Its main modern campus is at Zografou.
