Building permit

Building permit
Issued byLocal government authority
PurposeAuthorisation to construct or alter a building

A building permit (also construction permit or building approval) is an official approval issued by a local government agency that allows a person or organisation to proceed with a construction or remodeling project on a property. It is intended to ensure that plans comply with local standards for land use, zoning and construction, including structural safety, fire protection and accessibility requirements embodied in the applicable building code.

Process

The applicant typically submits plans and specifications, which are reviewed by municipal staff or a private certifier for code compliance. Fees are usually charged based on the size or value of the project. During construction, inspectors visit the site at defined stages — such as foundation, framing, plumbing, electrical work and final completion — to verify that the work matches the approved plans. A certificate of occupancy may be issued when the building is complete and fit for use.

When required

Permits are generally required for new construction, additions, structural alterations, changes of use, and significant mechanical, electrical or plumbing work. Minor cosmetic work such as painting or replacing flooring is usually exempt. Building without a required permit can result in fines, stop-work orders, or requirements to demolish or expose concealed work, and unpermitted work can complicate later property sales and insurance claims.

History

Formal control of building predates modern permits: the Code of Hammurabi imposed penalties on builders whose houses collapsed, and London's Rebuilding Act of 1667, passed after the Great Fire, imposed construction standards. Comprehensive permit systems developed in the 19th and 20th centuries alongside model building codes and professional licensing.
