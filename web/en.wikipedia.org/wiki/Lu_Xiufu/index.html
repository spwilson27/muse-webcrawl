Lu Xiufu (陸秀夫; 1236–1279), courtesy name Junshi (君實), was a Chinese statesman and military commander who served as Chancellor of the Song dynasty in its final years. He is remembered in Chinese history as a symbol of loyalty unto death.

Life

Lu passed the imperial examinations and rose through the civil service. As Mongol forces of the Yuan dynasty completed their conquest of Song China, Lu joined the loyalist court in the south, supporting the child emperors Duanzong and Zhao Bing, the last Song ruler. Alongside figures such as Wen Tianxiang and Zhang Shijie, Lu worked to sustain armed resistance from the court-in-exile along the southeastern coast.

Death at Yamen

After the final Song defeat at the Battle of Yamen on 19 March 1279, Lu, seeing that escape was impossible and refusing to let the eight-year-old emperor fall into enemy hands, carried Zhao Bing on his back and leapt into the sea; both drowned. Many members of the court and thousands of loyalists are said to have followed him into the water. His act is commemorated in temples and memorials, and Lu Xiufu remains one of the celebrated loyalist martyrs of the Song–Yuan transition.
