Colour photography

Colour photography is photography that uses media capable of capturing and reproducing colours. By contrast, black-and-white (monochrome) photography records only a single channel of luminance and has been the dominant medium for most of photography's history.

History

The first permanent colour photograph was made in 1861 by James Clerk Maxwell using the three-colour method he had proposed. Early practical processes included the Autochrome Lumière (1907), the first commercially successful colour process, followed by Kodachrome (1935) and other subtractive colour films. From the late 20th century, digital sensors using Bayer filter arrays made colour the default for nearly all photography.

Principles

Colour images are generally formed by recording three channels corresponding to red, green and blue light (additive colour), while prints and films use cyan, magenta and yellow dyes (subtractive colour).
