Antiphon

An antiphon (Greek ἀντίφωνον, ἀντί "opposite" and φωνή "voice") is a short chant in Christian ritual, sung as a refrain. The term can also refer to a refrain that is sung in alternate responses, as well as to the responsive singing itself, called antiphony or antiphonal psalmody, in which two choirs or a cantor and congregation sing verses in alternation.

Origins

Antiphonal singing has ancient roots in Jewish psalmody and was adopted by the early Church. Tradition credits St. Ambrose of Milan with introducing antiphonal psalm singing to the West in the 4th century, a practice he is said to have learned from the Eastern church; St. John Chrysostom promoted it in Constantinople. In the Roman tradition, antiphons became the standard refrains framing the psalms of the Liturgy of the Hours (Divine Office).

Types

Office antiphons

In the chant of the Divine Office, an antiphon is sung before and after each psalm or canticle, and its text, often drawn from Scripture, comments on the psalm or the feast of the day. The great "O Antiphons" of Advent — including O Sapientia and O Emmanuel — are sung with the Magnificat at Vespers on the seven days before Christmas Eve and inspired the hymn "O Come, O Come, Emmanuel".

Marian antiphons

Four seasonal antiphons in honour of the Virgin Mary close the office of Compline and are also sung at the end of Mass: Alma Redemptoris Mater, Ave Regina Caelorum, Regina Caeli, and Salve Regina. These have received countless polyphonic settings from the Renaissance onwards.

Processional and Mass antiphons

Chants of the Mass Proper such as the Introit, Offertory, and Communion are historically antiphons sung with psalm verses during processions or ritual actions, though the verses were largely dropped in later practice.

Antiphonal practice

The division of a choir into two halves (decani and cantoris in English cathedral practice) stationed on opposite sides of the chancel preserves the antiphonal principle. In Byzantine chant, antiphons structure the opening of the Divine Liturgy. After the liturgical reforms of the Second Vatican Council, antiphons in the Roman Rite may be sung or recited, and responsorial psalmody between the readings at Mass follows the same call-and-response pattern.
