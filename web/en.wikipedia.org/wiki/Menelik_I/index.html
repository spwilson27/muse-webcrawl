Menelik I

Menelik I is the legendary first emperor of Ethiopia, held by Ethiopian tradition to have been the son of King Solomon of Israel and the Queen of Sheba (Makeda). According to the medieval Ethiopian epic Kebra Nagast ("The Glory of the Kings"), Menelik travelled to Jerusalem to meet his father and returned to Ethiopia bringing the Ark of the Covenant, founding the Solomonic dynasty that claimed descent from him for some three thousand years.

The Kebra Nagast legend

In the Kebra Nagast, Makeda, queen of Ethiopia, visits Solomon and bears his son, born on her journey home. Raised in Ethiopia, Menelik travels as a young man to Jerusalem, where Solomon recognises him. Refusing the succession in Israel, Menelik returns home accompanied by the firstborn sons of Israel's elders; the Ark of the Covenant is brought with them — in some versions taken with the knowledge of Azariah, son of the high priest Zadok. The Ark is said to rest to this day in the Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion in Axum.

Historical status

There is no historical evidence for Menelik I outside Ethiopian tradition; he is a figure of national foundation myth rather than documented history. The historical Ethiopian monarchy traced its legitimacy to the legend: the Solomonic line, restored in 1270 under Yekuno Amlak, ruled until the deposition of Haile Selassie in 1974, and Ethiopia's 1955 constitution formally affirmed the dynasty's descent from Menelik I. He should not be confused with Emperor Menelik II (r. 1889–1913), the historical victor of the Battle of Adwa.
