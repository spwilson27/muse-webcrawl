Ironman Triathlon

An Ironman Triathlon is a long-distance triathlon race organised by the World Triathlon Corporation (WTC), consisting of a 2.4-mile (3.86 km) swim, a 112-mile (180.25 km) bicycle ride, and a marathon 26.22-mile (42.2 km) run, completed in that order and without a break. It is widely considered one of the most demanding single-day endurance events; athletes must finish within a 17-hour cut-off to be called an "Ironman".

History

The event grew out of a 1977 argument among Honolulu athletes about whether swimmers, cyclists, or runners were the fittest. US Navy Commander John Collins proposed combining three existing Oahu races — the Waikiki Roughwater Swim, the Around-Oahu Bike Race, and the Honolulu Marathon. The first Iron Man race was held on 18 February 1978 with 15 starters; Gordon Haller won in 11:46:58. In 1982, Julie Moss's televised crawl to the finish line brought the event worldwide attention. The Ironman World Championship has been held in Kailua-Kona, Hawaii, since 1981 (until a later format split between venues).

Format and qualifying

Full-distance Ironman races are held in dozens of countries, alongside the shorter Ironman 70.3 series (half the distances). Age-group athletes qualify for the World Championship by earning slots at regional races; professionals race for prize money and ranking points. Elite finish times have fallen below 7½ hours for men (on fast courses) and 8¼ hours for women. The brand and format spawned the wider long-course triathlon scene, including the independent Challenge Family series.
