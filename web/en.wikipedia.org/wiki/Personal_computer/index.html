Personal computer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A personal computer, often referred to as a PC, is a computer designed for individual use. They are distinguished from mainframes and shared terminals by being operated directly by an end user, rather than by a computer operator, and by their relatively low cost. PCs are used as home and business systems for tasks such as word processing, web browsing, email, multimedia playback, and gaming.

While the earliest "personal computers" were hobbyist kits of the mid-1970s, the term became widespread after the launch of the IBM Personal Computer in 1981. Since then the market has been dominated by desktop computers, laptops, and, in the broader definition, tablet computers.

History

The first machines marketed as personal computers appeared in the mid-1970s, including the Altair 8800 kit (1975) and the 1977 "trinity" of the Apple II, Commodore PET, and TRS-80. The IBM PC of 1981, built largely from off-the-shelf components, established an open standard that spawned a large industry of compatible machines. The Apple Macintosh (1984) popularised the graphical user interface, and Microsoft's Windows operating system became dominant on PC-compatibles during the 1990s. By the 2000s, laptops outsold desktops, and smartphones took over many casual computing tasks, though PCs remained standard for office work, content creation, and high-end gaming.

Hardware

A typical personal computer consists of a system unit containing a central processing unit (CPU), main memory (RAM), storage such as a solid-state drive, and a motherboard connecting them; a display; and input devices including a keyboard and mouse or trackpad. Expansion may be provided through ports such as USB and through internal slots. Laptops integrate the display, keyboard, battery, and pointing device into a single portable unit.

Software

PCs run an operating system — principally Microsoft Windows, macOS, and Linux distributions — which manages hardware and provides a platform for application software. Applications range from office suites and web browsers to development tools, engineering software, and games.

Types

Form factors include tower and all-in-one desktops, laptops and convertibles, tablets, single-board computers, and handheld gaming PCs. Workstations are high-end PCs optimised for technical or scientific applications.

Market

Annual worldwide PC shipments peaked at over 360 million units around 2011 and stabilised in the range of 250–300 million units. Major manufacturers include Lenovo, HP, Dell, Apple, Asus, and Acer.
