Sagami Trough

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Sagami Trough (Japanese: 相模トラフ, Sagami Torafu) is a submarine trough, or trench-like depression, located off the coast of central Japan. It runs from the vicinity of the Bōsō Peninsula southeastward along the boundary where the Philippine Sea Plate subducts beneath the Okhotsk Plate (or Amurian Plate), and forms the northern extension of the system that continues into the Japan Trench region and the Suruga Trough to the southwest.

Tectonics

The Sagami Trough marks the convergent plate boundary northwest of Sagami Bay. The Philippine Sea Plate is subducting beneath Honshu at a rate of a few centimetres per year. At the trough's western end, the plate boundary bends sharply around the Izu Peninsula, where the Izu–Bonin arc collides with Honshu.

Earthquakes

The trough is the source region of great megathrust earthquakes that periodically devastate the Kantō region. Notable events include the 1703 Genroku earthquake (estimated magnitude ~8.0), which generated a large tsunami, and the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake (magnitude 7.9), which destroyed much of Tokyo and Yokohama and killed more than 100,000 people. Japanese government assessments consider the Sagami Trough a major seismic hazard for the Tokyo metropolitan area, and earthquake scenarios along it are central to national disaster planning.
