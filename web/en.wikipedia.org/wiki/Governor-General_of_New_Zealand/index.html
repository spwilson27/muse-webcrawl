Governor-General of New Zealand

The Governor-General of New Zealand (Māori: Te Kāwana Tianara o Aotearoa) is the representative of the monarch of New Zealand, currently the King of New Zealand. The Governor-General performs the monarch's constitutional and ceremonial duties within New Zealand, including appointing the prime minister, dissolving Parliament, and granting royal assent to legislation.

The office evolved from the colonial governorship established after the Treaty of Waitangi in 1840; the title "Governor-General" was adopted in 1917. Since the late 20th century, holders have been New Zealand residents, appointed by the monarch on the advice of the New Zealand prime minister, typically for a five-year term.

Although formally the commander-in-chief and the head of the executive, the Governor-General acts almost always on the advice of ministers, exercising reserve powers only in exceptional circumstances. The official residences are Government House, Wellington, and Government House, Auckland.
