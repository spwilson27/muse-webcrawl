Permanent residency

Permanent residency is a person's legal resident status in a country of which they are not a citizen, granting the right to live and usually work there indefinitely, subject to conditions. Permanent residents typically remain citizens of their home country, hold its passport, and lack some rights of citizens, such as voting in national elections or holding certain public offices.

Rights and limitations

Permanent residents generally may live, work and study in the host country without time limits, access many public services, and travel in and out under conditions (often a requirement not to be absent beyond a set period). Status can be lost through long absence, serious criminal offending, or fraud in obtaining it. After a qualifying period — commonly three to five years — permanent residents may apply for naturalisation as citizens.

Examples

Well-known forms include the United States green card (lawful permanent resident status), Canadian permanent residence, Australian permanent residency, UK indefinite leave to remain and settled status, and long-term resident status in the European Union. Many states grant it through skilled-worker, family-reunification, investor or humanitarian routes.
