Third-wave feminism

Third-wave feminism is a feminist movement that began in the early 1990s, arising in response to the perceived failures of second-wave feminism and to a backlash against feminist initiatives and movements during the 1980s. The third wave sought to expand feminism to include women with a more diverse set of identities, recognising that women are of "many colors, ethnicities, nationalities, religions and cultural backgrounds". It also embraced individualism and diversity, reclaimed symbols and language previously considered degrading, and was closely associated with the riot grrrl punk subculture.

Origins

A commonly cited starting point is the Anita Hill hearings of 1991, in which Hill testified that Supreme Court nominee Clarence Thomas had sexually harassed her; Thomas was nonetheless confirmed. The following year saw an unprecedented number of women elected to the United States Congress, dubbed the "Year of the Woman". In 1992 Rebecca Walker published an article in Ms. magazine, "Becoming the Third Wave", declaring: "I am not a post-feminism feminist. I am the third-wave."

Key ideas

Intersectionality

Building on Kimberlé Crenshaw's concept of intersectionality, third-wave feminists argued that gender cannot be analysed in isolation from race, class, sexuality and other axes of identity. Where some second-wave accounts were criticised for universalising the experience of white, middle-class women, the third wave emphasised the diversity of women's experiences.

Reclaiming language and symbols

Third-wave writers reclaimed words and imagery they regarded as tools of empowerment rather than oppression. This extended to a more sex-positive attitude than parts of the second wave had allowed, and to the playful embrace of traditionally feminine fashion and cosmetics.

Riot grrrl and popular culture

The riot grrrl movement, associated with bands such as Bikini Kill, combined punk music with feminist politics and DIY zine culture. Third-wave feminism also engaged heavily with popular media, critiquing representations of women while treating figures such as Madonna as objects of serious analysis.

Criticism

Critics have questioned whether the "wave" model itself distorts feminist history, and whether the third wave's emphasis on individual choice amounted to a retreat from collective political action. Scholars also debate where the third wave ends and a fourth wave, associated with social media campaigns such as #MeToo, begins; the fourth wave is usually dated from around 2012.
