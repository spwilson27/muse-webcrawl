Melanocortin

Melanocortins
GroupPeptide hormones
PrecursorPro-opiomelanocortin (POMC)
Membersα-MSH, β-MSH, γ-MSH, ACTH
ReceptorsMC1R–MC5R

The melanocortins are a family of neuropeptide hormones which are the ligands of the melanocortin receptors. The melanocortin system consists of melanocortin receptors, ligands (the melanocortins derived from pro-opiomelanocortin, POMC, including adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) and the melanocyte-stimulating hormones (MSH)), and endogenous antagonists, chiefly agouti signalling protein and agouti-related peptide (AgRP).

Functions

The melanocortin system is involved in a wide range of physiological processes:

  • Pigmentation: α-MSH acting at MC1R on melanocytes stimulates eumelanin production and skin/hair darkening.
  • Energy homeostasis: POMC neurons in the arcuate nucleus of the hypothalamus release α-MSH, which suppresses appetite via MC4R; defects in POMC or MC4R cause severe early-onset obesity.
  • Adrenal function: ACTH acting at MC2R stimulates glucocorticoid production by the adrenal cortex.
  • Inflammation and immune modulation, exocrine gland function (MC5R), and sexual function.

Clinical significance

Synthetic melanocortin analogues include afamelanotide (used to treat erythropoietic protoporphyria), bremelanotide, and setmelanotide, which is approved for certain rare genetic obesity disorders involving the POMC pathway.
