Fourteen Holy Helpers

The Fourteen Holy Helpers (German: Vierzehn Nothelfer, "fourteen helpers in need") are a group of saints venerated together in Roman Catholicism, especially in Germany. Their intercession was considered particularly effective against diseases and in times of trouble. Devotion to the group arose in the Rhineland and Franconia during the epidemics of the Black Death in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries.

The fourteen saints

The standard group consists of three virgin martyrs—Barbara, Catherine of Alexandria, and Margaret of Antioch—along with Acacius, Blaise, Christopher, Cyriacus, Denis, Erasmus (Elmo), Eustace, George, Giles, Pantaleon, and Vitus. Each helper was invoked against particular afflictions: Blaise against throat diseases, Erasmus against abdominal pain, Vitus against epilepsy, Christopher against plague and sudden death, Denis against headache, and so on.

Cult and shrines

According to tradition, the cult was boosted by a vision in 1445–46 at Langheim Abbey in Franconia, where the Christ Child was said to appear surrounded by the fourteen saints. The pilgrimage basilica of Vierzehnheiligen ("Fourteen Saints") near Bad Staffelstein, designed by Balthasar Neumann in the eighteenth century, remains the most famous shrine of the group. The devotion spread through German-speaking lands and was carried abroad by emigrants, though the collective feast was removed from the general Roman calendar in 1969 while local observances continued.
