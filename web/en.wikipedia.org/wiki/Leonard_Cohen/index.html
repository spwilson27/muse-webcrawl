Leonard Cohen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Leonard Cohen on stage in 2008, aged 73, in a dark suit and fedora, tipping his hat to the crowd on the comeback tour]

Leonard Norman Cohen (21 September 1934 – 7 November 2016) was a Canadian singer-songwriter, poet and novelist whose baritone meditations on love, sex, God and power made him popular music's resident sage. Already an acclaimed poet and the novelist of Beautiful Losers (1966) when he turned to song at 32, he recorded Songs of Leonard Cohen (1967) and spent fifty years refining a body of work — 'Suzanne', 'Bird on the Wire', 'So Long, Marianne', 'Famous Blue Raincoat', 'Hallelujah' — that younger artists treat as scripture.

'Hallelujah' (1984), little noticed on its album Various Positions, became through John Cale's and Jeff Buckley's versions one of the most covered songs in the world, its verses endlessly re-edited for weddings, funerals and talent shows — a fate its author, who wrote dozens of verses for it, viewed with amusement.

Poet to singer

Born in Montreal to a Jewish family (his grandfather a Talmudic scholar), Cohen published Let Us Compare Mythologies at 22 and lived the bohemian stations — London, the Greek island of Hydra (where Marianne Ihlen inspired the songs), New York's Chelsea Hotel. Columbia's John Hammond signed him in 1966; the albums came slowly, the tours legendary for their duration and courtesy.

The tower of song, late

Cohen spent 1994–99 in a Zen monastery on Mount Baldy (ordained as the monk Jikan), emerged to find his manager had emptied his accounts, and at 73 returned to the road — the 2008–13 tours, three-and-a-half-hour shows of kneeling, skipping and gratitude, grossed over $100 million and restored both fortune and fame. He died in Los Angeles in November 2016, having released You Want It Darker three weeks before, its title track winning a posthumous Grammy.
