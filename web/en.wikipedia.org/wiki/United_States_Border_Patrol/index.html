The United States Border Patrol (USBP) is a federal law enforcement agency within U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), a component of the Department of Homeland Security. Its primary mission is to detect and prevent the illegal entry of persons and contraband into the United States between official ports of entry, along roughly 6,000 miles of international land border with Mexico and Canada and additional coastal areas.

History

The Border Patrol was established in 1924 under the Department of Labor, initially to interdict liquor smugglers during Prohibition and to enforce new immigration restrictions. It moved to the Department of Justice in 1940 and, after the September 11 attacks, to the newly created Department of Homeland Security in 2003, when it became part of CBP. The agency expanded rapidly in the 1990s and 2000s, adopting strategies such as "prevention through deterrence" in urban corridors like El Paso and San Diego.

Organization

Agents operate from stations organized into sectors, such as the Rio Grande Valley, Tucson, and El Paso sectors on the southwest border. Specialized units include BORTAC (the Border Patrol Tactical Unit), BORSTAR (search, trauma, and rescue), horse patrols, and checkpoint operations on highways near the border.
