The Köchel catalogue (German: Köchel-Verzeichnis) is a chronological catalogue of the compositions of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, compiled by Ludwig Ritter von Köchel and first published in 1862. Works are identified by their Köchel numbers, abbreviated "K." or "KV", such as the "Jupiter" Symphony, K. 551.

History

Köchel, an Austrian musicologist, botanist, and geologist, spent years gathering and organising Mozart's output, assigning numbers roughly in order of composition. The catalogue has been revised several times, notably in a third edition by Alfred Einstein (1937) and a sixth edition edited by Franz Giegling, Gerd Sievers, and Alexander Weinmann (1964), which renumbered some works or moved them to appendices while retaining the familiar numbers where possible.

Use

Köchel numbers are the standard reference for Mozart's works, preferred over opus numbers because Mozart's compositions were published irregularly during his lifetime. Later editions distinguish authentic works from doubtful and spurious ones, and newly discovered compositions have been added with suffixed numbers.
