West Sussex is a ceremonial and non-metropolitan county in South East England, on the English Channel coast. Its county town is Chichester, its only city, and its largest town is Crawley. The county was created in 1974 from the western part of the historic county of Sussex.

Geography

The chalk hills of the South Downs cross the county from west to east and form, since 2010, part of the South Downs National Park; Black Down (280 m) is the county's highest point. North of the Downs lies the wooded Weald, while the coastal plain hosts the resorts and towns of Worthing, Littlehampton and Bognor Regis, and the port of Shoreham. Gatwick Airport, the UK's second busiest, sits in the north-east of the county near Crawley.

History and economy

Historic sites include Arundel Castle, seat of the Duke of Norfolk; the Roman palace at Fishbourne; Chichester Cathedral; and Petworth House. The economy combines aviation and services around Gatwick, horticulture on the coastal plain, public services, and tourism, supported by one of the sunniest climates in Britain.
