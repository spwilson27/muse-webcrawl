Critically endangered

Critically Endangered (CR) is the highest-risk category assigned by the IUCN before extinction in the wild.

An IUCN Red List critically endangered (CR) species is one that has been categorised as facing an extremely high risk of extinction in the wild. It is the most severe conservation status for wild populations short of "extinct in the wild" and "extinct". As of recent editions of the Red List, several thousand species have been assessed as critically endangered, among them the vaquita, the Javan rhinoceros, the Amur leopard and many amphibians affected by habitat loss and disease.

Criteria

Species are assessed against quantitative criteria and assigned to the category if they meet thresholds such as a population reduction of at least 80–90 percent over ten years or three generations, an extent of occurrence below 100 km², a population of fewer than 250 mature individuals with continuing decline, fewer than 50 mature individuals, or a quantitative analysis showing at least a 50 percent probability of extinction in the wild within ten years or three generations.

Conservation

Critically endangered species are frequently the focus of intensive conservation measures, including legal protection, habitat restoration, captive breeding and reintroduction programmes. The category is also used by national and regional listing systems, and terms such as "critically endangered" appear in legislation including Australia's Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act.
