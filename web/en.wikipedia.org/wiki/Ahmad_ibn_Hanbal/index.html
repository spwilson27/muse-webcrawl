Ahmad ibn Hanbal

Ahmad ibn Hanbal
Born: November 780 CE (Rabi' al-awwal 164 AH), Baghdad, Abbasid Caliphate
Died: 2 August 855 CE (12 Rabi' al-awwal 241 AH), Baghdad
Known for: Founder of the Hanbali school of Islamic jurisprudence; compiler of the Musnad

Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hanbal (November 780 – 2 August 855 CE / 164–241 AH) was an Arab Muslim scholar, jurist, theologian and hadith traditionist from Baghdad. He became a leading figure of Sunni Islam, one of the most celebrated and influential in its history, and the eponym of the Hanbali school of Islamic law (fiqh), one of the four major schools of Sunni jurisprudence.

Life

Ibn Hanbal was born in Baghdad, where his family — of the Arab tribe of Banu Shayban — had settled after moving from Merv. Orphaned young, he studied the Qur'an and then travelled widely through Iraq, Syria, the Hejaz, and Yemen to collect hadith, studying under scholars including al-Shafi'i, from whom he learned jurisprudence. Returning to Baghdad, he became renowned for his prodigious memory of prophetic traditions, reportedly encompassing a million hadiths with their chains of transmission.

The Mihna

During the mihna, the inquisition instituted by the caliph al-Ma'mun to impose the Mu'tazilite doctrine that the Qur'an was created, Ibn Hanbal refused to affirm the doctrine, holding the Qur'an to be God's uncreated word. Under al-Mu'tasim he was imprisoned and flogged in 834 CE for his refusal, an ordeal that made him a popular hero of resistance and effectively discredited the inquisition, which was abandoned under al-Mutawakkil. His steadfastness became foundational to the self-image of Sunni traditionalism.

Works and legacy

His great compilation, the Musnad Ahmad ibn Hanbal, arranges some 27,000–30,000 hadiths by their first narrator among the Companions and is among the largest hadith collections. His legal opinions were systematised by his students into the Hanbali madhhab, historically dominant in parts of Arabia and influential through later figures such as Ibn Taymiyyah and, in the modern period, the movements of the Arabian Peninsula. He is venerated as one of the four great imams of Sunni law.
