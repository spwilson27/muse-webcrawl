The Bronze Age of Comic Books is an informal name for a period in the history of American comic books usually said to run from 1970 to 1985. It follows the Silver Age and precedes the Modern Age. The Bronze Age retained many of the conventions of the Silver Age, but superhero titles took on darker and more socially relevant storylines dealing with issues such as drug use, alcoholism, poverty and pollution — a shift popularly marked by stories such as Denny O'Neil and Neal Adams's Green Lantern/Green Arrow run and the 1971 Amazing Spider-Man story "The Night Gwen Stacy Died" (1973).

Characteristics

Comics of the era introduced many enduring characters, including Wolverine, the Punisher, Ghost Rider, Luke Cage and the revived X-Men under Chris Claremont. The 1971 revision of the Comics Code permitted depictions of drug use in a negative context and loosened restrictions on horror elements, fuelling titles such as Tomb of Dracula and Swamp Thing. The period also saw the growth of the direct market of specialist comic shops, the rise of independent publishers, and the emergence of the graphic novel with works such as Will Eisner's A Contract with God (1978).

End of the age

The Bronze Age is generally considered to end around 1985 with the publication of Crisis on Infinite Earths and, shortly after, The Dark Knight Returns and Watchmen, which ushered in the darker, more deconstructionist Modern Age.
