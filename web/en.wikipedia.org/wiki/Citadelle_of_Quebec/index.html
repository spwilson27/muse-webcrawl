Citadelle of Quebec

The Citadelle of Quebec (French: Citadelle de Québec), also known as La Citadelle, is an active Canadian Armed Forces installation and the secondary official residence of the Governor General of Canada. It is located atop Cap Diamant, adjoining the Plains of Abraham in Quebec City, Quebec, and forms the eastern end of the Fortifications of Quebec.

History

The star-shaped fortress was built by the British between 1820 and 1850, under the Duke of Wellington's plan to defend the city against a possible American attack, and designed by Lieutenant-Colonel Elias Walker Durnford. It occupies the highest point in Quebec City at 105 metres above the St. Lawrence River. The site also includes earlier French colonial defences.

Today

The Citadelle is the home station of the Royal 22e Régiment (the "Van Doos"), and its museum holds regimental archives dating to the First World War. In summer it is known for its Changing of the Guard ceremony, featuring the regimental goat Batisse. The Governor General's residence at the Citadelle, in use since 1872, hosts official ceremonies and state visits.
