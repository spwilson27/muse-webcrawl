Werner Herzog (born Werner Stipetić, 5 September 1942) is a German filmmaker, actor, opera director and author. A leading figure of New German Cinema, he is regarded as one of the most innovative and influential filmmakers of his generation, known for films shot in extreme conditions and for protagonists with impossible dreams, delusions of grandeur or conflicts with nature. His distinctive deadpan voice-over narration in accented English has made him a recognisable cultural figure well beyond cinema.

Career

Herzog grew up in a remote Bavarian village and made his first film in 1962 while working night shifts as a welder to fund it. His international breakthrough came with Aguirre, the Wrath of God (1972), the first of five films with the volatile actor Klaus Kinski, also including Nosferatu the Vampyre (1979) and Fitzcarraldo (1982), during whose Amazonian production a full-size steamship was hauled over a hill — an ordeal documented in Les Blank's Burden of Dreams. Fitzcarraldo won Herzog the Best Director prize at Cannes.

Documentaries and later work

From the 1990s Herzog became equally celebrated for documentaries including Little Dieter Needs to Fly (1997), Grizzly Man (2005), Encounters at the End of the World (2007, Oscar-nominated), Cave of Forgotten Dreams (2010) and Fireball: Visitors from Darker Worlds (2020). He has pursued what he calls "ecstatic truth" rather than literal factual accuracy. Herzog has also acted, notably as the Client in The Mandalorian (2019), directed opera, and published books including the memoir Every Man for Himself and God Against All (2022).
