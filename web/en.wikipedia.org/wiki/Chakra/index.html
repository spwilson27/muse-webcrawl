Chakra

Chakras (Sanskrit: चक्र, cakra, "wheel, circle") are various focal points used in a variety of ancient meditation practices, collectively denominated as Tantra, part of the esoteric traditions of Hinduism and Buddhism. The concept is found in the early traditions of Hinduism. A chakra is believed to be a center of spiritual power or life energy in the human body, located at major intersections of the subtle channels (nadi) through which energy (prana) flows.

The seven-chakra system

The system most familiar in the West posits seven principal chakras arranged along the spine from the base to the crown of the head:

History

Chakra concepts appear in the later Upanishads and are elaborated in Tantric texts of the first millennium CE, where systems of four, five, six, or more centers are described. The six-plus-one scheme was popularized through the sixteenth-century Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa, translated into English by Sir John Woodroffe ("Arthur Avalon") in The Serpent Power (1919). In the twentieth century the system was adopted and reinterpreted by Theosophy and later by New Age movements, which associated each chakra with colors, psychological states, and physical organs.

Practices

Practices involving chakras include kundalini yoga, meditation on seed syllables (bija mantras) and geometric diagrams (yantra), breath control, and visualization. In kundalini practice, dormant energy coiled at the root chakra is awakened and raised through the central channel (sushumna) to the crown, culminating in liberation. Within modern alternative medicine, "chakra balancing" is offered as a healing modality, though the existence of chakras is not supported by anatomical science.
