Tercet

A tercet is a stanza of three lines of poetry, usually rhyming together or with another tercet. When all three lines rhyme (AAA), the tercet is called a triplet. Tercets are among the most flexible stanza forms in Western verse.

Forms

The best-known tercet form is terza rima, invented by Dante for the Divine Comedy: interlocking tercets rhyming ABA BCB CDC…, which propels the poem forward. The villanelle is built from five tercets plus a final quatrain, with two refrains. Haiku in English-language practice are printed as tercets of three lines, and the French tercet also appears in sonnet variants. Closed tercets are grammatically self-contained; enjambed tercets flow across stanza breaks.

Examples

Notable tercet poems include Percy Bysshe Shelley's "Ode to the West Wind" (in terza rima), Dylan Thomas's villanelle "Do not go gentle into that good night", and the tercets of Thomas Hardy's and Robert Frost's lyrics.
