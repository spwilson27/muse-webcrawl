Marina Abramović

Marina Abramović (born 30 November 1946) is a Serbian conceptual and performance artist. Her work explores body art, endurance art, the relationship between performer and audience, the limits of the body, and the possibilities of the mind. Active for more than five decades, she has been described as the "grandmother of performance art". She pioneered a new notion of identity by bringing in the participation of observers, focusing on "confronting pain, blood, and physical limits of the body".

Early life

Abramović was born in Belgrade, then part of Yugoslavia. Her parents were Yugoslav Partisans during the Second World War; her father Vojo became a commander in Tito's guard, and her mother Danica was a major in the army and later director of the Museum of Revolution and Art in Belgrade. Abramović studied painting at the Academy of Fine Arts in Belgrade (1965–1970) and completed postgraduate studies in Zagreb. She began staging performances in the early 1970s.

Early performances

In the Rhythm series (1973–1974), Abramović tested the limits of her body and the audience's responsibility. In Rhythm 0 (1974), performed in Naples, she stood passively for six hours beside a table of 72 objects — including feathers, flowers, a scalpel and a loaded pistol — with instructions that the audience could do anything to her; the performance escalated to genuine danger before it ended.

Collaboration with Ulay

From 1976 to 1988 Abramović collaborated with the German artist Ulay (Frank Uwe Laysiepen). Their works explored duality and the ego, including Relation in Space and Imponderabilia (1977), in which visitors squeezed between their naked bodies at a gallery entrance. The partnership ended with The Lovers (1988): each walked from opposite ends of the Great Wall of China and met in the middle to say goodbye.

Later work

Abramović won the Golden Lion for Best Artist at the 1997 Venice Biennale for Balkan Baroque, in which she scrubbed a pile of cow bones while singing folk songs. Her 2010 retrospective at the Museum of Modern Art in New York included The Artist Is Present: for the run of the exhibition she sat silently in the museum atrium, making eye contact with individual visitors who sat opposite her — in total over 736 hours. The work drew enormous public attention and a celebrated documentary film.

She founded the Marina Abramović Institute (MAI), devoted to long-duration performance, and developed the "Abramović Method" of exercises for performers and audiences. In 2023 she became the first woman artist to have a solo exhibition in the Royal Academy of Arts in London since its founding in 1768.
