Acute bronchitis

Acute bronchitis, also known as a chest cold, is short-term inflammation of the bronchi — the large and medium-sized airways — of the lungs. The most common symptom is a cough, which may bring up mucus, and the condition usually develops from a viral upper respiratory infection.

Signs and symptoms

Acute bronchitis typically begins with the symptoms of a common cold: runny nose, sore throat, fatigue, and mild fever. A cough then develops, often lasting one to three weeks; sputum may be clear, white, yellowish-green, and occasionally blood-tinged. Wheezing and shortness of breath can occur, along with chest discomfort from coughing. The cough may persist for several weeks even after other symptoms resolve.

Causes

More than 90 percent of cases are caused by viral infections, most often the same viruses that cause the common cold, including rhinoviruses, coronaviruses, influenza, and respiratory syncytial virus; the viruses spread through the air when people cough and by direct contact. Bacterial causes such as Mycoplasma pneumoniae and Bordetella pertussis account for a small minority. Risk factors include tobacco smoke, dust, and air pollution.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based mainly on symptoms and physical examination; chest X-ray is used to exclude pneumonia when pneumonia is suspected. Acute bronchitis is distinguished from chronic bronchitis, which involves a productive cough lasting at least three months in two consecutive years and is associated with COPD.

Treatment

Most cases are self-limited and resolve without specific treatment. Antibiotics are generally not recommended, as the condition is usually viral and unnecessary use contributes to antibiotic resistance. Management emphasizes rest, fluids, and symptomatic relief; honey and cough medicines may offer modest benefit in adults. Smoking cessation is advised.

Epidemiology

Acute bronchitis is one of the most common illnesses, affecting roughly 5 percent of adults annually, more often in autumn and winter, and is a leading reason for medical visits.
