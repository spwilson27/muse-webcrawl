Group action

In mathematics, a group action of a group G on a set X is a group homomorphism from G to the group of bijections of X. Equivalently, it is a way of describing the symmetries of X using the elements of G: each element of the group "acts" by permuting the points of the set, in a manner compatible with the group operation.

Definition

A (left) group action of G on X is a function G × X → X, written g·x, such that the identity element fixes every point, e·x = x, and the action respects the group product, (gh)·x = g·(h·x).

Orbits and stabilizers

The orbit of a point x is the set of all points g·x reachable from it; the orbits partition the set X. The stabilizer of x is the subgroup of elements fixing x. The orbit–stabilizer theorem relates the size of an orbit to the index of the stabilizer.

Types of actions

An action is transitive if there is a single orbit, faithful if distinct group elements act differently, free if only the identity fixes any point, and regular if it is both transitive and free.

Examples

The symmetric group acts on a set by permutation; a group acts on itself by multiplication (Cayley's theorem) and by conjugation; the rotation group of a polyhedron acts on its vertices, edges and faces. Group actions are central to Burnside's lemma and the counting of orbits, and to the study of symmetry in geometry and physics.
