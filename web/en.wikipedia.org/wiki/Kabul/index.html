Kabul (Pashto/Dari: کابل) is the capital and largest city of Afghanistan. Located in the eastern part of the country in a high valley of the Hindu Kush along the Kabul River, it is also the capital of Kabul Province. With a history stretching back more than 3,500 years, Kabul has sat at the crossroads of Central and South Asia, and has been ruled, occupied and repeatedly devastated by a succession of empires and armies.

History

Ancient and medieval periods

Kabul is among the oldest cities in the region, mentioned in the Rigveda and in Ptolemy's geography. It formed part of the Achaemenid, Hellenistic, Kushan and Hindu Shahi realms before its conquest by Muslim armies; later it fell to the Samanids, Ghaznavids, Ghurids, Timurids and Mughals. Babur, founder of the Mughal Empire, loved the city, made it his capital from 1504, and is buried in the Bagh-e Babur (Gardens of Babur), which still overlook Kabul.

Modern Afghan capital

In 1776 Timur Shah Durrani transferred the capital of the Durrani Empire from Kandahar to Kabul. During the nineteenth century the city was the focus of the Anglo-Afghan wars: British forces occupied it in 1839, suffered the catastrophic retreat of 1842, and returned in 1842 and again in 1879–80. In the twentieth century Kabul became a relatively cosmopolitan capital under Kings Amanullah and Zahir Shah, with modern districts rising west of the old city.

War since 1978

The Saur Revolution (1978) and the Soviet invasion (1979) began four decades of conflict. After the Soviet withdrawal, fighting among mujahideen factions in 1992–96 reduced much of Kabul to ruins and killed tens of thousands. The Taliban ruled the city from 1996 until the United States-led intervention of late 2001. Under the Islamic Republic, Kabul grew explosively — its population multiplying several-fold — despite insurgent bombings. On 15 August 2021 Taliban forces re-entered Kabul as the republic collapsed, and the city has since been governed under the Taliban's restored Islamic Emirate.

Geography and the modern city

Kabul sits at about 1,790 metres above sea level in a narrow valley walled by steep mountains; the Kabul River flows through the city centre toward the Indus. Winters are cold and snowy, summers hot and dry. The old city on the south bank contains the Bala Hissar fortress, the Murad Khani quarter and historic mosques and bazaars, including the Chicken Street market; newer districts spread across the surrounding hillsides, much of them informally built. Landmarks include the Darul Aman Palace, the National Museum of Afghanistan, and Kabul International Airport.
