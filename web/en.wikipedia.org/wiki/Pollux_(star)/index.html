Pollux (star)

Bayer designation: β Geminorum

Constellation: Gemini

Apparent magnitude: 1.14

Spectral type: K0 III

Distance: ≈ 34 light-years

Pollux is the brightest star in the constellation of Gemini. It has the Bayer designation β Geminorum, which is Latinised to Beta Geminorum and abbreviated Beta Gem or β Gem. Despite its "beta" designation it outshines the "alpha" star, Castor. With an apparent visual magnitude of 1.14, it is the 17th brightest star in the night sky.

Characteristics

Pollux is an orange-hued giant star of spectral type K0 III, located about 34 light-years from the Sun. It is roughly nine times the radius of the Sun and about twice as massive, shining with around 43 times the Sun's luminosity from a surface with an effective temperature of about 4,866 K. It has evolved off the main sequence after exhausting the hydrogen in its core and now fuses helium.

Planetary system

In 2006 the existence of an extrasolar planet orbiting Pollux was confirmed. Designated Pollux b (and later named Thestias), it is a gas giant with a mass at least about 2.3 times that of Jupiter, orbiting at a distance of roughly 1.6 astronomical units with a period of about 590 days. It was detected by radial velocity measurements made over many years.

Nomenclature and mythology

The name Pollux refers to the twin brother of Castor in Greek and Roman mythology, the Dioscuri. In 2015 the International Astronomical Union approved the name "Pollux" formally for this star, and the planet's name Thestias derives from a patronymic of Leda, mother of the twins.
