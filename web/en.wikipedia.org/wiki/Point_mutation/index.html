Point mutation

A point mutation is a genetic mutation where a single nucleotide base is changed, inserted, or deleted from a DNA or RNA sequence of an organism's genome. Point mutations can arise spontaneously during DNA replication or be induced by mutagens such as radiation and certain chemicals.

Types

Substitutions replace one base with another. Transitions exchange a purine for a purine or a pyrimidine for a pyrimidine; transversions swap a purine for a pyrimidine or vice versa. Within a protein-coding sequence, substitutions are classified by their effect: silent (synonymous) mutations leave the amino acid unchanged; missense mutations change one amino acid; and nonsense mutations create a premature stop codon. Insertions and deletions of a single base shift the reading frame, producing a frameshift mutation with usually severe consequences.

Consequences

Most point mutations are neutral, but some cause disease: a single A→T transversion in the β-globin gene causes sickle cell disease. Point mutations are also the substrate of molecular evolution, and the rate at which they accumulate underlies the molecular clock used to date evolutionary divergences.
