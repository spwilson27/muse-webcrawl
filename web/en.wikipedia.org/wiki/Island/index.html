Island

[Image: Aerial view of a tropical island surrounded by reef]
Aerial view of a tropical island surrounded by reef

An island or isle is a piece of subcontinental land completely surrounded by water. Very small islands such as emergent land features on atolls can be called islets, skerries, cays or keys. A grouping of geographically or geologically related islands, such as the Philippines, is called an archipelago.

The largest island in the world is Greenland, at about 2.16 million square kilometres; Australia is larger but is regarded as a continent. Other very large islands include New Guinea, Borneo, Madagascar, Baffin Island and Sumatra.

Types of islands

Continental islands were once part of a continent and were separated by rising seas or rifting; examples include Great Britain, Borneo and Madagascar. Oceanic islands are typically volcanic in origin and were never part of a continent, such as Hawaii, Iceland and the Galápagos. Coral atolls form as volcanic islands subside, leaving rings of low coral land around a lagoon. Artificial islands are built by humans, for example for airports and ports.

Island biogeography

Because of their isolation, islands often evolve unique flora and fauna, a phenomenon studied as island biogeography. Famous examples include the finches of the Galápagos, the lemurs of Madagascar and the moa of New Zealand. Island species are often highly vulnerable to introduced predators and habitat change.
