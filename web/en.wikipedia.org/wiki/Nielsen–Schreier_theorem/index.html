Nielsen–Schreier theorem

In group theory, the Nielsen–Schreier theorem states that every subgroup of a free group is itself a free group. It is named after Jakob Nielsen and Otto Schreier.

Statement

If F is a free group and H is a subgroup of F, then H is free. If moreover F is free on n generators and H has finite index k in F, then H is free on k(n − 1) + 1 generators — the Schreier index formula. Nielsen proved the result for finitely generated subgroups in 1921 using the combinatorial method now called Nielsen transformations; Schreier proved the general case in 1926.

Proofs

A short modern proof uses algebraic topology: a free group is the fundamental group of a bouquet of circles; a subgroup corresponds to a covering space of that graph, which is itself a graph, and the fundamental group of any graph is free. A spanning tree of the covering graph supplies a free basis for the subgroup, and the index formula follows from the Euler characteristic of the graph. A purely algebraic proof uses group actions on trees (Bass–Serre theory): a group acting freely on a tree is free.

Significance

The theorem shows that free groups are exceptionally well behaved — by contrast, subgroups of free abelian groups need not behave so simply in higher-rank analogues. It is a cornerstone of combinatorial group theory and of the study of free groups via covering spaces.
