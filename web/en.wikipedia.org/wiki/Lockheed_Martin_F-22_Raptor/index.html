The Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor is an American single-seat, twin-engine, all-weather stealth tactical fighter aircraft developed for the United States Air Force. Designed primarily as an air-superiority fighter, the F-22 combines radar-evading shaping and materials with supercruise (sustained supersonic flight without afterburners), extreme agility aided by thrust-vectoring engines, and integrated sensor fusion. It was the world's first operational fifth-generation fighter.

Development

The F-22 emerged from the Advanced Tactical Fighter programme launched in the 1980s to counter new Soviet fighters such as the Su-27 and MiG-29. Lockheed's YF-22 prototype defeated Northrop's YF-23 in the 1991 fly-off competition, and the production F-22 first flew in 1997. Originally planned in much larger numbers, the programme was cut repeatedly as costs rose and the Cold War threat receded; Congress also barred exports, even to close allies. Production ended in 2011 after 195 aircraft (including test aircraft), and the last Raptor was delivered in 2012.

Capabilities and service

Powered by two Pratt & Whitney F119 engines with two-dimensional thrust-vectoring nozzles, the F-22 can supercruise at about Mach 1.5 and reach speeds above Mach 2. Its AN/APG-77 active electronically scanned array radar and passive sensors are fused into a single picture for the pilot, and armament—including AIM-120 AMRAAM and AIM-9 Sidewinder missiles and a 20 mm cannon—is carried internally to preserve stealth. The Raptor entered service in December 2005 and flew its first combat missions in 2014, striking Islamic State targets in Syria, and has since served in air-defence and deterrence roles worldwide. It is expected to remain in service into the 2030s and beyond, with a sixth-generation successor under development through the Next Generation Air Dominance programme.
