Salalah (Arabic: صَلَالَة, romanized: Ṣalālah) is the capital and largest city of the southern Omani governorate of Dhofar. It is the second-largest city in the Sultanate of Oman and the traditional residence of the Omani royal family; Sultan Qaboos bin Said was born in Salalah in 1940.

Climate and the khareef

Salalah is famous in Arabia for its khareef — the summer monsoon season from roughly June to September, when the southwest monsoon brings drizzle, fog and cool temperatures (around 25 °C) and transforms the surrounding mountains into green, waterfall-drained hillsides. The Khareef Festival draws hundreds of thousands of visitors from across the Gulf. Outside the khareef, the climate is hot and sunny, tempered by sea breezes.

History

The region around modern Salalah was a centre of the ancient frankincense trade; the ruined port of Sumhuram at Khor Rori, a UNESCO World Heritage site, dates to the first centuries BC. Al-Baleed, another ancient port city near modern Salalah, flourished from the 8th to the 16th centuries. During the Dhofar Rebellion (1964–1976), Salalah was a key government stronghold, and the Battle of Mirbat was fought nearby in 1972.

Economy and transport

The Port of Salalah, at Raysut, is one of the world's busiest transshipment ports and the centre of a free zone. Salalah Airport handles domestic and international flights, and the city remains a hub for frankincense, coconut and banana production from the coastal plain.

Tourism

Attractions include the Museum of the Frankincense Land at Al-Baleed Archaeological Park, the blowholes at Mughsail Beach, the tomb of Job (Nabi Ayyub) in the mountains, and nearby Wadi Darbat.
