Pacific Tsunami Warning Center

Founded1949
HeadquartersPearl Harbor, Hawaii, United States
Parent agencyNational Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA)
Area servedPacific Ocean basin

The Pacific Tsunami Warning Center (PTWC) is a tsunami warning centre operated by the United States National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration in Hawaii. Established in 1949 following the devastating 1946 Aleutian Islands tsunami that killed more than 150 people in Hawaii, it monitors seismic activity and sea-level gauges across the Pacific Ocean and issues tsunami warnings, watches, and advisories.

Operations

PTWC scientists analyse earthquake data within minutes of an event to determine whether a tsunami may have been generated, using a network of DART deep-ocean buoys and coastal tide gauges to confirm wave formation. It serves as an international warning centre for Pacific-rim nations and as the domestic warning centre for Hawaii and U.S. Pacific territories.

Notable events

The center issued warnings during major events including the 1960 Valdivia earthquake, the 2004 Indian Ocean earthquake, the 2010 Chile earthquake, and the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami in Japan.
