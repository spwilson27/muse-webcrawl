Doric Greek

Doric was a dialect group of Ancient Greek, spoken in the southern and eastern Peloponnese, Crete, Rhodes, parts of the southern Aegean, some cities on the Anatolian coast, Sicily and southern Italy (Magna Graecia), including Sparta, Corinth, Argos, Syracuse and Tarentum. Its varieties are conventionally grouped with Northwest Greek under the label "West Greek".

Characteristics

Doric is distinguished from Attic-Ionic by features such as the retention of long ā where Attic has ē (e.g., Doric mātēr versus Attic mētēr, "mother"), the retention of -ti where Attic assibilated to -si, distinctive verb endings such as first-person plural -mes, and characteristic vocabulary and contract vowel outcomes. Inscriptions show considerable local variation between, for example, Laconian (Spartan), Corinthian, Cretan and Rhodian forms.

Literature

Although most classical Greek literature was written in Attic or Ionic, Doric was used for the choral odes of tragedy and for the poetry of writers such as Pindar (in a literary Doric), Alcman, Stesichorus and the Syracusan comic poet Epicharmus, as well as the bucolic poetry of Theocritus. The mathematician Archimedes of Syracuse also wrote in Doric.

Descendants

Tsakonian, still spoken in a small area of the eastern Peloponnese, is generally considered to descend from ancient Laconian Doric rather than from the Koine, making it the only modern Greek variety with Doric ancestry.
