Ship registration

Ship registration is the process by which a ship is documented and given the nationality of the country to which it has been documented. The nationality allows a ship to travel internationally, as it is proof of ownership of the vessel. International law requires that every merchant ship be registered in a country, called its flag state. A ship is subject to the laws of its flag state, which exercises regulatory control over it and is obliged to inspect it regularly, certify its equipment and crew, and issue safety and pollution-prevention documents.

History and practice

Registration developed from the practice of flagging vessels to identify their origin for trade and neutrality; in Britain, the Navigation Acts and the Merchant Shipping Act 1854 established modern registers and certificates of registry. To be registered, a vessel is surveyed by a classification society or the flag administration to verify compliance with construction and equipment standards, measured for tonnage, and issued with a certificate of registry recording its name, official number, dimensions, tonnage, and owners' names. The ship's port of registry is marked on its stern, and it flies the ensign of its flag state.

Open registries and flags of convenience

Many states operate open registries, which allow foreign-owned ships to register with little connection to the state. Such vessels are said to fly flags of convenience (FOC). The largest registries by tonnage are Panama, Liberia, and the Marshall Islands. Owners choose open registries for lower taxes and fees, cheaper crewing under more flexible labour rules, and less restrictive regulation; critics, including the International Transport Workers' Federation, argue that some FOC states enforce safety and labour standards poorly, and the "genuine link" between ship and flag state required by the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea is often nominal. Bareboat charter registration allows a ship to be temporarily registered in a second state while chartered. Registers are also maintained for small pleasure craft in many countries.
