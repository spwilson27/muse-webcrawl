The New Routemaster, originally referred to as the New Bus for London, is a hybrid diesel-electric double-decker bus designed for and operated in London. Manufactured by Wrightbus of Northern Ireland and designed with the Heatherwick Studio, it entered service in February 2012 and was consciously styled as a successor to the beloved AEC Routemaster of 1956, whose open rear platform it revived in modern form. The project was a flagship pledge of Mayor Boris Johnson, and the buses are consequently nicknamed "Borismasters".

Design

The bus has three doors and two staircases — including a glazed, curving rear stair — and originally operated with a conductor so passengers could hop on and off the open platform. Its series-hybrid drivetrain uses a small diesel engine to generate electricity for the traction motor, offering substantial fuel and emissions savings over conventional buses of the time. Transport for London owns the design, an unusual departure from the normal model in which operators buy off-the-shelf buses.

Service history

A fleet of 1,000 was ordered, and the type spread across central London routes. The open platform proved costly and vulnerable to fare evasion, and from 2020 the rear doors were locked out of use between stops and conductor operation ended, making the buses effectively conventional. Production ended in 2017. Despite early criticisms of cost and of high summer temperatures before opening windows were retrofitted, the New Routemaster has become a familiar part of the London streetscape.
