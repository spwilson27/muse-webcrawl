Scotland the Brave

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"Scotland the Brave" (Scottish Gaelic: Alba an Àigh) is a Scottish patriotic song and one of the main contenders to be considered the unofficial national anthem of Scotland. The tune dates from the early twentieth century, while the best-known lyrics were written around 1950 by the journalist Cliff Hanley for the singer Robert Wilson as part of a show for the Glasgow Empire Theatre.

History and use

The melody was published under the title "Scotland the Brave" by 1911 in a collection of pipe music, though its origins are uncertain. It became one of the most recognisable pipe tunes in the world, a signature march of Scottish regiments and a staple of pipe bands everywhere. It served as the anthem for the Scottish team at the Commonwealth Games from 1958 until 2006, when it was replaced by "Scotland the Brave" polling behind "Flower of Scotland"; the latter is also used by the national football and rugby teams.

Lyrics

Hanley's lyrics celebrate Scotland's landscape and martial heritage, with the famous refrain: "Towering in gallant fame, / Scotland my mountain hame, / High may your proud standards gloriously wave, / Land of my high endeavour, / Land of the shining river, / Land of my heart for ever, / Scotland the brave." Because Scotland has no officially adopted national anthem, public polls over the years have divided mainly between "Scotland the Brave" and "Flower of Scotland".

In popular culture

The tune is heard worldwide at graduations, Highland games, tattoos, and St Andrew's Day celebrations, and was the regimental march of several Commonwealth units of Scottish heritage. Its rousing character has made it a byword for Scottish musical identity abroad.
