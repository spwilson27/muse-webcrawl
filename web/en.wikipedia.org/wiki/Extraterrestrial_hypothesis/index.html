Extraterrestrial hypothesis

Image placeholder: Depiction of a UFO as an alien spacecraft

The extraterrestrial hypothesis (ETH) proposes that some unidentified flying objects (UFOs), now often termed unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP), are spacecraft piloted or controlled by extraterrestrial life. It is the most popular interpretation of UFO reports in public culture, though it is not supported by scientific consensus, and most investigated UFO cases are identified as conventional objects or phenomena.

History

Speculation linking "flying saucers" to alien visitors arose with the wave of sightings beginning in 1947, amid Cold War anxieties and popular science fiction. Some early official studies, and figures such as Donald Keyhoe, promoted the hypothesis; the 1969 Condon Report concluded that further scientific study of UFOs was unlikely to be productive. Later advocates such as astronomer J. Allen Hynek argued that a residue of unexplained cases merited investigation.

Assessment

Critics note the absence of publicly verified physical evidence for extraterrestrial craft and the enormous distances involved in interstellar travel; rival explanations include misidentification, hoaxes, secret military aircraft, and the psychosocial hypothesis. Government programmes in the United States (Project Blue Book, and later the Pentagon's UAP task force and AARO) have reported no verified evidence of extraterrestrial technology, while acknowledging some incidents remain unexplained.
