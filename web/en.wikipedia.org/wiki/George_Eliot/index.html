George Eliot

Mary Ann Evans (22 November 1819 – 22 December 1880), known by her pen name George Eliot, was an English novelist, poet, journalist and translator, and one of the leading writers of the Victorian era.

George Eliot
Born: Mary Ann Evans, 22 Nov 1819, Nuneaton, Warwickshire
Died: 22 Dec 1880, London
Notable works: Middlemarch, Adam Bede, The Mill on the Floss, Silas Marner, Daniel Deronda

Life

Evans grew up in Warwickshire, lost her evangelical faith through wide reading, and moved to London, where she became assistant editor of the Westminster Review and translated Strauss and Feuerbach. She lived openly with philosopher George Henry Lewes from 1854 until his death in 1878, a union scandalous to Victorian society. She adopted a male pen name to ensure serious reception and shield their private life.

Novels

After Scenes of Clerical Life (1857) came Adam Bede (1859), The Mill on the Floss (1860), Silas Marner (1861), Romola (1863), Felix Holt (1866), and Daniel Deronda (1876). Her masterpiece Middlemarch (1871–72), subtitled "A Study of Provincial Life", intertwines many characters' aspirations and failures and is widely regarded as one of the greatest novels in English — Virginia Woolf called it "one of the few English novels written for grown-up people".

Themes

Eliot's fiction combines psychological depth, moral sympathy, social analysis and philosophical reflection, set largely in provincial England amid political reform and scientific change.
