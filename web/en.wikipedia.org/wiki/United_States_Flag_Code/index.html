United States Flag Code

Flag Code
Enacted: 22 June 1942
Codified: 4 U.S.C. §§ 1–10

The United States Flag Code establishes advisory rules for display and care of the national flag of the United States of America. It is Chapter 1 of Title 4 of the United States Code (4 U.S.C. § 1 et seq.). The code is a federal law, but there are no penalties for non-compliance, and following the Supreme Court's decision in Texas v. Johnson (1989), enforcement against civilians would be unconstitutional as an infringement of free speech.

Provisions

The code provides that the flag should be flown from sunrise to sunset, may be flown at night if properly illuminated, should not be flown in inclement weather unless an all-weather flag, and should never touch the ground. It specifies the flag's position of honor relative to other flags and prescribes the conduct during the hoisting, lowering, and passing of the flag, including standing at attention and saluting during the national anthem.

Half-staff

The code sets rules for flying the flag at half-staff upon the death of principal figures of the U.S. government and on designated days such as Memorial Day, when it flies at half-staff until noon.

Disposal

When a flag is worn or soiled, the code says it "should be destroyed in a dignified way, preferably by burning." Veterans' organizations such as the American Legion and the Boy Scouts conduct flag retirement ceremonies.

History

The code grew out of flag etiquette adopted by the National Flag Conference in 1923 and was adopted by Congress on Flag Day, June 22, 1942.
