Laysan albatross

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Laysan albatross (Phoebastria immutabilis) is a large seabird that ranges across the North Pacific. It is a medium-sized member of the albatross family, with a wingspan of about 2 metres (6.6 ft). It is named for its main breeding colony on the island of Laysan in the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands; the great majority of the world population breeds in the Hawaiian archipelago, with smaller colonies on islands off Japan and Mexico.

Description and behaviour

The plumage is dark grey-brown above with a white head, neck, and underparts, and a distinctive dark patch around the eye. Laysan albatrosses feed mainly at night on squid, fish, and crustaceans, ranging thousands of kilometres over the open ocean from their colonies. They mate for life, perform elaborate courtship dances, and lay a single egg per breeding attempt, which both parents incubate for about 65 days. Chicks fledge at roughly five and a half months.

Wisdom

The best-known individual is Wisdom, a female banded in 1956 and still breeding into the 2020s, making her the oldest known wild bird in the world — at least in her early seventies — and a celebrated example of albatross longevity.

Conservation

The species is listed as Near Threatened. Threats include ingestion of floating plastic, bycatch in longline fisheries, introduced predators at colonies, and sea-level rise flooding low-lying nesting atolls. The population numbers roughly 1.6 million mature individuals and is broadly stable.
