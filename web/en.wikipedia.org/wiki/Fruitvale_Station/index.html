Fruitvale Station is a 2013 American biographical drama film written and directed by Ryan Coogler in his feature directorial debut. It is based on the events leading to the death of Oscar Grant, a 22-year-old man who was killed in the early morning hours of New Year's Day 2009 by BART police officer Johannes Mehserle at the Fruitvale station in Oakland, California.

The film stars Michael B. Jordan as Grant, with Melonie Diaz as his girlfriend Sophina and Octavia Spencer as his mother Wanda. It follows the last day of Grant's life as he tries to get his job back, reconnect with his family, and celebrate the new year, intercut with flashbacks to his time in prison.

Release and reception

Fruitvale Station premiered at the 2013 Sundance Film Festival under the title Fruitvale, where it won the Grand Jury Prize and the Audience Award for dramatic features. The Weinstein Company acquired distribution rights, and the film was released in the United States in July 2013, earning about $17 million on a budget of under $1 million. Critics praised Coogler's direction, Jordan's performance and the film's restrained, humanistic treatment of its subject. It was named one of the top ten films of 2013 by the National Board of Review and won the award for Best First Film at the Independent Spirit Awards.
