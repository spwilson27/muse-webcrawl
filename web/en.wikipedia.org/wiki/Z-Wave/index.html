Z-Wave

Z-Wave is a wireless communications protocol primarily used for residential and commercial building automation. It is a mesh network using low-energy radio waves to communicate from appliance to appliance, allowing for wireless control of smart home devices such as lighting, door locks, thermostats, security systems and sensors, with a centrally controlled hub or gateway.

History and governance

The protocol was developed by the Danish company Zensys, founded in 1999, and introduced in 2001 in the United States. Sigma Designs acquired Zensys in 2008, and in 2018 Silicon Labs acquired Sigma's Z-Wave business for approximately US$240 million. In 2019–2020 the Z-Wave specification was opened up through the Z-Wave Alliance as a ratified, multi-source wireless standard, and in 2020 the alliance became an independent standards development organisation. The Z-Wave Long Range (Z-Wave LR) specification, announced in 2020, extended range to over a mile point-to-point and supports up to 4,000 nodes on one network.

Technical characteristics

Z-Wave operates in the sub-gigahertz ISM bands — around 908–916 MHz in North America and 868 MHz in Europe — avoiding the congested 2.4 GHz band used by Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Zigbee, and giving good wall penetration. Classic Z-Wave mesh networks support up to 232 nodes with up to four hops of relaying; data rates range from 9.6 to 100 kbit/s. Devices use AES-128 encryption under the S2 security framework. Interoperability between vendors is a design goal, enforced through a certification programme; thousands of certified products are on the market.

Comparison

Z-Wave competes and coexists with Zigbee, Thread, Bluetooth Mesh and Wi-Fi-based systems in the smart-home market, and hubs often bridge several protocols. The Matter standard, launched in 2022, is designed to interoperate with Z-Wave through bridges.
