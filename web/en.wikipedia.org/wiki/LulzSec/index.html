LulzSec

Lulz Security, commonly abbreviated as LulzSec, was a black-hat computer hacking group that claimed responsibility for a string of high-profile attacks in the spring and summer of 2011, including breaches of Sony Pictures, Nintendo, the United States Senate website, the British Serious Organised Crime Agency and an FBI affiliate, InfraGard. The group's name combines "lulz" (internet slang for laughter at others' expense) with "security", and its motto was "Laughing at your security since 2011!"

History

LulzSec emerged in May 2011 from members associated with the Anonymous collective and its earlier Operation Payback, gaining attention with its attack on Fox.com and the publication of user data. Over a self-declared "50 days of lulz", the group publicised stolen databases, defaced websites and taunted security agencies on Twitter, attracting hundreds of thousands of followers. It announced its retirement on 25 June 2011, though some members continued under the Anonymous banner.

Arrests and aftermath

In March 2012 it was revealed that the group's leader, Hector Xavier Monsegur (known as "Sabu"), had been arrested in 2011 and had cooperated with the FBI, leading to the arrests of other members including Jake Davis ("Topiary"), Ryan Cleary, Ryan Ackroyd ("Kayla") and Mustafa Al-Bassam ("tflow"). Several members pleaded guilty in British and American courts and received prison sentences. LulzSec's spree prompted broad debate about hacktivism, website security practices and password reuse.
