José Efraín Ríos Montt (16 June 1926 – 1 April 2018) was a Guatemalan general and politician who served as de facto president of Guatemala from 1982 to 1983 after seizing power in a military coup. His brief regime was one of the bloodiest periods of Guatemala's 36-year civil war, marked by scorched-earth counterinsurgency campaigns against the Maya Ixil people that courts later characterised as genocide.

Rise to power

Ríos Montt had run for president in 1974 and was widely believed to have won, but the official result gave victory to his opponent. After converting to evangelical Protestantism, he returned to politics and, on 23 March 1982, joined a junta of junior officers that overthrew President Fernando Romeo Lucas García. He soon consolidated power as sole head of state.

Rule and counterinsurgency

Under plans such as Victoria 82, the army carried out massacres of indigenous villagers accused of supporting leftist guerrillas, destroyed hundreds of villages, and organised civil patrols. An estimated tens of thousands were killed during his 17 months in office. He was overthrown by his defence minister, Óscar Humberto Mejía Víctores, in August 1983.

Trial

In 2013 a Guatemalan court convicted Ríos Montt of genocide and crimes against humanity and sentenced him to 80 years in prison, but the Constitutional Court annulled the verdict days later on procedural grounds. A retrial was ongoing when he died in 2018.
