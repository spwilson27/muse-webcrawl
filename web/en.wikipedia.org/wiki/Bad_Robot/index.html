Bad Robot

Bad Robot is an American film and television production company founded by filmmaker J. J. Abrams. It is responsible for television series such as Alias, Lost, Fringe, Person of Interest, and Westworld, and for feature films including Cloverfield, Star Trek (2009), Super 8, and Star Wars: The Force Awakens. The company's name and logo come from a robot character created by Abrams; its production bumper shows a red robot stomping across a field, with the words "Bad Robot!" spoken by the dubbed voices of Abrams's children.

History

Bad Robot was formed by Abrams in 2001 in association with Paramount Pictures and Touchstone Television/ABC. Its first production was the spy drama Alias (2001–2006), followed by the phenomenon Lost (2004–2010), co-created by Abrams, Damon Lindelof, and Jeffrey Lieber. Bryan Burk served for many years as executive vice-president and a key producing partner. The company expanded into features with Mission: Impossible III (2006), Abrams's directorial debut, and built a long relationship with Paramount before signing a major overall deal with WarnerMedia in 2019.

Divisions and other work

Bad Robot launched an interactive/games division (Bad Robot Games) in partnership with Tencent, and a stage arm, Bad Robot Live, which co-produced Broadway shows. The company is headquartered in Santa Monica, California, in a distinctive building notable for housing its own recording facilities and a rooftop sign.
