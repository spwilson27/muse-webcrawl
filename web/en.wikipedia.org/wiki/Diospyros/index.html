Diospyros

KingdomPlantae
FamilyEbenaceae
GenusDiospyros L.
SpeciesOver 700

Diospyros is a genus of over 700 species of deciduous and evergreen trees, shrubs and small bushes in the family Ebenaceae. The genus includes the ebony trees, valued for their dense black timber, and the persimmons, grown for their edible fruit.

Distribution

Species of Diospyros are distributed pantropically, with the greatest diversity in the Indo-Pacific; a smaller number of species occur in temperate regions. Most are trees of lowland tropical forests.

Uses

Ebony — the heartwood of species such as Diospyros ebenum — is used for musical instruments, furniture and carving. Persimmon species, especially the Japanese persimmon Diospyros kaki and the American persimmon Diospyros virginiana, produce sweet fruit eaten fresh or dried; unripe fruit is highly astringent owing to tannins. Persimmon wood is also hard and was traditionally used for golf club heads and shuttles.

Ecology

Most species are dioecious, with separate male and female trees. The fruits are berries consumed by birds and mammals, which disperse the seeds.
