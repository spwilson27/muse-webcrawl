Karnaphuli River

The Karnaphuli River (Bengali: কর্ণফুলী, also spelled Karnafuli) is the largest and most important river in the Chattogram (Chittagong) region of south-eastern Bangladesh. It flows from the Lushai Hills of Mizoram in north-eastern India, through the Chittagong Hill Tracts, and empties into the Bay of Bengal at the port city of Chittagong.

Course

The river rises in the hills of Mizoram and runs south-west for roughly 270 kilometres. In the Rangamati district it is dammed at Kaptai, forming Kaptai Lake, the largest artificial lake in Bangladesh, used for hydroelectric power at the Karnaphuli Hydroelectric Station — the country's only major hydroelectric plant, commissioned in the 1960s. Below the dam the river winds through hills and plains past Rangunia and Boalkhali to Chittagong.

Economic importance

The Port of Chittagong, Bangladesh's principal seaport handling the overwhelming majority of its maritime trade, lies on the river's banks near its mouth. Bridges at Shah Amanat (Kalurghat) and elsewhere cross the river, and a road tunnel beneath the Karnaphuli — the Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman Tunnel, opened in 2023 — was the first river tunnel in South Asia. The river and its valley are central to the economy, fisheries and transport of the Chittagong region, though it faces pollution and siltation pressures.
