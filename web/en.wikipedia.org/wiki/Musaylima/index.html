Musaylima ibn Habib (Arabic: مسيلمة بن حبيب), denounced in Islamic tradition as Musaylima al-Kadhdhab ("Musaylima the Liar"), was a preacher from the Banu Hanifa tribe of al-Yamama who claimed prophethood in the final years of Muhammad's life and led the most powerful resistance to Medina during the Ridda wars.

Prophetic claim

Musaylima proclaimed revelations in rhymed prose in the name of al-Rahman ("the Most Merciful"), taught prayer and fasting, and attracted a large following among the agricultural settlements of Yamama. Islamic sources record that he wrote to Muhammad proposing they share authority over Arabia, and that Muhammad replied by addressing him as "the Liar". He is said to have allied by marriage with another prophetic claimant, Sajah bint al-Harith.

Death

After Muhammad's death in 632, the first caliph Abu Bakr sent an army under Khalid ibn al-Walid against Musaylima. At the hard-fought Battle of Yamama (Aqraba), in which many Quran memorizers were killed — a loss that spurred the first collection of the Quran — Musaylima was slain, traditionally by Wahshi ibn Harb, the same man who had killed Hamza at Uhud. His defeat broke the strongest of the apostasy movements and secured Muslim control of Arabia.
