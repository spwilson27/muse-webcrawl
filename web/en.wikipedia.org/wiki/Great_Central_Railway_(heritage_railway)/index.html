The Great Central Railway (GCR) is a heritage railway in Leicestershire, England, named after the company that originally built the route. The railway runs for 8¼ miles (13 km) between Loughborough and Leicester North, on part of the former Great Central Main Line, the last main line built from the north of England to London.

History

The Great Central Main Line opened in 1899 and was built to the European loading gauge by Sir Edward Watkin, who envisaged a future Channel Tunnel link. The line closed in stages during the 1960s under the Beeching cuts. Preservation began in 1969, and the preserved line reopened in stages during the 1970s.

Operations

The GCR is notable as the only double-track main-line heritage railway in the United Kingdom, allowing trains to pass each other at speed. It operates steam and diesel locomotives, with stations at Loughborough Central, Quorn & Woodhouse, Rothley and Leicester North. A major project, "Bridging the Gap", aims to reconnect the line to the Great Central Railway (Nottingham) across the Midland Main Line at Loughborough.
