Gelfand–Naimark theorem

In mathematics, the Gelfand–Naimark theorem is a central result of functional analysis, proved in 1943 by Israel Gelfand and Mark Naimark. It states that certain abstractly defined algebras — the commutative C*-algebras — can be realized concretely as algebras of continuous functions on a topological space.

Statement

For a commutative C*-algebra A with unit, the set of nonzero multiplicative linear functionals on A (its spectrum) is a compact Hausdorff space X, and the Gelfand transform gives an isometric *-isomorphism from A onto C(X), the algebra of continuous complex-valued functions on X. A noncommutative extension of the result, also due to Gelfand and Naimark with Segal's independent contribution, represents every C*-algebra as an algebra of operators on a Hilbert space.

Significance

The theorem founds the Gelfand duality between commutative C*-algebras and compact Hausdorff spaces, and underlies the modern interpretation of abstract algebras in operator theory, quantum mechanics and noncommutative geometry.
