Opening act

An opening act, also known as a warm-up act, support act or opener, is a musical group or performer that plays before the headline artist at a concert. In comedy and theatre, opening acts similarly precede and warm up the audience for the main performance.

Role in the music industry

Support slots are a key career ladder for emerging artists, exposing them to the headliner's larger audience; touring openers are usually arranged through agents, labels or the headliner's management, and the opener plays a shorter set, often with restricted use of the stage, lighting and sound system. Historically, some openers eclipsed their headliners — audiences at early 1960s tours, for example, famously demanded more from acts such as the Rolling Stones supporting established stars.

Other senses

In drama, the "opening act" can also mean the first act of a play. In stand-up comedy, openers and hosts perform short sets to settle the audience before features and headliners.
