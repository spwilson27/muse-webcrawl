Moral education

Moral education is the process by which children and young people are taught values, moral reasoning, and the dispositions needed to act ethically and participate in a community. It encompasses the deliberate instruction of morality in families, schools, and religious institutions, as well as the informal "hidden curriculum" conveyed by example and social practice. Moral education has been debated since antiquity, from Plato and Aristotle's emphasis on the cultivation of virtue to modern theories of moral development.

History

In classical Greece, education (paideia) aimed at forming virtuous citizens. Aristotle held that virtue is acquired by habituation under good laws and teachers. Religious traditions worldwide made moral instruction central to schooling for centuries. In the modern era, Émile Durkheim treated moral education as the means by which society transmits its collective norms, while in many countries the subject became secularised as civic or citizenship education.

Theories of moral development

Jean Piaget proposed that children's moral judgement develops from heteronomous respect for rules to autonomous cooperation. Lawrence Kohlberg extended this into a stage theory of moral reasoning, from pre-conventional obedience through conventional conformity to post-conventional principled reasoning, assessed through dilemmas such as the Heinz dilemma. Critics, including Carol Gilligan, argued that Kohlberg's justice-centred model neglected an "ethic of care", and others have questioned the universality and ordering of the stages.

Approaches in schools

Approaches include character education, which teaches virtues such as honesty and responsibility; values clarification, which helps students articulate their own values; discussion-based methods using moral dilemmas; and service learning. Some countries, such as Japan, teach moral education as a distinct school subject (dōtoku). Debates continue over whose values schools should teach and how religious and cultural diversity should be accommodated.
