The Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia (Croatian: Kraljevina Hrvatska i Slavonija; Hungarian: Horvát-Szlavón Királyság) was a nominally autonomous kingdom and constitutionally defined political structure within the Austrian Empire and, after 1868, within the Hungarian half of Austria-Hungary. It was formed by uniting the older kingdoms of Croatia and Slavonia.

Formation: the Nagodba of 1868

Following the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867, Croatia-Slavonia's status was settled by the Croatian–Hungarian Settlement (Nagodba) of 1868. It recognised Croatia-Slavonia as a distinct political nation with its own territory, parliament (the Sabor), and government headed by a ban appointed by the king on the proposal of the Hungarian prime minister. Croatian became the official language, and the kingdom retained autonomy in internal affairs, justice, religion, and education, while finance, trade, and defence remained joint affairs decided in Budapest.

Territory and population

The kingdom covered about 42,500 km², comprising Croatia proper, Slavonia, and (from 1881) the demilitarised Croatian Military Frontier. The city of Fiume (Rijeka) was administered separately as a corpus separatum attached to Hungary, a standing grievance in Zagreb. By 1910 the population was about 2.6 million, predominantly Croats with a large Serb minority.

Politics

Politics in the kingdom revolved around the tension between unionist parties accepting the Nagodba and national parties seeking greater autonomy or South Slav (Yugoslav) unity. Long-serving ban Károly Khuen-Héderváry (1883–1903) governed through electoral manipulation and press restrictions. The early 20th century saw the Croat-Serb Coalition win power in 1906 and increasing conflict with Vienna and Budapest, including the Zagreb treason trial of 1909.

Dissolution

On 29 October 1918, in the collapse of Austria-Hungary at the end of the First World War, the Sabor severed all legal ties with Austria and Hungary and proclaimed the kingdom part of the short-lived State of Slovenes, Croats and Serbs, which united with Serbia on 1 December 1918 to form the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (later Yugoslavia).
