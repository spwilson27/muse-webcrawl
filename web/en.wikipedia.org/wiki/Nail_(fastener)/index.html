Nail (fastener)

In woodworking and construction, a nail is a small object made of metal (or wood, called a tree nail or "trunnel") which is used as a fastener, as a peg to hang something, or sometimes as a decoration. Generally, nails have a sharp point on one end and a flattened head on the other end and can be driven into material, usually by means of a hammer or nail gun.

Nails are most commonly used to fasten pieces of wood together but are also used with plastic, drywall, masonry, and concrete. Nails are typically made of steel, but can also be made of stainless steel, iron, copper, aluminium, or bronze.

History

The history of the nail spans at least as far back as ancient Egypt, with bronze nails found dating to around 3400 BC. The Bible contains numerous references to nails, including the story of Jael driving a tent peg into the head of Sisera. The Romans made extensive use of nails; the Roman army left behind many tons of nails when it evacuated the fortress of Inchtuthil in Perthshire in the United Kingdom in 86–87 AD.

Until around 1800, nails were hand-made by a nailer or nail-smith using a hammer and anvil. Cut nails, machine-made from flat sheets of metal, became common in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. Wire nails, drawn from wire rod, largely displaced cut nails by the end of the 19th century because they were cheaper to produce.

Types

Common nail types include:

  • Common nail – general-purpose framing nail with a thick shank and wide head.
  • Box nail – similar to a common nail but with a thinner shank, less likely to split wood.
  • Finishing nail – small head that can be driven below the wood surface and hidden with putty.
  • Brad – a thin, small finishing nail.
  • Roofing nail – short nail with a large flat head, used for attaching shingles and roofing felt.
  • Masonry nail – hardened steel nail designed for concrete and brick.
  • Duplex nail – double-headed nail used for temporary structures such as concrete forms.
  • Ring-shank and spiral-shank nails – deformed shanks that increase holding power.

Sizes

In the United States, the length of a nail is designated by its "penny size", written with a number and the abbreviation d for penny (for example, 10d). The system originates in England in the 15th century and originally denoted the price of one hundred nails of that size.

Terminology

The parts of a nail are the head, the shank (or shaft), and the point. The diameter of the shank is described by its gauge. Nails are usually driven with a hammer, but pneumatic and powder-actuated nail guns are widely used on construction sites for speed.
