Diketone

A diketone is a molecule containing two ketone groups. The simplest diketone is diacetyl, also known as 2,3-butanedione. Diketones are classified by the spacing between the two carbonyl groups: 1,2-diketones (α-diketones), 1,3-diketones (β-diketones), and 1,4-diketones (γ-diketones) are the most important classes, each with characteristic reactivity.

1,3-Diketones

1,3-Diketones such as acetylacetone (pentane-2,4-dione) readily form enols, stabilised by conjugation and intramolecular hydrogen bonding, and deprotonate to give enolate anions that are excellent bidentate ligands for metal ions — the resulting metal acetylacetonates are widely used in catalysis and materials chemistry. They are commonly prepared by Claisen condensation of ketones with esters. The central carbon is acidic (pKa ≈ 9 for acetylacetone) and undergoes alkylation and condensation reactions, making 1,3-diketones versatile synthetic intermediates.

1,2-Diketones

Diacetyl gives butter its characteristic flavour and is also a byproduct of fermentation; concerns were raised about inhalation exposure ("popcorn lung", bronchiolitis obliterans) among flavouring workers. Benzil is a classic photoinitiator and undergoes the benzilic acid rearrangement in base. 1,2-Diketones react with diamines to form heterocycles such as quinoxalines.

1,4-Diketones

1,4-Diketones undergo the Paal–Knorr synthesis to give furans, pyrroles, and thiophenes depending on the reagent used. Hexane-2,5-dione is notable as the neurotoxic metabolite of n-hexane.

Synthesis

Besides Claisen condensation, diketones can be made by oxidation of diols or hydroxy ketones, by hydration of diynes, and by acyloin-type and Stetter reactions (particularly for 1,4-diketones).
