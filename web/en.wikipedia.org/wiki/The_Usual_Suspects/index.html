The Usual Suspects is a 1995 American neo-noir crime thriller film directed by Bryan Singer and written by Christopher McQuarrie. It stars Stephen Baldwin, Gabriel Byrne, Benicio del Toro, Kevin Pollak, Chazz Palminteri, Pete Postlethwaite, and Kevin Spacey.

Plot

The film follows the interrogation of Roger "Verbal" Kint, a small-time con man with cerebral palsy and one of only two survivors of a massacre and blaze aboard a ship docked at the Port of Los Angeles. In flashbacks narrated by Kint, five criminals—Keaton, McManus, Fenster, Hockney, and Kint—meet in a police line-up and are drawn into the orbit of the legendary and feared crime lord Keyser Söze, who coerces them into a suicidal mission against a rival gang's drug shipment. The story concludes with one of cinema's most famous twist endings, as the detective realises that Kint fabricated his account from details scattered around the interrogation room, and that Kint himself is Söze.

Title

The title is taken from a line in Casablanca ("Round up the usual suspects") and from a column in Spy magazine.

Reception and legacy

The film premiered at the 1995 Sundance Film Festival and was screened at Cannes. It won Academy Awards for Best Original Screenplay (McQuarrie) and Best Supporting Actor (Spacey), and is regularly ranked among the greatest mystery and twist-ending films ever made. Its ending is frequently cited in popular culture, including the line "The greatest trick the devil ever pulled was convincing the world he didn't exist."
