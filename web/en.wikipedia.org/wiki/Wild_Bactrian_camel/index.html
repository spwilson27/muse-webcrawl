The wild Bactrian camel (Camelus ferus) is a critically endangered species of camel found in remote parts of the Gobi and Taklamakan deserts of Mongolia and China. It is the wild ancestor-relative of the domestic Bactrian camel (Camelus bactrianus), from which it diverged hundreds of thousands of years ago.

Description

Like its domestic counterpart, the wild Bactrian camel has two humps. It is somewhat smaller and slimmer than the domestic form, with narrower humps and a flatter skull. It tolerates extreme temperatures, from bitter winter cold to summer heat above 40 °C, and can survive for long periods without water. Remarkably, some populations drink salty slush and brackish water that domestic camels refuse.

Population and threats

Fewer than roughly 1,000 individuals survive in the wild. Populations are found in the Great Gobi Strictly Protected Area in Mongolia and around Lop Nur in Xinjiang, China. Threats include habitat loss, mining, hunting, competition with livestock, and hybridisation with domestic camels. The species is listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List.

Conservation

The Wild Camel Protection Foundation and government reserves in both countries work to protect the remaining herds and their desert habitat, including migration corridors between water sources.
