Castaway

[Image: A castaway signalling from a desert island]
The castaway is a recurring figure in literature and popular culture
DefinitionA person who has been shipwrecked and stranded in an isolated place
Famous real casesAlexander Selkirk; the Essex crew; Poon Lim
Famous fictional casesRobinson Crusoe; the Swiss Family Robinson

A castaway is a person who is cast adrift or ashore, typically after a shipwreck or by being marooned, and stranded in an isolated place with few or no resources. Castaways must survive on what they can salvage, hunt, grow or improvise until rescued or until they can escape.

Notable castaways

The Scottish sailor Alexander Selkirk spent over four years (1704–1709) alone on an island in the Juan Fernández archipelago after asking to be put ashore; his story inspired Daniel Defoe's Robinson Crusoe (1719), the archetypal castaway novel. Other celebrated cases include the survivors of the whaleship Essex (1820), who resorted to cannibalism in open boats; Margarete/dissident castaways of the Age of Sail left deliberately on islands (marooning was also a punishment); and Poon Lim, a Chinese seaman who survived 133 days alone on a raft in the Atlantic in 1942–43, a record for survival on a life raft. In 1965 six Tongan schoolboys, the "Tongan castaways", survived 15 months on the island of ʻAta and were rescued by captain Peter Warner.

In culture

The castaway narrative — the "Robinsonade" — became a major literary genre, from The Swiss Family Robinson (1812) to Lord of the Flies (1954), and the 2000 film Cast Away starring Tom Hanks. Castaways symbolise self-reliance, isolation and the stripping away of civilisation.
