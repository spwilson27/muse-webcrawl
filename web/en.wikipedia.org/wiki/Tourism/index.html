Tourism is travel for pleasure or business, and the industry that serves travellers. The World Tourism Organization defines a tourist as a person travelling to and staying in places outside their usual environment for not more than one consecutive year for leisure, business or other purposes. Tourism may be domestic or international; international tourism is one of the world's largest categories of trade in services, and destinations from Paris to Elmina compete for its receipts.

History

Leisure travel has roots in the pilgrim routes of the ancient world and the Grand Tour undertaken by young European aristocrats from the 17th century. The modern industry began with Thomas Cook's organised excursions by rail from 1841, grew with paid holidays and the railways, and exploded after the Second World War with charter flights, package holidays and the jet age. International arrivals grew from 25 million in 1950 to about 1.4 billion in 2019. The COVID-19 pandemic cut arrivals by more than 70 percent in 2020 — the industry's worst crisis — before a rapid recovery to near pre-pandemic levels by 2023–2024.

Types and impacts

Tourism takes many forms: cultural and heritage tourism, beach and cruise holidays, ecotourism, adventure tourism, medical tourism, gastronomy, sports events and business travel. It brings income, employment and infrastructure, but also congestion, environmental pressure and "overtourism" in hotspots such as Venice, Barcelona and Amsterdam, which have introduced visitor caps, taxes and marketing limits. Sustainable tourism seeks to spread benefits while protecting the places — and the residents — that travellers come to see.
