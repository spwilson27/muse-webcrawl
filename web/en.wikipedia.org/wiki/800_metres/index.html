The 800 metres is a common track running event. It is the shortest commonly run middle-distance event. Run over two laps of a standard outdoor 400 m track, it demands a blend of sprint speed and endurance and is often described as one of the most demanding events in athletics. Athletes run in lanes for the first part of the race (typically through the first turn, the "break line") before being allowed to cut toward the inside of the track.

History

The event developed from the half-mile run in English-speaking countries. It has been an Olympic event for men since the first modern Games in 1896. Women first contested it at the 1928 Amsterdam Olympics, but it was then removed from the program amid unfounded concerns about women's health and not restored until 1960. Memorable Olympic finals include Alberto Juantorena's double with the 400 m in 1976 and David Rudisha's world-record victory at London 2012.

Records

The men's world record of 1:40.91 was set by Kenya's David Rudisha in the 2012 Olympic final — the only sub-1:41 performance in history, run without a pacemaker. The women's record of 1:53.28, by Czechoslovakia's Jarmila Kratochvílová in Munich in 1983, is among the longest-standing records in athletics.

Racing tactics

Because the race is too long for an all-out sprint yet too short for a fully aerobic effort, runners divide between "front runners," who set a fast early pace, and "kickers," who conserve energy for a strong finish. Championship races frequently become tactical, with slow first laps followed by furious final 200 metres.
