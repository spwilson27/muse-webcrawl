Hotepsekhemwy is the Horus name of an early Egyptian king who was the founder of the Second Dynasty of Egypt. His exact position in the dynasty is debated, as is the length of his reign; the Turin King List credits him with 95 years, which is generally regarded as an exaggeration or a misreading, while modern Egyptologists propose a reign of 25 to 29 years. His name means "The Two Powers are at Peace" and may refer to a reconciliation between Upper and Lower Egypt, or between the gods Horus and Set, following a period of unrest at the end of the First Dynasty.

Identity

Hotepsekhemwy's name appears on clay seal impressions, stone vessels and bone cylinders from Saqqara and Abydos. Seal impressions found near the tomb of Qa'a, the last ruler of the First Dynasty, suggest Hotepsekhemwy may have been responsible for Qa'a's burial, and some scholars have proposed that he was related to the preceding dynasty by marriage. Later king lists give his birth name as Bedjau or Boethos.

Reign

Little is known about events during Hotepsekhemwy's rule. The Palermo Stone, which records the early dynastic annals, is fragmentary for this period, but it suggests the king celebrated festivals and may have introduced or promoted the cult of certain deities. The transition between the First and Second Dynasties appears to have involved some instability, and Hotepsekhemwy's name, invoking peace between the "two powers," is often interpreted as a programmatic statement of reunification.

Tomb

Hotepsekhemwy is generally believed to have been buried at Saqqara, in a vast underground gallery tomb (Tomb B) beneath the causeway of the Fifth Dynasty pyramid of Unas, where large numbers of his seal impressions were discovered.
