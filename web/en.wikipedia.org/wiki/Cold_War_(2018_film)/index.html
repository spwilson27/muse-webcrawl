Cold War (2018 film)

Cold War (Polish: Zimna wojna) is a 2018 historical drama film directed by Paweł Pawlikowski, who co-wrote the screenplay with Janusz Głowacki and Piotr Borkowski. Set in Poland and France during the Cold War from the late 1940s until the 1960s, the film follows a musical director who discovers a young singer and their subsequent love story in the era's political circumstances. The story was loosely inspired by the lives of Pawlikowski's parents.

Plot

In post-war Poland, Wiktor Warski tours the countryside recording folk music and auditioning performers for a state-sponsored folk ensemble. He selects the ambitious young singer Zula Lichon, and the two fall in love. The ensemble's repertoire is bent toward propaganda, and when the couple have the chance to defect during a tour in East Berlin, Zula hesitates. Over the following fifteen years the lovers meet and part repeatedly — in Poland, Paris, Yugoslavia and Berlin — divided by politics, temperament and their own choices, until a final reconciliation in Poland.

Style and reception

Shot in luminous black-and-white in the Academy ratio, like Pawlikowski's earlier Ida, the film was praised for its visuals, music and performances. It premiered at the 2018 Cannes Film Festival, where Pawlikowski won Best Director, and received three nominations at the 91st Academy Awards, for Best Foreign Language Film, Best Director and Best Cinematography. It was also nominated for Best Film Not in the English Language at the BAFTAs.
