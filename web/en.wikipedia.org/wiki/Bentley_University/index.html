Bentley University

Bentley's campus in Waltham, Massachusetts, about ten miles from Boston.

Bentley University is a private university in Waltham, Massachusetts, United States, focused on business education. Historically known for accounting and finance, it combines business study with the arts and sciences and enrols roughly 4,000 undergraduate and 1,000 graduate students.

History

The school was founded in 1917 by Harry C. Bentley as the Bentley School of Accounting and Finance on Huntington Avenue in Boston's Back Bay. Bentley, previously dean of a similar school, built the institution around practical training for the then-new profession of public accounting. It granted its first bachelor's degrees in 1961 and moved in 1968 to its present 163-acre campus in Waltham. The institution became Bentley College in 1971 and Bentley University in 2008, reflecting expanded graduate and doctoral programmes.

Academics

Bentley offers undergraduate majors across business disciplines — accounting, finance, marketing, management, computer information systems and actuarial science — alongside liberal studies majors and a general education core. The McCallum Graduate School of Business offers the Bentley MBA, master's degrees in fields such as business analytics, finance and taxation, and a PhD in business. The university is accredited by AACSB, EQUIS and AMBA, a combination known as triple accreditation.

Campus and student life

The Waltham campus is notable for technology-rich facilities, including the Trading Room, the Center for Marketing Technology and the Library's research resources. Bentley's athletic teams, the Falcons, compete in NCAA Division II, principally in the Northeast-10 Conference, while the men's ice hockey team plays in Division I. Career services and internship placements with Boston-area firms are a hallmark of the Bentley experience.
