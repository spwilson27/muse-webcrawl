The Scotland national football team represents Scotland in men's international football and is controlled by the Scottish Football Association. It competes in the three major professional tournaments: the FIFA World Cup, UEFA Nations League and the UEFA European Championship. Scotland, as a country of the United Kingdom, is not a member of the International Olympic Committee, and therefore the national team does not compete in the Olympic Games.

History

Scotland is the joint oldest national football team in the world, alongside England, whom it played in the world's first international football match in 1872 — a 0–0 draw at Hamilton Crescent, Glasgow. The annual fixture between the two became the centrepiece of the British Home Championship, which Scotland won outright 24 times.

Scotland have qualified for the FIFA World Cup on eight occasions and the UEFA European Championship four times, but have never progressed beyond the first group stage of any international tournament. Memorable moments include the victory over world champions England at Wembley in 1967 — when Scotland fans jokingly crowned their team "unofficial world champions" — and Archie Gemmill's celebrated goal against the Netherlands at the 1978 World Cup in Argentina. After a 23-year absence from major tournaments, Scotland qualified for UEFA Euro 2020 (held in 2021) and Euro 2024, under manager Steve Clarke.

Supporters and identity

The team's supporters are known as the Tartan Army, celebrated for their good-natured travelling support. The traditional home colours are dark blue shirts, white shorts and red socks, commemorating the colours of the pioneering Queen's Park club. Home matches are played at Hampden Park in Glasgow, and the team's unofficial anthem is "Flower of Scotland".
