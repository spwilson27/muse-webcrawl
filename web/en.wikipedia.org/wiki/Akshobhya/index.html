Akshobhya (Sanskrit: अक्षोभ्य, "Immovable One") is one of the Five Wisdom Buddhas of Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism. He presides over the eastern pure land of Abhirati ("great delight") and embodies the transformation of anger into mirror-like wisdom.

Origins

Akshobhya appears in the Akṣobhyatathāgatasyavyūha Sūtra, among the earliest known Mahayana sutras, which describes how a monk vowed never to feel anger or malice toward any being; after aeons of steadfast practice he became the Buddha Akshobhya in the eastern world of Abhirati. His vow of immovability explains his name and his characteristic mudra, the earth-touching gesture (bhūmisparśa) shared with Shakyamuni at the enlightenment.

Iconography

Akshobhya is depicted blue in colour, seated in vajra posture, his right hand in the earth-witness mudra and his left holding an alms bowl or resting in his lap; he is associated with the vajra, the elephant, and the element water. In the five-Buddha mandalas he occupies the centre or the east depending on the system. His consort is Locanā, and his retinue includes the bodhisattvas Vajrapani-related figures in some traditions.

In practice

Akshobhya is important in East Asian Buddhism, where devotion to him and rebirth in Abhirati was an early form of pure-land practice predating the dominance of Amitābha. In Tibetan Buddhism he is central to purification practices, including rites for the deceased, and his mantra is recited to purify negative karma.
