Bayan I

Reign: c. 562 – 602
Title: Khagan of the Avars
Successor: Bayan II

Bayan I (died 602) was the first and most famous khagan of the Avars, ruling from about 562 until his death in 602. He led the Avars from their arrival in Central Europe to the establishment of the Avar Khaganate in the Pannonian Basin, and for four decades he was the principal adversary of the Byzantine Empire in the Balkans.

Early reign

The Avars first appeared in European history in 558, when an embassy reached Constantinople seeking land and subsidies. Bayan succeeded to the khaganate around 562. Initially allied with the Lombards, he destroyed the Gepids in 567, and when the Lombards departed for Italy in 568, the Avars took possession of the whole Pannonian plain, which became the heartland of their khaganate.

Wars with Byzantium

Bayan repeatedly demanded increases in the tribute paid by Constantinople and campaigned deep into the Balkans. His forces captured the great fortress city of Sirmium in 582 after a long blockade, giving the Avars a bridgehead across the Sava. Avar and Slav raiding parties reached as far as the Long Walls of Constantinople and into Greece. Despite a heavy defeat inflicted by the Byzantine general Maurice's commanders in the campaigns of the 590s north of the Danube, Bayan's khaganate remained the dominant power of the Carpathian basin.

Death and legacy

Bayan died in 602, reportedly of the plague, and was succeeded by his son, remembered as Bayan II. Bayan I is regarded as the founder of the Avar state and one of the most capable steppe rulers of the early Middle Ages; his long reign fixed the pattern of Avar–Byzantine warfare for a generation.
