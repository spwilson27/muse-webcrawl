Jagdkommando

Country: Austria
Branch: Austrian Armed Forces (Bundesheer)
Type: Special forces
Garrison: Wiener Neustadt

The Jagdkommando (JaKdo, "Hunting Commando") is the special forces unit of the Austrian Armed Forces (Bundesheer). Based in Wiener Neustadt, Lower Austria, it is tasked with special reconnaissance, direct action, counter-terrorism, hostage rescue, and the protection of Austrian nationals abroad.

History

The unit traces its origins to courses begun in 1961, when Austrian officers attended US Army Ranger and Special Forces training and established a corresponding cadre at home; the Jagdkommando as a formation developed through the 1960s and 1970s into Austria's principal special-operations force.

Selection and training

Volunteers face a demanding selection process followed by basic commando training covering parachuting (including military free fall), diving, mountaineering, explosives, close-quarters battle, and survival in extreme environments. Operators specialise further in areas such as sniping, combat medicine, or communications. The unit's motto reflects readiness for worldwide deployment, and Jagdkommando soldiers have served in missions including Kosovo, Afghanistan, Chad, and Mali, as well as domestic counter-terrorism readiness alongside the Austrian police's EKO Cobra.

Equipment and structure

The Jagdkommando uses standard and specialised Austrian and allied equipment, including Steyr AUG rifles and Glock pistols, and operates in small task groups tailored to mission requirements. Exact strengths remain classified but are generally reported as a few hundred personnel.
