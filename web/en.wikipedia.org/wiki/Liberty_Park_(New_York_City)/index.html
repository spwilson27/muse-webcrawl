Liberty Park (New York City)

Liberty Park is a one-acre (0.40 ha) elevated public park at the World Trade Center site in Lower Manhattan, New York City. Opened on June 29, 2016, it sits above the site's Vehicular Security Center at a height of about 25 feet (7.6 m), overlooking the National September 11 Memorial & Museum to the north.

Features

The park was designed by landscape architect Joseph E. Brown and planted with lawns, trees and a "living wall" of planting along its southern edge. It contains several notable elements: the restored Sphere sculpture by Fritz Koenig, salvaged from the ruins of the Twin Towers; the America's Response Monument honouring US special forces in Afghanistan; and the Anne Frank Tree, grown from a sapling of the chestnut tree Anne Frank could see from her hiding place in Amsterdam. The St. Nicholas Greek Orthodox Church, rebuilt as a national shrine, stands at the park's eastern end.

Context

Liberty Park replaced the earlier Liberty Plaza Park (Zuccotti Park), a ground-level plaza on the neighbouring block that became famous as the site of the 2011 Occupy Wall Street encampment. The new park forms part of the redevelopment of the World Trade Center complex after the September 11 attacks.
