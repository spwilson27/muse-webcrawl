Chechen (Нохчийн мотт, Noxçiyn mott) is a Northeast Caucasian language spoken by about 1.4 million people, mainly in Chechnya in the Russian North Caucasus, and by a diaspora in the Middle East and Europe. Together with Ingush it forms the Vainakh branch of the Nakh languages.

Grammar and phonology

Chechen is notable for its large consonant inventory, including ejective and pharyngealised consonants, and for an extensive system of noun classes — commonly analysed as six — with agreement marked on verbs, adjectives, and some adverbs. Its case system is rich and ergative in alignment. Vowel systems differ considerably between dialects; the standard language is based on the plains dialect around Grozny.

Writing systems

Chechen was first written in an adapted Arabic script. A Latin-based alphabet was introduced in 1925 during the Soviet latinisation campaign, replaced in 1938 by a Cyrillic-based alphabet, which remains official in Russia. After the collapse of the USSR, the separatist government of Ichkeria proclaimed a new Latin alphabet in the 1990s, but Cyrillic remains dominant in practice.
