Yip Harburg

Born: April 8, 1896, New York City, U.S.
Died: March 5, 1981 (aged 84)
Occupation: Lyricist

Edgar Yipsel Harburg (born Isidore Hochberg; April 8, 1896 – March 5, 1981), known as E. Y. Harburg or Yip Harburg, was an American popular song lyricist who worked with many well-known composers. He wrote the lyrics to the standards "Brother, Can You Spare a Dime?" (with Jay Gorney), "April in Paris", and "It's Only a Paper Moon", and co-wrote all of the songs in the film The Wizard of Oz (1939), including "Over the Rainbow", with composer Harold Arlen.

Early life

Harburg was born on the Lower East Side of New York City to Jewish immigrants from Russia. Nicknamed "Yip" — short for "Yipsel", itself from the Yiddish for squirrel — he attended Townsend Harris High School, where he met Ira Gershwin, and later City College. After losing an electrical appliance business in the Wall Street Crash of 1929, he turned to songwriting, encouraged by Gershwin.

Career

His Depression-era anthem "Brother, Can You Spare a Dime?" (1932) became one of the defining songs of the era. With Harold Arlen he wrote the score for The Wizard of Oz, winning the Academy Award for Best Original Song for "Over the Rainbow". He also wrote the book and lyrics for the Broadway musical Finian's Rainbow (1947), with music by Burton Lane, remembered for "How Are Things in Glocca Morra?" and its satirical treatment of racism and finance. In the 1950s Harburg was blacklisted in Hollywood for alleged communist sympathies, though he continued to work in theatre. He died in a car accident in Los Angeles in 1981 and was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame.
