Teesta River

The Teesta River is a 414-kilometre (257 mi) long river that rises in the Pauhunri area of the eastern Himalayas in the Indian state of Sikkim, flows through West Bengal, and enters Bangladesh, where it joins the Jamuna River (the Bangladeshi name for the Brahmaputra).

Course

The river is formed by glacial streams including the Lachen Chu and Lachung Chu. It carves a deep gorge through Sikkim, passes the towns of Rangpo, Teesta Bazaar, and Jalpaiguri, and crosses into Bangladesh near Mekhliganj district, meeting the Jamuna at Teesta Barrage near Rangpur region. Its major tributaries include the Rangeet River.

Importance and disputes

The Teesta supports irrigation and hydroelectric projects, including the Teesta Barrage in Bangladesh and a cascade of dams in Sikkim. Sharing of its waters between India and Bangladesh has been a long-running diplomatic issue; a proposed agreement in 2011 was not signed, largely due to objections from West Bengal's government.
