Dolby Atmos

Dolby Atmos is a surround sound technology developed by Dolby Laboratories. It expands on existing surround sound systems by adding height channels, allowing sounds to be interpreted as three-dimensional objects with neither horizontal nor vertical limitations.

Following the release of Atmos for the cinema market, a variety of consumer technologies have been released under the Atmos brand, using in-ceiling and up-firing speakers.

History

Dolby Atmos was first installed for the premiere of Disney/Pixar's Brave in Los Angeles in June 2012. Unlike channel-based systems such as Dolby Digital 5.1 and 7.1, Atmos is object-based: a soundtrack can contain up to 128 audio tracks with metadata describing the position of each sound, rendered in real time to the available speakers — up to 64 in cinemas.

Home and mobile versions

Home theatre versions support configurations such as 5.1.2 and 7.1.4 (conventional channels, subwoofer, height channels). Atmos has been adopted for streaming services, video games, music (Dolby Atmos Music) and virtualised playback through headphones and soundbars.
