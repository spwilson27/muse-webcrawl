Resurrection

Resurrection is the concept of coming back to life after death. In a number of religions, a dying-and-rising god is a deity who dies and is resurrected. The idea of a general resurrection of the dead at the end of time is central to the Abrahamic faiths: in Christianity it is bound up with the resurrection of Jesus, confessed in the creeds as the pledge of believers' own resurrection; Judaism developed a belief in resurrection in the late Second Temple period; and in Islam the dead are raised for the Last Judgment (Yawm al-Qiyamah).

In ancient religions

Egyptian religion centred on the death and rebirth of Osiris; Greek myth told of figures such as Orpheus and Heracles descending to and returning from the underworld. Zoroastrianism taught a future resurrection of the dead (ristakhiz) at the renovation of the world, an idea that may have influenced Jewish eschatology during and after the Babylonian exile — explicit belief appears in the Book of Daniel (c. 2nd century BCE) and was held by the Pharisees against the Sadducees.

Christianity

For Christians, the resurrection of Jesus on the third day after his crucifixion is the foundation of the faith: "if Christ has not been raised," wrote Paul, "our preaching is in vain and your faith is in vain" (1 Corinthians 15). The doctrine of the general resurrection holds that at Christ's return the dead will be raised with transformed, imperishable bodies and judged. It is affirmed in the Nicene Creed: "I look forward to the resurrection of the dead and the life of the world to come."

Islam and other traditions

The Quran repeatedly asserts that God will raise the dead for judgment, and resurrection is one of the articles of Islamic faith. Belief in literal bodily resurrection is less prominent in Eastern religions centred on reincarnation, though analogous ideas of revival appear in folklore worldwide.
