Long non-coding RNA

AbbreviationlncRNA
LengthMore than 200 nucleotides
FunctionRegulation of gene expression, chromatin organisation, splicing and other processes
Well-known examplesXist, HOTAIR, MALAT1, H19

Long non-coding RNAs (long ncRNAs, lncRNAs) are a large and diverse class of RNA molecules defined as transcripts of more than 200 nucleotides in length that are not translated into protein. Tens of thousands of lncRNA genes have been annotated in the human genome, rivalling the number of protein-coding genes, although the fraction with demonstrated biological function remains debated.

Characteristics

Most lncRNAs are transcribed by RNA polymerase II, capped, spliced and polyadenylated much like messenger RNAs, but they tend to be expressed at lower levels and in more tissue-specific patterns. They are classified by their genomic position relative to protein-coding genes — intergenic, antisense, intronic, sense-overlapping and bidirectional lncRNAs among others.

Functions

Characterised lncRNAs act at many levels of gene regulation: guiding chromatin-modifying complexes to specific loci (as Xist does in coating the inactive X chromosome), scaffolding protein complexes, decoying transcription factors and microRNAs, and modulating splicing, mRNA stability and translation. Dysregulation of lncRNAs such as HOTAIR and MALAT1 has been linked to cancer progression, and others are implicated in development and neurological disease.
