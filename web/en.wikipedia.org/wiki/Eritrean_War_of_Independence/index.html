Eritrean War of Independence

The Eritrean War of Independence (1 September 1961 – 24 May 1991) was a conflict fought between successive Ethiopian governments and Eritrean independence movements. It ended with the Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) taking Asmara in 1991; a UN-supervised referendum in 1993 voted overwhelmingly for independence, making Eritrea a sovereign state after thirty years of war.

Background

Eritrea, an Italian colony from 1890, was federated with Ethiopia by the UN in 1952 with its own parliament and autonomy. Emperor Haile Selassie progressively dismantled the federation, annexing Eritrea as a province in 1962. The Eritrean Liberation Front (ELF) had already launched armed struggle under Hamid Idris Awate in September 1961.

Course of the war

The EPLF split from the ELF in the 1970s and became the dominant movement, building a disciplined army and underground state around Nakfa, which withstood massive offensives by the Soviet-backed Derg regime after 1977. The EPLF's victory at Afabet (1988), destroying an Ethiopian army corps, turned the war. In May 1991, as the Derg fell in Addis Ababa, EPLF forces liberated Asmara and Assab.

Aftermath

The 1993 referendum recorded 99.8% support for independence. The war cost an estimated 65,000 Eritrean fighters and many tens of thousands of civilians, and displaced hundreds of thousands as refugees.
