Aerial firefighting

Aerial firefighting is the use of aircraft and other aerial resources to combat wildfires. Aircraft can access steep, remote terrain quickly and deliver water or long-term fire retardant ahead of a fire front to slow its spread until ground crews arrive.

Aircraft types

Fixed-wing airtankers range from single-engine agricultural conversions carrying a few thousand litres to very large airtankers such as converted DC-10s and Boeing 747s carrying tens of thousands of litres of retardant. Water-scooping aircraft such as the Canadair CL-415 refill by skimming lakes. Helicopters carry water in buckets suspended beneath them or in internal tanks, and can work with great precision around structures.

Operations

Drops are coordinated by an air attack supervisor flying above the fire, and lead planes guide tankers along their run. Retardant, typically dyed red so pilots can see previous coverage, is laid as a fuel treatment rather than to extinguish flames directly. Aerial firefighting is hazardous: it involves low-level flight in smoke, turbulence, and mountainous terrain, and several fatal accidents have prompted stricter certification of ageing aircraft.
