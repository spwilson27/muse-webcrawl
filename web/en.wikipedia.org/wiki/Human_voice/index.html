Human voice

The human voice consists of sound made by a human being using the vocal tract, including talking, singing, laughing, crying, screaming, shouting, humming or yelling. The human voice is produced in the larynx when air from the lungs passes through the vocal folds, causing them to vibrate; the resulting sound is shaped by the resonating cavities of the throat, mouth and nose, and articulated by the tongue, lips and palate.

Phonation

Phonation occurs when the arytenoid cartilages draw the vocal folds together and subglottal air pressure sets them into self-sustained oscillation. Pitch is controlled mainly by the tension and length of the folds (via the cricothyroid muscle), while loudness depends on air pressure and the amplitude of vibration.

Voice types and registers

In singing, voices are classified by range and timbre: soprano, mezzo-soprano, contralto, tenor, baritone and bass are standard categories in Western classical music. Vocal registers include the modal register of ordinary speech, falsetto, vocal fry and the whistle register.

Speech and language

The voice is the primary medium of spoken language. Individual voice characteristics — pitch, timbre, accent and prosody — convey identity, emotion and social information, and form the basis of speaker recognition.

Disorders

Voice disorders include laryngitis, vocal nodules, spasmodic dysphonia and paralysis of the vocal folds; they are treated by speech-language pathologists and laryngologists.
