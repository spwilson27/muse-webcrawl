Giant isopod

Scientific classification

Kingdom: Animalia

Phylum: Arthropoda

Class: Malacostraca

Order: Isopoda

Family: Cirolanidae

Genus: Bathynomus

A giant isopod is any of the several species of large isopods in the genus Bathynomus. They are abundant in the cold, deep waters of the Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian Oceans. Bathynomus giganteus, the largest species, is often regarded as the world's largest isopod, although other comparable species exist. Giant isopods are a striking example of deep-sea gigantism and are close relatives of the much smaller terrestrial woodlice (pillbugs).

Description

Giant isopods typically range from 19 to 36 cm in length, with exceptional specimens reported exceeding 50 cm. Like their terrestrial relatives, they have a segmented, armored exoskeleton divided into a cephalon (head), thorax, and abdomen, seven pairs of walking legs, and large compound eyes. Their bodies are flattened and protected by overlapping calcified plates. The first pair of thoracic appendages is modified into maxillipeds used to manipulate food.

Distribution and habitat

Giant isopods live on the continental slope and abyssal seabed, most commonly at depths between 170 and 2,140 metres, where temperatures are near 4 °C. They are especially common in the Gulf of Mexico, the Caribbean Sea, and waters off Taiwan, Japan, and Australia.

Biology and behaviour

Giant isopods are primarily scavengers, feeding on dead whales, fish, and squid that sink to the ocean floor, though they may also prey on slow-moving animals such as sea cucumbers, sponges, and possibly even slow fish. They are able to survive very long periods without food — captive specimens have fasted for more than four years — gorging themselves when food arrives. They reproduce by laying large eggs, among the largest of any marine invertebrate, which hatch as mancae that resemble miniature adults.

Relationship to humans

Though occasionally caught as bycatch in deep trawl fisheries, giant isopods are of minor commercial importance; in parts of East Asia they are served as a novelty food. They are kept in several public aquariums worldwide.
