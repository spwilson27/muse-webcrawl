Rai 1 is an Italian free-to-air television channel owned and operated by the state-owned public broadcaster RAI. It is the company's flagship channel and the most-watched television channel in Italy, oriented towards family entertainment, drama, news, and major national events.

History

The channel began broadcasting on 3 January 1954 as the Programma Nazionale, the first regular television service in Italy, at a time when television sets were still a rarity. For two decades it held a monopoly, shaping Italian culture and even language through programmes such as the quiz Lascia o raddoppia? and the educational shows of Alberto Manzi. It was renamed Rete 1 in the 1975 reform, Rai Uno in 1983, and adopted the current Rai 1 name and square logo in 2010. A high-definition simulcast, Rai 1 HD, launched in 2013.

Programming

Rai 1's schedule mixes the flagship newscast TG1, long-running entertainment institutions such as Domenica in and the prime-time variety and ballroom competition Ballando con le stelle, hugely popular domestic fiction (including the detective series Don Matteo and Il commissario Montalbano), and cultural fixtures such as the Sanremo Music Festival, which it has broadcast since the festival's television debut. The channel also carries major state occasions, papal events, and Italy national football team matches.
