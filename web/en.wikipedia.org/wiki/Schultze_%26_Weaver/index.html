Schultze & Weaver

Schultze & Weaver was an American architectural firm established in New York City in 1921 by Leonard Schultze and S. Fullerton Weaver. The firm became famous in the 1920s for designing grand hotels, most notably the original Waldorf-Astoria Hotel on Park Avenue (opened 1931), then the largest hotel in the world.

Works

Major commissions included the Pierre Hotel, the Sherry-Netherland, and the Park Lane Hotel in New York; the Biltmore hotels in Los Angeles and Coral Gables (the latter with Phineas Paist); the Breakers in Palm Beach; and the U.S. Post Office and courthouse work of the 1930s. Schultze, trained with Carrère and Hastings, directed design, while Weaver, a real-estate developer, handled business and hotel operations.

Legacy

The firm's hotels defined the Jazz Age luxury hotel: lavish public rooms, rigorous back-of-house planning, and modern steel-frame construction behind Beaux-Arts and neo-classical façades. Schultze later designed the Beverly Wilshire-era apartment hotels and wartime government buildings before the practice wound down in the 1940s.
