The Chu River (Kyrgyz: Чүй; Kazakh: Шу) is a river in northern Kyrgyzstan and southern Kazakhstan, about 1,067 kilometres long. It rises in the Tian Shan mountains near the western end of Lake Issyk-Kul, flows through the Boom Gorge and the fertile Chu Valley — where it forms part of the border between Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan — and finally dissipates in the Moyunqum Desert without reaching any sea, making it an endorheic river.

Course and significance

The Chu Valley is one of the most densely populated and agriculturally productive regions of Central Asia; the Kyrgyz capital, Bishkek, lies just south of the river. Irrigation from the Chu and its tributaries supports extensive farming, and the Orto-Tokoy and other reservoirs regulate its flow. Agreements between Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan govern the sharing of its waters under the Chu–Talas commission, a frequently cited example of transboundary water cooperation.

History

The valley was a corridor of the Silk Road. Along the river stood the medieval city of Balasagun, capital of the Kara-Khanid Khanate, near the modern town of Tokmok and the Burana Tower. Suyab, another major trading city, also lay in the Chu Valley. In Chinese historical records the region was associated with the Western Turkic Khaganate, and the river's name is connected with the Shatuo Turks and the later Chuy ue clan groups of the area.
