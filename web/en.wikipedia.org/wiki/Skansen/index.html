Skansen

Skansen is the first open-air museum and zoo in Sweden, located on the island of Djurgården in Stockholm. It was opened on 11 October 1891 by Artur Hazelius (1833–1901), who also founded the Nordic Museum, in order to show the way of life in different parts of Sweden before the industrial era.

The name Skansen derives from a small fortification (skans) built for the young Crown Prince Oscar in the early 19th century, near which the museum was established. Skansen became the model for many other open-air museums in northern and eastern Europe, and indeed for the open-air museum concept worldwide.

History

Hazelius, concerned that Swedish folk culture was disappearing with industrialisation, began purchasing historic buildings from across Sweden and having them dismantled, transported and re-erected at Skansen. The museum grew to include around 150 houses and farmsteads from every part of the country, including a segment of a Stockholm town quarter, workshops, a church (Seglora Church, moved in 1918), windmills, and a Sami camp.

The museum today

Today Skansen covers about 75 acres (30 hectares) and attracts well over a million visitors annually, making it one of Stockholm's most popular attractions. Costumed interpreters demonstrate traditional crafts such as glass-blowing, pottery, baking and printing, and the museum hosts celebrations of Swedish traditions including Midsummer, Walpurgis Night, Lucia and Christmas markets. The annual Allsång på Skansen sing-along concert series, broadcast on Swedish television since 1979, is held at the Solliden stage.

Zoo

Skansen's zoo keeps Scandinavian animals including brown bears, wolves, lynx, wolverines, moose, reindeer, seals and farm animals of traditional Swedish breeds. There is also an aquarium and a children's zoo (Lill-Skansen). Since 1897 a herd of animals and the setting have been used to show native wildlife in naturalistic enclosures.
