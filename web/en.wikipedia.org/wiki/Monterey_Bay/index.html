Monterey Bay is a bay of the Pacific Ocean located on the coast of the U.S. state of California, south of the San Francisco Bay Area. Santa Cruz, California, is located at the northern end of the bay, and Monterey lies at the southern end, on the Monterey Peninsula. The bay was named by Sebastián Vizcaíno in 1602 in honour of the Count of Monterrey, then viceroy of New Spain.

Geology and the Monterey Canyon

Beneath the bay lies the Monterey Canyon, one of the largest submarine canyons on the west coast of North America, comparable in scale to the Grand Canyon. It begins just offshore of Moss Landing, near the middle of the bay, and extends more than 150 kilometres into the Pacific, reaching depths of over 3,600 metres. The canyon channels nutrient-rich deep water toward the surface, supporting exceptional marine productivity.

Ecology

Upwelling of cold, nutrient-rich water sustains kelp forests, sardine and anchovy schools, and a food web that attracts sea otters, sea lions, dolphins, and migrating whales, including humpback and blue whales. Much of the bay is protected within the Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary, designated in 1992 and one of the largest marine protected areas in the United States. Research institutions around the bay include the Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute (MBARI) and the Hopkins Marine Station.

History

Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo sighted the bay in 1542 and called it the Bay of Pines. Spanish and later Mexican Monterey became the capital of Alta California. The bay's rich fisheries supported a major sardine industry in the early 20th century, centred on Monterey's Cannery Row, before the fishery collapsed in the 1950s.
