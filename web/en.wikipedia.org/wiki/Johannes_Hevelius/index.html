Johannes Hevelius

Johannes Hevelius (Polish: Jan Heweliusz; 28 January 1611 – 28 January 1687) was a Polish-Lithuanian astronomer from Gdańsk (Danzig), known as the founder of lunar topography. A prosperous brewer and city councillor, he built one of the finest observatories of the 17th century on the rooftops of his houses in Gdańsk.

Astronomy

Hevelius's Selenographia, sive Lunae descriptio (1647) contained detailed maps of the Moon and described its libration. He observed sunspots, charted the phases of Mercury, and discovered several comets as well as four new constellations still in use: Scutum, Lacerta, Leo Minor, and Canes Venatici, among others he proposed.

Famous for his exceptionally keen eyesight, Hevelius made his observations with open sights rather than telescopes, which brought him into dispute with Robert Hooke. His posthumous star atlas Firmamentum Sobiescianum (1690), part of his Uranometria, was completed with the help of his wife Elisabeth, one of the earliest women astronomers.

Legacy

The lunar crater Hevelius and the asteroid 5703 Hevelius are named after him.
