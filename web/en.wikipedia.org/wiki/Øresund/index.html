Øresund

The Øresund (Danish: Øresund; Swedish: Öresund) is a strait between Denmark and Sweden. Together with the Great Belt and the Little Belt, it forms one of the three Danish straits connecting the Baltic Sea to the Kattegat and onward to the North Sea. It is one of the busiest shipping lanes in the world.

Geography

The strait is 118 kilometres long. At its northern end, between Helsingør in Denmark and Helsingborg in Sweden, it narrows to about 4 kilometres; at its southern end it opens between Copenhagen and Malmö. The island of Saltholm lies in the middle of the strait, and the artificial island of Peberholm was built south of it as part of the Øresund crossing. Because the Baltic is nearly tideless and brackish, water exchange through the shallow sills of the Øresund strongly influences the salinity of the Baltic Sea.

History

Control of the Øresund was long a source of conflict between Denmark and Sweden. From 1429 until 1857 Denmark levied the Sound Dues on foreign ships passing through, a major source of royal revenue, enforced from Kronborg Castle at Helsingør — the Elsinore of Shakespeare's Hamlet. The Treaty of Roskilde (1658) transferred the eastern shore (Scania) to Sweden, but Denmark retained the dues for another two centuries.

The Øresund Bridge

Since 2000 the strait has been crossed by the Øresund Bridge, a combined rail and road link between Copenhagen and Malmö consisting of a bridge, an artificial island and a tunnel. The crossing created the cross-border Øresund Region, integrating the labour markets of the two cities. Ferries still operate on the northern Helsingør–Helsingborg route.
