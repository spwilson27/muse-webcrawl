Shortwave listening

Shortwave listening, often abbreviated to SWLing, is the hobby of listening to shortwave radio broadcasts, which travel long distances by reflection from the ionosphere. Listeners, sometimes called SWLs, tune in international broadcasters, amateur radio operators, utility stations, and numbers stations.

Broadcasts

International broadcasters such as the BBC World Service, Voice of America, and Deutsche Welle historically transmitted on shortwave to reach audiences across borders. Although many services have moved to the internet and satellite, shortwave remains in use for audiences in remote regions and during emergencies.

DXing and QSL cards

Many listeners practice DXing — attempting to receive distant or rare stations — and send reception reports to stations in exchange for QSL cards verifying the contact. Listeners keep logs of frequencies, times (in UTC), and signal quality using the SINPO code.

Equipment

A basic setup consists of a communications receiver and a long-wire or dipole antenna; modern hobbyists also use software-defined radios connected to computers.
