Overview

The Pleistocene is the geological epoch that lasted from about 2.58 million to 11,700 years ago, spanning the Earth's most recent period of repeated glaciations — the "Ice Age". It is the preceding epoch of the Quaternary Period and is followed by the Holocene. At glacial maxima, ice sheets kilometres thick covered much of North America and northern Europe, and sea level fell by some 120 metres, joining continents with land bridges.

Life and humans

The Pleistocene world hosted megafauna — mammoths, woolly rhinoceroses, sabre-toothed cats, giant ground sloths — most of which vanished in the end-Pleistocene extinctions (causes debated between climate shifts and human hunting). Anatomically modern humans evolved in Africa during this epoch and spread worldwide; Neanderthals and Denisovans flourished and disappeared. The epoch's cycles — roughly 100,000-year glacial rhythms governed by Milankovitch orbital variations — define Quaternary science. Woolly mammoths survived on Wrangel Island until about 4,000 years ago.
