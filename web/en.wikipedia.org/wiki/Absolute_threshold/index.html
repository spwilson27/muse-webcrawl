Absolute threshold

Absolute threshold
Detecting a faint stimulus
FieldPsychophysics
DefinitionMinimum stimulus intensity detectable 50% of the time

In neuroscience and psychophysics, an absolute threshold was originally defined as the lowest level of a stimulus — light, sound, touch, etc. — that an organism could detect. In modern usage, it is the smallest detectable level of a stimulus, operationally defined as the intensity detected on 50% of trials.

Examples

Classic textbook examples of human absolute thresholds include seeing a candle flame at roughly 30 miles on a dark, clear night; hearing a watch ticking 20 feet away in quiet conditions; and tasting a teaspoon of sugar dissolved in two gallons of water. These values are illustrative rather than exact, as thresholds vary with conditions and individuals.

Measurement

Thresholds are measured using methods such as the method of limits, the method of constant stimuli, and adaptive staircase procedures. Signal detection theory later showed that measured thresholds reflect both sensory sensitivity and the observer's decision criterion, leading researchers to distinguish sensitivity (d′) from response bias.
