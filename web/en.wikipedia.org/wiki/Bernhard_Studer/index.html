Bernhard Studer (21 August 1794 – 2 May 1887) was a Swiss geologist regarded as one of the founders of Swiss geology and among the first to systematically describe the structure of the Alps.

Life and career

Studer was born in Büren an der Aare in the canton of Bern. He studied theology and mathematics at the academy in Bern before turning to natural science, continuing his studies at Göttingen and Paris. He became a teacher of mathematics and physics in Bern and later professor of geology at the University of Bern, where he taught for decades and helped establish geology as an academic discipline in Switzerland.

Geological work

Studer devoted his career to unravelling the complex stratigraphy and structure of the Swiss Alps, a region of intensely folded and faulted rocks that challenged early geologists. His two-volume Geologie der Schweiz (1851–1853) was the first comprehensive synthesis of Swiss geology, and he contributed to the geological mapping of the country. He studied the flysch and molasse deposits flanking the Alpine chain and corresponded with leading European geologists of his day. In 1879 the Geological Society of London awarded him the Wollaston Medal, its highest honour. A mineral, studerite, was named after him.
