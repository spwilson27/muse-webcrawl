Half-diminished seventh chord

The half-diminished seventh chord is a seventh chord composed of a root, minor third, diminished fifth and minor seventh — in other words a diminished triad with a minor seventh added. It is notated with the symbol ø7, or m7♭5 in jazz.

Function

In major keys it occurs naturally as the viiø7 chord; in minor keys it commonly functions as iiø7, a predominant leading to the dominant. In jazz it is a staple of ii–V–i progressions in minor keys.

Comparison

It differs from the fully diminished seventh chord, which has a diminished seventh (enharmonically a major sixth) above the root, and from the minor seventh chord, which has a perfect fifth. A famous example in classical music is the opening chord of Wagner's Tristan und Isolde, often analysed as a half-diminished-type sonority.
