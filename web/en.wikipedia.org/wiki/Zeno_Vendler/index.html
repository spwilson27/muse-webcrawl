Zeno Vendler

Zeno Vendler (22 December 1921 – 13 January 2004) was a Hungarian-born American philosopher and linguist, best known for his classification of verb types according to their temporal structure — the "Vendler classes" — which became foundational in the study of lexical aspect.

Life

Vendler studied in Budapest, Vienna (with the Jesuits) and Maastricht before emigrating to the United States, where he earned his PhD at Harvard University in 1959. He taught at several American universities and at Cornell, and in 1969 moved to the newly founded University of Calgary, where he helped build its philosophy department, before later joining the University of California, San Diego. After retirement he returned to Hungary.

Vendler classes

In his 1957 paper "Verbs and Times" (reprinted in Linguistics in Philosophy, 1967), Vendler distinguished four verb classes based on how verbs relate to time:

  • States — static and homogeneous (know, love, believe).
  • Activities — dynamic processes with no inherent endpoint (run, swim, push a cart).
  • Accomplishments — processes proceeding toward a definite climax (build a house, run a mile).
  • Achievements — near-instantaneous events (win the race, notice, reach the summit).

The classification, identified by linguistic tests such as compatibility with the progressive or with "in an hour" versus "for an hour" adverbials, was later elaborated by David Dowty and became standard in formal semantics and aspectology.

Other work

Vendler also wrote on factivity, adjectives, and speech acts, including Res Cogitans (1972), a study of Descartes and the epistemology of self-knowledge.
