Daisaku Ikeda

Daisaku Ikeda (2 January 1928 – 15 November 2023) was a Japanese Buddhist philosopher, educator, author, and nuclear disarmament advocate. He served as third president of the Soka Gakkai from 1960 to 1979 and founding president of Soka Gakkai International (SGI) from 1975 until his death.


Born: 2 January 1928, Tokyo, Japan
Died: 15 November 2023 (aged 95), Tokyo
Known for: President of Soka Gakkai International

Early life

Born in Ōta, Tokyo, the fifth of eight children in a family of seaweed farmers devastated by war, Ikeda's youth was marked by illness and World War II, shaping his commitment to peace. In 1947 he met Josei Toda and became his disciple in Nichiren Buddhism.

Presidency

Ikeda succeeded Toda in 1960. Under him Soka Gakkai grew into one of Japan's largest religious movements and expanded internationally. He founded Soka University, the Min-On Concert Association, and the Tokyo Fuji Art Museum, and in 1975 founded SGI.

Peace work and writings

He submitted annual peace proposals to the United Nations, dialogued with Arnold Toynbee (published as Choose Life), and advocated nuclear abolition. He authored The Human Revolution and many other works.

Death

Ikeda died of old age in Tokyo in November 2023.
