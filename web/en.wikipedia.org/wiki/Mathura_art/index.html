Mathura art

Mathura art refers to the sculpture and art produced in and around Mathura in northern India, flourishing especially under the Kushan Empire from about the 1st to 3rd centuries CE. Working in distinctive spotted red sandstone from Sikri, Mathura workshops created images for Buddhist, Jain, and Hindu patrons.

Buddha images

Mathura, alongside Gandhara, developed some of the earliest anthropomorphic images of the Buddha. The Mathura type typically shows a robust figure with the right hand in abhaya mudra, thin clinging sanghati, and an ushnisha with snail-shell curls.

Other subjects

The school produced Jain tirthankara images, yakshas and yakshis, and early Hindu deities including Vishnu and Shiva. Its influence spread widely across northern India and into the Gupta period.
