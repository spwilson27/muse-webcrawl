CiteScore

CiteScore is a metric for measuring the citation impact of peer-reviewed journals, book series, and conference proceedings indexed in the Scopus database. It was launched by the publisher Elsevier in December 2016 as an alternative to the journal impact factor (JIF) produced by Clarivate.

Calculation

Under the methodology revised in 2020, a journal's CiteScore for a given year is calculated by dividing the number of citations received in the four previous years to documents published in those same four years by the total number of those documents. Unlike the impact factor, CiteScore counts all document types indexed in Scopus — including editorials, letters, corrections, and news items — in both the numerator and the denominator, and the underlying data are freely available without a subscription.

Related metrics

Scopus also provides CiteScore Tracker, a monthly provisional figure, as well as CiteScore percentiles and ranks within subject categories. Other journal-level metrics include SCImago Journal Rank (SJR) and the Source Normalized Impact per Paper (SNIP).

Reception

CiteScore was welcomed for its transparency and broader coverage of titles than the impact factor, but criticised because Elsevier both publishes journals and owns the metric, and because counting all document types can disadvantage journals that publish many non-research items. Like other citation metrics, it is subject to the general criticism that journal-level averages should not be used to judge individual articles or researchers.
