United Kingdom cladding crisis

The United Kingdom cladding crisis, also called the cladding scandal, is an ongoing social, political and financial crisis that followed the Grenfell Tower fire of 14 June 2017. Investigations revealed that thousands of high-rise and medium-rise residential buildings across the UK had combustible cladding and other fire-safety defects, leaving leaseholders facing large remediation bills and unable to sell their flats.

Background

The Grenfell Tower fire, which killed 72 people, involved aluminium composite material (ACM) cladding with a polyethylene core. Subsequent testing and inspection found widespread use of combustible materials and missing fire barriers in other buildings. The introduction of the EWS1 (External Wall System) form in 2019 effectively froze mortgage lending on many affected flats.

Government response

The UK government established funds including the Building Safety Fund to remediate unsafe buildings, pressed developers to fund repairs, and passed the Building Safety Act 2022, which extended protections for leaseholders and created a new building safety regime. Disputes over costs, timescales and the scope of defects have continued, with campaign groups representing affected residents.
