Task force

A task force (TF) is a temporary grouping formed to work on a single defined task or activity.

A task force is a unit or formation established to work on a single defined task or activity. Originally introduced by the United States Navy, the term has now caught on for general usage and is a standard part of NATO terminology. Many non-military organizations now create "task forces" or task groups for temporary activities that might have once been performed by committees.

Naval usage

The concept of a task force originated in the United States Navy around the time of the Second World War. A task force was a temporary grouping of naval ships and other assets under a single commander, assembled for a particular operation and disbanded afterwards. Task forces were given numbers—such as Task Force 38, the fast carrier task force of the Pacific Fleet—and could be subdivided into task groups and task units. The system proved flexible enough to be retained and was widely adopted by allied navies and by NATO.

Government and law enforcement

In government, a task force is a temporary organization created to study or solve a particular problem, often bringing together personnel from several agencies. Law-enforcement task forces, such as joint terrorism task forces or drug task forces, combine officers from federal, state and local bodies under shared command for a defined mission.

Business and other organizations

Corporations and institutions use task forces for cross-functional projects—product launches, crisis response, or process redesign—where a standing department is not suited to the work. Such groups are typically disbanded once their objective is achieved or their report delivered.
