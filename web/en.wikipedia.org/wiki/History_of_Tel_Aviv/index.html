History of Tel Aviv

Founded1909
Founders66 Jewish families from Jaffa (Ahuzat Bayit)
MergedTel Aviv–Yafo (1950)

The history of Tel Aviv begins in 1909, when the neighbourhood of Ahuzat Bayit was founded on sand dunes north of the ancient port of Jaffa; it grew into Israel's economic and cultural centre.

Foundation

In 1909, 66 Jewish families from Jaffa, organised by Akiva Aryeh Weiss and Meir Dizengoff, raffled plots of land — an allocation legendarily recorded in a photograph of members standing on the empty dunes. The new garden suburb took the name Tel Aviv from Nahum Sokolow's Hebrew translation of Herzl's Altneuland.

British Mandate period

Under the British Mandate, Tel Aviv became a town in 1921 and a city in 1934. Waves of Jewish immigration, especially from Nazi-threatened Europe, drove rapid growth; Patrick Geddes's 1925 master plan and the International Style ("White City") architecture of the 1930s gave it a distinctive modernist fabric, later recognised by UNESCO.

After 1948

Tel Aviv was the provisional seat of government after Israel's declaration of independence on 14 May 1948. In 1950 it merged with Jaffa as Tel Aviv–Yafo. From the 1990s it became the hub of Israel's high-tech "Silicon Wadi", while remaining a centre of arts, nightlife and the country's Hebrew culture.
