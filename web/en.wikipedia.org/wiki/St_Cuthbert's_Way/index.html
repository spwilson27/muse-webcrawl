St Cuthbert's Way is a long-distance footpath in northern England and southern Scotland. It runs for about 100 kilometres (62.5 miles) from Melrose in the Scottish Borders, where Saint Cuthbert began his monastic life around 650, to Lindisfarne (Holy Island) off the Northumberland coast, where he served as bishop and where his shrine was established after his death in 687.

Route

The route, opened in 1996, crosses the Eildon Hills, follows stretches of the River Tweed, Dere Street and the Cheviot Hills, and passes through Jedburgh, Kirk Yetholm (where it meets the Pennine Way) and Wooler before descending to the coast. The final section crosses the tidal sands to Lindisfarne by the Pilgrims' Way, marked by tall poles, and must be timed to the tides; a refuge box stands part-way across for walkers caught by the water.

Context

The walk links sites central to the story of Cuthbert, the most venerated saint of early medieval northern England, whose monks later carried his coffin to Durham after Viking raids. It is one of Scotland's Great Trails and is walked both as a pilgrimage and as a scenic cross-border route.
