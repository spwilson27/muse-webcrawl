Kongsberg Silver Mines

The Kongsberg Silver Mines (Norwegian: Kongsberg Sølvverk) were a mining operation in Kongsberg, Norway, and for over three centuries the largest and most important silver works in the country. Silver was discovered in 1623, and King Christian IV founded the town and works the following year.

History

At their peak in the eighteenth century the mines employed more than 4,000 people, making Kongsberg Norway's largest pre-industrial workplace and its second-largest town. The mines produced native (wire) silver of exceptional quality, specimens of which are now in museums worldwide. Falling silver prices and a major fire in the deep King's Mine led to closure in 1958, after total production of about 1,350 tonnes of silver.

Legacy

The mines are now preserved as the Norwegian Mining Museum, which offers tours on a mine train into the King's Mine, and the mining landscape around Kongsberg is protected cultural heritage.
