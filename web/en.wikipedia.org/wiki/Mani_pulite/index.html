Mani pulite (Italian for "clean hands") was a nationwide judicial investigation into political corruption in Italy held in the early 1990s, conducted by a pool of Milanese magistrates including Antonio Di Pietro. It led to the collapse of the First Republic and the dissolution of most of Italy's traditional governing parties.

Background

The investigation began in February 1992 with the arrest of Mario Chiesa, a Socialist Party official caught accepting a bribe. His testimony exposed a vast system of kickbacks — tangenti — through which parties including the Christian Democrats and the Italian Socialist Party financed themselves from public contracts.

Consequences

Thousands of politicians, businesspeople and officials were investigated; roughly half of the members of parliament came under scrutiny. Former prime minister Bettino Craxi fled to Tunisia to avoid prosecution. The Christian Democracy and Socialist parties dissolved, and the vacuum in Italian politics was filled by new forces, most prominently Silvio Berlusconi's Forza Italia in 1994.
