The Evenks (also spelled Ewenki or Evenki) are a Tungusic people of North Asia, traditionally reindeer herders and hunters of the Siberian taiga. They number roughly 39,000 in Russia and about 30,000 in China, with smaller communities in Mongolia. Their language, Evenki, is the most widely spoken of the northern Tungusic languages.

History

Evenki groups ranged over an enormous territory from the Yenisei river eastward to the Pacific coast and south to the Amur. Russian expansion into Siberia from the 17th century brought them into the fur trade and later into Soviet collectivisation, which disrupted nomadic herding. In China, Evenki herders in Inner Mongolia's Evenki Autonomous Banner are noted as some of the last reindeer-herding communities in the country.

Culture

Traditional Evenki life centred on the conical tent (chum), reindeer transport, and shamanic religious practice — the word "shaman" itself comes from the Evenki language. Modern Evenks live in a mixture of towns, villages, and herding camps, and face pressures on their language and land from industrial development.
