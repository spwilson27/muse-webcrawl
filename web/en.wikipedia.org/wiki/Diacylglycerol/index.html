Diacylglycerol

A diacylglycerol (DAG), or diglyceride, is a glyceride consisting of a glycerol molecule bonded to two fatty acid chains through ester linkages. Diacylglycerols occur naturally in small amounts in fats and oils and play a central role in cell signalling.

Signalling

In signal transduction, DAG is produced when the enzyme phospholipase C cleaves the membrane phospholipid PIP2, also releasing inositol trisphosphate (IP3). DAG remains in the membrane and activates protein kinase C, a family of enzymes involved in cell growth, division and immune responses.

Food use

DAG-rich cooking oils, marketed in Japan and elsewhere, have been promoted on the basis that diacylglycerol is metabolised differently from ordinary triglycerides and may lead to less body-fat accumulation. Diglycerides are also used as emulsifiers (E471 together with monoglycerides) in processed foods.
