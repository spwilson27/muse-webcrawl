Corduene

Corduene (also known as Gorduene, Corduene, Korduene, Kardu, and in Kurdish tradition associated with the land of the Carduchians) was an ancient region located in northern Mesopotamia, in present-day southeastern Turkey and northern Iraq, between the upper Tigris River and the Zagros Mountains. Its inhabitants, the Carduchians, are described by Xenophon in the Anabasis as fierce independent tribesmen who harassed the retreating Ten Thousand.

History

Corduene appears in Assyrian, Persian, Hellenistic, Roman and Parthian sources as a strategically important borderland. It was at various times a vassal kingdom, a Roman province briefly organised under Pompey and again under Diocletian, and a contested frontier between Rome (and later Byzantium) and Persia. Strabo and Pliny mention the region, and Tacitus records its role in the wars of the first century AD.

Identification

The name Corduene is often linked etymologically with the Kurds, and the region corresponds broadly with parts of modern Kurdistan. Scholars debate the degree of continuity between the ancient Carduchians and later peoples of the region.
