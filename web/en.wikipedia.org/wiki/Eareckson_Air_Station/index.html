Eareckson Air Station (IATA: SYA, ICAO: PASY) is a United States Air Force facility located on Shemya Island in the Aleutian Islands chain of Alaska. Situated near the western end of the Aleutians, roughly 2,300 kilometres southwest of Anchorage, it is one of the most remote US military installations. The station is best known for its large Cobra Dane radar, used for tracking ballistic missiles and space objects.

History

Shemya was occupied by US forces during the Second World War, when an airfield was constructed in 1943 as a staging point on the great-circle route to the Pacific theatre and for operations against Japanese forces in the Kuril Islands. The island's flat terrain and strategic position made it valuable throughout the Cold War, when Shemya Air Force Base hosted reconnaissance and surveillance missions close to the Soviet Union. Aircraft from Shemya shadowed Soviet activity, and the island's intelligence role was highlighted by Cold War incidents involving reconnaissance flights in the Bering Sea region.

The base was renamed in honour of Colonel William O. Eareckson, a decorated Army Air Forces commander in the Aleutian campaign. As active flying operations declined, the installation was redesignated an Air Station and placed in caretaker status, with a small contractor-supported presence maintaining the airfield and radar.

Cobra Dane radar

The AN/FPS-108 Cobra Dane is a large phased-array radar installed at Eareckson in the 1970s. Facing northwest across the Bering Sea toward the Kamchatka Peninsula, it was built to observe Soviet missile tests, collecting data on launches from Russian test ranges. Today it also contributes to the United States' space surveillance and missile defence networks, tracking satellites and debris. The radar is operated in support of United States Space Force and Missile Defense Agency missions.

The airfield

Eareckson has a single asphalt runway of about 10,000 feet. Because of its position on the North Pacific great-circle routes, Shemya has served as an emergency diversion field for transpacific flights and as a refuelling stop. The island is notorious for severe and rapidly changing weather, with high winds, fog and storms common throughout the year.
