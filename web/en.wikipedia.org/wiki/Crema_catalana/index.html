Crema catalana

Crema catalana
CourseDessert
Place of originCatalonia, Spain
Main ingredientsMilk, egg yolks, sugar, cornstarch, cinnamon, lemon zest

Crema catalana (Catalan for "Catalan cream") is a custard dessert from Catalonia, Spain, similar to French crème brûlée but set with cornstarch rather than baked, and traditionally flavoured with cinnamon and lemon (or orange) zest rather than vanilla. It is topped with a layer of sugar that is caramelised with a hot iron or flame to form a crisp crust.

The dessert is among the oldest in Europe; recipes appear in Catalan cookbooks such as the fourteenth-century Llibre de Sent Soví tradition, and a custard recipe is documented in the 1745 cookbook of Juan de la Mata. It is also known as crema cremada ("burnt cream") or crema de Sant Josep, because it is traditionally eaten on Saint Joseph's Day (19 March), the feast of the saint considered the father of Jesus.

Preparation

Milk is infused with cinnamon and citrus zest, thickened with egg yolks and cornstarch, cooled in earthenware ramekins, then coated with sugar and caramelised just before serving so the crust stays crisp.
