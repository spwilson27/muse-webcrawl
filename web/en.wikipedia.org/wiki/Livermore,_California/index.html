Livermore, California

Livermore is a city in Alameda County, California, in the Tri-Valley region of the San Francisco Bay Area. Founded in 1869 by William Mendenhall and named for his friend Robert Livermore, it is home to the Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and one of California's oldest wine regions.

History

The area was part of Rancho Las Positas, granted in 1839 and settled by English sailor-turned-rancher Robert Livermore. The town grew with the arrival of the Central Pacific Railroad in 1869 and became known for gravel, ranching and wine—the Livermore Valley was one of the first Californian regions to plant varietal wine grapes.

Science and economy

Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (founded 1952) and Sandia National Laboratories' California site are the city's largest employers. The chemical element livermorium (Lv, atomic number 116) is named after the laboratory and, indirectly, the city. Livermore's Centennial Light Bulb, burning in a local fire station since 1901, is often cited as the world's longest-lasting light bulb.
