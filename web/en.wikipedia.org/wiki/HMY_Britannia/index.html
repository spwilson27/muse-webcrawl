HMY Britannia

Launched16 April 1953
Commissioned11 January 1954
Decommissioned11 December 1997
StatusMuseum ship at Leith, Edinburgh

Her Majesty's Yacht Britannia was the British royal yacht, in service from 1954 until 1997. Built by John Brown & Company at Clydebank, Scotland, she was launched by Queen Elizabeth II on 16 April 1953 and carried the royal family on 696 foreign visits and 272 visits in British waters during her career, travelling more than a million nautical miles.

Service

Britannia was designed to double as a hospital ship in time of war, though this conversion was never required. She served as the venue for state occasions, trade-promotion events and royal honeymoons, carrying a Royal Navy crew of officers and "yachtsmen" and a detachment of Royal Marines. Among her notable voyages was the evacuation of more than 1,000 refugees from Aden in 1986 during the South Yemen civil war. Her annual Western Isles cruise became a fixture of the royal family's summer.

Retirement

The government decided in 1994 not to refit or replace the ageing yacht, and Britannia was decommissioned on 11 December 1997 at Portsmouth in a ceremony attended by the Queen. After competition between British ports, she was berthed permanently at Leith in Edinburgh as a visitor attraction, where she is maintained by the Royal Yacht Britannia Trust and receives several hundred thousand visitors each year.
