National symbols of Australia

The Commonwealth Coat of Arms

The national symbols of Australia are the emblems and icons officially recognised as representing the Commonwealth of Australia and its national identity.

Principal symbols

  • Flag: the Australian national flag, a defaced Blue Ensign bearing the Commonwealth Star and the Southern Cross, adopted in 1901–1903 and confirmed by the Flags Act 1953.
  • Coat of arms: the Commonwealth Coat of Arms, granted in 1912, depicting a shield of the six state badges supported by a red kangaroo and an emu.
  • Anthem: "Advance Australia Fair", proclaimed the national anthem in 1984 (with lyrics amended in 2021); "God Save the King" remains the royal anthem.
  • Colours: green and gold, proclaimed the national colours in 1984, drawn from the golden wattle.
  • Floral emblem: the golden wattle (Acacia pycnantha), proclaimed in 1988.
  • Faunal emblems: the red kangaroo and emu appear on the coat of arms; the national gemstone is the opal (proclaimed 1993).

Other symbols

Each Australian state and territory has its own flag, arms, and floral, bird, animal and fish emblems. Additional national observances include Australia Day on 26 January and the national memorial days Anzac Day and Remembrance Day.
