Incenter

In geometry, the incenter of a triangle is a triangle center, the point where the internal angle bisectors of the triangle cross. It is also the center of the triangle's incircle — the largest circle that fits inside the triangle and is tangent to all three sides.

Properties

The incenter lies at the intersection of the three internal angle bisectors; since the bisectors are concurrent, a single point exists for every triangle. The incenter is equidistant from all three sides of the triangle, and that common distance is the inradius. In trilinear coordinates the incenter is 1 : 1 : 1, and in barycentric coordinates it is a : b : c, where a, b, c are the side lengths.

Cartesian coordinates

If the vertices of the triangle have Cartesian coordinates (x1, y1), (x2, y2), (x3, y3), the incenter is the weighted average of the vertices with weights equal to the lengths of the opposite sides. The inradius r relates to the area Δ and semiperimeter s by the formula Δ = r s.

Related points

The reflections of the setup give the three excenters, the centers of the excircles tangent to one side and the extensions of the other two. The incenter, centroid, circumcenter, and orthocenter are among the most studied of the thousands of catalogued triangle centers.
