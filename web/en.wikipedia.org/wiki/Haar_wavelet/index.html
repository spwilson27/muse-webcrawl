Haar wavelet

The Haar wavelet is a sequence of rescaled "square-shaped" functions which together form a wavelet family or basis. Proposed by Alfréd Haar in his 1909 doctoral thesis (published 1910), it is the simplest possible wavelet: a step function that takes the value 1 on the first half of its support and −1 on the second half, and zero elsewhere.

Definition

The Haar mother wavelet is ψ(t) = 1 for 0 ≤ t < 1/2, −1 for 1/2 ≤ t < 1, and 0 otherwise. Dilations and translations of this function, together with the constant scaling function, form an orthonormal basis for the space of square-integrable functions on the real line. The associated discrete transform computes pairwise averages and differences of a signal, which can be applied recursively.

Properties and uses

The Haar wavelet is discontinuous and not differentiable, which limits its use where smoothness is required, but its simplicity makes it valuable in signal and image processing, data compression, and as an introductory example in wavelet theory. It is also applied in computer graphics, for example in the efficient computation of light transport, and in time-series analysis.
