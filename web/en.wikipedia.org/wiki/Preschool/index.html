Preschool

Children engaged in play-based learning at a preschool

A preschool, also known as a nursery school, pre-primary school, or play school, is an educational establishment or learning space offering early childhood education to children before they begin compulsory education at primary school. Preschools typically serve children between the ages of about three and five, though definitions vary by country.

Purpose and curriculum

Preschool education aims to support children's development across social, emotional, physical, and cognitive domains. Rather than focusing on formal academic instruction, most preschool programs emphasize play-based learning, language development, early numeracy concepts, motor skills, and socialization with peers. Approaches vary widely: well-known philosophies include the Montessori method, the Reggio Emilia approach, Waldorf education, and HighScope.

History

The idea of educating young children outside the home developed in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. Friedrich Fröbel founded the first kindergarten in Germany in 1837, emphasizing play, songs, and creative activities. Infant schools for younger children were established in Britain by Robert Owen and others. In the United States, nursery schools grew during the early 20th century, and public programs expanded significantly with the launch of Head Start in 1965, which provided early education to children from low-income families.

Effects and policy debates

Research on preschool's effects is extensive. Long-term studies of intensive programs such as the Perry Preschool Project and the Abecedarian Project found lasting benefits in educational attainment, earnings, and reduced crime rates. However, the magnitude and persistence of benefits from large-scale public programs remain debated, and quality is considered a key factor. Many countries have moved toward universal preschool provision; in several European nations, attendance for children aged three to five is near-universal and heavily subsidized.

Terminology

In the United States, "preschool" generally refers to programs for three- and four-year-olds, while "pre-kindergarten" (pre-K) often denotes programs for four-year-olds in the year before kindergarten. In the United Kingdom, provision includes nursery schools and nursery classes within primary schools. In many systems preschool is distinct from childcare or daycare, though the two often overlap in practice.
