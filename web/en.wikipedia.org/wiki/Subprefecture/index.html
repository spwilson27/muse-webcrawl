Subprefecture

A subprefecture is an administrative division that sits below the level of a prefecture. The term, and translations of equivalent local terms, is used in several countries where prefectures form a major tier of government, with subprefectures serving as a regional branch office or an intermediate tier between the prefecture and municipalities.

Japan

In Japan, several prefectures — notably Hokkaido, Yamagata, and Nagano — maintain subprefectural bureaus (支庁, shichō) as branch offices of the prefectural government. Hokkaido's are the most prominent: its fourteen subprefectures were reorganized in 2010 into nine comprehensive and five ordinary subprefectural bureaus, delivering prefectural services across the island's large distances. Kagoshima Prefecture likewise retains subprefectures for its remote island territories, and Tokyo Metropolis has subprefectural offices administering its Pacific island chains (the Izu and Ogasawara islands).

France

In France, a sous-préfecture is the administrative centre of an arrondissement that is not the prefecture of its department. Each is headed by a subprefect (sous-préfet), who represents the state locally, coordinating communes and state services. There are roughly 230 subprefectures in metropolitan and overseas France.

China and other countries

In the People's Republic of China, a sub-prefectural city is a county-level city that is granted, in practice, economic and administrative powers approaching those of a prefecture-level city. The term also appears elsewhere: Guinea is divided into prefectures and subprefectures (sous-préfectures) as its second-level divisions, and Brazil historically used subprefeituras — São Paulo still administers its districts through subprefectures headed by appointed subprefects.
