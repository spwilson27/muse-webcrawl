Orhan Pamuk, Turkish novelist and Nobel laureate.

Ferit Orhan Pamuk (born 7 June 1952) is a Turkish novelist, screenwriter, academic, and recipient of the 2006 Nobel Prize in Literature. One of Turkey's most prominent novelists, his work has sold over thirteen million books in sixty-three languages, making him Turkey's best-selling writer. He is a professor of comparative literature and writing at Columbia University.

Pamuk is the author of the novels Silent House, The White Castle, The Black Book, The New Life, My Name Is Red, Snow, The Museum of Innocence, A Strangeness in My Mind, The Red-Haired Woman, and Nights of Plague. He is the Robert Yik-Fong Tam Professor in the Humanities at Columbia University, where he teaches comparative literature and writing. His work frequently explores the conflict between East and West, tradition and modernity, and the melancholic soul of his native Istanbul — a quality he calls hüzün.

Early life and career

Pamuk was born in Istanbul into a wealthy, secular family. He studied architecture at Istanbul Technical University for three years before abandoning it to become a full-time writer, later graduating from the Institute of Journalism at Istanbul University in 1976. His first novel, Cevdet Bey and His Sons (1982), a family saga in the manner of Thomas Mann, won the Orhan Kemal Novel Prize. The White Castle (1985), a historical novel about a Venetian slave and his Ottoman master in 17th-century Istanbul, brought him international attention.

Major works

My Name Is Red (1998), a murder mystery set among Ottoman miniaturist painters in 1591, won the IMPAC Dublin Literary Award in 2003 and is widely considered his masterpiece. Snow (2002), set in the border city of Kars, follows a poet investigating a wave of suicides among schoolgirls and was named one of the best books of 2004 by The New York Times. In 2008 he published The Museum of Innocence, and in 2012 he opened an actual museum of the same name in Istanbul, displaying objects evoked by the novel — the first of its kind. His memoir Istanbul: Memories and the City (2003) interweaves his own life with the history of the city.

Nobel Prize and controversies

In 2006 Pamuk became the first Turkish citizen to receive the Nobel Prize in Literature; the Swedish Academy praised him as an author who "in the quest for the melancholic soul of his native city has discovered new symbols for the clash and interlacing of cultures." In 2005 he had been charged under Article 301 of the Turkish penal code for remarks about the deaths of Armenians and Kurds; the charges were dropped in 2006 amid international pressure. He has lived in the United States since 2006, dividing his time between New York and Istanbul.
