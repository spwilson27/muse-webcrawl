Kew Bridge

Kew Bridge is a wide-span bridge over the Tideway (upper estuary of the River Thames) in London, linking Kew Green in the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames to Brentford and Chiswick in the London Borough of Hounslow. The present bridge, a stone arch structure, was opened by King Edward VII in 1903.

History

The first bridge on the site, built of wood, opened in 1759 and was privately built by Robert Tunstall. A second bridge, designed by James Paine, replaced it in 1789. The current bridge was designed by Sir John Wolfe Barry and Cuthbert Brereton, built of Cornish granite, and opened on 20 May 1903 as the King Edward VII Bridge, reverting to the name Kew Bridge. It carries the A205 South Circular Road and is Grade II listed.

Surroundings

On the south bank lies Kew Green and the entrance to the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew; on the north bank are Brentford and the former Kew Bridge railway station area. The bridge stands near the London Museum of Water & Steam.
