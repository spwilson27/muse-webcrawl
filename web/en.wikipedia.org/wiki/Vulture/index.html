Vulture

A vulture is a bird of prey that scavenges on carrion. There are 23 extant species in two groups that are not closely related: Old World vultures of Africa, Asia and Europe (family Accipitridae), and New World vultures of the Americas (family Cathartidae); their similarity is a classic example of convergent evolution.

GroupsOld World vultures; New World vultures
DietCarrion (scavengers)
Species23 extant

Characteristics

Vultures typically have bald or lightly feathered heads, an adaptation that keeps them clean when feeding inside carcasses, and extremely acidic stomachs that destroy pathogens such as anthrax and botulinum toxin. Old World vultures locate food by sight, while several New World species, such as the turkey vulture, find carrion by an acute sense of smell.

Conservation

Many vulture populations have crashed. In South Asia, the veterinary drug diclofenac in livestock carcasses caused declines of more than 95% in several Gyps species during the 1990s and 2000s; African vultures face poisoning and declines linked to poaching. By removing carcasses, vultures provide a major ecosystem service that limits the spread of disease.
