Howdy Doody

Howdy Doody is an American children's television program (with circus and Western frontier themes) that was created and produced by Victor F. Campbell and E. Roger Muir. It was broadcast on the NBC network in the United States from December 27, 1947, until September 24, 1960. It was a pioneer of children's programming and set the pattern for many similar shows.

Characters

The central character, Howdy Doody, is a freckle-faced boy marionette with 48 freckles, one for each state of the union at the time of his creation. The show's host was "Buffalo Bob" Smith. Other characters included Clarabell the Clown, played first by Bob Keeshan (later Captain Kangaroo) and later by Bobby Nicholson and Lew Anderson, and the puppet animals Flub-a-Dub and Phineas T. Bluster.

Legacy

The show's young audience members seated in the studio were collectively known as the "Peanut Gallery". The catchphrase "Say, kids, what time is it?" — answered "It's Howdy Doody time!" — became famous in early American television.
