Betulaceae, the birch family, is a family of flowering plants belonging to the order Fagales. It comprises about 167 species of trees and shrubs in six genera, chiefly native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere, with a few species reaching the South American Andes.

Description

Members of the family are mostly deciduous trees and shrubs with simple, alternate, toothed leaves. The flowers are unisexual and borne in catkins; the plants are monoecious and wind-pollinated. Fruits are small nuts or samaras, often grouped in cone-like clusters, as in the alders.

Uses

The family includes economically important trees: birches (Betula) for timber and ornamental planting, alders (Alnus) for soil improvement through nitrogen fixation, hornbeams and hop-hornbeams for hard wood, and hazels (Corylus), which produce hazelnuts.
