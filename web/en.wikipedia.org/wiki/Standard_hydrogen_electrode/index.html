The standard hydrogen electrode (abbreviated SHE) is a redox electrode which forms the basis of the thermodynamic scale of oxidation-reduction potentials. It consists of a platinum electrode immersed in an acidic solution of unit hydrogen-ion activity, over which pure hydrogen gas is bubbled at a pressure of one bar. Its standard electrode potential is declared to be zero volts at all temperatures, providing the reference against which all other electrode potentials are measured.

Construction

The SHE uses platinized platinum — platinum coated with a fine layer of platinum black to increase its surface area and catalyse the hydrogen reaction. Hydrogen gas at standard pressure is passed over the electrode, and the acid concentration is chosen so that the activity of hydrogen ions is unity. A salt bridge or porous junction connects the half-cell to the electrode being measured.

The scale of potentials

By connecting another half-cell to the SHE, the potential of the combination gives the standard electrode potential of that electrode directly. Strongly reducing couples such as lithium have potentials near −3.04 V versus SHE, while strongly oxidising couples such as fluorine lie near +2.87 V. In practice, more convenient secondary references — such as the saturated calomel electrode and the silver/silver chloride electrode — are used in laboratories and converted back to the SHE scale.
