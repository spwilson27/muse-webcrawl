The Amduat ("That Which Is in the Afterlife", also called the Text of the Hidden Chamber Which is in the Underworld) is an important ancient Egyptian funerary text of the New Kingdom. It describes the journey of the sun god Ra through the underworld (the Duat) during the twelve hours of the night, from sunset in the west to sunrise in the east, and his triumph over the forces of chaos.

Content

The text divides the night into twelve hours, each corresponding to a region of the underworld depicted in a three-register composition of images with captions. Ra travels in his solar barque, accompanied by deities such as Sia, Heka and the goddess of each hour. In the sixth hour the dead king's soul unites with Ra's corpse; in the seventh hour Ra confronts the serpent Apep (Apophis), who is bound and repelled by Isis and the cat of Ra; the twelfth hour ends with Ra reborn as Khepri, the scarab, emerging at dawn. Hundreds of named beings — gods, demons, the blessed dead and the damned — populate the registers, making the Amduat a kind of map and catalogue of the afterlife.

History

The earliest surviving version decorates the tomb of Thutmose III (KV34) in the Valley of the Kings, where the entire text is laid out like an unrolled papyrus on the burial chamber walls; a shorter version appears in the tomb of his vizier Useramun. The Amduat remained in use throughout the New Kingdom and influenced later underworld books such as the Book of Gates.
