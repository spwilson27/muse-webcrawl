Overview

Chinese science fiction is the body of speculative fiction written in China and in Chinese, from late Qing reformist novels to the internationally celebrated works of the twenty-first century. After periods of suppression and revival, the genre achieved global prominence in the 2010s, symbolised by Liu Cixin's Hugo Award for The Three-Body Problem (translated by Ken Liu) in 2015.

History

Early Chinese science fiction, such as the unfinished New Era (1908) and Lao She's satirical Cat Country (1932), used the genre to debate China's modernisation. After 1949 the genre was promoted mainly as children's science popularisation, flourished briefly in the late 1970s under authors such as Ye Yonglie, and fell under political suspicion in the 1983 Anti-Spiritual Pollution Campaign. A new generation centred on the Chengdu magazine Science Fiction World revived it in the 1990s and 2000s.

Contemporary scene

Alongside Liu Cixin's hard science fiction, writers such as Han Song, Hao Jingfang (winner of the 2016 Hugo Award for Best Novelette for 'Folding Beijing'), Wang Jinkang and Chen Qiufan explore social and political themes. The city of Chengdu hosted the 2023 World Science Fiction Convention (Worldcon), marking the genre's institutional arrival.
