The President of the Republic of Liberia is the head of state and head of government of Liberia, as well as commander-in-chief of the Armed Forces of Liberia. Modelled on the presidency of the United States, the office was created by the Constitution of 1847, adopted when Liberia declared independence from the American Colonization Society. The president is elected for a six-year term and may be re-elected once.

History

Joseph Jenkins Roberts, formerly governor of the Commonwealth of Liberia, became the first president in 1848. For more than a century the True Whig Party dominated Liberian politics, and from 1878 to 1980 every president came from the Americo-Liberian settler community. The longest-serving president, William Tubman (1944–1971), presided over modernization and an "Open Door" policy toward foreign investment. His successor, William Tolbert, was killed in the 1980 coup led by Samuel Doe, ending 133 years of settler rule.

Doe's regime gave way to civil war in 1989, and warlord Charles Taylor won the 1997 election held during a fragile peace. Renewed conflict forced Taylor into exile in 2003. Ellen Johnson Sirleaf, elected in 2005 and re-elected in 2011, became Africa's first elected female head of state and led postwar reconstruction and the response to the 2014–2016 Ebola epidemic. She was succeeded in 2018 by former football star George Weah — Liberia's first peaceful, democratic transfer of power between elected presidents in decades. Joseph Boakai defeated Weah in the 2023 election and took office in January 2024.

Powers

The president appoints cabinet ministers and judges with the consent of the Senate, commands the armed forces, conducts foreign relations, and may grant pardons and veto legislation, subject to override by a two-thirds majority of both houses of the Legislature. The president delivers an annual message to the Legislature each January.

Eligibility and election

Under the 1986 Constitution, a presidential candidate must be a natural-born Liberian citizen of at least 35 years of age, own real property of a prescribed value, and have resided in Liberia for at least ten years before the election. Presidents are chosen by popular vote, with a runoff between the top two candidates if no one wins an absolute majority in the first round.
