Alfred the Great

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Alfred Jewel, an enamel and gold artefact inscribed 'Alfred ordered me to be made']

Alfred the Great (c. 849 – 26 October 899) was King of the West Saxons from 871 and King of the Anglo-Saxons from about 886 until his death — the only English monarch called 'the Great'. He defended Wessex against the Viking Great Heathen Army when every other Anglo-Saxon kingdom had fallen, defeating the Danes at Edington (878) and confining them to the Danelaw; then rebuilt his kingdom with fortified burhs, a reorganised army and navy, law reform, and a famous programme of translation and education that revived learning in English. His successors — son Edward, daughter Æthelflæd, grandson Æthelstan — completed the unification of England on Alfred's foundations.

The Viking wars

Alfred succeeded his brother Æthelred I amid invasion in 871, the 'year of nine battles'. By 878 Guthrum's Danes had overrun Wessex itself in a midwinter surprise attack; Alfred held out in the Somerset marshes at Athelney, rallied his thegns, and crushed Guthrum at Edington. The peace brought Guthrum's baptism and a treaty defining the Danelaw boundary. Renewed invasions in the 890s were contained by Alfred's system of rotating fyrd service, burhs garrisoned within a day's march of anywhere in Wessex, and longships of his own design.

King of learning

Alfred lamented England's decayed learning and set scholars — Asser (his Welsh biographer), Grimbald, John the Old Saxon — to translate 'the books most necessary for all men to know' into English: Gregory's Pastoral Care, Boethius' Consolation, Orosius' history, Bede. He issued a law code (the Doom Book), encouraged literacy among his officials, and re-founded London (886). Later legend made him the founder of Oxford University (a fiction), burner of cakes (a later anecdote), and father of the Royal Navy — exaggerations of a real achievement.
