Condé Nast

Condé Nast is a global mass media company founded in 1909 by Condé Montrose Nast, and owned by Advance Publications. The company publishes many of the world's best-known magazine brands, including Vogue, The New Yorker, GQ, Vanity Fair, Wired, Architectural Digest and Bon Appétit.

History

Nast bought Vogue in 1909 and transformed it into a leading fashion magazine, pioneering class publications aimed at defined, affluent audiences attractive to advertisers. The company expanded internationally, added titles such as House & Garden and Glamour, and acquired The New Yorker in 1985. Since the 2010s it has restructured around digital media, video and global editions, headquartered at One World Trade Center.

Brands

Its portfolio spans fashion, culture, technology, food, travel and design, with international editions in many markets.
