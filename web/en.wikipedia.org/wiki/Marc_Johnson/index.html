Marc Johnson (born 21 October 1953) is an American jazz double bassist, composer, and bandleader. He is known for his work in the final trio of pianist Bill Evans, for his long association with pianist John Scofield, and for his own groups of the 1990s and later, which include recordings for ECM Records.

Early life and career

Johnson grew up in Omaha, Nebraska, and studied at North Texas State University and the University of Chicago. He first came to wide attention in 1977 when he joined the Woody Herman band. In 1978 he became the bassist in Bill Evans's last trio, alongside drummer Joe LaBarbera, remaining with Evans until the pianist's death in 1980 and recording with him on albums including Affinity and We Will Meet Again.

Later work

In the 1980s Johnson worked extensively with the pianist John Scofield, with Lyle Mays, and with the saxophonist Stan Getz, whose quartet he played in during the late 1980s. He also formed his own groups: Bass Desires (with guitarists Bill Frisell and John Scofield and drummer Peter Erskine), and, in the late 1990s, a quartet with pianist John Taylor, saxophonist John Surman, and drummer Joey Baron that recorded for ECM. He has recorded as a leader for Verve and ECM, and his 1999 album The Sound of Summer Running for Verve is among his best-known recordings. He is married to the Argentine pianist Eliane Elias, with whom he has frequently recorded.

Style

Johnson is admired for a warm, full sound on the double bass, melodic soloing, and a lyrical, supportive approach to accompaniment that recalls his time with Evans, balanced by a rhythmic drive shaped by his years in more groove-oriented bands.
