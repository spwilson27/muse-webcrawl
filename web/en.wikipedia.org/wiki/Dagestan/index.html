Dagestan

The Republic of Dagestan is a republic of Russia situated in the North Caucasus of Eastern Europe, along the Caspian Sea. As the southernmost part of Russia, it covers an area of about 50,300 square kilometres (19,400 sq mi) and has a population of roughly 3.2 million, making it the largest and most populous republic in the North Caucasian Federal District. Its capital and largest city is Makhachkala, on the Caspian coast. The name Dagestan derives from the Turkic word dağ, "mountain", and the Persian suffix -stan, "land" — "land of the mountains".

Geography and peoples

Dagestan borders Azerbaijan and Georgia to the south and southwest, the Russian republics of Chechnya and Kalmykia to the west and north, and Stavropol Krai to the northwest. The terrain rises from the Caspian lowland into the Greater Caucasus, whose gorges historically sheltered dozens of distinct communities. Dagestan is Russia’s most ethnically diverse republic: the largest groups are the Avars, Dargins, Kumyks, Lezgins, Laks, Tabasarans, Nogais, Rutuls, Aguls, and ethnic Russians, and more than thirty languages are spoken, with Russian serving as lingua franca.

History

The city of Derbent, one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Russia, was fortified by Sasanian Persia in the 6th century as a gateway between the steppe and Persia; its citadel and old town are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Arab armies brought Islam to the region from the 7th century, and in the 19th century Dagestan was the heart of Imam Shamil’s resistance during the Caucasian War, before being incorporated into the Russian Empire in 1860s. Under the USSR it became the Dagestan ASSR. In 1999 Islamist militants based in Chechnya invaded the republic and were repelled by federal forces and local militias, an event that helped precipitate the Second Chechen War. Since then Dagestan has experienced an Islamist insurgency of varying intensity, alongside its traditional Sufi religious establishment.

Economy and culture

The republic’s economy rests on oil and natural gas extraction, agriculture and viticulture (Dagestani brandy is well known), fishing on the Caspian, hydroelectric power, and handicrafts such as the silverwork of Kubachi and the carpets of Tabasaran villages. Dagestan is also celebrated for producing many world-class wrestlers, judoka, and mixed martial artists, including Khabib Nurmagomedov.
