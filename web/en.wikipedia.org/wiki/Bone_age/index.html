Bone age

Bone age is the degree of a person's skeletal maturation. A bone age study, typically an X-ray of the left hand and wrist, is used by doctors — especially paediatric endocrinologists — to assess how a child's skeleton is developing compared with the population average, and to help estimate the child's remaining growth and adult height.

Methods

The two classical approaches compare the radiograph against standards. The Greulich and Pyle method (1950, revised 1959) matches the image to plates in an atlas of typical hand films for each age and sex. The Tanner–Whitehouse method scores individual bones for their stage of maturation and sums the scores into an age. Computerised systems such as BoneXpert automate the measurement and have largely standardised the reading in many centres. In infants and very young children, a knee or foot film may be used instead.

Interpretation

The bone age is reported in years and compared with the chronological age. A bone age more than about two standard deviations from the mean is considered abnormal. Delayed bone age occurs in constitutional growth delay, hypothyroidism, growth hormone deficiency and chronic illness. Advanced bone age is seen in precocious puberty, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, hyperthyroidism and some genetic syndromes. Because bone maturation determines when the growth plates close, the bone age is combined with current height and parental heights to predict adult stature (for example by the Bayley–Pinneau tables).

Other uses

Bone age is also used in orthodontics, in monitoring treatment with growth hormone or puberty blockers, and controversially in forensic age estimation of young people without documents.
