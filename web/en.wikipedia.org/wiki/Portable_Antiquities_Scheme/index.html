Portable Antiquities Scheme

The Portable Antiquities Scheme (PAS) is a voluntary programme in England and Wales to record archaeological objects found by members of the public, including metal detectorists. It is managed by the British Museum in partnership with local museums and other bodies.

Operation

Pilot schemes began in 1997 alongside the Treasure Act 1996, and the scheme went nationwide in 2003. A network of Finds Liaison Officers identifies and records finds, photographing and describing them in a public online database (finds.org.uk), which has recorded well over a million objects. The PAS complements the legal requirement to report potential Treasure, and is credited with transforming knowledge of certain periods — notable discoveries recorded through it include the Staffordshire Hoard (2009) and the Crosby Garrett Helmet (2010).

Purpose

By recording finds and their findspots, the scheme captures information that would otherwise be lost, feeding research on settlement, economy, and material culture. It is regarded internationally as a model for engaging the public in archaeology, though it remains debated among archaeologists concerned about unrecorded detecting.
