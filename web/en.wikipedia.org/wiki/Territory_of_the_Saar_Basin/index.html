Territory of the Saar Basin

Capital: Saarbrücken
Period: 1920–1935
Status: League of Nations mandate territory

The Territory of the Saar Basin (German: Saarbeckengebiet; French: Territoire du bassin de la Sarre) was a region of Germany occupied and governed by the United Kingdom and France from 1920 to 1935 under a League of Nations mandate. It was created by the Treaty of Versailles following the First World War, largely so that France could exploit the Saar's rich coal mines as war reparations; the mines were placed under French ownership while the territory was administered by a Governing Commission appointed by the League of Nations.

Administration

The Saar Basin covered about 1,912 square kilometres and had a population of roughly 800,000. It formed a customs union with France and used the French franc. The Governing Commission, composed of five members, exercised executive authority. Local political life continued through an elected advisory council (Landesrat), dominated by German parties that campaigned for reunion with Germany.

1935 plebiscite

After fifteen years, the treaty provided for a plebiscite. On 13 January 1935, over 90% of voters chose reunification with Germany, about 8.9% voted for the status quo, and only 0.4% for union with France. The territory was returned to Germany on 1 March 1935 and renamed the Saarland. The outcome was celebrated by the Nazi regime as a propaganda triumph.
