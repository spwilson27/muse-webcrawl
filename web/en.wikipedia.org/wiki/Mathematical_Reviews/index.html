Mathematical Reviews is a journal and online bibliographic database published by the American Mathematical Society (AMS) that contains brief synopses and expert reviews of many articles in mathematics, statistics and theoretical computer science, as well as books and conference proceedings. Its electronic version is MathSciNet.

History

Mathematical Reviews was founded in 1940 by the AMS, with Otto E. Neugebauer — who had earlier founded the German reviewing journal Zentralblatt für Mathematik — as its first editor. It was created amid the disruption of European scholarly publishing by the Second World War and disputes over editorial policy at Zentralblatt, and quickly became the principal English-language reviewing service for mathematics. For decades it appeared as a monthly print journal; the underlying database was computerised from the 1980s, and in 1996 it was launched online as MathSciNet, which has since become the primary means of access.

Content and review process

For most indexed items, a reviewer — a practising mathematician chosen by the editors — writes a short review summarising the paper's results and context; some items receive only a brief listing. Each item receives a unique identifier (the MR number), and papers are classified using the Mathematics Subject Classification (MSC), maintained jointly with zbMATH. MathSciNet additionally provides author identification (each author has a unique ID), citation data and links between reviews, containing several million items and growing by well over one hundred thousand entries per year.
