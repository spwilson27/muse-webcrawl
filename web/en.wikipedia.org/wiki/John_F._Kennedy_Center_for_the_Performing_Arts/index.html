The John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts (commonly referred to as the Kennedy Center) is the national cultural center of the United States, located on the eastern bank of the Potomac River in Washington, D.C. Authorised by the National Cultural Center Act of 1958, it was dedicated as a "living memorial" to President John F. Kennedy after his assassination.

History and architecture

The building, designed by Edward Durell Stone, is a monumental marble-clad structure with a grand foyer over 630 feet long. It opened on September 8, 1971, with the premiere of Leonard Bernstein's Mass. The Concert Hall and Opera House are its largest venues; the Kennedy Center Honors, among the most prestigious American performing-arts awards, have been presented there annually since 1978, and the Mark Twain Prize for American Humor since 1998.

The REACH

In 2019 the center opened the REACH, an expansion designed by Steven Holl adding rehearsal, education and performance spaces and a pedestrian bridge to the Potomac riverfront.

Programming

The Kennedy Center presents theatre, dance, opera, ballet, orchestral, chamber, jazz and folk performances, and is home to the National Symphony Orchestra and the Washington National Opera. Its free daily Millennium Stage performances make it one of the most visited performing arts centers in the country.
