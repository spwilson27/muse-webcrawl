High-dynamic-range video

High-dynamic-range video (HDR video) is a video technology that represents images with a wider range of luminance and colour than standard dynamic range (SDR) video. HDR video allows brighter highlights, darker shadows, and more saturated colours to be reproduced simultaneously, more closely approximating the capabilities of the human visual system.

Characteristics

HDR video is typically mastered at 10 bits per channel or more, using a wide colour gamut such as Rec. 2020 and an absolute or relative transfer function. The two principal transfer functions are Perceptual Quantizer (PQ), an absolute function supporting up to 10,000 cd/m², and Hybrid Log-Gamma (HLG), a relative function designed for live broadcast.

Formats and distribution

The main HDR video formats are HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Vision, and HLG. HDR video is distributed on Ultra HD Blu-ray discs, through streaming platforms, and increasingly in broadcast television. Modern smartphones can also record HDR video, with Dolby Vision recording introduced on the iPhone 12 in 2020.

Production

Producing HDR video requires cameras capable of capturing a wide dynamic range, colour grading in an HDR pipeline, and metadata describing the mastering display. Tone mapping is used to convert HDR content for SDR displays, while the reverse process is applied when upconverting legacy material.
