Hierarchy of hazard controls

The hierarchy of hazard controls is a system used in occupational safety and health to minimize or eliminate exposure to hazards. It ranks control methods from most to least effective and is commonly drawn as an inverted pyramid or triangle. It is a core principle promoted by agencies such as NIOSH and OSHA.

The five levels

  • Elimination — physically removing the hazard entirely; the most effective control.
  • Substitution — replacing the hazard with a safer alternative, such as a less toxic chemical.
  • Engineering controls — isolating people from the hazard, e.g. ventilation, machine guards, and enclosures; these do not require worker behavior to work.
  • Administrative controls — changing the way people work: procedures, training, signage, schedules, and exposure limits.
  • Personal protective equipment (PPE) — respirators, gloves, eye protection; the least effective level because it depends on correct, consistent use and does not remove the hazard.

Rationale

Controls higher in the hierarchy are preferred because they are built into the design of the workplace and protect everyone regardless of individual behavior. In practice, employers usually apply combinations of controls, and the hierarchy guides prioritization during risk assessments and the design of safe systems of work.
