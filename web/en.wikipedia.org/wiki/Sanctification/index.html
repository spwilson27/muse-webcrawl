Sanctification

Field: Christian theology

Related concepts: Justification, holiness, theosis, Christian perfection

Sanctification is the process in Christian theology by which a believer is made holy — set apart for God and progressively conformed to the image of Christ — through the work of the Holy Spirit. The term derives from the Latin sanctificatio, itself translating the Greek hagiasmos, "consecration" or "purification".

Biblical basis

Scripture speaks of sanctification both as a definitive act at conversion — believers are described as already "sanctified" (1 Corinthians 6:11) — and as a lifelong growth in holiness (1 Thessalonians 4–5; Hebrews 12:14). It is closely related to, but in most Protestant traditions distinguished from, justification.

Views in different traditions

Roman Catholic theology understands sanctification as an infusion of grace, renewed and increased through the sacraments, by which the justified person genuinely becomes righteous. Reformed theology teaches a definitive breach with sin at regeneration followed by progressive sanctification until death. Wesleyan traditions emphasise the possibility of "entire sanctification" or Christian perfection in this life, while Eastern Orthodox theology frames the goal as theosis, participation in the divine life.

Practices

Sanctification is traditionally associated with the "means of grace": prayer, Scripture reading, the sacraments or ordinances, fellowship, and disciplines such as fasting and works of mercy.
