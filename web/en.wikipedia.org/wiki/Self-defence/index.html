Self-defence

Self-defence (self-defense in American English) is a countermeasure that involves defending the health and well-being of oneself from harm. The use of the right of self-defence as a legal justification for the use of force in times of danger is available in many jurisdictions.

Physical self-defence

Physical self-defence is the use of physical force to counter an immediate threat of violence. Such force can be either armed or unarmed. In many cases the chances of success depend on the severity of the threat, the mental and physical preparation of the defender, and the legal context. Training systems range from traditional martial arts such as judo, karate, and Brazilian jiu-jitsu to modern reality-based systems such as Krav Maga.

Legal aspects

In criminal law, self-defence is generally a defence to charges such as assault or homicide, provided the force used was reasonable and proportionate to the threat. Many jurisdictions impose a duty to retreat where it is safe to do so, while others recognise "stand your ground" rules. In international law, Article 51 of the United Nations Charter preserves the inherent right of individual or collective self-defence if an armed attack occurs; see self-defence in international law.

Other forms

Verbal self-defence, de-escalation, avoidance, and awareness of surroundings are widely taught as the first line of defence, since most confrontations can be prevented without physical force. Non-violent self-defence techniques emphasise escape and seeking help.
