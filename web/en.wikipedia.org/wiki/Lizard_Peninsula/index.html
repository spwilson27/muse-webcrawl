Lizard Peninsula

The Lizard
Location: Cornwall, England
Southernmost point: Lizard Point
Geology: Ophiolite complex, serpentine
Designation: Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty

The Lizard Peninsula, known locally as the Lizard, is a peninsula in southern Cornwall, England. It is the most southerly point of the British mainland at Lizard Point, and the peninsula's unusual geology — an exposed ophiolite complex of serpentine and other metamorphic rocks — gives it distinctive soils, flora and a mild maritime climate.

Geology and landscape

The Lizard complex is a fragment of ancient oceanic crust thrust onto the continent during the Variscan orogeny. Its serpentine rock polishes to a deep red-green stone used for ornaments, and its heathlands, such as Goonhilly Downs, support rare plants including the Cornish heath. The plateau is cut by coves and fishing villages including Cadgwith, Coverack, Mullion and Kynance Cove, famed for its turquoise water and serpentine cliffs.

History and landmarks

Lizard Point has a lighthouse (built 1751) guarding a dangerous shipping coast notorious for wrecks. The peninsula also hosts the former Marconi wireless station at Poldhu, from which early transatlantic signals were sent; the Lizard Wireless Station and the later satellite earth station at Goonhilly; and RNAS Culdrose near the largest settlement, Helston. Much of the coast is owned by the National Trust and forms part of the Cornwall Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, traversed by the South West Coast Path.
