Pearl Harbor

Location: Oahu, Hawaii, United States
Type: Lagoon harbour; US naval base
Known for: Attack on Pearl Harbor, 7 December 1941

Pearl Harbor is an American lagoon harbor on the island of Oahu, Hawaii, west of Honolulu. It has been long visited by the Naval fleet of the United States, before it was acquired by the United States and established as a naval base. As the home of the United States Pacific Fleet, the harbor was the target of the surprise attack by the Empire of Japan on 7 December 1941, which brought the United States into the Second World War.

History

Known to Native Hawaiians as Puʻuloa and as the "waters of the pearl oysters" (Wai Momi), the harbor was once home to pearl oysters in its shallows. The United States obtained exclusive rights to enter Pearl Harbor and maintain a coaling and repair station there under the Reciprocity Treaty of 1875 and its 1887 extension. Naval development accelerated after the Spanish–American War, and the base grew into one of the most important American naval installations in the Pacific.

Attack on Pearl Harbor

On the morning of Sunday, 7 December 1941, aircraft from six Japanese carriers struck the Pacific Fleet at anchor. The attack sank or damaged eight battleships — including USS Arizona, which exploded and sank with more than 1,100 of her crew — and killed 2,403 Americans. The Pacific Fleet's aircraft carriers were at sea and escaped destruction. The following day the United States declared war on Japan; Germany and Italy declared war on the United States three days later. President Franklin D. Roosevelt called 7 December "a date which will live in infamy."

Pearl Harbor today

The harbor remains an active naval base — Joint Base Pearl Harbor–Hickam — and the headquarters of the US Pacific Fleet. The Pearl Harbor National Memorial, including the USS Arizona Memorial built over the sunken battleship, the battleship USS Missouri, and the Pacific Aviation Museum, commemorates the attack and the wider Pacific War.
