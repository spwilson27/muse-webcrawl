Nueva trova

Nueva trova ("new trova") is a Cuban musical movement that emerged in the late 1960s and 1970s, combining traditional Cuban trova songwriting with contemporary influences such as rock, jazz, bossa nova, and the international protest-song and nueva canción movements. Its lyrics are often poetic, introspective, and engaged with the ideals — and later the complexities — of the Cuban Revolution.

History

The movement took shape around 1967–68, when young singer-songwriters including Silvio Rodríguez, Pablo Milanés, and Noel Nicola began performing together; an early landmark was the 1968 protest song festival at Casa de las Américas. In 1972 the performers were formally organized as the Movimiento de la Nueva Trova. Working with the film institute ICAIC's Grupo de Experimentación Sonora, the musicians blended trova's guitar-based lyricism with Beatles-influenced harmonies and modern arrangements.

Artists and style

Silvio Rodríguez ("Ojalá", "Unicornio") and Pablo Milanés ("Yolanda") became internationally celebrated, touring widely and influencing singer-songwriters throughout Latin America and Spain. A later generation, sometimes called novísima trova, included Carlos Varela and Frank Delgado, whose songs more openly addressed everyday frustrations of life in Cuba during the Special Period of the 1990s.

Legacy

Nueva trova is regarded as one of the most influential Latin American song movements, noted for elevating the singer-songwriter in Spanish-language music and for its literary lyricism.
