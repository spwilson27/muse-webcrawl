Tung Chee-hwa

Tung Chee-hwa (born 7 July 1937) is a Hong Kong businessman and politician who served as the first Chief Executive of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region from 1997 to 2005, following the transfer of sovereignty from the United Kingdom to China.

Early life and business career

Tung was born in Shanghai into the family of shipping magnate Tung Chao-yung, founder of the Orient Overseas Container Line (OOCL). He studied in the United Kingdom and the United States, graduating from the University of Liverpool. In the 1980s he led OOCL through a near-bankruptcy restructuring. He later served on Hong Kong's Executive Council under the last British governor, Chris Patten, and on preparatory bodies for the handover.

Chief Executive

Tung was selected by a Beijing-backed election committee in 1996 and took office on 1 July 1997. His administration faced the Asian financial crisis, a property downturn, the SARS epidemic of 2003, and mass protests in 2003 against proposed national-security legislation under Article 23 of the Basic Law, which drew some 500,000 demonstrators. He resigned in March 2005, officially citing health reasons, and was succeeded by Donald Tsang.

Later career

After leaving office, Tung became a vice-chairman of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference and founded the China–United States Exchange Foundation, which promotes dialogue between China and the United States.
