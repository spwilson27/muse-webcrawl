Imperial Japan

The Empire of Japan at its territorial peak during World War II

Imperial Japan refers to Japan during the period of the Empire of Japan, the historical nation-state that existed from the Meiji Restoration in 1868 until the enactment of the post-World War II constitution of Japan in 1947. It underwent an immense shift in a relatively short period, transforming from an isolated feudal shogunate into an industrialised world power.

Meiji era

Following the Boshin War and the restoration of imperial rule under Emperor Meiji, Japan rapidly modernised: feudal domains were abolished, a conscript army and navy were created, and the 1889 Meiji Constitution established a constitutional monarchy with an elected Diet, though sovereignty remained with the emperor. Victory in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95) and the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05) established Japan as a great power and brought Taiwan, Korea (annexed 1910), and southern Sakhalin under its rule.

Expansion and militarism

During the early Shōwa period, the military gained increasing influence over government. Japan seized Manchuria in 1931, establishing the puppet state of Manchukuo, and launched a full-scale war against China in 1937. In December 1941 Japan attacked Pearl Harbor, opening the Pacific War against the United States and its allies. Japanese forces rapidly overran Southeast Asia and the Pacific, but after the turning points of Midway and Guadalcanal the empire was progressively driven back.

Defeat and end

In August 1945, following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet declaration of war, Japan accepted the Potsdam Declaration and surrendered. The Allied occupation that followed rewrote Japan's constitution, demilitarised the country, and formally ended the Empire of Japan in 1947, transforming it into the modern State of Japan.
