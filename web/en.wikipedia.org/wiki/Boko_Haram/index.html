Boko Haram

Boko Haram, officially known as Jamā'at Ahl as-Sunnah lid-Da'wah wa'l-Jihād (Arabic: جماعة أهل السنة للدعوة والجهاد, "Group of the People of Sunnah for Dawah and Jihad"), is a militant Islamist organisation based in northeastern Nigeria. Founded in 2002 by Mohammed Yusuf in Maiduguri, the group launched a violent insurgency in 2009 seeking to establish an Islamic state and denouncing Western-style education — the popular name Boko Haram is usually rendered "Western education is forbidden". The insurgency has killed tens of thousands and displaced more than two million people across the Lake Chad Basin.

History

After a 2009 uprising in Bauchi State was crushed and Yusuf died in police custody, his deputy Abubakar Shekau rebuilt the group as a guerrilla force from 2010. Attacks escalated to include the 2011 bombing of the UN building in Abuja and, in April 2014, the abduction of 276 schoolgirls from Chibok, which drew global attention through the #BringBackOurGirls campaign. In 2014–15 the group seized territory the size of a small state and declared a caliphate; a regional offensive by Nigeria, Chad, Niger and Cameroon then recaptured most of it. In 2015 Shekau pledged allegiance to the Islamic State, but a leadership dispute produced the rival Islamic State West Africa Province (ISWAP) in 2016. Shekau was reported killed during fighting with ISWAP in May 2021. Both factions continued attacks on military and civilian targets in subsequent years.

Impact

The conflict has devastated northeastern Nigeria's Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states, with mass displacement, destruction of schools and health facilities, and a prolonged humanitarian emergency. Tactics have included suicide bombings, raids, mass abductions of schoolchildren and forced recruitment.
