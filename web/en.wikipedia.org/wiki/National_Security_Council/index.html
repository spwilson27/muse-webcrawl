National Security Council

The NSC meeting in the Situation Room

The term National Security Council (NSC) refers to governmental bodies, found in many countries, charged with coordinating policy on national security issues and advising the executive on matters of defence and foreign affairs. The best known is the United States National Security Council.

United States

The United States National Security Council was established by the National Security Act of 1947 under President Harry S. Truman. It is chaired by the President and includes the Vice President, the Secretaries of State, Defense, the Treasury and Energy, and is advised by the National Security Advisor, who directs the NSC staff within the Executive Office of the President. It serves as the President's principal forum for considering national security and foreign policy matters with senior advisors and cabinet officials.

Other countries

Similar bodies exist in many states. The United Kingdom's National Security Council was created in 2010 as a committee of the Cabinet. India established a National Security Council in 1998 headed by a National Security Advisor. Japan created its National Security Council in 2013, replacing the earlier Security Council. Israel, Turkey, Pakistan, the Philippines, South Korea and numerous other countries maintain councils of the same name with varying statutory powers and compositions.
