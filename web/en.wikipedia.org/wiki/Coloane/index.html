Coloane

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Coloane Village, at the southern end of Macau.

Coloane (Chinese: 路環; Cantonese: Louhwàahn), officially the Freguesia de São Francisco Xavier, is an island in Macau that forms the southernmost portion of the territory. Once a separate island, it is now connected to Taipa by the reclaimed land strip of Cotai, site of many of Macau's casino resorts, although the island itself retains a quieter, greener character.

Geography and history

Coloane has an area of about 8 square kilometres and is the least populated part of Macau. Historically it was a base for pirates in the Pearl River estuary until Portuguese forces cleared them in 1910. The island is hilly, rising to Alto de Coloane (172.4 m), the highest point in Macau, and preserves much of the territory's remaining forest and walking trails.

Sights

Coloane Village, on the southwest coast, has narrow lanes, pastel Portuguese-era buildings, and the Chapel of St. Francis Xavier (1928), which once held relics of St. Francis Xavier. Other sights include the Tam Kung Temple, Hac Sa Beach — Macau's largest natural beach, with black sand — Cheoc Van Beach, the Macau Giant Panda Pavilion, and the original shop of Lord Stow's Bakery, famous for its Portuguese egg tarts. The island also hosts the Coloane Karting Track and a large statue of the goddess A-Ma on the Alto de Coloane.
