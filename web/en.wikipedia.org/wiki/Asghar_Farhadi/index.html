Born: 7 May 1972, Homayoun Shahr (Khomeynishahr), Iran
Occupation: Film director, screenwriter

Asghar Farhadi (born 1972)

Asghar Farhadi (Persian: اصغر فرهادی; born 7 May 1972) is an Iranian film director and screenwriter, widely regarded as one of the most influential filmmakers of contemporary Iranian cinema. He is one of few directors to have won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film twice.

Career

Farhadi studied theatre at the University of Tehran and Tarbiat Modares University and made short films and television series before his feature debut, Dancing in the Dust (2003). About Elly (2009) won the Silver Bear for Best Director at Berlin. A Separation (2011), a drama about a disintegrating marriage and a disputed caretaking accident, won the Golden Bear at Berlin and the Academy Award — the first Iranian film to do so. He repeated the Oscar win with The Salesman (2016); Farhadi declined to attend the 2017 ceremony in protest at the United States travel ban on Iranian citizens, and the award was accepted on his behalf by Anousheh Ansari.

His other films include The Past (2013), made in France, the Spanish-language Everybody Knows (2018), and A Hero (2021), which won the Grand Prix at Cannes. His work is known for naturalistic acting, morally ambiguous characters and plots that pivot on small deceptions and conflicting testimony.
