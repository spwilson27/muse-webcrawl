Gudbrandsdal (Norwegian pronunciation: [ˈɡʉ̀ːbrɑnsˌdɑːɫ], English: Gudbrand Valley) is a valley and traditional district in the Norwegian county of Innlandet. The valley is oriented in a north-westerly direction from Lillehammer and the lake Mjøsa, extending 230 kilometres (143 mi) toward Romsdalen. The river Gudbrandsdalslågen flows through the valley, starting from the lake Lesjaskogsvatnet and ending at Mjøsa.

The valley is divided into northern, central, and southern parts (Norddalen, Midtdalen, and Sørdalen). Main settlements include Lillehammer, Ringebu, Vinstra, Otta, and Dombås.

Name and history

The name derives from Gudbrand, a powerful chieftain (hersir) who lived at Hundorp in the 10th century. According to the sagas, Gudbrand was confronted by Saint Olaf during the Christianization of Norway. The valley's stave churches, including those at Fåvang and Ringebu, date to the medieval period.

In August 1940, the valley saw fighting during the German invasion of Norway, and it is also remembered for the Scottish mercenary force defeated at the Battle of Kringen in 1612. The valley gave its name to Gudbrandsdalsost, the famous Norwegian brown cheese.

Culture and transport

Gudbrandsdal is the setting of Henrik Ibsen's play Peer Gynt, and the figure of Peer is associated with the valley's farms. The Peer Gynt Festival is held annually at Vinstra. The open-air museum Maihaugen is located in Lillehammer. The E6 highway and the Dovre railway line (Dovrebanen) run the length of the valley, connecting Oslo with Trondheim.
