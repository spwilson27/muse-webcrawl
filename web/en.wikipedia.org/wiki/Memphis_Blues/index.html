Memphis Blues

Sheet music for "The Memphis Blues" (1912)

"The Memphis Blues" is a song described by its composer, W. C. Handy, as a "Southern rag". It was published by Handy in September 1912 and has been recorded by many artists. It is regarded as one of the first blues songs ever published, and its success helped popularize the twelve-bar blues form with a national audience.

History

Handy, an African American bandleader from Alabama, wrote the piece in 1909 as a campaign song for Edward Crump, a candidate for mayor of Memphis, Tennessee; it was originally titled "Mr. Crump". Handy later rewrote it with new lyrics by George A. Norton and published it as "The Memphis Blues." According to Handy, he was cheated out of the copyright and sold the rights for a small sum, a loss he later recouped in part when he co-founded his own publishing company.

Recordings and influence

The Victor Military Band made an early recording in 1914, and the song was widely recorded in the following decades by artists including Prince's Orchestra, Bessie Smith (in a version associated with her early career), and many jazz and blues performers. "The Memphis Blues" was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame. Handy's later compositions, including "St. Louis Blues" (1914) and "Beale Street Blues" (1916), cemented his reputation as the "Father of the Blues".
