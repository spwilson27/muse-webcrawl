E-ticket

An electronic ticket, commonly abbreviated as e-ticket, is the digital record of a ticket sold or issued by a company, replacing the traditional paper ticket. The term is most associated with airline travel, where the passenger's reservation is stored in the airline's computer system and an itinerary receipt is provided as proof of purchase.

Airline e-tickets

Electronic tickets were introduced in the airline industry in the 1990s; United Airlines issued the first in 1994. In June 2008, the International Air Transport Association (IATA) mandated 100% e-ticketing for tickets issued through its billing and settlement plans, ending the use of paper tickets by member airlines. Passengers check in using identification and a booking reference or record locator, receiving a boarding pass in paper or mobile form.

Other uses

E-tickets are now standard for railways, cinemas, concerts, sporting events, and attractions, typically delivered by email or app as a barcode or QR code scanned at entry. Advantages include lower distribution costs, easy replacement of lost tickets, and richer data for operators; concerns include dependence on devices and transferability restrictions.
