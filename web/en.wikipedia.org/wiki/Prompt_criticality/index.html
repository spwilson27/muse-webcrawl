Prompt criticality

In nuclear engineering, prompt criticality is a state in which a nuclear chain reaction is self-sustaining based on prompt neutrons alone, without needing the contribution of delayed neutrons. A system is prompt critical when the effective neutron multiplication factor attributable to prompt neutrons reaches one.

Explanation

Most neutrons from fission are emitted essentially instantaneously (prompt neutrons), but a small fraction—about 0.65% in uranium-235—are emitted seconds to minutes later by fission fragments (delayed neutrons). In a normally critical reactor, the chain reaction depends on those delayed neutrons, which makes the power level controllable on human timescales. Above prompt critical, the reaction rate grows on the timescale of prompt neutron generations—microseconds or less—making control extremely difficult.

Significance

Nuclear weapons are designed to achieve prompt supercriticality for rapid energy release. In reactors, prompt criticality is associated with severe accidents: the Chernobyl disaster and the SL-1 accident involved prompt-critical excursions.
