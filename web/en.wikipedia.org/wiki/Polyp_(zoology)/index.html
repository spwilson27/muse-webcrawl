Polyp (zoology)

Polyp
PhylumCnidaria
Alternate stageMedusa

In zoology, a polyp is one of the two body forms found in the phylum Cnidaria — the other being the medusa (jellyfish form). A polyp is typically sessile and cylindrical, attached to a surface at its base (aboral end), with a mouth surrounded by tentacles at the free (oral) end, opening into a single gastrovascular cavity.

Sea anemones are large solitary polyps.

Occurrence

Polyps are the only body form in the class Anthozoa — sea anemones and corals — which lack a medusa stage. In Hydrozoa and Scyphozoa, the polyp is one phase of a life cycle that alternates with the medusa: hydrozoan polyps often form branching colonies (as in Hydra's relatives and Obelia) and bud off free-swimming medusae; the tiny scyphozoan polyp (scyphistoma) produces juvenile jellyfish by strobilation.

Colonial polyps

Many polyps live in colonies in which individuals (zooids) are connected and may specialise: feeding polyps (gastrozooids), defensive polyps (dactylozooids), and reproductive polyps (gonozooids). Reef-building corals are colonies of polyps sharing a common calcium carbonate skeleton secreted beneath them.

Reproduction

Polyps commonly reproduce asexually by budding or fission, and many can also reproduce sexually, releasing gametes into the water to produce a swimming planula larva that settles and grows into a new polyp.
