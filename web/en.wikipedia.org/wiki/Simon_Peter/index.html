Saint Peter (born Shimon Bar Yonah, or Simon son of Jonah), also known as Simon Peter and Cephas, was one of the Twelve Apostles of Jesus Christ and one of the first leaders of the early Christian Church. According to Christian tradition, Peter was crucified in Rome under Emperor Nero. He is traditionally counted as the first bishop of Rome — and thus the first pope — and is venerated as a saint in all major Christian denominations that honour saints.

Saint Peter, depicted with the keys given to him by Christ in Matthew 16.

Life in the Gospels

The New Testament presents Peter as a fisherman from Bethsaida on the Sea of Galilee, called by Jesus together with his brother Andrew. Jesus renamed him Cephas (Aramaic for "rock"; Greek Petros), declaring in Matthew 16:18–19, "on this rock I will build my church," and entrusting him with the "keys of the kingdom of heaven." Peter is portrayed as the most prominent of the apostles: he walks on water, confesses Jesus as the Messiah, witnesses the Transfiguration, and — after denying Jesus three times during the Passion, as foretold — is restored after the Resurrection and charged to "feed my sheep." In the Acts of the Apostles he leads the Jerusalem church, preaches at Pentecost, and opens the mission to Gentiles through the conversion of Cornelius.

Later ministry and death

Tradition associates Peter with the churches of Antioch and Rome. Two epistles in the New Testament bear his name (1 Peter and 2 Peter), though the authorship of the second is widely disputed among scholars. Early church writers held that Peter was martyred in Rome during Nero's persecution, crucified upside down at his own request because he felt unworthy to die as Jesus had. St Peter's Basilica in Vatican City is built over the site traditionally identified as his tomb, beneath its high altar.

Veneration and legacy

Peter's role is interpreted differently across traditions: Roman Catholicism grounds the papal office in his primacy and succession in Rome; Eastern Orthodoxy honours him as first among the apostles while understanding his primacy differently; Protestant traditions variously interpret the "rock" of Matthew 16 as Peter's confession of faith. He remains one of the most depicted figures in Christian art, recognizable by his keys, an inverted cross, or a rooster recalling his denial.
