The Lesser Caucasus is the second of the two main mountain ranges of the Caucasus Mountains, being about 600 km (370 mi) long. It runs parallel to the Greater Caucasus, roughly 100 km to the south, across the territories of Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, and into northeastern Turkey and northwestern Iran. Unlike the Greater Caucasus, which forms a single continuous watershed, the Lesser Caucasus consists of a complex of parallel and intersecting ranges and volcanic highlands.

Geography

The range extends from the Black Sea coast near Batumi southeastward to the Araz (Aras) River. Its western part is formed by the Meskheti and Trialeti ranges of Georgia; the Armenian highlands dominate its center, where its highest summit, Mount Aragats (4,090 m), an extinct volcano northwest of Yerevan, rises; further east, the Karabakh and Zangezur ranges extend into Azerbaijan, where Mount Kaputjugh reaches 3,904 m. The Lesser Caucasus is connected to the Greater Caucasus by the Likhi (Surami) Range in Georgia, which divides the country into eastern and western climatic zones.

Geology and climate

The range is largely of volcanic origin, formed during the Alpine orogeny by the collision of the Arabian Plate with Eurasia; extensive lava plateaus and extinct volcanic cones are characteristic. Climates range from humid subtropical on the western slopes facing the Black Sea to dry continental in the Armenian highlands. The Lesser Caucasus forms part of the Caucasus ecoregion, a global biodiversity hotspot, and encloses important watersheds feeding the Kura and Aras rivers.
