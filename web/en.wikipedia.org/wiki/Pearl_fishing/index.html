Pearl fishing

Pearl fishing, or pearl diving, is the traditional activity of harvesting pearls from wild oysters and mussels gathered from the seabed or riverbed by free divers. For thousands of years it was the only means of obtaining pearls, and it supported major trading economies around the Persian Gulf, the Red Sea, Sri Lanka, Japan and the Americas before the industry collapsed in the 20th century with the advent of cultured pearl farming.

Traditional pearl divers working from a dhow in the Persian Gulf.

History

Pearl fisheries are among the oldest in the world. The Gulf fishery centred on Bahrain and the coast of Qatar and the Trucial States was described by classical authors and sustained entire coastal communities; at its height tens of thousands of men spent the summer season at sea. Divers wore a leather nose clip, finger protectors and a weighted stone to descend, filling a basket with oysters during dives that could last over a minute, and were hauled up by rope. The Gulf of Mannar between India and Sri Lanka, the Sulu Archipelago, Aboriginal fisheries in Australia, and freshwater mussel fisheries in Scotland and North America also supported significant trades.

Decline

The industry was devastated in the 1930s–40s by the Great Depression, the development of cultured pearls by Kokichi Mikimoto and others in Japan, and, in the Gulf, the discovery of oil, which offered steadier incomes. Overfishing and pollution depleted many natural beds. Today wild pearl fishing survives only at a small scale, although natural pearls from old fisheries remain exceptionally valuable, and traditional Gulf pearling culture has been recognised by UNESCO — notably the Bahraini inscription "Pearling, testimony of an island economy".

Diving techniques

Traditional divers worked without breathing apparatus, relying on breath-hold dives of up to two minutes in the best practitioners, such as Japan's ama divers and Korea's haenyeo. The work was extremely dangerous, with risks from sharks, jellyfish, drowning and decompression illness.
