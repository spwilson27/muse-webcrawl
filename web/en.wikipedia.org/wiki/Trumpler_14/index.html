Trumpler 14 (Tr 14) is a young, massive open star cluster located in the Carina Nebula, about 8,000 light-years from Earth in the constellation Carina. With a diameter of roughly six light-years and an age of only a few hundred thousand years, it is one of the youngest and richest star clusters known in the Milky Way, containing several thousand stars including some of the most massive and luminous stars in the galaxy.

Properties

The cluster was catalogued by Robert Julius Trumpler in 1930. Together with the neighbouring cluster Trumpler 16, Tr 14 forms the heart of the Carina OB1 association, the largest star-forming complex in the Carina Nebula. Estimates of its total mass are around 4,300 solar masses, and its stellar population includes dozens of early O-type stars.

Trumpler 14's most famous member is HD 93129A, a binary system of two O-type supergiants that is among the most luminous star systems in the Milky Way, with a combined luminosity of several million times that of the Sun. Another member, HD 93128, is one of the youngest known O-type stars still on the zero-age main sequence.

Observations

In 2016 the Hubble Space Telescope released a detailed image of Trumpler 14, revealing the cluster's brilliant blue-white stars and the dark dust pillars sculpted by their intense radiation and stellar winds. The cluster's powerful outflows are progressively eroding the surrounding molecular cloud, and astronomers use Tr 14 as a laboratory for studying how massive stars form and how they affect their environment in the short time before the most massive members explode as supernovae.
