Instrument of Accession

The Instrument of Accession was a legal document first introduced by the Government of India Act 1935 and used in 1947 to enable each of the rulers of the princely states under British paramountcy to join one of the new dominions of India or Pakistan created by the Indian Independence Act 1947. By signing the instrument, a princely state agreed to accede to the Dominion of India or the Dominion of Pakistan, surrendering control over defence, external affairs and communications to the dominion government, while in most cases retaining internal autonomy.

Background

At the time of British withdrawal from India in 1947, there were more than 560 princely states covering roughly two-fifths of the subcontinent's territory. These states were not directly governed by the British Crown but were bound to it by treaties and the doctrine of paramountcy. The Indian Independence Act 1947 provided that paramountcy would lapse, technically leaving the states independent, but the British government made clear it would not grant dominion status to any of them and discouraged independence in practice.

The States Department, headed by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel with V. P. Menon as its secretary, drafted a standard Instrument of Accession, modelled on the form used under the Government of India Act 1935. Lord Mountbatten, the last Viceroy, urged rulers to accede to one dominion or the other, taking geographical contiguity and the wishes of their subjects into account.

Terms

The standard instrument provided that the ruler acceded on three subjects only: defence, external affairs and communications. The dominion legislature could legislate on those subjects for the state, but the instrument generally promised that accession would not commit the state to accepting any future constitution of India, nor fetter the ruler's discretion to enter into arrangements with the government of India. Rulers who signed before 15 August 1947 were also offered representation in the Constituent Assembly and various privy purse arrangements.

Accession and integration

By 15 August 1947 nearly all states geographically within India had acceded, with notable exceptions including Hyderabad, Junagadh and Jammu and Kashmir, each of which was resolved later: Junagadh acceded to Pakistan but was taken over by India and its accession reversed by plebiscite; Hyderabad was integrated after a police action in September 1948; and Jammu and Kashmir acceded to India in October 1947 under contested circumstances. States that acceded were subsequently merged into provinces or grouped into unions of states such as Rajasthan, Madhya Bharat and PEPSU, a process completed in large part by 1949–1950 and consolidated by the States Reorganisation Act 1956.

Legal status

The Instrument of Accession remains significant in constitutional and international law discussions, particularly in relation to the Kashmir dispute, where the wording of the instrument and the conditions attached to it by the Governor-General's acceptance continue to be debated.
