Grand Prix motorcycle racing is the highest level of motorcycle road racing, held on purpose-built circuits around the world. Its premier class is MotoGP, the oldest established motorsport world championship, first run in 1949 under the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM). Riders compete on purpose-built prototype racing motorcycles that are not available for road use.

Classes and format

A Grand Prix weekend features three world championship classes. MotoGP, the premier class, uses 1000 cc four-stroke prototypes producing well over 250 horsepower and capable of speeds above 350 km/h. Moto2 is a spec-engine intermediate class using 765 cc Triumph three-cylinder engines, and Moto3 uses 250 cc single-cylinder machines and typically produces exceptionally close racing among young riders. The all-electric MotoE series has supported the calendar as a cup class. Since 2023, MotoGP weekends have included a half-distance sprint race on Saturday in addition to the main Grand Prix on Sunday.

History

The championship began in 1949 with classes for 125, 250, 350 and 500 cc machines. The 500 cc class — won by legends such as Giacomo Agostini, Mike Hailwood and Mick Doohan — was the premier category until 2002, when four-stroke 990 cc machines replaced the two-strokes and the class was renamed MotoGP. Engine capacity was reduced to 800 cc in 2007 and raised to 1000 cc in 2012. The championship's modern era has been defined by riders including Valentino Rossi, whose nine world titles and showmanship made him the sport's global superstar, Marc Márquez, Jorge Lorenzo, Casey Stoner and Francesco Bagnaia.

Machines and technology

MotoGP motorcycles are pure prototypes built by manufacturers including Ducati, Honda, Yamaha, Aprilia and KTM. They employ carbon brakes, seamless-shift gearboxes, sophisticated electronics including traction control and anti-wheelie systems, and — increasingly prominent in the 2020s — aerodynamic winglets and ride-height devices, some of which have since been restricted by regulation. A major technical overhaul is scheduled for 2027, reducing engine capacity to 850 cc.
