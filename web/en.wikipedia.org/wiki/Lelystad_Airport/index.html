Lelystad Airport (IATA: LEY, ICAO: EHLE) is an airport near Lelystad in the Dutch province of Flevoland. It is the largest general aviation airport in the Netherlands and was expanded to serve as a relief airport for Amsterdam Airport Schiphol's leisure traffic, although its opening to commercial holiday flights was repeatedly postponed amid political and environmental debate.

History

The airfield opened in 1973 on land reclaimed from the IJsselmeer, serving flight schools, business aviation and recreational flying. The adjacent Aviodrome aviation museum, relocated from Schiphol in 2003, is a major attraction. In the 2010s the runway was lengthened to 2,700 metres and a new terminal built so the airport could handle up to 45,000 commercial movements a year, but successive governments deferred the opening date, and the airport long remained limited to general aviation.

Facilities

The single asphalt runway (05/23) lies about four metres below sea level, typical of the Flevoland polder landscape. Flight training organisations, including those training airline pilots, are based at the field.
