Maximum a posteriori estimation

In Bayesian statistics, a maximum a posteriori probability (MAP) estimate is an estimate of an unobserved quantity that equals the mode of the posterior distribution. Whereas the maximum likelihood estimate (MLE) chooses the parameter value that maximises the likelihood of the observed data, the MAP estimate maximises the product of the likelihood and the prior distribution:

θ̂MAP = argmaxθ f(x | θ) · g(θ)

where g is the prior density of the parameter θ and f(x | θ) the likelihood of the data x.

Properties

The MAP estimate is the Bayes estimator under a 0–1 loss approximating the entire posterior by a point mass. It can be computed by the same optimisation methods as maximum likelihood, since the posterior is proportional to likelihood times prior. Unlike the posterior mean or median, the MAP estimate is not invariant under reparameterisation of θ, because a change of variables introduces a Jacobian factor into the density whose mode it shifts.

Relation to regularisation

MAP estimation gives a Bayesian interpretation of regularisation in machine learning. A Gaussian prior on regression coefficients yields ridge regression (L2 penalty), while a Laplace (double-exponential) prior yields the lasso (L1 penalty). When the prior is uniform, the MAP estimate coincides with the MLE. As the amount of data grows, the likelihood dominates and MAP and MLE typically converge.

Example

For estimating the bias of a coin from observed tosses with a Beta prior, the MAP estimate is the mode of the Beta posterior — a weighted compromise between the prior mode and the observed frequency, with the prior acting like a number of "pseudo-observations".
