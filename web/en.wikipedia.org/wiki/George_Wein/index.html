George Wein

George Theodore Wein

Born: 3 October 1925, Boston, Massachusetts, U.S.

Died: 13 September 2021, New York City, U.S.

Occupation: Jazz promoter, pianist

Known for: Newport Jazz Festival, Newport Folk Festival, New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival

George Theodore Wein (3 October 1925 – 13 September 2021) was an American jazz promoter, pianist and producer who founded the Newport Jazz Festival in 1954, regarded as the first modern outdoor jazz festival, and went on to found or co-found many other major music festivals.

Career

A trained pianist who led his own band and ran the Storyville jazz club and record label in Boston, Wein was hired by Newport socialites Louis and Elaine Lorillard to organize a jazz festival at Newport, Rhode Island. Its success launched his career as an impresario. He founded the Newport Folk Festival in 1959 (with Pete Seeger, Theodore Bikel, Oscar Brand and Albert Grossman) and helped create the New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival in 1970.

Legacy

Through his company Festival Productions, Wein produced festivals and concerts around the world for more than half a century, presenting artists from Louis Armstrong and Duke Ellington to Miles Davis and Bob Dylan — including Dylan's controversial electric set at Newport in 1965. He received numerous honours, including a Grammy Trustees Award, and published his autobiography, Myself Among Others, in 2003.
