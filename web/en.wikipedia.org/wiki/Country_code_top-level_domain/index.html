Country code top-level domain

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A country code top-level domain (ccTLD) is an Internet top-level domain generally used or reserved for a country, sovereign state, or dependent territory identified by its ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code. Examples include .uk for the United Kingdom, .de for Germany (the most registered ccTLD), .jp for Japan, and .us for the United States.

Administration and policy

Each ccTLD is delegated by IANA to a national registry, which sets its own registration policies: some require local presence, while others market their domains internationally. Several ccTLDs have become popular for purposes unrelated to geography — .tv (Tuvalu) for video sites, .me (Montenegro) for personal sites, .co (Colombia) as an alternative to .com, and .io (British Indian Ocean Territory) among technology companies — generating licence revenue for the territories concerned. Internationalised ccTLDs in non-Latin scripts (such as .рф for Russia) were introduced from 2010.
