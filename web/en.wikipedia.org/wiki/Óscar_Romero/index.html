Óscar Romero

Óscar Arnulfo Romero y Galdámez (15 August 1917 – 24 March 1980) was a prelate of the Catholic Church in El Salvador who served as the fourth Archbishop of San Salvador. He spoke out against poverty, social injustice, assassinations, and torture, and was assassinated while celebrating Mass in 1980.


Born: 15 August 1917, Ciudad Barrios, El Salvador
Died: 24 March 1980 (aged 62), San Salvador
Archbishop of: San Salvador (1977–1980)
Canonized: 14 October 2018 by Pope Francis

Early life

Romero was born in Ciudad Barrios in the department of San Miguel. He entered the seminary at age twelve, studied in Rome, and was ordained in 1942. He served as a parish priest and as rector, and became known for conservative theology and careful administration.

Archbishop

Appointed Archbishop of San Salvador in 1977, Romero was expected to be a safe, traditional figure. The murder of his friend, Jesuit priest Rutilio Grande, weeks after his installation transformed his ministry. He denounced repression by the military government, defended the poor, and broadcast weekly sermons documenting abuses.

Assassination

On 23 March 1980 Romero called on soldiers to disobey orders to kill civilians. The next day, while celebrating Mass in the chapel of the Hospital de la Divina Providencia, he was shot by an assassin. His funeral was attacked by security forces, killing dozens.

Legacy and canonization

Romero is a national hero in El Salvador and a symbol of liberation theology and human rights. Beatified in 2015, he was canonized by Pope Francis on 14 October 2018. His feast day is 24 March.
