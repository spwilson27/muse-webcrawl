Timothy McVeigh

Timothy James McVeigh (April 23, 1968 – June 11, 2001) was an American domestic terrorist who perpetrated the Oklahoma City bombing on April 19, 1995. The truck bomb he detonated outside the Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building killed 168 people, including 19 children, and injured hundreds more. It remained the deadliest act of terrorism on American soil until the September 11 attacks of 2001, and is still the deadliest incident of domestic terrorism in United States history.

Early life and military service

McVeigh was born in Lockport, New York, and raised in the nearby town of Pendleton. Fascinated by guns from childhood, he enlisted in the US Army in 1988 and served as a gunner on a Bradley Fighting Vehicle during the Gulf War, earning a Bronze Star. He failed to complete the selection course for the Army Special Forces and left the service in 1991. Drifting through gun shows in the following years, he became steeped in far-right, anti-government literature — notably the white-supremacist novel The Turner Diaries — and developed a conviction that the federal government was a tyrannical enemy. The FBI sieges at Ruby Ridge (1992) and Waco (1993) radicalised him further.

The bombing

With his former army roommate Terry Nichols, and assistance from Michael Fortier, McVeigh built a bomb of roughly 7,000 pounds of ammonium nitrate fertiliser and fuel oil in a rented Ryder truck. He chose the Murrah Building because it housed the federal agencies he blamed for Waco, and chose April 19 — the second anniversary of the Waco fire and the date of the Battles of Lexington and Concord. The blast at 9:02 a.m. destroyed the building's north face. About ninety minutes later, McVeigh was arrested by an Oklahoma state trooper for driving without a licence plate and carrying a concealed weapon — a routine stop that led to his identification before he could be released.

Trial and execution

Tried in federal court in Denver, McVeigh was convicted on all eleven counts in June 1997 and sentenced to death. Nichols received life imprisonment; Fortier testified for the prosecution and received twelve years. McVeigh dropped his appeals and was executed by lethal injection at the federal penitentiary in Terre Haute, Indiana, on June 11, 2001 — the first federal execution in 38 years. He showed no remorse, describing the children killed as "collateral damage".
