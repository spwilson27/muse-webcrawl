Sedimentology

Placeholder image: cross-bedding in sandstone, recording ancient current flow.

Sedimentology is the branch of geology concerned with sediments and sedimentary rocks: their description, classification, origin, transport, deposition, and post-depositional alteration (diagenesis). Its central principle is uniformitarianism — "the present is the key to the past" — by which modern depositional environments such as rivers, deltas, beaches, and deep-sea fans are used to interpret the environments recorded in ancient strata.

Subject matter

Sedimentologists study grain size, composition, and sedimentary structures such as cross-bedding, ripple marks, graded bedding, and mud cracks, which reveal the energy and direction of ancient currents. Groupings of strata deposited in a particular environment are described as sedimentary facies, and their stacking patterns form the basis of sequence stratigraphy, a framework used to correlate rocks and predict the distribution of reservoir, seal, and source rocks.

Applications

Sedimentology is applied in petroleum and groundwater exploration, in reconstructing past climates and landscapes, and in assessing hazards such as coastal erosion and river flooding. Because sedimentary rocks preserve most of the fossil record, the field is closely allied with palaeontology and stratigraphy.
