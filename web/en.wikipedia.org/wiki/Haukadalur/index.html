Haukadalur

LocationSouthwestern Iceland
Known forGeysers Strokkur and Great Geysir
RoutePart of the Golden Circle

Haukadalur (Icelandic: [ˈhœyːkaˌtaːlʏr̥], from Old Norse: Haukadalr [ˈhɔukɑˌdɑlz̠], "hawk dale" or "valley of hawks") is a geothermal valley in Iceland. It lies in the south of Iceland in Laugardalur valley, north of Lake Laugarvatn. It contains geysers, most notably Strokkur and Great Geysir, from which the English word "geyser" is derived.

The valley is one of the stops on the Golden Circle, Iceland's most popular tourist route, together with Þingvellir National Park and the Gullfoss waterfall.

Geysers

The geysers of Haukadalur have been mentioned in written sources since 1294, when earthquakes caused them to activate. The largest geysers are Strokkur and Great Geysir. Strokkur erupts every 5 to 10 minutes, throwing water 20 metres (66 ft) or more into the air. Geysir itself, which gave the English word "geyser" its origin, now erupts only rarely; it was first mentioned in print in the 18th century.

There are also many smaller hot springs and geysers in the area, including the smaller geysers Litli Geysir and Strokkur's neighbours, as well as mud pots and fumaroles. The geothermal field at Haukadalur sits on the eastern edge of the western volcanic zone in Iceland and is associated with the Langjökull geothermal system.

History

The valley takes its name from Haukur Erlendsson (died 1334), a lawspeaker and author of Hauksbók, who is said to have owned the farm in the valley. The church at Haukadalur, Haukadalskirkja, dates from the 19th century, and the site has been a place of education: a school was operated in the valley for many centuries, teaching figures such as the geologist and poet Jónas Hallgrímsson.

In the 20th century, efforts were made to keep Geysir active, including cutting a channel through the silica rim of its basin in 1935, which temporarily lowered the water level and triggered eruptions. During the South Iceland earthquakes in 2000, Geysir was briefly reactivated.

Tourism

Haukadalur is one of Iceland's most visited natural attractions. Visitors can walk among the hot springs, watch Strokkur erupt at short intervals, and view the silica terraces and vividly coloured mineral deposits characteristic of high-temperature geothermal areas. A hotel, restaurant, and visitor facilities are located at the site.
