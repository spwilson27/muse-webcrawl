Pembroke College, a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England, is located in Pembroke Square on the site of the medieval Broadgates Hall. The college was founded in 1624 via a bequest of the Abingdon merchant Thomas Tesdale and the benefaction of Richard Wightwick, and was named for William Herbert, 3rd Earl of Pembroke, then Chancellor of the University.

History

Broadgates Hall had been a hall for law students since the fifteenth century. The 1624 foundation converted it into a college, whose early fellows came largely from Abingdon School under the Tesdale and Wightwick endowments. The college remained small until expansion in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, including the acquisition of buildings on the former site of St Aldate's and the construction of the Rokos Quad, opened in 2013, across the old city wall.

Notable members

Pembroke's best-known alumnus is Samuel Johnson, who entered in 1728 and left without a degree for lack of money; his rooms and desk remain college landmarks. Other alumni and fellows include the writer J. R. R. Tolkien, who was Rawlinson and Bosworth Professor of Anglo-Saxon and a fellow of Pembroke from 1925 to 1945 and wrote The Hobbit and much of The Lord of the Rings while there; the statesman William Blackstone was also a fellow, and the college educated the first Prime Minister of Canada, and politicians and scholars from many countries.
