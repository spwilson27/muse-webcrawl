Overview

Ursa Minor, also known as the Little Bear, is a constellation in the far northern sky. Like the Great Bear (Ursa Major), its name is from Latin and its tail may be likened to the handle of a ladle, giving the asterism the Little Dipper. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations.

Significance

Ursa Minor contains the north celestial pole and Polaris, the current pole star, at the end of the Little Dipper's handle; the two stars at the front of the dipper's bowl (Kochab and Pherkad) are the "Guardians of the Pole". Because it is circumpolar from most of the northern hemisphere, Ursa Minor never sets for mid-northern observers and has served navigation since the Phoenicians, who were said to steer by it where Greeks used the Great Bear.
