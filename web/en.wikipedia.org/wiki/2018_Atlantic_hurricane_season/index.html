The 2018 Atlantic hurricane season was the third in a consecutive series of above-average and damaging Atlantic hurricane seasons, running officially from 1 June to 30 November 2018. It featured Hurricanes Florence and Michael, which caused catastrophic damage in the southeastern United States.

Season summary

Fifteen named storms formed, of which eight became hurricanes and two — Florence and Michael — reached major hurricane strength. All named storms in the season caused some damage or fatalities, an unusual distinction.

Major storms

Hurricane Florence made landfall in North Carolina in September as a Category 1 but stalled and produced record rainfall and flooding, causing around $24 billion in damage. Hurricane Michael struck the Florida Panhandle near Mexico Beach in October as a Category 5 hurricane — the strongest ever to hit the Panhandle and one of the most intense U.S. landfalls on record — causing about $25 billion in damage. Hurricane Leslie also caused flooding in Portugal, Spain and France, including the deadly Aude floods.

Retirement

The names Florence and Michael were retired after the season and replaced by Francine and Milton for 2024.
