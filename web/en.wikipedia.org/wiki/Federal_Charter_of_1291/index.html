The Federal Charter of 1291 (German: Bundesbrief) is one of the founding documents of the Old Swiss Confederacy. It records an alliance concluded in early August 1291 among the valley communities of Uri, Schwyz, and Unterwalden (now split into Obwalden and Nidwalden) in central Switzerland. The three cantons promised mutual aid against all who might use force against them, agreed to accept no judge who had bought his office, and set rules for arbitrating disputes.

Content and significance

Written in Latin, the charter is largely a legal pact about security and the rule of law following the death of King Rudolf I of Habsburg. It was probably one of several similar alliances of the period, but it was elevated in the 19th and 20th centuries into the founding document of Switzerland; since 1891 the national holiday, Swiss National Day, has been celebrated on 1 August in its honour. The related legend of the Rütlischwur oath is a later tradition.

Preservation

The parchment survives and is displayed at the Museum of the Swiss Charters of Confederation in Schwyz.
