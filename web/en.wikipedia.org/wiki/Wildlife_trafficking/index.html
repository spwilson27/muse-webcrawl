Wildlife trafficking is the illegal collection, transportation, and sale of wild animals and plants and products derived from them, including ivory, rhino horn, pangolin scales, tiger bone, live exotic pets, and illegally logged timber. It is considered one of the largest forms of transnational organised crime and a major driver of species decline, threatening elephants, rhinoceroses, pangolins, tigers, and many other taxa.

International response

The trade is regulated under CITES (the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora, signed 1973), which lists species in appendices according to the degree of protection they need. Enforcement involves customs agencies, INTERPOL, the UN Office on Drugs and Crime, and NGOs such as TRAFFIC. Demand-reduction campaigns target consumer markets, particularly for ivory and rhino horn in Asia; China banned domestic ivory trade at the end of 2017. Illegal wildlife trade is also linked to zoonotic disease risk and to corruption and insecurity in source regions.
