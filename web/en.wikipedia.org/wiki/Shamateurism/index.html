Shamateurism

Shamateurism is a portmanteau of "sham" and "amateurism" describing athletes who present themselves as amateurs while effectively being paid, in violation of the amateur rules once enforced in sports such as Olympic competition, tennis and rugby.

History

Strict amateur codes in the late 19th and 20th centuries barred athletes who received money, but covert payments were widespread: under-the-table appearance fees in tennis, sinecure "jobs" for state-supported athletes in the Eastern Bloc, and inflated expenses in athletics. The contradiction between the rules and the reality led critics to coin the term "shamateur".

Decline of amateur rules

From the 1970s onward most sports abandoned strict amateurism: tennis entered the Open Era in 1968, the Olympics gradually admitted professionals, and rugby union declared itself "open" in 1995. The term is now mainly of historical interest, though debates continue over compensation for college athletes in the United States.
