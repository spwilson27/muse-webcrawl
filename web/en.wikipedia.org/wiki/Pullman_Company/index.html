Pullman Company

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A gleaming Pullman sleeping car interior with made berths and wood panelling, period photograph]
Inside a Pullman car.

The Pullman Company, founded by George Mortimer Pullman in 1867 in Chicago, built and operated luxury sleeping cars on American railroads and created the company town of Pullman, Illinois. Pullman cars — staffed for decades by Black porters whose Brotherhood of Sleeping Car Porters (1925, led by A. Philip Randolph) became a landmark of US labour and civil-rights history — set the standard for rail luxury. The company was broken up in 1947 (operations from manufacturing) after an antitrust ruling; car building ended in the early 1980s.

The Pullman system

Railroads hauled Pullman-owned cars and Pullman staff serviced them: porters, maids, chefs. The first true Pullman sleeper, Pioneer (1864), was wider than loading gauges allowed — famously accommodated when lines were rebuilt for Abraham Lincoln\'s funeral train (legend-flavoured, but the expansion was real). Standardised luxury made overnight rail travel respectable for the middle classes.

Labour history

The bitter Pullman Strike of 1894 (over wage cuts in the company town) was crushed by federal injunction and troops, costing lives and jailing Eugene V. Debs. Porters, paid poorly and dependent on tips, unionised in 1925; their 1937 contract was a major civil-rights-era precursor victory.

Decline

Antitrust (1944–47) forced the split of the operating pool (sold to railroads) from manufacturing (Pullman-Standard). Amtrak\'s creation ended most Pullman service in 1968–69; the name survives in British luxury trains (British Pullman) heritage and in \'Pullman\' as a generic for sleeper cars in Britain.
