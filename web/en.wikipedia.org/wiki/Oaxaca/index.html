Oaxaca

Oaxaca (Spanish pronunciation: [waˈxaka]), officially the Free and Sovereign State of Oaxaca, is one of the 32 states of Mexico. Located in southwestern Mexico, it borders Guerrero to the west, Puebla to the northwest, Veracruz to the north, and Chiapas to the east, with the Pacific Ocean to the south. Its capital and largest city is Oaxaca de Juárez.

Geography and people

Oaxaca is Mexico's most ethnically diverse state. Its rugged terrain of sierras and deep valleys has historically isolated communities, preserving an extraordinary variety of indigenous peoples and languages — at least 16 officially recognized groups, including the Zapotec, Mixtec, Mazatec, Mixe, and Chinantec peoples. Spanish is universal, but indigenous languages are spoken by a substantial share of the population, and Oaxaca has the highest proportion of indigenous-language speakers of any Mexican state.

Much of the state is governed at the municipal level under usos y costumbres (customary law), a system of assemblies, communal labor (tequio), and cargo offices.

History

The Valley of Oaxaca was a cradle of Mesoamerican civilization. The Zapotec city of Monte Albán, founded around 500 BCE on a flattened mountaintop above the valley, was one of the earliest cities in the Americas; the later city of Mitla is famed for its intricate stone mosaics. The region fell to the Spanish in the 1520s, and Hernán Cortés took the title Marquis of the Valley of Oaxaca.

Oaxaca produced two of Mexico's most important nineteenth-century leaders: Benito Juárez, the Zapotec lawyer who became president and led the Liberal Reform, and Porfirio Díaz, the general who dominated Mexico as president-dictator for three decades.

Culture and economy

Oaxaca is celebrated for its cuisine — chocolate, tlayudas, seven varieties of mole, chapulines (grasshoppers), and mezcal — and for crafts including black pottery, alebrijes, and handwoven textiles. The month-long Guelaguetza festival showcases the dances and dress of the state's seven traditional regions. The historic center of Oaxaca de Juárez and Monte Albán are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

In 2006 the state was convulsed by a teachers' strike that escalated into a months-long uprising led by the Popular Assembly of the Peoples of Oaxaca (APPO), drawing international attention to grievances over corruption and repression.
