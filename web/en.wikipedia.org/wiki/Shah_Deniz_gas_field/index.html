Shah Deniz gas field

Shah Deniz is the largest natural gas field in Azerbaijan. Discovered in 1999, it is situated in the South Caspian Sea, off the shore of Azerbaijan, approximately 70 kilometres (43 mi) southeast of Baku, in water depths of 50 to 550 metres. The field covers about 860 square kilometres and its name means "King of the Sea" in Persian. Reserves are estimated at roughly 1.2 trillion cubic metres of gas, making it one of the world's largest gas-condensate fields.

Development

The field is operated by BP under a production sharing agreement with partners including SOCAR, TotalEnergies, Lukoil, TPAO, and others. Stage 1 began production in 2006, supplying Azerbaijan, Georgia, and Turkey via the South Caucasus Pipeline (Baku–Tbilisi–Erzurum). Stage 2, sanctioned in December 2013, added subsea wells tied to a new platform complex and expanded the Sangachal terminal; first gas reached Turkey in 2018 and Europe in December 2020.

Southern Gas Corridor

Shah Deniz Stage 2 gas forms the initial supply for the Southern Gas Corridor: the expanded South Caucasus Pipeline through Azerbaijan and Georgia, the Trans-Anatolian Pipeline (TANAP) across Turkey, and the Trans-Adriatic Pipeline (TAP) through Greece and Albania to Italy. Total annual plateau production from both stages is about 26 billion cubic metres, of which roughly 10 bcm per year is contracted to European buyers, 6 bcm to Turkey, and the remainder to Azerbaijan and Georgia.

Significance

Shah Deniz transformed Azerbaijan into a major gas exporter and is central to European efforts to diversify gas supplies away from Russia. Further development phases, including deeper gas below the producing reservoirs, have been under study.
