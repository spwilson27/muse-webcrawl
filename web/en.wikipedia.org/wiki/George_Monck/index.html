George Monck

George Monck, 1st Duke of Albemarle KG (6 December 1608 – 3 January 1670) was an English soldier and politician.

Born: Potheridge, Devon
Died: London
Known for: Restoring Charles II in 1660

George Monck (1608–1670), later 1st Duke of Albemarle, was an English professional soldier who served on both sides during the Wars of the Three Kingdoms and played the decisive role in the Restoration of Charles II in 1660. (This short-title entry covers the same life treated at greater length in the full-titled article George Monck, 1st Duke of Albemarle.)

Career in brief

Monck gained military experience in Dutch service before fighting for Charles I in the Civil War; captured at Nantwich in 1644, he was imprisoned in the Tower of London, then joined Oliver Cromwell's forces, served in Ireland and Scotland, and became commander-in-chief in Scotland in 1654.

The Restoration

After Cromwell's death and the collapse of the Protectorate, Monck marched his army south from Coldstream in January 1660 — his regiment became the Coldstream Guards — secured London, insisted on fresh elections, and negotiated with Charles II, whose Declaration of Breda paved the way for the monarchy's return in May 1660.

Later honours

Charles rewarded Monck with the dukedom of Albemarle, the Order of the Garter, and high office. He commanded the English fleet alongside Prince Rupert in the Second Anglo-Dutch War and organised relief during the Great Plague and the Great Fire of London. He died in 1670 and was buried in Westminster Abbey.
