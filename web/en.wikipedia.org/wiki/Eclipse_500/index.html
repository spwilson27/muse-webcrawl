Eclipse 500

The Eclipse 500 (model EA500) is a small six-seat business jet developed by Eclipse Aviation of Albuquerque, New Mexico, United States. Powered by two Pratt & Whitney Canada PW610F turbofan engines mounted at the rear of the fuselage, it was the first of the "very light jets" (VLJs) to enter service, intended to enable low-cost per-seat air taxi operations.

Development

Eclipse Aviation was founded in 1998 by Vern Raburn. The 500 evolved from the Williams V-Jet II demonstrator after the originally planned Williams engines proved unsuitable, causing a redesign around Pratt & Whitney engines. The aircraft made its first flight in its definitive form in 2004 and received FAA certification in 2006. Its innovative construction used friction stir welding instead of rivets for much of the aluminium structure.

Service and bankruptcy

The jet cruises at about 370 knots (685 km/h) with a range of roughly 1,125 nautical miles and seats four passengers plus pilots in typical layout. Eclipse delivered around 260 aircraft, far short of the thousands ordered, before mounting losses forced it into bankruptcy in November 2008. The company's assets were bought by Eclipse Aerospace, which later developed the improved Eclipse 550 and upgrade programs for the 500.

Specifications

Length 10.2 m; wingspan 11.6 m; maximum takeoff weight 2,722 kg; service ceiling 41,000 ft. Crew: one or two pilots.
