Ultimate Fighting Championship

The Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) is an American mixed martial arts (MMA) promotion company based in Las Vegas, Nevada. It is the largest MMA promotion in the world, producing more than 40 live events per year and broadcasting to hundreds of millions of homes in over 170 countries. The UFC is owned by TKO Group Holdings, formed in 2023 when Endeavor merged the UFC with the professional wrestling promotion WWE.

History

The UFC was founded in 1993 by Art Davie and members of the Gracie family, including Rorion Gracie, as a one-night, eight-man tournament designed to pit different martial arts disciplines against each other with minimal rules. Brazilian jiu-jitsu practitioner Royce Gracie won the first events, demonstrating the effectiveness of grappling. Early UFC events were marketed as brutal spectacles, provoking political backlash — Senator John McCain famously called the sport "human cockfighting" — and driving it from pay-per-view.

The sport was reformed under the Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts, with weight classes, rounds, and judging, gaining athletic-commission sanctioning beginning in 2000–2001. In 2001 the Fertitta brothers' Zuffa LLC purchased the UFC for $2 million; under president Dana White it grew through the reality series The Ultimate Fighter (2005), landmark broadcasting deals, and stars such as Chuck Liddell, Georges St-Pierre, Anderson Silva, Ronda Rousey and Conor McGregor. Endeavor acquired the UFC in 2016 in a deal valued at about $4 billion.

Competition

Bouts take place in an eight-sided cage known as the Octagon, across weight divisions from strawweight to heavyweight for men and women. Fighters contest titles decided by a championship system, with performance judged on striking, grappling and octagon control when fights go the distance.
