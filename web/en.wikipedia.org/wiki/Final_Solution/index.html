Final Solution

The Final Solution (German: die Endlösung), formally the "Final Solution to the Jewish Question", was the Nazi plan for the genocide of the Jewish population during World War II. It was implemented through the systematic murder of approximately six million Jews across German-occupied Europe between 1941 and 1945, in the central event of the Holocaust.

Origins of the term

"Final solution" was a bureaucratic euphemism. Nazi policy toward Jews escalated through the 1930s from discrimination and forced emigration to ghettoisation after the invasion of Poland in 1939. Following the invasion of the Soviet Union in June 1941, Einsatzgruppen mobile killing units and their auxiliaries murdered over a million Jews in open-air shootings, including 33,771 at Babi Yar near Kyiv in two days of September 1941.

Authorisation and coordination

On 31 July 1941 Reichsmarschall Hermann Göring signed a written authorisation directing Reinhard Heydrich to organise a "total solution of the Jewish question". The Wannsee Conference of 20 January 1942 coordinated the government ministries and SS agencies in carrying out deportation and extermination and fixed the target population at eleven million Jews across all of Europe.

Implementation

From late 1941, extermination camps were constructed in occupied Poland: Chełmno began gassing operations in December 1941; Bełżec, Sobibór and Treblinka (Operation Reinhard) murdered some 1.7 million Jews in 1942–43; and Auschwitz-Birkenau and Majdanek combined concentration-camp and killing functions. Jews were deported by rail from ghettos and from countries across Europe, and most were killed in gas chambers on arrival. Roma, Poles, Soviet prisoners of war, disabled people and others were also murdered in vast numbers under related programmes, but the total extermination of the Jews as a people was the Final Solution's defining aim.

End and aftermath

As Allied and Soviet forces advanced in 1944–45, the SS evacuated camps and forced prisoners on death marches. After Germany's defeat, surviving perpetrators were prosecuted at Nuremberg and later trials; Adolf Eichmann, a principal organiser of the deportations, was captured in Argentina, tried in Jerusalem and executed in 1962. The documentary record — orders, minutes, statistics such as the Korherr Report — makes the Final Solution one of the most thoroughly documented crimes in history.
