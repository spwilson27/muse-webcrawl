The Legged Squad Support System (LS3) was a DARPA project for a legged robot designed to carry supplies for soldiers in the field. Developed by Boston Dynamics with an engineering team including engineers from Bell Helicopter, AAI Corporation, Carnegie Mellon University and others, it was a larger, more refined successor to the company's BigDog quadruped robot.

Development and trials

The LS3 could carry around 180 kg (400 lb) of equipment, follow a squad leader through rough terrain using computer vision, respond to simple voice commands, and operate for up to 24 hours on a tank of fuel, travelling about 32 km (20 mi). It was field-tested with the United States Marine Corps, including during the RIMPAC exercises in Hawaii in 2014.

Cancellation

Despite successful demonstrations, the programme was shelved in 2015: the robot's gasoline engine was judged too noisy for use alongside troops in combat, and its practical utility did not justify further investment. The LS3 nonetheless demonstrated major advances in dynamic balance and rough-terrain locomotion.
