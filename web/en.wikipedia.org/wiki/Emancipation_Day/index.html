Emancipation Day

Emancipation Day is a holiday in various countries and territories, often in the former British colonies of the Caribbean and in the United States, marking the anniversary of the abolition of slavery or of emancipatory acts. The dates vary by jurisdiction.

United States

In the United States, several states and the District of Columbia observe Emancipation Day. Washington, D.C., marks April 16, the date in 1862 when President Abraham Lincoln signed the District of Columbia Compensated Emancipation Act, freeing about 3,100 enslaved people — months before the Emancipation Proclamation. Juneteenth (June 19) commemorates the 1865 announcement of emancipation in Galveston, Texas, and became a federal holiday in 2021. Florida observes May 20, marking the 1865 reading of the Emancipation Proclamation in Tallahassee.

Caribbean

Many Caribbean nations celebrate Emancipation Day on August 1, commemorating the Slavery Abolition Act 1833, which freed enslaved people across most of the British Empire effective August 1, 1834 (with full freedom after the end of "apprenticeship" in 1838). Countries observing the day include Jamaica, Barbados, Trinidad and Tobago, Guyana, the Bahamas, and Bermuda (where it opens the Cup Match holiday). Canada also recognizes August 1 as Emancipation Day.

Observances

Celebrations typically include parades, cultural festivals, church services, and readings of emancipation proclamations.
