Dihydroxyacetone phosphate

Chemical formulaC3H7O6P
Molar mass170.06 g/mol
AbbreviationDHAP

Dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP, also glycerone phosphate in some texts) is an anion with the formula HOCH2C(O)CH2OPO32−. This phosphate group is involved in a number of metabolic processes, including the Calvin cycle in plants and glycolysis.

Role in glycolysis

In glycolysis, fructose 1,6-bisphosphate is split by the enzyme aldolase into two three-carbon sugars: dihydroxyacetone phosphate and glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate. The two products are isomers, and the enzyme triose-phosphate isomerase rapidly and reversibly interconverts them. Although the equilibrium strongly favors DHAP, only glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate continues through the glycolytic pathway, so DHAP is continually converted as the pathway proceeds — with the result that both halves of the original glucose molecule are ultimately metabolized.

Other roles

In the Calvin cycle of photosynthesis, DHAP is one of the products of the reduction of 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate by NADPH, and is used in the regeneration of ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate. DHAP is also the product of the dehydrogenation of glycerol phosphate, an entry point into glycolysis for the glycerol released from triglycerides, and it serves as a precursor in the biosynthesis of ether lipids. Because DHAP can decompose to the reactive dicarbonyl methylglyoxal, its interconversion is also relevant to the methylglyoxal pathway.
