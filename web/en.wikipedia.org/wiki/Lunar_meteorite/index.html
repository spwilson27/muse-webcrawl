Lunar meteorite

A lunar meteorite is a meteorite that originated from the Moon: a rock ejected from the lunar surface by an impact event that later travelled through space and fell to Earth. Several hundred are known, most found in hot deserts such as the Sahara and Oman, or in Antarctica.

Identification

The first lunar meteorite, Allan Hills A81005, was collected in Antarctica in 1982 and recognised as lunar by comparison with samples returned by the Apollo missions. Lunar meteorites are identified by their mineralogy, textures and oxygen-isotope composition, which match Apollo and Luna samples and differ from terrestrial and asteroidal rocks. Many are breccias — mixtures of rock fragments from the lunar highlands — while a few are mare basalts.

Significance

Because they come from random locations on the Moon, lunar meteorites sample regions never visited by Apollo, including, statistically, the far side. They thus complement the mission samples. Their transit time from the Moon and their time lying on Earth can be measured from cosmic-ray exposure and terrestrial weathering.

Lunar meteorites are rare and valuable; all known examples together weigh only a few hundred kilograms. Unlike Apollo samples, which cannot legally be privately owned in most cases, lunar meteorites can be bought and sold, though prices per gram are extremely high.
