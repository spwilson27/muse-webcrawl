Rebec


A rebec, a bowed string instrument of the medieval period.

The rebec (sometimes rebeck) is a bowed string instrument of the medieval and early Renaissance eras. Derived ultimately from the Arab rabāb, it was introduced into Europe through Spain and Byzantium by the 10th–11th centuries and was widely used by jongleurs, minstrels, and in courtly and dance music.

Description

The rebec typically has a pear- or boat-shaped body carved from a single block of wood, a short neck, and usually three (sometimes one to five) strings tuned in fifths. It was held against the chest, shoulder, or downward, and played with a small bow. Its soundboard often has a carved rosette or C-shaped sound holes. Soprano, alto, and tenor sizes existed.

History

The rebec flourished from the 13th to the 16th century before giving way to the violin family in art music, though it survived in folk traditions. Its influence is seen in later instruments, and it is a staple of modern early-music ensembles.
