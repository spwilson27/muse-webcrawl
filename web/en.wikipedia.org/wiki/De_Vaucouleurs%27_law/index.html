De Vaucouleurs's law

De Vaucouleurs's law, also called the de Vaucouleurs profile or r1/4 law, describes how the surface brightness of an elliptical galaxy or the bulge of a spiral galaxy varies with distance from its centre. It was proposed by the French-American astronomer Gérard de Vaucouleurs in 1948.

De Vaucouleurs's law
Proposed byGérard de Vaucouleurs (1948)
Applies toElliptical galaxies, galactic bulges
FormI(R) ∝ exp[−7.67((R/Re)1/4 − 1)]

Description

The law states that the logarithm of the surface brightness I at radius R is proportional to R1/4. Defining the effective radius Re as the radius enclosing half the galaxy's total light, the profile can be written I(R) = Ie exp{−7.67[(R/Re)1/4 − 1]}, where Ie is the surface brightness at Re. Integrated over the whole galaxy, the profile yields a finite total luminosity.

Generalisation

De Vaucouleurs's law is the n = 4 special case of the more general Sérsic profile, in which the exponent is 1/n and n is fitted to the data; disk galaxies are described by n ≈ 1 (an exponential profile). The success of the r1/4 law for luminous ellipticals reflects the outcome of violent relaxation during galaxy formation and merging.
