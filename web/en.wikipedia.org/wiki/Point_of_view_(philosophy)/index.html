Point of view (philosophy)

In philosophy, a point of view is a specific attitude or manner through which a person thinks about something. In a broader sense, it refers to the position from which a subject perceives, interprets and judges the world, shaped by their experiences, beliefs, mental states and conceptual framework.

FieldEpistemology, philosophy of mind, aesthetics
Related conceptsPerspective, subjectivity, standpoint theory, qualia

Subjectivity and perspective

A central issue is the relationship between a subjective point of view and objective reality. Thomas Nagel's famous essay "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974) argues that conscious experience has an essentially subjective character — there is something it is like to be a particular organism — that cannot be fully captured by an objective, third-person description. Nagel contrasted the "view from nowhere" with the view from a particular point within the world.

Perspectivism

Friedrich Nietzsche developed a doctrine often called perspectivism: the idea that all knowledge and evaluation are conditioned by the perspective of the knower, and that there is no perception wholly free of interpretation. Later thinkers, including José Ortega y Gasset, made the "point of view" a systematic category: each person occupies a unique vantage point on reality, and truth is approached by integrating many such perspectives.

Standpoint theory

In feminist and social epistemology, standpoint theory holds that social position systematically shapes what people can know, and that marginalised groups may enjoy an epistemic advantage on questions concerning their oppression. The theory descends in part from Georg Lukács's account of the standpoint of the proletariat.

First-person and third-person points of view

Philosophy of mind distinguishes the first-person point of view — direct access to one's own mental states — from the third-person point of view used to describe others. Questions about first-person authority, other minds, and the explanatory gap between subjective experience and physical processes all turn on this contrast.
