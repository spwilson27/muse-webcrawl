Gregorian calendar

The Gregorian calendar is the calendar used in most of the world today. It was proclaimed in 1582 by Pope Gregory XIII as a reform of the Julian calendar, correcting the drift of the calendar against the solar year.

Rules

Its leap-year rule — a leap day every four years, except century years not divisible by 400 — gives an average year of 365.2425 days. Adoption spread over centuries: Catholic countries first in 1582, Britain and its colonies in 1752, Russia only in 1918.
