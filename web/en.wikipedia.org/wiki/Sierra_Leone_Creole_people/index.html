The Sierra Leone Creole people, commonly called Krio, are an ethnic group of Sierra Leone descended from freed African-American, Afro-Caribbean and Liberated African slaves who settled in the Western Area of Sierra Leone between 1787 and about 1885. Although they form only a small minority of the national population, their language, Krio, is the lingua franca spoken throughout the country, and Creoles have historically played an outsized role in Sierra Leone's education, professions and politics.

Origins

The community's founding groups arrived in waves: the "Black Poor" from London in 1787; Black Loyalists from Nova Scotia in 1792, who founded Freetown; Jamaican Maroons deported in 1800; and, after Britain abolished the slave trade in 1807, tens of thousands of "Liberated Africans" or recaptives taken from intercepted slave ships. Creole society formed from the intermarriage and cultural blending of these settlers with recaptives of Yoruba, Igbo, Congolese and many other origins.

Culture

Creole culture blends West African heritage with British and Caribbean influences. It is visible in Krio — an English-based creole with African grammatical structures and vocabulary from Yoruba and other languages — in distinctive wooden board houses on stilts influenced by Caribbean architecture, in cuisine, and in institutions such as Fourah Bay College, founded in 1827, which made Freetown known as the "Athens of West Africa." Secret societies, church life and naming customs combine African and European elements.

History and prominence

Creoles dominated the civil service, medicine, law and commerce of 19th-century British West Africa, serving as administrators, doctors and missionaries as far afield as Nigeria and the Gambia. Figures such as Samuel Ajayi Crowther, the first African Anglican bishop, emerged from the recaptive community. Creole political influence declined in the 20th century as the protectorate peoples gained the franchise, but Krio language and culture diffused nationwide, and the diaspora remains prominent in the professions.
