Ministry of Sound is a nightclub and multimedia entertainment business based in Elephant and Castle, London. Opened on 21 September 1991 in a converted bus garage in Gaunt Street, it was modelled on the Paradise Garage and other New York clubs, prioritising sound quality — with a system built around bespoke specifications — over alcohol sales; unusually, it originally operated without a drinks licence. Co-founded by Jamie Gould, Humphrey Waterhouse and James Palumbo (later Lord Palumbo of Southwark), the club became one of the most famous venues of the global house and dance-music scene.

The club's main room, "The Box", is built as a container within the building to isolate its sound.

The club

The club has several rooms, of which the largest, The Box, features one of the best-regarded sound systems in the world, repeatedly re-engineered, including a Dolby Atmos installation — the first in a nightclub. Long-running club nights included Rulin, Smoove and Saturday Sessions, with resident and guest DJs central to the UK house scene. In the 2010s the club's future was threatened by nearby residential development; a 2013 agreement with the developer and the Mayor of London preserved its licence subject to noise conditions.

The business

Ministry of Sound expanded into a record label famous for its compilation series, beginning with The Annual (1995), which became some of the best-selling dance compilations ever released; its recordings business was sold to Sony Music UK in 2016. The group has operated international tours, radio, fitness (the "Ministry Does Fitness" classes held in the club), a co-working members' club (The Ministry, opened 2018 in Borough), and licensed venues abroad.
