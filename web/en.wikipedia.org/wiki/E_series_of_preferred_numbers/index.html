E series of preferred numbers

The E series is a system of preferred numbers (also called preferred values) derived from geometric sequences, used to standardize the nominal values of electronic components such as resistors, capacitors, and inductors. The series are defined in the international standard IEC 60063.

Definition

Each E series divides a decade into a fixed number of logarithmically spaced steps: E3 has 3 values per decade, E6 has 6, E12 has 12, E24 has 24, and the finer series E48, E96, and E192 have 48, 96, and 192 values per decade, respectively. The number of values corresponds to component tolerance: E6 suits ±20% tolerance, E12 ±10%, E24 ±5%, E96 ±1%, and E192 still finer tolerances down to ±0.1% and below.

For example, the E12 values in one decade are 1.0, 1.2, 1.5, 1.8, 2.2, 2.7, 3.3, 3.9, 4.7, 5.6, 6.8, and 8.2. Standard values repeat in every decade by scaling with powers of ten. The system is related to the Renard series used more broadly in engineering.
