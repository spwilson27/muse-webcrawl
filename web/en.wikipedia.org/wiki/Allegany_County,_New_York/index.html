Allegany County is a county in the Southern Tier of the U.S. state of New York. As of the 2020 census, the population was 46,456. Its county seat is Belmont. The name derives from a Lenape word for the Allegheny River.

History

The county was created in 1806 from Genesee County. Its lands were part of the Holland Purchase and the Phelps and Gorham Purchase. The Genesee River flows northward through the county, and oil was discovered near Richburg in the 1880s, part of the early Pennsylvania–New York oil region.

Geography

Allegany County covers about 1,034 square miles of rolling hills, much of it forested or farmed. It borders Pennsylvania to the south and Cattaraugus, Wyoming, Livingston, and Steuben counties in New York.

Communities

Principal communities include Wellsville, Alfred (home of Alfred University and Alfred State College), Cuba, Friendship, Bolivar, and Belmont.
