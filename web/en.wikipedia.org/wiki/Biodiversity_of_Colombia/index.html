Biodiversity of Colombia

The biodiversity of Colombia is among the richest on Earth. Colombia is recognised as a megadiverse country, ranking among the top nations for species richness: it holds the world's largest number of bird and orchid species and ranks near the top for plants, butterflies, amphibians and freshwater fish.

Geography and ecosystems

Straddling the equator between the Pacific Ocean and the Caribbean Sea, Colombia spans Amazon rainforest, Orinoco grasslands (Llanos), Andean highlands and páramos, the Chocó — one of the wettest places on Earth — and coastal deserts such as La Guajira. The Andes split into three cordilleras, creating steep climatic gradients and high endemism. The country contains part of the Tropical Andes and Tumbes–Chocó–Magdalena biodiversity hotspots.

Fauna and flora

Colombia has recorded roughly 1,900 bird species, more than 60,000 plant species by broad estimates, and emblematic animals including the spectacled bear, jaguar, Andean condor, pink river dolphin and numerous poison dart frogs. The wax palm is the national tree and the Andean condor the national bird.

Conservation

Threats include deforestation, mining, coca cultivation and armed conflict, which for decades also restricted scientific access. A national system of protected areas covers national natural parks such as Tayrona, Los Nevados and Chiribiquete, the latter a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
