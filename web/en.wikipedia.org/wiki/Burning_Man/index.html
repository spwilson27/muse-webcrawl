Burning Man

Burning Man is an annual event held since 1990 in the Black Rock Desert of northwestern Nevada, United States, in which a temporary city, Black Rock City, is erected for about a week around the end of summer, culminating in the burning of a large wooden effigy known as the Man on the Saturday evening before Labor Day. Attendance has grown to roughly 70,000–80,000 participants. The event is organised by the non-profit Burning Man Project and is guided by ten principles articulated by co-founder Larry Harvey in 2004.

History

The first burn took place on San Francisco's Baker Beach at the summer solstice of 1986, when Larry Harvey and Jerry James burned an 8-foot wooden figure. After the San Francisco police blocked the beach burn in 1990, the event moved to the Black Rock Desert, merging with a Cacophony Society "Zone Trip". Attendance, art installations, mutant vehicles and theme camps grew rapidly through the 1990s and 2000s, and regional "burns" on the Burning Man model now take place worldwide, from South Africa's AfrikaBurn to Israel's Midburn.

Principles and culture

The ten principles include radical inclusion, gifting, decommodification, radical self-reliance, radical self-expression, communal effort, civic responsibility, leaving no trace, participation and immediacy. No vending is permitted (only ice and, historically, coffee are sold); participants are expected to bring everything they need and remove all "MOOP" (matter out of place). The city is laid out in a great arc of streets around the open playa, where the Man and the Temple stand. In 2023 heavy rain flooded the playa, stranding participants for days and drawing worldwide attention, before conditions allowed exodus.
