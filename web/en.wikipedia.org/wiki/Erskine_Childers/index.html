Erskine Childers

Robert Erskine Childers (25 June 1870 – 24 November 1922) was an English-born Irish writer, soldier, and nationalist. He is best known for his influential spy novel The Riddle of the Sands (1903) and for his role in the Irish independence movement, which ended with his execution by the Irish Free State during the Irish Civil War.

Life

Childers worked as a clerk in the House of Commons, served in the Boer War, and achieved fame with The Riddle of the Sands, a warning about a German invasion plot. A keen sailor, he put his yacht Asgard to work in the Howth gun-running of July 1914, landing rifles for the Irish Volunteers. He served in naval intelligence in the First World War, then became a committed republican, Sinn Féin TD, and director of publicity for the First Dáil.

Death

Childers opposed the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 and joined the anti-Treaty IRA's publicity staff in the Civil War. Arrested in November 1922 carrying a pistol, he was court-martialled under emergency legislation and executed by firing squad at Beggars Bush Barracks, Dublin, on 24 November 1922. His son, also Erskine Childers, later became the fourth President of Ireland.
