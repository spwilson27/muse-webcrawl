The National University of Córdoba (Spanish: Universidad Nacional de Córdoba, UNC) is a public university in Córdoba, Argentina. Founded in 1613 by the Jesuits, it is the oldest university in Argentina and one of the oldest in the Americas.

History

The university began as the Colegio Máximo of the Jesuit order and received papal and royal authorisation to grant degrees in the 1620s. After the Jesuits' expulsion in 1767 it passed to the Franciscans and later to the civil government; it was nationalised in 1856.

University Reform of 1918

The UNC was the birthplace of the University Reform movement of 1918, when student protests demanding autonomy, co-governance, academic freedom and free tuition began in Córdoba and spread throughout Latin America, reshaping higher education across the continent.

Campus and heritage

The historic Jesuit Block (Manzana Jesuítica), including the university's original buildings and the Church of the Society of Jesus, was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000. Today the university comprises fifteen faculties, numerous research institutes and two major hospitals.
