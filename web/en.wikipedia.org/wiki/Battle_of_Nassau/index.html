Battle of Nassau

Date3–4 March 1776
LocationNew Providence, Bahamas
ResultAmerican victory; capture of military stores
BelligerentsUnited Colonies (Continental Navy and Marines) vs. Great Britain
CommandersEsek Hopkins; Samuel Nicholas vs. Governor Montfort Browne

The Battle of Nassau (3–4 March 1776) was a naval action of the American Revolutionary War in which a squadron of the new Continental Navy under Commodore Esek Hopkins raided the British colony of Nassau on New Providence in the Bahamas to seize gunpowder and military stores for the Continental Army. It was the first amphibious landing of the Continental Marines.

Background

George Washington's army besieging Boston was desperately short of gunpowder. Intelligence reported large stores at Nassau guarded by two small forts and a tiny garrison. Hopkins sailed from Cape Henlopen in February 1776 with eight armed vessels — the first fleet of the Continental Navy — carrying a detachment of Marines under Captain Samuel Nicholas.

The action

After capturing two British sloops off the Bahamas whose pilots guided the squadron in, Hopkins landed about 230 Marines and sailors east of Fort Montagu on 3 March. The fort's small detachment fired a few shots and withdrew, and Governor Montfort Browne ordered Fort Nassau's guns to fire, but surrendered the town the next day. The Americans occupied Nassau for two weeks, loading captured cannon, mortars and powder; much of the gunpowder had been spirited away on the first night, but the haul of artillery was substantial.

Aftermath

The squadron sailed for home on 17 March, fighting an inconclusive night action with HMS Glasgow off Block Island on 6 April. The captured ordnance materially aided the American cause, and the raid is celebrated by the United States Marine Corps as its first amphibious operation; the Marines' role is commemorated in the phrase "to the shores of Tripoli" tradition of expeditionary landings, and the Bahamas action of 1776 remains the Corps' founding operation.
