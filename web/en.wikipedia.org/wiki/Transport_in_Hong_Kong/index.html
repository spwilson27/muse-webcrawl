Transport in Hong Kong

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Transport in Hong Kong encompasses public and private transport in the territory. Hong Kong has a highly developed transport network, and roughly 90 per cent of daily journeys are taken on public transport — among the highest rates in the world. The system is coordinated by the Transport Department and unified for payment purposes by the Octopus card, a contactless stored-value smart card introduced in 1997.

Railways

The backbone of the system is the MTR, a rapid transit network of heavy rail lines — including the Tsuen Wan, Kwun Tong, Island, Tung Chung, Tseung Kwan O, East Rail, Tuen Ma, South Island, and Disneyland Resort lines, plus the Airport Express and the Light Rail in the northwest New Territories. The network carries around four to five million passengers a day. The Guangzhou–Shenzhen–Hong Kong Express Rail Link connects West Kowloon station to the mainland high-speed network. Hong Kong Tramways has operated double-decker trams along the north shore of Hong Kong Island since 1904, and the Peak Tram funicular serves Victoria Peak.

Roads and buses

Franchised bus companies — principally KMB, Citybus, and New Lantao Bus — operate several thousand buses on hundreds of routes, complemented by public light buses (minibuses) in red (unregulated-route) and green (fixed-route) schemes. Taxis come in three colour-coded types: red urban taxis, green New Territories taxis, and blue Lantau taxis. Private car ownership is low by international standards, restrained by high taxes, fuel prices, and parking costs. Cross-harbour traffic uses three road tunnels, and the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge, opened in 2018, links the city to the western Pearl River Delta.

Water and air

Ferries remain important: the Star Ferry has crossed Victoria Harbour since the late nineteenth century, and other operators serve outlying islands such as Lantau, Lamma, Cheung Chau, and Peng Chau, as well as high-speed routes to Macau and Pearl River Delta ports. The Port of Hong Kong, once the world's busiest container port, remains a major transshipment hub though now ranked behind mainland rivals. Hong Kong International Airport at Chek Lap Kok, opened in 1998, is one of the world's busiest cargo airports and a leading passenger hub.

Policy

Since the 1970s government policy has prioritised mass transit, using the "rail plus property" model — granting the railway corporation development rights above stations — to fund expansion without direct subsidy. Hillside escalators, notably the Central–Mid-Levels escalator system, assist pedestrian movement on steep terrain.
