Propaganda Movement

The Propaganda Movement was a campaign of literary and political reform in the Spanish Philippines in the late 19th century, carried out mainly by educated Filipinos (ilustrados) living in Europe, especially Spain. Active roughly from 1880 to 1895, the movement sought equality for Filipinos, representation in the Spanish Cortes, secularization of parishes, and freedom of speech and the press — assimilation as a province of Spain rather than outright independence, at least initially.

Key figures and publications

Leading propagandists included José Rizal, author of the novels Noli Me Tángere and El filibusterismo; Marcelo H. del Pilar; and Graciano López Jaena, who founded the movement's newspaper, La Solidaridad, published in Barcelona and later Madrid from 1889 to 1895.

Legacy

The failure of peaceful reform contributed to the founding of the more radical Katipunan and the outbreak of the Philippine Revolution in 1896.
