Field: Linear algebra

Conjugate transpose

In mathematics, the conjugate transpose of a matrix is the transpose of the matrix with its entries replaced by their complex conjugates. It is also called the Hermitian transpose or adjoint matrix, and is commonly denoted AH, A*, or A† (dagger).

Definition

If A is an m × n matrix with complex entries, its conjugate transpose AH is the n × m matrix whose (i, j) entry is the complex conjugate of the (j, i) entry of A. For real matrices, the conjugate transpose coincides with the ordinary transpose.

Properties

The conjugate transpose satisfies (A + B)H = AH + BH, (AB)H = BHAH, and (AH)H = A. A matrix equal to its own conjugate transpose is called Hermitian; such matrices have real eigenvalues and play a central role in quantum mechanics, where observables are represented by Hermitian operators. The operation is closely related to the adjoint of a linear operator on a Hilbert space and to the inner-product structures used throughout physics.
