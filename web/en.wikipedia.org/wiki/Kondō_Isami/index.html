Kondō Isami

Kondō Isami (1834–1868), commander of the Shinsengumi

Kondō Isami (近藤 勇, November 9, 1834 – May 17, 1868) was a Japanese swordsman and commander of the Shinsengumi, the special police force organised by the Tokugawa shogunate in Kyoto during the Bakumatsu period. A master of the Tennen Rishin-ryū school of swordsmanship, Kondō led the Shinsengumi through the turbulent final years of the shogunate until his capture and execution after the Battle of Toba–Fushimi.

Early life

Kondō was born Katsugorō in Kami-Ishihara village, Musashi Province (present-day Chōfu, Tokyo), the third son of a farmer. He began training in the Tennen Rishin-ryū at the Shieikan dojo in 1848 and so impressed its master, Kondō Shūsuke, that he was adopted into the family and succeeded as the fourth head of the school in 1861.

Shinsengumi

In 1863 Kondō and his students travelled to Kyoto as part of the Rōshigumi, a corps of masterless samurai recruited to protect the shōgun. When the corps dissolved, Kondō and a remnant group stayed on, forming the Shinsengumi under the patronage of Matsudaira Katamori, daimyō of Aizu. Kondō became its commander (kyokuchō), with Hijikata Toshizō as vice-commander. The group won fame for the Ikedaya incident of 1864 and for enforcing order in the imperial capital.

Death

After the shogunate's defeat at Toba–Fushimi in January 1868, Kondō retreated east and attempted to regroup, but was captured by imperial forces at Nagareyama. He was beheaded at Itabashi on May 17, 1868, at the age of 33.

Legacy

Kondō remains one of the most depicted figures of the Bakumatsu era in Japanese literature, film and television, typically portrayed as a sincere and earnest leader bound by loyalty to a failing cause.
