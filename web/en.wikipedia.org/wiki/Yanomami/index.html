Yanomami

The Yanomami, also spelled Yanomamö, are a group of approximately 35,000 Indigenous people living in some 200–250 villages in the Amazon rainforest on the border between Venezuela and Brazil.

Yanomami
Total population: c. 35,000
Regions: Amazonas (Venezuela), Roraima and Amazonas (Brazil)
Languages: Yanomaman languages
Religion: Animism, shamanism

Society

Villages live in a large communal ring-shaped house, the shabono, and practise horticulture of plantains and cassava supplemented by hunting and gathering. Kinship, shamanic ritual with yakoana snuff, and feasting structure social life. Napoleon Chagnon's ethnography made them famous but also controversial.

History and threats

Sustained outside contact began in the mid-20th century. Gold miners (garimpeiros) brought disease and violence, including the 1993 Haximu massacre in Brazil, ruled genocide by Brazilian courts. Illegal mining resurged in the 2010s–2020s, causing a humanitarian and health crisis in the Yanomami Indigenous Territory.

Advocacy

Shaman and spokesman Davi Kopenawa, co-author of The Falling Sky, has campaigned internationally for Yanomami land rights.
