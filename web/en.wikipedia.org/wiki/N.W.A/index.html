N.W.A, pioneers of gangsta rap.

N.W.A (an abbreviation for Niggaz Wit Attitudes) was an American hip hop group formed in Compton, California, in 1987. Widely credited with popularising gangsta rap and West Coast hip hop, the group's core line-up consisted of Eazy-E, Ice Cube, Dr. Dre, MC Ren and DJ Yella.

Career

N.W.A released its debut studio album, Straight Outta Compton, in 1988. With tracks such as the title song, "Gangsta Gangsta" and the incendiary "Fuck tha Police" — which drew a warning letter from an FBI official — the album brought the realities and grievances of inner-city Los Angeles to a national audience and became a landmark of hip hop despite minimal radio play. Ice Cube left in 1989 over royalty disputes; the group's second album, Niggaz4Life (1991), debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 before the group dissolved amid feuds between Eazy-E and Dr. Dre. Eazy-E died in 1995 of complications from AIDS.

Legacy

N.W.A's members went on to major solo careers, and the group's influence on rap music and popular culture has been widely recognised. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2016, and their story was dramatised in the 2015 biographical film Straight Outta Compton.
