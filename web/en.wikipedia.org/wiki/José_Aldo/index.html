José Aldo

José Aldo da Silva Oliveira Júnior (born 9 September 1986) is a Brazilian professional mixed martial artist. He is best known as the first UFC Featherweight Champion and the final WEC Featherweight Champion, dominating the 145-pound division for nearly a decade.

Early life

Aldo was born in Manaus, Amazonas, and grew up in poverty. A facial scar from a childhood accident involving a barbecue grill became one of his distinguishing features. He trained in Brazilian jiu-jitsu and later Muay Thai and striking at Nova União in Rio de Janeiro under André Pederneiras.

Career

Aldo won the WEC featherweight title in 2009 by knocking out Mike Brown, and when the WEC merged into the UFC he became the inaugural UFC featherweight champion. He defended the title seven times, defeating challengers including Urijah Faber, Chad Mendes, and Frankie Edgar. His reign ended in December 2015 when Conor McGregor knocked him out in 13 seconds at UFC 194. Aldo regained the interim and then undisputed title in 2016 before losing it to Max Holloway in 2017.

Later career and style

Later in his career Aldo dropped to bantamweight, remaining a top contender, and also pursued professional boxing. He is renowned for his devastating leg kicks, striking speed, and near-impenetrable takedown defense, and is widely regarded as the greatest featherweight in MMA history. He was inducted into the UFC Hall of Fame in 2023.
