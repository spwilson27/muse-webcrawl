libav is a free and open-source library and set of tools for handling multimedia data. It originated as a fork of the FFmpeg project in 2011, when a group of FFmpeg developers, dissatisfied with the project's leadership and development practices, relaunched the codebase under the new name. The fork comprised the libraries libavcodec, libavformat, libavutil, libavfilter, and others, together with the avconv command-line transcoding tool, the counterpart of ffmpeg.

History

The fork was announced in January 2011 by a group of developers who had been maintaining FFmpeg; the two projects thereafter developed in parallel, periodically merging changes from one another. Debian and its derivatives, including Ubuntu, switched their multimedia packages to libav for several releases, before returning to FFmpeg in 2015. Development of libav slowed in the late 2010s, and the project saw no new major release after version 12 in the years that followed.

Components

  • avconv — a command-line tool for converting audio and video formats (the fork's equivalent of the ffmpeg tool).
  • avplay — a simple media player based on the libraries.
  • avprobe — a tool for inspecting multimedia streams.
  • libavcodec — a library of encoders and decoders for audio and video codecs.
  • libavformat — a library handling container formats and muxing/demuxing.
  • libavfilter — a library for filtering audio and video.

The avconv/ffmpeg naming dispute

Because Debian shipped avconv in place of the ffmpeg command, users of those distributions who invoked "ffmpeg" received a message stating that the program was deprecated in favour of avconv—wording widely criticised as misleading, since the FFmpeg project itself remained active. The episode became a well-known case study in the social dynamics of open-source forks.
