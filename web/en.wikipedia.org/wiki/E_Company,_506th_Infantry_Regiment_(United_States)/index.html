E Company, 506th Infantry Regiment (United States)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Men of E Company before the Normandy jump.

E Company, 2nd Battalion, 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment, of the 101st Airborne Division — famously known as "Easy Company" — was a US Army rifle company in the Second World War. It is the best-known company-sized unit of the war, through Stephen Ambrose's 1992 book Band of Brothers and the 2001 HBO miniseries of the same name, produced by Steven Spielberg and Tom Hanks, which followed its men from training to the end of the war in Europe.

History

The company was formed in 1942 at Camp Toccoa, Georgia, under the demanding command of Captain Herbert Sobel, whose harsh training produced an exceptionally fit if resentful unit; Sobel was replaced before deployment by Lieutenant Thomas Meehan, and then, after Meehan's death on D-Day, by Lieutenant Richard "Dick" Winters, who became the company's defining commander. Easy Company parachuted into Normandy on 6 June 1944, where Winters led the celebrated assault on the German gun battery at Brécourt Manor — still used as a textbook example of an attack on a fixed position.

The company fought in Operation Market Garden in the Netherlands, endured the siege of Bastogne during the Battle of the Bulge (where Winters, by then battalion executive officer, and the company under Lieutenant Norman Dike and then Ronald Speirs held the Bois Jacques woods), and advanced into Germany in 1945, liberating the Kaufering concentration camp and ending the war at Berchtesgaden and in Austria. Of the 140 or so men who trained at Toccoa, many were killed or wounded — the company's casualties across the war exceeded 40 killed.

Notable members

Well-known members include Richard Winters, Lewis Nixon, Ronald Speirs, Carwood Lipton, Donald Malarkey, William "Wild Bill" Guarnere, Edward "Babe" Heffron, David Webster, Eugene Roe, Frank Perconte, Joseph Liebgott, Darrell "Shifty" Powers and Lynn "Buck" Compton.

Legacy

The story of Easy Company has become emblematic of the citizen-soldier experience of the US Army in Europe. Memorials stand at Brécourt Manor and near Bastogne, and the surviving veterans' reunions, memoirs and oral histories have kept the unit unusually well documented.
