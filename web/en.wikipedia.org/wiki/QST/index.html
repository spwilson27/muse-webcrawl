QST

QST is a magazine for amateur radio enthusiasts, published in the United States by the American Radio Relay League (ARRL), the national association for amateur radio. It has been published continuously since December 1915 and is the oldest amateur radio magazine in the world. ARRL members receive it as a benefit of membership.

History

The first issue of QST was published in December 1915 by Hiram Percy Maxim and Clarence D. Tuska, who had founded the ARRL the year before. The title is a radio Q signal meaning "calling all stations" — a general call to all amateur radio operators. Publication was suspended during the First World War when amateur radio was shut down, resuming in 1919; during the Second World War the magazine continued on reduced schedules while the ARRL's other operations adapted to the wartime ban on amateur transmissions.

Content

QST carries technical articles on radio equipment design and construction, antennas and propagation, operating news, contest results, equipment reviews from the ARRL Laboratory, and regular columns on topics from Morse code operation to amateur satellites and emergency communications. Like many association journals, it now appears in both print and digital editions.
