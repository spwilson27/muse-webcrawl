Teijin Limited (帝人株式会社, Teijin Kabushiki-gaisha) is a Japanese chemical, pharmaceutical, and materials company headquartered in Osaka. Founded in 1918 as Japan's first rayon manufacturer, it has grown into a diversified group known for high-performance fibres and films.

History

Teijin began production of rayon in Yonezawa in 1918. It expanded into synthetic fibres after the Second World War, commercializing polyester in cooperation with partners in the late 1950s, and later diversified into plastics, films, pharmaceuticals, and home-healthcare services. In 2001 it acquired the aramid business that produces Twaron, and in 2008 it absorbed Toho Tenax, a major carbon-fibre producer.

Businesses

The group's principal products include:

  • Aramid fibres — Twaron and Technora, used in ballistic protection, tyres, and friction materials.
  • Carbon fibre — Tenax, used in aircraft structures, pressure vessels, and sporting goods.
  • Films and resins — polyester films and polycarbonate resins for electronics and automotive uses.
  • Healthcare — prescription pharmaceuticals (including the gout treatment febuxostat) and home oxygen therapy services in Japan.
  • Composites and textiles — high-performance fabrics and composite materials.

Sustainability

Teijin promotes chemical and mechanical recycling of polyester, including fibre-to-fibre recycling programmes, and has set group targets for reducing greenhouse-gas emissions across its supply chain.
