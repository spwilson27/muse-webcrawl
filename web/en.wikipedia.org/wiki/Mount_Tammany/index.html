Mount Tammany is a 470-metre (1,540 ft) peak in the Worthington State Forest of northwestern New Jersey, forming the New Jersey side of the Delaware Water Gap. Together with Pennsylvania's Mount Minsi across the Delaware River, it guards one of the most scenic river gaps in the eastern United States. The mountain is named for Tamanend, the Lenni Lenape chief known as Saint Tammany.

Hiking

The mountain's steep, rocky trails are among New Jersey's most popular hikes. The Red Dot Trail climbs directly over boulder fields to the summit viewpoint, while the Blue Dot Trail offers a somewhat less strenuous ascent; together they form a classic loop of about 5.6 km. The Appalachian Trail passes along the river below. Summit vistas take in the Delaware River, Mount Minsi and the surrounding ridges.
