The Gatwick Express is a premium airport rail service between London Victoria station and Gatwick Airport in West Sussex, England. Running non-stop over the Brighton Main Line, it provides the fastest rail link between central London and the airport, with departures every 15 minutes at peak times and a journey time of around 30 minutes.

History

Rail services to the airport began when Gatwick Airport station opened in 1958, and a dedicated non-stop service was launched by British Rail in May 1984 with distinctive branded rolling stock. After privatization the service was operated as a standalone franchise by National Express from 1996, before being merged into the Southern franchise in 2008; since 2015 it has been part of the Govia Thameslink Railway operation, though it retains its separate brand and premium fares. Some services were extended to Brighton during peak hours from 2008.

Rolling stock and service

Since 2016 the service has used Class 387/2 Electrostar electric multiple units in Gatwick Express livery, fitted with airport-oriented luggage space and Wi-Fi. Trains use dedicated platforms at Victoria. The service competes with cheaper, slightly slower Southern and Thameslink trains serving the same route, which has periodically raised debate over its premium pricing.
