The Angeles National Forest is a national forest located in the San Gabriel Mountains and Sierra Pelona Mountains of Los Angeles County, California, immediately north of the Los Angeles metropolitan area.

Geography

The forest covers about 655,000 acres, with elevations ranging from roughly 1,200 to 10,064 feet at the summit of Mount San Antonio (Mount Baldy). Much of the terrain is chaparral and coniferous forest, and it includes the San Gabriel Mountains National Monument, designated in 2014.

History

The area traces its origin to the San Gabriel Timberland Reserve of 1892, the first forest reserve in California. The Angeles National Forest was officially established in 1908 by combining several reserves.

Recreation and ecology

The forest is one of the most heavily visited in the United States, offering hiking, skiing at Mount Baldy and Mountain High, and the Angeles Crest Highway. It provides habitat for bighorn sheep, black bears, and mountain lions, though it is periodically affected by large wildfires such as the Station Fire of 2009 and the Bobcat Fire of 2020.
