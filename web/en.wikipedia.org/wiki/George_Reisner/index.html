George Reisner

George Andrew Reisner Jr. (November 5, 1867 – June 6, 1942) was an American archaeologist of Ancient Egypt, Nubia and Palestine.

George Reisner
Born: 5 Nov 1867, Indianapolis, Indiana
Died: 6 June 1942, Giza, Egypt
Fields: Egyptology, archaeology
Institutions: Harvard University, Museum of Fine Arts, Boston

Career

Reisner studied at Harvard and Berlin, then directed excavations in Egypt for Harvard and the Boston Museum of Fine Arts for over 40 years. He excavated at Naga ed-Deir, Deir el-Ballas, Kerma and Meroë in Nubia, and extensively at Giza.

Discoveries

At Giza his team found the tomb of Queen Hetepheres I (1925), with its famous furniture, and the valley temple of Menkaure with its triad statues. In Nubia he reconstructed the sequence of Kerma culture and Kushite kings, and in 1916–1923 excavated the Napatan pyramids. Reisner pioneered meticulous archaeological recording — detailed notes, photographs and card indexes — setting professional standards for Egyptian excavation. He developed the method of seriation and classified Nubian cultures into A-, B-, C-Groups and X-Group.

Later life

Despite failing eyesight, Reisner worked at his Giza camp until his death there in 1942. His archives remain a primary resource for Giza research.
