Wilson Pickett Jr. (March 18, 1941 – January 19, 2006) was an American singer and songwriter. A major figure in the development of soul music, Pickett recorded over 50 songs that made the US R&B charts, many of them crossing over to the pop charts as well. His best-known hits include "In the Midnight Hour" (1965), "Mustang Sally" (1966), "Land of 1000 Dances" (1966), "634-5789 (Soulsville, U.S.A.)", and "Funky Broadway" (1967). Nicknamed "Wicked Pickett," he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1991.

Early life

Pickett was born in Prattville, Alabama, and sang in Baptist church choirs before moving to Detroit in 1955. There he joined the gospel group the Violinaires and then the Falcons, an early soul group whose 1962 single "I Found a Love" featured Pickett's lead vocal and gave him his first taste of chart success.

Solo career

Signing with Atlantic Records, Pickett travelled to Stax Records in Memphis in 1965, where he co-wrote and recorded "In the Midnight Hour" with guitarist Steve Cropper — one of the defining records of Southern soul. Further sessions at Fame Studios in Muscle Shoals, Alabama, produced "Mustang Sally" and "Funky Broadway." In the late 1960s he recorded in Philadelphia with Gamble and Huff, scoring hits such as "Engine No. 9" and "Don't Let the Green Grass Fool You."

Later years and legacy

Pickett's career declined in the 1970s and he struggled with legal and personal problems, but he continued to record and perform. He was featured in the 1998 film Blues Brothers 2000 and received renewed recognition late in life. He died of a heart attack in 2006. His raw, raspy vocal style remains a benchmark for soul singers, and his recordings are staples of the genre.
