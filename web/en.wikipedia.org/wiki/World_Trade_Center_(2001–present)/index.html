The World Trade Center in its present incarnation is the complex of buildings rebuilt in Lower Manhattan, New York City, on and around the site of the original complex destroyed in the September 11 attacks of 2001. Its centerpiece, One World Trade Center, is the tallest building in the Western Hemisphere at 1,776 feet (541 m), a height chosen to symbolize the year of American independence.

Background

After the attacks, debate over the future of the 16-acre site lasted years. A master plan by architect Daniel Libeskind was selected in 2003, framing the memorial footprints of the Twin Towers and a descending spiral of towers. The Port Authority retained ownership of the site, while developer Larry Silverstein held the lease on the office space.

Buildings

One World Trade Center (designed by David Childs of SOM) opened in November 2014. 7 World Trade Center was the first tower rebuilt, opening in 2006 on the site's northern edge. 4 World Trade Center opened in 2013 and 3 World Trade Center in 2018. Construction of 2 World Trade Center has proceeded in stages, and 5 World Trade Center is planned as a mixed-use tower.

Memorial and museum

The National September 11 Memorial & Museum occupies the western half of the site. The memorial, "Reflecting Absence" by Michael Arad and Peter Walker, consists of two acre-sized reflecting pools set in the footprints of the Twin Towers, with the names of the victims inscribed on bronze parapets. The museum opened in 2014.

Transportation hub

The World Trade Center Transportation Hub, designed by Santiago Calatrava and centered on the wing-like Oculus, opened in 2016, serving PATH trains and multiple New York City Subway lines.
