Namibia

Namibia, officially the Republic of Namibia, is a country in Southern Africa. Its western border is the Atlantic Ocean; it shares land borders with Zambia and Angola to the north, Botswana to the east and South Africa to the south and east. The capital and largest city is Windhoek.

History

Namibia was inhabited from prehistoric times by the San, Damara and Nama peoples, later joined by Bantu-speaking groups such as the Ovambo and Herero. The territory became a German colony, German South West Africa, in 1884. German forces committed the Herero and Nama genocide of 1904–1908. South Africa occupied the territory during the First World War and administered it under a League of Nations mandate, later refusing to relinquish control after the Second World War.

The South West Africa People's Organisation (SWAPO) led a long armed and diplomatic struggle against South African rule. Namibia finally gained independence on 21 March 1990, with Sam Nujoma as its first president.

Geography

Namibia is dominated by the Namib Desert along the Atlantic coast — among the oldest deserts on Earth — and the Kalahari Desert to the east. The central plateau rises between them. Despite its aridity, the country is known for wildlife areas such as Etosha National Park.

Economy and demographics

Mining (diamonds, uranium, gold), agriculture and tourism are major sectors. Namibia has one of the world's lowest population densities, with roughly three million inhabitants. English is the sole official language; Oshiwambo languages are the most widely spoken at home, and Afrikaans and German are also used.
