Hobart-class destroyer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hobart class is a class of three air warfare destroyers (AWDs) built for the Royal Australian Navy (RAN): HMAS Hobart (DDG 39), HMAS Brisbane (DDG 41), and HMAS Sydney (DDG 42). Based on the Spanish Navy's F100 Álvaro de Bazán-class frigate design by Navantia, they are Australia's most capable surface combatants.

Design and armament

Displacing about 7,000 tonnes at full load, the destroyers are built around the Aegis combat system with an AN/SPY-1D(V) phased-array radar, providing area air defence for a naval task group. Their 48-cell Mk 41 vertical launching system fires SM-2 and Evolved Sea Sparrow missiles, with provision for future SM-6 and Tomahawk integration. Other armament includes a 5-inch Mk 45 gun, Harpoon anti-ship missiles, Phalanx CIWS, lightweight torpedoes, and a hangar for an MH-60R Seahawk helicopter.

Construction and service

The ships were assembled at the ASC shipyard in Osborne, South Australia, from blocks fabricated in Australia, Spain, and the United Kingdom. The program ran over budget and behind schedule; Hobart commissioned in September 2017, Brisbane in October 2018, and Sydney — the last warship built at Osborne — in May 2020. The class replaced the retired Adelaide-class frigates in the air warfare role and regularly deploys to the Indo-Pacific and on exercises such as RIMPAC.
