Time on the Cross

Time on the Cross: The Economics of American Negro Slavery (1974) is a history book by Robert William Fogel and Stanley L. Engerman, two American economists. Applying the quantitative methods of the "new economic history" (cliometrics) to the economics of slavery in the United States, the book argued that slavery had been profitable, that slave agriculture was efficient, and that the material conditions of enslaved people compared better with those of free industrial workers than previously supposed.

Reception and controversy

The book won the Bancroft Prize but provoked intense controversy over its methods, data, and tone; critics charged that some calculations were flawed and that the framing minimised slavery's brutality. A companion volume, Evidence and Methods, presented the underlying data. The ensuing debate, including the critical volume Reckoning with Slavery (1976), reshaped scholarship on slavery, and Fogel revisited the subject in Without Consent or Contract (1989).
