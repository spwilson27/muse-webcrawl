DevOps

DevOps is a set of practices, cultural philosophies and tools that aim to shorten the software development life cycle and provide continuous delivery with high software quality, by unifying software development (Dev) and IT operations (Ops). The term emerged from the agile and lean movements in the late 2000s, notably the 2009 "DevOpsDays" conference in Ghent organised by Patrick Debois.

Practices

  • Continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD) — automatically building, testing and deploying code changes.
  • Infrastructure as code — managing servers and networks through machine-readable configuration.
  • Microservices and containers — small, independently deployable components.
  • Monitoring and observability — metrics, logging and tracing of production systems.
  • Collaboration and blameless postmortems — shared responsibility for reliability between dev and ops teams.

Adoption and evidence

The annual DORA State of DevOps reports measure performance by deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate and time to restore service, finding that high-performing teams deploy far more often with fewer failures. Influential books include The Phoenix Project (2013) and The DevOps Handbook (2016).

Criticism

Critics note that "DevOps engineer" job titles can recreate the silos the movement opposed, and that the term is sometimes used as marketing for tooling rather than cultural change.
