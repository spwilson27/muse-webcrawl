Maastricht

Maastricht is a city and municipality in the southeastern Netherlands, the capital of the province of Limburg. Situated on both banks of the Meuse (Dutch: Maas) river, close to the borders with Belgium and Germany, it is one of the oldest cities in the Netherlands, with a population of about 120,000.

History

The city grew around a Roman river crossing (Mosa Trajectum) and a bridge on the road between Cologne and the coast. In the Middle Ages it was a fortified pilgrimage and trading centre, long governed jointly by the Duke of Brabant and the Prince-Bishop of Liège. Maastricht was besieged many times, including in 1673, when the musketeer Charles de Batz de Castelmore, known as d'Artagnan, was killed outside its walls.

The Maastricht Treaty

The city is internationally known for the Maastricht Treaty, signed on 7 February 1992, which formally established the European Union and set out the path to the euro. Maastricht is also home to Maastricht University, the Bonnefanten Museum, and an extensive network of underground marl caves and fortifications, including the casemates used by residents during the Second World War.
