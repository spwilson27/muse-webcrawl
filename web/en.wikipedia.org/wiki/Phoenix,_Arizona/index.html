Phoenix (Navajo: Hoozdo; O'odham: S-ki:ki:gk) is the capital and most populous city of the US state of Arizona, and the fifth-most populous city in the United States. It anchors a metropolitan area known as the Valley of the Sun, part of the Salt River Valley in the Sonoran Desert.

History

The valley was inhabited for centuries by the Hohokam people, who built an extensive canal system before abandoning the area around the 15th century. The modern city was founded in 1867 by Jack Swilling, who saw farming potential in the valley and began rebuilding the ancient canals; Lord Darrell Duppa suggested the name Phoenix, for a city rising from the ruins of a former civilisation. Phoenix was incorporated in 1881, became the territorial capital in 1889, and grew with irrigation, railroads and, after statehood in 1912, federal water projects such as Roosevelt Dam.

Geography and climate

Phoenix lies in south-central Arizona at an elevation of about 1,086 feet, surrounded by mountains including Camelback Mountain and South Mountain Park, one of the largest municipal parks in the United States. It has a hot desert climate with extremely hot summers — average July highs exceed 106 °F (41 °C) — and mild winters, receiving roughly 8 inches of rain annually, some of it in summer monsoon storms.

Economy and culture

Once based on the "five Cs" (copper, cattle, cotton, citrus and climate), the economy now centres on technology, finance, real estate, healthcare and tourism. The city is home to Arizona State University's downtown campus, the Phoenix Art Museum, the Heard Museum, and professional sports franchises including the Suns (NBA), Cardinals (NFL), Diamondbacks (MLB) and Coyotes (formerly NHL). Sky Harbor International Airport is a major hub.
