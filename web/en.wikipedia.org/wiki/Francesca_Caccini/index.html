Francesca Caccini

Francesca Caccini (18 September 1587 – after 1641) was an Italian composer, singer, lutenist, poet and music teacher of the early Baroque era. The daughter of the composer Giulio Caccini, she was the most renowned female musician of her time at the Medici court in Florence, and her La liberazione di Ruggiero dall'isola d'Alcina (1625) is regarded as the earliest surviving opera composed by a woman.

Life

Caccini was trained by her father and first performed publicly as a singer in the festivities for the wedding of Maria de' Medici and Henry IV of France in 1600, in which her father's music, including the opera Euridice, was performed. She entered Medici service as a musician and teacher and by the 1610s was among the highest-paid musicians at the court, admired by visitors including Monteverdi's contemporaries for her singing and playing.

Works

Her only surviving published collection is Il primo libro delle musiche (1618), a large set of songs and arias for solo voice and continuo. She composed music for numerous court entertainments and at least five stage works, most now lost. La liberazione di Ruggiero, written for the visit of Prince Władysław of Poland in 1625, survives complete and is still performed.

Legacy

Long overlooked, Caccini's music was revived in the late twentieth century, and she is now recognised as a significant figure of the early Baroque and of the Florentine invention of opera.
