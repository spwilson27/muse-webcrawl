Eczema is a group of skin conditions, also called dermatitis, that cause inflammation of the skin characterised by itchiness, redness, dryness and rash. The most common type is atopic dermatitis (atopic eczema). The word derives from the Greek ekzema, "to boil over", describing the appearance of acute lesions.

Types

  • Atopic dermatitis — the most common form, often beginning in childhood and associated with asthma and hay fever (the "atopic triad"). Linked to genetic defects in the skin barrier, including filaggrin gene mutations.
  • Contact dermatitis — caused by irritants (soaps, detergents) or allergens (nickel, fragrances, poison ivy) touching the skin.
  • Seborrhoeic dermatitis — scaly patches on the scalp (dandruff), face and oily areas, associated with Malassezia yeasts.
  • Dyshidrotic eczema — small, intensely itchy blisters on the palms, fingers and soles.
  • Nummular (discoid) eczema — coin-shaped patches of irritated skin.
  • Stasis dermatitis — on the lower legs, related to poor venous circulation.

Causes and mechanism

Eczema results from an interaction of genetics, skin-barrier dysfunction, immune dysregulation and environmental triggers. A defective barrier lets water escape and irritants and microbes enter; the immune system overreacts, producing inflammation and itch, and scratching further damages the barrier — the "itch–scratch cycle". Staphylococcus aureus commonly colonises eczematous skin and can drive flares.

Management

There is no cure, but symptoms can usually be controlled. Cornerstones are daily moisturisers (emollients), avoidance of triggers, and topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors for flares. Antihistamines may help sleep. For moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, options include phototherapy, systemic immunosuppressants, and biologic drugs such as dupilumab, which blocks interleukin-4 and interleukin-13 signalling. Wet-wrap therapy and bleach baths are sometimes used. Eczema affects roughly 10–20 percent of children and 1–3 percent of adults in many developed countries, and its prevalence has risen over recent decades.
