Rummy

TypeMatching / melding card game
Players2–6
Origin19th century, with roots in Conquian and mahjong-like games
FamilyGin rummy, canasta, rummikub and many others

Rummy is a family of card games characterised by matching cards of the same rank or sequence and by the goal of forming sets and runs — known as melds — and being the first to "go out" or to accumulate the most points. It is one of the most popular card game families in the world.

Basic play

In most variants, players draw a card each turn (from the stock or discard pile) and discard one, melding sets of three or four cards of a rank or sequences of three or more in a suit. A round ends when a player melds or lays down all but one card and discards the last, or when the stock is exhausted. Remaining cards in opponents' hands count as penalty points.

Variants and origins

Rummy is thought to descend from the Mexican game Conquian, itself influenced by earlier matching games, and to share ancestry with mahjong. Famous variants include gin rummy (developed in New York in 1909), canasta, contract rummy, Indian rummy (13 cards) and the tile game Rummikub. In India the Supreme Court has ruled rummy a game of skill, shaping its legal status there.
