Robert James Shiller (born 29 March 1946) is an American economist, Sterling Professor of Economics at Yale University, and a leading figure in behavioural finance. He shared the 2013 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Eugene Fama and Lars Peter Hansen "for their empirical analysis of asset prices". Shiller is known for his work on excess volatility of asset prices, housing bubbles, and the role of popular narratives in the economy.

Work

In 1981 Shiller showed that stock prices fluctuate far more than can be explained by subsequent changes in dividends — the "excess volatility" puzzle challenging strong forms of the efficient-market hypothesis. His bestselling book Irrational Exuberance (2000; expanded 2005 and 2015) warned first of the dot-com bubble and then of the housing bubble. With Karl Case, he co-created the S&P CoreLogic Case-Shiller Home Price Indices (based on repeat-sales methodology) and the cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio (CAPE, "Shiller P/E"). With George Akerlof he wrote Animal Spirits (2009) and Phishing for Phools (2015); his Narrative Economics (2019) studies how viral stories drive economic behaviour.

Views

Shiller advocates expanded risk-sharing institutions — home-equity insurance, livelihood insurance, and continuous-workout mortgages — developed in The New Financial Order (2003) and through his firm MacroMarkets LLC.
