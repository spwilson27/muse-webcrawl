The Arktikum is a museum and science centre in Rovaniemi, Finland, on the bank of the river Ounasjoki. Opened on 6 December 1992, the 75th anniversary of Finnish independence, it houses the Arctic Centre of the University of Lapland and the Regional Museum of Lapland, and presents the nature, cultures, and history of the Arctic and Finnish Lapland.

Building

The building, designed by the Danish architectural firm Birch-Bonderup & Thorup-Waade, is famous for its long glass corridor — 172 metres of vaulted glass tube pointing north toward the Arctic Circle, which lies about 8 km away. The crescent-shaped main structure is largely sunk into the ground, recalling the shelters animals build against the cold; only the glass roof rises above the surface. Exhibitions are arranged along and below this corridor, including a dedicated theatre for films of the northern lights.

Exhibitions

The Arctic Centre's permanent exhibition examines Arctic ecosystems, climate change, and the lives of northern peoples, while the Regional Museum of Lapland covers the history of Rovaniemi and Peräpohjola, including the destruction of the town in the Lapland War and its rebuilding. Temporary exhibitions address contemporary Arctic research and Indigenous cultures of the circumpolar North.
