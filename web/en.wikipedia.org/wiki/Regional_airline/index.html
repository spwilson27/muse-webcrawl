Regional airline

A regional airline is a general classification of airline which typically operates scheduled passenger air service, using regional aircraft, between smaller communities or from a large hub or focal point airport, or to connect larger population centers. The regional airline concept is typical of North America and much of Europe and Australia.

Overview

Regional airlines typically operate aircraft with fewer than 100 seats, including turboprops such as the ATR 72 and regional jets such as the Embraer E-Jet and Bombardier CRJ families. In the United States, many regional airlines operate under capacity purchase agreements with major legacy carriers, flying under brands such as American Eagle, United Express, and Delta Connection, with the major airline controlling scheduling, pricing, and revenue while the regional carrier supplies aircraft, crew, and maintenance for a fixed fee.

History

The regional airline sector grew substantially after the Airline Deregulation Act of 1978, which allowed major carriers to withdraw from thinner markets and replace mainline service with contracted commuter flights. Aircraft seating grew from small 19-seat turboprops to 50-seat regional jets in the 1990s and larger 70- to 76-seat jets permitted under "scope clause" limits in mainline pilot contracts.
