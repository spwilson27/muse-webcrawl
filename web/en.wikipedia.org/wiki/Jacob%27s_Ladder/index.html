Jacob's Ladder

Jacob's Ladder (Hebrew: סֻלָּם יַעֲקֹב, Sulam Yaakov) is a ladder or staircase leading to heaven that appears in the biblical Book of Genesis (28:10–19), in a dream experienced by the patriarch Jacob while fleeing from his brother Esau on the way to Harran.

The narrative

Resting for the night with a stone for a pillow, Jacob dreams of a ladder set on the earth with its top reaching to heaven, with angels of God ascending and descending on it. God stands above it and renews the covenant promises made to Abraham: land, numerous descendants, and blessing to all families of the earth. Jacob awakes awestruck, names the place Bethel ("house of God"), and sets up the stone as a pillar.

Interpretation

Jewish and Christian commentators have read the ladder in many ways: as a symbol of the link between heaven and earth, of prayer, of the exile and return of Israel, and, in Christian exegesis — following John 1:51 — as a type of Christ. The image has given its name to plants, toys, electrical discharges, films, and the American folk hymn "We Are Climbing Jacob's Ladder."
