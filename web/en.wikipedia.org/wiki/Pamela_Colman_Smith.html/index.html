Pamela Colman Smith (16 February 1878 – 18 September 1951), also nicknamed Pixie, was a British artist, illustrator, writer, publisher, and occultist. She is best known as the artist who, in 1909, painted the images for the Rider–Waite tarot deck commissioned by Arthur Edward Waite — the most popular tarot deck in the world, whose fully illustrated Minor Arcana scenes were revolutionary for tarot design.

Life

Born in London to American parents, Smith spent parts of her childhood in Jamaica, studied at the Pratt Institute in Brooklyn under Arthur Wesley Dow, and became a member of the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn in 1901, where she met Waite. She illustrated books by W. B. Yeats, Ellen Terry, and Bram Stoker, produced the Jamaican folklore collections Annancy Stories (1899), and ran a small press and the magazine The Green Sheaf.

Legacy

Despite the deck's immense success, Smith received little recognition or money during her lifetime, and she died in relative obscurity in Cornwall in 1951. Modern editions increasingly credit the deck as the "Rider–Waite–Smith" or simply "Smith–Waite" tarot in acknowledgment of her work.
