Median (geometry)


The three medians of a triangle intersect at the centroid

In geometry, a median of a triangle is a line segment joining a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side, thus bisecting that side. Every triangle has exactly three medians, one from each vertex, and they all intersect each other at the triangle's centroid. In the case of isosceles and equilateral triangles, a median also bisects the angle at the vertex whose two adjacent sides are equal in length.

Properties

The three medians divide the triangle into six smaller triangles of equal area. Each median divides the triangle into two regions of equal area. The centroid divides each median in a 2:1 ratio, the longer part being between the vertex and the centroid. The length of a median can be computed from the side lengths by Apollonius's theorem: for sides a, b, c and median ma to side a, 2b2 + 2c2 − a2 = 4ma2.

Relation to other centres

The centroid is the centre of mass of a uniform triangular plate. The medians are concurrent with the triangle's other notable points — the orthocentre, circumcentre, and incentre — lying on the Euler line in a fixed relationship, with the centroid between the orthocentre and circumcentre.

Generalisation

The concept extends to tetrahedra, where a median is a segment joining a vertex to the centroid of the opposite face; the four medians of a tetrahedron are likewise concurrent.
