Mississippi Blues Trail

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Mississippi Blues Trail is a series of historical markers placed across the US state of Mississippi — and in selected locations beyond it — commemorating the history of blues music and the people and places that shaped it. Established by the Mississippi Blues Commission in 2006, the trail now comprises more than 200 markers.

The markers

Each blue cast-aluminium marker tells the story of a site, musician, record label or event. Locations include the Dockery Plantation, where Charley Patton lived and learned; the crossroads near Clarksdale associated with the Robert Johnson legend; the graves and birthplaces of Muddy Waters, Howlin' Wolf, B.B. King (in Berclair, with a trail marker in Indianola, home of the B.B. King Museum), and Son House; juke joints such as Po' Monkey's; and the studios of Trumpet Records in Jackson. Markers outside Mississippi honour Mississippians' influence in Memphis, Chicago and other centres of the Great Migration, and a few stand overseas, including in Norway and France.

Purpose

The trail is a heritage-tourism initiative intended to draw visitors to small towns across the Delta and hill country while preserving blues history. It is complemented by the Mississippi Blues Trail curriculum and app, and by institutions such as the Delta Blues Museum in Clarksdale. The first markers were unveiled in December 2006.
