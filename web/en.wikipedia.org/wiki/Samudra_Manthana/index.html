Samudra Manthana

The Samudra Manthana (Sanskrit: समुद्र मन्थन, lit. "churning of the ocean") is a prominent episode in Hindu mythology, described in the Bhagavata Purana, the Mahabharata, and the Vishnu Purana. The churning of the Ocean of Milk was performed by the devas (gods) and the asuras (demons) in order to obtain amrita, the nectar of immortality. The episode forms the mythical origin of the Kumbh Mela festival.

Legend

Indra, the king of the devas, was cursed by the sage Durvasa so that he and the other devas lost their strength and fortune. Seeking to regain immortality, the devas approached Vishnu, who advised them to churn the Ocean of Milk using Mount Mandara as the churning rod and the serpent Vasuki as the rope, and to promise the asuras a share of the nectar in exchange for their help. Vishnu himself took the form of the tortoise Kurma to support the mountain on his back so that it would not sink.

Treasures produced

Many divine treasures emerged from the churning, including:

  • Halahala, a terrible poison, which Shiva drank to protect the world, turning his throat blue;
  • Kamadhenu, the wish-granting cow;
  • Uchchaihshravas, the seven-headed flying horse;
  • Airavata, the elephant of Indra;
  • Kaustubha, the jewel worn by Vishnu;
  • Lakshmi, the goddess of fortune, who chose Vishnu as her consort;
  • Chandra, the moon;
  • Dhanvantari, the divine physician, bearing the pot of amrita.

Theft of the amrita

When the nectar finally appeared, the asuras seized it. Vishnu took the form of the enchantress Mohini to distract them and distribute the nectar to the devas. The asura Svarbhanu disguised himself as a deva and drank some nectar; Surya and Chandra alerted Vishnu, who severed the asura's head — the immortal head became Rahu and the body Ketu. Drops of amrita were said to have fallen at Prayagraj, Haridwar, Nashik, and Ujjain, the sites of the Kumbh Mela.
