Claude Nobs

Claude Nobs (4 February 1936 – 10 January 2013) was a Swiss concert promoter and the founder of the Montreux Jazz Festival, one of the world's best-known music festivals.

Life and career

Nobs was born in Territet, near Montreux, and trained as a chef before joining the Montreux tourist office. A jazz enthusiast, he travelled to New York to persuade Atlantic Records' Nesuhi and Ahmet Ertegun to back a festival in his home town; the first Montreux Jazz Festival was held in 1967. Nobs's warmth, energy and contacts drew virtually every major figure in jazz, blues, rock and soul to the small Swiss resort over the following decades, and he built the festival into a two-week event of global importance.

He is immortalised in Deep Purple's song "Smoke on the Water", which recounts the 1971 fire at the Montreux Casino during a Frank Zappa concert: "Funky Claude was running in and out, pulling kids out the ground." Nobs, who had organised the show, helped rescue audience members from the blaze.

Nobs died on 10 January 2013, aged 76, after falling into a coma following a cross-country skiing accident near his home at Caux on Christmas Eve 2012.
