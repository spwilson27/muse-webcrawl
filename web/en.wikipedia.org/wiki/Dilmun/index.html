Dilmun

Dilmun, or Telmun, was an ancient East Semitic-speaking civilisation in the Persian Gulf region, centred on the island of Bahrain and including parts of the eastern Arabian Peninsula and Failaka Island. Mentioned in Mesopotamian texts from the late third millennium BC onward, it was an important entrepôt in trade between Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley (Meluhha).

History and trade

Dilmun appears in Sumerian and Akkadian sources as a source and transshipment point for copper (originally from Magan), timber, ivory, carnelian and pearls. Its merchants used a distinctive system of weights and round stamp seals. At its height in the early second millennium BC, Dilmun controlled Gulf trade routes; it later came under Kassite, Assyrian and Babylonian influence and declined in the first millennium BC.

Mythology

In Sumerian myth, Dilmun was described as a pure, bright paradise land — "the place where the sun rises" — free of sickness and death, and associated with the god Enki and the fresh waters of the Apsu. The Epic of Gilgamesh locates the immortal flood survivor Utnapishtim in Dilmun, which has also been proposed as an inspiration for the Garden of Eden.

Archaeology

Major sites include Qal'at al-Bahrain, the Barbar Temple and the vast Dilmun burial mound fields, among the largest ancient cemeteries in the world.
