Gashapon

Gashapon (ガシャポン), also called gachapon, is a trademark of Bandai and a variety of vending-machine-dispensed capsule toys that originated in Japan. The name is onomatopoeic: gasha (or gacha) for the sound of the crank handle turning, and pon for the sound of the capsule dropping into the receptacle. Unlike the low-cost bulk toys of Western capsule machines, gashapon toys are typically highly detailed, licensed, and aimed at collectors as much as children.

History

Capsule vending machines reached Japan from the United States in the 1960s, with the Penetrate Company importing them in 1965. Bandai entered the market in 1977 and coined the Gashapon brand, using the hollow plastic capsule as packaging and introducing licensed character goods, notably Kinnikuman eraser figures (kinkeshi) in the 1980s and elaborate Gundam model series later. Capsules generally cost ¥100–¥500 per turn.

Products and culture

Gashapon sets are released in series — figurines, keychains, miniature replicas, and realistic animal or food models — encouraging repeat purchases to complete a set, with rare "secret" items adding scarcity. Because customers cannot choose the item, a large secondary market and trading culture exists. Since the 2010s, adult-oriented and novelty lines (miniature furniture, office items, animals in poses) have expanded the market, and dedicated gashapon shops with hundreds of machines operate across Japan and increasingly abroad. The randomized-reward mechanic lent its name to "gacha games," free-to-play mobile games with similar random draws.
