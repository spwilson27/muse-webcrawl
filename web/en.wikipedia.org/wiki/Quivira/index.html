Quivira

Quivira is a place named by explorer Francisco Vásquez de Coronado, who visited it in 1541 in search of the fabled wealthy cities of gold. The inhabitants of Quivira were the ancestors of the Wichita people, and their settlements were located in present-day central Kansas near the great bend of the Arkansas River, around modern Lyons and Great Bend.

Coronado travelled from New Mexico across the plains to Quivira in 1541.

Coronado's expedition

Coronado was guided onto the Great Plains by an Indigenous captive the Spanish called "the Turk", who told of Quivira's wealth in hopes of leading the expedition astray. After crossing the plains of Texas and Oklahoma, Coronado reached Quivira with a small party and found not gold but a well-organised farming society living in grass-thatched houses, growing maize, beans, and squash. The Turk confessed to the deception and was executed. Coronado returned to New Mexico empty-handed, and the episode became one of the famous disappointments of Spanish exploration in North America.

Location and archaeology

Archaeological sites in Rice County, Kansas — including the Tobias site and the Sharps Creek focus — are identified with the Quivira villages, where artefacts of chain mail and other Spanish goods confirm the contact. The myth of Quivira later migrated on European maps across the continent and was variously placed in the Great Plains or near the Pacific coast.
