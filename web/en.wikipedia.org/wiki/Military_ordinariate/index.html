Military ordinariate

A military ordinariate is a Latin Church ecclesiastical jurisdiction of the Catholic Church, of the nature of a personal diocese, that provides pastoral care to Catholics serving in the armed forces of a nation and to their families. It is headed by a military ordinary, usually a bishop.

Organisation

Military ordinariates were regularised by Pope John Paul II's apostolic constitution Spirituali militum curae (1986), which gave them a status comparable to dioceses. Chaplains commissioned in the armed forces exercise ministry under the ordinary's authority, often in cooperation with chaplains of other denominations. Examples include the Archdiocese for the Military Services of the United States and the Bishopric of the Forces in Britain.

History

Formal military chaplaincy structures developed in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries as mass armies required organised religious care beyond parish boundaries.
