Cranium

The cranium (from Greek kranion, "skull") is the part of the skull that encloses and protects the brain, excluding the facial bones and the mandible. Together, the cranium and the facial skeleton make up the skull. In human anatomy the cranium is also called the neurocranium, in contrast to the facial skeleton, the viscerocranium.

Structure

The human cranium consists of eight bones: the frontal bone, two parietal bones, two temporal bones, the occipital bone, the sphenoid bone and the ethmoid bone. These are joined by fibrous joints called sutures — the coronal, sagittal, lambdoid and squamosal sutures — which in infancy remain open (with soft spots called fontanelles) to allow the skull to expand with the growing brain, fusing progressively during adult life.

The cranium is divided into the domed cranial vault (calvaria), made of flat bones formed by intramembranous ossification, and the cranial base, formed largely by endochondral ossification and pierced by foramina for nerves and blood vessels. Its interior is divided into anterior, middle and posterior cranial fossae, which support the lobes of the brain and the cerebellum and brainstem.

Function

The cranium protects the brain from injury, supports the sense organs, and anchors the muscles of mastication and facial expression. A cavity within it, the cranial cavity, is lined by the meninges and contains the brain and cerebrospinal fluid.
