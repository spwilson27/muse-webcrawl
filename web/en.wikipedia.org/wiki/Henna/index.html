Henna

Henna is a reddish-brown dye prepared from the plant Lawsonia inermis, also known as the henna tree. The dye, used since antiquity in South Asia, the Middle East, and North Africa, stains skin, hair, fingernails, and fabrics. The active staining molecule is lawsone (2-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone), released when the powdered leaves are mixed into a paste with a mildly acidic liquid.

Body art

Temporary body art made with henna — called mehndi in South Asia — is applied in intricate patterns for weddings and festivals such as Eid and Diwali. The paste is drawn onto the skin, left to dry for several hours, and then removed, leaving a stain that deepens to a rich brown and fades over one to three weeks as the skin exfoliates. So-called "black henna" products may contain para-phenylenediamine (PPD), which can cause serious allergic reactions and is unsafe on skin.

Other uses

Henna has long been used as a hair dye and conditioner, for dyeing wool, silk, and leather, and in traditional medicine. The plant is a flowering shrub native to tropical and subtropical regions of Africa and Asia.
