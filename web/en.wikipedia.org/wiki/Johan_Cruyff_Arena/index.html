Johan Cruyff Arena

The Johan Cruyff Arena (Dutch: Johan Cruijff ArenA), stylised as Johan Cruijff ArenA, is a football stadium in Amsterdam, Netherlands, and the home ground of AFC Ajax and the Netherlands national football team. Opened in 1996 as the Amsterdam Arena, it was renamed in 2018 in honour of the Dutch footballer Johan Cruyff. With a capacity of around 55,000, it is the largest stadium in the Netherlands.

The arena, with its retractable roof, from the south.

History

The stadium was built as part of Amsterdam's bid for the 1992 Summer Olympics and to replace Ajax's De Meer Stadion. It was the first football stadium in Europe with a retractable roof and famously had a motorway passing beneath the pitch. Early problems with the grass — repeatedly replaced because the closed roof limited sunlight — were eventually solved with grow-light technology. The stadium hosted the 1998 UEFA Champions League Final and the 2013 UEFA Europa League Final, and was a venue for UEFA Euro 2020.

Events

Beyond football, the arena is a major concert venue, hosting acts such as Michael Jackson, the Rolling Stones and Coldplay. It also stages the annual Amsterdam Music Festival and other events. A museum and stadium tours cover Ajax's history and Cruyff's career.

The name

Following Cruyff's death in March 2016, a campaign to rename the stadium gained support from the city of Amsterdam, Ajax and the Cruyff family; the change was formally announced in April 2018, with the new name taking effect for the 2018–19 season.
