The Holocaust in Slovakia was the systematic persecution and murder of Jews in the Slovak Republic, a client state of Nazi Germany ruled by Jozef Tiso's Slovak People's Party. Slovakia was unusual among Axis states in that its own government organised and paid for the deportation of its Jewish citizens.

Deportations of 1942

Following the anti-Jewish "Jewish Code" of September 1941, among the harshest such laws in Europe, Slovakia agreed in early 1942 to deport its Jews for "resettlement" in German-occupied Poland, paying Germany 500 Reichsmarks per deportee ostensibly for their upkeep and training — in fact, a fee for their murder. Between March and October 1942, 57 transports carried some 58,000 Jews to Auschwitz, Majdanek and the Lublin district, where nearly all were killed. The first transport of young women from Poprad on 25 March 1942 was the first mass transport of Jews to Auschwitz. Deportations halted in late 1942 amid Vatican protests, reports of the deportees' fate, and pressure from Slovak bishops and the underground "Working Group" of Gisi Fleischmann and Rabbi Michael Dov Weissmandl, which bribed officials and later attempted the Europa Plan negotiations.

1944–1945 and aftermath

After the Slovak National Uprising of August 1944, German forces occupied the country and, with Hlinka Guard collaboration, deported a further 13,500 Jews to Auschwitz, Sachsenhausen and Theresienstadt, and murdered others in Slovakia itself, including at Kremnička and Nemecká. In total roughly 68,000–71,000 of Slovakia's approximately 89,000 Jews were murdered. Tiso was executed for treason in 1947. Post-communist Slovakia has gradually confronted this history; the Museum of the Holocaust in Sereď occupies the site of a former labour and concentration camp.
