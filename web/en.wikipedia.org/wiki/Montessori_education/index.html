Montessori education

The Montessori method of education is a type of educational method that involves children's natural interests and activities rather than formal teaching methods. It was developed by Italian physician Maria Montessori, who opened her first Casa dei Bambini ("Children's House") in Rome in 1907.

Principles

Montessori emphasized that children learn best in a prepared environment designed for self-directed activity, hands-on learning, and collaborative play. Key features include mixed-age classrooms, specially designed didactic materials (such as the pink tower, sandpaper letters, and bead chains), long uninterrupted blocks of work time, and teachers who act as guides rather than lecturers. Montessori described developmental stages she called "planes of development" and periods of heightened sensitivity to particular learning, the "sensitive periods."

Spread and research

The method spread internationally in the early twentieth century, declined in some countries mid-century, and revived strongly from the 1960s. Today thousands of schools worldwide use the Montessori name — which is not trademarked — with varying fidelity. Research on outcomes is mixed but studies have found associations with gains in executive function, social skills, and academic achievement, particularly for children in high-fidelity programs.
