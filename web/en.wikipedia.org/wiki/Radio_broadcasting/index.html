Radio broadcasting is the broadcasting of audio (sound), and sometimes related metadata, by radio waves to radio receivers belonging to a public audience. In terrestrial radio broadcasting the radio waves are transmitted by a land-based radio station, while in satellite radio the radio waves are transmitted by a satellite in Earth orbit. To receive the content the listener must have a broadcast radio receiver.

Stations are often affiliated with a radio network that provides content in a common format, either in broadcast syndication or simulcast, or both. Radio stations broadcast with several different types of modulation: AM radio stations transmit in AM (amplitude modulation), FM radio stations transmit in FM (frequency modulation), and digital systems such as DAB and HD Radio have been introduced in many countries.

History

The first voice and music signals heard over radio were transmitted in experiments by Reginald Fessenden, including a notable broadcast on Christmas Eve 1906. Regular commercial broadcasting began around 1920; station KDKA in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, is often credited with the first licensed commercial broadcast in the United States, reporting the Harding–Cox election results on November 2, 1920. In the United Kingdom the BBC began regular broadcasts in 1922.

During the 1930s and 1940s — the "Golden Age of Radio" — radio was the dominant home entertainment medium, carrying drama, comedy, news, and music. Television displaced radio as the main entertainment medium in the 1950s, and radio programming shifted toward music formats, talk, news, and sports.

Types of radio broadcasting

Broadcasting is divided broadly into public service broadcasting, funded by licence fees or government and typified by the BBC and NPR, and commercial broadcasting, funded by advertising. Community radio serves local areas with volunteer participation. Pirate radio operates without a licence. Internet radio and podcasting have extended audio broadcasting beyond the radio spectrum.
