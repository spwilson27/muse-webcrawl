Théoden

Théoden is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's novel The Lord of the Rings. The seventeenth King of Rohan, and lord of the Rohirrim, he appears in The Two Towers and The Return of the King. His name derives from the Old English word þēoden, meaning "lord" or "king".

In the novel

Théoden, son of Thengel, became king in T.A. 2980. By the time of the War of the Ring he had fallen under the influence of his chief adviser Gríma Wormtongue, an agent of the wizard Saruman, and had sunk into a premature dotage. The wizard Gandalf healed him, casting out Gríma's influence; Théoden then took up his sword Herugrim and led his people to the relief of the Hornburg in the Battle of Helm's Deep.

In The Return of the King, Théoden led the Rohirrim to the Battle of the Pelennor Fields, where he defeated the forces of Harad but was mortally wounded when his horse Snowmane, panicked by the Witch-king of Angmar's fell beast, fell upon him. He died in the arms of his nephew's squire Meriadoc Brandybuck, believing his niece Éowyn dead; she in fact survived, having slain the Witch-king. Théoden was succeeded by his nephew Éomer.

Portrayal in adaptations

In Peter Jackson's film trilogy, Théoden is played by Bernard Hill. The films emphasize his restoration by Gandalf and his rousing speech before the charge at the Pelennor Fields.
