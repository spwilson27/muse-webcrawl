Marketing strategy

A marketing strategy is an organisation's long-term plan for reaching prospective consumers and turning them into customers of its products or services. It identifies target markets, the value proposition the organisation will offer those markets, and the overall approach by which it will create and sustain competitive advantage. A comprehensive marketing strategy underpins the shorter-term marketing plan and its tactical elements, such as the marketing mix.

Components

Marketing strategy typically begins with analysis: of the market, customers, competitors, and the firm's own capabilities, often summarised in a SWOT analysis. From this the firm sets marketing goals and objectives, selects which market segments to target, and decides how to position its offering in customers' minds relative to rivals. Segmentation, targeting and positioning (STP) is the classic sequence in developing the strategy.

Strategic frameworks

Common frameworks used in marketing strategy include the Ansoff Matrix (market penetration, product development, market development, diversification) for growth choices; Porter's generic strategies (cost leadership, differentiation, focus) for competitive positioning; and the marketing mix — product, price, place and promotion (the "4 Ps") — for translating strategy into tactics. At corporate level, portfolio tools such as the BCG growth-share matrix guide resource allocation among products and business units.

Levels

Strategy operates at several levels: corporate strategy determines the markets in which the firm competes; business-unit strategy determines how it competes in each; and functional marketing strategy plans individual elements such as branding, pricing and communication. Digital marketing has added channels and data-driven methods — search, social media, content marketing and marketing automation — that increasingly integrate with traditional strategy.

Criticism and evolution

Critics argue that formal marketing planning can be too rigid for fast-moving markets, and approaches such as effectuation and lean marketing emphasise experimentation. Nevertheless, research generally associates a clear market orientation and strategic plan with stronger business performance.
