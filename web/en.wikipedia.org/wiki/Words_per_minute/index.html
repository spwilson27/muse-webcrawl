Words per minute

Words per minute (commonly abbreviated WPM) is a measure of the speed of reading, typing, handwriting, speech and Morse code transmission. Because word lengths vary, the unit is standardised differently in each field: in typing, one "word" is conventionally five characters including spaces.

Typing

Average computer typing speeds are around 40 WPM, while professional typists commonly reach 65–75 WPM or more. Speed-typing competitions and records exceed 150 WPM on standard keyboards, and higher still on stenotype machines, where court reporters are trained to capture speech at over 200 WPM using chords of keys.

Reading and speech

Adult silent reading of English prose averages roughly 200–250 WPM with comprehension, while oral reading and ordinary conversation are slower, around 150–160 WPM. Speed-reading claims of 1,000 WPM and above are treated sceptically by researchers because comprehension declines sharply at such rates.

Morse code

In Morse telegraphy, speed is measured against standard words such as "PARIS": the time to send that word defines the rate, with skilled operators working at 20–40 WPM and contest operators higher.
