Neural stem cell

Neural stem cells (NSCs) are self-renewing, multipotent cells that generate the neurons, astrocytes and oligodendrocytes of the nervous system. During development they proliferate in the ventricular zone of the neural tube; in the adult mammalian brain they persist mainly in niches such as the subventricular zone of the lateral ventricles and the subgranular zone of the hippocampal dentate gyrus, where adult neurogenesis occurs.

Identification and culture

NSCs can be expanded in vitro as floating clusters called neurospheres in the presence of growth factors EGF and FGF-2, and express markers such as nestin and Sox2. They can also be derived from pluripotent stem cells or by direct reprogramming of somatic cells.

Research and therapy

Transplantation of neural stem and progenitor cells is under investigation for spinal cord injury, stroke, Parkinson's disease and other neurodegenerative conditions, though controlling differentiation, survival and integration, and avoiding tumour formation, remain major challenges.
