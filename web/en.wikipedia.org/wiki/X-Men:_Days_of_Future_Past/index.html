X-Men: Days of Future Past is a 2014 American superhero film directed by Bryan Singer and based on the Marvel Comics superhero team the X-Men. It is the seventh installment in the X-Men film series and adapts the classic 1981 comics storyline "Days of Future Past" by Chris Claremont and John Byrne. The film serves as both a sequel to X-Men: First Class (2011) and to X-Men: The Last Stand (2006), uniting the casts of the original trilogy and the prequel films.

Plot

In a dystopian future, mutantkind is hunted nearly to extinction by the Sentinels, adaptable robotic weapons developed from the DNA of the shapeshifter Mystique. The surviving X-Men send Wolverine's consciousness back in time to 1973 to prevent Mystique from assassinating Sentinel creator Bolivar Trask, the event that set the dark future in motion. In the past, Wolverine must reunite the estranged young Charles Xavier and Erik Lehnsherr, free Magneto from a Pentagon prison with the help of the super-fast mutant Quicksilver, and stop Mystique without destroying the hope that Xavier has lost.

Cast

Hugh Jackman stars as Logan / Wolverine, with James McAvoy and Patrick Stewart as the young and old Charles Xavier / Professor X, Michael Fassbender and Ian McKellen as Erik Lehnsherr / Magneto, Jennifer Lawrence as Raven Darkhölme / Mystique, Peter Dinklage as Bolivar Trask, and Evan Peters as Peter Maximoff / Quicksilver, whose slow-motion "Time in a Bottle" sequence became a standout of the film.

Reception

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing about $746 million worldwide and becoming the highest-grossing X-Men film at the time. It was praised for its story, acting, and visual effects, and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects. An extended alternate version, The Rogue Cut, was released on home media in 2015.
