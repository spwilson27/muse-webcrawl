Constitution of Georgia (country)

The Constitution of Georgia (Georgian: საქართველოს კონსტიტუცია) is the supreme law of Georgia. It was approved by the Parliament of Georgia on 24 August 1995 and entered into force on 17 October 1995, replacing the decrees by which the country had been governed since the restoration of independence from the Soviet Union in 1991.

History

The 1995 constitution restored elements of the Democratic Republic of Georgia's 1921 constitution, regarded as one of the most democratic documents of its era. Since 1995 the constitution has been amended many times — notably in 2004, when executive power was concentrated in the presidency; in 2010, when powers were shifted towards the prime minister and parliament; and in 2017–2018, when Georgia completed its transition to a fully parliamentary system of government.

Contents

The constitution defines Georgia as a democratic social and legal state, guarantees fundamental human rights and freedoms, establishes the separation of powers between the President (head of state), the Government headed by the Prime Minister, the unicameral Parliament, and the judiciary, including the Constitutional Court of Georgia.
