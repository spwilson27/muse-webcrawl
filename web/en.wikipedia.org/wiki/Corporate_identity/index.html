Corporate identity

A corporate identity or corporate image is the manner in which a corporation, firm, or business enterprise presents itself to the public, such as customers, investors, and employees. It is commonly expressed through branding and the use of trademarks, and encompasses the organization's visual identity, communications, behavior, and design.

Components

Corporate identity is conventionally understood to rest on three pillars:

  • Corporate design — the visual elements: logo, typography, color palette, stationery, signage, packaging, and uniforms.
  • Corporate communication — advertising, public relations, internal communications, and other messaging directed at stakeholders.
  • Corporate behavior — how the organization acts toward employees, customers, and society, including its values and social conduct.

History

Although businesses had long used marks and liveries, corporate identity as a systematic discipline emerged in the early 20th century. Peter Behrens's work for the German electrical company AEG beginning in 1907 is often cited as the first comprehensive corporate identity program, covering products, graphics, and architecture. After the Second World War, designers such as Paul Rand (IBM, Westinghouse, ABC) and consultancies like Lippincott and Wolff Olins professionalized the field.

Identity and image

Scholars distinguish identity — what the organization is and projects — from image — how audiences actually perceive it — and from reputation, the longer-term evaluation built from repeated experience. A gap between identity and image can signal communication problems or deeper organizational issues.

Brand architecture

Organizations manage multiple identities through different architectures: a monolithic identity, where one name and visual style is used throughout (e.g., Virgin); an endorsed identity, where subsidiaries keep their own identity but are visibly linked to a parent; and a branded identity, where products or subsidiaries appear independent, as with many Procter & Gamble brands.
