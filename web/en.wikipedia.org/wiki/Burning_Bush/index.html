The Burning Bush

The Burning Bush (or the Unburnt Bush) is the object described by the Book of Exodus as being located on Mount Horeb. According to the narrative, the bush was on fire but was not consumed by the flames, hence the name. In the biblical account, the burning bush is the location at which Moses was appointed by God to lead the Israelites out of Egypt and into Canaan.

Biblical narrative

Exodus 3 relates that Moses, tending the flock of his father-in-law Jethro in Midian, came to Horeb, "the mountain of God", where the angel of the Lord appeared to him in flames from within a bush. Seeing that the bush burned without being consumed, Moses approached and was told to remove his sandals, for the ground was holy. From the bush, God called Moses by name, revealed himself as the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and announced that he had heard the cry of the enslaved Israelites in Egypt. Moses was commissioned to confront Pharaoh and lead the Exodus. When Moses asked for God's name, he received the reply "I Am that I Am" (Ehyeh-Asher-Ehyeh), and the divine name YHWH was revealed. Moses protested his inadequacy — "slow of speech" — and was promised the help of his brother Aaron and signs, including his staff turning into a serpent.

Location and tradition

The event is traditionally located at Mount Sinai (identified with Horeb in the narrative). Saint Catherine's Monastery, at the foot of Mount Sinai in Egypt, preserves a living bush venerated as the original burning bush, identified by the monks as a rare bramble (Rubus sanctus). A chapel stands behind the monastery's altar over the bush's reputed roots.

Interpretation

The burning bush is one of the most commented-upon images in Jewish and Christian tradition: in Jewish interpretation the unconsumed bush symbolises Israel surviving persecution, and God's presence in a lowly thornbush his humility; in Orthodox Christianity the bush is a type of the Virgin Mary, who bore God yet remained unburned by divinity, and it appears in icons and hymnography as the "Unburnt Bush". The phrase also enters liturgy and art widely, and the burning bush is the emblem of the Church of Scotland and of several Reformed churches.
