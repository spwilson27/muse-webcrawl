Overview

A compass rose is the figure printed on charts and maps that shows the orientation of the cardinal directions. In its traditional form it is a star of 32 points — the divisions of the mariner's compass card — radiating from a centre, with the north point often crowned by a fleur-de-lis, a convention inherited from the portolan charts of the medieval Mediterranean.

Before compasses, such stars on charts were wind roses, naming the winds that blew from each direction — Tramontana, Levante, Sirocco and the rest. Modern maps reduce the rose to a simple north arrow, but the full rose survives as the emblem of cartography itself — it forms, for example, the courtyard pavement at the Royal Observatory and the logo-like centrepiece of many an atlas frontispiece.
