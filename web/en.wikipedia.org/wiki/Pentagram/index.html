Pentagram

A pentagram (sometimes known as a pentalpha, pentangle, or star polygon) is a five-pointed polygonal star polygon, formed by drawing a diagonal of a regular pentagon. It consists of a regular pentagon extended with its five edges extended until they intersect, enclosing a smaller pentagon. The word pentagram combines the Greek prefix penta- (five) and gramma (a letter or something written or drawn).

History

Early pentagrams have been found on Sumerian pottery from around 3500 BCE, and the pentagram was the symbol of the Babylonian goddess Ishtar. The Pythagoreans used it as a symbol of health and mutual recognition, calling it hygieia, and it later appeared in contexts as diverse as the five wounds of Christ in medieval Christianity, the occult and Neoplatonic traditions of the Renaissance, and modern neopagan movements such as Wicca, where it is enclosed in a circle (a pentacle).

Geometry

The pentagram is the simplest regular star polygon. The constant proportion of its segments is the golden ratio, φ = (1+√5)/2 ≈ 1.618. Each intersection point divides the longer segments in the golden ratio, and the pentagon at its center gives rise to an infinite self-similar "star polygonal" nesting.

Symbolism

Throughout history the pentagram has been associated with the five elements (earth, air, fire, water, and spirit), the five senses, and the planet Venus, whose apparent path against the stars traces a near-perfect pentagram every eight years. Inverted pentagrams appear in Satanic symbolism and in the flags of Ethiopia and Morocco in upright form.
