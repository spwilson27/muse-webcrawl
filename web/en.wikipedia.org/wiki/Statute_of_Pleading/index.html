Statute of Pleading

The Statute of Pleading (36 Edw. 3, c. 15), enacted in 1362 during the reign of Edward III, is an English statute famous for requiring that pleas in the royal courts be made in English rather than French. It is often cited as a milestone in the history of the English language.

Provisions

The statute declared that, because the French language was "much unknown" in the realm, all pleas in any court should be "pleaded, shewed, defended, answered, debated, and judged in the English tongue" — though they were still to be entered and enrolled in Latin, as had been customary. The preamble complained that laws and customs were being misunderstood because pleading was conducted in a language most people did not understand.

Significance

Although records continued to be kept in Latin for centuries (until the Proceedings in Courts of Justice Act 1730), the statute marked official recognition of English as the language of oral legal proceedings and reflected the growing status of English after the Black Death and during the Hundred Years' War.
