Thomas à Kempis (c. 1380 – 25 July 1471) was a German-Dutch canon regular of the late medieval period and the probable author of The Imitation of Christ, one of the most widely read Christian devotional books of all time. A leading figure of the Devotio Moderna movement, he spent nearly his entire adult life at the Mount St Agnes monastery (Agnietenberg) near Zwolle in the Netherlands.

Life

Thomas was born Thomas Hemerken ("little hammer") at Kempen near Cologne. At thirteen he went to Deventer to be educated by the Brethren of the Common Life, whose practical piety shaped him. In 1399 he entered the Augustinian priory of Mount St Agnes, founded by followers of Geert Groote; he was professed in 1406 and ordained priest in 1413 or 1414. At the monastery he copied manuscripts — famously transcribing the entire Bible at least once — trained novices as sub-prior, and wrote sermons, letters, saints' lives and devotional treatises. He died in 1471 at about ninety-one years of age.

The Imitation of Christ

The Imitation of Christ (Latin: De Imitatione Christi), compiled from Thomas's writings and circulated from about the 1420s, teaches the interior life: humility, contempt of worldly vanity, meditation on death and judgment, and loving conformity to Christ, culminating in a book on Holy Communion. Translated into many languages and reprinted in thousands of editions, it is often described as the most widely read devotional work in Christianity after the Bible. Although earlier scholars sometimes disputed its authorship, an autograph manuscript of 1441 signed by Thomas establishes his central role.
