Lana Turner (born Julia Jean Turner; February 8, 1921 – June 29, 1995) was an American actress. Over a career spanning nearly five decades, she achieved fame as both a pin-up model and a dramatic actress, and was one of the most popular and highest-paid stars of Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer (MGM) in the 1940s and 1950s.

Early life and discovery

Turner was born in Wallace, Idaho, and moved with her mother to California after her father was murdered. According to famous Hollywood lore, she was discovered at age 16 sipping a soda at the Top Hat Malt Shop in Hollywood — a story often wrongly retold as the Schwab's Pharmacy discovery. Signed by Warner Bros. and soon sold to MGM, she gained attention in They Won't Forget (1937), which earned her the nickname "the Sweater Girl" for her form-fitting outfit.

Career

Turner became one of MGM's biggest stars in films such as Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde (1941), Honky Tonk (1941), and the film noir classic The Postman Always Rings Twice (1946), in which she played a femme fatale opposite John Garfield — one of her most acclaimed performances. Other notable films include The Bad and the Beautiful (1952), Peyton Place (1957), for which she received an Academy Award nomination for Best Actress, and Douglas Sirk's Imitation of Life (1959), one of the biggest commercial successes of her career.

Personal life and the Stompanato case

Turner's personal life was highly publicized; she was married eight times. In 1958 her teenage daughter, Cheryl Crane, fatally stabbed Turner's abusive lover, mob associate Johnny Stompanato, during a domestic confrontation; the killing was ruled justifiable homicide at an inquest that became a media sensation. Turner later worked in television, notably the prime-time soap Falcon Crest in the 1980s, and published an autobiography, Lana: The Lady, the Legend, the Truth (1982).

Death

A longtime heavy smoker, Turner died of throat cancer in Los Angeles in 1995, aged 74.
