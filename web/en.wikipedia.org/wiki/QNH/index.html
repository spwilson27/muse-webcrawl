QNH

QNH is an aeronautical Q-code term for the atmospheric pressure adjusted to mean sea level: specifically, the barometric altimeter setting such that an aircraft's altimeter reads the elevation above mean sea level when on the ground at an airfield, and altitude above sea level in flight. It is given in hectopascals (or inches of mercury in North America) and is passed to pilots in weather reports (METARs) and by air traffic control.

Related settings

QNH is distinguished from QFE, the setting that makes the altimeter read height above the airfield itself (zero on the ground), and from the standard pressure setting (1013.25 hPa), which aircraft use at and above the transition altitude so that all traffic is referenced to a common datum, yielding flight levels. Regional pressure settings and QNE (used in some states) serve related purposes. The name derives from a mnemonic in the obsolete Morse Q-code list, glossed as "nautical height".

Use

Setting the correct QNH is essential for terrain clearance on approach and departure: an altimeter set with a pressure higher than actual will over-read, placing the aircraft lower than indicated. Controllers routinely update QNH with changing pressure.
