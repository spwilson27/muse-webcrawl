Ras Dashen (Amharic: ራስ ዳሸን ras dāshən), also known as Ras Dejen, is the highest mountain in Ethiopia and the tenth-highest mountain in Africa. Part of the Simien Mountains National Park, it stands in the North Gondar Zone of the Amhara Region. Its elevation is commonly given as 4,550 metres (14,930 ft) above sea level, though figures vary between surveys.

Elevation

The elevation of Ras Dashen has been reported variously. Older maps commonly gave 4,620 m, while a 2005 survey by the Ethiopian Mapping Authority supported by earlier Italian military survey work produced the widely cited figure of 4,550 m. The mountain consists of two peaks separated by a saddle: the western peak is the highest, while the eastern peak, sometimes called Ras Dashen's "sister", reaches about 4,460 m.

Geology and climate

The Simien Mountains are the eroded remains of a vast shield volcano built up by flood basalts some 40 to 25 million years ago. Ras Dashen's jagged ridges and deep gorges were carved by erosion and Pleistocene glaciation; small glaciers existed on the summit during the last glacial period, though none remain today. Despite its tropical latitude, temperatures at the summit regularly fall below freezing at night, and snow occasionally falls.

Climbing history

The first recorded ascent by a European was made in 1841 by the French officer Ferret and Galinier. Local people had almost certainly climbed the peak long before. The mountain is a popular trekking objective within the national park, usually approached over several days from the village of Debark through Sankaber and Geech camps, passing the village of Beyeda. Trekkers pass through dramatic escarpment scenery inhabited by gelada monkeys, walia ibex and Ethiopian wolves.
