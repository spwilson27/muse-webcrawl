Obturator membrane

Part of: Hip bone

Attaches to: Margins of the obturator foramen

Latin: membrana obturatoria

The obturator membrane is a thin fibrous sheet that almost completely closes the obturator foramen of the hip bone. Its fibers are arranged in interlacing bundles, running predominantly transversely; the membrane is attached around the margins of the foramen formed by the pubis and ischium.

Structure

The membrane does not close the foramen entirely. At the upper part of the foramen, below the obturator groove on the superior pubic ramus, a small opening remains — the obturator canal — through which the obturator nerve, artery, and vein pass out of the pelvis. The membrane serves as the origin for two muscles: the obturator internus arises from its pelvic (inner) surface, and the obturator externus arises from its outer surface and from the surrounding bone.

Function

Together with the obturator muscles, the membrane completes the lateral wall of the pelvis in this region and provides a broad surface for muscle attachment while closing off the large skeletal opening, preventing displacement of pelvic contents through the foramen. Rarely, in elderly women with loss of the protective fat pad, abdominal contents can herniate through the obturator canal beside the membrane, producing an obturator hernia.
