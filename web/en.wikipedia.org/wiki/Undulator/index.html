Undulator

An undulator is an insertion device from high-energy physics and consists of a periodic structure of dipole magnets. It forces a beam of charged particles to undergo oscillations and thus to radiate energy. The emitted radiation is concentrated in narrow energy bands whose wavelength can be tuned by changing the beam energy or the magnetic field strength and gap.

Operation

An undulator consists of two arrays of magnets with alternating polarity, between which a relativistic electron beam passes. The electrons follow a sinusoidal path and emit synchrotron radiation at each bend; in an undulator, the radiation from successive periods interferes constructively, producing a bright, quasi-monochromatic beam. The dimensionless undulator parameter K distinguishes undulators (K around or below 1, narrow-band emission) from wigglers (large K, broad-band emission).

Applications

Undulators are the principal radiation sources at modern synchrotron light sources and are the key component of free-electron lasers, where micro-bunching lets the electrons radiate coherently, producing extremely intense X-ray pulses. They are fabricated from permanent magnets (often in hybrid or cryogenic designs) or superconducting coils, and can be many metres long.
