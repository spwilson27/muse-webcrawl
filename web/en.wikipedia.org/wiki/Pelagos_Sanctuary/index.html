Pelagos Sanctuary

Location: Northwestern Mediterranean Sea
Area: approx. 87,500 km²
Established: 1999 (treaty); SPAMI listing 2001
Parties: France, Italy, Monaco

The Pelagos Sanctuary for Mediterranean Marine Mammals is a marine protected area covering about 87,500 km² of the northwestern Mediterranean Sea between southeastern France, Monaco, northwestern Italy and northern Sardinia, including the Ligurian Sea. Established by a treaty signed by France, Italy and Monaco in Rome on 25 November 1999 and in force from 2002, it is the first transboundary marine sanctuary dedicated to the protection of marine mammals, and it was inscribed on the list of Specially Protected Areas of Mediterranean Importance (SPAMI) under the Barcelona Convention in 2001.

Ecology

The sanctuary overlays one of the most productive areas of the Mediterranean, where deep upwelling and frontal systems concentrate krill and other prey. Eight cetacean species are regularly present, including the fin whale — the sanctuary is the principal summer feeding ground for the Mediterranean fin whale population — as well as sperm whales, striped dolphins, Risso's dolphins, bottlenose dolphins, common dolphins, pilot whales and Cuvier's beaked whales. Estimates have suggested several thousand whales and tens of thousands of dolphins use the area in summer.

Protection and threats

Within the sanctuary, deliberate capture of and disturbance to marine mammals is prohibited, whale watching is regulated, and high-speed offshore powerboat races are banned. Significant threats remain: collisions with the heavy ferry and cargo traffic crossing the area, plastic pollution, chemical contaminants, underwater noise and entanglement in fishing gear. The sanctuary works with shipping and ferry operators on collision-avoidance systems such as REPCET, and has been designated an area requiring particular attention through the IMO, with a Particularly Sensitive Sea Area for the northwestern Mediterranean approved in the 2020s.

Name

"Pelagos" derives from the Greek word for the open sea, reflecting the sanctuary's protection of pelagic ecosystems rather than only coastal waters.
