Macassan contact with Australia

Macassan contact with Australia refers to the historic voyages of sailors and fishermen from Makassar, on the island of Sulawesi (in what is now Indonesia), to the northern coast of Australia, beginning by the early eighteenth century (and possibly earlier) and continuing until the early twentieth century. The Macassans came each wet season in sailing vessels called praus to collect trepang (sea cucumber), which they traded to China.

Trade and exchange

Macassan fleets visited the Kimberley and Arnhem Land coasts, establishing seasonal camps for processing trepang. They traded cloth, rice, tobacco, metal knives and alcohol with Aboriginal peoples, and borrowed words — many Yolngu words for trade goods and sailing derive from Macassarese. Some Aboriginal people travelled back to Makassar. The trade ended after the South Australian government imposed duties and restrictions, with the last prau sailing in 1906–07.
