Benito Juárez

Benito Pablo Juárez García (21 March 1806 – 18 July 1872) was a Mexican liberal lawyer and statesman who served as the 26th president of Mexico from 1858 until his death in 1872. A Zapotec from Oaxaca, he was the first Indigenous president of Mexico and remains one of the country's most revered national heroes — often styled Benemérito de las Américas ("the Meritorious One of the Americas") — for defending the republic against the French intervention and the empire of Maximilian I.

Early life and the Reform

Orphaned as a child and speaking only Zapotec until adolescence, Juárez taught himself Spanish, studied law in Oaxaca City, and rose through municipal and state politics to become governor of Oaxaca. Exiled by Santa Anna, he returned with the Revolution of Ayutla. As minister of justice he authored the Juárez Law (1855) abolishing clerical and military fueros; as president of the Supreme Court he succeeded to the presidency when Comonfort resigned in 1858.

Presidency

Juárez's government enacted the Reform Laws — nationalising church property, instituting civil marriage and separating church and state — provoking the Reform War (1858–1861), which the liberals won. When Mexico suspended foreign debt payments, France intervened and installed Maximilian as emperor (1864–1867). Juárez led the republican resistance from the north, refusing to leave Mexican soil; after the United States Civil War ended, French withdrawal and republican victories led to Maximilian's capture and execution in 1867. Juárez famously declared: "Among individuals, as among nations, respect for the rights of others is peace." Re-elected in 1867 and 1871, he died in office in 1872.

Legacy

Juárez is commemorated throughout Mexico — cities, an airport and countless streets bear his name — and his birthday, 21 March, is a national holiday. His austere personal honesty and defence of liberal principles made him a symbol of Mexican nationalism and republican virtue.
