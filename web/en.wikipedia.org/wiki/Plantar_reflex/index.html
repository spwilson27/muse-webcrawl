Plantar reflex

Testing the plantar reflex

The plantar reflex is a reflex elicited when the sole of the foot is stimulated with a blunt instrument. In healthy adults the toes flex downward (a flexor, or "downgoing", response). An extensor response – dorsiflexion of the big toe with fanning of the other toes – is known as the Babinski sign and is abnormal in adults.

Technique

The examiner strokes the lateral aspect of the sole from the heel toward the little toe, then across the ball of the foot, using a blunt point such as the end of a reflex hammer handle. The stimulus should be firm but not painful, and the response of the toes observed.

Interpretation

The Babinski sign, described by the French neurologist Joseph Babinski in 1896, indicates dysfunction of the corticospinal tract – the upper motor neuron pathway – as seen in conditions such as stroke, multiple sclerosis, and spinal cord lesions. In infants the extensor response is normal, because the corticospinal tract is not yet fully myelinated; the flexor response usually develops by about 12–24 months of age as the child begins to walk.

Related responses, such as Chaddock's sign (elicited by stroking the lateral foot below the ankle) and Oppenheim's sign, may be used when the plantar response is equivocal or withdrawal makes it hard to interpret.
