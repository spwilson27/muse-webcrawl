Autoimmune hepatitis
Specialty: Hepatology
Symptoms: Fatigue, jaundice, abdominal discomfort; may be asymptomatic
Complications: Cirrhosis, liver failure
Treatment: Corticosteroids, azathioprine

Autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) is a chronic autoimmune disease of the liver in which the body's immune system attacks hepatocytes, causing inflammation that can progress to cirrhosis and liver failure if untreated. It affects women more often than men and can present at any age.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on elevated liver enzymes and serum IgG, the presence of autoantibodies — antinuclear antibodies and anti-smooth muscle antibodies in type 1 AIH, and anti-LKM-1 antibodies in type 2, which more often affects children — exclusion of viral, metabolic and drug-induced causes, and a liver biopsy typically showing interface hepatitis. Scoring systems from the International Autoimmune Hepatitis Group support the diagnosis.

Treatment

Standard treatment is immunosuppression with prednis(ol)one, often combined with azathioprine, which induces remission in the majority of patients; budesonide is an alternative in non-cirrhotic patients. Therapy is usually long term, and liver transplantation may be required for decompensated disease.
