Drogheda (Irish: Droichead Átha, meaning "bridge of the ford") is an industrial and port town in County Louth on the east coast of Ireland, just north of Dublin. The town is situated on the River Boyne, which forms the historic border between Counties Louth and Meath, and much of the town's southern environs lie in County Meath. With a population of about 44,000 in 2022, it is one of Ireland's largest towns and has become a commuter settlement for Dublin, about 50 km to the south.

History

The area has been settled since Neolithic times; the great passage tombs of Brú na Bóinne—Newgrange, Knowth, and Dowth—lie a few kilometres west of the town. Drogheda itself was founded as two separate towns: Drogheda-in-Meath, established by the Normans under Hugh de Lacy around 1194, and Drogheda-in-Oriel across the river; they were united by charter in 1412. The town hosted parliaments in the fifteenth century, including the 1494 parliament that passed Poynings' Law.

Drogheda is notorious for the siege of September 1649, when Oliver Cromwell's New Model Army stormed the town during the Cromwellian conquest of Ireland and massacred much of the Royalist garrison and many civilians—an atrocity that remains central to Irish historical memory. The Battle of the Boyne, fought in 1690 between William III and the deposed James II, took place just west of the town at Oldbridge.

Landmarks

Surviving medieval features include St Laurence's Gate, a thirteenth-century barbican, and stretches of the town wall. St Peter's Roman Catholic Church on West Street holds the shrine of St Oliver Plunkett, the archbishop executed at Tyburn in 1681, whose preserved head is displayed there. The Boyne Viaduct (1855), carrying the Dublin–Belfast railway, is a landmark of Victorian engineering; the town is also a gateway to the Brú na Bóinne UNESCO World Heritage Site visitor centre.

Economy and transport

Traditionally a port and centre for linen, brewing, and cement manufacture, Drogheda today serves as a regional retail and services centre with a growing commuter population. It lies on the M1 Dublin–Belfast motorway and the Dublin–Belfast railway line, with the port handling bulk and container traffic.
