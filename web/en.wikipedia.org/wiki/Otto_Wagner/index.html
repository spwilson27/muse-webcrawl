Otto Wagner

Otto Koloman Wagner (13 July 1841 – 11 April 1918) was an Austrian architect and urban designer, one of the most influential figures in the emergence of modern architecture in Europe. His shift from historicism to a functional, materially honest style helped define Viennese modernism.

As chief planner of Vienna's municipal infrastructure, Wagner designed the Stadtbahn (city railway) stations, the Nussdorf weir and locks on the Danube Canal, and major bridges. His landmark buildings include the Majolica House and other Linke Wienzeile apartment houses, the Austrian Postal Savings Bank (1904–1906), a pioneering work of modernism with its aluminium-bolted marble facade and glass-vaulted hall, and the Kirche am Steinhof church (1907) at the Steinhof psychiatric hospital.

Wagner taught at the Academy of Fine Arts Vienna, where his students included Josef Hoffmann, Joseph Maria Olbrich and Adolf Loos, and his 1895 book Moderne Architektur argued that new construction must reflect modern life. He died in Vienna in 1918.
