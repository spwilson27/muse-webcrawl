Demand draft

A demand draft (DD) is a method of transferring money in which a bank issues a negotiable instrument drawn on itself or another bank, instructing payment of a specified sum to a named payee on demand. Unlike a personal cheque, the funds are taken from the purchaser's account (or paid in cash) when the draft is issued, so a demand draft cannot bounce for lack of funds.

How it works

The purchaser pays the issuing bank the face amount plus a fee. The bank issues a draft payable at a specified branch or its correspondent in another city, and the payee presents it for payment or deposits it for clearing. Because the bank itself is the drawer, the payee bears much less risk than with a customer's cheque.

Uses

Demand drafts are widely used in India and other countries where cheques are less trusted, for payments such as examination and application fees, rent deposits, and business transactions between cities. In the United States the term is also used for "remotely created cheques" — paper cheques created by a payee with the account holder's authorisation rather than signature, used in telephone and internet payments.

Comparison with other instruments

A demand draft differs from a banker's draft or cashier's cheque only in detail of which bank pays; it differs from a Demand Note (a form of US paper currency) entirely. Drafts can usually be cancelled only if the original instrument is surrendered or an indemnity is given.
