Sam Barris (born Samuel Salapatas; May 21, 1924 – September 29, 1962) was an American custom car designer and builder, and, with his younger brother George Barris, a co-founder of Barris Kustom Autos in Los Angeles. The sons of Greek immigrants, the brothers grew up in the Sacramento area and began modifying cars as teenagers. Sam is widely regarded as the more gifted metalworker of the two, and as one of the most influential figures of the early postwar custom car movement.

Sam Barris at work in the Lynwood shop, where the brothers built some of the most famous customs of the 1950s.

Career

After serving in the United States Navy during the Second World War, Sam joined George in Los Angeles, and in 1946 the brothers opened a shop in Lynwood, California — the business that became Barris Kustom Autos. Sam developed a reputation for flawless metal finishing, pioneering techniques such as chopping tops, sectioning bodies, and blending fenders that defined the "lead sled" style.

His best-known creations include his own 1949 Mercury coupe and the celebrated Hirohata Merc (1952), built for Bob Hirohata and frequently cited as the most famous custom car ever built, with its dramatically chopped top, frenched headlights and hardtop-style side glass. Sam's personal style favoured restraint and proportion, and his cars set the standard for the custom show circuit of the early 1950s.

Later life and death

Sam eventually left the day-to-day business to George and moved back to the Sacramento area. He died of cancer in 1962, aged 38. His work has since been the subject of retrospectives, and original Sam Barris customs are among the most valuable and sought-after American custom cars.
