Hospital-acquired infection


Hand hygiene is the key preventive measure

A hospital-acquired infection (HAI), also known as a nosocomial infection (from the Greek nosokomeion, "hospital"), is an infection that is acquired in a hospital or other healthcare facility. Such infections typically appear 48 hours or more after admission, or within a period after discharge, and were neither present nor incubating at the time of admission. HAIs are a major cause of preventable illness and death worldwide.

Types and causes

The most common types are urinary tract infections (often catheter-associated), surgical site infections, bloodstream infections associated with central lines, hospital-acquired pneumonia including ventilator-associated pneumonia, and gastrointestinal infections such as Clostridioides difficile. Important pathogens include methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), vancomycin-resistant enterococci, and multidrug-resistant Gram-negative bacteria. Transmission occurs mainly via the hands of healthcare workers, contaminated equipment, and invasive devices that breach the body's defences.

Prevalence and prevention

The World Health Organization estimates that in high-income countries about 7 in 100 hospitalised patients acquire at least one HAI, with a higher burden in low- and middle-income countries. Prevention rests on infection prevention and control programmes: hand hygiene (the single most effective measure), sterilisation and disinfection, aseptic technique, antimicrobial stewardship, isolation of infectious patients, and surveillance with feedback of infection rates.
