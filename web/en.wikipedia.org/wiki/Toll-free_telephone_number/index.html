Toll-free telephone number

A toll-free telephone number or freephone number is a telephone number that is billed for all arriving calls instead of incurring charges to the originating caller. The called party, usually a business, pays for the calls, encouraging customers to phone without cost.

History and prefixes

In the North American Numbering Plan, toll-free service began in 1967 with the 800 area code (InWATS). As 800 numbers were exhausted, additional codes were added: 888 (1996), 877, 866, 855, 844, and 833. Similar systems exist internationally: in the United Kingdom "0800" and "0808" numbers are free to callers, and the international +800 service provides universal international freephone numbers.

Operation

Toll-free numbers are not tied to a physical line; a database (in North America, the SMS/800 system) translates the dialled number into a destination number, allowing routing by time of day, caller location, or call volume. Vanity numbers, spelling words on the keypad such as 1-800-FLOWERS, are widely used in advertising. Portability rules let subscribers keep numbers when changing service providers.
