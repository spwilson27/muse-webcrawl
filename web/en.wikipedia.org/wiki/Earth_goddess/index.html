Earth goddess

An Earth goddess is a deity personifying the Earth, fertility, agriculture and nature, typically conceived as a maternal figure—the "Earth Mother". Earth goddesses appear in religions and mythologies worldwide and are often paired with a sky god in a cosmic dualism, and associated with the harvest, the seasons and the underworld.

Female figurines have been interpreted as images of an earth or mother goddess since prehistory.

Examples by culture

  • Gaia (Greek): the primordial personification of the Earth, mother of the Titans and the gods.
  • Terra / Tellus (Roman): goddess of the earth, invoked in oaths and associated with fertility.
  • Demeter (Greek) and Ceres (Roman): goddesses of grain and the harvest, central to the Eleusinian Mysteries.
  • Prithvi / Bhudevi (Hindu): the earth goddess, consort of Vishnu in some traditions.
  • Pachamama (Andean): the earth mother of the Quechua and Aymara peoples, still honoured with offerings in August.
  • Jörð (Norse): personification of the earth and mother of Thor.
  • Ki (Sumerian) and Ninhursag: early Mesopotamian earth and mother goddesses.
  • Coatlicue and Tlaltecuhtli (Aztec): earth goddesses tied to creation and sacrifice.

Interpretation

Nineteenth- and twentieth-century scholars such as Johann Jakob Bachofen and Marija Gimbutas proposed that prehistoric Europe worshipped a universal Great Goddess centred on fertility; James Lovelock's Gaia hypothesis later borrowed the Greek name for a scientific metaphor of the self-regulating Earth system. Modern Pagan and Goddess movements have revived earth-goddess worship as a spiritual practice, though claims of an unbroken ancient matriarchal religion remain contested among archaeologists.
