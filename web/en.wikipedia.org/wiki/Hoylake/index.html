Hoylake is a seaside town in the Metropolitan Borough of Wirral, Merseyside, England. It lies at the north-western corner of the Wirral Peninsula, near the towns of West Kirby and Meols, on the coast of the Irish Sea at the mouth of the Dee Estuary. Historically part of Cheshire, Hoylake developed in the 19th century from a fishing and anchorage village — its name derives from the "high lake", a channel once used as a safe anchorage by ships bound for Liverpool — into a residential resort town.

Golf

Hoylake is internationally known for the Royal Liverpool Golf Club, founded in 1869, whose links course has hosted The Open Championship many times, including the 2006 championship won by Tiger Woods and the 2014 championship won by Rory McIlroy, and the 2023 Open won by Brian Harman. The course also hosted the inaugural Amateur Championship in 1885 and the Walker Cup in 1983 and 2019.

Features

The town has a long promenade facing the Irish Sea, a lifeboat station dating from 1803, and views across the Dee Estuary to the Hilbre Islands and the Welsh hills. Hoylake railway station is on the West Kirby branch of the Wirral line of Merseyrail. The town was the home of the Victorian philanthropist and antiquarian Charles William Jones, and the Red Rocks area at its northern edge is a Site of Special Scientific Interest known for reed beds and migrating birds.
