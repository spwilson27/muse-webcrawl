Glasses, also known as eyeglasses or spectacles, are vision aids consisting of glass or hard plastic lenses mounted in a frame that holds them in front of a person's eyes, typically using a bridge over the nose and hinged arms (temples) that rest over the ears. Glasses are typically used for vision correction, such as for myopia (short-sightedness), hyperopia (long-sightedness), astigmatism, and presbyopia. Safety glasses protect the eyes from flying debris, and sunglasses reduce exposure to bright light and ultraviolet radiation.

History

Corrective spectacles were invented in northern Italy in the late 13th century, around the 1280s–1300; the Dominican friar Giordano da Pisa mentioned them in a sermon of 1306. Early glasses used convex quartz or glass lenses held before the eyes or perched on the nose. Concave lenses for myopia appeared in the 16th century. Benjamin Franklin is credited with inventing bifocals in the 1780s; progressive lenses were patented in the early 20th century and commercialized by Varilux in 1959. Modern frames rest on the ears with spring hinges; lenses are now usually plastic (CR-39 or polycarbonate) with anti-reflective and scratch-resistant coatings.

Types

  • Corrective — single-vision, bifocal, trifocal, and progressive lenses.
  • Safety glasses and goggles — impact-resistant eye protection.
  • Sunglasses — tinted, polarized, or photochromic lenses.
  • Specialized — reading glasses, 3D glasses, magnifying glasses, and smart glasses.

Fashion and culture

Beyond function, glasses are a major fashion accessory, with styles cycling from wire rims to thick acetate frames. Over four billion adults worldwide wear glasses, and the global eyewear market is worth well over US$100 billion annually.
