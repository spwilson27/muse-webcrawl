Endomorphism ring

FieldAbstract algebra

In mathematics, the endomorphism ring of an abelian group or module is the set of all its endomorphisms — homomorphisms from the object to itself — equipped with addition and composition as operations. It is denoted End(X) for an object X.

Definition

Let M be a module over a ring R. The set of all R-module homomorphisms from M to itself forms a ring under pointwise addition and composition of maps. The identity map serves as the multiplicative identity. Endomorphism rings are generally noncommutative, even when the underlying scalars commute.

Examples

The endomorphism ring of a vector space of dimension n over a field is isomorphic to the ring of n×n matrices over that field. The endomorphism ring of the additive group of integers is isomorphic to the integers themselves, while that of a cyclic group of prime order is a finite field.

Properties

Endomorphism rings play a central role in module theory: properties of a module can often be studied through its endomorphism ring, and Schur's lemma states that the endomorphism ring of a simple module is a division ring.
