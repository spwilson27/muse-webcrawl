Eustachius Benoît De Lannoy (1715 – 1 June 1777) was a Flemish naval officer of the Dutch East India Company who became commander-in-chief of the army of the Indian kingdom of Travancore. Captured after the Dutch defeat at the Battle of Colachel in 1741, he entered the service of Maharaja Marthanda Varma and spent 36 years modernizing Travancore's military, earning the title Valiya Kapithan ("the great captain").

Battle of Colachel and capture

In 1741 De Lannoy commanded a Dutch expedition sent from Ceylon to check Travancore's expansion along the Malabar Coast. Marthanda Varma's forces routed the Dutch at Colachel — a rare decisive defeat of a European power by an Indian state — and De Lannoy and his men were taken prisoner. Impressed by his abilities, the Maharaja pardoned him and appointed him to train Travancore's army on European lines.

Service to Travancore

De Lannoy raised disciplined regiments, introduced European drill, musketry, and artillery manufacture, and directed the construction of fortifications, including Udayagiri Fort and the northern defensive line of forts known as the Nedumkotta, which later resisted Mysore. Under his command Travancore's forces defeated the neighbouring kingdoms and extended the state's territory northward to Cochin. He was granted an estate at Udayagiri, converted to (or at least practised) Christianity in its Catholic form, and married locally.

Death and legacy

De Lannoy died in 1777 and was buried in the chapel at Udayagiri Fort, where his tomb and the tombs of his wife and son survive, bearing Latin and Tamil inscriptions. His career is a celebrated episode in Kerala's history, and the Colachel victory is commemorated by the Indian Navy.
