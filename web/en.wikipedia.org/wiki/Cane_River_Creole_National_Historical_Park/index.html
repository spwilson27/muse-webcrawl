Cane River Creole National Historical Park

Cane River Creole National Historical Park is a United States National Historical Park in Natchitoches Parish, Louisiana, preserving the Creole culture of the Cane River region. It was established in 1994.

Sites

The park includes two French Creole cotton plantations: Oakland Plantation and Magnolia Plantation, among the most intact plantation complexes in the American South. Together they preserve some 65 historic structures — main houses, overseers' houses, slave/Tenant cabins, a plantation store, blacksmith shop, gin barns and other outbuildings — illustrating plantation life from the 18th to the 20th century.

History

The Cane River area was settled by French colonists and developed a distinctive Creole community, including a prominent community of free people of colour, notably the Metoyer family who built Magnolia Plantation. The park interprets the lives of landowners, enslaved people and later tenant farmers and sharecroppers alike.

The park is part of the wider Cane River National Heritage Area.
