Grade I listed buildings in Cheshire

[Image: Grade I listed buildings in Cheshire]

There are over 9,000 Grade I listed buildings in England. This page is a list of these buildings in the county of Cheshire. Grade I is the highest of the three grades of listing, given to buildings of "exceptional interest, sometimes considered to be internationally important".

Buildings

Cheshire's Grade I buildings include Chester Cathedral, whose fabric dates largely from the 13th to 16th centuries; the medieval parish churches of St Mary, Acton, St Oswald, Malpas, and St Helen, Ashby-de-la-Zouch's neighbour St Mary, Nantwich; and great country houses such as Lyme Park, Tatton Park's predecessor structures, Eaton Hall's chapel and stables, Combermere Abbey, and Tabley House. Chester's Roman amphitheatre and the city walls are also designated at high grade, as is the Iron Bridge at Aldford's inspiration, the early cast-iron structures of the county.

See also

Listed buildings in Cheshire are recorded by the National Heritage List for England, maintained by Historic England.
