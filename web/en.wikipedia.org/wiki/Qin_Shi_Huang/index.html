Qin Shi Huang

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Standard portrait of Qin Shi Huang in imperial robes and mian crown]

Qin Shi Huang (259–210 BC), born Ying Zheng, was the king of Qin who conquered the other Warring States and became the first emperor of a unified China in 221 BC, founding the Qin dynasty. The name he coined for himself — Shi Huangdi, 'First Emperor' — set the title of Chinese rulers for two thousand years. His regime, guided by the Legalist chancellor Li Si, standardised script, currency, weights and measures, built roads, canals and the first Great Wall joining the northern frontier walls, and raised the mausoleum guarded by the Terracotta Army. Chinese tradition long judged him a tyrant for the burning of books and burying of scholars; modern assessments weigh his unification's permanence alongside its brutality.

Conquest and unification

Ascending the Qin throne at thirteen (246 BC) under the regency of Lü Buwei and his mother, Zheng took personal power in 238 BC and, with generals such as Wang Jian, extinguished Han (230), Zhao (228), Yan (226), Wei (225), Chu (223) and Qi (221 BC) in sequence. He refused to enfeoff his sons, dividing the empire instead into 36 (later more) commanderies under appointed officials, moved 120,000 wealthy families to the capital Xianyang, and collected and melted down the realm's weapons into colossal statues — the classic programme of centralisation.

Last years and death

Obsessed with immortality, the emperor patronised fangshi magicians, swallowed mercury-laced elixirs (which may well have hastened his death), survived assassination attempts (Jing Ke's dagger, 227 BC, is the famous one), and made great tours of inspection across the empire. He died suddenly at Shaqiu in 210 BC on the fifth tour; the eunuch Zhao Gao and Li Si forged an edict ordering the heir Fusu's suicide and installed the younger son Huhai — a palace coup that doomed the dynasty within three years. Excavation of his tomb mound and the Terracotta Army pits (1974) made him world-famous in the modern age.
