Transavantgarde
Italian: Transavanguardia
Term coined by: Achille Bonito Oliva, 1979
Artists: Sandro Chia, Francesco Clemente, Enzo Cucchi, Nicola De Maria, Mimmo Paladino

The Transavantgarde (Italian: Transavanguardia) is an Italian art movement that flourished in the late 1970s and 1980s, characterised by a return to painting, figuration, myth and expressive colour after the dominance of conceptual and minimal art.

History

Critic Achille Bonito Oliva coined the term in a 1979 article and promoted its five leading artists — Sandro Chia, Francesco Clemente, Enzo Cucchi, Nicola De Maria and Mimmo Paladino — who exhibited internationally, including at documenta and the Venice Biennale. Oliva argued the artist could move freely 'beyond' (trans-) the avant-garde, quoting styles and imagery eclectically.

Context

The movement paralleled Neo-Expressionism in Germany and the 'New Image' painters elsewhere, and is often classed under postmodernism. Critics sympathetic to conceptualism accused it of market-driven regression, but it restored Italian painting to international prominence. See Neo-expressionism and Postmodernism.
