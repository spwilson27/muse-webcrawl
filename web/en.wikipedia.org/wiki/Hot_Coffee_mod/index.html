Hot Coffee is a normally inaccessible minigame in the 2004 video game Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas developed by Rockstar North. In the unmodified game, the protagonist can date girlfriends and is invited in for "coffee", but the encounter is not shown. The shipped code contained a disabled interactive sex minigame; in 2005 modders released the Hot Coffee mod, which unlocked it, first on PC and then, via cheat devices, on consoles.

Controversy

Discovery of playable sexual content in a game rated M (Mature) provoked a major controversy. Politicians including Senator Hillary Clinton called for investigation; the ESRB re-rated the game Adults Only in July 2005, prompting retailers to pull it from shelves. Rockstar's parent company Take-Two Interactive issued a corrected edition, faced class-action lawsuits, and settled with the Federal Trade Commission in 2006.

Aftermath

The episode reshaped industry practice on ratings disclosure and cut content, and "Hot Coffee" entered popular usage as shorthand for hidden content controversies in games. Later re-releases of San Andreas removed the minigame's assets entirely.
