Arkady Natanovich Strugatsky (1925–1991) and Boris Natanovich Strugatsky (1933–2012) were Soviet-Russian science fiction authors who collaborated on most of their fiction, becoming the most famous and influential SF writers of the Soviet Union.

Works

Their early novels built the interconnected "Noon Universe", a utopian future of scientific communism. Later works grew darker and more philosophical: Hard to Be a God (1964), Monday Begins on Saturday (1965), The Snail on the Slope, Roadside Picnic (1972) — the source of Andrei Tarkovsky's film Stalker and the S.T.A.L.K.E.R. video games — and The Inhabited Island. Several works faced censorship or were published abroad under pseudonyms because of their veiled political satire.

Legacy

The brothers' books have been translated into dozens of languages; the asteroid 3054 Strugatskia is named in their honour, and their blend of hard SF, social commentary and moral ambiguity shaped generations of Eastern European writers.
