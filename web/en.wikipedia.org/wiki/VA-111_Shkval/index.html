VA-111 Shkval

VA-111 Shkval
Type: Supercavitating torpedo
Origin: Soviet Union / Russia
In service: 1977–present
Speed: over 200 knots (370 km/h)
Range: ~7–13 km (later versions longer)

The VA-111 Shkval (Russian: шквал, "squall") is a Soviet and Russian supercavitating torpedo, one of the fastest underwater weapons ever deployed. Instead of pushing through the water, the Shkval encloses itself in a bubble of gas — a supercavity — so that only its nose touches the water, dramatically reducing drag and allowing speeds in excess of 200 knots (370 km/h), several times faster than conventional torpedoes.

Design

Development began in the 1960s at the State Scientific Research Institute of Hydroaviation (under the design bureau that became Region Scientific and Production Association). The weapon is launched from a standard 533 mm torpedo tube; a solid-fuel rocket motor accelerates it, and hot exhaust gas is vented through its specially shaped cavitator nose to sustain the cavity. Steering is by four small fins that project through the cavity wall into the water.

The original VA-111 carried a nuclear warhead; a conventional version with a roughly 210 kg explosive warhead followed. Early models were unguided after launch, running straight at a pre-set depth — acceptable given that a target had almost no time to evade once the weapon was in the water. The export variant, the Shkval-E, was offered with conventional guidance improvements.

Context

The Shkval's existence became widely known after the sinking of the Russian submarine Kursk in August 2000, in which a practice version of a heavyweight torpedo was implicated; the Shkval itself was much discussed in coverage of Russian naval weaponry. Its concept has inspired supercavitation research worldwide, including the German Barracuda programme and Iranian claims of a similar weapon (the Hoot, announced in 2006).
