Benjamin Sinclair "Ben" Johnson (born 30 December 1961) is a Canadian former track and field sprinter who won gold medals at the 1984 and 1988 Summer Olympics before being stripped of his titles for doping. Born in Jamaica, he emigrated to Canada as a teenager and came to prominence under coach Charlie Francis, winning bronze medals in the 100 metres and 4 × 100 metres relay at the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics.

Rise to prominence

Johnson won the 100 m at the 1987 World Championships in Rome in a world record of 9.83 seconds, defeating his great rival Carl Lewis. He entered the 1988 Seoul Olympics as the fastest man in the world.

Seoul 1988 and disqualification

On 24 September 1988, Johnson won the Olympic 100 m final in a new world record of 9.79 seconds. Three days later he was disqualified after testing positive for the anabolic steroid stanozolol, and his gold medal and world records were annulled. The scandal prompted the Canadian government's Dubin Inquiry into drug use in sport. Johnson admitted long-term steroid use under Francis's programme.

Comeback and later life

Johnson returned to competition in 1991 after a two-year ban, but in 1993 he tested positive again and was banned for life by the IAAF. In later years he worked as a coach and trainer and published an autobiography, Seoul to Soul (2010).
