Kodachrome

Kodachrome is a colour reversal film introduced by Eastman Kodak in 1935. It was one of the first successful mass-market colour materials, used for both cinematography and still photography, and was renowned for its rich, saturated colours and exceptional archival stability in dark storage.

History

Kodachrome was developed by Leopold Godowsky Jr. and Leopold Mannes. It was sold first as 16 mm movie film in 1935, then as 35 mm still film. The complex K-14 development process, in which colour couplers were added during processing rather than being in the film, meant processing had to be done by Kodak or licensed labs.

Decline and legacy

Falling sales in the digital era led Kodak to discontinue Kodachrome in 2009; the last roll was processed by Dwayne's Photo in Kansas in December 2010. The film is celebrated in popular culture, including Paul Simon's 1973 song "Kodachrome", and images shot on it — such as Steve McCurry's Afghan Girl — are iconic.
