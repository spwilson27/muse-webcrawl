Methane (CH₄) is the simplest hydrocarbon and the simplest organic compound: one carbon atom bonded tetrahedrally to four hydrogens. It is the main component of natural gas and biogas, colourless and odourless (the 'gas smell' of domestic supply is an added odorant).

Methane is a potent greenhouse gas — over 80 times more warming than CO₂ over 20 years by common estimates — released by wetlands, ruminants, rice paddies, landfills and fossil-fuel operations, though it survives only about a decade in the atmosphere. It is also found on other worlds: Titan's lakes are liquid methane and ethane.

[Image: A blue natural-gas flame on a burner ring]

Properties

Methane burns in oxygen to carbon dioxide and water (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O) and was the 'firedamp' of coal-mining explosions and the will-o'-the-wisp of marshes ('swamp gas'). Alessandro Volta first collected and studied it from Lake Maggiore marshes in 1776.

Uses

Beyond fuel, methane feeds the chemical industry via steam reforming to hydrogen and synthesis gas.
