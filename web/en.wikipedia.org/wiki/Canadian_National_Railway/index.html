Canadian National Railway

The Canadian National Railway Company (French: Compagnie des chemins de fer nationaux du Canada), abbreviated CN, is a Canadian Class I freight railway headquartered in Montreal, Quebec, that serves Canada and the Midwestern and Southern United States. CN is Canada's largest railway, in terms of both revenue and the physical size of its rail network, spanning roughly 32,000 kilometres (20,000 miles) of track from the Atlantic coast to the Pacific coast and south to the Gulf of Mexico.

History

CN was created by the Government of Canada between 1918 and 1923 out of several bankrupt railway systems, including the Canadian Northern Railway, the Grand Trunk Pacific Railway, and the Grand Trunk Railway, in an effort to rescue them from insolvency. It became a Crown corporation and grew into one of the largest enterprises in Canada, at various times operating hotels, a telecommunications company, and the CN Tower in Toronto. The company was privatized in 1995 in what was then the largest initial public offering in Canadian history.

Operations and expansion

Under the leadership of E. Hunter Harrison in the late 1990s and 2000s, CN pioneered "precision scheduled railroading." The railway expanded into the United States through acquisitions including the Illinois Central Railroad (1998), the Wisconsin Central (2001), and the Elgin, Joliet and Eastern Railway (2009), giving it a network encircling the Great Lakes and reaching Chicago and New Orleans. CN carries a wide variety of freight: intermodal containers, petroleum and chemicals, grain, forest products, metals and minerals, and automobiles.

Passenger services

CN operated Canada's principal passenger trains until 1978, when most intercity passenger service was transferred to the new Crown corporation Via Rail. The name "Canadian National" remains associated with landmarks such as the CN Tower, completed in 1976 and once the world's tallest free-standing structure.
