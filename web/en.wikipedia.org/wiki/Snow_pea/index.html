Snow pea

The snow pea is an edible-pod pea cultivar group, Pisum sativum var. saccharatum, eaten whole in its pod while still unripe. Its name comes from the whitened appearance of the pods in reflected light, and the French name mangetout ("eat all") reflects that the entire pod is consumed.

Description

Snow peas have flat pods with very thin walls and small, immature peas inside; the pods lack the tough parchment-like membrane found in shelling peas, so they do not need to be shelled. The plants are climbing vines that bear white or purple flowers. Pods are harvested young, before the seeds swell.

Culinary use

Snow peas are a common ingredient in East Asian and stir-fried dishes, where their crisp texture and mild sweetness are valued. They are usually cooked only briefly or eaten raw. Before cooking, the tough "strings" along the pod edges are typically pulled off.

Related cultivars

The snap pea (sugar snap pea) is a cross between the snow pea and a mutant shelling pea, with plump, rounded pods. Both belong to the edible-pod groups of the garden pea.
