Santa Cruz Province, Argentina

Santa Cruz Province
Country: Argentina
Capital: Río Gallegos
Area: about 243,943 km²

Santa Cruz Province is a province of Argentina in the southern Patagonia region. It is the country's second-largest province by area and one of the least densely populated. Its capital is Río Gallegos.

Geography

The province stretches from the Atlantic coast to the Andes. The west contains part of Los Glaciares National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with the Perito Moreno Glacier and Mount Fitz Roy; large glacial lakes include Lago Argentino and Lago Viedma. The central plateau is arid steppe cut by river valleys.

History

Indigenous Tehuelche peoples inhabited the region before European settlement. The territory was organised in 1884 and became a province in 1957. Sheep ranching, then oil production around Caleta Olivia and Pico Truncado, drove development; the political career of Néstor Kirchner, governor before becoming president of Argentina, is closely tied to the province.

Economy

Key sectors are petroleum and natural gas, mining (including gold at Cerro Vanguardia), fishing, sheep farming, and tourism centred on El Calafate and El Chaltén.
