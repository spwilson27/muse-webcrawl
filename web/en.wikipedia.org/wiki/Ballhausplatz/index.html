Ballhausplatz is a square in central Vienna, Austria, located in the first district (Innere Stadt) beside the Hofburg Palace. It is home to the Federal Chancellery of Austria at Ballhausplatz 2 and the Office of the Federal President in the Hofburg's Leopoldine Wing, making "Ballhausplatz" a metonym for the Austrian federal government, much as "Downing Street" is used in Britain.

History

The square takes its name from a ball house (Ballhaus) — a court facility for ball games — built in the 16th century. The Chancellery building, erected in Baroque style in 1717–1719 as the Geheime Hofkanzlei, later housed the Austrian State Chancellery and Foreign Ministry. Under Prince Metternich it was the centre of the diplomacy of the Congress of Vienna era. In 1934 Chancellor Engelbert Dollfuss was assassinated in the building during the Nazi July Putsch. A memorial to the victims of National Socialism stands on the square.

Today

Ballhausplatz 1 houses the Federal President's offices; No. 2 is the seat of the Federal Chancellor and the Council of Ministers. The square is a frequent site of demonstrations, vigils — including a long-running weekly protest presence — and state ceremonies.
