Awadhi (Devanagari: अवधी) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken primarily in the Awadh region of present-day Uttar Pradesh, India, and in the Terai region of Nepal. It is one of the Eastern Hindi languages, and historically was the vehicle of a distinguished literature. Although the Indian census often groups Awadhi under "Hindi", linguists treat it as a distinct language.

Literature

Awadhi is famous for two masterpieces of medieval Hindi literature: Malik Muhammad Jayasi's Sufi romance Padmavat (1540), and Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas (c. 1574), the retelling of the Ramayana that became one of the most beloved books of northern India. Awadhi also served the Sufi premakhyana romances and a rich folk tradition of songs and epics such as the Alha-Khand.

Geographic distribution

The language is spoken in districts including Lucknow, Ayodhya (Faizabad), Prayagraj, Sultanpur, Rae Bareli, Sitapur and Bahraich, and by indentured-labour diaspora communities: Fiji Hindi and Caribbean Hindustani descend largely from Awadhi and Bhojpuri.
