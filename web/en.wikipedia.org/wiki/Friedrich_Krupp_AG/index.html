Friedrich Krupp AG

Friedrich Krupp AG was the legal form, from 1903, of the German steel and armaments concern founded by Friedrich Krupp in Essen in 1811 and built into one of Europe's largest industrial enterprises under his son Alfred Krupp, the "Cannon King". The company became famous for its cast steel, artillery, and later for products from rails and locomotives to stainless steel.

History

On Friedrich Alfred Krupp's death in 1902 the firm passed to his daughter Bertha and was incorporated as Friedrich Krupp AG in 1903, with the Kaiser himself a shareholder. Krupp supplied armaments in both World Wars; after 1945 Alfried Krupp was convicted at the Krupp trial in Nuremberg for the use of slave labour. The company was converted into a GmbH in 1968 and, after severe financial difficulties, merged with Hoesch in the 1990s and ultimately with Thyssen in 1999 to form ThyssenKrupp.
