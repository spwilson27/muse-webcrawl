Flatbrød

Flatbrød
TypeFlatbread
Place of originNorway
Main ingredientsBarley flour, salt, water (sometimes potato, rye, wheat)

Flatbrød (literally "flat-bread") is a traditional Norwegian flatbread which is currently usually eaten with fish, salted meats and soups. Originally it was the staple food of Norwegian farmers, shepherds and peasants.

Preparation

The basic ingredients are barley flour, salt, and water, though many varieties exist that may incorporate potato, rye or wheat flour. The dough is rolled out very thin with a grooved rolling pin, then baked briefly on a large griddle (takke) until crisp and dry. Because of its low moisture content, flatbrød keeps for long periods without spoiling, and was traditionally baked in large batches a few times a year and stored on the farm.

Use

Flatbrød is served with cured meats, cheeses, and stews, and is crumbled into dishes such as the porridge-like spekemat accompaniments. It resembles other Scandinavian crispbreads, such as Swedish knäckebröd, but is typically thinner and made without leavening.
