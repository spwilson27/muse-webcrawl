IMVU

IMVU
Type: Virtual world, social platform
Launched: 15 April 2004
Founder: Eric Ries (co-founder)
Headquarters: Redwood City, California
Users: Millions of registered accounts; large creator economy

IMVU (pronounced letter-by-letter, or "im-view") is an online virtual world and social networking platform launched in 2004 by IMVU, Inc. Users create customizable 3D avatars, meet and chat in virtual rooms, play games, and buy virtual goods from a vast user-generated catalog. The name derives from "instant messaging virtual universe."

Features and economy

IMVU's catalog contains tens of millions of items — clothing, hairstyles, furniture, rooms, animations, and avatar bodies — the overwhelming majority created by users themselves through the platform's creator program. Items are bought with credits purchased with real money or earned through the platform, and creators receive a share of revenue, making IMVU an early example of a large-scale virtual goods creator economy. Social activity centers on public and private chat rooms, avatar dress-up, roleplay communities, and, on mobile versions (launched in the 2010s), a swipe-and-match social discovery interface. The platform is rated for older teens and adults, with a separate restricted-content tier.

Company history

IMVU is notable in startup lore because its co-founder Eric Ries developed his experiments there into the "lean startup" methodology, later popularized in his 2011 book The Lean Startup; IMVU's practice of rapid A/B testing and continuous deployment became a canonical case study. The company raised substantial venture funding and reported tens of millions of registered users at its peak popularity, remaining an active niche platform decades after launch.
