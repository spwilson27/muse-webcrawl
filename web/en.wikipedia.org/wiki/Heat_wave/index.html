Heat wave

A heat wave (or heatwave), sometimes described as extreme heat, is a period of abnormally hot weather, generally lasting more than two days. Heat waves can occur with or without high humidity and are measured relative to the usual climate in the area and the normal temperatures for the season. Definitions vary, but the World Meteorological Organization defines a heat wave as five or more consecutive days during which the daily maximum temperature exceeds the average maximum temperature by 5 °C (9 °F) or more.

Heat waves are usually associated with a persistent high-pressure system, or "heat dome".

Formation

Heat waves form when high pressure aloft (from about 3,000–7,600 metres, or 10,000–25,000 feet) strengthens and remains over a region for several days up to several weeks. In summer, the jet stream follows the Sun; when it weakens or becomes trapped in a stationary pattern, such as an omega block, air in the high-pressure system sinks and warms by compression, suppressing clouds and rainfall. The resulting "heat dome" traps hot air over a region.

Health impacts

Extreme heat is the deadliest form of extreme weather in many countries. When the human body cannot cool itself adequately, heat-related illnesses arise, including heat cramps, heat exhaustion and heat stroke, which can be fatal. High nighttime temperatures are particularly dangerous because they prevent recovery. Older adults, young children, people with chronic illness, outdoor workers, and those without access to cooling are most at risk. Urban areas experience amplified temperatures through the urban heat island effect.

The 2003 European heat wave is estimated to have caused more than 70,000 excess deaths. Other deadly events include the 1995 Chicago heat wave, the 2010 Russian heat wave, the 2021 Pacific Northwest heat dome, and the heat waves affecting India, Pakistan and Europe in 2022 and subsequent years.

Other impacts

  • Increased electricity demand for air conditioning, which can strain or collapse power grids.
  • Drought amplification, crop losses and heightened wildfire risk.
  • Buckling of roads and railway tracks, and harm to livestock and ecosystems.
  • Reduced water quality and stress on aquatic life as rivers and lakes warm.

Climate change

Climate change has increased the frequency, intensity and duration of heat waves worldwide, and attribution studies have found that many recent extreme events would have been virtually impossible without human-caused warming. Heat waves are projected to become more severe as global temperatures rise, prompting heat action plans, early-warning systems and urban greening programs in many cities.
