ComposerRichard Strauss

Premiere25 October 1912 (first version), Stuttgart; 4 October 1916 (revised), Vienna

Ariadne auf Naxos, Op. 60, is an opera by Richard Strauss with a German libretto by Hugo von Hofmannsthal. It combines a serious retelling of the Greek myth of Ariadne, abandoned on Naxos and found by the god Bacchus, with a commedia dell'arte troupe in a celebrated "play within a play" structure that contrasts high art with popular entertainment.


The opera juxtaposes a tragic heroine with a troupe of comedians.

Versions

The first version (1912) was designed to follow a performance of Molière's play Le Bourgeois gentilhomme with incidental music by Strauss; the long evening proved impractical. In the revised version of 1916, the play was replaced by a sung Prologue set backstage at a rich man's house in Vienna, in which a young Composer learns that his serious opera must be performed simultaneously with a comedy troupe's act. The opera proper then presents both entertainments at once: Ariadne (soprano) laments on a desert island while Zerbinetta and her companions try to cheer her, before Bacchus arrives and the two are transformed by love.

Music and reception

Strauss scored the work for a chamber orchestra of about 37 players, producing a transparent, neoclassical sound. Famous numbers include Zerbinetta's coloratura aria "Großmächtige Prinzessin" and the final duet. Ariadne auf Naxos is one of Strauss's most frequently performed operas and a landmark of the Strauss–Hofmannsthal collaboration that also produced Elektra and Der Rosenkavalier.
