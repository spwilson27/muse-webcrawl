Scale (music)

Musical scale
DefinitionAn ordered set of pitches spanning (usually) one octave
Common typesMajor, natural minor, harmonic minor, melodic minor, pentatonic, chromatic

In music theory, a scale is any set of musical notes ordered by fundamental frequency or pitch. A scale ordered by increasing pitch is an ascending scale, and one ordered by decreasing pitch is a descending scale. Scales are usually conceived as spanning a single octave, with the pattern of intervals repeating at each octave.

Intervals and construction

A scale is defined by its pattern of intervals — in Western music typically whole steps and half steps (tones and semitones). The major scale, for example, follows the pattern tone–tone–semitone–tone–tone–tone–semitone. The notes of a scale are numbered as degrees (tonic, supertonic, mediant, subdominant, dominant, submediant, leading tone) and melodies and chords within a key are built from them.

Common scales

  • Major scale — the basis of most Western tonal music.
  • Minor scales — natural, harmonic, and melodic minor forms.
  • Pentatonic scales — five-note scales found worldwide, central to much folk music, blues, and rock.
  • Chromatic scale — all twelve pitches of the octave.
  • Modes — rotations of a parent scale, such as Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, and Mixolydian.
  • Whole-tone and octatonic scales — symmetrical scales used notably by Debussy and later composers.

Scales in world music

Many musical cultures use scales that do not divide the octave into twelve equal semitones: Indian music organises melody through ragas, Arabic and Turkish music through maqam systems with microtonal intervals, and Indonesian gamelan uses pelog and slendro tunings.
