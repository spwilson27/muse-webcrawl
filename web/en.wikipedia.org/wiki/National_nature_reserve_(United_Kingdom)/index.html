National nature reserves in the United Kingdom are areas designated to protect the most significant examples of the country's wildlife habitats and geological formations, and to provide opportunities for scientific research and public appreciation of nature. They were established under the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949, and are declared by the statutory nature conservation bodies of the four countries of the UK: Natural England, NatureScot, Natural Resources Wales, and the Northern Ireland Environment Agency.

Designation and management

NNRs are selected as nationally important examples of habitats such as ancient woodland, lowland heath, chalk grassland, blanket bog, estuaries, and coastal systems. Many are owned and managed directly by the conservation bodies; others are managed by approved bodies such as the National Trust, the RSPB, the Wildlife Trusts, local authorities, or private landowners under management agreements. Almost all NNRs are also notified as Sites of Special Scientific Interest (SSSIs), and many form part of the Natura 2000 network of Special Areas of Conservation and Special Protection Areas.

Distribution

  • England — more than 220 NNRs, the largest being The Wash, and others including the Lizard in Cornwall, the Somerset Levels reserves, and Lindisfarne in Northumberland.
  • Scotland — 43 NNRs, including Beinn Eighe (the first in Britain, declared in 1951), the Cairngorms, and St Kilda.
  • Wales — around 76 NNRs, including Cors Caron (Tregaron Bog), Snowdon (Yr Wyddfa), and the Dyfi estuary reserves.
  • Northern Ireland — a smaller network of reserves designated under equivalent legislation.

Purpose

The reserves serve three broad purposes: the conservation of flora, fauna, and geological features; scientific study, providing long-term monitoring sites for ecology and climate research; and public engagement, since the great majority are open to visitors with trails, hides, and interpretation facilities. A series of new "Super NNRs" and the King's Series of National Nature Reserves, announced in 2023, have expanded the network by joining neighbouring reserves under coordinated landscape-scale management.
