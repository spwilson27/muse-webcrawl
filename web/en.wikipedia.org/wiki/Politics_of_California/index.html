The politics of California are dominated by the Democratic Party, which holds the governorship, supermajorities in both houses of the state legislature, and most of the state's seats in the U.S. Congress. California is regarded as one of the most liberal states in the United States, though its inland and rural regions lean Republican.

The California State Capitol in Sacramento.

Government structure

California's government consists of an executive branch headed by the Governor; a bicameral legislature — the State Senate (40 members) and State Assembly (80 members); and a judiciary headed by the Supreme Court of California. Voters also directly elect other executive officers including the Lieutenant Governor, Attorney General, and Secretary of State.

Direct democracy

California is known for its extensive system of direct democracy, a legacy of the Progressive Era reforms of Governor Hiram Johnson in 1911. Citizens may propose laws and constitutional amendments through ballot initiatives, repeal statutes by referendum, and recall elected officials — most famously in the 2003 recall of Governor Gray Davis, who was replaced by Arnold Schwarzenegger, and the unsuccessful 2021 recall attempt against Governor Gavin Newsom.

Political history

For much of the twentieth century California was a competitive or Republican-leaning state, producing Presidents Richard Nixon and Ronald Reagan. Since the 1990s — a shift often linked to debates over immigration measures such as Proposition 187 (1994) and demographic change — the state has trended strongly Democratic in federal and statewide elections.
