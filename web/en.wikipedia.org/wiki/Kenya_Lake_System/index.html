The Kenya Lake System in the Great Rift Valley is a network of three shallow, interlinked alkaline lakes — Lake Elementaita, Lake Nakuru and Lake Bogoria — in the Great Rift Valley of Kenya. Covering some 32,034 hectares, the system was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2011 for its exceptional biodiversity and as one of the most important feeding grounds in the world for the lesser flamingo.

Ecology

The lakes are surrounded by steep rift escarpments, waterfalls, geysers and hot springs (notably at Lake Bogoria). Their alkaline waters support green algae (Arthrospira fusiformis) that sustain huge congregations of lesser flamingos — at times the largest gatherings of birds on Earth, with millions of flamingos moving between the three lakes. The area is also globally important for greater flamingos and great white pelicans and supports 13 globally threatened bird species, as well as mammals including Rothschild's giraffe, black rhino, greater kudu, lion, leopard and cheetah in the surrounding reserves.

Conservation

Lake Nakuru National Park and Lake Bogoria National Reserve provide legal protection, and all three lakes are listed as Ramsar wetlands of international importance. Threats include water abstraction, pollution, deforestation in catchment areas and fluctuating lake levels, which in recent years have seen unusual flooding of the Rift Valley lakes.
