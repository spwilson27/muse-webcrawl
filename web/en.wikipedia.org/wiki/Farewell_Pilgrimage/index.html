Farewell Pilgrimage

The Farewell Pilgrimage (Arabic: Ḥajjat al-Wadāʿ, "the Pilgrimage of the Farewell") was the only Hajj pilgrimage performed by the Islamic prophet Muhammad, in the year 10 AH (632 CE), a few months before his death. It is notable for the Farewell Sermon, delivered at Mount Arafat and at Ghadir Khumm (in Shia tradition), which summarised central teachings of Islam.

Background

After the conquest of Mecca in 8 AH and the consolidation of Islam across Arabia, Muhammad announced his intention to perform the Hajj. He departed Medina in Dhu al-Qa'dah 10 AH accompanied by his wives and a large number of followers, entering Mecca in the state of ihram.

The Farewell Sermon

On 9 Dhu al-Hijjah, Muhammad delivered a sermon from Mount Arafat declaring the sanctity of life and property, the abolition of usury and of blood feuds from the pre-Islamic period, the rights and kind treatment of women, and equality among believers regardless of race: "No Arab has superiority over a non-Arab, nor a white over a black, except by piety." Tradition holds that the verse "This day I have perfected for you your religion..." (Qur'an 5:3) was revealed during the pilgrimage. Shia Muslims additionally commemorate the stop at Ghadir Khumm on the return journey, where they believe Muhammad designated Ali ibn Abi Talib as his successor.

Significance

The rites Muhammad performed during this pilgrimage form the template for the modern Hajj. He died in Medina about three months later, in Rabi' al-awwal 11 AH (June 632 CE).
