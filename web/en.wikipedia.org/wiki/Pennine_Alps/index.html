Pennine Alps

The Pennine Alps (also called the Valais Alps) are a mountain range in the western Alps, lying mainly in the Swiss canton of Valais and extending into northern Italy. They contain many of the highest peaks in the Alps, including the Monte Rosa massif, whose Dufourspitze (4,634 m) is the second-highest mountain in the Alps, and the iconic Matterhorn (4,478 m).

Other notable summits include the Dom, the Weisshorn, the Lyskamm, and the Breithorn. The range is heavily glaciated and includes the Gorner Glacier, one of the largest in Switzerland. The Great St Bernard Pass, historically one of the principal crossings of the Alps, lies at the western end of the range, while the Simplon Pass lies to the east.

The Pennine Alps are a major centre for mountaineering, skiing, and tourism, with resorts such as Zermatt and Saas-Fee on the Swiss side.
