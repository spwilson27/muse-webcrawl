Yuga

A yuga (Sanskrit: युग, "age" or "epoch") is, in Hinduism, one of the four ages of the world cycle, or yuga cycle (mahayuga). The four yugas—Satya (Krita), Treta, Dvapara and Kali—descend in length and in the virtue (dharma) of humankind, from a golden age of truth to the present degenerate age of strife. Together the four ages last 4,320,000 human years; a thousand such cycles make one day of the creator god Brahma.

The yuga cycle

The four ages

In the Satya Yuga (also Krita, "accomplished"), dharma stands on four legs: humans are truthful, lifespans are immense and there is no disease or labour. In the Treta Yuga, dharma has three legs; sacrifice is introduced and the avatars Vamana, Parashurama and Rama are said to appear. In the Dvapara Yuga, dharma has two legs, the Veda is divided into four, and the age culminates in the Mahabharata war and the life of Krishna. In the Kali Yuga, dharma stands on one leg: it is an age of conflict, ignorance and short lives, which began, by traditional reckoning, in 3102 BCE upon Krishna's departure from the world.

Cosmology

One mahayuga (great age) of 4.32 million years is composed of the four yugas in the ratio 4:3:2:1. Seventy-one mahayugas form a manvantara, the reign of one Manu, and fourteen manvantaras make a kalpa, a single day in the life of Brahma, at whose end the universe is dissolved and reborn.

Interpretations

Some modern teachers, notably Sri Yukteswar Giri in The Holy Science (1894), proposed a much shorter 24,000-year yuga cycle linked to the precession of the equinoxes. The yuga scheme also appears in Jainism and Buddhism in adapted forms.
