Lutetium

Lutetium
Symbol: Lu
Atomic number: 71
Group: Lanthanide
Standard atomic weight: 174.97
Electron configuration: [Xe] 4f14 5d1 6s2

Lutetium is a chemical element with the symbol Lu and atomic number 71. It is a silvery white metal and the last element of the lanthanide series; it is sometimes considered the first transition metal of the sixth period. It resists corrosion in dry air but tarnishes in moist air.

Discovery

Lutetium was independently discovered in 1907 by French scientist Georges Urbain, Austrian mineralogist Carl Auer von Welsbach, and American chemist Charles James, who separated it from the mineral ytterbia. Urbain's name, lutetium (from Lutetia, the Latin name for Paris), was adopted by the International Commission on Atomic Weights.

Properties

Lutetium is the densest and hardest of the lanthanides, with one of the highest melting points in the series. Its compounds are almost always in the +3 oxidation state. The isotope lutetium-176 is slightly radioactive and is used in lutetium–hafnium dating of meteorites and rocks.

Applications

Because it is rare and expensive, lutetium has few commercial uses. Lutetium-177 is used in targeted radionuclide therapy for certain cancers, lutetium oxyorthosilicate serves as a scintillator in PET scanners, and lutetium compounds find niche roles in catalysts and phosphors.
