Miniature park

Miniature park
Type: Open-air exhibition
Common scales: 1:25, 1:87
Famous examples: Madurodam, Mini-Europe, Minimundus

A miniature park is an open-air exhibition of scale models of famous buildings, landmarks and sometimes whole landscapes, built to a consistent scale and arranged for visitors to walk among them. Most miniature parks use a scale of 1:25, large enough for detailed models yet small enough to display dozens of monuments in one visit.

Notable parks

Famous examples include Madurodam in The Hague, Netherlands (opened 1952, featuring Dutch landmarks, and named after resistance fighter George Maduro), Mini-Europe in Brussels, displaying monuments from across the European Union, Minimundus in Klagenfurt, Austria, with models from around the world, and the long-running Bekonscot Model Village in England (opened 1929), the oldest miniature village of its kind. China has large examples such as Beijing World Park and Window of the World in Shenzhen.

Features

Parks often animate their scenes with moving model trains, boats, trams and windmills, and many models are built with extraordinary craft, sometimes taking years to complete. Miniature parks are popular tourist attractions and are frequently associated with charitable causes, as with Madurodam's long-standing support of youth charities.
