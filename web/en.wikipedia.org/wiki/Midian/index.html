Midian

Midian (Hebrew: מִדְיָן, Miḏyān) is a geographical region and a people described in the Hebrew Bible and the Quran. The Midianites are presented as descendants of Midian, a son of Abraham through his wife Keturah (Genesis 25), and are generally located in the northwestern Arabian Peninsula, east of the Gulf of Aqaba, in the region of modern northwestern Saudi Arabia and southern Jordan.

In the Hebrew Bible

Midian figures prominently in the story of Moses, who fled Egypt to Midian, married Zipporah, daughter of the Midianite priest Jethro (also called Reuel), and received his call at the burning bush while tending Jethro's flocks. Relations later soured: the Book of Numbers recounts a war against Midian, and in Judges the Midianites oppress Israel with camel-borne raids until defeated by Gideon. Midianite traders also appear in Genesis as the merchants who bought Joseph and carried him to Egypt. These alternating portraits — in-laws and allies on the one hand, enemies on the other — have long interested scholars.

Archaeology

Archaeologists associate Midian with the distinctive Qurayyah painted ware (once called "Midianite pottery"), a bichrome decorated pottery of the late Bronze and early Iron Ages found in the Hejaz and at sites such as Timna in the Arabah, where an Egyptian temple of Hathor was converted into a tent shrine attributed to Midianite metalworkers. The oasis of Qurayyah is often considered a major Midianite centre.

In the Quran

In the Quran, Madyan is a city or people to whom the prophet Shu'ayb (often identified with Jethro) was sent; its inhabitants are condemned for fraud in weights and measures and destroyed for rejecting the message. The region is also mentioned in connection with Moses' flight from Egypt.
