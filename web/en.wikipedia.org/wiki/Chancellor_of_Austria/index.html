The chancellor of Austria, officially the federal chancellor of the Republic of Austria (German: Bundeskanzler der Republik Österreich), is the head of government of the Republic of Austria. The chancellor chairs and leads the federal government and is the country's chief political executive, although the federal president is the head of state.

Role and appointment

Under the Federal Constitutional Law, the president appoints the chancellor, conventionally the leader of the party best placed to form a government following elections to the National Council. The chancellor proposes the other members of the federal government for appointment. The government must enjoy the confidence of the National Council, which can dismiss the chancellor or the whole government by a vote of no confidence — as happened for the first time in 2019 with the government of Sebastian Kurz.

History

The office traces its origins to the State Chancellor of the Republic of German-Austria in 1918, first held by Karl Renner. The Federal Constitutional Law of 1920 created the modern office. During the authoritarian period of the 1930s, chancellors Engelbert Dollfuss and Kurt Schuschnigg ruled until the Anschluss of 1938. The Second Republic restored the office in 1945, again under Karl Renner. Notable post-war chancellors include Julius Raab, Bruno Kreisky, Franz Vranitzky, Wolfgang Schüssel and Sebastian Kurz, who became the youngest head of government in Austrian history in 2017.

Residence and office

The Federal Chancellery (Bundeskanzleramt) on Ballhausplatz in Vienna's Innere Stadt houses the chancellor's office, directly opposite the Hofburg palace, the president's seat.
