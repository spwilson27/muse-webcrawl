Coco is a 2017 American animated fantasy film produced by Pixar Animation Studios and released by Walt Disney Pictures. Directed by Lee Unkrich and co-directed by Adrian Molina, the film is inspired by the Mexican holiday Day of the Dead (Día de los Muertos). It follows Miguel, a 12-year-old boy who dreams of becoming a musician despite his family's generations-old ban on music, and who is accidentally transported to the Land of the Dead, where he seeks the help of his idol, the late singer Ernesto de la Cruz, to return to his family and lift the ban.

Plot

In the fictional town of Santa Cecilia, Miguel's family of shoemakers forbids music because his great-great-grandmother Mamá Imelda was abandoned by her musician husband. On Día de los Muertos, Miguel, believing the famous singer Ernesto de la Cruz is his great-great-grandfather, "borrows" de la Cruz's guitar from his mausoleum and is cursed — becoming visible only to the dead. In the Land of the Dead, aided by the trickster Héctor, Miguel uncovers the truth: Héctor was de la Cruz's songwriting partner, murdered by de la Cruz, who stole his songs and fame. Reunited with Mamá Imelda, Miguel returns home and sings Héctor's lullaby "Remember Me" to his great-grandmother Mamá Coco, restoring her memories and reconciling the family with music and with Héctor's memory.

Production

Unkrich first pitched the concept in 2010; the team made repeated research trips to Mexico, and the film was announced in 2012. Pixar recruited Mexican cultural consultants and an all-Latino principal cast, including Anthony Gonzalez as Miguel, Gael García Bernal as Héctor and Benjamin Bratt as de la Cruz. Songs were written by Kristen Anderson-Lopez and Robert Lopez, Germaine Franco and others, with a score by Michael Giacchino. The marigold-petal bridge and the vast city of the dead were among the film's most challenging visual achievements.

Reception

Coco premiered at the Morelia International Film Festival in October 2017 and grossed over $814 million worldwide. It won the Academy Awards for Best Animated Feature and Best Original Song ("Remember Me"), along with the Golden Globe, BAFTA and Critics' Choice awards for best animated film. Critics praised its animation, voice acting, emotional story and respectful portrayal of Mexican culture; it is widely regarded as one of Pixar's finest films.
