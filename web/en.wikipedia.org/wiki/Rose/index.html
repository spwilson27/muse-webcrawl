Rose

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A rose is a woody perennial flowering plant of the genus Rosa, in the family Rosaceae, with over 300 species and tens of thousands of cultivars. Prized for their beauty and fragrance — and defended, usually, by prickles on their stems — roses have been cultivated for at least 5,000 years and are among the world's most popular garden and cut flowers, and a universal symbol of love.

Cultivation and history

Wild roses have five petals and produce vitamin-C-rich fruit (rose hips). Garden roses were bred in China and Persia and transformed in Europe after the 18th century, when repeat-flowering China roses arrived; the hybrid tea (from 1867) set the modern form. Growers classify roses as hybrid teas, floribundas, climbers, shrub and old garden roses, among many groups. Roses need sun, rich soil and pruning, and suffer pests such as aphids and diseases like black spot.

Culture

Roses carry rich symbolism: red for love, white for purity, yellow for friendship; the rose was the emblem of rival houses in England's Wars of the Roses and is the national flower of England and the United States. Rose water and attar of roses flavour food and perfume in Middle Eastern and South Asian cuisines and cosmetics, and the rose-growing industry is global, with major production in the Netherlands, Kenya and Ecuador.
