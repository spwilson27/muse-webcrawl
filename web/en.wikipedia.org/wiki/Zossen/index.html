Zossen

Zossen is a town in the district of Teltow-Fläming in Brandenburg, Germany. It lies about 30 kilometres (19 miles) south of Berlin, near the Berlin–Dresden railway line. The town has a population of roughly 20,000.

History

Zossen was first mentioned in documents in the 14th century. During the Second World War, the nearby military complex at Wünsdorf, known as the Maybach I and II bunker complexes, served as headquarters of the German Army High Command (OKH). After 1945, Wünsdorf became a major Soviet military base, sometimes called "Little Moscow", housing the headquarters of the Group of Soviet Forces in Germany. Soviet troops withdrew in 1994, and the area has since been converted in part to civilian use, including the so-called "Book Town" of Wünsdorf.

Geography

The municipality of Zossen comprises several districts, including Dabendorf, Glienick, Kallinchen, Nächst Neuendorf, Nunsdorf, Schöneiche, Wünsdorf, Funkenmühle, Lindenbrück, Zossen proper, and Zesch am See. The Notte Canal flows through the town.

Transport

Zossen station lies on the Berlin–Dresden railway and is served by regional trains providing connections to Berlin. The Bundesstraße 96 and 246 highways pass through the area.
