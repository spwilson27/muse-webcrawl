LacZ

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

lacZ is a gene in the lac operon of the bacterium Escherichia coli that encodes the enzyme β-galactosidase, which cleaves the disaccharide lactose into glucose and galactose. Its study by François Jacob and Jacques Monod was central to the discovery of operons and gene regulation, recognised by the 1965 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Use as a reporter gene

Because β-galactosidase activity is easy to detect, lacZ is one of the most widely used reporter genes in molecular biology:

  • Blue–white screening — colonies carrying a functional lacZα fragment turn blue on plates containing the substrate X-gal; insertion of cloned DNA disrupts the fragment, giving white colonies.
  • Reporter assays — lacZ placed under control of a promoter of interest reports its activity through enzyme activity measured with substrates such as ONPG.
  • Transgenic animals — lacZ expression marks cells and tissues in mouse development studies, visualised by X-gal staining.

Complementation

The widely used lacZΔM15 deletion produces an inactive protein fragment; the small α-peptide encoded by many cloning vectors restores activity by α-complementation, the basis of the colour screen.
