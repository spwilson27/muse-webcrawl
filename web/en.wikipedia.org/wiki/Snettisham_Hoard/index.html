Snettisham Hoard

The Snettisham Hoard or Snettisham Treasure is a series of discoveries of Iron Age precious metal, found near Snettisham in Norfolk, East Anglia, England, beginning in 1948. The hoard consists of metalworking finds, including elaborate gold torcs, and is thought to be connected with the Iceni tribe, later ruled by Boudica.

Discoveries

The first finds were made in 1948 when a field was being ploughed; further hoards were found in 1950 and in subsequent decades, notably in 1990. In total more than a dozen separate hoards were buried in the same small area between roughly 150 BC and the late first century BC. They contained thousands of coins, ingots, jewellery and vessels in gold, silver and bronze.

The most famous single item is the Snettisham Great Torc, a masterpiece of Iron Age goldworking made from multiple strands of twisted gold wires with decorated terminals, weighing about 1 kg. Other hoards included fragmentary material apparently gathered for recycling, suggesting the site may have been a workshop or treasury of the Iceni royal house.

Interpretation

The repeated burial of wealth in one place suggests it was a significant royal or ritual site. The main finds are now in the British Museum, with some material in Norwich Castle Museum.
