Sloan Kettering

Sloan Kettering most commonly refers to the Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (MSK) in New York City, one of the world's leading cancer hospitals and research institutions, and its research arm, the Sloan Kettering Institute.

Namesakes

The name honours Alfred P. Sloan, the longtime chairman of General Motors, and Charles F. Kettering, the inventor and GM research director. In 1945, Sloan and Kettering founded the Sloan Kettering Institute for Cancer Research, which in 1960 merged with the neighbouring Memorial Hospital (founded 1884) to form the modern centre.

Institution

Located mainly on Manhattan's Upper East Side, MSK treats hundreds of thousands of patients annually, trains oncologists and scientists, and conducts extensive clinical trials and basic research in cancer biology, immunotherapy and genomics.
