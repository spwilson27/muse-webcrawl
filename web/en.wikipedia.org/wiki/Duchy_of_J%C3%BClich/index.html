The Duchy of Jülich (German: Herzogtum Jülich) was a state within the Holy Roman Empire in the Rhineland, lying west of the Rhine between Aachen, Cologne and the Meuse, in the modern German state of North Rhine-Westphalia. Its capital was the fortified town of Jülich.

History

Jülich began as a county (gau) in the early Middle Ages; its counts rose in power, and in 1356 Emperor Charles IV raised Margrave William V to ducal rank. From 1423 the duchy was united with the neighbouring Duchy of Berg, and in 1521 Duke John III brought together Jülich, Berg, Cleves and Mark in a major territorial bloc known as the United Duchies.

War of succession

When the last duke, John William, died childless in 1609, the War of the Jülich Succession broke out among rival claimants, a prelude to the Thirty Years' War. The Treaty of Xanten (1614) partitioned the inheritance: Jülich and Berg went to the Palatinate-Neuburg line of the Wittelsbachs, while Cleves and Mark fell to Brandenburg-Prussia.

Later history

Jülich remained with Palatinate-Neuburg and later the Electorate of the Palatinate until French revolutionary forces occupied it in 1794; it was formally ceded to France in 1801 and, after 1815, became part of Prussia's Rhine Province.
