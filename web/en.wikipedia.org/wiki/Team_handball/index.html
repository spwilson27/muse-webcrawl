Team handball

Team handball, also known simply as handball, is an indoor team sport in which two teams of seven players each (six court players and one goalkeeper) pass a ball using their hands with the aim of throwing it into the goal of the opposing team. A standard match consists of two periods of 30 minutes, and the team that scores more goals wins.

History

Modern handball was codified in northern Europe, particularly Denmark, Germany and Sweden, in the early twentieth century. Danish gymnastics teacher Holger Nielsen drew up rules in 1898 and 1906, while German sports teachers Karl Schelenz, Max Heiser and Erich Konigh published a set of rules in 1917. The game was originally played outdoors on a football field with eleven players per side; the indoor seven-a-side version developed later.

Men's handball was first played at the Olympic Games in Berlin in 1936 as an outdoor game, and returned as an indoor sport at Munich in 1972. Women's handball became an Olympic sport at Montreal in 1976.

Rules

The game is played on a court 40 by 20 metres (131 ft × 66 ft), with a goal in the middle of each end. Around the goals are semicircular zones, called creases, with radii of 6 metres. Players may not enter the goal area, though goalkeepers defend from within it. The ball is smaller than a football, allowing players to grip it in one hand.

Players may take up to three steps with the ball without dribbling, and may hold the ball for at most three seconds. Body contact is permitted within limits, but hitting, pushing or tackling an opponent is punished with free throws, two-minute suspensions or disqualification.

Playing positions

The seven players on court consist of the goalkeeper, two wingers, two backs, a centre back and a pivot (line player). Substitutions are unlimited and can be made without notifying the referees, provided players enter and exit through the substitution area.

International competitions

The International Handball Federation (IHF), founded in 1946, organizes the World Men's Handball Championship and World Women's Handball Championship, held every two years. France, Sweden, Denmark, Spain and Germany are among the most successful national teams. The EHF Champions League is the leading club competition in Europe.
