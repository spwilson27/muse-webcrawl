Abdulmejid I (25 April 1823 – 25 June 1861) was the 31st sultan of the Ottoman Empire. Succeeding his father Mahmud II at sixteen, he presided over the Tanzimat era of reform, issuing the Edict of Gülhane (1839) and the Reform Edict of 1856, which promised legal equality to Ottoman subjects regardless of religion.

Tanzimat reforms

Guided by ministers such as Mustafa Reşid Pasha, Abdulmejid's government modernised the army, administration, taxation, and education, and introduced the empire's first paper currency and telegraph lines. The reforms aimed to centralise the state and secure the loyalty of non-Muslim populations.

Crimean War

Abdulmejid drew the Ottomans into alliance with Britain and France during the Crimean War (1853–1856) against Russia. The Treaty of Paris (1856) admitted the empire to the Concert of Europe and guaranteed its territorial integrity, in exchange for the reform promises of the 1856 edict.

Personal life and death

The sultan was known for his interest in Western music and literature and for commissioning the Dolmabahçe Palace, the empire's new European-style court on the Bosphorus, completed in 1856. Like his father, he suffered from tuberculosis and died in 1861 aged 38, leaving the throne to his brother Abdulaziz. Four of his sons — Murad V, Abdul Hamid II, Mehmed V, and Mehmed VI — later became sultans.
