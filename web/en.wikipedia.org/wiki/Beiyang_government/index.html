The Beiyang government was the internationally recognized government of the Republic of China between 1912 and 1928, based in Beijing. It takes its name from the Beiyang Army, the modernized force created under the Qing dynasty official Yuan Shikai, whose officers dominated the state. Although it possessed the formal trappings of a republic — a constitution, parliament, and ministries — real power lay with military strongmen, and after 1916 the government was repeatedly captured by rival warlord cliques.

History

Yuan Shikai era (1912–1916)

Following the Xinhai Revolution, Sun Yat-sen's provisional government ceded the presidency to Yuan Shikai in 1912 in exchange for the Qing emperor's abdication. Yuan suppressed the Kuomintang after the failed Second Revolution of 1913, dissolved parliament, and in 1915–16 attempted to restore the monarchy with himself as emperor. The bid provoked the National Protection War and collapsed within months; Yuan died in June 1916.

Warlord fragmentation (1916–1928)

Yuan's death splintered the Beiyang Army into cliques — principally the Anhui clique of Duan Qirui, the Zhili clique of Feng Guozhang and later Cao Kun and Wu Peifu, and the Fengtian clique of Zhang Zuolin in Manchuria. These factions fought a series of wars (the Zhili–Anhui War of 1920 and the Zhili–Fengtian Wars of 1922 and 1924) while presidents and cabinets rotated rapidly; Cao Kun notoriously secured the presidency in 1923 through open bribery of parliamentarians. Nevertheless, the Beiyang state maintained China's diplomatic representation, joined the Allies in the First World War, and attended the Washington Conference of 1921–22, even as its authority over the provinces waned. The era also saw major cultural movements, including the New Culture Movement and the May Fourth Movement of 1919, sparked by the government's weak response to the Treaty of Versailles' award of Shandong rights to Japan.

End

In 1926 the Kuomintang, allied with the Communists and advised by the Soviet Union, launched the Northern Expedition from Guangzhou. By June 1928 Nationalist forces took Beijing, Zhang Zuolin was assassinated by Japanese officers, and his son Zhang Xueliang pledged allegiance to the new Nationalist government at Nanjing, formally reuniting China and ending the Beiyang era.
