List of national forests of the United States

The United States has 154 protected areas classified as national forests, covering about 188 million acres (about 762,000 km², roughly the size of Texas plus more). They are administered by the U.S. Forest Service, an agency of the Department of Agriculture, for multiple uses including timber, grazing, watershed protection, wildlife habitat and recreation. The system also includes 20 national grasslands and other units. The largest national forest is the Tongass in Alaska; the smallest are tiny units in Puerto Rico (El Toro Wilderness within El Yunque) and elsewhere. Many forests in the eastern United States were assembled from purchased private land under the Weeks Act of 1911, while western forests were mostly reserved from the public domain.

History

The forest reserve system began with the Forest Reserve Act of 1891; the first reserve was the Yellowstone Park Timber Land Reserve. The Organic Act of 1897 defined the purposes of the reserves. In 1905 the reserves were transferred to the new Forest Service under Gifford Pinchot in the Department of Agriculture, and in 1907 they were renamed national forests. The Weeks Act (1911) enabled eastern expansion, the Wilderness Act (1964) created protected wilderness areas within forests, and the National Forest Management Act (1976) governs modern planning.
