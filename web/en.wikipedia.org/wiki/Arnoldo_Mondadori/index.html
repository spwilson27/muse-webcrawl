Arnoldo Mondadori (2 November 1889 – 8 June 1971) was an Italian publisher, founder in 1907 of Arnoldo Mondadori Editore, which became the largest publishing company in Italy.

Early life and founding

Born into a modest family in Poggio Rusco, in the province of Mantua, Mondadori left school early and worked as an apprentice. In 1907, aged seventeen, he founded his publishing venture at Ostiglia, initially printing a socialist newspaper and school materials with a single friend as staff. With the help of a loan and his marriage into the Ghinelli family, he expanded into books for young readers.

Growth of the publishing house

During and after the First World War Mondadori moved the company to Verona and then Milan, publishing children's books, novels and magazines. In the 1930s the firm launched successful ventures including the Italian edition of Walt Disney comics (Topolino, 1932), the Medusa foreign-fiction series (1933), which brought authors such as Hemingway and Faulkner to Italian readers, and the Gialli crime-fiction series, whose yellow covers gave the Italian genre its name. After the Second World War the company added news magazines such as Epoca (1950) and Panorama (1962), and the Oscar paperback series (1965), the first large-scale Italian paperback imprint sold at newsstands.

Legacy

Mondadori remained at the head of the company until his death in Milan in 1971. He is remembered for combining commercial instinct with cultural ambition, industrialising Italian publishing while maintaining relationships with leading authors of his time.
