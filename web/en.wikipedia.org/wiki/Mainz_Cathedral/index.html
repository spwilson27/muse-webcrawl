Mainz Cathedral

Mainz Cathedral (German: Mainzer Dom), dedicated to Saint Martin of Tours, is a Roman Catholic cathedral in Mainz, Germany, and the seat of the Bishop of Mainz. Built of red sandstone in predominantly Romanesque style, it is one of the three so-called Imperial Cathedrals (Kaiserdome) on the Upper Rhine, alongside Speyer and Worms, and one of the most important church buildings in Germany.

History

The cathedral was founded by Archbishop Willigis, who began construction around 975–978; the first building burned on the eve of its consecration in 1009 and was rebuilt over the following decades, with consecration in 1037 under Archbishop Bardo. The church was repeatedly enlarged and altered: the distinctive eastern group of towers and the Gothic chapels and cloister were added in later centuries, and the great central tower received its Baroque-influenced form after a lightning strike in 1767 prompted repairs under Franz Ignaz Michael Neumann. Several Holy Roman Emperors and kings were crowned in the cathedral in the Middle Ages.

Architecture

The cathedral is a three-aisled vaulted basilica with sanctuaries at both ends — an eastern choir of St Martin and a western choir of St Stephen — flanked by tower groups, giving the building its characteristic silhouette over the market square. Inside, it preserves numerous monuments of the archbishops of Mainz, who were among the most powerful prince-electors of the Holy Roman Empire.
