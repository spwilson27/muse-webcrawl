ATM card

An ATM card is a payment card issued by a financial institution that enables a customer to access an automated teller machine (ATM) to withdraw cash, deposit funds, check balances, and perform other banking transactions. Unlike a general-purpose credit card, an ATM card historically operated only within ATM networks and required a personal identification number (PIN) for authentication.

Function

ATM cards carry a magnetic stripe—and increasingly an EMV chip—encoding the cardholder's account details. When inserted into an ATM, the card, combined with the customer's PIN, authenticates the session; the machine then communicates with the issuing bank, typically via interbank networks such as Plus, Cirrus, STAR, or NYCE, to authorize transactions against the linked deposit account.

ATM cards versus debit cards

The boundary between ATM cards and debit cards has blurred. Many cards branded as debit cards also function as ATM cards, and can be used for purchases at merchants either with a PIN or, when carrying a Visa or Mastercard logo, through signature-based credit-card networks. Pure ATM cards, which lack card-network branding, are now relatively uncommon in some markets but were the original form issued from the late 1970s as ATMs proliferated.

Security

Card security relies on the two-factor combination of the physical card and the PIN. Fraud risks include skimming devices fitted to ATMs, shoulder surfing, and card trapping; the migration from magnetic stripes to EMV chips and the growth of cardless ATM access via mobile apps are responses to these threats.
