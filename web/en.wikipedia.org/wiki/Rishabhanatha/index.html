Rishabhanatha

Rishabhanatha (also known as Ṛṣabhanātha, Rishabhadeva, or Ādinātha, "first lord") is the first tirthankara (ford-maker, or spiritual teacher) of Jainism in the present half-cycle of time. Jains regard him as the founder of the faith in this age and as a civilising culture hero who taught humanity agriculture, writing, arithmetic, crafts and social organisation.

Traditional biography

According to Jain tradition, Rishabhanatha was born in Ayodhya to King Nabhi and Queen Marudevi of the Ikshvaku dynasty. He is credited with inventing, among many arts, cooking, pottery, painting and the institution of marriage. He had one hundred sons, of whom the eldest, Bharata, became the first universal emperor (chakravartin) and gives India its traditional name, Bharata; another son, Bahubali, is venerated as a great ascetic. After ruling for an immense span of years, Rishabhanatha renounced the world, attained omniscience (kevala jnana) after a thousand years of austerity, and finally achieved liberation (moksha) on Mount Ashtapada.

Iconography and legacy

Rishabhanatha is usually depicted seated in meditation or standing in the kayotsarga posture, with long hair falling to his shoulders, and is identified by his emblem, the bull. He is also mentioned in Hindu texts as an avatar of Vishnu in some Puranic lists. The colossal statue of his son Bahubali at Shravanabelagola is among the most famous monuments of Jain art.
