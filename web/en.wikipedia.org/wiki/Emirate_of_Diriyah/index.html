The Emirate of Diriyah, also known as the First Saudi State, was a state in the Arabian Peninsula established in 1744 and lasting until 1818. It arose from the alliance between Muhammad ibn Saud, ruler of the oasis town of Diriyah in Najd, and the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab.

Foundation

In 1744 Ibn Saud granted protection to Ibn Abd al-Wahhab, who had been expelled from neighbouring Uyayna for his preaching against practices he regarded as idolatrous. The two swore an oath — the Diriyah pact — uniting political and military power with the reformer's call to restore Islam to what he held to be its original purity. The House of Saud provided the sword; the Al ash-Sheikh, descendants of Ibn Abd al-Wahhab, provided religious legitimacy.

Expansion

From Diriyah the emirate expanded across Najd, conquering Riyadh in 1773, and under Saud ibn Abdulaziz (r. 1803–1814) extended to the Hejaz, capturing Mecca (1803) and Medina (1804), and raiding into Iraq and Syria, including the sack of Karbala in 1802. At its height it controlled most of the Arabian Peninsula.

Fall

The Ottoman Empire, alarmed at the loss of the holy cities, tasked Muhammad Ali of Egypt with destroying the emirate. After a long campaign, his son Ibrahim Pasha besieged Diriyah in 1818; Emir Abdullah ibn Saud surrendered and was executed in Constantinople, and Diriyah was razed. The Saudi state was revived six years later as the Emirate of Nejd, the second Saudi state.
