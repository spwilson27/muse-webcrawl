Moiré pattern

A moiré pattern is a large-scale interference pattern that appears when one opaque ruled pattern with transparent gaps is superimposed on another similar pattern. For the pattern to appear, the two designs must not be completely identical; slight differences in spacing, angle, or position cause new visible bands and fringes to emerge.

Formation

Moiré patterns arise from the interaction of two periodic structures. When two gratings of slightly different pitch overlap, a beat pattern of much larger periodicity results. Rotating one grating relative to the other produces patterns that change dramatically with angle, an effect exploited in measurement instruments.

Occurrences

Moiré fringes appear in photographs of television and computer screens, in scanned printed images where halftone dots interact with the scanning grid, in textiles such as watered silk, and on overlapping security fences. In printing, careful choice of screen angles minimizes unwanted moiré.

Applications

Because moiré patterns magnify small displacements, they are used in strain measurement, optical alignment, interferometry, and document security features. Moiré magnification also underlies certain anti-counterfeiting devices on banknotes and identification documents.
