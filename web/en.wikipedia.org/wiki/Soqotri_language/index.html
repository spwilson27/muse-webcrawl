Soqotri (also spelt Socotri or Suqutri; endonym méthel d-saqátri) is a South Semitic language spoken on the island of Socotra and the neighbouring islands of Abd al Kuri and Samhah in the Socotra archipelago, Yemen. It belongs to the Modern South Arabian languages, together with Mehri, Shehri (Jibbali), Harsusi, Bathari, and Hobyot, and is not a dialect of Arabic, though it has been heavily influenced by Arabic through contact.

Characteristics

Soqotri preserves archaic Semitic features lost in Arabic, including a rich consonant inventory with emphatic and lateral fricatives. It has a rich oral literature of poetry, proverbs, and tales, traditionally transmitted by memory. The language shows dialectal variation between the island's regions, and the speech of Abd al Kuri is sometimes treated as a distinct variety.

Status and documentation

Soqotri has no official status and was historically unwritten; schooling and media on the island operate in Arabic, putting pressure on the language, which UNESCO classifies as definitely endangered. Scholarly documentation began with the Austrian South Arabian expedition of 1898–99 led by David Heinrich Müller, whose texts appeared in the early 20th century. Major modern documentation includes the Corpus of Soqotri Oral Literature (2014–2018) produced by a Russian-led team under Vitaly Naumkin and Leonid Kogan, which published texts with translation and a lexical database, and recent efforts to write Soqotri in an adapted Arabic script for literacy materials.
