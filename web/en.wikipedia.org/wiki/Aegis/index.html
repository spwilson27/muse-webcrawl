Aegis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The aegis (Greek: αἰγίς, aigís), in ancient Greek religion and mythology, is a divine shield or protective garment associated with Zeus and Athena. It is often described as bearing the head of the Gorgon Medusa and fringed with snakes and tassels, producing a dreadful sound and appearance in battle.

In Homer

In the Iliad, the aegis belongs primarily to Zeus, who lends it to Apollo and Athena. Homer describes it as fashioned by Hephaestus, everlasting and ageless, set with a hundred tassels of gold and the Gorgon's head at its centre, surrounded by figures of Strife, Strength, Tumult, and Pursuit. When Zeus shakes the aegis, thunder follows and panic spreads among mortals; Apollo uses it to scatter the Achaean army.

Athena's aegis

In later tradition the aegis is chiefly the attribute of Athena, depicted as a short scaly cloak or breastplate fringed with snakes, with the gorgoneion (Medusa's head) fixed at its centre. According to one myth, Athena received the Gorgon's head from the hero Perseus, who had slain Medusa, and mounted it on her shield or aegis. Artists of the archaic and classical periods regularly portrayed Athena wearing the aegis.

Interpretations

Ancient authors offered various explanations of the aegis. Some connected the word with aix (goat), supposing it originally a goatskin shield; others derived it from a verb meaning "to rush" or "to storm", linking it with the storm cloud of Zeus. Herodotus suggested that Athena's dress and aegis were borrowed from Libyan women's garments. A story in later mythographers tells of a monster named Aegis, a fire-breathing creature slain by Athena, whose skin she thereafter wore.

Legacy

The word survives in modern usage in the phrase "under the aegis of", meaning under the protection, sponsorship, or auspices of a person or organisation. The name has also been adopted for modern defensive systems, most notably the United States Navy's Aegis Combat System, an advanced naval air-defence system, and its land-based counterpart, Aegis Ashore.
