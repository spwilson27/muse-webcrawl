Metamerism (color)

In colorimetry, metamerism is a perceived matching of colours with different (nonmatching) spectral power distributions. Colours that match this way are called metamers. Because human vision reduces spectra to three trichromatic responses, physically different lights or surface colours can stimulate identical cone responses and thus appear the same — under one illuminant, for one observer.

Types

Illuminant metamerism occurs when two samples match under one light source but differ under another, a common problem when fabrics or paints matched in a shop look different outdoors. Observer metamerism arises from differences between individuals' colour vision, and geometric metamerism from changes in viewing angle, relevant to pearlescent and metallic finishes. The degree of mismatch is quantified by a metamerism index.

Practical significance

Metamerism is both a nuisance and a tool. It complicates colour matching in textiles, printing, dentistry and automotive finishes, but it also makes colour reproduction possible at all: displays and prints need only produce metamers of real-world colours, not their exact spectra.
