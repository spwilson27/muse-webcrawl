Johan August Brinell (21 June 1849 – 17 November 1925) was a Swedish metallurgical engineer. He is best known as the inventor of the Brinell hardness test, a method for measuring the hardness of metals that remains in widespread industrial use.

Brinell worked as chief engineer at ironworks in Lesjöfors and later at Fagersta, where his studies of the internal structure of steel during heating and cooling led to important work on phase transformations in steel. He presented his ball-indentation hardness test at the Paris Exposition of 1900.

The Brinell test

In the Brinell test, a hard steel or carbide ball of a specified diameter is pressed into the surface of the material under a standard load, and the diameter of the resulting indentation is measured. The Brinell hardness number is calculated from the load and the surface area of the impression.
