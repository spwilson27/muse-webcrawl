Admiralty Arch forms the eastern gateway to The Mall.

Admiralty Arch is a landmark building in London, incorporating an archway that provides road and pedestrian access between The Mall and Trafalgar Square. Commissioned by King Edward VII in memory of his mother, Queen Victoria, it was designed by Sir Aston Webb and completed in 1912 as part of the Victoria Memorial scheme.

Design and use

The Portland stone building has three arches — the central one reserved for use by the monarch on state occasions — flanked by two wings. It adjoins the Old Admiralty Building, from which it takes its name, and long housed government offices, including for the Cabinet Office. Its Latin inscription reads "In the tenth year of King Edward VII, to Queen Victoria, from most grateful citizens, 1910."

Recent history

In 2012 the government sold a long lease on the building, and it has been redeveloped as a luxury Waldorf Astoria hotel with residences, which opened in the mid-2020s. A curious feature is the "Admiralty Arch nose", a small nose-shaped protrusion on one of the inner arches, of uncertain origin — sometimes said to honour the Duke of Wellington — which mounted soldiers traditionally touch for luck.
