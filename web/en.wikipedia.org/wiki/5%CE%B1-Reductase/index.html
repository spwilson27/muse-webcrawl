5α-Reductase

5α-Reductases, also known as 3-oxo-5α-steroid 4-dehydrogenases, are enzymes involved in steroid metabolism. They participate in the biosynthesis of neurosteroids and are best known for converting the androgen testosterone into the more potent dihydrotestosterone (DHT).

Isozymes

Three isozymes are known in humans: steroid 5α-reductase 1, 2, and 3, encoded by the genes SRD5A1, SRD5A2, and SRD5A3. Type 2 is expressed mainly in the prostate and hair follicles and is essential for normal male external genital development; inherited deficiency causes a form of differences of sex development.

Clinical significance

Because DHT drives prostate enlargement and pattern hair loss, 5α-reductase inhibitors such as finasteride and dutasteride are used to treat benign prostatic hyperplasia and androgenetic alopecia. The enzyme also converts progesterone and other steroids into neuroactive compounds in the brain.
