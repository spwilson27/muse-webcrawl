Non-Resident Indian and Overseas Citizen of India

A Non-Resident Indian (NRI) is an Indian citizen who resides outside India for employment, business, education or other purposes indicating an intention to stay abroad for an uncertain period. An Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) is a foreign citizen of Indian origin registered under the Citizenship Act, 1955, receiving a lifelong visa and many of the privileges of Indian citizens, though without voting rights or the ability to hold certain public offices.

Definitions

For tax and foreign-exchange purposes under FEMA, NRI status generally depends on physical presence: a person resident in India for fewer than a threshold number of days in a financial year (commonly 182 days) is treated as non-resident. NRIs may hold special NRE and NRO bank accounts and invest in India under specified rules. The related category of Person of Indian Origin (PIO) was merged into OCI in 2015.

Overseas Citizenship of India

India does not permit general dual citizenship. The OCI scheme, introduced in 2005, offers a compromise: registered OCI cardholders enjoy a multiple-entry, multi-purpose lifelong visa for India, exemption from police reporting, and parity with NRIs in most economic and financial fields, but they are not Indian citizens, cannot vote, and cannot buy agricultural land.

Diaspora

The Indian diaspora is among the world's largest, with major NRI and OCI populations in the Gulf states, the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and Southeast Asia. Remittances from the diaspora exceed US$100 billion annually, the highest of any country. Pravasi Bharatiya Divas, held on 9 January, celebrates the diaspora's contribution.
