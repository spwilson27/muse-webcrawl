Legalization of cannabis

[Image: Cannabis legality by country]

The legalization of cannabis is the process of removing legal prohibitions on the production, sale, possession and use of cannabis (marijuana). Policies range from decriminalisation — removing criminal penalties for small-scale personal possession — to full legalisation with regulated commercial markets. The movement gained momentum in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries amid debate over the effectiveness and social costs of prohibition.

History

Cannabis was widely criminalised in the early and mid-twentieth century, and the 1961 UN Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs placed it among strictly controlled substances. The Netherlands adopted a policy of tolerated retail sale through "coffeeshops" from the 1970s without formally legalising production. Decriminalisation spread in parts of Europe, Australia and the Americas in subsequent decades.

Full legalisation

Uruguay became the first country to fully legalise recreational cannabis in 2013, creating a state-regulated market. Canada followed in 2018 with the Cannabis Act, becoming the first G7 country to legalise nationwide. In the United States, beginning with Colorado and Washington state ballot measures in 2012, roughly half of the states have legalised recreational use despite cannabis remaining illegal under federal law; many more permit medical use. Malta (2021) and Luxembourg (2023) became the first European Union states to legalise personal recreational use and home cultivation, and Germany enacted partial legalisation with social clubs in 2024. Thailand liberalised in 2022 before moving to re-tighten medical controls.

Arguments

Supporters cite the reduction of arrests and organised-crime profits, tax revenue, product safety regulation and personal liberty; opponents raise concerns about public health, youth use, impaired driving and increased consumption. Medical cannabis legalisation, which preceded recreational reform in many jurisdictions, is a related but distinct policy area.
