Varicocele

A varicocele is an abnormal enlargement of the pampiniform venous plexus in the scrotum, the network of veins that drains the testicles. It results from incompetent valves in the testicular (internal spermatic) vein, allowing retrograde blood flow; around 90% occur on the left side, because the left testicular vein drains at a right angle into the left renal vein. Varicoceles are common, affecting roughly 15% of adult men, and are found in a substantially higher proportion of men evaluated for infertility.

Clinical features

Varicoceles are classically described as feeling like a "bag of worms" above the testicle, enlarging when standing or straining (Valsalva manoeuvre) and decreasing when lying down. They can impair testicular growth in adolescents and are associated with reduced sperm count and quality, probably through raised scrotal temperature and oxidative stress. Most are harmless and require no treatment; repair is considered for pain, testicular atrophy, or infertility with abnormal semen parameters, by surgical ligation (often microsurgical) or radiological embolisation.
