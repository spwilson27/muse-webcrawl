Nikolai Lobachevsky

Nikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky (1 December 1792 – 24 February 1856) was a Russian mathematician and geometer, known primarily for his pioneering work on hyperbolic geometry, also known as Lobachevskian geometry. His achievement showed that Euclid's parallel postulate could not be proved from the other axioms of geometry.

Nikolai Lobachevsky

Born: 1 December 1792, Nizhny Novgorod, Russian Empire

Died: 24 February 1856, Kazan, Russian Empire

Institution: Kazan University

Known for: Hyperbolic geometry

Life

Lobachevsky studied at Kazan University, where he spent his entire career, becoming a professor in 1822 and rector in 1827. He rebuilt the university after a fire, saved its library and collections during a cholera epidemic, and taught a wide range of subjects from mathematics and astronomy to physics.

Hyperbolic geometry

In 1829–1830 Lobachevsky published the first account of a non-Euclidean geometry in which, through a point not on a given line, more than one line can pass without intersecting the given line. He developed the resulting system of geometry in detail, deriving its trigonometry and showing it to be logically consistent. The work was largely ignored during his lifetime — János Bolyai had independently published similar results, and Carl Friedrich Gauss had privately reached similar conclusions — but was recognized after his death, transforming the foundations of mathematics and later informing the geometry of general relativity.
