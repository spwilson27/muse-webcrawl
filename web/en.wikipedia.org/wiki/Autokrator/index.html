Autokrator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Autokratōr (Greek: αὐτοκράτωρ, "self-ruler", from autos, "self", and kratein, "to rule") is a Greek epithet applied to an individual who exercises absolute power, unrestricted by superior authority. It was used in classical antiquity for independent commanders with full authority — the Greek rendering of the Roman title imperator and of the dictatorial powers of generals such as those granted extraordinary commissions.

Byzantine usage

In the Byzantine Empire, autokratōr became a formal title of the senior emperor. From the ninth and tenth centuries, as co-emperors were regularly crowned, the senior ruler was styled basileus autokratōr to distinguish him from junior co-emperors (basileis). The title appears in imperial signatures, on coinage and in acclamations, and emphasised that the emperor held power in his own right, answerable to God alone.

Later usage

The term passed into other languages through the Byzantine and classical traditions. In Russian, samoderzhets ("autocrat") was the calque used in the imperial title "Emperor and Autocrat of all the Russias," borne by the tsars until 1917. The English word autocrat and the concept of autocracy derive from the same root.
