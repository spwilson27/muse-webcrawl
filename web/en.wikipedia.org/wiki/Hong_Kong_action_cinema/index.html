Hong Kong action cinema

Peak: 1970s–mid-1990s
Key figures: Bruce Lee, Jackie Chan, John Woo, Tsui Hark
Genres: Kung fu, wuxia, heroic bloodshed, wire fu

Hong Kong action cinema is the principal source of the Hong Kong film industry's global fame, known for its highly choreographed martial arts, innovative stunt work and inventive camera techniques, and influential worldwide from the 1970s onward.

Development

Wuxia swordplay films and Cantonese kung fu pictures of the 1960s at Shaw Brothers and Golden Harvest gave way to Bruce Lee's explosive hits (The Big Boss, Fist of Fury, Enter the Dragon, 1971–73), which created a global kung fu craze. Jackie Chan and Sammo Hung fused comedy, acrobatics and dangerous stunts in the 1980s, while John Woo's "heroic bloodshed" gun films (A Better Tomorrow, The Killer) and Tsui Hark's wire-enhanced wuxia defined another wave.

Influence and decline

Hong Kong choreographers (notably Yuen Woo-ping, who choreographed The Matrix) reshaped Hollywood action. The local industry contracted from the mid-1990s due to piracy, the Asian financial crisis and the rise of mainland co-productions, though its style remains foundational. See Cinema of Hong Kong.
