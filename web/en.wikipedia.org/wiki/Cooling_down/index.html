Cooling down

Cooling down is the practice of performing low-intensity exercise after strenuous physical activity, allowing the body to gradually transition back toward a resting state. It is the counterpart of warming up.

Procedure and rationale

A typical cool-down involves several minutes of progressively easier aerobic activity — for example, slowing from a run to a jog to a walk — often followed by gentle stretching. Continuing light muscle contractions helps the skeletal muscle pump return blood from the limbs to the heart, reducing venous pooling that can cause dizziness or fainting if exercise stops abruptly. Heart rate and breathing decline gradually, and body temperature begins to normalize.

Evidence

Research on injury prevention and muscle soreness is mixed: cooling down appears to have little effect on delayed onset muscle soreness, but it does promote recovery of heart rate and blood lactate clearance when active rather than passive, and it is widely recommended for cardiovascular safety, particularly in older exercisers and those with heart disease. The stretching component may help maintain flexibility and range of motion.
