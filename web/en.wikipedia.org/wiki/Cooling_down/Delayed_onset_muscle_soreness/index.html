Delayed onset muscle soreness

Delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) is the pain and stiffness felt in muscles several hours to days after unaccustomed or strenuous exercise. The soreness typically begins 6–12 hours after exercise, peaks between 24 and 72 hours, and subsides within about a week.

Mechanism

DOMS is caused primarily by eccentric exercise — contractions in which the muscle lengthens under load, such as the lowering phase of a biceps curl or downhill running. Eccentric contractions produce microscopic damage to muscle fibres and surrounding connective tissue, followed by inflammation that sensitizes nociceptors. Contrary to a persistent popular belief, lactic acid is not the cause; lactate is cleared from muscle within about an hour of exercise.

Repeated bout effect

A single bout of eccentric exercise confers a protective adaptation: repeating the same exercise within several weeks produces much less soreness and damage. This "repeated bout effect" is one reason progressive training programs introduce new loads gradually.

Treatment and prevention

No treatment reliably cures DOMS, though massage, cold-water immersion, compression garments and light exercise may modestly reduce symptoms, and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs provide partial relief. Stretching before or after exercise has little preventive effect. DOMS is generally benign and distinct from the sharp pain of an acute injury or strain.
