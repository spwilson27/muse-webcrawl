Stretching

Stretching is a form of physical exercise in which a specific muscle or tendon (or muscle group) is deliberately flexed or stretched in order to improve the muscle's felt elasticity and achieve comfortable muscle tone. The result is a feeling of increased muscle control, flexibility, and range of motion. Stretching is also used therapeutically to alleviate cramps and to improve function in daily activities by increasing range of motion.

In its most basic form, stretching is a natural and instinctive activity; it is performed by humans and many other animals. It can be accompanied by yawning. Stretching often occurs instinctively after waking from sleep, after long periods of inactivity, or after exiting confined spaces and areas. Increasing flexibility through stretching is one of the basic tenets of physical fitness. It is common for athletes to stretch before (as warming up) and after exercise in an attempt to reduce risk of injury and increase performance.

Types of stretching

  • Static stretching — holding a stretch position for a period of time, commonly 15 to 60 seconds.
  • Dynamic stretching — moving parts of the body through a full range of motion in a controlled manner.
  • Ballistic stretching — using momentum and bouncing movements to force a limb beyond its normal range of motion; generally discouraged because of injury risk.
  • Passive stretching — using an external force, such as a partner, strap, or gravity, to hold the stretch.
  • Active stretching — holding a position using only the strength of the opposing muscles.
  • Proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation (PNF) — techniques that combine contraction and relaxation of the target muscle group.

Physiology

Studies have shed light on the function of a large protein within the myofibrils of skeletal muscles named titin in stretching. When a muscle is stretched, sensory receptors called muscle spindles signal changes in muscle length, while the Golgi tendon organ monitors tension. The stretch reflex can be accommodated over time through regular practice.

Stretching and cooling down

Stretching is a common component of a cooling down period after vigorous exercise, alongside low-intensity aerobic activity. Although evidence that post-exercise stretching prevents muscle soreness is weak, gentle stretching after exercise is widely practised to restore resting muscle length and promote relaxation.
