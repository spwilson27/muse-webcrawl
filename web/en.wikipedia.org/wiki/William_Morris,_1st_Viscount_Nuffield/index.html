William Richard Morris, 1st Viscount Nuffield (10 October 1877 – 22 August 1963), known as Lord Nuffield, was an English motor manufacturer and philanthropist. He founded Morris Motors in Oxford and brought affordable motoring to interwar Britain with cars such as the Morris Cowley and Morris Minor. He was among the greatest British philanthropists of the twentieth century.

Morris Motors

Morris began by repairing and building bicycles and motorcycles in Oxford before producing his first car, the Morris Oxford, in 1913, using bought-in components. His Cowley factory expanded enormously; the low-priced Morris Cowley of the 1920s made him Britain's leading carmaker. His empire grew to include MG, Wolseley and Riley, and Morris Motors merged with Austin in 1952 to form the British Motor Corporation.

Philanthropy

Having no children, Morris gave away the bulk of his fortune — estimated at some £30 million — largely to medicine and education. Benefactions included the Nuffield Foundation (1943), Nuffield College, Oxford, the Nuffield Institute for Medical Research, and numerous hospital departments, including the Nuffield Orthopaedic Centre.

Honours

Morris was knighted in 1929, created Baron Nuffield in 1934 and Viscount Nuffield in 1938, taking his title from the Oxfordshire village where he lived.
