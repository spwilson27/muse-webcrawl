Campbeltown

Campbeltown (Scottish Gaelic: Ceann Loch Chille Chiarain or Ceann Locha) is a town and former royal burgh in Argyll and Bute, Scotland. It lies by Campbeltown Loch on the Kintyre peninsula. Originally known as Kinlochkilkerran ("head of the loch by the kirk of Ciarán"), it was renamed in the 17th century as Campbell's Town after Archibald Campbell, 7th Earl of Argyll, who granted the site the status of a royal burgh in 1700.

Whisky

Campbeltown was once a major producer of Scotch whisky, with as many as 34 distilleries operating in the 19th century, and it proclaimed itself "the whisky capital of the world." However, a focus on quantity over quality, the collapse of the herring fishing industry, and the closure of the local coal mine led to a severe downturn, and by the 1930s most distilleries had closed. Only three remain active today: Springbank, Glen Scotia, and Glengyle, which produce the Springbank, Hazelburn, Longrow, Glen Scotia, and Kilkerran single malts. Campbeltown remains one of the five recognised Scotch whisky regions, alongside Speyside, Highland, Lowland, and Islay.

Economy and transport

Fishing and agriculture remain important to the local economy, along with tourism and wind turbine manufacturing. Campbell's town is one of the most remote mainland towns in Scotland: the drive to Glasgow is about 137 miles (220 km) via the A83. Because of its isolation, Campbeltown Airport at nearby Machrihanish provides scheduled flights to Glasgow, and the town was briefly linked to Ballycastle in Northern Ireland by ferry.

History

A fortified settlement, believed to have been named Ceann Locha, existed at the site from an early period. Saint Ciarán is associated with a cave on nearby Davaar Island, which contains early Christian rock art. In the 1970s, plans to develop the nearby former RAF Machrihanish airbase, which has one of the longest runways in Europe, as a spaceport attracted some attention, though the site is best known locally as a golfing destination at Machrihanish.
