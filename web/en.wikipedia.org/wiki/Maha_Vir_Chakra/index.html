The Maha Vir Chakra (MVC) (lit. 'Great Warrior Award') is the second-highest military decoration of India, after the Param Vir Chakra, awarded for acts of conspicuous gallantry in the presence of the enemy, whether on land, at sea, or in the air. It may be awarded posthumously. Instituted on 26 January 1950, the decoration replaced the British awards formerly used by Indian personnel, and it can be conferred on members of the armed forces, nursing services, and civilians under military orders.

Design

The medal is a circular silver piece, 1⅜ inches in diameter, bearing on the obverse a five-pointed heraldic star with the national emblem at its centre, and on the reverse the words "Maha Vir Chakra" in Hindi and English with two lotus flowers. It is suspended from a ribbon of half maroon and half white. A bar is awarded for each subsequent act of gallantry.

Notable recipients

The first awards were made for the Indo-Pakistani War of 1947–48. Among the best-known recipients is Brigadier Mohammad Usman, the "Lion of Naushera", who was killed in 1948. Lieutenant Colonel Dhan Singh Thapa, later a Param Vir Chakra recipient, also received the MVC. In total, more than 200 awards have been made, covering the wars of 1947–48, 1962, 1965, and 1971, the Kargil conflict of 1999, and various counter-insurgency operations. A monthly monetary allowance is paid to recipients and, after their death, to their widows.
