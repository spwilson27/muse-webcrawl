Overview

Geoffrey Manning Bawa (1919–2003) was a Sri Lankan architect who became the most influential Asian architect of his generation and the father of "tropical modernism" — a modernism of courtyards, verandas, pitched roofs and framed landscapes suited to monsoon climates. Trained first as a lawyer, he qualified in architecture in London (AA, 1956) aged 38 and returned to Ceylon to build an oeuvre of houses, hotels, schools and public buildings.

Works and legacy

Bawa's life's work was his country estate at Lunuganga (begun 1948), a composed landscape of terraces, vistas and follies near Bentota. His Kandalama Hotel (1991–94) wraps a kilometre of corridor around a rock outcrop above a jungle reservoir, an icon of eco-architecture; he also designed Sri Lanka's Parliament at Kotte (1979–82) as an island pavilion in a lake. The 1986 Aga Khan Chairman's Award recognised his contribution. Lunuganga and his Colombo home (No. 11, 33rd Lane) are now house-museums run by the Bawa Trust.
