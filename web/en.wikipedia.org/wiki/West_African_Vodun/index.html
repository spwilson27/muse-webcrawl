West African Vodún

West African Vodún (also spelled Vodun, Vodon, Voudou or Vudu) is an African traditional religion practised by the Fon, Ewe and related peoples of Benin, Togo and Ghana in West Africa. The word vodún in the Fon language means "spirit" or "deity". The religion venerates a pantheon of spirits (also called vodun) mediating between humanity and a remote creator divinity, Mawu-Lisa, and it is recognised as an official religion in Benin, where National Vodun Day is celebrated on 10 January.

Beliefs and practices

Practitioners believe that the spirits govern natural forces and aspects of life: Sakpata is associated with the earth and smallpox, Xêvioso (Hevioso) with thunder, Mami Wata with water, and the serpent Dan with movement and fluidity. Worship takes place at shrines and convents where initiates undergo training, and rituals involve drumming, dance, offerings and spirit possession. The Fa (Ifa) divination system guides decisions and interprets the will of the spirits. Ancestor veneration is central, with ceremonies honouring the dead, including the Egungun festivals.

Diaspora

Enslaved Africans carried Vodun to the Americas, where it gave rise to Haitian Vodou, Louisiana Voodoo, and influenced Cuban Santería (Regla de Ocha) and Brazilian traditions: Fon religion merged with Yoruba orisha worship and Catholicism in the Caribbean and Brazil's Candomblé Jeje. Despite lurid portrayals in Western media, Vodun is a structured world religion with millions of adherents, and has been studied extensively by anthropologists and historians of religion.
