Victim blaming

Victim blaming occurs when the victim of a crime or any wrongful act is held entirely or partially at fault for the harm that befell them. There is historical and current prejudice against the victims of domestic violence and sex crimes, such as the greater tendency to blame victims of rape than victims of other crimes. The study of victimology seeks to mitigate the prejudice against victims, and the perception that victims are in any way responsible for the actions of offenders.

History of the term

The phrase "blaming the victim" was coined by sociologist William Ryan in his 1971 book of the same name, which criticised explanations of poverty that located its causes in the behaviour and culture of poor families rather than in social structures. The concept is linked to Melvin Lerner's just-world hypothesis, the cognitive bias that leads people to believe the world is fundamentally fair and that individuals therefore get what they deserve.

Secondary victimisation

Victims may suffer "secondary victimisation" through the reactions of institutions and individuals with whom they interact after an offence — for example, insensitivity by police, courts, or medical personnel, or disbelief and minimisation by family and community. Victim blaming is associated with reduced reporting of crime, poorer mental-health outcomes for victims, and rape myths that shift responsibility from perpetrators to victims.

Responses

Protest movements such as SlutWalk, which began in Toronto in 2011 after a police officer suggested women could avoid sexual assault by not dressing "like sluts", have sought to challenge victim-blaming attitudes. Many jurisdictions have introduced rape shield laws, specialist training for investigators, and victim-support services in response.
