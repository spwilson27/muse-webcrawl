Rio Arriba County is a county in the U.S. state of New Mexico. Its name is Spanish for "upper river", referring to the upper course of the Rio Grande, which flows through the county. As of the 2020 census, the population was 40,363. The county seat is Tierra Amarilla, while the largest city is Española, part of which also lies in Santa Fe County.

Geography

Rio Arriba County covers about 5,896 square miles in north-central New Mexico, bordering Colorado to the north. The terrain ranges from the high desert valley of the Rio Grande and its tributary the Rio Chama to forested mountains in the San Juan range and the Tusas Mountains. Elevations rise above 10,000 feet on peaks such as Truchas Peak near the Santa Fe County line. Significant water features include El Vado Lake, Heron Lake and Abiquiu Lake reservoirs on the Rio Chama.

History

The region has been inhabited for centuries by Pueblo peoples, and parts of the Ohkay Owingeh and Santa Clara Pueblo reservations lie within the county. Spanish colonists settled the area in the 18th century along the Rio Grande and Rio Chama valleys, establishing communities such as San Juan de los Caballeros in 1598 nearby. Rio Arriba County was one of the original counties created by the New Mexico territorial legislature in 1852. The county was the site of the Tierra Amarilla courthouse raid of 1967, when activist Reies Tijerina and the Alianza Federal de Mercedes asserted Hispano land-grant claims.

Communities and culture

Communities include Española, Tierra Amarilla, Chama, Abiquiú — long associated with artist Georgia O'Keeffe, whose home and studio are preserved there — and the village of Chimayó on the county's southern edge, famous for its Santuario and woven textiles. The population is majority Hispanic, with significant Native American communities, and Spanish remains widely spoken. The economy rests on agriculture, ranching, government services, tourism and commuting to Los Alamos National Laboratory.
