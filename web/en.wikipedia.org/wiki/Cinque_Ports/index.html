Cinque Ports

The Cinque Ports (pronounced "sink ports") are a confederation of coastal towns in south-east England, originally formed for military and trade purposes in the Norman period. In return for furnishing ships and men for the Crown's naval service — in an age before a standing navy — the ports received extensive privileges, including self-government, exemption from many taxes, and their own courts.

The five ports

The original five head ports (from the Old French cinq, "five") were:

  • Hastings
  • New Romney
  • Hythe
  • Dover
  • Sandwich

Later, Rye and Winchelsea joined as "Ancient Towns", and numerous other communities were attached as limbs or corporate members, among them Deal, Ramsgate, Faversham, Folkestone, Margate and Tenterden.

Duties and privileges

The confederation's ship service traditionally required the ports to provide 57 ships, each with a crew of 21 men and a boy, for 15 days a year at their own expense — later commuted into money payments. Barons of the Cinque Ports enjoyed ceremonial privileges, including carrying the canopy over the monarch at coronations. The confederation was headed by the Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports, who was also constable of Dover Castle; notable holders include the Duke of Wellington, Winston Churchill and Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother.

Decline

The confederation's importance declined from the 14th century as harbours silted (New Romney shrank after the great storm of 1287 diverted the Rother), ships grew larger than the old ports could handle, and a professional Royal Navy emerged. The confederation nevertheless survives today as a ceremonial body and historical institution centred on its mayors and its Lord Warden, whose official residence is Walmer Castle.
