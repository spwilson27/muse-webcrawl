Multiple feedback topology

The multiple feedback topology is an electronic filter circuit topology used to implement active filters — low-pass, high-pass, and band-pass — with a single operational amplifier and as few as five passive components (three resistors and two capacitors in the basic form).

Operation

As its name suggests, the circuit provides two feedback paths around the op-amp: one through a resistor and one through a capacitor network. The configuration is inverting, produces a second-order (two-pole) response, and allows independent adjustment of gain, cutoff frequency, and quality factor Q — though with more component interaction than the rival Sallen–Key topology.

Characteristics

Multiple feedback filters offer low sensitivity to component variations at high Q, good stopband performance, and low output impedance, making them common in audio and instrumentation designs. Their main disadvantages are an inverting output, a practical limit on achievable gain and Q, and design equations more involved than those of Sallen–Key.
