Norwegian architecture

Norwegian architecture has evolved in response to the country's climate, terrain and available materials, notably timber and stone.

History

Medieval Norway is famed for its stave churches — wooden churches built with post-and-beam construction, around 28 of which survive, including Borgund. Hanseatic Bryggen in Bergen preserves colourful wooden warehouses. Later centuries brought log farmhouses, turf roofs, and Danish-influenced town houses.

Modern era

Twentieth-century Norway embraced functionalism, and contemporary landmarks include the Oslo Opera House (2008) by Snøhetta, with its sloping marble roof rising from the fjord, and Sverre Fehn's pavilion work, which won the Pritzker Prize in 1997.
