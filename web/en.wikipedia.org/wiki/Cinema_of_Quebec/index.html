Cinema of Quebec

The cinema of Quebec is the predominantly French-language film industry of the Canadian province of Quebec, distinct within Canadian cinema for its cultural identity, star system and strong domestic audiences.

Cinema of Quebec
Language: French (mainly)
Key directors: Gilles Carle, Denys Arcand, Xavier Dolan, Denis Villeneuve
Awards: Prix Iris (formerly Jutra Awards)

History

Early production was shaped by the National Film Board of Canada and direct cinema documentaries of the 1960s Quiet Revolution, including works by Pierre Perrault and Michel Brault.

Features and acclaim

Denys Arcand's The Decline of the American Empire (1986) and The Barbarian Invasions (2003) — the latter winning the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film — achieved international success. Other notable films include C.R.A.Z.Y., Incendies (directed by Denis Villeneuve), and Xavier Dolan's Mommy (2014).

Industry

Quebec films routinely take a large share of the province's box office, a rarity for non-English cinema in North America. Funding comes from Telefilm Canada, SODEC and provincial tax credits.
