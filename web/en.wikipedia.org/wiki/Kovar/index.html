Kovar (trademark of CRS Holdings, a subsidiary of Carpenter Technology) is a nickel–cobalt ferrous alloy compositionally identical to Fernico, designed to be compatible with the thermal expansion characteristics of borosilicate glass (such as Pyrex). It serves as an electroplated conductor carried through the glass envelope of a radio valve (vacuum tube), early X-ray tubes, and much of modern semiconductor packaging.

Properties

Kovar's coefficient of thermal expansion, approximately 5–6 × 10⁻⁶/K between 30 °C and 400 °C, closely matches that of borosilicate glass (≈3.3 × 10⁻⁶/K for Pyrex, with hard sealing glasses engineered nearer to Kovar's value). This match allows strong, hermetic glass-to-metal seals that survive thermal cycling without cracking the glass or breaking the seal.

Applications

Kovar is used for the feedthrough pins and headers of transistors, diodes, integrated-circuit packages (especially hermetic metal and side-brazed ceramic packages), microwave tubes, and for sealing into glass envelopes in lamps and scientific instruments. Before sealing, the alloy is typically oxidised in a controlled manner so that its surface oxide bonds chemically to the molten glass.

Related controlled-expansion alloys include Invar (very low expansion), Alloy 42 and Fernico. Kovar itself is a registered trademark; the standardised material is designated ASTM F15 / UNS K94610.
