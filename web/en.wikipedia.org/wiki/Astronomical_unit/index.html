Astronomical unit

Symbolau

The astronomical unit (symbol: au) is a unit of length defined as exactly 149,597,870,700 metres since 2012. Historically it was the average distance between Earth and the Sun, approximately the semi-major axis of Earth's orbit. It is used mainly for measuring distances within the Solar System.

History

Estimates date to Aristarchus of Samos. Transits of Venus in 1761 and 1769, and later radar measurements of Venus, progressively refined the value. In 2012 the International Astronomical Union fixed the au to an exact number of metres, decoupling it from the changing measured solar mass.

Examples

Mercury orbits at about 0.39 au, Jupiter at 5.2 au, Neptune at 30 au. Light takes about 8 minutes 19 seconds to travel 1 au. Larger distances use the light-year (about 63,241 au) and the parsec (about 206,265 au).
