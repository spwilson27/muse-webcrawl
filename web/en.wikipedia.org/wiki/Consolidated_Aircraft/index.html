The Consolidated Aircraft Corporation was an American aircraft manufacturer founded in 1923 by Reuben Fleet in Buffalo, New York. It became one of the largest United States aircraft producers of the Second World War before merging with Vultee Aircraft in 1943 to form Consolidated Vultee, better known as Convair.

History

Consolidated began by building training aircraft and acquired the Thomas-Morse Aircraft company in 1929. Its successful Commodore and Admiral flying boats for the US Navy led to the PBY Catalina, first flown in 1935, which became the most numerous flying boat of the war, used for patrol, bombing, and air-sea rescue. In 1935 the company moved to San Diego, California, for its flying weather and harbour. Consolidated's Model 32 became the B-24 Liberator heavy bomber, built in greater numbers (over 18,000, including licensees such as Ford's Willow Run plant) than any other American combat aircraft of the war; the company also produced the B-32 Dominator and the PB4Y Privateer.

Legacy

Through Convair, the firm's lineage continued into the jet age with aircraft such as the B-36 Peacemaker and F-102 Delta Dagger, and into the Atlas missile and space programmes.
