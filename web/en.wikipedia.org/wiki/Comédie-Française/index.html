Comédie-Française

The Comédie-Française (French: [kɔmedi fʁɑ̃sɛːz]), also known as the Théâtre-Français and popularly as la Maison de Molière ("the House of Molière"), is one of the few state theatres in France and the country's only state theatre with a permanent troupe of actors. Founded by decree of Louis XIV on 21 October 1680, it merged the troupe of the Hôtel Guénégaud with that of the Hôtel de Bourgogne, joining the heirs of Molière's company — Molière himself had died in 1673 — into a single company that holds claim to being the oldest still-active national theatre company in the world.

The troupe

The company is organized on a system established in its early centuries: actors join first as pensionnaires (salaried boarders) and may, by vote of the existing members, rise to sociétaires, who share in the company's profits and governance; the most senior sociétaire is the doyen (dean). The repertoire centers on the French classical canon — Molière above all, along with Racine, Corneille, Marivaux, Beaumarchais, and Musset — while also staging modern and foreign works.

History and home

After periods in various Paris venues and a turbulent Revolutionary era (during which the company was split and, in 1793, its actors imprisoned), the troupe reunified in 1799 in the theatre near the Palais-Royal known as the Salle Richelieu, which remains its home. The chair in which Molière collapsed during a performance of Le Malade imaginaire shortly before his death is preserved and displayed in the lobby. The Comédie-Française today also performs at two additional Paris venues, the Théâtre du Vieux-Colombier and the Studio-Théâtre, and tours internationally.
