Ras Al Khaimah

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ras Al Khaimah (Arabic: رأس الخيمة, "top of the tent") is the northernmost of the seven emirates that make up the United Arab Emirates. Its capital is the city of the same name. The emirate covers about 2,500 square kilometres and includes a fertile coastal plain, the rugged Hajar Mountains — including Jebel Jais, the UAE's highest peak — and an exclave in the south of the country.

History

The area has been inhabited for millennia; the port town of Julfar was a noted medieval trading centre, famous for pearling and maritime commerce, and was associated with the legendary navigator Ahmad ibn Majid. The ruling Al Qasimi family came into conflict with Britain in the early 19th century, leading to British expeditions and the maritime treaties that created the Trucial States. Ras Al Khaimah joined the federation of the United Arab Emirates in February 1972, a few months after the other emirates.

Economy

Without large oil reserves, the emirate developed industry — cement, ceramics (RAK Ceramics is among the world's largest ceramics manufacturers) and pharmaceuticals — along with free zones, tourism and real estate. Mountain tourism at Jebel Jais, home to a long zipline attraction, beaches and archaeological sites are promoted draws for visitors.
