Khorat Plateau

Location: Northeastern Thailand (Isan) and central Laos
Area: ~155,000 km²
Elevation: 90–300 m

The Khorat Plateau (also Korat Plateau) is a vast elevated plain in northeastern Thailand, corresponding roughly to the region of Isan. It is bounded by the Phetchabun Mountains to the west, the Annamite Range to the east, and the Dangrek Mountains to the south.

Geography

The plateau is underlain by Mesozoic sandstone and siltstone, and its soils include large saline areas. It is drained by the Mun and Chi rivers, which flow east to the Mekong. The relatively dry climate and poor soils make the region Thailand's poorest agriculturally, though jasmine rice is grown widely.

Prehistory

The plateau hosts major archaeological sites, including Ban Chiang, famous for early bronze metallurgy and painted pottery, and numerous Khmer-period sanctuaries such as Phimai.
