Diederik Korteweg

Born31 March 1848, 's-Hertogenbosch, Netherlands
Died10 May 1941, Amsterdam
Known forKorteweg–de Vries equation

Diederik Johannes Korteweg (31 March 1848 – 10 May 1941) was a Dutch mathematician. He is best known for his work with his doctoral student Gustav de Vries on the Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation, a nonlinear partial differential equation describing shallow-water waves and solitons.

Life and work

Korteweg studied at Delft and Amsterdam and became a professor of mathematics at the University of Amsterdam, where he was the first occupant of a chair in mathematics, mechanics and astronomy. He supervised the 1895 dissertation of de Vries in which the KdV equation was derived. He also worked on capillary phenomena and edited the works of Christiaan Huygens.

Legacy

The KdV equation became central to the theory of integrable systems after the discovery of solitons in the 1960s. The Korteweg-de Vries Institute for Mathematics at the University of Amsterdam is named after him.
