Urk


Urk harbour and lighthouse

Urk is a municipality and fishing village in the province of Flevoland in the Netherlands, with a population of around 21,000. For a thousand years it was an island — first in the Zuiderzee and then, after the Afsluitdijk (1932), in the freshwater IJsselmeer — until the Noordoostpolder reclamation joined it to the mainland in the 1940s. It remains the oldest and most distinctive community of the polder region.

History

Urk is first mentioned in a donation deed of 966. As an island it sat astride the shipping route to Amsterdam, and its fishermen, pilots and merchants were prominent on the Zuiderzee. The islanders developed a close-knit, strongly church-going culture, a distinctive dialect (Urks, among the most divergent of Dutch dialects), traditional costume still worn by some older residents, and a famous fishery. Friday fish auctions and the monument to fishermen lost at sea on the harbour wall commemorate the many Urkers drowned over the centuries.

Economy

Although no longer an island, Urk retains one of the largest fishing fleets and fish-processing sectors in the Netherlands; its fish auction is among the biggest in Europe. The community is noted for high birth rates, strong family businesses, and its male-voice choir tradition.
