Los Angeles County, officially the County of Los Angeles and sometimes abbreviated as L.A. County, is the most populous county in the United States, with roughly ten million residents. Its county seat, Los Angeles, is the second-most populous city in the country. The county contains 88 incorporated cities and many unincorporated areas; more than one-quarter of California's population lives within it.

Geography

The county covers 4,753 square miles (12,310 km²) of Southern California, from the Pacific coast at Malibu and the Palos Verdes Peninsula to the San Gabriel Mountains and the western Mojave Desert. It includes the Channel Islands of San Clemente and Santa Catalina. Elevations range from sea level to Mount San Antonio (Mount Baldy) at 10,064 feet (3,068 m). Major regions include the Los Angeles Basin, the San Fernando and San Gabriel valleys, and the Antelope Valley.

History

Los Angeles was one of California's original 27 counties, created in 1850 at statehood, with territory that originally included what are now Kern, Orange, San Bernardino, Riverside, Ventura and other counties. The arrival of the railroads in the 1870s–80s and the discovery of oil triggered explosive growth, and by the twentieth century the county had become the centre of the American film and aerospace industries.

Government and economy

The county is governed by a five-member Board of Supervisors, each representing a district of about two million people, making it one of the largest local governments in the United States. Its economy — larger than that of most U.S. states — rests on entertainment, international trade through the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach, healthcare, technology, manufacturing and tourism.
