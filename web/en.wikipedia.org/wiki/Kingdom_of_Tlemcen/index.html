Kingdom of Tlemcen

The Kingdom of Tlemcen was a Berber kingdom in northwestern Algeria, centred on the city of Tlemcen, ruled by the Zayyanid dynasty from the 1230s until the mid-16th century. Founded by Yaghmurasen Ibn Zyan after the collapse of Almohad rule, it controlled key trans-Saharan trade routes linking the Mediterranean to the Sudan.

History

The kingdom repeatedly defended itself against the Marinids of Fez to the west and the Hafsids of Tunis to the east, enduring several sieges of Tlemcen itself. It prospered on trade in gold, slaves and textiles, and its court patronised architecture and scholarship. In the 16th century the kingdom fell between the expanding Ottoman regency of Algiers and Spanish outposts on the coast, and Zayyanid rule ended in the 1550s.

Heritage

Tlemcen's Great Mosque and the ruins of Mansura preserve monuments of the period, and the city remains a centre of Andalusi music and culture.
