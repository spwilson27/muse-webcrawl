Toyota

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A white Toyota Corolla sedan on a city street]
A modern Toyota Corolla.

Toyota Motor Corporation is a Japanese multinational automotive manufacturer headquartered in Toyota City, Aichi. It is the world\'s largest automaker by sales volume (in most recent years), selling around 10–11 million vehicles annually including its Daihatsu and Hino subsidiaries. Toyota is famed for the Toyota Production System (\'lean\', just-in-time), the best-selling Corolla nameplate, the Lexus luxury brand, and pioneering hybrid cars with the Prius (1997).

History

Kiichiro Toyoda founded Toyota\'s automobile division in 1936 within his father Sakichi\'s loom company (the source of the name change from Toyoda to Toyota). Post-war Japan\'s constraints bred fuel-efficient small cars; the Corolla (1966) became history\'s best-selling nameplate (over 50 million). The 1957 Toyopet Crown began US sales, and Lexus launched in 1989 with the LS 400.

Production system and quality

Taiichi Ohno and Eiji Toyoda developed just-in-time flow, jidoka (stop-on-defect) and kaizen continuous improvement, transforming global manufacturing far beyond cars. Toyota\'s reliability reputation underpins strong resale values and its dominance in many markets.

Today

Toyota pursues a \'multi-pathway\' strategy — hybrids, plug-in hybrids, battery EVs (bZ series), and hydrogen (Mirai, fuel-cell and hydrogen-combustion experiments) — criticised by some as slow on pure EVs yet commercially successful as hybrid sales surge. It also leads in motorsport from WRC and Le Mans (Toyota Gazoo Racing) to NASCAR.
