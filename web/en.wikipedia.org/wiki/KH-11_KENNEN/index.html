KH-11 KENNEN

The KH-11 KENNEN (later renamed CRYSTAL, and sometimes given the misleading designation Advanced KH-11) is a type of electro-optical reconnaissance satellite first launched by the United States National Reconnaissance Office (NRO) in December 1976. It was the first American spy satellite to use electro-optical digital imaging and to transmit imagery in near real time, replacing film-return satellites. Successive improved blocks have remained in service for decades; the design is commonly believed to resemble the Hubble Space Telescope, with a primary mirror of about 2.4 metres.

Development and design

Developed under the codename KENNEN and the program designation 1010, the KH-11 was built by Lockheed (now Lockheed Martin). Its charge-coupled-device-based digital imaging sensor sent pictures to ground stations via relay satellites, eliminating the need to recover film capsules. Estimates suggest ground resolution on the order of 10–15 cm under ideal conditions from its roughly 250–500 km orbit. The satellites have a long operational life and periodically reboost their orbits with onboard propellant.

Launch history

The first KH-11 was launched on 19 December 1976 from Vandenberg Air Force Base on a Titan IIID. Launches continued through the 1980s and 1990s, with improved "blocks" adding broader spectral coverage and better communications. Later evolutions — sometimes referred to as the Evolved Enhanced CRYSTAL System — were launched on Delta IV Heavy rockets in the 2000s and 2010s under NRO mission designations such as USA-186, USA-224, and USA-245. In 2019 the NRO acknowledged donating two Hubble-class space telescope assemblies, originally built for the program's successors, to NASA; one became the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope's wide-field instrument.

Compromise

In 1978, CIA employee William Kampiles sold a KH-11 technical manual to Soviet intelligence and was convicted of espionage. In 1984, naval intelligence analyst Samuel Loring Morison leaked KH-11 photographs of a Soviet shipyard to Jane's Defence Weekly and was convicted under the Espionage Act. In 2019, former president Donald Trump tweeted a high-resolution image widely identified as coming from a KH-11 (USA-224), declassifying details of the satellite's capabilities in the process.
