Chumming

Chumming is the fishing practice of scattering bait material, called chum, in the water to attract fish to an area. Chum typically consists of chopped fish, fish offal, blood, oils, corn, or breadcrumb mixtures, released slowly — often from a mesh bag or a perforated container hung overboard — to create a scent trail (a "chum slick") that draws target species toward the angler's hooks.

Uses

Chumming is widely used in saltwater fishing for sharks, tuna, mackerel, and snapper, and in freshwater for catfish and carp. Related techniques include groundbaiting in coarse fishing and "berleying" (the term used in Australia and New Zealand).

Regulation and controversy

Some jurisdictions restrict or ban chumming, especially for freshwater species or in catch-and-release waters, because it can condition fish behaviour, pollute water, or attract sharks to swimming areas. Chumming for shark cage-diving tourism is particularly debated: supporters say it enables research and tourism income; critics argue it may alter shark behaviour near beaches.
