Lewis and Clark Expedition

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lewis and Clark Expedition
Dates: May 1804 – September 1806
Leaders: Meriwether Lewis and William Clark
Commissioned by: President Thomas Jefferson

The Lewis and Clark Expedition, also known as the Corps of Discovery Expedition, was a United States expedition (1804–1806) to explore the newly acquired Louisiana Purchase territory and find a practical route to the Pacific Ocean. Commissioned by President Thomas Jefferson soon after the Purchase, it was led by Captain Meriwether Lewis and Second Lieutenant William Clark.

The Corps of about forty men wintered near St. Louis, ascended the Missouri River, crossed the Rockies with the decisive help of Sacagawea — a Lemhi Shoshone woman who guided, interpreted and, by her presence with her infant son, signalled peaceful intent — and reached the Pacific in November 1805. They wintered at Fort Clatsop (in modern Oregon) and returned to St. Louis in September 1806, having lost only one man (to appendicitis).

The expedition mapped the trans-Mississippi West, described hundreds of species new to science, and established American claims and relations — by turns diplomatic and ominous — with dozens of Native nations whose lands it crossed. Its journals remain a founding text of American exploration, and its route is commemorated along the Missouri and Columbia rivers to this day.
