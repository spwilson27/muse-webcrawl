The Arctic Archipelago, also known as the Canadian Arctic Archipelago, is a vast group of islands lying north of the Canadian mainland in the Arctic Ocean. Comprising 36,563 islands and covering about 1.4 million square kilometres, it is one of the largest archipelagos in the world. Most of its territory lies within the territory of Nunavut, with the western portion in the Northwest Territories.

Geography

The archipelago extends some 2,400 km from the mainland to Cape Columbia on Ellesmere Island, Canada's northernmost point. Its largest islands include Baffin Island (the fifth-largest island in the world), Victoria Island, Ellesmere Island, Banks Island, Devon Island, Axel Heiberg Island, Melville Island, Southampton Island, Prince of Wales Island, and Somerset Island. The channels between the islands form the Northwest Passage, the sea route between the Atlantic and Pacific oceans long sought by European explorers.

The Queen Elizabeth Islands, the northernmost group, contain much of the archipelago's permanent ice, including the great ice caps of Ellesmere and Devon islands. The terrain ranges from the high mountains of the Arctic Cordillera in the east, some exceeding 2,600 m at Mount Barbeau, to low-lying sedimentary plains in the west.

Climate and ecology

The climate is polar: long, severe winters, short cool summers, and very low precipitation, making much of the region a polar desert. Sea ice binds the channels for most of the year, though its extent is declining with climate warming. Wildlife includes polar bears, muskoxen, Peary caribou, narwhals, and beluga whales.

History and settlement

The islands were inhabited by Paleo-Eskimo cultures from about 2500 BC and later by the Thule, ancestors of the modern Inuit. European exploration began with Frobisher in the 1570s and peaked during the nineteenth-century searches for the Northwest Passage and for Franklin's lost expedition. Britain transferred sovereignty over the islands to Canada in 1880. Permanent communities are small — the largest is Iqaluit on Baffin Island — and Canada maintains sovereignty patrols, research stations, and the military outpost at Alert, the world's northernmost permanently inhabited place.
