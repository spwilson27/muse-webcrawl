The Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan (PDKI; Kurdish: Partî Dêmokiratî Kurdistanî Êran), also known as the Kurdistan Democratic Party of Iran (KDPI), is a Kurdish political party in Iran, founded in Mahabad in 1945. It is the oldest Kurdish political party in Iran and advocates a federal, democratic and secular Iran in which the Kurdish people enjoy national rights; its long-standing slogan is "Democracy for Iran, Autonomy for Kurdistan". The party is banned in Iran and has waged periods of armed insurgency against both the Pahlavi monarchy and the Islamic Republic.

History

The party was founded on 16 August 1945 by Qazi Muhammad, replacing the earlier Komala. In January 1946, with Soviet backing, the short-lived Republic of Mahabad was proclaimed with Qazi Muhammad as president; when Soviet forces withdrew, Iranian troops retook Mahabad in December 1946 and Qazi Muhammad was hanged in March 1947. The party survived underground and in exile, reviving during the 1979 Iranian Revolution under Abdul Rahman Ghassemlou, when Kurds briefly controlled much of Iranian Kurdistan before being suppressed by the new Islamic Republic in a campaign declared a jihad by Ayatollah Khomeini.

Assassinations and exile

Ghassemlou was assassinated in Vienna on 13 July 1989 while meeting Iranian government representatives; no one was convicted. His successor, Sadegh Sharafkandi, was assassinated with three colleagues in the Mykonos restaurant shooting in Berlin on 17 September 1992; a German court later concluded the killings were ordered by Iran's leadership. The party's headquarters and bases have since been located mainly in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, which has exposed them to Iranian cross-border attacks, including missile strikes on the party's headquarters at Koy Sinjaq in 2018 and during the 2022 Mahsa Amini protests.

Organisation and recent activity

A 2006 split produced a breakaway faction (KDPI), but the two parties reunified in 2022, restoring a single PDKI under the leadership of Mustafa Hijri. The party maintains Peshmerga forces in camps in Iraqi Kurdistan and announced a return to armed activity in Rojhelat (Iranian Kurdistan) in 2015–2016, leading to clashes with the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. It is a member of the Socialist International and works with other Iranian opposition groups; it participated in the broader protest movement following the death of Mahsa (Jina) Amini, herself an Iranian Kurd, in 2022.
