Støð

The stød (Danish: [ˈstøð], literally "push" or "thrust") is a phonetic phenomenon of Danish prosody: a laryngealisation or glottal constriction that occurs in certain stressed syllables. It is phonemic in most varieties of Danish, meaning it can distinguish words — for example, hun "she" without stød contrasts with hund "dog" with stød.

Phonetics

Acoustically, the stød is a period of creaky voice or irregular vocal-fold vibration occurring in the latter part of a syllable's sonorant portion, sometimes ending in a complete glottal stop. It occurs only in syllables with a long vowel, or a short vowel followed by a sonorant consonant, giving the syllable the heavy structure required to host it.

Distribution

Most Danish dialects have the stød, though it is absent from some southern and island varieties, which may instead use pitch distinctions or nothing at all. Comparable features occur in related languages, such as the broken tone in Latvian and the glottalisation found in some Low German and Limburgish dialects. Historically, the stød is thought to correspond to the pitch-accent distinctions preserved as Accent 1 and Accent 2 in Norwegian and Swedish.

Acquisition

The stød is famously difficult for adult learners of Danish, and Danish children acquire it relatively late compared with other features of the language, contributing to the language's reputation as a hard one for young children to segment and learn.
