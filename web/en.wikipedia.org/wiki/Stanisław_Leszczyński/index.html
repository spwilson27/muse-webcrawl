Stanisław Leszczyński

Stanisław I Leszczyński (Polish pronunciation: [staˈɲiswaf lɛʂˈtʂɨj̃skʲi]; 20 October 1677 – 23 February 1766) was King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania twice — from 1704 to 1709 and again from 1733 to 1736 — and later Duke of Lorraine and Bar for nearly thirty years. He was the father-in-law of Louis XV of France, through his daughter Maria Leszczyńska. In exile in Lorraine he became a celebrated enlightened patron whose court at Lunéville and building works in Nancy left a lasting architectural legacy.

King of Poland

Leszczyński was a wealthy magnate and voivode of Poznań when Sweden's Charles XII invaded Poland during the Great Northern War and deposed Augustus II. In 1704 Stanisław was elected king under Swedish protection and crowned in 1705. His position depended entirely on Swedish arms: after Charles XII's defeat at Poltava in 1709, Augustus returned and Stanisław fled into a long exile in Sweden, the Ottoman Empire (where he was held at Bender), and eventually France.

His daughter Maria married Louis XV in 1725, making the ex-king father-in-law of the French monarch. On Augustus II's death in 1733, Stanisław was elected king again by an overwhelming majority of the Polish nobility and slipped into Warsaw in disguise; but Russia and Austria intervened in favour of Augustus III, triggering the War of the Polish Succession. Besieged in Danzig (Gdańsk), Stanisław escaped in peasant disguise, and in 1736 he formally abdicated.

Duke of Lorraine

By the Treaty of Vienna (1738), Stanisław received the duchies of Lorraine and Bar for life, after which they would pass to France. Ruling from Lunéville and Nancy, he became a model enlightened prince: he founded academies and libraries, patronised philosophers and scientists, received Voltaire and Montesquieu, and rebuilt Nancy, creating the celebrated ensemble of Place Stanislas — now a UNESCO World Heritage site — designed by his architect Emmanuel Héré. He wrote philosophical and political essays, including a plan for reforming the Polish Commonwealth. Stanisław died in 1766, aged 88, from burns suffered when his clothes caught fire at a fireplace; Lorraine then passed to the French crown as agreed.

Legacy

Known in Lorraine as "Stanislas the Benevolent," he is remembered as the last duke of an independent Lorraine and as one of the most cultivated monarchs of his century. The baba au rhum pastry is popularly said to have been devised at his court. In Poland he is remembered as a patriot king twice thwarted by foreign intervention.
