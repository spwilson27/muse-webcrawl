Mount Waialeale (Hawaiian: Waiʻaleʻale, "rippling water") is a shield volcano and the second-highest point on the island of Kauai in Hawaii, at 1,569 metres (5,148 ft). Its summit plateau is famous as one of the wettest spots on Earth, averaging roughly 9,760 mm (384 inches) of rain per year, with a record 17,300 mm (683 inches) falling in 1982.

Climate

Trade winds forced up the mountain's steep flanks cool and condense, producing near-constant cloud and rain. The summit is almost always shrouded in mist, and its bogs support a unique high-elevation ecosystem of rare Hawaiian plants, protected within the Alakaʻi Wilderness Preserve.

The "Wall of Tears"

Water draining from the summit plateau plunges down the mountain's flanks in numerous waterfalls, most famously the "Wall of Tears" in the Wailua River headwaters, visible on helicopter tours in clear weather. Streams radiating from Waialeale feed Kauai's major rivers.
