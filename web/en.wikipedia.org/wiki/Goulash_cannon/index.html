Goulash cannon

Goulash cannon (German: Gulaschkanone) is the humorous soldiers' nickname for a military mobile field kitchen, a wheeled cooking unit used to prepare hot food for troops in the field. The name, coined by German soldiers in the First World War, compares the kitchen's tall chimney and horizontal boiler to an artillery piece, and refers to goulash, a staple one-pot dish such kitchens produced.

History

Armies adopted mobile field kitchens in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The Imperial German Army introduced a standardised horse-drawn model, the Große Feldküche Hf. 11, shortly before the First World War; it could be cooked on the move and served around 200 men. Many armies developed similar designs, and the type remained in service, in motorised form, through the Second World War and beyond.

Design

A typical goulash cannon consists of a wood- or coal-fired boiler kettle (around 200 litres), a smaller kettle for coffee or water, storage boxes and a folding chimney, mounted on a two-wheeled limber-like carriage with the cooking section at the rear so meals could be prepared while travelling.

In culture

The term remains common in German for both historic military kitchens and, jokingly, large catering cookers used at festivals and by relief organisations.
