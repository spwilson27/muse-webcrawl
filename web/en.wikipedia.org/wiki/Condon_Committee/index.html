Condon Committee

The Condon Committee was the informal name of the University of Colorado UFO Project, a scientific study of unidentified flying objects (UFOs) conducted from 1966 to 1968 under a contract from the United States Air Force. It was directed by physicist Edward U. Condon of the University of Colorado, and its final publication, the Condon Report (Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects, 1968/1969), concluded that further scientific study of UFOs was unlikely to yield significant results.

Background

By the mid-1960s the Air Force's UFO investigation, Project Blue Book, was under public and congressional pressure. Following hearings and criticism from scientists, the Air Force sought an independent university-based review. After several institutions declined, the University of Colorado accepted a contract of about $325,000 (later supplemented) in October 1966.

The study and controversy

The project examined dozens of UFO cases, including photographic cases, radar reports and sightings by pilots and other witnesses, and reviewed earlier investigations. The project was dogged by internal controversy: a leaked memorandum by project coordinator Robert J. Low appeared to suggest that the outcome was predetermined, leading to the departure or dismissal of several staff members, including David Saunders and Norman Levine. Astronomer J. Allen Hynek, a former Blue Book consultant, was among the critics of the project's approach.

Conclusions and legacy

Condon's summary concluded that "nothing has come from the study of UFOs in the past 21 years that has added to scientific knowledge" and that extended study "probably cannot be justified." A review panel of the National Academy of Sciences endorsed the report. Within months, the Air Force closed Project Blue Book (December 1969), ending the official U.S. government investigation of UFOs. Critics have long argued that the report's case studies, which included a substantial fraction of unexplained sightings, did not support Condon's negative summary, and the report remains a central document in debates over the scientific study of UFO phenomena.
