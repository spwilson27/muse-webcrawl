East Bay

The East Bay is the eastern region of the San Francisco Bay Area and includes cities along the eastern shores of the San Francisco Bay and San Pablo Bay in Northern California, United States. The region has grown to include inland communities in Alameda and Contra Costa counties. With a population of roughly 2.8 million, the East Bay is the third largest subregion in the Bay Area.


Oakland, the largest city of the East Bay

The largest city in the region is Oakland, followed by Fremont and Richmond. The East Bay is home to the University of California, Berkeley, and major employers including Kaiser Permanente, Chevron (in San Ramon), and Lawrence Livermore and Lawrence Berkeley national laboratories. The region's economy spans healthcare, technology, logistics centred on the Port of Oakland, and higher education.

Geography and transport

The East Bay ranges from the bayside flatlands through the Oakland–Berkeley Hills to inland valleys such as the San Ramon and Livermore valleys. Mount Diablo (3,849 ft) dominates Contra Costa County. Transport links include the Bay Bridge to San Francisco, the BART rapid transit system, and freeways I-80, I-580, I-680, and I-880.

History

The area was inhabited for millennia by Ohlone peoples. Spanish colonisation began with the founding of Mission San José in 1797. Oakland was incorporated in 1852 and grew as the western terminus of the transcontinental railroad. The 1906 San Francisco earthquake spurred growth across the bay, and World War II shipyards in Richmond drew large numbers of workers, reshaping the region's demographics. Berkeley became a centre of the 1960s Free Speech Movement and counterculture.
