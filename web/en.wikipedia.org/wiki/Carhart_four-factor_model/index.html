Carhart four-factor model

Carhart four-factor model
AuthorMark Carhart (1997)
FactorsMarket, size (SMB), value (HML), momentum (UMD)
ExtendsFama–French three-factor model

The Carhart four-factor model is an asset pricing model in finance proposed by Mark Carhart in his 1997 paper "On Persistence in Mutual Fund Performance". It extends the Fama–French three-factor model by adding a momentum factor, capturing the tendency of stocks that have performed well in the recent past to continue outperforming in the near term.

The model

The excess return of a portfolio is regressed on four factors:

Rit − RFt = αi + β1(RMt − RFt) + β2SMBt + β3HMLt + β4UMDt + εit

where RM − RF is the market risk premium, SMB ("small minus big") captures the size effect, HML ("high minus low") the value effect based on book-to-market ratio, and UMD ("up minus down", also called WML, "winners minus losers") the momentum effect, based on returns over the prior 2–12 months.

Applications

Carhart developed the model to evaluate mutual fund performance. He showed that apparent persistence in fund returns was largely explained by exposure to the momentum factor and by expenses, rather than by stock-picking skill: funds holding "winner" stocks looked good simply because momentum itself performed well. The four-factor model became a standard benchmark in performance attribution and academic finance, and was itself later extended by the Fama–French five-factor model (2015), which adds profitability and investment factors but omits momentum.
