Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying (or online bullying) is a form of bullying or harassment using electronic means. Cyberbullying and cyberharassment are also known as online bullying. It has become increasingly common, especially among teenagers and adolescents, due to young people's increased use of social media and digital devices.

Definition

Cyberbullying is bullying that takes place over digital devices like cell phones, computers, and tablets. It can occur through SMS, text messages, apps, or online in social media, forums, or gaming where people can view, participate in, or share content. It includes sending, posting, or sharing negative, harmful, false, or mean content about someone else, or sharing personal information to cause embarrassment or humiliation. Tactics include harassment, impersonation, exclusion, outing (doxing), and flaming.

Effects

Research links victimization to anxiety, depression, low self-esteem, academic difficulties, and, in severe cases, self-harm and suicide. Unlike traditional bullying, cyberbullying can reach victims at any time, can spread rapidly to a large audience, and can be difficult to escape or to have removed. Perpetrators may act anonymously and with reduced inhibition.

Prevalence and prevention

Surveys by organizations such as the Pew Research Center have found that a majority of U.S. teens have experienced some form of cyberbullying. Prevention strategies include school anti-bullying programs, digital literacy and citizenship education, parental involvement, platform moderation and reporting tools, and legislation in many jurisdictions criminalizing or addressing online harassment. High-profile cases, such as those of Megan Meier and Amanda Todd, drew public attention and prompted legal reforms.
