Sports nutrition

Sports nutrition is the study and practice of nutrition and diet as they relate to athletic performance. It concerns the type and quantity of food and fluids taken by athletes, the timing of intake around training and competition, and the use of supplements where evidence supports them.

Key principles

Energy needs rise with training load. Carbohydrate is the principal fuel for high-intensity exercise, and athletes are often advised to consume roughly 5–12 grams of carbohydrate per kilogram of body mass per day depending on workload, with strategies such as carbohydrate loading before endurance events to maximise glycogen stores. Protein requirements for athletes are higher than for sedentary people — typically 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day — to support training adaptation and muscle repair. Hydration plans aim to limit body-mass losses from sweating, replacing fluid and electrolytes, particularly sodium, during and after exercise.

Timing

Pre-exercise meals emphasise carbohydrate and low fat/fibre close to competition; during events lasting longer than about an hour, consuming 30–90 g of carbohydrate per hour helps maintain performance; and post-exercise intake of carbohydrate and protein supports glycogen resynthesis and recovery.

Supplements

A small number of supplements have strong evidence of benefit, including caffeine, creatine monohydrate, sports drinks and gels, and (more modestly) nitrate and beta-alanine. Sports-nutrition guidance from bodies such as the International Society of Sports Nutrition and the International Olympic Committee warns about contamination and anti-doping risks in poorly regulated products.
