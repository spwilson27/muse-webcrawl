Alfonso VI (c. 1040/1041 – 1 July 1109), nicknamed the Brave (el Bravo), was king of León from 1065 and of Castile and Galicia from 1072 until his death. He styled himself "Emperor of all Spain" and was the most powerful Christian ruler of the Iberian Peninsula in his time.

Reign

After a civil war against his brothers following the division of Ferdinand I's kingdom, Alfonso united León, Castile, and Galicia. His greatest achievement was the capture of Toledo in 1085, the former Visigothic capital, which greatly expanded Christian territory. The advance provoked the intervention of the Almoravids from Morocco, who defeated Alfonso at Sagrajas (1086) and Uclés (1108), where his only son, Sancho, was killed.

Legacy

Alfonso's court was closely associated with the epic figure of El Cid, whom he exiled twice. He encouraged Cluniac reform, pilgrimage to Santiago de Compostela, and repopulation of the meseta, shaping the medieval kingdoms that became Spain.
