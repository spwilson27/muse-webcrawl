Gorkhaland

Gorkhaland is a proposed state in India demanded by the Nepali-speaking Gorkha population of the Darjeeling hills and surrounding areas of northern West Bengal. Supporters argue that a separate state would protect the cultural identity of the Gorkhas and remedy the economic neglect of the hill region.

History of the movement

Demands for separate administration for the Darjeeling hills date to the early 20th century. The agitation led by Subhas Ghisingh and the Gorkha National Liberation Front (GNLF) in 1986–88, marked by widespread violence, ended with the creation of the Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council. In 2007 the movement was revived under Bimal Gurung's Gorkha Janmukti Morcha (GJM), leading in 2011 to the semi-autonomous Gorkhaland Territorial Administration (GTA). A further prolonged shutdown and unrest in 2017 kept the demand for full statehood in the news.

Arguments and opposition

Supporters cite the distinct language, culture, and history of the Indian Gorkhas, and the region's reliance on tea, tourism, and timber. The West Bengal government and many political parties in the plains oppose dividing the state, and the central government has not conceded statehood.
