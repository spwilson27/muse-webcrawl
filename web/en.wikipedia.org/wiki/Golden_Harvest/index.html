Golden Harvest

Golden Harvest (Chinese: 嘉禾娛樂事業集團) is a Hong Kong film production and distribution company founded in 1970 by Raymond Chow, Leonard Ho and Leung Fung after they left Shaw Brothers. Golden Harvest became the dominant force in Hong Kong cinema in the 1970s and 1980s, launching the international careers of Bruce Lee and later Jackie Chan, and pioneering the model of contracting independent producers and stars on flexible deals rather than keeping them as salaried studio employees.

History

Raymond Chow, Shaw Brothers' former head of production, left in 1970 after a salary dispute and founded Golden Harvest. Its breakthrough came with Bruce Lee's The Big Boss (1971), followed by Fist of Fury (1972) and Way of the Dragon (1972), which shattered Hong Kong box-office records and made Lee a global star; Golden Harvest co-produced his Hollywood vehicle Enter the Dragon (1973) with Warner Bros.

After Lee's death the company nurtured a new generation: the Hui brothers' comedies, Sammo Hung, and above all Jackie Chan, whose Snake in the Eagle's Shadow and Drunken Master (both 1978, made on loan to Seasonal Film) and Golden Harvest hits such as Project A (1983) and Police Story (1985) defined the modern Hong Kong action film. The company also expanded internationally, distributing films abroad and producing English-language titles.

Later years

Golden Harvest listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in 1994. As Hong Kong cinema contracted in the late 1990s and 2000s, the group diversified into cinema exhibition, including in mainland China and Southeast Asia, and film production wound down; its library and brand remain significant assets of Orange Sky Golden Harvest Entertainment.
