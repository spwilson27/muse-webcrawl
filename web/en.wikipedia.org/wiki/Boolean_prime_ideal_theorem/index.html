Boolean prime ideal theorem

In mathematics, the Boolean prime ideal theorem states that ideals in a Boolean algebra can be extended to prime ideals. A variation of this statement for filters on sets is known as the ultrafilter lemma. The theorem is named for the fact that a Boolean algebra's spectrum of prime ideals captures much of its structure.

Statement

An ideal in a Boolean algebra B is a subset closed under finite joins that contains the bottom element and is downward closed. An ideal is prime if it is proper (not all of B) and for any x and y in B, if x ∧ y is in the ideal, then x or y is in the ideal. The Boolean prime ideal theorem (BPI) asserts that every proper ideal in a Boolean algebra can be extended to a prime ideal. Equivalently, for every Boolean algebra there exists an ultrafilter (the complement of a prime ideal) containing any given proper filter.

Relation to the axiom of choice

BPI is provable from the axiom of choice but is strictly weaker: Halpern and Lévy showed in 1971 that BPI does not imply the full axiom of choice. It does imply many useful results, including the compactness theorem for propositional logic, the existence of ultrafilters on any set (the ultrafilter lemma), and Tychonoff's theorem for Hausdorff spaces. It is independent of ZF set theory (assuming consistency).

Applications

Through Stone's representation theorem, BPI guarantees that every Boolean algebra embeds into a field of sets. It is also used in model theory, general topology and functional analysis (e.g. extending positive linear functionals and the existence of Banach limits via dual formulations).
