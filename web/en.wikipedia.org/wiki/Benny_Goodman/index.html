Benjamin David Goodman (1909–1986) was an American clarinetist and bandleader known as the "King of Swing". His orchestra's success in the mid-1930s is generally regarded as the beginning of the swing era.

Career

The son of Jewish immigrants in Chicago, Goodman was a child prodigy who played professionally as a teenager. With Fletcher Henderson's arrangements, his band broke through nationally in 1935 with the radio show Let's Dance and a triumphant engagement at the Palomar Ballroom in Los Angeles. In 1938 his band gave a landmark concert at Carnegie Hall, bringing jazz into America's most prestigious concert venue.

Small groups and integration

Goodman's trios and quartets with Teddy Wilson, Lionel Hampton and Gene Krupa featured virtuoso chamber jazz and were among the first prominent racially integrated groups in American music. Standards associated with him include "Sing, Sing, Sing" and "Stompin' at the Savoy".
