Ride cymbal

Classification: Percussion (cymbal)
Typical diameter: 20 in (51 cm), commonly 18–24 in
Role: Sustained rhythmic pattern

The ride cymbal is a standard cymbal in most drum kits. Rather than the explosive accent of a crash cymbal, it provides a steady, shimmering "ping" pattern that maintains the beat, and is usually the largest cymbal in a basic kit after the bass drum itself.

Sound and technique

The ride is played with the tip of the stick on its bow for a defined ping, on its bell (the raised centre dome) for a cutting, bell-like tone, or on its edge for a washier crash-like sound. Typical sizes range from 18 to 24 inches; jazz drummers often favour larger, thinner rides with pronounced wash and sizzle, sometimes fitted with rivets, while rock rides are heavier with a clearer ping.

Patterns

In jazz, the classic ride pattern (the "spang-a-lang") underpins swing; in rock, eighth- or sixteenth-note patterns on the ride alternate with the hi-hat.
