Potassium is the chemical element with the symbol K (from Neo-Latin kalium) and atomic number 19. It is a soft, silvery alkali metal, even more reactive than sodium, that tarnishes in seconds and bursts into lilac flame on contact with water.

Potassium ions are essential to all living cells — they drive nerve impulses and muscle contraction — and potassium is one of the three primary plant fertilisers (the K in NPK), mined as potash salts. The isotope potassium-40 makes ordinary potassium slightly radioactive.

[Image: Potassium metal reacting on water with a lilac flame]

History

Potassium was the first metal isolated by electrolysis: in 1807 Humphry Davy passed a current through molten potash (potassium hydroxide) and watched globules of the new metal burst through the crust. Its name comes from 'pot ash', the plant ashes leached in pots that gave potash.

Properties

Potassium floats on water (density 0.86 g/cm³) while reacting, melting from its own heat and igniting the hydrogen produced. It burns in a flame test with a distinctive lilac (pale purple) flame.
