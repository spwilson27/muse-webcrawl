Daimler AG

Daimler AG was a German multinational automotive corporation headquartered in Stuttgart, Baden-Württemberg. It traced its origins to the 1926 merger of Benz & Cie., founded by Karl Benz, and Daimler Motoren Gesellschaft, founded by Gottlieb Daimler, which created Daimler-Benz AG and the Mercedes-Benz brand. On 1 February 2022, Daimler AG renamed itself Mercedes-Benz Group AG following the spin-off of its commercial-vehicle division as the independent Daimler Truck Holding AG in December 2021.

History

The company’s predecessor firms built some of the earliest automobiles: Benz patented the Patent-Motorwagen in 1886, while Daimler and Wilhelm Maybach developed high-speed petrol engines. After the 1926 merger, Daimler-Benz became known for Mercedes-Benz passenger cars and trucks. In 1998, under chief executive Jürgen Schrempp, the company merged with the American Chrysler Corporation to form DaimlerChrysler AG, in what was billed as a "merger of equals"; the combination proved unsuccessful, and Chrysler was sold to Cerberus Capital Management in 2007, when the name Daimler AG was adopted. In the 2010s Daimler expanded into electric mobility and mobility services, and under Ola Källenius (chairman from 2019) it moved to split the group, listing Daimler Truck on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange in December 2021.

Brands and operations

At the time of its renaming, the group’s car business comprised Mercedes-Benz, Mercedes-AMG, and Mercedes-Maybach, together with the Smart brand (later a joint venture with Geely) and the EQ electric sub-brand, while its vans division operated as Mercedes-Benz Vans. Financial services were provided by Mercedes-Benz Mobility (formerly Daimler Financial Services). The company was one of the world’s largest manufacturers of premium and luxury cars, employing around 170,000 people at the time of the split.
