Emperor Gaozu of Tang

Emperor Gaozu of Tang (7 April 566 – 25 June 635), personal name Li Yuan, was the founding emperor of the Tang dynasty of China, reigning from 618 to 626. A governor of Taiyuan under the Sui dynasty, he rebelled during the chaos of the Sui collapse and seized the capital Chang'an, proclaiming first a puppet Sui emperor and then, in 618, himself as emperor of the new Tang dynasty.

Founding of the Tang

Li Yuan came from a northwestern aristocratic family closely intermarried with the Sui imperial house. Encouraged by his ambitious second son Li Shimin, he raised an army in 617, allied tactically with the Eastern Turks, and captured Chang'an. Over the next several years his sons, especially Li Shimin and Li Jiancheng, defeated rival claimants and reunified China.

Reign and abdication

Gaozu's reign laid the administrative foundations of the Tang, including reforms of coinage, law, and taxation. Rivalry between his heir Li Jiancheng and Li Shimin culminated in the Xuanwu Gate Incident of 626, in which Li Shimin killed his brothers and forced Gaozu to abdicate. Gaozu lived as retired emperor (Taishang Huang) until his death in 635.
