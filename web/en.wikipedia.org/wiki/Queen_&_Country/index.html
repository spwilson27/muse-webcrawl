Queen & Country is a 2014 British drama film written and directed by John Boorman. It is the sequel to his autobiographical film Hope and Glory (1987), continuing the story of Bill Rohan, now grown up. The title also refers to the expression "for Queen and country", and "Queen & Country" is the name of a long-running comic book series by Greg Rucka about British intelligence officer Tara Chace (published by Oni Press, 2001–2007) — this article primarily concerns Boorman's film.

Plot

Set in the early 1950s, the film follows young Bill Rohan (Callum Turner) as he is conscripted for National Service during the Korean War era. At basic training he befriends the rebellious Percy, clashes with superiors including the rule-obsessed Sergeant Major Bradley (David Thewlis) and the martinet RSM, and falls in love with a troubled young woman he calls Ophelia. The film mixes comedy and melancholy as Bill confronts the absurdities of military bureaucracy.

Reception

Queen & Country premiered in the Directors' Fortnight section of the 2014 Cannes Film Festival and was generally warmly received as a companion piece to Hope and Glory, though criticized by some as slighter than its predecessor.

The comic

Greg Rucka's espionage comic Queen & Country won the Eisner Award for Best New Series in 2002 and is noted for its realistic depiction of the British Secret Intelligence Service.
