The American Academy in Rome is a research and arts institution in Rome, Italy, and one of the leading American overseas centers for independent study in the arts and humanities. Founded in 1894, it awards the annual Rome Prize, which brings together artists, architects, composers, writers, and scholars for residencies of several months to a year.

History

The Academy grew out of the American School of Architecture in Rome, founded in 1894 with support from figures including architect Charles Follen McKim. It merged with the American School of Classical Studies in Rome in 1913. Its main building on the Janiculum Hill, designed by McKim, Mead & White, opened in 1914. The campus also includes the Villa Aurelia and extensive gardens.

Rome Prize

Each year the Academy awards roughly thirty Rome Prizes through national competitions in fields such as architecture, landscape architecture, design, historic preservation, literature, musical composition, visual arts, ancient studies, medieval studies, Renaissance and early modern studies, and modern Italian studies. Fellows live and work at the Academy, pursuing individual projects within an interdisciplinary community.
