Sir Alexander Matthew Busby (26 May 1909 – 20 January 1994) was a Scottish football player and manager, best known for managing Manchester United between 1945 and 1969. A former Manchester City and Liverpool player, Busby rebuilt United after the Second World War, pioneered the club's faith in youth development, and led it through the tragedy of the Munich air disaster to become the first English club to win the European Cup, in 1968.

Rebuilding Manchester United

Appointed in 1945, Busby demanded unprecedented control over team affairs and built a succession of great sides. His young 1950s team, nicknamed the "Busby Babes", won league titles in 1956 and 1957 with one of the youngest squads in English football. On 6 February 1958, returning from a European Cup tie in Belgrade, the team's aircraft crashed on takeoff at Munich, killing 23 people including eight players. Busby himself was gravely injured and twice given the last rites, but recovered.

European triumph and legacy

Busby rebuilt the side around Bobby Charlton, Denis Law, and George Best, winning the FA Cup in 1963 and league titles in 1965 and 1967 before beating Benfica 4–1 at Wembley in the 1968 European Cup Final, ten years after Munich. He was knighted that year. Busby retired in 1969, briefly returning as caretaker in 1970–71, and later served as club president. A statue of Busby stands outside Old Trafford, and the road to the stadium is named Sir Matt Busby Way.
