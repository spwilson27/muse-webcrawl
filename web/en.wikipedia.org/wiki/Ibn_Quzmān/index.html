Abū Bakr Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Quzmān (c. 1078–1160), commonly known as Ibn Quzmān, was a poet of al-Andalus, born and based in Córdoba. He is the most famous author of zajal — strophic poems composed in the colloquial Arabic dialect of Islamic Spain, often mixed with Romance words and phrases.

Unlike the classical qaṣīda, which used formal literary Arabic and elevated themes, Ibn Quzmān's zajals celebrated wine, pleasure, music, and the everyday life of Córdoba, with wit, satire, and self-deprecating humour about his own poverty and perpetual begging from patrons. His surviving dīwān contains about 149 zajals and is a major source for the dialect and mixed-language culture of medieval Iberia.

Significance

Ibn Quzmān's verses, written largely in the vernacular with Romance refrains and vocabulary, are important evidence for the linguistic history of the Iberian Peninsula and for the debate over the relationship between Andalusi strophic poetry, the kharja tradition, and the early troubadour lyric of southern France.
