Vanity Fair (novel)


Thackeray's illustration of Becky Sharp

Vanity Fair is a novel by the English author William Makepeace Thackeray, which follows the lives of Becky Sharp and Amelia Sedley amid their friends and families during and after the Napoleonic Wars. It was first published as a 19-volume monthly serial (January 1847 to July 1848), carrying the subtitle Pen and Pencil Sketches of English Society, and illustrated by Thackeray himself. A single-volume edition followed in 1848 with the subtitle A Novel without a Hero.

The story is framed as a puppet play, narrated by an omniscient and ironic manager of the performance. The title derives from the fair in John Bunyan's The Pilgrim's Progress, a place representing man's sinful attachment to worldly things. Becky Sharp, a poor but clever and ruthless orphan, schemes her way up the social ladder, while her gentle, passive school friend Amelia ("Emmy") Sedley suffers the loss of her family's fortune and the death of her husband George Osborne at the Battle of Waterloo. Around them orbit the bluff Rawdon Crawley, the dissipated Pitt Crawley family, the foolish Dobbin—who loves Amelia faithfully—and Jos Sedley, a cowardly East India Company collector.

Themes and style

Thackeray satirizes early 19th-century British society: its snobbery, greed, hypocrisy and obsession with rank and money. The absence of a hero is deliberate; the narrator repeatedly undercuts every character's pretensions, remarking near the end, "Come, children, let us shut up the box and the puppets, for our play is played out." The novel is considered a classic of English literature and is frequently listed among the greatest novels ever written; it has been adapted many times for stage, radio, film and television, including the 2004 film directed by Mira Nair and the 2018 ITV/Amazon television series.
