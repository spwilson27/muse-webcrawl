Characteristic class

A characteristic class measures the twisting of a bundle

A characteristic class is a way of associating to each principal bundle on a topological space a cohomology class of the space, in such a way that isomorphic bundles receive the same class and the assignment is natural under pullback. Characteristic classes are fundamental invariants in algebraic topology and differential geometry: they detect how a bundle is "twisted".

Examples

The best-known families are the Stiefel–Whitney classes (mod 2 classes of real vector bundles), the Chern classes (of complex vector bundles), the Pontryagin classes (of real bundles), and the Euler class, whose evaluation on a closed orientable manifold gives the Euler characteristic.

Theory

Formally, a characteristic class is a natural transformation from the functor of principal G-bundles to a cohomology functor. By universality, such classes are exactly the cohomology classes of the classifying space BG, pulled back along the classifying map of the bundle. A celebrated application is the Chern–Weil theory, which constructs characteristic classes from the curvature of a connection.

Applications

Characteristic classes obstruct the existence of structures on bundles (such as spin structures), appear in index theorems such as the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, and underlie notions such as the Wu manifold and genera of manifolds.
