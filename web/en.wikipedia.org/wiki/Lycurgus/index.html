Lycurgus

Lycurgus (Greek: Λυκοῦργος) is the legendary lawgiver credited with establishing the constitution and militarised social system of ancient Sparta. Ancient sources placed him variously in the 9th or 8th century BCE, though his historicity has long been debated, and modern scholars often treat him as a legendary figure to whom later reforms were attributed.

The Lycurgan reforms

According to Plutarch's Life of Lycurgus and other sources, Lycurgus, having travelled to Crete, Ionia, and (in some accounts) Egypt, introduced reforms embodied in the Great Rhetra. These included the gerousia (council of elders), the apella (assembly), the system of common messes (syssitia), the redistribution of land into equal lots (kleroi), the harsh upbringing of boys in the agoge, and the replacement of gold and silver coinage with heavy iron money. The ephors were also associated by later tradition with his settlement.

Legacy

Later tradition held that Lycurgus made the Spartans swear to keep his laws unchanged until he returned from a journey to Delphi, then starved himself to death abroad so the oath would bind them forever. His reforms were revered as the foundation of Sparta's stability and military discipline, and "Lycurgan" became shorthand for austerity and communal discipline.
