Hexameter


Hexameter in Homer

Hexameter is a metrical line of verse consisting of six metrical feet. The most famous form is the dactylic hexameter, the standard meter of classical epic poetry in Greek and Latin, used in Homer's Iliad and Odyssey, Hesiod's works, Virgil's Aeneid, and Ovid's Metamorphoses.

Dactylic hexameter

In dactylic hexameter, each of the first four feet may be either a dactyl (— ∪ ∪) or a spondee (— —); the fifth foot is usually a dactyl, and the sixth is a spondee or trochee. The line therefore contains between twelve and seventeen syllables. A caesura — a word break within a foot — typically occurs in the third or fourth foot.

Other forms and later use

Hexameter was also adapted into other languages. In German, poets such as Klopstock and Goethe used it for works including Goethe's Hermann and Dorothea. In English, accentual adaptations appear in Longfellow's Evangeline and Clough's The Bothie, though the fit with English stress patterns is often considered awkward. The elegiac couplet pairs a hexameter line with a pentameter line.
