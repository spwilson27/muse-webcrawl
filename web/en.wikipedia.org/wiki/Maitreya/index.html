Maitreya (Sanskrit: मैत्रेय; Pali: Metteyya) is regarded in Buddhist eschatology as the future Buddha of this world who will appear on Earth, achieve complete enlightenment, and teach the pure dharma. He is the successor of the current Buddha, Gautama, and is the only future Buddha anticipated in the canonical scriptures of all major Buddhist traditions. His name derives from maitrī (Pali: mettā), meaning benevolence or loving-kindness.

In scripture

According to tradition, Maitreya currently resides as a bodhisattva in Tusita heaven, awaiting the time when the teachings of Gautama Buddha have been completely forgotten. He will then be born in the city of Ketumati, awaken under a dragon-tree, and turn the wheel of dharma again, saving innumerable beings. The prophecy appears in the Pali Canon's Cakkavatti-Sihanada Sutta and was elaborated in Mahayana sutras such as the Maitreyavyakarana. The treatises of the Yogacara school — the "Five Treatises of Maitreya" — are traditionally attributed to him, as taught to Asanga.

Iconography and worship

Maitreya is depicted in two principal forms: as a bodhisattva, often seated in the "Western" posture with legs pendent or in contemplation pose (notably in Korean and Japanese art, as the Pensive Bodhisattva); and, in Chinese Buddhism, as the rotund Laughing Buddha (Budai), a 10th-century monk identified as his emanation, symbol of contentment and abundance. Devotion to Maitreya inspired millenarian movements across Asia — rebellions in China repeatedly invoked his imminent descent — and he remains central to East Asian and Tibetan practice. The colossal Leshan Giant Buddha (71 m, completed in 803) is a statue of Maitreya.

Parallels

The expectation of Maitreya has been compared with messianic figures in other religions, and some scholars have debated possible historical cross-influences with Iranian and Central Asian religious ideas along the Silk Road.
