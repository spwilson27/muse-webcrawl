Licchavi (kingdom)

The Licchavi (also Lichchhavi) were a dynasty and kingdom that ruled the Kathmandu Valley of present-day Nepal from roughly the 4th to the 9th century CE. They are the earliest dynasty of Nepal known from inscriptions, and their period is regarded as a golden age of classical art, architecture and Sanskrit learning in the valley.

History

The Licchavis claimed descent from the ancient Licchavi clan of Vaishali in India. King Mānadeva I (r. c. 464–505) is the first historically well-attested ruler; his victory pillar inscription at Changu Narayan, dated 464 CE, is the oldest dated inscription in Nepal. Licchavi kings governed with the help of powerful prime ministers, and by the late 7th century the Aṃśuvarman family effectively shared or usurped power. The dynasty gradually declined and was succeeded by the Thakuri period.

Culture

Licchavi rule produced masterpieces of stone sculpture and temple architecture, including early forms of the pagoda-style temple, and patronised both Hinduism (especially Vaishnavism and Shaivism) and Mahayana Buddhism. Sanskrit was the language of the court and of the numerous surviving inscriptions.
