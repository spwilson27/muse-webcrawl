Concert promoter

A concert promoter (also called a talent buyer in some contexts) is an individual or company responsible for organizing live concerts and music events. Promoters book artists, secure venues, arrange marketing and ticket sales, and take on much of the financial risk of a show.

Role

The promoter negotiates with an artist's agent or manager, guarantees a fee or a share of revenue, rents the venue, arranges production (sound, lighting, staging), obtains permits and insurance, and promotes the event to the public. Profit comes from ticket and ancillary sales after costs; losses are typically borne by the promoter.

Industry

The live music business became increasingly consolidated in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, with large firms such as Live Nation and AEG Presents promoting tours globally alongside independent local promoters. Legendary promoters include Bill Graham, whose Fillmore venues shaped rock concert culture, and Harvey Goldsmith in the United Kingdom.

Deal structures

Common arrangements include flat guarantees, guarantee-plus-percentage deals, and "versus" deals against a share of gross receipts. Promoters may also promote festivals, comedy, and theatrical tours.
