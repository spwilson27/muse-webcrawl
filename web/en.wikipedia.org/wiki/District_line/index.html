The District line is a London Underground line running from Upminster in the east and Edgware Road in the west to Earl's Court in west London, where it splits into multiple branches. Coloured green on the Tube map, it serves 60 stations over about 64 kilometres (40 miles) of track. It is the busiest of the sub-surface lines and the fifth-busiest line overall on the Underground.

History

The line began as the Metropolitan District Railway, which opened in 1868 between South Kensington and Westminster as part of what became the Circle line, jointly operated with the Metropolitan Railway. The District expanded rapidly across west and south-west London — to Richmond (1877), Ealing Broadway (1879), Wimbledon (1889) and eastwards to Whitechapel and Upminster — and was electrified in the first years of the 20th century under the financier Charles Tyson Yerkes. It became part of London Underground in 1933.

Route and branches

From Upminster the line runs west through Barking, Whitechapel and along the Victoria Embankment to Westminster, South Kensington and Earl's Court. West of Earl's Court it divides into branches to Richmond, Ealing Broadway and Wimbledon, with a short branch to Kensington (Olympia). A separate service links Edgware Road with Wimbledon via High Street Kensington. Between Earl's Court and Tower Hill the line shares tracks with the Circle line, and it also shares the Richmond and Wimbledon approaches with National Rail services and the London Overground.

Rolling stock and modernisation

The line is now worked entirely by S7 Stock trains introduced from 2013, replacing C Stock and D78 Stock. Signalling has been upgraded under the Four Lines Modernisation programme, introducing automatic train operation on parts of the sub-surface network.
