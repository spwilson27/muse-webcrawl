Pubis (bone)

The pubis, or pubic bone, is one of the three principal bones forming each half of the pelvis, together with the ilium and the ischium. It forms the anterior and inferior part of the hip bone.

Structure

The pubis consists of a body and two rami (branches). The body lies medially and its inner surface forms part of the wall of the pelvic cavity; the two pubic bodies meet at the midline at the pubic symphysis, a cartilaginous joint. The superior ramus extends laterally to join the ilium and ischium at the acetabulum, while the inferior ramus curves downward to join the ramus of the ischium, together bounding the obturator foramen. The pubic crest and pubic tubercle on the superior border give attachment to abdominal muscles and the inguinal ligament.

Function

The pubis helps protect the pelvic organs, including the bladder, and provides attachment for muscles of the abdomen, perineum, and medial thigh, including the adductor group via the rami.

Clinical significance

Pubic ramus fractures are common in elderly patients after falls and in high-energy trauma as part of pelvic ring injuries. The age-related changes of the pubic symphysis face are also used in forensic anthropology to estimate age at death.
