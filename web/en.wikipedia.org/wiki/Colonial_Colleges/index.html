Colonial Colleges

Number: Nine
Earliest: Harvard, 1636
Latest: Rutgers / Dartmouth, 1766–1769

The Colonial Colleges are nine institutions of higher education founded in the American colonies before the United States became an independent nation following the American Revolution. They are among the oldest universities in the United States and several are now members of the Ivy League.

The nine

  • Harvard University (1636, Massachusetts)
  • College of William & Mary (1693, Virginia)
  • Yale University (1701, Connecticut)
  • University of Pennsylvania (1740)
  • Princeton University (1746)
  • Columbia University (1754, as King's College)
  • Brown University (1764)
  • Rutgers University (1766, as Queen's College)
  • Dartmouth College (1769)

Character

Most were founded by religious denominations to train clergy and educated gentlemen, with curricula centred on Latin, Greek, theology, logic and classics under colonial charters. Seven of the nine are now Ivy League institutions; William & Mary is a public research university and Rutgers is New Jersey's public flagship.
