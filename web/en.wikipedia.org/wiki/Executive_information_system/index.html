Executive information system

An executive information system (EIS), also known as an executive support system (ESS), is a type of management support system that facilitates and supports senior executive information and decision-making needs. It provides easy access to internal and external information relevant to organisational goals, typically through graphical displays and dashboards.

History

EIS emerged in the late 1970s and 1980s at large corporations such as Lockheed and Northwest Industries, building on earlier decision support systems. Early systems ran on mainframes and minicomputers, delivering pre-formatted reports with drill-down capability.

Components and features

Typical features include exception reporting, key performance indicators, trend analysis, and the ability to drill down from summaries to underlying detail. Modern EIS functions are largely delivered through business-intelligence platforms and executive dashboards accessed via web browsers and mobile devices.

Advantages and limitations

Advantages include timely, tailored information for executives and improved monitoring of the firm and its environment. Limitations include high development cost, dependence on data quality, and the risk that simplified indicators obscure important detail.
