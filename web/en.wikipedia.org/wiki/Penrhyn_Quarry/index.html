Penrhyn Quarry


Location: Bethesda, Gwynedd, Wales
Product: Slate
Opened: 18th century

Penrhyn Quarry is a slate quarry near Bethesda in Gwynedd, north-west Wales. At its peak in the late nineteenth century it was the largest slate quarry in the world, employing nearly 3,000 men and producing much of the roofing slate used across industrial Britain. It is part of the Slate Landscape of Northwest Wales UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2021.

History

Small-scale slate working on the site dates to the late eighteenth century under the ownership of the Pennant family, later Lords Penrhyn, who developed the quarry from the 1780s onwards with a horse-drawn tramway (later the Penrhyn Quarry Railway) to Port Penrhyn at Bangor. The quarry's terraced galleries, each named and worked by gangs of quarrymen under the "bargain" system, became a byword for the Welsh slate industry.

The great strike

Penrhyn was the scene of one of the longest industrial disputes in British history: the lockout and strike of 1900–1903, when some 2,800 men struck against Lord Penrhyn over union recognition and conditions. The dispute ended in defeat for the men, embittered the community of Bethesda for generations, and is commemorated in Welsh song and literature.

Today

The quarry still produces slate on a reduced scale. Its long worked-out terraces now host Zip World Velocity, one of the world's fastest zip lines, which carries riders over the flooded quarry pit.
