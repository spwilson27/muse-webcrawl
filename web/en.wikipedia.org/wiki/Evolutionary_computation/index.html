Evolutionary computation

Evolutionary computation is a family of algorithms for global optimization inspired by biological evolution, and the subfield of artificial intelligence (more precisely computational intelligence) studying these algorithms. Candidate solutions to a problem are treated as individuals in a population, and a fitness function determines the quality of each solution. Evolution of the population then takes place after repeated application of variation operators (mutation and recombination) and selection.

Main families

  • Genetic algorithms — solutions encoded as strings, evolved with crossover and mutation; developed by John Holland in the 1970s.
  • Evolution strategies — real-valued optimization with self-adaptive mutation step sizes, developed by Ingo Rechenberg and Hans-Paul Schwefel.
  • Genetic programming — evolves computer programs, typically represented as trees, popularized by John Koza.
  • Evolutionary programming — originally evolved finite-state machines, developed by Lawrence Fogel.

Applications

Evolutionary algorithms are used in antenna and circuit design (including NASA's evolved ST5 antenna), scheduling, engineering optimization, automated machine learning, and procedural content generation in games. They are especially useful where the fitness landscape is rugged, discontinuous, or poorly understood, though they offer no guarantee of finding a global optimum.
