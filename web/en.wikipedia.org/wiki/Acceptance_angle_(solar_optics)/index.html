Acceptance angle (solar optics)

Acceptance angle (solar optics)
FieldNonimaging optics, solar energy
Symbolθa (half-angle)
Related quantityConcentration ratio

In solar optics, the acceptance angle is the maximum angle at which incoming sunlight can strike a solar concentrator and still be directed onto its absorber (receiver). It is usually expressed as a half-angle, θa, measured from the concentrator's optical axis. Radiation arriving within the acceptance angle is accepted; radiation outside it is rejected.

Relation to concentration

The acceptance angle is inversely related to the geometric concentration ratio. For an ideal three-dimensional (point-focus) concentrator, the maximum concentration is Cmax = 1/sin²θa, and for a two-dimensional (line-focus) trough, Cmax = 1/sin θa — a consequence of the conservation of étendue. High-concentration systems therefore have small acceptance angles and require accurate sun tracking, while stationary compound parabolic concentrators accept a wide angular range at low concentration.

Design significance

The acceptance angle must accommodate the finite angular size of the sun (about 0.27° half-angle), optical errors, and tracking tolerances. Designers choose a compromise between concentration and angular tolerance for applications such as solar thermal collectors and concentrator photovoltaics.
