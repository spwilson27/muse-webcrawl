Svalbard

Svalbard is a Norwegian archipelago in the Arctic Ocean, lying about midway between continental Norway and the North Pole, between 74° and 81° north latitude. Its largest island is Spitsbergen; the administrative centre is Longyearbyen, the world's northernmost settlement of its size (about 2,400 residents).

Svalbard is governed by the 1920 Svalbard Treaty, which gives Norway sovereignty while allowing all signatory states to conduct economic activity — Russia maintains the mining settlement of Barentsburg. Coal mining dominated for a century but has wound down; today research (the University Centre in Svalbard, satellite stations), tourism and the Svalbard Global Seed Vault — storing backup copies of the world's crop seeds in a mountain near Longyearbyen — define the archipelago. Polar bears outnumber people, and residents carry rifles outdoors by law.
