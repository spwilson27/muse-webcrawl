Theme (Byzantine district)

[Hero image placeholder: map of the Byzantine themes in Anatolia around 780 AD.]

A theme (Greek: θέμα, thema, plural θέματα, themata) was the main administrative division of the middle Byzantine Empire. Themes were established in the mid-7th century in the aftermath of the Slavic invasions of the Balkans and the Muslim conquests in the East, replacing the earlier diocesan and provincial system established by Diocletian and Constantine I.

Structure

Each theme was governed by a military commander, the strategos, who wielded both military and (eventually) civil authority. Originally the term referred to the army corps stationed in a region; by the 8th and 9th centuries it designated the territory itself. Soldiers were settled on military lands, whose revenues supported them, creating a provincial army that could respond quickly to Arab raids.

Development

The first themes were the large Anatolian commands: the Opsikion, Anatolikon, Armeniakon and Thrakesion, together with the maritime Karabisianoi. Over the 8th–10th centuries the great themes were repeatedly subdivided into smaller ones, and the system spread to the Balkans and southern Italy. From the late 11th century the thematic system declined as professional tagmata and, later, pronoia grants and ducal commands replaced it.
