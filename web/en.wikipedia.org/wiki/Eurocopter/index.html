The Eurocopter Group was a European helicopter manufacturing company formed in 1992 by the merger of the helicopter divisions of the French company Aérospatiale and the German company Deutsche Aerospace (Daimler-Benz). It became a subsidiary of EADS (European Aeronautic Defence and Space Company). On 1 January 2014, Eurocopter was renamed Airbus Helicopters as part of the rebranding of its parent group as Airbus Group, ending the Eurocopter name after 22 years.

History

The merger brought together two complementary product lines. Aérospatiale contributed successful French designs including the Alouette, Gazelle, Dauphin, Puma and Super Puma families, while the German side, descended from Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm, contributed the Bo 105 and BK 117. Eurocopter became the world's largest helicopter manufacturer by revenue and deliveries, with main production sites at Marignane in France and Donauwörth in Germany, and subsidiaries including American Eurocopter in the United States.

Products

Eurocopter's range spanned light single-engine helicopters such as the EC120 Colibri and the Ecureuil/AS350 family; light twins such as the EC135, EC145 and the Dauphin/AS365 series; medium and heavy types including the Super Puma/Cougar family and the EC225; and military helicopters, notably the Tiger attack helicopter and the NH90 transport and naval helicopter, the latter developed through the NHIndustries consortium. Its aircraft were widely used for emergency medical services, police work, offshore transport and military operations. The EC135 in particular became one of the most popular air ambulance helicopters worldwide.

Rebranding

In 2013 EADS announced it would rename itself Airbus Group; its helicopter division accordingly became Airbus Helicopters on 1 January 2014, and its products were progressively redesignated with the "H" prefix (for example, the EC135 became the H135).
