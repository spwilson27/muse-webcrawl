Hydraulic cylinder

A hydraulic cylinder (also called a linear hydraulic motor) is a mechanical actuator used to give a unidirectional force through a unidirectional stroke. Pressurised hydraulic fluid acts on a piston inside a cylindrical barrel, converting fluid power into linear mechanical work. Hydraulic cylinders are used in construction equipment (excavators, loaders), manufacturing machinery, presses, aircraft controls, and civil engineering.

Construction

A typical cylinder consists of a barrel, a piston attached to a piston rod, end caps (cap and head), seals, and ports. In a single-acting cylinder, fluid pressure extends the piston and a spring or the load retracts it. In a double-acting cylinder, fluid can be supplied to both sides of the piston so that both extension and retraction are powered. Common construction types include tie-rod cylinders (held together by threaded rods, common in industrial service) and welded-body cylinders (compact and robust, common on mobile equipment). Telescopic cylinders nest several stages to give a long stroke from a short retracted length, as in dump-truck hoists.

Operation

The force produced equals the fluid pressure multiplied by the effective piston area (less, on the rod side, by the rod's cross-section), so high working pressures — commonly 210 bar (3,000 psi) and above — yield very large forces in compact devices. Cushions, position sensors, and proportional valves can be integrated for controlled motion. Rod failure, seal leakage, and buckling under compressive load are principal design concerns.
