Core sample

A core sample is a cylindrical section of (usually) a naturally occurring substance. Most core samples are obtained by drilling with special drills into the substance, for example sediment or rock, with a hollow steel tube, called a core drill. The hole made for the core sample is called the "core hole". A variety of core samplers exist to sample different media under different conditions; there is continuing scientific and engineering innovation in the field.

Overview

Used for: Geology, petroleum exploration, climate science, materials testing

Examples: Rock cores, ice cores, sediment cores, concrete cores

Applications

In geology and petroleum exploration, rock cores reveal porosity, permeability and the presence of hydrocarbons. Ice cores drilled from glaciers and ice sheets preserve a layered record of past climate stretching back hundreds of thousands of years. Sediment cores from lake and ocean beds record environmental change, while cores of concrete and asphalt are used to test the strength and thickness of structures.

Sampling and analysis

After extraction, cores are logged, photographed and often split lengthwise; one half may be archived while the other is used for destructive analysis. Preserving the orientation and depth order of the sample is essential to its scientific value.
