Synaptogenesis

Synaptogenesis is the formation of synapses between neurons in the nervous system. Although it occurs throughout a healthy person's lifespan, an explosion of synapse formation occurs during early brain development, a process known as exuberant synaptogenesis, particularly in the first years of life.

Mechanism

Synaptogenesis involves the growth of axons and dendrites toward target cells, recognition of appropriate synaptic partners, and the assembly of presynaptic and postsynaptic specialisations. Cell adhesion molecules such as neurexins and neuroligins help stabilise nascent contacts, while neurotrophic factors and activity-dependent signalling refine which synapses survive. During development many more synapses are produced than are ultimately retained; excess connections are eliminated through synaptic pruning, a process that continues into adolescence.

Role in development

The timing of synaptogenesis differs among brain regions. Synapses form at a high rate in the sensory cortices early in life, whereas the prefrontal cortex shows a more protracted course extending into adolescence. Disruptions of synaptogenesis have been implicated in neurodevelopmental conditions such as autism spectrum disorders and schizophrenia.
