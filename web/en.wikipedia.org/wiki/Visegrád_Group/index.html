Visegrád Group

The Visegrád Group (also known as the Visegrád Four, the V4, or the European Quartet) is a cultural and political alliance of four Central European countries: the Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland and Slovakia. The group aims to advance cooperation in military, cultural, economic and energy matters, and to further the member states' integration within the European Union.

History

The group takes its name from the Hungarian town of Visegrád, where in 1335 Kings John I of Bohemia, Casimir III of Poland and Charles I of Hungary met to negotiate trade and alliances. The modern grouping was founded on 15 February 1991 at a summit in Visegrád, where the leaders of Czechoslovakia, Hungary and Poland signed a declaration of cooperation on the path to European integration. After the dissolution of Czechoslovakia in 1993, its two successor states became separate members, making the group four.

The V4 countries joined NATO in 1999 (Poland, Hungary, Czech Republic) and 2004 (Slovakia), and all four became members of the European Union on 1 May 2004.

Cooperation

Presidency of the group rotates annually. The International Visegrad Fund, established in 2000 and based in Bratislava, supports cultural, scientific, educational and youth-exchange projects. The group also cooperates in the Visegrád Battlegroup under the EU's battlegroup concept, and in the "V4+" format with partners such as Austria, Slovenia and the Baltic and Balkan states.

Common positions and tensions

The four states coordinated closely during the 2015 European migrant crisis, opposing mandatory refugee relocation quotas. In other periods their unity has frayed — particularly after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, when Hungary's stance diverged from Poland's strongly pro-Ukrainian position — yet the format has persisted as the principal vehicle for Central European consultation.

Demographics and economy

Together the Visegrád countries have a population of roughly 64 million. If counted as a single economy, the V4 would rank among the largest in the EU, with a combined GDP driven by strong manufacturing sectors integrated into German supply chains.
