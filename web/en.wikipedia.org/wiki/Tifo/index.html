Tifo

Labelled placeholder: a stadium-wide tifo choreography.

Tifo (Italian: [ˈtiːfo]) is the phenomenon whereby tifosi of a sports team make a large visual display of any choreographed flag, sign or banner in the stands of a stadium, mostly as part of an association football match.

Tifos are mostly used by ultras and football supporter groups. They are typically unveiled at the start of a match, and are meant to impress, intimidate opponents, or celebrate a club's history or identity. A tifo may consist of a large painted cloth banner (a "choreography"), card displays in which thousands of fans hold coloured cards to form an image, flags, or confetti and balloon displays.

Etymology

Italian tifo originally means "typhus"; the behaviour of feverish fans was compared to delirium, and tifosi ("those infected") became the standard Italian word for sports supporters, especially in football and cycling.

Around the world

Elaborate tifos are a hallmark of supporter culture in Italy, Germany, Poland, Turkey, the Balkans and North Africa, and have become prominent in North American soccer, notably among MLS supporters' groups in Portland, Seattle and Los Angeles. Preparation can take weeks and cost tens of thousands of dollars raised by the supporters themselves. Mocking or provocative tifos aimed at rival clubs are common, and the largest displays occasionally set records for the size of a single painted banner.
