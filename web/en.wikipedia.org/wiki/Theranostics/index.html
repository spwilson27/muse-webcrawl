Theranostics

Theranostics is a field of medicine that combines targeted therapy with specific diagnostic tests (diagnostics): a diagnostic agent first identifies a molecular target in a patient's disease, and a companion therapeutic agent then acts on the same target. The term is a portmanteau and is closely associated with nuclear medicine and personalised medicine.

Radiotheranostics

In the most common implementation, a tumour target — such as the somatostatin receptor in neuroendocrine tumours or prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) in prostate cancer — is imaged with a positron- or gamma-emitting isotope (for example gallium-68) attached to a targeting molecule. The same molecule is then labelled with a therapeutic isotope such as lutetium-177 or actinium-225 to deliver cytotoxic radiation selectively to the tumour. Radioiodine treatment of thyroid disease is the historical prototype.

Applications

Approved examples include lutetium-177 dotatate (Lutathera) for neuroendocrine tumours and lutetium-177 PSMA-617 (Pluvicto) for metastatic prostate cancer. Theranostic approaches are being extended to nanoparticles, antibodies and other cancers.
