Eyeglass prescription

An eyeglass prescription is an order written by an eyewear prescriber, such as an optometrist or ophthalmologist, that specifies the value of all parameters the prescriber has deemed necessary to construct and/or dispense corrective lenses appropriate for a patient. If the prescription uses parameters beyond those needed to correct ametropia, additional details for prisms, base curves, decentration, and other lens design features may be included.

Components

A typical prescription lists, for each eye — OD (oculus dexter, right) and OS (oculus sinister, left) — the sphere (SPH), the power in dioptres needed to correct nearsightedness (myopia, negative values) or farsightedness (hyperopia, positive values); the cylinder (CYL) and axis (1–180 degrees) correcting astigmatism; and, for multifocal lenses, the add power for near vision, used to treat presbyopia. Pupillary distance (PD) may also be recorded. A plano lens is written as 0.00 or "pl".

Measurement

Refraction is measured with a phoropter or retinoscopy, often after determining objective values with an autorefractor. Lens powers are specified in quarter-dioptre steps (0.25 D). Prescriptions are usually valid for one to two years depending on jurisdiction, and in many places patients are legally entitled to a copy of their prescription after an eye examination.

Differences from contact lens prescriptions

Contact lens prescriptions differ because a contact lens sits directly on the cornea rather than about 12 mm in front of it (the vertex distance), so powers diverge especially above ±4.00 D; they also specify lens brand, base curve, and diameter, and require a separate fitting evaluation.
