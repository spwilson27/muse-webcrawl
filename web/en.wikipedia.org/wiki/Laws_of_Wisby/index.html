Laws of Wisby

The Laws of Wisby (Sea Laws of Wisby) are a set of laws governing maritime commerce and navigation that originated in the late Middle Ages and were named after Visby (Wisby), the principal trading town of the Hanseatic League on the island of Gotland in the Baltic Sea.

Content and origins

The laws drew on earlier maritime codes, including the Rolls of Oléron, the Consolato del Mare, the ordinances of Lübeck and the sea laws of Amsterdam. They covered matters such as the duties of shipmasters and crews, freight, jettison and general average, collisions, salvage, and the discipline of sailors.

Influence

The Laws of Wisby were printed in the 16th century (first at Copenhagen in 1505) and became the dominant maritime code of the Baltic and much of northern Europe, exercising a lasting influence on modern admiralty law and the rules of general average.
