Forfar bridie

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Forfar bridie is a Scottish meat pastry originating from the town of Forfar in Angus. It consists of minced beef, onion, and seasoning enclosed in shortcrust or flaky pastry, formed into a distinctive horseshoe or half-moon shape with the crimped edge on the side. Traditionally, a bridie made with onion has two holes in the top, while a plain bridie has one.

History

The bridie is said to have been created in the 19th century and popularised by local bakers in Forfar. One tradition attributes its name to Margaret Bridie, a travelling food seller whose pastries were served at weddings (brides' meals) in the area. In 2016 the Forfar bridie was awarded Protected Geographical Indication (PGI) status by the European Union, recognising its specific link to the town.

Preparation

Bridies are made with lean minced beef steak mixed with finely chopped onion, suet or butter, salt and pepper, wrapped in pastry, crimped, and baked until golden. They are traditionally eaten hot, sometimes with mashed potato and baked beans or with gravy.
