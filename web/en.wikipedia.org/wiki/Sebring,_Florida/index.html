Sebring, Florida

Sebring is a city and the county seat of Highlands County, Florida, United States. Nicknamed "The City on the Circle", in reference to Circle Drive, the town's central roundabout, it had a population of about 10,700 at the 2020 census. Sebring is internationally known for the Sebring International Raceway and its 12 Hours of Sebring endurance race.

History

Sebring was founded in 1912 by George E. Sebring, a pottery manufacturer from Ohio, who planned the town around a circular central park. It was chartered in 1913 and grew with the Florida land boom of the 1920s. The historic downtown and the Kenilworth Lodge are listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Geography

The city lies on the Lake Wales Ridge in central Florida, beside Lake Jackson, with several other lakes—including Lake Sebring and Dinner Lake—within and around its limits. The climate is humid subtropical, with hot, rainy summers.

Motorsport

Sebring International Raceway, built on the former Hendricks Army Airfield, has hosted the 12 Hours of Sebring since 1952, one of the premier endurance sports car races in the world and part of the informal "triple crown" of endurance racing.
