Video game genre

A video game genre is a category of video games defined primarily by the kind of gameplay and challenges the player faces, rather than by setting or narrative content. Unlike film or literary genres, which are usually defined by theme and story, video game genres describe what the player does: shooting, jumping, solving puzzles, managing resources, or role-playing a character, for example.

Because games are hybrid creations, most titles combine several genres, and new genres emerge as developers blend mechanics in novel ways. Genres are often subdivided into subgenres (the first-person shooter within the shooter genre, for instance) and described by compound labels such as "action-adventure" or "action role-playing game."

Major genres

  • Action — games emphasizing physical challenges and hand–eye coordination: platformers, shooters (first-person, third-person), fighting games, beat 'em ups, and survival games
  • Action-adventure — combines action gameplay with exploration, puzzle-solving, and narrative
  • Adventure — story-driven exploration and puzzle-solving, from text adventures to point-and-click and walking simulators
  • Role-playing (RPG) — character progression, statistics, and quests; includes Japanese RPGs, Western RPGs, action RPGs, and massively multiplayer online RPGs (MMORPGs)
  • Simulation — vehicle simulation, life simulation, construction and management, and sports games
  • Strategy — real-time strategy (RTS), turn-based strategy, tower defense, and multiplayer online battle arenas (MOBAs)
  • Puzzle — logic and pattern-solving games
  • Idle/incremental, party, horror, and sandbox games

Classification challenges

Scholars and the industry disagree on genre boundaries: genres may be defined by perspective (first-person), by structure (roguelike), by platform traditions, or by marketing. As games converge around hybrid designs — open-world action RPGs with survival elements, for example — genre labels increasingly serve as shorthand for clusters of mechanics rather than strict categories.
