Qianling Mausoleum

[Image: The headless statues of foreign envoys along the spirit way at Qianling]

The Qianling Mausoleum (乾陵, Qiánlíng) is a Tang-dynasty tomb complex on Mount Liang, in Qian County, Shaanxi, China, some 85 km northwest of Xi'an. Built in 684 AD, it is the joint burial place of Emperor Gaozong (d. 683) and his wife Wu Zetian — the only woman to rule China as emperor in her own name — who was interred there in 706. It is the best known of the eighteen Tang imperial mausoleums and, unusually, is believed never to have been successfully plundered.

The site

The tomb follows the Tang practice of using a natural mountain as the burial mound, with a walled precinct modelled on the capital Chang'an. A long spirit way (sacred way) lined with stone statues — horses, ostriches, officials, winged horses and a pair of great stelae — leads to the burial peak. The two stelae are famous: Gaozong's, the "Seven-Jointed Stele," is inscribed with his deeds, while Wu Zetian's is the celebrated Wordless Stele (Wuzibei), left blank — traditionally explained as Wu's judgement that her merits and faults were beyond words, to be assessed by posterity. Along the avenue also stand 61 headless stone statues of foreign envoys and allied rulers who attended Gaozong's funeral, their names carved on their backs; how and when they lost their heads is uncertain.

Satellite tombs and treasures

Seventeen attendant tombs surround the main precinct; five excavated in the 1960s–70s — those of Crown Prince Zhanghuai, Prince Yide and Princess Yongtai — yielded superb murals of court life, polo games, guards and palace women, along with thousands of painted pottery figures (mingqi) and sancai-glazed ceramics. Repeated attempts to locate and open the main tomb passage in modern times have been deferred by the Chinese government; survey work suggests the burial chamber and its treasures remain intact behind stone doors sealed with iron.
