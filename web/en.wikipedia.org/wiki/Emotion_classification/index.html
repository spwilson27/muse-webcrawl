Emotion classification

Emotion classification is the problem of distinguishing and categorizing distinct types of emotion, studied in psychology, philosophy, and affective computing. Researchers have proposed competing schemes: some argue that a small set of basic emotions is universal and biologically hard-wired, while others hold that emotions are constructed from more primitive dimensions such as valence and arousal.

Basic emotions approaches

Charles Darwin proposed in The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals (1872) that emotional expressions are evolved and universal. In the 20th century, Paul Ekman's cross-cultural studies of facial expressions identified six emotions he argued are recognized worldwide: anger, disgust, fear, happiness, sadness, and surprise. Robert Plutchik proposed eight primary emotions arranged in a "wheel of emotions," with blends producing complex feelings. Silvan Tomkins identified nine affect programs, and Jaak Panksepp proposed seven primary affective systems derived from animal neuroscience.

Dimensional models

Dimensional theories classify emotions by continuous variables rather than discrete categories. The circumplex model of affect, developed by James Russell, places each emotion in a two-dimensional space of valence (pleasure–displeasure) and arousal (activation–sleepiness). The PAD model adds a third dimension, dominance.

Appraisal and constructed emotion

Appraisal theories hold that emotions arise from a person's evaluation of events in relation to their goals and well-being. Lisa Feldman Barrett's theory of constructed emotion argues that emotions are not triggered but are made by the brain categorising bodily sensations using learned emotion concepts. Emotion classification in machine learning applies these schemes to text, speech, and facial-expression data for sentiment and affect recognition.
