Yew

Yew is a common name given to various species of trees in the genus Taxus, in the family Taxaceae — most commonly the English or European yew, Taxus baccata. Yews are slow-growing, long-lived evergreen conifers.

Yew
Genus: Taxus
Family: Taxaceae
Type: Evergreen conifer
Notable species: Taxus baccata, T. brevifolia, T. canadensis

Description

Yews have reddish bark, flat dark-green needle-like leaves, and seeds enclosed in a fleshy red cup called an aril. Most parts are highly toxic due to taxine alkaloids, though the aril flesh itself is not. Some churchyard yews in Britain are over 1,000 years old; the Fortingall Yew may be several thousand years old.

Uses

Yew wood is hard, elastic, and prized for longbows and cabinetry. The bark of the Pacific yew (T. brevifolia) yielded paclitaxel (Taxol), an important chemotherapy drug, now produced semi-synthetically. Yews are also widely planted as ornamental hedges.

Cultural significance

Associated with death, mourning, and immortality, yews are traditionally planted in churchyards across Britain, Ireland, and Normandy.
