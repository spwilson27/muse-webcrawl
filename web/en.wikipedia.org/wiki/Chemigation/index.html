Chemigation

Chemigation through a centre-pivot irrigation system

Chemigation is the application of agricultural chemicals — fertilisers, pesticides, herbicides and soil amendments — through an irrigation system. The chemical is injected into the irrigation water and distributed over the field together with the water. When the chemical is specifically a fertiliser the practice is called fertigation; chemigation is the broader term.

Methods

Chemigation is most closely associated with centre-pivot and linear-move sprinkler systems, where an injection pump meters chemical from a supply tank into the mainline at a controlled rate. It is also used with drip and micro-irrigation, where precise dosing to the root zone is possible, and less commonly with furrow and flood irrigation. Agitation and calibration determine uniformity: because the chemical is distributed in proportion to the water, application uniformity mirrors irrigation uniformity.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include low application cost (the irrigation pass does double duty), timely application regardless of field conditions, reduced soil compaction and crop damage from machinery, and the ability to spoon-feed nutrients in small doses matched to crop demand. Disadvantages include dependence on uniform water distribution, the risk of leaching or runoff if over-irrigated, corrosion of equipment by some chemicals, and the serious hazard of contaminating the water source if the system backflows.

Safety and regulation

Because chemigation connects chemical tanks to water supplies — sometimes wells or public water — anti-backflow protection is mandatory in many jurisdictions. Typical requirements include a check valve and vacuum relief on the mainline, a normally closed solenoid or interlock so the injection pump stops when the water pump stops, and a low-pressure drain. In the United States, the Environmental Protection Agency requires specific safety devices and labelling for pesticides applied through irrigation systems, and many states require chemigation permits or certified applicators.
