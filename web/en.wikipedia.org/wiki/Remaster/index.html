Remaster

A remaster is a new version of a previously released recording, film or video game, created by producing a new master from the original source materials. Remastering typically aims to improve the quality of the work — for example by reducing noise, correcting earlier limitations, or adapting it to a newer medium — without fundamentally altering its content, in contrast to a remake, which recreates the work, or a remix, which recombines its elements.

Audio remastering

In music, remasters became common with the shift to compact disc, when catalogue albums were transferred from tape to digital. Engineers may adjust equalisation, dynamics and stereo imaging, or create new mixes from multitrack tapes. Critics have debated some remastering practices associated with the "loudness war", in which aggressive dynamic-range compression made reissues louder but less nuanced.

Film and games

Film remasters may involve scanning original negatives at high resolution, digital restoration of scratches and fading, and colour regrading. In video games, remasters typically update graphics, resolution, frame rates and controls for newer hardware while preserving the original design and content, whereas remakes rebuild the game from scratch.
