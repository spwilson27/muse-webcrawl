Stefan–Boltzmann constant

The Stefan–Boltzmann constant (σ) is a physical constant involved in the Stefan–Boltzmann law, which states that the total radiant heat power emitted from a surface is proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature. Its value is σ = 5.670374419 × 10−8 W·m−2·K−4.

Stefan–Boltzmann constant σ
Value: 5.670374419×10−8 W·m−2·K−4
Named after: Jožef Stefan, Ludwig Boltzmann
Status: Exact (since 2019 SI redefinition)

Definition

σ = (2π5k4)/(15c2h3), where k is Boltzmann's constant, c the speed of light, and h Planck's constant. Since the 2019 redefinition of the SI base units, those constants are exact, so σ is now exact. Josef Stefan found the law empirically in 1879; Ludwig Boltzmann derived it theoretically in 1884.

Applications

The constant is used to estimate stellar temperatures and radii, planetary equilibrium temperatures, and radiative heat transfer in engineering.
