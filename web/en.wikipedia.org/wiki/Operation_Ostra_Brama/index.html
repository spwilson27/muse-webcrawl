Operation Ostra Brama

Date: 7–13 July 1944

Location: Vilnius (Wilno), Lithuania

Part of: Operation Tempest, Eastern Front, Second World War

Belligerents: Polish Home Army vs. Germany; aftermath involving the Soviet Union

Result: City liberated with Soviet forces; Polish commanders arrested by the NKVD

Operation Ostra Brama (Polish: Operacja „Ostra Brama”, named after the Gate of Dawn in Vilnius) was an armed operation by the Polish Home Army (Armia Krajowa) in July 1944 to seize Vilnius from German occupation before the arrival of the Red Army. It formed part of the nationwide Operation Tempest, intended to assert the authority of the Polish government-in-exile as Soviet forces advanced into pre-war Polish territory.

Background

Vilnius (Wilno) had been part of Poland before 1939 and was claimed by the Polish government-in-exile; the Soviet Union considered it part of the Lithuanian SSR it had annexed in 1940. As the Red Army's Operation Bagration destroyed German Army Group Centre, the Home Army's Wilno district, commanded by Lt. Col. Aleksander Krzyżanowski ("Wilk"), prepared an uprising of some 12,000 soldiers.

The operation

The attack began prematurely on the evening of 6–7 July 1944, before all units had assembled, after German movements suggested the garrison might be reinforced. Polish forces attacked the city from the southeast and rose up inside it, taking parts of the city but failing to capture the heavily defended centre and railway station. When Soviet forces of the 3rd Belorussian Front arrived on 8 July, the two sides jointly fought the German garrison, and Vilnius was fully liberated on 13 July, with the Polish flag raised over the Gediminas Tower.

Aftermath

Within days, the NKVD moved against the Home Army: Krzyżanowski and his officers were arrested at a supposed conference on 17 July, and thousands of Polish soldiers were disarmed and interned, many being given the choice of joining the Soviet-controlled Polish forces or deportation to camps in the Soviet Union. The episode became an emblematic episode in the fraught Polish–Soviet relations of the war's final phase, foreshadowing the fate of the Warsaw Uprising later that summer.
