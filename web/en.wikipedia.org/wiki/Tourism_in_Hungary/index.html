Tourism in Hungary

Tourism in Hungary is a significant sector of the national economy. Hungary received approximately 31.6 million international visitors in 2023, and is known for its rich history and culture, its thermal springs, and Lake Balaton, the largest lake in Central Europe. The capital, Budapest, is regarded as one of the most beautiful cities in the world and accounts for the large majority of foreign visits.

Destinations

Budapest

Budapest is widely known for its well-preserved pre-war cityscape, including the Hungarian Parliament Building, Buda Castle, the Széchenyi Chain Bridge, and Andrássy Avenue — the Banks of the Danube, the Buda Castle Quarter and Andrássy Avenue form a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city is also famous for its thermal baths, such as the Széchenyi and Gellért baths, fed by the city's many hot springs, and for its "ruin bars" in the Jewish Quarter.

Lake Balaton

Lake Balaton, sometimes called the "Hungarian Sea", is the country's principal domestic holiday destination, with resort towns such as Siófok, Balatonfüred and Keszthely, the Tihany Abbey, and the nearby Badacsony wine region.

Other destinations

Other important destinations include the historic cities of Eger, Pécs, Sopron and Szentendre; the Tokaj wine region, a World Heritage Site; Hortobágy National Park, known for the Hungarian puszta; and Hollókő, an preserved Palóc village.

Statistics

Most visitors arrive from neighbouring countries, with Germany, Austria, Romania, Slovakia and the United Kingdom among the largest long-haul and European source markets. Tourism directly and indirectly contributes roughly a tenth of Hungary's GDP.
