Back pain

Back pain is pain felt in the back, usually divided anatomically into neck pain (cervical), middle back pain (thoracic), lower back pain (lumbar) and coccydynia (tailbone). Low back pain is the most common form and is the single leading cause of disability worldwide: about 80% of adults experience it at some point in their lives. Most cases are "non-specific" musculoskeletal pain that resolves within weeks, but a minority signal serious underlying disease.

Causes and red flags

The great majority of episodes arise from mechanical causes — strained muscles and ligaments, degenerative discs and facet joints, or a herniated disc pressing on a nerve root (causing sciatica). Clinicians screen for "red flags" suggesting serious pathology: trauma, unexplained weight loss, fever, history of cancer, intravenous drug use, progressive neurological deficit, and bladder or bowel dysfunction (suggesting cauda equina syndrome, a surgical emergency). Imaging is not recommended routinely for non-specific low back pain.

Management

Guidelines emphasise reassurance, advice to remain active, and exercise therapy; heat, spinal manipulation and NSAIDs may help, while bed rest is discouraged. Opioids are discouraged for chronic primary back pain. Surgery — such as discectomy for persistent sciatica or decompression for spinal stenosis — benefits selected patients but is not indicated for non-specific pain. Chronic back pain is best managed with multidisciplinary rehabilitation addressing physical, psychological and occupational factors.
