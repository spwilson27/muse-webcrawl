The identity of the longest word in English depends on the definition of a word and of what counts as English. Words may be artificially constructed, be technical terms, or appear in literature without entering general use.

Candidates

Pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis, at 45 letters, is the longest word in major English dictionaries; it is a word coined by Everett M. Smith in the 1930s, said to be a lung disease caused by inhaling very fine sand and ash dust (silicosis).

Antidisestablishmentarianism (28 letters) is often cited as the longest non-technical, non-coined common word; it refers to opposition to the disestablishment of the Church of England. Floccinaucinihilipilification (29 letters), the act of regarding something as worthless, is the longest non-technical word in the Oxford English Dictionary.

Technical nomenclature can produce far longer words: the full systematic chemical name for the protein titin has 189,819 letters, though it is not considered a dictionary word. Supercalifragilisticexpialidocious (34 letters), popularised by the film Mary Poppins, is the longest widely known coined word in popular culture.
