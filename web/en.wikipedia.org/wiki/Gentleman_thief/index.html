Gentleman thief

A gentleman thief, gentleman burglar, lady thief, or phantom thief is a stock character in fiction. A gentleman thief usually leads a double life as a wealthy member of society, while secretly stealing from other rich people. They are characterised by impeccable manners, stylish dress, charm, and a reluctance to use violence, and they often steal for the thrill or for a personal code of honour rather than from need.

Origins and notable examples

The archetype was established by E. W. Hornung's A. J. Raffles, introduced in 1898, a cricketer and burglar inspired by the Sherlock Holmes stories. In France, Maurice Leblanc created Arsène Lupin in 1905, perhaps the most famous gentleman thief in literature, a master of disguise who frequently outwits the police. Other examples include Leslie Charteris's Simon Templar ("The Saint"), the jewel thief John Robie in To Catch a Thief, and Lupin III, the grandson of Leblanc's character in the Japanese manga and anime franchise.

Characteristics

Gentleman thieves typically target the wealthy, corrupt, or insured, may return items or leave calling cards, and are pursued by a recurring detective or police antagonist. Their stories blend elements of the detective novel, the caper, and romance, with the thief often presented as more sympathetic than the victims or the authorities.
