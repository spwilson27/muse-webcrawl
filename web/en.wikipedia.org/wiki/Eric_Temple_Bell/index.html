Eric Temple Bell

Eric Temple Bell (7 February 1883 – 21 December 1960) was a Scottish-born American mathematician and writer of science fiction and popular mathematics. A professor at the University of Washington and then at the California Institute of Technology, he is remembered for Bell numbers in combinatorics and for his popular book Men of Mathematics (1937).

Life and work

Born in Peterhead, Scotland, Bell emigrated to the United States as a child and was educated at Stanford University and the University of Washington, taking his PhD at Columbia University in 1912. His research concerned number theory and combinatorics; the Bell numbers count the partitions of a set, and Bell polynomials are named for him. Under the pseudonym John Taine he wrote pioneering science-fiction novels, including The Purple Sapphire and The Time Stream.

Men of Mathematics

Bell's 1937 biographical collection Men of Mathematics introduced generations of readers to the lives of famous mathematicians. It is credited with inspiring many later mathematicians, though historians have criticised some of its colourful anecdotes—notably concerning Évariste Galois—as romanticised or inaccurate.
