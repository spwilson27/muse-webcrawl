The Westminster Shorter Catechism is a catechism written in 1646 and 1647 by the Westminster Assembly, a synod of English and Scottish theologians intended to bring the Church of England into greater conformity with the Church of Scotland. It was composed for the instruction of children and those of "weaker capacity" in the Reformed faith, alongside the more detailed Westminster Larger Catechism.

Content

The catechism consists of 107 questions and answers in a format designed for memorization. Its opening question is among the best known lines in Reformed theology: "What is the chief end of man?" — answered, "Man's chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever." The questions proceed through the doctrines of God, creation, sin, Christ's person and work, the application of redemption, the Ten Commandments, the sacraments and prayer.

Use

The Shorter Catechism was adopted by the Church of Scotland in 1648 and remains a subordinate standard in Presbyterian churches around the world. Generations of children learned it by rote, and it has influenced later catechisms in the Reformed tradition.
