The Second Mexican Empire (Spanish: Segundo Imperio mexicano) was a constitutional monarchy established in Mexico from 1864 to 1867 with the support of the French Empire of Napoleon III and Mexican conservatives. Its only emperor was the Austrian archduke Ferdinand Maximilian of Habsburg, who reigned as Maximilian I.

Origins

The empire arose from the French intervention in Mexico. When President Benito Juárez's liberal government suspended foreign debt payments in 1861, France, Britain, and Spain intervened; France alone pursued regime change. After initial defeat at the Battle of Puebla on 5 May 1862, French forces took Mexico City in 1863. A conservative assembly offered the crown to Maximilian, who accepted — believing, on the basis of a managed plebiscite, that the Mexican people had called him — and arrived in May 1864 under the Convention of Miramar, which committed France to military support.

Reign

Maximilian, a liberal-minded monarch, alienated his conservative backers by retaining many Juárez-era reforms, decreeing religious tolerance and peonage reforms, while republicans under Juárez waged relentless guerrilla war from the north. The United States, emerging from its Civil War in 1865, pressed France diplomatically under the Monroe Doctrine and supplied the republicans. Napoleon III, facing Prussian power in Europe, announced the withdrawal of French troops in 1866. Empress Carlota's mission to Europe to plead for support failed, and she suffered a mental collapse.

Collapse

Maximilian refused abdication, was besieged at Querétaro, and was betrayed and captured in May 1867. Despite international pleas for clemency, he was executed by firing squad on 19 June 1867 alongside generals Miguel Miramón and Tomás Mejía — an event immortalized in Édouard Manet's paintings of The Execution of Emperor Maximilian. Juárez restored the republic, and the empire remains a potent symbol in Mexican memory of foreign intervention resisted.
