Volga Bulgars

The Volga Bulgars were a Turkic people who settled in the middle Volga region of what is now Russia in the 7th century and founded the state of Volga Bulgaria (c. 7th century – 1240s), the first organized state in the region. They are the ancestors, in whole or in part, of the modern Volga Tatars and Chuvash people.

Origins

The Bulgars originated among the Oghuric Turkic tribes of the Pontic-Caspian steppe. After the collapse of Old Great Bulgaria under Kubrat in the 7th century, one group led by Kotrag migrated north to the confluence of the Volga and Kama rivers, where they subjugated and merged with local Finno-Ugric and Turkic populations. Their kin who migrated southwest under Asparuh founded Danubian Bulgaria, the ancestor of modern Bulgaria.

Volga Bulgaria

By the 10th century the Volga Bulgars had built a prosperous state with capitals at Bolghar and Bilär, controlling trade along the Volga between Rus', the Baltic, Central Asia, and the Islamic world. In 922 the ruler Almış formally adopted Islam after an embassy from the Abbasid Caliph — the mission chronicled by its secretary, Ibn Fadlan, whose travel account is a key source on the peoples of the steppe, including an early description of the Rus'. The Bulgars farmed, mined, worked leather and metal, and exported furs, grain, and slaves.

Mongol conquest and legacy

Volga Bulgaria repelled an initial Mongol probe in 1223 but was overwhelmed in 1236 during Batu Khan's invasion, its cities destroyed and much of its population killed or absorbed into the Golden Horde. Bulgar cities revived under the Horde, and Bolghar remained a major trade center. The Bulgar legacy is contested in modern identity politics: Volga Tatars and Chuvash (whose language is the sole survivor of the Oghuric branch of Turkic) both claim descent from the Volga Bulgars, and a "Bulgarist" movement in Tatarstan asserts direct continuity.
