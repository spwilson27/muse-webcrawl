Parti Québécois


Abbreviation: PQ
Founded: 11 October 1968
Headquarters: Montreal, Quebec
Ideology: Quebec nationalism, Quebec sovereignty, social democracy
Political position: Centre-left

The Parti Québécois (PQ) is a sovereignist and social democratic provincial political party in Quebec, Canada. It advocates national sovereignty for Quebec and the defence and promotion of the French language and Quebec culture. The party has formed the government of Quebec several times since its first election victory in 1976.

History

The party was founded in 1968 by René Lévesque, a former Liberal cabinet minister who had left that party over its refusal to endorse sovereignty-association. It united several sovereignist groups, including Lévesque's Mouvement Souveraineté-Association and the Ralliement national.

On 15 November 1976 the PQ won a majority government, and Lévesque became premier. His government enacted the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101) in 1977 and held the 1980 referendum on sovereignty-association, which was defeated with about 60 percent voting No. The party lost power in 1985.

Under Jacques Parizeau, the PQ returned to office in 1994 and held a second referendum in 1995, which the Yes side lost extremely narrowly, 50.58 percent to 49.42 percent. Lucien Bouchard succeeded Parizeau as premier. The party subsequently governed under Bernard Landry, and again from 2012 to 2014 under Pauline Marois, Quebec's first female premier, in a minority government.

Later years

The PQ declined in the 2010s amid competition from the Coalition Avenir Québec, falling to reduced standings in the National Assembly, including its worst results in the 2018 and 2022 elections. Under leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon, elected in 2020, the party campaigned on a renewed commitment to holding a sovereignty referendum.

Ideology

The party combines Quebec nationalism with social democratic policies, historically supporting an interventionist state, public services, and labour rights, while drawing support from a broad coalition of sovereignists across the economic spectrum.
