Deep Purple are an English rock band formed in Hertford in 1968. They are considered to be among the pioneers of heavy metal and modern hard rock, although their musical style has changed several times over the years. Originally formed as a progressive rock band, the band shifted to a heavier sound in 1970. Deep Purple, together with Led Zeppelin and Black Sabbath, have been referred to as the "unholy trinity of British hard rock and heavy metal in the early to mid-1970s".

History

The band was formed in 1968 by guitarist Ritchie Blackmore, keyboardist Jon Lord, drummer Ian Paice, bassist Nick Simper and singer Rod Evans. Their early albums combined psychedelic and progressive rock. In 1969 Gillan and Glover joined, and the classic "Mark II" line-up produced the landmark albums In Rock (1970), Fireball (1971) and Machine Head (1972), the latter containing the hit single "Smoke on the Water", whose opening riff is among the most famous in rock music.

Later years

After line-up changes that brought in David Coverdale and Glenn Hughes, and a final incarnation with Tommy Bolin, the band split in 1976. The Mark II line-up reformed in 1984 with Perfect Strangers. Despite further changes, including the departure of Blackmore in 1993 and the death of founding keyboardist Jon Lord in 2012, the band has continued to record and tour. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2016, and have sold well over 100 million records worldwide.
