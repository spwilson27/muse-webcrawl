Flint is a city in the U.S. state of Michigan, located along the Flint River 66 miles (106 km) northwest of Detroit. It is the largest city in Genesee County and was a major center of the American automobile industry, earning it the nickname "Vehicle City".

History

Founded as a fur-trading post, Flint became a lumber center, then a center of carriage manufacture, and from 1908 the birthplace of General Motors. Buick and Chevrolet plants made it one of the great boom towns of the twentieth century; the 1936–37 sit-down strike against GM was a landmark in the history of the United Auto Workers. From the 1980s, plant closures drove steep population decline.

Water crisis

In 2014, as a cost-saving measure under state-appointed emergency managers, Flint switched its water source to the Flint River. Inadequate corrosion treatment caused lead to leach from pipes, producing the Flint water crisis — a public health emergency and national scandal that led to criminal charges, a state takeover of the water system, and a return to Detroit-supplied water in 2015.
