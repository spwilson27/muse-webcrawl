Kettlebell

Kettlebell
OriginRussia (18th century); Russian: гиря (girya)
FormSpherical weight with a single handle
UnitsTraditionally in poods (1 pood ≈ 16.38 kg)
UseStrength and conditioning; kettlebell sport (girevoy sport)

A kettlebell is a cast-iron or steel weight resembling a cannonball with a handle attached to the top, used for ballistic and strength exercises. Kettlebells originated in Russia, where the girya was used first as a counterweight for measuring grain and later for demonstrations of strength at fairs and circuses. Because the weight hangs below the handle, its centre of mass extends beyond the hand, giving kettlebell exercises a distinctive swinging, momentum-based character.

History

Kettlebell training was systematised in the Russian and Soviet military and popularised in the Russian Empire's strongman culture of the late 19th century. Competitive kettlebell lifting — girevoy sport — developed in the Soviet Union, with athletes performing the snatch and the jerk for maximum repetitions with a fixed weight over ten minutes. In the early 2000s, former Soviet special forces trainer Pavel Tsatsouline introduced kettlebells to a mass Western audience, and they became a staple of gyms and functional-fitness programmes.

Exercises

Classic movements include the two-handed swing, the snatch, the clean and press, the goblet squat, the get-up (Turkish get-up) and the windmill. Competition kettlebells ("pro grade") have a uniform 33–35 mm handle and the same shell dimensions in all weights, colour-coded by mass, whereas general fitness kettlebells increase in size with weight.
