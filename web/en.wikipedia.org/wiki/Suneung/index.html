Suneung

The Suneung (Korean: 수능), officially the College Scholastic Ability Test (Daehak Suhak Neungnyeok Siheom), is South Korea's national university entrance examination, held on the third Thursday of November each year. Administered by the Korea Institute for Curriculum and Evaluation, it is taken by several hundred thousand students, and its results are the single most important factor in admission to South Korean universities.

Acronym: CSAT
Type: Standardised test
Administrator: Korea Institute for Curriculum and Evaluation
First held: 1994

Structure

The examination is taken in a single day of back-to-back sessions covering Korean language, mathematics, English, Korean history, and two elective subjects chosen from social studies, science and vocational tracks, with a second foreign language or classical Chinese section at the end. The listening portion of the English test famously prompts nationwide measures: aircraft take-offs and landings are halted during the listening section, banks and offices open late to reduce traffic, and police escort late-arriving students to test centres.

Social significance

The Suneung dominates South Korean secondary education, driving an enormous private tutoring (hagwon) industry and intense pressure on students, many of whom retake the test in later years to improve their scores. In recent years falling birth rates have sharply reduced candidate numbers, and reforms have sought to exclude so-called "killer questions" beyond the public curriculum.
