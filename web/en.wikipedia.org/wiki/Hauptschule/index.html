Hauptschule

A Hauptschule (German: "main school") is a type of secondary school in Germany, Austria and Switzerland offering a lower-secondary education with a practical orientation, typically covering grades 5 to 9 or 10. It is one strand of the traditional tripartite school system, alongside the intermediate Realschule and the academic Gymnasium.

Structure

Pupils generally enter after four years of primary school, at about age ten. The curriculum emphasises German, mathematics, a foreign language (usually English), basic sciences, social studies and pre-vocational subjects. The leaving certificate, the Hauptschulabschluss, qualifies pupils for vocational training in the dual system of apprenticeships, and strong students can progress to further qualifications or transfer to other school types.

Development and debate

Enrolment in Hauptschulen has declined sharply since the 1970s as more families chose Realschulen and Gymnasien, and as comprehensive schools expanded. Several German states have merged the Hauptschule with the Realschule into new school types such as the Oberschule or Sekundarschule, and the standalone Hauptschule has been abolished in Berlin, Hamburg and elsewhere. Critics argue the early tracking of children entrenches social inequality; defenders point to the system's strong vocational pathways.
