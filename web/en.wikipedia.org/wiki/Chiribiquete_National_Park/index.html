Chiribiquete National Park

Chiribiquete National Natural Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional Natural Serranía de Chiribiquete) is the largest national park in Colombia and the largest tropical rainforest national park in the world. It lies in the departments of Caquetá and Guaviare in the Colombian Amazon. The park was established in 1989 and greatly expanded in 2018 to about 43,000 square kilometres (17,000 sq mi). In 2018 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as both a natural and cultural site.

Geography and nature

The park protects the Serranía de Chiribiquete, a group of tepui-like table-top mountains rising from the Amazon rainforest, together with vast areas of lowland forest, savanna, and rivers. The area lies at the junction of four biogeographical provinces—the Amazon, the Andes, the Orinoquia, and the Guiana Shield—giving it exceptionally high biodiversity. Species recorded include jaguars, giant otters, lowland tapirs, and the endemic Chiribiquete emerald hummingbird.

Rock art

Chiribiquete is famous for its extensive prehistoric rock paintings, with tens of thousands of images painted on rock shelters at the bases of the tepuis. Some of the paintings are believed to date back more than 20,000 years, among the earliest evidence of human presence in the Amazon. Motifs include hunters, warriors, dancers, and animals, including depictions of now-extinct megafauna. The area remains home to indigenous peoples living in voluntary isolation, and access to most of the park is restricted.
