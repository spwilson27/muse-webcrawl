Israeli settlement

Israeli settlements are civilian communities of Israeli citizens built on lands captured by Israel in the Six-Day War of 1967, principally in the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) and the Golan Heights; settlements also existed in the Sinai Peninsula and the Gaza Strip until Israel withdrew from them in 1982 and 2005 respectively. As of the mid-2020s, roughly 500,000 settlers live in the West Bank (excluding East Jerusalem, where about 230,000 more reside) and around 20,000 in the Golan Heights, in well over 140 settlements recognised by the Israeli government plus numerous unauthorised outposts.

History

The first settlement, Kfar Etzion, was re-established in September 1967 on the site of a Jewish community destroyed in 1948. Early settlements followed the Allon Plan, placing communities in the Jordan Valley and other strategically valued, sparsely populated areas. After 1977, governments led by Likud, influenced by the religious-nationalist Gush Emunim movement, expanded settlement throughout the West Bank hill country for ideological and religious reasons as well as security ones. Settlement growth continued through the Oslo peace process of the 1990s and after. In 2005, under Ariel Sharon's disengagement plan, Israel evacuated all 21 Gaza settlements and four in the northern West Bank, forcibly removing about 8,000 settlers.

Legal status and international reaction

The international community overwhelmingly considers the settlements illegal under international law, citing Article 49 of the Fourth Geneva Convention, which forbids an occupying power from transferring parts of its own civilian population into occupied territory. This position has been affirmed by the International Court of Justice (2004 advisory opinion on the separation barrier; 2024 advisory opinion declaring Israel's occupation unlawful), the UN Security Council (including Resolution 2334 of 2016), and the International Committee of the Red Cross. Israel disputes this, arguing that the territories are "disputed" rather than occupied and that the Geneva Conventions do not apply de jure; some Israeli legal opinions accept de facto application of humanitarian provisions. In 2019 the United States under the Trump administration stated it no longer viewed settlements as per se inconsistent with international law, a departure from most states' positions.

Administration and politics

Settlers are Israeli citizens who vote in Israeli elections and receive state services, while Palestinian residents of the West Bank live under military law — a dual legal system that critics, including Israeli and international human rights organisations, describe as discriminatory and supporters describe as required by the territory's unresolved status. Settlements are a central issue in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and in negotiations over a two-state solution, with proposed land swaps in past negotiations intended to let Israel retain major settlement blocs near the pre-1967 line.
