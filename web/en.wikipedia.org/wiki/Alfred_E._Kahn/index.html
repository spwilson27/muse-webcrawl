Alfred E. Kahn

Alfred Edward Kahn
Born: 17 October 1917, Paterson, New Jersey
Died: 27 December 2010 (aged 93), Ithaca, New York
Occupation: Economist, Cornell professor
Known for: Airline deregulation; "father of airline deregulation"

Alfred Edward Kahn (October 17, 1917 – December 27, 2010) was an American economist and a leading figure in the deregulation of the American airline industry. He served as chairman of the Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB) under President Jimmy Carter and is often called the "father of airline deregulation."

Career

Kahn taught at Cornell University for most of his career, serving as dean of the College of Arts and Sciences. His two-volume The Economics of Regulation (1970–71) became the standard text on regulated industries, arguing that where competition was workable it should replace regulation.

Appointed to the New York Public Service Commission in 1974 and then to chair the CAB in 1977, Kahn pushed administrative reforms — approving discount fares and new routes — that anticipated the Airline Deregulation Act of 1978, which phased out federal control of routes and fares and abolished the CAB itself in 1985. In 1978–79 he also served as Carter's adviser on inflation, famously cautioning against using the word "depression," then quipping about "banana" instead.

Style and legacy

Known for wit, plain English and self-deprecating humour — he called himself a "good economist" rather than a great one — Kahn defended deregulation's lower fares while acknowledging losses for some communities and workers. He also testified for deregulation in trucking, telecommunications and other sectors.
