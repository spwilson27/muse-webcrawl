Putna Monastery


Putna Monastery, burial place of Stephen the Great.

Putna Monastery (Romanian: Mănăstirea Putna) is a Romanian Orthodox monastery in Putna, Suceava County, in the historical region of Bukovina in northeastern Romania. Founded by Prince Stephen the Great (Ștefan cel Mare), it is one of the most important cultural, religious, and artistic centres of medieval Moldavia and is sometimes called the "Jerusalem of the Romanian people."

History

Stephen the Great built the monastery between 1466 and 1469, according to tradition after victory in battle, choosing the site—legend says—by shooting an arrow from a hilltop and building where it fell. The church was consecrated in 1470. Stephen endowed it lavishly and chose it as his burial place; he was interred there in 1504, along with members of his family. The monastery suffered fires and raids, and the church was rebuilt in the 1650s under Prince Vasile Lupu and his successors, retaining the original plan and much of the fabric of the walls.

Treasury and museum

Putna preserves an exceptional collection of medieval embroidery, manuscripts, and liturgical objects, including Byzantine-style embroidered epitaphoi and veils produced in the monastery's workshops, some commissioned by Stephen himself. Its scriptorium was a major centre of Slavonic and Romanian manuscript production. The monastery's museum displays the tomb cover of Maria of Mangup, Stephen's wife, among the masterpieces of Romanian medieval embroidery.

Significance

Putna is a major pilgrimage site. In 1871, celebrations at Stephen's tomb—organized with the involvement of the young poet Mihai Eminescu and other Romanian students—became a landmark demonstration of Romanian national unity. In 2004, the 500th anniversary of Stephen's death was marked with large commemorations at the monastery.
