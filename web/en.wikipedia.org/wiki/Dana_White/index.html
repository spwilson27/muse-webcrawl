Dana Frederick White Jr. (born 28 July 1969) is an American businessman who serves as the chief executive officer and president of the Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC), the world's largest mixed martial arts organisation. Under his leadership the UFC grew from a struggling, nearly banned promotion into a global sports property worth billions of dollars.

Career

White worked as a boxing trainer and manager in Las Vegas, managing fighters including Tito Ortiz and Chuck Liddell. Learning that the UFC's parent company was looking to sell, he approached childhood friend Lorenzo Fertitta and his brother Frank, whose company Zuffa bought the UFC in 2001 for a reported $2 million, installing White as president. The success of the reality series The Ultimate Fighter (2005) is credited with saving and then transforming the promotion.

In 2016 Zuffa sold the UFC to a group led by WME-IMG (later Endeavor) for about $4 billion; White remained president and received an ownership stake. In 2023, following Endeavor's merger of UFC and WWE into TKO Group Holdings, White's title became CEO of the UFC. He also launched the controversial slap-fighting promotion Power Slap in 2022.

Personal life

White was born in Manchester, Connecticut, and raised mainly in Las Vegas and Maine. In 2012 he was diagnosed with Ménière's disease. He is an outspoken public figure, known for a combative management style, disputes with fighters over pay, and prominent support for Donald Trump, speaking at Republican National Conventions.
