All-India Muslim League

The All-India Muslim League was a political party established in Dhaka (now Bangladesh) on 30 December 1906 to safeguard the political rights of Muslims in British India. Under Muhammad Ali Jinnah it came to demand a separate Muslim state, leading to the creation of Pakistan in 1947.

Founded30 December 1906, Dhaka
FoundersAga Khan III, Nawab Salimullah, Syed Ahmad Khan's movement (precursor)
Key leaderMuhammad Ali Jinnah (from 1913)
IdeologyMuslim nationalism, two-nation theory
OutcomePartition of India; Pakistan, 1947

Early years

Following the Simla Deputation to the Viceroy, Muslim elites founded the League to promote loyalty to the British and protect Muslim interests. It won separate electorates for Muslims in the Indian Councils Act 1909. Jinnah, also a Congress member, brokered the 1916 Lucknow Pact between League and Congress.

Toward Pakistan

Relations deteriorated in the 1930s. The Lahore Resolution of March 1940 demanded independent states in Muslim-majority regions — the basis of the Pakistan demand. The League swept Muslim seats in the 1945–46 elections, and after the failure of the Cabinet Mission Plan and Direct Action Day violence, partition followed in August 1947. The League's main body became Pakistan's ruling party before fragmenting.
