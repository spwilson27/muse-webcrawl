Suona

Suona
ClassificationDouble-reed aerophone (shawm)
Other namesLaba; haidi
OriginChina (from Central Asian surnay, by the Ming dynasty)
Related instrumentsZurna, shehnai, nadaswaram, taepyeongso

The suona (simplified Chinese: 唢呐; traditional Chinese: 嗩吶), also called the laba or haidi, is a Chinese double-reed woodwind instrument. It consists of a conical wooden body with finger holes, a double reed set in a small metal tube, and a detachable flared metal bell. The suona produces a bright, piercing and exceptionally loud tone, and is traditionally played at weddings, funerals, festivals, temple fairs and processions across China, as well as in Chinese opera and modern Chinese orchestras.

History

The instrument derives from the Central Asian surnay (zurna), transmitted to China along the Silk Road, and is depicted in art as early as the 3rd–5th centuries along the route; it became firmly established in China by the Ming dynasty, when it was used in court and military music. Sizes range from small, high-pitched instruments to large bass suona more than a metre long, and regional variants are found throughout China and in the Chinese diaspora. A keyed, Western-influenced version was developed in the 20th century for use in Chinese orchestras.

Technique and repertoire

Suona players employ circular breathing for sustained notes and are famed for virtuosic imitation of birdsong and other sounds; the celebrated solo piece "A Hundred Birds Paying Homage to the Phoenix" (Bainiao Chaofeng) showcases these effects. The instrument is a core member of chuida (blowing-and-drumming) ensembles and of the accompaniment of many regional opera forms.
