Sangh Parivar

The Sangh Parivar (Hindi: संघ परिवार, "Family of the Sangh" or "RSS family") is an umbrella term for the network of Hindu nationalist organisations inspired by the ideology of the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS), founded in 1925. The organisations of the Parivar work in politics, labour, education, religion, student activism, and social service, and are united by the ideology of Hindutva.

Constituent organisations

  • Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) — the parent organisation, a volunteer paramilitary-structured cultural body.
  • Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) — the political wing and one of India's two major national parties; in power nationally since 2014.
  • Vishva Hindu Parishad (VHP) — religious organisation founded in 1964 to consolidate Hindu society; central to the Ram Janmabhoomi movement.
  • Bajrang Dal — the VHP's youth wing.
  • Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (ABVP) — one of the world's largest student organisations.
  • Bharatiya Mazdoor Sangh (BMS) — a major national trade union federation.
  • Vidya Bharati — a network running thousands of schools; Seva Bharati coordinates welfare work; Rashtra Sevika Samiti is the women's counterpart of the RSS.

Influence and criticism

The Sangh Parivar is among the most influential socio-political movements in modern India: BJP governments led by Atal Bihari Vajpayee and Narendra Modi have drawn senior leadership largely from RSS ranks. Critics accuse the Parivar of promoting majoritarianism and contributing to communal tensions, pointing to episodes such as the 1992 demolition of the Babri Masjid by activists linked to the movement; supporters describe it as a nationalist, charitable, and cultural renaissance movement engaged in disaster relief and social uplift. Scholars debate the degree of central control the RSS exercises over formally independent affiliates.
