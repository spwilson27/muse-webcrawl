Pub quiz

A pub quiz is a quiz event held in a pub or bar, in which teams of participants answer questions read aloud by a quizmaster (or, increasingly, delivered by audio-visual equipment or smartphone apps), competing for prizes. Pub quizzes are a fixture of social life in the United Kingdom and Ireland and have spread to many other countries, where they are often called trivia nights.

History

Organised pub quizzes took off in Britain in the 1970s as breweries and landlords sought to attract customers on quiet weekday evenings. Companies such as Burnt Oak Quiz Leagues supplied ready-made question sets, and local leagues codified formats with teams, rounds and aggregate scoring. The format crossed the Atlantic, becoming a staple of bars in the United States, and spread through the Commonwealth and Europe.

Format

A typical quiz comprises several rounds of questions on general knowledge and themed topics such as sport, music, geography, film and current affairs. Special rounds may include picture rounds, music clips, dingbats or a "wipeout" round in which a wrong answer zeroes the round score. Teams usually write answers on paper sheets that are swapped and marked between rounds; a running scoreboard is kept, and the evening often ends with a jackpot question or rollover prize. Entry fees, if charged, commonly fund the prize pot.

Rules and controversies

Rules vary by venue, but most prohibit the use of phones and reference materials — a rule increasingly tested by smartphones, leading some quizzes to adopt app-based locked answering systems. Disputes over ambiguous answers are resolved by the quizmaster, whose decision is traditionally final. Prizes range from cash and bar tabs to novelty items, and charity quizzes are a common fundraising format.

In popular culture

The pub quiz has featured in British television and film as an emblem of pub culture, and competitive quizzing more broadly is served by national championships and the World Quizzing Championships.
