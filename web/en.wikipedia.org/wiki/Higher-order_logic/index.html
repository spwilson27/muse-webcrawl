Higher-order logic

In mathematics and logic, a higher-order logic (HOL) is a form of logic that is distinguished from first-order logic by additional quantifiers and, sometimes, stronger semantics. Higher-order logics with their standard semantics are more expressive, but their model-theoretic properties are less well-behaved than those of first-order logic.

The term "higher-order logic" is commonly used to mean higher-order simple predicate logic. Here "simple" indicates that the underlying type theory is the theory of simple types, as in the work of Alonzo Church, who introduced the simple theory of types and higher-order logic in "A Formulation of the Simple Theory of Types" (1940).

Quantification scope

First-order logic quantifies only over individual variables. Second-order logic additionally quantifies over sets, relations, and functions of individuals. Third-order logic quantifies over sets of sets, and so forth. Higher-order logic is the union of all these logics: it permits quantification over variables of any type or order. In typed formulations, variables range over types, and one may quantify over predicates, functions, and even over types themselves (in impredicative systems).

Semantics

There are two kinds of semantics for higher-order logic:

  • Standard (or full) semantics — predicate variables of a given type range over all sets or relations of that type over the domain. Under standard semantics, second-order logic is highly expressive (for example, it characterizes the natural numbers up to isomorphism) but lacks a complete proof system and fails compactness and the Löwenheim–Skolem properties, as follows from Gödel's incompleteness theorems.
  • Henkin (general) semantics — introduced by Leon Henkin, models include only a specified collection of sets of each type. Under Henkin semantics, higher-order logic behaves much like many-sorted first-order logic: it admits a complete proof calculus, compactness, and Löwenheim–Skolem theorems.

Expressive power and properties

With standard semantics, second-order Peano arithmetic is categorical — all its models are isomorphic to the standard natural numbers — something first-order arithmetic cannot achieve. The price is metalogical: the set of validities of full second-order logic is not recursively enumerable, so no effective deductive system can be complete for it. Higher-order logics also fail Lindström-type maximality properties that single out first-order logic.

Proof assistants

Higher-order logic is the logical foundation of several interactive theorem provers, collectively called the HOL family (such as HOL Light and Isabelle/HOL), as well as Coq, which uses a still stronger higher-order system, the calculus of constructions. These tools are used in formal verification of hardware and software and in formalized mathematics.
