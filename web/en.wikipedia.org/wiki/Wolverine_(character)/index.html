Wolverine (character)

Wolverine (born James Howlett), commonly known by his alias Logan, is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics, mostly in association with the X-Men. Created by writer Len Wein and art director John Romita Sr., with his first full appearance in The Incredible Hulk #181 (1974), he is a mutant with animal-keen senses, enhanced physical abilities, a powerful regenerative healing factor, and three retractable claws in each hand.

Publication history

Wolverine joined the X-Men in Giant-Size X-Men #1 (1975) and, under writer Chris Claremont and artists Dave Cockrum and John Byrne, developed from a one-note berserker into one of Marvel's most popular characters. The 1982 Wolverine limited series by Claremont and Frank Miller deepened his character through his ties to Japan. His deliberately murky past was explored for decades before the Origin miniseries (2001) revealed his childhood as James Howlett in 19th-century Canada.

Fictional biography

Born in the 1830s, Logan fought in both World Wars and numerous covert programmes. In the Weapon X project his skeleton was bonded with the indestructible metal adamantium and his memories repeatedly tampered with. As a long-standing X-Man he serves as both the team's most dangerous fighter and, at times, a mentor and headmaster of the Jean Grey School. His children and clones include Daken and X-23 (Laura Kinney), who later took up the Wolverine name.

In other media

Hugh Jackman portrayed Wolverine in the X-Men film series from X-Men (2000) through Logan (2017) and Deadpool & Wolverine (2024), one of the longest-running portrayals of a comic-book character in film.
