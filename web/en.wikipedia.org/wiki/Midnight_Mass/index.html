Midnight Mass

Midnight Mass
GenreLiturgical celebration
Observed byRoman Catholics, Anglicans, Lutherans and other Christians

Midnight Mass is a tradition in the Roman Catholic Church and in some Anglican, Lutheran and other Christian communities, in which the first liturgy of Christmas Day is celebrated at (or shortly before) midnight on Christmas Eve. It is one of the most popular and widely attended services of the Christmas season, marking the birth of Jesus in Bethlehem.

History

The custom is commonly traced to the fourth century and the Church in Jerusalem and Rome. In Rome, the pope traditionally celebrated a Mass at midnight in the Basilica of Saint Mary Major, where relics associated with the crib of Christ are kept. Over the centuries three Christmas Masses became customary — at midnight, at dawn, and during the day — each with its own readings and theological emphasis; the midnight celebration focuses on the birth of Christ announced to the shepherds.

Liturgy

The service commonly includes the singing of carols before Mass, the blessing of the crib or nativity scene, readings from Isaiah and the Gospel of Luke's account of the Nativity, and hymns such as "O Come, All Ye Faithful", "Silent Night" and "Hark! The Herald Angels Sing". Candlelight and incense are frequently used, and in many parishes the Mass concludes with the singing of "Joy to the World".

Variations

Although traditionally celebrated at midnight, many churches now hold the service earlier in the evening ("Midnight Mass" at 10 or 11 p.m., or an early-evening family Mass), owing to pastoral practicality. The Pope's Christmas Midnight Mass in St Peter's Basilica in Vatican City is broadcast worldwide. In the Eastern Orthodox tradition, a comparable festal vigil and Divine Liturgy are served for the Nativity. The term "Midnight Mass" may also refer to the Easter Vigil celebrated on Holy Saturday night.

In popular culture

The name was used as the title of the 2021 Netflix supernatural horror miniseries Midnight Mass created by Mike Flanagan, set in an isolated island fishing community; the title refers both to the liturgy and to its role in the story's climax.
