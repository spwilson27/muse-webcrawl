William McGonagall

William Topaz McGonagall (c. March 1825 – 29 September 1902) was a Scottish poet and actor, famed — and affectionately mocked — as reputedly the worst poet in British history, known for doggerel such as The Tay Bridge Disaster.

Born: c. 1825, Edinburgh (of Irish parents)
Died: 1902, Edinburgh
Occupation: Weaver, poet, actor

Life

A Dundee handloom weaver, McGonagall began writing poetry in 1877, self-publishing broadsheets and performing dramatic readings, often amid audience heckling he took as praise. He walked to Balmoral hoping to meet Queen Victoria.

Poetry

His work, marked by forced rhymes and scansion, commemorated disasters, battles, and temperance themes. The Tay Bridge poem (1880) is his best known. Later writers celebrated his unintentional comedy; he inspired characters in popular culture. See Doggerel and Dundee.
