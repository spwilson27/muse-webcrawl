Blowback (intelligence)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Blowback is a term originating from the Central Intelligence Agency, defined as the unintended consequences, suffered by the civil population of a state, of that government's international activities, usually of a secret or covert nature. The term was first used in the CIA's internal history of the 1953 Iranian coup.

Usage

Because the operations producing blowback are kept secret from the public, the retaliatory effects appear to come from nowhere, which commentators argue distorts public debate. The writer Chalmers Johnson popularised the broader political usage in his 2000 book Blowback: The Costs and Consequences of American Empire.

Examples discussed in the literature

Analysts frequently cite the support given to Afghan mujahideen in the 1980s and the 1953 coup in Iran as cases whose long-term consequences are described as blowback. See also Covert operation and Unintended consequences.
