Henri Nestlé, founder of the world's largest food company.

Henri Nestlé (born Heinrich Nestle; 1814–1890) was a German-born Swiss pharmacist and businessman who founded the food company Nestlé in Vevey, Switzerland, and developed one of the first commercial infant formulas. Frenchifying his name after settling in francophone Switzerland, he built his business first on mustard, cereals, lamp oil, and gas lighting before turning to infant nutrition.

Farine Lactée and the company

Concerned by high infant mortality and the plight of babies who could not be breastfed, Nestlé combined cow's milk, wheat flour, and sugar into a dried "milk food," Farine Lactée, launched in 1867. Legend in company history holds that the product saved the life of a premature infant who could take nothing else, driving its rapid adoption. Nestlé's factories soon exported across Europe, the Americas, and Asia; his trademark — a bird's nest with a mother bird feeding her young, derived from his family name (Swabian for "little nest") — became one of the world's most recognised logos.

Later life

Nestlé sold the company in 1875 to a group of businessmen for one million francs and retired to Montreux and Glion, where he funded local amenities including a fountain and street lighting. The firm bearing his name merged with the Anglo-Swiss Condensed Milk Company in 1905 and grew into the world's largest food and beverage corporation.
