History of Australia (1788–1850)

The history of Australia from 1788 to 1850 covers the early British colonial period, beginning with the arrival of the First Fleet at Sydney Cove in January 1788 and the establishment of the penal colony of New South Wales.

Colonisation

Governor Arthur Phillip founded the settlement at Port Jackson. Further colonies followed: Van Diemen's Land (1803), Moreton Bay, the Swan River Colony (1829, free settlers), South Australia (1836), Port Phillip District (later Victoria) and New Zealand briefly administered from NSW.

Impact on Indigenous peoples

Colonisation brought disease, dispossession and frontier violence that devastated Aboriginal populations. Pastoral expansion, convict transportation (ending for NSW in 1840 and continuing elsewhere), and the beginnings of self-government shaped the era that ended as gold rushes began in 1851.
