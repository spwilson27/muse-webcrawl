Bangladesh Bank robbery
Date4–5 February 2016
LocationDhaka, Bangladesh
TypeCyber heist
TargetBangladesh Bank account at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York
LossesUS$81 million transferred (of US$951 million attempted)

The Bangladesh Bank robbery, also known colloquially as the Bangladesh Bank cyber heist, took place in February 2016, when instructions to steal US$951 million from the account of Bangladesh Bank, the central bank of Bangladesh, at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York were carried out via the SWIFT network. Five of the thirty-five fraudulent transfer instructions succeeded, moving US$101 million in total, of which US$81 million went to accounts in the Philippines and US$20 million to Sri Lanka. The Sri Lankan transfer was recovered; most of the Philippine money was laundered through casinos and only partly recovered.

How the attack worked

Attackers compromised Bangladesh Bank's systems and its Alliance Access SWIFT terminal, using malware to manipulate confirmation messages and conceal the fraud over a weekend. A printer that produced paper confirmations of transfers was disabled, delaying discovery. A spelling error in one beneficiary name ("Shalika Foundation") helped trigger scrutiny that stopped the remaining transfers.

Aftermath

The heist led to the resignation of Bangladesh Bank governor Atiur Rahman and a long-running international investigation. US officials and security researchers linked the operation to the Lazarus Group and to North Korea. In 2019 Bangladesh Bank sued the Philippine Rizal Commercial Banking Corporation (RCBC) and other parties in a US court to recover the funds.
