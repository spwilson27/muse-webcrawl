Bahauddin Zakariya (Urdu: بہاء الدین زکریا; c. 1170 – 1262), also known as Baha-ul-Haq, was a Punjabi Sunni Muslim scholar, saint and poet who established the Suhrawardiyya Sufi order in South Asia from his base in Multan, in present-day Pakistan. He is among the most revered saints of the region.

Life

Descended from a family that had migrated from Mecca, Bahauddin travelled to study in Khurasan, Bukhara and Baghdad, where he became a disciple of Shihab al-Din Umar al-Suhrawardi, author of the Awarif al-Ma'arif. Returning to Multan, he built a great khanqah (hospice) that became a centre of learning and relief, attracting rulers and commoners alike; he was a contemporary and correspondent of Baba Farid and Lal Shahbaz Qalandar.

Legacy

His octagonal shrine in Multan, partly rebuilt after damage in the 1848 siege, remains a major pilgrimage centre, and his descendants — the Qureshi family — long held influence in the city. Bahauddin Zakariya University in Multan is named after him.
