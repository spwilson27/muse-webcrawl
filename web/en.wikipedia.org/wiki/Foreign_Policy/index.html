Foreign Policy is an American news publication, founded in 1970 and focused on global affairs, current events and domestic and international policy. It began as a quarterly scholarly journal offering alternatives to establishment views during the Vietnam War era, and has since become a magazine and website with daily reporting and analysis.

History

The magazine was founded by Samuel P. Huntington and Warren Demian Manshel as the Foreign Policy quarterly. For three decades it was published by the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, which sold it in 2008 to the Washington Post Company (now Graham Holdings). Under editor Moisés Naím in the 2000s it was redesigned and refocused on a general readership, winning National Magazine Awards.

Features

Foreign Policy is known for long-form analysis, its blogs and newsletters, and the annual "Global Thinkers" issues. Its website carries reporting on diplomacy, security, economics and climate, aimed at policymakers and internationally engaged readers.
