Dos Erres massacre

LocationDos Erres, La Libertad, Petén, Guatemala
Date6–8 December 1982
TargetVillage residents
DeathsMore than 200
PerpetratorsKaibiles, Guatemalan Army

The Dos Erres massacre of 6 December 1982 took place in Dos Erres, a small village in the municipality of La Libertad, in the northern Petén department of Guatemala. The name of the village, occasionally given as "Las Dos Erres", literally means "two Rs", originating from the two Rs in the surnames of the original land grant petitioners.

In December 1982, under the de facto presidency of General Efraín Ríos Montt, a group of soldiers belonging to the Kaibiles, the elite commando unit of the Guatemalan Army, killed over 200 civilians in Dos Erres in an effort to find weapons stolen from the army by guerrillas, following an ambush of an army convoy nearby. The events were subsequently covered up, and the village was abandoned.

Background

Dos Erres was a farming village founded in the 1970s in the Petén jungle as part of a government colonization scheme. By 1982 Guatemala was in the midst of a brutal civil war between the military government and leftist guerrilla organizations, including the Fuerzas Armadas Rebeldes (FAR). On 21 November 1982 a guerrilla ambush near the village killed 17 soldiers and resulted in the theft of military weapons. The army suspected the villagers of sympathizing with the guerrillas.

Events

On the night of 5–6 December 1982, a patrol of around 40 Kaibiles entered Dos Erres disguised as guerrillas. Over the following days the soldiers systematically killed the inhabitants of the village — men, women and children — accusing them of being guerrilla collaborators. Many victims were thrown into the village well. Two young girls were raped before being killed. In total, more than 200 people were murdered; a small number of children were taken away by soldiers, and two boys survived and later became key witnesses.

Aftermath and prosecutions

The massacre was denied by the Guatemalan government for years. Investigations in the 1990s, including exhumations by forensic anthropologists, confirmed the mass killings. The case became one of the emblematic crimes examined by Guatemala's Historical Clarification Commission (CEH), which concluded in 1999 that state forces had committed acts of genocide in parts of the country during the scorched-earth campaigns of 1981–1983.

In 2011 a Guatemalan court sentenced three former Kaibiles and a former army lieutenant to more than 6,000 years in prison each for their roles in the massacre. Another former Kaibil, Jorge Vinicio Orantes Sosa, was prosecuted in the United States for lying on immigration forms and was sentenced in 2014. Former president Efraín Ríos Montt died in 2018 while facing retrial on genocide charges.
