Hank Williams

Hiram 'Hank' Williams
Born: 17 September 1923, Mount Olive, Alabama
Died: 1 January 1953, Oak Hill, West Virginia
Genres: Country, honky-tonk, blues
Hits: 'Your Cheatin' Heart', 'Hey, Good Lookin'', 'I'm So Lonesome I Could Cry'

Hank Williams (1923–1953) was an American singer-songwriter regarded as one of the most influential figures in country music. In a career cut short at 29, he recorded dozens of hits that became standards.

Early life

Born with spina bifida occulta, which caused lifelong pain, Williams learned guitar from bluesman Rufus 'Tee-Tot' Payne, performed on Alabama radio, and formed the Drifting Cowboys.

Success

Signed to MGM Records, he scored his breakthrough with 'Move It On Over' (1947) and 'Lovesick Blues' (1949), joined the Grand Ole Opry, and wrote classics including 'Cold, Cold Heart', 'Jambalaya (On the Bayou)' and 'Your Cheatin' Heart'.

Decline and death

Alcohol and painkiller dependence led to his Opry dismissal in 1952. He died of heart failure in a car en route to a show on New Year's Day 1953. His son Hank Williams Jr. and grandson Hank Williams III also became country performers.
