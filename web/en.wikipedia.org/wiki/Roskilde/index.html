Roskilde

Country: Denmark

Region: Zealand

Population: approx. 53,000

Founded: c. 980s (Viking Age)

Roskilde is a city in Denmark, west of Copenhagen on the island of Zealand, at the southern end of Roskilde Fjord. One of Denmark's oldest cities, it was founded in the Viking Age, served as the Danish capital until the fifteenth century, and remains famous for Roskilde Cathedral, the burial church of the Danish monarchs, and for the Roskilde Festival, one of Europe's largest music festivals.

History

According to Adam of Bremen and Saxo Grammaticus, Roskilde was founded in the 980s by King Harald Bluetooth, who built a church and royal estate there. It became a bishop's seat around 1020 and grew into medieval Denmark's most important city. The Treaty of Roskilde of 1658, by which Denmark ceded Scania and other provinces to Sweden, was signed here. The first Danish railway, from Copenhagen, reached Roskilde in 1847.

Landmarks

Roskilde Cathedral (c. 1170–1280) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the earliest Gothic churches built of brick; nearly 40 Danish kings and queens are buried there, in chapels spanning eight centuries. The Viking Ship Museum, on the fjord, displays five original Viking ships (the Skuldelev ships) scuttled around 1070 to blockade the harbour channel, along with reconstructed sailing vessels. Roskilde University (founded 1972) lies east of the city.

Roskilde Festival

Held since 1971 on grounds south of the city, the Roskilde Festival is a non-profit event run by volunteers, attracting around 130,000 participants and major international acts; its proceeds support charitable and cultural causes. The festival is equally known for its tent city and traditions such as the annual naked run around the campsite.
