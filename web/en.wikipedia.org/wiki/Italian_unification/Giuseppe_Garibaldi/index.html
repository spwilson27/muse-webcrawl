Giuseppe Garibaldi

Giuseppe Maria Garibaldi (4 July 1807 – 2 June 1882) was an Italian general, patriot, and republican who contributed to the Italian unification and the creation of the Kingdom of Italy. He is considered one of Italy's "fathers of the fatherland" and a central figure of the Risorgimento, celebrated internationally as the "Hero of Two Worlds" for his campaigns in both South America and Europe.

Early life and South America

Born in Nice (then part of the Kingdom of Sardinia), Garibaldi became a sailor and joined Giuseppe Mazzini's Young Italy movement. Condemned to death after a failed 1834 insurrection, he fled to South America, where he fought for the Riograndense Republic in Brazil and for Uruguay against Argentina, learning the guerrilla tactics that would define his career.

Expedition of the Thousand

In May 1860, Garibaldi sailed from Genoa with about a thousand volunteers — the famous redshirts (camicie rosse) — landing at Marsala, Sicily. In a stunning campaign he defeated the Bourbon Kingdom of the Two Sicilies' much larger army, taking Palermo and Naples. At Teano in October 1860 he handed the conquered south to Victor Emmanuel II, famously refusing personal power.

Later campaigns and legacy

Garibaldi later led volunteers against Austria (1866) and against the Papal States at Aspromonte (1862) and Mentana (1867), where he was wounded and defeated. In 1870–71 he fought for the French Republic against Prussia. He retired to the island of Caprera, where he died in 1882. His red shirt became a global symbol of revolutionary volunteerism.
