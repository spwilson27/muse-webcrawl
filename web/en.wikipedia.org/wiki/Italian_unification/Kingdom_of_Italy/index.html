Kingdom of Italy

The Kingdom of Italy (Regno d'Italia) was a state formed on 17 March 1861, when Victor Emmanuel II of Sardinia was proclaimed King of Italy, uniting most of the peninsula for the first time since antiquity. It existed until 1946, when Italians voted in a referendum to abolish the monarchy and establish the Italian Republic.

Unification and liberal Italy

The kingdom grew out of the Risorgimento, incorporating Venetia in 1866 and Rome in 1870, when the capture of Porta Pia ended the Papal States' temporal rule. The liberal era (1861–1915) saw parliamentary government under prime ministers from Cavour's successors to Giovanni Giolitti, alongside colonial expansion in Eritrea, Somalia, and (after the Italo-Turkish War of 1911–12) Libya and the Dodecanese.

The Fascist period

Italy fought on the Allied side in the First World War, gaining Trentino and Trieste but experiencing postwar discontent at the "mutilated victory." Benito Mussolini's Fascist movement seized power with the March on Rome in October 1922. The dictatorship annexed Ethiopia in 1936, proclaimed an empire, allied with Nazi Germany in the Axis, and entered the Second World War in 1940.

Collapse

Mussolini was dismissed by King Victor Emmanuel III in July 1943; Italy co-belligerenced with the Allies while German forces occupied the north and a civil war raged between partisans and the Fascist Italian Social Republic. The referendum of 2 June 1946 ended the monarchy by a narrow margin, and the kingdom gave way to the Republic.
