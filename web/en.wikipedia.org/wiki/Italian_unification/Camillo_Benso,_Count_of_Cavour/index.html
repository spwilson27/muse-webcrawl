Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour

Camillo Paolo Filippo Giulio Benso, Count of Cavour (10 August 1810 – 6 June 1861) was an Italian statesman and a leading figure in the movement toward Italian unification. As Prime Minister of the Kingdom of Sardinia, he engineered the diplomatic and military alliances that made unification possible, and he became the first Prime Minister of the Kingdom of Italy.

Early life and career

Born into the Piedmontese nobility, Cavour trained as a military engineer before turning to journalism and agricultural reform. He founded the newspaper Il Risorgimento in 1847, advocating constitutional monarchy, free trade, and railway development. Elected to the Piedmontese parliament in 1848, he became Minister of Agriculture, then Finance, and in 1852 Prime Minister of Sardinia-Piedmont under Victor Emmanuel II.

Path to unification

Cavour modernized Piedmont's economy and army, then pursued a brilliant diplomatic strategy. Piedmont's participation in the Crimean War (1855) won it a seat at the Congress of Paris, where Cavour raised the Italian question. The secret Plombières Agreement (1858) with Napoleon III secured French military support against Austria in exchange for Savoy and Nice. The resulting Second Italian War of Independence (1859) expelled Austria from Lombardy.

When Giuseppe Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand conquered the south in 1860, Cavour adroitly annexed the Papal States' northern territories to link up with him, and engineered the plebiscites that united the peninsula. The Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed on 17 March 1861.

Death and legacy

Cavour died of illness on 6 June 1861, only months after unification, reportedly saying "Italy is made." Alongside Garibaldi, Mazzini, and Victor Emmanuel II, he is counted among the "fathers of the fatherland" (padri della patria). Historians credit his pragmatic, anti-revolutionary liberalism as the decisive instrument of the Risorgimento.
