Lansdowne is a cantonment town in the Pauri Garhwal district of Uttarakhand, India. Situated at an elevation of about 1,700 metres amid thick oak and pine forests, it is one of the quietest hill stations in the Garhwal Himalayas. The town was founded in 1887 by the British as a training centre for the Garhwal Rifles and was named after Lord Lansdowne, then Viceroy of India; its original name was Kaludanda, "black forest" in Garhwali.

Lansdowne remains the regimental centre of the Garhwal Rifles of the Indian Army, whose presence shapes the town's orderly, uncrowded character. The regiment's Darwan Singh Museum displays its history and honours its Victoria Cross recipients.

History

The site was developed from 1887 as a cantonment for Garhwali recruits. Unlike many Himalayan hill stations, Lansdowne was primarily a military station rather than a summer retreat, and it retains a small resident population and a gentle pace.

Places of interest

Local sights include Tip-n-Top viewpoint, Bhulla Tal (an artificial lake maintained by the regiment), St Mary's and St John's churches, the Santoshi Mata temple, and walks through the surrounding forest; on clear days the town offers views of the Chaukhamba and other Garhwal snow peaks.

Transport

The nearest railhead is Kotdwar, about 40 km away, connected by road; Lansdowne lies roughly 250 km from Delhi.
