Electrical ballast

An electrical ballast is a device placed in series with an electrical load to limit the amount of current flowing through it. Ballasts are essential for gas-discharge lamps such as fluorescent lamps and high-intensity discharge lamps, which exhibit negative resistance: once struck, the arc would draw ever-increasing current and destroy itself without a current-limiting device.

Magnetic ballasts

The traditional ballast is a coil of wire wound on an iron core — essentially an inductor — which limits alternating current through its inductive reactance and can also, in transformer-type designs, provide the high voltage needed to strike the lamp. Simple resistor ballasts were used with some early lamps but waste energy as heat. Magnetic ballasts operate at mains frequency, which can cause flicker and an audible hum.

Electronic ballasts

Electronic ballasts use semiconductor circuitry to convert mains power to a high-frequency alternating current (typically 20–100 kHz) before supplying the lamp.operating at high frequency improves lamp efficacy, eliminates perceptible flicker and hum, and allows features such as dimming and programmed start. With the decline of fluorescent lighting in favour of LEDs, the role of ballasts has diminished, though LED lamps have analogous current-regulating "driver" circuits.
