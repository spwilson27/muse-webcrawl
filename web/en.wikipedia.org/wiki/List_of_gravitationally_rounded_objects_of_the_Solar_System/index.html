List of gravitationally rounded objects of the Solar System

Gravitationally rounded objects
Bodies massive enough for their own gravity to pull them into hydrostatic equilibrium — a spheroid shape.

This is a list of Solar System objects that are in, or near, hydrostatic equilibrium: bodies massive enough that self-gravity overcomes rigid-body forces and pulls them into a rounded (ellipsoidal) shape. The list includes the Sun, the eight planets, the IAU-recognised dwarf planets, and dozens of planetary-mass moons and candidate dwarf planets.

Moons and other candidates

Rounded moons include Earth's Moon, the four Galilean moons of Jupiter (Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto — Ganymede being the largest moon in the Solar System), Saturn's Titan, Enceladus and others, Uranus's five major moons, Neptune's Triton and Pluto's Charon. Trans-Neptunian candidates such as Gonggong, Quaoar, Sedna and Orcus are also believed to be rounded based on their size and density.

The IAU has not formally recognised most candidates as dwarf planets; planetary geologists such as Michael Brown argue the hydrostatic-equilibrium list numbers several dozen objects.

References: IAU resolutions (2006); JPL Small-Body Database; Brown, M. E., "How many dwarf planets are there in the outer solar system?"
