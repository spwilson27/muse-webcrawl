Anthony Frank Hawk (born May 12, 1968), nicknamed the Birdman, is an American professional skateboarder, entrepreneur, and the owner of the skateboard company Birdhouse. A pioneer of modern vertical skateboarding, he completed the first documented "900" — a trick involving two and a half mid-air revolutions — at the 1999 X Games, and won 73 titles in his competitive career.

Career

Hawk turned professional at age 14 and by 16 was widely regarded as the best competitive skateboarder in the world, dominating vert competitions throughout the 1980s as a member of the Bones Brigade team. When skateboarding's popularity collapsed in the early 1990s, he founded Birdhouse in 1992. The sport's resurgence through the X Games restored his prominence, and in 1999, after eleven attempts in a single contest, he landed the 900, retiring from professional competition that year.

Video games and media

In 1999 Hawk licensed his name to Activision for Tony Hawk's Pro Skater, which became a landmark video game franchise credited with popularising skateboarding worldwide and generating over $1.4 billion in sales across its many instalments. He has appeared in films and television, founded the action sports tour Boom Boom HuckJam, and in 2022 was the subject of the documentary Tony Hawk: Until the Wheels Fall Off. In 2021 he performed a final series of his signature tricks, and later came back from a broken leg in 2022 to continue skating.

Philanthropy

Hawk founded the Tony Hawk Foundation in 2002 (renamed The Skatepark Project in 2020), which has funded the construction of hundreds of public skateparks in low-income communities across the United States.
