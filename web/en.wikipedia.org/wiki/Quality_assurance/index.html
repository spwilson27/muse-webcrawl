Quality assurance

Quality assurance (QA) is the systematic maintenance of a desired level of quality in a service or product, especially by means of attention to every stage of the process of delivery or production. Where quality control focuses on detecting defects in finished output, quality assurance is process-oriented and preventive: it aims to build quality into the way work is done so that defects do not arise in the first place.

The plan–do–check–act cycle underlies many QA systems.

Concepts

Quality assurance encompasses the planned and systematic activities implemented within a quality management system — process definition, training, auditing, documentation and continuous improvement — so that quality requirements will be fulfilled. Failure testing, statistical process control and root-cause analysis are typical QA tools. In software engineering, QA includes code review, testing strategies and release management; the term is sometimes used loosely to mean software testing itself, though testing is properly only one part of QA.

Standards

The ISO 9000 family of standards, first published in 1987, provides an international framework for quality management systems and is used by organisations of every size to demonstrate consistent quality. Regulated industries impose their own regimes, such as Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) in pharmaceuticals and food, and the capability maturity models used in software and services.

History

Formal quality ideas emerged with mass production in the early twentieth century and the statistical methods of Walter Shewhart. After the Second World War, W. Edwards Deming and Joseph Juran carried quality management to Japan, where practices such as total quality management and kaizen developed, later influencing industry worldwide.
