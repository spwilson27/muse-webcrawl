Esrom Tom Kruse MBE (28 August 1914 – 30 June 2011) was an Australian mailman and outback legend, famed for delivering mail and supplies along the Birdsville Track in the remote north of South Australia and south-west Queensland for nearly thirty years. His exploits were made famous by John Heyer's 1954 documentary film The Back of Beyond.

The Birdsville mail run

Kruse took over the mail contract in 1936 from pioneer Harry Ding, running from Marree in South Australia to Birdsville in Queensland — a route of about 500 kilometres across the Sturt Stony Desert and the floodplains of Cooper Creek. Driving war-surplus Leyland Badger trucks, he made the fortnightly trip in conditions of extreme heat, bogging sand, and periodic floods, often repairing his vehicle with bush improvisation. A broken back suffered in a 1950s accident — after which he drove hundreds of kilometres in agony — became part of his legend. He held the run until 1963, and continued cartage and engineering work in the region afterwards.

Recognition

Kruse was appointed a Member of the Order of the British Empire (MBE) in 1955. The Back of Beyond, which followed his mail run, won the Golden Lion at Venice and remains a landmark of Australian documentary cinema. In later life he restored his original Badger truck, "Back of Beyond," now displayed at the National Motor Museum in Birdwood. He was inducted into the Australian Stockman's Hall of Fame, and his story was retold in the 2010 documentary The Mailmen Back of Beyond and Kristin Weidenbach's biography Mailman of the Birdsville Track.

Death

Tom Kruse died in Adelaide on 30 June 2011, aged 96. He is commemorated by a memorial at Marree and by the continuing mythology of the outback mailman.
