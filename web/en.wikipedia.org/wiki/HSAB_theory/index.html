HSAB theory

HSAB theory (hard–soft acid–base theory) is a qualitative concept in chemistry used to explain the stability of compounds and reaction mechanisms. Proposed by Ralph Pearson in the early 1960s, it states that hard acids prefer to bind to hard bases, and soft acids prefer soft bases.

Concepts

Hard species are small, highly charged and weakly polarisable; soft species are large, low-charged and strongly polarisable. The principle rationalises trends such as the affinity of soft metal cations (e.g. Hg²⁺, Pt²⁺) for soft donors like sulfur, and hard cations (e.g. Al³⁺) for hard donors like oxygen and fluorine.
