Critical point (mathematics)

The red points are stationary points of the function — critical points where the derivative vanishes.

In mathematics, a critical point of a function of a real variable is any value in its domain where its derivative is 0 or undefined. For a function of several real variables, a critical point is a value in its domain where the gradient is zero or undefined; more generally, for a differentiable map between manifolds, a critical point is a point where the differential (Jacobian) is not of full rank — its image is a critical value.

Functions of one variable

By Fermat's theorem, local maxima and minima of a differentiable function can occur only at critical points, though a critical point need not be an extremum: at a horizontal inflection point such as that of f(x) = x³ at 0, the derivative vanishes but the point is neither a maximum nor a minimum. The nature of a critical point is investigated with the first derivative test and the second derivative test.

Several variables and applications

For functions of several variables, critical points are classified by the Hessian matrix as local maxima, local minima, or saddle points (where the function rises in some directions and falls in others). Critical points are central to optimisation, where they are the candidates for extrema of objective functions; to Morse theory and catastrophe theory, which study how the topology of level sets changes at critical points; and in physics, where equilibrium configurations of dynamical systems correspond to critical points of a potential function.
