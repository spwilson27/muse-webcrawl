Right of abode in the United Kingdom

The right of abode is a statutory immigration status in the United Kingdom that gives a person the unconditional right to live, work and settle in the United Kingdom. A person with the right of abode is free from immigration control: they do not need permission to enter or remain, and there is no limit on the length of time they may spend in the country.

Legal basis

The status was created by the Immigration Act 1971, section 1 of which provides that those with the right of abode are free to live in, and to come and go into and from, the United Kingdom without let or hindrance. Section 2 of the Act, as amended by the British Nationality Act 1981 and the Nationality, Immigration and Asylum Act 2002, defines who qualifies.

Who has the right of abode

All British citizens have the right of abode. In addition, certain Commonwealth citizens who held the right before 1 January 1983 retained it: broadly, a Commonwealth citizen who was a citizen of the United Kingdom and Colonies with a UK-born parent or grandparent, or who was settled in the UK on that date. Since 1983 it has not generally been possible to acquire the right of abode without also becoming a British citizen.

Evidence

The right of abode is normally evidenced by a British passport describing the holder as a British citizen. Non-British Commonwealth citizens who qualify may apply for a certificate of entitlement, which is placed in their passport. A person with the right of abode who holds it through British citizenship can only lose it by renouncing that citizenship.
