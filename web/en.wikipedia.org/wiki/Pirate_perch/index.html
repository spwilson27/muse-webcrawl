Pirate perch

The pirate perch (Aphredoderus sayanus) is a small freshwater fish of North America, the only living member of the family Aphredoderidae. Despite its name it is not a true perch; it belongs to the order Percopsiformes, with the trout-perches and cavefishes.

Description and behaviour

Pirate perch reach about 14 cm (5.5 in) in length and are dark olive to nearly black, mottled, with a single dorsal fin. They inhabit slow-moving, heavily vegetated waters — swamps, oxbow lakes, sloughs, and the backwaters of the Mississippi basin and Atlantic and Gulf coastal drainages. They are nocturnal predators of insect larvae, crustaceans, and small fish. The common name is said to come from an early naturalist's observation that a specimen ate only other fish in an aquarium.

Unusual anatomy

As a juvenile, the pirate perch's anus and urogenital opening are in the normal position near the anal fin, but as the fish matures they migrate forward under the head, ending between the gill covers and pelvic fins — an arrangement unique among fishes. This forward migration is thought to let females deposit eggs directly into their gill chambers, where the eggs incubate before being released.
