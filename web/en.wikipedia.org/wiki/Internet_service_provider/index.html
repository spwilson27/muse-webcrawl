Internet service provider

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Photograph of an ISP's network operations centre with rows of server racks]

An Internet service provider (ISP) is an organisation that provides services for accessing, using, managing or participating in the Internet. ISPs can be organised in various forms, such as commercial, community-owned, non-profit, or otherwise privately owned.

Historically, Internet access began with university and research networks, then reached the public in the early 1990s through dial-up providers — companies such as CompuServe, AOL and thousands of local firms sold timed or flat-rate access over telephone lines with a modem. As broadband arrived, access consolidated around telephone companies (DSL, then fibre) and cable companies, with mobile operators joining as 3G and 4G spread. ISPs interconnect with each other at Internet exchange points and through transit agreements, forming the tiers of the backbone.

Beyond connectivity, ISPs typically provide email accounts, web hosting, domain name registration and customer routers. Their role in carrying all traffic has made them central to policy debates, including net neutrality — whether providers may favour or throttle particular services — and data-retention and lawful-access laws.
