Eric of Pomerania (1381 or 1382 – 3 May 1459) was the first king of the Kalmar Union, reigning over Denmark, Norway and Sweden. Born Bogislaw of Pomerania, he was the grandnephew and adopted heir of Queen Margaret I, and was crowned king of the three Scandinavian kingdoms at Kalmar in 1397. His reign was marked by conflict with the Hanseatic League, rebellions in Sweden, and increasing discontent with his appointments of foreign officials.

Reign

Although Eric was crowned in 1397, Margaret I remained the effective ruler until her death in 1412. Eric then pursued an assertive policy: he introduced the Sound Dues on shipping through the Øresund, built strongholds such as Malmöhus, waged a long and costly war over Schleswig, and fought the Hanseatic League in the 1420s. His marriage to Philippa of England in 1406 produced no surviving children.

Deposition and later life

The Swedish Engelbrekt rebellion of 1434 and the burden of war taxation eroded Eric's position. He withdrew to Gotland, was deposed in Denmark and Sweden in 1439 and in Norway in 1442, and from Visborg castle pursued piracy in the Baltic for a decade. In 1449 he returned to his native Pomerania, where he ruled as duke in Rügenwalde (Darłowo) until his death in 1459.
