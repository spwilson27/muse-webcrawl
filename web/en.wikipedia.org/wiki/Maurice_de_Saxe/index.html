Maurice de Saxe

Maurice de Saxe, Count of Saxony (German: Hermann Moritz von Sachsen; 28 October 1696 – 20 November 1750) was a German soldier who became a Marshal of France and one of the most celebrated commanders of the eighteenth century. An illegitimate son of Augustus the Strong, King of Poland and Elector of Saxony, he served in several armies before entering French service, where his victories in the War of the Austrian Succession made him famous across Europe.

Early career

Maurice saw his first campaign at the age of twelve, serving under Prince Eugene of Savoy at the Siege of Tournai and the Battle of Malplaquet in 1709. He later campaigned in Pomerania, served Peter the Great of Russia, and was briefly elected Duke of Courland in 1726 before Russian pressure forced his withdrawal. In 1734 he became a French lieutenant-general and fought in the War of the Polish Succession.

Marshal of France

Promoted marshal in 1744, Maurice commanded the French army in Flanders during the War of the Austrian Succession. His greatest triumph was the Battle of Fontenoy (11 May 1745), where — gravely ill and directing the battle from a litter — he defeated the Pragmatic Army under the Duke of Cumberland; Louis XV was present on the field. He followed with victories at Rocoux (1746) and Lauffeld (1747) and captured Maastricht in 1748, effectively conquering the Austrian Netherlands for France.

Later life and writings

Granted the Château de Chambord by the king, Maurice spent his final years there, writing his military treatise Mes Rêveries ("My Reveries"), published posthumously in 1757. It anticipated later theories of war in its emphasis on manoeuvre, morale, conscription, and the formation of legions, and is considered one of the earliest modern works of military theory. He died at Chambord in 1750.
