Country: France
Region: Occitanie
Department: Lot (prefecture)
Population: approx. 20,000

Cahors is a commune in southern France, the prefecture of the Lot department in the Occitanie region. It lies in a meander of the Lot river in the historic province of Quercy.

History

The site was the Roman town of Divona Cadurcorum, capital of the Cadurci tribe, noted for its linen. In the Middle Ages Cahors prospered on trade and banking — the "Cahorsins" became famous (and infamous) as moneylenders across Europe. Pope John XXII, born Jacques Duèze in Cahors, founded its university in 1332.

Sights

The city's emblem is the Pont Valentré, a fortified bridge with three towers built in the fourteenth century, now a UNESCO World Heritage site as part of the pilgrimage routes to Santiago de Compostela. The Romanesque cathedral of Saint-Étienne has one of the largest medieval domes in France.

Wine

Cahors is the centre of the Cahors wine appellation, known for dark, tannic red wines made principally from the Malbec grape (locally called Auxerrois or Côt), historically known in England as "black wine".
