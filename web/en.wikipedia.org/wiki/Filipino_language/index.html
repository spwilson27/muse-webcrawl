Filipino (English: /ˌfɪlɪˈpiːnoʊ/; Wikang Filipino) is the national language of the Philippines and, together with English, one of its two official languages. It is the standardised variety of the Tagalog language, an Austronesian regional language spoken widely in the central parts of the country.

History

In 1937 the Institute of National Language chose Tagalog as the basis of the national language, and in 1939 Manuel L. Quezon proclaimed the "National Language of the Philippines", later called Pilipino. The 1973 and 1987 constitutions renamed and redefined it as Filipino, envisioned as a language to be further developed from all the languages of the Philippines. In practice, Filipino and Tagalog are largely the same language, with Filipino distinguished by a more permissive alphabet (28 letters, including F, J, V and Z) and acceptance of loanwords.

Features

Filipino has a verb-initial basic word order (often verb–subject–object), a system of verbal focus (trigger) marking, and extensive affixation and reduplication. Its vocabulary is largely Tagalog with heavy borrowing from Spanish, English, Malay, Chinese (Hokkien), Sanskrit, Arabic and Nahuatl. It is written in the Latin alphabet; before Spanish colonisation Tagalog was written in the Baybayin script.

Usage

Filipino is the language of national media, primary education and government in much of the country, and is understood across the archipelago, where more than 170 other languages are spoken.
