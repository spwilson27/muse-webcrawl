Allah Rakha Rahman (born A. S. Dileep Kumar; 6 January 1967) is an Indian music composer, record producer, singer and songwriter, popular for his works in Indian cinema, predominantly in Tamil and Hindi films, with occasional forays in international cinema. He is a winner of two Academy Awards, two Grammy Awards, a BAFTA Award and a Golden Globe, in addition to six National Film Awards and numerous Filmfare Awards in India. The Government of India honoured him with the Padma Bhushan in 2010.

Early life and breakthrough

Rahman was born in Madras (now Chennai) into a musically inclined family; his father, R. K. Shekhar, was a film-score composer and conductor for Tamil and Malayalam films. After his father's early death, Rahman worked as a keyboard player and arranger and composed advertising jingles. He converted to Islam, his mother's family faith, and changed his name in his early twenties. His breakthrough came with Mani Ratnam's Roja (1992), whose soundtrack — blending Indian melody with contemporary electronic production — transformed the sound of Indian film music and won him the National Film Award for Best Music Direction.

Career

Through the 1990s and 2000s Rahman composed landmark scores for films including Bombay, Dil Se.., Taal, Lagaan, Rang De Basanti, Guru and Jodhaa Akbar. He also composed for the stage musical Bombay Dreams (2002) with Andrew Lloyd Webber. International fame peaked with Danny Boyle's Slumdog Millionaire (2008), for which Rahman won two Academy Awards — Best Original Score and Best Original Song for "Jai Ho" — and two Grammy Awards. His subsequent work ranges from 127 Hours and Rockstar to Ponniyin Selvan. Rahman founded the KM Music Conservatory in Chennai and his own studio, Panchathan Record Inn, and is noted for fusing Indian classical music with electronic music, world music and orchestral arrangements. Time magazine has dubbed him the "Mozart of Madras".
