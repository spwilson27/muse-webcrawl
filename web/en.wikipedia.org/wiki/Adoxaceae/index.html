Adoxaceae

Adoxaceae, commonly known as the moschatel family, is a small family of flowering plants in the order Dipsacales. The family as now circumscribed in the APG classification includes the elderberries (Sambucus) and viburnums (Viburnum), which were formerly placed in the honeysuckle family Caprifoliaceae.

Genera

The family contains five genera and about 150–200 species: Adoxa, Oreinotinus, Sambucus, Sinadoxa, and Viburnum. The type genus Adoxa contains the single well-known species Adoxa moschatellina, the moschatel or town-hall clock, a small herbaceous plant of northern temperate woodlands.

Characteristics

Members are characterized by opposite leaves, small five-petalled flowers arranged in corymbs or umbels, and fruit that is a drupe or berry-like. The flowers often have a somewhat musky odor, reflected in the name moschatel. Elders and viburnums are widely grown as ornamentals and have long histories of use in traditional medicine and, in the case of elderberry, food and drink when properly prepared.
