Louis-Alexandre Berthier

Louis-Alexandre Berthier

Born: 20 November 1753, Versailles

Died: 1 June 1815, Bamberg

Rank: Marshal of the Empire

Role: Napoleon's chief of staff, 1796–1814

Louis-Alexandre Berthier (20 November 1753 – 1 June 1815), Prince of Neuchâtel and Valengin, Prince of Wagram, was a French military commander who served during the French Revolutionary Wars and the Napoleonic Wars. He was Napoleon Bonaparte's chief of staff from the Italian campaign of 1796 until his abdication in 1814, and is widely regarded as the model of an efficient staff officer.

Early career

The son of an officer of engineers, Berthier served in the American Revolutionary War on Rochambeau's staff. During the French Revolutionary Wars he rose through staff and command posts in Italy, becoming Bonaparte's chief of staff in 1796, where his extraordinary capacity for work, memory for detail and ability to translate Napoleon's intentions into clear orders proved indispensable.

Chief of staff

Berthier organised the Grande Armée's staff system, handled its marches, logistics and communications, and served as Minister of War from 1799 to 1807. He was made a Marshal of the Empire in 1804 and Prince of Wagram after the battle of 1809. Napoleon called him indispensable; when Berthier was absent in 1815, the French staff work during the Waterloo campaign suffered noticeably.

1814–1815 and death

After Napoleon's abdication in 1814, Berthier rallied to Louis XVIII and retired to his estate at Bamberg. During the Hundred Days he did not join Napoleon. On 1 June 1815 he died after falling from a window of his castle at Bamberg — whether by accident, suicide or assassination remains disputed.
