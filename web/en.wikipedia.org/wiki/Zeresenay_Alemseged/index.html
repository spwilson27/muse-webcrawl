Zeresenay Alemseged

Zeresenay Alemseged (born 4 June 1969) is an Ethiopian paleoanthropologist known for discovering "Selam," the exceptionally complete fossil skeleton of a 3.3-million-year-old Australopithecus afarensis child, sometimes called "Lucy's baby."

Zeresenay Alemseged
Born: 4 June 1969, Axum, Ethiopia
Field: Paleoanthropology
Institution: University of Chicago
Known for: Discovery of Selam (DIK-1-1)

Career

Alemseged studied geology at Addis Ababa University and earned his PhD in paleoanthropology in France. He led the Dikika Research Project in Ethiopia's Afar region. In 2000 his team found the Selam fossil (announced in 2006 in Nature), which preserved rare bones including the hyoid and shoulder blades, informing debates about ape-like versus human-like traits in early hominins.

Positions

He was a curator at the California Academy of Sciences before becoming professor of organismal biology and anatomy at the University of Chicago.
