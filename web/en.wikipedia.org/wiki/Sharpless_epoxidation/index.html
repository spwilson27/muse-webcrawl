Sharpless epoxidation

The Sharpless epoxidation reaction is an enantioselective chemical reaction for preparing 2,3-epoxy alcohols from primary and secondary allylic alcohols. It was developed by K. Barry Sharpless and co-workers, reported in 1980, and the oxidant is tert-butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP). The reaction is catalysed by titanium tetra(isopropoxide) together with a chiral tartrate ester, typically diethyl tartrate (DET).

Stereochemistry

The great practical value of the reaction is its predictability: choosing (+)- or (−)-tartrate determines which face of the alkene receives the oxygen atom, according to a simple mnemonic in which the allylic alcohol is drawn with its hydroxyl group at the lower right. Enantiomeric excesses routinely exceed 90%. The reaction also performs kinetic resolution of secondary allylic alcohols, since one enantiomer reacts much faster than the other.

Significance

Sharpless epoxidation was one of the first broadly useful methods of asymmetric catalysis and found extensive use in the synthesis of sugars, pharmaceuticals and natural products; the epoxy alcohol products can be opened regioselectively to give a variety of chiral building blocks. For his work on chirally catalysed oxidation reactions, Sharpless shared the 2001 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, receiving a second share of a Nobel Prize in 2022 for click chemistry.
