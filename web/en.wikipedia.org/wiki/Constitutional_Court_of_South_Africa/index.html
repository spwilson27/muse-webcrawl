Constitutional Court of South Africa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Constitutional Court of South Africa is a supreme court of South Africa, established by the Interim Constitution of 1993 and continued under the final Constitution of 1996. Since the Superior Courts Act took effect in 2013 it has been the country's highest court on all matters, not only constitutional ones. The Court sits in Johannesburg on Constitution Hill, in a purpose-built seat opened in 2004 on the site of the Old Fort prison complex where both Mahatma Gandhi and Nelson Mandela were once held.

Composition and jurisdiction

The Court consists of the Chief Justice of South Africa, the Deputy Chief Justice, and nine other judges, appointed by the President after consultation with the Judicial Service Commission. Cases are generally heard by at least eight judges. The Court has jurisdiction over disputes between organs of state, the constitutionality of legislation and executive conduct, and appeals raising constitutional matters or arguable points of law of general public importance. Its decisions are final.

Notable judgments

The Court's first judgment, S v Makwanyane (1995), abolished the death penalty. Other landmark decisions include Government v Grootboom (2000) on the right of access to housing, Minister of Health v Treatment Action Campaign (2002) ordering the provision of antiretroviral drugs to prevent mother-to-child transmission of HIV, Port Elizabeth Municipality v Various Occupiers on eviction law, and Economic Freedom Fighters v Speaker of the National Assembly (2016) concerning the Nkandla scandal. First Chief Justice Arthur Chaskalson was succeeded by Pius Langa, Dikgang Moseneke (acting), Mogoeng Mogoeng, and in 2022 Raymond Zondo; Mandisa Maya became Chief Justice in 2024, the first woman to hold the office.
