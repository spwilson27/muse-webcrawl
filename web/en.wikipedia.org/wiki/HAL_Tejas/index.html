The HAL Tejas (Sanskrit for "radiance") is an Indian single-engine, delta-wing, multirole light fighter aircraft designed by the Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA) in collaboration with the Aircraft Research and Design Centre of Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL) for the Indian Air Force and Indian Navy. It was developed from the Light Combat Aircraft (LCA) programme, begun in the 1980s to replace India's ageing MiG-21 fighters. The Tejas is the smallest and lightest in its class of contemporary supersonic combat aircraft.

Development

The LCA programme was sanctioned in 1983, and the ADA was established in 1984 to manage it. The aircraft's tailless compound delta-wing configuration, relaxed static stability, and quadruplex fly-by-wire flight control system were designed with extensive foreign consultancy but largely Indian engineering. The first technology demonstrator flew on 4 January 2001. After a prolonged flight-test campaign the Tejas received initial operational clearance in 2013 and was inducted into the Indian Air Force on 1 July 2016 with No. 45 Squadron ("Flying Daggers"), based at Sulur. Final operational clearance for the Mk 1 followed in 2019.

The improved Mk 1A variant adds an AESA radar, electronic warfare suite, beyond-visual-range missile capability and reduced maintenance requirements; the Indian Air Force ordered 83 Mk 1A aircraft in 2021, with further orders following. A larger twin-engine derivative line, including the Tejas Mk 2 medium-weight fighter, is in development.

Design

The Tejas is built largely of composite materials, which make up much of its airframe surface and primary structure by weight. It is powered by the General Electric F404-GE-IN20 afterburning turbofan (the Mk 1/Mk 1A), giving a maximum speed of about Mach 1.6. Armament is carried on eight hardpoints and includes an internal 23 mm GSh-23 cannon, air-to-air missiles such as the Astra and Derby, and precision-guided and conventional bombs. A naval variant has flown from Indian aircraft carriers during trials.

Operational history

The Tejas first deployed abroad for exercises including Exercise Gagan Shakti and participated in international air shows such as Bahrain and Singapore. In 2023–2024 it took part in multinational exercises including Desert Flag in the UAE and Tarang Shakti in India. The type has been marketed for export, and has been evaluated by several air forces in South America, Southeast Asia and Africa.

Variants

  • Tejas Mk 1 – initial production version.
  • Tejas Mk 1A – upgraded avionics, AESA radar, improved maintainability.
  • Tejas Trainer – two-seat conversion/operational trainer.
  • Tejas Navy (NP) – carrier-capable prototypes.
  • Tejas Mk 2 – larger canard-delta medium-weight fighter in development, powered by the GE F414.

Specifications (Tejas Mk 1)

  • Crew: 1 or 2
  • Length: 13.2 m; wingspan: 8.2 m; height: 4.4 m
  • Maximum takeoff weight: 13,500 kg
  • Maximum speed: Mach 1.6
  • Combat range: ~500 km; ferry range ~1,700 km (without refuelling, varies with fit)
  • Service ceiling: ~15,240 m (50,000 ft)
