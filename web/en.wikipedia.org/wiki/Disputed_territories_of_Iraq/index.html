Disputed territories of Iraq

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The disputed territories of Iraq are regions claimed both by the federal government of Iraq and by the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG), principally along the boundary between Arab-majority Iraq and the Kurdistan Region in the north. The dispute is one of the central unresolved issues of post-2003 Iraqi politics. The most significant of the territories is the oil-rich region of Kirkuk; others include parts of Nineveh (such as Sinjar and the Nineveh Plains), Makhmur, Khanaqin in Diyala, and Tuz Khurmatu in Saladin Governorate.

Background

Under Saddam Hussein, the Ba'athist government pursued "Arabisation" campaigns in the north: Kurds and other minorities (Turkmens, Assyrians, Yazidis) were expelled from areas such as Kirkuk and replaced with Arab settlers, and provincial boundaries were redrawn. After the 2003 invasion of Iraq, many displaced Kurds returned, and Kurdish Peshmerga forces established a presence in much of the disputed zone.

Article 140

Article 140 of the 2005 Iraqi constitution set out a process to resolve the territories' status: "normalisation" (reversing Arabisation), a census, and then a referendum in Kirkuk and other disputed areas, to be completed by the end of 2007. The deadline passed without the referendum being held, and the article's implementation has remained stalled amid political disagreement.

Developments since 2014

In 2014, as the Iraqi army collapsed before the Islamic State offensive, Peshmerga forces took full control of Kirkuk and other disputed areas, which the KRG then administered. After the September 2017 Kurdish independence referendum, Iraqi federal forces and allied militias retook Kirkuk and most of the disputed territories in October 2017. The areas remain under federal control but contested, with periodic tensions and disputes over security arrangements, land and property claims, and administration.
