The Sun is a British tabloid newspaper owned by News UK (a News Corp subsidiary). Launched in 1964 as a broadsheet successor to the Daily Herald and relaunched as a tabloid after Rupert Murdoch's 1969 purchase, it became Britain's best-selling newspaper for decades, known for its populist voice, sports coverage, showbiz news and the former Page 3 feature.

Origins

The Sun rose from the Daily Herald, the TUC-backed paper, relaunched in 1964 with ambitions for a modern popular paper; it struggled until Murdoch bought it in 1969.

The Murdoch formula

Relaunched as a compact tabloid with bold typography, television promotion and showbiz coverage, the Sun overtook the Daily Mirror in the late 1970s and dominated the market for decades.

Politics and controversies

The paper's endorsements are courted by politicians ('It's The Sun Wot Won It', its famous 1992 headline). Its Hillsborough coverage in 1989 caused a lasting boycott in Liverpool; it supported Brexit in the 2016 referendum. Page 3 topless photography ran from 1970 until it ended in print in 2015.

Digital era

A paywall (Sun+) launched in 2013 was dropped in 2015; the paper is now free online and among the UK's most-read news sites, though no longer the print market leader it was. Sister papers include The Sun on Sunday, launched in 2012 after the News of the World's closure.
