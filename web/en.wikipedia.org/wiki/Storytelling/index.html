Storytelling is the social and cultural activity of sharing stories, sometimes with improvisation, theatrics, or embellishment. Every culture has its own stories and narratives, which are shared as entertainment, education, cultural preservation, and the instilling of moral values. Crucial elements of stories and storytelling include plot, characters, and narrative point of view.

Oral storytelling

Oral storytelling predates writing by tens of thousands of years. Cave paintings, such as those at Lascaux, may have served as prompts for performed narratives. Professional storytellers — bards, minstrels, griots in West Africa, and ashik in the Turkic world — preserved epics such as the Odyssey, the Epic of Gilgamesh, and the Sundiata across generations by memory and formulaic verse.

Story structure

Narratologists have identified recurring structures in stories: Vladimir Propp analysed the functions of folktale characters, Joseph Campbell proposed the "hero's journey" monomyth, and Aristotle's Poetics described plot as a unified whole with beginning, middle, and end. Modern screenwriting codifies structures such as the three-act structure.

Modern forms

Storytelling now spans novels, film, theatre, podcasting, video games, and social media, and has been adopted by fields as diverse as marketing, law, and medicine ("narrative medicine"), where personal stories shape persuasion and care.
