Liana

A liana is a long-stemmed, woody vine that is rooted in the soil at ground level and uses trees, as well as other means of vertical support, to climb up to the canopy in search of direct sunlight. Lianas are characteristic of tropical moist broadleaf forests (especially seasonal forests), but may be found in temperate rainforests and temperate deciduous forests. The word liana does not refer to a taxonomic grouping, but rather a habit of growth — like the words "tree" and "shrub" — and lianas may be found in many different plant families.

Ecology

Lianas are characteristic of tropical forests, where they may make up a large fraction of the woody plant species present. Competition with lianas can greatly reduce tree growth and tree reproduction, substantially reduce tree seedling establishment, and increase tree mortality. Lianas also provide many animals with access to the canopy — and, by linking trees together, they can stabilise the canopy against wind damage. However, lianas can also be destructive: when trees covered in lianas fall, several other trees may be pulled down with them.

Lianas are found in every tropical forest, and tend to be most abundant in seasonal tropical forests and secondary forests undergoing succession. Some lianas grow as long as 60 metres (200 feet) or more, and may span distances of several hundred metres when supported by multiple trees.

Structure

Because lianas rely on other plants for support, they have evolved structural adaptations to grow quickly toward light while investing relatively little in their own support tissues. Their stems are often unusually shaped — flattened, ribbon-like, or lobed — and their wood anatomy frequently features wide vessels that conduct water efficiently, together with flexible tissues that allow the stem to twist and bend without breaking as host trees sway.

Uses

Some well-known lianas include monkey ladder (Bauhinia glabra), "water vine", rattan palms (such as Calamus species, whose stems are harvested for furniture and crafts), poison ivy, bittersweet, and grape vines. Several lianas produce edible fruits or useful fibres, while others are sources of poisons, including curare, derived from species of Strychnos and related genera.

Evolution

The climbing habit has evolved many times independently across the flowering plants, and lianas are thought to have played an important role in the evolution of tropical forest diversity. Fossil evidence suggests that liana diversity has fluctuated with climate, increasing during warm, wet periods in Earth's history.
