Oirat language

Oirat (Clear Script: Todo Mongγol bičiq) is a Mongolic language spoken by the Oirat peoples, principally in Mongolia, the Xinjiang and Qinghai regions of China, and the Republic of Kalmykia in Russia, where its Kalmyk variety is an official language. It is generally considered a distinct language from Khalkha Mongolian, though some classifications treat it as a dialect group of Mongolian.

Distribution and dialects

Oirat dialects include Torgut, Dorbet, and Olot (Eleuth), spoken in western Mongolia and Xinjiang; Sart Kalmyk in Kyrgyzstan; and Kalmyk in the Russian Federation, spoken by descendants of Oirats who migrated to the Volga steppe in the seventeenth century. Speaker numbers are uncertain but total a few hundred thousand; most varieties are endangered as speakers shift to Khalkha Mongolian, Chinese, or Russian.

Writing systems

In 1648 the Oirat Buddhist monk Zaya Pandita devised the todo bičiq ("Clear Script"), adapting the classical Mongolian script to represent Oirat sounds more precisely — for example by distinguishing vowel lengths and certain consonants. Clear Script was used for centuries in Oirat literary and religious texts. Today, Kalmyk is written in Cyrillic, while Oirats in China use the Mongolian script, and those in Mongolia typically use Cyrillic like the Khalkha majority.

Linguistic features

Oirat shares the agglutinative structure, vowel harmony, and subject–object–verb word order of other Mongolic languages, but is distinguished by features such as the preservation and systematic marking of long vowels and particular consonant developments that set it apart from central Mongolian dialects.
