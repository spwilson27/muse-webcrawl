Theora is a free lossy video compression format developed by the Xiph.Org Foundation. Derived from On2 Technologies' VP3 codec, which On2 donated to the public in 2001, Theora was designed as a patent-unencumbered alternative to proprietary formats such as MPEG-4 and Windows Media Video, and is distributed without licensing fees.

History

The Theora project began in 2002, building on the VP3.2 codebase released by On2 under an open-source licence. After a long series of alpha and beta releases, the bitstream format was frozen in 2004 and version 1.0 of the reference implementation, libtheora, was released in November 2008. An improved encoder, codenamed "Thusnelda", followed in version 1.1 (2009), substantially improving quality at a given bitrate.

Technical details

Theora uses block-based motion compensation and an 8×8 discrete cosine transform, broadly comparable in efficiency to early MPEG-4 Part 2 codecs. It is most commonly stored in the Ogg container alongside Vorbis audio. Theora was one of the two codecs originally considered for the HTML5 <video> element and was supported natively by Firefox, Chrome and Opera; Wikipedia and the Internet Archive long used it for web video. Interest later shifted to successors such as VP8/WebM and AV1, whose development involved many Theora contributors.
