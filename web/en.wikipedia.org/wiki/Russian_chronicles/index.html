Russian chronicles

The Russian chronicles are a body of historical annals written in Rus' and later Russia from the eleventh to the eighteenth centuries. Compiled mainly in monasteries and princely courts, they are the principal written sources for the history of Kievan Rus' and the early Russian principalities.

Principal chronicles

The most famous is the Primary Chronicle (Tale of Bygone Years), compiled in Kiev in the early twelfth century and traditionally attributed to the monk Nestor; it survives in later copies such as the Laurentian Codex (1377) and the Hypatian Codex. Other major texts include the Novgorod First Chronicle, the Galician–Volhynian Chronicle — notable for its literary, non-annalistic narrative — and the later Muscovite compilations such as the Nikon Chronicle and the lavishly illustrated Illustrated Chronicle of Ivan the Terrible.

Characteristics

Chronicles typically record events year by year ("In the year 6496…"), mixing political and military events with weather, famines, eclipses, and church affairs, all interpreted within a providential Christian framework. Compilers freely edited, expanded, and combined earlier texts (svody), so modern scholarship reconstructs their relationships through textual criticism.
