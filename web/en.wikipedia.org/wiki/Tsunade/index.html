Tsunade

Tsunade
First appearance:The Tale of the Gallant Jiraiya (19th century)
Tradition:Japanese folklore, kabuki, ningyō jōruri
Associated with:Jiraiya, Orochimaru; slug magic

Tsunade (綱手) is a figure of Japanese folklore, a female ninja (kunoichi) skilled in slug magic who appears in the 19th-century tale cycle Jiraiya Gōketsu Monogatari (The Tale of the Gallant Jiraiya), serialized from 1839 to 1868 and adapted for kabuki and puppet theatre. In the tales she is the wife — in some versions childhood companion — of the hero Jiraiya, master of toad magic, and uses her command of giant slugs to aid him against the serpent sorcerer Orochimaru.

Folklore background

The trio of Tsunade, Jiraiya and Orochimaru embodies a rock-paper-scissors-like cycle drawn from the Chinese "three confronting animals" motif: the slug dissolves the serpent, the serpent swallows the frog or toad, and the toad eats the slug — so that each of the three magicians can defeat one rival but not the other. Tsunade's own name is associated with the sacred shimenawa rope of Shinto shrines, reflecting a folk etymology linking her to consecrated cords.

In popular culture

The folkloric Tsunade is the namesake and inspiration for Tsunade in Masashi Kishimoto's manga and anime series Naruto, where she is a legendary medical ninja (one of the Sannin alongside versions of Jiraiya and Orochimaru), a summoner of the giant slug Katsuyu, and the Fifth Hokage of Konohagakure. Kishimoto's character preserves the slug association and the love of gambling found in some folk versions while adding her superhuman strength, healing techniques, and fear of blood overcome in the story.
