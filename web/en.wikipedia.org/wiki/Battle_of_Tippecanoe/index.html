Battle of Tippecanoe
Date: 7 November 1811
Location: Near present-day Battle Ground, Indiana
Part of: Tecumseh's War

The Battle of Tippecanoe was fought on 7 November 1811 in Indiana Territory between United States forces led by Governor William Henry Harrison and Native American warriors associated with the Shawnee leader Tecumseh. Tecumseh's brother Tenskwatawa, the Prophet, led the attack on Harrison's camp near Prophetstown.

Background

Tecumseh built a confederacy to resist U.S. expansion and land cessions such as the 1809 Treaty of Fort Wayne. While Tecumseh travelled south recruiting allies, Harrison marched on Prophetstown.

The battle

Before dawn, warriors attacked Harrison's encampment near Burnett's Creek. After fierce fighting the attackers withdrew; Harrison's men then burned Prophetstown.

Aftermath

Harrison's reputation as "Old Tippecanoe" later propelled him to the presidency in 1840 with the slogan "Tippecanoe and Tyler Too". The confederacy recovered, and conflict merged into the War of 1812.
