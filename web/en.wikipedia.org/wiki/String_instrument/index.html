String instrument

String instruments, or chordophones, are musical instruments that produce sound from vibrating strings stretched between two points when the strings are plucked, bowed, struck or otherwise excited. In the Hornbostel–Sachs classification they form the chordophone family, subdivided into lutes, harps, lyres and zithers according to the arrangement of strings relative to the soundbox.

Techniques

Strings may be sounded by bowing (violin family, cello, double bass), plucking with fingers or a plectrum (guitar, harp, lute, banjo, mandolin), or striking (piano, hammered dulcimer, clavichord — in which the strings are hit by tangents or hammers). The vibrating length, tension and mass of the string determine its pitch, according to Mersenne's laws.

History

Stringed instruments are among the oldest known: lyres and harps are depicted in ancient Mesopotamian and Egyptian art, the lute family spread through Asia and Europe, and the violin family was perfected in 16th-century Italy. Modern strings are made of gut, steel, nylon or wound metal over a fibre core.
