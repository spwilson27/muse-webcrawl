iButton

ManufacturerDallas Semiconductor (later Maxim Integrated; now Analog Devices)
Type1-Wire device in a stainless-steel can
Package16 mm diameter "MicroCan", F3 or F5 size

An iButton is a computer chip housed in a small, rugged stainless-steel enclosure, 16 mm in diameter and resembling a watch battery or button, developed by Dallas Semiconductor. iButtons communicate over the 1-Wire protocol: a momentary contact with a reader provides both power and a data connection through the can's lid (data) and base (ground). Each device carries a globally unique 64-bit serial number laser-etched at manufacture.

Types and applications

iButtons are produced in many variants, including:

  • Memory iButtons — NVRAM or EEPROM storage for data such as ID records or settings.
  • Temperature loggers (Thermochron) — record temperature over time; widely used in cold-chain monitoring of food, pharmaceuticals, and vaccines.
  • Humidity loggers (Hygrochron) — combine temperature and humidity sensing.
  • Secure iButtons — with cryptographic functions such as SHA-1 challenge-response, used for authentication and electronic cash.
  • Address-only devices — used as electronic keys and asset tags.

Uses

Because the steel can resists moisture, dirt, impact, and corrosion, iButtons have been used for access control, guard-tour verification, time-and-attendance, transit and parking payment — notably in Istanbul's Akbil system and Moscow transport — vending, data logging in harsh environments, and as personal identification tokens. Accessories such as key fobs, mounts, and probe readers allow the devices to be carried and interrogated conveniently.
