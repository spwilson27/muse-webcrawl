Lochgilphead
Scottish Gaelic: Ceann Loch Gilb
Population: approx. 2,300
Council area: Argyll and Bute
Country: Scotland

Lochgilphead (Scottish Gaelic: Ceann Loch Gilb) is a town and former burgh in Argyll and Bute, Scotland, with a population of around 2,300. It lies at the head of Loch Gilp, an inlet of Loch Fyne, and is the administrative centre of Argyll and Bute.

Lochgilphead's front green and waterfront.

History

Lochgilphead was planned and built around 1790, shortly after the completion of the road from Inveraray to Campbeltown. It developed as a planned settlement, and its importance grew with the opening of the Crinan Canal in 1801, whose eastern entrance at Ardrishaig lies two miles to the south. The canal links Loch Fyne with the Sound of Jura and was a major route for Clyde shipping avoiding the long passage around the Mull of Kintyre.

Governance and services

The headquarters of Argyll and Bute Council are at Kilmory Castle, a large 19th-century house on the edge of the town. The town is served by Lochgilphead Joint Campus, combining primary and secondary education, and by the Mid Argyll Community Hospital.

Sport

The town is home to the shinty club Kilmory Camanachd and to Lochgilphead Golf Club. Nearby attractions include the prehistoric monuments of Kilmartin Glen to the north, one of the richest archaeological landscapes in Scotland.
