Vigil

A vigil, from the Latin vigilia meaning wakefulness, is a period of purposeful sleeplessness, an occasion for devotional watching, or a solemn gathering, often held at night.

Religious vigils

In Christianity, a vigil is the eve of a major feast, kept with prayer; the Easter Vigil on Holy Saturday is the most solemn liturgy of the Roman Catholic year. An all-night vigil is also an Orthodox service combining vespers and matins. Traditionally mourners kept a wake or vigil over the dead before burial.

Public vigils

Candlelight vigils are held to commemorate victims of tragedy, protest injustice, or pray for the sick. Participants gather, frequently in silence, holding candles — a form of public mourning and solidarity seen worldwide after disasters and acts of violence.

Other uses

Historically a knight's vigil was the night of prayer before knighting. Hospital vigils describe family members keeping watch at a bedside.
