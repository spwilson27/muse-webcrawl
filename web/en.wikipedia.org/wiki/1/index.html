1

1 (one, unit, unity) is a number, numeral and glyph. It is the first and smallest positive integer of the infinite sequence of natural numbers. This fundamental property has led to its unique uses in other fields, ranging from science to sports, where it commonly denotes the first, leading, or top thing in a group.

In mathematics

The number 1 is the multiplicative identity of the integers, real numbers and complex numbers: for any number x, 1 · x = x. By convention, 1 is not considered a prime number; although once regarded as prime by some, the modern definition of primality excludes it so that the fundamental theorem of arithmetic — that every integer greater than 1 factors uniquely into primes — holds with a cleaner statement. 1 is also the first figurate number of many kinds, such as triangular numbers, pentagonal numbers and hexagonal numbers, and it is the value of the empty product.

One is the most common leading digit in many sets of real-world numerical data, a consequence of Benford's law. In the Peano axioms, 1 is the successor of 0. In Principia Mathematica, Whitehead and Russell defined 1 as the set of all singletons.

In technology and science

In computing, 1 together with 0 forms the basis of binary code. The resin identification code used in recycling to identify polyethylene terephthalate is 1. In physics, certain dimensionless physical constants are set to 1 in natural unit systems. The atomic number of hydrogen is 1, making it the lightest and most abundant chemical element in the universe.

In culture

In many cultures, 1 represents unity, beginnings and primacy. It is the number of "first place" in competition. The glyph used today in the Western world to represent the number 1, a vertical line, often with a serif at the top and sometimes a short horizontal line at the bottom, traces its roots back to the Indians, who wrote 1 as a horizontal line.
