Geometric series

A geometric series is a series in which each successive term is obtained by multiplying the previous term by a fixed number, the common ratio. The series a + ar + ar² + ar³ + ⋯ converges when |r| < 1, with sum a/(1 − r).

Example

The series 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ⋯, reached from the article on that specific series, is the geometric series with a = 1/2 and r = 1/2, summing to 1. It appears in connection with Zeno's paradoxes, where infinitely many halved distances must be traversed in finite time.

Applications

Geometric series are used in the analysis of algorithms, compound interest, signal processing and the study of fractals.
