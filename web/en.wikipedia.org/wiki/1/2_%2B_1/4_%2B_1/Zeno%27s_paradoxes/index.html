Zeno's paradoxes

Zeno's paradoxes are a set of philosophical problems devised by the Greek philosopher Zeno of Elea (c. 490–430 BCE) to support Parmenides' doctrine that motion and change are illusions. The best known are the Dichotomy, the Achilles and the Tortoise, the Arrow, and the Stadium.

The Dichotomy and the series 1/2 + 1/4 + ⋯

The Dichotomy argues that to reach a destination one must first traverse half the distance, then half the remainder, and so on—an infinite series 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ⋯. Since the geometric series converges to 1, modern calculus resolves the paradox in the sense that infinitely many sub-tasks can sum to a finite distance and time. Legacy

The paradoxes have provoked discussion for over two millennia and have influenced the development of mathematics, physics and philosophy.
