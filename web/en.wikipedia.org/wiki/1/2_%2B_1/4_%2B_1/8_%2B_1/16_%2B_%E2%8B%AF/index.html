1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/16 + ⋯

In mathematics, the infinite series 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/16 + ⋯ is an elementary example of a geometric series that converges absolutely. The sum of the series is 1. In summation notation,

Σn=1∞ 1/2n = 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/16 + ⋯ = 1.

This series was one of the first infinite series to be summed in the history of mathematics; it was used by Archimedes circa 250–200 BC. As usual for such series, the partial sums converge to 1, with each additional term bringing the total closer by half of the remaining distance.

Proof

As with any series, the infinite sum is defined as the limit of the partial sums. The partial sum of the first n terms can be written in closed form as sn = 1 − 1/2n, which follows from the standard formula for the sum of a finite geometric series, since the sum of the first n terms of a geometric series with first term a and ratio r is a(1 − rn)/(1 − r). With a = 1/2 and r = 1/2 this gives sn = 1 − (1/2)n. As n increases without bound, (1/2)n tends to zero and the partial sums converge to 1.

History

The series is one half of the more famous series-related work of antiquity. Zeno's paradox of Achilles and the tortoise effectively involves this series, since Achilles must first cover half the distance, then half of the remainder, and so on. Archimedes used the series with ratio 1/4 (whose sum is 4/3) in his Quadrature of the Parabola to compute the area enclosed by a parabola and a straight line. The successive fractions 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32 and 1/64 also appear in Egyptian mathematics as the parts of the Eye of Horus in the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus, whose values sum to 63/64.
