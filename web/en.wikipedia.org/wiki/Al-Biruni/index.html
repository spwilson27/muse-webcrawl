Abū Rayḥān Muḥammad ibn Aḥmad al-Bīrūnī (973 – c. 1050) was a Khwarazmian Iranian scholar and polymath of the Islamic Golden Age. Regarded as one of the greatest scholars of the medieval Islamic world, he made fundamental contributions to astronomy, mathematics, geography, comparative religion, and the study of India, mastering Persian, Arabic, Sanskrit, Greek, and several other languages.

Life

Al-Biruni was born at Kath in Khwarazm, on the Aral Sea's southern shore. He spent his early years under the patronage of local rulers and at the court of Jurjan. In 1017, when Mahmud of Ghazna conquered Khwarazm, al-Biruni was taken to Ghazna, and he accompanied Mahmud's military campaigns into India — an experience he turned into scholarship.

Works and contributions

His Kitāb al-Hind ("Book of India", c. 1030) is a systematic, remarkably objective study of Indian religion, science, literature, and customs, based on Sanskrit texts he studied with Indian scholars; it is often called the founding work of comparative religion and of Indology. His Chronology of Ancient Nations (1000) compared the calendars and eras of many cultures.

In geodesy, al-Biruni measured the radius of the Earth by an ingenious method: observing the dip of the horizon from a mountain of known height at Nandana in the Punjab, he obtained a value within about 1 percent of the modern figure. He wrote on trigonometry, the density of metals and gems, pharmacology, astrology (which he treated critically), and corresponded with the young Ibn Sina (Avicenna) on questions of natural philosophy.

Legacy

A lunar crater and an asteroid are named for him, and he is a national figure in Uzbekistan, Iran, and Afghanistan. Scholars praise the empirical and comparative method of his writing, unusual for his age.
