Charge (physics)

In physics, a charge is any of many different quantities, such as the electric charge in electromagnetism or the colour charge in quantum chromodynamics. Charges correspond to the time-invariant generators of a physical symmetry: formally, a charge is a conserved quantum number associated, via Noether's theorem, with a continuous symmetry of the system, and it is the source of the corresponding fundamental interaction.

Different kinds of charge couple matter to the fundamental interactions.

Types of charge

  • Electric charge: the property of matter that causes it to experience the electromagnetic force. It is quantised in units of the elementary charge e (quarks carry fractional charges of ±1/3e and ±2/3e) and is conserved in all known processes; it sources the electromagnetic field.
  • Colour charge: the charge of the strong interaction, carried by quarks and gluons in three "colours" (red, green, blue) and their anticolours. Colour is confined: observable particles are colour-neutral.
  • Weak isospin and weak hypercharge: the charges of the electroweak interaction, combined in the electric charge through the Gell-Mann–Nishijima formula.
  • Flavour charges: quantum numbers such as strangeness, charm, baryon number and lepton number, conserved by some interactions but violated by the weak force.
  • Mass-energy acts as the "charge" of gravitation: it is the source of the gravitational field in general relativity.

Theoretical framework

In quantum field theory, charges are defined by the generators of symmetry groups acting on the fields; gauge symmetries require the existence of force-carrying fields (photons, gluons, W and Z bosons) that couple to the respective charges. Conservation of charge follows from gauge invariance and is among the most precisely tested principles in physics.
