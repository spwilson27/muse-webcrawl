Colne Valley Regional Park

The Colne Valley Regional Park is a 43-square-mile (110 km²) area of parks, green spaces and reservoirs alongside the often multi-channel River Colne and parallel Grand Union Canal, mainly in Hertfordshire and Buckinghamshire, with parts in the London Borough of Hillingdon, Berkshire and Surrey, in England. It is the first large area of countryside to the west of London, stretching from Rickmansworth in the north to Staines and the Thames in the south, and from Uxbridge in the east to Slough in the west.

Landscape

The park contains a network of more than 200 lakes and reservoirs created largely by former gravel workings, many now important for wildlife; over 70 miles of river and canal; and areas of farmland, woodland and wetland, including sites such as Black Park, Denham Country Park and the Rickmansworth Aquadrome. Parts are designated as Sites of Special Scientific Interest and local nature reserves, and the lakes host nationally significant populations of wintering waterbirds.

Management

The park was first proposed in 1965 and is managed through a community interest company working with local authorities, landowners and community groups to protect the landscape, enhance biodiversity and provide recreation close to London. It lies within the Metropolitan Green Belt, which has helped resist development pressures from the adjacent Heathrow area and expanding towns.
