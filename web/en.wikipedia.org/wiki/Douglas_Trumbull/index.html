Douglas Trumbull

Douglas Hunt Trumbull (April 8, 1942 – February 7, 2022) was an American film director and visual effects supervisor. He is known for his pioneering work on 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968), for which he helped create the groundbreaking "Stargate" slit-scan sequence, and as visual effects supervisor on Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977), Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979), and Blade Runner (1982). He directed the films Silent Running (1972) and Brainstorm (1983).

Career

Trumbull began his career at Graphic Films, which produced work for NASA and contributed to Stanley Kubrick's 2001. He went on to found several effects companies and spent decades developing immersive cinema technology, including the Showscan process, which filmed and projected at 60 frames per second, and later the MAGI system for high-frame-rate, high-brightness 3D presentation. He received numerous honours, including an Academy Honorary Award in 2012 for his contributions to visual effects.
