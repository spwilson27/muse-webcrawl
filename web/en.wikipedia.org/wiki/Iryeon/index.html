Iryeon

Iryeon (一然)
Born: 1206
Died: 1289
Nationality: Korean (Goryeo)
Occupation: Buddhist monk, historian
Notable work: Samguk yusa

Iryeon (Korean: 일연; Hanja: 一然; 1206–1289) was a Buddhist monk and the compiler of the Samguk yusa (Memorabilia of the Three Kingdoms), one of the most important sources for early Korean history and mythology.

Life

Iryeon was born with the surname Kim (金) and the given name Gyeon-myeong. At the age of nine he entered a Buddhist monastery, and he became a monk formally in 1219. He passed the monastic examinations in 1227 and rose through the hierarchy of the Goryeo Buddhist establishment, eventually being appointed National Preceptor (Guksa) in 1283 by King Chungnyeol.

During the Mongol invasions of Korea, Iryeon lived and taught at several temples, including Unmun Temple and Ingak Temple in what is now North Gyeongsang Province. In his later years he devoted himself to writing.

Samguk yusa

The Samguk yusa, compiled in the late 13th century, supplements the official history Samguk sagi (1145) by preserving legends, folklore, and Buddhist tales omitted from the earlier, Confucian-oriented work. Among its most famous contents is the myth of Dangun, the legendary founder of Gojoseon, which became central to Korean national identity.

The text is written in Classical Chinese and consists of five volumes covering chronicles of the Three Kingdoms, marvels, Buddhist statues and temples, pagodas, and notable monks.

Legacy

Iryeon is regarded as one of the most important figures in Korean historiography. His work preserved oral traditions and foundation myths that would otherwise have been lost, and it remains a primary source for the study of ancient Korean religion and folklore.
