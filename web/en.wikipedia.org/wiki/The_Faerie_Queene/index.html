The Faerie Queene is an English epic poem by Edmund Spenser, published first in three books in 1590 and later in six books in 1596. An allegorical celebration of the Tudor dynasty and of Elizabeth I — represented by the Faerie Queene, Gloriana — it is one of the longest poems in English literature and a foundational work of English poetry.

Structure

Spenser planned twelve books, each following a knight who exemplifies a virtue, but only six were completed: Holiness (the Redcrosse Knight), Temperance (Guyon), Chastity (Britomart), Friendship (Cambell and Triamond), Justice (Artegall), and Courtesy (Calidore), together with the fragmentary Mutabilitie Cantos. The poem is written in the nine-line Spenserian stanza, a form Spenser invented for it.

Themes and influence

The poem blends medieval romance, classical epic, Protestant allegory, and topical commentary on Elizabethan politics, including England's involvement in Ireland, where Spenser served as an administrator. Its archaic diction and allegorical richness influenced poets from Milton to the Romantics.
