The Solheim Cup is a biennial women's team golf competition between teams representing the United States and Europe, and the premier team event in the women's game — the counterpart of the men's Ryder Cup. Named for Karsten Solheim, the golf-club manufacturer who drove its creation, it was first played in 1990 in Florida. Twelve-player teams, qualifying through the LPGA and Ladies European Tour points lists plus captain's picks, contest foursomes, fourballs and twelve singles over three days in the same match-play format as the Ryder Cup.

History

The United States won most of the early editions, but Europe has become increasingly competitive, winning at Gleneagles in 2019 on Suzann Pettersen's famous final putt and retaining the cup in Spain in 2023 with a 14–14 tie, the first in the event's history. The United States regained the cup in Virginia in 2024. The matches are known for the same partisan crowds and intensity as the Ryder Cup and have become a central showcase for women's professional golf.
