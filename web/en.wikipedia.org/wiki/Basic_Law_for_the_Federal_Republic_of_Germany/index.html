Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany (Grundgesetz) is the constitution of Germany, approved on 8 May 1949 by the Parlamentarischer Rat in Bonn, promulgated on 23 May 1949 and originally intended as a provisional arrangement for the Western zones pending reunification — hence “Basic Law” rather than “constitution”. On reunification in 1990 it was extended to the whole country and retained, provisional no longer.

Its design answers the Nazi dictatorship and Weimar's failure point by point: human dignity is inviolable and first (Article 1); an extensive catalogue of basic rights binds all state authority as directly applicable law; the “eternity clause” (Article 79(3)) forbids amendments touching dignity, democracy, federalism and the rule of law; the chancellor can be removed only by constructive no-confidence (Article 67); militant democracy permits banning anti-constitutional parties and forfeiting abusers' basic rights; federalism is entrenched through the Bundesrat; and the Federal Constitutional Court guards the whole. The Basic Law has been amended more than sixty times (notably the 1968 emergency constitution and reunification-era changes) while its core has proved stable and exportable — the model for post-1989 constitutions in Central Europe and beyond.
