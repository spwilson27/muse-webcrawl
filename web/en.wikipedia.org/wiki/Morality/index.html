Morality
FromLatin moralitas — manner, character, proper behaviour
Studied byEthics (moral philosophy); moral psychology; evolutionary biology

Morality (from Latin moralitas) is the differentiation of intentions, decisions, and actions between those that are distinguished as proper (right) and those that are improper (wrong). Morality can be a body of standards or principles derived from a code of conduct from a particular philosophy, religion, or culture, or it can derive from a standard that a person believes should be universal. Morality may also be understood simply as "goodness" or "rightness".

Descriptive and normative senses

Philosophers distinguish morality in a descriptive sense — the codes of conduct actually put forward by a society, group, or individual — from morality in a normative sense: a code of conduct that, given specified conditions, would be put forward by all rational people. Moral philosophy (ethics) studies the normative question of what people ought to do, while anthropology, sociology, and psychology study descriptive morality.

Approaches in ethics

  • Deontology — morality as duty and rules; an act is right if it conforms to a binding norm (Kant's categorical imperative).
  • Consequentialism — an act is right insofar as it produces the best outcomes (utilitarianism).
  • Virtue ethics — morality centred on the character of the agent and human flourishing (Aristotle).
  • Divine command theory — morality grounded in the commands of God.

Evolution and psychology

Evolutionary approaches explain moral capacities — empathy, reciprocity, fairness, punishment of cheats — as adaptations supporting cooperation in social groups; precursors are observed in other primates. Moral psychology studies how judgements are actually made: Jonathan Haidt's social intuitionist model holds that moral judgements are typically fast, emotional intuitions followed by post-hoc reasoning, and moral foundations theory identifies recurring value clusters such as care, fairness, loyalty, authority, and sanctity.

Morality and religion

Most religions contain moral codes, and many people ground morality in religious teaching; others argue for secular foundations. Whether morality depends on religion is a long-standing philosophical question, posed since Plato's Euthyphro.
