Northern Rock, formerly the Northern Rock Building Society, was a British bank which operated in the United Kingdom. Based in Newcastle upon Tyne and demutualised in 1997, it grew rapidly by funding mortgage lending through wholesale markets and securitisation rather than retail deposits. In September 2007 it became the first British bank in over a century to suffer a bank run, and it was taken into public ownership in February 2008.

The 2007 crisis

When credit markets froze in August 2007 during the subprime crisis, Northern Rock could no longer refinance its short-term borrowing to fund long-term mortgages. News that it had sought emergency support from the Bank of England leaked on 13 September 2007, prompting depositors to queue outside branches and withdraw some £1 billion in a day — the first run on a British bank since 1866. The run stopped only when the government guaranteed all deposits. Chancellor Alistair Darling later authorised the bank's nationalisation on 22 February 2008.

Aftermath

On 1 January 2010 the bank was split into a "good bank" (Northern Rock plc, holding deposits and performing mortgages) and a "bad bank" (Northern Rock (Asset Management)) holding its risky loan book. The good bank was sold to Virgin Money in 2012 and its branches rebranded; the asset-management company was wound down over subsequent years, and the taxpayer was ultimately repaid.
