Freddie Mercury (born Farrokh Bulsara; 5 September 1946 – 24 November 1991) was a British singer and songwriter who achieved worldwide fame as the lead vocalist and pianist of the rock band Queen. Regarded as one of the greatest singers in the history of rock music, he was known for his flamboyant stage persona and four-octave vocal range.

Early life

Mercury was born in Stone Town, Zanzibar, to Parsi-Indian parents and spent much of his childhood in India, where he attended St. Peter's School near Bombay and began piano lessons. His family fled the Zanzibar Revolution in 1964 and settled in Middlesex, England. He later studied graphic design at Ealing Art College.

Career with Queen

In 1970 Mercury joined guitarist Brian May and drummer Roger Taylor in the band Smile, soon renamed Queen, with bassist John Deacon completing the line-up. He wrote many of the band's hits, including "Bohemian Rhapsody," "Killer Queen," "Somebody to Love," "Don't Stop Me Now," "Crazy Little Thing Called Love," and "We Are the Champions." Queen's performance at Live Aid in 1985 is frequently cited as one of the greatest live performances in rock history.

Solo work

Mercury released the solo album Mr. Bad Guy (1985) and, with soprano Montserrat Caballé, the album Barcelona (1988), whose title track became a hit after his death.

Death and legacy

Mercury died on 24 November 1991 of bronchial pneumonia resulting from AIDS, one day after publicly confirming his illness. In 1992 the remaining members of Queen held the Freddie Mercury Tribute Concert and founded the Mercury Phoenix Trust to fight HIV/AIDS. Mercury was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with Queen in 2001, and the 2018 biographical film Bohemian Rhapsody became the highest-grossing music biopic of all time.
