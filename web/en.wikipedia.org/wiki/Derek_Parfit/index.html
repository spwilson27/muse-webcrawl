Derek Antony Parfit (11 December 1942 – 1 January 2017) was a British philosopher who specialised in problems of personal identity, rationality, and ethics. He is widely considered one of the most important and influential moral philosophers of the late 20th and early 21st centuries.

Life

Parfit was born in Chengdu, China, to British medical missionaries, and educated at Eton and Balliol College, Oxford, where he read modern history before turning to philosophy. He spent nearly his whole career as a fellow of All Souls College, Oxford, also holding visiting posts at Harvard, NYU and Rutgers. He was also a keen architectural photographer of Oxford and Venice.

Reasons and Persons

His first book, Reasons and Persons (1984), is one of the most celebrated works of modern philosophy. Its four parts cover theories of rationality and morality, rational irrationality and self-defeating theories, and personal identity. Parfit defended a reductionist view of personal identity — arguing that identity is not what fundamentally matters in survival, which instead consists in psychological connectedness and continuity ("Relation R") — illustrated by thought experiments involving teletransportation and fission. The book's final part develops population ethics and presents the "repugnant conclusion" and the mere addition paradox.

On What Matters

His second book, On What Matters (2011, two volumes; a third appeared posthumously in 2017), argues that Kantian ethics, contractualism and consequentialism, properly understood, converge on the same principles — his "Triple Theory" — famously claiming that the three traditions are "climbing the same mountain on different sides."

Legacy

Parfit's work reshaped debates on personal identity, future generations and climate ethics, and influenced the effective altruism movement. He received the Rolf Schock Prize in Logic and Philosophy in 2014.
