Daddy Yankee

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ramón Luis Ayala Rodríguez (born 3 February 1977), known professionally as Daddy Yankee, is a Puerto Rican rapper, singer and songwriter. He is widely regarded as the artist most responsible for popularising reggaeton worldwide, and is often called the "King of Reggaeton" (El Rey del Reguetón).

Early life and career

Ayala was born in Río Piedras, San Juan, Puerto Rico. He aspired to become a professional baseball player, but his career was cut short when he was shot in the leg during a gang-related incident as a teenager; the recovery period pushed him toward music. He emerged in the early 1990s Puerto Rican underground scene, appearing on DJ Playero's mixtapes, and is credited with coining the word "reggaeton" in 1994 to describe the new genre.

Breakthrough

His third studio album, Barrio Fino (2004), brought reggaeton to an international audience, driven by the hit single "Gasolina", one of the first reggaeton songs to achieve global success. The album became the best-selling Latin music album of the decade in the United States. Subsequent albums included El Cartel: The Big Boss (2007), Mundial (2010) and Prestige (2012).

"Despacito" and later work

In 2017 Daddy Yankee featured on Luis Fonsi's "Despacito", which became a worldwide phenomenon: it topped the charts in nearly 50 countries, tied the record for the longest run at number one on the Billboard Hot 100 at the time, and its video became one of the most viewed in YouTube history. His 2019 single "Con Calma" also reached number one in more than 30 countries.

In March 2022 he announced his retirement from music, releasing his final studio album, Legendaddy, and embarking on the farewell tour La Última Vuelta. Over his career he sold tens of millions of records, won numerous awards including Latin Grammy Awards, and was inducted into the Billboard Latin Music Hall of Fame in 2021.
