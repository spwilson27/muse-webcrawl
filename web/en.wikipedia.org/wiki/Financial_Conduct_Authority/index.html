Financial Conduct Authority

The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) is a financial regulatory body in the United Kingdom, but operates independently of the UK Government, and is financed by charging fees to members of the financial services industry.

History

The FCA was established on 1 April 2013, taking over responsibility for conduct regulation and relevant prudential regulation from the Financial Services Authority, under reforms following the financial crisis of 2007–08. It works alongside the Prudential Regulation Authority, part of the Bank of England.

Responsibilities

The FCA regulates around 50,000 financial services firms and markets in the UK, with statutory objectives of protecting consumers, enhancing market integrity, and promoting competition. It has powers to authorise, supervise, and discipline firms and individuals, and to ban financial products.
