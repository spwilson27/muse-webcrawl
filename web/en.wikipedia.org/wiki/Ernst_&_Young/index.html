Ernst & Young Global Limited, trade name EY, is a multinational professional services partnership headquartered in London, England. EY is one of the largest professional services networks in the world and, along with Deloitte, KPMG and PwC, is considered one of the "Big Four" accounting firms. It provides assurance (including financial audit), tax, consulting, and strategy and transactions services to its clients.

History

The firm traces its origins to 1849, with the founding of Harding & Pullein in England. Its name derives from Alwin C. Ernst, who founded Ernst & Ernst in Cleveland in 1903, and Arthur Young, who founded Arthur Young & Co. in Chicago in 1906; the two men never met, both firms allied with other firms over the following decades. In 1989 Ernst & Whinney merged with Arthur Young to form Ernst & Young, then the second-largest professional services firm in the world. The organisation rebranded globally to "EY" in 2013.

Structure and services

EY is organised as a network of legally separate member firms employing hundreds of thousands of people in more than 150 countries. Its service lines are assurance, tax, consulting, and strategy and transactions (including the EY-Parthenon strategy practice). The firm has faced litigation and regulatory scrutiny arising from audits of clients such as Lehman Brothers (for whose Repo 105 transactions EY was criticised) and Wirecard.
