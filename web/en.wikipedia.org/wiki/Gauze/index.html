Gauze

MaterialCotton, silk, or synthetic fibres
WeaveOpen leno or plain weave
UsesMedical dressings, clothing, bookbinding, filtration

Gauze is a thin, translucent fabric with a loose open weave. The name is traditionally said to derive from Gaza, the Palestinian city historically associated with the production of such cloth. Gauze was originally made of silk and used for clothing; modern gauze is usually made of cotton or synthetic fibres.

Uses

Medical

Medical gauze is widely used for wound care: as pads and sponges to absorb blood and exudate, as bandages to hold dressings in place, and as packing for wounds. Because the open weave lets the wound breathe while absorbing fluid, cotton gauze became a standard dressing material in surgery and first aid. Gauze may be impregnated with substances such as petrolatum or antiseptics for specialised uses.

Other uses

Beyond medicine, gauze-like fabrics are used in clothing (especially lightweight summer garments), in bookbinding to reinforce spines, in cooking as a strainer (cheesecloth is a related loose cotton cloth), in theatre and film as scrim, and as wire gauze in laboratory settings, where a woven metal mesh supports glassware over a flame.
