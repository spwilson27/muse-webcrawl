Damascius (Greek: Δαμάσκιος; c. 458 – after 538) was a Syrian Neoplatonist philosopher and the last head (diadochos) of the Platonic Academy in Athens. A student of Marinus and Isidore, he reorganised the school and was one of the seven philosophers who fled Athens for the court of the Persian king Khosrow I after Justinian closed the Academy in 529.

Life

Born in Damascus — hence his name — Damascius studied rhetoric in Alexandria before turning to philosophy at Athens under Marinus, the successor of Proclus. He later taught at Alexandria and then returned to Athens, becoming head of the school around 515. After the closure of 529, Damascius and six colleagues, including Simplicius, accepted Khosrow's invitation to Ctesiphon; disillusioned, they returned under a treaty clause in 532 guaranteeing their safety and freedom to practise their religion. He may have ended his days at Emesa in Syria.

Works

His principal surviving work, the Problems and Solutions Concerning First Principles, pushes Neoplatonic theology to its limit, positing an utterly ineffable principle beyond even the One, of which nothing whatsoever can be affirmed. He also wrote commentaries on Plato's Phaedo, Philebus and Parmenides, and a partly preserved Life of Isidore, a rich source for the pagan intellectual world of late antiquity.
