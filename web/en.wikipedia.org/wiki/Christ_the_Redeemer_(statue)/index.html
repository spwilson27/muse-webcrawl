Christ the Redeemer (statue)

Christ the Redeemer
Portuguese: Cristo Redentor
Location: Corcovado, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
Height: 30 m (38 m with pedestal)
Completed: 1931
Material: reinforced concrete, soapstone

Christ the Redeemer (Portuguese: Cristo Redentor) is an Art Deco statue of Jesus Christ in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, created by French sculptor Paul Landowski and built by Brazilian engineer Heitor da Silva Costa in collaboration with French engineer Albert Caquot. Romanian sculptor Gheorghe Leonida sculpted the face.

Construction

Constructed between 1922 and 1931, the statue is 30 metres high, excluding its 8-metre pedestal, and its outstretched arms span 28 metres. It is made of reinforced concrete and clad in some six million soapstone tiles, and stands atop the 700-metre Corcovado mountain overlooking the city. It weighs about 635 metric tons.

History

The idea of a monumental statue on Corcovado dated to the mid-19th century, but the project only advanced after Brazil's centenary of independence in 1922. Funds were raised by Brazilian Catholics. The statue was inaugurated on 12 October 1931.

Significance

The statue has become a cultural icon of both Rio de Janeiro and Brazil, and in 2007 it was named one of the New7Wonders of the World. It is struck by lightning several times a year and has been periodically restored.
