Haganah

The Haganah (Hebrew: הַהֲגָנָה, lit. "The Defense") was the main paramilitary organization of the Jewish Yishuv in Mandatory Palestine. It was founded in 1920 to defend Jewish settlements and evolved from a small militia into a large, well-organized force. After Israeli independence in 1948, the Haganah became the core of the Israel Defense Forces (IDF).

Origins

The Haganah was formed after the 1920 Nebi Musa riots and the 1921 Jaffa riots convinced Jewish leaders that the British authorities could not be relied upon to protect Jewish communities. It succeeded earlier Jewish self-defence groups such as Bar-Giora (1907) and Hashomer (1909), but was placed under the civilian control of the Histadrut labour federation and later the Jewish Agency.

Development

During the 1936–1939 Arab revolt the Haganah expanded greatly, organising field units (the Fosh), the Special Night Squads trained with Orde Wingate, and later the Palmach striking force (1941). It established clandestine arms production and illegal immigration operations (Aliyah Bet) bringing Holocaust survivors to Palestine in defiance of British restrictions.

1947–1949 war

In the civil war that followed the UN Partition Plan of November 1947 and the ensuing 1948 Arab–Israeli War, the Haganah fielded some 30,000–35,000 troops and executed Plan Dalet to secure the territory of the proposed Jewish state. On 28 May 1948, two weeks after the Declaration of Independence, David Ben-Gurion's provisional government ordered the Haganah incorporated into the new IDF, and the rival Irgun and Lehi militias were disbanded.
