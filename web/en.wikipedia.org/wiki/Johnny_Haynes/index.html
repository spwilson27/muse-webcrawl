Johnny Haynes

Born: 17 October 1934, Edmonton, London, England
Died: 18 October 2005 (aged 71), Edinburgh, Scotland
Position: Inside forward
Clubs: Fulham (1952–1970)
National team: England, 56 caps, 18 goals

John Norman Haynes (17 October 1934 – 18 October 2005) was an English association footballer who played as an inside forward. He made 594 appearances for Fulham between 1952 and 1970, scoring 147 goals, and earned 56 caps for England, captaining the side 22 times. Widely regarded as one of the greatest passers of the ball in the history of the English game, he spent his entire professional career at Craven Cottage.

Career

Haynes joined Fulham as a schoolboy and signed professional terms in 1952, turning down approaches from bigger clubs. A creative playmaker famous for the accuracy and vision of his passing, he helped Fulham win promotion to the First Division in 1959. He captained England at the 1962 World Cup in Chile, having also played at the 1958 tournament in Sweden. Pelé described him as "the best passer of the ball I've ever seen."

The £100-a-week footballer

In 1961, following the abolition of the maximum wage for professional footballers in England, Fulham chairman Tommy Trinder made Haynes the first player in Britain to be paid £100 per week—an enormous sum at the time and a landmark in the professionalisation of the sport. Despite lucrative offers from Italian clubs including AC Milan, Haynes remained loyal to Fulham for his whole career.

Later life and legacy

After retiring in 1970, Haynes briefly played in South Africa and later worked in business in Edinburgh. A statue of Haynes stands outside Craven Cottage, unveiled in 2008; he is remembered as Fulham's greatest-ever player.
