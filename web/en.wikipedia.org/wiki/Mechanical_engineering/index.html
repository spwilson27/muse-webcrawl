Mechanical engineering

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Mechanical engineers' tools: gears, a turbine rotor and engineering drawings on a workbench]

Mechanical engineering is the branch of engineering concerned with the design, analysis, manufacture and maintenance of mechanical systems. It applies principles of physics and materials science — including mechanics, dynamics, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics and structural analysis.

It is one of the oldest and broadest engineering disciplines. Mechanical engineers work in industries from automotive and aerospace to energy, robotics, manufacturing and biomedical devices.

History

The field emerged with the Industrial Revolution in Europe in the 18th century, driven by the steam engine, although its roots reach back to classical machines, and to figures such as Archimedes. Professional institutions formed in the 19th century, including the Institution of Mechanical Engineers in Britain (1847).

Sub-disciplines and tools

Sub-fields include mechatronics, robotics, structural analysis, thermodynamics and heating/cooling systems, and vehicle engineering. Modern practice relies heavily on computer-aided design (CAD), computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) and finite-element analysis.
