Suzuki reaction

The Suzuki–Miyaura cross-coupling

The Suzuki reaction (or Suzuki–Miyaura coupling) is an organic reaction in which an organoboron compound, typically a boronic acid, is cross-coupled with an organic halide or triflate in the presence of a palladium(0) catalyst and a base, forming a new carbon–carbon bond. It was first published in 1979 by Akira Suzuki and Norio Miyaura at Hokkaido University, and Akira Suzuki shared the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for palladium-catalysed cross-couplings, together with Richard Heck and Ei-ichi Negishi. (It is unrelated to the Suzuki method of music teaching.)

Mechanism

The catalytic cycle proceeds by oxidative addition of the organic halide to Pd(0), transmetalation of the organic group from boron to palladium — a step promoted by base, which forms a more reactive boronate — and reductive elimination, which expels the coupled product and regenerates Pd(0). Boronic acids are prized because they are air-stable, commercially diverse, low in toxicity, and tolerant of many functional groups.

Applications

The Suzuki coupling is one of the most widely used reactions in pharmaceutical and materials chemistry, employed in the industrial synthesis of drug candidates (such as the fungicide boscalid) and of conjugated polymers and organic semiconductors. Variants couple alkylboron reagents, and nickel catalysis and photocatalyst systems have extended its scope.
