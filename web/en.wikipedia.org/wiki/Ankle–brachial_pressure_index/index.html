Ankle–brachial pressure index

The ankle–brachial pressure index (ABPI), also called the ankle–brachial index (ABI), is the ratio of the systolic blood pressure measured at the ankle to that measured at the brachial artery of the arm. It is a simple, non-invasive test used principally to diagnose peripheral arterial disease (PAD) of the legs and to assess cardiovascular risk, since a low index reflects generalised atherosclerosis.

Method

Pressures are measured with the patient lying supine and rested, using a sphygmomanometer cuff and a handheld Doppler ultrasound probe to detect flow in the dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial arteries at the ankle and the brachial artery in the arm. For each leg, the higher ankle pressure is divided by the higher of the two brachial pressures.

Interpretation

In healthy people, ankle pressure is slightly higher than arm pressure, giving an index of about 1.0–1.4. An ABPI of 0.90 or below is diagnostic of peripheral arterial disease, and progressively lower values indicate more severe disease; values below about 0.5 suggest critical limb ischaemia. Values above 1.40 indicate non-compressible, calcified arteries—common in diabetes and chronic kidney disease—and are also associated with increased risk; in such cases a toe–brachial index can be used instead. A low ABPI is a strong independent predictor of heart attack and stroke.
