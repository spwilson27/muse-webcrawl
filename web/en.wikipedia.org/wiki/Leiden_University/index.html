Leiden University (Dutch: Universiteit Leiden) is a public research university in Leiden, Netherlands, founded in 1575 by William, Prince of Orange, as a reward to the city for its heroic defence against Spanish besiegers during the Eighty Years' War. It is the oldest university in the Netherlands and one of Europe's leading research institutions, consistently ranked among the world's top universities.

History

Founded with the motto Praesidium Libertatis — "Bastion of Freedom" — the university quickly became a centre of the Dutch Golden Age. Joseph Justus Scaliger and other scholars of European renown taught there; its botanical garden, the Hortus Botanicus (1590), was among Europe's first, and its anatomical theatre and observatory (1633) pioneered new forms of teaching and research. Herman Boerhaave made Leiden the foremost medical school of 18th-century Europe. The university later became famous for physics: Heike Kamerlingh Onnes discovered superconductivity in its cryogenic laboratory in 1911, and Hendrik Lorentz and Pieter Zeeman also worked there.

Academics

The university comprises seven faculties — Archaeology, Governance and Global Affairs, Humanities, Law, Medicine (at Leiden University Medical Center), Science, and Social and Behavioural Sciences — with campuses in Leiden and The Hague. Its library holds major special collections, including the archives of the East India Company era and the Kern Institute's Indological collections. Leiden counts sixteen Nobel laureates among its alumni and staff, as well as members of the Dutch royal family, Dutch prime ministers, and foreign heads of state among its graduates.

Traditions

The historic Academy Building on the Rapenburg canal remains the ceremonial heart of the university, where doctoral defences are held beneath portraits of professors past. The university's founding is celebrated annually on its dies natalis, 8 February.
