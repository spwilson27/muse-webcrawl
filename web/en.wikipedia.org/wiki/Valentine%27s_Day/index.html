Valentine's Day

Date14 February
ObservancesSending cards, flowers, and gifts to loved ones
SignificanceFeast day of Saint Valentine; celebration of romantic love

Valentine's Day, also called Saint Valentine's Day or the Feast of Saint Valentine, is celebrated annually on 14 February. It originated as a Christian feast day honouring a martyr named Valentine, and through later folk traditions it has become a major cultural and commercial celebration of romance and love in many parts of the world.

History

Several martyrs named Valentine were commemorated on 14 February in early martyrologies. Pope Gelasius I established the feast in 496, possibly replacing the Roman fertility festival of Lupercalia in the popular imagination, though no direct link is documented. The association with romantic love developed in the circle of Geoffrey Chaucer in the fourteenth century, when courtly love flourished; Chaucer's Parliament of Fowls links St Valentine's Day with birds choosing their mates.

Customs

By the eighteenth century in England, lovers exchanged flowers, confectionery, and handwritten "valentines". In the nineteenth century, mass-produced greeting cards — pioneered in the United States by Esther Howland — replaced letters. Today the most common gifts are cards, red roses, chocolates, and jewellery, and the day is a major date for restaurant meals and marriage proposals.

Around the world

The day is observed widely in the Americas, Europe, and parts of Asia. In Japan and South Korea, women traditionally give chocolate on 14 February and men reciprocate on White Day, 14 March. In some countries the day has faced official or religious opposition, while in others it has merged with local traditions of celebrating love.
