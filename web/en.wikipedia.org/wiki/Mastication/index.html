Mastication

Mastication (chewing) is the process by which food is crushed and ground by the teeth, the first step of digestion. It increases the surface area of foods, mixes them with saliva and its enzymes, and forms a bolus suitable for swallowing.

Mechanism

The muscles of mastication — masseter, temporalis, and medial and lateral pterygoids — move the mandible in coordinated cycles controlled by a central pattern generator in the brainstem. The tongue and cheeks position food between the teeth.

In animals

Ruminants chew cud; many herbivores have specialised flat molars. Some animals, such as birds, use a gizzard instead of teeth.
