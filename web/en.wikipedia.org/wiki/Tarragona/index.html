Tarragona

Tarragona is a port city in northeastern Spain, capital of its province in southern Catalonia, on the Mediterranean about 100 km south-west of Barcelona. Founded as Roman Tarraco, capital of Hispania Citerior and one of the largest cities of Roman Spain, its extensive Roman remains — amphitheatre, circus, forum, aqueduct and walls — are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Today it is a major petrochemical and port centre, with about 135,000 inhabitants, and a gateway to the Costa Daurada resorts.

History

The Scipios made Tarraco Rome's first base in Iberia during the Second Punic War (218 BC); Augustus wintered here and made it capital of the vast province of Tarraconensis. A large early Christian community (Bishop Fructuosus was martyred in the amphitheatre in 259) preceded Visigothic and brief Muslim rule; the medieval city, re-Christianised in the twelfth century, raised its cathedral on the Roman temple platform. In the Peninsular War Tarragona endured a bloody French siege and sack in 1811 — one of the war's notorious atrocities. The modern city grew with wine exports (phylloxera then devastated it), the port, and from the 1960s one of Spain's largest petrochemical complexes.

The old town

The Part Alta, within Roman and medieval walls, climbs from the amphitheatre above the sea to the cathedral; the Rambla Nova runs to the Balcó del Mediterrani viewpoint. Every two years the Concurs de Castells fills the old bullring with Catalonia's best human-tower teams — a tradition centred on Tarragona and nearby Valls.
