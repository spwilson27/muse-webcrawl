Flag of Finland

The national flag of Finland (Finnish: Suomen lippu, Swedish: Finlands flagga), also called the siniristilippu ("Blue Cross Flag"), dates from the beginning of the 20th century. On a white background, it features a blue Nordic cross, which represents Christianity. The state flag has the coat of arms in the centre of the square formed by the cross, but is otherwise identical to the civil flag.

History

Finland declared independence from Russia in December 1917. After a short period in which a red flag bearing a golden lion was used, the Finnish Senate held a competition for a national flag. The blue-and-white cross design, associated with the poet Zachris Topelius's earlier suggestions and widely flown by Finnish yachts clubs, was adopted by law on 29 May 1918.

The blue is said to represent Finland's thousands of lakes and the sky, while the white represents the snow that covers the country in winter. The proportions and exact shade of blue were standardized by later legislation, most comprehensively in 1978.

Variants

The state flag bears the coat of arms of Finland — a crowned golden lion on a red field — in the square at the centre of the cross. A swallow-tailed version is used as the military flag and naval ensign; the presidential standard adds the Cross of Liberty in the upper hoist canton.

Flag days

Finland has a number of official flag days, including Midsummer, Independence Day (6 December), and the birthdays of notable cultural figures, when flags are flown from public buildings.
