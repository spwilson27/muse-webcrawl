North Slope, Alaska

[Image: North Slope tundra]

The North Slope is the region of the U.S. state of Alaska located on the northern slope of the Brooks Range along the coast of two marginal seas of the Arctic Ocean, the Chukchi Sea being on the western side of Point Barrow, and the Beaufort Sea on the eastern. Administratively it corresponds largely to the North Slope Borough, the northernmost county-equivalent in the United States, whose seat is Utqiagvik (formerly Barrow).

Geography and ecology

The region consists of Arctic foothills and a flat coastal plain of tundra pocked with thaw lakes, underlain by continuous permafrost. It is home to caribou herds, grizzly bears, muskoxen, and vast numbers of migratory waterfowl, and includes much of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge's coastal plain and the National Petroleum Reserve–Alaska (NPR-A).

Petroleum

Oil was discovered at Prudhoe Bay in 1968, the largest oil field yet found in North America; production began in 1977 after completion of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline System to Valdez. Employment at Prudhoe Bay and satellite fields such as Kuparuk and Alpine far exceeds the region's resident population of about 10,000, most of them Iñupiat Alaska Natives. Drilling in the Arctic Refuge coastal plain and the Willow project in the NPR-A have been long-running political controversies.
