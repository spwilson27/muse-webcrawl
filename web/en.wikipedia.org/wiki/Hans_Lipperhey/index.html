Hans Lipperhey

Hans Lipperhey (c. 1570 – buried 29 September 1619), also known as Johann Lippershey or Lippershey, was a German-Dutch spectacle maker. He is commonly credited with the invention of the telescope, because he was the first person to attempt to obtain a patent for it in 1608.

Life and patent application

Lipperhey was born in Wesel, in western Germany, and settled in Middelburg in Zeeland, the Netherlands, where he worked as a lens grinder and spectacle maker. On 2 October 1608 he applied to the States General of the Netherlands for a patent for a device "for seeing things far away as if they were nearby" — a refracting telescope using a convex objective lens and a concave eyepiece, magnifying about three times.

The States General did not grant the patent, partly because similar claims were made almost simultaneously by other Middelburg spectacle makers, including Jacob Metius and, later, Zacharias Janssen. Lipperhey was instead paid to make several instruments, including a binocular version for military use.

Legacy

News of the device spread rapidly across Europe; within a year Galileo Galilei had built his own improved telescopes and turned them to the sky. The lunar crater Lippershey and the exoplanet Lipperhey (55 Cancri d) are named in his honour.
