Carmilla is a Gothic novella by the Irish author Sheridan Le Fanu, first published in 1871–72 and collected in In a Glass Darkly (1872). It is one of the earliest works of vampire fiction and predates Bram Stoker's Dracula (1897) by 26 years; it strongly influenced Stoker's novel.

Plot

The story is narrated by Laura, a young woman living with her widowed father in a castle in Styria, Austria. After a strange childhood visitation from a beautiful lady with sharp teeth, Laura longs for a companion when their expected guest dies mysteriously. A carriage accident outside the castle leaves a young woman, Carmilla, in the family's care. Laura and Carmilla become intensely devoted friends, but Carmilla behaves oddly: she keeps strange hours, suffers languid episodes, and is evasive about her past.

Meanwhile, young women in the district are dying of a mysterious wasting illness. Laura herself falls ill and has nocturnal visions of a cat-like creature at her bed. With the help of General Spielsdorf, whose niece died after a similar friendship, and the scholar Dr. Hesselius, Carmilla is identified as Mircalla, Countess Karnstein, a vampire who has preyed on the region for over a century — "Carmilla" being an anagram of her name. Her tomb is opened and she is destroyed by staking, beheading, and burning.

Themes and legacy

Carmilla is celebrated for its atmospheric style, its psychological ambiguity, and its portrayal of an intimate, predatory friendship between the vampire and her victim, frequently read as an early depiction of lesbian desire in Gothic literature. It established many conventions of the vampire genre, including the vampire's aristocratic lineage, ancestral portraits, and destruction rituals, and has been adapted many times for film, stage, and a popular web series.
