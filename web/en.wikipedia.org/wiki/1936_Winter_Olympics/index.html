The 1936 Winter Olympics, officially the IV Olympic Winter Games, were held from February 6 to 16, 1936, in the Bavarian towns of Garmisch and Partenkirchen, by then combined as Garmisch-Partenkirchen. They were the last Winter Games before the Second World War and took place in Nazi Germany only months before the Berlin Summer Olympics.

Events

The programme included bobsleigh, ice hockey, speed skating, figure skating, Nordic skiing, ski jumping and alpine skiing, which made its Olympic debut with combined events for men and women. Demonstration sports included military patrol and ice stock sport. Norway led the medal table, powered by speed skater Ivar Ballangrud and skier Oddbjørn Hagen.

Notable results

Great Britain won a celebrated gold medal in ice hockey, defeating Canada in a tournament remembered for eligibility disputes involving British-born players raised in Canada. Sonja Henie of Norway won her third consecutive women's figure skating title. Germany used the Games as a showcase of organization, though the event unfolded under the shadow of the regime's racial policies and rearmament.

Legacy

The Garmisch-Partenkirchen Games expanded the Winter Olympics by adding alpine skiing and by drawing a then-record field. They remain inseparable from the politics of 1936, when sport, propaganda and international tension converged in Germany.
