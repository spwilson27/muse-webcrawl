Heisei

The Heisei era (Japanese: 平成) is the period of Japanese history corresponding to the reign of Emperor Akihito, from 8 January 1989, the day after the death of Emperor Hirohito (the Shōwa Emperor), until Akihito's abdication on 30 April 2019. It was followed by the Reiwa era of Emperor Naruhito.

History and events

The era opened as Japan's asset price bubble collapsed, ushering in the "Lost Decades" of economic stagnation and deflation. Major events included the Great Hanshin earthquake and the Tokyo subway sarin attack (both 1995), Japan co-hosting the 2002 FIFA World Cup, the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami and the resulting Fukushima nuclear disaster, and the economic policies known as Abenomics.

Era name

Heisei, meaning "achieving peace" or "peace everywhere", derives from phrases in the Chinese classics, the Records of the Grand Historian and the Book of Documents. The era ended with the first imperial abdication in about two centuries, authorised by special legislation.

Conversion

To convert a Heisei year to the Gregorian calendar, add 1988: for example, Heisei 31 is 2019.
