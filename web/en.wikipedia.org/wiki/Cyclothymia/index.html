Cyclothymia

Cyclothymia, also known as cyclothymic disorder, is a mental and mood disorder characterised by numerous periods of hypomanic symptoms and numerous periods of depressive symptoms that do not meet the criteria for a major depressive episode. It is classified within the bipolar spectrum, as a milder but chronic form of bipolar disorder.

Symptoms and diagnosis

Under the DSM-5, cyclothymia is diagnosed when, for at least two years (one year in children and adolescents), there have been many periods with hypomanic symptoms and many periods with depressive symptoms, with the person never symptom-free for more than two months at a time. The symptoms must cause clinically significant distress or impairment, yet the person has never met full criteria for a manic, hypomanic, or major depressive episode — if they later do, the diagnosis changes.

Hypomanic periods may involve elevated or irritable mood, increased energy and talkativeness, decreased need for sleep, and racing thoughts; depressive periods involve low mood, fatigue, low self-esteem, and difficulty concentrating. Because symptoms are sub-threshold, cyclothymia is often under-recognised or mistaken for personality traits.

Causes and course

The causes are not fully understood but include genetic vulnerability — cyclothymia is more common in relatives of people with bipolar disorder — together with neurochemical factors and life stress. The condition usually begins in adolescence or early adulthood. A proportion of people with cyclothymia later develop bipolar I or bipolar II disorder.

Treatment

Treatment typically combines psychoeducation, mood-charting, and psychotherapy such as cognitive behavioural therapy, with attention to regular sleep and routines. Mood-stabilising medications used in bipolar disorder may be prescribed, while antidepressants are used cautiously because of the risk of mood destabilisation.
