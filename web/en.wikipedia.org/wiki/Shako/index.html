Shako

A shako is a tall, cylindrical military cap, usually with a visor (peak), decorative insignia and sometimes a plume. Derived from the Hungarian csákó, the shako became the standard infantry headdress of European and American armies during the Napoleonic Wars and remained in widespread use through the 19th century.

History

The shako originated with Hungarian hussar dress in the 18th century and was adopted for line infantry from around 1800. The French "stovepipe" shako and the British "Albert" shako of 1844 are among its classic forms. Tall and conspicuous, it offered some protection and considerable regimental identity, but little comfort or practicality; by the First World War most armies had relegated it to ceremonial dress, replacing it in the field with soft caps and steel helmets.

Surviving use

Shakos survive today in ceremonial uniforms — for example with the Swiss Guard of the Vatican, some Latin American presidential guards, and university marching bands, where the shako with a plume remains standard dress.
