The Litani River (Arabic: نهر الليطاني, Nahr al-Līṭānī), known in antiquity as the Leontes, is the longest river in Lebanon, flowing entirely within the country's borders. It rises in the fertile Beqaa Valley west of Baalbek, flows southward between the Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon ranges, and, at the southern end of the valley near Marjayoun, turns sharply west through the mountains of southern Lebanon to empty into the Mediterranean Sea just north of Tyre.

Course

The river runs for about 170 kilometres. Its upper course in the Beqaa is slow and heavily used for irrigation. The Qaraoun Dam, completed in 1959, impounds Lake Qaraoun, the largest artificial lake in Lebanon, whose waters drive the Markaba, Awali and Joun hydroelectric plants of the Litani River Authority through tunnels bored through Mount Lebanon, discharging to the Awali River basin — an unusual inter-basin transfer that roughly doubles the river's power output.

History and politics

The Litani has long figured in regional strategy. In the 1978 Israeli invasion of Lebanon (Operation Litani) and again in 1982 and 2006, the river's line served as a reference boundary. UN Security Council Resolution 1701 (2006) called for the area between the Blue Line and the Litani to be free of armed personnel other than Lebanese forces and UNIFIL. Claims that the river's waters were a motive in regional conflicts have been debated by historians.

Environment

Untreated sewage and agricultural runoff have seriously polluted parts of the Litani basin, and Lake Qaraoun has suffered algal blooms and fish die-offs. A long-running national cleanup programme aims to intercept wastewater from Beqaa towns.
