Frederick Temple

Born30 November 1821, Santa Maura, Ionian Islands
Died23 December 1902, London
OfficesHeadmaster of Rugby School; Bishop of Exeter; Bishop of London; Archbishop of Canterbury

Frederick Temple (30 November 1821 – 23 December 1902) was an English academic, schoolmaster and Anglican cleric who served as Bishop of Exeter, Bishop of London and finally Archbishop of Canterbury from 1896 until his death.

Early life and education

Temple was born in the Ionian Islands, where his father was an army officer. He attended Blundell's School in Tiverton and won a scholarship to Balliol College, Oxford, where he took a double first and became a fellow. He worked for the Education Office and became principal of Kneller Hall, a training college for teachers.

Rugby and the Church

In 1858 Temple became headmaster of Rugby School, succeeding Thomas Arnold's legacy; he was a firm but respected disciplinarian and strengthened the school's teaching of science. His contribution to the controversial volume Essays and Reviews (1860), which questioned orthodox views of biblical inspiration, initially damaged his prospects, but he was consecrated Bishop of Exeter in 1869. He became Bishop of London in 1885 and Archbishop of Canterbury in 1896. As archbishop he presided over the Lambeth Conference of 1897 and led the Church of England's response to ritualist controversies. His son William Temple also became Archbishop of Canterbury.
