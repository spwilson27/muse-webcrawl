Vaisakhi

Vaisakhi (also spelled Baisakhi) is an ancient spring harvest festival celebrated primarily in the Punjab region of northern India and Pakistan, falling usually on 13 or 14 April. For Sikhs it is one of the most important dates in the calendar, marking the founding of the Khalsa in 1699. It also traditionally marks the Punjabi New Year and the beginning of the rabi harvest season.

Sikh significance

On Vaisakhi 1699, at Anandpur Sahib, the tenth Sikh Guru, Guru Gobind Singh, called together a great gathering and invited volunteers willing to give their heads for the faith. Five men came forward — remembered as the Panj Pyare, the "Five Beloved Ones" — and were initiated with amrit (sweetened water stirred with a double-edged sword), forming the first Khalsa, the community of initiated Sikhs. The Guru abolished the masand intermediaries and established the visible identity of the Khalsa, including the Five Ks.

Celebrations

Vaisakhi is marked by nagar kirtan processions through towns, led by the Panj Pyare in saffron dress and carrying the Guru Granth Sahib with honour. Gurdwaras hold special services and langar (community meals), fairs are held in market towns, and farmers celebrate the wheat harvest with bhangra dancing. Major celebrations take place at the Golden Temple in Amritsar and at Anandpur Sahib, and large parades are held by Sikh communities in Canada, the United Kingdom and elsewhere.

Wider observance

Related spring festivals fall on the same date across India under regional names such as Puthandu in Tamil Nadu, Vishu in Kerala and Pohela Boishakh in Bengal, reflecting the date's traditional role as the solar new year.
