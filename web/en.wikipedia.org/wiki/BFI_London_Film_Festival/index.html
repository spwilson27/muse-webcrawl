The BFI London Film Festival is an annual film festival held in London, England, organised by the British Film Institute (BFI). Founded in 1957, it is the United Kingdom's largest public film festival, screening several hundred films from around the world each October.

History

The festival was established in 1957 by a group of film critics including Dilys Powell of The Sunday Times, with the idea of creating a "festival of festivals" that would bring prizewinning films from other international festivals to London audiences. The first edition screened around 15–20 films at the National Film Theatre (now BFI Southbank).

Over the decades the festival expanded greatly, moving its gala screenings to cinemas in Leicester Square and elsewhere in the West End, and later adding venues across the capital. Since 2020 the festival has also offered digital screenings and touring programmes in cinemas across the UK.

Programme and awards

The programme is organised into strands including Galas, Official Competition, First Feature Competition (for the Sutherland Award), Documentary Competition (for the Grierson Award) and Short Film Competition, alongside thematic strands such as Love, Debate, Dare, Thrill, Cult, Journey, Create, Family and Treasures (archive restorations). Awards include Best Film, the Sutherland Award for most original and imaginative first feature, and the Grierson Award for documentary.
