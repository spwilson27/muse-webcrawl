Midewiwin

The Midewiwin (also spelled Midewewin or Midewin) is a traditional Indigenous religious society of the Anishinaabe peoples — including the Ojibwe, Odawa, and Potawatomi — of the Great Lakes region of North America. The word is often translated as "the Grand Medicine Society".

Beliefs and practice

Midewiwin practitioners, known as Mide, preserve teachings about medicine, healing, and spiritual life transmitted through staged initiations, commonly described as four degrees. Teachings were recorded in pictographic form on birchbark scrolls (wiigwaasabak), which served as mnemonic devices for songs and ceremonies.

Origins and history

Oral traditions attribute the society's founding to the culture hero and trickster figure Nanabozho. Scholars debate whether the society in its documented form predates European contact or developed in response to it. The society remains active, and its ceremonies and scrolls are treated as sacred and private by many Anishinaabe communities.
