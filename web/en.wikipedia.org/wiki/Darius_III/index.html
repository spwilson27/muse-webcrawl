Darius III

Darius III (Old Persian: Dārayavaʰuš; c. 380 – 330 BC), born Artashata and called Codomannus by the Greeks, was the last Achaemenid King of Kings of Persia, reigning from 336 BC to his death in 330 BC. His reign ended with the conquest of the Persian Empire by Alexander the Great.

Accession

Darius was a distant member of the royal house who rose after the eunuch Bagoas murdered Arses and placed him on the throne, expecting a puppet; Darius instead had Bagoas executed by making him drink his own poison. He inherited an empire already strained by revolts in Egypt and elsewhere.

War with Alexander

Alexander invaded Asia in 334 BC. Darius personally commanded the Persian army at the Battle of Issus (333 BC), where Alexander's victory forced Darius to flee, leaving his mother, wife, and children in Macedonian hands. He rebuilt a larger army but was decisively defeated again at Gaugamela (331 BC), after which Babylon, Susa, Persepolis, and Ecbatana fell in succession. Fleeing eastward in 330 BC, Darius was arrested and murdered by his own satrap Bessus and fellow conspirators; Alexander, arriving shortly after, is said to have covered the body with his cloak and later gave Darius a royal burial at Persepolis.
