Hobgoblin

A hobgoblin, a creature of folklore

A hobgoblin is a household spirit in English folklore and mythology. Once considered helpful beings, similar to brownies, they moved from being helpful to being harmful or vexing. The term "hobgoblin" comes from "hob" ("elf"), an abbreviation of Robert or Robin, and was a synonym for a good-natured household spirit; "goblin" denotes a mischievous or evil being. In later usage, hobgoblins were often imagined as larger, more grotesque, and more malevolent than ordinary goblins.

Folklore

In traditional folklore, hobgoblins were small, hairy little men who, like their counterparts the brownies, were said to do household chores while the inhabitants slept. They were thought to be helpful but also easily offended: a gift of new clothing, in many tales, causes the spirit to depart forever. Hobgoblins were associated with particular houses and farms, and stories of them are especially common in the north of England and the Scottish Borders.

In literature

Shakespeare's Puck in A Midsummer Night's Dream is called a hobgoblin ("that shrewd and knavish sprite call'd Robin Goodfellow"). John Bunyan, in The Pilgrim's Progress, used "hobgoblin" metaphorically, a usage echoed in the famous line adapted from Ralph Waldo Emerson: "A foolish consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds." Hobgoblins also appear in the works of J. R. R. Tolkien, where the term denotes the larger orcs of The Hobbit, and in fantasy role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons, where hobgoblins are a militaristic goblinoid race.

Modern depictions

In modern popular culture the name is used for the Hobgoblin, a supervillain in Marvel Comics' Spider-Man stories, and appears widely in fantasy games, novels, and films as a stock monstrous humanoid.
