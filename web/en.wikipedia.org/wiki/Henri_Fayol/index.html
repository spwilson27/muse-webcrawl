Henri Fayol

Henri Fayol (29 July 1841 – 19 November 1925) was a French mining engineer, mining executive, author and director of mines who developed the general theory of business administration known as Fayolism. His work, set out in Administration Industrielle et Générale (1916), made him one of the founders of modern management theory, alongside contemporaries such as Frederick Winslow Taylor.

Management theory

Fayol identified five functions of management: forecasting and planning, organising, commanding, coordinating, and controlling. He also proposed fourteen principles of administration, including division of work, authority and responsibility, discipline, unity of command, unity of direction, subordination of individual interest to the general interest, remuneration, centralisation, scalar chain, order, equity, stability of tenure of personnel, initiative, and esprit de corps.

Career

Fayol spent his career with the French mining company Commentry-Fourchambault-Decazeville, rising from engineer to managing director; he is credited with turning the financially troubled company around. After retiring he founded a centre for administrative studies and promoted the teaching of management in France.
