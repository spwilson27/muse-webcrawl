Repeat unit

A repeat unit (also called a repeating unit) in polymer chemistry is the smallest structural unit whose repetition describes the regular part of a polymer chain. It is the part of a polymer whose repeated application constitutes the whole chain, apart from end-groups.

Definition

According to IUPAC recommendations, a constitutional repeating unit is an atom or group of atoms (with pendant atoms or groups, if any) comprising the essential part of a single macromolecular chain, such that repetition generates the complete polymer (excluding end groups). For example, the repeat unit of poly(ethylene) is written –CH2–CH2–, conventionally abbreviated [CH2CH2]n.

Repeat unit vs monomer

The repeat unit is not necessarily identical to the monomer from which the polymer was made. In addition polymerisation the repeat unit retains all the atoms of the monomer (as in poly(ethylene)); in condensation polymerisation, small molecules such as water are eliminated, so the repeat unit differs from the monomers — for example, the nylon 6,6 repeat unit derives jointly from hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid minus two water molecules. In some cases a repeat unit may be composed of more than one monomer unit.

Use

The molar mass of the repeat unit, multiplied by the degree of polymerisation, gives the number-average molar mass of the polymer (with correction for end groups). Repeat units are conventionally drawn enclosed in brackets with a subscript n indicating an unspecified number of repetitions.
