Checkers speech

Checkers speech
Date: September 23, 1952
Speaker: Richard Nixon
Occasion: Fund crisis speech

The Checkers speech or Fund speech was an address made on September 23, 1952, by California Senator Richard Nixon, six weeks before the 1952 United States presidential election, in which he was the Republican candidate for Vice President. Nixon had been accused of improprieties relating to a fund established by his backers to reimburse him for his political expenses. His place on the Republican ticket was in jeopardy, so he flew to Los Angeles and delivered a half-hour television address in which he defended himself, attacked his opponents, and urged the audience to contact the Republican National Committee (RNC) to tell it whether he should remain on the ticket.

Background

Nixon, who had been elected to Congress in 1946 and the Senate in 1950, was Dwight D. Eisenhower's running mate in 1952. A story in the New York Post alleged that Nixon had a secret "$18,000 fund" for personal use, raising questions of influence and gifts.

The speech

During the speech, Nixon stated that he intended to keep one gift regardless of what people said about it: a black-and-white cocker spaniel named Checkers, sent to his family by a supporter in Texas. The line gave the speech its name and became one of the most famous moments in early television politics.

Aftermath

The speech prompted an outpouring of public support, and Eisenhower chose to keep Nixon on the ticket. The pair went on to win the 1952 election in a landslide.
