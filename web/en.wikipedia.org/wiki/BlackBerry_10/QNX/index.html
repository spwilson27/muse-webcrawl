QNX

DeveloperQNX Software Systems (BlackBerry subsidiary)
First released1982
Kernel typeMicrokernel (real-time)
UsesEmbedded systems, automotive, industrial control

QNX is a commercial Unix-like real-time operating system, aimed primarily at embedded systems. First released in 1982 by Canadian company Quantum Software Systems, it is built around a microkernel architecture in which most operating-system services run as separate processes communicating by message passing.

History

QNX became widely used in industrial automation, medical devices, and networking equipment because of its reliability and real-time performance. BlackBerry (then Research In Motion) acquired QNX Software Systems in 2010 and used the system as the foundation of the BlackBerry Tablet OS and BlackBerry 10.

Uses today

QNX remains a major platform in the automotive industry, supplying the software core for infotainment, digital instrument clusters, and driver-assistance systems in hundreds of millions of vehicles, as well as in rail, defence, and industrial control applications.
