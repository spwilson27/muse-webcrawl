BlackBerry 10 (BB10) is a proprietary mobile operating system for the BlackBerry line of smartphones, developed by BlackBerry Limited. It is based on the QNX operating system, which the company (then Research In Motion) had acquired in 2010. BlackBerry 10 was launched on 30–31 January 2013 together with the touchscreen BlackBerry Z10 and the keyboard-equipped Q10.

Features

The system used a gesture-based interface built around the BlackBerry Hub, a unified inbox aggregating email, texts, calls, and social media messages, accessible by a swipe from anywhere. Its multitasking showed running apps as "Active Frames" on the home screen, and the touch keyboard was praised for predictive text that suggested words above keys, dismissible with an upward flick. BB10 supported a runtime for Android applications, and from version 10.2.1 users could install Android APK packages directly; the BlackBerry Classic and Passport devices of 2014 continued the physical-keyboard tradition.

Reception and decline

Reviews praised the operating system's multitasking and keyboard, but the platform arrived late against iOS and Android and suffered from a sparse native app catalogue. Sales disappointed, contributing to large write-downs and the company's strategic shift toward enterprise software. BlackBerry ended development after version 10.3.3 (2016) and licensed its phone brand to third-party manufacturers producing Android devices.
