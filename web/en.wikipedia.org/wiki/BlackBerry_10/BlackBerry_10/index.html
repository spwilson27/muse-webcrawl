BlackBerry 10 (BB10) is a mobile operating system developed by BlackBerry Limited (then Research In Motion) for its line of BlackBerry smartphones. Based on the QNX Neutrino real-time operating system kernel acquired by the company in 2010, BlackBerry 10 replaced the legacy BlackBerry OS and was unveiled on January 30, 2013 alongside the BlackBerry Z10 and Q10 handsets.

Features

The interface is built around gestures and the BlackBerry Hub, a unified inbox aggregating email, text messages, social networking and notifications. Multitasking uses live "Active Frames" — reduced live tiles of open applications. The system shipped with BlackBerry Balance for separating work and personal data, the BlackBerry Assistant voice assistant (from 10.3), and an Android runtime that allowed many Android applications to run, later including access to the Amazon Appstore.

Devices and reception

Devices included the touchscreen Z10 and Z30, the keyboard-equipped Q10, Q5, Classic and Passport, and the Porsche Design models. Although praised for its multitasking and Hub, the platform launched with a much smaller app ecosystem than iOS and Android, and BlackBerry's smartphone market share continued to fall. In 2015 the company released its first Android phone, the Priv, and in 2016 it exited in-house hardware production. BlackBerry 10 devices lost access to legacy BlackBerry services on January 4, 2022.
