Providence Park

Providence Park is an outdoor soccer venue in Portland, Oregon, United States. It is the home of the Portland Timbers of Major League Soccer and the Portland Thorns of the National Women's Soccer League. The stadium is located in the Goose Hollow neighborhood, just west of downtown Portland, and is one of the oldest soccer-specific stadiums in the United States, with roots as an athletic field dating to the 1890s.

History

The site was first used for athletics in the 1890s by the Multnomah Athletic Club. A stadium was constructed in 1926 and known for decades as Multnomah Stadium, later Civic Stadium. It hosted a wide variety of events, including college football (notably games of the University of Oregon and Oregon State University), minor league baseball, greyhound racing, and concerts.

In 2001 the venue was renovated and renamed PGE Park. In 2010–2011 it was converted into a soccer-specific stadium for the Portland Timbers' entry into Major League Soccer, and briefly carried the name Jeld-Wen Field. In 2014, Providence Health & Services acquired the naming rights, giving the stadium its current name.

Expansion

A major expansion completed in 2019 added roughly 4,000 seats on the east side, raising capacity to 25,218 and adding a distinctive four-tier stand with a new facade along SW 18th Avenue. The design was intended to echo classic urban stadiums while preserving the venue's historic character.

Atmosphere

Providence Park is noted for the Timbers Army, the Timbers' supporters' group, whose tifo displays and continuous chanting occupy the north end of the stadium. The Thorns regularly draw some of the largest crowds in women's club soccer worldwide.
