In organic chemistry, an acetyl group is a functional group with the chemical formula CH3–C(=O)– and the structure of an acyl group derived from acetic acid. It is sometimes represented by the symbol Ac (not to be confused with the element actinium). An acetyl group contains a methyl group single-bonded to a carbonyl carbon.

Acetylation

The introduction of an acetyl group into a molecule is called acetylation (or acetylating). In biology, acetyl groups are transferred by the cofactor acetyl-CoA. Acetyltransferase enzymes attach acetyl groups to lysine residues of histones and other proteins, a modification that can alter gene expression and protein function. In synthesis, common acetylating agents include acetyl chloride and acetic anhydride.

Compounds containing acetyl groups

  • Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid)
  • Acetylcholine, a neurotransmitter
  • Acetylcysteine, a drug and supplement
  • Heroin (diamorphine), the diacetyl ester of morphine
  • Acetyl-CoA, the central metabolic carrier

Pharmacology

Many drugs contain acetyl groups, and acetylation in the liver is a major route of drug metabolism. Individuals are sometimes described as "slow" or "fast" acetylators of drugs such as isoniazid, owing to inherited differences in N-acetyltransferase activity.
