Chechnya, officially the Chechen Republic, is a republic of Russia located in the North Caucasus, in the northern foothills of the Greater Caucasus range. Its capital is Grozny. The republic has a population of around 1.5 million, predominantly ethnic Chechens, a Nakh people whose language is Chechen.

History

The Chechens resisted Russian conquest through the Caucasian War of the nineteenth century under leaders such as Imam Shamil. In 1944 the entire Chechen population was deported to Central Asia by Stalin's regime on charges of collaboration; they were permitted to return only after 1957, when the Chechen-Ingush ASSR was restored. In 1991, as the Soviet Union dissolved, the nationalist leader Dzhokhar Dudayev declared independence as the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria. Two devastating wars followed — the First Chechen War (1994–1996), which ended with de facto independence, and the Second Chechen War (1999–2009), which restored Russian control and left Grozny largely destroyed.

Modern period

Since 2007 Chechnya has been governed by Ramzan Kadyrov, whose administration has rebuilt Grozny with large federal subsidies — including the Akhmad Kadyrov Mosque, the "Heart of Chechnya" — while drawing sustained criticism from human rights organisations over enforced disappearances, persecution of LGBT people, and the cult of personality around its leadership.

Geography

The republic ranges from the steppe and the Terek River lowlands in the north to high Caucasus peaks in the south, where it borders Georgia. The Argun and Sunzha rivers drain the central plains.
