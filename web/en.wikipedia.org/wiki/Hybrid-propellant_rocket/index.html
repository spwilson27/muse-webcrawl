Hybrid-propellant rocket

A hybrid-propellant rocket is a rocket with a rocket motor that uses rocket propellants in two different phases: one solid and the other either gas or liquid. The hybrid concept can be traced back to the early 1930s. Hybrid rockets avoid some of the disadvantages of solid rockets like the dangers of propellant handling, while also avoiding some disadvantages of liquid rockets like their mechanical complexity. Because it is often hard for the different propellants to be intimately mixed, hybrid rockets tend to fail somewhere in the middle between solids and liquids in performance.

History

Early work on hybrid rockets was carried out in the 1930s by groups in the Soviet Union and Germany, and by researchers such as Leonid Andrussow. In the United States, the Pacific Rocket Society experimented with hybrids in the late 1940s. The concept attracted renewed interest in the 1960s, including work by United Technologies and tests of a hybrid sounding rocket called HYSR. The best-known application is the motor of SpaceShipOne, the first privately developed crewed spacecraft, which used hydroxyl-terminated polybutadiene (HTPB) fuel and nitrous oxide oxidizer and won the Ansari X Prize in 2004.

Design

In a typical hybrid motor, the solid fuel grain lines the combustion chamber and contains one or more ports along its length. The liquid or gaseous oxidizer is injected at the head end and flows along the port, where it mixes with fuel vapour produced at the burning surface. Because the fuel regression rate is generally low, multi-port grains or liquefying fuels such as paraffin wax are used to increase the fuel flow.

Advantages and disadvantages

Hybrids are generally safer than solid rockets because the fuel grain is inert without oxidizer, cracks in the grain are not catastrophic, and the motor can be throttled, shut down and restarted by controlling the oxidizer valve. Thrust can therefore be regulated more like a liquid engine. On the other hand, the specific impulse is usually below that of the best liquid bipropellant engines, the fuel regression rate limits thrust density, and combustion efficiency can be lower because mixing depends on the boundary layer along the port.

Applications

Hybrid motors have been flown on sounding rockets, target vehicles and experimental aircraft, and have been studied for small launch vehicles and lunar landers. SpaceShipTwo uses a hybrid motor, and companies such as Virgin Galactic, Firehawk Aerospace and HyImpulse have pursued hybrid propulsion for crewed flight and small-satellite launch.
