Yahoo! Search

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Screenshot-style image of the Yahoo! Search results page with its purple branding]

Yahoo! Search is a search engine owned by Yahoo! Inc. For much of the web's history it was one of the top search properties — Yahoo! began in 1994 as a human-edited directory of websites ('Jerry and David's Guide to the World Wide Web', by Jerry Yang and David Filo) and only gradually became a search engine, at first licensing results from others (AltaVista, Inktomi, then Google itself from 2000 to 2004).

Yahoo! built its own crawler-based engine in 2003–2004 (acquiring Inktomi and Overture, hence AltaVista and AlltheWeb) and was for several years the world's number-two search engine. But it never matched Google's results quality or advertising economics; in 2009 Yahoo! exited the technology race, signing a ten-year deal for Microsoft's Bing to power its results — an admission that the search war was over, from the company that had once been the front door of the web.

Yahoo! Search persists as a Bing-powered service within the shrunken Yahoo! portal. Its arc — directory, portal, search also-ran — is the canonical story of the web's first era, when guiding humans to websites was the industry's centre and owning the guide seemed like owning the future.
