Ambulocetus (Latin ambulare "to walk" + cetus "whale") is a genus of early amphibious cetacean from the Early Eocene of Pakistan. Its only known species, Ambulocetus natans ("the walking, swimming whale"), is a celebrated transitional fossil in the evolution of whales from land-dwelling mammals.

Discovery

The fossil was recovered in 1992 from the Kuldana Formation in Punjab, Pakistan, by a team including Hans Thewissen, and formally described in 1994. The specimen preserved a nearly complete skeleton, remarkable for its powerful limbs.

Description and lifestyle

Ambulocetus was about 3 metres long and weighed an estimated 180–200 kg. It had short limbs with large, hooved feet, a long tail, and a robust, crocodile-like build. It could walk on land, probably clumsily like a sea lion, and swam by undulating its body and hind limbs like an otter. Its ears show early specialisation for underwater hearing. Isotopic evidence suggests it drank fresh water and hunted in rivers and nearshore waters, ambushing prey much as a crocodile does.

Significance

Together with Pakicetus and later forms such as Rodhocetus, Ambulocetus documents the stepwise transition from terrestrial artiodactyl ancestors to fully aquatic whales, one of the best-supported macroevolutionary sequences in the fossil record.
