Yukata

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A yukata (浴衣, lit. "bathing cloth") is a Japanese garment, a casual summer version of the kimono. It is a robe usually made of cotton or synthetic fabric, wrapped around the body and fastened with a sash called an obi. The name derives from its origin as a garment worn after bathing at hot springs (onsen) and bathhouses.

Description and use

Unlike the formal kimono, the yukata is unlined, lightweight, and worn without the layered undergarments and tabi socks of formal dress; it is typically worn with wooden geta sandals. The left side of the garment is always wrapped over the right (the reverse is reserved for dressing the dead). Women wear yukata with bright floral or geometric patterns and a decorative obi tied in elaborate bows, while men's designs are usually darker and simpler.

Yukata are standard attire at Japanese inns (ryokan), where guests wear them in the room, at dinner, and around the resort town, and they are widely worn to summer festivals and fireworks displays. Towns such as Kinosaki Onsen encourage visitors to stroll the streets in yukata provided by their lodging.
