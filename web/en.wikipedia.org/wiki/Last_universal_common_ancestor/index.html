Last universal common ancestor

The last universal common ancestor (LUCA) is the hypothetical common ancestral cell population from which all subsequent life on Earth descends, including all bacteria, archaea, and eukaryotes. LUCA is not thought to be the first life on Earth, but rather the most recent organism (or population) from which all currently living organisms descend; earlier lineages and their descendants may have existed but left no surviving descendants. Most estimates place LUCA as having lived roughly 3.5 to 4.3 billion years ago, during the late Hadean or early Archean eons.

A phylogenetic tree of life, rooted at LUCA, showing the three domains: Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya.

Characteristics

By comparing the genomes of modern organisms, scientists infer that LUCA already possessed a considerable degree of biological complexity: it used DNA as its genetic material, transcribed it into RNA, and translated RNA into proteins using ribosomes and the genetic code essentially as it exists today. Reconstructions suggest LUCA had several hundred genes, including those for a basic metabolism, cell membrane maintenance, and DNA replication and repair. Studies have proposed that LUCA was an anaerobic, thermophilic (heat-loving) organism that fixed carbon dioxide using hydrogen, possibly living near hydrothermal vents. A widely reported 2016 study identified 355 protein families likely present in LUCA and portrayed it as an anaerobic autotroph dependent on hydrogen gas in a geochemically active, vent-like environment.

Evidence

The strongest evidence for a single common ancestor is the universality of the genetic code and of core molecular machinery — such as the ribosome, ATP synthase, and many metabolic pathways — across all known life. Formal statistical tests comparing the hypothesis of universal common ancestry against multiple independent origins have found overwhelming support for a single origin of extant life (while not ruling out extinct independent lineages).

Misconceptions

LUCA is sometimes misunderstood as the very first organism, as a single isolated cell, or as a simple "primitive" organism. In reality, LUCA was likely a population of cells exchanging genes horizontally, and it was the product of a long prior history of evolution since the origin of life (abiogenesis). LUCA also should not be confused with the last common ancestors of particular groups, such as the last eukaryotic common ancestor (LECA) or the most recent common ancestor of humans and chimpanzees.
