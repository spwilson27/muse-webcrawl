Reviews of Modern Physics
Discipline: Physics
Language: English
First published: 1929
Publisher: American Physical Society
Abbreviation: Rev. Mod. Phys.

Reviews of Modern Physics (often abbreviated RMP) is a quarterly peer-reviewed scientific journal published by the American Physical Society. Founded in 1929, it is among the oldest and most prestigious journals in physics. Unlike journals that publish original research, Reviews of Modern Physics publishes lengthy, pedagogical review articles that survey the state of a research area for non-specialists as well as experts.

Scope and significance

The journal covers all branches of physics, including atomic, molecular and optical physics, condensed matter, particle physics, nuclear physics, gravitation and cosmology, and statistical physics. Many of its articles become standard references: famous examples include Bethe and Salpeter's work on relativistic bound states, and numerous Nobel-lecture-style reviews. Because review articles attract heavy citation, the journal consistently has one of the highest impact factors of any physics journal.

Colloquia and Nobel lectures

In addition to full reviews, the journal publishes shorter "Colloquia" articles describing recent developments of interest to a broad audience. For many years it also served as a venue for publishing Nobel Prize lectures in physics.

History

The journal began in 1929 as a companion to the Physical Review, originally intended to provide reviews supplementing the growing research literature. It has been published continuously since, becoming the American Physical Society's flagship review journal.
