The Lebel Model 1886 rifle (French: Fusil Mle 1886 M93, after its 1893 revision) is an 8 mm bolt-action infantry rifle that entered service in the French Army in 1887. It is a repeating rifle that can hold eight rounds in its forestock tube magazine, plus one in the elevator and one in the chamber, for a total of ten.

Significance

The Lebel was revolutionary as the first military rifle designed to use smokeless powder ammunition, the Poudre B developed by Paul Vieille. Its 8×50mmR Lebel cartridge, designed by Nicolas Lebel's commission, was the first smokeless-powder military cartridge and made all existing black-powder rifles obsolete overnight, triggering a worldwide arms race in rifle and propellant design.

Design and service

The rifle used a bolt design derived from the Gras and Kropatschek systems, with a distinctive tubular magazine under the barrel. It was manufactured at the Châtellerault, Saint-Étienne, and Tulle arsenals; about 3.45 million were produced. The Lebel served as the standard French infantry rifle through the First World War and remained in service, increasingly supplemented by the Berthier rifles and the MAS-36, into the Second World War. Shortcomings included the slow-loading tube magazine, the rimmed tapered cartridge, and the lack of a safety catch.
