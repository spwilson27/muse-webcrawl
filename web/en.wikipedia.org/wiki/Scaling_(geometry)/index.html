Scaling (geometry)

In Euclidean geometry, scaling (also called a dilation or homothety) is an affine transformation that enlarges or reduces objects by a scale factor that is the same in all directions; this is called uniform scaling, and its result is similar (in the geometric sense) to the original. Scaling in which the factors differ by direction is called non-uniform scaling.

Matrix representation

A scaling with factors sx, sy, sz along the coordinate axes is represented by the diagonal matrix diag(sx, sy, sz). In homogeneous coordinates, scaling can be combined with translation to move the centre of scaling away from the origin.

Properties

Uniform scaling preserves angles and ratios of lengths, and multiplies areas by the square and volumes by the cube of the scale factor. A factor greater than 1 enlarges, a factor between 0 and 1 shrinks, and a negative factor combines scaling with reflection.

Applications

Scaling is fundamental in computer graphics, image resizing, cartography, and in the fractal geometry of self-similar objects, where iterated scalings define Hutchinson operators.
