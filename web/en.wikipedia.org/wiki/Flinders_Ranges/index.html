Flinders Ranges

The Flinders Ranges are the largest mountain ranges in South Australia, which start about 200 km (125 mi) north of Adelaide. The discontinuous ranges stretch for over 430 km (265 mi) from Port Pirie to Lake Callabonna.

Geology and landscape

The ranges were formed by folding and uplift of ancient sedimentary rocks of the Adelaide Geosyncline, including quartzites and sandstones, some dating back over 800 million years. Notable features include Wilpena Pound, a large natural amphitheatre, and the gorges of Brachina and Bunyeroo. The region contains important Ediacaran fossil localities in the Ediacara Hills.

History and culture

The Adnyamathanha people are the traditional owners of the Flinders Ranges region. European exploration followed Matthew Flinders' 1802 voyage, after whom the ranges are named. The area saw copper mining, pastoral settlement, and the short-lived wheat farming ventures of the 19th century. Ikara-Flinders Ranges National Park protects much of the central ranges.
