Spermatogenesis

Spermatogenesis is the process by which haploid spermatozoa develop from germ cells in the seminiferous tubules of the testis. It begins at puberty and continues throughout life, producing millions of sperm each day in a healthy adult male. The full process in humans takes roughly 74 days.

Stages

Spermatogenesis is usually divided into three phases:

  1. Spermatocytogenesis — diploid spermatogonia divide by mitosis, maintaining the stem-cell pool and producing primary spermatocytes.
  2. Meiosis — each primary spermatocyte divides into two secondary spermatocytes, which in turn produce four haploid spermatids.
  3. Spermiogenesis — spermatids, without further division, transform into spermatozoa: the acrosome forms, the nucleus condenses, a flagellum develops, and excess cytoplasm is shed as residual bodies.

Regulation

The process is supported by Sertoli cells, which nourish developing germ cells and form the blood–testis barrier, and is driven by testosterone from Leydig cells under the control of the pituitary hormones LH and FSH. Spermatogenesis is temperature-sensitive and proceeds most efficiently slightly below core body temperature, which is why the testes are held in the scrotum.
