Mardin

Mardin (Arabic: ماردين; Kurdish: Mêrdîn; Syriac: ܡܪܕܝܢ) is a city in southeastern Turkey and the capital of Mardin Province. It lies on a rocky hill overlooking the plains of northern Mesopotamia, near the border with Syria.

History

The site has been inhabited since antiquity, passing through Assyrian, Roman, Byzantine, and Arab hands before becoming a stronghold of the Turkmen Artuqid dynasty in the 11th–12th centuries, under whom much of its characteristic architecture was built. It later came under the Ak Koyunlu, the Safavids, and, from the early 16th century, the Ottoman Empire.

Architecture and culture

Old Mardin is famed for its honey-colored limestone houses, mosques, madrasas, and churches cascading down the hillside below its citadel. Notable monuments include the Great Mosque (Ulu Cami), the Zinciriye and Kasımiye madrasas, and the Syriac Orthodox Mor Hananyo Monastery (Deyrulzafaran) outside the city, a seat of the Syriac Orthodox Patriarchate for centuries. The population has historically been religiously and ethnically mixed—Turks, Kurds, Arabs, and Assyrian/Syriac Christians—and the old town has been proposed for UNESCO World Heritage listing.
