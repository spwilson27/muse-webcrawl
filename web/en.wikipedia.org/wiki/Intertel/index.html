Intertel is a high-IQ society founded in 1966 by Ralph Haines, a former member of Mensa, who wanted an organisation adapted to the needs of the exceptionally gifted and free of some of Mensa's internal politics and fees disputes. Membership requires a score at or above the 99th percentile (top 1%) on a standardised intelligence test — a stricter threshold than Mensa's top 2% — corresponding roughly to an IQ of 135 or above on the Wechsler scales. It is thus one step below the Triple Nine Society (top 0.1%).

Organisation and activities

Intertel has roughly 1,700 members in more than 30 countries, organised into geographic regions that hold local gatherings and annual meetings. Its journal is Integra, and members communicate through regional newsletters and online groups. The society states three purposes: to foster intellectual exchange among the highly gifted, to encourage research into high intelligence, and to provide fellowship. It has historically had a somewhat lower public profile than Mensa, with comparatively restrained publicity and certification services.

Comparison with other societies

High-IQ societies are ordered by admission percentile: Mensa (98th percentile), Intertel (99th), the Triple Nine Society (99.9th), and the much smaller and more exclusive Prometheus and Mega societies (up to 99.9999th percentile claims). Test acceptance lists — which standardised exams and minimum scores qualify — are a perennial subject of negotiation among the societies.
