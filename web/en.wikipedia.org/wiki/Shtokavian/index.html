Shtokavian

Shtokavian (Serbo-Croatian: štokavski / штокавски), also known as Štokavian, is the standard dialect of the pluricentric Serbo-Croatian language. It is the basis of the Bosnian, Croatian, Serbian, and Montenegrin standards. The name derives from the interrogative pronoun for "what" in Shtokavian, što (or šta), as opposed to Chakavian ča and Kajkavian kaj.

Dialects

Shtokavian is traditionally divided on the basis of the reflex of the Proto-Slavic vowel yat (ѣ) into Ekavian, Ijekavian, and Ikavian varieties:

  • Ekavian (yat → e): the basis of standard Serbian in Serbia proper.
  • Ijekavian (yat → ije/je): the basis of standard Croatian, Bosnian, Montenegrin, and of Serbian as spoken in Bosnia, Montenegro, and parts of Croatia.
  • Ikavian (yat → i): spoken in parts of Dalmatia, Lika, and western Herzegovina; not the basis of any standard.

A further division distinguishes older (Neo-Shtokavian and Old-Shtokavian) forms. The Neo-Shtokavian Ijekavian dialects of eastern Herzegovina were selected in the 19th century, following the work of Vuk Stefanović Karadžić and the 1850 Vienna Literary Agreement, as the common standard.

History

Old-Shtokavian texts appear from the medieval period in both Latin and Cyrillic scripts. The Neo-Shtokavian dialectal shift, involving the retraction of stress accents and vowel changes, spread between the 14th and 17th centuries, partly through population movements during the Ottoman period. In the 19th century standardisation efforts unified the literary language of Serbs and Croats on the Shtokavian base, a unity that survived in the Serbo-Croatian standard until the 1990s, after which separate Bosnian, Croatian, Montenegrin, and Serbian standards were codified — all still Shtokavian-based and mutually intelligible.
