Spinning wheel

A spinning wheel is a device for spinning thread or yarn from fibres. It replaced the hand spindle as the principal method of spinning in many parts of the world and was foundational to the textile industry before spinning was mechanised during the Industrial Revolution. The spinner draws out fibres while the wheel, driven by a treadle or hand crank, turns a spindle or bobbin that twists the fibres and winds on the finished yarn.

History

The spinning wheel was probably invented in India or China — depictions and references appear by roughly the 11th century — and spread through the Islamic world to Europe by the 13th century. Key improvements included the flyer-and-bobbin assembly (allowing continuous spinning) and the treadle. In India, the charkha became a symbol of self-reliance under Mahatma Gandhi, who promoted hand spinning of khadi cloth.

Types

Major forms include the great wheel (or walking wheel), the saxony or flax wheel, the castle wheel, and the portable charkha. Modern hobby wheels retain the same principles. Spinning wheels feature prominently in folklore, from Sleeping Beauty to Rumpelstiltskin.
