California English

California English (or Californian English) is the variety of English spoken in the U.S. state of California. As California became one of the most ethnically diverse states in the union, English speakers picked up influences from everywhere. California English has become a subject of study in sociolinguistics and dialectology.

Vowel shift

The most distinctive feature of California English is the California vowel shift, a chain shift in the vowels documented by researchers including those in the Atlas of North American English. Features include the merger of the vowels in cot and caught, fronting of back vowels such as /u/ (so that dude is pronounced with a fronted vowel), and lowering and retraction of front vowels, so that bit may sound closer to bet and bet closer to bat in some speakers.

Vocabulary

Californians characteristically refer to freeways in Southern California with the definite article — "the 405", "the 101" — a usage largely absent in Northern California, where speakers tend to say "I-80" or "Highway 101". Other regional terms include hella (Northern California slang for "very" or "a lot"), and surfer and Valley girl speech patterns popularized in media during the 20th century.

History

English arrived in California in large volume with the Gold Rush of 1849, bringing speakers from across the United States. For much of the 20th century, California speech was considered close to a "standard" Western American English, but research since the 1990s has documented the emerging vowel shift, particularly among younger speakers.
