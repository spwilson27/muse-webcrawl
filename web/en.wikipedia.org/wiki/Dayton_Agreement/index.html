Dayton Agreement

The Dayton Agreement (General Framework Agreement for Peace in Bosnia and Herzegovina), initialled at Dayton, Ohio, on 21 November 1995 and signed in Paris on 14 December 1995, ended the Bosnian War (1992–1995).


Signed: 14 December 1995, Paris
Parties: Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, FR Yugoslavia
Effect: Ended Bosnian War

Background and negotiations

After years of war, ethnic cleansing, NATO airstrikes, and shifting front lines, US-led talks at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base brought together Alija Izetbegović, Franjo Tuđman, and Slobodan Milošević under chief negotiator Richard Holbrooke. Maps dividing territory roughly 51/49 proved decisive.

Terms

Bosnia and Herzegovina remained a single state composed of two entities — the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and Republika Srpska — plus later the Brčko District, with a tripartite presidency, a constitution (Annex 4), refugee-return rights, and a NATO-led implementation force (IFOR) overseen by a High Representative.

Legacy

The agreement stopped the killing but entrenched ethnic power-sharing that many blame for Bosnia's political dysfunction; reform debates continue.
