Mini LED

Mini LED is a display backlighting technology that uses very small light-emitting diodes — typically on the order of 100–300 micrometres — as the backlight for an LCD panel. Thousands of mini LEDs grouped into hundreds or thousands of independently dimmable zones allow far more precise local dimming than conventional LED backlights, improving contrast, black levels, and peak brightness.

Applications

Mini LED backlights entered consumer products in the late 2010s, first in high-end televisions and gaming monitors, and later in laptops and tablets, notably Apple's iPad Pro (2021) and MacBook Pro models with "Liquid Retina XDR" displays. Because the front panel remains an LCD, mini LED displays avoid the burn-in risk of OLED, though they can exhibit a "blooming" halo around bright objects on dark backgrounds where a dimming zone is larger than the object.

Comparison with MicroLED

In mini LED, the LEDs serve only as a backlight behind a liquid-crystal layer. In MicroLED, far smaller LEDs form the pixels themselves, emitting light directly. Mini LED is therefore an evolutionary improvement of LCD technology rather than a new class of emissive display, and is generally cheaper to manufacture at large sizes.
