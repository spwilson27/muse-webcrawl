Catan

Designer: Klaus Teuber
Publisher: Kosmos (Germany)
Publication: 1995
Players: 3–4 (up to 6 with expansions)
Playing time: 60–120 minutes

Catan, previously known as The Settlers of Catan or simply Settlers, is a multiplayer board game designed by Klaus Teuber. It was first published in 1995 in Germany by Franckh-Kosmos Verlag (Kosmos) as Die Siedler von Catan.

Players take on the roles of settlers, attempting to build and develop holdings while trading and acquiring resources. Players gain victory points as their settlements grow and the first to reach a set number of points, typically 10, wins.

Gameplay

The game board, which represents the island of Catan, is composed of hexagonal tiles, each depicting a terrain type (fields, forests, hills, mountains and pastures) associated with a resource. On each turn, a player rolls two dice to determine which terrains produce their resources; players with settlements or cities adjacent to the producing tile collect the corresponding resource cards.

Resources — brick, lumber, ore, grain and wool — are spent to build roads, settlements and cities, and to buy development cards. Players may trade resources with each other and with the bank. A robber figure, moved when a seven is rolled, blocks production on a tile and allows the moving player to steal a resource card from an adjacent player.

Reception and legacy

The game won the Spiel des Jahres award in 1995 and is widely credited with popularising the modern "Eurogame" style of board game in the United States and beyond. It has sold tens of millions of copies worldwide, been translated into dozens of languages, and spawned numerous expansions and spin-offs, including Catan: Seafarers, Catan: Cities & Knights and Catan: Traders & Barbarians, as well as digital adaptations.
