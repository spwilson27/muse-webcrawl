Prince Fumimaro Konoe (Japanese: 近衞 文麿, sometimes Konoye, 12 October 1891 – 16 December 1945) was a Japanese politician who served as Prime Minister of Japan from 1937 to 1939 and again from 1940 to 1941. A scion of the ancient Fujiwara-descended Konoe family, he was president of the House of Peers before first becoming premier.

Premiership

Konoe's first cabinet coincided with the outbreak of the Second Sino-Japanese War in July 1937, which he failed to contain despite seeking a negotiated settlement, and which escalated into full-scale war. In his second term he presided over the signing of the Tripartite Pact with Germany and Italy (1940), the dissolution of political parties into the Imperial Rule Assistance Association, the occupation of French Indochina, and the increasingly desperate negotiations with the United States. Unable to secure terms that both Washington and the Japanese military would accept, he resigned in October 1941 and was succeeded by Hideki Tojo, two months before the attack on Pearl Harbor.

Final years

Konoe remained an imperial adviser and, in February 1945, urged the Emperor to end the war. After Japan's surrender he served briefly in the Higashikuni cabinet and worked on constitutional revision, but was named as a suspected Class-A war criminal; on the morning he was due to report to the occupation authorities, 16 December 1945, he died by suicide, taking potassium cyanide at his home.
