Forum
OriginRoman civic architecture (Latin: forum, "marketplace")
FunctionsMarket, assembly, law courts, ceremonies
Most famousRoman Forum, Rome

A forum (Latin forum, plural fora) was, in a Roman city, a rectangular public square or marketplace at the centre of civic life, surrounded by important government buildings, temples and shops. The word has since come to mean any public place of assembly, discussion or debate — including, in modern usage, online discussion forums.

Roman fora

Originally marketplaces on the model of the Greek agora, Roman fora developed into the political, commercial, judicial and religious heart of a city. The Roman Forum in Rome — the Forum Romanum — accumulated the Senate house (Curia), the Rostra speakers' platform, basilicas for law courts and business, triumphal arches and major temples, and emperors from Julius Caesar and Augustus onwards added their own Imperial fora. Every Roman colony and provincial town had its forum, typically at the junction of the main streets, the cardo and decumanus.

Other meanings

In law, "forum" denotes a court or jurisdiction, as in forum shopping. In modern political vocabulary it denotes a meeting or medium for exchanging ideas — conferences such as the World Economic Forum — and on the internet, an online forum is a discussion site where users hold threaded conversations, a usage that underpins communities from Usenet and bulletin boards to countless web communities.
