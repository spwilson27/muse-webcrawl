The Sendai Mediatheque is a multi-purpose cultural centre in Sendai, Japan, designed by architect Toyo Ito and completed in 2001. It combines a library, art gallery, audio-visual library, film studio, and café behind a fully glazed facade.

Design

Ito won the 1995 competition with a concept of fluid, aquarium-like transparency: seven floor plates are supported not by conventional columns but by thirteen lattice-like steel tubes of varying diameter, which also carry services and light — the project's structural engineer was Mutsuro Sasaki. The building redefined the public library as an open media centre.

Recognition and resilience

The mediatheque is regarded as a landmark of contemporary architecture and a career-defining work for Ito, later a Pritzker Prize laureate. It survived the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake with limited damage (including ceiling collapses that forced a period of closure and repair) and became a symbol of Sendai's recovery.
