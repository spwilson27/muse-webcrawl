Men's rights movement
Emergence1970s, United States
RelatedMen's movement, fathers' rights movement
Key concernsFamily law, child custody, education, health, conscription

The men's rights movement (MRM) is a branch of the men's movement concerned with social issues affecting men and boys, which its adherents argue involve discrimination and disadvantage. It emerged in the 1970s partly in response to, and partly in opposition to, second-wave feminism.

Issues

Areas emphasised by men's rights activists include family court outcomes and child custody, men's higher rates of suicide and workplace death, boys' educational underachievement, male reproductive rights, circumcision, military conscription, and the treatment of male victims of domestic violence and sexual assault.

Criticism

The movement is controversial. Critics, including the Southern Poverty Law Center, have characterised parts of the online men's rights milieu as misogynistic, while other commentators distinguish mainstream advocacy on the listed policy questions from extremist fringe rhetoric.
