Bartolomé de las Casas (1484–1566) was a Spanish Dominican friar, historian, and social reformer, remembered as the "Defender of the Indians" for his lifelong campaign against the abuse and enslavement of the Indigenous peoples of the Americas.

Life and advocacy

Las Casas first sailed to Hispaniola in 1502 and initially benefited from the encomienda system before renouncing it in 1514. Thereafter he repeatedly crossed the Atlantic to lobby the Spanish Crown, contributing to the New Laws of 1542, which restricted encomiendas. As bishop of Chiapas (1544) he clashed with colonists who resisted his reforms. In the famous Valladolid debate (1550–1551) he argued against Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda that Indigenous peoples were fully human, rational, and entitled to their lands and freedom.

Writings

His A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies (1552) catalogued Spanish atrocities and was translated across Europe, feeding the "Black Legend" of Spanish cruelty; his vast Historia de las Indias remains a key source for the conquest era. Early in his advocacy he controversially suggested substituting African slave labour for Indigenous labour, a position he later repented of in writing.
