Xbox 360

The Xbox 360 is a home video game console developed by Microsoft, released in November 2005 as the successor to the original Xbox and the second console of the Xbox series. It competed with Sony's PlayStation 3 and Nintendo's Wii as part of the seventh generation of consoles. Over 84 million units were sold worldwide, making it Microsoft's best-selling console until it was surpassed by the Nintendo Switch-era market and its own successors; production ended in 2016.

Hardware and services

The console was built around a triple-core IBM PowerPC "Xenon" CPU and an ATI "Xenos" GPU, with 512 MB of unified memory. Its online service, Xbox Live, expanded subscriptions, digital downloads, achievements and media apps, while the Kinect motion sensor (2010) became one of the fastest-selling consumer devices of its time. Early models were notorious for the "Red Ring of Death" general hardware failure, which led to a large warranty extension programme costing Microsoft over $1 billion. Redesigned models — the slimmer Xbox 360 S (2010) and Xbox 360 E (2013) — improved reliability.

Software

Key titles include Halo 3, Gears of War, Grand Theft Auto IV and The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim, and the platform popularised downloadable indie games through Xbox Live Arcade. The Xbox 360 store was closed in July 2024, although purchased games remain playable and backward compatibility carries many titles to Xbox One and Series X/S.
