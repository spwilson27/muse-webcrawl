Flemish people

The Flemish people (Dutch: Vlamingen) are a Germanic ethnic group native to Flanders in northern Belgium. They speak Dutch (historically Flemish dialects) and number roughly 6–7 million in Belgium, forming the majority of the country’s population, with diaspora communities worldwide.

History

Flanders grew wealthy in the Middle Ages through cloth trade centred on Bruges, Ghent and Ypres. The region passed through Burgundian, Spanish and Austrian Habsburg rule, and after Belgian independence in 1830 French dominated public life despite the Flemish majority. The Flemish Movement campaigned for Dutch language rights, culminating in Belgium’s federalisation and a Flemish Region and Community with its own parliament.

Culture

Flemish art produced masters such as Jan van Eyck, Peter Paul Rubens and Pieter Bruegel the Elder. Cycling — especially the Tour of Flanders — beer, fries and lace are enduring cultural symbols. The Flemish lion flag derives from the County of Flanders.
