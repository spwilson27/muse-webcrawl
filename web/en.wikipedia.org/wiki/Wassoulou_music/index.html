Wassoulou music

Wassoulou music is a genre of West African popular music from the Wassoulou region, which spans southwestern Mali, northwestern Ivory Coast, and northeastern Guinea. It is named after the region and performed mainly by women, with songs addressing women's experiences, relationships, marriage, fertility, and social commentary.

Instrumentation

Wassoulou music is built around traditional instruments including the kamele n'goni (a youthful variant of the hunter's harp-lute, the donso n'goni), the soku (a single-string fiddle), the karinyan (a metal scraper), and djembe and other percussion. Modern recordings add guitars, keyboards, and drum machines, but the pentatonic melodies and the rolling n'goni patterns remain the core of the sound.

Artists

The genre gained international attention through singers such as Oumou Sangaré, whose 1989 album Moussolou ("Women") was a landmark hit across West Africa and beyond. Other notable Wassoulou performers include Coumba Sidibé, Kagbe Sidibe, Dieneba Diakité, and Sali Sidibe. Many Wassoulou divas began as jelis (griots) or in regional orchestras before solo careers.

Themes

Wassoulou songs frequently challenge social arrangements affecting women, including polygamy and forced marriage, while also celebrating dance, love, and community identity; the music's popularity at weddings and naming ceremonies makes it both entertainment and a vehicle for public discussion of women's issues.
