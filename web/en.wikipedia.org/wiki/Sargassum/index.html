DomainEukaryota
ClassPhaeophyceae (brown algae)
OrderFucales
GenusSargassum

Sargassum is a genus of brown macroalgae (seaweed) in the order Fucales, comprising several hundred species. Many species have a bushy form with small berry-like air bladders (pneumatocysts) that keep the plant afloat. Two species, S. natans and S. fluitans, are entirely pelagic, spending their whole lives floating on the open ocean.

Ecology

Floating rafts of Sargassum form a unique ecosystem in the Sargasso Sea of the North Atlantic, supporting juvenile fish, sea turtles, crabs, shrimp, and endemic species such as the sargassum fish. The mats serve as a crucial nursery habitat and feeding ground in otherwise nutrient-poor waters.

Great Atlantic Sargassum Belt

Since 2011, massive blooms of pelagic Sargassum have repeatedly formed a "Great Atlantic Sargassum Belt" stretching from West Africa to the Caribbean and Gulf of Mexico. When the weed washes ashore in huge quantities it smothers beaches, harms tourism, and, as it decomposes, releases hydrogen sulfide. The blooms are linked to nutrient inputs from sources including the Amazon River and upwelling off Africa.

Uses

Some attached species are eaten in East Asian cuisines, and Sargassum is used as fertilizer and as a source of alginates and other compounds.
