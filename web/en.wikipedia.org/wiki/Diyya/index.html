Diyya

Diyya (Arabic: دية; plural diyyāt, ديات) in Islamic law is the financial compensation paid to the heirs of a victim in the cases of murder, bodily harm, or damage to property. It is an alternative to qisas (retaliatory punishment) in common law jurisdictions and is sometimes translated as "blood money", though the term carries different connotations in different legal traditions.

Basis

The institution of diyya predates Islam in Arabian tribal custom and was retained and regulated in the Quran and hadith. In cases of unintentional killing, the Quran prescribes freeing a slave and paying diyya to the victim's family. Classical jurists fixed the full diyya for a free Muslim man at 100 camels, or its equivalent in gold (1,000 dinars), silver (10,000–12,000 dirhams), or cattle.

Modern application

Diyya remains part of the legal systems of several countries, including Saudi Arabia, Iran, Sudan, and parts of Pakistan, where courts set tariff amounts and disputes may be settled by payment accepted by the victim's heirs (wali al-dam), who in murder cases may choose between qisas, diyya, or pardon.
