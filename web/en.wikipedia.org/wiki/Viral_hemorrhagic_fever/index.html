Viral hemorrhagic fever

Viral hemorrhagic fevers (VHFs) are a diverse group of animal and human illnesses caused by several distinct families of RNA viruses. In general, the term "viral hemorrhagic fever" is used to describe a severe multisystem syndrome in which the overall vascular system is damaged and the body's ability to regulate itself is impaired; bleeding (hemorrhage) may occur, though it is rarely itself life-threatening.

Causative viruses

Five families of RNA viruses are recognised as causes: Arenaviridae (Lassa fever, Junín, Machupo), Filoviridae (Ebola and Marburg), Bunyavirales (Crimean–Congo hemorrhagic fever, hantaviruses, Rift Valley fever), Flaviviridae (yellow fever, dengue, Omsk hemorrhagic fever), and Togaviridae (chikungunya, which only rarely presents hemorrhagically). Most are zoonoses maintained in animal or arthropod reservoirs, and several are classified as high-priority bioterrorism and pandemic threats.

Signs and symptoms

Illness typically begins abruptly with fever, fatigue, myalgia, dizziness, and exhaustion, progressing in severe cases to bleeding under the skin and from body orifices, shock, nervous-system malfunction, coma, delirium, seizures, and multi-organ failure. Case fatality rates vary widely, from under 1% for some hantavirus infections to up to 90% in Ebola outbreaks.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention relies on avoiding contact with reservoirs and vectors, barrier nursing, and safe burial practices; vaccines exist for yellow fever and (since 2019) Ebola virus disease. Treatment is chiefly supportive, with ribavirin effective against some arenaviruses and monoclonal antibodies now available for Ebola.
