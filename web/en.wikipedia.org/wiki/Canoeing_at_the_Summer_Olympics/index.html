Canoeing at the Summer Olympics

Canoeing has been featured at the Summer Olympic Games since the 1936 Games in Berlin. It comprises two disciplines governed by the International Canoe Federation: canoe slalom (formerly canoe/kayak slalom), raced on whitewater courses, and canoe sprint (formerly flatwater racing), raced on calm water over straight courses.

History

Canoeing appeared as a demonstration sport at the 1924 Paris Olympics and became an official sport in 1936 with sprint events for men. Women's sprint kayak events were added in 1948. Slalom was introduced at the 1972 Munich Games, dropped, and returned permanently at Barcelona in 1992. A further discipline, kayak cross (formerly extreme slalom), made its debut at Paris 2024.

Events

Sprint races are held in single-bladed canoes (C) and double-bladed kayaks (K), for singles, doubles and fours, over 200, 500 and 1000 metres. Slalom competitors negotiate a course of hanging gates on rapids in kayaks or canoes, incurring time penalties for touching or missing gates.

Leading nations

Germany (including the former East and West Germany) and the Soviet Union have historically dominated the medal table, with Hungary especially strong in sprint racing. Notable athletes include Birgit Fischer of Germany, the most decorated Olympic canoeist with eight gold medals, and Lisa Carrington of New Zealand, a multiple sprint champion. In slalom, Slovakia's Michal Martikán and France's Tony Estanguet are among the most successful competitors.
