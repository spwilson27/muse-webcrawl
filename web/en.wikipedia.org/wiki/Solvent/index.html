Solvent

A solvent is a substance that dissolves a solute, resulting in a solution. A solvent is usually a liquid but can also be a solid, a gas, or a supercritical fluid. The solvent is the component of a solution present in the greatest amount; water is the most widely used solvent and is sometimes called the "universal solvent" because it dissolves more substances than any other liquid.

Classification

Solvents are commonly classified by polarity. Polar protic solvents (such as water, ethanol, and acetic acid) have O–H or N–H bonds and can donate hydrogen bonds; polar aprotic solvents (such as acetone, dimethyl sulfoxide, and acetonitrile) have large dipole moments but do not donate hydrogen bonds; nonpolar solvents (such as hexane, benzene, and diethyl ether) dissolve mainly nonpolar compounds. The rule of thumb "like dissolves like" summarises these tendencies. Dielectric constant and dipole moment are quantitative measures of polarity.

Uses

Solvents are used in paints and coatings, adhesives, inks, dry cleaning (tetrachloroethylene), nail polish removers (acetone, ethyl acetate), pharmaceuticals, chemical synthesis, and extractions. Solvent choice strongly influences reaction rates and mechanisms in chemistry — for example, polar aprotic solvents greatly accelerate SN2 reactions by solvating cations while leaving nucleophiles comparatively free.

Health and environment

Many organic solvents are flammable and volatile; chronic exposure to some, such as benzene and carbon tetrachloride, is toxic or carcinogenic. Solvent emissions contribute to air pollution, and industry has increasingly adopted greener solvents, solvent-free processes, and supercritical carbon dioxide to reduce hazards.
