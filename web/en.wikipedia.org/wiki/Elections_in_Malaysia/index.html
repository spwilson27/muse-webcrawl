Elections in Malaysia

LegislatureDewan Rakyat (222 seats)
SystemFirst-past-the-post in single-member constituencies
Election authorityElection Commission of Malaysia (SPR)
Voting age18 (since 2021, with automatic registration)

Elections in Malaysia are held at the federal level for the Dewan Rakyat, the lower house of Parliament, and at the state level for the State Legislative Assemblies. General elections must be held at least every five years, though the prime minister may advise the King to dissolve Parliament earlier.

System

Malaysia uses first-past-the-post voting in single-member constituencies. The head of government is the leader who commands the confidence of the Dewan Rakyat. Constituency boundaries are drawn by the Election Commission and approved by Parliament; their delineation has often been politically contentious because of large variations in electorate size between urban and rural seats.

History

The first federal elections were held in 1955, before independence from Britain in 1957. The Alliance, and later the Barisan Nasional coalition, won every general election from independence until 2018, when the Pakatan Harapan coalition under Mahathir Mohamad achieved the first change of government in Malaysian history. The 2022 general election produced a hung parliament followed by a unity government.

Reforms

A constitutional amendment passed in 2019 lowered the voting age from 21 to 18 and introduced automatic voter registration, implemented for the 2022 election and greatly expanding the electorate.
