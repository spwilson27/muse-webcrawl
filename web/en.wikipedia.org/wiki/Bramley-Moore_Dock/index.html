Bramley-Moore Dock

Bramley-Moore Dock is a dock on the River Mersey in Vauxhall, Liverpool, England, forming part of the historic Port of Liverpool. It was designed by the dock engineer Jesse Hartley and opened in 1848. The dock is notable in the 21st century as the site of Everton Stadium, the new home ground of Everton Football Club, which opened in 2025.

History

Bramley-Moore Dock was constructed in the 1840s as part of the great northward expansion of Liverpool's docks under Jesse Hartley, the Dock Board's engineer. It was named after John Bramley-Moore, chairman of the Mersey Docks committee and a former Mayor of Liverpool. The dock handled cargo including imports associated with the Brazilian trade, which Bramley-Moore had championed. Like other Hartley docks, it featured massive granite and sandstone masonry and fireproof warehouse construction.

During the later 20th century the dock's commercial importance declined with containerisation and the migration of shipping to Seaforth. The dock and its hydraulic tower became part of the Liverpool Maritime Mercantile City World Heritage Site, inscribed by UNESCO in 2004.

Everton Stadium

In 2017 Everton F.C. selected Bramley-Moore Dock as the site for a new stadium to replace Goodison Park. Planning approval was granted in 2021. Because the stadium required infilling the dock basin, the proposal contributed to UNESCO's decision in 2021 to remove Liverpool from the World Heritage List. The dock was filled with sand dredged from the Irish Sea, and the stadium — designed by architect Dan Meis's MEIS studio with Pattern Design — was built with a capacity of around 52,888. Everton played their first competitive matches at the ground, officially named Hill Dickinson Stadium under a naming-rights agreement, at the start of the 2025–26 season. Heritage features including the dock's hydraulic tower and railway lines were restored and incorporated into the stadium plaza.
