Tay–Sachs disease

Tay–Sachs disease is a rare autosomal recessive genetic disorder that causes progressive destruction of nerve cells in the brain and spinal cord. It results from mutations in the HEXA gene, which lead to deficiency of the enzyme hexosaminidase A; the resulting accumulation of GM2 ganglioside in neurons is toxic.

InheritanceAutosomal recessive
GeneHEXA (chromosome 15)
EnzymeHexosaminidase A
Named afterWarren Tay; Bernard Sachs

Forms and symptoms

The classic infantile form appears around six months of age with developmental regression, an exaggerated startle response, a cherry-red spot on the retina, seizures and loss of motor skills; death usually occurs in early childhood. Juvenile and late-onset forms progress more slowly. The disease historically occurred at much higher frequency among Ashkenazi Jews, making it a prominent focus of carrier screening programs, which have greatly reduced its incidence in that population.
