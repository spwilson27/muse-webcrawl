Christen Raunkiær

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christen Christensen Raunkiær (29 March 1860 – 11 March 1938) was a Danish botanist, who was a pioneer of plant ecology. He is mainly remembered for his system of classifying plants by their life forms, based on the position of the perennating (survival) buds during the unfavorable season.

Life and career

Raunkiær was born in Jutland and studied botany at the University of Copenhagen, where he later became professor of botany (1911–1923) and director of the Botanical Garden. He carried out field research in Denmark, the Danish West Indies and around the Mediterranean.

Life-form system

In works published from 1904 onward, Raunkiær classified plants into life forms: phanerophytes (buds high on trees and shrubs), chamaephytes (buds near the ground), hemicryptophytes (buds at the soil surface), cryptophytes (buds below ground or under water) and therophytes (annuals surviving as seeds). By comparing the proportions of these forms in a flora with a global "normal spectrum", botanists could characterize the climate of a region — a foundational technique of ecological biogeography.

Other work

Raunkiær also contributed to studies of plant dispersal, the flora of Denmark, and quantitative methods for sampling vegetation, including the widely used frequency method that bears his name.
