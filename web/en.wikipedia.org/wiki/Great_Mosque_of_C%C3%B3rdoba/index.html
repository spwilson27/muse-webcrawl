The Mosque–Cathedral of Córdoba (Spanish: Mezquita-Catedral de Córdoba), officially known by its ecclesiastical name of Our Lady of the Assumption, is the cathedral of the Roman Catholic Diocese of Córdoba in Andalusia, Spain. Originally built as the Great Mosque of Córdoba beginning in 785 under Abd al-Rahman I, it was one of the largest mosques in the Islamic world and is regarded as a masterpiece of Moorish architecture.

Architecture

The building is famous for its vast hypostyle hall of some 856 columns of jasper, onyx, marble and granite, supporting double arches of red brick and white stone that allow an unusually high ceiling. Successive caliphs expanded the mosque; al-Hakam II added the ornate mihrab and maqsurah in the 10th century.

History

After the Christian conquest of Córdoba in 1236, the mosque was consecrated as a cathedral. In the 16th century a Renaissance cathedral nave was inserted into the centre of the prayer hall — a change Charles V is said to have regretted on seeing the result. The building was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984 as part of the Historic Centre of Córdoba.
