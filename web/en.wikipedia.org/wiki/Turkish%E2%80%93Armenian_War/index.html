Turkish–Armenian War

Date24 September – 2 December 1920
LocationTranscaucasia and eastern Anatolia
ResultTurkish victory; Treaty of Alexandropol
CombatantsFirst Republic of Armenia vs. Turkish National Movement

The Turkish–Armenian War, known in Turkey as the Eastern Operation or Eastern Front of the Turkish War of Independence, was a conflict fought in the autumn of 1920 between the First Republic of Armenia and the Turkish nationalists of the Grand National Assembly of Turkey.

Course of the war

The war followed the Allied-imposed Treaty of Sèvres, which granted Armenia a large territory in eastern Anatolia. Turkish forces under Kâzım Karabekir advanced into Armenian-controlled territory in September 1920, capturing Sarikamish, Kars, and Alexandropol (Gyumri). Exhausted, isolated, and facing a simultaneous Soviet advance, the Armenian government was compelled to negotiate.

Treaty of Alexandropol

The Treaty of Alexandropol, signed on the night of 2–3 December 1920, ended the war. Armenia renounced its claims under Sèvres, ceded roughly half its territory including Kars and the Mount Ararat region, and accepted a drastically reduced army. The treaty was never ratified by a legitimate Armenian government because Armenia was Sovietised at the same moment; the subsequent Treaty of Kars (1921) between Turkey and the Soviet republics confirmed the new frontier.
