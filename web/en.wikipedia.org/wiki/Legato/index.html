Legato

[Image: A legato slur in notation]

In music performance and notation, legato (Italian for "tied together"; French lié; German gebunden) indicates that musical notes are played or sung smoothly and connected. That is, the player transitions from note to note with no intervening silence. Legato, like staccato, is a kind of articulation; there are intermediate articulations that are sometimes called mezzo-legato or legato ma non troppo.

Notation

Legato is typically indicated with either the word legato or by a slur (a curved line) above or below the notes. In some cases, particularly in vocal music, a slur may indicate a melisma — several notes sung to one syllable — rather than an articulation. Slurs and phrase marks look similar but phrase marks indicate musical phrasing rather than a strictly legato connection.

Technique

For string players, legato is achieved in a single bow stroke; wind players use continuous breath with tonguing suppressed between slurred notes, and singers connect notes on a single stream of air. Guitarists achieve legato with hammer-ons and pull-offs, allowing runs to be played without picking each note. On the piano, where each note decays after being struck, legato is simulated by slightly overlapping successive notes and by careful pedalling. Legato technique is foundational in classical singing and instrumental pedagogy, producing a smooth, singing line, in contrast to the detached articulation of staccato.
