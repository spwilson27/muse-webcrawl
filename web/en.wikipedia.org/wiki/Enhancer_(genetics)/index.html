Enhancer (genetics)

In genetics, an enhancer is a short (50–1500 bp) region of DNA that can be bound by proteins (activators) to increase the likelihood that transcription of a particular gene will occur. These proteins are usually referred to as transcription factors. Enhancers are cis-acting; they can be located up to 1 Mbp away from the gene, upstream or downstream, and even within introns of other genes.

Discovery

The first enhancer was discovered in 1981 in the genome of the simian virus SV40. Shortly afterwards the first cellular enhancer was found in the immunoglobulin heavy chain gene, establishing that eukaryotic genomes use distant regulatory elements.

Mechanism

Enhancer DNA loops through space so bound activators contact the Mediator complex and RNA polymerase II machinery at a promoter. Chromatin marks such as H3K27ac and H3K4me1 distinguish active and poised enhancers. Clusters of enhancers, called super-enhancers, drive genes defining cell identity. Insulator elements and topologically associating domains (TADs) constrain which promoters an enhancer can reach.

Development and disease

Enhancers provide the tissue-specific and stage-specific control that makes development possible; mutations in enhancers — most of the genome-wide association hits for common disease lie in non-coding regulatory DNA — can misregulate genes. The famous Sonic hedgehog limb enhancer ZRS, a megabase away from its gene, causes polydactyly when mutated.
