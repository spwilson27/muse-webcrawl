Atmosphere (unit)

The standard atmosphere (symbol: atm) is a unit of pressure defined as exactly 101,325 pascals. It corresponds approximately to the mean air pressure at sea level on Earth and was originally defined by the pressure exerted by a 760 mm column of mercury at 0 °C — hence the equivalence 1 atm = 760 torr = 760 mmHg.

History and definition

The unit arose from barometric measurement: Evangelista Torricelli's mercury barometer (1643) showed that air pressure supports a column of mercury about 760 mm high. In 1954 the 10th General Conference on Weights and Measures adopted the standard atmosphere as exactly 1,013,250 dynes per square centimetre, i.e. 101,325 Pa in SI units. Although not an SI unit, the atmosphere remains in wide use — in chemistry (where gas data are often tabulated at 1 atm), diving, aviation and engineering. For reference, the closely related technical atmosphere (at) equals exactly 1 kgf/cm² = 98,066.5 Pa and differs by about 3%.

Since 1982, IUPAC has recommended the standard pressure for reporting gas properties be 10⁵ Pa (1 bar) rather than 1 atm, though the atmosphere is still common in textbooks and industry.
