DBpedia ("DB" stands for "database") is a project aiming to extract structured content from the information created in the Wikipedia project. This structured information is made available on the World Wide Web using Open Linked Data standards, allowing users to query relationships and properties associated with Wikipedia resources, including links to other related datasets.

Background

The project was started in 2006–2007 by researchers from the Free University of Berlin, Leipzig University and Mannheim University, together with the company OpenLink Software, and the first dataset was published in 2007. DBpedia data is extracted principally from Wikipedia infoboxes, categories, page links, geographic coordinates and other structured elements, and is published as RDF triples. The English edition describes millions of entities, and localised chapters maintain editions in well over one hundred languages.

Uses

DBpedia forms a central hub of the Linked Open Data cloud and has been used in applications involving question answering, information extraction, semantic search and data integration. Its data is accessible through a public SPARQL endpoint, as downloadable dumps, and via a lookup service. The ontology is maintained collaboratively through a wiki of mappings from Wikipedia infobox templates to ontology classes and properties.
