Sport Lisboa e Benfica (Portuguese pronunciation: [sɨˈpɔɾ liʒˈβoɐ i βɐ̃jˈfikɐ]), commonly known as Benfica, is a professional football club based in Lisbon, Portugal, that competes in the Primeira Liga, the top flight of Portuguese football. Founded on 28 February 1904, it is one of the "Big Three" clubs in Portugal that have never been relegated from the Primeira Liga, along with rivals Sporting CP and FC Porto.

Nicknamed As Águias ("the Eagles") for the symbol atop the club's crest, and O Glorioso ("the Glorious One"), Benfica plays its home games at the Estádio da Luz, opened in 2003 to replace the original stadium of the same name. The club is the most supported Portuguese club and among the most supported in the world, and holds the Guinness record for the largest number of paying members of any sports club.

History

Benfica was formed from the merger of Sport Lisboa and Grupo Sport Benfica in 1908. The club's golden era came in the 1960s under Hungarian coach Béla Guttmann, when, led by the great Mozambican-born striker Eusébio, Benfica won the European Cup in 1961 and 1962, defeating Barcelona and Real Madrid in the finals. After Guttmann's departure in 1962, the club famously lost eight European finals in a row, a run attributed in legend to the "curse of Béla Guttmann".

Honours

Benfica is the most decorated club in Portuguese football, with a record number of Primeira Liga titles (38), a record number of Taça de Portugal wins, and two European Cups (1961, 1962). Its academy at Seixal (Benfica Campus) is regarded as one of the most productive in world football, having developed players such as Bernardo Silva, Rúben Dias, and João Félix.

Rivalries

Benfica's fiercest rivalry is with Sporting CP, contested in the Lisbon derby (Dérbi da Capital), and with FC Porto in O Clássico, the defining fixture between Portugal's two most successful clubs of recent decades.
