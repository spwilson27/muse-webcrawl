Empress Jitō (持統天皇, Jitō-tennō, 645 – 13 January 703) was the 41st monarch of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession, and the third of eight women to hold the position of empress regnant. She reigned from 686 to 697, first ruling on behalf of her husband's legacy and then being formally enthroned in 690.

Reign

Jitō was the wife of Emperor Tenmu and mother of Prince Kusakabe, the intended heir. After Tenmu's death in 686 she governed while arranging the succession; when Kusakabe died suddenly in 689, she took the throne herself in 690 to secure the line for her grandson, the future Emperor Monmu.

Her reign completed many of Tenmu's projects: she promulgated the Asuka Kiyomihara Code's successor legal framework, issued Japan's early coinage reforms, and in 694 moved the capital to the purpose-built Fujiwara-kyō, the first Japanese capital laid out on a Chinese-style grid. She abdicated in 697 in favour of Monmu but remained influential as retired empress (daijō tennō). Jitō is also remembered as a poet in the Man'yōshū anthology.
