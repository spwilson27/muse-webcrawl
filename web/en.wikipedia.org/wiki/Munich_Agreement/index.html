Munich Agreement

The Munich Agreement was an agreement concluded at Munich on 30 September 1938, by Nazi Germany, the United Kingdom, France, and Italy. It provided "cession to Germany of the Sudeten German territory" of Czechoslovakia, despite the existence of a 1924 alliance agreement and 1925 military pact between France and Czechoslovakia. Czechoslovakia itself was not invited to the conference.

Background

After the Anschluss with Austria, Hitler demanded the Sudetenland, home to ethnic Germans, with support from Konrad Henlein's Sudeten German Party. Britain's Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain pursued appeasement, flying to meet Hitler at Berchtesgaden and Bad Godesberg before the four-power Munich conference.

Terms and aftermath

Germany occupied the Sudetenland from 1–10 October 1938; Poland and Hungary also seized Czechoslovak territory. Chamberlain famously claimed "peace for our time." In March 1939 Germany occupied the remainder of Bohemia and Moravia, destroying the agreement's premise. Munich became a byword for the futility of appeasing expansionist dictators.
