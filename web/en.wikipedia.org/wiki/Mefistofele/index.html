Mefistofele

Mefistofele is an opera in a prologue and five acts (later revised to a prologue and four acts), the only completed opera by the Italian composer-librettist Arrigo Boito. Boito wrote his own libretto, based on Johann Wolfgang von Goethe's Faust. The work is notable among Faust operas for attempting to encompass both parts of Goethe's drama, unlike Charles Gounod's Faust, which concentrates on the Margherita (Gretchen) episode.

Premiere and revision

The opera was first performed at La Scala, Milan, on 5 March 1868. The original version, lasting over five hours, was a notorious fiasco: the audience was exhausted, the singers and orchestra were under-rehearsed, and Boito's ambitions outran the theatre's resources. Boito withdrew the work and extensively revised it. The revised version, shorter and with the role of Faust rewritten from baritone to tenor, was premiered in Bologna on 4 October 1875 to great success, and this version became the standard one.

Synopsis

In the Prologue in Heaven, the devil Mefistofele wagers with God that he can corrupt the soul of Faust. On Earth, the aged scholar Faust makes his pact with Mefistofele, is restored to youth, and seduces Margherita, who is later imprisoned for the deaths of her mother and brother; Faust tries in vain to free her, and she dies trusting in God's mercy. In the second part, Faust is transported to ancient Greece, where he encounters Helen of Troy. In the Epilogue, the now ancient Faust, reflecting on his life, resists Mefistofele's final temptations; heavenly voices proclaim his redemption, and the devil is defeated.

Music

The opera's best-known numbers include the Prologue's celestial choruses, Mefistofele's whistling aria "Son lo spirito che nega" ("I am the spirit that denies"), Faust's "Dai campi, dai prati," Margherita's prison scene, and the duet "Lontano, lontano, lontano." The bass role of Mefistofele has been a signature part for singers such as Feodor Chaliapin, Ezio Pinza, and Samuel Ramey.
