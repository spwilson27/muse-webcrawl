Merian C. Cooper

Merian C. Cooper
Born: October 24, 1893, Jacksonville, Florida
Died: April 21, 1973, San Diego, California
Occupation: filmmaker, aviator, producer

Merian Caldwell Cooper (October 24, 1893 – April 21, 1973) was an American filmmaker, adventurer, and airman. He is best known as the co-creator, co-director, and producer of the 1933 film King Kong. Before his film career he served as a bomber pilot in World War I, fought as a volunteer pilot in the Polish–Soviet War, and flew in World War II, rising to brigadier general in the U.S. Army Air Forces.

Early career

Expelled from the U.S. Naval Academy, Cooper became a pilot and was shot down and captured by the Germans in 1918. After the war he joined the Kościuszko Squadron of American volunteers flying for Poland against Soviet Russia, was shot down again, and escaped from a Soviet POW camp, later receiving Poland's highest military decoration.

Film career

With Ernest B. Schoedsack, Cooper made the pioneering documentaries Grass: A Nation's Battle for Life (1925), filmed with Persian Bakhtiari nomads, and Chang: A Drama of the Wilderness (1927) in Siam. As a production executive at RKO Pictures he championed King Kong (1933), which he co-directed with Schoedsack and in which he appears as the pilot who shoots Kong from the Empire State Building. He later worked with John Ford at Argosy Pictures, produced films including The Quiet Man and The Searchers era productions, and helped pioneer the Cinerama widescreen process.

Later life

Cooper served in the China-Burma-India theater during World War II as chief of staff to Claire Chennault. He was married to actress Dorothy Jordan. He received an honorary Academy Award in 1952 for his innovations in motion pictures.
