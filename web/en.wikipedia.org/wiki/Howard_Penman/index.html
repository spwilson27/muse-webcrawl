Howard Penman

Howard Penman is a British philosopher known for his work on the philosophy of communication, pragmatism and the relationship between language, media and thought. He has written extensively on how communication technologies reshape concepts of meaning and community, and on the philosophy of the American pragmatist tradition.

Work

Penman's scholarship addresses the theory of communication, arguing that the study of communication should be treated as a central philosophical discipline rather than a derivative of language or media studies. His writings engage with figures such as John Dewey, Charles Sanders Peirce and Ludwig Wittgenstein, examining how signs, habits and publics interact, and he has contributed to debate on the conceptual foundations of communication research — including the question of what "communication" itself means across its many theoretical uses.

Themes

Recurring themes in his work include the critique of transmission models of communication, the ritual and constitutive dimensions of communicative practice, and the influence of successive media — print, telegraphy, broadcasting and digital networks — on philosophy itself. His essays have appeared in journals of philosophy and communication studies and in edited collections on pragmatism and media theory.
