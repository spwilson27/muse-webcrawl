Jazz rap

Jazz rap is a subgenre of hip hop that fuses hip hop production and delivery with jazz instrumentation, harmony, and samples. Emerging in the late 1980s and flourishing in the early 1990s, it is associated with Afrocentric lyrics, live instrumentation, and loops drawn from bebop, hard bop, and jazz-funk records.

History

Precursors include the Last Poets and Gil Scott-Heron, who blended spoken word with jazz backing. The style crystallised around 1988–1993 with groups such as A Tribe Called Quest (notably People's Instinctive Travels and the Paths of Rhythm and The Low End Theory), De La Soul, Jungle Brothers, and Gang Starr — the latter teaming DJ Premier's jazz-sample production with Guru's rapping, the latter also releasing the Jazzmatazz series with live jazz musicians. The Native Tongues collective embodied the movement's Afrocentric, musically eclectic ethos. Digable Planets won a Grammy for "Rebirth of Slick (Cool Like Dat)" in 1994.

Later developments

As commercial hip hop moved toward harder sounds, jazz rap receded from the mainstream but persisted underground and influenced alternative hip hop, neo-soul, and lo-fi hip hop. Its legacy is audible in later work by artists such as Kendrick Lamar (the jazz-saturated To Pimp a Butterfly) and in collaborations such as Kamasi Washington's and Robert Glasper's with rappers.
