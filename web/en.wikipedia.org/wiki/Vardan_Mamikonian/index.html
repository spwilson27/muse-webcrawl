Vardan Mamikonian

Died451, Battle of Avarayr
TitleSparapet (commander-in-chief) of Armenia

Vardan Mamikonian (Armenian: Վարդան Մամիկոնյան; c. 387 – 451) was an Armenian military leader and sparapet (commander-in-chief) who led the Armenian rebellion against the Sasanian Empire in 450–451. He was killed at the Battle of Avarayr in 451 but is revered as a national hero and saint of the Armenian Apostolic Church for defending the Christian faith of Armenia.

Background

Vardan was a member of the Mamikonian noble family, which traditionally held the hereditary office of sparapet of the Kingdom of Armenia. After the partition of Armenia between the Roman and Persian empires, the eastern part came under Sasanian rule. In 449 the Sasanian king Yazdegerd II demanded that the Armenians abandon Christianity and adopt Zoroastrianism.

The revolt

The Armenian nobility and clergy rejected the demand at the Council of Artaxata. Vardan initially appeared to accept the king's edicts while serving at the Persian court, but on returning home he renounced his coerced conversion and led an armed revolt. Armenian forces won several engagements and appealed to Constantinople for help, which never arrived.

On 26 May (or 2 June) 451, Vardan's army of some 66,000 men met a much larger Persian army, equipped with war elephants, on the plain of Avarayr in Vaspurakan. Vardan and many of the senior Armenian princes were killed, but the Persians suffered heavy losses and the battle is regarded as a moral victory: in 484 the Treaty of Nvarsak granted the Armenians religious freedom and autonomy, vindicating Vardan's stand.

Legacy

Vardan and his companions, the Vardanank, are commemorated as saints in the Armenian Church, with a feast day before Lent. The battle was chronicled by the historian Yeghishe in his History of Vardan and the Armenian War. Statues of Vardan stand in Yerevan and elsewhere, and he remains one of the most celebrated figures in Armenian history.
