The 2011 Census of India was the fifteenth national census of the country and the seventh since independence. Conducted in two phases — house listing in 2010 and population enumeration in February 2011, with the reference date of 1 March 2011 — it counted 1.21 billion people, making it one of the largest administrative exercises in the world, employing some 2.7 million enumerators. Its motto was "Our Census, Our Future."

Key findings

India's population grew by more than 181 million in the decade, a decadal growth rate of 17.7 percent, slower than the previous decade. Uttar Pradesh remained the most populous state (about 200 million), followed by Maharashtra; Sikkim was the least populous. Literacy rose to 74.04 percent (82.14 percent male, 65.46 percent female), and the child sex ratio (914 girls per 1,000 boys aged 0–6) fell to its lowest since independence, prompting concern. The census also collected, for the first time through a separate exercise, socio-economic and caste data — whose caste results were not released in full.

Process

The census is conducted under the Census Act, 1948, by the Office of the Registrar General and Census Commissioner of India, then headed by C. Chandramouli. Provisional totals were released on 31 March 2011, with final tables rolled out over subsequent years. The 2021 census was postponed due to the COVID-19 pandemic — the first interruption of the decennial series since 1881.
