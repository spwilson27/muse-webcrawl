Cecil Taylor

Cecil Percival Taylor (March 25, 1929 – April 5, 2018) was an American pianist and poet. Classically trained, he was one of the pioneers of free jazz, applying African rhythmic and improvisational traditions together with techniques drawn from 20th-century European art music. His music is characterised by an extremely energetic, physical approach producing complex clusters of improvised sounds.

Career

Taylor studied at the New York College of Music and the New England Conservatory, where he absorbed composers such as Bartók and Stravinsky. His first album, Jazz Advance (1956), already showed his distinctive approach. With his quartet's appearance at the 1957 Newport Jazz Festival and albums such as Unit Structures and Conquistador! (both 1966), he became a central figure of the jazz avant-garde. His long collaboration with alto saxophonist Jimmy Lyons lasted until Lyons's death in 1986.

Style and legacy

Taylor treated the piano as "eighty-eight tuned drums," constructing music from small motivic cells developed at high density and intensity. He also incorporated dance and spoken poetry into performances. He received a Guggenheim Fellowship, a MacArthur Fellowship (1991), and the Kyoto Prize (2013), and was named an NEA Jazz Master.
