Respiratory syncytial virus

FamilyPneumoviridae
GenomeNegative-sense single-stranded RNA
DiseaseBronchiolitis, pneumonia

RSV virions

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a common respiratory virus that causes infections of the lungs and respiratory tract. It is so widespread that most children have been infected by the age of two. In healthy adults it usually causes mild, cold-like symptoms, but it can be severe in infants, older adults, and people with weakened immune systems.

Clinical features

In infants, RSV is the leading cause of bronchiolitis and pneumonia, with symptoms including cough, wheezing, laboured breathing, and poor feeding. Seasonal epidemics occur in winter in temperate climates. Diagnosis is by antigen or PCR testing of respiratory secretions.

Prevention and treatment

Treatment is mainly supportive. Preventive options include monoclonal antibodies such as palivizumab and the long-acting nirsevimab for infants, and vaccines approved from 2023 for older adults and for maternal immunisation to protect newborns.
