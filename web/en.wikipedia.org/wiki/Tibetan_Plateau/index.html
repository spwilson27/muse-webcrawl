Tibetan Plateau

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Tibetan Plateau: a vast high plain of brown grassland and snow-streaked mountains, with a turquoise lake]
The 'Roof of the World'.

The Tibetan Plateau is a vast elevated plateau in Central and East Asia, covering most of the Tibet Autonomous Region of China, much of Qinghai, and Ladakh in India. Nicknamed the 'Roof of the World', it averages over 4,500 metres above sea level and is the world's highest and largest plateau, formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates that also raised the {W('Himalayas')}.

Contents1. Rivers and climate

Rivers and climate

The plateau is the source of Asia's great rivers — the Indus, Ganges system headwaters, Brahmaputra, Yangtze, Yellow and Mekong — earning it the name 'the Water Tower of Asia'. Its glaciers and permafrost are sensitive indicators of climate change, with implications for water supplies of well over a billion people downstream.
