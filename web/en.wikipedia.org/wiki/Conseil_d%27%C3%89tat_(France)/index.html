Conseil d'État (France)

Conseil d'État
Established1799
LocationPalais Royal, Paris
RoleSupreme administrative court; legal adviser to the government

The Conseil d'État (Council of State) is a French institution with a dual role: it is the supreme court for administrative law and the principal legal adviser of the executive branch. It is seated at the Palais Royal in Paris and headed nominally by the Prime Minister, in practice by its vice-president.

History

The council descends from the medieval royal council and was reorganised in its modern form by Napoleon Bonaparte under the Constitution of Year VIII (1799). Initially it advised on legislation and decided administrative disputes in the sovereign's name; reforms in 1872 gave it delegated jurisdiction as a true court. Its landmark case law, including the Blanco decision of 1873, founded modern French administrative law.

Functions

As adviser, the Conseil must be consulted on government bills and major decrees, reviewing them for legality and drafting quality. As a court, it hears at first and last instance challenges to national regulations and acts of central government, and acts as the appellate and cassation court for the administrative courts of appeal and tribunals below it. Its members, the conseillers d'État and other ranks, are recruited largely from graduates of the École nationale d'administration (now the Institut national du service public).
