Sugamo Prison (Sugamo Kōchisho) was a prison in Tokyo, Japan. It was located in the district of Ikebukuro, which is now part of the Toshima ward. Built in the 1920s using plans for prisons in Europe and the United States as a model, it operated through the Second World War.

History

Before and during the war, Sugamo held political prisoners, including communists and others detained under the Peace Preservation Law. The Soviet spy Richard Sorge was hanged there in November 1944.

Postwar use

After the war the prison was taken over by the Allied occupation forces and used to detain Japanese suspected of war crimes while they awaited trial before the International Military Tribunal for the Far East. Seven Class-A war criminals sentenced to death at the Tokyo Trial, including former prime minister Hideki Tojo, were executed by hanging at Sugamo on 23 December 1948.

Control of the prison returned to Japan in 1952. The remaining war criminals were released by 1958 following amnesties and the restoration of Japanese sovereignty.

Demolition and site

The prison was demolished in 1971. On its site was built the Sunshine 60 building, completed in 1978 and at the time the tallest skyscraper in Asia, as part of the Sunshine City complex. A plaque at the site commemorates the prison and the executions that took place there.
