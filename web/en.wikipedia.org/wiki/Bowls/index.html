Bowls

Bowls, also known as lawn bowls, is a sport in which players roll biased balls — bowls — so that they stop close to a smaller ball called the jack. Because the bowls are asymmetrically weighted, they follow a curved path. Bowls is played outdoors on flat green (flat-green bowls), on convex or uneven greens (crown green bowls), and indoors on carpet.

Play

Players deliver their bowls alternately from a mat toward the jack; after all bowls are played, shots are counted for each bowl closer to the jack than the opponent's best bowl. Players may aim to draw close, or play with more weight to disturb the head. Games are played as singles, pairs, triples, or fours, and ends alternate direction along the rink.

History

Forms of the game date back to ancient Egypt and Rome and were established in Britain by the medieval period — the Southampton Old Bowling Green claims use since 1299. The modern game was codified in Scotland in the nineteenth century, with the seminal code drawn up by William Wallace Mitchell in 1864. Bowls is a core sport of the Commonwealth Games and is governed internationally by World Bowls.
