Intimidation

Intimidation is intentional behavior by a person or group that would cause a person of ordinary sensibilities to fear injury or harm. It is not necessary to prove that the behavior caused terror or that the victim was actually frightened. Intimidation may be manifested physically, verbally, or through demonstrations of power, and it ranges from subtle social pressure to criminal threats.

Intimidation exploits an imbalance of real or perceived power.

Forms

  • Physical intimidation — looming, blocking, gestures, display of weapons, or property damage.
  • Verbal intimidation — threats, taunts, coercive demands; includes witness intimidation.
  • Psychological and social intimidation — ostracism, humiliation, and workplace or school bullying.
  • Economic and legal intimidation — abuse of litigation or financial power, including strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPPs).

Legal aspects

Many jurisdictions criminalize intimidation in specific contexts: witness tampering and obstruction of justice, voter intimidation, extortion and blackmail, criminal threatening, and workplace harassment laws. In United States federal law, 18 U.S.C. § 1512 covers tampering with a witness, victim, or informant, and the Voting Rights Act prohibits intimidating voters. Ethnic intimidation statutes add penalties where intimidation targets protected characteristics.

In animal behavior

Ethologists describe threat displays—posturing, vocalization and size exaggeration—by which animals settle contests without fighting, a form of intimidation that reduces the costs of aggression.
