Armed merchant cruiser

A requisitioned liner fitted with naval guns as an armed merchant cruiser

An armed merchant cruiser (AMC), also known as an armed merchantman, is a merchant ship — usually a passenger liner — requisitioned by a navy and fitted with guns for service as an auxiliary warship. AMCs were used extensively by the Royal Navy in both World Wars for convoy escort, blockade patrol, and commerce protection.

Role and history

Fast liners offered speed, range, and sea-keeping that navies needed quickly in wartime. In the First World War, British AMCs enforced the Northern Patrol between Scotland and Iceland. In the Second World War they escorted convoys, though their size and light armament made them vulnerable: HMS Rawalpindi was sunk in 1939 fighting German capital ships, and HMS Jervis Bay sacrificed herself in 1940 to save a convoy from the pocket battleship Admiral Scheer.

Other navies

Germany employed disguised merchant raiders (Hilfskreuzer) such as Atlantis and Kormoran for commerce raiding — a related but distinct concept, as they operated offensively under false flags.
