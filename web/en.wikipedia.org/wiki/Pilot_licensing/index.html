Pilot licensing

Pilot licensing is the process by which a civil aviation authority authorizes an individual to operate an aircraft. A pilot licence (or certificate, in the United States) attests that the holder has demonstrated the knowledge, skill, and medical fitness required for a defined category of flying, such as private, commercial, or airline transport operations.

History

Pilot certification began in the early twentieth century, when the Aero Club de France issued the first licences in 1909 under the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale. Britain followed in 1910 and the United States in 1927, after the Air Commerce Act of 1926. The International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), created by the Chicago Convention of 1944, established common international standards so that licences issued in one contracting state could be recognized in others.

Licence categories

Common grades of licence include the student pilot certificate, private pilot licence (PPL), commercial pilot licence (CPL), and airline transport pilot licence (ATPL). Additional ratings — such as instrument, multi-engine, night, and type ratings — qualify the holder for specific conditions or aircraft. Light-aircraft and sport-pilot categories exist in several jurisdictions for simpler, smaller aeroplanes.

Requirements

Applicants must typically meet minimum age and flight-time requirements, pass written knowledge examinations, complete flight training with authorized instructors, pass a practical flight test (checkride) with an examiner, and hold a medical certificate appropriate to the privileges sought.
