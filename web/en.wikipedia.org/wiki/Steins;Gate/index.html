Steins;Gate is a Japanese science fiction visual novel developed by 5pb. (now Mages) and Nitroplus, released in 2009 as the second entry in the Science Adventure series, following Chaos;Head. Set in Akihabara, Tokyo, in 2010, it follows the self-styled mad scientist Rintaro Okabe and his friends, who accidentally turn a modified microwave into a device that can send text messages into the past — attracting the attention of the shadowy organisation SERN, a fictionalised version of CERN.

Plot

Okabe, with his friends Mayuri Shiina and Itaru "Daru" Hashida, runs the Future Gadget Laboratory. After discovering that their "PhoneWave" microwave can send short "D-Mails" to the past, they are joined by the neuroscientist Kurisu Makise and begin experimenting — each change shifting the world line and altering the present, measured by Okabe's "divergence meter". SERN, which has secretly researched time travel for decades, raids the lab, and Okabe must undo every D-Mail in reverse order to return to the original "Steins Gate" world line and prevent the dystopian future ruled by SERN or the World War III of alternate timelines. The story weaves in real internet lore, notably the "John Titor" time-traveller postings of 2000–2001.

Gameplay

The novel uses a "phone trigger" system: the player chooses whether and how Okabe answers calls and messages on his phone, steering the branching narrative toward multiple endings. A remake with animated footage, Steins;Gate Elite, appeared in 2018.

Adaptations and reception

A manga adaptation began in 2009, and a 24-episode anime by White Fox aired in 2011, followed by the film Steins;Gate: Load Region of Déjà Vu (2013) and the midquel Steins;Gate 0 (game 2015; anime 2018). The visual novel and especially the anime are critically acclaimed and frequently ranked among the greatest anime series of all time, praised for the slow-burn first half that pays off as a tightly plotted time-travel thriller.
