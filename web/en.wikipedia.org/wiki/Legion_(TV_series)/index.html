Legion (TV series)

Legion is an American superhero television series created by Noah Hawley for the cable network FX, based on the Marvel Comics character David Haller (Legion). It ran for three seasons and 27 episodes from 8 February 2017 to 12 August 2019. Dan Stevens stars as Haller, a man diagnosed with schizophrenia who discovers that the voices and visions he experiences are manifestations of enormous mutant psychic powers — and that a parasitic mutant, the Shadow King, has haunted his mind since infancy. The series was produced by FX Productions and Marvel Television and was loosely connected to the X-Men film universe.

Premise and plot

David Haller, institutionalised at Clockwork Psychiatric Hospital, meets fellow patient Syd Barrett (Rachel Keller), a mutant who swaps bodies with anyone she touches. Escaping the government agency Division 3, David comes under the protection of therapist Melanie Bird (Jean Smart) and her mutant sanctuary Summerland, where he learns his "illness" conceals near-limitless psychic abilities. Across the series David battles the Shadow King, Amahl Farouk (Navid Negahban), who possessed him as a child and seeks his body — while David's own growing power and ruthlessness lead, in the second and third seasons, to his transformation into an antagonist in his own right, pursued by his former allies and the time-travelling mutant Switch. The final season confronts David with his father, Charles Xavier, and resolves the timelines his actions have damaged.

Style and reception

Legion was widely acclaimed for its striking, surreal visual style — drawing on 1960s and 1970s design, Kubrickian imagery, and elaborate musical and dance sequences — and for Aubrey Plaza's performance as Lenny Busker, a role originally written for a middle-aged man. Critics praised its unreliable narration and ambitious, dreamlike treatment of mental illness and power, though some found later seasons increasingly opaque. Its first season holds near-universal critical approval, and the series is frequently cited among the most inventive comic-book adaptations on television.
