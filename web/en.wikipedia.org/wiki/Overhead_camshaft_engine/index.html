Overhead camshaft engine

An overhead camshaft (OHC) engine is a piston engine in which the camshaft is located in the cylinder head above the combustion chamber. This contrasts with earlier overhead valve and side-valve designs where the camshaft sits in the engine block.

In a single overhead camshaft (SOHC) engine, one camshaft per cylinder bank operates the intake and exhaust valves, usually via rocker arms or direct followers. A double overhead camshaft (DOHC) engine uses separate camshafts for intake and exhaust valves, allowing four valves per cylinder and easier implementation of variable valve timing.

OHC designs permit higher engine speeds and better breathing than pushrod designs, at the cost of greater complexity. The camshaft is driven from the crankshaft by a timing belt, chain or gears.
