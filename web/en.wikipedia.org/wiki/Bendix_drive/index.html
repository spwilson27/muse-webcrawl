Bendix drive

A Bendix drive is a type of engagement mechanism used in a starter motor of an internal combustion engine. The system allows the pinion gear of the starter motor to engage or disengage the engine's ring gear automatically, using the inertia of the pinion and a helical spline. It is named after its inventor, Vincent Hugo Bendix, and the Bendix Corporation.

Operation

The pinion is mounted on a threaded, helical shaft and is free to slide along it. When the starter motor spins up, the inertia of the pinion causes it to lag behind the rotating shaft; the helical thread converts this lag into axial motion, screwing the pinion forward into mesh with the flywheel ring gear. Once the engine starts and outruns the starter, the pinion is thrown back out of mesh, preventing the engine from driving the starter motor.

Applications and successors

The Bendix drive was used in automobiles, trucks and aircraft starters for much of the twentieth century. In modern vehicles it has largely been replaced by pre-engaged starter motors, in which a solenoid first slides the pinion into mesh before the motor is energised — a quieter and more reliable arrangement — although the name "Bendix" remains in common use for starter pinion assemblies generally.
