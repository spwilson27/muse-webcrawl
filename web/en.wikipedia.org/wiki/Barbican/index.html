Barbican
TypeFortified outpost / gateway
EraMedieval fortification
Famous examplesKraków Barbican; Warsaw Barbican

A barbican (from Old French barbacane) is a fortified outpost or fortified gateway, such as at an outer defence perimeter of a city or castle, or any tower situated over a gate or bridge which was used for defensive purposes. Barbicans typically formed a semi-circular or polygonal fortification placed before a gate, connected to the main walls by a walled road called the neck, forcing attackers into a confined killing ground.

History

Barbicans were common in medieval European and Middle Eastern fortification from roughly the 12th century onward, and similar gate defences appear in Chinese city walls. As gunpowder artillery made high medieval walls obsolete, barbicans declined in military importance, though many survive as monuments. The word is also used for the Barbican Estate and Barbican Centre in London, named after a lost defensive tower on the old city wall.
