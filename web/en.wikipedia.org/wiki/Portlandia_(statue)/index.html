Portlandia (statue)

Portlandia is a sculpture by Raymond Kaskey located above the entrance of the Portland Building in downtown Portland, Oregon. Installed in 1985, the hammered-copper figure depicts a classical female personification of the city — based on the woman on Portland's city seal — reaching down to welcome visitors. At roughly 36 feet (11 m) tall and 6.5 tons, it is often described as the second-largest copper repoussé statue in the United States after the Statue of Liberty.

Installation

The statue was fabricated in Maryland, shipped to Portland in pieces aboard a barge that traveled through the Panama Canal in September 1985, then trucked through downtown in a widely attended procession before being hoisted into place over the building's third-floor entrance on SW Fifth Avenue. Because of its crouched position above the entryway, the statue is notoriously difficult to photograph in full and is best viewed from across the street.

Copyright controversy

Unlike most public art, Portlandia remained under the artist's copyright, and Kaskey restricted commercial reproductions of its image. The dispute became locally famous when the television series Portlandia — which took its name from the statue — used a stylized version of the figure in its opening credits; the conflict was eventually resolved, and the artist's restrictions became a recurring topic in discussions of public art ownership.

Poem

A poem titled "Portlandia" by Ronald Talney, written for the statue's unveiling, is inscribed nearby and begins by describing the figure rising "from the sea" to claim the city.
