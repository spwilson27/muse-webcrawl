Ganzak (also Ganzaca, Gazaca; from Old Iranian *ganza-ka-, "treasury") was an ancient city in Media Atropatene, in what is now northwestern Iran. It served as the capital of the kingdom of Atropatene and was one of the most important religious centres of ancient Iran, famed as the location of the great fire temple of Adur Gushnasp.

History

Ganzak rose to prominence in the Hellenistic period as the capital of Atropatene, the kingdom established by the satrap Atropates after the death of Alexander the Great. The city appears in classical sources — Strabo and Pliny mention it as Gazaca, a royal residence city built on a plain and strongly fortified.

Under the Parthian and Sasanian empires, Ganzak's importance was above all religious. The fire of Adur Gushnasp was one of the three great sacred fires of Zoroastrianism, associated with the warrior estate, and Sasanian kings traditionally travelled to the temple on foot after their coronation to make offerings.

In 624 AD, during the last great Byzantine–Sasanian war, the emperor Heraclius invaded Atropatene and destroyed Ganzak and its fire temple, one of the most famous episodes of the war. The city never fully recovered its former status, though the site remained occupied into the early Islamic period.

Location and identification

The site of Ganzak is usually identified with the ruins around the fortified hill and volcanic lake at Takht-e Soleyman ("Throne of Solomon") in West Azerbaijan province, where the remains of the Adur Gushnasp temple complex have been excavated. Some scholars have instead proposed a location near modern Leylan, southeast of Lake Urmia. The Takht-e Soleyman complex is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
