Sir Raymond Douglas Davies (born 21 June 1944) is an English singer, songwriter, and guitarist, best known as the lead vocalist, rhythm guitarist, and main songwriter of the Kinks, the band he formed with his younger brother Dave Davies. He is widely regarded as one of the most distinctive and literate songwriters in British popular music.

Early life

Davies was born in Fortis Green, Muswell Hill, north London, the seventh of eight children, and grew up in a working-class household that held musical get-togethers in the front room. He attended art college before dedicating himself to the band that became the Kinks.

The Kinks

The Kinks broke through in 1964 with the power-chord riff of "You Really Got Me". Davies went on to write a string of observational, quintessentially English classics — "Sunny Afternoon", "Waterloo Sunset", "Lola", "Dedicated Follower of Fashion" — and the concept albums The Kinks Are the Village Green Preservation Society (1968) and Arthur (1969). A long-running feud with his brother Dave marked the band's troubled internal dynamics.

Solo work

After the Kinks disbanded in 1996, Davies released solo albums including Other People's Lives (2006), Working Man's Café (2007), and Americana (2017), as well as the memoir X-Ray (1994) and musical theatre projects such as Come Dancing.

Honours and legacy

Davies was appointed CBE in 2004 and knighted in 2017 for services to the arts. Songwriters from Pete Townshend to Damon Albarn cite his melodic craftsmanship and character sketches as a major influence.
