ClearType

ClearType is Microsoft's implementation of subpixel rendering technology in rendering text in a font system. ClearType attempts to improve the appearance of text on certain types of computer display screens by sacrificing color fidelity for additional intensity variation. This trade-off is asserted to work well on LCD flat panel monitors.

History

ClearType was first announced at the COMDEX exhibition in November 1998 and shipped with Windows XP in 2001, enabled by default for certain monitors. It was substantially revised in Windows Vista and in the Windows Presentation Foundation, with subpixel positioning and improved hinting. The underlying patents on subpixel rendering date back to the 1970s work at Xerox PARC and the Apple II computer's color graphics.

How it works

Each pixel on an LCD consists of red, green, and blue subpixels. By addressing the subpixels individually, ClearType effectively triples the horizontal resolution available for rendering letter shapes, smoothing jagged edges. The technique depends on the known subpixel geometry of the display, so it is less effective on rotated screens or CRT monitors.
