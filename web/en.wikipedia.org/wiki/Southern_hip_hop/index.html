Southern hip hop

Southern hip hop, also known as Southern rap, South Coast hip hop, or dirty south, is a blanket term for a regional genre of American hip hop music that emerged in the Southern United States, especially in cities such as Atlanta, New Orleans, Houston, Memphis, and Miami.

History

Through the 1980s and early 1990s the hip hop industry was dominated by the East and West Coasts. Southern acts broke through independently: Miami bass acts such as 2 Live Crew, Houston's Geto Boys and UGK, and Memphis artists built regional audiences through local labels and distribution. The term "Dirty South" was popularized by Atlanta group Goodie Mob in 1995. Outkast's success — culminating in André 3000's famous declaration at the 1995 Source Awards that "the South got something to say" — announced the region's arrival.

Styles

Southern hip hop encompasses many subgenres: the bounce music of New Orleans, Houston's chopped and screwed style pioneered by DJ Screw, Memphis crunk (and its Atlanta development by Lil Jon), and Atlanta trap music, pioneered by T.I., Young Jeezy, and Gucci Mane in the 2000s and dominant in mainstream hip hop in the 2010s. Production often emphasizes heavy 808 bass, synthesizers, and dance rhythms suited to clubs and car systems.

Dominance

By the mid-2000s Southern artists accounted for a majority of mainstream hip hop hits, and Atlanta became widely regarded as the centre of the hip hop industry, a position consolidated by trap's global influence on pop production in the 2010s and 2020s.
