Kingdom of Iberia

[Image placeholder: map of the Kingdom of Iberia, c. 300 BC – AD 580]

The Kingdom of Iberia (Georgian: ქართლის სამეფო, kartlis samepo) was an ancient Georgian monarchy in the Caucasus, centred on the region of Kartli in present-day eastern Georgia. It flourished from about 300 BC until AD 580, when the Sassanid Persians abolished the monarchy. Its name derives from Greek and Roman usage and is unrelated to the Iberian Peninsula of western Europe.

Origins

According to Georgian tradition, the kingdom was founded by Pharnavaz I, who united the Kartvelian tribes and established his capital at Mtskheta. Iberia sat astride important trade routes between the Black and Caspian Seas and was contested by Rome, Parthia, and later Sassanid Persia and Byzantium. In the first century AD, kings such as Pharasmanes I played a prominent role in Roman–Parthian rivalry over Armenia.

Christianisation and later history

In the early fourth century, under King Mirian III and the mission of Saint Nino, Iberia adopted Christianity, traditionally dated to AD 327 or 337, making it one of the earliest Christian states. The Georgian alphabet is also traditionally credited to this era. Iberia's later centuries were dominated by warfare between Byzantium and Sassanid Persia; after the death of King Vakhtang I Gorgasali and further Persian pressure, the Sassanids abolished the Iberian monarchy in 580, replacing it with a marzpan (governor), though the local princely dynasties endured and Iberia re-emerged as a principality.
