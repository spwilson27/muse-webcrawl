Gran Paradiso

Gran Paradiso (Italian for "Great Paradise") is a mountain in the Graian Alps in north-west Italy, rising to 4,061 metres (13,323 ft). It is the highest mountain entirely within Italy and lies at the heart of Gran Paradiso National Park, the country's oldest national park.

Geography and climbing

The mountain stands on the border between the Aosta Valley and Piedmont. Its normal route, from the Rifugio Vittorio Emanuele II, is a glacier climb regarded as one of the more accessible 4,000-metre ascents in the Alps, though the summit ridge — crowned by a statue of the Madonna — is exposed. The first ascent was made on 4 September 1860 by a party including John J. Cowell and the guide Michel Payot.

National park

The surrounding massif was a royal hunting reserve of the House of Savoy before being donated to the state and established as a national park in 1922, largely to protect the Alpine ibex, which had nearly been hunted to extinction.
