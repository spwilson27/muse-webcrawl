First Strike Ration

The First Strike Ration (FSR) is a compact assault ration used by the United States Armed Forces. It is designed to sustain a service member during the first 72 hours of intense, highly mobile combat operations, before regular field feeding can be established. Unlike the Meal, Ready-to-Eat (MRE), its contents are packaged to be eaten on the move, with little or no preparation, and are frequently in the form of pocket sandwiches, wraps and bars that can be consumed one-handed.

Development

The FSR was developed at the U.S. Army Soldier Systems Center at Natick, Massachusetts, in response to feedback from operations in Iraq and Afghanistan that soldiers in the assault phase discarded much of the standard MRE to save weight and bulk, and often went short of calories. Fielding began around 2007–2008. A single FSR pack contains food for 24 hours — roughly the contents of three MREs — but weighs substantially less (about 1.4 kg) and occupies about half the volume.

Contents

Menus typically include shelf-stable pocket sandwiches (such as barbecue beef, bacon-cheddar or Italian-style), tortilla wraps, beef sticks and jerky, energy and cereal bars, dried fruit, nuts and trail mix, crackers or filled pretzels, cheese or peanut-butter spread, candy, and powdered carbohydrate-electrolyte beverage mixes; many components are fortified with caffeine or other performance ingredients, and an accessory pack provides items such as moist towelettes and matches. The ration is intended to deliver on the order of 2,900 kilocalories per day, weighted toward carbohydrate for rapid energy.

Use

Doctrine limits continuous use of the FSR to the initial 72 hours of an operation, after which troops are to transition to MREs or group rations such as the Unitized Group Ration, since the FSR is not designed to meet nutritional needs over extended periods. Its concept influenced other lightweight assault rations developed by allied militaries.
