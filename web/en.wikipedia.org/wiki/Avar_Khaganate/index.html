The Avar Khaganate was a confederation of Eurasian nomads, ruled by a khagan, that controlled the Carpathian Basin and large parts of Central and Southeast Europe from about 567 to the early 9th century. The Pannonian Avars, a people possibly related to or distinct from the Rouran of Mongolia, established the khaganate after migrating westward across the Pontic steppe, and for over two centuries they were a major power between the Frankish kingdoms, the Byzantine Empire, and the Lombards.

History

The Avars first appeared in Byzantine diplomacy in 557–558, when an embassy reached Constantinople. In alliance with the Lombards, they destroyed the Gepid kingdom in 567; when the Lombards departed for Italy in 568, the Avars under Khagan Bayan I took possession of the entire Carpathian Basin. From their fortified circular capital, the Ring, the khagans campaigned deep into the Balkans, capturing Sirmium in 582 and besieging Constantinople itself in 626 in alliance with the Sasanian Persians — an attack that failed and marked the high point and turning point of Avar power.

After 626 the khaganate weakened: subject Slavic peoples revolted (the realm of Samo, c. 623–658, was the first Slavic state), the Bulgars under Kubrat broke away in the east, and civil war followed a disputed succession. The khaganate survived in diminished form through the 8th century, but Charlemagne's campaigns of 791–803 destroyed the Ring, seized the Avar treasure, and reduced the khaganate to a Frankish client. The last references to the Avars as a distinct polity date to the 820s–870s, after which they were assimilated into the Slavic and Hungarian populations of the basin.

Society and culture

The Avars introduced the stirrup to Europe, transforming cavalry warfare. Their armies combined armoured lancers with subject Slavic infantry and Bulgar and Gepid contingents. Archaeology has uncovered tens of thousands of Avar-period graves with belt fittings, horse burials, and Byzantine gold solidi reflecting the huge tribute payments Constantinople paid for peace.
