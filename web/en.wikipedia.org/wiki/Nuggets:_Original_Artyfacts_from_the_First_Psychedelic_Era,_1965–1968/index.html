Nuggets: Original Artyfacts from the First Psychedelic Era, 1965–1968 is a compilation album of American psychedelic and garage rock singles released between 1965 and 1968, assembled by musician and rock writer Lenny Kaye and released as a double album by Elektra Records in 1972. Although it sold poorly on release, it became one of the most influential compilations ever made: it preserved a body of raw, regional American rock that mainstream histories had ignored, and its title track-listing effectively defined the genre later christened "garage rock". Kaye's liner notes famously used the term "punk rock" to describe this mid-1960s style.

Content

The 27 tracks include the Count Five's "Psychotic Reaction", the Standells' "Dirty Water", the Electric Prunes' "I Had Too Much to Dream (Last Night)", the Seeds' "Pushin' Too Hard", the Leaves' "Hey Joe", the Knickerbockers' Beatlesque "Lies", Question Mark and the Mysterians' "96 Tears", the 13th Floor Elevators' "You're Gonna Miss Me", the Amboy Dukes' "Baby Please Don't Go", and the Chocolate Watchband's "Let's Talk About Girls". Many were one-hit regional successes that had fallen out of print.

Legacy

The album's influence grew through the 1970s: it was championed by critics and by the musicians who formed the punk and new wave scenes, and Sire Records reissued it in 1976. Rhino Records expanded the concept into a 1998 four-CD box set of the same name, followed by further volumes covering British and international psychedelia (Nuggets II) and other scenes. The original album was selected for preservation in the United States National Recording Registry by the Library of Congress in 2012, recognised as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".
