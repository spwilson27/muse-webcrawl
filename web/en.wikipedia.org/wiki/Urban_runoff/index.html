Urban runoff

Urban runoff is surface runoff of rainwater, landscape irrigation, and car washing created by urbanization. Impervious surfaces such as roads, parking lots, and sidewalks prevent water from soaking into the ground, so rainfall is converted into runoff that is collected by storm drains and discharged into local waterways.

Effects

Urban runoff is a major source of urban flooding and water pollution. As it flows over streets and other surfaces, it picks up pollutants including oil, heavy metals, fertilizers, pesticides, sediment, and bacteria, and carries them into rivers, lakes, and coastal waters. Increased runoff volumes also erode stream banks and degrade aquatic habitats.

Management

Stormwater management techniques aim to reduce runoff and remove pollutants. Best management practices include detention and retention ponds, constructed wetlands, permeable pavement, rain gardens, and green infrastructure such as bioswales and green roofs, which slow, filter, and infiltrate runoff close to where it falls.
