The Bureau International des Expositions (BIE) is an intergovernmental organisation created by the Convention Relating to International Exhibitions, signed in Paris in 1928, to supervise and regulate all international exhibitions that are not purely commercial, most famously the world expositions known as World Expos.

Role

The BIE classifies exhibitions, selects host cities and countries by vote of its member states, sets the rules for participation, and ensures that expos serve educational and public purposes. It recognises two main kinds of expo: large World Expos, held every five years for up to six months, and smaller Specialised Expos on specific themes held between them. It also recognises horticultural exhibitions (with the AIPH) and the Triennale di Milano.

Notable expos

Under the BIE system, landmark exhibitions have included Brussels 1958 (Expo 58, with the Atomium), Montreal 1967 (Expo 67), Osaka 1970, Seville 1992, Shanghai 2010 — the largest in history — Milan 2015, Dubai 2020 (held 2021–22), and Osaka 2025.

Governance

The BIE is headquartered in Paris and governed by a General Assembly of delegates from member states, an Executive Committee and specialised committees. Member states vote on host selection, often after years of competitive bidding.
