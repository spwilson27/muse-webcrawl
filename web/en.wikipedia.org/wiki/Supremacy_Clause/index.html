Supremacy Clause

Supremacy Clause
Location: Article VI, Clause 2, U.S. Constitution
Subject: Federal preemption

The Supremacy Clause of the Constitution of the United States (Article VI, Clause 2) establishes that the Constitution, federal laws made pursuant to it, and treaties made under its authority constitute the "supreme Law of the Land," overriding conflicting state laws.

Text

"This Constitution, and the Laws of the United States which shall be made in Pursuance thereof; and all Treaties made, or which shall be made, under the Authority of the United States, shall be the supreme Law of the Land; and the Judges in every State shall be bound thereby, any Thing in the Constitution or Laws of any State to the Contrary notwithstanding."

Doctrine

The clause underlies federal preemption: express preemption, field preemption, and conflict preemption (impossibility and obstacle). Landmark cases include McCulloch v. Maryland (1819), Gibbons v. Ogden (1824), Arizona v. United States (2012), and cases on state resistance such as Cooper v. Aaron (1958).
