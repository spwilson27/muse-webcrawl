WIC program

The Special Supplemental Food Program for Women, Infants, and Children, commonly known as the WIC program, is a federal assistance program of the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) that provides nutrition support to low-income pregnant women, breastfeeding women, and children under the age of five. It is one of the largest food and nutrition assistance programs in the United States.

Services

WIC provides participants with benefits to purchase specific nutritious foods — such as milk, eggs, cereal, fruits and vegetables, peanut butter, and infant formula — along with nutrition education, breastfeeding support, and referrals to health care and social services. Benefits were historically delivered by paper vouchers and have largely transitioned to electronic benefit transfer (EBT) cards.

History and eligibility

The program was established as a pilot in 1972 and made permanent in 1975. Eligibility requires categorical status (pregnant, postpartum, or breastfeeding women, infants, and children up to age five), household income at or below 185 percent of the U.S. federal poverty guidelines (or participation in programs such as SNAP or Medicaid), state residency, and a nutritional risk assessment by a health professional. Studies of the program associate participation with improved birth outcomes and reduced infant mortality.
