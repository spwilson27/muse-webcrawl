PayPal Mafia

The "PayPal Mafia" is the name given to a group of former founders and early employees of the payments company PayPal who went on to found, lead, or fund many of Silicon Valley's most prominent technology companies. The term was popularised by a 2007 Fortune magazine article and photo shoot that dressed the group in mobster-style attire.

Origins

PayPal emerged from the merger of Confinity (founded by Max Levchin, Peter Thiel, and Luke Nosek) and Elon Musk's X.com. eBay acquired PayPal in 2002 for about $1.5 billion, and many of its young executives departed with substantial capital, networks, and experience in building internet businesses.

Members and ventures

  • Peter Thiel — co-founder of Palantir Technologies and Founders Fund; first outside investor in Facebook.
  • Elon Musk — founder of SpaceX and leader of Tesla, later owner of X (Twitter).
  • Reid Hoffman — co-founder of LinkedIn; partner at Greylock.
  • Max Levchin — co-founder of Slide and Affirm.
  • Steve Chen, Chad Hurley, Jawed Karim — co-founders of YouTube.
  • David Sacks — founder of Yammer; Keith Rabois — executive at Square and venture capitalist.
  • Roelof Botha — partner at Sequoia Capital; Jeremy Stoppelman and Russel Simmons — co-founders of Yelp.
  • Dave Morin — Facebook and Path; Premal Shah — president of Kiva.

Influence

Analysts attribute the group's outsized influence to the dense trust network formed during PayPal's adversarial early years (including its fight against online fraud), the wealth generated by the eBay acquisition, and a shared culture of intense, meritocratic company-building. The network has been both celebrated as an engine of innovation and criticised as emblematic of concentrated power in the technology industry.
