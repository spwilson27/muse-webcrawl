The Energy Charter Treaty (ECT) is an international agreement that establishes a multilateral framework for cross-border cooperation in the energy industry, principally the fossil fuel industry. The treaty covers all aspects of commercial energy activities including trade, transit, investments and energy efficiency, and is legally binding, including dispute resolution procedures.

History

The treaty grew out of the 1991 European Energy Charter declaration, a political initiative launched after the end of the Cold War to integrate the energy sectors of Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union into European and world markets. The treaty itself was signed in Lisbon in December 1994 and entered into force in April 1998.

Provisions

The ECT's investor-state dispute settlement (ISDS) provisions allow foreign energy investors to sue host states before international arbitral tribunals for measures alleged to breach the treaty's fair and equitable treatment standard or to amount to expropriation. The treaty became the most frequently invoked investment agreement in the world, with claims frequently brought against states that curtailed fossil fuel projects or reformed renewable-energy subsidies, such as Spain, Italy and the Netherlands.

Withdrawal and modernisation

From the late 2010s the treaty faced growing criticism that it obstructed climate policy. Italy withdrew in 2016. A modernisation agreement in principle was reached in June 2022, but in 2022–2024 numerous European states — including France, Germany, Poland, Spain, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom — announced or completed withdrawal, and the European Union and Euratom themselves decided to withdraw, citing incompatibility with the European Green Deal and the Paris Agreement. A "sunset clause" (Article 47) keeps the treaty's protections in force for existing investments for 20 years after a party's withdrawal.
