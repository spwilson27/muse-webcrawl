Webvan

Founded1996, Foster City, California
FounderLouis Borders
DefunctJuly 2001
IndustryOnline grocery delivery

Webvan was a dot-com company and online grocery business that delivered products to customers' homes within a 30-minute window of their choosing. Founded in 1996 by Louis Borders, co-founder of the Borders bookstore chain, Webvan raised more than $375 million in its November 1999 initial public offering and at its peak was valued in the billions of dollars. It became one of the most famous failures of the dot-com bubble, filing for bankruptcy in July 2001 after burning through over $800 million.

Business model and expansion

Webvan built huge, highly automated distribution centers — each costing tens of millions of dollars — designed to process grocery orders using conveyor and carousel systems. The company launched service in the San Francisco Bay Area in 1999 and expanded rapidly to cities including Los Angeles, San Diego, Seattle, Chicago, Atlanta, and Dallas, often through acquisition, most notably of rival HomeGrocer in 2000. Its plan called for dozens of distribution centers nationwide.

Failure and legacy

Webvan's costs vastly outran its sales: order volumes in each market fell far below the levels needed to support its capital-intensive warehouses, and it was losing money on many deliveries. When capital markets closed to unprofitable internet companies in 2000–2001, Webvan could not raise further funding; it ceased operations on July 9, 2001, laying off about 2,000 employees. Analysts later cited Webvan as a cautionary tale about scaling ahead of demand, though its model anticipated later, more successful grocery delivery services such as Instacart and Amazon Fresh.
