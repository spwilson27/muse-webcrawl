The Blue Nile Falls is a waterfall on the Blue Nile river in Ethiopia, about 30 km downstream from the town of Bahir Dar and Lake Tana. It is known locally as Tis Issat ("smoking water") or Tis Abay in Amharic, for the clouds of mist and spray the falls generate. It is one of Ethiopia's best-known tourist attractions.

Description

The falls are estimated to be between 37 and 45 metres high, consisting of four streams that originally varied from a trickle in the dry season to more than 400 metres wide in the rainy season. The Portuguese bridge below the falls, built in the 17th century under Emperor Susenyos, is one of the oldest stone bridges in Ethiopia.

Regulation

Regulation of Lake Tana's outflow and, since 2003, diversion of much of the river's flow to the Tis Abay hydroelectric stations have greatly reduced the volume passing over the falls except during the peak of the rainy season (roughly July to September). A flow sufficient for tourism is nominally maintained, though visitors in the dry season may find the falls much diminished.
