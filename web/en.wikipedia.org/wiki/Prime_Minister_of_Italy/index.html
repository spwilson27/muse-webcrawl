Prime Minister of Italy

The Prime Minister of Italy, officially the President of the Council of Ministers (Italian: Presidente del Consiglio dei ministri), is the head of government of the Italian Republic.

President of the Council of Ministers
ResidencePalazzo Chigi, Rome
AppointerPresident of the Republic
Formation1861 (Kingdom); 1946 (Republic)

Role

The prime minister is appointed by the President of the Republic after consultations and must win confidence votes in both houses of Parliament. The office directs the general policy of the government and countersigns acts of the President.

History

First holders in the Kingdom of Italy included Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour. In the Republic, long-serving premiers include Alcide De Gasperi, Giulio Andreotti and Silvio Berlusconi. See Enrico Letta, who served 2013–2014.

List

Italy has had frequent government changes, with more than 60 governments since 1946, reflecting coalition politics in a parliamentary system.
