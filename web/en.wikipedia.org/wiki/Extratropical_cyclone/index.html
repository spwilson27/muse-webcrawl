Extratropical cyclone

An extratropical cyclone (also mid-latitude cyclone) is a large low-pressure weather system forming outside the tropics along fronts, typically 1,000 km or more across, bringing much of the variable weather of mid-latitudes. Unlike tropical cyclones, they draw energy from horizontal temperature contrasts (baroclinic instability).

Extratropical cyclone
Type: Low-pressure system
Features: Cold and warm fronts, occlusion

Structure and life cycle

The Norwegian cyclone model describes development along the polar front: a wave forms, warm and cold fronts intensify around a deepening low, then the cold front catches up forming an occluded front as the system decays. Jet streams and upper-level troughs drive cyclogenesis; intense examples include nor'easters and European windstorms, occasionally undergoing explosive "bomb" deepening.
