The Chashma Nuclear Power Plant (often abbreviated CHASNUPP) is a nuclear power complex near Chashma in Mianwali District, Punjab, Pakistan, on the bank of the Indus River. Operated by the Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission, the site hosts four pressurised water reactors supplied by China, with a combined generating capacity of roughly 1,330 megawatts. Together with the Karachi Nuclear Power Plant, it forms the backbone of Pakistan's civil nuclear power programme, and all its units operate under International Atomic Energy Agency safeguards.

Units

The first unit, Chashma-1, is a 325 MW pressurised water reactor modelled on China's Qinshan plant; construction began in 1993 and commercial operation started in 2000. The near-identical Chashma-2 followed, entering service in 2011. Two larger units of the CNP-300-derived ACP-1000 family design — Chashma-3 (operation 2016) and Chashma-4 (operation 2017), each rated at about 340 MW — completed the present station. In 2023 Pakistan and China signed an agreement for a much larger fifth unit, Chashma-5, of the Hualong One (HPR1000) type at about 1,200 MW, which would make Chashma Pakistan's largest nuclear site.

Cooperation with China

The Chashma project is the centrepiece of long-running nuclear cooperation between Pakistan and China, begun before China joined the Nuclear Suppliers Group in 2004; later units were defended by Beijing as grandfathered under the original agreements, a position disputed by some NSG members. The plant draws cooling water from the Chashma Barrage on the Indus, and its output feeds Pakistan's national grid, helping to offset chronic electricity shortages.
