The Government of Portugal, also referred to in Portuguese as the Governo da República Portuguesa, is one of the four sovereignty bodies of the Portuguese Republic, together with the President of the Republic, the Assembly of the Republic, and the courts. It is the body that conducts the general policy of the country and the superior organ of the public administration.

Composition and functions

The Government comprises the Prime Minister, deputy prime ministers, ministers, and secretaries of state. It is accountable to the President of the Republic and to the Assembly of the Republic. Its functions include negotiating and concluding international treaties, proposing legislation to the Assembly, issuing decree-laws, preparing the state budget, and directing the civil service and armed forces administration.

Formation

After legislative elections, the President appoints the Prime Minister, usually the leader of the party or coalition with the largest representation, after consulting the parties in the Assembly. The Prime Minister then selects the other members of the Government, who are formally appointed by the President. The Government's programme must be submitted to the Assembly and may be rejected by an absolute majority, leading to dismissal.

Constitutional governments

Since the 1976 Constitution, Portugal's governments are numbered as "Constitutional Governments" (Governos Constitucionais). Single-party and coalition governments have alternated, most often formed by the Socialist Party (PS) or the Social Democratic Party (PSD).
