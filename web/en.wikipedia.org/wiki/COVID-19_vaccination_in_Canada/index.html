COVID-19 vaccination in Canada

COVID-19 vaccination in Canada is an ongoing immunization campaign against severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), the virus that causes COVID-19. The campaign began on 14 December 2020, days after Health Canada authorized the Pfizer–BioNTech vaccine, and was administered by the provinces and territories under guidance from the National Advisory Committee on Immunization (NACI). Canada achieved among the highest vaccination rates in the world, with over 80% of the total population receiving at least two doses by 2022.

Rollout

Initial doses went to long-term care residents and staff, front-line health workers and Indigenous communities. Early supply shortages led Canada to delay second doses by up to four months in 2021 — a strategy later credited with maximizing population coverage during the Delta wave. Eligibility expanded through spring 2021 to all adults, then to adolescents and children aged five and older in November 2021, and to children under five in 2022.

Policies

In autumn 2021 the federal government mandated vaccination for federal public servants and for travellers on planes and interprovincial trains; most provinces introduced vaccine passports for non-essential services. The mandates and passports were lifted through 2022. Opposition to mandates fuelled the 2022 "Freedom Convoy" protests in Ottawa.

Boosters

Booster doses were recommended from late 2021, with bivalent vaccines targeting Omicron variants introduced in autumn 2022 and updated XBB.1.5 formulations in 2023, after which COVID-19 vaccination settled into a seasonal, risk-based program.
