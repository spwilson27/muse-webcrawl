Central Bank of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea

Central Bank of the DPRK
Headquarters: Pyongyang, North Korea
Established: 1947 (as Central Bank of North Korea)
Currency: North Korean won (KPW)

The Central Bank of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea is the central bank of North Korea. Established in 1947, it issues the North Korean won and, in North Korea's mono-bank system, historically combined central banking with commercial banking functions, operating a network of branches across the country.

Functions

The bank issues currency, holds state funds, extends credit to state enterprises, and settles payments within the planned economy; foreign trade banking is handled separately, principally by the Foreign Trade Bank. A currency revaluation in November–December 2009, which exchanged old notes for new at 100:1 with limited amounts, was widely reported to have wiped out private savings and provoked public discontent. Information about the bank's operations is limited, and it is subject to international sanctions related to North Korea's nuclear and missile programmes.
