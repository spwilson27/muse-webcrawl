The NFL Draft, officially the NFL Annual Player Selection Meeting, is the annual event in which the 32 teams of the National Football League select newly eligible players, primarily from American college football. It is the league's most common source of player recruitment and one of the most watched off-season events in American sport.

The NFL Draft has become a major televised spectacle.

Format

The draft consists of seven rounds. In each round, teams select in reverse order of the previous season's standings — the team with the worst record picks first — subject to trades of picks between teams. The Super Bowl champion selects last in each round. Additional compensatory picks are awarded to teams that lost valuable free agents. The first player selected overall receives enormous attention; the last pick is humorously dubbed "Mr. Irrelevant".

History

The first draft was held in 1936 at the Ritz-Carlton Hotel in Philadelphia, an idea of commissioner Bert Bell to curb bidding wars for college players and improve competitive balance. For decades a low-key affair, the draft was transformed by television: ESPN began broadcasting it in 1980, and it grew into a multi-day primetime event, moving from New York City to rotating host cities beginning in 2015.

Eligibility and evaluation

Players are generally eligible three years after graduating from high school. Prospects are evaluated through game film, the NFL Scouting Combine, and pro days. The months before the draft are filled with mock drafts and analysis by media and fans.
