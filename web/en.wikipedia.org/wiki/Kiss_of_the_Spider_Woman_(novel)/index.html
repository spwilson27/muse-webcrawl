Kiss of the Spider Woman (Spanish: El beso de la mujer araña) is a 1976 novel by the Argentine writer Manuel Puig. Set in a Buenos Aires prison in 1975, it unfolds almost entirely as dialogue between two cellmates: Luis Molina, a gay window dresser imprisoned for "corruption of a minor", and Valentín Arregui, a Marxist revolutionary tortured by the regime.

Plot and form

To pass the time and win Valentín's trust, Molina retells the plots of old films — most prominently a Nazi propaganda melodrama and a B-movie about a panther woman — while footnotes interrupt the narrative with pseudo-scholarly essays on the psychoanalytic theory of homosexuality. Unbeknownst to Valentín, Molina has been planted by the prison authorities to extract information, yet genuine love develops between the men, and Molina ultimately sacrifices himself carrying a message for Valentín's comrades after his release.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into the 1985 film Kiss of the Spider Woman, for which William Hurt won the Academy Award for Best Actor as Molina, and into the 1993 Broadway musical by John Kander, Fred Ebb, and Terrence McNally.
