A foot-candle (sometimes foot candle; abbreviated fc, lm/ft², or sometimes ft-c) is a non-SI unit of illuminance. It is defined as one lumen per square foot. This unit is commonly used in lighting design in the United States, where building codes, industry standards, and recommended practice guides (such as those of the Illuminating Engineering Society, IES) specify illuminance levels in foot-candles. The corresponding SI unit is the lux, one lumen per square metre; one foot-candle equals approximately 10.764 lux.

Definition and use

Historically, a foot-candle was the illuminance cast on a surface by a one-candela source one foot away. In practice, lighting designers use foot-candles as targets for spaces and tasks: a typical office is designed for roughly 30–50 fc on the work surface, corridors and storage for much less (about 5–20 fc), and fine inspection tasks for 100 fc or more. Direct sunlight is on the order of 10,000 fc, an overcast day about 100–1,000 fc, and full moonlight about 0.01 fc.

The foot-candle remains standard in American practice in architecture, theatrical and film lighting (where cinematographers traditionally set exposure by foot-candles measured with an incident meter), horticultural lighting, and sports lighting standards, even as metric lux predominates internationally and in scientific contexts. Light meters frequently display both units.

Typical recommended levels (IES)

  • Parking lots, exterior: 0.5–2 fc
  • Corridors, lobbies: 5–10 fc
  • Classrooms, general offices: 30–50 fc
  • Detailed drafting, sewing, inspection: 75–200 fc
  • Operating theatres (general): 100 fc and above
