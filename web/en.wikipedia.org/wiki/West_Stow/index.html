West Stow

West Stow is a small village in Suffolk, England, in the Lark valley north-west of Bury St Edmunds. It is best known for the West Stow Anglo-Saxon Village, an open-air museum and experimental archaeology site built on the excavated remains of an early Anglo-Saxon settlement.

The Anglo-Saxon village

Excavations between 1965 and 1972 uncovered a settlement occupied from about AD 420 to 650, comprising some 70 sunken-featured buildings (grubenhäuser), several larger halls, and a cemetery with pagan burials. The site is unusual for having been fully excavated. Since the 1970s, reconstructed houses have been built on the original house sites using period tools and techniques, allowing archaeologists to test theories about Anglo-Saxon construction; a reconstructed hall burned in an experiment and rebuilt has informed understanding of building lifespans.

The area

West Stow lies within the Breckland landscape and West Stow Country Park, which surrounds the village site with heath, woodland and riverside walks. The nearby prehistoric flint mines of Grimes Graves are a related attraction.
