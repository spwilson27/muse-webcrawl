Lwa

Lwa (also spelled loa) are the spirits of Haitian Vodou and related traditions such as Louisiana Voodoo. Practitioners serve the lwa, who act as intermediaries between humans and Bondye, the supreme creator god, who remains remote from everyday affairs.

Nanchons

The lwa are grouped into families or "nations" (nanchon), notably the Rada (generally benevolent spirits with West African roots), the Petwo (often fiercer spirits associated with the Congo and the era of slavery), and the Ghede, spirits of the dead and fertility associated with death and humour. Well-known lwa include Papa Legba, guardian of the crossroads; Ogoun, spirit of iron and war; Erzulie, spirit of love; Baron Samedi, head of the Ghede; Damballa, the serpent; and Agwé, spirit of the sea.

Service

In ceremonies led by priests (oungan) or priestesses (manbo), the lwa are invoked with drumming, song, and offerings, and are believed to temporarily "mount" a devotee, possessing them in order to give advice, healing, or warnings. Many lwa are associated with Catholic saints through syncretism developed during the colonial period.
