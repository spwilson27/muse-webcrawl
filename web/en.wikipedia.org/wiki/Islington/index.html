Islington is a district in inner London, forming the core of the London Borough of Islington. Centred on Upper Street between the Angel and Highbury Corner, it is known for its Georgian terraces and squares, theatres, restaurants and antique shops around Camden Passage.

History

Recorded in the Domesday Book as Iseldone, Islington was long a rural village supplying London with dairy produce and recreation, famed in the 17th and 18th centuries for its tea gardens and spas such as Sadler's Wells. Urban development followed in the 19th century, bringing dense terraced housing and industry along the Regent's Canal.

After mid-20th-century decline, Islington was extensively gentrified from the 1960s onward, becoming associated with media and political professionals; in the 1990s it was famously linked with the New Labour leadership around Tony Blair, who lived there before becoming prime minister.

Culture

Islington is home to the Almeida Theatre, the King's Head Theatre (a pioneering pub theatre), Sadler's Wells dance theatre and the Union Chapel music venue. The Emirates Stadium, home of Arsenal F.C., stands in nearby Holloway within the borough.
