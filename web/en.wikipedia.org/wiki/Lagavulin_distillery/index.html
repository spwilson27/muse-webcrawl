Lagavulin distillery on Islay's south coast

Lagavulin distillery is a producer of single malt Scotch whisky in the village of Lagavulin on the south coast of the island of Islay, Scotland. Its name derives from the Gaelic Lag a' Mhuilinn, "the hollow of the mill". The distillery is owned by Diageo and its 16-year-old expression is one of the six original Classic Malts of Scotland.

History

Illicit distilling took place on the site as early as 1742. John Johnston founded the first legal distillery in 1816, and a second, Ardmore, followed in 1817; the two were later combined. In the late nineteenth century Lagavulin's agent Peter Mackie built the neighbouring Laphroaig rivalry into a famous feud and created the Malt Mill distillery on the site in a failed attempt to replicate Laphroaig's spirit. Lagavulin's fortunes rose sharply after 1990 when its heavily peated 16-year-old was promoted as a Classic Malt.

Character

Lagavulin is renowned for an intensely peaty, smoky and maritime style, produced from malted barley dried over peat fires, a slow distillation in pear-shaped stills and long maturation, principally in ex-bourbon casks. Core bottlings include the 16-year-old, a Distillers Edition finished in Pedro Ximénez sherry casks, and an 8-year-old released for the distillery's bicentenary in 2016.
