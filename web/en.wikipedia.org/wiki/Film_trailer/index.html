Trailer (promotion)

A trailer (also known as a preview or coming attraction) is a short advertisement, usually for a feature film to be exhibited at a cinema or released on a streaming platform. Trailers consist of selected shots and dialogue from the film, edited together with music and narration to attract an audience, and typically run between thirty seconds and three minutes.

History

The name derives from early practice: trailers were originally shown after the feature film, trailing the programme, though they were soon moved before it because audiences left at the credits. Early trailers were produced by the National Screen Service from the 1920s; by the 1960s editors such as Andrew J. Kuehn developed the modern trailer form of a self-contained mini-narrative. Specialized trailer houses in Los Angeles and elsewhere now produce most major campaigns.

Types

Studios release teaser trailers — very short spots produced before a film is finished — followed by full theatrical trailers, television spots, and red-band trailers for mature content. Trailers for major releases are themselves major media events, launched online to millions of views, and their editing conventions (montage structure, title cards, and music cues) have become a recognized genre of short filmmaking.
