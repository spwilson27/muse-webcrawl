Real estate investment trust

TypeInvestment vehicle
OriginUnited States, 1960
Requirement (US)Distribute ≥90% of taxable income as dividends

A real estate investment trust (REIT) is a company that owns, and in most cases operates, income-producing real estate. REITs pool capital to own portfolios of properties — apartments, offices, warehouses, hospitals, hotels, cell towers — and many trade on major stock exchanges.

History and rules

The United States Congress created REITs in 1960 to give small investors access to large income-producing properties. To qualify, a US REIT must invest at least 75% of assets in real estate, derive most income from rents or mortgages, and pay out at least 90% of taxable income to shareholders, in return for exemption from corporate-level tax on distributed income.

Types

Equity REITs own and operate properties; mortgage REITs lend against real estate or hold mortgage-backed securities; hybrid REITs combine both. Similar regimes now exist in dozens of countries, including Japan (J-REITs) and the United Kingdom (UK-REITs).

Performance measures

Because depreciation distorts earnings, analysts evaluate REITs using funds from operations (FFO) and adjusted FFO rather than net income.
