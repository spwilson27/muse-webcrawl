Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Promulgated: 18 October 1990 by Pope John Paul II
In force: 1 October 1991
Canons: 1,546
Applies to: The 23 Eastern Catholic Churches sui iuris

The Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches (Latin: Codex Canonum Ecclesiarum Orientalium, abbreviated CCEO) is the single body of canon law governing the Eastern Catholic Churches—the 23 self-governing (sui iuris) Eastern churches in full communion with the Bishop of Rome. It was promulgated by Pope John Paul II with the apostolic constitution Sacri Canones on 18 October 1990 and took effect on 1 October 1991.

Development

The Latin Church's law was codified in 1917 and again in the 1983 Code of Canon Law, but Eastern law remained fragmented in partial codifications issued between 1949 and 1958 covering marriage, trials, temporal goods, and religious. Work on a complete Eastern code began in 1929 and resumed after the Second Vatican Council, whose decree Orientalium Ecclesiarum (1964) affirmed the equal dignity and distinct heritage of the Eastern churches. The final draft was revised by a pontifical commission with Eastern bishops and canonists.

Contents

The CCEO contains 1,546 canons arranged in 30 titles, covering the rights and obligations of the Christian faithful, the structure of patriarchal and major archiepiscopal churches, eparchies (dioceses), clergy, monks and religious, the sacraments and divine worship, teaching, temporal goods, penal law, and procedural law. Its first canons express a guiding principle: the code is to respect and foster the legitimate traditions of each Eastern church.

Differences from the Latin code

Reflecting Eastern tradition, the code gives a strong role to patriarchal synods, allows a married clergy in many Eastern churches, treats the sacrament of chrismation (confirmation) as administered by priests at baptism, and uses terminology such as "eparchy" and "exarchy" in place of Latin "diocese".
