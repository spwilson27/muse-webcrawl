Harrier jump jet

The Harrier, informally referred to as the Harrier jump jet, is a family of jet-powered attack aircraft capable of vertical/short takeoff and landing operations (V/STOL). Named after the bird of prey, it was originally developed by British manufacturer Hawker Siddeley in the 1960s. The Harrier emerged as the most successful application of V/STOL technology and the only truly successful design of its kind to enter service for many years.

Role: V/STOL attack aircraft

Manufacturer: Hawker Siddeley; British Aerospace; McDonnell Douglas / Boeing

First flight: 28 December 1960 (P.1127)

Introduction: 1 April 1969

Status: In service with some operators

Design

The Harrier's V/STOL capability comes from its Rolls-Royce Pegasus turbofan engine, whose thrust is ducted through four rotating nozzles that can be swivelled from horizontal (for forward flight) to slightly past vertical, vectoring thrust for takeoff, landing, and hovering. Small reaction-control valves in the nose, tail, and wingtips provide control at low speeds where aerodynamic surfaces are ineffective. The aircraft has bicycle-style main landing gear with outrigger wheels on the wingtips.

Variants and service

The first-generation Harrier GR.1 entered Royal Air Force service in 1969, and the type was adopted by the United States Marine Corps as the AV-8A. A naval variant, the Sea Harrier, operated from Royal Navy aircraft carriers and achieved fame in the 1982 Falklands War, where Sea Harriers shot down 20 Argentine aircraft without air-to-air loss. The second-generation Harrier II, developed jointly by McDonnell Douglas and British Aerospace, featured a larger composite wing, improved avionics, and greater payload; it served as the RAF's Harrier GR5/7/9 and the USMC's AV-8B Harrier II and radar-equipped AV-8B+.

Britain retired its Harrier fleet in 2010 as a cost-saving measure. The US Marine Corps, the Spanish Navy, and the Italian Navy continued operating Harrier IIs into the 2020s, pending replacement by the F-35B Lightning II, the short-takeoff/vertical-landing variant of the Joint Strike Fighter whose lift system descends conceptually from the Harrier.
