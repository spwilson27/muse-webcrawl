The Codex Manesse (also called the Große Heidelberger Liederhandschrift, "Great Heidelberg Song Manuscript") is a illuminated manuscript songbook compiled in Zurich in the early fourteenth century. It is the single most comprehensive source of Middle High German Minnesang (courtly love lyric), containing works by 137 poets arranged by social rank from emperors and kings down to commoners.

Contents and miniatures

The codex is famous for its 137 full-page miniatures, each depicting a poet — such as Walther von der Vogelweide, Wolfram von Eschenbach, and Hartmann von Aue — in idealised scenes with coats of arms, making it an invaluable record of medieval heraldry, costume, and courtly culture.

History

The manuscript may have been commissioned by the Manesse family of Zurich, noted collectors of song. After centuries of travel through noble libraries — and a celebrated "rescue" return from Paris in 1888 — it is now preserved at Heidelberg University Library, where it is regarded as one of the treasures of German literature.
