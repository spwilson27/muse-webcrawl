The 1872 United States presidential election was the 22nd quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, 5 November 1872. Incumbent President Ulysses S. Grant of the Republican Party defeated Horace Greeley, the candidate jointly nominated by the Democratic Party and the breakaway Liberal Republican Party.

Background

The Liberal Republicans, alienated by corruption in the Grant administration and by continued military Reconstruction in the South, nominated newspaper editor Horace Greeley on a platform of civil service reform, amnesty and an end to Reconstruction. The Democrats, weakened and divided, endorsed Greeley despite his long history of denouncing their party. Grant campaigned on his record of enforcing civil rights in the South, where the Enforcement Acts had suppressed the Ku Klux Klan, and on prosperity.

Results

Grant won 55.6 per cent of the popular vote and carried 31 of 37 states, including most of the reconstructed South, where African American voters backed him heavily. Greeley carried only six states. Exhausted and bereaved by the death of his wife days before the election, Greeley died on 29 November 1872, before the Electoral College met. His pledged electors scattered their votes among several candidates, chiefly Thomas A. Hendricks, and three Georgia electors who voted for the deceased Greeley had their votes rejected by Congress.

Significance

Greeley remains the only major-party presidential nominee to die between the popular election and the Electoral College vote. The election also marked the last time until 2000 that a Republican carried a majority of Southern states in the way Grant did, and it was followed by the gradual collapse of Reconstruction after the disputed election of 1876.
