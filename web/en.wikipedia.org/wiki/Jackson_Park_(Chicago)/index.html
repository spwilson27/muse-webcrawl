Jackson Park (Chicago)

Jackson Park

Location: South Side, Chicago, Illinois

Area: about 542 acres (219 ha)

Created: 1871 (parkland reserved); redesigned for 1893 fair

Designers: Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux; Daniel Burnham

Status: On the National Register of Historic Places

Jackson Park is a 542-acre park on the South Side of Chicago, Illinois, bordering Lake Michigan in the Woodlawn, Hyde Park, and South Shore neighborhoods. Named for President Andrew Jackson, it was laid out in the 1870s by landscape architects Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux and became famous as the principal site of the World's Columbian Exposition of 1893.

World's Columbian Exposition

Under Daniel Burnham's direction, the marshy parkland was transformed in 1891–93 into the "White City": a Court of Honor lined with gleaming neoclassical buildings around a grand basin, with lagoons, canals, and a Wooded Island designed by Olmsted as a naturalistic refuge. Most fair buildings were temporary plaster constructions and were demolished or burned after the fair; the notable survivor, the Palace of Fine Arts, was rebuilt in stone and today houses the Griffin Museum of Science and Industry. A gilded replica of Daniel Chester French's Statue of the Republic ("Golden Lady") stands on the fair's basin site.

Features

The park includes the serene Wooded Island, the Osaka Japanese Garden (Phoenix Garden) on its western shore, beaches, a golf course, harbors, and the scenic Lake Shore Drive corridor. It was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1972.

Obama Presidential Center

In 2016 the park was selected as the site of the Obama Presidential Center, the library and museum of Barack Obama, the 44th president, whose home neighborhood of Hyde Park adjoins the park. The selection and construction — ground was broken in 2021 — drew both support for investment on the South Side and litigation and criticism over the use of historic parkland, which federal courts ultimately allowed to proceed.
