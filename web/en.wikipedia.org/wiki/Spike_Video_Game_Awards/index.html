Spike Video Game Awards

The Spike Video Game Awards (commonly known as the VGAs, and later the VGX) was an annual award show hosted by the American television network Spike, honouring the best video games of the year. First held in 2003 and produced by journalist Geoff Keighley, the show ran until 2013 and was famous — and sometimes notorious — for its celebrity presenters, world-premiere game trailers and comedic tone.

History

The inaugural ceremony took place in Las Vegas in December 2003. Categories included Game of the Year, Best Action Game, Best Sports Game and a "Gamer God" honorary award. Winners of Game of the Year over the run included Madden NFL 2004, Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas, The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion, BioShock, Grand Theft Auto IV, Uncharted 2: Among Thieves, Red Dead Redemption, The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim, The Walking Dead and, in 2013, Grand Theft Auto V.

Legacy

After Spike ended the show, Keighley launched a successor, The Game Awards, in 2014, which adopted a somewhat more industry-focused tone while retaining premieres and musical performances. The VGAs are remembered for popularising the televised year-end games awards format and for debuting major trailers, including early reveals of several blockbuster titles.
