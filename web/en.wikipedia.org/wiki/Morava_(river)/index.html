The Morava (German: March) is a river in Central Europe, a left tributary of the Danube. It is the principal river of Moravia, the historical region of the Czech Republic that takes its name from the river. The Morava rises on the slopes of Králický Sněžník in the northeastern Czech Republic, flows south through Olomouc and the Moravian lowlands, and for much of its lower course forms the border between Slovakia and the Czech Republic, and then between Slovakia and Austria, before joining the Danube at Devín, now part of Bratislava, beneath Devín Castle.

Geography

The river is 352 km long and drains a basin of about 26,658 km². Its largest tributary is the Thaya (Dyje), which joins it from the west near Hohenau an der March; other significant tributaries include the Bečva, the Haná, and the Myjava. The lower Morava flows through one of Central Europe's most important remaining floodplain landscapes — a mosaic of wet meadows and floodplain forests protected under the Ramsar Convention and as part of the trilateral Morava–Dyje floodplain site shared by the Czech Republic, Slovakia, and Austria.

History

The Morava valley was the heartland of the 9th-century Slavic state of Great Moravia, whose fortified centres such as Mikulčice stood along its banks. In Roman times the river (Latin: Marus) marked the northern frontier zone of the province of Pannonia; the emperor Marcus Aurelius campaigned beyond it during the Marcomannic Wars. For centuries the lower river formed part of the frontier between the Habsburg lands and, successively, Hungary and the Ottoman sphere, and during the Cold War it lay along the Iron Curtain between Austria and Czechoslovakia.

Ecology

The Morava floodplains support rich bird life, beaver, and old-growth floodplain forest. Cross-border nature protection and restoration projects have expanded since 1989, and the river corridor functions as a major ecological link between the Alps, the Carpathians, and the Danube basin.
