Bifunctional

FieldChemistry
MeaningHaving two functional groups or functions

In chemistry, bifunctional describes a molecule, reagent or catalyst that possesses two functional groups or performs two distinct functions. The term is used across organic chemistry, polymer science and biochemistry.

Examples

Amino acids are bifunctional, carrying both an amine and a carboxylic acid group, which allows them to polymerise into proteins. Bifunctional monomers such as diols and diacids form polyesters and polyamides through step-growth polymerisation. Bifunctional catalysts carry two active sites that cooperate in a single transformation, and bifunctional antibodies are engineered to bind two different targets at once.

Related terms

Analogous terms include monofunctional (one functional group) and polyfunctional (several). Bifunctionality is a key design principle in crosslinkers, linkers for bioconjugation and chelating agents.
