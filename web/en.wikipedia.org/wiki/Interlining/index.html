Interlining

Interlining is a practice in the airline industry in which a passenger's journey is ticketed across flights operated by different airlines under a single ticket, with checked baggage transferred between carriers. Interline agreements are commercial arrangements between airlines covering ticketing, baggage handling, and the settlement of revenue through clearing houses such as IATA's Clearing House.

Interline agreements

An interline traffic agreement allows one airline (the marketing/plating carrier) to sell seats on another's flights on a single electronic ticket. Baggage agreements specify whether bags are checked through to the final destination. Most major airlines have dozens to hundreds of interline partners; membership of a global alliance (Star Alliance, SkyTeam, Oneworld) typically includes interline ticketing among members as a baseline.

Relation to other arrangements

Interlining is less integrated than a codeshare, in which one airline places its flight code on another's service, and less than a joint venture. "Virtual interlining" offered by some online travel agencies stitches separate tickets together without any airline agreement, leaving the passenger to bear misconnection risk.

Freight

Interline arrangements in air cargo similarly allow freight forwarders and airlines to route shipments across multiple carriers under a single air waybill.
