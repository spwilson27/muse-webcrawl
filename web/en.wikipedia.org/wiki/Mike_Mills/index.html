Michael Edward Mills (born 17 December 1958) is an American multi-instrumentalist, singer and composer best known as a founding member, bassist and backing vocalist of the alternative rock band R.E.M. Mills' melodic bass lines, piano playing and harmony vocals — often described as the band's secret weapon — were central to its sound from 1980 until its amicable split in 2011.

Early life

Mills was born in Orange County, California, and grew up in Macon, Georgia, where he met future bandmate Bill Berry at school. He attended the University of Georgia in Athens, where he, Berry, Peter Buck and Michael Stipe formed R.E.M. in 1980.

Career with R.E.M.

With R.E.M., Mills released fifteen studio albums, from Murmur (1983) and the breakthrough Out of Time (1991) — featuring "Losing My Religion" and "Shiny Happy People", on which Mills sang prominent harmonies and shared lead vocals — to Collapse into Now (2011). He sang lead on several tracks, including "Near Wild Heaven" and "Superman". Mills contributed piano and keyboards throughout, co-wrote much of the band's material, and was inducted with the band into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2007.

After R.E.M.

Since 2011 Mills has toured with the baseball-themed supergroup The Baseball Project, performed orchestral collaborations including a concerto tour with violinist Robert McDuffie (Concerto for Violin, Rock Band and String Orchestra), and campaigned on political and environmental causes in Georgia.
