Slip ring

A slip ring is an electromechanical device that allows the transmission of power and electrical signals from a stationary to a rotating structure. Also called a rotary electrical joint, collector ring, or electric swivel, it consists of conductive rings mounted on the rotating shaft, against which stationary brushes (contacts of graphite, metal fibre, or precious metal) press and slide.

Applications

Slip rings are used wherever continuous rotation with electrical connection is required: in wound-rotor induction motors and alternators, wind turbines (to transfer power and data from the rotating hub and to control blade pitch), radar antennas, CT scanners — where the rotating gantry carries X-ray equipment — cable reels, rotary tables, packaging machinery, and amusement rides. In alternators, slip rings deliver excitation current to the rotor field windings, carrying much less current than the main output, which is taken from the stationary stator.

Design and alternatives

Assemblies may contain from two to over a hundred circuits, transmitting anything from high power to delicate Ethernet, video, or fibre-optic signals — the latter via fibre-optic rotary joints. Sliding contacts suffer wear, electrical noise, and limited life; brushless alternatives include mercury-wetted contacts (now restricted for toxicity), inductive (wireless) rotary couplers, and capacitive or optical links for data. Pancake (flat, axial) slip rings suit height-constrained equipment, while through-bore designs allow a shaft or hydraulics to pass through the centre.
