Arts Council of Wales

The Arts Council of Wales supports arts across Wales, including national companies

The Arts Council of Wales (Welsh: Cyngor Celfyddydau Cymru) is a Welsh Government-sponsored body responsible for funding and developing the arts in Wales. Established in 1994 when the former Arts Council of Great Britain was split into separate councils for England, Scotland and Wales, it distributes public funding from the Welsh Government and the National Lottery.

Role

The council funds a portfolio of revenue-funded organisations, including Welsh National Opera, the BBC National Orchestra of Wales, National Theatre Wales and Theatr Cymru, as well as galleries, theatres, literature and community arts projects. It publishes strategy documents in both Welsh and English and has promoted the principle of "arts for all" alongside support for the Welsh language in the arts.

History

Arts funding in Wales was administered from 1946 by the Welsh Arts Council, a committee of the Arts Council of Great Britain. In 1994 it became an independent body under its present name. Periodic Welsh Government reviews, including the 2016 Baroness Andrews review of culture and poverty, have examined its remit and its relationship with Amgueddfa Cymru – Museum Wales and the National Library of Wales.
