Howard Thurston (July 20, 1869 – April 13, 1936) was an American stage magician, regarded as the most famous magician of his era and the successor to Harry Kellar as the leading illusionist in the United States. His travelling magic show was for years the largest and most lavish of its kind, requiring several railway cars to transport its scenery and equipment.

Career

Born in Columbus, Ohio, Thurston ran away as a boy to join a circus and taught himself card manipulation. His elegant card flourishes — including rising cards and cards thrown into the audience that returned like boomerangs — brought him vaudeville success and a celebrated engagement at London's Palace Theatre. In 1908 he purchased the great illusion show of the retiring Harry Kellar, inheriting its apparatus and, with it, the mantle of America's premier magician.

Thurston's full-evening spectacle featured grand illusions such as levitations, vanishings of automobiles and horses, and the sawing of a woman in pieces. His posters — among the most striking lithographs of the era — billed him as the "King of Cards" and promised wonders beyond those of his rival Harry Houdini, with whom he competed for the title of the age's leading magician. Thurston also wrote popular books on magic and card tricks for amateur performers.

Death and legacy

Thurston died in Miami Beach, Florida, in 1936, following a cerebral haemorrhage. His show continued briefly under others, but the age of the great touring magic spectacles faded with him. His elaborate posters remain highly prized by collectors, and he is remembered as the last of the great American magician-showmen of the golden age of stage magic.
