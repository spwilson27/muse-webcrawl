The Aden Emergency, also called the Radfan Uprising or the South Arabian Revolution, was an armed rebellion by nationalist groups against British colonial rule in the colony of Aden and the protectorates of the Federation of South Arabia between 1963 and 1967. It was part of the wider decolonization of the British Empire and of the Arab Cold War, with the rebels backed by Egypt and the British intent on retaining their strategic base at the entrance to the Red Sea.

Background

Aden had been a British possession since 1839 and by the 1950s was one of the busiest ports in the world, serving shipping through the Suez Canal. In 1963 Britain merged the colony with the hinterland protectorates to form the Federation of South Arabia, a move opposed by nationalists who saw it as entrenching British influence. The National Liberation Front (NLF), founded in 1963 and influenced by Egyptian president Gamal Abdel Nasser's Arab nationalism, launched its campaign on 14 October 1963 by attacking a British patrol in the Radfan mountains; a grenade attack on the British High Commissioner in December led to the declaration of a state of emergency.

Conflict

British forces fought campaigns in the Radfan in 1964 and then faced a growing urban insurgency in Aden itself, involving grenade attacks, assassinations of police and intelligencers, and strikes. A rival organization, the Front for the Liberation of Occupied South Yemen (FLOSY), competed with the NLF, and the two groups fought each other as well as the British. In June 1967, Arab police and army units mutinied, killing British soldiers in the Crater district; the district was reoccupied in July by the Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders under Lieutenant Colonel Colin Mitchell, popularly known as "Mad Mitch." The violence formed a backdrop to the wider regional crisis of the Six-Day War.

Withdrawal and aftermath

The British Labour government decided in 1966 to withdraw from its bases "east of Suez." The last British troops left Aden on 29–30 November 1967, and power passed to the NLF, which proclaimed the People's Republic of South Yemen — the only avowedly Marxist state in the Arab world, which in 1990 unified with North Yemen to form the present Republic of Yemen. Around 90 British personnel were killed during the emergency, along with hundreds of local combatants and civilians.
