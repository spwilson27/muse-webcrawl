The Eurasian wryneck (Jynx torquilla) is a species of bird in the woodpecker family. It breeds in temperate Europe and Asia and migrates to winter in tropical Africa and southern Asia. Its name derives from its ability to twist its head nearly 180 degrees; when disturbed at the nest it makes snake-like hissing and writhing movements.

Description

About 16–18 cm long, the wryneck is cryptically patterned in browns and greys, feeding mainly on ants, which it takes from the ground with its long tongue. Unlike true woodpeckers it has a softer bill and often perches horizontally.

Conservation

Populations have declined in parts of northwestern Europe, including England, where it is now a rare passage migrant rather than the regular breeder it was in the 19th century, largely due to changes in grassland and orchard management.
