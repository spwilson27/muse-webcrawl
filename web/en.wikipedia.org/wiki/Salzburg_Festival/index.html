The Salzburg Festival (German: Salzburger Festspiele) is a prominent festival of music and drama established in 1920. It is held each summer for five weeks starting in late July in Salzburg, Austria, the birthplace of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart. It is widely regarded as one of the world's leading classical music festivals.

History

The festival was founded by poet Hugo von Hofmannsthal, theatre director Max Reinhardt, composer Richard Strauss, scenic designer Alfred Roller, and conductor Franz Schalk, who sought to create a cultural counterweight to the trauma of the First World War. The inaugural performance, Hofmannsthal's morality play Jedermann ("Everyman"), was staged by Reinhardt on the steps of Salzburg Cathedral on 22 August 1920; the play remains a festival signature, performed in Cathedral Square nearly every year since.

The first opera productions followed in 1922, and the festival's first purpose-built home, the Festspielhaus, opened in 1925, later joined by the Haus für Mozart (formerly the Kleines Festspielhaus) and the Grosses Festspielhaus, opened in 1960 under Herbert von Karajan. After the Anschluss of 1938 the festival lost Reinhardt and many Jewish artists to exile; it resumed after the war and grew under conductors including Karajan, and later artistic directors such as Gerard Mortier and Markus Hinterhäuser.

Programme and venues

Each summer the festival presents operas, orchestral and chamber concerts, recitals, and spoken theatre across more than a dozen venues, including the Felsenreitschule (the former riding school carved into the Mönchsberg rock), the collegiate church, and open-air stages. The resident orchestra is the Vienna Philharmonic. Offshoots include the Whitsun Festival (Pfingstfestspiele), directed for many years by Cecilia Bartoli, and the Easter Festival founded by Karajan in 1967, which is administratively separate.
