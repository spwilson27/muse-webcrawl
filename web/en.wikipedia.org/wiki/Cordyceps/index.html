Cordyceps

Kingdom: Fungi

Phylum: Ascomycota

Class: Sordariomycetes

Order: Hypocreales

Family: Cordycipitaceae (in part)

Genus: Cordyceps (Fr.) Link

Cordyceps is a genus of ascomycete fungi (sac fungi) that includes several hundred described species, most of which are parasites on insects and other arthropods; some parasitize other fungi, notably the false truffles of the genus Elaphomyces. When a Cordyceps fungus attacks a host, its mycelium invades and eventually replaces the host tissue, from which elongated fruiting bodies (ascocarps) grow out to release spores. Taxonomic revisions in the 2000s moved many former Cordyceps species into related genera such as Ophiocordyceps.

Notable species

Ophiocordyceps sinensis, the "caterpillar fungus" (formerly Cordyceps sinensis), parasitizes ghost moth larvae in the high meadows of the Tibetan Plateau and Himalayas. The mummified caterpillar with its fungal stalk — called yartsa gunbu in Tibetan and dōng chóng xià cǎo ("winter worm, summer grass") in Chinese — has been used in traditional Chinese and Tibetan medicine for centuries and commands extremely high prices, making it a major source of income in rural regions and a driver of overharvesting concerns.

Ophiocordyceps unilateralis is famous for manipulating the behavior of infected carpenter ants: the fungus induces the ant to climb vegetation and clamp onto a leaf vein in a "death grip" at a height and microclimate favorable to fungal growth, after which a fruiting body grows from the ant's head. Cordyceps militaris, a bright orange species parasitic on Lepidoptera pupae, is cultivated commercially as a substitute for the rarer caterpillar fungus.

Uses

Preparations of cultivated Cordyceps mycelium are widely sold as dietary supplements claimed to improve stamina, libido, and kidney and lung function; clinical evidence for most such claims remains limited and inconclusive. The genus also yields pharmacologically interesting compounds: cordycepin (3′-deoxyadenosine) from C. militaris has been studied for anticancer and antiviral activity, and the immunosuppressant drug ciclosporin was derived from Tolypocladium inflatum, an anamorph stage related to the cordyceps group. Myriocin, from Isaria sinclairii, led to the drug fingolimod used in multiple sclerosis.

In popular culture

Fictionalized, behavior-manipulating Cordyceps fungi that infect humans are the premise of the video game and television series The Last of Us and feature in the novel The Girl with All the Gifts.
