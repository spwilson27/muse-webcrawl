K'iche' people

RegionsGuatemalan highlands (Quiché, Totonicapán, Quetzaltenango, Sololá)
LanguageK'iche', Spanish
ReligionRoman Catholicism, Evangelical Christianity, Maya religion

The K'iche' (also spelled Quiché) are an Indigenous Maya people of the Guatemalan highlands and one of the largest Indigenous groups in Guatemala. Their language, K'iche', has the most speakers of any Mayan language.

History

Before the Spanish conquest, the K'iche' kingdom of Q'umarkaj (Utatlán) dominated much of the western highlands. It was conquered in 1524 by Pedro de Alvarado and his allies. The most famous work of colonial-era Indigenous literature, the Popol Vuh, was written in K'iche' in the 1550s and recounts K'iche' mythology and dynastic history.

Modern era

K'iche' communities suffered severe violence during Guatemala's civil war (1960–1996), particularly in the early 1980s. The K'iche' activist Rigoberta Menchú received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1992. Today the K'iche' maintain strong traditions of weaving, markets — such as Chichicastenango — and municipal Indigenous governance, alongside widespread bilingual education efforts.
