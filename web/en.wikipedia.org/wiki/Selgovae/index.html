Selgovae

The Selgovae were a Celtic tribe of the late Iron Age and Roman period who inhabited what is now south-western Scotland, roughly the modern regions of Dumfries and Galloway and the Stewartry of Kirkcudbright, along the northern shore of the Solway Firth. They are known almost entirely from the geography of Ptolemy, written in the second century AD.

Ptolemy's account

Ptolemy placed the Selgovae between the Novantae to the west and the Votadini to the east, with the Damnonii to the north. He named four towns of the Selgovae: Carbantorigum, Uxellum, Corda, and Trimontium. Trimontium, the "three mountains," has been identified with the Roman fort complex at Newstead near Melrose, beneath the three Eildon Hills — though this lies some distance east of the tribe's presumed core territory, which has led scholars to debate the extent of their lands or the location of their towns.

History

Little is known of the Selgovae before the Roman invasions of northern Britain. Their territory was overrun during the campaigns of Gnaeus Julius Agricola around AD 79–80, and the region later lay in the contested zone between Hadrian's Wall and the Antonine Wall. After the Roman withdrawal, the area formed part of the Brittonic-speaking kingdoms of the Old North.
