Wolf–Rayet star

Wolf–Rayet stars, named after French astronomers Charles Wolf and Georges Rayet who discovered them in 1867, are rare, massive, evolved stars with strong, broad emission lines and powerful stellar winds that strip away their outer hydrogen layers.

Characteristics

Wolf–Rayet stars have surface temperatures typically between 30,000 K and 200,000 K and lose mass at rates up to a hundred thousand times that of the Sun. Their spectra are classified as WN (nitrogen-rich), WC (carbon-rich), or WO (oxygen-rich), reflecting successive stages of nuclear burning products exposed at the surface.

Evolution

They are thought to be the progenitors of Type Ib and Ic supernovae and possibly long gamma-ray bursts. Famous examples include Gamma Velorum and WR 104. Only a few hundred are known in the Milky Way, though many more are inferred in other galaxies.
