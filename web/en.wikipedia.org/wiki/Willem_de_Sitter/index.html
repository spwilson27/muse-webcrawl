Willem de Sitter (6 May 1872 – 20 November 1934) was a Dutch mathematician, physicist and astronomer who made major contributions to physical cosmology and to the study of Jupiter's moons.

Career

De Sitter studied mathematics and astronomy at Groningen and worked at the Cape Observatory in South Africa before joining Leiden Observatory. In 1908 he became professor of astronomy at the University of Leiden, and from 1919 until his death he directed Leiden Observatory, building it into a leading research institution. Dirk Brouwer and Jan Oort were among those who worked under his influence.

Cosmology

De Sitter was an early expositor of Albert Einstein's general theory of relativity in the English-speaking world, corresponding with Einstein and Arthur Eddington during the First World War. In 1917 he published a solution of Einstein's field equations with a positive cosmological constant but no matter — the "de Sitter universe", describing an exponentially expanding empty universe whose geometry is now called de Sitter space. In 1932 he and Einstein jointly proposed the Einstein–de Sitter model, a flat matter-filled expanding universe that served as the standard cosmological model for decades.

Legacy

De Sitter space remains fundamental in cosmology and theoretical physics, including modern discussions of inflation and dark energy. The lunar crater De Sitter and the asteroid 1686 De Sitter are named after him.
