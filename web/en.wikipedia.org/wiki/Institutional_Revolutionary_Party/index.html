The Institutional Revolutionary Party (Spanish: Partido Revolucionario Institucional, PRI) is a political party in Mexico that was founded in 1929 and held uninterrupted power in the country for 71 years, from 1929 to 2000, first as the National Revolutionary Party (Partido Nacional Revolucionario, PNR), then as the Party of the Mexican Revolution (Partido de la Revolución Mexicana, PRM) and finally as the PRI from 1946.

Formed by the powerful revolutionary general and former president Plutarco Elías Calles in the aftermath of the assassination of president-elect Álvaro Obregón, the party was created as a vehicle to institutionalise the power of the victorious factions of the Mexican Revolution and to end the cycle of military rebellions that followed the conflict. Every president of Mexico between 1929 and 2000 was a member of the party, in a period frequently described by critics as a "perfect dictatorship" — a phrase coined by the Peruvian writer Mario Vargas Llosa.

History

Foundation and early decades

Calles founded the PNR in March 1929, uniting regional parties and revolutionary caudillos under a single organisation. During the period known as the Maximato (1928–1934), Calles remained the power behind a succession of puppet presidents. In 1934 Lázaro Cárdenas became president and broke with Calles, reorganising the party as the PRM in 1938 along corporatist lines, with sectors representing peasants, labour, the military and the "popular" sector. Cárdenas carried out sweeping land reform and nationalised the oil industry in 1938.

Hegemony, 1946–1988

Renamed the PRI in 1946, the party presided over the "Mexican Miracle" of rapid economic growth in the 1950s and 1960s. Its dominance was maintained through a combination of economic patronage, control of electoral machinery, co-optation of unions and the press, and periodic repression, most notoriously the Tlatelolco massacre of student protesters in 1968. Presidential succession was managed through the dedazo, the incumbent's designation of his successor.

Decline and loss of power

The disputed 1988 election, in which the official count gave victory to Carlos Salinas de Gortari amid allegations of fraud, marked a turning point. In 2000 the PRI lost the presidency to Vicente Fox of the National Action Party (PAN), ending 71 years of rule. The party returned to power in 2012 with Enrique Peña Nieto, but lost the presidency again in 2018 to Andrés Manuel López Obrador of Morena, after which it declined to a minor force in national politics.

Ideology

The PRI historically described its ideology as "revolutionary nationalism". In practice it was a catch-all, corporatist party that shifted between statist, protectionist policies and, under the technocratic administrations of the 1980s and 1990s, neoliberal reforms including privatisation and the North American Free Trade Agreement.
