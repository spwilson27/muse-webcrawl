Brand journalism

Brand journalism is a form of content marketing in which a company produces news-style content — articles, videos, podcasts, and reports — using the techniques and formats of journalism, in order to build audience engagement and brand awareness rather than to advertise products directly. It is closely related to brand publishing and corporate media, and its practitioners are often professional journalists hired by brands to run in-house editorial operations, sometimes called brand newsrooms.

History

Although companies have published customer magazines since the nineteenth century — John Deere's The Furrow, founded in 1895, is frequently cited as an early example — the modern concept of brand journalism grew with the decline of traditional media advertising in the 2000s and 2010s. The term was popularised by former McDonald's marketing executive Larry Light, who described brand journalism as a marketing approach that tells a brand's story across multiple media in a journalistic, non-linear fashion. Technology and retail companies such as Red Bull (with Red Bull Media House), Coca-Cola, Adobe, and Marriott built substantial editorial operations, and agencies and publishers established branded-content studios, such as the New York Times' T Brand Studio.

Practice

Brand journalism typically involves producing content of genuine interest to a target audience — industry reporting, lifestyle features, data journalism, and interviews — which mentions the sponsoring brand lightly or not at all. Distribution is through owned channels (websites, newsletters, social media) and paid promotion. Advocates argue the approach builds trust and long-term audience relationships that conventional advertising cannot; success is measured in engagement, audience growth, and brand perception rather than immediate sales.

Criticism

Critics, including many journalists and media ethicists, argue that brand journalism blurs the line between editorial content and advertising, can mislead audiences about the independence of the material, and contributes to the hollowing-out of independent media by absorbing journalistic talent and audience attention. Regulatory bodies in several countries require sponsored content to be clearly labelled, and news organisations have debated standards for distinguishing branded content from their own reporting.
