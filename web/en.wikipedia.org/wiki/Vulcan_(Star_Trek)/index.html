Vulcan (Star Trek)

Vulcans are a fictional extraterrestrial humanoid species in the Star Trek universe, known for their devotion to logic and the suppression of emotion. They originate from the planet Vulcan, a hot, desert world, and are distinguished by pointed ears, upswept eyebrows, and green, copper-based blood. Vulcans were the first extraterrestrials to make contact with humanity (depicted in Star Trek: First Contact) and are founding members of the United Federation of Planets.

Physiology and culture

Vulcans are stronger and longer-lived than humans, with an inner eyelid and a heart located where a human's liver would be. They are touch-telepaths, capable of the mind meld. Though biologically passionate and historically warlike, Vulcan society was transformed by the philosopher Surak in the 4th century CE, whose teachings of logic and emotional control — later codified in practices such as the kolinahr discipline — averted self-destruction. Vulcans nonetheless undergo the pon farr, a periodic mating drive, every seven years of adult life. Their philosophy is summarised by the principle of IDIC — "Infinite Diversity in Infinite Combinations" — and by the greeting "Live long and prosper", accompanied by the split-fingered Vulcan salute devised by actor Leonard Nimoy.

Notable Vulcans and portrayal

The archetypal Vulcan is Spock (Leonard Nimoy), the half-human science officer of the starship Enterprise. Other major Vulcan characters include Sarek, T'Pol of Enterprise, Tuvok of Voyager, and Saavik. In the 2009 film Star Trek, set in an alternate timeline, the planet Vulcan is destroyed by the Romulan Nero, leaving the Vulcans an endangered species — a fate that does not occur in the original timeline. Vulcans and Romulans share common ancestry, the Romulans being descendants of Vulcans who rejected Surak's teachings and left the planet.
