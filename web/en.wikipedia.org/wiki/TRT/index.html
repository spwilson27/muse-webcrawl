TRT, the Turkish Radio and Television Corporation (Turkish: Türkiye Radyo ve Televizyon Kurumu), is Turkey's national public broadcaster. Founded on 1 May 1964, it held a monopoly on radio and television broadcasting in Turkey until the launch of private stations in the early 1990s. TRT operates numerous television channels and radio networks in Turkish and several other languages, broadcasting domestically and internationally.

History

Radio broadcasting in Turkey began in 1927 with stations in Istanbul and Ankara operated by a private concession company; the state took over broadcasting in the 1930s. The Turkish Radio and Television Corporation was established by law in 1964 as an autonomous public institution, taking over the radio networks. Regular television broadcasts began experimentally in Ankara in 1968, and TRT's television service gradually expanded nationwide during the 1970s, introducing color broadcasts in the early 1980s.

For decades TRT was the sole television broadcaster in Turkey. The monopoly effectively ended in 1990 when private satellite channels began broadcasting from abroad, and the constitution was amended in 1993 to permit private broadcasting. In the following decades TRT expanded into thematic channels — news, sports, documentaries, children's programming, and Kurdish-language broadcasting with TRT Kurdî — and in 2015 launched TRT World, an English-language international news channel headquartered in Istanbul.

Services

TRT's principal channels include TRT 1 (general entertainment and drama), TRT Haber (news), TRT Spor (sport), TRT Belgesel (documentaries), TRT Çocuk (children), TRT Müzik, and the international services TRT World (English) and TRT Arabi (Arabic). Its television dramas, particularly historical series, have achieved large audiences abroad. TRT radio operates national and regional networks, and the Voice of Turkey provides shortwave and online external services in many languages.

TRT is a member of the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) and organized the Eurovision Song Contest 2004 in Istanbul after Turkey's victory the previous year. The corporation was historically funded by a share of electricity bills and license fees on receiving devices, supplemented by government appropriations and advertising, an arrangement that has periodically drawn debate about its editorial independence.
