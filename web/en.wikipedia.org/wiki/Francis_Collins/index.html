Francis Collins

Francis Sellers Collins (born 14 April 1950) is an American physician-geneticist who discovered genes associated with a number of diseases and led the Human Genome Project. He served as director of the National Institutes of Health (NIH) in Bethesda, Maryland, from 2009 to 2021, under three presidents.

Career

Collins was educated at the University of Virginia, Yale University (PhD in physical chemistry) and the University of North Carolina (MD). As a researcher he used "positional cloning" to identify genes for cystic fibrosis (1989, with Lap-Chee Tsui's team), neurofibromatosis, Huntington's disease, and other conditions. He directed the National Human Genome Research Institute from 1993 and led the public Human Genome Project to its completion in 2003. As NIH director (appointed by President Barack Obama), he oversaw initiatives including the BRAIN Initiative and the All of Us precision-medicine programme, and led the agency through the COVID-19 pandemic response era.

Views and writings

Collins is an evangelical Christian who has written on science and faith, notably in The Language of God (2006), and founded the BioLogos Foundation to promote dialogue between science and religion. He received the Templeton Prize in 2020.
