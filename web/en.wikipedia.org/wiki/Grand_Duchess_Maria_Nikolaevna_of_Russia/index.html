Grand Duchess Maria Nikolaevna of Russia

Grand Duchess Maria Nikolaevna of Russia (26 June 1899 – 17 July 1918) was the third daughter of Tsar Nicholas II of Russia and Tsarina Alexandra Fyodorovna. She was murdered with her family by the Bolsheviks in Yekaterinburg in 1918, during the Russian Civil War.

Life

Maria was raised with her sisters Olga, Tatiana, and Anastasia — known collectively by the acronym OTMA — and her younger brother, Tsarevich Alexei, who suffered from haemophilia. She was described as sweet-natured and pretty, with blue eyes and light brown hair, and was considered the most beautiful of the sisters by some contemporaries. During the First World War she visited wounded soldiers in hospital with her mother and sisters, though she was too young to work as a nurse like her elder sisters.

Death and canonization

After the February Revolution, the family was held under house arrest at Tsarskoye Selo, then exiled to Tobolsk and finally to Yekaterinburg, where they were shot and bayoneted in the basement of the Ipatiev House on the night of 16–17 July 1918. Maria's remains were among those identified by DNA testing; two sets of remains found in 2007 were confirmed in 2008 as belonging to Maria and Alexei. The Russian Orthodox Church canonized the family as passion bearers in 2000.
