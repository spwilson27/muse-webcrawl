Free Peru (Spanish: Perú Libre), officially the National Political Party Free Peru (Partido Político Nacional Perú Libre), is a socialist political party in Peru. Founded in the Junín Region by former regional governor Vladimir Cerrón, the party describes itself as Marxist–Leninist and advocates a popular, nationalist, and anti-imperialist programme, including a new constitution, greater state control over natural resources, and free public education and health care.

History

The movement began in 2008 as the Free Peru Regional Political Movement in Junín, where Cerrón was elected regional governor in 2010 and again in 2018. It was registered as a national political party in 2016. The party initially achieved modest national results, but built a strong organisation among teachers' unions and provincial networks in the Peruvian Andes and south.

Free Peru rose to national prominence in the 2021 general election. Its presidential candidate, rural schoolteacher and union leader Pedro Castillo, unexpectedly placed first in the first round and narrowly defeated Keiko Fujimori in the run-off, winning the presidency. The party also won 37 seats in Congress, becoming the largest single bloc, though it soon split into rival factions and many of its congresspeople left to form other groups.

Ideology

The party's statutes declare adherence to Marxism–Leninism and the thought of José Carlos Mariátegui, the founder of Peruvian socialism. Its programme calls for a constituent assembly to replace the 1993 constitution, nationalisation or renegotiation of contracts over mining and hydrocarbons, agrarian reform, and opposition to what it terms United States imperialism. Critics have accused the party of authoritarian tendencies and of links to radical movements; its leaders deny these charges.

Electoral performance

Before 2021 the party's national vote share was small, but it won a number of regional and municipal governments in the central and southern highlands. Following Castillo's removal from office in December 2022 after his attempt to dissolve Congress, Free Peru remained a significant force in Congress and in regional politics, while Cerrón faced legal proceedings and spent periods as a fugitive from corruption charges.
