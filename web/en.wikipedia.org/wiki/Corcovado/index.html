Corcovado

Corcovado
Elevation: 710 m (2,329 ft)
Location: Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
Range: Carioca Range, Serra do Mar

Corcovado (Portuguese for "hunchback") is a mountain in central Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. It is a 710-metre (2,329 ft) granite peak located in the Tijuca Forest, a national park. It is sometimes confused with the larger Sugarloaf Mountain nearby.

Christ the Redeemer

The mountain is crowned by the famous statue of Christ the Redeemer (Cristo Redentor), a 30-metre (98 ft) tall Art Deco statue of Jesus, created by Brazilian engineer Heitor da Silva Costa together with French sculptor Paul Landowski, and inaugurated on 12 October 1931. The statue and its 8-metre pedestal were built of reinforced concrete and soapstone, and in 2007 were named one of the New7Wonders of the World.

Access

The summit is reached by the Corcovado Rack Railway, a 3.8 km cog railway opened in 1884 by Emperor Pedro II — the first electrified railway in Brazil — which carries visitors from Cosme Velho to a station below the summit, from which escalators and lifts (installed in 2003) lead to the statue. The peak offers panoramic views over Rio, Guanabara Bay, Sugarloaf Mountain and the beaches of Copacabana and Ipanema.
