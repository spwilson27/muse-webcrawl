Mexico Beach, Florida

Placeholder image: The Mexico Beach shoreline on Florida's Forgotten Coast

Mexico Beach is a city in Bay County, Florida, United States. It is located on the Gulf of Mexico along Florida's Panhandle, on the stretch of coast marketed as the "Forgotten Coast." The population was 1,072 at the 2010 census and grew modestly before the city was devastated by Hurricane Michael in 2018. Mexico Beach is known for its relaxed pace, strict limits on high-rise development, sport fishing in the Gulf, and its artificial reef program.

History

The community developed in the mid-twentieth century as a quiet beach and fishing town east of Tyndall Air Force Base and northwest of Port St. Joe. Growth was deliberately kept small-scale: building codes long limited heights and density, preserving a small-town atmosphere unusual on Florida's Gulf coast.

Hurricane Michael

On October 10, 2018, Hurricane Michael made landfall just east of Mexico Beach near Tyndall Air Force Base as a Category 5 hurricane with sustained winds of 160 mph (260 km/h) — the strongest storm on record to strike the Florida Panhandle. The storm surge and extreme winds destroyed or severely damaged the great majority of structures in Mexico Beach; aerial surveys showed whole blocks of beach homes reduced to foundations or swept away entirely. Remarkably, a beachfront house built to exceed code with reinforced concrete — dubbed the "Sand Palace" — survived largely intact and became widely cited in discussions of resilient construction.

Recovery and geography

Rebuilding proceeded under revised, stricter construction codes, and the city has gradually restored its housing stock, marina, and beach amenities while debating how to balance growth with resilience. Mexico Beach covers about 1.8 square miles, lies along U.S. Route 98, and sits on the boundary between the Eastern and Central time zones. The waters offshore are noted for charter fishing for species such as red snapper, grouper, and king mackerel.
