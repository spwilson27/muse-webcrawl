Gramm–Rudman–Hollings Balanced Budget Act

Gramm–Rudman–Hollings Act
EnactedDecember 12, 1985
SponsorsPhil Gramm, Warren Rudman, Ernest Hollings
PurposeFederal deficit reduction through automatic spending cuts

The Gramm–Rudman–Hollings Balanced Budget and Emergency Deficit Control Act of 1985 was a United States federal law intended to reduce the federal budget deficit through a series of declining annual deficit targets, enforced by automatic across-the-board spending cuts known as sequestration if the targets were not met.

Provisions

The Act set maximum deficit amounts for fiscal years 1986 through 1991, aiming for a balanced budget by 1991. If the projected deficit exceeded the target, the President was required to issue a sequestration order cutting non-exempt spending programs by a uniform percentage, split between defence and domestic programs.

Constitutional challenge

In Bowsher v. Synar (1986), the Supreme Court struck down the provision giving the Comptroller General — an officer accountable to Congress — the power to determine the cuts, on separation-of-powers grounds. A revised version of the Act was passed in 1987 with the mechanism reassigned, and its targets were later replaced by the Budget Enforcement Act of 1990.
