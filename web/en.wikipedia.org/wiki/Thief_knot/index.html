Thief knot

The thief knot resembles the reef (square) knot except that the free, or working, ends are on opposite sides. It is said to have been used by sailors who tied their belongings in a bundle: if a thief untied the bundle and retied it with a common reef knot, the tampering would be revealed by the changed knot.

Description

The thief knot is formed like a reef knot but with one end passed differently so that the standing parts emerge on opposite sides from the working ends. It is far less secure than the reef knot and can capsize or spill under load, so it should never be used where safety matters. Its interest is mainly historical and as a cautionary example of how similar two knots can look while behaving differently.

Related knots

Closely related bends and binding knots include the reef knot, the granny knot, and the grief knot, which differ only in the arrangement and orientation of the ends.
