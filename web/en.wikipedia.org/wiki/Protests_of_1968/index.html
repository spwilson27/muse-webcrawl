Protests of 1968

The protests of 1968 comprised a worldwide escalation of social conflicts, which was predominantly characterised by popular rebellions against state militaries and bureaucracies. In the United States, protests were marked by opposition to the Vietnam War, while civil rights, student and labour movements converged in many countries. The year is widely seen as a cultural and political turning point of the post-war era — a "global revolt" of the post-war generation against established authority.

Background

Post-war economic growth and expanding universities produced a large, educated youth cohort coming of age amid the Cold War, decolonisation and the televised Vietnam War. The American civil rights movement provided tactics and inspiration, while New Left ideas critiqued both Western capitalism and Soviet-style communism.

Major movements

France — May 1968

Student occupations at Nanterre and the Sorbonne triggered clashes with police in Paris's Latin Quarter in May; a general strike of some ten million workers followed, and factories were occupied across France. President Charles de Gaulle briefly fled to a French military base in Germany before dissolving the National Assembly and winning the June elections, but the events transformed French society and culture.

United States

The Tet Offensive (January–February) shattered official optimism about Vietnam. The assassinations of Martin Luther King Jr. (April) and Robert F. Kennedy (June) convulsed the country; student occupations hit Columbia University, and anti-war demonstrations outside the Democratic National Convention in Chicago in August ended in widely televised police violence.

Eastern Europe

The Prague Spring in Czechoslovakia sought "socialism with a human face" under Alexander Dubček and was crushed by a Warsaw Pact invasion in August. In Poland, student protests in March were suppressed and accompanied by an antisemitic purge. In Yugoslavia, students protested in Belgrade.

Elsewhere

Mexico's student movement was massacred at Tlatelolco days before the Mexico City Olympics. West German students protested after the attempted assassination of Rudi Dutschke. Italy entered its "Hot Autumn" of strikes; Brazil saw mass marches against the military dictatorship; Japan's Zengakuren fought university authorities and the US–Japan security treaty.

Legacy

Although few movements achieved their immediate political aims, 1968 reshaped culture, universities, gender relations and the media landscape, and remains a reference point — celebrated and contested — in debates about authority, generational change and protest politics.
