Schläfli symbol

The Schläfli symbol is a notation of the form {p, q, r, ...} that defines regular polytopes and tessellations. It is named after the 19th-century Swiss mathematician Ludwig Schläfli, who generalized Euclidean geometry to more than three dimensions and discovered all the convex regular polytopes in four dimensions.

Definition

A regular polygon with p sides is denoted {p}. For example, {3} is an equilateral triangle, {4} is a square, and {5} is a regular pentagon. A regular polyhedron whose faces are {p} polygons, with q such faces meeting at each vertex, is denoted {p, q}. For example, the cube is {4, 3}: square faces, three meeting at each vertex. The five Platonic solids are {3,3} (tetrahedron), {4,3} (cube), {3,4} (octahedron), {5,3} (dodecahedron), and {3,5} (icosahedron).

Higher dimensions

The notation extends recursively: a regular 4-polytope with cells {p, q} and r cells around each edge is {p, q, r}. There are six convex regular 4-polytopes, including the 5-cell {3,3,3}, the tesseract {4,3,3}, and the 120-cell {5,3,3}. Star polytopes use rational numbers, as in the great dodecahedron {5, 5/2}.

Tessellations

Schläfli symbols also describe regular tilings of the plane: {3,6} is the triangular tiling, {4,4} the square tiling, and {6,3} the hexagonal tiling. Hyperbolic tilings satisfy 1/p + 1/q < 1/2, such as the order-3 heptagonal tiling {7,3}.

See also

Related concepts include the vertex figure, Coxeter–Dynkin diagrams, and Wythoff symbols, which generalize the notation to uniform polytopes.
