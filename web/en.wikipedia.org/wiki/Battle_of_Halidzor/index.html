Battle of Halidzor

The Battle of Halidzor was fought in 1727 (some accounts date it to 1726) near the Halidzor fortress in Syunik, in present-day Armenia. Armenian forces led by David Bek, the leader of the Armenian liberation movement in Syunik, defeated a much larger Ottoman army that had besieged the fortress, one of the most celebrated victories of the Armenian resistance of the 1720s.

Background

In the 1720s, as Safavid Persia collapsed under Afghan invasion and Ottoman armies advanced into the Caucasus, the Armenian meliks of Syunik rose in revolt under David Bek, a commander sent from Georgia. From strongholds such as Halidzor, David Bek's forces defended the mountainous region against repeated Ottoman expeditions.

The battle

An Ottoman army — Armenian sources give figures as high as 70,000 men, certainly many times the size of David Bek's few thousand — besieged Halidzor for about a week. The defenders, running short of food but holding strong walls, resolved on a desperate sortie. Armenian accounts describe the garrison descending from the fortress and falling suddenly on the besiegers' camp; panic spread through the Ottoman ranks, which broke and fled through the mountain passes with heavy losses, abandoning artillery, baggage and treasure.

Aftermath

The victory secured Syunik for several more years and became a central episode of Armenian national memory, celebrated in Raffi's historical novel David Bek and in a Soviet-era film of the same name. David Bek died in 1728, and his successor Mkhitar Sparapet continued the resistance until his death in 1730, after which Ottoman control was gradually imposed, though Syunik retained a measure of local autonomy.
