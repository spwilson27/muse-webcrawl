Lottie and Lisa (German: Das doppelte Lottchen, "The Double Lottie") is a 1949 children's novel by the German author Erich Kästner. It tells the story of identical twin sisters, separated in infancy by their divorced parents, who meet by chance at a summer camp, discover their relationship, swap places, and engineer the reconciliation of their mother and father. The novel is one of Kästner's most beloved works and the source of numerous film adaptations, most famously Disney's The Parent Trap.

Plot

At a girls' summer camp at Seebühl on Lake Bühl, the quiet, well-behaved Lottie Horn from Vienna meets the lively, curly-haired Lisa Palfy from Munich. Apart from their hairstyles the two girls are identical. Comparing photographs and birth dates, they deduce that they are twin sisters: after their parents' divorce, their father, the composer and conductor Ludwig Palfy, kept Lisa in Munich, while their mother Luiselotte, a journalist, raised Lottie in Vienna, and neither twin was told of the other's existence.

The sisters spend the summer teaching each other their lives, cut and style their hair alike, and at the end of camp swap identities: Lisa goes to Vienna as "Lottie" and Lottie to Munich as "Lisa". Each must maintain the deception — Lisa, who cannot cook, struggles with the housekeeping Lottie performed; Lottie must fend off her father's intended new fiancée, Irene Gerlach. When "Lottie" in Vienna falls seriously ill and murmurs the truth, the mother learns of the exchange; the family is reunited in Munich, the father abandons his marriage plans, and on the twins' birthday the parents remarry.

Background and reception

Kästner first developed the idea as a film treatment in the 1930s–40s, during which time he was banned from publishing under the Nazi regime; the novel appeared in 1949 after the war. It has been praised for treating divorce and separated families with unusual frankness and warmth for its era. The book has been translated into many languages and is a staple of German children's literature.

Adaptations

The story was first filmed in West Germany as Das doppelte Lottchen (1950), directed by Josef von Báky, which won the German Film Award for best feature film. Walt Disney's The Parent Trap (1961), starring Hayley Mills in the dual role, transplanted the story to America, and was remade in 1998 with Lindsay Lohan, also as The Parent Trap. Other adaptations include the German animated film Charlie & Louise (1994) and versions in numerous countries and languages, including Japanese anime and Indian films.
