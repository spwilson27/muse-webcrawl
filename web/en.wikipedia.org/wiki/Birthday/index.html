Birthday
ObservedAnnually, on the anniversary of a person's birth
CustomsParties, cake with candles, gifts, songs

A birthday is the anniversary of the birth of a person, typically celebrated each year with parties, gifts, and special foods such as birthday cake. Birthdays mark milestones in a person's age and are observed in most cultures around the world.

Customs

Common traditions include blowing out candles on a cake while making a wish, singing "Happy Birthday to You" — one of the most widely recognized songs in English — and giving presents to the person celebrating. Milestone birthdays, such as the 18th and 21st, often mark legal adulthood, while celebrations like the quinceañera in Latin American cultures and the Chinese longevity banquets for elders carry particular cultural significance.

History

Ancient Egyptians marked coronation days of pharaohs as birthdays of gods, and the Greeks celebrated the monthly birthdays of deities such as Artemis with cakes and candles. Roman citizens celebrated birthdays of friends and family, and the modern children's birthday party developed in nineteenth-century Germany as the Kinderfeste.

Legal significance

Birthdays determine legal age for purposes such as voting, drinking, driving, and retirement. Some religious groups, such as Jehovah's Witnesses, do not celebrate birthdays.
