National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame

The National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame is a museum and hall of fame for American Thoroughbred horse racing, located in Saratoga Springs, New York, across Union Avenue from the historic Saratoga Race Course. Founded in 1950, the museum collects and preserves the history of Thoroughbred racing in the United States, and its Hall of Fame honours outstanding horses, jockeys, trainers and Pillars of the Turf.

History

The museum was founded in 1950 and first housed in the Canfield Casino in Saratoga's Congress Park. In 1955 the Hall of Fame was established with its first class of inductees, and the museum moved to its present purpose-built home on Union Avenue. The building has been expanded and renovated several times, notably with a major capital campaign completed in 2020 that modernised the Hall of Fame gallery with interactive digital displays for each inductee.

Hall of Fame

Inductions are held each summer during the Saratoga racing meeting. Horses honoured include legends such as Man o' War, Secretariat, Citation, Kelso, Seabiscuit, Affirmed, American Pharoah and Zenyatta. Jockeys inducted include Eddie Arcaro, Bill Shoemaker, Laffit Pincay Jr., Angel Cordero Jr. and Julie Krone; trainers include H. Allen Jerkens, D. Wayne Lukas, Bob Baffert and "Sunny Jim" Fitzsimmons. A fourth category, Pillars of the Turf, recognises owners, breeders and racing leaders. Induction is determined by voting among racing writers, historians and previous voters, coordinated by a nominating committee.

Collections and exhibits

The museum's galleries trace racing in America from colonial times, with trophies — including Triple Crown trophies — silks, paintings by noted equine artists, photographs, and interactive exhibits such as a race-calling booth and a replica starting gate. A signature film, What It Takes: Journey to the Hall of Fame, is screened in the museum's theatre. The museum also maintains a library and archives used by racing researchers.
