Spring break

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Spring break, known variously as Easter vacation, Easter holiday, Easter break, spring vacation, spring recess, midterm break, study week, reading week, reading period, Easter week, or March break, is a school holiday that typically occurs in March or April, near academic midpoints and often around Easter. In the United States it is a week-long (or occasionally two-week) vacation for students at all levels, and it has become a major travel phenomenon, with warm-weather destinations drawing hundreds of thousands of college students.

History

The American spring break tradition grew in the 1930s, when college swim teams travelled to Fort Lauderdale, Florida, for spring training, and the city's 1935 College Coaches' Swim Forum popularised the destination. After the Second World War, and with the spread of inexpensive air travel, the trip became a rite of passage; Glamour magazine and the 1960 film Where the Boys Are fixed Fort Lauderdale's image as the capital of spring break. Cable television coverage in the 1980s amplified the party culture, and many Florida cities later imposed restrictions that dispersed students to other destinations.

Destinations

Traditional destinations include Panama City Beach, Daytona Beach, South Padre Island, and Cancún, Mexico, with Las Vegas, the Bahamas, and European city breaks also popular. Universities elsewhere observe similar breaks: Canadian schools schedule "reading week" in February, and Japanese students take holidays around the end of the academic year in March.

Criticism and alternatives

Spring break is associated with heavy drinking and risky behaviour, prompting health campaigns and local crackdowns; several jurisdictions have banned alcohol on beaches during March. Many students instead join "alternative spring break" programmes of community service and volunteering, organised by universities and non-profit groups since the 1980s.
