The Irish Independent is an Irish national daily newspaper published in Dublin by Independent News & Media (now Mediahuis Ireland). Founded in 1905 by businessman William Martin Murphy, it became Ireland's best-selling daily newspaper for much of the twentieth century and remains one of the country's most widely read papers, with a large online readership through its website, independent.ie.

History

William Martin Murphy, a wealthy Dublin businessman and former nationalist MP, had acquired the Irish Daily Independent (founded in 1891 as the voice of the Parnellite faction) and relaunched it in January 1905 as the Irish Independent. Murphy applied the techniques of the British "new journalism" — a low cover price of a halfpenny, bold headlines and mass advertising — and circulation climbed rapidly, making it Ireland's first genuinely mass-circulation daily.

The paper was strongly nationalist but constitutional in its politics, hostile to Sinn Féin after the 1916 Easter Rising: its editorial response to the Rising was widely criticised for calling for severe punishment of the rebels. Murphy's paper had also been a leading employer voice during the Dublin Lockout of 1913.

For much of the twentieth century the Independent was closely associated with the Fine Gael tradition and with a commercially minded, socially conservative readership. The paper was long controlled by the O'Reilly family through Independent News & Media, built up by Tony O'Reilly from the 1970s. In 2019 INM was acquired by the Belgian group Mediahuis.

Content and editions

The paper covers national and international news, business, sport and features. It switched from broadsheet to compact format in 2012. Its sister titles have included the Sunday Independent, Ireland's best-selling Sunday newspaper, and the Evening Herald (later the Herald).

Circulation

Historically Ireland's best-selling daily, the Irish Independent faced declining print circulation common to the industry, offset by growth in digital subscriptions to independent.ie, one of Ireland's most visited news websites.
