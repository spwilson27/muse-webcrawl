Alexander Cox (born 15 December 1954) is a British film director, screenwriter, actor and broadcaster. An independent filmmaker strongly associated with the punk aesthetic, he is best known for the cult films Repo Man (1984) and Sid and Nancy (1986).

Early life and education

Cox was born in Bebington, Cheshire, and studied law at Oxford before turning to film, completing a master's degree in film production at UCLA, where he made his first features.

Career

Repo Man, starring Emilio Estevez and Harry Dean Stanton, became a cult classic for its surreal, satirical portrait of Los Angeles. Sid and Nancy (1986), starring Gary Oldman as Sex Pistols bassist Sid Vicious, was critically acclaimed. Cox's western Walker (1987), filmed in Nicaragua during the Contra war, was a commercial failure that ended his Hollywood career, and he thereafter worked independently in Britain, Mexico and Spain, making films such as El Patrullero (1991), Three Businessmen (1998) and Revengers Tragedy (2002). He also presented the BBC film series Moviedrome and has written books on cinema, including studies of spaghetti westerns.

Style and themes

Cox's work combines anarchic humour, political satire and sympathy for outsiders, and frequently revisits the western genre and Latin American settings.
