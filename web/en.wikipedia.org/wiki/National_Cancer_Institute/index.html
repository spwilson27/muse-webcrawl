National Cancer Institute

National Cancer Institute (NCI)
Founded: August 5, 1937
Headquarters: Bethesda and Rockville, Maryland
Parent agency: National Institutes of Health (NIH), U.S. Department of Health and Human Services
Purpose: Cancer research, training, and information dissemination

The National Cancer Institute (NCI) is the United States federal government's principal agency for cancer research and training. Established by the National Cancer Institute Act of 1937, it is the oldest and largest of the 27 institutes and centers of the National Institutes of Health (NIH).

Mission and programs

The NCI coordinates the National Cancer Program, conducts and supports research on the causes, prevention, detection, diagnosis, and treatment of cancer, and disseminates information through services such as cancer.gov and the PDQ database of evidence-based summaries. Its budget, in excess of $6 billion annually, funds an intramural research program on the NIH campus and at Frederick, Maryland, as well as extramural grants to universities and research centers across the country.

The NCI designates a network of NCI-designated Cancer Centers—more than 70 institutions recognized for scientific leadership—and supports the National Clinical Trials Network. The National Cancer Act of 1971 greatly expanded the NCI's authorities as part of President Richard Nixon's declared "War on Cancer," including special budgetary procedures and the establishment of the President's Cancer Panel. More recently, the NCI has been a central participant in the Cancer Moonshot initiative launched in 2016 to accelerate progress against the disease.

Research contributions

NCI-supported research contributed to foundational discoveries in cancer biology, including the oncogene concept, advances in chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and the SEER (Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results) program, which has collected US cancer statistics since 1973.
