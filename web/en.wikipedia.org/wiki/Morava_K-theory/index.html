Morava K-theory

In stable homotopy theory, a branch of mathematics, Morava K-theory is one of a collection of cohomology theories introduced in algebraic topology by Jack Morava. For each prime number p and each non-negative integer n there is a Morava K-theory K(n), forming a family of "atomic" building blocks of chromatic homotopy theory.

Definition

Fix a prime p. For n ≥ 1, the theory K(n) has coefficient ring

K(n)* = Fp[vn, vn−1],

where vn has degree 2(pn − 1). The theory K(0) is rational homology, and K(1) is a summand of mod-p complex K-theory. Each K(n) is a field theory in the sense that any module spectrum over it is a wedge of suspensions of it, and it is characterised by its formal group law of height n.

Role in chromatic homotopy theory

Morava K-theories are the prime fields of the stable homotopy category at each chromatic level. The chromatic convergence and chromatic splitting conjectures describe how a spectrum can be assembled from its localisations with respect to the K(n). Associated constructions include Morava E-theory and the Morava stabilizer groups, which control the symmetries of the formal group of height n.
