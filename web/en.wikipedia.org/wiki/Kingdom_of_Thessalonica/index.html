Kingdom of Thessalonica

The Kingdom of Thessalonica was a short-lived Crusader state founded after the Fourth Crusade partitioned the Byzantine Empire in 1204. Its territory comprised Thessalonica and much of Macedonia and Thessaly in northern Greece, and it was nominally a vassal of the Latin Empire of Constantinople.

History

The kingdom was awarded to Boniface of Montferrat, a leading commander of the Fourth Crusade who had expected to become Latin emperor. Boniface extended his rule southward into Greece before being killed in 1207 fighting the Bulgarians. His infant son Demetrius succeeded, but the kingdom was weakened by internal strife and pressure from the Greek Despotate of Epirus. In 1224 Theodore Komnenos Doukas of Epirus captured Thessalonica, ending the kingdom after only twenty years, though the Montferrat family retained its claim for decades.

Significance

The kingdom illustrates the fragility of the Crusader principalities carved from Byzantium, and its fall marked the resurgence of Greek power in the region.
