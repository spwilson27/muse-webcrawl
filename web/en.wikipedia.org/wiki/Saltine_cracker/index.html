A saltine or soda cracker is a thin, usually square cracker made from white flour, sometimes shortening, yeast and baking soda, with most varieties lightly sprinkled with coarse salt. It has perforations over its surface and a crisp, dry texture. Saltines are commonly eaten as a snack or crumbled into soups and chowders.

History

Soda crackers were an early commercial success of the F. L. Smithers company in the 19th century. The best-known brand, Premium Saltines, was introduced in 1876 by the Josiah Bent bakery in Massachusetts; the term "saltine" was originally a trademark of that bakery but became generic after being widely copied, a status confirmed in a 1907 merger-era trademark dispute. The crackers were distributed by the National Biscuit Company (Nabisco) and are now sold under the Premium brand by Mondelez International.

Uses

Saltines are a standard accompaniment to soups, chilli and clam chowder, and are crushed as a topping for casseroles. They are also eaten during illness as a bland food, and are the basis of the "saltine cracker challenge", an eating contest in which participants attempt to eat six saltines in sixty seconds without drinking.

Similar products

In the United Kingdom, similar baked products are sold as cream crackers, though these are usually unsalted on top and slightly richer. Saltines are closely related to hardtack and to the oyster cracker, a smaller round variant used in chowders.
