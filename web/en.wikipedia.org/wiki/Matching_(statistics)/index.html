Matching (statistics)

Matching is a statistical technique used in observational studies to evaluate the effect of a treatment by comparing treated units to similar non-treated units. Each treated subject is paired with one or more untreated subjects with similar observed covariates, mimicking a randomised experiment and reducing bias from confounding.

Methods

Common approaches include propensity score matching, Mahalanobis distance matching, exact and coarsened exact matching, and nearest-neighbour matching with or without replacement and calipers.

Assumptions and limitations

Matching balances only observed covariates; unobserved confounding can still bias results. Poor matches and loss of unmatched units are trade-offs. Balance diagnostics check covariate distributions after matching.

References

Rosenbaum & Rubin on propensity scores; Stuart (2010) on matching methods.
