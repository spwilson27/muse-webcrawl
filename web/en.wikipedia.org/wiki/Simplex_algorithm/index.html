Simplex algorithm

In mathematical optimization, Dantzig's simplex algorithm (or simplex method) is a popular algorithm for linear programming. It was created by George Dantzig in 1947.

The name of the algorithm is derived from the concept of a simplex and was suggested by T. S. Motzkin. Simplices are not actually used in the method, but one interpretation of it is that it operates on simplicial cones, and these become proper simplices with an additional constraint. The simplicial cones in question are the corners (i.e., the neighbourhoods of the vertices) of a geometric object called a polytope. The shape of this polytope is defined by the constraints applied to the objective function.

Overview

The algorithm proceeds by moving along the edges of the feasible polytope from one vertex (basic feasible solution) to an adjacent vertex with a better (or no worse) objective value, via operations called pivots on a tableau representation of the problem. Because there are finitely many vertices and the objective improves at each step, the method terminates at an optimal vertex, provided degeneracies (cycling) are handled by rules such as Bland's rule.

Complexity

Although exponential in the worst case (the Klee–Minty cube is a classic counterexample family), the simplex algorithm is extremely efficient in practice and was one of the first algorithms suitable for large-scale industrial optimisation. Polynomial-time alternatives include Khachiyan's ellipsoid method (1979) and Karmarkar's interior-point method (1984), but simplex methods and their dual and revised variants remain widely used in commercial solvers.
