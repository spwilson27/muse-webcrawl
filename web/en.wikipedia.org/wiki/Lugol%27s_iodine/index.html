Lugol's iodine, also known as Lugol's solution, is a solution of potassium iodide and elemental iodine in water, first made in 1829 by the French physician Jean Lugol. Because elemental iodine is only sparingly soluble in water, the iodide ions form triiodide (I3−), which dissolves readily.

Uses

Lugol's solution turns blue-black in the presence of starch, making it a standard laboratory test for starch and a stain for cell nuclei and glycogen in microscopy (Gram's iodine is a weaker variant). Medically it has been used as an antiseptic and disinfectant, to treat thyroid storm and prepare patients for thyroid surgery by reducing thyroid hormone release (the Wolff–Chaikoff effect), and to protect the thyroid from radioactive iodine after nuclear accidents. Applied to the cervix in the Schiller test, it helps identify abnormal tissue during colposcopy.

Formulations

The original 5% solution contains 5 g iodine and 10 g potassium iodide per 100 mL in some conventions, or 2%/4% in others; weaker 1–2% solutions are used for internal applications. It appears on the WHO List of Essential Medicines as an antithyroid preparation.
