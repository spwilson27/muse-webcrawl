Narmada River

Length1,312 km
SourceAmarkantak Plateau, Madhya Pradesh
MouthGulf of Khambhat, Arabian Sea
StatesMadhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat
Major damSardar Sarovar Dam

The Narmada River is the fifth-longest river in India and the largest west-flowing river on the Indian peninsula. It rises on the Amarkantak Plateau in Madhya Pradesh and flows west for about 1,312 km through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges, passing through Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Gujarat before emptying into the Gulf of Khambhat in the Arabian Sea near Bharuch.

Geography and significance

Unlike most great Indian rivers, which flow east to the Bay of Bengal, the Narmada runs west through a graben, and it traditionally forms the boundary between North and South India. It is one of the most sacred rivers in Hinduism, worshipped as the goddess Narmada, and pilgrims perform the Narmada Parikrama, a circumambulation of the entire river from source to sea and back. Celebrated sites on its banks include the marble rocks of Bhedaghat near Jabalpur, the temple town of Omkareshwar, one of the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines, and Maheshwar.

Dams and controversy

The basin has been the focus of some of India's largest dam projects, above all the Sardar Sarovar Dam in Gujarat, completed to its full height in 2017. The projects promised irrigation, drinking water and power, but displaced large numbers of people, prompting the Narmada Bachao Andolan ("Save Narmada") movement led by Medha Patkar, one of the most prominent environmental and social campaigns in modern India. Near the Sardar Sarovar Dam stands the Statue of Unity, the world's tallest statue, inaugurated in 2018.
