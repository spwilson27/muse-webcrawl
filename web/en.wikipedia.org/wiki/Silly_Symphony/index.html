Silly Symphony (also marketed as Silly Symphonies) is an American series of animated short films produced by Walt Disney Productions between 1929 and 1939. Unlike the contemporary Mickey Mouse series, the Silly Symphonies did not feature recurring characters; instead each short was a self-contained story, usually set to music. The series served as a training ground and experimental laboratory for Disney's animators, pioneering techniques such as the multiplane camera and the use of Technicolor in animation.

History

The series began with The Skeleton Dance (1929), animated primarily by Ub Iwerks and scored by Carl Stalling, who proposed the concept of a series of musical novelty shorts. Early entries were distributed by Columbia Pictures; Disney later moved distribution to United Artists and then to RKO Radio Pictures.

The Silly Symphonies became famous for introducing Donald Duck in The Wise Little Hen (1934) and for landmark shorts such as Flowers and Trees (1932), the first cartoon produced in full-color Technicolor; Three Little Pigs (1933), a Depression-era phenomenon whose song "Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Wolf?" became a national catchphrase; and The Old Mill (1937), the first use of Disney's multiplane camera.

Legacy

Several Silly Symphonies won the Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film, and the series is credited with raising animation from a novelty to an art form. The title was later revived for a series of comic books and for reissue packages of the shorts.
