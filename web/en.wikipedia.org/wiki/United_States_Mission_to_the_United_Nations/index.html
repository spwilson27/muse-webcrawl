The United States Mission to the United Nations (USUN) serves as the United States' delegation to the United Nations. USUN is responsible for carrying out the nation's participation in the world body and is part of the U.S. Department of State. The mission is headed by the United States Ambassador to the United Nations, who holds the title of Permanent Representative and sits on the President's Cabinet.

Role

The mission represents the United States in the Security Council, the General Assembly, and the other organs and specialized agencies of the UN system headquartered in New York. Its staff comprises diplomats and Foreign Service officers covering political, economic and social, management and reform, and public affairs portfolios. As the host country mission, USUN also liaises with the UN Secretariat and the 193 other member-state missions.

Ambassadors

The first U.S. Permanent Representative was Edward Stettinius Jr., followed by figures including Warren Austin, Henry Cabot Lodge Jr., Adlai Stevenson II — famous for his confrontation with the Soviet ambassador during the Cuban Missile Crisis — Arthur Goldberg, George H. W. Bush, Daniel Patrick Moynihan, Jeane Kirkpatrick, Madeleine Albright, John Negroponte, John Bolton, Susan Rice, Samantha Power, Nikki Haley and Linda Thomas-Greenfield.

Building

The mission is headquartered at 799 United Nations Plaza, directly across First Avenue from the UN Secretariat Building in Manhattan. The 32-story tower, dedicated in 2010, consolidates the mission's offices and includes the Ronald H. Brown United States Mission Auditorium.
