WannaCry ransomware attack

The WannaCry ransomware attack was a worldwide cyberattack in May 2017 by the WannaCry ransomware cryptoworm, which targeted computers running Microsoft Windows by encrypting data and demanding ransom payments in Bitcoin. It spread using EternalBlue, an exploit of the SMB protocol developed by the US National Security Agency and leaked by the hacker group The Shadow Brokers. More than 200,000 computers in about 150 countries were infected, with the UK's National Health Service among the worst affected.

Attack

WannaCry began spreading on Friday 12 May 2017. It encrypted user files and displayed a ransom note demanding $300, doubling after three days. Britain's NHS was severely disrupted — hospitals diverted ambulances and cancelled appointments — and Telefónica, FedEx, Deutsche Bahn and Renault were also hit. Microsoft had patched the vulnerability (MS17-010) in March 2017, but many organisations had not applied it; Microsoft took the unusual step of releasing patches for out-of-support systems such as Windows XP.

Kill switch and attribution

The spread was dramatically slowed when security researcher Marcus Hutchins registered an unregistered domain hardcoded in the malware, which acted as a kill switch. The US, UK and other governments later attributed the attack to North Korea's Lazarus Group; the US indicted North Korean programmer Park Jin-hyok in 2018. Total damages were estimated in the billions of dollars.
