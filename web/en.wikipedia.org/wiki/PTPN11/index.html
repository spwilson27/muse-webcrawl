PTPN11

PTPN11 (protein tyrosine phosphatase, non-receptor type 11) is a human gene encoding the protein tyrosine phosphatase SHP2, an enzyme involved in cell signalling downstream of many growth factor and cytokine receptors.

Function

SHP2 contains two N-terminal SH2 domains and a C-terminal protein tyrosine phosphatase domain. In its resting state the enzyme is autoinhibited; binding of the SH2 domains to phosphorylated targets opens and activates the phosphatase. SHP2 is a positive regulator of the RAS–MAPK signalling pathway and is required for normal development and haematopoiesis.

Clinical significance

Germline gain-of-function mutations in PTPN11 cause about half of cases of Noonan syndrome, while other mutations cause Noonan syndrome with multiple lentigines (formerly LEOPARD syndrome). Somatic mutations are found in juvenile myelomonocytic leukaemia and other cancers, making SHP2 a target of interest for allosteric inhibitor drugs.
