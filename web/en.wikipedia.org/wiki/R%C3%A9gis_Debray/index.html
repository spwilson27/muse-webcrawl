Régis Debray (born 2 September 1940) is a French philosopher, journalist and writer. He is known for his involvement with Marxist revolutionary movements in Latin America in the 1960s, his later work as an adviser to President François Mitterrand, and for founding the discipline of mediology, the study of how ideas are transmitted through material and institutional means.

Latin America and imprisonment

A graduate of the École Normale Supérieure, Debray travelled to Cuba and then to Bolivia, where he joined the guerrilla campaign of Che Guevara. His 1967 book Revolution in the Revolution? systematised the "foco" theory of guerrilla warfare. Captured by the Bolivian army in 1967, he was sentenced to thirty years in prison but released in 1970 after an international campaign.

Later career

Debray served as an adviser on foreign affairs to Mitterrand in the 1980s, held academic posts, and wrote extensively on religion, media and national identity, including Critique of Political Reason and mediological works such as Transmitting Culture.
