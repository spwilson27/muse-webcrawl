Axial fan design

Flow: Parallel to axis of rotation
Types: Propeller, tube-axial, vane-axial
Uses: Ventilation, cooling, jet engines

Axial fan design concerns fans in which air flows parallel to the axis of the impeller, in contrast to centrifugal fans. Axial fans move large volumes of air at relatively low pressure increases and are used in ventilation, electronic cooling, wind tunnels and as the fan stage of turbofan engines.

Design principles

Blades act as airfoils: lift generated by blade rotation drives the flow. Performance depends on blade pitch, number of blades, tip clearance, hub-to-tip ratio and the fan laws relating flow, pressure and power to speed and diameter. Guide vanes can recover swirl energy and raise efficiency.

Types

Propeller fans are simplest; tube-axial fans place the impeller in a casing; vane-axial fans add downstream guide vanes for higher pressure. Counter-rotating and variable-pitch designs serve specialised applications.
