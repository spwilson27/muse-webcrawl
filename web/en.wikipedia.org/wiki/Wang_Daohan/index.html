Wang Daohan

Wang Daohan (Chinese: 汪道涵; 7 March 1915 – 24 December 2005) was a Chinese politician best known as the first president of the Association for Relations Across the Taiwan Straits (ARATS), the mainland body handling semi-official dialogue with Taiwan.

Career

Born in Jiashan County, Anhui, Wang joined the Chinese Communist Party in the 1930s and held economic and planning posts after 1949. A protégé of Jiang Zemin, he served as vice-mayor and then mayor of Shanghai (1981–1985) before becoming a senior adviser. In December 1991 he was appointed president of the newly founded ARATS.

Cross-strait talks

Wang's counterpart in Taiwan was Koo Chen-fu of the Straits Exchange Foundation. The two men held the landmark Wang–Koo summit in Singapore in April 1993 — the first authorised high-level meeting between the two sides since 1949 — signing agreements on document authentication, postal services and a framework for further talks. A second round of talks was held in Shanghai in 1998. Planned visits and negotiations were suspended after political tensions in 1999, but Wang remained the symbolic head of mainland cross-strait diplomacy until his death in Shanghai in 2005.
