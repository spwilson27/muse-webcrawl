Czech National Revival

The Czech National Revival was a cultural movement which took place in the Czech lands during the 18th and 19th centuries. Its goal was to revive the Czech language, culture, and national identity, which had been suppressed in favour of German after the Battle of White Mountain (1620).

Course of the movement

The revival is usually divided into phases: a defensive phase (1770s–1805) of scholarly codification led by figures such as Josef Dobrovský, who wrote a grammar of Czech; an offensive phase (1805–1848) associated with Josef Jungmann, who translated world literature and compiled a Czech–German dictionary; and a culminating phase after 1848, when Czech national life achieved mass expression in institutions like the National Theatre in Prague, opened in 1881.

Legacy

Historians and linguists such as František Palacký, author of the History of the Czech Nation, laid the ideological foundations of the modern Czech nation. The revival's cultural and political work paved the way for Czechoslovak independence in 1918.
