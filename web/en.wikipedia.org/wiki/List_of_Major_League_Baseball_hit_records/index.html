List of Major League Baseball hit records

This is a list of records in Major League Baseball (MLB) for base hits, including career, season, and single-game totals, as well as records for hits by position, by team, and in the postseason.

Hits are the most fundamental measure of a batter's productivity.

Career hits

Pete Rose holds the all-time record with 4,256 career hits, accumulated over 24 seasons with the Cincinnati Reds, Philadelphia Phillies, and Montreal Expos. He passed Ty Cobb's long-standing mark of 4,189 (or 4,191 by some accounts) in 1985. Only three players have reached the 4,000-hit plateau: Rose, Cobb, and Hank Aaron (3,771). Ichiro Suzuki finished with 3,089 MLB hits, in addition to 1,278 in Japan's Nippon Professional Baseball, giving him a combined professional total of 4,367.

Single-season hits

The single-season record belongs to Ichiro Suzuki, who collected 262 hits for the Seattle Mariners in 2004, breaking George Sisler's record of 257 set in 1920. Sisler also had a 246-hit season in 1922. Lefty O'Doul (254 in 1929) and Bill Terry (254 in 1930) rank among the next highest totals.

Single-game hits

The record for hits in a nine-inning game is seven, held by Wilbert Robinson (1892) and Rennie Stennett (1975). Numerous players have recorded six hits in a nine-inning game. Johnny Burnett holds the overall record with nine hits in an 18-inning game in 1932.

Other hit records

  • Most hits by a rookie: Ichiro Suzuki, 242 (2001).
  • Most consecutive games with a hit: Joe DiMaggio, 56 (1941).
  • Most hits in a World Series: Paul Molitor, 10 in a five-game series context, with the overall Series record shared by several players at 13.
  • Most career postseason hits: Derek Jeter, 200.
