Gram Parsons

Gram Parsons (born Ingram Cecil Connor III; November 5, 1946 – September 19, 1973) was an American singer, songwriter, guitarist, and pianist. He is regarded as a founder of country rock and a pioneer of the style he called "Cosmic American Music", blending country, rock, soul, and folk.

Career

Parsons recorded with the International Submarine Band before joining the Byrds in 1968, where he was a driving force behind the landmark country-rock album Sweetheart of the Rodeo. He then co-founded the Flying Burrito Brothers, whose debut The Gilded Palace of Sin (1969) became a touchstone of the genre. A close friend and musical collaborator of Keith Richards, Parsons influenced the Rolling Stones' country-tinged work of the period.

Solo work and death

His two solo albums, GP (1973) and the posthumous Grievous Angel (1974), featured singer Emmylou Harris, whose harmonies became central to his sound. Parsons died of a drug overdose in Joshua Tree, California, at age 26; in a notorious aftermath, his road manager stole his coffin and burned it in Joshua Tree National Park, honouring a pact the two had made.

Legacy

Parsons' fusion of country and rock profoundly influenced later generations of musicians, and he was ranked among the greatest artists of all time in later critical lists. He was inducted into no major hall of fame for decades, a long-running point of controversy among his admirers.
