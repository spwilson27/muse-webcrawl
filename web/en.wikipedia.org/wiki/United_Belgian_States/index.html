United Belgian States

The United Belgian States (Dutch: Verenigde Belgische Staten; French: États-Belgiques-Unis), also known as the United States of Belgium, was a short-lived confederation in the Southern Netherlands (roughly modern Belgium) that existed from January to December 1790 during the Brabant Revolution, a revolt against the Habsburg Emperor Joseph II.

The lion arms of the United Belgian States.

Background

Joseph II's sweeping reforms — administrative, judicial, and religious — provoked strong opposition in the Austrian Netherlands, where they were seen as attacks on traditional liberties guaranteed by documents such as the Joyous Entry of Brabant. Resistance crystallized in 1789; at the Battle of Turnhout (27 October 1789) patriot forces defeated the Austrian troops, and the provinces successively declared independence.

The confederation

On 11 January 1790 the rebellious provinces concluded a treaty of union forming the United Belgian States, modelled partly on the Dutch Republic and the American Articles of Confederation. Sovereignty remained largely with the provinces; a Sovereign Congress in Brussels handled common affairs. The conservative "Statist" faction led by Henri Van der Noot dominated the government and suppressed the more democratic "Vonckist" followers of Jan Frans Vonck.

Collapse

Weakened by internal factional conflict and lacking foreign recognition, the new state could not resist the return of Austrian forces under the new Emperor Leopold II, who combined concessions with military pressure. Austrian troops re-entered Brussels in December 1790, ending the United Belgian States after less than a year. The episode is regarded as a precursor of Belgian independence in 1830.
