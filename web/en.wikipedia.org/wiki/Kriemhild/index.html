Kriemhild

Kriemhild (also spelled Grimhild or Kriemhilde) is a major figure in Germanic heroic legend, best known as a protagonist of the Middle High German epic Nibelungenlied. She is the sister of the Burgundian kings Gunther, Gernot and Giselher, the wife first of the hero Siegfried and later of Etzel (Attila), king of the Huns. In Old Norse tradition her counterpart is Guðrún.


Kriemhild as depicted in a 19th-century illustration.

In the Nibelungenlied

The poem opens with Kriemhild at the Burgundian court at Worms, where she dreams of a falcon torn apart by two eagles — an omen of her future husband's death. She marries Siegfried after he helps Gunther win Brünhild. After Hagen murders Siegfried and sinks his treasure in the Rhine, Kriemhild mourns for years. She then marries Etzel and, thirteen years later, invites her brothers and Hagen to Etzel's court, where her revenge unfolds in a catastrophic battle in which nearly all the Burgundians and Huns are killed. In the end Kriemhild kills Hagen with Siegfried's sword and is herself cut down by Hildebrand.

Origins and parallels

Kriemhild's character may derive in part from the historical Ildico, a Germanic woman said to have been Attila's last wife. In the Norse Völsunga saga and the Poetic Edda, Guðrún instead avenges her brothers on her husband Atli, reversing the German version's loyalties.
