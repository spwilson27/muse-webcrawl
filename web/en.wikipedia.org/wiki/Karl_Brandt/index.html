Karl Brandt

Karl Brandt (8 January 1904 – 2 June 1948) was a German physician and SS officer. He was Adolf Hitler's personal physician from 1934 and, as Reich Commissioner for Health and Sanitation, one of the most powerful figures in Nazi medicine. He was a principal organiser of Aktion T4, the programme that murdered tens of thousands of people with disabilities.

Career

Born in Mulhouse, Brandt trained as a surgeon and joined the Nazi Party in 1932 and the SS in 1933. He accompanied Hitler as escort physician and treated members of the inner circle. In 1939 Hitler authorised Brandt and Philipp Bouhler to organise the "euthanasia" killings of institutionalised patients; Brandt also held sweeping authority over medical services, research and hospital resources during the war. He fell from favour in 1945 after conflicts with rivals and was briefly arrested by the SS.

Trial and execution

Brandt was the lead defendant in the Doctors' Trial at Nuremberg (United States v. Karl Brandt et al.), the first of the subsequent Nuremberg trials. He was convicted of war crimes and crimes against humanity in connection with medical experiments and the euthanasia programme, and was hanged at Landsberg Prison on 2 June 1948.
