The Passover Seder (Hebrew: סֵדֶר, sēder, "order") is a ritual feast at the beginning of the Jewish holiday of Passover. It is conducted throughout the world on the eve of the 15th day of Nisan in the Hebrew calendar (and, outside Israel, again on the 16th). The Seder is a retelling of the story of the Exodus of the Israelites from ancient Egypt, as described in the Torah, performed according to a fixed order of fifteen steps and guided by the text of the Haggadah.

The Seder plate

Central to the table is the Seder plate (ka'arah) holding symbolic foods: maror (bitter herbs, for the bitterness of slavery), chazeret, charoset (a sweet paste recalling the mortar of the slaves), karpas (a vegetable dipped in salt water), zeroa (a roasted shank bone, recalling the Paschal lamb), and beitzah (a roasted egg, symbolising renewal and the cycle of life).

Order of the evening

The ceremony proceeds through fifteen stages, including the sanctification over the first of four cups of wine (Kadesh), the telling of the Exodus story (Maggid) — introduced by the Four Questions, traditionally recited by the youngest child — ritual hand-washings, the eating of matzah (unleavened bread), the festive meal (Shulchan Orech), the search for and eating of the hidden afikoman, grace after meals, the singing of psalms of praise (Hallel), and concluding songs such as "Chad Gadya". A cup of wine is also poured for the prophet Elijah, and the door is opened for his arrival.
