Henry Graham Greene OM CH (2 October 1904 – 3 April 1991) was an English novelist, short story writer and journalist, regarded by many as one of the leading novelists of the 20th century. His works often explored moral and political issues of the modern world, frequently infused with his Roman Catholic faith — though he resisted being labelled a "Catholic novelist".

Life

Greene was educated at Berkhamsted School, where his father was headmaster, and at Balliol College, Oxford. He worked as a journalist for The Times before the success of his early novels. During the Second World War he served in MI6 in West Africa, an experience reflected in The Heart of the Matter.

Works

Among his best-known novels are Stamboul Train (1932), Brighton Rock (1938), The Power and the Glory (1940), The Heart of the Matter (1948), The End of the Affair (1951), The Quiet American (1955) and Our Man in Havana (1958). Many of his books were adapted into successful films.

Legacy

Greene was shortlisted for the Nobel Prize in Literature several times. The fictional world of intrigue, moral ambiguity and decaying colonial outposts he depicted became known as "Greeneland".
