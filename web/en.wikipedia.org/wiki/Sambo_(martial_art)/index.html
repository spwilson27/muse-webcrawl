Sambo (martial art)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A sambo bout: the jacketed kurtka and shorts are distinctive of the sport

Sambo (Russian: самбо) is a martial art and combat sport developed in the Soviet Union in the early twentieth century. The name is an acronym of the Russian phrase SAMozashchita Bez Oruzhiya («самозащита без оружия»), meaning "self-defence without weapons".

History

Sambo grew out of efforts by the Red Army to improve hand-to-hand combat training. Its principal founders were Viktor Spiridonov, a specialist in Russian folk wrestling styles, and Vasily Oshchepkov, who had studied judo in Japan under Kanō Jigorō. The two men's systems were eventually merged, drawing on judo, catch wrestling, and the traditional grappling styles of the peoples of the Soviet Union, such as Georgian chidaoba, Armenian kokh, Tatar köräsh and Uzbek kurash. Sambo was officially recognised as a sport in the USSR in 1938.

Styles

Sport sambo resembles judo and jacket wrestling: competitors wear a jacket (kurtka) with a belt, shorts and soft shoes, and score with throws, pins and submission holds. Unusually for jacket grappling, leg locks are permitted, though chokeholds are not allowed in sport sambo. Combat sambo, developed for military use, additionally permits punches, kicks, elbows, knees, headbutts, groin strikes and chokes, and is often compared with mixed martial arts.

Competition and influence

The international governing body is the International Sambo Federation (FIAS), and world championships have been held since 1973. Sambo techniques have strongly influenced mixed martial arts; champions such as Fedor Emelianenko came from a combat sambo background.
