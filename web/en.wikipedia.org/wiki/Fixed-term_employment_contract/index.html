Fixed-term employment contract

A fixed-term employment contract is an employment contract that ends on a specific date, or on the completion of a specified task or project, rather than continuing indefinitely. It contrasts with a permanent (open-ended) contract, which continues until either party gives notice of termination.

Uses

Fixed-term contracts are commonly used to cover temporary increases in workload, seasonal work, maternity or sickness cover, specific funded projects (such as research grants in universities), and probationary or training arrangements. Athletes and performers are frequently employed on fixed-term contracts.

Legal regulation

Because successive renewals can be used to deny workers the security of permanent employment, many legal systems regulate fixed-term work. In the European Union, the Fixed-Term Work Directive (1999/70/EC) requires that fixed-term workers not be treated less favourably than comparable permanent workers, and requires member states to prevent abuse arising from successive fixed-term contracts, for example by limiting the total duration or number of renewals. Under some national laws, a fixed-term employee who is kept on beyond a statutory period is deemed to become permanent.

When a fixed-term contract expires, the employment ends automatically on the agreed date in many jurisdictions; in some, however, expiry is treated in law as a dismissal, so that the employer must show a fair reason for not renewing.
