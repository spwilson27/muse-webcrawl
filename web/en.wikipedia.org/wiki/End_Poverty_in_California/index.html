End Poverty in California

End Poverty in California (EPIC) was a political campaign started in 1934 by author and activist Upton Sinclair, author of The Jungle. The EPIC movement formed the basis for Sinclair's campaign for Governor of California in 1934, in which he won the Democratic nomination but lost the general election to Republican Frank Merriam.

The plan

Sinclair set out the programme in his 1933 book I, Governor of California, and How I Ended Poverty: A True Story of the Future. It proposed that the state take over idle factories and farmland and operate them as cooperatives employing the jobless — "production for use" rather than for profit — financed through state-issued scrip and credit, together with a state pension of $50 a month for those over sixty, a graduated income tax, and repeal of the sales tax. The plan drew hundreds of thousands of supporters organised in EPIC clubs across California during the depths of the Great Depression.

The 1934 campaign and aftermath

Sinclair's primary victory alarmed business interests, and the general election saw one of the first modern media-driven negative campaigns against him, including fabricated newsreels produced by Hollywood studios. Though defeated, EPIC candidates won seats in the state legislature, and the movement influenced New Deal-era debates and later California pension movements such as "Ham and Eggs". Sinclair wrote up the experience in I, Candidate for Governor: And How I Got Licked (1935).
