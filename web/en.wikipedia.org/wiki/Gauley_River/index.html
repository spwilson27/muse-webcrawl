The Gauley River is a 105-mile-long (169 km) river in West Virginia, United States. It merges with the New River at the town of Gauley Bridge to form the Kanawha River, a tributary of the Ohio River. Much of the river's lower course is protected within the Gauley River National Recreation Area.

Course

The river rises in Pocahontas County in the Allegheny Mountains and flows generally southwest through Nicholas and Fayette counties. The Summersville Dam (completed 1966) impounds Summersville Lake on the river. Below the dam the river cuts a steep gorge with continuous whitewater.

Whitewater recreation

The Gauley is one of the most famous whitewater rivers in the eastern United States. The Upper Gauley is renowned for five large rapids — Insignificant, Pillow Rock, Lost Paddle, Iron Ring and Sweet's Falls — rated Class V. Controlled releases from Summersville Dam on autumn weekends ("Gauley Season") draw thousands of rafters and kayakers, and the river is a major driver of tourism in the region.

History

The area saw fighting during the American Civil War, including the Battle of Carnifex Ferry (1861) near the river.
