Vehicle routing problem

The vehicle routing problem (VRP) is a combinatorial optimization and integer programming problem in operations research that asks: what is the optimal set of routes for a fleet of vehicles to deliver goods or services to a given set of customers? It generalises the travelling salesman problem (TSP). The problem was first proposed by George Dantzig and John Ramser in 1959 in a paper on truck dispatching, and it is one of the most studied problems in combinatorial optimization because of its central role in logistics and distribution management.

Formulation

In the basic problem, vehicles based at a central depot must serve customers with known demands at known locations. The objective is typically to minimise total route cost (distance, time, or number of vehicles) subject to constraints: each customer is visited exactly once, each route starts and ends at the depot, and the total demand on any route does not exceed the vehicle's capacity — the latter restriction defining the capacitated VRP (CVRP). VRP is NP-hard, so exact methods solve only modest instances, and practical solution relies on heuristics and metaheuristics.

Variants

  • VRPTW — with time windows within which each customer must be served;
  • Pickup and delivery problems, in which goods must be moved between locations;
  • VRP with multiple depots, heterogeneous fleets, split deliveries, or periodic visits;
  • Green and dynamic VRPs, incorporating emissions objectives or real-time requests.

Solution methods

Classical heuristics include the Clarke–Wright savings algorithm and sweep methods; modern approaches use tabu search, simulated annealing, genetic algorithms, large neighbourhood search, and exact branch-and-cut methods. Commercial routing software based on these techniques is used throughout the delivery, parcel, waste-collection, and field-service industries.
