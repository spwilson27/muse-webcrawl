The Call of Cthulhu

"The Call of Cthulhu" is a short story by American writer H. P. Lovecraft, written in 1926 and published in the pulp magazine Weird Tales in February 1928. It is the central text of the Cthulhu Mythos and introduced the entity Cthulhu, a gigantic octopus-headed, dragon-winged being worshipped by cults around the world.

Plot

Narrator Francis Wayland Thurston pieces together his late great-uncle's papers: an artist's dreams of a cyclopean sunken city, a police raid on a Louisiana cult, and a sailor's fatal encounter with the risen island-city of R'lyeh in the Pacific, where Cthulhu briefly awakens before the city sinks again. The story's famous refrain runs: "Ph'nglui mglw'nafh Cthulhu R'lyeh wgah'nagl fhtagn" — "In his house at R'lyeh, dead Cthulhu waits dreaming."

Influence

The story exemplifies Lovecraft's "cosmicism" — humanity's insignificance before indifferent ancient powers — and has inspired countless adaptations, including the 1981 role-playing game Call of Cthulhu, films, music and games.
