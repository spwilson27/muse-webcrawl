George Robert Price (16 October 1922 – 6 January 1975) was an American population geneticist. Originally a physical chemist who worked on the Manhattan Project and a science journalist, he later moved to London and made foundational contributions to evolutionary theory, in particular the Price equation, a mathematical description of how a trait or gene frequency changes over time.

Career

Price earned his doctorate in chemistry from the University of Chicago in 1946 and worked at Harvard Medical School and the University of Minnesota. After a career in science journalism, he moved to London in 1967 and worked at University College London's Galton Laboratory. There, reading W. D. Hamilton's papers on kin selection, he derived the Price equation, published in Nature in 1970, which Hamilton recognised as a profound and general result underpinning the theory of group and kin selection. Price also applied game theory to animal behaviour, work later developed by John Maynard Smith.

Later life

In 1973 Price underwent a religious conversion and devoted himself to helping the homeless and giving away his possessions. Suffering from depression, he died by suicide in January 1975. His life and work were popularised in Oren Harman's biography The Price of Altruism (2010).
