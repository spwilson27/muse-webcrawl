Dymaxion

The word Dymaxion is a brand name that Buckminster Fuller used for several of his inventions. It is a portmanteau of the words dynamic, maximum, and tension. The name was coined for Fuller around 1928 by the advertising executive Waldo Warren, who combined syllables from words Fuller habitually used.

Applications

  • Dymaxion house — prefabricated, mass-producible dwellings suspended from a central mast, designed by Fuller beginning in the late 1920s.
  • Dymaxion car — a streamlined, three-wheeled vehicle of 1933, intended as part of a larger transport system.
  • Dymaxion map — a world map projection onto an icosahedron that can be unfolded flat with minimal distortion of land areas.
  • Dymaxion Chronofile — Fuller's exhaustive personal archive of his life and work.
  • Dymaxion deployment units and the Dymaxion bathroom were among other projects carrying the name.

The term came to symbolize Fuller's philosophy of "ephemeralization" — doing more with less — and his aim of applying technology comprehensively to shelter and transport.
