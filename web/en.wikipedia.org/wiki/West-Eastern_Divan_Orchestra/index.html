West-Eastern Divan Orchestra


The orchestra brings together young musicians from Israel and Arab countries.

The West-Eastern Divan Orchestra is a youth orchestra based in Seville, Spain, made up of musicians from Israel, Palestine, and other Arab countries of the Middle East and North Africa, along with Spanish members. It was founded in 1999 by conductor and pianist Daniel Barenboim and the Palestinian literary scholar Edward Said. The orchestra's name comes from the West-östlicher Divan, a collection of poems by Goethe inspired by the Persian poet Hafez.

Aims and activities

The project was conceived not as a political statement but as a space in which young musicians from adversarial backgrounds could rehearse, perform, and discuss together, discovering that making music requires listening to one another. The orchestra holds an annual summer workshop and tour, and has performed at major venues and festivals worldwide, including the BBC Proms and Carnegie Hall, as well as giving a notable concert in Ramallah in 2005. It is supported by the Barenboim-Said Foundation and the regional government of Andalusia.

Reception

The orchestra has been widely honoured for its contribution to intercultural dialogue, while its founders have emphasised that music alone cannot bring peace — only the conditions for greater mutual understanding.
