Niccolò Machiavelli

Born3 May 1469, Florence, Italy
Died21 June 1527, Florence
Notable worksThe Prince; Discourses on Livy

Niccolò di Bernardo dei Machiavelli (3 May 1469 – 21 June 1527) was an Italian diplomat, author, philosopher and historian who lived during the Renaissance. He is best known for his political treatise The Prince (Il Principe), written around 1513 and published posthumously in 1532, which has made him a central figure in modern political philosophy.

Career in Florence

Machiavelli served as a senior official in the Florentine Republic from 1498 to 1512, undertaking diplomatic missions to the courts of France, Spain and the papacy. When the Medici returned to power in 1512, he was dismissed, accused of conspiracy, imprisoned and tortured, then released and retired to his farm at Sant'Andrea in Percussina.

Works

In retirement he wrote The Prince, a handbook for rulers on acquiring and maintaining power; the Discourses on the First Ten Books of Titus Livy, a republican-leaning commentary; The Art of War; the comic play The Mandrake; and a history of Florence. The adjective "Machiavellian" has come to describe cunning, duplicitous political conduct.

Legacy

Machiavelli is often called the father of modern political science for his insistence on describing politics as it is, rather than as it ought to be, and for his analysis of republics, armies and fortune (fortuna) opposed to human skill (virtù).
