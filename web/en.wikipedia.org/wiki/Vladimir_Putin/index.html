Vladimir Putin

Vladimir Vladimirovich Putin (born 7 October 1952) is a Russian politician and former intelligence officer who has been President of Russia since 2012, having previously served from 2000 to 2008 and as Prime Minister from 1999 to 2000 and 2008 to 2012. He is the longest-serving Russian leader since Joseph Stalin.

Early life and career

Putin was born in Leningrad (now Saint Petersburg) into a working-class family; his father was a factory foreman and his mother a factory worker. He studied law at Leningrad State University, graduating in 1975, and joined the KGB, serving 16 years as a foreign intelligence officer, rising to lieutenant colonel before resigning in 1991 during the collapse of the Soviet Union. Much of his service was spent in Dresden, East Germany.

Entering politics in Saint Petersburg under Mayor Anatoly Sobchak, he moved to Moscow in 1996, became director of the FSB (the KGB's successor) in 1998, and was appointed Prime Minister by President Boris Yeltsin in August 1999. When Yeltsin resigned on 31 December 1999, Putin became acting president, and won election in his own right in March 2000.

Presidency

His first two terms brought economic growth powered by high energy prices, a restoration of central state control, war in Chechnya, and increasing authoritarianism. Barred by term limits, he served as Prime Minister under President Dmitry Medvedev (2008–2012) before returning to the presidency in 2012 amid protests. Constitutional changes in 2020 reset his term count, allowing him to remain in office potentially until 2036.

Foreign policy

Putin's Russia intervened militarily in Georgia (2008), annexed Crimea from Ukraine in 2014, intervened in the Syrian civil war (2015), and launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 — the largest war in Europe since 1945 — triggering sweeping Western sanctions and Russia's suspension from many international bodies. In 2023 the International Criminal Court issued an arrest warrant against him over the deportation of Ukrainian children.
