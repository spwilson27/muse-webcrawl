Super Meat Boy

Super Meat Boy is a platform video game designed by Edmund McMillen and Tommy Refenes and developed by Team Meat. It was released for Xbox 360 through Xbox Live Arcade in October 2010 and for Microsoft Windows in November 2010, with later ports to many platforms. Players control Meat Boy, a small cube of raw meat, as he races through more than 300 short, hazard-filled levels to rescue his girlfriend, Bandage Girl, from the villain Dr. Fetus. The game is renowned for its precise controls and extreme difficulty, and it is regarded as one of the landmark indie games of its era.

Super Meat Boy is famous for tight controls and punishing, fast-restarting levels.

Gameplay

Each level is a compact obstacle course of buzzsaws, spikes, crumbling blocks, and salt. Meat Boy can run, jump, and wall-jump with highly responsive controls; death is instant but respawning is immediate, and a replay shows all of the player's failed attempts simultaneously. Levels are grouped into chapters, each ending with a boss fight, and completing a level quickly unlocks a harder "Dark World" variant.

Development

The game grew out of Meat Boy, a 2008 Flash game by McMillen and Jonathan McEntee. McMillen and programmer Tommy Refenes formed Team Meat and rebuilt the concept for consoles; the notoriously demanding development was documented in the 2012 film Indie Game: The Movie. Danny Baranowsky composed the soundtrack.

Reception

Super Meat Boy received critical acclaim, with review aggregators placing scores around 90 out of 100, and sold over a million copies within its first years. Critics praised its level design and the satisfying loop of failure and instant retry. A sequel, Super Meat Boy Forever, an auto-runner, was released in 2020.
