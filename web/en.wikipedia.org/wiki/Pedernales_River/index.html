Pedernales River


Location: Texas Hill Country, United States
Length: approx. 106 miles (171 km)
Source: near Harper, Kimble County, Texas
Mouth: Colorado River (Lake Travis)

The Pedernales River is a river in the Texas Hill Country of central Texas, United States. It flows roughly eastward for about 106 miles (171 km) from springs in Kimble County through Gillespie, Blanco, and Hays counties to its confluence with the Colorado River at Lake Travis. The name derives from the Spanish pedernal, meaning flint, for the flint rocks found along its banks.

Course and features

The river passes near Fredericksburg and Johnson City. Its best-known stretch runs through Pedernales Falls State Park in Blanco County, where the river crosses broad slabs of limestone in a series of falls and pools. The lower river widens as it enters the headwaters of Lake Travis, a Colorado River reservoir.

History

Lyndon B. Johnson National Historical Park includes the LBJ Ranch along the river near Stonewall; President Lyndon B. Johnson, who was born nearby, frequently hosted visitors and conducted business there, and the river featured prominently in his public image. The basin has historically been subject to severe flash flooding, including major floods in 1952 and later events, as is typical of Hill Country streams.

Recreation and ecology

The river supports fishing, swimming, and paddling where flows permit, and its riparian corridors provide habitat of the Edwards Plateau ecoregion, including cypress-lined banks and populations of the Guadalupe bass, the state fish of Texas.
