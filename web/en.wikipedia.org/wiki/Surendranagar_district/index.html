Surendranagar district

Surendranagar district is a district in the state of Gujarat in India. It is part of the Saurashtra (Kathiawar) peninsula region. The district headquarters is the city of Surendranagar. The district is named after the city, which in turn commemorates a ruler of the former princely state of Wadhwan.

Geography

The district lies in central Saurashtra and is bordered by the districts of Rajkot, Botad, Ahmedabad, Patan, Kutch and Morbi. Much of its northern edge touches the Little Rann of Kutch, a seasonal salt marsh. The Bhogavo and other seasonal rivers drain the area.

Economy

Surendranagar is an important centre for cotton ginning and trading, and the district is known for salt production along the Rann, ceramics, and textile and engineering industries. The city of Wadhwan, the twin town of Surendranagar, is a historic commercial centre.

Demographics

According to the 2011 census, Surendranagar district had a population of about 1.76 million. Gujarati is the predominant language. Major towns include Surendranagar, Wadhwan, Limbdi, Halvad, Dhrangadhra and Chotila, the last famous for its hilltop temple of the goddess Chamunda.

Places of interest

Attractions include the Tarnetar fair and temple near Thangadh, the Chamunda temple at Chotila, and wetlands such as Nalsarovar nearby that attract migratory birds.
