Amber Road

The Amber Road was an ancient trade route for the transfer of amber from coastal areas of the North Sea and the Baltic Sea to the Mediterranean Sea. Prehistoric trade routes between Northern and Southern Europe were defined by the amber trade, which was of particular significance because amber was prized for decoration and believed to have healing properties.

Overview

As an important commodity, sometimes dubbed "the gold of the north", amber was transported from the northern seas to the Mediterranean overland by way of the Vistula and Dnieper rivers as well as by sea. The trade is known from at least the time of the Egyptian pharaoh Tutankhamun, in whose tomb Baltic amber was found. During the Roman era, the main route ran south from the Baltic coast in present-day Kaliningrad Oblast and Lithuania, through the lands later known as Poland and Bohemia, to the Adriatic Sea and the Roman entrepôt of Aquileia.

History

Amber deposits in the Sambia Peninsula and along the Gulf of Gdańsk were the principal sources. Roman demand for amber grew sharply in the first century AD; the emperor Nero is said to have sent an expedition north to secure large quantities for decorating an amphitheatre. The route continued in use through the Middle Ages, and cities such as Gdańsk (Danzig) became wealthy centres of the amber trade and craftsmanship, a tradition reflected today in the region's amber jewellery industry and museums.

Routes

Several branches of the road are reconstructed from archaeological finds of amber hoards. The western branch ran along the Elbe and Rhine, while the central route passed through Moravia and the Danube valley to Carnuntum and Aquileia. Eastern branches reached the Black Sea and connected with trade networks extending toward Greece and beyond.

Legacy

The modern "Amber Road" name is used for tourist routes and cultural projects in Poland, Lithuania, Russia's Kaliningrad region, and other countries along the historic path, and parts of the corridor are promoted as a European Cultural Route.
