Field Spaniel

The Field Spaniel is a medium-sized breed of spaniel dog, developed in England in the late 19th century as a show and working gundog. Closely related to the Cocker Spaniel and English Springer Spaniel, it is distinguished by its solid-colour coat — typically black or liver, sometimes with tan points or roan markings — and its long, low-slung body.

History

The breed was developed from land spaniels at a time when spaniel types were classified by weight rather than function. Exaggerated breeding for an elongated body and short legs in the show ring nearly ruined the breed's health and popularity in the early 20th century, and it came close to extinction after the Second World War. Crosses with English Springer and Cocker Spaniels were used to revive it, restoring a more moderate, athletic build.

Temperament and health

Field Spaniels are known as docile, affectionate and independent dogs, active outdoors but calm in the home. They are a rare breed, registered in small numbers in the UK and North America. Reported health concerns include hip dysplasia and some eye and ear conditions.
