Creation and evolution in public education

Image: The Rhea County courthouse in Dayton, Tennessee, site of the Scopes trial (placeholder)

The creation and evolution controversy in public education concerns the teaching of biological origins in publicly funded schools, principally in the United States. Since the 1920s, religious groups — initially fundamentalist Christians opposed to the theory of evolution, and later advocates of creation science and intelligent design — have sought to have creationist accounts taught in science classes, while scientific and civil-liberties organisations have resisted on constitutional and scientific grounds.

Legal history in the United States

The 1925 Scopes "Monkey Trial" in Tennessee tested a state law forbidding the teaching of human evolution; teacher John Scopes was convicted, though the verdict was later overturned on a technicality. In Epperson v. Arkansas (1968) the Supreme Court struck down anti-evolution statutes as violations of the Establishment Clause. In Edwards v. Aguillard (1987) the Court invalidated Louisiana's "Creationism Act" requiring "balanced treatment" for creation science. In Kitzmiller v. Dover (2005), a federal district court held that intelligent design is a religious view and that teaching it in public-school science classes is unconstitutional.

Approaches and strategies

After successive court defeats, creationist strategies shifted from outright bans to "teach the controversy", "evidence against evolution" and "academic freedom" laws, such as the Louisiana Science Education Act (2008) and Tennessee's 2012 law, which permit teachers to present purported weaknesses of evolution. The scientific community maintains that evolution is a settled, central theory of biology and that creationist alternatives are not science. National science standards, including the Next Generation Science Standards (2013), teach evolution without creationist content.

Outside the United States

Most countries teach evolution in state schools without significant controversy. Creationism in education has nonetheless been debated in the United Kingdom (where government guidance bars teaching creationism as science), Turkey, South Korea and parts of Australia and Canada, generally with evolution retained in the official science curriculum.
