The Novgorod Detinets (Russian: Новгородский детинец), also known as the Novgorod Kremlin, is the historic fortified citadel of Veliky Novgorod in northwestern Russia. Standing on the left bank of the Volkhov River, it was the political and religious centre of the medieval Novgorod Republic. A detinets is the Old Russian term for the inner fortress of a city, equivalent to a kremlin.

History

The fortress was first mentioned in chronicles under the year 1044, when Prince Vladimir Yaroslavich built a wooden citadel enclosing the episcopal court and the newly built Cathedral of St Sophia (1045–1050). Stone walls gradually replaced the wooden defences from the 14th century; the present brick walls and towers were largely built in the late 15th century, around the time Novgorod was absorbed into the Grand Duchy of Moscow in 1478. The walls form an irregular oval about 1,487 metres long, and nine towers survive of the original twelve.

During the Second World War the kremlin was damaged while Novgorod was occupied and fought over; it was subsequently restored. It now houses the Novgorod State United Museum-Reserve and regional institutions.

Landmarks

  • Cathedral of St Sophia — built 1045–1050, one of the oldest stone churches in Russia, famous for its Byzantine-style frescoes and the 12th-century Magdeburg Gates.
  • Millennium of Russia monument — unveiled in 1862 to mark a thousand years since the legendary calling of the Varangians in 862, depicting 129 figures from Russian history.
  • Chamber of Facets (Vladychny Court) — a Gothic-influenced brick palace of 1433, among the oldest civic buildings in Russia.
  • St Nicholas Cathedral and the Yaroslav's Court — across the river on the Trade Side.
