Māori Battalion

28 (Māori) Battalion
Country: New Zealand
Active: 1940–1946
Size: about 3,600 served
Theatres: Greece, Crete, North Africa, Italy
Motto: Ake, ake, kia kaha ("For ever and ever be strong")

The 28 (Māori) Battalion was an infantry battalion of the New Zealand Army that served during the Second World War as part of the 2nd New Zealand Division. Formed in 1940 after Māori leaders pressed for a distinct Māori unit, it was organised into companies largely reflecting tribal groupings.

Service

The battalion fought in Greece and Crete in 1941, in the North African campaign — including at El Alamein — and in Italy, where it distinguished itself at Monte Cassino and in the advance to Trieste. Its soldiers earned a formidable reputation; the German commander Erwin Rommel is reported to have praised the battalion, and its members won numerous decorations, including a Victoria Cross for Te Moananui-a-Kiwa Ngarimu at Tebaga Gap in 1943.

Losses and legacy

Of about 3,600 men who served, roughly 650 were killed and some 1,700 wounded — a heavy price described by Māori leader Sir Āpirana Ngata as the price of citizenship. The battalion was disbanded in 1946; its veterans' association kept its memory alive until its final wind-up in 2012.
