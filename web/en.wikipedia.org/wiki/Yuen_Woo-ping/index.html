Yuen Woo-ping (袁和平)
Born: 1 January 1945, Guangzhou, China
Occupation: Martial arts choreographer, film director
Years active: 1971–present

Yuen Woo-ping (Chinese: 袁和平; born 1945) is a Hong Kong martial arts choreographer and film director who is widely considered one of the most influential figures in action cinema. He is credited with bringing wirework ("wire fu") fight choreography to international prominence.

Career

The son of martial arts actor Simon Yuen Siu-tien, Yuen Woo-ping began as a stuntman and made his directorial debut with Snake in the Eagle's Shadow (1978) and Drunken Master (1978), which launched Jackie Chan to stardom. Through the 1980s and 1990s he choreographed and directed Hong Kong classics including Iron Monkey (1993) and Fist of Legend (1994).

His choreography for The Matrix (1999) brought him global fame, and he subsequently choreographed Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (2000), Quentin Tarantino's Kill Bill films, House of Flying Daggers (2004), and Kung Fu Hustle (2004). He won the Hong Kong Film Award for Best Action Choreography multiple times. His brothers formed the famed Yuen clan of stunt performers and choreographers.
