Hydrosalpinx

A hydrosalpinx is a fallopian tube that is blocked at its distal (fimbrial) end and distended with clear, watery fluid. The name derives from Greek hydro (water) and salpinx (trumpet/tube).

Causes

The most common cause is pelvic inflammatory disease, often from chlamydia or gonorrhoea infection, which damages the fimbriae and causes them to seal shut. Other causes include endometriosis, previous surgery, appendicitis, and tubal ligation.

Clinical significance

Hydrosalpinges cause tubal-factor infertility: the blockage prevents egg and sperm meeting, and fluid may drain into the uterus and reduce implantation rates during IVF. Management may include salpingectomy or proximal tubal occlusion before IVF, or in selected cases reconstructive surgery. Diagnosis is by ultrasound, hysterosalpingography, or laparoscopy.
