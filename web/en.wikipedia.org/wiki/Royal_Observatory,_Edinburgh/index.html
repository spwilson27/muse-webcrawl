The Royal Observatory, Edinburgh (ROE) is an astronomical institution located on Blackford Hill in Edinburgh, Scotland. Founded on Calton Hill in 1822 as the Astronomical Institution of Edinburgh's observatory, it became a government establishment in 1853 and moved to its present site in 1896. The observatory played an important role in astronomy and in the provision of an accurate time service for the city and for mariners.

History

The first Astronomer Royal for Scotland, appointed in 1834, was Thomas Henderson, who from observations at the Cape of Good Hope made the first reliable measurement of stellar parallax, for Alpha Centauri. Under later directors including Charles Piazzi Smyth, the observatory ran the famous "time ball" and one-o'clock gun time signals for Edinburgh. Growth of city lighting forced the 1896 move from Calton Hill — whose Playfair building remains an Edinburgh landmark — to the darker Blackford Hill site, with twin domes housing a 36-inch reflector and a 24/16-inch Schmidt camera in later years.

Modern role

Today the hill hosts the University of Edinburgh's Institute for Astronomy and the Science and Technology Facilities Council's UK Astronomy Technology Centre, a world leader in building instruments for telescopes such as the James Webb Space Telescope and the Extremely Large Telescope. The observatory maintains a popular visitor centre and public astronomy program; its archive includes the Crawford Library of rare astronomical books.
