Safe and Sophie Germain primes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A safe prime is a prime number of the form 2p + 1, where p is also a prime. Conversely, the prime p in this relationship is called a Sophie Germain prime, after the French mathematician Sophie Germain, who used such primes in her pioneering work on Fermat's Last Theorem. The first Sophie Germain primes are 2, 3, 5, 11, 23, 29, 41, 53, 83, 89, …, giving safe primes 5, 7, 11, 23, 47, 59, 83, 107, 167, 179, …

Properties

Every safe prime greater than 7 can be written in the form 12k − 1. It is conjectured but not proved that there are infinitely many Sophie Germain primes; the largest known examples have hundreds of thousands of digits and are found by distributed computing projects. Chains in which each term generates the next (Cunningham chains) generalise the concept.

Applications

Safe primes are important in cryptography. In the Diffie–Hellman key exchange and related protocols, working modulo a safe prime q = 2p + 1 guarantees a large prime-order subgroup, frustrating attacks such as the Pohlig–Hellman algorithm that exploit smooth group orders. Safe primes were traditionally used in DSA parameter generation for the same reason.
