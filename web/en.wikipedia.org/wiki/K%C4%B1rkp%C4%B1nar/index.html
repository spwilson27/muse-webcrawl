Kırkpınar

LocationEdirne, Turkey
SportOil wrestling (yağlı güreş)
First heldTraditionally 1361/1362

Kırkpınar is a Turkish oil wrestling (yağlı güreş) tournament held annually in Edirne, usually in late June or early July. It is widely regarded as the oldest continuously held sporting competition in the world, with a traditional founding date of 1361 or 1362, and is inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

History

Legend traces the event to Ottoman soldiers wrestling near the frontier; one famous bout between two brothers lasted until both died, and a fig tree and spring — the "forty springs" (Kırkpınar) — marked the site. Historically held in the village of Samona and later at Ahıköy, the tournament moved to Sarayiçi island in Edirne in 1924.

The tournament

Wrestlers (pehlivan) wearing leather trousers called kispet are doused in olive oil and compete in graded categories, accompanied by davul and zurna musicians and a master of ceremonies (cazgır) who introduces them with rhyming praise. The winner of the top category receives the title Başpehlivan and a golden belt; winning three consecutive years earns permanent possession of the belt.
