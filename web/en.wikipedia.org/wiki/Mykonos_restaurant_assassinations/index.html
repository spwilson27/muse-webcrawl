Mykonos restaurant assassinations

The Mykonos restaurant in Berlin, scene of the 1992 attack.

The Mykonos restaurant assassinations were a shooting attack on 17 September 1992 at the Mykonos Greek restaurant in Berlin, Germany, in which gunmen killed four exiled Iranian-Kurdish opposition figures: Sadegh Sharafkandi, secretary-general of the Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan (KDPI), his aides Fattah Abdoli and Homayoun Ardalan, and their translator Nouri Dehkordi. A waiter was wounded. The trial that followed, known in Germany as the Mykonos trial, concluded that the murders had been ordered by senior officials of the Iranian state, causing a major diplomatic rupture between Iran and the European Union.

Attack and investigation

Two masked gunmen entered the restaurant shortly after 22:45 and opened fire with a machine pistol and a handgun on the group, which had gathered after a Socialist International congress in Berlin; the visiting delegation had asked for, but not received, police protection. German investigators linked the weapons and suspects to Iranian intelligence networks and to Hezbollah operatives. Five men were arrested and tried from 1993 in a high-security proceeding lasting over three years, partly based on testimony from a former Iranian intelligence defector.

Verdict and aftermath

On 10 April 1997 the Berlin Higher Regional Court convicted Kazem Darabi, an Iranian intelligence operative, and Abbas Rhayel of the murders and sentenced them to life imprisonment; two others received prison terms. The judgment stated that the decision to kill had been taken by a secret Iranian "Committee for Special Operations" that included Iran's Supreme Leader, president, foreign minister and intelligence minister — a judicial finding, unprecedented in Europe, that Iran's leadership had ordered assassinations abroad. EU member states recalled their ambassadors from Tehran, and relations were frozen for a time. Darabi was released and deported to Iran in 2007 after serving 15 years. See also Sadegh Sharafkandi.
