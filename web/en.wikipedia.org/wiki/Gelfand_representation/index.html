Gelfand representation

The Gelfand transform identifies an algebra with functions on its spectrum
FieldFunctional analysis
Named forIsrael Gelfand

In mathematics, the Gelfand representation of a Banach algebra expresses the algebra as an algebra of continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space, its character space (or spectrum). Developed by Israel Gelfand in the 1940s, it is a cornerstone of functional analysis and of the theory of commutative C*-algebras.

Construction

For a commutative Banach algebra A, a character is a nonzero multiplicative linear functional on A. The set of all characters, equipped with the weak-* topology, forms a locally compact Hausdorff space ΦA, called the spectrum of A. The Gelfand transform of an element a is the function â on ΦA defined by â(φ) = φ(a). This transform is an algebra homomorphism from A into the algebra of continuous functions vanishing at infinity on ΦA.

Gelfand–Naimark theorem

For a commutative C*-algebra, the Gelfand transform is an isometric *-isomorphism onto C0(ΦA): every commutative C*-algebra is exactly the algebra of continuous functions on its spectrum. This correspondence reverses arrows, identifying commutative C*-algebras with locally compact Hausdorff spaces, and is often summarized by saying that commutative C*-algebra theory is topology in disguise. It also yields the continuous functional calculus for normal operators on Hilbert space and underlies the spectral theorem.

Applications

The Gelfand representation gives proofs of Wiener's theorem on absolutely convergent Fourier series, a framework for the spectral theory of bounded self-adjoint and normal operators, and the starting point for noncommutative geometry, in which general C*-algebras are treated as "noncommutative topological spaces".
