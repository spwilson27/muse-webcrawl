The Tridentine Mass, also known as the Traditional Latin Mass, is the form of the Roman Rite Mass contained in the Roman Missals published from 1570 to 1962. It was codified by Pope Pius V in 1570 following the Council of Trent, from which it takes its name, and it largely replaced the many local variants of the Roman Rite in the Latin Church.

History

Pius V's bull Quo primum tempore (1570) standardised the missal for most of the Latin Church. Minor revisions followed under later popes, the last being the 1962 missal of John XXIII. After the Second Vatican Council, the Mass of Paul VI (1969) became the ordinary form of the Roman Rite. Pope Benedict XVI's motu proprio Summorum Pontificum (2007) designated the 1962 form the "extraordinary form" and liberalised its use; Pope Francis's Traditionis custodes (2021) reimposed restrictions.

Characteristics

The Tridentine Mass is celebrated almost entirely in Latin, with the priest generally facing the altar (ad orientem) rather than the congregation. It includes fixed prayers such as the Prayers at the Foot of the Altar, the Roman Canon (the only Eucharistic prayer), the Last Gospel (the prologue of John), and chants of the Gregorian tradition. Sung forms are classified as Solemn Mass, Missa cantata and Low Mass.
