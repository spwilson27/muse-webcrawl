Video assistant referee

The video assistant referee (VAR) is a match official in association football who assists the referee by reviewing decisions using video footage. The system was incorporated into the Laws of the Game in 2018 by the International Football Association Board (IFAB) following trials in major competitions.

Procedure

VAR may be used only for four categories of incidents: goals and offences leading to a goal, penalty decisions, direct red card incidents, and mistaken identity. The video assistant referee monitors the match from a video operation room and alerts the referee to clear and obvious errors or serious missed incidents. The referee may conduct an on-field review at a pitchside monitor before making a final decision; the referee retains ultimate authority.

History

The system was trialled in the 2016 FIFA Club World Cup, used at the 2017 FIFA Confederations Cup, and deployed at a World Cup for the first time at the 2018 FIFA World Cup in Russia. It subsequently spread to most major domestic leagues, including the Premier League from 2019–20.

Criticism

VAR has been praised for correcting clear errors but criticised for delays, marginal offside calls measured in millimetres, and inconsistency in handball interpretations. Semi-automated offside technology was introduced at the 2022 World Cup to speed decisions.
