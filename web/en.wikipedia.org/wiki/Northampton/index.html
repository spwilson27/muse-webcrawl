Northampton is a town and civil parish in the East Midlands of England, lying on the River Nene, some 60 miles (97 km) north-west of London and 45 miles (72 km) south-east of Birmingham. It is the county town of Northamptonshire and one of the largest towns in the United Kingdom. The town grew around a Saxon settlement and a Norman castle, and became famous for its shoemaking and leather industries, which supplied boots to the British Army.

History

Archaeological finds show settlement in the area from the Bronze Age. The Saxon settlement of Hamtun grew near a ford of the Nene. Northampton Castle, built shortly after the Norman Conquest, hosted parliaments and was where Thomas Becket was tried in 1164. In 1460 the Battle of Northampton was fought during the Wars of the Roses, resulting in the capture of King Henry VI by the Yorkists.

The Great Fire of Northampton in 1675 destroyed much of the town, which was rebuilt in stone. The rebuilding produced the handsome Sessions House and the church of All Saints. During the English Civil War the town supported Parliament and its shoemakers supplied boots to the Parliamentary army — a tradition repeated in both World Wars.

Shoemaking

Northampton became the centre of the English boot and shoe industry, thanks to local supplies of oak bark for tanning, water, and ironstone. By the 19th century hundreds of factories operated in the town. Although most mass production has moved abroad, firms such as Church's, John Lobb, Crockett & Jones and Edward Green still make high-quality shoes in and around Northampton.

Landmarks

Notable landmarks include the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, one of only four round churches in England; the Guildhall, designed by E. W. Godwin; and the National Lift Tower (the "Northampton Lighthouse"), a 127-metre tower built for testing lifts. The town is also home to the Northampton Museum and Art Gallery, which holds one of the world's largest collections of footwear.

Sport

Northampton Saints rugby union club play at Franklin's Gardens, and Northampton Town F.C. ("the Cobblers") play at Sixfields Stadium. The nearby Silverstone Circuit, home of the British Grand Prix, lies close to the county border.
