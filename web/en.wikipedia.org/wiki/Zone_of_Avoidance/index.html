Zone of Avoidance

Zone of Avoidance
TypeRegion of sky obscured by the Milky Way's galactic plane
ExtentRoughly 20% of the extragalactic sky (varies by wavelength)
CauseInterstellar dust, gas, and dense star fields
Notable hidden objectsGreat Attractor; Maffei 1 and 2; IC 342

The Zone of Avoidance (ZOA, or Zone of Galactic Obscuration) is the area of the sky that is obscured by the Milky Way. The interstellar dust, gas, and dense star fields in the galactic plane block visible light from roughly a fifth of the extragalactic sky, creating a band across the sky in which very few external galaxies were historically catalogued.

Observing through the zone

Extinction decreases at longer wavelengths, so infrared, radio, and X-ray surveys have filled in much of the zone: the 21-cm line of neutral hydrogen passes through dust largely unaffected, revealing spirals hidden behind the plane. Nevertheless, near the galactic centre the zone remains wide and completeness drops sharply.

Significant objects

The Zone of Avoidance conceals part of the local large-scale structure of the universe. The Great Attractor, a gravitational anomaly drawing in the Milky Way and neighbouring galaxy groups, lies within it, as do nearby galaxies such as Maffei 1 and Maffei 2, IC 342 (the "Hidden Galaxy"), and Dwingeloo 1. Mapping the zone has been essential for understanding the local cosmic flow and the full extent of structures such as the Norma Cluster and the Vela Supercluster.
