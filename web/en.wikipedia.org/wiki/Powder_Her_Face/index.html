Powder Her Face

Composer: Thomas Adès

Librettist: Philip Hensher

Premiere: 1 July 1995, Cheltenham Music Festival

Language: English

Powder Her Face, Op. 14, is a chamber opera in two acts by the British composer Thomas Adès, with a libretto by Philip Hensher. It was first performed on 1 July 1995 at the Cheltenham Music Festival, with Jill Gomez in the leading role. The opera is loosely based on the scandalous life of Margaret Campbell, Duchess of Argyll (1912–1993), whose 1963 divorce case made headlines after compromising photographs of her were produced in court.

Background

The opera was commissioned by Almeida Opera and written when Adès was only 24. Its title derives from the instruction in cosmetic advertising of the period, and the work combines lyrical writing with cabaret, tango and popular-song idioms. Adès scored it for just three singers besides the soprano lead: a maid, an electrician and a waiter, who double numerous smaller roles, plus an ensemble of some fifteen players.

Synopsis

The opera traces the Duchess from her dazzling debut in 1930s high society through her marriage, infidelities, public disgrace in the 1963 divorce proceedings, and her final impoverished years living in a hotel room, ending with her eviction in 1990. Scenes alternate between her youthful glamour and her lonely old age, with the other characters commenting on, and profiting from, her rise and fall.

Reception

Powder Her Face was an immediate critical success and has become one of the most performed British operas of recent decades, with productions in the United States, Germany, Israel and elsewhere. Critics praised its wit, virtuosity and the pathos of its closing scenes.
