Atlantic coastal plain

The Atlantic coastal plain is a physiographic region of low relief along the East Coast of the United States. It extends 2,200 miles (3,500 km) from the New York Bight southward to a Georgia/Florida section of the Eastern Continental Divide, which demarcates the plain from the ACF River Basin in the Gulf Coastal Plain to the west.

Geology

The province is underlain by Cretaceous and Cenozoic sediments deposited as the Atlantic margin subsided after the rifting of Pangaea. The sediments dip and thicken seaward, forming a wedge over older crystalline basement. The "fall line", where harder Piedmont rocks meet the soft coastal sediments, marks its inland edge and historically provided water power and head-of-navigation sites for cities such as Richmond, Columbia and Trenton.

Geography and ecology

The plain includes barrier islands, salt marshes, estuaries such as Chesapeake and Delaware bays, and the pine barrens and longleaf pine ecosystems of the southeast. Elevations rarely exceed 100 metres. Large areas are vulnerable to flooding, hurricanes and sea-level rise.
