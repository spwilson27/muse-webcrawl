The Third Punic War (149–146 BC) was the third and last of the Punic Wars fought between Carthage and Rome. The war was a much smaller engagement than the two previous Punic Wars and consisted principally of a single main action, the Battle of Carthage, but resulted in the complete destruction of the city of Carthage, the annexation of all remaining Carthaginian territory by Rome, and the death or enslavement of the entire Carthaginian population.

Background

In the 50 years between the end of the Second Punic War and the start of the Third, Carthage made a series of five appeals to Rome to adjudicate in boundary disputes with the Numidian kingdom of Masinissa, and in each case Rome decided in Numidia's favour. In 151 BC the Carthaginians levied an army to repel Numidian encroachments, in breach of the treaty that had ended the Second Punic War, which forbade Carthage from making war without Roman permission. Senator Cato the Elder had long urged Carthage's destruction, reportedly ending every speech with the phrase Carthago delenda est — "Carthage must be destroyed".

Course of the war

When Rome declared war in 149 BC, the Carthaginians surrendered all of their weapons and handed over hostages, hoping to satisfy Roman demands. The Romans then demanded that the population abandon the city and resettle away from the coast — a demand the Carthaginians refused, resolving to resist. The city was placed under siege, which the Romans initially prosecuted ineffectively. In 147 BC command passed to Scipio Aemilianus, who tightened the blockade, built a mole to close the harbour, and in the spring of 146 BC stormed the city. After a week of house-to-house fighting, the surviving defenders surrendered; the city was systematically burnt and demolished.

Aftermath

The ruins of Carthage were ploughed over and, according to later tradition, sown with salt — a detail not found in ancient sources. Carthage's remaining territory became the Roman province of Africa. A century later Julius Caesar and then Augustus refounded the city as Colonia Julia Carthago, which grew into one of the great cities of the Roman Empire.
