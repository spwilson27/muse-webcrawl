Turks in Germany

Turks in Germany form the largest ethnic minority group in the Federal Republic of Germany. Estimates of the population of Turkish origin, including Kurds from Turkey, range from about 2.8 to 3 million people, many of them German citizens.

History

Large-scale migration began with the 1961 recruitment agreement between West Germany and Turkey, under which "guest workers" (Gastarbeiter) came to fill labour shortages during the economic miracle. Recruitment stopped in 1973, but family reunification continued, and substantial communities formed in cities such as Berlin, Cologne, Hamburg, and the Ruhr industrial region. Asylum migration, including many Kurds, added to the community in the 1980s and 1990s.

Society and culture

Turkish Germans have made significant contributions to German culture — in cuisine (the döner kebab was popularised in Berlin), football (players such as İlkay Gündoğan and Mesut Özil), cinema (director Fatih Akin), and politics. Debates over integration, dual citizenship, and the role of Turkish state-linked institutions such as DİTİB mosques remain recurring themes in German public life. A 2000 reform of citizenship law allowed many German-born children of Turkish parents to acquire German citizenship.
