Emirate of Diriyah

The Emirate of Diriyah, also known as the First Saudi State, was established in 1727 when Muhammad ibn Saud, the ruler of the oasis town of Diriyah in the Najd, joined forces with the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab. The emirate expanded to rule most of the Arabian Peninsula before it was destroyed by Ottoman–Egyptian forces in the Wahhabi War of 1811–1818.


The ruins of Diriyah, destroyed in 1818 and now a heritage site.

Foundation

In 1744 Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, expelled from neighbouring Uyayna for his preaching against practices he regarded as idolatrous (such as veneration of saints and tombs), took refuge with Muhammad ibn Saud at Diriyah. Their alliance — the Diriyah pact — combined the Saud family's political and military leadership with ibn Abd al-Wahhab's programme of puritanical Islamic reform (tawhid, the absolute oneness of God). The doctrine's followers were known to opponents as Wahhabis.

Expansion

Under Muhammad ibn Saud (d. 1765), his son Abd al-Aziz (d. 1803), and his grandson Saud ibn Abd al-Aziz ("Saud the Great", d. 1814), the emirate absorbed the Najd, broke the power of rivals such as the rulers of Riyadh (conquered 1773), and expanded into eastern Arabia (al-Ahsa), the coasts of the Gulf, and eventually the Hejaz. In 1802 Saudi forces sacked the Shia holy city of Karbala in Ottoman Iraq, and in 1803–1805 they took Mecca and Medina, removing their rulers from Ottoman allegiance and imposing their religious practices on the holy cities.

Destruction

The loss of the Hejaz provoked the Ottoman Sultan to commission Muhammad Ali Pasha of Egypt to recover it. In the Wahhabi War (1811–1818), Egyptian forces under Tusun Pasha and then Ibrahim Pasha retook the Hejaz and invaded the Najd. After a six-month siege, Diriyah surrendered in September 1818; the town was razed and its ruler, Abdullah ibn Saud, was sent to Constantinople and executed. A second Saudi state (the Emirate of Nejd) was re-established by Turki ibn Abdullah in 1824.

Legacy

The Diriyah alliance remains the founding compact of the Saudi dynasty: political rule by the Al Saud legitimated by the religious establishment descended from ibn Abd al-Wahhab. Diriyah's ruins at At-Turaif are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
