Secret history (genre)

Secret history
ExemplarProcopius, Secret History (Anekdota)
ThemesHidden motives, scandals, revisionist accounts

Secret history is a genre of non-fiction (and a narrative device in fiction) in which the author claims to reveal hidden, suppressed, or behind-the-scenes accounts of historical events, often contradicting official versions.

Origins

The namesake work is Procopius's Secret History (Greek: Anekdota, "unpublished things"), a scathing private account of Justinian I, Theodora, and Belisarius that sharply contradicted Procopius's own official histories of the Byzantine wars. It circulated only after his death.

Later examples

The genre expanded with court memoirs, exposés, and revisionist histories presenting concealed motives, conspiracies, or private lives of rulers. In fiction, "secret history" denotes stories inserting hidden events into real history — for example, that supernatural or conspiratorial forces explain known events — a usage popularized for works like Tim Powers's novels. Related fields include the pamphlet literature of the French court and modern investigative history.
