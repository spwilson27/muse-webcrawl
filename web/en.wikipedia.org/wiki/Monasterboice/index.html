Monasterboice

Monasterboice (Irish: Mainistir Bhuithe) are the ruins of an early Christian monastic settlement in County Louth, Ireland, north of Drogheda. Founded in the late 5th century by Saint Buithe, a follower of Saint Patrick, the site is famous for its round tower and, above all, for some of the finest surviving Irish high crosses, including Muiredach's High Cross, regarded as the masterpiece of Irish monumental sculpture.

History

The monastery was founded around 521 by St Buithe, who according to tradition died in that year. Monasterboice became an important centre of learning and craftsmanship; its abbots included Muiredach mac Domnaill (died 923), under whose patronage the great cross was erected. The monastery was raided by Vikings and was burned in 1097, a fire that destroyed its library and treasures. After the foundation of the Cistercian abbey of Mellifont nearby in 1142, Monasterboice declined in importance.

Features

The site contains the remains of two churches, a sundial and a round tower some 28 metres high, which lost its conical cap in the past. The round tower was used for defence and storage; tradition holds that its interior was burned in the fire of 1097.

The high crosses

Muiredach's High Cross, dating to the 10th century, is 5.8 metres tall and carved with panels of biblical scenes on both faces — the most extensive programme of sculptured iconography on any Irish cross. An inscription at its base asks for "a prayer for Muiredach by whom this cross was made." The West Cross (the Tall Cross), at about 6.5 metres, is among the tallest in Ireland, and the plainer North Cross also survives on the site.
