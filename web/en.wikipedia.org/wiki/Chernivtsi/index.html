Chernivtsi

Chernivtsi (Ukrainian: Чернівці; Romanian: Cernăuți; German: Czernowitz) is a city in southwestern Ukraine, on the upper course of the River Prut. It is the administrative center of Chernivtsi Oblast and of Chernivtsi Raion. Chernivtsi is also a major cultural and educational center, known for the Residence of Bukovinian and Dalmatian Metropolitans, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2011.

History

A fortified settlement existed on the site from the time of Kievan Rus'. The town developed as a trade centre on the route between Central Europe and the Black Sea. From the 14th century it belonged to the Principality of Moldavia; the first written mention dates to 1408, in a charter of Alexander the Good. In 1775 the region of Bukovina was annexed by the Habsburg Monarchy, and Czernowitz became its capital. Under Austrian rule the city grew into a multicultural centre with Ukrainian, Romanian, German, Jewish, and Polish communities, and was sometimes called "Little Vienna".

After the First World War, Bukovina was united with the Kingdom of Romania, and the city was renamed Cernăuți. In 1940 northern Bukovina was occupied by the Soviet Union and incorporated into the Ukrainian SSR; after a period of Romanian reoccupation during the Second World War, it returned to Soviet control in 1944. Since 1991 it has been part of independent Ukraine.

Culture and education

Chernivtsi University, founded in 1875 as Franz-Josephs-Universität, is housed in the former Residence of Bukovinian and Dalmatian Metropolitans, built in the 19th century to designs by the Czech architect Josef Hlávka. The city was a notable centre of German-language literature; the poet Paul Celan was born there in 1920, as was the writer Rose Ausländer. The novelist Gregor von Rezzori also came from Chernivtsi.
