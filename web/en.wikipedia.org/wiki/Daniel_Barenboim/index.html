Daniel Barenboim

Born: November 15, 1942, Buenos Aires, Argentina
Occupations: Pianist, conductor
Posts: Chicago Symphony Orchestra; Staatskapelle Berlin; La Scala
Citizenships: Argentine, Israeli, Spanish, Palestinian

Daniel Barenboim (born November 15, 1942) is an Argentine-Israeli classical pianist and conductor. A child prodigy who debuted in Buenos Aires at age seven, he built a dual career as a leading pianist — known for cycles of Beethoven and Mozart — and as music director of the Orchestre de Paris, the Chicago Symphony Orchestra (1991–2006) and the Berlin Staatskapelle, and general music director of La Scala, Milan.

West–Eastern Divan Orchestra

In 1999 Barenboim co-founded the West–Eastern Divan Orchestra with the Palestinian scholar Edward Said, bringing together young Israeli and Arab musicians to perform and study together. The project earned him international recognition as an advocate for dialogue in the Middle East, alongside criticism from various sides for his outspoken views.

Life and honours

Barenboim holds Argentine, Israeli and Spanish citizenship and in 2008 accepted an honorary Palestinian passport — widely reported as the first person to hold Israeli and Palestinian citizenship simultaneously. He was married to the cellist Jacqueline du Pré until her death in 1987, and later to pianist Elena Bashkirova. He has received numerous honours, including multiple Grammy Awards and an honorary British knighthood (KBE).
