Philippe Quinault


Philippe Quinault (3 June 1635 – 26 November 1688) was a French dramatist and librettist, best known as the collaborator of Jean-Baptiste Lully in creating French opera.

Philippe Quinault (3 June 1635 – 26 November 1688) was a French dramatist and librettist. Born in Paris, the son of a master baker, he was educated thanks to the patronage of the poet Tristan l'Hermite, and his first play was performed when he was eighteen.

Career

Quinault wrote comedies, tragedies and tragicomedies for the Paris stage, including La mère coquette (1665) and the tragedy Astrate (1665). He was elected to the Académie française in 1670 and later became auditor in the Chambre des comptes.

Collaboration with Lully

From 1673 until shortly before his death, Quinault supplied the libretti for nearly all of the operas (tragédies en musique) of Jean-Baptiste Lully, the dominant composer at the court of Louis XIV. Their works — among them Alceste (1674), Atys (1676), Phaëton (1683), Armide (1686) and Acis et Galatée (1686) — established the classical form of French Baroque opera, with a five-act structure, a prologue glorifying the king, and subjects drawn from mythology and chivalric romance, using libretti by turns drawn from Ovid, Ariosto and Tasso.

Quinault's verse was admired for its tenderness and naturalness, though satirists such as Boileau mocked its softness. His libretto for Armide, based on Tasso's Gerusalemme liberata, was still being set to music a century later, most famously by Gluck (1777).
