Charles Friedel

Charles Friedel (12 March 1832 – 20 April 1899) was a French chemist and mineralogist. Together with his collaborator James Crafts, he developed the Friedel–Crafts alkylation and acylation reactions in 1877, among the most important methods in organic synthesis.

Life

Friedel was born in Strasbourg and studied at the University of Strasbourg and the Sorbonne in Paris. He worked in the laboratory of Charles Adolphe Wurtz and later became professor of mineralogy and then of organic chemistry at the Sorbonne. He was a founder of the Société Chimique de France and a member of the French Academy of Sciences.

Scientific work

Working with James Crafts, Friedel discovered in 1877 that aluminium chloride catalyses the attachment of alkyl and acyl groups to aromatic rings — the reactions that bear their names. In mineralogy he studied pyroelectricity in crystals and synthesised several minerals, including wurtzite. His son, Georges Friedel, became a noted crystallographer.
