Takarai Kikaku

Takarai Kikaku (1661–1707)
Also known as: Enomoto Kikaku
Occupation: Haiku poet
School: Bashō school

Takarai Kikaku (宝井 其角, 1661 – April 1, 1707), also known as Enomoto Kikaku, was a Japanese haiku poet of the Edo period and one of the most prominent disciples of Matsuo Bashō.

Life and work

Born in Edo, Kikaku studied haikai under Bashō from his teens and became one of the Bashō school's leading figures, famed across the city for wit and polished technique. Where Bashō sought depth and lightness, Kikaku favoured cleverness, urban elegance, and wordplay, and the two are often contrasted in accounts of haiku aesthetics. A famous exchange has the young Kikaku propose a verse about a red dragonfly with its wings removed becoming a chilli pepper; Bashō reversed it — a chilli pepper given wings becomes a dragonfly — valuing added life over taken life.

Legacy

Kikaku edited collections of haikai and influenced later Edo poets. His verses remain anthologised, and he appears as a character in accounts of Bashō's circle alongside poets such as Hattori Ransetsu.
