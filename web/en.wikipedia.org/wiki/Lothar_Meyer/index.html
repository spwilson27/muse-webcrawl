Overview

Julius Lothar Meyer (1830–1895) was a German chemist who, independently of Dmitri Mendeleev, developed a periodic table of the elements. In his 1864 textbook he arranged 28 elements by valency; in 1870 he published a nearly complete table ordered by atomic weight together with his famous curve of atomic volume against atomic weight, which vividly displayed periodicity.

Priority

Meyer and Mendeleev disputed priority politely for years; the Royal Society awarded them the Davy Medal jointly in 1882. Meyer, however, did not use his table to predict new elements as boldly as Mendeleev did, and history has credited the Russian more. Meyer spent most of his career at Tübingen, where he also worked on gas theory and chemical education.
