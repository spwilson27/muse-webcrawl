Adams–Onís Treaty

Adams–Onís Treaty
SignedFebruary 22, 1819, Washington, D.C.
EffectiveFebruary 22, 1821
PartiesUnited States; Spain
NegotiatorsJohn Quincy Adams; Luis de Onís

The Adams–Onís Treaty of 1819, also known as the Transcontinental Treaty, the Spanish Cession, the Florida Purchase Treaty, or the Florida Treaty, was a treaty between the United States and Spain that ceded Florida to the U.S. and defined the boundary between the U.S. and New Spain.

Terms

Spain ceded East Florida and renounced claims to West Florida. In exchange, the United States assumed up to $5 million in claims by American citizens against Spain and renounced its claims to Texas. The treaty fixed the western boundary of the Louisiana Purchase along the Sabine River, the Red River, the Arkansas River, and the 42nd parallel to the Pacific Ocean, giving the United States a claim to the Pacific Northwest.

Legacy

Negotiated by Secretary of State John Quincy Adams and Spanish minister Luis de Onís, the treaty was ratified in 1821 after delays caused by unrest in Spain and its American colonies. It is regarded as a triumph of Adams's diplomacy and a milestone in the continental expansion of the United States.
