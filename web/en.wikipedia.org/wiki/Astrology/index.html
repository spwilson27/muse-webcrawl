Astrology is a range of divinatory practices, recognised as pseudoscientific since the 18th century, that claim to discern information about human affairs and terrestrial events by studying the apparent positions of celestial objects. Different cultures have employed forms of astrology since at least the 2nd millennium BCE, and the practice originated in calendrical systems used to predict seasonal shifts and interpret celestial cycles as signs of divine communications.

Most, if not all, cultures have attached importance to what they observed in the sky, and some — such as the Hindus, Chinese, and Maya — developed elaborate systems for predicting terrestrial events from celestial observations. Western astrology, one of the oldest astrological systems still in use, can be traced to 19th–17th century BCE Mesopotamia, from where it spread to Ancient Greece, Rome, the Islamic world, and eventually Central and Western Europe.

History

Babylonian astrologers developed a system of omens based on celestial phenomena, and the twelve-sign zodiac had emerged by the 5th century BCE. In Hellenistic Egypt, Babylonian astrology was combined with the Egyptian system of decanic astrology to create horoscopic astrology, which assigns significance to the position of the planets at the moment of a person's birth. Ptolemy's 2nd-century work Tetrabiblos became a foundational text of Western astrology. Through the medieval Islamic world, astrology flourished in courts and universities, and it returned to Europe where figures such as Johannes Kepler practised it alongside astronomy.

During the Enlightenment, astrology lost its academic standing; astronomy and astrology, long treated as one discipline, were separated. Controlled studies have failed to find evidence that astrological claims describe reality, and the scientific community regards astrology as a pseudoscience.

Western astrology

Western astrology is based on the tropical zodiac, a coordinate system divided into twelve signs of 30 degrees each, beginning with Aries at the vernal equinox. A horoscope is a chart cast for a specific time and place recording the positions of the Sun, Moon, and planets in the signs and in twelve houses, whose meanings derive from the hour of birth. Practitioners interpret aspects — angular relationships such as conjunctions, sextiles, squares, trines, and oppositions — between these bodies.

Other traditions

Vedic astrology (Jyotiṣa) uses the sidereal zodiac, which accounts for the precession of the equinoxes, and gives central importance to the Moon's sign and the lunar mansions (nakshatras). Chinese astrology is organised around a twelve-year cycle of animal signs linked to the orbit of Jupiter, combined with five elements and yin–yang theory.

Scientific evaluation

Astrology has not demonstrated its effectiveness in controlled studies and has no scientific validity; the Barnum effect — people's tendency to accept vague, general statements as personally meaningful — helps explain its continued popularity. Nonetheless, astrology remains a significant cultural and commercial phenomenon, with newspaper horoscopes, apps, and professional practitioners worldwide.
