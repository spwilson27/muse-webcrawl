Greek (Modern Greek: Ελληνικά, Elliniká) is an independent branch of the Indo-European language family, native to Greece, Cyprus and diaspora communities around the world. It has the longest documented history of any Indo-European language, spanning about 3,400 years of written records: from Mycenaean Greek in the Linear B script (c. 1450 BCE), through Classical and Koine Greek, to the modern language spoken by some 13.5 million people. Greek has profoundly influenced the vocabulary of other European languages, supplying thousands of words in science, medicine, philosophy and politics.

History

Ancient Greek

The earliest Greek was spoken in the southern Balkans by the early second millennium BCE. Mycenaean Greek, the language of the Mycenaean palaces, is recorded in Linear B tablets deciphered by Michael Ventris in 1952. After the Bronze Age collapse, writing was lost and then reintroduced with the adaptation of the Phoenician alphabet, to which the Greeks added letters for vowels — the first true alphabet. Archaic and Classical Greek existed in dialects (Ionic, Attic, Doric, Aeolic, Arcadocypriot), each associated with regions and literary genres; Attic Greek of Athens became the basis of the standard language.

Koine and Medieval Greek

The conquests of Alexander the Great spread a common form, Koine Greek ("common"), across the eastern Mediterranean; it is the language of the Septuagint and the New Testament and the administrative lingua franca of the Roman East. Through the Byzantine Empire, Greek developed as Medieval (Byzantine) Greek, with scholars deliberately writing in an archaising learned style while the spoken language evolved.

Modern Greek

Modern Greek descends from the spoken medieval language. After independence in the nineteenth century, the state adopted Katharevousa, a purified, archaising register, while the vernacular Demotic became the language of literature. The resulting "language question" was settled in 1976, when Demotic (in a standardised form absorbing Katharevousa elements) became the official language of Greece.

Features

Greek is written in the 24-letter Greek alphabet. Ancient Greek had a pitch accent, three genders, five cases for nouns, a dual number, and a rich system of verbal aspect and mood. Modern Greek has a stress accent, simplified inflection (three cases in ordinary use, loss of the infinitive and dative), and a five-vowel system, but retains much of the ancient core vocabulary, making it strikingly continuous with its classical ancestor by the standards of most languages.

Influence

Greek words entered Latin in antiquity and from there the languages of Europe; since the Renaissance, Greek roots have been the standard raw material for new scientific and technical terms (biology, telephone, democracy, physics). English alone contains thousands of Greek-derived words.
