Guardianship

Guardianship is a legal relationship in which a person or institution (the guardian) is appointed by a court or by a parent's will to make decisions for another person (the ward) who is unable to manage their own affairs, most commonly a minor child or an incapacitated adult.

Guardianship of minors

Where a child has no parent able to care for them, a court may appoint a guardian to provide for the child's upbringing, education, and welfare, and to manage any property the child owns. Parents commonly nominate guardians in their wills. Guardianship of a minor usually ends when the child reaches the age of majority.

Guardianship of adults

For adults who lack capacity — for example because of dementia, intellectual disability, or mental illness — a court may appoint a guardian (in some jurisdictions called a conservator) to make personal, medical, or financial decisions. Because guardianship removes significant rights, modern law generally favours the least restrictive alternative, such as powers of attorney or supported decision-making.

Duties and oversight

Guardians owe fiduciary duties to their wards and are typically required to report periodically to the appointing court, to act in the ward's best interests, and to keep the ward's assets separate from their own.
