Betty Boop is an animated cartoon character created by Max Fleischer, with help from animators including Grim Natwick. She originally appeared in the Talkartoon and Betty Boop film series produced by Fleischer Studios and released by Paramount Pictures. With her distinctive voice, spit-curl hairstyle and flapper style, she became one of the most famous cartoon characters of the 1930s and an enduring icon of the Jazz Age.

History

Betty first appeared in Dizzy Dishes (1930) as a caricature of a French poodle, and was gradually transformed into a fully human character by 1932. Modelled in part on singer Helen Kane (whose "boop-boop-a-doop" style she imitated, leading to a famous lawsuit Kane lost), Betty starred in dozens of cartoons, notable for surreal humour and musical performances by artists such as Cab Calloway in Minnie the Moocher (1932) and Snow-White (1933).

Enforcement of the Hays Code from 1934 toned down the character's suggestive image, and her series ended in 1939. Betty Boop has since remained popular through merchandising, reissues, and appearances in later productions.
