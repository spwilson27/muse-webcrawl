Survival suit

A survival suit, more formally an immersion suit or anti-exposure suit, is a special type of waterproof dry suit intended to protect a wearer from hypothermia if immersed in cold water after abandoning a sinking or capsized vessel. Survival suits are standard safety equipment on ships, offshore platforms and some aircraft operating over cold water.

Design

Suits are usually made of bright orange or red neoprene or similar insulating material, with attached gloves, boots and a hood, reflective tape, a whistle and sometimes an inflatable pillow to keep the wearer's head supported. They are designed to be donned quickly — SOLAS regulations expect a person to put one on within two minutes — and to keep the wearer afloat face-up.

Regulations

Under the International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS), cargo ships must carry an immersion suit for every person on board on vessels operating in cold regions, and passenger ships carry them for passengers and crew at remote muster stations. Insulated suits are required to prevent a drop of more than 2 °C in core body temperature after six hours in water of 0–2 °C.
