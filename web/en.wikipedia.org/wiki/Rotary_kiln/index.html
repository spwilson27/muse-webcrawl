Rotary kiln

Rotary kiln
Pyroprocessing device for raising materials to high temperatures
TypeIndustrial kiln / pyroprocessing equipment
Typical length50–230 m
Typical diameter3–6 m
UsesCement clinker production, calcination of limestone, alumina, iron ore reduction, waste incineration

A rotary kiln is a pyroprocessing device used to raise materials to a high temperature (calcination) in a continuous process. It consists of a large, slightly inclined cylindrical steel vessel lined with refractory material, which rotates slowly about its longitudinal axis. Material fed into the upper end gradually moves down toward the lower end as the kiln rotates, undergoing drying, heating and chemical reactions along the way, while hot gases from a burner at the lower end flow in the opposite direction.

History

The rotary kiln was invented in 1873 by Frederick Ransome in England, and was developed commercially for cement manufacture in the late 19th century after improvements by Thomas Crampton and others. Its adoption revolutionised the cement industry, replacing batch-process shaft kilns with a continuous process capable of far greater output.

Construction

The kiln shell is made of rolled steel plate, typically supported on two or more stations of support rollers (trunnions) and driven by a girth gear and motor at a speed of roughly 1–4 revolutions per minute. The inclination is generally 3–4% from horizontal. Internally the shell is protected by refractory brick or castable lining chosen to withstand temperatures that can exceed 1,450 °C in the burning zone.

Applications

The best-known application is the production of Portland cement clinker, in which a rotary kiln is the heart of the cement plant. Other uses include calcining limestone and dolomite, producing alumina from bauxite, roasting and reducing metal ores, calcining petroleum coke, and the incineration of hazardous waste. Related equipment includes rotary dryers and coolers, which use the same basic geometry at lower temperatures. See also kiln and calcination.
