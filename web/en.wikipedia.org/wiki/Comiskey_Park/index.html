Comiskey Park, home of the White Sox for 80 years

Comiskey Park was a baseball park in Chicago, Illinois, located in the Armour Square neighborhood on the near-south side of the city. It was the home of the Chicago White Sox of Major League Baseball from 1910 to 1990. Built by White Sox owner Charles Comiskey and designed by architect Zachary Taylor Davis, it opened on July 1, 1910, and was at the time regarded as one of the finest steel-and-concrete ballparks in the United States.

History

The park hosted the 1917 World Series, won by the White Sox, and was the home field during the 1919 "Black Sox" scandal. It hosted the very first Major League Baseball All-Star Game in 1933, the brainchild of Chicago Tribune sports editor Arch Ward, and again in 1950 and 1983. Under later owner Bill Veeck it gained an "exploding scoreboard" in 1960, which showered fireworks after White Sox home runs — a device widely copied since. The park also staged boxing matches, including Joe Louis's 1937 heavyweight title win over James J. Braddock, football games, and in 1979 the infamous Disco Demolition Night promotion.

Closure

By the 1980s the ageing park was the oldest in the majors. The White Sox moved across 35th Street to the new Comiskey Park (now Rate Field) in 1991, and the original was demolished that year; its site is now a parking lot, with home plate marked in the pavement. At closure it had been the oldest ballpark in Major League Baseball.
