Lagash


The Stele of the Vultures from Lagash

Lagash (Sumerian: Lagaš) was an ancient city-state of Sumer in southern Mesopotamia, located at modern Tell al-Hiba in Dhi Qar Governorate, Iraq. The state of Lagash encompassed the cities of Lagash itself, Girsu (its religious centre) and Nina. It flourished in the third millennium BC and is one of the best-documented early states thanks to the tens of thousands of cuneiform tablets recovered from its archives.

History

Lagash rose to prominence in the Early Dynastic period (c. 2550–2400 BC). Its ruler Eannatum conquered much of Sumer and commemorated his victory over the rival city of Umma on the famous Stele of the Vultures, one of the earliest historical monuments. A generation of reforming rulers culminated in Urukagina, whose inscriptions are among the oldest known law and reform texts. After a period of Akkadian domination, Lagash enjoyed a second golden age under Gudea (c. 2144–2124 BC), a pious ruler remembered for his temple building and the many statues of him found at Girsu.

Archaeology

French excavators under Ernest de Sarzec began digging at Girsu (Tello) in 1877, recovering the Gudea statues and vast archives. Work at Tell al-Hiba itself, begun by American teams in the late 1960s, has resumed in recent years, revealing temples, administrative buildings and evidence of craft production in the ancient city.
