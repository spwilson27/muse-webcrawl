Philokalia
Editors: Nicodemus the Hagiorite; Macarius of Corinth
First published: 1782, Venice (Greek)
Subject: Eastern Orthodox spirituality, hesychasm
Contents: Texts by some 36 authors, 4th–15th centuries

The Philokalia (Greek: Φιλοκαλία, "love of the beautiful, the good") is a collection of texts written between the 4th and 15th centuries by spiritual masters of the Eastern Orthodox Church's hesychast tradition. Compiled by St Nicodemus the Hagiorite of Mount Athos and St Macarius of Corinth, it was first published in Venice in 1782.

Contents and themes

The collection gathers writings by authors including Anthony the Great, Evagrius Ponticus, John Cassian, Maximus the Confessor, John of Damascus, Symeon the New Theologian and Gregory Palamas. Its recurring themes are prayer — especially the Jesus Prayer and "prayer of the heart" — watchfulness (nepsis), stillness (hesychia), repentance, and the purification, illumination and deification (theosis) of the human person through the uncreated energies of God. The full Greek title describes it as the Philokalia "of the holy neptic Fathers", through which the intellect is purified, illumined and perfected.

Influence

A Church Slavonic translation by Paisius Velichkovsky, the Dobrotolublye (1793), and a Russian version by Theophan the Recluse spread the Philokalia's influence across the Slavic Orthodox world, fuelling the 19th-century Russian spiritual revival depicted in the anonymous classic The Way of a Pilgrim. A complete English translation, prepared by G. E. H. Palmer, Philip Sherrard and Kallistos Ware, appeared in several volumes from 1979, making the Philokalia a key source for contemporary interest in Orthodox contemplative prayer in the West.
