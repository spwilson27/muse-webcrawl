Lithium

Lithium is a chemical element (symbol Li, atomic number 3) and, as lithium salts, one of psychiatry's oldest and most effective medicines — the classic mood stabiliser for Bipolar disorder, in use since 1949.

Medical use

John Cade's 1949 discovery in Melbourne launched modern psychopharmacology. Lithium carbonate reduces mania and, uniquely among mood drugs, lowers suicide risk in bipolar disorder; it is also used to augment antidepressants in Depression. Its exact mechanism is still debated — effects on inositol signalling and neuroprotection are leading candidates.

Monitoring

Lithium has a narrow therapeutic window: blood levels are monitored (roughly 0.6–1.0 mmol/L for maintenance), since excess causes tremor, confusion and toxicity. Long-term use can impair the Thyroid and kidneys, so both are checked regularly. The element itself also powers lithium-ion batteries — a separate story of energy storage.
