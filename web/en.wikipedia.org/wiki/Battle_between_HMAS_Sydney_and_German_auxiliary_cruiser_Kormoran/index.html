Battle between HMAS Sydney and German auxiliary cruiser Kormoran

The battle between HMAS Sydney and German auxiliary cruiser Kormoran was a mutually destructive naval engagement fought on 19 November 1941 off the coast of Western Australia, during the Second World War. The Australian light cruiser HMAS Sydney, returning from escort duties, intercepted the disguised German commerce raider Kormoran. In the ensuing close-range battle both ships were fatally damaged: Kormoran was scuttled by her crew, while Sydney sank with all 645 hands — the largest loss of life in the history of the Royal Australian Navy.

The engagement of 19 November 1941.

The battle

Kormoran, commanded by Kapitän zur See Theodor Detmers, was disguised as the Dutch merchantman Straat Malakka. When Sydney, under Captain Joseph Burnett, closed to within about 1,000 metres to investigate, Detmers ordered the German ensign raised and opened fire with guns and torpedoes. The raider's opening salvos wrecked Sydney's bridge and fire control; the cruiser replied, fatally damaging Kormoran's engine room, but was hit by a torpedo and drifted away burning, sinking during the night.

Aftermath

Most of Kormoran's crew — 317 of 399 — survived and were captured in Australia. No trace of Sydney was found at the time, and the loss generated decades of speculation and controversy. Both wrecks were located in March 2008 by the Finding Sydney Foundation, lying about 22 kilometres apart in roughly 2,500 metres of water. A 2009 commission of inquiry concluded that Burnett had placed his ship dangerously close to an unknown vessel, but that the German account of the battle was essentially truthful.
