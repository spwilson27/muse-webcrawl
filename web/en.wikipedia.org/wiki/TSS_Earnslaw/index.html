TSS Earnslaw

TSS Earnslaw is a 1912 Edwardian twin-screw steamer based at Queenstown, New Zealand. She is one of the oldest tourist attractions in Otago and the only remaining commercial passenger-carrying coal-fired steamship in the Southern Hemisphere, plying Lake Wakatipu on cruises to Walter Peak Station.

History

The vessel was named after Mount Earnslaw, which overlooks the head of the lake. Her steel hull components were fabricated in Dunedin, shipped inland by rail to Kingston at the southern end of the lake, and assembled there; she was launched on 24 February 1912 and made her maiden voyage later that year. Built for the New Zealand Railways Department, she carried passengers, sheep, cattle and freight between the lakeside settlements in the decades when the lake was the region's main highway.

Present day

Now operated by tourism company RealNZ, the Earnslaw — nicknamed "The Lady of the Lake" — carries sightseers from Queenstown to the Walter Peak High Country Farm. Passengers can watch her original triple-expansion steam engines working from a viewing platform around the engine room. She celebrated her centenary in 2012 and remains in annual survey, with periodic refits.
