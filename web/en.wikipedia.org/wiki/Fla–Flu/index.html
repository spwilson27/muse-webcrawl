Fla–Flu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fla–Flu is the name of the football derby between two of Rio de Janeiro's biggest clubs, Flamengo (Fla) and Fluminense (Flu). One of Brazil's most famous rivalries, it regularly fills the Maracanã Stadium and is noted for its enormous crowds, color, and pageantry. The name was coined by the journalist Mário Filho, after whom the Maracanã is officially named.

Origins

Flamengo's football department was founded in 1911 by dissident players from Fluminense, after a dispute within Fluminense's squad; the defection gave the derby an intimate, fraternal edge from the start. The first match between the clubs was played on 7 July 1912, with Fluminense winning 3–2. The rivalry grew with Rio football itself, acquiring a class dimension in the popular imagination — Flamengo identified with the masses and Fluminense with the elite — though both clubs draw support across society.

Records and famous matches

The derby holds the world record attendance for a club match: on 15 December 1963, an official crowd of 194,603 watched a goalless draw at the Maracanã in the Carioca championship decider. The Fla–Flu of 1995 is famous for Renato Gaúcho's "belly goal," which won the state title for Fluminense on the centenary of Flamengo's founding. A Brazilian saying, popularized by playwright Nelson Rodrigues — himself a Fluminense supporter, whose brother Mário Filho chronicled the fixture — holds that the Fla–Flu began "40 minutes before nothing," capturing its mythic status in Rio culture.
