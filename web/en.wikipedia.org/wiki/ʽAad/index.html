ʽAad

For the capitalised epigraphic form, see ʽAAd.

ʽAad (Arabic: عاد, ʿĀd) is the name given in the Qur'an and in Arabic tradition to an ancient Arab tribe or people of southern Arabia, associated with the prophet Hūd. The ʽAad are counted among the "extinct Arabs" (al-ʿArab al-bāʾida) and are remembered as a powerful, wealthy people who were destroyed by a violent wind after rejecting the message of their prophet.

In the Qur'an

The story of the ʽAad is told in several surahs, including al-Aʿrāf (7), Hūd (11), al-Shuʿarāʾ (26), Fuṣṣilat (41), al-Aḥqāf (46), al-Dhāriyāt (51), al-Qamar (54) and al-Ḥāqqah (69). They are described as successors of the people of Noah, builders of lofty monuments and dwellers in al-Aḥqāf, "the winding sand tracts", who boasted "Who is mightier than us in strength?" After they dismissed Hūd as a liar, God sent against them a furious, bitterly cold wind that blew for seven nights and eight days, leaving them lying prostrate "as if they were hollow trunks of palm trees".

Tradition and identification

Later Muslim tradition connected the ʽAad with the legendary city of Iram of the Pillars (Iram dhāt al-ʿimād), mentioned in Sūrat al-Fajr (89). Their homeland is generally placed in the southern Arabian desert between Yemen and Oman, in the region of the Rubʿ al-Khālī. Some scholars have proposed links with the South Arabian kingdoms known from inscriptions, and with sites such as Shisr in Oman, excavated in the 1990s and publicised as a candidate for Iram, though such identifications remain speculative and unproven.
