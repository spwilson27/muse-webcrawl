General Electric

The General Electric Company (GE) was an American multinational conglomerate founded in 1892, incorporated in New York and headquartered in Boston. For over a century it was among the largest and most diversified corporations in the world, with businesses spanning aircraft engines, power generation, oil and gas, healthcare imaging, locomotives, lighting, appliances, broadcasting (NBC) and finance (GE Capital). An original component of the Dow Jones Industrial Average in 1896, it was the last of the original twelve to be removed, in 2018. In 2021 GE announced it would split into three public companies; the breakup was completed on 2 April 2024, with the GE brand carried on by GE Aerospace.

History

GE was formed by the merger of Thomas Edison's Edison General Electric Company with the Thomson-Houston Electric Company, arranged by financier J. P. Morgan. Charles A. Coffin, its first president, built the company in Schenectady, New York. GE's research laboratory (founded 1900) produced inventions from ductile tungsten lamp filaments to the first American jet engine (1942). The company built the first U.S. television broadcasts, created the NBC radio and television networks, and under chief executives such as Ralph Cordiner, Reginald Jones and Jack Welch (1981–2001) grew into a management icon; Welch's tenure raised market capitalisation roughly thirtyfold but also expanded GE Capital into one of America's largest financial institutions.

Decline and breakup

GE Capital's losses in the 2008 financial crisis exposed the conglomerate's risks. Under Jeff Immelt and his successors the company shed NBCUniversal, its appliances and lighting businesses, and most of GE Capital, while a disastrous power-business acquisition and a 2018 dividend cut erased most of its value. In November 2021 CEO H. Lawrence Culp Jr. announced a three-way split: GE HealthCare was spun off in January 2023, GE Vernova (energy) in April 2024, and the remaining aviation business renamed GE Aerospace, retaining the GE ticker and monogram.

Successor companies

GE Aerospace is the world's largest maker of jet engines, including the CFM56 and LEAP families produced with Safran. GE HealthCare is a leader in medical imaging and diagnostics. GE Vernova combines gas and steam power, wind turbines and grid equipment. GE-branded appliances and lighting are produced under licence by Haier and Savant respectively.
