Hampshire is a ceremonial county in South East England, on the coast of the English Channel. Its county town is the cathedral city of Winchester, historically the capital of Wessex and of Anglo-Saxon England. The largest settlements are the port cities of Southampton and Portsmouth, both of which are administratively separate unitary authorities within the ceremonial county.

Geography

The county is known for its chalk downland, including part of the South Downs National Park, and for the New Forest National Park, a former royal hunting forest created by William the Conqueror. The Isle of Wight lies just off its coast across the Solent. Hampshire has a strong maritime heritage: Portsmouth is home to the Royal Navy's historic dockyard and HMS Victory, while Southampton is a major commercial port and cruise terminal.

History and culture

Hampshire was one of the earliest Saxon kingdoms' territories and features in Domesday Book. The novelist Jane Austen was born in Steventon and is buried in Winchester Cathedral, and the naturalist Gilbert White wrote about the parish of Selborne. Hampshire County Cricket Club plays at the Utilita Bowl near Southampton.
