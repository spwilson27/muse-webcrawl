The First Dáil was Dáil Éireann as it convened between 1919 and 1921. It was established by members of Sinn Féin elected in the 1918 United Kingdom general election who, in accordance with their manifesto, refused to take their seats at Westminster and instead formed a unicameral revolutionary parliament for the proclaimed Irish Republic in Dublin.

First meeting

The Dáil first met publicly on 21 January 1919 in the Round Room of the Mansion House, Dublin. Only 27 of the 73 Sinn Féin MPs were present, many of the rest being imprisoned. It adopted a Declaration of Independence, ratifying the Proclamation of the Republic of 1916, a Democratic Programme, and a provisional constitution, and elected a ministry (Aireacht) headed by Cathal Brugha and then Éamon de Valera as Príomh Aire (later President of the Republic).

Suppression and war

The British authorities declared the Dáil illegal in September 1919, and it met thereafter in secret during the Irish War of Independence, while operating parallel courts, a loan, and local government. It was succeeded by the Second Dáil after the 1921 elections.
