Architect

An architect is a person who plans, designs, and oversees the construction of buildings. To practice architecture means to provide services in connection with the design of buildings and the space within the site surrounding the buildings, with the principal purpose of human occupancy or use. Etymologically, the term derives from the Latin architectus, from the Greek ἀρχιτέκτων (arkhitekton, "chief builder").

The profession

Architects work on projects ranging from individual houses to large commercial, civic, and infrastructural complexes. Their decisions affect public safety and welfare, and in most jurisdictions the title "architect" is legally protected: practitioners must complete an accredited degree, a period of supervised internship, and pass a licensing registration examination, such as the Architect Registration Examination in the United States or the professional examinations overseen by the Royal Institute of British Architects (RIBA) and the Architects Registration Board in the United Kingdom.

Work stages

In a typical engagement the architect develops schematic designs from the client's brief, refines them into design-development and construction documents, assists with permits and contractor selection, and administers construction, inspecting work for conformity with the design. Computer-aided design (CAD) and building information modelling (BIM) have largely replaced hand drafting, and architects increasingly coordinate with structural, mechanical, and civil engineers, landscape architects, and interior designers.

History

Master builders directed construction from antiquity — Imhotep in Egypt, Ictinus at the Parthenon — but architecture emerged as a distinct liberal profession in the Italian Renaissance, when figures such as Brunelleschi and Alberti asserted the architect as an intellectual designer separate from the craftsman. The industrial era brought formal professional institutions: the Royal Institute of British Architects was founded in 1834 and the American Institute of Architects in 1857.
