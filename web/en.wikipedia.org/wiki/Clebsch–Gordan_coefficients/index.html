Clebsch–Gordan coefficients

In physics, the Clebsch–Gordan (CG) coefficients are numbers that arise in angular momentum coupling in quantum mechanics. They appear as the expansion coefficients of total angular momentum eigenstates in an uncoupled tensor product basis. In more mathematical terms, the CG coefficients are used in representation theory, particularly of compact Lie groups, to perform the explicit direct sum decomposition of the tensor product of two irreducible representations (known as the Clebsch–Gordan decomposition) into irreducible representations, in cases where the numbers and types of irreducible components are already known abstractly. The name derives from the German mathematicians Alfred Clebsch (1833–1872) and Paul Gordan (1837–1912), who encountered the equivalent problem in invariant theory.

Angular momentum coupling

When two systems with angular momentum quantum numbers j1 and j2 are combined, the total angular momentum j can range from |j1 − j2| to j1 + j2 in integer steps. The coupled eigenstate |j, m⟩ is a weighted sum of product states |j1, m1⟩|j2, m2⟩ with m1 + m2 = m, and the weights in that sum are the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients ⟨j1 m1 j2 m2 | j m⟩.

Properties

The coefficients are real in the standard (Condon–Shortley) phase convention. They vanish unless m1 + m2 = m and the triangle inequality |j1 − j2| ≤ j ≤ j1 + j2 is satisfied. They obey orthogonality relations expressing the unitarity of the change of basis, and symmetry relations under exchange of the two angular momenta and under sign reversal of the magnetic quantum numbers.

Relation to Wigner 3-j symbols

Clebsch–Gordan coefficients are closely related to Wigner 3-j symbols, differing by a phase and normalisation factor; the 3-j symbols possess greater symmetry and are often preferred in tabulations and in the theory of recoupling coefficients such as the 6-j and 9-j symbols.

Explicit formulas, given by Giulio Racah, express the coefficients as finite sums involving factorials of the angular momentum quantum numbers.
