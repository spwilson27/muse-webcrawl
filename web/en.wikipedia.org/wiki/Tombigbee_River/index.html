Tombigbee River

The Tombigbee River is a tributary of the Mobile River, approximately 200 miles (320 km) long, in the U.S. states of Mississippi and Alabama. Together with the Alabama River, it forms the short Mobile River before flowing into Mobile Bay.

The river rises in northeastern Mississippi and flows generally southward, passing through Columbus, Mississippi, and Demopolis, Alabama. Its name is thought to derive from Choctaw words meaning "box maker" or "coffin maker".

History and use

The Tombigbee was an important transportation route for Native peoples and later for cotton planters, who shipped bales downstream to Mobile. In the 20th century the Tennessee–Tombigbee Waterway connected the upper Tombigbee to the Tennessee River, creating a navigable link between the Gulf of Mexico and the Tennessee Valley. The river is controlled by a series of locks and dams and remains in use for commercial barge traffic, while its reservoirs and bottomland forests support fishing, hunting, and wildlife habitat.
