Data analysis

Data analysis is the process of inspecting, cleansing, transforming and modelling data with the goal of discovering useful information, informing conclusions and supporting decision-making. It is used in business, science, government and many other fields, and draws on techniques from statistics, computer science and domain expertise.

Process

A typical data analysis proceeds through several phases: specifying requirements and collecting data; cleaning and preprocessing the data to handle errors, duplicates and missing values; exploratory analysis to summarise characteristics and detect patterns, often with visualisations; modelling, in which statistical or machine-learning methods are fitted; and communicating results through reports, dashboards or publications. Analyst John Tukey's work in the 1970s established exploratory data analysis as a discipline distinct from confirmatory statistical testing.

Types

Analyses are commonly categorised as descriptive (what happened), diagnostic (why it happened), predictive (what is likely to happen) and prescriptive (what action to take). Quantitative analysis works with numerical measurements, while qualitative analysis interprets text, interviews and other non-numerical material.

Tools

Analysts use programming languages such as Python and R, spreadsheet software, SQL databases, and business-intelligence platforms. The growth of big data and data science has expanded the field to very large and unstructured datasets.
