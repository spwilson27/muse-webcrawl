The 93rd (Sutherland Highlanders) Regiment of Foot was a Line Infantry Regiment of the British Army, raised in 1799 by Major-General William Wemyss on behalf of the Countess of Sutherland from the Sutherland Fencibles. It served until 1881, when it amalgamated with the 91st Regiment to form the Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders.

Service

The regiment served in the Napoleonic Wars, including the capture of the Cape of Good Hope and the disastrous assault on New Orleans in 1815. It won lasting fame in the Crimean War at the Battle of Balaclava on 25 October 1854, where, standing in a line two deep rather than forming square, it repelled a Russian cavalry charge — the action immortalized by war correspondent William Howard Russell as "that thin red streak tipped with a line of steel," giving rise to the phrase "the thin red line."

Indian Mutiny and amalgamation

The 93rd fought in the relief of Lucknow during the Indian Mutiny of 1857, earning multiple Victoria Crosses. Under the Childers Reforms, it became the 2nd Battalion of Princess Louise's (Sutherland and Argyll Highlanders) in 1881.
