Charles V (24 February 1500 – 21 September 1558) was Holy Roman Emperor and Archduke of Austria from 1519 to 1556, King of Spain from 1516 to 1556, and Lord of the Netherlands from 1506 to 1555. Through the Habsburg inheritance he ruled an empire spanning Europe and the Americas, often described as "the empire on which the sun never sets".

Reign

Charles inherited Spain and its American colonies from his maternal grandparents Ferdinand and Isabella, the Netherlands and Franche-Comté from his father Philip the Handsome, and the Austrian lands from his grandfather Maximilian I. His reign was dominated by wars with France under Francis I, conflict with the Ottoman Empire of Suleiman the Magnificent, and the challenge of the Protestant Reformation: he presided over the Diet of Worms (1521) that outlawed Martin Luther, yet was ultimately unable to prevent the religious division of Germany, settled provisionally by the Peace of Augsburg (1555).

Abdication

Exhausted, Charles abdicated in stages in 1555–56, dividing his realms between his son Philip II (Spain, the Netherlands, and Italy) and his brother Ferdinand I (the Empire and Austria). He retired to the monastery of Yuste in Spain, where he died in 1558.
