Amber Room

The Amber Room was a chamber decorated in amber panels backed with gold leaf and mirrors, located in the Catherine Palace of Tsarskoye Selo near Saint Petersburg. Constructed in the 18th century in Prussia, it was dismantled and eventually disappeared during the Second World War. It was once considered the "Eighth Wonder of the World". A reconstruction, built from 1979 and completed in 2003, stands in the Catherine Palace today.

History

The room was begun in 1701 for Charlottenburg Palace in Berlin, designed by Andreas Schlüter and crafted by Danish amber worker Gottfried Wolfram. In 1716 the Prussian king Frederick William I presented the panels to Tsar Peter the Great of Russia, cementing an alliance. In Russia the room was installed in the Winter Palace and later the Catherine Palace, where it was expanded under Empress Elizabeth by Bartolomeo Rastrelli to cover more than 55 square metres, using some six tonnes of amber.

Disappearance

During the Nazi invasion of 1941, German forces looted the room and reinstalled it in Königsberg Castle. It vanished amid the destruction of Königsberg in 1945; theories range from destruction by fire to concealment in mines or a sunken ship. A few original fragments — a mosaic and a commode — surfaced in Germany in the 1990s and were returned to Russia.

Reconstruction

Using black-and-white photographs, Russian craftsmen rebuilt the room from 1979 with funding that later included a German sponsor; the reconstructed Amber Room was inaugurated in 2003 for the tricentenary of Saint Petersburg.
