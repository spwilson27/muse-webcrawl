USA Gymnastics sex abuse scandal

The USA Gymnastics sex abuse scandal concerns the sexual abuse of hundreds of gymnasts, primarily minors, by former USA Gymnastics national team doctor Larry Nassar, and the institutional failures that allowed his abuse to continue for decades. It is considered one of the largest sexual abuse scandals in the history of sport. More than 500 survivors — including Olympic champions Simone Biles, Aly Raisman, McKayla Maroney and Gabby Douglas — reported abuse by Nassar.

Exposure and prosecutions

The scandal broke publicly in September 2016 when the Indianapolis Star published an investigation into USA Gymnastics' handling of abuse complaints, and former gymnast Rachael Denhollander publicly accused Nassar. Nassar, who had worked at Michigan State University and at the Karolyi Ranch training centre in Texas, pleaded guilty to federal child pornography charges (receiving 60 years in 2017) and to multiple counts of criminal sexual conduct in Michigan, receiving sentences of 40 to 175 years in January 2018 after a widely covered sentencing hearing at which over 150 survivors gave statements. Former Michigan State president Lou Anna Simon and other officials faced charges or resigned.

Institutional fallout

Investigations found that USA Gymnastics, Michigan State and the U.S. Olympic Committee had received complaints about Nassar years before his arrest and failed to act; the FBI's handling of 2015 allegations was later severely criticised by the Justice Department's inspector general. USA Gymnastics' entire board resigned in 2018, and the organisation filed for bankruptcy that December. In 2021 a $380 million settlement was approved for survivors, and in 2024 the U.S. Department of Justice agreed to pay nearly $139 million to survivors over the FBI's failures. The scandal prompted the Empowering Olympic, Paralympic, and Amateur Athletes Act of 2020, strengthening Congressional oversight and the U.S. Center for SafeSport.
