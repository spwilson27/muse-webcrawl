The Fast and the Furious (2001 film)

The Fast and the Furious is a 2001 American action film directed by Rob Cohen and written by Gary Scott Thompson, Erik Bergquist, and David Ayer. It is the first instalment in the Fast & Furious franchise and stars Paul Walker as Brian O'Conner, an undercover police officer sent to infiltrate a Los Angeles street-racing crew led by Dominic Toretto, played by Vin Diesel.

Plot

A crew of highway robbers using black Honda Civics has been hijacking semi-trailer trucks carrying expensive electronics. LAPD officer Brian O'Conner goes undercover in the street-racing scene and earns the trust of Dominic Toretto, charismatic leader of a racing team suspected of the robberies. As O'Conner falls for Toretto's sister Mia and becomes torn in his loyalties, the crew's final heist goes wrong; in the closing scene, after a climactic quarter-mile race over a railroad crossing, O'Conner lets Toretto escape in his own car.

Production

The film was inspired by "Racer X", a 1998 Vibe magazine article by Ken Li about street racing in New York City, and licensed its title from a 1954 Roger Corman film. Filming took place in Los Angeles and Southern California in 2000. Ayer was brought in to rewrite the script, shifting the setting and fleshing out the characters.

Reception and legacy

The film received mixed reviews but was a surprise box-office hit, and it is credited with launching the careers of Walker and Diesel. It spawned one of the highest-grossing film franchises of all time, with numerous sequels following an increasingly international action format. Its success also spurred a wave of street-racing culture in popular media.
