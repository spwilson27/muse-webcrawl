Saint Mary Parish, Jamaica

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Saint Mary Parish, Jamaica]
The parish coast near Port Maria

Saint Mary is a parish located in the northeast section of Jamaica. With a population of roughly 113,000, it lies in the historic county of Middlesex and covers about 610 square kilometres (235 sq mi). Its capital is Port Maria, on the coast, and other significant towns include Annotto Bay, Highgate, and Oracabessa. Saint Mary is largely agricultural, known for bananas, sugar, pimento, and cocoa, and is one of the smaller and less developed but most scenic parishes of Jamaica.

History

The area was inhabited by Taíno peoples before Columbus sighted the coast in 1494; under Spanish rule the harbour at present-day Port Maria was one of the island's first settlements. After the English conquest of Jamaica in 1655 the parish was organised under the name Saint Mary. In the era of slavery it saw repeated resistance, including Tacky's War of 1760, which began in Saint Mary and spread across the island. The parish later produced figures such as the planter and writer, and was home to estates like Brimmer Hall and the former Ian Fleming estate, Goldeneye, in Oracabessa, where the James Bond novels were written.

Geography

Saint Mary is bordered by Portland to the east, St. Catherine to the southwest, St. Andrew and St. Ann to the west. The interior rises toward the Grand Ridge of the Blue Mountain range in the south, and rivers such as the Wag Water cut through fertile valleys. The coastal strip features beaches, cliffs, and Firefly, the former hilltop home of playwright Noël Coward, now a museum.

Notable people

Notable residents and natives include Noël Coward; Ian Fleming at Goldeneye; musician Capleton; sprinter and three-time Olympian Aleen Bailey; and Lady Saw, the dancehall artist. The parish is also associated with the estate of Chris Blackwell's family and the development of Jamaica's north-coast tourism.
