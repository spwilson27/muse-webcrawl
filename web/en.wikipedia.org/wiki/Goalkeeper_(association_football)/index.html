Goalkeeper (association football)

The goalkeeper (sometimes written as goal-keeper, abbreviated as GK, keeper, goalie, or colloquially as nets) is a position in association football. It is the most specialised position in the sport. The goalkeeper's main role is to stop the opposing team from scoring (putting the ball over the goal line of the goal) by directly defending the goal. This is accomplished by the goalkeeper moving into the trajectory of the ball to either catch it or direct it further from the vicinity of the goal line. Within the penalty area, goalkeepers are allowed to use their hands. Outside it, they are subject to the same rules as other players.

The goalkeeper is indicated by the wearing of a different coloured kit from that of their teammates and the opposing number-one player; this is a mandatory requirement in the Laws of the Game. Goalkeepers also often wear gloves to aid grip and protect their hands.

History

The goalkeeper position has evolved considerably since the game was first codified. Early football formations featured no dedicated goalkeeper; the role emerged in the 19th century, and the Laws of the Game were progressively tightened to restrict the goalkeeper's handling of the ball. The introduction of the back-pass rule in 1992 — which prevents a goalkeeper from handling a ball deliberately kicked to them by a teammate — profoundly changed the position, requiring modern keepers to be proficient with their feet.

Responsibilities

The goalkeeper's primary responsibility is to prevent the opposition from scoring. Beyond shot-stopping, the goalkeeper organises the defence at set pieces, commands the penalty area on crosses, and increasingly initiates attacks through distribution by hand or foot. The modern "sweeper-keeper" acts as an additional outfield player when the team is in possession, playing a high defensive line behind the defence.

Techniques

Goalkeeping technique includes positioning, footwork, handling, diving, dealing with crosses, one-on-one situations, and distribution. Reflexes, agility, anticipation and decision-making under pressure are key attributes, as is the psychological resilience to recover from errors that often lead directly to goals.
