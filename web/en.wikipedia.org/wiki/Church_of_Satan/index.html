The Church of Satan is a religious organisation founded in 1966 by Anton Szandor LaVey in San Francisco, dedicated to the philosophy of LaVeyan Satanism as codified in his 1969 book The Satanic Bible. The Church does not worship a literal Satan; its members are atheists who use Satan as a symbol of individualism, self-indulgence, rational self-interest and opposition to conventional morality.

Beliefs

LaVeyan Satanism is summarised in the Nine Satanic Statements, which celebrate indulgence, vital existence, undefiled wisdom, kindness only to those who deserve it, vengeance, personal responsibility and the view of man as an animal among animals. Magic, in the Church's usage, is a form of ritual psychodrama for focusing the will, not supernatural practice. Its ethical code — the Eleven Satanic Rules of the Earth — stresses consent and non-interference.

History

Launched on Walpurgisnacht 1966 at LaVey's "Black House" in San Francisco, the Church gained notoriety through publicity-conscious events, celebrity associations and media coverage. After LaVey's death in 1997, leadership passed to his partner Blanche Barton and then to Peter H. Gilmore as High Priest. It has no central temple, consisting of registered members and a priesthood organised in degrees; various schisms, notably the Temple of Set (1975), split from it. The Church is distinct from The Satanic Temple, a separate, activist organisation founded in 2013.
