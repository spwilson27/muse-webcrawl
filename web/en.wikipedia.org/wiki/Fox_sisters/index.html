Fox sisters

The Fox sisters — Leah (1813–1890), Margaret or Maggie (1833–1893) and Catherine or Kate (1837–1892) — were American siblings from Hydesville, New York, whose claimed ability to communicate with spirits through mysterious knocking or "rapping" sounds launched the religious movement of Spiritualism in the United States in 1848.

The Hydesville rappings

On 31 March 1848 the younger sisters reported that knocking sounds in their farmhouse answered questions by code, identifying a spirit they called "Mr Splitfoot." News spread quickly; the sisters gave public demonstrations in Rochester and then toured widely, holding séances for paying audiences and celebrities. Promoters and converts built the rappings into an international movement embraced by millions, despite investigations (including a Buffalo physicians' report in 1851) suggesting the sounds were made by cracking joints in the feet and knees.

Confession and aftermath

In 1888 Margaret publicly confessed that the rappings were a fraud, demonstrating on stage at the New York Academy of Music how she produced them by toe-cracking; she retracted the confession the following year, under financial and movement pressure. The sisters died in poverty, but Spiritualism long outlived them. Their cottage was later moved to the Spiritualist community of Lily Dale, New York.
