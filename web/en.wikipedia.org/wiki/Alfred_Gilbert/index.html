Sir Alfred Gilbert RA (12 August 1854 – 4 November 1934) was an English sculptor and goldsmith, a leading figure of the New Sculpture movement. He is best known for the Shaftesbury Memorial Fountain in Piccadilly Circus, London, crowned by the winged figure popularly known as Eros.

Early life and training

Gilbert was born in Berners Street, London, the son of a music teacher. He studied at the Heatherley School of Art and the Royal Academy Schools, and in Paris under Pierre Cavelier at the École des Beaux-Arts. He then worked in Rome in the studio of Sir Joseph Boehm's circle, absorbing the influence of Renaissance masters such as Donatello and Cellini.

Career

Gilbert's virtuoso bronze Perseus Arming (1882) announced a new naturalism and technical brilliance in British sculpture. His Icarus (1884) and the elaborate Clarence Memorial in St George's Chapel, Windsor, confirmed his reputation. In 1893 his Shaftesbury Memorial was unveiled: a fountain surmounted by an aluminium figure of Anteros, the god of requited love — one of the first statues cast in aluminium — which the public immediately renamed Eros.

Later years

Perfectionism and escalating costs dogged Gilbert's commissions. The Clarence Memorial dispute left him in debt; he withdrew his Royal Academy membership and emigrated to Bruges in 1901, where he lived for over two decades. Returning to England in 1926, he was reconciled with the establishment, completed long-delayed works including the Queen Alexandra Memorial, and was knighted in 1932. He died in London in 1934.
