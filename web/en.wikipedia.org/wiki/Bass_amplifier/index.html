Bass amplifier

A bass amp head and speaker cabinets

A bass amplifier, or bass amp, is a musical instrument amplifier designed to reproduce the low-frequency sound of a bass guitar or double bass. Because low frequencies require significantly more power and larger loudspeakers to reproduce at stage volume than the range of a regular guitar amplifier, bass amplifiers use heavier-duty power amplifiers and larger, more robust speaker enclosures.

Design

Compared with guitar amps, bass amplifiers emphasize a clean, high-headroom sound that preserves low-end punch without unwanted distortion, though many models include overdrive or fuzz circuits. Tone controls typically include multi-band equalization, and many modern amps add a compressor, a DI (direct injection) output for the mixing console, and an effects loop.

Types

Combo amplifiers combine the amplifier and one or more speakers in a single cabinet and suit rehearsal and small venues. Larger rigs separate the amplifier head from speaker cabinets, often in the classic configuration of cabinets with 10-inch speakers or a single 15-inch speaker. Classic designs such as the Ampeg SVT, introduced in 1969 with its 8×10 cabinet, remain benchmarks, while modern Class D amplifiers deliver hundreds of watts in a lightweight package.

Use

In live performance the bass amp functions mainly as the player's on-stage monitor, with the audible sound for the audience usually supplied by the venue's PA system via the DI output or a microphone placed before the cabinet.
