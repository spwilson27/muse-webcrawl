Northeast blackout of 2003

The Northeast blackout of 2003 was a widespread power outage that occurred on Thursday, 14 August 2003, beginning just after 4:10 p.m. EDT. It affected an estimated 55 million people — about 45 million in eight U.S. states and 10 million in Ontario, Canada — making it one of the largest blackouts in North American history. Most power was restored within hours in some areas, but parts of New York City and Toronto remained dark into the following day, and some areas lacked full service for days.

Causes

The joint U.S.–Canada investigation traced the blackout to a combination of failures by the Ohio utility FirstEnergy: inadequately trimmed trees contacted sagging high-voltage lines in Ohio, tripping them out, while a software bug in the utility's control-room alarm system left operators unaware of the developing emergency. The resulting overloads cascaded through the grid in a matter of minutes, tripping lines, generators and interconnections around the Great Lakes and along the East Coast.

Effects

Transport ground to a halt: subways and commuter trains stopped, hundreds of flights were cancelled, and traffic lights failed across the region. Water pressure dropped in several cities, cellular networks were congested, and thousands of people were evacuated from stalled trains and elevators. Despite initial fears, the night passed largely peacefully; in New York, comparisons were drawn with the orderly response rather than the looting of the 1977 blackout.

Aftermath

The blackout led to mandatory reliability standards for North American utilities under the U.S. Energy Policy Act of 2005, giving the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission authority to enforce grid reliability rules.
