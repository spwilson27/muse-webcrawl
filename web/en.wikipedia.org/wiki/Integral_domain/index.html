Integral domain

In mathematics, an integral domain is a nonzero commutative ring in which the product of any two nonzero elements is nonzero. In other words, an integral domain is a commutative ring with no zero divisors.

Examples include the integers, polynomial rings over a field, and the ring of Gaussian integers. Every field is an integral domain, and every finite integral domain is a field. An integral domain can be embedded in its field of fractions.

Properties

In an integral domain, the cancellation law holds for multiplication by nonzero elements. Subrings of integral domains are integral domains, and the characteristic of an integral domain is either zero or a prime number.
