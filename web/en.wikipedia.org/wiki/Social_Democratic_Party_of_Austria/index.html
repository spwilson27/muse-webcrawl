The Social Democratic Party of Austria (German: Sozialdemokratische Partei Österreichs, SPÖ) is a social democratic political party in Austria. Founded in 1889 as the Social Democratic Workers' Party (SDAPÖ), it is one of the country's oldest parties and, together with the Austrian People's Party (ÖVP), one of the two traditional major parties of the Austrian political system. The SPÖ has provided many of Austria's federal chancellors, including Bruno Kreisky, Fred Sinowatz, Franz Vranitzky, Viktor Klima, Alfred Gusenbauer and Christian Kern.

History

Monarchy and First Republic

Unified under Victor Adler at the Hainfeld congress of 1888–89, the party became the leading force of the Austrian labour movement and a major current of Austromarxism. After the collapse of the Habsburg monarchy in 1918, the party led the first republican governments under Karl Renner, introduced major social reforms, and built the celebrated municipal housing programme of "Red Vienna". In February 1934, after Chancellor Engelbert Dollfuß established an authoritarian regime, the party was banned following a brief civil war; it was driven underground again under Nazi rule after the Anschluss of 1938.

Second Republic

Refounded in 1945 as the Socialist Party of Austria (SPÖ), it governed in a "grand coalition" with the ÖVP for much of the postwar period under the Proporz system of power-sharing. From 1970 to 1983 Bruno Kreisky led single-party SPÖ governments that modernised Austria's welfare state and raised its international profile. The party renamed itself the Social Democratic Party of Austria in 1991. It returned to grand-coalition government in 2007–2017, then moved into opposition. In 2023 Andreas Babler became party leader after a contested membership ballot; following the 2024 National Council election, in which the SPÖ finished third, the party entered government in 2025 in a three-party coalition with the ÖVP and NEOS, with Babler as Vice-Chancellor.

Ideology and policies

The SPÖ advocates a strong welfare state, public services, workers' rights, progressive taxation and social housing, and is closely linked with the trade union federation (ÖGB) and the Chamber of Labour. Its traditional symbols include the colour red and the Three Arrows, emblem of resistance to Nazism, monarchism and communism adopted in the 1930s.
