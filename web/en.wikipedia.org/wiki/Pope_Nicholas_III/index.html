Pope Nicholas III

Pope Nicholas III (Latin: Nicolaus III; c. 1225 – 22 August 1280), born Giovanni Gaetano Orsini, was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 25 November 1277 to his death in 1280. He was a Roman nobleman who had served under eight popes and was made a cardinal by Pope Innocent IV in 1244.

Pontificate

Elected at Viterbo after a six-month conclave, Nicholas III worked to strengthen the Papal States in central Italy, negotiating with Rudolf I of Germany to secure papal claims in the Romagna. He sought to reduce Angevin influence in Italy, distancing the papacy from Charles I of Anjou, who had dominated his predecessors.

Church affairs

In 1279 he issued the bull Exiit qui seminat, an authoritative interpretation of the Franciscan rule concerning poverty, which became fundamental for the Franciscan Order. He also carried out extensive building works at the Vatican, where he established the first papal residence and gardens, and rebuilt the Sancta Sanctorum.

Reputation

Nicholas was criticised for nepotism, advancing many Orsini relatives; Dante placed him among the simoniacs in the Inferno, while other contemporaries praised his ability and piety. He died at Soriano nel Cimino in August 1280.
