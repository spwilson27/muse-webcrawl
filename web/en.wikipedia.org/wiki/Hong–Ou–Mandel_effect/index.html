Hong–Ou–Mandel effect


Schematic of the Hong–Ou–Mandel experiment

The Hong–Ou–Mandel effect is a two-photon interference effect in quantum optics. It describes how two identical photons incident on the two input ports of a 50:50 beam splitter will always emerge together from the same output port. The effect was demonstrated in 1987 by physicists Chung Ki Hong, Zhe Yu Ou, and Leonard Mandel at the University of Rochester.

Explanation

When two indistinguishable photons arrive simultaneously at a beam splitter, there are two possible outcomes in which they exit through different ports: both photons are transmitted, or both are reflected. Because the photons are identical, the probability amplitudes for these two outcomes interfere destructively, cancelling the coincidence events. The photons therefore "bunch," always leaving through the same output port. If the photons are distinguishable—for example, by arriving at different times—this cancellation does not occur.

The HOM dip

Experimentally, the effect is observed by scanning the relative delay between the two photons and counting coincidences between detectors at the two outputs. As the delay approaches zero, the coincidence rate falls into a characteristic minimum known as the Hong–Ou–Mandel dip. The width of the dip is inversely related to the spectral bandwidth of the photons, and a perfectly deep dip is a signature of high photon indistinguishability.

Applications

The effect is fundamental to linear optical quantum computing, where it underlies two-qubit gates such as those in the KLM protocol, and it is used to characterise single-photon sources, perform Bell state measurements for quantum teleportation and entanglement swapping, and in precision metrology and quantum sensing.
