Banksy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Girl with Balloon (2002): a small girl reaching toward a red heart-shaped balloon carried off by the wind, stencilled on a wall]

Banksy is a pseudonymous England-based street artist, political activist and film director whose identity remains unconfirmed. Emerging from the Bristol underground scene of the 1990s, he combined stencilled graffiti with black humour and anti-authoritarian, anti-war and anti-capitalist messages; his works on streets, walls and bridges worldwide are among the most famous images of contemporary art.

Iconic works include Girl with Balloon (2002), the Flower Thrower, the murals on the West Bank barrier (2005), the Dismaland pop-up exhibition (2015), and the painting that partly shredded itself at auction in 2018, retitled Love is in the Bin.

Rise and anonymity

Banksy began as a freehand graffiti writer in Bristol before adopting stencils — faster, and escapable — under the influence of the French artist Blek le Rat. Exhibitions such as Turf War (2003), Crude Oils (2005, with live rats) and Barely Legal (Los Angeles, 2006) built a celebrity following while he refused interviews and verification; authentication is controlled by his office, Pest Control. His Oscar-nominated film Exit Through the Gift Shop (2010) plays elaborate games with documentary truth.

Market and public incidents

Works are routinely cut from walls and sold, councils debate preserving or painting over them, and murals have appeared overnight from Bethlehem to Venice to Kyiv (2022). In 2018 a framed Girl with Balloon passed through a hidden shredder moments after selling for £1.04 million; the half-shredded Love is in the Bin resold in 2021 for £18.6 million, a Banksy record.
