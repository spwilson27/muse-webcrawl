Cinderella (Prokofiev)

A scene from Prokofiev's Cinderella

Cinderella (Russian: Золушка, Zolushka), Op. 87, is a ballet composed by Sergei Prokofiev to a scenario by Nikolai Volkov, based on the fairy tale by Charles Perrault. It is one of Prokofiev's most popular and melodious compositions and has inspired many choreographers since its creation.

History

Prokofiev began the work in 1940 for the Kirov Ballet, but the German invasion of the Soviet Union interrupted composition. He completed the ballet in 1944, and it was premiered at the Bolshoi Theatre in Moscow on November 21, 1945, choreographed by Rostislav Zakharov, with Galina Ulanova in the title role. Frederick Ashton's 1948 production for the Sadler's Wells Ballet was the first full-length ballet by an English choreographer and remains a landmark staging.

Music

Written in three acts, the score combines lyrical waltzes for Cinderella and the Prince with grotesque, angular music for the stepmother and stepsisters. Prokofiev extracted three orchestral suites from the ballet, and its richly orchestrated numbers — including the midnight scene with its chiming clocks — are frequently performed in concert.
