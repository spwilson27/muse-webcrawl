Sheraton Hotels and Resorts is an American international hotel brand owned by Marriott International. As one of the largest and oldest upscale hotel brands in the world, Sheraton operates hundreds of hotels and resorts in more than 70 countries, oriented toward business travellers, conventions and leisure guests. The brand's related flags include the luxury Sheraton Grand tier and the select-service Four Points by Sheraton.

History

Sheraton began in 1937, when Harvard classmates Ernest Henderson and Robert Moore bought the Stonehaven Hotel in Springfield, Massachusetts. The chain took its name from a later-acquired Boston-area hotel whose large "Sheraton" sign on the roof would have been too expensive to remove. The company expanded rapidly after the Second World War, purchasing hotels across the United States and Canada, and in 1949 went international. Sheraton was among the first hotel chains to list on the New York Stock Exchange (1947), to introduce a centralised reservation system (the "Reservatron", 1955) and a toll-free reservation number, and to expand into the Middle East and China, where the Great Wall Sheraton in Beijing (1985) was an early joint-venture hotel of the reform era.

The conglomerate ITT acquired Sheraton in 1968. In 1998 Starwood Hotels and Resorts bought the brand from ITT, making Sheraton its flagship. Following Marriott International's acquisition of Starwood in 2016, Sheraton became part of Marriott's portfolio, and in 2018 Marriott announced a multi-year "Sheraton transformation" programme to renovate properties and reposition the brand around communal lobby spaces and local community.

Brand tiers

  • Sheraton — the core full-service brand.
  • Sheraton Grand — premier properties meeting higher standards, introduced 2015.
  • Four Points by Sheraton — midscale/select-service sister brand, launched 1995.
  • Sheraton Vacation Club — timeshare properties.
