Information security

Information security, sometimes shortened to InfoSec, is the practice of protecting information by mitigating information risks. It involves procedures and technology created to prevent unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction of information, whether in electronic or physical form. Information security is typically considered a part of information assurance and is closely related to, but distinct from, computer security and cybersecurity.

The CIA triad

The traditional model of information security is the CIA triad, consisting of confidentiality, integrity, and availability. Confidentiality ensures that information is accessible only to those authorized to have access, typically enforced through encryption, access controls, and authentication. Integrity involves maintaining the consistency, accuracy, and trustworthiness of data over its entire life cycle, so that data cannot be modified in an unauthorized or undetected manner. Availability ensures that information is available when needed by authorized users, which requires properly maintained hardware, software, and network infrastructure, as well as protection against denial-of-service attacks and other disruptions.

Some models extend the triad with additional attributes such as authenticity, accountability, non-repudiation, and reliability. The Parkerian hexad, proposed by Donn B. Parker in 1998, adds possession or control, authenticity, and utility to the classic three elements.

Risk management

Information security is fundamentally a risk management discipline. Organizations identify their information assets, assess threats and vulnerabilities, estimate the likelihood and impact of adverse events, and implement controls proportionate to the risk. Common controls include administrative measures such as security policies and training, technical measures such as firewalls, intrusion detection systems, and multi-factor authentication, and physical measures such as locks and surveillance.

The process is cyclical: controls are monitored, incidents are analyzed, and the security program is continually improved. Because perfect security is unattainable, the goal is to reduce risk to an acceptable level at reasonable cost.

History

The need to protect sensitive information is ancient: Julius Caesar is said to have used a substitution cipher, now known as the Caesar cipher, to protect military messages. Modern information security developed with the spread of computing in the twentieth century. The 1988 Morris worm, one of the first computer worms distributed via the Internet, led to the creation of the first Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT). The growth of the World Wide Web in the 1990s and the increasing value of digital data in the 2000s transformed information security into a major profession and industry.

Standards and governance

International standards such as the ISO/IEC 27000 family specify requirements for information security management systems (ISMS). The NIST Cybersecurity Framework, published by the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology, organizes security activity around the functions Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover. Laws and regulations including the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the U.S. Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) impose legal duties to safeguard particular categories of information.

Careers

Information security professionals include security analysts, security engineers, penetration testers, incident responders, and chief information security officers (CISOs). Certifications such as CISSP, CISM, and CompTIA Security+ are widely recognized credentials in the field.
