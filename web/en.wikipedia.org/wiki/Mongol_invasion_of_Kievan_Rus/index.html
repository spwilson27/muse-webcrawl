Mongol invasion of Kievan Rus'

The Mongol invasion of Kievan Rus' was part of the Mongol invasion of Europe in which the Mongol Empire under Batu Khan and the general Subutai conquered the principalities of Kievan Rus' between 1237 and 1242. Nearly every major Rus' city was besieged and sacked, including Ryazan, Vladimir, and Kiev itself, which fell in December 1240. The invasion brought the Rus' lands under the dominion of the Golden Horde, a subjugation remembered in Russian historiography as the "Tatar yoke", lasting in some form until 1480.

Background

Rus' forces had first encountered the Mongols at the Battle of the Kalka River in 1223, where a coalition of Rus' princes and Cumans was decisively defeated. The fragmented Rus' principalities, frequently at war with one another, failed to unite against the renewed threat when the Mongols returned in force in 1237.

The campaigns

In December 1237 the Mongols destroyed Ryazan after a five-day siege. Kolomna and Moscow fell in January 1238, and the grand prince Yuri II of Vladimir was killed at the Battle of the Sit River in March 1238. After a pause, the Mongols turned south: Pereyaslavl and Chernigov fell in 1239, and on 6 December 1240 Kiev, the former capital of the Rus', was stormed after a fierce siege. Galicia and Volhynia were subdued in 1241 before the Mongols moved on into Poland and Hungary.

Consequences

The destruction was severe: many cities lost much of their population, craft production declined, and stone building largely ceased for a generation. Surviving princes travelled to the khan's court to receive patents to rule and paid regular tribute. Novgorod, which submitted without a siege, retained greater autonomy under Alexander Nevsky. The two centuries of Mongol overlordship reshaped the political development of the region and are traditionally blamed for isolating Rus' from western Europe.
