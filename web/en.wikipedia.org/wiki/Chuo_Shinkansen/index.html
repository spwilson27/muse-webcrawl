Chuo Shinkansen

The L0 Series maglev trainset used for Chuo Shinkansen testing

The Chuo Shinkansen (中央新幹線, Chūō Shinkansen) is a Japanese maglev line under construction between Tokyo and Nagoya, with plans for extension to Osaka. It will use the SCMaglev superconducting magnetic levitation technology developed by the Central Japan Railway Company (JR Central), with trains travelling at a maximum operating speed of 500 km/h (311 mph).

When complete, the line will connect Tokyo and Nagoya in about 40 minutes and Tokyo and Osaka in about 67 minutes, running largely through deep tunnels beneath the Japanese Alps. Roughly 86 percent of the first section will be underground or in tunnels.

History

Maglev research in Japan began in the 1960s, and testing on the Yamanashi Maglev Test Line has taken place since 1997. In 2011 the government authorised construction of the Chuo Shinkansen, and JR Central began building the Shinagawa–Nagoya section in 2014. Disputes with Shizuoka Prefecture over water resources in the Oi River basin delayed tunnelling work in that section, pushing back the planned opening beyond the original 2027 target.

Technology

The SCMaglev system uses superconducting magnets aboard the train interacting with coils in the guideway to provide levitation, guidance, and propulsion by linear synchronous motor. The L0 Series trainsets set a world speed record for rail vehicles of 603 km/h in 2015.
