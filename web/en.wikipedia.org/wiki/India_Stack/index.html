India Stack

India Stack is a set of open APIs and digital public infrastructure built in India that allows governments, businesses and developers to deliver services using a unique digital identity. The name "stack" refers to its layered architecture, which is commonly described in four layers: a presence-less (identity) layer, a paper-less layer, a cash-less layer, and a consent layer. It is regarded as one of the world's most prominent examples of digital public infrastructure (DPI).

Layers and components

The identity layer is built on Aadhaar, the biometric identity system administered by the Unique Identification Authority of India, which has enrolled over a billion residents and supports e-KYC authentication. The paper-less layer includes DigiLocker for verifiable digital documents, eSign for electronic signatures, and the Unified Payments Interface's document-sharing counterpart systems. The cash-less layer centres on the Unified Payments Interface (UPI), launched in 2016 by the National Payments Corporation of India, which enables instant, interoperable bank-to-bank transfers and became one of the world's largest real-time payment systems. The consent layer is represented by the DEPA (Data Empowerment and Protection Architecture) framework and account aggregators, which let individuals share financial data with consent.

Development and impact

The stack was promoted by the Indian Software Product Industry Round Table (iSPIRT) think tank and figures such as Nandan Nilekani, and it underpins programmes including Jan Dhan bank accounts, direct benefit transfers, the BHIM app, and the CoWIN vaccination platform. International institutions including the World Bank and the IMF have studied India Stack as a model of low-cost, population-scale digital infrastructure, and several of its components have inspired modular open-source projects such as MOSIP for identity systems in other countries.

Criticism

India Stack has drawn criticism over privacy, exclusion errors in welfare delivery, and the security of biometric data — concerns that contributed to India's Supreme Court restricting mandatory Aadhaar use in its 2018 judgment, and to the eventual passage of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023.
