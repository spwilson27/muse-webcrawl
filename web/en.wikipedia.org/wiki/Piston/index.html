Piston

A piston is a component of reciprocating engines, reciprocating pumps, gas compressors, hydraulic cylinders, and pneumatic cylinders, among other similar mechanisms. It is the moving component that is contained by a cylinder and is made gas-tight by piston rings. In an engine, its purpose is to transfer force from expanding gas in the cylinder to the crankshaft via a connecting rod.

Design

A typical piston has a crown (the head facing the combustion chamber), ring grooves carrying compression and oil-control rings, a skirt that guides it in the bore, and a gudgeon pin (wrist pin) connecting it to the connecting rod. Pistons are usually cast or forged from aluminium alloys for lightness and heat conduction, though cast iron was common in early engines.

Operation

In a four-stroke petrol engine, the piston moves through intake, compression, power, and exhaust strokes. Pressure from combustion pushes the piston down on the power stroke; the connecting rod converts this linear motion into rotation of the crankshaft.

Other applications

Pistons are also used in steam engines, Stirling engines, air compressors, syringes, and pneumatic actuators. In hydraulic cylinders the piston transmits force from pressurised fluid without combustion.
