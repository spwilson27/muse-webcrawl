Viticulture

Viticulture (from the Latin word for vine) or winegrowing is the cultivation and harvesting of grapes. It is a branch of the science of horticulture. While the native territory of Vitis vinifera, the common grape vine, ranges from Western Europe to the Persian shores of the Caspian Sea, the vine has demonstrated high levels of adaptability to new environments; for this reason, viticulture can be found on every continent except Antarctica.

History

The earliest evidence of grape domestication comes from the South Caucasus and Near East around 6000–5800 BC. Viticulture spread with Phoenician, Greek, and Roman trade throughout the Mediterranean; the Romans systematised vineyard practice in works by Cato, Varro, Columella, and Pliny. In the Middle Ages, monasteries preserved and refined grape growing. The phylloxera epidemic of the later 19th century devastated European vineyards, which were saved by grafting European scions onto resistant American rootstocks — a practice now nearly universal. The 20th and 21st centuries saw the rise of New World wine regions and scientific viticulture research at institutions such as Geisenheim and UC Davis.

The grapevine

The annual growth cycle of the vine runs from budburst in spring through flowering, fruit set, veraison (the onset of ripening), harvest in autumn, and winter dormancy. Vines are propagated by cuttings to preserve clonal identity, trained and trellised in various systems (such as Guyot, cordon, and goblet/bush vine), and pruned annually to balance vegetative growth with fruit production.

Terroir and site selection

Viticulture is governed by terroir — the interaction of climate, soil, topography, and local practice. Ideal vineyard sites typically offer a long, moderate growing season, well-drained soils, and slopes with good sun exposure and air drainage to reduce frost risk. Climate categories range from cool (Champagne, Mosel) through maritime and Mediterranean to continental and tropical systems.

Pests and diseases

Major threats include the root louse phylloxera, fungal diseases such as powdery mildew and downy mildew, botrytis bunch rot, and viral diseases spread by grafting and insects. Management strategies include resistant rootstocks, canopy management, fungicides, and integrated pest management; organic and biodynamic viticulture minimise synthetic inputs.
