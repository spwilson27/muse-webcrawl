Roof


Illustration placeholder: a pitched tiled roof

A roof (from Proto-Germanic *hrof) is the top covering of a building, including all materials and constructions necessary to support it on the walls of the building or on uprights, providing protection against rain, snow, sunlight, extremes of temperature, and wind. A roof is part of the building envelope.

The characteristics of a roof are dependent upon the purpose of the building that it covers, the available roofing materials and the local traditions of construction and wider concepts of architectural design and practice, and may also be governed by local or national legislation. In most countries a roof protects primarily against rain; depending upon the nature of the building it may also protect against heat, sunlight, cold and wind.

Design

Roofs are built either pitched (sloped, shedding water by gravity) or flat (in practice slightly sloped, and dependent on membranes and drainage). Common pitched forms include the gable, hip, gambrel, mansard, saltbox, and butterfly roofs; flat or vaulted roofs include domes and barrel vaults. The supporting structure may be of timber rafters and trusses, steel, or reinforced concrete, with a deck or substrate carrying the weatherproof covering.

Materials

Traditional coverings include thatch, wooden shingles, clay and concrete tiles, slate, and corrugated metal. Modern coverings include asphalt shingles, built-up bituminous roofing, single-ply membranes (EPDM, TPO, PVC), metal standing-seam panels, and increasingly photovoltaic shingles and panels. The choice depends on climate, budget, fire performance, and aesthetics.

Parts of a roof

The sloping planes meet at a ridge (top), hips (external angles), and valleys (internal angles). The edges are the eaves (lower, often with gutters) and verges or gable ends. Penetrations such as chimneys and vents require flashing to seal against water ingress, and roofs are detailed with fascias, soffits, ridge tiles, and underlayment ("roofing felt" or synthetic membranes).

Functions and modern developments

Beyond weatherproofing, roofs carry insulation, ventilation of attics, and increasingly equipment: solar panels, HVAC units, and roof gardens. "Cool roofs" with reflective surfaces and green roofs planted with vegetation reduce urban heat-island effects and manage stormwater.
