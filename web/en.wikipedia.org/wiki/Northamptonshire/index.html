Northamptonshire is a ceremonial county in the East Midlands of England. Its county town is Northampton, and other major towns include Kettering, Corby, Wellingborough, Rushden and Daventry. The county is known for its honey-coloured limestone villages, country houses and its historic boot and shoe industry.

Geography

The county is landlocked and largely low-lying, drained by the River Nene, which rises near Daventry and flows north-east through Northampton. A low watershed running across the county separates the Nene valley from the basins of the Welland, the Great Ouse and the Thames tributaries. Much of the building stone of the county's villages comes from Jurassic limestone and ironstone outcrops.

History and economy

Northampton became a centre of boot and shoe manufacture in the 19th century, supplying footwear to the British Army; a few high-end shoemakers survive. Corby grew as a steel town from the 1930s. In 2021 the county council was replaced by two unitary authorities, North Northamptonshire and West Northamptonshire. The county is also known for motorsport: Silverstone Circuit, home of the British Grand Prix, lies on its southern border, and many Formula One teams are based nearby.

Places of interest

Notable houses include Althorp, the Spencer family seat and burial place of Diana, Princess of Wales; Burghley House (just across the historic Soke of Peterborough border associations), Sulgrave Manor, ancestral home of George Washington's family, and Rockingham Castle. The poet John Clare was born in Helpston in the Soke.
