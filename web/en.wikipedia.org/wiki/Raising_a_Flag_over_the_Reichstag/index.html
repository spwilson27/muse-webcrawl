Raising a Flag over the Reichstag

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Raising a Flag over the Reichstag is a historic World War II photograph, taken during the Battle of Berlin on 2 May 1945 by Yevgeny Khaldei. It became one of the most famous and recognisable images of the war, symbolising the Soviet victory over Nazi Germany. The photograph depicts Soviet soldiers raising the flag of the Soviet Union atop the Reichstag building in Berlin.

Background

The Reichstag was seen by the Soviets as a symbol of the Nazi regime, and its capture was a central objective of the final Soviet assault on Berlin. After fierce fighting, Red Army soldiers reached the building on 30 April 1945. Several flags were raised over the building during the battle; the first was reportedly planted on the night of 30 April by soldiers including Mikhail Minin, but it could not be photographed in the darkness.

The photograph

Khaldei, a Red Navy photographer working for the TASS news agency, had carried a large Soviet flag sewn from tablecloths by his uncle. On 2 May, after the fighting had subsided, he gathered a group of soldiers and posed them on the roof. The best-known image shows two soldiers: one holding the flagpole while the other supports him. The soldiers are commonly identified as Meliton Kantaria and Mikhail Yegorov, although the men in the photograph have also been identified as Alexei Kovalev and Abdulkhakim Ismailov.

Manipulation and legacy

Before publication, the photograph was retouched: smoke was added in the background for dramatic effect, and a second wristwatch visible on one soldier's arm — which might have suggested looting — was removed. The image was first published in the magazine Ogoniok on 13 May 1945 and has since become an enduring symbol of the Soviet role in defeating Nazi Germany. It is frequently compared with the American photograph Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima.
