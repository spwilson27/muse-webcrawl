Württemberg is a historical German state in Swabia, in the southwest of Germany, roughly corresponding to the eastern part of the modern state of Baden-Württemberg. Its capital was Stuttgart, and it was ruled for over eight centuries by the House of Württemberg.

History

The counts of Württemberg, first recorded in the 11th century, steadily expanded their territory along the Neckar valley. In 1495 Emperor Maximilian I raised the county to a duchy at the Diet of Worms. Württemberg became Protestant under Duke Ulrich and later Christoph in the 16th century, and suffered terribly in the Thirty Years' War, losing much of its population.

Enlarged by the mediatisations of the Napoleonic era, Württemberg became an electorate in 1803 and a kingdom in 1806 under Frederick I. It joined the German Confederation and, in 1871, the German Empire, retaining certain reserved rights including its own army administration and postal service. King William II abdicated in November 1918, and Württemberg became the Free People's State of Württemberg within the Weimar Republic.

After 1945

After the Second World War the territory was divided between the American and French occupation zones (Württemberg-Baden and Württemberg-Hohenzollern), which merged with South Baden in 1952 to form the present Land of Baden-Württemberg.
