Irish Rugby Football Union

The Irish Rugby Football Union (IRFU; Irish: Cumann Rugbaí na hÉireann) is the governing body for rugby union on the island of Ireland. Founded in 1879, it organises the sport across both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland, fielding a single Ireland national team that represents the whole island, and it is a founding member of the body now known as World Rugby.

History

The IRFU was formed in 1879 by the merger of the Irish Football Union (based in Dublin) and the Northern Football Union (based in Belfast). Ireland played its first international against England in 1875 and was a founder participant of the Home Championship, the predecessor of today's Six Nations Championship. The union's cross-border structure survived the partition of Ireland in 1921, making rugby one of the prominent all-island sports; its national team uses the specially composed anthem "Ireland's Call" alongside the Irish national anthem at home matches.

Structure

The IRFU is organised through four provincial branches — Leinster, Munster, Ulster, and Connacht — whose professional teams compete in the United Rugby Championship and European competitions. The professional era brought major success: Ireland won Six Nations Grand Slams in 2009, 2018, and 2023, and reached the top of the world rankings, while Leinster and Munster became leading European club sides. The union's headquarters are in Dublin, and the national team plays at the Aviva Stadium, opened in 2010 on the site of the former Lansdowne Road.
