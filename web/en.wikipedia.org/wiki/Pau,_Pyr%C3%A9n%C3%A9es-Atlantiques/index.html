Pau is a commune in southwestern France, capital of the Pyrénées-Atlantiques department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. The historic capital of Béarn, it sits on the Gave de Pau at the foot of the Pyrenees, with celebrated views of the mountain range from its Boulevard des Pyrénées.

History

Pau grew around a castle founded in the 11th century. It became the capital of Béarn and, under the Albret family, of the Kingdom of Navarre's French holdings. The Château de Pau is the birthplace of Henry IV of France (1553), the first Bourbon king, whose cradle — a giant tortoise shell — is preserved there. In the 19th century Pau became a fashionable winter resort for the British, who built villas, gardens, and what is considered the oldest golf course in continental Europe.

The city today

Modern Pau is a university city and a center for aeronautics and geoscience research linked to the Lacq gas field. It is a regular stage town of the Tour de France and hosts the Grand Prix de Pau motor race. Its rugby club, Section Paloise, is a fixture of French rugby.
