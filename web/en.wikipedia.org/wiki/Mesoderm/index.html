Mesoderm

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The mesoderm is the middle layer of the three germ layers that develops during gastrulation in the very early stage of the development of most animals. The outer layer is the ectoderm, and the inner layer is the endoderm. In bilaterians, the mesoderm gives rise to muscle, bone, cartilage, blood and blood vessels, connective tissue, the dermis of the skin, and much of the reproductive and excretory systems.

Formation

In human development, during the third week of embryogenesis, cells of the epiblast migrate through the primitive streak and come to lie between the ectoderm and endoderm, forming the mesoderm. This process, gastrulation, establishes the trilaminar embryonic disc. The notochord forms along the midline from mesodermal cells and acts as an important signalling centre and axial support.

Subdivisions

The mesoderm differentiates into several regions:

  • Paraxial mesoderm — segments into somites along the neural tube; somites produce the sclerotome (vertebrae and ribs), myotome (skeletal muscle), and dermatome (dermis).
  • Intermediate mesoderm — forms the urogenital system, including kidneys and gonads.
  • Lateral plate mesoderm — splits into somatic (parietal) and splanchnic (visceral) layers around the intraembryonic coelom; it contributes to the body wall, limbs, heart, blood vessels, and the smooth muscle and connective tissue of the gut.
  • Chordamesoderm — forms the notochord.

Derivatives

Mesodermal derivatives include all skeletal, cardiac, and most smooth muscle; the skeleton and connective tissues; blood and lymphatic cells and vessels; the spleen; the kidneys and gonads; the adrenal cortex; and the dermis. Signalling pathways involving BMP, Wnt, FGF, and the transcription factor brachyury (T) pattern the mesoderm along the body axes.
