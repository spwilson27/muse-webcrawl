Respiratory alkalosis

Respiratory alkalosis is a medical condition in which increased respiration elevates the blood pH beyond the normal range (7.35–7.45), with a concurrent reduction in arterial levels of carbon dioxide. This condition is one of the four basic categories of disruption of acid-base homeostasis.

Causes

Respiratory alkalosis generally occurs in response to a stimulus that makes a person hyperventilate. Increased production of carbon dioxide occurs in numerous situations, but respiratory alkalosis is specifically caused by an increased rate of breathing relative to carbon dioxide production. Causes include central nervous system stimulation (fever, pain, anxiety, stroke, meningitis, trauma, or brain tumours), stimulation of chest receptors (pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, asthma, pulmonary edema), drugs and toxins (salicylates, catecholamines, medroxyprogesterone), and high altitude.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms may include light-headedness, confusion, paresthesias (especially perioral and in the extremities), cramps, and syncope. Alkalosis also lowers ionized calcium, which can produce signs such as the Chvostek sign and Trousseau sign.

Treatment

Respiratory alkalosis is very rarely life-threatening, though pH level should not be allowed to rise above 7.6. Treatment consists of supplementing oxygen if needed and treating the underlying cause; in some cases, sedatives or breathing into a paper bag (rebreathing exhaled carbon dioxide) have been used for hyperventilation syndrome.
