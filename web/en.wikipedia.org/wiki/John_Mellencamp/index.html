John J. Mellencamp (born 7 October 1951), previously known as Johnny Cougar, John Cougar and John Cougar Mellencamp, is an American singer-songwriter, musician and painter. Known for his catchy brand of heartland rock emphasising traditional instrumentation, he rose to fame in the 1980s with a string of Top 10 singles including "Hurts So Good", "Jack & Diane", "Pink Houses", "Small Town" and "R.O.C.K. in the U.S.A." He has sold more than 60 million albums worldwide and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2008.

Early life

Mellencamp was born in Seymour, Indiana, with spina bifida, for which he had corrective surgery as an infant. He formed his first band as a teenager, attended Vincennes University, and played in local glam-rock bands before moving to New York in the mid-1970s, where manager Tony DeFries signed him and imposed the stage name "Johnny Cougar".

Career

His breakthrough came with the album American Fool (1982), which reached number one in the United States and produced the hit singles "Hurts So Good" and "Jack & Diane", the latter spending four weeks at number one. Now billed as John Cougar Mellencamp, he followed with Uh-Huh (1983) and Scarecrow (1985), whose songs portrayed small-town American life and the struggles of family farmers. Later acclaimed albums include The Lonesome Jubilee (1987), Big Daddy (1989) and the stripped-down, roots-oriented records of the 2000s and 2010s produced by T Bone Burnett, such as Life, Death, Love and Freedom (2008) and No Better Than This (2010).

Activism

In 1985 Mellencamp co-founded Farm Aid with Willie Nelson and Neil Young to raise awareness and funds for American family farmers; the organisation's annual concerts have continued for decades and raised tens of millions of dollars.

Painting

Mellencamp is also an accomplished painter whose expressionist portraits and social-realist canvases have been exhibited in galleries and museums; a major retrospective, "Life, Death, Love, Freedom", toured in the 2010s.
