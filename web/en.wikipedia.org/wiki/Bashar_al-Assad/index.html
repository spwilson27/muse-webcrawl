Bashar al-Assad

Bashar al-Assad (born 11 September 1965) is a Syrian former politician and military officer who served as president of Syria from 2000 until his government was overthrown in December 2024. The second son of Hafez al-Assad, he inherited a Ba'athist one-party state and ruled through the Syrian civil war before fleeing to Russia as rebels captured Damascus.

Early life and rise

Trained as an ophthalmologist in Damascus and London, Assad was not the intended heir until his elder brother Bassel died in a 1994 car crash. He was recalled, groomed through the military, and succeeded his father in July 2000 after the constitution's minimum age was lowered from 40 to his age of 34. A brief "Damascus Spring" of debate clubs was followed by renewed repression.

Presidency and civil war

Assad liberalized parts of the economy while retaining the security apparatus. In 2011, protests in the Arab Spring were met with force, escalating into a civil war involving rebel groups, Kurdish forces, jihadists including Islamic State, and foreign backers — Russia and Iran for Assad; Turkey, Gulf states, and the U.S. in shifting roles. UN investigators accused his government of war crimes including chemical weapons attacks at Ghouta (2013) and Khan Shaykhun (2017) and mass detention and torture. The war killed over half a million people and displaced roughly half the population.

Fall

Weakened by the loss of Hezbollah and Russian support amid other wars, the regime collapsed during an offensive launched in late November 2024 by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham and allied forces. On 8 December 2024 rebels took Damascus; Assad and his family received asylum in Moscow. See also Syrian civil war.
