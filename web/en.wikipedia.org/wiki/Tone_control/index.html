Tone control

A tone control is an audio circuit that adjusts the balance of low and high frequencies in a signal — most commonly separate bass and treble controls on amplifiers, radios, and instruments. Tone controls are a simple form of equalisation, allowing listeners to boost or cut frequency ranges to taste or to compensate for speakers and rooms.

Circuits

The classic design is the passive Baxandall tone-control circuit, described by Peter Baxandall in 1952, which provides smooth, symmetric boost and cut of bass and treble with low distortion and remains standard in hi-fi equipment. Simpler single-knob controls, such as those on electric guitars, act as a variable low-pass filter: the guitar's tone knob rolls off treble by shunting high frequencies to ground through a capacitor, and many amplifier "presence" controls adjust upper-midrange feedback.

Use

Tone controls differ from graphic and parametric equalisers, which offer finer control over multiple bands. Recording engineers often prefer to record "flat" and shape tone during mixing, while guitarists use instrument and amp tone knobs expressively in real time.
