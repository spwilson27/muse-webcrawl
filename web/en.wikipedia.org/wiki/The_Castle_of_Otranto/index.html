The Castle of Otranto is a novel by Horace Walpole, first published in 1764. It is generally regarded as the first Gothic novel and initiated a literary genre that became extremely popular in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. Walpole initially presented the work as a translation of a medieval Italian manuscript by a fictitious author, "Onuphrio Muralto," and only acknowledged his authorship in the second edition.

Plot

The story concerns Manfred, the usurping prince of Otranto, who is determined to secure his family's hold on the principality. On the wedding day of his sickly son Conrad to the beautiful Isabella, Conrad is found crushed to death in the castle courtyard by an enormous helmet bearing the crest of Alfonso, the former rightful prince. Manfred, suspecting a prophecy that the castle shall pass from the present family when the true owner grows too large to inhabit it, resolves to divorce his wife Hippolita and marry Isabella himself. Isabella flees into the castle's subterranean passages, where she is aided by a young peasant, Theodore, who resembles the statue of Alfonso. Manfred's schemes unravel as supernatural portents accumulate — a giant hand, sighing portraits, and bleeding statues — until Theodore is revealed to be the true heir, Manfred accidentally kills his own daughter Matilda, and the tyrant repents and retires to a convent.

Publication history

The first edition, issued in December 1764, was presented as a translation by "William Marshal" of an Italian work printed at Naples in 1529. Walpole admitted authorship in the second edition of 1765, which added the subtitle "A Gothic Story" — the first use of "Gothic" to describe a literary form. The novel was inspired in part by a dream Walpole had at his Gothic Revival house, Strawberry Hill.

Influence

The novel established many conventions of Gothic fiction: ancient castles, supernatural events, persecuted heroines, tyrannical villains, and hidden identities. It influenced writers including Ann Radcliffe, Matthew Lewis, Bram Stoker, and Edgar Allan Poe, and its blend of medieval romance and realistic narrative helped shape the modern horror and fantasy genres.
