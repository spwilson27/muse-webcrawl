Count Basie

William James "Count" Basie (August 21, 1904 – April 26, 1984) was an American jazz pianist, organist, bandleader and composer. He founded the Count Basie Orchestra in 1935 and led it for nearly 50 years, making it one of the defining ensembles of the swing era with a rhythm section celebrated for its relaxed, propulsive feel.

Career

Born in Red Bank, New Jersey, Basie learned piano from his mother and from Fats Waller's organ playing. After touring in vaudeville he settled in Kansas City, playing with Walter Page's Blue Devils and Bennie Moten's band, whose leadership he assumed after Moten's death in 1935. Broadcasts from Kansas City's Reno Club brought the band to the attention of producer John Hammond, and national fame followed in New York.

The "All-American Rhythm Section" — Basie, Page (bass), Freddie Green (guitar) and Jo Jones (drums) — set the standard for swing rhythm. Signature recordings include "One O'Clock Jump," "Jumpin' at the Woodside" and, with singer Joe Williams and arranger Neal Hefti in the 1950s "New Testament" band, "April in Paris." Basie won multiple Grammy Awards and was among the first inductees honoured by the Kennedy Center.
