Eadie–Hofstee diagram

Eadie–Hofstee diagram
Field: Enzyme kinetics
Related: Michaelis–Menten kinetics

In biochemistry, an Eadie–Hofstee diagram is a graphical representation of enzyme kinetics in which the reaction rate v is plotted against the ratio of rate to substrate concentration v/[S]. It derives from rearranging the Michaelis–Menten equation and is named after George Eadie and Barend Hofstee, who published versions in the 1940s and 1950s.

Interpretation

For Michaelis–Menten kinetics the plot is a straight line with slope −Km, y-intercept Vmax and x-intercept Vmax/Km. Because the variables on each axis are spread more evenly than in a Lineweaver–Burk plot, departures from linearity — such as those caused by two enzymes or substrate inhibition — are easier to detect.

Limitations

Since v appears on both axes, experimental errors are correlated between axes, which can distort statistical estimates. Modern practice therefore prefers direct nonlinear fitting of the Michaelis–Menten equation, with Eadie–Hofstee plots used mainly for display and diagnosis.
