Frisia

Frisia is a coastal region along the southeastern shore of the North Sea, stretching from the northwestern Netherlands through northwestern Germany to the border of Denmark. It is the homeland of the Frisians, a Germanic ethnic group speaking the Frisian languages, the closest living relatives of English.

Divisions

The region is conventionally divided into West Frisia in the Netherlands (including the province of Friesland), East Frisia (Ostfriesland) in Lower Saxony, and North Frisia (Nordfriesland) in Schleswig-Holstein, together with the Frisian Islands strung along the coast, known collectively as the Wadden Sea islands.

History

In the early Middle Ages a Frisian kingdom and trading network centred on Dorestad flourished before Frankish conquest. Frisian communities later enjoyed a tradition of self-governing "Frisian freedom" without feudal lords. Today Frisian language and culture are officially recognized in both the Netherlands and Germany.
