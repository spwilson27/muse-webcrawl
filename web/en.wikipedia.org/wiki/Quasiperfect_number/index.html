Quasiperfect number

In number theory, a quasiperfect number is a positive integer n whose sum of proper divisors (equivalently, σ(n), the sum-of-divisors function) equals 2n + 1. Equivalently, its abundance is 1: the number is one away from being perfect (σ(n) = 2n), but on the abundant side. No quasiperfect number is known to exist, and their existence is an open problem.

Properties

Every quasiperfect number must be an odd square greater than 1035, and must have at least seven distinct prime factors. These bounds are due to Hakhnan and others. The concept is related to almost perfect numbers (σ(n) = 2n − 1), whose existence is also unknown, and to Descartes numbers and spoof perfect numbers, artificial constructs that mimic the properties of odd perfect numbers.
