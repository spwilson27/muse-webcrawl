Centauromachy

A Lapith battles a centaur, from the Parthenon metopes.

The Centauromachy is the legendary battle in Greek mythology between the Lapiths and the centaurs, fought at the wedding feast of the Lapith king Pirithous and Hippodamia. It is one of the most frequently depicted episodes in Greek art, symbolising the struggle between civilisation and barbarism, order and chaos.

The myth

The centaurs, invited to the wedding, became drunk on undiluted wine — to which they were unaccustomed — and one of them, Eurytion, attempted to abduct the bride. The other centaurs joined the assault on the Lapith women and boys, and a general battle erupted in the hall, fought with improvised weapons such as goblets, furniture, and tree trunks. Aided by the hero Theseus, the Lapiths prevailed; the centaurs were defeated and driven from Mount Pelion. The story appears in the Iliad and is told at length in Ovid's Metamorphoses.

In art

The Centauromachy decorated the west pediment of the Temple of Zeus at Olympia and the south metopes of the Parthenon. Renaissance and later artists, including Michelangelo in an early marble relief, returned to the theme as a vehicle for depicting violent interlocked bodies.
