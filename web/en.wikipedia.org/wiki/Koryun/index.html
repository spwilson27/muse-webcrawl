Koryun (Armenian: Կորյուն, c. 380 – c. 450) was an Armenian historian and writer of the 5th century, a pupil of Mesrop Mashtots, the inventor of the Armenian alphabet. He is best known as the author of The Life of Mashtots (Vark' Mashtots'i), the earliest surviving work of Armenian historiography written in Armenian.

Life and work

Koryun studied under Mashtots and the Catholicos Sahak Partev, and was among the students sent to Edessa, Constantinople, and other centers to acquire Greek and Syriac learning and to collect manuscripts for translation into the newly devised Armenian script. He participated in the translation movement that rendered the Bible and Greek patristic works into Armenian, ushering in the golden age of Armenian literature.

The Life of Mashtots, composed in the 440s at the request of Catholicos Hovsep Hoghotsmetsi, is a biography of Mashtots that also describes the creation of the Armenian alphabet around 405 AD, the invention of scripts for Georgian and Caucasian Albanian (the authorship of which Koryun also attributes to Mashtots), and the early evangelization and schooling efforts of the Armenian Church. The work survives in later recensions, including an expanded version, and its historical claims have been studied and debated extensively.
