Neuro-linguistic programming

Neuro-linguistic programming (NLP) is a pseudoscientific approach to communication, personal development, and psychotherapy that first appeared in Richard Bandler and John Grinder's 1975 book The Structure of Magic I. NLP asserts that there is a connection between neurological processes, language, and acquired behavioral patterns, and that these can be changed to achieve specific goals in life.

NLP originated in the 1970s with the work of Bandler and Grinder.

History and method

Bandler and Grinder claimed to have "modelled" the techniques of prominent therapists Fritz Perls (gestalt therapy), Virginia Satir (family therapy), and Milton H. Erickson (hypnotherapy). NLP techniques include rapport building, anchoring, reframing, the meta model of language, and attention to "representational systems" — the idea that people experience the world primarily through one sense modality, supposedly revealed by their word choices and eye movements.

Scientific assessment

There is no scientific evidence supporting the claims made by NLP advocates, and it has been called a pseudoscience. Scientific reviews have shown that NLP contains numerous factual errors and fails to produce results asserted by proponents. A 2012 systematic review concluded that there is little evidence that NLP interventions improve health-related outcomes, and surveys of academics rank it among discredited therapies. It nevertheless remains popular in self-help, management training, coaching, and sales contexts.
