The Bersaglieri (Italian for "sharpshooters" or "marksmen") are a corps of light infantry in the Italian Army, originally created by General Alessandro Ferrero La Marmora on 18 June 1836 in the army of the Kingdom of Sardinia. They are famous for their distinctive wide-brimmed hat decorated with black capercaillie feathers, worn even with modern helmets, and for their tradition of moving at a jog — Bersaglieri units traditionally run, rather than march, on parade, accompanied by their regimental brass bands.

History

Conceived as fast-moving skirmishers and marksmen — the name derives from bersaglio, "target" — the Bersaglieri fought in the Italian wars of independence. Their most celebrated moment came on 20 September 1870, when Bersaglieri of the Royal Italian Army were the first troops through the breach at Porta Pia, completing the capture of Rome and the unification of Italy. They served extensively in both world wars, on the Italian Front, in North and East Africa, and on the Eastern Front.

Modern role

Today's Bersaglieri regiments serve as mechanised infantry, equipped with armoured fighting vehicles, within brigades such as the Bersaglieri Brigade "Garibaldi" and the Mechanized Brigade "Aosta". They have deployed on peacekeeping and combat operations in Lebanon, Somalia, the Balkans, Iraq, and Afghanistan.

Traditions

The corps' feathered hat (vai­ra or moretto), the crimson flame-shaped collar patches, the running step, and the fanfare bands are enduring symbols. The Bersaglieri have no motto in the usual sense; their war cry is "Hurrah!" and their patron is celebrated each 18 June, the corps' foundation day.
