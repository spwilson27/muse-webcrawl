Frank Marshall (filmmaker)

SpouseKathleen Kennedy

Frank Wilton Marshall (born September 13, 1946) is an American film producer and director. He is one of the most commercially successful producers in Hollywood history, frequently collaborating with Steven Spielberg, and is the co-founder, with his wife Kathleen Kennedy, of The Kennedy/Marshall Company.

Career

Marshall began in the film industry working for director Peter Bogdanovich, appearing in small acting roles and serving in various production capacities on films such as The Last Picture Show (1971) and What's Up, Doc? (1972). He first worked with Spielberg on Raiders of the Lost Ark (1981), which earned him an Academy Award nomination for Best Picture.

In 1981 Marshall co-founded Amblin Entertainment with Spielberg and Kathleen Kennedy. His producing credits include the Indiana Jones films, Poltergeist (1982), Gremlins (1984), The Color Purple (1985), Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988), the Back to the Future trilogy, The Sixth Sense (1999), Seabiscuit (2003), the Bourne series, The Curious Case of Benjamin Button (2008), Jurassic World (2015) and its sequels, and The Bee Gees: How Can You Mend a Broken Heart (2020). Films he has produced have collectively grossed billions of dollars worldwide.

Directing

As a director, Marshall helmed Arachnophobia (1990), Alive (1993), Congo (1995), and the Antarctic adventure Eight Below (2006), as well as the ESPN documentary Right to Play.

Honors

Marshall has received multiple Academy Award nominations for Best Picture. In 2018, he and Kennedy received the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences. Marshall is also an accomplished athlete and has served in leadership roles with U.S. Olympic organizations.
