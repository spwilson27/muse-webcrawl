Hudson Valley

The Hudson Valley (also known as the Hudson River Valley) comprises the valley of the Hudson River and its adjacent communities in the U.S. state of New York, from the region of Albany southward to Yonkers at the edge of New York City.

Geography

The valley is carved by the Hudson River through the Appalachian ridges, most dramatically at the Hudson Highlands, where the river passes between steep hills near West Point and Bear Mountain. The region includes counties such as Orange, Dutchess, Ulster, Putnam, Rockland, Columbia, and Greene.

History

Explored by Henry Hudson in 1609, the valley became part of New Netherland with Dutch patroon estates along its banks, later passing to English control. It was strategically contested during the American Revolutionary War (the battles of Saratoga and the post at West Point). In the 19th century the Hudson River School of painters celebrated its landscapes, and great estates lined the eastern bank.

Today

The Hudson Valley is known for agriculture and farm-to-table food culture, historic sites such as the Franklin D. Roosevelt home at Hyde Park and the Culinary Institute of America, and growing communities of artists and commuters connected to New York City by Metro-North Railroad.
