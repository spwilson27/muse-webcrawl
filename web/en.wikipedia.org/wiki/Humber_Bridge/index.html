The Humber Bridge is a 2.22 km (2,430 yd; 7,300 ft; 1.38 mi) single-span road suspension bridge near Kingston upon Hull, East Riding of Yorkshire, England. When it opened to traffic on 24 June 1981, it was the longest of its type in the world; the main span of 1,410 m (4,630 ft) was not surpassed until 1998, with the completion of the Akashi Kaikyō Bridge in Japan. As of 2024 it remains the world's twelfth-longest single-span suspension bridge.

The Humber Bridge spans the Humber Estuary between Hessle and Barton-upon-Humber.

History

Plans for a bridge over the Humber had been discussed since the 19th century, as the estuary formed a barrier to trade and development between the East Riding of Yorkshire on the north bank and Lincolnshire on the south. The Humber Bridge Act was passed in 1959, but construction did not begin until 1972, under the Humber Bridge Board. The bridge was designed by Freeman Fox & Partners, with Sir Ralph Freeman and Dr Gilbert Roberts among the principal engineers; the consulting architect was R. E. Slater. The towers are 155.5 m (510 ft) tall and support two main cables from which the deck is suspended.

Structure

The bridge consists of a central span of 1,410 m, a north side span of 280 m, and a south side span of 530 m. Unusually, the towers lean toward each other slightly because of the curvature of the Earth: the tops of the towers are about 36 mm further apart than the bases. The deck carries the dual-carriageway A15, with a footpath and cycleway on each side.

Tolls and debt

Construction cost around £98 million, financed by loans whose interest accumulated to a debt of over £439 million by the 2010s. Following campaigns by local businesses and politicians, tolls were halved in 2012. The bridge was granted Grade I listed status in 2017.
