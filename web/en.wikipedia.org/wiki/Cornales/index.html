Cornales

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Clade: Angiosperms
Clade: Eudicots
Clade: Asterids
Order: Cornales

The Cornales are an order of flowering plants, basal among the asterids, containing about 600 species in roughly six families. The order includes the dogwoods (Cornaceae), hydrangeas (Hydrangeaceae) and loasas (Loasaceae), and its members are found on all continents except Antarctica.

Description

Members of the Cornales are mostly shrubs and trees, though some are perennial herbs. Common features include often four- or five-merous flowers, an inferior ovary in many groups, and drupe-like fruits. The order is notable for containing the Hydrangeaceae, popular ornamentals, and the Cornaceae, which include the showy flowering dogwoods of North America and Asia.

Classification

In the APG IV system, the Cornales comprise the families Cornaceae (including the former Nyssaceae in some treatments), Curtisiaceae, Grubbiaceae, Hydrangeaceae, Hydrostachyaceae and Loasaceae. Molecular phylogenetic studies place the Cornales as the sister group to all other asterids, making it one of the earliest-diverging lineages of that huge clade.

Fossil record

The order has a record extending to the Late Cretaceous; fossil fruits assigned to the Cornaceae and related families are known from Paleogene deposits of the Northern Hemisphere, indicating the group diversified early in asterid history.
