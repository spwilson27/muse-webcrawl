Nevşehirli Damat Ibrahim Pasha (1666 – October 1, 1730) was an Ottoman statesman who served as Grand Vizier of the Ottoman Empire under Sultan Ahmed III from 1718 to 1730, a period known in Turkish history as the Tulip Period (Lâle Devri). His byname Damat ("bridegroom") referred to his marriage to Fatma Sultan, the sultan's daughter.

Grand vizierate

Born in the village of Muşkara in Cappadocia—which he later rebuilt and renamed Nevşehir ("New City")—Ibrahim Pasha rose through palace service to become grand vizier in 1718. His tenure began with the Treaty of Passarowitz, which ended war with Austria and Venice and opened a period of relative peace. He promoted diplomacy with Europe, sent ambassadors to Vienna and Paris, encouraged the first Muslim-run Turkish printing press of İbrahim Müteferrika (1727), and presided over a court culture of lavish gardens, tulip cultivation, and palace building at Sa'dabad.

Downfall

The era ended in 1730 with the Patrona Halil rebellion, a revolt of janissaries and city crowds angered by taxation and elite luxury. Ahmed III was forced to surrender Ibrahim Pasha, who was executed; the sultan himself was soon deposed.
