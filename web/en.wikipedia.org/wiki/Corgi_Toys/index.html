Corgi Toys

Industry: Toys (die-cast models)

Founded: 1956

Founder: Mettoy (Philip Ullmann)

Headquarters: Swansea, Wales (original)

Parent: Hornby plc (since 2008)

Corgi Toys Limited is a British manufacturer of die-cast scale model vehicles, founded in Swansea, Wales, in 1956 by the toy company Mettoy. The brand — named for the Welsh Corgi dog breed — became one of the world's best-known model car marques, rivaling Dinky Toys and later Hot Wheels, and remains in production under Hornby ownership.

History

Mettoy had manufactured pressed-tin toys at Swansea since the 1930s. Launching Corgi in July 1956, it distinguished its models with plastic glazing — marketed as "the ones with windows" — at a time when rival Dinky models had open apertures. Early releases modelled contemporary British cars such as the Ford Consul, Austin A40, and Morris Cowley in approximately 1:43 scale. Rapid innovation followed: the 1960s "Golden Jacks" with removable wheels, opening features ("Corgi Majors" for larger vehicles), and jewelled headlights.

Corgi's greatest commercial successes were its film and television tie-ins: the James Bond Aston Martin DB5 from Goldfinger (1965), complete with ejector seat, was the best-selling toy car of its era; other famous issues include the Batmobile, the Green Hornet's Black Beauty, the Monkeemobile, Chitty Chitty Bang Bang, the Lotus Formula 1 cars, and the Beatles' Yellow Submarine. In 1969 the brand expanded into the United States.

Decline and revival

Competition from Mattel's Hot Wheels (from 1968) with their low-friction axles hurt sales, and a factory fire in 1974 compounded difficulties. Mettoy went into receivership in 1983; Corgi was revived through a management buyout in 1984, passed to Mattel in 1989, and became independent again in 1995. In 2008 the brand was acquired by Hornby plc, which continues to issue models aimed principally at the adult collector market, alongside the aviation range The Aviation Archive and detailed 1:50-scale truck models.

Collecting

Vintage Corgi models — particularly early windowed issues in their original pictorial boxes and the film tie-ins — are widely collected, and reference works cataloguing variations of colour, casting, and packaging are extensive. The Corgi name also survives on model buses in the Original Omnibus Company range.
