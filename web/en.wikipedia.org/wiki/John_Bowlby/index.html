John Bowlby

Edward John Mostyn Bowlby (26 February 1907 – 2 September 1990) was a British psychologist, psychiatrist, and psychoanalyst, notable for his interest in child development and for his pioneering work in attachment theory. His WHO report Maternal Care and Mental Health (1951) and trilogy Attachment and Loss (1969–1980) transformed understanding of early bonds and separation.

Life

Born into an upper-middle-class London family and raised largely by a nanny, Bowlby studied at Trinity College, Cambridge, worked with delinquent and maladjusted children, then trained in medicine and psychoanalysis. He served in the Royal Army Medical Corps during the Second World War and later directed the children and parents department at the Tavistock Clinic.

Attachment theory

Drawing on ethology (Konrad Lorenz, Harry Harlow) and evolutionary theory, Bowlby argued infants have an innate need to form a close bond with a primary caregiver, and that prolonged separation or deprivation can cause anxiety, anger, depression, and later difficulties. His colleague Mary Ainsworth developed the Strange Situation procedure and attachment patterns. Critics debated monotropy and maternal deprivation claims, leading to more nuanced views of multiple attachments and resilience.
