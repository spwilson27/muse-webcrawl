Blue Mountains (Jamaica)

Blue Mountains
CountryJamaica
Highest pointBlue Mountain Peak, 2,256 m (7,402 ft)

Protected areaBlue and John Crow Mountains National Park (UNESCO World Heritage Site, 2015)

The Blue Mountains are the longest mountain range in Jamaica, spanning the eastern parishes of Portland, St Thomas, St Andrew and St Mary. They include the island's highest point, Blue Mountain Peak at 2,256 metres, from which, on a clear day, Cuba can reportedly be seen. The range takes its name from the blue haze that often shrouds its forested ridges.

Environment

The mountains rise steeply from the coast and capture trade-wind moisture, making their northern slopes among the wettest places in the Caribbean. They support lush montane rainforest with tree ferns, bromeliads and endemic species, and were historically a refuge for Maroon communities of escaped enslaved Africans, whose cultural heritage, together with the biodiversity, earned UNESCO World Heritage listing in 2015.

Coffee

The cool, misty climate at elevations between roughly 900 and 1,700 metres produces Jamaica Blue Mountain Coffee, one of the world's most expensive and sought-after coffees, known for its mild flavour and lack of bitterness. Most production is exported, much of it to Japan, and the name is protected by certification.
