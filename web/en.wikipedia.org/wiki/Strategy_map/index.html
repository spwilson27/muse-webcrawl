Strategy map

A strategy map is a diagram that documents the strategic goals being pursued by an organisation. It is a central element of the balanced scorecard framework developed by Robert S. Kaplan and David P. Norton, showing cause-and-effect relationships among objectives across four perspectives: financial, customer, internal processes, and learning and growth.

A generic strategy map structured around the four balanced-scorecard perspectives.

Structure

In the classic template the map is read from the bottom up:

  • Learning and growth — human capital, information capital, and organisational capital objectives (skills, culture, systems).
  • Internal processes — objectives for operations management, customer management, innovation, and regulatory/social processes.
  • Customer — the value proposition offered to customers: product/service attributes, relationship, and image.
  • Financial — long-term shareholder value, achieved through revenue growth and productivity.

Arrows between objectives express hypotheses: improving employee capabilities improves process quality, which improves customer outcomes, which in turn drives financial results. Each objective on the map is associated with measures, targets, and initiatives on the corresponding balanced scorecard.

Development

Kaplan and Norton introduced strategy maps in articles in the 1990s and in their 2004 book Strategy Maps: Converting Intangible Assets into Tangible Outcomes. Templates differ for private-sector, public-sector, and non-profit organisations, where mission outcomes typically sit at the top rather than financial results.
