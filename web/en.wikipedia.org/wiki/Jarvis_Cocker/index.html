Jarvis Cocker

Image placeholder: Jarvis Cocker

Jarvis Branson Cocker (born 19 September 1963) is an English musician, singer-songwriter, DJ, and broadcaster. He is best known as the founder, frontman, lyricist, and only consistent member of the band Pulp, which he formed in Sheffield in 1978. As the lyricist and primary songwriter of the group he became a figurehead of the Britpop genre of the mid-1990s following the success of the album Different Class (1995), which included the hit single "Common People".

Career

Cocker formed Pulp (originally Arabacus Pulp) while at school in Sheffield. After more than a decade of obscurity, the band rose to fame during the Britpop era with the albums His 'n' Hers (1994) and Different Class (1995). At the 1996 Brit Awards, Cocker famously invaded the stage during Michael Jackson's performance in protest.

After Pulp's hiatus, Cocker released solo albums Jarvis (2006) and Further Complications (2009), recorded with Steve Albini. He has presented the BBC Radio 6 Music programme Jarvis Cocker's Sunday Service and collaborated with artists including Chilly Gonzales on Room 29 (2017). His project Jarv Is released the album Beyond the Pale in 2020. In 2023 Pulp reunited for a series of concerts.

Personal life

Cocker was born in Sheffield and studied fine art and film at Central Saint Martins in London. His memoir and songbook Good Pop, Bad Pop was published in 2022.
