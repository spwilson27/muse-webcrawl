Neil Percival Young (born 12 November 1945) is a Canadian and American singer-songwriter and musician. After beginning his career in Winnipeg in the 1960s, Young moved to Los Angeles, joining Buffalo Springfield with Stephen Stills, Richie Furay and others. His solo career and his work with Crazy Horse and as a member of Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young have made him one of the most influential figures in rock music.

Early life

Young was born in Toronto, Ontario, to journalist and sportswriter Scott Young and Edna "Rassy" Young. He spent part of his childhood in Omemee, Ontario, and later Winnipeg, Manitoba, where he formed his first bands, including the Squires. A childhood bout of polio left lasting effects, and he later lived with epilepsy and type 1 diabetes.

Career

Young's early solo albums Everybody Knows This Is Nowhere (1969), with Crazy Horse, and After the Gold Rush (1970) established his reputation. Harvest (1972), containing the hit "Heart of Gold," became the best-selling album in the United States that year. The darker "Ditch Trilogy" — Time Fades Away, On the Beach, and Tonight's the Night — followed, alongside the acclaimed Zuma (1975) and Rust Never Sleeps (1979), whose song "Hey Hey, My My (Into the Black)" exemplified his harder-edged sound with Crazy Horse.

Through the 1980s Young experimented widely — electronic music on Trans, rockabilly, country, and blues — before returning to prominence with Freedom (1989), featuring "Rockin' in the Free World," and Harvest Moon (1992). He has remained prolific into the 21st century, recording dozens of studio albums and curating the Neil Young Archives.

Musical style and legacy

Young is noted for his personal, often politically charged lyrics, his high tenor voice, and his heavily distorted electric guitar work — particularly on his 1953 Gibson Les Paul, "Old Black" — contrasted with gentle acoustic folk and country material. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest songwriters of the rock era and has been inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice: as a solo artist (1995) and as a member of Buffalo Springfield (1997). His influence on grunge earned him the nickname "Godfather of Grunge."

Activism and other ventures

Young co-founded the Farm Aid benefit concerts and the Bridge School for children with severe speech and physical impairments. He has been an outspoken environmentalist and critic of corporate agriculture and the music industry's compressed audio formats, launching the Pono high-resolution audio player in 2014.
