Barbara Dana Broccoli CBE (born 18 June 1960) is an American-British film producer best known for her work on the James Bond film series through Eon Productions, the company founded by her father, Albert R. "Cubby" Broccoli, and Harry Saltzman. Together with her half-brother Michael G. Wilson, she controlled the creative direction of the franchise for three decades.

Early life

Broccoli was born in Los Angeles, California, the daughter of Albert R. Broccoli and actress Dana Wilson (née Natol). She studied film and television communications at Loyola Marymount University. She began working in the Eon Productions publicity department as a teenager on The Spy Who Loved Me (1977) and progressed through production roles on the Bond films of the 1980s, becoming an associate producer on The Living Daylights (1987) and Licence to Kill (1989).

James Bond

Following her father's death in 1996, Broccoli and Wilson assumed full control of the series, beginning with GoldenEye (1995), which relaunched Bond with Pierce Brosnan. They oversaw the Brosnan era and then the Daniel Craig era, which began with Casino Royale (2006) and included Skyfall (2012), the highest-grossing film in the series, and concluded with No Time to Die (2021). In 2025, Broccoli and Wilson ceded creative control of the franchise to Amazon MGM Studios, while remaining co-owners of the series' intellectual property.

Other work

Broccoli has produced independent films including Film Stars Don't Die in Liverpool (2017), Nancy (2018), The Rhythm Section (2020) and Till (2022), and stage productions such as Chitty Chitty Bang Bang, Once and Othello. She was made a Commander of the Order of the British Empire (CBE) in the 2022 New Year Honours for services to film, drama and philanthropy.
