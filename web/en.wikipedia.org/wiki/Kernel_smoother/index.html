Kernel smoother

A kernel smoother is a statistical technique for estimating a real-valued function as a weighted average of neighbouring observed data, where the weights are defined by a kernel function. It is a core method of non-parametric regression.

Nadaraya–Watson estimator

The best-known kernel smoother, proposed independently by Nadaraya and Watson in 1964, estimates the regression function m(x) as a locally weighted average of the response values, with weights given by a kernel centred at each observation and scaled by a bandwidth. It can suffer from bias at the boundaries of the data.

Local polynomial variants

Local linear and local polynomial smoothers fit a low-degree polynomial within each kernel window, reducing boundary bias. Bandwidth selection — by cross-validation or plug-in rules — governs the bias–variance trade-off of all kernel smoothers.
