The Orion Molecular Cloud Complex (or, simply, the Orion Complex) is a large group of bright nebulae, dark clouds, and young stars in the constellation of Orion. The cloud is between 1,300 and 1,600 light years away, and hundreds of light years across. Several parts of the nebula can be observed through binoculars and small telescopes, and some parts (such as the Orion Nebula) are visible to the naked eye.

The nebula is important because of its sheer size, spanning several degrees from Orion's Belt to his sword. It is also one of the most active regions of stellar formation visible from Earth, being rich in protoplanetary discs and young stars. The nebula is bright in infrared wavelengths due to the heat-intensive processes involved in star formation, though the path of the nebula is complicated by dark absorption clouds and emission from ionized gas.

Constituent nebulae and clouds

The complex is dominated by two giant molecular clouds, Orion A and Orion B. Orion A includes the Orion Nebula (M42), the brightest part of the complex, together with M43 and the Running Man Nebula, and stretches southward along Orion's sword. Orion B, to the east of the Belt stars, contains the Flame Nebula and the famous Horsehead Nebula, a dark cloud silhouetted against the emission nebula IC 434. The enormous arc of Barnard's Loop, probably the remnant of a supernova explosion about two million years old, encloses much of the complex, and the Lambda Orionis ring surrounds the head of Orion.

Star formation

The Orion Complex is the nearest region of massive star formation to Earth and serves as a laboratory for understanding how stars and planetary systems are born. The Trapezium Cluster at the heart of the Orion Nebula contains hot, massive young stars whose ultraviolet radiation ionizes the surrounding gas. Observations by the Hubble Space Telescope revealed numerous proplyds — circumstellar discs around infant stars being photoevaporated by the Trapezium's radiation. Star formation in the complex appears to have proceeded in waves over the past roughly 10–12 million years, with the oldest populations near the Belt and the youngest in the Orion Nebula itself.
