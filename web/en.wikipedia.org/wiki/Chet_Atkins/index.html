Overview

Chester Burton 'Chet' Atkins (20 June 1924 – 30 June 2001) was an American guitarist and record producer who, along with Owen Bradley, created the smoother country music style known as the Nashville sound, broadening country music's appeal to adult pop audiences. Often called 'Mr. Guitar' and 'The Country Gentleman', he is widely regarded as one of the most influential guitarists of the twentieth century.

Career

Atkins developed a fingerstyle technique — playing melody, bass and rhythm simultaneously — influenced by Merle Travis. As an RCA Victor producer and executive from the late 1950s, he produced records for Elvis Presley, the Everly Brothers, Jim Reeves, Dolly Parton and many others, replacing honky-tonk fiddles and steel guitar with string sections and background choruses. His own recordings, including 'Yakety Axe' and collaborations with Mark Knopfler (Neck and Neck, 1990), demonstrated wide stylistic range.

Honours

Atkins won 14 Grammy Awards, including a Lifetime Achievement Award, was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (posthumously, 2002) and the Country Music Hall of Fame, and received nine Country Music Association Instrumentalist of the Year awards. The Gretsch company issued Chet Atkins signature guitars, including the Country Gentleman model.
