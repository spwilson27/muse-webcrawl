ResultAllied victory; Armistice of 11 November 1918

The Western Front was the main theatre of war during the First World War. Following the outbreak of war in August 1914, the German Army opened the Western Front by invading Luxembourg and Belgium, then gaining control of important industrial regions in France. The advance was halted at the First Battle of the Marne, and both sides dug in along a meandering line of trenches that stretched from the North Sea through Flanders and northern France to the Swiss frontier — a line that changed little for the next four years despite enormous casualties.

Trench warfare

The front became synonymous with attritional trench warfare: barbed wire, machine guns, poison gas and massed artillery made defence dominant, and offensives such as Verdun and the Somme (1916) and Passchendaele (1917) each cost hundreds of thousands of casualties for minimal gains. New weapons — tanks, aircraft and infiltration tactics — gradually developed to break the deadlock.

End of the war

Germany's Spring Offensive of 1918, a last attempt to win before American manpower arrived in force, ultimately failed; the Allied Hundred Days Offensive from August 1918 drove the German army back until the Armistice ended the fighting. More than nine million soldiers were killed or wounded on the Western Front; its cemeteries and memorials still mark the landscape of Belgium and northern France.
