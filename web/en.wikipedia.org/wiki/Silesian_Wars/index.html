Silesian Wars

The Silesian Wars (German: Schlesische Kriege) were three wars fought in the mid-18th century between Prussia (under King Frederick the Great) and Austria (under Archduchess Maria Theresa) for control of the Central European region of Silesia (now in south-western Poland). The First (1740–1742) and Second (1744–1745) Silesian Wars were parts of the wider War of the Austrian Succession, while the Third (1756–1763) was part of the Seven Years' War. All three ended with Prussia retaining Silesia, a result that established Prussia as a great power and began the long Austro-Prussian rivalry for mastery of Germany.

First Silesian War (1740–1742)

Taking advantage of the disputed Austrian succession after the death of Emperor Charles VI, Frederick invaded Silesia in December 1740, citing old Brandenburg claims. Prussian victories at Mollwitz (1741) and Chotusitz (1742) forced Maria Theresa to cede most of Silesia by the Treaty of Breslau (1742).

Second Silesian War (1744–1745)

Fearing Austria would recover the province, Frederick invaded Bohemia in 1744. Prussian victories at Hohenfriedberg, Soor and Kesselsdorf led to the Treaty of Dresden (December 1745), which confirmed Prussian possession of Silesia in exchange for Frederick's recognition of Maria Theresa's husband, Francis Stephen, as Holy Roman Emperor.

Third Silesian War (1756–1763)

The third war formed the European theatre of the Seven Years' War. Frederick struck first, invading Saxony in 1756, and won famous victories at Rossbach and Leuthen (1757) but suffered crushing defeats at Kolin (1757) and Kunersdorf (1759) against Austria and Russia. Prussia was saved by the death of Empress Elizabeth of Russia in 1762 (the "Miracle of the House of Brandenburg"). The Treaty of Hubertusburg (1763) restored the status quo ante, leaving Silesia Prussian.

Legacy

The wars made Prussia the fifth European great power, roughly doubled its population and resources, and entrenched dualism between Berlin and Vienna that lasted until 1866.
