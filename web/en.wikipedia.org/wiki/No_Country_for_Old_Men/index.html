No Country for Old Men

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Theatrical release poster (2007)

No Country for Old Men is a 2007 American neo-Western crime thriller film written and directed by Joel and Ethan Coen, adapted from Cormac McCarthy's 2005 novel of the same name. It stars Tommy Lee Jones as Sheriff Ed Tom Bell, Javier Bardem as the hitman Anton Chigurh, and Josh Brolin as Llewelyn Moss, a hunter who stumbles upon $2 million at the scene of a drug deal gone wrong on the Texas–Mexico border.

Plot

In 1980 West Texas, Moss discovers the aftermath of a shootout between drug dealers and takes a satchel of cash, setting off a chain of violence as cartel enforcers and the implacable Chigurh — who decides victims' fates with a coin toss and kills with a captive bolt pistol — pursue him. Sheriff Bell, nearing retirement, trails the case and reflects on a world he increasingly fails to understand.

Reception

The film premiered at Cannes and was a critical and commercial success, grossing over $170 million worldwide. At the 80th Academy Awards it won four Oscars, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay, and Best Supporting Actor for Bardem, whose portrayal of Chigurh is frequently ranked among the greatest screen villains. The title derives from the opening line of W. B. Yeats's poem "Sailing to Byzantium".

Themes

Critics have emphasised the film's treatment of fate and chance, the erosion of an older moral order, and its subversion of thriller conventions — key confrontations occur off-screen, and the expected showdown between hero and villain never takes place.
