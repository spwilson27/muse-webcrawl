The Detroit Tigers are an American professional baseball team based in Detroit, Michigan. They compete in Major League Baseball as a member of the American League Central Division. One of the American League's eight charter franchises, the club was founded in 1894 as a member of the Western League and joined the American League at its creation in 1901.

History

The Tigers won their first American League pennants in 1907, 1908 and 1909 behind outfielder Ty Cobb, one of the greatest players of the era, but lost all three World Series. The franchise won its first championship in 1935, defeating the Chicago Cubs, and again in 1945. The 1968 team, led by pitcher Denny McLain and outfielder Al Kaline, beat the St. Louis Cardinals in a seven-game World Series. The 1984 Tigers, managed by Sparky Anderson, started the season 35–5 and defeated the San Diego Padres to win the franchise's fourth title. The Tigers returned to the World Series in 2006 and 2012, losing both.

Ballparks

The team played at Tiger Stadium (opened as Navin Field in 1912) for most of the twentieth century before moving to Comerica Park in downtown Detroit in 2000.

Notable players

Hall of Famers associated with the Tigers include Ty Cobb, Hank Greenberg, Al Kaline, Charlie Gehringer, Hal Newhouser and Jack Morris; more recent stars include Miguel Cabrera, winner of the American League Triple Crown in 2012, and pitcher Justin Verlander.
