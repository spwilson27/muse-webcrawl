Rotating building

A rotating building is a structure designed to rotate, either in whole or in part, around a central core. Rotation may optimise views and sunlight, generate a changing architectural form, or serve a functional purpose such as in revolving restaurants.

Examples

Early examples include revolving houses proposed in the early 20th century. The Suite Vollard in Curitiba, Brazil (2001), has independently rotating floors. The proposed Dynamic Tower in Dubai, designed by David Fisher, envisioned each floor rotating independently. Many towers feature a revolving restaurant at the top, such as the Space Needle in Seattle.

Engineering

Rotating buildings require a stationary core for elevators, stairs and utilities, with flexible connections for water, sewage and electricity. Rotation is typically slow — one revolution in one to several hours — driven by electric motors.
