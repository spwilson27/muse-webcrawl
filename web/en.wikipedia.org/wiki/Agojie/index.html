The Agojie (Fon: Mino, "our mothers"), known to Europeans as the Dahomey Amazons, were an all-female military regiment of the Kingdom of Dahomey, in present-day Benin. They served as the king's bodyguards and as front-line troops from the reign of King Houegbadja (c. 1645–1685) until the kingdom's defeat by France in the Second Franco-Dahomean War, and were formally disbanded in 1904.

Organization and training

The Mino were recruited from enslaved women, war captives, criminals, and volunteers. They swore celibacy, trained with muskets, machetes, and clubs, and underwent brutal exercises to build aggression and endurance. Units included elephant huntresses, riflewomen, and razor-women armed with folding blades. At their height in the mid-19th century under King Ghezo, they may have numbered between 1,000 and 6,000.

Warfare

The Agojie fought in Dahomey's expansionist wars, including the attacks on Abeokuta in 1851 and 1864 against the Egba people. European visitors such as Richard Burton recorded both their discipline and their ferocity. In the Franco-Dahomean wars (1890–1894) they fought the French with courage but were outmatched by modern artillery and machine guns.

Legacy

The Agojie have become symbols of female military power, inspiring the Dora Milaje of Marvel Comics and the 2022 film The Woman King, starring Viola Davis as a general modeled partly on the warrior Nanisca.
