Hyperboloid

In geometry, a hyperboloid of revolution is a quadric surface obtained by rotating a hyperbola about one of its principal axes. Hyperboloids come in two varieties: the hyperboloid of one sheet, a connected surface, and the hyperboloid of two sheets, which has two separate components.

Equations

A hyperboloid of one sheet centred at the origin satisfies x²/a² + y²/b² − z²/c² = 1, while a hyperboloid of two sheets satisfies x²/a² + y²/b² − z²/c² = −1. When a = b the surface is a surface of revolution. The one-sheeted hyperboloid is a doubly ruled surface: through every point on it pass two straight lines lying entirely in the surface, a property shared with the hyperbolic paraboloid.

Applications

The ruled structure of the one-sheeted hyperboloid makes it valuable in architecture: it can be constructed from straight beams while achieving great strength, as in the hyperboloid cooling towers of power stations and Vladimir Shukhov's pioneering lattice towers in Russia, beginning with the Shukhov Tower in Moscow (1922). Hyperboloid geometry also appears in special relativity, where the set of four-velocities forms a hyperboloid in Minkowski space, and in the design of certain gears and reflectors.
