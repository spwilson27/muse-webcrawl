Japanese battleship Mikasa

Mikasa (三笠) is a pre-dreadnought battleship built for the Imperial Japanese Navy in the United Kingdom in the late 1890s. Named after Mount Mikasa in Nara, she served as the flagship of Admiral Tōgō Heihachirō at the Battle of Tsushima in 1905, one of the most decisive naval battles in history. She is the only surviving example of a pre-dreadnought battleship and is preserved as a museum ship at Yokosuka.

Design

Ordered under the 1896 Ten Year Naval Expansion Programme and built by Vickers at Barrow-in-Furness, Mikasa displaced about 15,140 long tons and was armed with four 12-inch guns in twin turrets, fourteen 6-inch guns and extensive armour using Krupp cemented steel. She was delivered in 1902.

Service

During the Russo-Japanese War Mikasa fought at the Battle of Port Arthur, the Battle of the Yellow Sea, and at Tsushima on 27 May 1905, where Tōgō's fleet annihilated the Russian Baltic Fleet. Days after the war ended she sank at her moorings after a magazine fire and explosion killed 251 men; she was refloated, repaired and served through World War I. Under the Washington Naval Treaty she was decommissioned in 1923 but preserved, encased in concrete at Yokosuka. Restored after deterioration during the post-1945 occupation, she reopened as a museum in 1961.
