United Launch Alliance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

United Launch Alliance
Founded1 December 2006 (Boeing + Lockheed Martin joint venture)
HeadquartersCentennial, Colorado
VehiclesAtlas V, Delta IV (retired), Vulcan Centaur
Vulcan at SLC-41.

United Launch Alliance (ULA) is an American launch services provider formed in 2006 as a joint venture of Boeing and Lockheed Martin, merging their Delta and Atlas rocket businesses to guarantee the US government assured access to space. For over a decade ULA's rockets — Atlas V and the Delta IV family — held a near-monopoly on American national-security launches, compiling a perfect mission-success record across more than 150 flights.

The arrival of SpaceX competition, and political pressure over Atlas V's Russian RD-180 engines, pushed ULA to develop the Vulcan Centaur around Blue Origin's BE-4 engine; Vulcan flew successfully in January 2024 and began national-security certification flights. ULA's parents have periodically explored selling the company, without a completed deal.

Style

Under longtime CEO Tory Bruno, ULA presented itself as the reliability-first incumbent — 'the most experienced, most reliable' — against SpaceX's disruption, a rivalry that defined 2010s launch politics and forced prices down across the industry.
