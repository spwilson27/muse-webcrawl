Joubert syndrome

Joubert syndrome is a rare autosomal recessive genetic disorder that affects development of the cerebellar vermis and the brain stem. Its diagnostic hallmark on brain MRI is the "molar tooth sign", formed by thickened superior cerebellar peduncles and a deep interpeduncular fossa. The condition is a ciliopathy, caused by mutations in genes encoding proteins of the primary cilium.

Signs and symptoms

Infants typically present with hypotonia, abnormal breathing patterns (episodes of hyperpnea or apnea), abnormal eye movements and developmental delay. Ataxia and intellectual disability of varying severity are common. Some patients have additional features such as polydactyly, retinal dystrophy, kidney disease (nephronophthisis) or liver fibrosis; these overlapping presentations are grouped as Joubert syndrome and related disorders.

Genetics and diagnosis

More than thirty genes have been implicated, including INPP5E, CEP290, AHI1 and TMEM67. Diagnosis rests on the combination of clinical features and the molar tooth sign on axial MRI; genetic testing can confirm the subtype and inform recurrence risk.

Management

There is no cure; treatment is supportive and multidisciplinary, including developmental therapies, monitoring of breathing in infancy, and surveillance of the eyes, kidneys and liver.
