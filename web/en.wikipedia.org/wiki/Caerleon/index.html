Caerleon

Caerleon (Welsh: Caerllion) is a town and community in Newport, Wales. Situated on the River Usk, it lies 5 miles (8 km) northeast of Newport city centre and is a historic site of considerable archaeological importance. It was the location of the Roman legionary fortress of Isca Augusta and features one of the best-preserved Roman amphitheatres in Britain. Caerleon also has associations with the legends of King Arthur.

Roman Isca

Isca was founded about AD 74–75 as the permanent fortress of Legio II Augusta during the Roman conquest of Wales. The fortress covered some 50 acres and included barracks, a headquarters building, baths, and an amphitheatre seating around 6,000 spectators, used for military training, gladiatorial shows, and wild beast displays. Substantial remains survive, including the amphitheatre, parts of the fortress baths (now housing the National Roman Legion Museum), and sections of the fortress walls. The legion remained at Isca, with interruptions, until the late 3rd century.

Arthurian associations

Geoffrey of Monmouth located King Arthur's court at Caerleon in his 12th-century Historia Regum Britanniae, and later writers, including Alfred, Lord Tennyson, who wrote part of Idylls of the King while staying in the town, reinforced the connection. The Welsh name means "fortress of the legion".

Later history

A Norman castle was built at Caerleon in the late 11th century, and the town became a small port on the Usk. The medieval church of St Cadoc stands within the fortress area. In the 19th century Caerleon was a centre of the tinplate industry. Today it is largely a commuter settlement for Newport, and a campus of the University of South Wales was formerly located there.
