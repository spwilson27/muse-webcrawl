Russian oligarch

Russian oligarchs are business oligarchs of the former Soviet republics who rapidly accumulated wealth in the 1990s during the era of Russian privatization in the aftermath of the dissolution of the Soviet Union. The term derives from the Ancient Greek oligarkhia, meaning "the rule of the few".

The failing Soviet state left the ownership of state assets contested, which allowed for informal deals with former officials as a means to acquire state property. Oligarchs emerged as business magnates during the presidency of Boris Yeltsin, who served from 1991 to 1999. During Yeltsin's presidency, they amassed significant wealth and political influence by acquiring formerly state-owned assets at low prices through government auctions, most notably the controversial "loans-for-shares" program of 1995, in which a small group of bankers lent money to the government in exchange for shares in valuable natural-resource and media companies.

History

During Mikhail Gorbachev's perestroika reforms in the late 1980s, some entrepreneurs were able to accumulate capital through cooperatives and joint ventures. After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, the Russian government under Yegor Gaidar launched mass privatization, issuing vouchers to citizens that could be exchanged for shares in state enterprises. Many vouchers were bought up cheaply by insiders.

Under Vladimir Putin, who became president in 2000, the state moved to rein in the oligarchs' political power. In July 2000, Putin reportedly offered the leading oligarchs a bargain: they could keep their assets if they stayed out of politics. Some, such as Mikhail Khodorkovsky of the Yukos oil company, defied the arrangement and were prosecuted; Khodorkovsky was arrested in 2003 and Yukos was dismantled. Others, including Boris Berezovsky and Vladimir Gusinsky, fled into exile.

Wealth and sectors

Oligarch fortunes were concentrated in oil and gas, metals and mining (aluminium, nickel, steel), banking, and media. Notable figures have included Roman Abramovich, Oleg Deripaska, Mikhail Fridman, Alisher Usmanov, Vladimir Potanin, and Leonid Mikhelson. Many held their assets through offshore structures and spent heavily on luxury property, football clubs, and superyachts abroad, particularly in London — a phenomenon sometimes called "Londongrad".

Sanctions

Following Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014 and especially the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Western governments imposed extensive sanctions on Russian oligarchs, freezing assets, seizing yachts, and restricting travel, on the grounds that many were closely connected to the Kremlin.
