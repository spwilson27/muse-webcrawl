Yosa Buson

Yosa Buson or Yosa no Buson (Japanese: 与謝蕪村; 1716 – January 17, 1784) was a Japanese poet and painter of the Edo period. Along with Matsuo Bashō and Kobayashi Issa, Buson is considered one of the greatest haiku poets, and he was also an accomplished painter in the bunjinga (literati painting) style.

Life

Buson was born in the village of Kema in Settsu Province (now part of Osaka). Orphaned early, he moved to Edo (Tokyo) to study painting and haiku, later travelling in northern Honshu in the footsteps of Bashō. He eventually settled in Kyoto, where he taught poetry, led the revival of the Bashō school through his Yahantei circle, and pursued painting professionally.

Poetry and painting

Buson's haiku are noted for their visual, romantic and sometimes experimental qualities, contrasting with Bashō's spiritual austerity. As a painter he developed a style influenced by Chinese literati painting and the Japanese Nanga school, producing landscapes and haiga (paintings combined with haiku). He was posthumously recognised, with Bashō, as central to the haiku tradition, and his works remain widely anthologised.
