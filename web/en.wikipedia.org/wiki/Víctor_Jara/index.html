Víctor Jara

Born: Víctor Lidio Jara Martínez, 28 September 1932, San Ignacio, Chile

Died: 16 September 1973, Santiago, Chile

Occupations: Singer-songwriter, theatre director, teacher, political activist

Movement: Nueva canción chilena

Víctor Lidio Jara Martínez (28 September 1932 – 16 September 1973) was a Chilean teacher, theatre director, poet, singer-songwriter and communist political activist. He was a pioneer of the nueva canción chilena (new Chilean song) movement, which fused folk music traditions with socially engaged lyrics, and became one of the most prominent artistic supporters of President Salvador Allende and the Popular Unity coalition.

Early life

Jara was born in the rural town of San Ignacio in the Ñuble region to a poor farming family. His father was an illiterate labourer and his mother, Amanda Martínez, was a singer and guitarist who introduced him to Chilean folk music. After his mother's death he studied accounting and then entered a seminary, before leaving to serve in the Chilean army. He subsequently studied theatre at the University of Chile, joining its experimental theatre company and directing notable productions, including works by Maxim Gorky and Samuel Beckett.

Music career

In the 1960s Jara became a central figure of the nueva canción movement alongside Violeta Parra, Patricio Manns and the groups Quilapayún and Inti-Illimani. His songs, often accompanied by his own guitar, dealt with poverty, peasant life and social justice. Among his best-known compositions are "Plegaria a un labrador" ("Prayer to a Labourer"), which won first prize at the first Festival of New Chilean Song in 1969, "Te recuerdo Amanda," "El aparecido," "El derecho de vivir en paz" and "Manifiesto," recorded shortly before his death and released posthumously.

Death

Following the military coup of 11 September 1973 that overthrew Allende, Jara was arrested and taken to the Estadio Chile, a stadium in Santiago converted into a detention and torture centre. He was tortured and murdered; his body, bearing multiple gunshot wounds, was found dumped outside the stadium. He was 40 years old. In 2003 the stadium was renamed Estadio Víctor Jara in his honour. In 2016 a United States jury found former Chilean army officer Pedro Barrientos liable for his torture and murder, and in 2023 Barrientos was arrested in Florida and extradited to Chile to face charges.

Legacy

Jara's music and story became symbols of resistance to the Pinochet dictatorship and of the international human rights movement. His widow Joan Jara founded the Víctor Jara Foundation in Santiago. Artists around the world, including Bruce Springsteen, U2 and Rage Against the Machine, have cited his influence or performed his songs.
