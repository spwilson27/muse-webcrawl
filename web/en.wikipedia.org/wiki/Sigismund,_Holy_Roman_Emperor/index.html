Sigismund of Luxembourg (15 February 1368 – 9 December 1437) was Holy Roman Emperor from 1433 until his death, the last emperor of the House of Luxembourg. The younger son of Charles IV, he was also King of Hungary and Croatia (from 1387), King of Germany (from 1410/1411), King of Bohemia (from 1419, effectively 1436) and a tireless, peripatetic diplomat whose greatest achievement was ending the Western Schism at the Council of Constance — and whose greatest failure was the Hussite Wars ignited by the burning of Jan Hus under his safe conduct.

King of Hungary

Through marriage to Queen Mary of Hungary, Sigismund was crowned king in 1387, then spent years fighting rival claimants, the Angevin Ladislaus of Naples, and the rising power of the Ottoman Turks. His crusade against the Ottomans ended in catastrophe at the Battle of Nicopolis (1396), from which he barely escaped by galley. He founded the Order of the Dragon (1408) to bind his leading barons and defend the kingdom's southern frontier.

Empire and the Council of Constance

Elected King of the Romans in 1410 (confirmed 1411) after the death of Rupert and the deposition dispute with Jobst of Moravia, Sigismund devoted his prestige to healing the forty-year Western Schism of the papacy. He pressured the rival popes to summon the Council of Constance (1414–1418), which deposed or accepted the resignations of the claimants and elected Martin V, restoring a single papal line. His far-reaching European diplomacy — including the Treaty of Canterbury with Henry V of England against France (1416) — made him the arbiter of Europe, though his projected reform of the Empire came to little.

Bohemia and the Hussites

At Constance, Sigismund had guaranteed safe passage to the Bohemian reformer Jan Hus; Hus's execution in 1415 outraged Bohemia. When Sigismund inherited the Bohemian crown from his brother Wenceslaus IV in 1419, the Hussites rejected him. Five crusades against the Hussites were defeated by armies led by Jan Žižka and Prokop the Great. Only after the Church and moderate Hussites agreed the Compacts at the Council of Basel was Sigismund recognised as king in 1436, a year before his death.

Imperial coronation and death

Crowned emperor by Pope Eugene IV in Rome in 1433, Sigismund died at Znojmo in December 1437 while retreating from Bohemia. With no surviving sons, his crowns passed through his daughter Elizabeth to her husband Albert II of Habsburg, inaugurating the Habsburgs' long tenure of the imperial dignity. Sigismund is remembered as an intelligent, energetic but chronically indebted ruler — "above all kings in cunning", in a contemporary phrase — and in Hungary for the long, stabilising second half of his reign.
