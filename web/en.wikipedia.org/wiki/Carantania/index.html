Carantania

Image placeholder: The Prince's Stone (Knežji kamen).

Carantania (Slovene: Karantanija; German: Karantanien) was a Slavic principality that emerged in the second half of the 7th century in the territory of present-day southern Austria and north-eastern Slovenia. It is considered one of the first stable Slavic states and an important part of Slovenian and Austrian historical heritage.

History

Carantania formed after the Slavic settlement of the Eastern Alps, in the vacuum left by the weakening of Avar power. Around the mid-8th century, threatened by the Avars, the Carantanian prince Boruth sought Bavarian help, which led to growing Frankish influence and Christianisation from the Archbishopric of Salzburg. After a failed revolt, Carantania lost its independence: by the early 9th century it had been incorporated into the Frankish (Carolingian) Empire and was later organised as the March of Carinthia.

Society and the Prince's Stone

Carantanian society included a distinctive class of free peasants (kosezi, German Edlinger). The duchy's rulers were installed in a remarkable ceremony, conducted in the Slovene language, centred on the Prince's Stone near Karnburg: a commoner seated on the stone questioned the incoming prince before yielding his place — a ritual of apparently contractual, proto-democratic character that later writers, including Jean Bodin and Thomas Jefferson's contemporaries, found striking. The custom survived for centuries in the installation of Carinthian dukes.
