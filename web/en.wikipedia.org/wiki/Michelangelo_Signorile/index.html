Michelangelo Signorile

Born: 19 December 1960, Brooklyn, New York, U.S.

Occupations: Journalist, author, radio host

Known for: The Michelangelo Signorile Show (SiriusXM); OutWeek; Queer in America

Michelangelo Signorile (/ˌsiːnjəˈriːli/; born December 19, 1960) is an American journalist, author, and radio talk show host. His radio program, The Michelangelo Signorile Show, airs on SiriusXM's progressive talk channel. He has been a prominent figure in American LGBT politics and media since the late 1980s and was editor-at-large of HuffPost's Queer Voices section.

Outing controversy

Signorile came to national attention as a columnist for OutWeek magazine in 1989–1991, where he was a leading voice of the "outing" debate — the practice of revealing the homosexuality of closeted public figures. His 1990 column outing the publishing magnate Malcolm Forbes after Forbes's death intensified the controversy. In his book Queer in America: Sex, the Media, and the Closets of Power (1993), he defended outing powerful closeted figures whose actions harmed gay people, while criticizing the mainstream media's complicity in maintaining the closet. The book is considered a landmark of post-Stonewall gay political writing.

Later work

Signorile's other books include Outing Yourself (1995), Life Outside (1997), Hitting Hard (2005), and It's Not Over (2015), which warned against complacency after the nationwide legalization of same-sex marriage in the United States. He was a columnist for The Advocate and Out magazines and has written for many national publications. His SiriusXM show has been a platform for interviews with politicians and for documenting anti-LGBT politics, including extensive coverage of the Trump administration. In 2011 he was inducted into the National Lesbian and Gay Journalists Association's LGBT Journalists Hall of Fame.
