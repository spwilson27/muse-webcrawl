Gull-wing door

A gull-wing door (also called a falcon-wing door in some applications) is a car door that is hinged at the roof rather than the side, so that it opens upward, resembling a seagull's wings.

History

The design was pioneered by Jean Bugatti on the 1939 Bugatti Type 57 SC Atlantic and made famous by the Mercedes-Benz 300 SL of 1954, where the doors were a structural necessity: the car's tubular spaceframe chassis had high, deep sills that made conventional doors impractical. The 300 SL's racing success turned the design into an enduring style icon.

Examples

Other production cars with gull-wing or similar upward-opening doors include the Bricklin SV-1, the DeLorean DMC-12 (1981), the Mercedes-Benz SLS AMG, the Pagani Huayra, and the Tesla Model X, whose rear "falcon wing" doors use sensors to articulate around obstacles. Drawbacks include difficulty opening in rollover accidents, sealing problems, and the need for strong struts.
