Sistema Central

The Sistema Central, also known as the Central System (Spanish and Portuguese: Sistema Central), is one of the principal systems of mountain ranges in the Iberian Peninsula. Its highest peak, Pico Almanzor (2,592 m), lies in the Sierra de Gredos. The Sistema Central runs roughly west-southwest to east-northeast for about 600 km, from Portugal through the middle of Spain, and divides the Central Meseta (Meseta Central) into northern and southern sub-plateaus.

Geography

The range begins in Portugal with the Serra da Estrela — whose Torre (1,993 m) is the highest point of mainland Portugal — and continues through the Spanish sierras of Gata, Francia, Béjar, Gredos, Guadarrama, Somosierra, and Ayllón. Large rivers originate in or are bounded by the system: the Duero drains the northern slopes, while the Tagus and its tributaries drain the south.

The Sistema Central separates the catchment areas of the Douro (north) and the Tagus (south). North of the range lies the northern Meseta of Castile and León, and south of it the southern Meseta of Madrid, Extremadura, and Castile-La Mancha. The city of Madrid lies at the foot of the Sierra de Guadarrama.

Geology

The Sistema Central is composed mainly of granite and metamorphic rocks of Paleozoic origin. It was uplifted during the Alpine orogeny in the Tertiary period, when the Iberian microplate's collision with Europe reactivated old Variscan basement blocks. Glacial action during the Pleistocene shaped cirques and small lakes in the highest massifs, particularly in Gredos and Guadarrama.

Nature and protection

Parts of the range are protected as national parks, including the Sierra de Guadarrama National Park (declared 2013) and the Sierra de Gredos regional park. The mountains host Iberian ibex, griffon vultures, Spanish imperial eagles, and extensive forests of Scots pine and Pyrenean oak. Valleys such as the Jerte Valley are famous for cherry orchards.
