Sniper

A sniper is a military or paramilitary marksman who engages targets from positions of concealment or at distances exceeding the detection capabilities of enemy personnel. Snipers generally have specialised training and are equipped with high-precision rifles and high-magnification optics; their duties include reconnaissance, surveillance and anti-sniper measures. The term derives from the verb "to snipe", originating in British India in the 1770s, for shooting the elusive snipe bird.

Role

Snipers typically work in a two-person team with a spotter, who locates targets, estimates range and wind, and observes the fall of shot. Beyond long-range precision fire, military snipers gather battlefield intelligence and call in supporting fires. Police snipers (sharpshooters) operate under different legal constraints, usually in hostage and counter-terrorist situations. A designated marksman, by contrast, is an infantryman with an accurised rifle who extends a squad's effective range but does not operate independently like a true sniper.

History

Sharpshooting specialists appeared with rifled muskets in the 18th century; organised sniping developed during the American Civil War and the Boer War, and came of age in the trench warfare of the First World War, when armies established sniper schools and telescopic-sighted rifles. The Second World War produced famous snipers such as the Soviet Vasily Zaitsev and the Finn Simo Häyhä. Modern precision rifles chambered in cartridges such as .338 Lapua Magnum and .50 BMG have extended effective ranges beyond 1,500 m, and the longest recorded sniper kills exceed 2,400 m and even 3,500 m.

Training and equipment

Selection and training emphasise marksmanship, fieldcraft, camouflage (including the ghillie suit), stalking, range estimation and observation. Snipers must compute ballistic corrections for distance, wind, temperature and angle. Modern equipment includes bolt-action or semi-automatic precision rifles, spotting scopes, laser rangefinders and ballistic calculators.
