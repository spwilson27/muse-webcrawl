Land sailing

Land sailing, also known as sand yachting, land yachting or dirtboating, is the sport of moving across land in a wheeled vehicle powered only by the wind through a sail. Land yachts are raced on beaches with firm wet sand, on dry lake beds such as those of the Mojave Desert and Australia, and on disused airfields and purpose-built sites. Because rolling resistance is low, land yachts can sail several times faster than the true wind speed.

History

Wind-driven wheeled vehicles are ancient: Egyptian and Chinese sources describe sail-carrying chariots and wheelbarrows, and in 1600 the Flemish scientist Simon Stevin built a large sail wagon for Prince Maurice of Orange that carried 28 passengers along the Dutch coast at speeds said to exceed 30 km/h. Recreational land yachting developed in Belgium and northern France in the 1890s–1900s (the De Panne resort remains a centre), and in the United States on California's dry lakes after the Second World War. The international federation, FISLY, was founded in 1962 and coordinates racing classes and world and European championships.

Craft and classes

A typical land yacht has three wheels (one steerable, at front or rear), a lightweight chassis with a seat or prone cockpit for the pilot, and a mast carrying a large sail controlled by a mainsheet. Major racing classes include Class 2 and Class 3 (large yachts with limited sail area and, in Class 3, wing masts), Class 5 and the smaller, strictly one-design Class 7 and "mini yacht" classes popular for beach sailing; kite buggies and Blokarts are related craft. Steering and sheet handling must counter the heeling force that tries to lift the windward wheel; pilots wear helmets and harness themselves in for high-speed downwind legs.

Records

The outright land-sailing speed record was set by the British yacht Greenbird, driven by Richard Jenkins at Ivanpah Lake, California, in March 2009, at 202.9 km/h (126.2 mph). The wind-powered speed record on water or land was subsequently contested by wing-sailed craft; in 2022 the land yacht Horonuku of Emirates Team New Zealand, piloted by Glenn Ashby, reached 222.4 km/h on Lake Gairdner, South Australia.
