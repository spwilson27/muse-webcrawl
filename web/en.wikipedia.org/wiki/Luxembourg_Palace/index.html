Luxembourg Palace

Palais du Luxembourg
Location: Paris, France
Construction: 1615–1645
Architect: Salomon de Brosse
Architectural style: French Baroque
Current use: Seat of the French Senate

The Luxembourg Palace (French: Palais du Luxembourg) is a palace in the 6th arrondissement of Paris, originally built for Marie de' Medici, regent of France and widow of Henry IV. Designed by Salomon de Brosse in a style recalling the Palazzo Pitti in her native Florence, it now houses the Senate, the upper house of the French Parliament.

History

Marie de' Medici purchased the estate of François de Luxembourg in 1612 and built her palace beside it; she moved in before its completion but was exiled in 1630 after the Day of the Dupes. The palace later passed through several royal and state uses: during the Revolution it served as a prison and then as seat of the Directory, and under Napoleon it became the seat of the Sénat conservateur.

Architecture

The palace is organised around a cour d'honneur, with pavilions and a domed central corps de logis. In the nineteenth century the garden façade was extended forward, creating the present hemicycle chamber. It faces the Luxembourg Garden to the south.

Present day

The palace is the working seat of the Senate; parts of it, including the library and the Salle du Livre d'Or, are opened to visitors on heritage days.
