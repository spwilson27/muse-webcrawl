Adi Shamir (Hebrew: עדי שמיר; born July 6, 1952) is an Israeli cryptographer and one of the most influential figures in the history of computer security. He is best known as a co-inventor of the RSA algorithm, together with Ron Rivest and Leonard Adleman, an achievement recognised by the 2002 ACM Turing Award.

Born: July 6, 1952, Tel Aviv, Israel
Known for: RSA, secret sharing, differential cryptanalysis
Awards: Turing Award (2002)

Education and career

Shamir received his BSc in mathematics from Tel Aviv University in 1973 and his MSc and PhD in computer science from the Weizmann Institute of Science in 1975 and 1977. After postdoctoral work at the University of Warwick and MIT, he joined the Weizmann Institute, where he has spent most of his career as a professor in the Department of Computer Science and Applied Mathematics.

Contributions

With Rivest and Adleman, Shamir developed RSA, the first practical public-key cryptosystem, published in 1977. He independently invented secret sharing, in which a secret is split into parts so that a threshold number of parts is needed to reconstruct it. With Eli Biham he developed differential cryptanalysis, a powerful general method of attacking block ciphers. His other work includes the TWIRL and TWINKLE devices for factoring, attacks on knapsack cryptosystems, visual cryptography (with Moni Naor), and the Fiat–Shamir heuristic for turning identification schemes into digital signatures.

Honours

Shamir shared the 2002 Turing Award with Rivest and Adleman. He has also received the Israel Prize, the Paris Kanellakis Award, and the BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award, and is a member of numerous national academies.
