Waxy flexibility

Waxy flexibility is a psychomotor symptom of catatonia as a result of a neuropsychiatric condition, such as schizophrenia. If one were to move the arm of someone with waxy flexibility, the patient would maintain that position as if it were "bendable wax", moving the limb slowly and with slight resistance, much like bending a warm candle.

Description

Waxy flexibility is a form of catalepsy in which the patient's limbs remain in whatever position they are placed by the examiner. It is one of the classic signs described by early psychiatrists in catatonic schizophrenia, though catatonia is now recognised to occur in association with many psychiatric and medical conditions, including mood disorders, autism spectrum conditions and autoimmune encephalitis.

The sign is typically assessed during a neurological or psychiatric examination: the examiner passively moves the patient's arm or leg and observes whether the new posture is maintained. Related catatonic signs include posturing, stupor, mutism, negativism, echolalia and echopraxia.

Clinical significance

Recognition of waxy flexibility and other catatonic signs is clinically important because catatonia often responds well to treatment with benzodiazepines such as lorazepam, or to electroconvulsive therapy, and because it must be distinguished from conditions such as neuroleptic malignant syndrome and Parkinson's disease.
