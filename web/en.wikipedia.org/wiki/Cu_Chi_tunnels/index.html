Cu Chi tunnels
Location: Cu Chi District, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
Length: ~121 km (75 mi) network
Wars: First Indochina War, Vietnam War

The tunnels of Cu Chi are an immense network of underground tunnels in the Cu Chi District northwest of Saigon (now Ho Chi Minh City), Vietnam. Built by Viet Cong forces, they served as hiding spots, supply routes, hospitals, kitchens and living quarters during the Vietnam War.

History

Digging began in the late 1940s during the war against French colonial forces and expanded enormously in the 1960s. At their peak the multi-level system, dug by hand into hard laterite clay, ran for over 120 km and withstood bombing, defoliation and US "tunnel rat" incursions.

Military significance

The tunnels let guerrillas operate close to the US and South Vietnamese capital, notably supporting the 1968 Tet Offensive. American efforts — Operation Crimp and Operation Cedar Falls — failed to destroy them.

Today

Sections at Ben Dinh and Ben Duoc are preserved as war memorials and a major tourist attraction, where visitors can crawl through enlarged tunnel segments and see booby-trap displays.
