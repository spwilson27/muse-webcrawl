The Symphony No. 3, Op. 36, also known as the Symphony of Sorrowful Songs (Symfonia pieśni żałosnych), is a symphony in three movements composed by the Polish composer Henryk Górecki in 1976. It is scored for solo soprano and large orchestra, and each movement sets a Polish text concerning the separation of a mother from her child.

History

Górecki composed the symphony between October and December 1976, on commission connected with the Südwestfunk Baden-Baden. It was premiered on 4 April 1977 at the Royan International Festival by soprano Stefania Woytowicz and the SWF Sinfonieorchester conducted by Ernest Bour. Initially met with mixed critical response, the work later became one of the best-known pieces of late 20th-century classical music.

Movements and texts

The first movement sets a 15th-century Polish lament of the Virgin Mary from the Łysa Góra Songs. The second movement sets words inscribed by Helena Błażusiakówna, an 18-year-old prisoner, on the wall of a Gestapo cell in Zakopane during the Second World War. The third movement sets a Silesian folk song about a mother searching for her son killed in the Silesian Uprisings.

Reception and recording

A 1992 recording by the London Sinfonietta conducted by David Zinman, with soprano Dawn Upshaw, released by Nonesuch Records, became a surprise international bestseller, selling over a million copies and reaching the top of classical charts in the United Kingdom and the United States. The symphony is frequently associated with the "holy minimalism" movement and with music memorialising the victims of war.
