Gacha game

A gacha game is a video game, usually a free-to-play mobile game, that implements the gacha mechanic: players spend in-game currency — often bought with real money — to receive a random virtual item, most commonly a playable character, through a system resembling Japan's gashapon capsule-toy vending machines, from which the mechanic takes its name.

Mechanic

Players perform "pulls" or "rolls" on limited-time banners featuring particular characters or items, with rare rewards having very low probabilities. Games typically include a "pity" system guaranteeing a rare reward after a fixed number of unsuccessful pulls. Items are graded by rarity tiers, and collecting powerful or beloved characters drives both engagement and revenue.

History

The model emerged in Japanese mobile games around 2010–2012, with titles such as Puzzle & Dragons popularising it. It spread worldwide through games including Fate/Grand Order, Fire Emblem Heroes, Genshin Impact (2020), and Honkai: Star Rail, some of which earn billions of dollars in revenue.

Criticism and regulation

Gacha mechanics are frequently compared to gambling: the randomised rewards, opaque odds, and aggressive monetisation have led several countries to require disclosure of drop rates (as China has since 2017), and some jurisdictions treat similar loot-box systems under gambling law. Japan banned the related "complete gacha" (kompu gacha) model in 2012.
