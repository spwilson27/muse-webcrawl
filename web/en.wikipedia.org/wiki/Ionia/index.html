Ionia (Ancient Greek: Ἰωνία) was an ancient region on the western coast of Anatolia, in present-day Turkey, settled by Greek Ionians from about the 11th century BC. Its cities — among them Miletus, Ephesus, Smyrna, Phocaea, Priene and the island states of Samos and Chios — formed the Ionian League and became some of the wealthiest and most intellectually productive communities of the Greek world.

History

Prosperous through maritime trade, the Ionian cities produced the earliest Greek philosophy and science: Thales, Anaximander and Anaximenes of Miletus; Heraclitus of Ephesus; and Pythagoras, who emigrated from Samos, all worked in or came from Ionia. The region fell under Lydian and then Persian rule in the 6th century BC. The Ionian Revolt (499–493 BC) against Persia, though crushed, helped trigger the Greco-Persian Wars. Ionia later passed between Athenian, Spartan and Persian control, was absorbed into the Hellenistic kingdoms, and eventually became part of the Roman province of Asia.

Legacy

Ionia gave its name to the Ionic dialect of Greek, the Ionic order of architecture — exemplified by the great temples of Ephesus and Priene — and, through the word "Yavan/Yunan", to the name for Greeks in many Asian languages. The region's cities preserve some of the finest classical ruins in the Mediterranean.
