Original Six

The six teams that made up the NHL from 1942 to 1967.

The Original Six is the group of six teams that comprised the National Hockey League (NHL) for 25 seasons between 1942–43 and 1966–67. They are the Boston Bruins, Chicago Black Hawks (now Blackhawks), Detroit Red Wings, Montreal Canadiens, New York Rangers and Toronto Maple Leafs.

History

The six-team league emerged after the Brooklyn Americans suspended operations in 1942, following a decade in which several franchises had folded during the Great Depression and Second World War. Although the term "Original Six" is retrospective — the NHL had in fact been founded in 1917 with different members, and only Montreal and Toronto are charter franchises — the era is remembered as a golden age marked by fierce rivalries and dynasties, notably Montreal's five consecutive Stanley Cups from 1956 to 1960.

End of the era

The era ended with the 1967 NHL expansion, which doubled the league to twelve teams, adding clubs in Los Angeles, Minnesota, Oakland, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh and St. Louis. The Original Six teams remain among the league's most valuable and storied franchises.
