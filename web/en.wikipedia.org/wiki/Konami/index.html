Konami Group Corporation (Japanese: コナミ) is a Japanese entertainment conglomerate and video game company founded in 1969 in Toyonaka, Osaka, by Kagemasa Kōzuki, initially as a jukebox rental and repair business. The name "Konami" combines the surnames of its early executives: Kagemasa Kozuki, Yoshinobu Nakama, and Tatsuo Miyasako.

History

Konami entered the arcade business in the late 1970s and achieved early hits with Frogger (1981) and Scramble. On home consoles it became a leading third-party developer for the Nintendo Entertainment System, with series such as Castlevania, Contra, Gradius, and Metal Gear (1987), the latter created by Hideo Kojima. The 1990s and 2000s added Silent Hill, Dance Dance Revolution, the Pro Evolution Soccer series (later eFootball), and the hugely successful Yu-Gi-Oh! trading card game.

Business areas

Beyond video games, Konami operates pachinko and slot machines (particularly in North America), health and fitness clubs in Japan, and animation. In the mid-2010s the company controversially reduced its focus on large-budget console games following a public split with Kojima in 2015, before returning to revivals of its classic franchises in the 2020s.

The "Konami Code" (↑↑↓↓←→←→BA), a cheat sequence first popularised by Contra, became a broader pop-culture reference.
