Cis–trans isomerism

Cis–trans isomerism, also known as geometric isomerism, describes certain types of stereoisomerism in which substituents are arranged on the same side (cis, Latin "on this side") or opposite sides (trans, "across") of a double bond or ring.

Cis–trans isomerism
Type: Stereoisomerism
Example: cis- vs trans-but-2-ene
Related system: E–Z notation

Organic chemistry

In alkenes, restricted rotation about the carbon–carbon double bond creates distinct isomers with different physical properties: cis-but-2-ene and trans-but-2-ene differ in boiling and melting points. When substituents are more complex, the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog E–Z notation replaces cis/trans.

Inorganic chemistry

The terms also describe square-planar and octahedral complexes, e.g. the anticancer drug cisplatin, whose trans isomer is inactive.

Biological significance

Vision depends on light-driven conversion of 11-cis-retinal to all-trans-retinal in the eye. Trans fats arise from partial hydrogenation producing trans double bonds.
