Boy in da Corner is the debut studio album by English rapper and producer Dizzee Rascal. It was released on 21 July 2003 by XL Recordings. Recorded largely while Dizzee Rascal was still a teenager, the album is widely regarded as the first major grime album and one of the most influential British records of its era, bringing the raw sound of East London pirate radio to a national audience.

Background

Dylan Mills, known as Dizzee Rascal, grew up on council estates in Bow, East London, and came up through the UK garage and nascent grime scenes as a member of the Roll Deep crew. He produced much of Boy in da Corner himself using minimal equipment—early tracks were made with a PlayStation and basic software—creating sparse, abrasive electronic beats with jittery rhythms, video-game bleeps, and heavy bass. Lyrically the album documents the frustrations, violence, humour, and ambition of inner-city youth, delivered in Dizzee's distinctive high-pitched, rapid-fire flow.

Reception and legacy

The album was met with critical acclaim on release. In September 2003 it won the Mercury Prize, making the 18-year-old Dizzee Rascal the youngest winner of the award at the time. Singles included "I Luv U", "Fix Up, Look Sharp", and "Jus' a Rascal". "Fix Up, Look Sharp", built on a sample of Billy Squier's "The Big Beat", became his first top-20 hit.

In the years since, Boy in da Corner has consistently appeared on lists of the greatest albums of all time. Pitchfork gave the album a rare perfect score in a retrospective review, and in 2016 Dizzee Rascal performed the album in full at a series of Red Bull Music Academy shows in London and New York, underlining its status as the foundational text of grime. The record's success opened the door for the commercial breakthrough of grime in the following decade.
