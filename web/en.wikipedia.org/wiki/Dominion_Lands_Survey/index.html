Dominion Lands Survey

The Dominion Lands Survey (DLS) is the method used to divide most of Western Canada into one-square-mile (2.6 km²) sections for agricultural and other purposes. It is based on the layout of the Public Land Survey System used in the United States, but has several differences. The DLS is the dominant survey system of the Canadian Prairies, and its grid is visible from the air across Manitoba, Saskatchewan and Alberta, extending also into parts of British Columbia and the Northwest Territories.

Structure

Land is divided into townships of 36 sections, each section 640 acres, further divided into quarter sections of 160 acres — the standard homestead unit under the Dominion Lands Act 1872. Townships are numbered north from the 49th parallel and ranges west from a series of principal meridians. Sections were allocated for homesteads, schools, and endowments such as the Hudson's Bay Company reserve and railway land grants.

History

The survey began in 1869–71 following the transfer of Rupert's Land to Canada, and proceeded westward for decades; by its completion it was among the largest survey systems ever undertaken, covering some 800,000 square kilometres.
