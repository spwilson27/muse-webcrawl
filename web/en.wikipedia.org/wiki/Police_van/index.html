Police van

A police van is a type of vehicle operated by police forces. Police vans are usually employed for the transport of prisoners inside a specially adapted cell in the vehicle, or for the rapid transport of groups of officers to an incident scene. They range from minivans to full-size vans and, in some countries, armoured vehicles used by public-order units.

Types and regional names

In the United Kingdom, a van with a fitted cell for prisoners is sometimes called a "cell van"; historically, prisoner transport vehicles in the United States were nicknamed "paddy wagons", a term of disputed origin also applied to police vans generally. German police use the term Mannschaftswagen for personnel carriers. Many forces distinguish small vans used for prisoner conveyance from larger personnel carriers fitted with bench seating, shields, and protective grilles over the windows for riot duties.

Design

Prisoner compartments are typically separated from the cab, fitted with secure doors, washable surfaces, and sometimes CCTV. Public-order vans may carry equipment lockers, roof hatches, and reinforced bodywork.
