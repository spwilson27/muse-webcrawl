Cochlear nerve

The cochlear nerve, also known as the auditory or acoustic nerve, is the branch of the vestibulocochlear nerve (cranial nerve VIII) that carries sound information from the cochlea of the inner ear to the brainstem.

Part ofVestibulocochlear nerve (CN VIII)
InnervatesCochlea (organ of Corti)
FunctionHearing

Anatomy and function

Cell bodies of cochlear nerve fibres lie in the spiral ganglion within the cochlea. Their axons carry signals generated by inner hair cells through the internal acoustic meatus to the cochlear nuclei in the medulla, beginning the central auditory pathway. Damage causes sensorineural hearing loss.
