The Kuwaiti dinar (Arabic: دينار كويتي, code: KWD) is the currency of the State of Kuwait. It is sub-divided into 1,000 fils and is the world's highest-valued currency unit, worth roughly US$3.25 per dinar in recent years. The dinar is issued by the Central Bank of Kuwait.

History

The dinar was introduced in 1961, replacing the Gulf rupee issued by the Government of India, at a rate of 1 dinar = 13⅓ rupees, initially on par with the pound sterling. When Kuwait was invaded by Iraq in 1990, the Iraqi dinar replaced it, and huge quantities of banknotes were stolen; after liberation, the Kuwaiti dinar was restored and a new banknote series was introduced to invalidate the stolen notes.

Kuwait has issued six series of banknotes, the sixth in 2014, printed on polymer-style substrate with advanced security features. Denominations in circulation are ¼, ½, 1, 5, 10 and 20 dinars; coins run from 5 to 100 fils.

Exchange rate

Since 2007 the dinar has been pegged to an undisclosed weighted basket of international currencies, after a period (2003–2007) of pegging to the US dollar. Its high unit value reflects Kuwait's large oil revenues and the decision not to split the currency into smaller units; the dinar has been the world's most valuable currency unit for decades.
