Patrick Ferguson

Patrick Ferguson (1744 – 7 October 1780) was a Scottish officer in the British Army, designer of the Ferguson rifle, and a prominent Loyalist commander in the American Revolutionary War. He was killed at the Battle of Kings Mountain in South Carolina.

Early career and rifle

Born in Aberdeenshire, Ferguson joined the army as a teenager and served in Germany and the Caribbean. He developed and patented in 1776 an improved breech-loading flintlock rifle with a rotating screw breech, demonstrated before senior officers; a small corps of riflemen armed with it fought at Brandywine in 1777, where Ferguson was wounded in the arm.

Southern campaign

Promoted major, Ferguson campaigned in the south from 1780, recruiting and organising Loyalist militia in the Carolinas. His threat to lay waste to the backcountry helped provoke the Overmountain Men to march against him; his force was destroyed and he was killed at Kings Mountain, a turning point of the war in the south.
