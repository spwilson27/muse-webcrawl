John Dudley, 1st Duke of Northumberland (1504 – 22 August 1553) was an English general, admiral, and politician, who led the government of the young King Edward VI from 1550 until 1553, and unsuccessfully tried to install Lady Jane Grey on the English throne after the King's death.

Early career

Dudley was the son of Edmund Dudley, a minister of Henry VII executed in 1510. Restored in blood, he rose through military service in France and Scotland and as Lord Admiral, becoming Viscount Lisle and then Earl of Warwick. He distinguished himself at the Battle of Pinkie in 1547 under the Duke of Somerset.

Rule under Edward VI

In 1549–1550 Dudley outmanoeuvred Somerset, becoming Lord President of the Privy Council and, in 1551, Duke of Northumberland. He ended the costly wars with Scotland and France, reformed finances, advanced Protestant religious reform, and governed through the teenaged king. His regime saw the 1552 Book of Common Prayer and the Forty-two Articles.

The succession crisis and death

As Edward VI lay dying in 1553, Dudley married his son Guildford to Lady Jane Grey and supported Edward's "Devise for the Succession", which excluded the King's half-sisters Mary and Elizabeth in Jane's favour. Jane was proclaimed queen on 10 July 1553, but the country rallied to Mary. Dudley surrendered at Cambridge, was tried for high treason, and was executed on Tower Hill on 22 August 1553, despite a public recantation of his Protestantism.
