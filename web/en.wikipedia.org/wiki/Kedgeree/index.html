Kedgeree

Kedgeree
CourseBreakfast, brunch
Place of originIndia / United Kingdom
Main ingredientsRice, smoked haddock, eggs, curry spices, butter
VariationsWith salmon or other fish; with peas

Kedgeree is a dish consisting of cooked, flaked fish (traditionally smoked haddock), boiled rice, parsley, hard-boiled eggs, curry powder, butter or cream, and occasionally sultanas. It is a well-known example of Anglo-Indian cuisine, brought back to Britain by colonial administrators and soldiers returning from India, and became a popular Victorian breakfast dish.

History

Kedgeree is thought to have originated from the Indian dish khichdi (or khichri), a preparation of rice and lentils seasoned with spices, which dates back centuries in Indian cooking. A recipe resembling khichdi appears in accounts of India as early as the 14th century, and the dish was described by travellers such as Ibn Battuta. During the period of British rule in India, the dish was adapted to British tastes: the lentils were dropped, fish and eggs were added, and the result was served at breakfast in country houses across Victorian Britain.

The name "kedgeree" is an Anglicisation of "khichdi". Some food historians suggest the dish reached Scotland, where smoked haddock was plentiful, and from there spread throughout Britain. By the mid-19th century it was firmly established as a breakfast item; recipes appear in Mrs Beeton's Book of Household Management (1861) and other cookery books of the era.

Preparation

The fish — usually undyed smoked haddock, though salmon and other fish are used — is poached in milk or water, then flaked into large pieces with the skin and bones removed. Long-grain rice is cooked with curry powder or a mixture of turmeric, cumin and coriander, then folded together with the fish, chopped hard-boiled eggs, butter and fresh parsley. The dish is served hot, often with lemon wedges.
