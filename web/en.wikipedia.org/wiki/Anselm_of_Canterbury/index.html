Saint Anselm of Canterbury (1033–1109) was an Italian Benedictine monk, abbot and Archbishop of Canterbury, called the father of Scholasticism. His motto 'faith seeking understanding' produced the ontological argument for God's existence in the Proslogion (1078) and, in Cur Deus Homo, the satisfaction theory of atonement: Christ's death repays the infinite debt human sin owes God's honour.

Career

Anselm twice clashed with kings (William II, Henry I) over investiture and was exiled; his rigorous, prayerful treatises modelled the scholastic method of reasoned inquiry within faith. He was canonised (1494 in the usual reckoning) and declared a Doctor of the Church in 1720.
