Detergent
Type: Cleaning agent
Key components: Surfactants, builders, enzymes
Uses: Laundry, dishwashing, industrial cleaning
Related: Soap, Surfactant

A detergent is a surfactant, or mixture containing surfactants, with cleaning properties when in dilute solutions with water. Detergents are similar to soap but generally more effective in hard water.

How they work

Surfactant molecules have a hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tail. They reduce surface tension, surround grease and soil, and form micelles that can be rinsed away.

Composition

Modern laundry detergents include builders to soften water, enzymes to break down protein and starch stains, bleaches, fragrances and optical brighteners. Formulations include powders, liquids, pods and sheets.

History and environment

Synthetic detergents expanded during and after the Second World War. Phosphate builders caused eutrophication in waterways and were restricted in many places; biodegradability of surfactants is now regulated.
