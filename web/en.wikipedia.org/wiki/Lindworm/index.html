Lindworm

The lindworm (from Old Norse linnormr, and German Lindwurm) is a mythological creature in Northern, Central and Eastern European folklore and heraldry: a serpent-like dragon with either no legs, two front legs, or sometimes none of the wings typical of dragons. Depictions vary widely between regions and periods.

Folklore

In Scandinavian tradition, lindworms were great serpents said to inhabit the countryside, growing ever larger and sometimes coiling around churches or hoarding treasure. A well-known motif is the "lindworm prince": a king and queen longing for a child are granted a snake child who later sheds his skin to become a handsome prince. In Germanic legend, the dragon Fafnir of the Völsunga saga is sometimes described in terms akin to a lindworm, crawling on his belly over his gold.

The Lambton Worm and other tales

English folklore includes related worm legends, such as the Lambton Worm of County Durham, a monstrous serpent fished from the River Wear and slain by the young heir of Lambton. In Austria, a lindworm legend explains the founding of Klagenfurt, whose coat of arms bears a winged lindworm; a famous Lindwurm fountain stands in the city.

Heraldry

In heraldry the lindworm appears as a charge in German, Austrian, and Scandinavian civic arms, generally as a dragon-like serpent. The creature remains popular in modern fantasy literature and games, usually as a wingless, venomous wyrm.
