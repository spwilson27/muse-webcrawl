Scruggs style

Scruggs style is a method of playing the five-string banjo developed by American musician Earl Scruggs (1924–2012). It is the defining technique of bluegrass banjo, characterised by a three-finger picking style in which the thumb and first two fingers, fitted with metal picks, play rapid arpeggiated patterns ("rolls") that weave the melody through a continuous stream of notes.

The five-string banjo as played in Scruggs style.

Technique

The right hand plays patterns such as the forward roll, backward roll and foggy mountain roll across the strings, while the short fifth string acts as a drone. Unlike earlier clawhammer and two-finger styles, Scruggs style emphasises precise, driving syncopation and allows the banjo to take lead breaks alongside fiddle and mandolin.

History and influence

Scruggs joined Bill Monroe's Blue Grass Boys in 1945; recordings with Monroe and later with Lester Flatt in the Foggy Mountain Boys — including "Foggy Mountain Breakdown" (1949) — made the style famous. His instruction book Earl Scruggs and the 5-String Banjo (1968) taught generations of players, and Scruggs-style picking remains the foundation of bluegrass banjo instruction.
