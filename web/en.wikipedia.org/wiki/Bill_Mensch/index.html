William David Mensch Jr. (born February 9, 1945) is an American electrical engineer who was a key designer of the MOS Technology 6502 microprocessor and the founder of the Western Design Center (WDC).

Early life and career

Mensch was born in Quakertown, Pennsylvania. He earned an associate degree from Temple University and a bachelor's degree in electrical engineering from the University of Arizona in 1966. He worked at Philco-Ford, Hughes Aircraft and Motorola, where he contributed to the Motorola 6800 microprocessor project.

MOS Technology and the 6502

In 1974 Mensch joined MOS Technology with Chuck Peddle and other former Motorola engineers. Although Peddle led the project, Mensch was responsible for much of the 6502's circuit and layout design, producing a chip that was dramatically cheaper than competitors' offerings when launched in 1975. The 6502 went on to power landmark home computers including the Apple II, Commodore 64, Atari 2600 (in its 6507 variant) and BBC Micro.

Western Design Center

Mensch founded the Western Design Center in Mesa, Arizona, in 1978, one of the first fabless semiconductor companies. At WDC he designed the 65C816, a 16-bit successor to the 6502 used in the Apple IIGS and the Super Nintendo Entertainment System (via Ricoh's derivative), and continues to license 65xx processor cores for embedded applications.

Recognition

Mensch was inducted into the Computer History Museum Hall of Fellows in 2005, together with the other members of the 6502 design team.
