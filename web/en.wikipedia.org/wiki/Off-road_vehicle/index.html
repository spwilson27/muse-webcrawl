Off-road vehicle

An off-road vehicle is designed to be driven on unpaved and rough surfaces.

An off-road vehicle (ORV), also called an off-highway vehicle, is any motorised vehicle capable of driving on unpaved, rough surfaces such as sand, gravel, mud, snow, and rock. Common features include high ground clearance, large tyres with deep tread, flexible suspension, and four-wheel drive with low-range gearing.

Types

Off-road vehicles range from modified or purpose-built four-wheel-drive cars and SUVs — such as the Jeep Wrangler, Land Rover Defender, and Toyota Land Cruiser — to pickup trucks, military vehicles, all-terrain vehicles (ATVs), side-by-sides (UTVs), dirt bikes, dune buggies, and heavy machinery such as tractors and agricultural vehicles. Amphibious and tracked vehicles are used in the most extreme terrain.

Use

Off-road capability is essential for agriculture, forestry, mining, construction, military operations, and travel in remote areas with poor roads. Recreational off-roading, trail riding, and off-road racing are popular pastimes. Many countries regulate recreational ORV use on public land because of concerns about erosion, habitat damage, noise, and conflicts with other users; designated trail systems and parks are a common management approach.

Design considerations

Approach, departure, and breakover angles, axle articulation, differential locks, and the ability to ford water determine a vehicle's real off-road performance, beyond simple ground clearance.
