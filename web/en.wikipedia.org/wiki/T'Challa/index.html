T'Challa is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics, created by writer-editor Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby. He is the Black Panther, king and protector of the fictional African nation of Wakanda. First appearing in Fantastic Four #52 (1966), he was the first Black superhero in mainstream American comics.

Fictional biography

T'Challa is the son of T'Chaka, the previous Black Panther. After his father was murdered by the adventurer Ulysses Klaw, T'Challa earned the title of Black Panther by defeating the tribal champions and ingesting the heart-shaped herb, which grants enhanced senses, strength, speed and agility through a mystical link to the panther god Bast. He rules Wakanda, the most technologically advanced nation on Earth, built on its monopoly of the metal vibranium. Educated at Oxford and trained in the sciences, T'Challa is also a brilliant physicist and inventor.

As Black Panther he has been a long-standing member of the Avengers, was briefly married to the X-Men's Storm (Ororo Munroe), and has served with the Fantastic Four and the secretive Illuminati. Supporting characters include his sister Shuri, his mother Ramonda, the Dora Milaje royal guard — among them Okoye and Nakia — and adversaries such as Erik Killmonger, Man-Ape (M'Baku) and Klaw.

In other media

Chadwick Boseman portrayed T'Challa in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, debuting in Captain America: Civil War (2016) and headlining Black Panther (2018), which grossed over $1.3 billion worldwide and became the first superhero film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture. Following Boseman's death in 2020, Marvel chose not to recast the role; the 2022 sequel Black Panther: Wakanda Forever addresses T'Challa's death within the story.
