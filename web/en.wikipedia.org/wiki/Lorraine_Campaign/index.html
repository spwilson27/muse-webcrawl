Lorraine campaign

The Lorraine campaign was a campaign of the United States Third Army in France during the Second World War, fought from September to December 1944 in the Lorraine region of north-eastern France. Commanded by Lieutenant General George S. Patton, the Third Army advanced against German forces defending the Moselle river line and the fortified city of Metz.

Background

After the breakout from Normandy and the rapid pursuit across France in August 1944, Allied forces outran their supply lines. The Third Army was halted at the Moselle at the end of August for lack of fuel, giving the German First Army time to reorganise behind the river and the old Maginot and Westwall fortifications around Metz.

The campaign

Patton's forces crossed the Moselle in September and fought a series of costly armoured battles around Arracourt, among the largest tank engagements fought by the United States Army in the Second World War, in which American forces defeated several German panzer brigades. The fortified city of Metz, which had never been taken by assault, resisted until late November 1944. In November the Third Army launched a major offensive in heavy rain and flooding, capturing Metz on 22 November and advancing toward the Saar river and the German frontier by early December. Operations were then overtaken by the German Ardennes offensive of 16 December 1944, which drew the Third Army northward into the Battle of the Bulge.

The campaign has been criticised for its slow progress compared with the spectacular advances of August, though historians note the severe supply shortages, poor weather and strong fortifications facing Patton's army. See also George S. Patton and Battle of Metz.
