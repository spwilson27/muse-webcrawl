The Placodontia ("tablet teeth") are an order of extinct marine reptiles that lived during the Triassic period, from about 247 to 201 million years ago. Placodonts are members of the Sauropterygia, the group that also includes plesiosaurs and nothosaurs, and they were among the earliest marine reptiles to evolve after the end-Permian mass extinction.

Description

Placodonts were stocky, barrel-bodied animals, typically 1 to 2 metres (3.3 to 6.6 ft) long and occasionally up to 3 metres. Their most distinctive feature was their dentition: large, flat, plate-like teeth on the palate and at the back of the jaw, specialised for crushing the shells of molluscs and other hard-shelled prey, with protruding incisor-like teeth at the front for plucking prey from the sea floor.

Early forms such as Placodus resembled heavily built lizards, while later cyamodontoids such as Psephoderma and Henodus developed broad turtle-like carapaces of fused bony scutes, a striking case of convergent evolution with sea turtles — though placodonts are not their ancestors.

Distribution and extinction

Fossils are known chiefly from the shallow seas that covered Europe (the Germanic Basin and the Tethys) and from South China. Placodonts disappeared in the end-Triassic mass extinction, along with many other marine reptile groups.
