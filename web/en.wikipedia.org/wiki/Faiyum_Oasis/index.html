Faiyum Oasis

The Faiyum Oasis is a depression in the Western Desert of Egypt, about 100 kilometres southwest of Cairo, watered not by groundwater springs like a true oasis but by Nile water carried through the Bahr Yussef canal into the Faiyum basin. The basin contains Lake Qarun (ancient Lake Moeris) and the city of Faiyum, and corresponds roughly to modern Faiyum Governorate.

Geography and history

The depression lies below sea level and is connected to the Nile Valley by a narrow corridor. In Pharaonic times, Middle Kingdom kings — especially of the 12th Dynasty — undertook major land reclamation and irrigation works, greatly expanding cultivation and regulating the inflow to Lake Moeris. Under the Ptolemies the region, named the Arsinoite nome after Arsinoe, was densely settled by Greek veterans and developed with new villages and canals.

Archaeology

The Faiyum is famed for its rich archaeological record: prehistoric sites of the Faiyum A culture around the lake shore; the pyramids of Senusret II at Lahun and Amenemhat III at Hawara, with its legendary Labyrinth; Graeco-Roman towns such as Karanis and Tebtunis, which yielded vast quantities of papyri; and the celebrated Faiyum mummy portraits of the Roman period. Neolithic sites in the depression preserve some of Egypt's earliest evidence of agriculture.
