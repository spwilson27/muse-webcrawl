The Battle of Camden, fought on 16 August 1780 near Camden, South Carolina, was one of the worst American defeats of the American Revolutionary War. A British army under Lieutenant General Lord Cornwallis routed the larger American force of Major General Horatio Gates, the victor of Saratoga, whose career never recovered.

Background

After the fall of Charleston in May 1780, the British established a chain of outposts in the South Carolina backcountry, including Camden. Congress sent Gates south with a new army of Continentals and militia, perhaps 3,700 effectives (of some 4,100 on paper), weakened by short rations of green corn and molasses that left many men ill. Gates marched directly for Camden, unaware that Cornwallis had reinforced it with about 2,200 regulars.

The battle

The two armies blundered into each other on a night march before dawn on 16 August, skirmished, and deployed at first light. Gates placed his unreliable militia on the left opposite the British regulars. When the British advanced with the bayonet, the Virginia and North Carolina militia broke and fled almost without firing, exposing the flank of the Continental troops under Major General Johann de Kalb. The Continentals fought stubbornly but were overwhelmed; de Kalb was mortally wounded and captured. American losses were roughly 900 killed and wounded and 1,000 captured, with all artillery and baggage lost; British casualties were about 324.

Aftermath

Gates rode nearly 200 miles in three days to Hillsborough, North Carolina, and was replaced by Nathanael Greene, who rebuilt the southern army. The disaster at Camden, followed by the American victory at Kings Mountain in October, set the pattern for the war in the South that culminated at Yorktown in 1781.
