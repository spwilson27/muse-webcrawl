Common fibular nerve

The common fibular nerve (also known as the common peroneal nerve, external popliteal nerve, or lateral popliteal nerve) is a nerve in the lower leg that is one of the two major branches of the sciatic nerve, the other being the tibial nerve. It derives from the dorsal divisions of the fourth and fifth lumbar and first and second sacral spinal nerves (L4–S2). The term "fibular" has increasingly replaced "peroneal" in anatomical terminology, following the Terminologia Anatomica's preference for names based on the fibula rather than the Greek perone ("pin" or "brooch").

Course

The common fibular nerve separates from the tibial nerve at the apex of the popliteal fossa behind the knee, follows the medial border of the biceps femoris tendon, and winds around the neck of the fibula — where it lies just beneath the skin and is especially vulnerable to injury — before piercing the fibularis (peroneus) longus muscle. There it divides into its two terminal branches: the superficial fibular nerve, which supplies the lateral compartment muscles of the leg (the fibularis longus and brevis, responsible for foot eversion) and much of the skin of the lower leg and dorsum of the foot; and the deep fibular nerve, which supplies the anterior compartment muscles (responsible for dorsiflexion) and a small patch of skin between the first and second toes.

Clinical significance

Compression or trauma of the nerve at the fibular neck — from fractures, casts, prolonged leg crossing, or squatting — is the most common mononeuropathy of the lower limb. Damage paralyzes the dorsiflexors and evertors of the foot, producing "foot drop" and a high-stepping gait, together with sensory loss over the lateral leg and top of the foot.
