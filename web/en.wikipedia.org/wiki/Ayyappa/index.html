Ayyappa (also spelled Ayyappan or Sastha) is a Hindu deity widely worshipped in South India, especially in the state of Kerala. In Hindu tradition he is regarded as the son of Shiva and Mohini — the female form of Vishnu — and is thus sometimes styled Hariharasuthan, "son of Hari (Vishnu) and Hara (Shiva)". He is venerated as a celibate god of self-control, righteousness (dharma) and yogic discipline.

Legend

According to the legends, Ayyappa was born to slay the demoness Mahishi, sister of the buffalo-demon Mahishasura, who could be killed only by a child born of Shiva and Vishnu. The divine infant was found on the banks of the Pampa River by King Rajashekhara of Pandalam, who raised him as his own son and named him Manikandan, after the bell (mani) around his neck. After fulfilling his purpose by defeating Mahishi, Ayyappa is said to have merged into the deity at Sabarimala.

Sabarimala pilgrimage

Ayyappa's principal shrine is the Sabarimala temple, set in the Periyar Tiger Reserve forests of Kerala's Pathanamthitta district. The pilgrimage, undertaken mainly during the Mandalam–Makaravilakku season (roughly November to January), is one of the largest annual pilgrimages in the world, attracting tens of millions of devotees. Pilgrims traditionally observe a 41-day period of austerities (vratham), wear black or saffron clothing, and climb the eighteen sacred steps (pathinettampadi) to the sanctum chanting "Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa".

Worship and significance

Ayyappa worship is notable for its egalitarian ethos: devotees address one another as "Swami" or "Ayyappa", irrespective of caste, and the deity is venerated by people of several faiths; the shrine of Vavar, a Muslim associate of Ayyappa, stands on the pilgrimage route at Erumely. Temples dedicated to Ayyappa are found throughout Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and in the Indian diaspora worldwide.

In scriptures

The deity's mythology is elaborated in texts such as the Bhutanathopakhyanam, a Malayalam work on the legend of Bhuthanatha (Ayyappa), and in the Puranic tradition surrounding the god Shasta, with whom Ayyappa is closely identified in Tamil and Sanskrit sources.
