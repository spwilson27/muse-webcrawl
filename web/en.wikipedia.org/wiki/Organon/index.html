Organon

The Organon (Greek: Ὄργανον, meaning "instrument, tool, organ") is the standard collection of Aristotle's six works on logical analysis and dialectic. The name Organon was given by Aristotle's followers, the Peripatetics, who maintained against the Stoics that logic was not a part of philosophy (as the Stoics argued) but an instrument of philosophy—hence the collection's name, "the instrument".

The six works

  1. Categories — classifies all possible objects of thought into ten categories.
  2. On Interpretation (De Interpretatione) — deals with propositions, truth, and the relation between language and logic, including the problem of future contingents.
  3. Prior Analytics — introduces the syllogism and deductive reasoning.
  4. Posterior Analytics — treats demonstration, definition and scientific knowledge.
  5. Topics — a guide to dialectical argument from commonly held opinions (endoxa).
  6. On Sophistical Refutations — examines logical fallacies.

Transmission and influence

The Organon formed the foundation of the late-antique and medieval logica vetus and logica nova. Boethius translated the works into Latin, and commentators such as Porphyry (whose Isagoge was traditionally prefixed to the collection) and the Arabic philosophers al-Farabi, Avicenna and Averroes developed its doctrines extensively. Aristotelian logic, as codified here, dominated Western and Islamic thought until the rise of modern formal logic in the 19th century with Frege.
