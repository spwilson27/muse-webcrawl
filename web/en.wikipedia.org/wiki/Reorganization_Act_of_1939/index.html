Reorganization Act of 1939

The Reorganization Act of 1939 was an act of the United States Congress, enacted April 3, 1939, that gave the President of the United States authority to reorganize agencies of the executive branch, subject to a congressional veto by concurrent resolution. It followed the recommendations of the Brownlow Committee (the President's Committee on Administrative Management), whose 1937 report famously declared that "the President needs help".

Provisions and effects

Under the Act, President Franklin D. Roosevelt issued Reorganization Plan No. 1 of 1939, which created the Executive Office of the President and transferred agencies such as the Bureau of the Budget into it, and consolidated agencies into the Federal Security Agency, Federal Works Agency, and Federal Loan Agency. The Act authorized the President to hire six administrative assistants, laying the foundation for the modern White House staff, and established the Office of Emergency Management.

Legacy

The Act is considered a landmark in the development of the modern presidency and the Executive Office of the President, which was formally created by Executive Order 8248 of September 8, 1939.
