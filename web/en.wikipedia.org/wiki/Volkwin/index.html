Volkwin

Volkwin von Naumburg zu Winterstätten (died 22 September 1236) was the second master of the Livonian Brothers of the Sword, a German military order active in the Baltic region during the Northern Crusades. He succeeded the order's founder, Wenno, in 1209 and led the Sword Brothers for 27 years.

Leadership of the order

Under Volkwin, the Livonian Brothers of the Sword consolidated their hold over Livonia and extended their campaigns into Estonia, fighting alongside the Bishop of Riga, Albert. The order built stone castles along the Daugava and Gauja rivers, including at Sigulda and Wenden (Cēsis), which served as its headquarters. Volkwin's tenure saw repeated warfare with the Estonians, Lithuanians and the Novgorod Republic.

Battle of Saule and death

In 1236 Volkwin led a campaign south into Samogitia (in modern Lithuania), against the wishes of some of his allies. On the return march, on 22 September 1236, his force was ambushed and destroyed at the Battle of Saule by Samogitians and Semigallians. Volkwin and some 48–60 knights of the order were killed.

Aftermath

The disaster at Saule broke the Sword Brothers as an independent order. In 1237 the survivors were absorbed into the Teutonic Order as its Livonian branch, the Livonian Order, which continued the crusade in the Baltic for another three centuries.
