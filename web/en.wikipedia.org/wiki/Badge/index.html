Badge

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A badge is a device or accessory, often containing the insignia of an organization, which is presented or displayed to indicate some feat of service, a special accomplishment, a symbol of authority granted by taking an oath (e.g., police and fire services), a sign of legitimate employment or student status, or as a simple means of identification. They are also used in advertising, publicity, and for branding purposes.

Badges can be made from metal, plastic, leather, textile, rubber, or other materials, and are commonly attached to clothing, bags, footwear, vehicles, home electrical equipment or display surfaces. Textile badges may be woven or embroidered, and can be attached by gluing, ironing on, sewing or appliqué.

History

Badges have been used since ancient times as emblems of office, allegiance and achievement. In the Middle Ages, livery badges — personal devices worn by the retainers of great nobles — proliferated in England. Heraldic badges were distinct from coats of arms and could be borne by followers as well as the owner. Pilgrim badges, sold at shrines, were worn by medieval pilgrims as proof of their journeys.

Uses

  • Authority and identification: Police officers, firefighters and other officials wear metal badges as symbols of lawful authority; employees and students wear photo ID badges for access control.
  • Achievement: Scouting and guiding movements award embroidered merit badges for skills mastered; the military awards badges for qualifications such as parachuting or marksmanship.
  • Membership and allegiance: Political parties, clubs and supporters' groups distribute pin badges (button badges) to display affiliation.
  • Digital badges: In online learning and gaming, digital badges serve as verifiable indicators of skills and accomplishments.

Collecting badges, particularly pin badges, is a popular hobby; rare political and Olympic pins can be valuable.
