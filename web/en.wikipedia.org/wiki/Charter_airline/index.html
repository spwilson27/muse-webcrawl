A charter airline is an airline company that operates flights outside a published scheduled timetable. In a charter arrangement, the entire aircraft — or a defined block of capacity — is hired ("chartered") by a customer, which may be a tour operator selling package holidays, a sports team, a corporation, a military or government agency, or a private individual or group. The charterer, rather than the airline, typically bears the commercial risk of filling the seats.

History

Charter flying grew rapidly after the Second World War, when surplus aircraft and a rising appetite for affordable holidays enabled operators in Europe to fly tourists to Mediterranean resorts far more cheaply than scheduled carriers, whose fares were fixed by cartel-like international agreements. In the United States, "supplemental" carriers performed a similar role. Inclusive-tour charters drove the development of mass tourism to Spain, Greece, and later the Caribbean and Southeast Asia; British operators such as Britannia Airways and German operators such as Condor were emblematic leisure-charter carriers.

Business models

Charter operations include ad hoc charters arranged on demand; seasonal series charters contracted by tour operators for a holiday season; ACMI (aircraft, crew, maintenance, insurance) "wet leases" provided to other airlines; and air-taxi services with small aircraft. The rise of low-cost scheduled carriers in the 1990s and 2000s eroded the price advantage of traditional charter airlines, pushing many either into scheduled low-cost competition or into consolidation within integrated travel groups such as TUI.

Regulation

Although charter flights are non-scheduled, they are subject to the same safety regulation as scheduled services; consumer-protection regimes in the European Union and elsewhere also extend compensation and repatriation rights to package and charter passengers, a point illustrated when the collapses of Monarch Airlines (2017) and Thomas Cook (2019) required government-organized repatriation airlifts.
