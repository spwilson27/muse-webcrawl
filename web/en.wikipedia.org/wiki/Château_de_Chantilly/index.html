The Château de Chantilly is a historic French château located in the town of Chantilly, in the Oise department, about 50 kilometres north of Paris. The estate, set in a park of some 115 hectares within the great forest of Chantilly, is one of the finest jewels of French heritage, built up over the centuries by the Montmorency and Condé families, princes of the blood.

History

The Petit Château was built around 1560 for Anne de Montmorency, Constable of France. The Grand Château, designed in the 17th century in part by André Le Nôtre, who laid out its famous gardens and the Grand Canal, belonged to the princes of Condé. It was largely destroyed during the French Revolution and rebuilt in the 1870s by Henri d'Orléans, Duke of Aumale, the greatest art collector of his time, to house his collections in the Musée Condé.

Musée Condé

The Duke of Aumale bequeathed the estate to the Institut de France in 1886. The Musée Condé holds one of France's finest collections of paintings after the Louvre, including works by Raphael, Poussin, Watteau and Ingres, and a library of precious manuscripts, among them the Très Riches Heures du Duc de Berry. Under the terms of the bequest, the presentation of the collections may never be changed and no work may be lent.

Park and stables

The park includes French formal gardens by Le Nôtre, an Anglo-Chinese garden and an English garden, as well as the Hameau de Chantilly, a rustic hamlet that partly inspired Marie-Antoinette's at Versailles. The Great Stables (Grandes Écuries), built in the 18th century by the Prince of Condé, are among the largest in Europe and now house the Living Museum of the Horse. The town gives its name to Chantilly lace and to Chantilly cream.
