Twistor space

In theoretical and mathematical physics, twistor space is a mathematical construction introduced by Roger Penrose in 1967 as part of his twistor programme, which aims to reformulate the geometry of spacetime in terms of light rays and complex geometry. Twistor space is the complex projective space CP³ of three complex dimensions; its points ("twistors") correspond not to points of spacetime but to light rays (null geodesics) in complexified Minkowski space.

The twistor correspondence

The central idea is a duality called the twistor correspondence: a point in complexified Minkowski spacetime corresponds to a whole complex projective line (a Riemann sphere, CP¹) in twistor space, while a point in twistor space corresponds to a light ray in spacetime (strictly, an alpha-plane or a null geodesic). Physics that is local on one side becomes non-local — encoded holomorphically — on the other. Two twistors are related by an "incidence relation" that encodes whether the corresponding light rays meet.

Massless fields, such as electromagnetic waves and gravitational radiation, are described in twistor space by holomorphic objects (cohomology classes of sheaves), and the Penrose transform converts these into solutions of the massless field equations in spacetime. Self-dual gauge fields correspond to holomorphic vector bundles (the Ward construction), and self-dual gravity to deformations of the complex structure of twistor space (the nonlinear graviton construction).

Applications

Twistor methods have proved powerful in solving the self-dual Yang–Mills and Einstein equations and in classifying instantons. In the 2000s, Edward Witten's twistor string theory recast scattering amplitudes of N=4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory in twistor terms, and twistor-inspired methods (such as the construction of amplitudes from curves in twistor space) transformed the calculation of scattering amplitudes in quantum field theory. Twistors are also the natural variables for "split signature" and celestial holography approaches to quantum gravity.
