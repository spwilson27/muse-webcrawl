Orazio Vecchi

Orazio Vecchi
Born: 6 December 1550, Modena
Died: 19 February 1605, Modena
Occupation: Composer
Known for: L'Amfiparnaso (1597)

Orazio Vecchi (1550–1605) was an Italian composer of the late Renaissance, known especially for his madrigal comedies. He was a cleric as well as a musician, and spent most of his career in Modena, with periods in Reggio Emilia, Correggio and Salò.

Life

Vecchi took holy orders and obtained a canonry at Correggio. He served as maestro di cappella at Modena Cathedral (from 1584, and again in the 1590s after a period at court and cathedral posts elsewhere) and was appointed maestro of the court of Duke Cesare d'Este in 1598. He published many books of madrigals, canzonettas, motets and masses.

Madrigal comedies

Vecchi is celebrated for the madrigal comedy, a sequence of madrigals that together tell a story — a forerunner of opera. His masterpiece is L'Amfiparnaso (published 1597), a "comedia harmonica" in a prologue and three acts whose characters, drawn from the commedia dell'arte, are portrayed entirely through ensemble madrigals. Vecchi insisted in his preface that the work was for the ear rather than the stage: the spectacle "is to be seen in the mind, where it enters by way of the ears, not the eyes". Earlier collections such as Selva di varia ricreazione (1590) and Le convito musicale (1597) mix serious and comic pieces, including imitations of languages and animal sounds.
