The 1962 FIFA World Cup was the seventh FIFA World Cup, held in Chile from May 30 to June 17, 1962. Brazil successfully defended its title, defeating Czechoslovakia 3–1 in the final at the Estadio Nacional in Santiago, becoming the second team (after Italy in 1938) to win consecutive World Cups. Host nation Chile finished third, its best result ever.

Background

Chile's preparations were severely disrupted by the 1960 Valdivia earthquake, the most powerful earthquake ever recorded, which forced organizers to rebuild plans around fewer stadiums. FIFA president Carlos Dittborn's plea—"Because we have nothing, we want to do everything"—became famous; Dittborn died weeks before the tournament began.

Tournament summary

The tournament is remembered for excessive violence, notably the "Battle of Santiago," Chile's group match against Italy, one of the most ill-tempered games in World Cup history. Pelé was injured in Brazil's second match and missed the rest of the tournament, but Garrincha rose to the occasion, leading Brazil through the knockout rounds with brilliant performances against England and Chile.

Final

In the final, Czechoslovakia led early through Josef Masopust, but Brazil replied through Amarildo, Zito, and Vavá to win 3–1. Six players shared the top-scorer honor with four goals each, among them Garrincha and Vavá.
