Land ethic

A land ethic is a philosophy or theoretical framework about how, ethically, humans should regard the land on which they live and the natural world more broadly. The term was coined by Aldo Leopold in his 1949 book A Sand County Almanac, where he wrote: "A thing is right when it tends to preserve the integrity, stability, and beauty of the biotic community. It is wrong when it tends otherwise."

Leopold's formulation

Leopold argued that ethics should be extended beyond interpersonal relations to encompass soils, waters, plants, and animals — collectively, "the land". He saw humans not as conquerors of the land-community but as "plain member and citizen of it". The land ethic enlarges the boundaries of the community to include the whole ecosystem.

Influence

The land ethic became a foundational concept in environmental ethics and conservation philosophy, influencing the modern environmental movement and later thinkers such as J. Baird Callicott, who systematized Leopold's ideas.
