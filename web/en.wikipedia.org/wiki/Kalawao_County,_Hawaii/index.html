Kalawao County is a county in the U.S. state of Hawaii. It is the smallest county in the United States by land area and the second-smallest by population, consisting of the Kalaupapa Peninsula on the north coast of the island of Molokai.

History

From 1866, people afflicted with Hansen's disease (leprosy) were exiled to the isolated peninsula, where the settlement of Kalaupapa was served by Saint Damien of Molokai and Saint Marianne Cope. The county was formally established in 1905 and, uniquely, is administered by the state Department of Health rather than an elected county government; it has no county government of its own.

Today

A small number of former patients continue to live on the peninsula by choice, and the area is preserved as the Kalaupapa National Historical Park. Kalawao County famously recorded strikingly high reported vote shares and was the last county in the nation to report results in some recent elections.
