September equinox

September equinox
Typical date: 22 or 23 September
Counterpart: March equinox

The September equinox is the moment when the plane of Earth's equator passes through the geometric centre of the Sun's disc, occurring each year around 22 or 23 September. It is one of two annual equinoxes, when day and night are roughly equal in length across the planet.

Astronomy

At the equinox, the Sun is directly above the equator as it appears to cross from the northern to the southern celestial hemisphere. In the Northern Hemisphere the September equinox marks the start of astronomical autumn, while in the Southern Hemisphere it marks the start of spring.

Dates

Because of leap years and the details of Earth's orbit, the exact date varies between 21 and 24 September, most commonly falling on 22 or 23 September in the 21st century.

Cultural significance

The equinox is associated with harvest festivals in many cultures, including the Mid-Autumn Festival in East Asia and Mabon in modern pagan traditions.
