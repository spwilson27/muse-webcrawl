Malwa is a historical and geographical region in west-central India, occupying a plateau of volcanic origin in present-day western Madhya Pradesh and parts of southeastern Rajasthan and Gujarat. Historically, "Malwa" has referred both to the political territory of successive kingdoms and sultanates and to the distinctive cultural region centered on Ujjain, Dhar, Mandu, and Indore.

Geography

The Malwa Plateau lies north of the Vindhya Range and is drained by the Chambal, Shipra, and other tributaries of the Yamuna and the Narmada. Its black cotton soils support agriculture, especially soybeans, wheat, and cotton; in the 18th and 19th centuries the region was also notorious for opium production, traded as "Malwa opium."

History

Malwa was an important region from ancient times: Ujjain was a major city of early Indian civilization and a center of learning. The region was ruled by the Paramaras (whose capital was Dhar), conquered by the Delhi Sultanate, and became an independent sultanate in the 15th century with its capital at Mandu, famed for the architecture of Hoshang Shah and the romance of Baz Bahadur and Rupmati. Malwa later passed to the Mughals, then to the Marathas — notably the Holkars of Indore and the Scindias of Gwalior — before coming under British influence.

Culture

The regional language is Malvi, and the region has its own folk traditions, cuisine, and festivals. Ujjain hosts the Simhastha Kumbh Mela, one of the largest religious gatherings in the world.
