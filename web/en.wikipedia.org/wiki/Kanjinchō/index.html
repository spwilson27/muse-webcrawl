Kanjinchō

Kanjinchō (勧進帳, "The Subscription List") is a kabuki dance-drama by Namiki Gohei III, based on the Noh play Ataka. Premiered in 1840, it is one of the most popular plays in the kabuki repertoire and a celebrated vehicle for leading actors in the role of the warrior-monk Benkei.

Plot

Minamoto no Yoshitsune, fleeing the forces of his brother Yoritomo, travels in disguise with his loyal retainer Musashibō Benkei and a party of mountain priests (yamabushi). At the Ataka barrier, the guard captain Togashi Saemon suspects their identity. Benkei improvises: he pretends to read from a kanjinchō — a subscription list for the rebuilding of a temple — which is in fact a blank scroll. When a porter in the party (Yoshitsune in disguise) is questioned, Benkei strikes his own lord with his staff to maintain the deception, an act of apparent impiety that paradoxically convinces Togashi of their innocence. Togashi, though suspecting the truth, lets them pass; in the finale Benkei dances in celebration, performing the famous tobi-rokppō exit down the hanamichi.

Significance

The play dramatises loyalty, sacrifice and quick wit, and its central paradox — a retainer striking his master to save him — is one of the best-known scenes in Japanese theatre. The story derives from episodes in the Gikeiki (Chronicle of Yoshitsune) and other medieval war tales.
