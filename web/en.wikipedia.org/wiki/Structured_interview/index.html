Structured interview

Structured interviews use the same questions and scoring criteria for every candidate

A structured interview is a quantitative research method in which every interviewee is asked the same set of predetermined questions in the same order, with responses recorded in a standardised way. In personnel selection, it is an employment interview using job-related questions, anchored rating scales, and trained interviewers who score each answer against agreed criteria.

Validity

Meta-analyses of personnel selection (notably Schmidt and Hunter, 1998) found structured interviews to be among the most valid predictors of job performance — substantially more predictive than unstructured interviews — and modern re-analyses, while revising the exact coefficients, continue to rank them near the top of selection methods. Structure improves reliability because candidates can be compared on identical material and because idiosyncratic interviewer biases have less room to operate.

Forms

Structured interviews may be behavioural ("Tell me about a time when…"), asking candidates to describe past behaviour, or situational, presenting hypothetical job scenarios. In clinical and survey research, fully structured diagnostic interviews (such as the SCID for psychiatric diagnosis) trade conversational flexibility for comparability and are contrasted with semi-structured and unstructured interviews, where questioning follows the respondent's answers.
