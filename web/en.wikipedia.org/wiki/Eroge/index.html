Eroge

An eroge (エロゲ, from erotic game, エロチックゲーム) is a Japanese video game genre featuring erotic or adult content. Eroge grew out of the Japanese personal-computer game market of the 1980s and are most commonly visual novels and dating sims, in which narrative text and branching choices form the bulk of gameplay, though the term also covers adult role-playing games, adventure games and other genres. They are sold for adults in Japan under industry self-regulation.

History

Adult games appeared on Japanese home computers such as the PC-8801 and PC-9801 in the early 1980s, with companies like Koei — which released the early title Night Life in 1982 — among the pioneers. The genre matured in the 1990s with story-driven visual novels from studios such as Elf, Leaf and Key, whose works often mixed substantial drama with adult content. Titles like Kanon (1999) and Air (2000) demonstrated that eroge could sustain emotionally serious narratives, and sanitised all-ages ports brought their stories to consoles and anime adaptations.

Characteristics

Most eroge are text-heavy: the player reads narration and dialogue, views character sprites against backgrounds, and makes occasional choices that branch the story toward different heroines and endings. A smaller number incorporate simulation, strategy or role-playing systems. Adult scenes, where present, typically form a small fraction of total playing time in story-focused titles.

Regulation and overseas release

In Japan, eroge are sold only to adults and regulated by industry bodies such as the Ethics Organization of Computer Software (EOCS), with explicit imagery subject to Japanese obscenity law and censorship conventions. Since the 2000s, publishers such as MangaGamer, JAST USA and Sekai Project have licensed eroge for English-language release, often with both adult and all-ages editions.
