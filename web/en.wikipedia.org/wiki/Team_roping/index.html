Team roping

Team roping, also known as heading and heeling, is a rodeo event that features a steer and two mounted riders, the first called the "header" and the second the "heeler." It is the only rodeo event in which two competitors work together.

How it works

The steer is released from a chute with a head start. The header ropes the steer first — around both horns, one horn and the head, or around the neck — and then dallies and turns the steer so its hind legs are exposed. The heeler then ropes both hind legs. The run is timed when the ropes are taut and both horses face the steer. Legal catches and time penalties (such as five seconds for roping only one hind leg) are governed by sanctioning bodies such as the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA).

Origins

The event grew out of ranch work, where pairs of cowboys roped cattle for branding and doctoring. It became a standard professional rodeo event and is also contested in numerous amateur "ropings" using numbered handicap systems.
