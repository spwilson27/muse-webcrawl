Aircraft livery

An aircraft livery is a set of comprehensive insignia comprising colour, graphic, and typographical identifiers which operators — airlines, governments, air forces, and private operators — apply to their aircraft. Airline liveries present a strong visual identity, functioning as flying advertisements and as a means for passengers, ground crews, and air traffic control to recognise the operator. Livery design is usually codified in brand standards covering the fuselage, tail fin, engines, and winglets, together with the aircraft registration and legally required markings.

Each airline's livery presents its brand identity across the fleet.

Elements

The principal elements of an airline livery are the colour scheme of the fuselage, the design applied to the vertical stabilizer (often carrying the airline's logo or a national symbol), the airline's titles in a corporate typeface, and cheatlines or other graphic devices. Since the late twentieth century the dominant fashion has been the "Eurowhite" scheme — a mostly white fuselage with the operator's colours concentrated on the tail — which reflects heat, makes corrosion and damage easier to spot, and simplifies leasing between operators. Earlier fashions included polished bare-metal finishes, popularised by American Airlines, and bold full-length cheatlines.

Special liveries

Airlines regularly apply temporary or one-off schemes: alliance branding (Star Alliance, Oneworld, and SkyTeam each have standard liveries worn by selected aircraft of member airlines), retro liveries reproducing historic schemes, promotional schemes for films, sporting events, and destinations, and awareness campaigns. Military aircraft, by contrast, usually wear camouflage or low-visibility grey schemes, with national insignia and unit markings; government and head-of-state aircraft often carry national colours and flags.

Application and cost

Liveries are applied as paint, as adhesive decals (especially for short-term promotional schemes), or a combination. Repainting a wide-body airliner can require hundreds of litres of paint, add significant weight, and keep the aircraft out of service for one to two weeks, so fleet-wide rebrandings are phased in over several years as aircraft come due for heavy maintenance.
