Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542

Enacted1535 and 1542, Parliament of England
ReignHenry VIII
EffectAnnexation of Wales to England; English law and administration extended to Wales

The Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542 (also known as the Acts of Union) were two acts of the Parliament of England under Henry VIII that formally annexed the Principality of Wales and the Marcher lordships to the Kingdom of England, extending English law, administration, and parliamentary representation to Wales.

Provisions

The 1535 act declared Wales incorporated into England, abolished the legal distinction between the Principality and the Marches, and provided that English common law alone would apply. The Welsh legal system of Hywel Dda was displaced, and the use of the Welsh language was barred in the courts and public office. The 1542 act completed the settlement, organising Wales into shires with courts of great sessions and granting Welsh constituencies representation at Westminster.

Legacy

The acts remained the constitutional foundation of the union of England and Wales for centuries. Their provisions on language were long a source of grievance, and they were progressively dismantled in the twentieth century as Welsh devolution developed.
