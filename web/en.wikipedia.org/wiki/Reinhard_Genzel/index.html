Reinhard Genzel

Reinhard Genzel

AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (2020, with Andrea Ghez)
[Image: Photograph of Reinhard Genzel at the Very Large Telescope in Chile]
Genzel in 2019.

Reinhard Genzel (born 24 March 1952) is a German astrophysicist, director at the Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics in Garching. He shared one half of the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics with Andrea Ghez 'for the discovery of a supermassive compact object at the centre of our galaxy'.

From 1992 Genzel's group used infrared imaging and spectroscopy at ESO's telescopes in Chile (NTT, then the VLT) to follow the 'S-stars' whipping around Sagittarius A*. The star S2's 16-year orbit, mapped in full by 2018, and the GRAVITY interferometer's measurements of its gravitational redshift and orbital precession (2020), turned the Galactic Centre into both the best case for a supermassive black hole and a laboratory for General relativity. His long, fruitful competition with Ghez's UCLA group — different hemispheres, same stars — is a model case study in scientific rivalry.

Career

Genzel studied at Freiburg and Bonn (PhD 1978), worked at Berkeley's Space Sciences Laboratory, and has directed at MPE since 1986. Beyond the Galactic Centre he pioneered infrared astronomy of star-forming galaxies at cosmic noon, showing that early disc galaxies are turbulent, clumpy systems — work recognised by the Shaw Prize (2008) and many other honours.
