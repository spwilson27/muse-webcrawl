Digital micromirror device

The digital micromirror device, or DMD, is the optical semiconductor that is the core of DLP (Digital Light Processing) projection technology invented by Texas Instruments. It was originally invented in 1987 by physicist Larry Hornbeck, who had been working on deformable mirror devices since 1977.

Structure and operation

A DMD chip has on its surface up to several million microscopic aluminium mirrors, each about 16 micrometres square or smaller, mounted on tiny hinges above CMOS memory cells. Each mirror corresponds to one pixel in the projected image and can be electrostatically tilted between two stable angles (typically ±10–12°): an "on" state reflecting light through the projection lens, and an "off" state deflecting it onto a light absorber. Rapid pulse-width modulation of the tilt — thousands of times per second — produces shades of grey; colour is produced by a colour wheel or three separate DMDs.

Applications

DMDs are used in video projectors, digital cinema projection, rear-projection televisions, and in research applications such as structured-light 3D scanning, microscopy and lithography.
