Sébastien Le Prestre de Vauban

Born1633, Saint-Léger-de-Foucheret (now Saint-Léger-Vauban), France
Died30 March 1707, Paris
OccupationMilitary engineer; Marshal of France (1703)
Known forBastion fortification design and siege warfare method

Sébastien Le Prestre, marquis de Vauban (1633 – 30 March 1707), commonly referred to simply as Vauban, was a French military engineer in the service of Louis XIV and the most influential designer of fortifications and practitioner of siege warfare of his age. Over a career of more than fifty years he directed some 53 sieges — he was never defeated when conducting one, and fortresses he defended were rarely taken — and he designed or rebuilt more than 160 fortresses along France's frontiers.

Career

Born into minor Burgundian nobility, Vauban first served the Prince of Condé during the Fronde and was captured by royal forces in 1653; impressed by his talent, Cardinal Mazarin won him over to the king's service. He rose under the war minister Louvois to become Commissioner-General of Fortifications in 1678. Vauban systematised both attack and defence: his siegecraft relied on methodically advanced parallel trenches, a ricochet fire technique he introduced at the siege of Maastricht in 1673, and overwhelming artillery, drastically reducing the cost of sieges in men. His fortifications — graded "first", "second", and "third" systems of bastioned traces — ringed France with a defensive belt known as the pré carré.

Other works and writings

Vauban's engineering extended beyond fortresses: he designed the Canal du Midi's later works and aqueducts, ports such as Dunkirk, and new towns including Neuf-Brisach, his masterpiece of an ideal fortified city. In his later years he wrote candid memoranda on fiscal and social questions; his Projet d'une dîme royale (1707), proposing a single proportional income tax and criticising the fiscal oppression of the poor, was suppressed and hastened his fall from favour shortly before his death.

Legacy

Twelve of Vauban's works were inscribed by UNESCO in 2008 as the World Heritage site "Fortifications of Vauban". His methods dominated European siegecraft and fortification until the mid-nineteenth century, when rifled artillery made bastioned masonry obsolete. His heart is interred at Les Invalides in Paris.
