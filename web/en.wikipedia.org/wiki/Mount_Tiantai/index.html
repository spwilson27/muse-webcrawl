Mount Tiantai (Chinese: 天台山; pinyin: Tiāntāi Shān, "Terrace of Heaven Mountain") is a mountain range in Tiantai County, Zhejiang Province, eastern China. It is one of the most important sacred mountains of Chinese Buddhism, the ancestral home of the Tiantai school, and also revered in Daoism and Chinese literary culture.

Buddhism

The monk Zhiyi (538–597) settled on the mountain and systematised the teachings that became the Tiantai (Japanese: Tendai) school, based on the Lotus Sutra. The school's head temple, Guoqing Temple, founded in 598 under the Sui dynasty, stands at the mountain's foot and long served as a centre from which Tiantai spread to Japan — the monk Saichō studied there before founding Tendai at Mount Hiei — and to Korea. Other sites include Fangguang Temple, the thousand-year-old Sui plum tree at Guoqing, and the mountain's dramatic cliff forms such as Shiliang waterfall.

Culture

Mount Tiantai is inseparable from the legend of Hanshan ("Cold Mountain") and Shide, the eccentric Tang-dynasty poet-hermits associated with Guoqing Temple, whose poems became classics translated worldwide. The range is also tied to the Tang poet Li Bai's celebrated dream-journey poem, and to Daoist lore of immortals. It is now a national scenic area known as much for its waterfalls, old temples and hermitages as for its religious history.
