Nimbostratus

Nimbostratus is a low-level, dark grey cloud layer of large horizontal extent that produces continuous rain or snow. It is a stratiform cloud genus in the family of precipitating clouds, abbreviated Ns in meteorological codes.

Characteristics

Nimbostratus forms when a broad sheet of moist air is lifted gently over a large area, typically along a warm front or in the warm sector of an extratropical cyclone. The cloud is thick enough to obscure the sun completely and often merges with lower ragged fragments called pannus or scud. Unlike cumulonimbus, nimbostratus produces steady, prolonged precipitation without thunder or lightning.

The base of nimbostratus is often ill-defined because of falling precipitation (virga or rain) evaporating into the air below. Visibility beneath the cloud is typically poor. In aviation, extensive nimbostratus is associated with widespread icing conditions and low ceilings.

Formation

Altostratus frequently thickens and lowers into nimbostratus as a frontal system approaches. The cloud may extend through a deep layer of the troposphere, from a few hundred metres above the ground to altitudes of 6 km or more, even though it is classified as a low cloud by its base height.
