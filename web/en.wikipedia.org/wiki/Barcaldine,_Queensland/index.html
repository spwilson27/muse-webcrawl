Barcaldine, Queensland

Barcaldine
Location: Central West Queensland, Australia, about 520 km west of Rockhampton
Population: roughly 1,400
Known for: 1891 shearers' strike; Tree of Knowledge

Barcaldine is a rural town and locality in the Barcaldine Region in Central West Queensland, Australia. It is one of the most historically significant towns in Australian labour history: the 1891 Australian shearers' strike, one of the earliest and most important industrial disputes in the country, was centred on the town, and the Australian Labor Party traces part of its origins to meetings held there.

History

The town was founded in 1886 as the terminus of the Central Western railway line from Rockhampton, and was named after Barcaldine in Argyll, Scotland, by railway engineer Donald Charles Cameron. Wool-growing dominated the district. During the 1891 shearers' strike, striking shearers established a camp outside the town and held meetings under a large ghost gum beside the railway station—the Tree of Knowledge. Although the strike was defeated and its leaders imprisoned, the strike accelerated the formation of political labour organisation; in 1892 the world's first short-lived Labor government in Queensland drew support from the movement. The tree was poisoned in 2006 and died, but its remains were preserved and a striking timber memorial structure was erected over the site in 2009.

The town today

Barcaldine serves a surrounding pastoral district and is home to the Australian Workers' Heritage Centre, a museum interpreting the history of working Australians. It lies on the Capricorn Highway at the junction with the road to Longreach and Winton.
