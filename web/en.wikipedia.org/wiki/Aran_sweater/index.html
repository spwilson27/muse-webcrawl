An Aran sweater (Irish: Geansaí Árann) is a style of knitted sweater that takes its name from the Aran Islands off the west coast of Ireland. Traditionally made from unscoured, undyed cream wool (báinín) that retained its natural lanolin, the sweater is decorated with panels of textured stitches — cables, diamonds, zigzags, honeycomb and trellis patterns.

History

The style was developed in the early 20th century and commercialised from the 1930s–1950s, when pattern books and export markets — helped by features in Vogue Knitting — made it an emblem of Irish craft. Popular lore assigns symbolic meanings to the stitches and claims families identified drowned fishermen by clan patterns, but historians regard much of this as marketing mythology.

Modern production

Today Aran sweaters are produced both by island hand-knitters and by machine in Ireland and abroad, in traditional cream and in many colours, and remain a staple of Irish tourist and fashion trade.
