Monterozzi necropolis

LocationTarquinia, Lazio, Italy
CivilisationEtruscan
Period7th–2nd century BC
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (2004)

The Monterozzi necropolis (Italian: Necropoli dei Monterozzi) is an Etruscan burial ground on the Monterozzi hill near Tarquinia in northern Lazio, Italy. It contains some 6,000 graves cut into the rock, of which about 200 are decorated with wall paintings — the largest surviving corpus of Etruscan painting and a unique record of Etruscan daily life, religion, and funerary custom.

The painted tombs

The tombs generally consist of a single rock-cut chamber reached by a descending stair or corridor, imitating the interior of a house. Their paintings, dating mainly from the 6th to the 2nd century BC, depict banquets, dancing, music, athletics, hunting, and scenes from mythology and the afterlife.

Celebrated examples include the Tomb of the Augurs (c. 530 BC), among the earliest painted tombs; the Tomb of the Lionesses (c. 520 BC) with its musicians and dancers beneath a checkered ceiling; the Tomb of Hunting and Fishing; the Tomb of the Leopards (c. 480–470 BC), showing a banquet beneath fruit trees; the Tomb of the Triclinium; and the later Tomb of the Ship, Tomb of the Jugglers, and the Tomb of Orcus, which contains one of the earliest depictions of the Etruscan underworld demon Charun and the only known portrait of the hero Velia Velcha's family themes.

Heritage

In 2004 the necropolis was inscribed, together with the Banditaccia necropolis at Cerveteri, on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "Etruscan Necropolises of Cerveteri and Tarquinia". The graves are protected today with controlled access and climate monitoring, as the paintings are vulnerable to humidity and temperature change. Finds from the tombs are displayed in the National Archaeological Museum of Tarquinia in the Palazzo Vitelleschi.
