Estadio Riazor

Estadio Municipal de Riazor

Location: A Coruña, Galicia, Spain

Capacity: 32,490

Opened: October 28, 1944

Tenants: Deportivo de La Coruña

The Estadio Municipal de Riazor, known as Riazor, is a football stadium in A Coruña, Galicia, Spain. It has been the home ground of Deportivo de La Coruña since its inauguration in 1944 and takes its name from the adjacent Riazor beach on the Atlantic coast. Its current capacity is 32,490.

History

Riazor opened on October 28, 1944, with a match between Deportivo and Valencia. The original stadium featured a distinctive marathon tower and an athletics track, retained until renovations for the 1982 FIFA World Cup, for which Riazor hosted three group-stage matches. Between 1995 and 1998 the stadium was comprehensively remodelled: the running track was removed, the stands were brought close to the pitch, and all four sides were roofed, transforming it into a compact all-seater football ground.

The stadium witnessed Deportivo's golden era under president Augusto César Lendoiro, including the La Liga championship of 1999–2000 and famous European nights such as the 2004 Champions League quarter-final comeback against AC Milan, when Deportivo overturned a 4–1 first-leg deficit with a 4–0 win at Riazor.

Sponsorship name

In 2017 the stadium's name was commercially sponsored by the bank Abanca, under which it is officially styled the Estadio Abanca-Riazor, while remaining universally known as Riazor.
