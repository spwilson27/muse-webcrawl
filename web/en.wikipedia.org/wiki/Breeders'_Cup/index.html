The Breeders' Cup World Championships is an annual series of Grade I thoroughbred horse races, operated by Breeders' Cup Limited, formed in 1982. It was conceived by horse breeder John R. Gaines as a year-end championship for North American racing that would also showcase the sport to a wider audience.

History

The first Breeders' Cup was held in 1984 at Hollywood Park. Originally a single day of seven races, it expanded to two days (Friday and Saturday) in 2007 and now comprises fourteen championship races with purses totalling over US$30 million. The venue rotates among major tracks including Santa Anita Park, Churchill Downs, Keeneland and Del Mar.

Races

The feature race is the Breeders' Cup Classic, run over 1¼ miles on dirt for three-year-olds and older with a purse of $7 million. Other races include the Turf, Distaff, Mile, Sprint, Juvenile and Filly & Mare Turf. "Win and You're In" challenge races around the world award automatic berths. Many winners go on to Eclipse Award honours, and standout performances include Zenyatta's 2009 Classic win and American Pharoah's 2015 "Grand Slam" completion.
