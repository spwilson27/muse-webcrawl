Assam

Assam (Assamese: অসম) is a state in northeastern India, situated south of the eastern Himalayas along the Brahmaputra and Barak river valleys. With an area of 78,438 km² (30,285 sq mi), it is the most populous state in Northeast India, with a population of over 31 million according to the 2011 census. Dispur, a neighbourhood of Guwahati, is its capital, and Guwahati is its largest city.

Geography

Assam is bordered by Bhutan and the state of Arunachal Pradesh to the north; Nagaland and Manipur to the east; Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram and Bangladesh to the south; and West Bengal to the west. The state is dominated by the valley of the Brahmaputra River, one of the largest rivers in the world, and by the Barak valley in the south. The Karbi Anglong and North Cachar hills separate the two valleys.

History

The region was ruled for some six centuries by the Ahom kingdom (1228–1826), which successfully resisted Mughal expansion. Following the First Anglo-Burmese War, the Treaty of Yandabo (1826) brought Assam under British rule, and it became part of Bengal and later a separate province. After Indian independence in 1947, Assam's territory was progressively reduced as new states — Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh — were carved out of it during the 1960s to 1980s.

Economy

Assam is famous for its tea: the state produces more than half of India's tea, and Assam tea is a well-known variety of black tea. Petroleum was first drilled in India at Digboi in Assam in 1889, and the state remains a significant producer of crude oil and natural gas. Agriculture, dominated by rice cultivation, employs the majority of the workforce.

Culture

Assamese culture is a blend of Indo-Aryan and Tibeto-Burman influences. The spring festival of Bihu is the most important cultural celebration. The one-horned Indian rhinoceros of Kaziranga National Park is an emblem of the state, and the island of Majuli in the Brahmaputra is a centre of Vaishnavite culture founded by the saint Srimanta Sankardev.
