Mohini

Mohini (Sanskrit: मोहिनी, Mohinī) is a Hindu goddess and the only female avatar of the god Vishnu. She is depicted as an enchantress who uses her allure to help the devas (gods) and deceive the asuras (demons). Mohini is first mentioned in the epic Mahabharata and appears prominently in the Bhagavata Purana and other Puranic texts.

Churning of the ocean

In the most famous legend, during the Samudra Manthana (churning of the ocean of milk), the devas and asuras jointly churned the ocean to obtain amrita, the nectar of immortality. When the pot of amrita emerged, the asuras seized it. Vishnu then assumed the form of Mohini, a beautiful woman whose charm so distracted the asuras that she was able to distribute the nectar to the devas alone. The asura Svarbhanu, disguised as a deva, received a portion but was beheaded, becoming the planets Rahu and Ketu.

Other legends

In another cycle, Mohini enchanted the demon Bhasmasura, who had gained the power to reduce anyone to ashes by touching their head; she tricked him into touching his own head. In South Indian tradition, Mohini's union with Shiva produced the god Ayyappa (Sastha). Mohini is also associated with the Mohiniyattam dance form of Kerala, whose name derives from her.

Worship

Mohini is worshipped as a goddess in her own right in some traditions, including as Mhalsa, a form worshipped in Maharashtra and Goa, where she is regarded as an incarnation linked to Shiva's consort. Her cult emphasises illusion (maya) and the power of divine beauty.
