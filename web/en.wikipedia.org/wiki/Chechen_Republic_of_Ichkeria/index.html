The Chechen Republic of Ichkeria was an unrecognised secessionist state that controlled much of Chechnya in the North Caucasus during the 1990s. Its name derives from Ichkeria, the historical Chechen name for the mountainous southeast of the country. No United Nations member state recognised its independence; the only formal recognition came from Taliban-ruled Afghanistan in 2000.

Independence and the first war

In 1991, as the Soviet Union collapsed, the All-National Congress of the Chechen People under former Soviet air force general Dzhokhar Dudayev declared Chechnya independent of Russia. Dudayev was elected president, and Grozny was renamed Dzokhar-Ghala in his honour. Russia refused to accept secession, and in December 1994 Russian forces invaded, beginning the First Chechen War. Despite initial disasters, Chechen fighters retook Grozny in August 1996; Dudayev had been killed by a Russian missile in April 1996. The Khasavyurt Accord of August 1996 ended the war and deferred Chechnya's final status until 2001.

The interwar republic

Aslan Maskhadov, the separatists' chief of staff, won presidential elections in January 1997 that international observers judged broadly free. The republic, however, was devastated by war, plagued by kidnapping and warlordism, and Maskhadov progressively lost control to field commanders and Islamist factions, some of which proclaimed sharia rule. In August 1999 militants led by Shamil Basayev and Ibn al-Khattab invaded neighbouring Dagestan, providing the pretext — along with apartment bombings blamed on Chechen terrorists — for Russia's return.

Second war and exile

The Second Chechen War began in autumn 1999. Russian forces retook Grozny after a massive siege in early 2000, and the Ichkerian government disintegrated into guerrilla resistance. Maskhadov was killed in 2005; his successors were killed in turn. In 2007 the last Ichkerian president, Dokka Umarov, abolished the republic in favour of a "Caucasus Emirate", splitting the movement; loyalists under Akhmed Zakayev maintained a government-in-exile. Russia declared its counter-terrorist operation over in 2009, by which time the pro-Moscow government of Ramzan Kadyrov controlled Chechnya.
