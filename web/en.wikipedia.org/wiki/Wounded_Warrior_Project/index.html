Wounded Warrior Project

Founded: 2003
Headquarters: Jacksonville, Florida
Type: Nonprofit, veterans' service organisation
Focus: Wounded veterans of the Iraq and Afghanistan wars

The Wounded Warrior Project (WWP) is an American charity and veterans' service organisation that offers a variety of programs, services and events for wounded veterans who incurred a physical or mental injury while serving on or after 11 September 2001. It is headquartered in Jacksonville, Florida.

History

WWP was founded in 2003 by John Melia, who had been severely wounded in a helicopter crash in Somalia in 1992. The project began by delivering backpacks filled with comfort items to wounded service members arriving at military hospitals from Afghanistan. It grew rapidly into one of the largest veterans' charities in the United States, with registered "alumni" numbering well over 100,000.

Programs

The organisation runs programs in mental wellness (including the Warrior Care Network for post-traumatic stress treatment), career counselling, physical health and wellness, benefits assistance, and connection events such as Soldier Ride, an adaptive cycling event. It also advocates for veterans' legislation in Washington.

Controversy and reform

In 2016, media investigations into spending on staff conferences and fundraising led to the dismissal of WWP's chief executive and chief operating officer and a sharp drop in donations. Under new leadership the charity implemented expense reforms, and its ratings with charity evaluators subsequently recovered.
