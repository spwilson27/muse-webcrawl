Negative reinforcement

In operant conditioning, negative reinforcement is the strengthening of a behaviour by the removal, termination, or avoidance of an aversive (unpleasant) stimulus following the behaviour. A common everyday example is fastening a seat belt to stop an annoying warning buzzer: the behaviour (buckling up) is reinforced because it removes the aversive sound.

Distinction from punishment

Negative reinforcement is frequently confused with punishment, but the two are opposites in effect: reinforcement (positive or negative) increases the future probability of a behaviour, whereas punishment decreases it. Negative reinforcement increases behaviour by removing something aversive; positive punishment decreases behaviour by adding something aversive.

Escape and avoidance

Two related processes are distinguished. In escape learning, the organism performs a behaviour that terminates an aversive stimulus already present. In avoidance learning, the behaviour prevents the aversive stimulus from occurring at all. Avoidance behaviour is notably persistent because the organism rarely has the opportunity to learn that the aversive event would no longer occur.

Examples

  • Taking painkillers to remove a headache reinforces pill-taking.
  • Studying to avoid the anxiety of failing an examination.
  • A rat pressing a lever to switch off an electric shock in a Skinner box.
