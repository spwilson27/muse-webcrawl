The New York Knickerbockers, shortened and commonly known as the New York Knicks, are an American professional basketball team based in the New York City borough of Manhattan. The Knicks compete in the National Basketball Association (NBA) as a member of the Atlantic Division of the Eastern Conference.

History

Founded in 1946, the Knicks are one of two original NBA teams still located in its original city (along with the Boston Celtics). Under coach Red Holzman, the Knicks won their two championships in 1970 and 1973 with teams featuring Willis Reed, Walt Frazier, Dave DeBusschere, Bill Bradley and Earl Monroe; the 1970 title is remembered for Reed limping onto the court for Game 7 of the Finals.

Later eras

The 1990s Knicks, built around centre Patrick Ewing and coached by Pat Riley, were perennial contenders and reached the NBA Finals in 1994 and 1999 but did not win a title. Subsequent decades included brief resurgences, notably the 2012–13 season with Carmelo Anthony and a return to competitiveness in the 2020s under players such as Jalen Brunson.

Home and identity

The Knicks play at Madison Square Garden, marketed as 'The World's Most Famous Arena'. Their colours are blue, orange, silver, black and white, and they maintain fierce rivalries with the Boston Celtics, Brooklyn Nets, Miami Heat and Indiana Pacers.
