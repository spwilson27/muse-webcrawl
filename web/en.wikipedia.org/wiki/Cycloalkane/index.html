Cycloalkane

Cycloalkanes (also called naphthenes, but distinct from naphthalene) are types of alkanes that have one or more rings of carbon atoms in the chemical structure of their molecules. Alkanes are types of organic hydrocarbon compounds that have only single chemical bonds in their chemical structure. Cycloalkanes consist of only carbon and hydrogen atoms and are saturated because all the carbon bonds contain hydrogen atoms.

Cycloalkanes are generally as reactive as or less reactive than alkanes, except for small-ring systems such as cyclopropane and cyclobutane, whose ring strain makes them noticeably more reactive.

Nomenclature

The unbranched, saturated cyclic hydrocarbons are called cycloalkanes. Their names generally retain the name of the corresponding open-chain alkane, prefixed with "cyclo-": thus cyclopropane, cyclobutane, cyclopentane, and so on. The generic name is CnH2n for monocyclic cycloalkanes, where n is the number of carbon atoms in the ring.

Structure and ring strain

In cycloalkanes, the carbon atoms are sp3 hybridized and thus prefer a tetrahedral arrangement with bond angles of about 109.5 degrees. Deviations from this ideal angle in small rings create ring strain (angle strain plus torsional strain). Cyclopropane, with bond angles compressed to 60 degrees, is highly strained and highly reactive. Cyclohexane can adopt a puckered chair conformation that is essentially strain-free, and is therefore the most common ring size in nature.

Isomerism

Cycloalkanes with a single ring have the general formula CnH2n, which makes them isomers of alkenes. They can also exhibit cis-trans isomerism when substituents lie on the same face or on opposite faces of the ring.

Occurrence and uses

Cycloalkanes occur in petroleum, where they are a significant class of components known as naphthenes; cyclopentane and cyclohexane are among the most abundant. Cyclohexane is an important industrial solvent and the feedstock for nylon production via cyclohexanone and cyclohexanol.
