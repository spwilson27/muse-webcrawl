Tyne Cot Commonwealth War Graves Cemetery and Memorial to the Missing is a burial ground for the dead of the First World War in the Ypres Salient on the Western Front. Located near Passendale in Belgium, it is the largest cemetery maintained by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission in terms of number of burials, with 11,965 graves, most of them unidentified. The Tyne Cot Memorial on its rear wall commemorates nearly 35,000 British and New Zealand servicemen killed in the salient after 16 August 1917 who have no known grave.

History

The name derives from "Tyne Cottage", a barn position named by Northumberland Fusiliers after the River Tyne. German pillboxes on the site were captured during the Battle of Passchendaele in October 1917, and the largest was used as a dressing station; burials began around it. Two of the captured concrete blockhouses remain within the cemetery, which was designed by Sir Herbert Baker after the war and visited by King George V on his 1922 pilgrimage to the cemeteries.

Memorial

The semicircular Tyne Cot Memorial forms the northeastern boundary wall and carries the names of 34,996 missing men, complementing the Menin Gate memorial in Ypres, which covers the missing before mid-August 1917. New Zealand's missing from the salient are commemorated here on a separate apse, New Zealand having chosen this site for its memorial to the missing of the sector.
