Active packaging

Active packaging
FieldPackaging technology / food science
DefinitionPackaging that interacts with its contents or environment to extend shelf life or improve quality
Common systemsOxygen scavengers, moisture absorbers, ethylene absorbers, antimicrobial films, CO₂ emitters

Active packaging, also known as interactive packaging, refers to packaging systems that deliberately interact with the packaged product or the headspace inside the package in order to extend shelf life, maintain or improve quality and safety, or provide convenience. Unlike passive packaging, which merely contains and protects, active packaging changes the conditions inside the pack over time.

Active components such as oxygen-scavenging sachets and pads help keep perishable foods fresher for longer.

Technologies

Major active packaging technologies include oxygen scavengers (often iron-based sachets used in packaged meats and baked goods), moisture absorbers and regulators, ethylene absorbers for fruit and vegetables, carbon dioxide emitters and absorbers, antimicrobial films and coatings releasing agents such as silver ions or essential oils, and antioxidant-releasing packaging. Active packaging is frequently combined with modified atmosphere packaging (MAP).

Related concepts

Active packaging is distinguished from intelligent (smart) packaging, which monitors and communicates the condition of the product — for example time–temperature indicators and freshness sensors — rather than acting on it, though commercial systems may combine both.
