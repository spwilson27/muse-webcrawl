Kandahar Province

Kandahar Province (Pashto: کندهار; Dari: قندهار) is one of the 34 provinces of Afghanistan, located in the southern part of the country, bordering Pakistan to the south. It is largely desert and semi-desert, watered by the Arghandab, Tarnak and Arghistan rivers. The provincial capital is the city of Kandahar, the second-largest city in Afghanistan and a historic trading hub on the routes between Persia, Central Asia and the Indian subcontinent.

Geography

The province lies on the Kandahar plateau at the edge of the Registan desert. The Arghandab River, dammed at the Dahla Dam north of the capital, supports irrigated agriculture producing pomegranates, grapes and melons for which the region is famous. Summers are extremely hot, with temperatures regularly above 40°C.

History

The region was part of ancient Arachosia and was conquered by Alexander the Great, who founded Alexandria Arachosia near modern Kandahar. The city later passed through Persian, Arab, Mongol and Mughal hands. In 1747 Ahmad Shah Durrani was crowned at Kandahar and made it the first capital of the Durrani Empire, founding modern Afghanistan. During the Soviet–Afghan War and the subsequent civil wars the province saw heavy fighting, and it was the birthplace of the Taliban movement in 1994 under Mullah Mohammed Omar. After 2001 it was a major focus of the NATO-led mission, and it returned to Taliban control in August 2021.

Districts

Districts include Arghandab, Arghistan, Daman, Ghorak, Khakrez, Maiwand, Maruf, Miyanishin, Nesh, Panjwai, Reg, Shah Wali Kot, Shorabak, Spin Boldak, Takhta Pul and Zhari, the latter two strongly associated with the Taliban's origins and with Canadian operations during the war.

Economy

Agriculture dominates, particularly fruit production; Kandahar's pomegranates are exported across the region. The border crossing at Spin Boldak–Chaman with Pakistan is a major trade and smuggling route.
