Battle of Nieuwpoort

The Battle of Nieuwpoort was a battle of the Eighty Years' War fought on 2 July 1600 between the forces of the Dutch Republic under Maurice of Nassau and the Spanish army of the Spanish Netherlands under Archduke Albert VII, on the dunes near Nieuwpoort on the Flemish coast (in present-day Belgium). It was the first major pitched battle won by Maurice's reformed army.

Background

The States General ordered Maurice to invade coastal Flanders to destroy the privateer base at Dunkirk, against his better judgment. After skirmishing around Ostend, Maurice's army was nearly trapped, with the sea at its back, when Albert marched to give battle.

The battle

After hard fighting in the dunes in which the Spanish tercios initially drove back the Dutch forward units, Maurice fed in his reserves in disciplined order. His reformed battalions, drilled to fire by rank, and his cavalry eventually broke the Spanish army, whose soldiers were exhausted by their long march in the heat.

Aftermath

Despite the triumph, Maurice could not capture Nieuwpoort or Dunkirk and withdrew to Ostend; the strategic gains were minimal, and the great Siege of Ostend followed. Militarily, however, Nieuwpoort demonstrated the effectiveness of Maurice's reforms and became a celebrated victory in the Dutch Republic.
