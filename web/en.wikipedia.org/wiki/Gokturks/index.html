Göktürks

The Göktürks, also known as the Celestial Turks or Blue Turks, were a nomadic confederation of Turkic peoples in medieval Inner Asia. Under the leadership of Bumin Qaghan and his sons, they established the First Turkic Khaganate in 552, succeeding the Rouran Khaganate as the dominant power of the Mongolian Plateau and rapidly building the largest contiguous land empire of its era, stretching from Manchuria to the Black Sea.

The name derives from the Ashina clan and the word türk combined with gök, "blue" or "celestial". The Göktürks were the first Turkic people to write their ethnonym in a script of their own: the Orkhon inscriptions, carved on stelae in the Orkhon Valley of Mongolia in the early 8th century, are the oldest surviving texts in a Turkic language, written in the Old Turkic (Orkhon) script.

History

The khaganate split in 603 into eastern and western halves amid civil war and pressure from Sui and Tang China. The Eastern Turkic Khaganate was subjugated by the Tang in 630, but was restored in 682 by Ilterish Qaghan as the Second Turkic Khaganate, which produced the Orkhon inscriptions under Bilge Khagan and Kul Tigin. The khaganate fell to the Uyghurs in 744. Göktürk influence persisted through successor states such as the Khazar Khaganate, and the name "Türk" spread as a general designation for Turkic peoples.

Relations with China and Byzantium

The Göktürks controlled key sections of the Silk Road and conducted diplomacy and trade with Sui and Tang China, Sasanian Persia, and the Byzantine Empire, with whom they allied against the Sasanians in the 620s.
