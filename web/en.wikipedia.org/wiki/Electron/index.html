Electron

[Image: a stylised atom — a central nucleus with electrons in glowing orbitals around it]

The electron is a subatomic particle with a negative elementary electric charge. It is a lepton, a fundamental particle with no known substructure, and it orbits (in quantum-mechanical terms, occupies orbitals around) atomic nuclei; the arrangement of electrons determines the chemical properties of the elements. Electrons flowing in conductors constitute electric current, and their behaviour underlies all of electronics.

The electron was identified as a particle by J. J. Thomson in 1897, in experiments with cathode rays at Cambridge; its charge was measured by Robert Millikan's oil-drop experiment (1909 onwards), and its wave nature was demonstrated by electron diffraction in 1927.

Properties and role

The electron's mass is about 1/1836 that of a proton. In quantum mechanics electrons exhibit wave–particle duality, obey the Pauli exclusion principle, and fill atomic shells — the basis of the periodic table and of chemical bonding, from covalent bonds sharing electrons to the 'sea' of free electrons that makes metals conduct.

Electron beams are used in old-style cathode-ray televisions, electron microscopes (which resolve far finer detail than light microscopes), and particle accelerators; the free-electron laser and electron lithography are tools of modern science and chip-making.
