Balkan League


The Balkan League member states, 1912.

The Balkan League was an alliance formed in 1912 by the Orthodox Christian states of the Balkans — Bulgaria, Serbia, Greece, and Montenegro — directed against the declining Ottoman Empire. Encouraged diplomatically by the Russian Empire, the League's members concluded a series of bilateral treaties in the spring and summer of 1912 and jointly went to war against the Ottomans in October 1912, beginning the First Balkan War.

Formation

The alliance grew out of negotiations conducted under Russian patronage, which sought a Balkan bloc that might also serve against Austria-Hungary. Serbia and Bulgaria signed a treaty of alliance on 13 March 1912, with a secret annex dividing Ottoman Macedonia between them — though the most contested zone was left for arbitration by the Russian Tsar. Bulgaria and Greece concluded a defensive treaty on 29 May 1912, deliberately leaving territorial questions unresolved. Montenegro joined through verbal agreements in the autumn, and on 8 October 1912 Montenegro declared war on the Ottoman Empire; the other allies followed on 17–18 October.

The First Balkan War

The League's armies, numbering roughly 750,000 men in total, overwhelmed the Ottoman forces in Europe. Bulgarian armies won at Kirk Kilisse and Lule Burgas and besieged Adrianople; Serbian forces triumphed at Kumanovo and took Bitola; Greek forces liberated Thessaloniki on 8 November 1912, narrowly ahead of a Bulgarian division, and advanced in Epirus; Montenegrin troops besieged Scutari. By the armistice of December 1912 the Ottomans retained only a few besieged enclaves in Europe.

The Treaty of London (30 May 1913) ceded nearly all Ottoman territory west of the Enos–Midye line to the allies but left the division of the spoils to the League members themselves.

Collapse

Disputes over Macedonia destroyed the League almost immediately. Serbia, denied an outlet on the Adriatic by the Great Powers' creation of Albania, demanded compensation in the zone assigned to Bulgaria; Greece and Serbia concluded an alliance against Bulgaria on 1 June 1913. On the night of 29–30 June 1913 Bulgarian forces attacked their former allies, opening the Second Balkan War. Romania, the Ottoman Empire, and later a Serbian-aligned Montenegro joined against Bulgaria, which was defeated and lost territory under the Treaty of Bucharest (10 August 1913). The League's collapse, and the resulting grievances, formed part of the background to the outbreak of the First World War in 1914.
