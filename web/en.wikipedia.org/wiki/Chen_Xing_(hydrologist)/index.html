Chen Xing, Chinese hydrologist

Chen Xing is a Chinese hydrologist whose work concerns hydrological processes, rainfall–runoff modelling and the assessment and management of water resources in river basins. His research belongs to the modern Chinese school of hydrology, which combines field observation with distributed hydrological models to address flood control, water supply and the effects of climate change and human activity on the water cycle.

Research

Chen's published work addresses topics central to contemporary hydrology in China, including the simulation of runoff generation in humid and semi-humid catchments, the evaluation of hydrological models under changing environmental conditions, and the analysis of extreme precipitation and flood frequency. Such studies are particularly relevant to China's major river basins, where monsoon rainfall, intensive land use and extensive water engineering make the separation of climatic and human influences on runoff a pressing scientific and practical problem.

Context

Chinese hydrology developed rapidly in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, driven by national priorities in flood prevention, hydropower development and water transfer projects. Researchers such as Chen Xing contribute to this programme through peer-reviewed studies of catchment behaviour, model calibration and uncertainty analysis, and the use of remote-sensing and reanalysis data to supplement gauge records in data-scarce regions.
