Beamwidth

Beamwidth is the angular separation between the points at which the radiated power of an antenna's main lobe falls to a specified fraction of its maximum, usually one half. In antenna theory, the term most commonly refers to the half-power beamwidth (HPBW), measured in a plane containing the direction of maximum radiation: the angle between the two directions at which the power density is half (i.e. −3 dB of) its peak value.

Definition and measurement

Beamwidth is normally quoted separately for the horizontal (azimuth) and vertical (elevation) planes. For an aperture antenna, the half-power beamwidth is approximately proportional to the wavelength divided by the aperture dimension in that plane (θ ≈ kλ/D, with k near 0.89–1.2 depending on illumination taper). A larger antenna, or a higher frequency, produces a narrower beam and correspondingly higher directivity and gain.

Applications

Beamwidth is a central specification in radar, satellite communications, radio astronomy and wireless networking, where it determines angular resolution, coverage area, interference rejection and pointing requirements. Radar engineers also use the first-null beamwidth — the angle between the first nulls on either side of the main lobe — which is roughly twice the half-power beamwidth for typical apertures.
