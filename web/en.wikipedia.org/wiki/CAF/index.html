CAF

CAF most commonly refers to the Confederation of African Football (French: Confédération Africaine de Football), the administrative and controlling body for association football, futsal and beach football in Africa. Founded in 1957 in Khartoum, Sudan, by the Egyptian, Ethiopian, South African and Sudanese football associations, CAF is one of FIFA's six continental confederations, representing 54 member associations. This article surveys CAF and other notable uses of the abbreviation.

Confederation of African Football

CAF organised the first Africa Cup of Nations in 1957, won by Egypt, and launched the African Cup of Champions Clubs (now the CAF Champions League) in 1964. African representation at the FIFA World Cup grew from a single playoff place in 1970 to five guaranteed places from 1998 and nine under the expanded 48-team format. Long-serving president Issa Hayatou (1988–2017) was succeeded by Ahmad Ahmad and then, in 2021, South African mining magnate Patrice Motsepe. CAF also runs the Women's Africa Cup of Nations, the African Nations Championship (CHAN) and youth tournaments.
