The 1926 Miami hurricane, popularly known as the Great Miami Hurricane, was a large and intense tropical cyclone that struck the Bahamas and the southeastern United States in September 1926. Its landfall in the Miami area on 18 September devastated the young city, killed at least 372 people, and effectively ended the Florida land boom of the 1920s.

Meteorological history

The storm developed east of the Lesser Antilles on 11 September, crossed the Turks and Caicos and the Bahamas as a major hurricane, and made landfall near Miami Beach in the early hours of 18 September with winds estimated at 150 mph and a pressure near 930 mbar. Many residents, unfamiliar with hurricanes, went outside during the calm passage of the eye and were caught by the storm's stronger back side; a storm surge of up to about 15 feet inundated Miami Beach. The cyclone crossed the Florida peninsula, entered the Gulf of Mexico, weakened, and struck the Alabama–Mississippi coast as a lesser storm before dissipating.

Impact

Miami, Hollywood, Fort Lauderdale, and other boomtowns were wrecked: thousands of buildings were damaged or destroyed, ships were driven ashore in Biscayne Bay, and the Red Cross reported over 370 deaths and more than 6,000 injuries in Florida alone. Damage totalled about $105 million in 1926 dollars; adjusted for inflation, wealth, and population, the storm is often ranked among the costliest U.S. hurricanes ever. Inland flooding around Lake Okeechobee, particularly at Moore Haven, killed dozens more.

Aftermath

The hurricane burst the speculative Florida land boom, sent banks reeling, and pushed Florida into depression years before the rest of the nation. It also spurred stronger building codes in South Florida and greater investment in hurricane forecasting and warning by the U.S. Weather Bureau.
