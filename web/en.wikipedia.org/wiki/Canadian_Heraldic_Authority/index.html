Canadian Heraldic Authority

The Canadian Heraldic Authority (French: Autorité héraldique du Canada) is the government body responsible for granting coats of arms, flags, badges and other heraldic emblems in Canada. It was established on June 4, 1988, when Queen Elizabeth II authorised the Governor General to exercise the royal prerogative for heraldry in Canada.

Role

The Authority, headquartered at Rideau Hall in Ottawa, is headed by the Herald Chancellor and the Chief Herald of Canada. It grants arms to Canadian citizens, municipalities, corporations and institutions, maintains the Public Register of Arms, Flags and Badges of Canada, and promotes a distinctly Canadian heraldic tradition that incorporates Indigenous, French and British symbols and motifs.

Grants

Grants are made by letters patent signed by the heralds and are published in the Canada Gazette. The Authority's herald painters and calligraphers produce the grant documents. Notable grants include arms for provinces, cities, universities, and individuals such as former prime ministers and governors general.
