Toyosu Market (Japanese: 豊洲市場, Toyosu Shijō) is a wholesale market in Toyosu, Kōtō Ward, Tokyo, Japan. It opened on October 11, 2018, replacing the famous Tsukiji Market as Tokyo's central wholesale market for fish, meat and produce, and is one of the largest wholesale seafood markets in the world.

Development and controversy

The market was built on reclaimed land in Tokyo Bay that had previously hosted a Tokyo Gas plant. The relocation from Tsukiji was debated for decades and was repeatedly delayed, most notably in 2016 when Tokyo governor Yuriko Koike postponed the opening after the discovery of toxic substances — including benzene and arsenic — in soil and groundwater beneath the site, prompting additional remediation works and a concrete sealing of the ground under the buildings. The move finally went ahead in October 2018; the last tuna auction at Tsukiji was held days before the transfer.

Facilities

The market occupies three main buildings on the island: the Fisheries Wholesale Market Building, the Fisheries Intermediate Wholesale Market Building, and the Fruit and Vegetables Market Building. Unlike the open-air Tsukiji inner market, Toyosu is a closed, temperature-controlled facility designed for modern hygiene standards. Its famous early-morning tuna auctions continue the Tsukiji tradition; visitors watch from a glass-enclosed observation deck and, by reservation, from a lower viewing corridor beside the auction floor. Restaurants and shops migrated from Tsukiji serve the sushi breakfasts popular with tourists.

Access

The market is served by Shijō-mae Station on the Yurikamome line. The adjacent commercial complex Senkyaku Banrai, with shops, restaurants and a hot-spring facility, opened in 2024 to draw visitors to the area.
