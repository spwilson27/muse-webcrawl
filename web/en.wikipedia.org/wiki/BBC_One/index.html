BBC One is a British free-to-air public broadcast television channel owned and operated by the BBC. It is the corporation's flagship channel in the United Kingdom and is funded principally by the television licence fee. The channel began life on 2 November 1936 as the BBC Television Service — the world's first regular scheduled television service with a high level of image resolution — broadcasting from Alexandra Palace in London.

History

Television broadcasting was suspended for most of the Second World War, resuming in 1946. The channel was renamed BBC1 in 1964, when BBC2 launched, and styled BBC One from 1997. Colour broadcasts began in 1969, and the channel converted fully to digital production and widescreen over the following decades. A high-definition simulcast, BBC One HD, launched in 2010, and regional variations serve Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland, and the English regions.

Programming

BBC One carries the BBC's most mainstream output: national news bulletins, the soap opera EastEnders (from 1985), dramas and entertainment such as Doctor Who and Strictly Come Dancing, comedy, documentaries, films, and major national events including royal occasions and state ceremonies. It historically held a large share of the UK television audience and remains among the most-watched channels in the country.

Presentation

The channel's on-screen identity has included the famous globe idents, the "Balloon" idents of 1997–2002, the red-themed "Rhythm & Movement" and "Circle" idents, and the "Oneness" and "Lens" idents of later years.
