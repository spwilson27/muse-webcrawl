Clear Lake (California)

Clear Lake (Native American: Lypoyomi) is a natural freshwater lake in Lake County in the U.S. state of California, north of Napa County and San Francisco. It is the largest natural freshwater lake wholly within the state, with a surface area of 68 square miles (180 km2) and a surface elevation of about 1,325 feet (404 m) above sea level. The lake is roughly 19 miles (31 km) long and 8 miles (13 km) wide at its widest point.

Clear Lake is believed to be one of the oldest lakes in North America; geological evidence suggests a lake has existed at the site for at least 450,000 years, and possibly as long as 2.5 million years. It sits atop a slowly subsiding basin of volcanic and faulted terrain near Mount Konocti, a dormant volcano that forms its most prominent landmark. The lake's only natural outlet is Cache Creek, which flows eastward toward the Sacramento Valley.

Ecology

The lake is naturally eutrophic and supports abundant aquatic life, including largemouth bass, crappie, bluegill, channel catfish, and the native Clear Lake hitch, a minnow species found nowhere else. Its warm, nutrient-rich waters, mild climate, and long growing season drew Pomo peoples to its shores for thousands of years; the lake region remains culturally significant to the Pomo tribes of Lake County.

Issues

Clear Lake has experienced recurring harmful algal blooms, in part because of nutrient loading from surrounding development and agriculture, and elevated levels of cyanotoxins have periodically forced drinking-water advisories for lakeside communities that draw water directly from the lake. The abandoned Sulphur Bank Mercury Mine on its eastern shore, a federal Superfund site, contaminated sediments with mercury and acid mine drainage during the twentieth century.

Towns on its shores include Lakeport, the county seat, on the western shore, and Clearlake on the southeastern arm.
