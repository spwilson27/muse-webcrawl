All That Jazz is a 1979 American musical drama film directed by Bob Fosse and starring Roy Scheider. The screenplay, by Robert Alan Aurthur and Fosse, is a semi-autobiographical fantasy based on aspects of Fosse's life and career as a dancer, choreographer and director. The film was inspired by Fosse's manic effort to edit his film Lenny while simultaneously staging the 1975 Broadway musical Chicago.

The film portrays Joe Gideon, a workaholic theatre director and choreographer juggling a Broadway production, a film edit, his ex-wife, his girlfriend, his daughter, and a regimen of pills, cigarettes and show-stopping fantasies, all while conversing with Angelique, a flirtatious angel of death played by Jessica Lange.

Reception and legacy

All That Jazz won four Academy Awards (including Best Art Direction, Best Costume Design, Best Film Editing and Best Original Song Score) from nine nominations, and shared the Palme d'Or at the 1980 Cannes Film Festival with Akira Kurosawa's Kagemusha. In 2001 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".
