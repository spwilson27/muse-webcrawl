Toddler

A toddler is a child approximately 12 to 36 months old, though definitions vary. The toddler period is a time of rapid physical growth, motor development, and cognitive and social change, named for the characteristic unsteady "toddling" gait of a child learning to walk.

Development

During the toddler years, children typically progress from crawling and pulling to stand, to walking, running, and climbing. Fine motor skills develop as children learn to stack blocks, turn pages, and use simple utensils. Language development is rapid: most toddlers move from single words to combining words into short phrases, and their vocabulary may grow from a handful of words to several hundred.

Behaviour and care

Toddlers assert growing independence, and the period is sometimes associated with tantrums and the so-called "terrible twos". Toilet training usually begins during this stage. Nutrition, sleep (typically 11–14 hours including naps), and childproofing against household hazards are central concerns for caregivers.

Milestones

Developmental screening uses milestones such as walking independently by about 15 months, using two-word phrases by age two, and engaging in pretend play. Variation within a broad normal range is common, but significant delays may prompt evaluation for developmental disorders.
