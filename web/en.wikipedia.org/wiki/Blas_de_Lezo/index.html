Blas de Lezo y Olavarrieta (3 February 1689 – 7 September 1741) was a Spanish naval officer best remembered for his successful defence of Cartagena de Indias against a much larger British fleet in 1741. Despite losing a leg, an eye and the use of an arm in earlier battles, he became one of Spain's most celebrated admirals.

Early career

Lezo joined the Spanish Navy as a midshipman in 1702. During the War of the Spanish Succession he lost his left leg to cannon fire at the Battle of Vélez-Málaga in 1704, lost his left eye in the defence of Toulon in 1707, and lost the use of his right arm at the siege of Barcelona in 1714. He later served as a privateer in the Pacific, capturing British and Dutch vessels off South America.

Cartagena de Indias

In the Battle of Cartagena de Indias during the War of Jenkins' Ear, Lezo's roughly 3,600 defenders resisted Admiral Edward Vernon's force of some 186 ships and nearly 30,000 men. After weeks of siege, disease and failed assaults forced the British to withdraw in May 1741. Lezo died of typhoid fever a few months later.

Legacy

Monuments to Lezo stand in Madrid and Cartagena, and the Spanish Navy has named several warships after him.
