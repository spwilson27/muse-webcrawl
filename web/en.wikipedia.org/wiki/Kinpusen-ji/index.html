Kinpusen-ji (Japanese: 金峯山寺) is the head temple of the Shugendō religion's Kinpusen tradition, located on Mount Yoshino in Nara Prefecture, Japan. According to tradition it was founded by En no Gyōja, the legendary founder of Shugendō, in the 7th century. It is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range".

Zaōdō

The temple's main hall, the Zaōdō, is the second-largest wooden structure in Japan after the Daibutsuden at Tōdai-ji, measuring about 34 metres high and 28 metres across. It houses three massive statues of Zaō Gongen—7-metre-tall fierce blue deities representing the past, present, and future Buddhas—normally kept hidden and displayed only on special occasions. The present hall dates to 1592, built under Toyotomi Hideyoshi.

Shugendō and Mount Yoshino

Kinpusen-ji is the principal centre for yamabushi (mountain ascetics) practising austerities along the Ōmine Okugake pilgrimage route to Mount Sanjō and Ōminesan-ji. Mount Yoshino, sacred to Shugendō, is also Japan's most famous cherry-blossom site, with some 30,000 trees planted in groves up the slopes—a tradition linked to Zaō Gongen devotion—and the temple grounds are thronged each spring.

History

The temple flourished under imperial and aristocratic patronage in the Heian period and was entangled in medieval conflicts, including Go-Daigo's Southern Court at Yoshino during the Nanboku-chō period. Suppressed with the rest of Shugendō in the early Meiji period (1870s), the temple was revived as an independent religious body after the Second World War. In keeping with ancient taboos, the inner sanctuary mountain (Mount Sanjō) remains closed to women, a tradition that has drawn modern debate.
