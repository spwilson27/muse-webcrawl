Four-velocity

The four-velocity of an object is its rate of change of four-position with respect to proper time along its world line. It is the relativistic generalisation of ordinary (three-)velocity and is a fundamental quantity in the theory of special relativity and general relativity.

Definition

If the four-position of an object is X(τ) = (ct, x, y, z), parameterised by the proper time τ measured by a clock moving with the object, then the four-velocity is U = dX/dτ. In terms of the ordinary velocity v measured in some inertial frame, the components are U = γ(c, vx, vy, vz), where γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²) is the Lorentz factor.

Properties

Because proper time is defined by the spacetime interval, the magnitude of the four-velocity is constant: the inner product of U with itself equals c² (in the +−−− metric signature). In the object's own rest frame, the four-velocity is simply (c, 0, 0, 0), expressing that the object moves through time at the speed of light even when it is at rest in space. Unlike ordinary velocity, the four-velocity always exists and is finite even for objects moving close to light speed, and it transforms as a genuine four-vector under Lorentz transformations.

Use

Multiplying the four-velocity by mass gives the four-momentum, whose time component is energy divided by c and whose spatial components are the relativistic momentum. Differentiating the four-velocity with respect to proper time gives the four-acceleration.
