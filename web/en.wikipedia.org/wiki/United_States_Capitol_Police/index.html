The United States Capitol Police (USCP) is a federal law enforcement agency in the legislative branch of the United States government charged with protecting the United States Congress and the United States Capitol complex in Washington, D.C. It is one of the oldest federal police forces in the country, tracing its origins to the appointment of a single watchman for the Capitol in 1801 and its formal establishment in 1828.

Duties

The Capitol Police are responsible for security throughout the Capitol grounds and the House and Senate office buildings, the Library of Congress buildings, and other facilities, covering an area of about 270 acres. Officers staff screening checkpoints, patrol the grounds, protect congressional leadership, and investigate threats against members of Congress — a protective mission whose jurisdiction extends across the United States. The force includes specialised units such as the Containment and Emergency Response Team, K-9 units, a hazardous devices section, and the First Responders Unit.

Notable events

Capitol Police officers have been killed in the line of duty in several attacks, including the 1998 shooting at the Capitol in which officers Jacob Chestnut and John Gibson were killed. The force played the central defensive role during the January 6, 2021 attack on the Capitol, during which more than 140 officers were injured; Officer Brian Sicknick died the following day. The attack spurred reforms, leadership changes, and expanded authority for the Capitol Police chief to request emergency assistance.
