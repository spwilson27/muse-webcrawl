British Indians

Total populationAbout 1.86 million (2021 census, England and Wales); 3.1% of the population
RegionsLondon, Leicester, Birmingham, Manchester, West Yorkshire

ReligionsHinduism, Sikhism, Islam, Christianity, Jainism, others

British Indians are citizens and residents of the United Kingdom whose ancestry traces to India. They form the largest single subgroup within the British Asian population and one of the largest Indian diaspora communities in the world. According to the 2021 census, about 1.86 million people in England and Wales identified as Indian, around 3.1 per cent of the population.

History

Indian migration to Britain dates to the era of the East India Company, when seamen (lascars), servants, ayahs and students came in small numbers. Mass migration began after the Second World War, driven by labour shortages in Britain and by the Partition of India in 1947. Large communities settled in London, the West Midlands, Leicester and the textile towns of northern England. In the late 1960s and early 1970s, many Indians arrived from East Africa — notably from Kenya and Uganda, from where Asians were expelled by Idi Amin in 1972 — bringing established business communities to cities such as Leicester.

Demographics and culture

British Indians are religiously diverse: Hindus form the largest group, followed by Sikhs, Muslims, Christians and smaller Jain, Buddhist and Parsi communities. Languages spoken include Punjabi, Gujarati, Hindi, Urdu, Bengali and Tamil alongside English. The community is prominent in medicine, business, law, retail and politics; people of Indian origin have served as cabinet ministers, and Rishi Sunak became the first British Indian Prime Minister in 2022. Festivals such as Diwali in Leicester are among the largest outside India.

Socioeconomics

British Indians are frequently cited in official statistics as among the highest-achieving ethnic groups in education and professional employment, with median earnings above the national average, though outcomes vary significantly by religion, region and migration history.
