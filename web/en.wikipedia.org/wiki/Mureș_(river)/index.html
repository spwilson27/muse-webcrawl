The Mureș (Hungarian: Maros) is a river in Eastern Europe, flowing through Romania and Hungary. At 789 kilometres, it is one of the most important rivers of the Carpathian Basin and the longest tributary of the Tisza.

Course

The Mureș rises in the Giurgeu Mountains of the Eastern Carpathians in Harghita County, Romania. It flows westward through Transylvania, passing Târgu Mureș, Alba Iulia, and Arad, then crosses into Hungary, where it joins the Tisza at Szeged.

History

Known in antiquity as the Marisus, the river formed part of the frontier of Roman Dacia. Its valley served as a major transport and trade corridor, and salt and timber were historically floated down its course.
