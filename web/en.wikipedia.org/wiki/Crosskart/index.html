Crosskart

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A crosskart is a type of lightweight off-road racing vehicle, essentially a strengthened go-kart fitted with long-travel suspension, a roll cage, and knobbly tyres for use on dirt, gravel, and autocross-style circuits. Crosskarts are sometimes described as a cross between a kart and a small off-road buggy.

Design

Crosskarts are typically powered by motorcycle engines, commonly in the 450–750 cm³ range, driving the rear wheels through a chain. Unlike circuit karts, they have full independent suspension with substantial wheel travel, a protective steel roll cage, a racing seat with a multi-point harness, and bodywork intended to deflect mud and stones. Their power-to-weight ratio is high, giving rapid acceleration on loose surfaces.

Competition

Crosskart racing is popular in Scandinavia and elsewhere in northern Europe, where national championships are run alongside rallycross and folkrace events. Classes are divided by engine capacity, including junior categories for younger drivers. The vehicles are also used as an inexpensive stepping stone toward rallying and rallycross, and crosskart-style machines form the basis of the FIA's Cross Car category in rallying.
