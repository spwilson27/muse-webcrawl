House of Omri

The House of Omri (Hebrew: בית עמרי‎, Beit Omri) was the ruling dynasty of the northern Kingdom of Israel in the 9th century BCE, founded by King Omri (reigned c. 885–874 BCE). Assyrian inscriptions long afterwards called Israel Bit Khumri, "House of Omri".

History

Omri, an army commander, seized the throne after a civil war and founded Samaria as his capital. His son Ahab expanded the kingdom, allied with Phoenicia through marriage to Jezebel, and fought the Arameans and Assyrians; Ahab appears in the Assyrian records of the Battle of Qarqar (853 BCE). The dynasty continued under Ahaziah and Jehoram until Jehu's revolt in 841 BCE exterminated the royal line.

The biblical account in the Books of Kings is hostile to the Omrides, condemning their promotion of Baal worship, while archaeology — including the Mesha Stele and the Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III — shows the dynasty ruled a powerful regional state.
