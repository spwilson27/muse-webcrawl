Congenital iodine deficiency syndrome

Congenital iodine deficiency syndrome, formerly known by the now-avoided term "cretinism", is a condition of severely stunted physical and mental growth caused by untreated congenital deficiency of thyroid hormones, usually because the mother and fetus lacked sufficient dietary iodine during pregnancy.

Cause

Thyroid hormones are essential for fetal brain development, and their production requires iodine. In regions with iodine-poor soils—historically mountainous areas such as the Alps, Himalayas, and parts of central Africa and Asia—severe deficiency was endemic. The condition differs from congenital hypothyroidism due to thyroid gland defects, though the two share features and are detected by the same newborn screening tests.

Features

Endemic cases were traditionally divided into a neurological type, with intellectual disability, deaf-mutism, and spasticity, and a myxoedematous type, with dwarfism, hypothyroid features, and delayed development. Severity depends on the timing and degree of deficiency.

Prevention and treatment

The syndrome is one of the world's most preventable causes of intellectual disability. Universal salt iodisation, promoted by UNICEF and the World Health Organization, has eliminated endemic deficiency in most countries; iodine supplements and iodised oil are used where salt iodisation is impractical. Where hypothyroidism is present, levothyroxine treatment begun early improves outcomes.
