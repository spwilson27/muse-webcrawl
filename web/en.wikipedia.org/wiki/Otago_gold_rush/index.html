Otago gold rush

Dates1861 – mid-1860s (later finds into the 1870s)
LocationOtago, South Island, New Zealand
TriggerGabriel Read's discovery at Gabriel's Gully, May 1861

The Otago gold rush (often called the Central Otago gold rush) was a gold rush that occurred during the 1860s in Central Otago, New Zealand. It was the country's biggest gold strike and transformed the young Otago province: thousands of miners arrived from Australia and further afield, Dunedin briefly became New Zealand's largest and richest city, and the University of Otago was founded with gold wealth.

Gold miners at work in Central Otago in the 1860s

Discovery and spread

Although small amounts of gold had been reported earlier, the rush began in May 1861 when Gabriel Read found payable gold at Gabriel's Gully near present-day Lawrence. Further major finds followed along the Tuapeka, Dunstan (Clyde), Wakatipu — where gold was found in the Shotover and Arrow rivers in 1862 — and the West Coast rushes soon after drew many miners away. Boom towns such as Queenstown, Arrowtown and Naseby sprang up; Arrowtown and other fields attracted significant numbers of Chinese miners, invited by the provincial council from 1865 despite growing local prejudice.

Legacy

As alluvial gold was exhausted, companies moved to hydraulic sluicing and quartz reef mining, scarring landscapes that are now heritage features. Gold money funded Dunedin's grand Victorian buildings, and the goldfields are commemorated in the Otago Goldfields Heritage Trail.
