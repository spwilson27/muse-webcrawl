The Archivo General de Indias (Spanish for "General Archive of the Indies") is the principal repository of documents relating to the Spanish Empire in the Americas and the Philippines. Housed in the Casa Lonja de Mercaderes in Seville, it was created in 1785 by King Charles III to centralise the records of Spain's overseas administration, and it remains one of the most important archives in the world for the colonial history of the Americas.

History

Before 1785 the records of the Council of the Indies, the Casa de Contratación, the consulates, and the various ministries were scattered between Simancas, Cádiz, and Madrid, and the archive at Simancas had run out of room. At the urging of the historian Juan Bautista Muñoz, Charles III ordered the papers transferred to the merchants' exchange (lonja) of Seville, a grand Renaissance building designed by Juan de Herrera and built from 1584 beside the cathedral. The first consignments of documents arrived in October 1785.

Collections

The archive occupies some nine kilometres of shelving, with about 43,000 bundles (legajos) containing roughly 80 million pages dating from 1492 into the nineteenth century. Its sections include the records of the Council of the Indies, the Casa de Contratación (which regulated trade and emigration), the treasury offices of the colonies, the state secretariats, the courts (audiencias) of Santo Domingo, Mexico, Guatemala, Guadalajara, Quito, Lima, Charcas, Chile, Buenos Aires and others, the Captaincy General of Cuba, and the mail service. Among its treasures are Columbus's correspondence and the Capitulations of Santa Fe, the papers of Cortés, Pizarro, and Magellan, Cervantes's application for a post in the Indies, maps and plans of colonial cities, and the original of the Treaty of Tordesillas.

Heritage status

Together with Seville Cathedral and the Royal Alcázar, the archive building was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1987. Much of the collection has been digitised and is accessible through the Spanish Archives Portal (PARES).
