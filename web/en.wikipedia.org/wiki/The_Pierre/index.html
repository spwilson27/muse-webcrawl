The Pierre is a luxury hotel located at 2 East 61st Street, at the intersection of Fifth Avenue and 61st Street on the Upper East Side of Manhattan in New York City, facing Central Park. The 41-story, 525-foot (160 m) building opened in 1930 and contains both hotel rooms and private cooperative residences. It has been operated by Taj Hotels, part of India's Tata Group, since 2005, under the name The Pierre, A Taj Hotel, New York.

History

The hotel was founded by Charles Pierre Casalasco, an Italian immigrant who had worked his way from busboy to celebrated restaurateur in New York, and a group of Wall Street financiers including Otto H. Kahn. Designed by the architects Schultze & Weaver — known for grand hotels including the Waldorf-Astoria — the tower is clad in limestone and brick with a mansard roof crowned by a distinctive copper-clad top, modelled in part on French Renaissance châteaux. The Pierre opened on October 1, 1930, at a cost of about $15 million, just as the Great Depression was deepening, and the venture soon went into receivership.

Oilman J. Paul Getty acquired the hotel in 1938. In 1959 a substantial part of the building was converted into cooperative apartments, whose residents have included some of the city's wealthiest families; the remaining rooms continued to operate as a hotel. The Pierre hosted notable long-term and famous residents and guests over the decades, and its ballrooms — including the Cotillion Ballroom, the Grand Ballroom, and the rotunda with its trompe-l'œil murals painted in the 1960s by Edward Melcarth — became fixtures of New York society events and charity galas.

The 1972 robbery

On January 2, 1972, the Pierre was the scene of what was then recorded as the largest hotel robbery in history: a crew of eight robbers associated with the Lucchese crime family took staff and guests hostage and looted safe-deposit boxes of cash, jewellery and securities valued at an estimated $3 million (some estimates run far higher). Most of the property was never recovered. The heist was later recounted in the book The Pierre Hotel Affair.

Today

The hotel underwent a major renovation completed in 2009. Alongside guest rooms and suites it maintains restaurants, the Two E bar, and event spaces, while roughly a third of the building remains private cooperative residences. Its penthouse — a triplex atop the tower — has figured in widely reported listings, at one point offered at $125 million, among the highest asking prices for a New York residence.
