The Swiss Family Robinson

AuthorJohann David Wyss
Published1812
Original titleDer Schweizerische Robinson
GenreAdventure novel

The Swiss Family Robinson (German: Der Schweizerische Robinson) is an adventure novel by Swiss pastor Johann David Wyss, first published in 1812. It recounts the adventures of a Swiss family — a father, mother and four sons (Fritz, Ernest, Jack and Franz) — shipwrecked in the East Indies on their way to Port Jackson, Australia.

Plot and character

Unlike Robinson Crusoe, its inspiration, the novel is a family story intended as instruction for Wyss's own children: the father uses every episode — salvaging the wreck, building a treehouse, taming animals, surviving monsoons — as a lesson in natural history, self-reliance and faith. The island is populated with an implausible mixture of animals from every continent, a liberty that later editors and critics frequently noted.

Editions and adaptations

The book was edited for publication by Wyss's son Johann Rudolf Wyss. The fullest English versions derive from the expanded French adaptation by Isabelle de Montolieu. Countless abridgements followed, and the story was filmed many times — most famously Walt Disney's 1960 live-action film with its celebrated treehouse, later recreated as an attraction at Disney theme parks.
