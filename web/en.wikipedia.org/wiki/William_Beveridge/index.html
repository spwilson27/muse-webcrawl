William Henry Beveridge, 1st Baron Beveridge (5 March 1879 – 16 March 1963), was a British economist, social reformer, and civil servant best known for his 1942 report Social Insurance and Allied Services — the Beveridge Report — which provided the blueprint for the post-war welfare state in the United Kingdom, including the National Health Service.

Career and report

Beveridge directed the London School of Economics from 1919 to 1937 and was Master of University College, Oxford. Commissioned in 1941 to review social insurance, his report identified "five giants" to be slain: Want, Disease, Ignorance, Squalor, and Idleness. It proposed a comprehensive system of flat-rate social insurance contributions in return for flat-rate benefits covering unemployment, sickness, retirement, and child allowances. The report became a bestseller, and the Labour government elected in 1945 implemented most of its recommendations.

Later life

Beveridge served briefly as a Liberal MP for Berwick-upon-Tweed (1944–45) and was raised to the peerage in 1946, leading the Liberals in the House of Lords.
