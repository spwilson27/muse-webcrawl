Ritchie Blackmore

Richard Hugh Blackmore (born 14 April 1945) is an English guitarist. He was a founding member of Deep Purple in 1968; the band's early fusion of hard rock and classical influences, along with Blackmore's riffs such as "Smoke on the Water", helped define heavy metal guitar playing. He left Deep Purple in 1975 to form Rainbow, rejoined Deep Purple in 1984, and left again in 1993.

Career

Blackmore began as a session guitarist in the 1960s, working with producer Joe Meek and playing in The Outlaws and with Screaming Lord Sutch. With Deep Purple he recorded landmark albums including Machine Head (1972). Rainbow, initially a collaboration with singer Ronnie James Dio, produced songs such as "Man on the Silver Mountain" and "Stargazer", later becoming more commercial under Graham Bonnet and Joe Lynn Turner.

Blackmore's Night and legacy

Since 1997 Blackmore has performed Renaissance and folk-inspired music with his wife Candice Night in Blackmore's Night. In 2016 he briefly revived Rainbow for rock concerts. Blackmore was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Deep Purple in 2016 and is widely ranked among the greatest guitarists of all time.
