The Truman Show

The Truman Show is a 1998 American psychological comedy-drama film directed by Peter Weir, produced by Scott Rudin, and written by Andrew Niccol. It stars Jim Carrey as Truman Burbank, a man whose entire life is a television show broadcast around the clock to a worldwide audience without his knowledge.

Plot

Truman lives in the idyllic seaside town of Seahaven, unaware that it is an enormous studio dome, that everyone around him — including his wife and best friend — is an actor, and that the show's creator, Christof (Ed Harris), controls the weather and every detail of his world from a control room in the artificial sky. As Truman notices inconsistencies and remembers a woman who tried to tell him the truth, he overcomes his manufactured fear of water, sails to the edge of the dome, and walks through an exit door into the real world.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success and is frequently cited as prescient of reality television, social media and life under constant surveillance. Carrey won a Golden Globe for his dramatic performance, and the term "Truman Show delusion" entered psychiatric discussion for patients who believe their lives are staged.
