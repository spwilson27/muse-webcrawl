Bergen School of Meteorology

The Bergen School's polar front model of the mid-latitude cyclone.

The Bergen School of Meteorology was a group of meteorologists based at the Geophysical Institute in Bergen, Norway, who in the years after the First World War revolutionised weather forecasting by developing the polar front theory of mid-latitude cyclones and the frontal methods of weather analysis still used today.

Founding

The school formed around the Norwegian physicist Vilhelm Bjerknes, who had earlier founded modern numerical weather prediction theory in Leipzig and in 1917 took charge of geophysics in Bergen. In 1918, partly to forecast weather for Norway's coastal fisheries amid wartime shortages of upper-air data, Bjerknes and his assistants built a dense network of surface observation stations along the Norwegian coast. The close spacing of observations revealed sharp boundaries between air masses that earlier, sparser maps had smoothed away.

The polar front and cyclone model

Working with his son Jacob Bjerknes, Halvor Solberg and later Tor Bergeron, Carl-Gustaf Rossby and Erik Palmén, the Bergen group argued that mid-latitude weather is governed by the polar front — the boundary between cold polar air and warm tropical air. Jacob Bjerknes's 1919 paper described the structure of moving cyclones, and the group's 1922 life-cycle model traced a cyclone from a wave on the front through maturity to occlusion, introducing the concepts and terminology of warm fronts, cold fronts and occluded fronts. Bergeron later added the three-dimensional analysis of air masses and the Bergeron process of precipitation formation.

Legacy

The Bergen frontal methods spread through Europe and North America in the 1920s and 1930s, carried by the school's many students, and became the standard conceptual framework of synoptic meteorology. Vilhelm Bjerknes's earlier dream of numerical prediction was eventually realised with electronic computers, making Bergen a birthplace of both the frontal model and the mathematical approach to forecasting.
