Creedence Clearwater Revival, commonly abbreviated as CCR or Creedence, was an American rock band formed in El Cerrito, California, in 1967. The band consisted of lead vocalist, lead guitarist, and primary songwriter John Fogerty, his brother rhythm guitarist Tom Fogerty, bassist Stu Cook, and drummer Doug Clifford. Their musical style encompassed roots rock, swamp rock, blues rock, Southern rock and country rock.

History

The group had played together since 1959, first as the Blue Velvets and then as the Golliwogs, before adopting the name Creedence Clearwater Revival in 1967. Signed to Fantasy Records, they broke through in 1968 with a cover of Dale Hawkins's "Suzie Q". Between 1969 and 1971 the band released five studio albums — Bayou Country, Green River, Willy and the Poor Boys, Cosmo's Factory and Pendulum — producing hits including "Proud Mary", "Bad Moon Rising", "Green River", "Down on the Corner", "Fortunate Son", "Travelin' Band", "Up Around the Bend" and "Have You Ever Seen the Rain?". They also had a number-one hit with their version of "I Heard It Through the Grapevine" and performed at the Woodstock festival in 1969.

Breakup and legacy

Tom Fogerty left in 1971, and the band's final album, Mardi Gras (1972), was panned; CCR disbanded later that year amid legal and personal disputes, chiefly between John Fogerty and Fantasy Records owner Saul Zaentz. Despite a career of barely five years, the band sold more than 45 million records worldwide and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1993. Their songs remain staples of classic rock radio and are closely associated with the Vietnam War era.
