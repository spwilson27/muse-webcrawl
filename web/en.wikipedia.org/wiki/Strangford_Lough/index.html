Strangford Lough is a large sea lough or inlet in County Down, in the east of Northern Ireland. It is the largest sea lough in Ireland, covering an area of about 150 square kilometres (58 sq mi). The lough is almost fully enclosed by the Ards Peninsula and is connected to the Irish Sea through a long narrow channel known as Strangford Narrows, where some of the fastest tidal currents in Europe occur.

Geography

The lough contains more than seventy named islands, many of them drumlins drowned by rising sea levels after the last Ice Age, together with numerous smaller islets and rocky plinths at low water. The main town on its shores is Killyleagh, with other settlements including Portaferry on the Ards Peninsula side and Strangford village at the narrows entrance.

Wildlife and conservation

Strangford Lough is an important wildlife site, designated as a Special Area of Conservation, a Special Protection Area and a Ramsar site. It supports extensive mudflats, saltmarsh and eelgrass beds, and is of international importance for wintering wildfowl, notably pale-bellied brent geese that migrate from Arctic Canada. The lough also supports common seals and a rich marine fauna in its rocky reefs.

Tidal energy

The fast tidal stream in the Narrows was the site of SeaGen, installed in 2008, which at the time was the world's first large-scale commercial tidal stream generator supplying electricity to the grid.
