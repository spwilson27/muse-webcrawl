Danmarks Nationalbank

Danmarks Nationalbank (in Danish often simply Nationalbanken) is the central bank of the Kingdom of Denmark. It is a non-eurozone member of the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and is responsible for maintaining a stable monetary system in Denmark, the Faroe Islands and Greenland.

History

The bank was founded on 1 August 1818 by King Frederick VI. It was granted a 90-year exclusive right to issue banknotes, a right that was repeatedly extended. Danmarks Nationalbank became fully independent of the government under the Nationalbank Act of 1936.

Functions

The bank's primary objective is to ensure stable prices. As Denmark pursues a fixed exchange rate policy, the krone is pegged to the euro within the ERM II framework, and the bank's interest rate policy is directed at keeping the krone stable against the euro. Danmarks Nationalbank also issues Danish banknotes and coins, manages the government's debt, and oversees the stability of the financial system.

Building

The bank's headquarters in central Copenhagen, completed in 1978, was designed by the architect Arne Jacobsen and is regarded as one of his late masterpieces. The building is a listed monument.
