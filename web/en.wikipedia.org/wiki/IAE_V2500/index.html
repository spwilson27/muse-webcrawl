IAE V2500

TypeTurbofan
ManufacturerInternational Aero Engines
First run1987
Major applicationsAirbus A320 family, McDonnell Douglas MD-90, Embraer KC-390

The IAE V2500 is a two-shaft high-bypass turbofan engine produced by International Aero Engines (IAE), a consortium originally formed by Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce, Japanese Aero Engine Corporation and MTU Aero Engines. The "V" denotes the five founding partners (including FiatAvio, which later withdrew) and "2500" its original 25,000 lbf thrust class.

Development and design

The engine was developed to power the Airbus A320 and first ran in 1987, entering service in 1989. It has a single-stage fan, a 10-stage high-pressure compressor derived in part from Rolls-Royce research, an annular combustor and two-stage high- and five-stage low-pressure turbines. Variants range from about 22,000 to 33,000 lbf of thrust (the V2533-A5). It also powers the McDonnell Douglas MD-90 and was selected for the Embraer KC-390 military transport.

Market

The V2500 competes with the CFM56 on the A320 family and has accumulated one of the largest installed bases of any civil engine, with several thousand engines delivered; the improved V2500-E5 serves the KC-390.
