BBC Weather is the department of the British Broadcasting Corporation responsible for the preparation and broadcasting of weather forecasts across the BBC's television, radio and online services. It is one of the most widely recognised weather brands in the United Kingdom, and its online service is among the most visited weather websites in the world.

History

The BBC broadcast its first weather forecast on 14 November 1922, read from information supplied by the Met Office. Television weather forecasts began in 1949, initially using hand-drawn charts. The service became famous for a number of long-serving presenters, and for the introduction of computer-generated weather graphics in the 1980s.

For most of its history, the BBC's forecasts were supplied by the Met Office. In 2018, following a competitive tender, the contract passed to MeteoGroup, a private weather company. In 2025 the BBC announced that the contract would return to the Met Office, reuniting the two organisations after a seven-year interval.

Services

BBC Weather provides forecasts on BBC One, BBC Two, the BBC News channel, BBC World News, national and local radio stations, and through the BBC Weather website and mobile app. Forecasts are presented from the weather studio at Broadcasting House in London, with regional centres around the UK providing local bulletins.

Presenters

Well-known presenters have included Michael Fish, remembered for his 1987 broadcast ahead of the Great Storm, as well as Carol Kirkwood, Tomasz Schafernaker, Matt Taylor and Stav Danaos. The service has also been noted for the presenters' informal style compared with other broadcasters.
