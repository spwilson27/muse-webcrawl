1972 Munich massacre

DateSeptember 5–6, 1972
LocationMunich, West Germany
AttackersBlack September
Deaths11 Israeli team members, 1 West German police officer, 5 attackers

The 1972 Munich massacre was a terrorist attack during the Summer Olympics in Munich, West Germany. On September 5, 1972, eight members of the Palestinian group Black September entered the Olympic Village, killed two members of the Israeli team and took nine others hostage, demanding the release of prisoners held by Israel and two left-wing militants held in West Germany.

Hostage crisis and rescue failure

The attackers were transported with their hostages to Fürstenfeldbruck Air Base after negotiations for a flight out of Germany. A poorly prepared rescue attempt by Bavarian police ended in a firefight. All nine remaining Israeli hostages were killed, as were five of the attackers and West German police officer Anton Fliegerbauer. Three surviving attackers were later released after a Lufthansa hijacking.

Aftermath

The Games were suspended for a memorial service and then resumed, a decision still debated. The massacre exposed grave weaknesses in Olympic security and led to major changes in host-city policing. Israel responded with an assassination campaign against those it held responsible. The victims were commemorated prominently at later Olympics, including during the Tokyo 2020 opening ceremony's moment of silence.
