The Mars Society is a nonprofit organization that advocates for human exploration and colonization of the planet Mars. Founded in 1998 by aerospace engineer Robert Zubrin and others at a convention at the University of Colorado Boulder attended by about 700 people, the society promotes the Mars Direct mission architecture — a plan for economical crewed Mars missions using existing technology and in-situ resource utilization — and works to build public and political support through chapters worldwide, conferences, publications, and research projects.

Research stations

The society operates Mars-analog research stations where volunteer crews live and work under simulated Martian conditions. The Flashline Mars Arctic Research Station (FMARS), established in 2000 on Devon Island in the Canadian Arctic near the Haughton impact crater, was the first such station. The Mars Desert Research Station (MDRS) in the Utah desert, opened in 2001, hosts rotating two-week crews conducting geology, biology, and engineering studies in Mars-like terrain. Further stations have been developed in Iceland and Australia.

Activities

The society holds an annual International Mars Society Convention, publishes the Journal of Mars Society materials and conference proceedings, and has run competitions including the University Rover Challenge, in which student teams design rovers for simulated Mars missions. It has also supported mission proposals and analog studies relevant to long-duration exploration, and lobbied space agencies for human Mars programs.
