Constrained Delaunay triangulation

In computational geometry, a constrained Delaunay triangulation (CDT) is a generalization of the Delaunay triangulation that forces certain edges to be present in the triangulation. Given a planar straight-line graph, the CDT is a triangulation of its vertices that includes all the graph's edges, and among all such triangulations is as close as possible to the Delaunay triangulation: every triangle's circumcircle contains no vertex that is visible to all three of the triangle's vertices.

Properties and algorithms

Constrained Delaunay triangulations can be constructed in O(n log n) time, for example by modifying incremental Delaunay insertion algorithms to insert constraint edges and restore the Delaunay property by edge flipping. They are widely used in mesh generation, geographic information systems, terrain modelling and finite element analysis, where input features such as boundaries, roads or break-lines must be respected by the mesh.
