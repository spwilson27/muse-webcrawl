Columbia River Treaty
Signed17 January 1961, Washington, D.C.
In force16 September 1964
PartiesCanada and the United States
SubjectHydroelectric development and flood control on the Columbia River

The Columbia River Treaty is an agreement between Canada and the United States on the development and operation of dams in the upper Columbia River basin for power generation and flood control. Signed in 1961 and brought into force in 1964 after ratification and a protocol negotiated with the province of British Columbia, it is regarded as a landmark of international water management.

Terms

Under the treaty, Canada built three storage dams in British Columbia — Duncan Dam (1967), Keenleyside Dam (originally Arrow Dam, 1968) and Mica Dam (1973) — providing 15.5 million acre-feet of water storage. The stored water, released to optimise generation at dams downstream in the United States, entitled Canada to one-half of the additional power produced there, the "Canadian Entitlement", which British Columbia initially sold to United States utilities for a lump sum. The treaty also enabled the United States to build Libby Dam in Montana, whose reservoir backs up into Canada.

Benefits and criticisms

Coordinated storage greatly reduced flooding on the lower Columbia — the treaty followed the destructive 1948 Vanport flood — and increased firm power generation across the Pacific Northwest. Criticisms have focused on the flooding of valleys in British Columbia, which displaced communities and inundated farmland, orchards, wildlife habitat and Indigenous archaeological sites, and on the treaty's silence about ecosystems and salmon, reflecting the priorities of its era.

Review and modernisation

Key flood-control provisions were scheduled to change in 2024 regardless of the treaty's continuation, prompting formal reviews and, from 2018, negotiations between the two countries to modernise the agreement, with Indigenous nations and ecosystem health given a larger role. In July 2024 the two governments announced an agreement in principle on updated terms covering power, flood-risk management and flows for salmon.
