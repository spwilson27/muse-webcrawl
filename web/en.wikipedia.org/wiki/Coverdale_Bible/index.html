Coverdale Bible

The Coverdale Bible, compiled by Myles Coverdale and published in 1535, was the first complete Modern English translation of the Bible to appear in print, and the first complete printed English Bible. Its full title was Biblia, the Bible, that is, the Holy Scripture of the Old and New Testament, faithfully and truly translated out of Douche and Latin into English.

Translation

Unlike William Tyndale, who translated from the original Hebrew and Greek, Coverdale — who had assisted Tyndale — worked "out of Douche and Latin": from German versions including Luther's Bible, the Zurich Bible, the Latin Peshitta and other sources, alongside Tyndale's own published New Testament and Pentateuch. Coverdale translated the remaining books of the Old Testament and the Apocrypha himself to complete the volume, which was printed abroad, probably in Antwerp or Cologne.

Reception and legacy

Though not formally licensed at first, the Coverdale Bible was followed by the Matthew Bible (1537) and then the Great Bible (1539), which Coverdale edited and which was ordered to be placed in every parish church in England. Coverdale's translation of the Psalms from the Great Bible was retained in the Book of Common Prayer for centuries and is still the version sung and read in many Anglican services, giving his phrasing an enduring influence on English religious language — including expressions such as "the valley of the shadow of death".
