3D modeling

3D modeling
FieldComputer graphics
Common techniquesPolygonal modeling, NURBS, sculpting, procedural
SoftwareBlender, Maya, 3ds Max, ZBrush, SolidWorks

In 3D computer graphics, 3D modeling is the process of developing a mathematical coordinate-based representation of the surface of an object (inanimate or living) in three dimensions, using specialised software. The product is called a 3D model. Models are used in video games, film, architecture, engineering, product design and 3D printing.

Representations

Polygonal modeling approximates surfaces with meshes of vertices, edges and faces (usually triangles or quads) and is dominant in games and animation. NURBS surfaces give smooth, mathematically precise curves favoured in industrial design. Digital sculpting manipulates very dense meshes like clay for organic characters. Solid modeling in CAD represents volume unambiguously for manufacturing, while procedural modeling generates geometry from rules and parameters.

Workflow

A typical pipeline moves from modeling to UV mapping and texturing, rigging for animation, then rendering — producing a 2D image or animation through techniques such as ray tracing. Models may also be created automatically from photographs (photogrammetry) or 3D scans of real objects. Common exchange formats include OBJ, FBX and STL.
