LGBT is an initialism standing for lesbian, gay, bisexual, and transgender. It is an umbrella term used to refer collectively to people and communities whose sexual orientation or gender identity differs from heterosexual and cisgender norms, and to the social and political movements associated with them.

History of the term

The term emerged in the United States in the late 1980s and became widespread in the 1990s, replacing "gay community" as a more inclusive label. The earlier "LGB" gained the "T" as transgender inclusion became a mainstream movement goal. Ordering and expansion of the letters have long been debated; extended variants add Q (queer/questioning), I (intersex), A (asexual/aromantic/agender) and a plus sign for further identities.

Communities and culture

Associated cultural markers include the rainbow flag, Pride parades (originating in commemorations of the 1969 Stonewall riots), and a wide range of community organisations. The initialism is also used for fields such as LGBT rights, LGBT history and LGBT literature. Reception of the term varies internationally, and some prefer alternatives such as "queer" (reclaimed) or specific identity labels.
