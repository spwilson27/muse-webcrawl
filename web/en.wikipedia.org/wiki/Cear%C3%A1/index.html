Ceará

Ceará
Country: Brazil
Region: Northeast
Capital: Fortaleza
Area: about 148,900 km²
Population: about 9 million

Ceará is one of the 26 states of Brazil, located in the northeastern part of the country on the Atlantic coast. Its capital and largest city is Fortaleza. The state is known for its long coastline of beaches and dunes, its semi-arid interior known as the sertão, and its rich folk culture.

Geography and climate

Ceará has roughly 573 km of coastline. The interior is dominated by caatinga scrubland subject to periodic droughts, while coastal areas are warmer and more humid. The state is a major producer of cashew nuts, melons and other fruit, and its beaches — including Jericoacoara and Canoa Quebrada — are major tourist destinations.

History

Colonised by the Portuguese from the 17th century, Ceará was an early centre of the movement for Brazilian independence and, in 1884, became the first Brazilian province to abolish slavery, four years before nationwide abolition. Its nickname is "Terra da Luz" (Land of Light).
