The World Conker Championships is an annual competition in the traditional British game of conkers, played with the seeds of the horse chestnut tree threaded on lengths of string. Founded in 1965, the championships take place on the second Sunday of October and draw several hundred entrants in fancy dress from around the world, competing in men's, women's, junior and team categories while raising money for charity.

History

The championships began in 1965 in the village of Ashton, Northamptonshire, when a group of local men, unable to go fishing because of bad weather, instead played conkers for a small prize; the organiser, the Ashton Conker Club, donated the proceeds to charity, a practice that has continued ever since, raising substantial sums chiefly for causes supporting blind and partially sighted people. As entries grew, the event moved to a larger site at nearby Southwick. Entrants are supplied with official conkers, drilled and strung by the organisers, and must use the conker issued to them.

Rules of play

Matches are knockout bouts between two players, each with a conker dangling from about 25 cm (10 in) of string. Players take alternate strikes at the opponent's conker; the first to shatter the other's conker wins. If a conker is knocked out of a player's hand, or if strings become entangled, the bout may be paused or restarted at the umpire's discretion. A conker that has won games traditionally accumulates a score — a "sixer", for example, has destroyed six opponents — but championship play uses fresh official nuts for fairness. Titles awarded include the Men's Champion ("King Conker"), Women's Champion ("Queen Conker"), junior champion and team champion.

2024 steel conker controversy

The 2024 championships attracted worldwide attention after the men's champion, 82-year-old David Jakins — the event's veteran "King Conker", competing since 1977 — was accused of cheating when a steel conker was found in his pocket after the final. Jakins said the metal conker was a humorous prop he carried but had not used, and an investigation by the organising committee concluded there was no evidence he had used it in play, clearing him and letting his title stand.
