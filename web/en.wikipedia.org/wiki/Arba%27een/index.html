Arba'een

Arba'een (Arabic: الأربعين, "forty") is a Shia religious observance that occurs forty days after the Day of Ashura. It commemorates the martyrdom of Husayn ibn Ali, grandson of Muhammad, who was killed on the 10th day of Muharram.

Pilgrimage

The Arba'een pilgrimage, or Arba'een Walk, is one of the largest annual public gatherings in the world. Pilgrims walk from Najaf to Karbala in Iraq — about 80 kilometres — to visit the shrine of Husayn. Attendance has been estimated in the tens of millions in some years.

History

Tradition holds that Jabir ibn Abd Allah visited Husayn's grave forty days after Ashura. Under Saddam Hussein public observance was suppressed; after 2003 it revived dramatically. Volunteers provide free food, water and lodging (mawakib) along the route.

Significance

Arba'een symbolises resistance to injustice and devotion to Husayn's cause. Sunni Muslims and people of other faiths also participate in some capacities.
