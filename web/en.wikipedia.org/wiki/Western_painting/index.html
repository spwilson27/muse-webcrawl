The history of Western painting represents a continuous tradition from antiquity through the present day, shaped by religious patronage, the rediscovery of classical antiquity, the development of linear perspective and oil paint, and successive revolutions in style from the Renaissance to modern art.

From medieval to Renaissance

After the fall of Rome, painting in Europe was dominated by Byzantine icons, illuminated manuscripts, and Gothic altarpieces. In early 15th-century Florence, artists including Masaccio combined Brunelleschi's linear perspective with naturalistic observation, launching the Renaissance. The High Renaissance masters — Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, and Raphael — brought the style to its zenith, while Venetian painters such as Titian developed its expressive use of colour. Northern artists including Jan van Eyck and Albrecht Dürer perfected oil technique and detail.

Baroque to the nineteenth century

The Baroque style of Caravaggio, Rubens, and Rembrandt favoured drama, movement, and strong contrasts of light. The 18th century added the decorative Rococo and the severe revival of classical themes in Neoclassicism (Jacques-Louis David). Romanticism (Goya, Delacroix, Turner) stressed emotion and nature; Realism and then Impressionism (Monet, Renoir) turned to modern life and optical sensation, recorded directly from observation.

Modern and contemporary

Post-Impressionism, Fauvism, Cubism (Picasso, Braque), abstraction (Kandinsky, Mondrian), Surrealism, and Abstract Expressionism (Pollock, Rothko) successively dismantled traditional representation across the 20th century. Pop art, minimalism, and conceptual art further questioned the nature of painting itself, which nonetheless remains a central medium in contemporary art.
