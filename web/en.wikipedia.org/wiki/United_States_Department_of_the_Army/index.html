United States Department of the Army

The United States Department of the Army (DA) is one of the three military departments within the US Department of Defense. It is the federal government agency within which the United States Army (Regular Army, Army Reserve and Army National Guard when federalised) is organised, and it is led by the Secretary of the Army, a civilian appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate. The department's headquarters is at the Pentagon.

History

The department's origins lie in the Department of War, created on 7 August 1789 to oversee the nation's military forces. The National Security Act of 1947 renamed it the Department of the Army and placed it within the new National Military Establishment (renamed the Department of Defense in 1949), alongside the Departments of the Navy and the Air Force. The Secretary of the Army, formerly the Secretary of War, became subordinate to the Secretary of Defense.

Organisation

The Secretary of the Army is responsible for organising, training and equipping land forces. Professional military leadership is provided by the Chief of Staff of the Army, who since the Goldwater–Nichols Act of 1986 serves in an advisory and administrative (rather than operational command) capacity. The department's Office of the Secretary includes under and assistant secretaries for areas such as acquisition, installations and civil works — the latter overseeing the Army Corps of Engineers' navigation, flood-control and water-resource missions. Below the department are the Army Commands, Army Service Component Commands and Direct Reporting Units, including Forces Command, Training and Doctrine Command, Materiel Command and Futures Command.

The Department of the Army administers roughly one million soldiers and a large civilian workforce, making it one of the biggest organisations in the federal government.
