Harold Bloom

Harold Bloom (July 11, 1930 – October 14, 2019) was an American literary critic and the Sterling Professor of Humanities at Yale University. In 2017, Bloom was described as "probably the most famous literary critic in the English-speaking world". After publishing his first book in 1959, Bloom wrote more than 50 books, including over 40 books of literary criticism, several books discussing religion, and a novel. He edited hundreds of anthologies for Chelsea House Publishers.

Born: July 11, 1930, New York City, U.S.

Died: October 14, 2019 (aged 89), New Haven, Connecticut, U.S.

Education: Cornell University; Yale University (PhD)

Occupation: Literary critic, professor

Known for: The Anxiety of Influence, The Western Canon

Career

Born in the Bronx to Jewish immigrants from Eastern Europe, Bloom was a precocious reader who earned his undergraduate degree at Cornell, where he studied under M. H. Abrams, and his doctorate at Yale in 1955. His early books, including Shelley's Mythmaking (1959) and The Visionary Company (1961), established him as a leading interpreter of English Romantic poetry.

The Anxiety of Influence

In The Anxiety of Influence (1973), Bloom proposed his influential theory of poetic influence: strong poets creatively misread their precursors through six "revisionary ratios" in order to clear imaginative space for their own work. The book, along with A Map of Misreading (1975) and Kabbalah and Criticism (1975), made Bloom a central, controversial figure in literary theory.

Defence of the Western canon

Bloom's Shakespeare: The Invention of the Human (1998) argued that Shakespeare fundamentally created modern conceptions of human personality. In The Western Canon (1994), he defended the traditional canon of Western literature against what he called the "School of Resentment" — approaches he associated with Marxist, feminist, and postcolonial criticism that, in his view, subordinate aesthetic value to political aims. The book's appendix listing canonical works became a widely debated reference.

Bloom taught at Yale for more than six decades and continued teaching shortly before his death at age 89.
