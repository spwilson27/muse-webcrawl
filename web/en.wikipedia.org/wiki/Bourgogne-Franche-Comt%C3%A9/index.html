Bourgogne-Franche-Comté
Region of eastern France
Capital: Dijon (regional prefecture)
Departments: 8

Bourgogne-Franche-Comté is a region in eastern France created by the territorial reform of 2014, effective 1 January 2016, from the merger of the former regions of Burgundy and Franche-Comté. Dijon serves as the seat of the regional prefecture and council. The region covers about 47,800 square kilometres with a population of roughly 2.8 million.

Geography and departments

The region comprises eight departments: Côte-d'Or, Doubs, Haute-Saône, Jura, Nièvre, Saône-et-Loire, Territoire de Belfort, and Yonne. Its landscapes range from the Morvan hills and the Saône plain to the Jura mountains along the Swiss border, and include famous Burgundy wine country along the Côte d'Or escarpment.

Economy

Agriculture and wine (Burgundy wines, Comté cheese), metallurgy, the automotive industry around Belfort and Montbéliard, and microtechnology in Besançon are major activities. Besançon, the former capital of Franche-Comté, is the region's hub for precision engineering and watchmaking heritage, while Dijon is noted for its mustard and its historic ducal palace.
