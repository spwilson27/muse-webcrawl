Hair clipper

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A hair clipper is a specialised implement used to cut human head hair. Clippers work on the same principle as scissors, but are distinct from scissors and razors: two sharp, comb-like blades slide sideways against each other so that hair positioned between their teeth is sheared off as the blades close. They are used by barbers and at home to produce short, uniform cuts, fades and buzz cuts, and come in manual and electric forms.

History

Manual clippers, powered by squeezing two handles together, were invented in the nineteenth century — they are attributed to the Serbian inventor Nikola Bizumić around 1850 — and remained standard in barbershops into the twentieth century. Electric clippers followed: Leo J. Wahl began developing an electromagnetic clipper in Sterling, Illinois, patented it in 1919 and founded the Wahl Clipper Corporation, whose products, along with those of Oster and Andis, came to dominate professional barbering. Modern clippers use rotary, magnetic or pivot motors, and cordless models with lithium-ion batteries are now common.

Use and guard sizes

Cutting length is set either by an adjustable taper lever on the blade or by detachable plastic guide combs ("guards"), numbered by a widely used convention: a #0 leaves about 1.5 mm, #1 about 3 mm (1/8 inch), #2 about 6 mm, #3 about 10 mm, #4 about 13 mm (1/2 inch), and so on up to #8 at roughly 25 mm (1 inch). Barbers blend between lengths to create fades, tapers and crew cuts. Clippers require regular cleaning and oiling of the blades, and are distinct from electric trimmers — smaller, finer-toothed tools used for outlines and detail — and from electric shavers.

Cultural notes

The clipper is the emblematic tool of the barbershop and of military "induction" haircuts given to new recruits. Self-administered buzz cuts with clippers surged during the COVID-19 pandemic lockdowns, when barbershops were closed.
