The 2001 Gujarat earthquake, also known as the Bhuj earthquake, occurred on 26 January 2001, India's 51st Republic Day, at 08:46 local time. The intraplate earthquake measured 7.7 on the moment magnitude scale, with an epicentre near the town of Bhachau in the Kutch district of Gujarat.

Around 20,000 people were killed, some 166,000 injured, and hundreds of thousands of homes were destroyed or damaged. The town of Bhuj was devastated, and the shock was felt across northern India and in Pakistan. Damage extended to Ahmedabad, Gujarat's commercial capital, where many multi-storey buildings collapsed. The total economic loss was estimated at several billion dollars.

Reconstruction

The Gujarat government undertook a large reconstruction programme, rebuilding towns such as Bhuj with new town planning and earthquake-resistant construction, and the disaster led to stricter enforcement of seismic building codes across India. A memorial, Smritivan, was later built on Bhujia Hill to commemorate the victims.
