Cartola

Angenor de Oliveira (October 11, 1908 – November 30, 1980), known as Cartola, was a Brazilian singer, composer, and poet regarded as one of the most important figures in the history of samba. He was a founder of the Estação Primeira de Mangueira samba school in Rio de Janeiro.

Life

Born in Rio's Catete neighborhood, Cartola moved with his family to the Mangueira favela as a boy. He learned the cavaquinho and guitar, left school to work in construction, and earned his nickname ("top hat") from the bowler-style hat he wore to keep cement out of his hair. In 1928 he co-founded the bloco that became the Mangueira samba school, choosing its green and pink colors.

Music

Cartola composed classics including "As Rosas Não Falam," "O Mundo É um Moinho," "Tive Sim," and "Corra e Olhe o Céu." After years of obscurity working as a car washer and elevator operator, he was rediscovered in the 1950s and returned to prominence. He recorded his first solo album only in 1974, at age 65, followed by further acclaimed records. With his wife Dona Zica he ran the Zicartola restaurant, a hub of 1960s samba.

Legacy

Cartola died in Rio de Janeiro in 1980. His songs have been recorded by many of Brazil's greatest artists, and he is celebrated as a founding master of samba composition.
