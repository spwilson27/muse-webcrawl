The Oliver Hazard Perry class is a class of guided-missile frigates named after the American commodore Oliver Hazard Perry, hero of the Battle of Lake Erie. Designed in the 1970s as a cost-effective escort for amphibious and underway replenishment groups, 71 ships were built for the United States, Australia, Spain and Taiwan, and the type became one of the most numerous Western warship classes of the Cold War.

Design and service

The ships were designed around the Mk 13 single-arm launcher firing Standard SM-1MR surface-to-air missiles and Harpoon anti-ship missiles, with a 76 mm gun, Phalanx close-in weapon system and facilities for LAMPS helicopters; "long-hull" ships accommodated the SH-60 Seahawk. The class proved rugged in combat: USS Samuel B. Roberts survived a mine strike in 1988 and USS Stark survived two Exocet hits in 1987. The last US Navy ship, USS Simpson, was decommissioned in 2015; many hulls were transferred to allied navies including Turkey, Egypt, Poland, Pakistan, Bahrain and Chile, where several remain in service, and the design inspired the US Navy's later Constellation-class frigates' predecessor debates.
