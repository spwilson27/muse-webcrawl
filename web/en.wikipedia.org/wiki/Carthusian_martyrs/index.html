Carthusian martyrs

The Carthusian martyrs were members of the Carthusian order executed during persecutions, most famously the eighteen monks of the London Charterhouse put to death under Henry VIII between 1535 and 1540 for refusing to acknowledge the king as head of the Church of England.

London Charterhouse

Prior John Houghton and others were hanged, drawn and quartered at Tyburn beginning 4 May 1535. Others were imprisoned at Newgate and deliberately starved; ten died there in 1537. Houghton and companions were beatified in 1886 and canonised by Paul VI in 1970 among the Forty Martyrs of England and Wales.

Other martyrs

Carthusians also died during the French Revolution, including monks of various charterhouses guillotined or killed in prison, and during the Spanish Civil War. Their austerity — solitary cells, silence, and refusal of compromise — made them emblematic witnesses in Catholic martyrology.
