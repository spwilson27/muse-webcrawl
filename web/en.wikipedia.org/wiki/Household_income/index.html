Household income

Household income is a measure of the combined incomes of all people sharing a particular household or place of residence. It includes every form of earnings — wages and salaries, self-employment income, pensions, benefits, investment income and other receipts — of each household member aged fifteen or over (in some definitions sixteen).

Measurement

Statistics are usually reported as the median household income, the level at which half of households earn more and half less, because the mean is distorted by very high incomes. To compare households of different sizes, income is often equivalised — adjusted using an equivalence scale — or reported per capita. Figures may be gross (before tax) or disposable (after taxes and transfers), and international comparisons typically adjust for purchasing power parity.

Household income is a widely used indicator of living standards and of the size of the middle class, and trends in it are central to debates about economic inequality. In the United States it is published by the Census Bureau from the Current Population Survey; internationally, the Luxembourg Income Study and the OECD compile comparable data.
