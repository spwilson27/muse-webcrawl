Prost–Senna rivalry

The Prost–Senna rivalry was a Formula One rivalry between French driver Alain Prost and Brazilian driver Ayrton Senna. It is widely regarded as one of the fiercest and most famous rivalries in the history of motorsport, defining Formula One between the mid-1980s and early 1990s. Between them, Prost and Senna won seven World Drivers' Championships and dominated the sport for nearly a decade.

Origins

The rivalry began in earnest in 1988, when Senna joined Prost at McLaren. The team won 15 of the 16 races that season, with Senna taking the championship. Although their relationship was initially cordial, tensions grew as the younger Senna challenged the established Prost, known as "The Professor" for his calculated, methodical driving style — in contrast to Senna's raw speed and single-minded intensity.

1989: Suzuka collision

In 1989 the championship came down to the Japanese Grand Prix at Suzuka. Senna needed to win the final two races to beat Prost. On lap 47 the two collided at the chicane. Prost retired immediately; Senna rejoined with marshals' assistance, pitted for a new nose cone and won the race — but was disqualified for missing the chicane and receiving outside assistance, handing the title to Prost. Senna believed Prost and FIA president Jean-Marie Balestre had conspired against him, a grievance that shaped the following season.

1990: Suzuka again

Prost moved to Ferrari for 1990. At Suzuka, Senna — who had been given the disadvantage of starting from the dirty side of the grid — collided with Prost at the first corner, taking both out and securing the championship for Senna, who later admitted the move was deliberate payback for 1989.

Later years and reconciliation

Prost retired at the end of 1993 after winning his fourth championship with Williams, the seat Senna then took for 1994. The two men reconciled in Prost's final season, and Senna frequently sought Prost's counsel. Senna was killed at the 1994 San Marino Grand Prix; Prost was a pallbearer at his funeral. The rivalry has been extensively chronicled, most notably in the 2010 documentary film Senna.
