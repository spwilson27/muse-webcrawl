Nieuw Land National Park

Nieuw Land National Park (Dutch: Nationaal Park Nieuw Land) is a national park in the province of Flevoland in the Netherlands. Established on 1 October 2018, it is the country's youngest national park, created almost entirely on land reclaimed from the sea during the twentieth century. The park covers about 29,000 hectares and combines four nature areas: the Oostvaardersplassen, the Lepelaarplassen, the Marker Wadden and part of the Markermeer.

History

The land on which the park sits did not exist a century ago. Flevoland was reclaimed from the Zuiderzee and IJsselmeer as part of the Zuiderzee Works. In 1968 the southern polder of Flevoland was drained; a low-lying section intended for industry near Lelystad remained wet and developed spontaneously into the Oostvaardersplassen wetland. In 2018 the Dutch government designated Nieuw Land as the nation's 21st national park under a programme to strengthen the national park system.

Component areas

Oostvaardersplassen

A 56 km² marsh and grassland reserve between Almere and Lelystad, famous for its large herbivores — Heck cattle, Konik horses and red deer — introduced as part of a rewilding experiment, and for its rich birdlife including white-tailed eagles, spoonbills and bitterns.

Marker Wadden

An artificial archipelago of islands built from sand, clay and silt dredged from the Markermeer, begun in 2016. The islands create shallow, sheltered water and new habitat for fish and birds, and one island is open to visitors.

Lepelaarplassen and Markermeer

The Lepelaarplassen is a reed-marsh lake area near Almere named for its spoonbill (lepelaar) colony. Large parts of the open water of the Markermeer, one of the Netherlands' largest lakes, are also included in the park.

Fauna and flora

The park is one of the most important bird areas in north-western Europe, supporting breeding and wintering populations of grebes, ducks, geese, waders and raptors. The Oostvaardersplassen grazers maintain an open mosaic of marsh and grassland. Management of the large herbivore populations, particularly winter mortality of red deer, has been the subject of public and political debate.
