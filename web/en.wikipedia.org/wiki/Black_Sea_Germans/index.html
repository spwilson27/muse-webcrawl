Black Sea Germans

GroupBlack Sea Germans (German: Schwarzmeerdeutsche)
RegionsNorthern Black Sea coast: present-day Ukraine, Moldova and southern Russia
Settlement periodMainly 1803–1860s
LanguagesGerman dialects (notably Swabian), Russian
ReligionsLutheran, Catholic, Mennonite

The Black Sea Germans (German: Schwarzmeerdeutsche) were ethnic Germans who settled in the territories north of the Black Sea, in the Russian Empire's region of Novorossiya (New Russia), principally in what is now southern Ukraine and Moldova. Invited by the manifestos of Catherine the Great and Alexander I, German colonists founded hundreds of agricultural colonies from the early nineteenth century around Odessa, in Bessarabia, in Crimea and in the Caucasus.

Settlement

The largest groups settled in three principal areas near Odessa — the Liebental, Kutschurgan and Glückstal colonies — as well as in Bessarabia from 1814 and the Beresan district. The settlers, many from Württemberg, the Palatinate and Alsace, received land, tax exemptions and exemption from military service, and developed prosperous farming villages famous for grain, viticulture and livestock. They formed a distinct branch of the Germans of Russia, with Lutheran, Catholic and Mennonite congregations maintaining German-language schools and churches.

Twentieth century

After the Russian Revolution, the Black Sea Germans suffered expropriation, famine (including the Holodomor), and mass arrests during the Great Terror. Following the Nazi invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941, ethnic Germans in the region came under Nazi administration; in 1944, as the Red Army advanced, some 350,000 were evacuated westward in the trek organised by the SS. Those who remained, or who were repatriated from Germany after 1945, were deported to Siberia and Kazakhstan and held in special settlements until the mid-1950s. Since the late 1980s many descendants have emigrated to Germany as Aussiedler.
