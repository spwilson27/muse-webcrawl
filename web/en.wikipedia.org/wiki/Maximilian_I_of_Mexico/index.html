Maximilian I (6 July 1832 – 19 June 1867) was an Austrian archduke who became Emperor of Mexico in 1864, installed with French military backing during the Second French intervention in Mexico. His short-lived empire collapsed once French support was withdrawn, and he was captured and executed by firing squad by forces loyal to President Benito Juárez.

Early life

Ferdinand Maximilian was the younger brother of Emperor Franz Joseph I of Austria. Intelligent and liberal-minded, he made his career in the Imperial Austrian Navy, becoming its commander-in-chief, and from 1857 to 1859 served as governor-general of Lombardy–Venetia, where his conciliatory policies displeased Vienna. He married Princess Charlotte of Belgium in 1857 and retired to Miramare Castle near Trieste, which he had built.

Emperor of Mexico

After French forces occupied Mexico City in 1863, a French-backed Assembly of Notables offered the Mexican crown to Maximilian, at the urging of Napoleon III and Mexican conservatives. Misled by a manipulated plebiscite into believing the Mexican people had voted for a monarchy, Maximilian accepted the crown under the Treaty of Miramar (April 1864) and arrived at Veracruz in May.

As emperor, Maximilian surprised his conservative backers by upholding many liberal Reform laws, confirming the sale of church lands, and abolishing peonage and child labour; yet he never won over Juárez's republicans, who waged a continuing guerrilla war. His court at Chapultepec Castle, and the Empress Carlota's energetic role, could not compensate for an empty treasury and total dependence on French bayonets.

Fall and execution

Under pressure from the United States, which after its Civil War invoked the Monroe Doctrine, and facing war with Prussia at home, Napoleon III withdrew French troops in 1866–67. Carlota's mission to Europe to plead for aid failed, and she suffered a mental collapse from which she never recovered. Maximilian refused to abdicate and was besieged at Querétaro, where betrayal led to his capture in May 1867. Despite international appeals for clemency, Juárez had him shot on 19 June 1867 at the Cerro de las Campanas, together with his generals Miramón and Mejía; his last words were reported as "I forgive everyone, and I ask everyone to forgive me. May my blood, which is about to be shed, be for the good of the country. Viva Mexico, viva la independencia!"

The execution was famously painted by Édouard Manet in The Execution of Emperor Maximilian. Maximilian's body was returned to Austria in 1868 aboard SMS Novara — the same frigate that had brought him to Mexico — and buried in the Imperial Crypt in Vienna.
