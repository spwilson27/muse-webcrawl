Southern Baptist Convention

Southern Baptist Convention
Abbreviation: SBC
Founded: 1845, Augusta, Georgia
Headquarters: Nashville, Tennessee
Membership: roughly 13 million (early 2020s)
Congregations: about 47,000

The Southern Baptist Convention (SBC), sometimes called the Great Commission Baptists, is a Christian denomination based in the United States. It is the world's largest Baptist denomination, the largest Protestant denomination in the United States, and the second-largest Christian denomination in the United States after the Roman Catholic Church. Its member churches are autonomous and cooperate voluntarily through the Convention for missions, education, and publishing.

History

The SBC was organized in Augusta, Georgia, in May 1845, when Baptists in the South split from northern Baptists over slavery: northern mission boards had declined to appoint slaveholders as missionaries, and southern Baptists formed their own convention in response. In 1995, on its 150th anniversary, the Convention formally apologized for its defence of slavery and repudiated racism.

In the late 20th century the Convention underwent a "conservative resurgence" (or "fundamentalist takeover"), beginning in 1979, in which conservatives gained control of its agencies and seminaries and affirmed biblical inerrancy. Its confession of faith is the Baptist Faith and Message (1925, revised 1963 and 2000).

Organization and work

The Convention meets annually and acts through entities including the International Mission Board, the North American Mission Board, LifeWay Christian Resources, and six seminaries. Churches support shared work through the Cooperative Program (established 1925). Membership peaked at about 16.3 million in 2006 and has declined since. In the 2020s the SBC faced sustained scrutiny over its handling of sexual abuse cases and internal debates over the role of women as pastors.
