Peter Wohlleben

Peter Wohlleben (born 3 June 1964) is a German forester and author who writes on ecological themes. He is best known for his 2015 book The Hidden Life of Trees: What They Feel, How They Communicate (German: Das geheime Leben der Bäume), an international bestseller that popularised ideas about tree communication, cooperation through fungal networks — the so-called "wood wide web" — and the social life of forests.

Career

Wohlleben studied forestry and worked for more than twenty years for the forestry commission of Rhineland-Palatinate. Growing disillusioned with industrial plantation forestry, he took over management of a beech forest near Hümmel in the Eifel region, where he introduced gentler, horse-logged, chemical-free methods and created a "forest cemetery" to help fund preservation of the old-growth stand. He left government service to run the forest for the municipality and to write and run a forest academy.

Writing and reception

His books, including The Inner Life of Animals (2016) and The Secret Wisdom of Nature, use accessible, anthropomorphic language to argue that trees and animals have rich inner lives and that forests function as communities. While praised for engaging the public with ecology, some scientists have criticised Wohlleben's claims as overstated or insufficiently supported, a debate he has publicly engaged with.
