B-flat major is a major scale based on B♭, consisting of the pitches B♭, C, D, E♭, F, G and A. Its key signature has two flats, B♭ and E♭. Its relative minor is G minor and its parallel minor is B-flat minor. B-flat major is a very common key for wind and brass instruments — most clarinets, trumpets and saxophones are "B♭ instruments" whose written C sounds B♭ — and it is accordingly central to concert band, jazz and brass-band repertoire.

Notable compositions

  • Mozart — Piano Concerto No. 27, K. 595; Clarinet Concerto is in A major but his String Quintet No. 2 context aside, the "Coronation" mass is in C; B-flat works include the Piano Sonata K. 570
  • Beethoven — Symphony No. 4; Piano Concerto No. 2; "Hammerklavier" Sonata, Op. 106
  • Schubert — Symphony No. 5; Piano Sonata D. 960
  • Brahms — Piano Concerto No. 2, Op. 83; String Sextet No. 1
  • Mendelssohn — Symphony No. 2 "Lobgesang"
  • Prokofiev — Piano Concerto No. 5 is in G; his Symphony No. 5 is in B♭ major

Because of the friendly fingering for brass and woodwind, B-flat major is also a default key in jazz standards and New Orleans brass-band music.
