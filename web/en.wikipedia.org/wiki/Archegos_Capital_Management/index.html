Archegos Capital Management was an American family office run by investor Bill Hwang (Sung Kook Hwang). In March 2021 Archegos collapsed in one of the largest margin-call defaults in modern financial history, inflicting more than US$10 billion of losses on global investment banks and wiping out tens of billions in market value in a handful of stocks within days.

Background

Hwang, a former protégé of Julian Robertson at Tiger Management, had previously run Tiger Asia Management, which pleaded guilty to wire fraud in 2012 over insider trading in Chinese bank stocks and paid settlements of over $60 million; Hwang was barred from the industry in Hong Kong for four years. Reconstituted as the family office Archegos (Greek for "leader" or "prince"), the firm escaped the disclosure and regulatory obligations that apply to registered investment advisers.

Positions and collapse

Using total return swaps with multiple prime brokers, Archegos built enormous, largely invisible leveraged positions — reportedly five times leverage or more — concentrated in ViacomCBS, Discovery, and Chinese education and tech stocks including GSX Techedu, Baidu, and Tencent Music. When ViacomCBS shares fell after a secondary offering in late March 2021, Archegos failed to meet margin calls. On 26 March 2021 the banks dumped billions of dollars of stock in block trades; ViacomCBS and Discovery lost roughly half their value in a week.

Bank losses and aftermath

Credit Suisse lost about $5.5 billion and dismissed senior executives; its Archegos and Greensill failures contributed to the bank's collapse into a UBS takeover in 2023. Nomura lost about $2.9 billion, Morgan Stanley about $1 billion, and others smaller sums, while Goldman Sachs and Deutsche Bank largely escaped. Hwang and his CFO Patrick Halligan were arrested in April 2022 on federal racketeering, securities fraud, and market manipulation charges; in July 2024 Hwang was convicted on 10 of 11 counts and in November 2024 was sentenced to 18 years in prison. The affair prompted regulatory reviews of swap disclosure and family-office oversight worldwide.
