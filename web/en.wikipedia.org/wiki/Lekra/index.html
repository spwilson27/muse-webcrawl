Lekra, short for Lembaga Kebudajaan Rakjat (Institute of People's Culture), was a prolific and influential cultural organisation in Indonesia associated with the Communist Party of Indonesia (PKI). Founded on 17 August 1950, it embraced writers, artists, musicians and filmmakers committed to socialist realism and to putting art in the service of the people; by the early 1960s it claimed well over 100,000 members, with many more in affiliated organisations.

Ideology and activity

Lekra promoted the slogan "politics in command" and sent members to live and work in villages ("going down" — turun ke bawah) to draw inspiration from peasant life. It campaigned against rival cultural groups, most bitterly against the signatories of the 1963 Cultural Manifesto (Manifes Kebudayaan), whom it denounced as reactionaries and succeeded in having banned by President Sukarno in 1964.

Destruction

After the abortive 30 September Movement of 1965 and the ensuing anti-communist massacres, Lekra was banned, its works destroyed and its members killed, imprisoned or exiled; the writer Pramoedya Ananta Toer, associated with Lekra, was imprisoned for 14 years. Under Suharto's New Order, Lekra-affiliated art was erased from Indonesian cultural history for decades.
