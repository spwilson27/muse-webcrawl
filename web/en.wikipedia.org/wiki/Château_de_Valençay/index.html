Château de Valençay

The Château de Valençay is a large château located in the commune of Valençay in the Indre department of France. One of the Châteaux of the Loire Valley, it is best known as the estate purchased in 1803 by the diplomat Charles Maurice de Talleyrand at the urging of Napoleon Bonaparte, who wanted a grand residence suitable for receiving foreign dignitaries.

History

The château was begun in 1540 by Jacques d'Estampes on the site of a demolished 12th-century castle. Its architecture blends Renaissance and classical styles, and has been favourably compared to the Château de Chambord. In 1803 Talleyrand acquired the property and made it a centre of diplomacy and gastronomy; the celebrated chef Marie-Antoine Carême worked in its kitchens. From 1808 to 1813, Ferdinand VII of Spain and his brother were held in comfortable captivity at Valençay under the Treaty of Valençay negotiations.

Description

The building has over a hundred rooms set among extensive gardens laid out in the French style, with an English-style park created in the early twentieth century. The interior preserves Talleyrand's apartments, period furniture, and a museum dedicated to the statesman. The estate remains associated with the descendants of the Prince of Talleyrand's family and is open to visitors.
