The Trans-Karakoram Tract, also known as the Shaksgam Tract, is an area of approximately 5,180 square kilometres north of the Karakoram watershed in the Kashmir region. It is administered by China as part of Kargilik County and Taxkorgan Tajik Autonomous County in the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, but is claimed by India as part of its union territory of Ladakh.

History

The tract lies in the valley of the Shaksgam River, a tributary of the Yarkand River. Historically the area was used as grazing ground by the people of Hunza, and its northern boundary was never formally demarcated between British India and China. Proposed boundary lines, such as the Macartney–MacDonald Line of 1899, would have placed the tract largely on the Chinese side of the Karakoram watershed.

1963 agreement

Under the Sino-Pakistani Frontier Agreement of 2 March 1963, Pakistan transferred administration of the tract to China, while China recognised Pakistani control of other areas in northern Kashmir. India does not recognise the agreement, holding that Pakistan had no right to cede territory that India claims as its own. Article Six of the agreement provides that the settlement is provisional and subject to renegotiation after the Kashmir dispute is resolved between India and Pakistan.

Geography

The tract is a high-altitude, largely uninhabited region of mountains and glaciers west of the Siachen Glacier area and north of K2, bounded by the Karakoram range to the south. The Shaksgam River drains northward through the region.
