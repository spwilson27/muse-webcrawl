Edgar Miles Bronfman Jr. (born May 16, 1955) is an American businessman, a member of the Bronfman family, the Canadian dynasty that built the Seagram liquor empire. He is best known for leading Seagram's transformation into an entertainment conglomerate in the 1990s and for later heading Warner Music Group.

Career

Bronfman began his career in film production and songwriting before joining the family business. He became president of Seagram in 1989 and chief executive in 1994. Under his leadership Seagram sold its stake in DuPont and acquired the entertainment companies MCA/Universal and PolyGram, moves that were controversial with investors. In 2000 Seagram was sold to Vivendi of France, ending family control.

In 2004 Bronfman led an investor group that bought Warner Music Group from Time Warner, and he served as its chairman and CEO, steering the company through the industry's digital transition before stepping down as CEO in 2011 following the company's sale to Access Industries. He later became involved in streaming ventures, including as chairman of the video service FuboTV.

Personal life

Bronfman is the son of Edgar Bronfman Sr., longtime president of the World Jewish Congress. His half-sisters Sara and Clare Bronfman were associated with the NXIVM organisation, a connection that drew public attention; Edgar Jr. publicly distanced himself from the group.
