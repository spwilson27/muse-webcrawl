Ruhr (river)

The Ruhr is a river in western Germany, a right tributary of the Rhine. It is approximately 219 kilometres (136 mi) long. Its source is in the Sauerland hills near Winterberg, and it flows westward through the Ruhr valley, passing Meschede, Arnsberg, Wickede, Fröndenberg, Hagen, Dortmund's southern districts, Witten, Bochum's outskirts, Hattingen, Essen, Mülheim and Duisburg, where it joins the Rhine at Ruhrort. The river gives its name to the Ruhr area (Ruhrgebiet), Germany's historic industrial heartland.

Course and use

Although the Ruhr valley became synonymous with coal mining and steelmaking, the river itself remained comparatively clean, because most industry drew on and discharged into the nearby Emscher. A chain of reservoirs built in the 20th century — including the Möhne, Sorpe, Henne, Bigge (on the tributary Lenne system), Baldeneysee and Kettwiger See — regulates flow and supplies drinking water to several million people, coordinated by the Ruhrverband (Ruhr Association), founded in 1913. The dams also generate hydroelectric power and provide recreation.

History

The Möhne Dam, one of the reservoirs on a Ruhr tributary, was breached in May 1943 during Operation Chastise, the famous "Dambusters" raid, causing catastrophic flooding in the Ruhr valley. In the 1920s the French occupation of the Ruhr (1923–1925), following disputes over reparations, centred on the industrial valley of the lower river.
