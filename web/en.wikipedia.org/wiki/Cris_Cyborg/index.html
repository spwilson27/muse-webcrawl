Cristiane Justino Venâncio (born 9 July 1985), known professionally as Cris Cyborg, is a Brazilian-American mixed martial artist. She is regarded as one of the greatest female fighters in the history of the sport, and is the only fighter — male or female — to hold world championship titles in four major MMA promotions: Strikeforce, Invicta FC, the UFC, and Bellator MMA, at featherweight.

Career

Cyborg began her career in Brazil in 2005 and rose to prominence in Strikeforce, where in 2009 she defeated Gina Carano in the first major women's MMA main event to become the Strikeforce Women's Featherweight Champion. She went unbeaten for more than a decade, building a reputation for overwhelming striking power and aggressive Muay Thai.

After dominant title runs in Invicta FC, Cyborg joined the UFC, winning its inaugural women's featherweight title in 2017 against Tonya Evinger. In December 2018 she lost the title to Amanda Nunes in 51 seconds — her first defeat since her debut. Released by the UFC in 2019, she signed with Bellator and won its women's featherweight championship from Julia Budd in January 2020, defending it multiple times and completing her collection of titles in every major promotion to have fielded her division.

Cyborg has also competed in professional boxing and in Muay Thai, and her rivalry-laden career — including a 2011 suspension after a positive test for the anabolic steroid stanozolol, which cost her the Strikeforce title — made her one of the most scrutinised athletes in women's MMA.

Legacy

With a professional record featuring more than twenty wins, most by knockout, Cyborg is routinely ranked alongside Amanda Nunes and Ronda Rousey in debates over the greatest female fighter of all time, and is credited with establishing the women's featherweight division.
