Lomo saltado

Lomo saltado
CourseMain course
Place of originPeru
Main ingredientsBeef, onions, tomatoes, French fries, soy sauce, rice

Lomo saltado is a popular, traditional Peruvian dish, a stir fry that typically combines marinated strips of sirloin (or other beef steak) with onions, tomatoes, French fries, and other ingredients, and is typically served with rice. The dish originated as part of the chifa tradition, the Chinese cuisine of Peru, reflecting the blend of Chinese and Peruvian cooking techniques.

Preparation

Strips of beef are marinated in vinegar, soy sauce, and spices, then stir-fried at high heat with red onions, parsley, and tomatoes. The mixture is traditionally served over French fries (or with the fries incorporated into the stir fry) alongside white rice, so the juices flavour both.

History

The dish is recorded as early as the late 19th century, when it was known as lomito de vaca. The word saltado refers to stir-frying (from the Spanish saltar, "to jump"). Chinese immigrants arrived in Peru in the mid-19th century, and their wok techniques and ingredients such as soy sauce were absorbed into Criollo cooking, producing the chifa tradition of which lomo saltado is the emblematic dish.
