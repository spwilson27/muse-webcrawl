Odaiba

Odaiba (お台場) today is a large artificial island in Tokyo Bay, Japan, across the Rainbow Bridge from central Tokyo. Odaiba was initially built in this area for defensive purposes in the 1850s. The land was dramatically expanded during the late 20th century as a seaport district, and has developed since the 1990s as a major commercial, residential and leisure area.

History

Odaiba originates from a set of six islands constructed in 1853 by the Tokugawa shogunate to protect Tokyo from attack by sea, primarily in response to the Perry Expedition. In Japanese, daiba refers to the cannon batteries placed on the islands. In 1928, the Dai-San Daiba had been refurbished and opened to the public as Metropolitan Daiba Park.

Attractions

Today Odaiba is a popular shopping and sightseeing destination for locals and tourists alike. Major attractions include the Fuji Television headquarters, DiverCity Tokyo Plaza with its life-size Unicorn Gundam statue, Palette Town, teamLab Borderless (formerly), and the Miraikan science museum.
