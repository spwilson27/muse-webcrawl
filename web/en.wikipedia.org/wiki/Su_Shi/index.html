Su Shi

Su Shi (Chinese: 蘇軾; 8 January 1037 – 24 August 1101), courtesy name Zizhan (子瞻), art name Dongpo Jushi (東坡居士, "Resident of the Eastern Slope"), often known as Su Dongpo, was a Chinese polymath of the Song dynasty: a poet, essayist, calligrapher, painter, politician, and gastronome. Together with his father Su Xun and brother Su Zhe, he is counted among the Eight Masters of the Tang and Song prose tradition.

Life

Su Shi was born in Meishan, in present-day Sichuan. In 1057 he and his younger brother Su Zhe passed the imperial examinations with distinction, impressing the examiner Ouyang Xiu. He served in various posts and opposed Wang Anshi's New Policies; in 1079 he was arrested in the Crow Terrace Poetry Case, imprisoned and tried for poems construed as slandering the court, then exiled to Huangzhou in Hubei. There, farming a plot on the Eastern Slope — from which he took his art name — he wrote some of his most famous works, including the two prose odes on Red Cliffs (Chibi) and the lyric "Water Melody" style poems.

Recalled to office under the regency of Empress Dowager Gao, he later fell from favour again and was exiled successively to Huizhou in Guangdong and to remote Danzhou on Hainan island. Pardoned in 1100, he died the following year at Changzhou while travelling north.

Works and influence

Nearly 2,400 of his poems survive, along with some 800 letters and celebrated essays. Su Shi transformed the ci lyric, expanding its subjects from romance to philosophy, history, and landscape, and is the leading figure of the "heroic" (haofang) school of ci. His calligraphy ranks among the finest of the Song dynasty — his Cold Food Observance manuscript is considered one of the three greatest works of Chinese calligraphy — and he theorized literati painting (wenrenhua), arguing that painting and poetry share one essence. In popular memory he is also celebrated as a food lover; Dongpo pork, the braised pork belly dish of Hangzhou, bears his name.
