Seven seals

Seven seals
The Lamb and the book with seven seals
SourceBook of Revelation, chapters 5–8
Opened byThe Lamb of God (Christ)

The Seven Seals are seals on a scroll or book described in the Book of Revelation, the final book of the New Testament. The phrase appears in Revelation 5:1, where a book "written within and on the backside, sealed with seven seals" is held in the right hand of God. Only the Lamb — the risen Christ, pictured as "a Lamb as it had been slain" — is found worthy to open the seals, an act that unfolds the apocalyptic judgements of chapters 6–8.

The seals

  • First seal — a white horse; its rider, holding a bow, goes forth conquering.
  • Second seal — a red horse; its rider takes peace from the earth with a great sword: war.
  • Third seal — a black horse; its rider holds scales: famine and scarcity pricing.
  • Fourth seal — a pale horse; its rider is Death, with Hades following.

These first four riders are known collectively as the Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse.

  • Fifth seal — the souls of the martyrs under the altar cry out for judgement and are given white robes.
  • Sixth seal — a great earthquake, the sun blackened, the moon like blood, stars falling, and the sky rolled up like a scroll.
  • Seventh seal — silence in heaven for about half an hour, followed by the seven trumpets.

Interpretation

Preterist interpreters connect the seals to events of the first century AD; futurist interpreters place them in an eschatological tribulation yet to come; historicist and idealist readings treat them as recurring patterns of judgement across church history. The imagery of the sealed scroll draws on Ezekiel and Daniel.
