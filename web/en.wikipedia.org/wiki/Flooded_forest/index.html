Flooded forest

Flooded forests are forests that are inundated with water, either permanently or seasonally. They occur in many parts of the world, most famously in the Amazon basin, where seasonally flooded forests cover large areas along whitewater and blackwater rivers. Flooded forests are important ecosystems that support distinctive communities of plants and animals adapted to periodic inundation, and they include freshwater swamp forests, peat swamp forests, and flooded grassland and savanna mosaics.

Amazonian flooded forests

In Amazonia, flooded forests are classified by water type. Várzea forests grow on floodplains of nutrient-rich whitewater rivers such as the Amazon and Madeira, whose sediments fertilize the soil each flood season. Igapó forests grow along nutrient-poor blackwater and clearwater rivers such as the Rio Negro, where the water is acidic and stained dark by humic substances. Trees in these forests may be submerged for up to eight or nine months a year, and many species time their fruiting to the flood season so that fish disperse their seeds.

Other regions

Seasonally flooded forests also occur in the Congo Basin, in Southeast Asia — including the peat swamp forests of Borneo and Sumatra and the flooded forest around Cambodia's Tonlé Sap lake — and in North America, where bottomland hardwood forests and bald cypress swamps line the rivers of the southeastern United States.

Ecology

Flooding creates low-oxygen conditions in the soil, so flooded-forest trees often have adaptations such as pneumatophores (breathing roots), buttressed trunks, and seeds that germinate in water. Flooded forests provide critical habitat and feeding grounds for fish, including many species that migrate into the forest to feed and spawn during high water, as well as for birds, mammals, and invertebrates. They also store significant carbon, particularly in peat swamp forests.

Threats

Flooded forests are threatened by deforestation, drainage for agriculture (including oil palm plantations on peatlands), dam construction that alters flood pulses, and climate change. Loss of peat swamp forest releases large quantities of stored carbon dioxide.
