Church of Sweden

Church of Sweden
Swedish: Svenska kyrkan
Classification: Protestant (Lutheran)
Primate: Archbishop of Uppsala
Members: about 5.5 million (just over half the population)

The Church of Sweden (Swedish: Svenska kyrkan) is an Evangelical Lutheran church in Sweden. A former state church, it separated from the state on 1 January 2000 but remains the country's national church and largest Christian denomination. Its primate is the Archbishop of Uppsala, the historic see of Saint Ansgar's mission territory.

History

Christianity reached Sweden through missions beginning with Ansgar in the 9th century and took firm hold in the 11th–12th centuries. The Swedish Reformation, launched under Gustav Vasa in the 1520s–1540s, broke with Rome and confiscated church property; the Uppsala Synod of 1593 adopted the Augsburg Confession and defined the Church of Sweden as Lutheran.

Organization

The church comprises 13 dioceses and around 3,400 congregations. Its highest decision-making body is the General Synod (Kyrkomötet), elected by members, and its central board handles national administration. It ordains women as priests (since 1960) and bishops, and it performs same-sex marriages. The Church of Sweden is a member of the Lutheran World Federation and the Porvoo Communion.

Membership

Membership has declined steadily since disestablishment, from roughly 85% of the population in 2000 to around 52–55% in recent years, with members paying a church fee collected through the tax system. Despite low weekly attendance, the church remains culturally significant and maintains nearly 3,000 church buildings, including Uppsala Cathedral, Scandinavia's largest church.
