Jerry Orbach

Jerry Orbach
Born: October 20, 1935, New York City, U.S.
Died: December 28, 2004 (aged 69), New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Actor, singer
Years active: 1955–2004

Jerome Bernard Orbach (October 20, 1935 – December 28, 2004) was an American actor and singer, described as "one of the last bona fide leading men of the Broadway musical". He is best known to television audiences for his long-running role as Detective Lennie Briscoe on the NBC crime drama Law & Order, which he played from 1992 to 2004.

Early life

Orbach was born in the Bronx, New York City, the son of Emily, a greeting-card manufacturer and singer, and Leon Orbach, a vaudeville performer of German Jewish descent. He grew up partly in the Midwest and studied drama at the University of Miami and Northwestern University before leaving to pursue acting in New York, where he studied at the Actors Studio under Lee Strasberg.

Stage career

Orbach created the role of El Gallo in the original 1960 Off-Broadway production of The Fantasticks, introducing the song "Try to Remember", and the show became the longest-running musical in history. On Broadway he originated roles in Carnival!, Promises, Promises — for which he won the Tony Award for Best Actor in a Musical in 1969 — Chicago, in which he created the role of Billy Flynn, and the original production of 42nd Street.

Film and television

His film roles included the gangster drama Prince of the City (1981), Crimes and Misdemeanors (1989), and Dirty Dancing (1987), in which he played Dr. Jake Houseman. He provided the voice of Lumière, the candelabra, in Disney's animated film Beauty and the Beast (1991), performing the song "Be Our Guest".

As Lennie Briscoe on Law & Order, Orbach became one of the most recognisable faces on American television; the character's sardonic one-liners and long tenure made him an institution of the franchise. He briefly reprised the role in the spin-off Law & Order: Trial by Jury before his death from prostate cancer in December 2004.

Legacy

The corner of 53rd Street and Eighth Avenue in New York City, near his home, was renamed Jerry Orbach Way in his honour. He was elected to the American Theater Hall of Fame.
