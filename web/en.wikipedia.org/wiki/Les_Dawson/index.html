Les Dawson

Les Dawson
Born: 2 February 1931, Collyhurst, Manchester
Died: 10 June 1993, Manchester
Occupation: comedian, author, presenter

Leslie Dawson (2 February 1931 – 10 June 1993) was an English comedian, musician and writer. Deadpan and lugubrious, he was famous for his mother-in-law jokes, his monologues full of deliberately over-elaborate vocabulary, and his virtuoso "bad" piano playing.

Career

Dawson worked as a pianist in clubs and wrote journalism before his television breakthrough on Opportunity Knocks in 1967. He became a fixture of British light entertainment with The Les Dawson Show, the sketch show Dawson's Weekly, and, from 1984, as host of the comedy panel game Blankety Blank, where his sardonic handling of contestants and prizes became a national institution. With Roy Barraclough he created the gossiping housewives Cissie and Ada.

Writing and piano

Away from television Dawson wrote several novels and an autobiography, and was regarded as a genuinely gifted writer and pianist; his act's signature wrong-notes piano routine required considerable skill to perform convincingly. His sculpted facial control and gurning were trademarks of his act.

Legacy

Dawson died suddenly of a heart attack in 1993 while awaiting a medical examination. He remains one of Britain's most quoted stand-up comedians; a statue of him stands in Lytham St Annes, where he lived for many years.
