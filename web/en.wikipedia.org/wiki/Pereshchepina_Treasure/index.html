MaterialGold, silver, garnets, glass
Created7th century AD
Discovered1912, near Mala Pereshchepina, Poltava Governorate (now Ukraine)

The Pereshchepina Treasure is a major hoard of Bulgar and Byzantine gold and silver objects from the seventh century AD, discovered in 1912 near the village of Mala Pereshchepina in present-day Ukraine. It is one of the largest surviving treasures of the early medieval steppe world and is now held by the Hermitage Museum in Saint Petersburg.

Discovery

The hoard was found by a shepherd boy who stumbled over a buried gold vessel; excavations recovered some 800 objects weighing roughly 25 kg of gold and 50 kg of silver. According to the dominant scholarly interpretation, the treasure is associated with Kubrat, founder of Great Bulgaria, and may mark his burial place or a deposit of his treasury; a signet ring bearing his monogram was among the finds.

Contents

The treasure includes Byzantine silver plate (some pieces stamped with imperial control stamps of the early seventh century), a great gold eagle standard, swords with gold and garnet-inlaid hilts, belt fittings, drinking vessels, coins, a necklace, and remnants of a wooden coffin with gold appliqués. Two sets of vessels are thought to reflect Kubrat's dual position as a Christianised ruler allied to Byzantium and a steppe khan: one set for use at the Byzantine court, another for nomadic feasting.

Interpretation

Scholars debate whether the deposit is a grave or a hidden treasury, and attributions have at times been politically contested between Bulgar, Khazar and Byzantine contexts. The monogram ring and the dating of the Byzantine silver are the key evidence linking the hoard to Kubrat's generation, the decades around his death (c. 665).
