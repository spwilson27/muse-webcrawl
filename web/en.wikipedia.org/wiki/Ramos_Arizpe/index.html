Ramos Arizpe

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ramos Arizpe is a city and municipality in the Mexican state of Coahuila. It forms part of the Saltillo metropolitan area in northeastern Mexico and is an important industrial centre, particularly for the automotive industry.

History

The area was settled during the Spanish colonial period as part of the agricultural valley supplying the nearby city of Saltillo, founded in the late 16th century. The town was earlier known as Valle de San Nicolás de la Capellanía. It was renamed in honour of Miguel Ramos Arizpe, a priest and politician born in the region who is regarded as the father of Mexican federalism for his role in drafting federal provisions in Mexico's early constitutions.

Geography and economy

Ramos Arizpe lies just north of Saltillo, the state capital, at an elevation of roughly 1,400 metres in a semi-arid valley of the Sierra Madre Oriental. Since the late 20th century it has grown rapidly as manufacturing, especially automotive assembly and parts plants, expanded into the Saltillo–Ramos Arizpe corridor. The Plan de Guadalupe International Airport serving Saltillo is located in Ramos Arizpe. The municipality has a population of over 100,000.
