Otis Dudley Duncan

Otis Dudley Duncan (April 2, 1921 – November 16, 2004) was an American sociologist who made foundational contributions to quantitative sociology, social stratification and demography. He spent much of his career at the University of Michigan and later at the University of Arizona and UC Santa Barbara.

Duncan is best known for developing the Duncan Socioeconomic Index (SEI), a measure of occupational status, and for co-authoring with Peter Blau the landmark study The American Occupational Structure (1967), which introduced path analysis to sociology and established the "Blau–Duncan model" of status attainment. His work on the socioeconomic measurement of occupations, on population distribution (with Beverly Duncan), and on statistical methods shaped postwar American sociology.

He also wrote extensively on the philosophy of social science and was a critic of crude hereditarian claims about intelligence. Duncan was elected to the National Academy of Sciences and served as president of the Population Association of America.
