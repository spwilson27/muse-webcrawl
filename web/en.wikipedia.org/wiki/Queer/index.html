Queer

Queer
Meaning: Umbrella term for non-heterosexual and/or non-cisgender identities
Original sense: "Strange, peculiar" (16th century)

Queer is an umbrella term for people whose sexual orientation or gender identity differs from the heterosexual and cisgender majority. Originally meaning "strange" or "peculiar", the word came to be used pejoratively against gay people in the late 19th and 20th centuries; beginning in the late 1980s, many in the LGBT communities reclaimed it as a deliberately provocative and politically radical self-label.

History

The word entered English in the 16th century, probably from German quer ("crosswise, at an angle"), meaning odd or unusual. Its earliest recorded use in reference to homosexuality dates from the 1890s, and for much of the 20th century "queer" functioned mainly as a slur in mainstream usage, though it was also used in-group.

Reclamation

During the AIDS crisis of the late 1980s, activist groups such as Queer Nation (founded 1990) adopted the word as a defiant identity, popularised by the slogan "We're here, we're queer, get used to it." The academic field of queer theory emerged at the same time, drawing on the work of Michel Foucault and theorists such as Judith Butler and Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick, and questioning fixed categories of gender and sexuality.

Contemporary use

Today "queer" is widely used as a broad, inclusive umbrella term and as an alternative to ever-lengthening acronyms such as LGBTQIA+. Some older people and others still regard the word as offensive because of its history as a slur, and usage varies by generation, region, and context. It also functions as a verb — "queering" — meaning to read or interpret something against normative assumptions.
