The Rocketdyne F-1 is a rocket engine developed by Rocketdyne. The F-1 remains the most powerful single combustion-chamber liquid-propellant rocket engine ever developed. It was used in the first stage (S-IC) of the Saturn V rocket, with five F-1 engines clustered together to launch the Apollo program's crewed missions to the Moon.

History

Rocketdyne began developing the F-1 in the late 1950s under a United States Air Force contract for a very large rocket engine. NASA adopted the engine for the Saturn V. Early development was plagued by combustion instability, a problem eventually solved by detonating small explosive charges in the combustion chamber during test firings and redesigning the injector plate with baffles. The F-1 first flew on Apollo 4 in 1967.

Design

The engine burns RP-1 (a refined kerosene) with liquid oxygen as oxidizer in a gas-generator cycle. Its huge thrust chamber, regeneratively cooled by fuel flowing through tubes forming the chamber walls, stands over 5.6 metres tall. Each engine consumed about 2,600 kilograms of propellant per second. Five F-1s produced a combined thrust of about 34.5 million newtons at liftoff, more than the rest of the Saturn V's stages and enough to lift the 2.9-million-kilogram vehicle from the launch pad.
