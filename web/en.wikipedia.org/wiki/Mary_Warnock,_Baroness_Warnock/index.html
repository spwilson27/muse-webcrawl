Helen Mary Warnock, Baroness Warnock (née Wilson; 14 April 1924 – 20 March 2019), known as Mary Warnock, was an English philosopher, academic, and writer. She chaired two influential government inquiries: the committee on special educational needs, whose 1978 report reshaped British policy, and the Committee of Inquiry into Human Fertilisation and Embryology (1982–84), whose "Warnock Report" led to the Human Fertilisation and Embryology Act 1990.

Life and career

Warnock studied classics at Lady Margaret Hall, Oxford. She taught philosophy at St Hugh's College, Oxford, from 1949 to 1966, and wrote widely read books on existentialism, ethics, and the philosophy of mind, including Ethics Since 1900 and Imagination. From 1984 to 1991 she was Mistress of Girton College, Cambridge. She was made a life peer in 1985 and sat in the House of Lords as a crossbencher.

The Warnock inquiries

The 1978 Report of the Committee of Inquiry into the Education of Handicapped Children and Young People introduced the concept of "special educational needs," rejected rigid categories of handicap, and promoted the integration of disabled children into mainstream schools, with "statements" of need for those requiring extra provision.

The 1984 report on fertilisation and embryology sought a middle path on embryo research: it recommended licensing and a statutory authority, and proposed the fourteen-day limit on keeping human embryos in vitro — a rule adopted in Britain and emulated worldwide. Warnock later chaired inquiries on animal experimentation and wrote extensively on euthanasia, education, and bioethics.
