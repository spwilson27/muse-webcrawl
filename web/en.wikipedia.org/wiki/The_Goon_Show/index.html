The Goon Show is a British radio comedy programme, originally produced and broadcast by the BBC Home Service from 1951 to 1960. Written principally by Spike Milligan, it starred Milligan, Harry Secombe and Peter Sellers, with Michael Bentine in the first two series.

Style and characters

The show pioneered surreal, fast-moving comedy built from absurd plots, puns, catchphrases, elaborate sound effects and sudden collapses of the narrative. Its regular characters included the heroic idiot Neddie Seagoon (Secombe), the squeaky-voiced Bluebottle and the drawling Major Bloodnok (Sellers), the upper-class twits Henry Crun and Minnie Bannister (Sellers and Milligan), and the imbecilic Eccles (Milligan). Episodes typically sent Seagoon on some deranged quest involving Count Jim Moriarty and his accomplice Grytpype-Thynne.

Influence

The Goon Show transformed British comedy, directly influencing Monty Python, The Beatles and generations of comedians; Charles, Prince of Wales (now Charles III) was a famous fan. Many episodes survive and are regularly repeated on BBC Radio 4 Extra. A final reunion performance, The Last Goon Show of All, was recorded in 1972.
