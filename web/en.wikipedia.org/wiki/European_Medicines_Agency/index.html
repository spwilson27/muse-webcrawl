European Medicines Agency

Image: EMA headquarters, Amsterdam (placeholder)

The European Medicines Agency (EMA) is an agency of the European Union responsible for the scientific evaluation, supervision, and safety monitoring of medicines for human and veterinary use. Founded in 1995 and long based in London, it relocated to Amsterdam in 2019 as a consequence of Brexit.

Functions

The EMA coordinates the centralised authorisation procedure, under which a single marketing authorisation is valid across all EU member states, and its Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) issues scientific opinions on new medicines. The European Commission then makes the legally binding approval decision. The agency also runs pharmacovigilance systems such as EudraVigilance to detect adverse drug reactions, and evaluates orphan medicines and advanced therapies.

Recent history

During the COVID-19 pandemic the EMA assessed and recommended conditional marketing authorisations for several vaccines and treatments, and it played a leading role in investigating rare adverse events. Its Amsterdam headquarters, a purpose-built tower in the Zuidas district, opened in 2019.
