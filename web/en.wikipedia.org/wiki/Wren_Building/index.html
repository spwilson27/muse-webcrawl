Wren Building

The Wren Building is the signature building of the College of William & Mary in Williamsburg, Virginia. Construction began on August 8, 1695, and the building was completed in 1700, making it the oldest academic building in continuous use in the United States — classes are still held in its classrooms. It is traditionally attributed to the English architect Sir Christopher Wren, though the extent of his involvement is debated; the design was described in 1724 by Hugh Jones as "first modelled by Sir Christopher Wren, adapted to the nature of the country by the gentlemen there".

History

The building was constructed on the western end of Duke of Gloucester Street after the colonial capital moved from Jamestown to Williamsburg. It burned three times — in 1705, during the Revolutionary War in 1781 when it served as a hospital, and in 1859 — and was rebuilt each time within its surviving brick walls; it was restored to its colonial appearance in 1928–1931 as part of John D. Rockefeller Jr.'s restoration of Colonial Williamsburg. During the Civil War it was used by Confederate forces and burned again in 1862, with the library destroyed.

The building housed the entire college — dormitory, refectory, classrooms, chapel, and library — in its early decades. The chapel wing (1732) contains the crypt of colonial figures including Governor Norborne Berkeley, Baron de Botetourt. The Great Hall served as a meeting place and dining hall. The building appears on the college seal and gave its name to the surrounding "Ancient Campus".

Notable traditions include the ringing of the Wren bell for special occasions and the signing of the Honor Code by new students in the building's courtyard.
