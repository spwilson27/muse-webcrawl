Layover

Layover
Also calledStopover (for longer stays)
Typical durationUnder 4 hours (domestic, US conventions); under 24 hours (international)
Related conceptsConnecting flight; minimum connect time; multi-city ticket

In scheduled transportation, a layover (also called a lay over or stopover) is a point where a vehicle stops, with passengers possibly changing vehicles. In public transit, this typically takes a few minutes at a trip terminal. For air travel, a delay is more often called a layover when passengers wait in the airport, while a stopover commonly denotes a longer break — often of 24 hours or more on international itineraries — during which travellers may leave the airport.

Layovers give passengers time to transfer between flights and give airlines, bus and rail operators time to service vehicles, change crews and comply with mandatory rest requirements. Airlines schedule connections around a minimum connect time (MCT) specific to each airport, which can range from under 30 minutes at small hubs to several hours where immigration and customs must be cleared.

Air travel

For air passengers, layovers are a consequence of the hub-and-spoke model: travellers fly into a hub airport and connect to a second flight to their destination. A "short" layover risks a missed connection if the inbound flight is delayed, whereas a long layover may allow sightseeing — some airlines and tourism boards actively promote stopover programmes, notably in Iceland, Singapore, Doha and Dubai, permitting stays of several days on a single ticket without extra airfare.

Whether checked baggage is transferred automatically depends on the itinerary, the carriers involved and whether the passenger must clear customs at the connecting point, as is required on arrival in the United States. Missed connections on a single ticket are generally rebooked by the airline at no charge, and compensation rules such as EU Regulation 261/2004 may apply.

Other transport

In long-distance bus and rail travel, layovers may occur at terminals where passengers transfer between routes or wait out crew rest periods. For transit drivers, a layover is also the scheduled recovery time at the end of a trip before the return journey begins.
