Internship (medicine)

Medical internship
OccupationPhysician-in-training (intern)
PredecessorMedical school
SuccessorResidency
Typical length1 year (US/Canada, Australia); varies worldwide

A medical internship is a period of supervised clinical training undertaken by newly graduated physicians, generally lasting one year, before full medical licensure or entry into specialty training. A doctor in this period is called an intern — in the United States, equivalently, a first-year resident (PGY-1). Interns have completed medical school and hold a medical degree but practise under the supervision of senior physicians, taking histories, examining patients, ordering investigations, performing procedures and managing day-to-day ward care.

By country

United States and Canada

Historically a free-standing rotating internship preceded residency; today most US trainees proceed directly into a residency whose first year is the internship year, allocated through the National Resident Matching Program. Preliminary (one-year) and transitional-year internships remain for specialties that require them. Duty hours are regulated by the ACGME — since 2011 first-year residents have been limited in shift length, following earlier reforms after the Libby Zion case.

United Kingdom

The equivalent stage is the two-year Foundation Programme (FY1 and FY2); FY1 corresponds to the former pre-registration house officer year and is required for full registration with the General Medical Council.

Australia and New Zealand

Graduates complete a 12-month internship (PGY1) of accredited rotations in medicine, surgery and emergency care before general registration, followed by residency years.

Other systems

India requires a one-year compulsory rotating internship after the MBBS before registration; South Africa requires two years of internship plus community service; many European countries integrate equivalent supervised practice into final medical-school years or a subsequent "Arzt im Praktikum"-style period.
