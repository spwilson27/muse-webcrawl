Turkey meat

Turkey meat, commonly referred to simply as turkey, is the meat from turkeys, typically domesticated turkeys but also wild turkeys. It is a popular poultry product, widely consumed in the United States, where a whole roasted turkey is the centrepiece of Thanksgiving dinner, and in the United Kingdom at Christmas.

Production

Most turkey meat comes from broad-breasted white turkeys bred for rapid growth. The United States is the world's largest producer, with major production in Minnesota, North Carolina, and Arkansas. Turkey is sold whole, as breast meat, ground, or in processed products such as deli slices, sausages, and bacon substitutes.

Nutrition

Turkey meat is a lean source of protein, rich in B vitamins, selenium, and phosphorus, and generally lower in fat than red meat; white breast meat is leaner than dark leg meat. Turkey contains the amino acid tryptophan, though the drowsiness often attributed to it after holiday meals is more likely due to large meals generally. Cooked turkey should reach a safe internal temperature of 74 °C (165 °F).
