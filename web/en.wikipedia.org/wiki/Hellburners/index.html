Hellburners

TypeExplosive fireship
UsedSiege of Antwerp, 1585
DesignerFederigo Giambelli

Hellburners (Dutch: hellebranders) were explosive fireships used during the Siege of Antwerp (1584–85) in the Eighty Years' War, designed by Italian engineer Federigo Giambelli for the Dutch defenders.

Design

Unlike ordinary fireships, hellburners carried a large gunpowder charge in a masonry chamber, topped with rubble and metal to act as shrapnel, and triggered by clockwork or slow fuse — in effect early weapons of mass destruction.

Attack on Antwerp

On the night of 4–5 April 1585 two hellburners were floated toward Alexander Farnese's pontoon bridge across the Scheldt. One exploded catastrophically, killing perhaps 800–1,000 Spanish troops and destroying part of the bridge, though the Dutch failed to exploit the breach and Antwerp fell that August.

Legacy

The fear they inspired persisted: at the Spanish Armada campaign in 1588, the mere approach of Dutch fireships at Calais panicked the Spanish fleet into cutting anchor. The word 'hellburner' entered English accounts of naval warfare.
