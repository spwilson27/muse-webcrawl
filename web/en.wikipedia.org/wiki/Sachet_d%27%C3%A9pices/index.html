Sachet d'épices

A sachet d'épices (French for "bag of spices") is a small bag of herbs and spices used to flavour stocks, soups, sauces and braising liquids in classical French cuisine. The aromatics are enclosed in a piece of cheesecloth (muslin) tied with kitchen string — or, in modern kitchens, placed in a tea ball or spice bag — so that they can infuse the cooking liquid and then be removed cleanly before service.

Composition

A standard sachet contains dried or fresh thyme, parsley stems, a bay leaf, cracked peppercorns and often whole cloves and a clove of garlic; variations add celery leaves, leeks or other aromatics according to the dish. Quantities are scaled to the volume of liquid.

Comparison with bouquet garni

The sachet is closely related to the bouquet garni, which is a bundle of fresh herbs tied together (sometimes wrapped in a leek leaf). The distinction usually drawn is that the bouquet garni emphasises fresh herbs in bundle form, while the sachet d'épices is a cheesecloth bag that can also hold dry, small or loose ingredients such as peppercorns and cloves that would otherwise be lost in the liquid. Both are removed and discarded at the end of cooking.
