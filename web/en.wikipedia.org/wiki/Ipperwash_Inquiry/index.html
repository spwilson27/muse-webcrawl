Ipperwash Inquiry

The Ipperwash Inquiry was a public inquiry established by the Government of Ontario in 2003 to investigate the events surrounding the death of Dudley George, an unarmed Ojibwa protester shot by an Ontario Provincial Police officer during the Ipperwash Crisis of September 1995. Commissioner Sidney Linden conducted hearings and delivered his report on May 31, 2007.

Background

In 1995, members of the Stoney Point First Nation occupied Ipperwash Provincial Park, asserting that it contained a burial ground and land wrongfully taken — the adjacent Camp Ipperwash had been expropriated during the Second World War and never returned. On the night of September 6, 1995, police moved against the occupiers; Acting Sergeant Kenneth Deane shot Dudley George, who died. Deane was later convicted of criminal negligence causing death.

Findings and aftermath

The inquiry's four-volume report criticised the police operation and the provincial government's handling of the crisis, found racism within the police to be a factor, and made 98 recommendations concerning policing of Aboriginal protests, land-claims processes, and the creation of a provincial ministry for Aboriginal affairs. In 2009 the federal government formally returned the Camp Ipperwash land to the Kettle and Stony Point First Nation, and the park itself was later transferred.
