The Kunstmuseum Bern (Bern Museum of Fine Arts) is the oldest art museum in Switzerland with a permanent collection. Its holdings range from the Middle Ages to contemporary art, with particular strengths in Italian Trecento painting, Bernese art, Swiss art from the 15th century onward, and international art of the 19th and 20th centuries, including major groups of works by Paul Klee, Pablo Picasso, Ferdinand Hodler and Meret Oppenheim.

History

The museum's origins lie in the art collections assembled by the canton and city of Bern from the early 19th century; it was formally founded in 1879. Its building on Hodlerstrasse was designed by Eugen Stettler and opened in 1879, with major extensions in 1936 and an additional modern wing (the Atelier 5/Salvisberg building) in 1983. Since 2015 the Kunstmuseum Bern and the Zentrum Paul Klee have been administered under a common umbrella foundation.

Gurlitt collection

In 2014 the museum was named sole heir of Cornelius Gurlitt, son of the Nazi-era art dealer Hildebrand Gurlitt, inheriting the controversial "Gurlitt hoard" of some 1,500 works. The museum accepted the bequest under an agreement with Germany and the Canton of Bern committing it to provenance research and restitution of any Nazi-looted works; exhibitions of the collection ("Gurlitt: Status Report") followed in Bern and Bonn in 2017–18.

Collections

Highlights include works by Duccio di Buoninsegna, Niklaus Manuel Deutsch, Albert Anker, Ferdinand Hodler, Cuno Amiet, Paul Klee (supplemented by the nearby Zentrum Paul Klee), Wassily Kandinsky, Pablo Picasso, Joan Miró, Jackson Pollock, and contemporary Swiss and international artists. The museum also administers the estates and foundations of several major Swiss artists and collectors.
