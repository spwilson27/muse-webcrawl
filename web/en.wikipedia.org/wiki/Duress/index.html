Duress

Duress
Legal area: Criminal law, contract law
Types: Duress by threats; duress by circumstances; economic duress
Related: Necessity, coercion, undue influence

In jurisprudence, duress is a situation where a person is coerced, by threat of harm, to act against their wishes. It operates both as a defence in criminal law and as a doctrine that can make a contract voidable in civil law.

Criminal law

Duress by threats excuses a defendant who committed an offence because of a threat of death or serious injury that a reasonable person could not resist; in English law it is traditionally unavailable for murder and attempted murder (R v Howe). Duress by circumstances covers threats arising from situations rather than a human threatener.

Contract law

A contract entered under physical threats is voidable; economic duress—illegitimate commercial pressure leaving no practical alternative—can also permit rescission, as developed in cases such as Universe Tankships v ITWF. Duress differs from undue influence, which concerns abuse of a relationship of trust rather than threats.
