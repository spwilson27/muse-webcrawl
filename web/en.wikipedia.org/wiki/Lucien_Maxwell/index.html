Lucien Bonaparte Maxwell (1818–1875) was an American mountain man, rancher, scout, and landowner who became one of the largest private landowners in United States history as proprietor of the Maxwell Land Grant in present-day New Mexico and Colorado.

Early life and fur trade

Born in Kaskaskia, Illinois, Maxwell went west as a teenager and joined the Rocky Mountain fur trade, travelling with and befriending Kit Carson. In 1841 he settled in Taos, New Mexico, and married María de la Luz Beaubien, daughter of the French-Canadian trader Carlos Beaubien, co-holder of a vast Mexican land grant.

The Maxwell Land Grant

Through marriage and purchase, Maxwell consolidated sole ownership of the Beaubien–Miranda grant, roughly 1.7 million acres. He founded the town of Cimarron, built mills and a famous saloon, and entertained travellers on the Santa Fe Trail. In 1870 he sold the grant to a syndicate of investors — reportedly for $1.35 million — and moved to Fort Sumner, where he bought the abandoned army post.

Death and legacy

Maxwell died at Fort Sumner in 1875 and is buried there near the grave of Billy the Kid, whom his son Pete Maxwell knew; it was in Pete Maxwell's bedroom that Sheriff Pat Garrett shot the outlaw in 1881. The sale of Maxwell's grant triggered the Colfax County War and decades of litigation over settler rights.
