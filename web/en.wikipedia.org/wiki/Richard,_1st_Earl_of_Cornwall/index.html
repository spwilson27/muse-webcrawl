Richard, 1st Earl of Cornwall

Richard of Cornwall
Born: 5 January 1209, Winchester
Died: 2 April 1272, Berkhamsted
Reign as King of the Romans: 1257–1272
Father: King John
Brother: Henry III

Richard, 1st Earl of Cornwall (5 January 1209 – 2 April 1272) was an English prince, second son of King John and Isabella of Angoulême. He was made Earl of Cornwall in 1227 and became one of the wealthiest men in Europe. In 1257 he was elected King of the Romans (King of Germany) by four of the seven prince-electors, though he never secured the imperial crown from the Pope and his authority in Germany remained limited.

Early life and earldom

Richard was knighted by his brother Henry III and given the county of Cornwall, whose tin mines and stannary revenues were the foundation of his fortune. He quarrelled periodically with the king over grants and his marriage, and in 1238 was the target of a baronial plot. He went on crusade in 1240–41, negotiating the refortification of Ascalon, though he avoided major battles.

King of the Romans

After the death of William of Holland in 1256, a disputed imperial election followed. Richard, backed by English money and bribes, was elected by the electors of Cologne, Mainz, the Palatinate and Bohemia in January 1257, while Alfonso X of Castile was chosen by the rival electors. Richard was crowned at Aachen in May 1257 and visited Germany four times, but he never went to Rome for imperial coronation and German affairs were largely managed in his absence.

Barons' wars and death

During the Second Barons' War Richard supported his brother and was captured at the Battle of Lewes in 1264, famously hiding in a windmill, and imprisoned until 1265. He founded Hailes Abbey in Gloucestershire, where he was eventually buried beside his second wife Sanchia of Provence. He died at Berkhamsted in 1272; his son Edmund succeeded him as Earl of Cornwall.
