The Haudenosaunee Nationals are the national lacrosse teams representing the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, the Indigenous peoples credited with inventing the game. Founded as the Iroquois Nationals and renamed in 2022, the programme fields men's field and box teams and a women's team. The Haudenosaunee are a full member of World Lacrosse — the only Indigenous nation so recognised — and compete as a sovereign nation, travelling on Haudenosaunee passports.

Background

Lacrosse originates in the Haudenosaunee game known as Tewaaraton, the "Creator's game," played for spiritual, medicinal, and diplomatic purposes for centuries. The Nationals were organised in 1983 and admitted to the International Lacrosse Federation in 1988 after the federation amended its rules to admit them — a landmark assertion of Indigenous sovereignty in sport.

Competition history

The men's field team has won bronze at multiple World Lacrosse Championships (2014, 2018, 2023), finishing behind only the United States and Canada. In box lacrosse, the team has repeatedly taken silver at the World Indoor Lacrosse Championship. The women's programme, long excluded from tournaments for want of sanctioning, made history by competing at the 2022 World Games cycle events and has campaigned for full championship inclusion.

Passport dispute and Olympics

In 2010 the team was prevented from travelling to the world championship in England when the United Kingdom refused to recognise Haudenosaunee passports — a controversy that drew international attention to Indigenous sovereignty. With lacrosse returning at the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, the Haudenosaunee have sought inclusion as a distinct team; the campaign has received support from the U.S. and Canadian governments, though Olympic rules tie eligibility to recognised national Olympic committees.
