Data governance

Data governance is the process of managing the availability, usability, integrity and security of the data in enterprise systems, based on internal data standards and policies that also control data usage. Effective data governance ensures that data is consistent and trustworthy and doesn't get misused. It becomes increasingly critical as organizations face data privacy regulations and rely on data analytics to help optimize operations and drive business decision-making.

Overview

Data governance comprises the people, processes and technology required to create consistent handling of an organization's data across the business enterprise. Key focus areas include availability, usability, consistency, data integrity and data security, and it includes establishing processes to ensure effective data management throughout the enterprise, such as accountability for the adverse effects of poor data quality and ensuring that data can be used by the entire organization.

Key components

  • Data stewardship — individuals or teams responsible for data assets in a given domain.
  • Data quality management — profiling, cleansing and monitoring of data against defined rules.
  • Master data management — maintaining a single authoritative version of core entities such as customers and products.
  • Metadata management — catalogues and glossaries describing what data means and where it resides.
  • Compliance and privacy — controls supporting regulations such as the GDPR and CCPA.

Frameworks

Widely used frameworks include DAMA International's DMBOK (Data Management Body of Knowledge), the Data Governance Institute framework, and COBIT. Many organizations establish a data governance council of senior stakeholders, with working groups implementing policy.
