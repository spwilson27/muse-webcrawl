VCSEL

A vertical-cavity surface-emitting laser (VCSEL) is a type of semiconductor laser diode with laser beam emission perpendicular from the top surface, contrary to conventional edge-emitting laser diodes which emit from surfaces formed by cleaving the individual chip out of a wafer.

Structure

The VCSEL's optical cavity consists of two distributed Bragg reflector (DBR) mirrors parallel to the wafer surface, with an active region of quantum wells between them. Common emission wavelengths are 850 nm and 980 nm for gallium arsenide-based devices, with longer wavelengths (1,300–1,550 nm) used in telecommunications.

Advantages and applications

VCSELs offer low threshold currents, circular low-divergence output beams, on-wafer testing and easy fabrication into two-dimensional arrays. They are widely used in short-reach optical fibre data communications, computer mice and other optical sensors, laser printers, and 3D sensing systems such as smartphone facial recognition (e.g. Apple's Face ID).
