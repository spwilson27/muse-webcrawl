Children of Muhammad

The Islamic prophet Muhammad had seven children, according to most Islamic traditions: three sons and four daughters. Six were born to his first wife, Khadija bint Khuwaylid, and one son, Ibrahim, was born to Maria al-Qibtiyya. All of Muhammad's sons died in infancy or early childhood. His daughters mostly reached adulthood, but only Fatima outlived him, and only briefly.

Sons

Qasim ibn Muhammad, the eldest child, died in infancy around 598 CE; Muhammad's kunya Abu al-Qasim derives from him. Abdullah ibn Muhammad also died young, before Muhammad's prophetic mission or shortly after it began. Ibrahim ibn Muhammad, born to Maria al-Qibtiyya in Medina around 630, died at about 16–18 months of age; Muhammad is reported to have grieved openly at his death.

Daughters

Zaynab, the eldest daughter, married Abu al-As ibn al-Rabi' and died in 629. Ruqayya married Uthman ibn Affan, later the third caliph, and died during the Battle of Badr in 624. Umm Kulthum subsequently married Uthman as well, earning him the title "Possessor of Two Lights", and died in 630. Fatima, the youngest and most prominent, married Ali ibn Abi Talib; through her sons Hasan and Husayn descend all of Muhammad's surviving progeny (sayyids). Fatima died about six months after her father, in 632.

Significance

Because Muhammad left no surviving sons, questions of succession after his death in 632 centred on his wider family and companions. In Shia Islam, the line of Fatima and Ali holds central religious significance, while Sunni tradition reveres all of Muhammad's children as members of his household (Ahl al-Bayt).
