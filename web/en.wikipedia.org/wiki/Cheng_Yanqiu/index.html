Cheng Yanqiu (Chinese: 程硯秋; 1904–1958) was a Chinese Peking opera singer, one of the most celebrated performers of the twentieth century and one of the "Four Great Dan" (四大名旦) alongside Mei Lanfang, Shang Xiaoyun and Xun Huisheng. Specialising in female dan roles, he created a distinctive, melancholy singing style known as the Cheng school (程派).

Early life

Born into a poor Manchu family in Beijing, Cheng was apprenticed as a child to the opera performer Luo Yinggong and later studied under Wang Yaoqing, the influential teacher of many dan actors. He began performing professionally in his early teens and gained fame in the 1920s with a voice that deepened and lost brightness after illness — a setback he turned into an advantage by developing a low, muffled, winding vocal technique full of pathos.

Artistry

The Cheng style is characterised by subtle, introspective emotion, elaborate ornamentation in slow tempos (the "ghostly" gliding tones of his signature arias), and a repertoire centred on tragic women. Famous Cheng-school plays include The Unicorn Purse (鎖麟囊), first performed in 1940 and regarded as his masterpiece, Tears of Barren Mountain (荒山淚) and Spring Boudoir Dream (春閨夢), the last two noted for their anti-war themes. In 1932 he toured Europe to study Western theatre, an experience that influenced his reformist approach to staging.

Later life and legacy

After 1949 Cheng held cultural posts in the People's Republic, including leadership roles connected with the China Peking Opera Theatre, and trained a generation of successors. He died of a heart attack in Beijing in 1958. His art remains one of the principal schools in which dan performers are trained today.
