Adoptionism, also called dynamic monarchianism, is an early Christian theological doctrine holding that Jesus was born as an ordinary human being and was adopted as the Son of God — typically at his baptism in the Jordan, in some versions at his resurrection — because of his sinless life and perfect obedience. It denies that Jesus was God by nature from eternity, and was condemned as heretical by the early Church, whose orthodox doctrine confessed Christ as eternally the Son, of one being with the Father.

History

Adoptionist ideas appear in some of the earliest Christian texts and communities: the Shepherd of Hermas has adoptionist passages, and the Ebionites reportedly held that Jesus was the human son of Joseph and Mary chosen as Messiah. Theodotus of Byzantium, excommunicated at Rome around 190, taught that Jesus was a mere man (psilos anthropos, hence "psilanthropism") upon whom the Christ-Spirit descended at baptism. Paul of Samosata, bishop of Antioch deposed in 268, taught a related doctrine in which the Logos was an impersonal power of God dwelling in Jesus. The doctrine resurfaced in eighth-century Spain ("Spanish Adoptionism"), when Archbishop Elipandus of Toledo and Bishop Felix of Urgell described Christ, in his humanity, as the adoptive Son of God — a formula attacked by Beatus of Liébana and Alcuin and condemned at the Council of Frankfurt in 794. Echoes of adoptionism reappear in some later unitarian and liberal theologies.
