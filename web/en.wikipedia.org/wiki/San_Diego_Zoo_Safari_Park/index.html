San Diego Zoo Safari Park

San Diego Zoo Safari Park
Opened: May 10, 1972
Location: Escondido, California, United States
Land area: about 1,800 acres (730 ha)
Operated by: San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance

The San Diego Zoo Safari Park, originally the San Diego Wild Animal Park (1972–2010), is an 1,800-acre (730 ha) zoo in the San Pasqual Valley area of Escondido, California, operated by the San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance. It is one of the largest tourist attractions in San Diego County, and is renowned for its very large free-range enclosures, in which herds of African and Asian animals roam over naturalistic savanna habitats.

History

The park grew out of the San Diego Zoo's need for a spacious auxiliary breeding facility for large hoofed animals. It opened in 1972 as the Wild Animal Park, offering a monorail tour around the savanna enclosures. It was renamed the San Diego Zoo Safari Park in 2010 to emphasise the safari-style experiences it offers, including guided truck tours, a balloon ride, and the Flightline zip line.

Conservation

The Safari Park is celebrated for its breeding successes with endangered species. It played a central role in the recovery of the California condor and has bred southern white rhinoceroses in exceptional numbers; the park's herd provided animals for reintroduction efforts, and it has worked on the northern white rhinoceros rescue project using southern white surrogates. A notable resident was Nola, one of the last four northern white rhinos in the world, who died in 2015. The park also houses a large veterinary hospital, the Paul Harter Veterinary Medical Center.

Exhibits

Major areas include African Plains, Asian Savanna, Tiger Trail (opened 2014 for Sumatran tigers), Elephant Valley, Walkabout Australia, and the Nairobi Village with its petting kraal and animal encounters. The park's planted collections, like those of the San Diego Zoo, are significant in their own right.
