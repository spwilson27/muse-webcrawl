Street light

A street light, light pole, lamp standard, or lamp post is a raised source of light on the edge of a road or path. When urban electric power distribution became ubiquitous in developed countries in the 20th century, lights for urban streets followed, or sometimes led. Many lamps have light-sensitive photocells that activate the light automatically when needed, at times such as dusk or dawn, or at the onset of dark weather.

History

Street lighting began with oil lamps in ancient cities and expanded with gas lighting in the early 19th century — London's Pall Mall was lit by gas in 1807. Arc lamps appeared in the late 19th century, followed by incandescent lamps. Through the 20th century, high-intensity discharge lamps, especially high-pressure sodium lamps with their characteristic orange glow, became standard. In the 21st century, LED street lights have increasingly replaced older technologies due to their energy efficiency, long service life, and controllable output.

Purpose and effects

Roadway lighting is intended to improve visibility and safety for drivers and pedestrians at night. Research suggests lighting can reduce nighttime crashes and may also reduce crime, though effects vary by context. Poorly designed lighting contributes to light pollution, glare, and skyglow, and can disrupt ecosystems and human circadian rhythms, motivating "dark sky" friendly designs with shielded fixtures and warmer colour temperatures.
