Central Railway zone

Central Railway
Headquarters: Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus, Mumbai
Dates of operation: 1951–present
Predecessor: Great Indian Peninsula Railway
Divisions: Mumbai, Bhusawal, Nagpur, Pune, Solapur

Central Railway (abbreviated CR) is one of the zones of Indian Railways. Formed on 5 November 1951 by combining several state-managed railways including the Great Indian Peninsula Railway, it is headquartered at Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus in Mumbai, Maharashtra.

Network

The zone covers a large part of Maharashtra, southern Madhya Pradesh and a small portion of north-eastern Karnataka, with more than 4,000 route kilometres. It operates the Mumbai suburban railway's Central and Harbour lines from Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus, carrying millions of commuters daily, as well as long-distance services such as the Deccan Queen to Pune.

Divisions

Central Railway has five divisions: Mumbai, Bhusawal, Nagpur, Pune and Solapur. Its workshops at Matunga and Parel maintain coaching stock, and the zone pioneered the introduction of 25 kV AC traction and the conversion of Mumbai's suburban network from 1,500 V DC.

Heritage

The Great Indian Peninsula Railway built India's first railway line, from Bori Bunder (Mumbai) to Thane in 1853, over territory now administered by Central Railway.
