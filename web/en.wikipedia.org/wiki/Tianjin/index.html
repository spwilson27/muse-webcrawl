Tianjin

Tianjin (Chinese: 天津; pinyin: Tiānjīn) is a direct-controlled municipality and one of the largest cities in northern China, on the shore of the Bohai Sea, adjoining Beijing to the northwest. With more than 13 million residents in the municipality, it is among China's most populous urban centres and serves as the principal port for the capital region.

History

Tianjin grew as a canal and river port where the Grand Canal meets the Hai River. After the Second Opium War, the Treaties of Tientsin (1858) opened it as a treaty port, and foreign concessions with distinctive European architecture were established along the Hai River. It was occupied during the Boxer Rebellion in 1900.

Economy

The Binhai New Area hosts aerospace, petrochemical, and manufacturing industries, while the Port of Tianjin is one of the world's busiest cargo ports. Landmarks include the Tianjin Eye ferris wheel, the Five Great Avenues of former concession villas, and the Italian-style town.
