Psychotic break

A psychotic break, also called a psychotic episode, is a period in which a person experiences a break from reality, characterised by symptoms of psychosis such as delusions, hallucinations, disorganised speech or behaviour, and impaired insight. It is not a diagnosis in itself but a descriptive term for an acute manifestation of a psychotic disorder or another medical or psychiatric condition.

Causes

Psychotic breaks may occur in schizophrenia, bipolar disorder during manic or depressive episodes, major depressive disorder with psychotic features, schizoaffective disorder, brief psychotic disorder, substance-induced psychosis, postpartum psychosis, and various medical conditions including infections, metabolic disorders and neurological disease. Severe sleep deprivation, extreme stress and certain medications can also precipitate episodes in vulnerable individuals.

Presentation and treatment

Onset may be abrupt or follow a prodromal phase of social withdrawal, suspiciousness and declining function. Treatment typically involves antipsychotic medication, hospitalisation when there is risk to the person or others, and psychosocial support. Early intervention services aim to reduce the duration of untreated psychosis, which is associated with better long-term outcomes.
