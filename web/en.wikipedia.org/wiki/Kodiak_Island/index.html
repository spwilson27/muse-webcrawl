Kodiak Island (Alutiiq: Qikertaq) is a large island on the south coast of the U.S. state of Alaska, separated from the Alaska mainland by the Shelikof Strait. It is the largest island in the Kodiak Archipelago and the second-largest island in the United States, after the island of Hawaii. Most of the island's land area is included in the Kodiak National Wildlife Refuge.

Geography

The island is about 160 km (99 mi) long and ranges from 16 to 96 km (10 to 60 mi) in width. Its heavily indented coastline was carved by glaciation, and much of the interior is mountainous, covered in the south-west by tundra and grassland and in the north-east by dense Sitka spruce forest. The climate is subpolar oceanic, wet and cool.

History

Kodiak has been inhabited for at least 7,500 years by the ancestors of the Alutiiq (Sugpiaq) people. In 1784 Grigory Shelikhov founded the first permanent Russian settlement in Alaska at Three Saints Bay on Kodiak Island, beginning the Russian colonisation of Alaska. The colonial capital was later moved to Pavlovsk (present-day Kodiak). Under United States rule after the Alaska Purchase of 1867, the island developed commercial fishing and canning industries. The 1964 Good Friday earthquake and its tsunami devastated the town of Kodiak and several villages, and the 1989 Exxon Valdez oil spill affected the archipelago's fisheries.

Wildlife

The island is famous for the Kodiak bear, a large subspecies of brown bear that evolved in isolation since the last ice age; the Kodiak National Wildlife Refuge, established in 1941, protects about two-thirds of the island. Other wildlife includes Sitka black-tailed deer, red fox, sea otters, and rich salmon runs that support one of Alaska's most important commercial fisheries.

Communities

The city of Kodiak, home to a major U.S. Coast Guard base and one of the largest fishing ports in the United States by value of landings, is the main settlement. Alutiiq villages such as Akhiok, Karluk, Larsen Bay, Old Harbor, Ouzinkie and Port Lions are accessible only by air or water.
