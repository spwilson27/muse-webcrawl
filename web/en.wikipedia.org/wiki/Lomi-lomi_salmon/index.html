Lomi-lomi salmon (or lomi salmon) is a side dish in Hawaiian cuisine. It is a fresh tomato and salmon salad, originally introduced to Hawaiians by early Western sailors. It is typically prepared by mixing salted, shredded salmon with diced tomatoes, sweet Maui onions (or green onions), and occasionally crushed ice or chili pepper. The name derives from the Hawaiian word lomi, meaning "to rub, knead, crush, or massage", referring to the way the ingredients are massaged together by hand.

Preparation

The salmon used is typically salted raw salmon (often salmon that has been cured with sea salt overnight, then rinsed and diced, with the skin and bones removed). It is combined with chopped tomatoes and onions and worked together by hand until evenly mixed, then chilled before serving. Some recipes add crushed ice to keep the mixture cold and slightly dilute the saltiness.

Cultural significance

Lomi-lomi salmon is a standard accompaniment at a traditional Hawaiian lūʻau, where it is served alongside kālua pig, poi, laulau, and haupia. Although salmon is not native to Hawaiian waters, the dish has been fully absorbed into Native Hawaiian food culture since its introduction in the early nineteenth century, when salted salmon was traded from the Pacific Northwest.
