The Burana Tower is a large minaret in the Chuy Valley in northern Kyrgyzstan, about 80 kilometres east of the capital Bishkek and near the town of Tokmok. It is all that remains of the ancient city of Balasagun, a capital of the Karakhanid Khanate founded by Central Asian Turks at the end of the 9th century. Together with grave markers, earthworks, and the ruins of a citadel and shops, the tower is the centrepiece of an archaeological and museum complex.

History

Balasagun was established by the Karakhanids, the first Turkic dynasty to convert to Islam, and flourished as a Silk Road trading city. The minaret was built in the 11th century as a model for other minarets in the region; it originally stood about 45 metres tall. Over the centuries, earthquakes damaged the structure, and a major quake in the 15th century destroyed its upper half, leaving it at its present height of 25 metres. The city itself declined after the Mongol period and was eventually abandoned. In the 1970s the tower was restored, its foundation repaired, and the western and southern sides reconstructed.

Description

The tower is built of red brick with decorative banding, on an octagonal stone socle. A spiral staircase inside leads to the top, from which the surrounding ruins and the Tian Shan mountains can be seen. Around the site lie balbals — Turkic grave steles collected from across Kyrgyzstan — along with petroglyphs and a small museum displaying artefacts excavated at Balasagun, among them ceramics, coins, and items associated with the city's Sogdian, Turkic, and Buddhist past.

Legend

A popular legend tells of a witch's prophecy that a khan's daughter would die from a spider's bite on her eighteenth birthday; the khan imprisoned her in the tower to protect her, but a poisonous spider hidden in grapes brought to her caused her death, and the tower was abandoned in the khan's grief.
