Angel food cake, or angel cake, is a type of sponge cake made with egg whites, flour and sugar. Unlike most cakes it contains no butter or egg yolks, giving it a very light, airy texture and a pure white colour — hence the name, said to suggest "the food of the angels". A whipping agent, usually cream of tartar, is added to stabilise the beaten whites. It is the counterpart of the yellow, yolk-rich "devil's food cake".

History

Angel food cake originated in the United States in the late 19th century. Recipes appear in print by the 1880s, following the spread of earlier "white" or "snow" cakes and of efficient rotary egg beaters and the tube pan. Its invention is often credited to the Pennsylvania Dutch country and to St. Louis bakers; dedicated angel food cake pans, with a central tube and removable bottom, were patented and marketed from the 1880s onward. The cake became a classic of American home baking and church cookbooks.

Preparation

A large number of egg whites (typically 10–12) are beaten with cream of tartar to stiff peaks, sugar is folded in, and sifted cake flour is gently incorporated so as not to deflate the foam. Because the batter contains no fat and relies entirely on the egg-white foam for structure, the pan is left ungreased so the batter can climb its sides. After baking, the cake is cooled upside down — traditional pans have feet for this — so gravity stretches rather than compresses the crumb while it sets. It is usually served plain or with berries, whipped cream or a light glaze.

Characteristics

Angel food cake is fat-free and relatively low in calories for a cake, with a delicate, slightly chewy texture. Its high proportion of egg whites makes it a traditional way to use whites left over from yolk-rich dishes.
