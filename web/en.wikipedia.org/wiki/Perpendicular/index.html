Perpendicular

In geometry, two lines, line segments, or planes are perpendicular if they meet at a right angle (90 degrees). A line is said to be perpendicular to another if the two lines intersect at a right angle; the symbol ⊥ denotes perpendicularity.

Properties

If two lines are perpendicular, the product of their slopes in a Cartesian plane is −1 (unless one is vertical). The foot of a perpendicular is the point where a perpendicular line from a point meets another line or plane, giving the shortest distance between them. In three dimensions, a line perpendicular to a plane is perpendicular to every line in that plane through its foot.

Construction

A perpendicular can be drawn with compass and straightedge by constructing equal circles centred on a line and joining their intersections. The perpendicular bisector of a segment passes through its midpoint at a right angle.
