Chicken Run is a 2000 stop-motion animated comedy film produced by the British studio Aardman Animations in partnership with DreamWorks Animation. Directed by Peter Lord and Nick Park, the film is a parody of the prisoner-of-war escape film The Great Escape (1963), set on a chicken farm in Yorkshire in the 1950s. It features the voices of Julia Sawalha, Mel Gibson, Miranda Richardson, Jane Horrocks, and Timothy Spall. Chicken Run was a critical and commercial success, becoming the highest-grossing stop-motion animated film of all time. A sequel, Chicken Run: Dawn of the Nugget, was released on Netflix in 2023.

Plot

On a chicken farm run by the sinister Mr. and Mrs. Tweedy, the hens live in fear: any hen that fails to lay enough eggs is slaughtered for dinner. Led by the determined Ginger, the flock makes repeated failed escape attempts. When Mrs. Tweedy buys a pie-making machine to convert the farm's output from eggs to chicken pies, the hens' deadline becomes literal. Hope arrives in the form of Rocky Rhodes, an American rooster who crash-lands in the farm claiming he can fly, and whom Ginger blackmails into teaching the flock to fly. Though Rocky eventually flees when his deception is exposed, he returns in time to help the hens complete a pedal-powered flying machine, and the flock escapes over the fence to freedom, founding a sanctuary on a nearby island.

Production

The film was Aardman's first feature, expanding on the studio's success with the Wallace and Gromit shorts. Stop-motion production employed dozens of animators working on large-scale sets; the chickens' beaks were replaced frame by frame to form speech. The screenplay was written by Karey Kirkpatrick from a story by Lord and Park.

Reception

The film received near-universal critical acclaim for its humor, voice acting, and animation, and it grossed over $227 million worldwide against a budget of about $45 million. It remains the highest-grossing stop-motion film, not adjusted for inflation.
