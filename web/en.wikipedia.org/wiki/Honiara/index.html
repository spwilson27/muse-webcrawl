Honiara

Honiara is the capital and largest city of Solomon Islands, situated on the northwestern coast of Guadalcanal. Its population was about 85,000 in the 2019 census. The city grew out of the American military base established during the Guadalcanal campaign of World War II, developing around Henderson Field (now Honiara International Airport).

History

Honiara replaced Tulagi as the capital of the British Solomon Islands Protectorate in 1952. After independence in 1978 it remained the national capital. The city was a focal point of the ethnic tensions of 1998–2003, and in November 2021 riots caused extensive damage in its Chinatown district.

Geography

The city stretches along the Kukum Highway between the Mataniko and Lunga rivers, beneath ridges that saw heavy fighting in 1942–43, including Bloody Ridge. Its climate is tropical rainforest, hot and humid year-round.

Landmarks and life

Sites include the National Parliament, the Solomon Islands National Museum, the central market, and the American War Memorial on Skyline Ridge. Honiara hosts the country's main port and most government institutions, and hosted the 2023 Pacific Games, centred on a new national stadium built with Chinese assistance.
