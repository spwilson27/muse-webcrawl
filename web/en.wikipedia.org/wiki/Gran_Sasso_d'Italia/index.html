The Gran Sasso d'Italia (Italian for "Great Stone of Italy") is a massif in the Apennines in the Abruzzo region of central Italy. Its highest peak, Corno Grande (2,912 m), is the highest point of the Apennine chain and of the Italian peninsula south of the Alps.

Geography

The massif stretches for about 50 km between the provinces of Teramo, L'Aquila, and Pescara. On its northern side lies the Calderone glacier, traditionally described as the southernmost glacier in Europe. The high plateau of Campo Imperatore, sometimes called the "Little Tibet" of Italy, sits at about 1,800–2,200 m on the southern side.

History

In September 1943, Benito Mussolini, deposed and imprisoned at the Hotel Campo Imperatore on the plateau, was rescued by German paratroopers and glider troops led in part by Otto Skorzeny in the Gran Sasso raid. The area's underground physics facility, the Laboratori Nazionali del Gran Sasso, the world's largest underground laboratory for particle physics, was constructed in the 1980s beneath the massif beside the Traforo del Gran Sasso highway tunnel.

Nature and tourism

The massif forms the core of the Gran Sasso e Monti della Laga National Park, home to chamois, wolves, and the Marsican brown bear nearby. It is popular for hiking, climbing, and skiing at Campo Imperatore and Prati di Tivo.
