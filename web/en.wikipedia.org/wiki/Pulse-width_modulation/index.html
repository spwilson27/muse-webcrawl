Pulse-width modulation

Pulse-width modulation (PWM) is a method of controlling the average power delivered to an electrical load by rapidly switching the supply on and off. The average value of voltage (and current) fed to the load is controlled by turning the switch between supply and load on and off at a fast rate. The longer the switch is on compared to the off periods, the higher the total power supplied to the load.

Principle

The duty cycle describes the proportion of the period during which the signal is high. A duty cycle of 50% at a given switching frequency delivers half the maximum average power. PWM is widely used because power electronic switches are efficient when fully on or fully off, dissipating little power in either state.

Applications

PWM is used in motor speed controllers, LED dimming, Class-D audio amplifiers, switching power supplies, solar charge controllers, telecommunications encoding, and heating control. In servo control, a 50 Hz PWM signal with pulse widths of 1–2 ms sets shaft position.

History

The technique grew with power electronics in the mid-20th century and became ubiquitous with inexpensive MOSFETs and microcontrollers that can generate precise timing in hardware timers.
