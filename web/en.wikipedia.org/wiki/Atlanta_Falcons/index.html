The Atlanta Falcons are a professional American football team based in Atlanta, Georgia. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the National Football Conference (NFC) South division. The franchise was founded in 1966 by Rankin Smith Sr. and joined the NFL as an expansion team.

History

The Falcons played their first seasons at Atlanta–Fulton County Stadium before moving to the Georgia Dome in 1992 and to Mercedes-Benz Stadium in 2017. The team reached its first Super Bowl in the 1998 season (Super Bowl XXXIII), losing to the Denver Broncos. In the 2016 season, behind league MVP quarterback Matt Ryan, Atlanta reached Super Bowl LI, where it famously surrendered a 28–3 lead and lost in overtime to the New England Patriots.

Notable players

  • Deion Sanders – Hall of Fame cornerback, drafted 1989.
  • Michael Vick – first overall pick of the 2001 draft.
  • Matt Ryan – franchise passing leader and 2016 NFL MVP.
  • Julio Jones – star wide receiver of the 2010s.
  • Morten Andersen – Hall of Fame kicker.

Franchise records

Home games have been played at Mercedes-Benz Stadium since 2017, a retractable-roof venue shared with Atlanta United FC of Major League Soccer, both owned by Arthur Blank's AMB Group.
