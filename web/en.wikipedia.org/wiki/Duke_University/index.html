Duke University

Duke University is a private research university in Durham, North Carolina, United States. Founded by Methodists and Quakers in 1838 in the present-day town of Trinity, the school moved to Durham in 1892. In 1924, tobacco and electric power industrialist James Buchanan Duke established The Duke Endowment and the institution changed its name to honour his deceased father, Washington Duke.

History

The university began as Brown's Schoolhouse, a private subscription school, and became Trinity College in 1859. Washington Duke, a Methodist from Durham, gave substantial gifts, and the college moved to Durham in 1892. James B. Duke's endowment of 1924 funded a major expansion, including the building of the West Campus in the Collegiate Gothic style, centred on Duke Chapel.

Academics

Duke has ten schools and colleges, including the Trinity College of Arts and Sciences, the Pratt School of Engineering, the Fuqua School of Business, the School of Medicine and the School of Law. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" and its undergraduate admissions are highly selective.

Athletics

The Duke Blue Devils compete in the Atlantic Coast Conference. The men's basketball program, based at Cameron Indoor Stadium, is one of the most successful in college history, having won multiple NCAA championships under coach Mike Krzyzewski.
