Active site

FieldBiochemistry / enzymology
DefinitionThe region of an enzyme where substrate molecules bind and catalysis occurs

The active site is the region of an enzyme where substrate molecules bind and undergo a chemical reaction. It is generally a pocket or cleft formed by amino acid residues from different parts of the protein's sequence brought together by folding. Within the active site, a binding site orients the substrate through weak non-covalent interactions, and a catalytic site carries the residues that perform the chemistry, lowering the reaction's activation energy.

A substrate molecule docked in the active-site cleft of an enzyme.

Binding models

Emil Fischer's lock-and-key model (1894) proposed rigid complementarity between enzyme and substrate; Daniel Koshland's induced-fit model (1958) proposed that binding reshapes the active site into its catalytic form. Modern views emphasise conformational selection and the stabilisation of the transition state, which is the basis of enzymatic rate enhancement and specificity.

Significance

Because reactions occur at the active site, it is the principal target of enzyme inhibitors — including many drugs (such as statins and HIV protease inhibitors) and poisons — which compete with the substrate (competitive inhibition) or bind elsewhere to alter the site (allosteric inhibition). Environmental factors such as pH and temperature affect the active site's shape and the ionisation of its residues, and hence enzyme activity.
