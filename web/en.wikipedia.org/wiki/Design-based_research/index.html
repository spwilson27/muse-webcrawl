Design-based research

Design-based research (DBR) is a research methodology used in the learning sciences and educational research that aims to address problems of practice while generating theoretical understanding. Introduced by Allan Collins and later developed by Ann Brown (who called her approach "design experiments") in the early 1990s, it involves the iterative design, enactment, analysis, and redesign of an educational intervention in real-world settings.

Unlike laboratory studies, DBR takes place in authentic classrooms or other learning environments, and interventions — curricula, tools, technologies, or teaching practices — evolve through successive cycles. The methodology is characterised as interventionist, iterative, process-oriented, and theory-driven, with a dual goal: refining the design itself and refining the theoretical models of learning that the design embodies.

DBR is related to but distinct from action research and design science; it has been formalised in collections such as The Design-Based Research Collective (2003) and is a mainstay of research on innovative learning environments.
