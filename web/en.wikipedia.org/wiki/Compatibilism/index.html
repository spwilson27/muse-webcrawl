Compatibilism

Compatibilism
FieldPhilosophy (free will)
ThesisFree will and determinism are compatible

[Image: A chess player choosing a move, free within the rules of the game]

Compatibilism is the thesis that free will and determinism can both be true — that being free consists not in escaping causation but in acting from one's own desires, reasons and character without coercion. Held in forms by David Hume and most contemporary philosophers (per professional surveys a majority), it faces the consequence argument (van Inwagen): if determinism is true, our acts are consequences of remote events beyond our control.

Arguments

Harry Frankfurt's cases (1969) attack the principle that responsibility requires alternatives: an agent who acts on his own, unaware a device would have forced the same act, seems responsible anyway. P. F. Strawson grounded responsibility in reactive attitudes (resentment, gratitude) woven into human relationships; Daniel Dennett defends an 'elbow room' of evolved competence rather than metaphysical ultimacy.
