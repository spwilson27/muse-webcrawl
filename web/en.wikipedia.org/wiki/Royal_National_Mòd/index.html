Royal National Mòd

Royal National Mòd (Am Mòd Nàiseanta Rìoghail)
First held: 1892, Oban
Organiser: An Comunn Gàidhealach
Type: Festival of Scottish Gaelic language and culture
Frequency: Annual, October

The Royal National Mòd (Scottish Gaelic: Am Mòd Nàiseanta Rìoghail) is an annual festival celebrating Scottish Gaelic language, literature, music and culture. Modelled in part on the Welsh Eisteddfod, it is the principal festival of the Gaelic community and is organised by An Comunn Gàidhealach.

History

An Comunn Gàidhealach was founded in Oban in 1891, and the first Mòd was held there in 1892 with just ten competitors. The festival grew steadily, adding choral competitions, instrumental events, drama, sport (including shinty) and children's competitions. It received royal patronage and the "Royal" title in the 1990s, during the reign of Queen Elizabeth II, who had attended the Mòd.

Competitions

The centrepiece competitions are for Gaelic singing — solo and choral — with the most prestigious individual prizes being the Gold Medal (awarded since 1893), the Traditional Gold Medal, and for choirs the Lovat and Tullibardine Shield. Other events include fiddle, accordion, clàrsach (Gaelic harp), piping, literature prizes for poetry and prose, and the Gaelic learners' competitions.

The Mòd moves to a different host town or city each year, returning regularly to Oban, and attracts thousands of competitors and spectators, along with significant media coverage from BBC Alba. A network of local provincial mòds acts as feeders to the national event.
