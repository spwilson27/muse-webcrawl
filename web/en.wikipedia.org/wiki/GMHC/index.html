GMHC

GMHC
Founded: 1982 (as Gay Men's Health Crisis)
Headquarters: New York City, United States
Type: Nonprofit organization
Focus: HIV/AIDS prevention, care and advocacy

GMHC (formerly Gay Men's Health Crisis) is a New York City-based non-profit, volunteer-supported and community-based AIDS service organization whose mission statement is to "end the epidemic and uplift the lives of all affected." Founded in 1982, it is widely regarded as the world's first and leading provider of HIV/AIDS prevention, care and advocacy services.

History

The organization began in 1981 as informal meetings in the apartment of writer Larry Kramer, where volunteers staffed a hotline answering questions about the new disease then being called GRID (gay-related immune deficiency). It was formally incorporated in 1982 by Kramer, Nathan Fain, Lawrence D. Mass, Paul Rapoport, Joseph Sonnabend, Paul Popham and others. In its earliest months the group raised funds for research and provided crisis counselling, legal aid, and buddy services to people dying of AIDS when few other institutions would help.

Services and advocacy

GMHC grew into a comprehensive service organization offering HIV testing, mental-health and substance-use services, housing assistance, nutrition programmes (including a communal dining room), legal support, and public-policy advocacy. Its safer-sex education campaigns became models for prevention work internationally. In 2010 it moved into a new headquarters in Chelsea, and it later shortened its name to GMHC to reflect the broader communities it serves.

Legacy

Frustrated by the pace of government action, several GMHC founders and volunteers went on to found ACT UP in 1987. GMHC remains a central institution in the history of the community response to the AIDS epidemic.
