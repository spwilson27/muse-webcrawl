Egyptian Premier League

Cairo International Stadium, home to the Cairo derby

The Egyptian Premier League is the top professional association football league in Egypt and one of the oldest and strongest leagues in Africa. Founded in 1948, it is dominated by the two Cairo giants, Al Ahly and Zamalek, whose rivalry — the Cairo derby — is among the most intense in world football.

History

The league began in 1948 with 11 clubs, Al Ahly winning the first championship. Only seven clubs have ever won the title; Al Ahly holds the record with more than forty championships, followed by Zamalek, with Ismaily, Tersana, Ghazl El Mahalla, Al Mokawloon and Olympic Club the only other winners. Egyptian clubs have also dominated the CAF Champions League, Al Ahly being Africa's most successful club.

Interruptions

The league was suspended during wars and crises, most notably after the 2012 Port Said Stadium riot, in which 74 Al Ahly supporters died; the season was cancelled and matches were long played behind closed doors, reshaping Egyptian football's economics and fan culture.

Format

The league is played as a double round-robin: each of the 18 clubs plays the others home and away, with the bottom clubs relegated to the second tier. Top finishers qualify for the CAF Champions League and CAF Confederation Cup.
