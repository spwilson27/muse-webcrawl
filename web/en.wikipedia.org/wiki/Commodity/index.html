Commodity

Examples: Crude oil, gold, wheat, coffee, copper
Markets: Commodity exchanges, futures markets

In economics, a commodity is an economic good, usually a resource, that specifically has full or substantial fungibility: the market treats instances of the good as equivalent or nearly so with no regard to who produced them. Most commodities are basic resources and agricultural products such as iron ore, crude oil, coal, salt, sugar, coffee beans, soybeans, aluminium, copper, rice, wheat, gold and silver.

Characteristics

The price of a commodity is generally determined by supply and demand in a broad market, rather than by the identity of a particular supplier, and standardised grades allow trading on exchanges. Soft commodities are agricultural or livestock products; hard commodities are mined or extracted resources, including energy.

Trading

Commodities are traded on specialised exchanges (such as the Chicago Mercantile Exchange and ICE) through spot, futures and options contracts, which producers and consumers use to hedge price risk and speculators use to seek profit. Commoditisation describes the process by which differentiated products become treated as interchangeable commodities.

In Marxist theory

Karl Marx analysed the commodity as the elementary form of wealth in capitalism, having both use-value and exchange-value, and developed the theory of commodity fetishism.
