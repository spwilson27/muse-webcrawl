Hunt seat

Hunt seat is a style of forward riding used in the American hunter and jumper disciplines and in equitation competition. It derives from the tradition of fox hunting and from the forward seat developed in the early 20th century by the Italian cavalry officer Federico Caprilli, whose system of riding with shortened stirrups and a seat that follows the horse's motion revolutionised jumping technique.

The two-point position used over fences in hunt seat riding.

Position

The hunt seat rider sits in a close-contact English saddle with stirrups shorter than in dressage. On the flat the rider uses a full seat or light half-seat; over fences the rider rises into the "two-point" or jumping position, closing the hip angle, shortening the reins, and keeping weight in the heels so the horse's mouth is free. The ideal combines a secure, balanced seat with an elegant, quiet appearance, judged in hunter and equitation classes on the horse's manners, movement, and jumping form as well as the rider's position.

Competition

Hunt seat is the basis of hunter, jumper, and hunt seat equitation classes at horse shows governed in the United States by the United States Equestrian Federation and the United States Hunter Jumper Association. Prestigious equitation finals include the ASPCA Maclay, the USEF Medal, and the Washington International Horse Show Equitation Final. At the collegiate level, the Intercollegiate Horse Shows Association conducts hunt seat competition on unfamiliar horses drawn by lot. Attire is conservative: dark coat, tan breeches, tall boots, and a velvet-covered helmet or hunt cap tradition.
