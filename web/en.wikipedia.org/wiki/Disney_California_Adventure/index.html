Disney California Adventure Park, commonly referred to as California Adventure or by its initials DCA, is a theme park at the Disneyland Resort in Anaheim, California. It is owned and operated by The Walt Disney Company through its Experiences division. The 72-acre park is themed after the history and culture of California, and celebrates the state's adventure, landscapes, and storytelling spirit through attractions drawn from Disney, Pixar, and Marvel properties.

History

The park opened on February 8, 2001, as Disney's California Adventure, built on the former Disneyland parking lot at a reported cost of about $1.4 billion together with the resort expansion that added Disney's Grand Californian Hotel and Downtown Disney. Initial attendance and critical reception fell well short of expectations: reviewers criticized its contemporary Californian theme, off-the-shelf rides, and shortage of attractions for children. In 2007 Disney announced a $1.1 billion redesign and expansion, which added Buena Vista Street and the 12-acre Cars Land, new theming throughout, and the World of Color nighttime show; the reworked park was rededicated on June 15, 2012, and attendance rose sharply, exceeding 9 million visitors a year by the late 2010s.

Lands and attractions

The park's areas include Buena Vista Street, Hollywood Land, Avengers Campus (opened 2021 on the former A Bug's Land), Cars Land, San Fransokyo Square, Performance Corridor, Pixar Pier, Paradise Gardens Park, and Grizzly Peak. Signature attractions include Radiator Springs Racers, the Incredicoaster, Guardians of the Galaxy – Mission: Breakout!, Soarin' Around the World, Grizzly River Run, Toy Story Midway Mania!, and WEB Slingers: A Spider-Man Adventure. The park hosts seasonal festivals, including the Food & Wine Festival and Lunar New Year and Festival of Holidays celebrations, which emphasize California's multicultural cuisines.
