2020 Nagorno-Karabakh ceasefire agreement

The 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh ceasefire agreement was a trilateral armistice signed on 9 November 2020 by the leaders of Azerbaijan, Armenia and Russia, ending the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war. Under the agreement, Azerbaijan retained the territories it had captured during the war, Armenia agreed to withdraw from surrounding districts it had held since the 1990s, and Russian peacekeepers were deployed to Nagorno-Karabakh and the Lachin corridor.

Agreement details

Signed: 9 November 2020
Effective: 10 November 2020 (Moscow time)
Signatories: Ilham Aliyev, Nikol Pashinyan, Vladimir Putin
Peacekeepers: ~1,960 Russian personnel
Term: 5 years, renewable

Terms

The statement provided for a complete ceasefire, the return of the Kalbajar, Aghdam and Lachin districts to Azerbaijan on a staggered timetable, and the deployment of a Russian peacekeeping contingent along the contact line and the Lachin corridor connecting Nagorno-Karabakh to Armenia. It also called for the unblocking of regional transport links, including a connection between mainland Azerbaijan and its Nakhchivan exclave, and for the return of internally displaced persons and refugees.

Reactions

In Azerbaijan the agreement was celebrated as a victory. In Armenia it sparked mass protests, with demonstrators storming government buildings in Yerevan and demanding the resignation of Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan, who described signing the document as "incredibly painful" but necessary to avoid a total collapse of the front after the fall of Shusha.
