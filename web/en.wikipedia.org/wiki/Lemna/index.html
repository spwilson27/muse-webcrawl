Lemna

Lemna
Family: Araceae
Common name: duckweed
Type: free-floating aquatic plant

Lemna is a genus of free-floating aquatic plants referred to by the common name "duckweed". They are morphologically highly simplified flowering plants in the arum family Araceae (subfamily Lemnoideae), lacking distinct stems and leaves; the plant body is a small flattened oval structure called a frond or thallus, usually with a single root hanging in the water.

Description

Fronds of Lemna species are typically 1–15 mm long, bright green, and float on or just beneath the surface of still or slow-moving fresh water. Reproduction is overwhelmingly vegetative: daughter fronds bud from pockets in the parent frond and may double the population within days under warm, nutrient-rich conditions. Flowering is rare and the flowers are minute, making Lemna among the smallest flowering plants (only the related genus Wolffia is smaller).

Ecology and uses

Duckweed mats provide food and shelter for aquatic invertebrates and waterfowl, and help suppress algal growth by shading and absorbing nutrients. Because of its rapid growth and high protein content, Lemna has been investigated as animal feed, a biofuel feedstock, a system for wastewater treatment and nutrient recovery, and even as a protein source for human consumption. Conversely, dense mats can deoxygenate water and are sometimes treated as a nuisance growth.

Species

About a dozen species are commonly recognised, including Lemna minor (common duckweed), Lemna gibba, Lemna trisulca and Lemna minuta, an invasive alien in parts of Europe.
