Turbot War

Date9 March – 17 April 1995
LocationGrand Banks of Newfoundland, North Atlantic
PartiesCanada vs. Spain / European Union
ResultNegotiated agreement on quotas and enforcement

The Turbot War (Spanish: Guerra del Fletán) was an international fishing dispute in 1995 between Canada and Spain, backed by the European Union, over Greenland halibut — called "turbot" in Canada — on the Grand Banks of Newfoundland, just outside Canada's 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zone. It is regarded as the most serious modern confrontation over fisheries jurisdiction in the North Atlantic, and sometimes dubbed the last "war" between Canada and a European power.

Background

After the collapse of northern cod stocks and Canada's 1992 cod moratorium, which put tens of thousands of Newfoundlanders out of work, attention turned to the Greenland halibut fishery in the international waters regulated by the Northwest Atlantic Fisheries Organization (NAFO). Canada accused Spanish and Portuguese trawlers of systematically exceeding quotas and using undersized mesh. In 1994 Canada amended its Coastal Fisheries Protection Act to assert enforcement rights over straddling stocks beyond its zone.

The Estai incident

On 9 March 1995, Canadian fisheries patrol vessels and the navy pursued and boarded the Spanish trawler Estai in international waters, cut away its nets, arrested the vessel and escorted it to St. John's, Newfoundland, where its catch and logbooks were displayed to the press — revealing nets with mesh far below legal size. Spain dispatched naval patrol vessels to protect its fleet, and the EU denounced Canada's action as illegal, while Canadian fisheries minister Brian Tobin became a popular hero at home by brandishing an undersized turbot at a United Nations press conference in New York.

Resolution

Weeks of negotiations produced an agreement on 15–17 April 1995: the EU accepted stricter NAFO enforcement, independent observers on vessels, satellite tracking and revised quotas giving Canada a substantially larger share of the turbot catch, while Canada repealed the disputed application of its law and released the Estai. The affair strengthened international law on straddling fish stocks, contributing to the 1995 United Nations Fish Stocks Agreement.
