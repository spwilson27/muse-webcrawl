John Fogerty

John Cameron Fogerty (born May 28, 1945) is an American singer, songwriter, and guitarist best known as the lead singer, lead guitarist, and chief songwriter of Creedence Clearwater Revival (CCR).

Creedence Clearwater Revival

Formed in El Cerrito, California, CCR had a remarkable run between 1968 and 1972 with Fogerty-penned hits including "Proud Mary," "Bad Moon Rising," "Green River," "Down on the Corner," "Fortunate Son," and "Have You Ever Seen the Rain?" — roots-rock songs steeped in swamp, country, and R&B influences.

Solo career and disputes

After CCR's acrimonious breakup, Fogerty's solo career included the hit album Centerfield (1985). His decades-long feud with Fantasy Records executive Saul Zaentz — over royalties and control of the CCR catalogue — led to the extraordinary 1988 plagiarism trial in which Fogerty was accused of plagiarising his own earlier song; he won the case. His later albums include Blue Moon Swamp (1997), which won a Grammy, and in 2023 he finally regained majority ownership of his CCR publishing. Fogerty was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with CCR in 1993.
