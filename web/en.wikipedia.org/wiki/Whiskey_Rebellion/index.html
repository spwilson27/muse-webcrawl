Whiskey Rebellion

The Whiskey Rebellion (also the Whiskey Insurrection) was a violent tax protest in the United States beginning in 1791 and culminating in 1794, in western Pennsylvania against a federal excise tax on distilled spirits.

Background

Treasury Secretary Alexander Hamilton proposed the 1791 whiskey tax to fund assumption of state debts. Frontier farmers, for whom whiskey was a currency and store of value, saw it as discriminatory, echoing grievances of the American Revolutionary War.

Insurrection

Protests turned violent in July 1794: tax inspector John Neville's home was attacked and a federal marshal was confronted. President George Washington led about 13,000 militiamen west — the only time a sitting U.S. president commanded troops in the field. The rebels dispersed without battle.

Legacy

Two men were convicted of treason and pardoned. The episode established federal authority to enforce laws, and the tax was repealed under Jefferson in 1802.
