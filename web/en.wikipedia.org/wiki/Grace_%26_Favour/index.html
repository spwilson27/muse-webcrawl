Grace and favour

Grace and favour residences are homes owned by the British monarch or the United Kingdom government that are provided free of rent, or at a greatly reduced cost, to certain individuals as part of their official duties or in recognition of past service. The term derives from the idea that the occupant holds the property by the "grace and favour" of the sovereign.

Overview

Such properties are not owned by their occupants and cannot be sold or altered freely. They include official residences allocated to senior ministers, retired public servants, members of the Royal Household, and others granted accommodation by the Crown. Well-known examples of grace and favour homes associated with the Crown Estate and the Ministry of Defence include apartments in Hampton Court Palace, Kensington Palace, and St James's Palace.

Notable residents

Over the centuries, many courtiers, retired officers, and widows of distinguished servants of the Crown have been housed in grace and favour apartments. In more recent times, the allocation of such homes has occasionally attracted political attention, particularly where former officials remained in residence after leaving office.
