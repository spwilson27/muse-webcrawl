Instruments of the Passion

The Instruments of the Passion, also called the Arma Christi ("weapons of Christ"), are the objects associated with the Passion of Jesus Christ in Christian tradition, including the sufferings he endured before and during the Crucifixion. They are frequently depicted in Christian art and were venerated as relics.

Principal instruments

The most commonly shown instruments include the Cross, the nails, the Crown of Thorns, the Holy Lance that pierced Christ's side, the sponge soaked in vinegar, the pillar of the Flagellation, the scourge, the hammer and pincers, the dice cast for Christ's garments, and the ladder and reed. Other items include the lantern used at the arrest, the sword and ear of Malchus, the rooster that crowed at Peter's denial, and Veronica's veil.

In art

From the late Middle Ages the Arma Christi appeared as heraldic-like arrangements, often carried by angels, and in devotional images such as the Mass of Saint Gregory. They symbolized Christ's triumph over death, presented as a knight's victorious arms.
