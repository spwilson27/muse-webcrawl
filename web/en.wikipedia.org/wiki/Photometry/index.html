Photometry

Photometry is the science of the measurement of light in terms of its perceived brightness to the human eye, and also the astronomical technique of measuring the flux or intensity of light emitted by celestial objects.

Photometric quantities

Unlike radiometry, which measures absolute optical power, photometry weights radiation by the luminosity function, modelling the eye's spectral sensitivity. Key quantities include luminous flux (lumen), luminous intensity (candela), illuminance (lux), and luminance.

Astronomical photometry

Astronomers use photometry through standard filter bands (such as the Johnson UBV system) to determine magnitudes, colours, and variability of stars and galaxies. Precise photometry from space telescopes detects exoplanets by the transit method, and underpins the period–luminosity relation used to measure cosmic distances.
