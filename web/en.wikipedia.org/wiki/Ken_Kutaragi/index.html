Ken Kutaragi (久夛良木 健, Kutaragi Ken, born 2 August 1950) is a Japanese engineering technologist and businessman, best known as the creator of the PlayStation video game console and former chairman and CEO of Sony Computer Entertainment.

Career

An electrical engineer at Sony, Kutaragi became interested in video games watching his daughter play Nintendo's Famicom. He designed the SPC700 sound chip for Nintendo's Super Famicom (SNES), a project he pursued partly in secret from Sony management. He then persuaded Sony to enter the console business, leading the engineering of the original PlayStation (1994), PlayStation 2 (2000) — the best-selling home console of all time — and the PlayStation Portable.

Later years

As head of Sony Computer Entertainment from 1999, Kutaragi championed the ambitious Cell processor for the PlayStation 3; the console's high launch price and difficult development strained his standing, and he stepped down as chairman and CEO in 2007, becoming honorary chairman. He later founded Cyber AI Entertainment and has served as an outside director and adviser to technology companies. He received a Lifetime Achievement Award at the Game Developers Choice Awards in 2014.
