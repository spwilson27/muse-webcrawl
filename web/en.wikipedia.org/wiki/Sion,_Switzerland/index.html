Sion (German: Sitten; Latin: Sedunum) is the capital of the canton of Valais in southern Switzerland, a town of about 35,000 inhabitants on the Rhône river. It is one of the oldest continuously settled sites in Switzerland and is famous for the twin fortified hills that rise from the valley floor above its old town: Valère, crowned by a fortified basilica, and Tourbillon, with the ruins of a medieval castle.

History

Archaeological traces around Sion reach back to the Neolithic, and the settlement was the chief place of the Celtic Seduni before becoming the Roman town of Sedunum. In the late Roman and early medieval period it became a bishop's seat; the prince-bishops of Sion ruled much of the Valais for centuries and built the castles that still dominate the town. The Basilica of Valère houses one of the world's oldest playable church organs, dating to about 1435. Sion joined the Swiss Confederation's orbit as the Valais became an associate and then, in 1815, a canton, with Sion as its capital.

Geography and climate

Sion sits in the broad Rhône valley between the Bernese Alps to the north and the Pennine Alps to the south. Sheltered by the mountains, it enjoys one of the driest and sunniest climates in Switzerland, favouring vineyards and orchards on the valley slopes; the surrounding Valais is a major wine-producing region, noted for white Fendant (Chasselas) and red Pinot noir and local varieties such as Petite Arvine and Cornalin.

Culture and sport

The town hosts museums in the Valère and Tourbillon complex, an old town of narrow lanes, and the seat of the cantonal government. Its football club, FC Sion, based at the Stade Tourbillon, is famous in Switzerland for its extraordinary record in Swiss Cup finals. Sion has several times bid to host the Winter Olympic Games, most famously losing the 2006 Games to Turin. Sion Airport serves both civilian and Swiss Air Force traffic.
