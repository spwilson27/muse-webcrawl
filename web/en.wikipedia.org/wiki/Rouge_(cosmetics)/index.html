Rouge (cosmetics)

Rouge
Cosmetic for colouring the cheeks
TypeCosmetic
ApplicationCheeks (also historically lips)

Rouge (French for "red"), also called blush or blusher, is a cosmetic applied to the cheeks to give them a rosier, more youthful colour. It is typically a powder, cream or liquid containing red or pink pigment — historically derived from sources such as safflower, cochineal (carmine), or crushed minerals — and is applied with a brush, sponge or the fingers to the apples of the cheeks and blended upward toward the temples.

History

The use of cheek colour dates to ancient Egypt, where rouge was made from red ochre and fats, and to ancient Greece and Rome, where mulberries, beet juice and vermilion were used. In the 18th century, heavy rouge was fashionable at the French court, though its popularity fluctuated with moral and social attitudes; in Victorian England, visible make-up was considered vulgar and rouge was applied discreetly or disavowed entirely. In the 20th century rouge became a mass-market staple of the cosmetics industry.

Composition and safety

Early rouges sometimes contained toxic ingredients such as lead compounds and mercury-based vermilion. Modern products are regulated in most jurisdictions and typically use iron oxides, carmine and synthetic organic pigments in a base of talc, mica or silicones. Cream and liquid blushes have grown in popularity for their natural finish.

Cultural significance

Rouged cheeks have variously signalled health, youth, fertility, wealth and stage presence. In Japanese and Chinese theatre, and in Peking opera, stylised rouge patterns help communicate a character's identity and temperament. See also cosmetics and lipstick.
