Promontory, Utah

Promontory is an area in Box Elder County, Utah, United States, in the Promontory Mountains north of the Great Salt Lake, at an elevation of about 4,900 feet. It is famous as Promontory Summit, the site where the First Transcontinental Railroad was completed on May 10, 1869, when the Union Pacific and Central Pacific railroads joined their rails.

The Golden Spike

At the ceremony, a ceremonial final spike — the Golden Spike, made of 17.6-karat gold and presented by Leland Stanford — was driven to connect the two lines, completing 1,776 miles of continuous track between Omaha and Sacramento. The event was celebrated nationwide and is immortalized in A. J. Russell's photograph of the meeting of the locomotives Jupiter and No. 119. The site was often misreported as "Promontory Point", a different location on a peninsula into the Great Salt Lake.

Later history

A small railroad town at Promontory declined after the Lucin Cutoff (1903–1904) bypassed the summit route across the Great Salt Lake; the original rails were removed for scrap in 1942. The site is preserved as Golden Spike National Historical Park (established as a National Historic Site in 1957 and redesignated in 2019), which operates replica steam locomotives and holds annual reenactments of the driving of the last spike.
