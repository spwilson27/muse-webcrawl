Hiibel v. Sixth Judicial District Court of Nevada

Hiibel v. Sixth Judicial District Court of Nevada, 542 U.S. 177 (2004), is a United States Supreme Court case in which the Court upheld the constitutionality of "stop and identify" statutes, holding that a Nevada law requiring a person detained on reasonable suspicion to identify himself did not violate the Fourth or Fifth Amendments.

Background

Larry Hiibel was arrested and charged under Nevada's stop-and-identify law after repeatedly refusing to give his name to a sheriff's deputy who was investigating a report that he had assaulted his daughter in his truck. The ensuing encounter was recorded on video and attracted wide attention; Hiibel argued the statute compelled self-incrimination and authorised an unreasonable seizure.

Decision

In a 5–4 decision authored by Justice Kennedy, the Court held that during a lawful Terry stop, requiring a suspect to disclose his name falls within the ordinary investigative scope of the stop, and that mere disclosure of a name presents no reasonable danger of incrimination. Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissented.
