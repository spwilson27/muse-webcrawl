Political prisoner

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A political prisoner is a person imprisoned for their political beliefs, identity or nonviolent political activity, or whose prosecution and detention are politically motivated — a term of international advocacy rather than a status defined in international law (no treaty grants "political prisoners" distinct rights, though arbitrary-detention, fair-trial and free-expression law covers the substance).

Definitions and practice

Amnesty International's narrower category, the prisoner of conscience — persons imprisoned for the peaceful expression of beliefs or for their identity, who have not used or advocated violence — anchored its letter-writing model from 1961. Wider definitions (the Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe's criteria of 2012) include those whose cases are politically fabricated or grossly disproportionate. Iconic cases span systems: Nelson Mandela and the Rivonia trialists under apartheid; Soviet dissidents in camps and psychiatric hospitals (punitive psychiatry's exposure being a Cold War scandal); Aung San Suu Kyi's decades of house arrest; contemporary cases argued over in China, Russia (Navalny, who died in custody in 2024), Iran, Egypt, Nicaragua, Venezuela, Belarus and elsewhere, with governments uniformly denying the label ("criminals", "terrorists", "foreign agents") — the denial itself being why independent monitoring (Amnesty's and the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention's opinions) matters. Prisoner exchanges and amnesties that free them are diplomatic currency, and their release a standard index of political thaw.
