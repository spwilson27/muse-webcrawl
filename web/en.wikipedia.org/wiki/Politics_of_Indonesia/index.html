Politics of Indonesia

The politics of Indonesia take place in the framework of a presidential representative democratic republic, whereby the President of Indonesia is both head of state and head of government, and of a multi-party system. Executive power is exercised by the government. Legislative power is vested in both the government and the People's Consultative Assembly (MPR). The judiciary is formally independent of the executive and the legislature.

Historical background

Indonesia declared independence in 1945. After a period of parliamentary democracy in the 1950s, President Sukarno established "Guided Democracy" in 1959. Following the turmoil of 1965–66, General Suharto's New Order regime governed in an authoritarian manner for more than three decades. Suharto's fall amid the Asian financial crisis in 1998 opened the Reformasi era: democratic elections, constitutional amendments establishing term limits and direct presidential elections, regional autonomy and decentralization, a free press, and the creation of new institutions including a Constitutional Court and an anti-corruption commission (KPK).

Executive

The president and vice president are directly elected together for a five-year term, renewable once. The president appoints a cabinet, commands the armed forces (TNI), and conducts foreign policy. Notable presidents of the reform era include Abdurrahman Wahid, Megawati Sukarnoputri, Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono (2004–2014), Joko Widodo (2014–2024), and Prabowo Subianto, who took office in October 2024.

Legislature

The People's Consultative Assembly (MPR) consists of the People's Representative Council (DPR), elected by proportional representation, and the Regional Representative Council (DPD), representing the provinces. The MPR can amend the constitution and inaugurate or impeach the president.

Political parties and elections

Major parties include the Indonesian Democratic Party of Struggle (PDI-P), Golkar, Gerindra, the National Awakening Party (PKB), the Prosperous Justice Party (PKS), the Democratic Party, and NasDem. Elections are held every five years, with the 2024 general election choosing the president, national legislature, and regional bodies simultaneously.
