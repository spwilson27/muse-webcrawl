Utena County (Lithuanian: Utenos apskritis) is one of the ten counties of Lithuania, located in the northeast of the country within the ethnographic region of Aukštaitija. Its capital and largest city is Utena. The county is known for its lake district, forests, and the Aukštaitija National Park, Lithuania's first national park, established in 1974.

Geography

Utena County is the country's most sparsely populated county. Its landscape was shaped by glaciation and contains hundreds of lakes, including Lake Drūkšiai on the border with Belarus — Lithuania's largest lake — together with large areas of pine forest. The county borders Latvia to the north and Belarus to the east, and the counties of Panevėžys and Vilnius to the west and south.

Economy and notable features

The now-decommissioned Ignalina Nuclear Power Plant, near the town of Visaginas, was the county's largest industrial installation and a major employer; Visaginas itself was founded in 1975 to house its workers. Tourism centred on lakes and national parks, food processing, textiles, and beer brewing in Utena are other pillars of the regional economy. Like much of eastern Lithuania, the county has experienced population decline and emigration since the 1990s.
