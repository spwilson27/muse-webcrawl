Razor wire

Razor wire is a mesh of metal strips with sharp edges whose purpose is to prevent passage by humans. The term "razor wire", through long usage, has generally been used to describe barbed tape products. While barbed wire is made of wire with pointed barbs, razor wire is made from a central strand of high-tensile wire around which sharp tape is machine-crimped, producing edges like those of a razor.

Use

Razor wire is much sharper than standard barbed wire and far harder to cut or crush; it is designed to cause serious lacerations to anyone attempting to climb through it. It is used atop fences and walls at prisons, military installations, borders and high-security facilities, and is commonly deployed in concertina coils — hence the related term concertina wire — which appeared extensively in trench warfare.

History

Barbed tape was developed in Germany during the First World War as a faster-deploying alternative to barbed wire; modern stainless and galvanised versions were commercialised in the late 20th century for correctional and military security.
