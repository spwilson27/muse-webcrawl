Konark Sun Temple

Konark Sun Temple is a thirteenth-century temple dedicated to the Hindu sun god Surya, located at Konark on the coast of Odisha, India. Built around 1250 CE under King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty, it is conceived as the colossal chariot of Surya, with twelve pairs of intricately carved stone wheels and seven horses.

Architecture

The temple exemplifies the Kalinga style of architecture. Its surviving audience hall (jagamohana) rises about 39 metres; the main sanctuary tower, now lost, was once far taller. The wheels function as sundials, and the walls are covered with sculpture depicting deities, dancers, musicians, animals and scenes of everyday and erotic life.

History and conservation

The reasons for the main tower's collapse are debated — natural decay, lightning and deliberate destruction have all been proposed. The temple was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984 and is protected by the Archaeological Survey of India.
