The Derveni papyrus is an ancient Greek papyrus roll that was found in 1962 near Derveni, a pass between Thessaloniki and Kavala in Macedonia, Greece. It dates to around 340 BC, during the reign of Philip II of Macedon, making it the oldest surviving "book" in Europe. The text itself — an allegorical commentary on an Orphic poem about the birth of the gods — was probably composed in the late 5th century BC, in the circle of the philosopher Anaxagoras.

Discovery and preservation

The roll was partially burned on a funeral pyre in an aristocratic tomb; the carbonisation, which destroyed part of the text, paradoxically preserved the rest. Fragments were painstakingly unrolled, photographed under infrared light, and reassembled into 26 columns of text. A full official edition appeared only in 2006, decades after the discovery.

Content

The anonymous author interprets Orphic verses allegorically, equating deities with natural and cosmic principles — Zeus with air and mind (nous), Oceanus with air, and so on — in a manner influenced by Presocratic philosophy and the physical theories of Anaxagoras and Diogenes of Apollonia. The papyrus is a crucial source for Orphic religion, early Greek exegesis, and the reception of Presocratic thought.
