World Thinking Day, formerly simply Thinking Day, is celebrated annually on 22 February by Girl Guides and Girl Scouts in more than 150 countries, and also marked by some Scout associations belonging to the World Organization of the Scout Movement. It is a day for members to think about their "sisters" (and "brothers") in other countries, the meaning of Guiding, and its global impact, and to raise funds for the World Thinking Day Fund, which supports Guiding projects worldwide.

History

The day was established at the fourth International Conference of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts, held at Camp Edith Macy in New York in 1926, where delegates agreed that there should be a special day each year when members thought of one another around the world. The date, 22 February, was chosen because it was the joint birthday of Robert Baden-Powell, founder of Scouting, and his wife Olave Baden-Powell, the World Chief Guide. In 1932 the World Thinking Day Fund was established at the seventh World Conference in Poland, after a Belgian delegate suggested that members who celebrated the day give a voluntary contribution — traditionally a penny — to support Guiding worldwide. The name was changed to World Thinking Day in 1999.

Celebrations

Each year the World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts selects a theme — recent themes have addressed issues such as peace, the environment, gender equality and leadership — and produces an activity pack for member organisations. Traditions include ceremonies held at dawn or dusk in which candles are lit, messages exchanged with Guides and Scouts in other countries, and, in London, a service at Westminster Abbey. Some Scout associations celebrate 22 February as Founder's Day or B.-P. Day.
