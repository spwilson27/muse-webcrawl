Separation anxiety disorder (SAD) is an anxiety disorder in which an individual experiences excessive anxiety regarding separation from home or from people to whom the individual has a strong emotional attachment (e.g., a parent, caregiver, significant other, or siblings). While some separation anxiety is a normal developmental stage in infants and young children, the disorder is diagnosed when the fear is developmentally inappropriate, persistent, and causes significant impairment — for example, school refusal, nightmares about separation, physical complaints, and refusal to sleep away from home.

Diagnosis

Under DSM-5 criteria, symptoms must persist for at least four weeks in children and adolescents (typically six months in adults) and cause clinically significant distress or impairment. Separation anxiety disorder is among the most common anxiety disorders of childhood, and DSM-5 removed the requirement of onset before age 18, recognizing that the disorder also occurs in adults, often focused on partners or children.

Treatment

First-line treatment is cognitive behavioural therapy, including graded exposure to separations and parent training; school-based interventions are common for school refusal. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors may be used in moderate to severe cases, often in combination with therapy.
