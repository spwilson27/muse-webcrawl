Drum tower (Chinese)

A drum tower (Chinese: 鼓楼; pinyin: gǔlóu) is a tower in the centre of a traditional Chinese city or temple complex that houses large drums. Together with a bell tower, it was used to tell the time, announce the opening and closing of city gates, sound alarms and mark ceremonies.

History and function

Bell and drum towers were standard features of Chinese capitals from the Han dynasty onward and were placed on the central axis of cities such as Beijing and Xi'an. Drums were beaten in sequences during the night watches. Famous examples include the Beijing Drum Tower, first built in 1272 under the Yuan dynasty, and the Xi'an Drum Tower, built in 1380 under the Ming dynasty.

Architecture

Towers are typically built on a massive masonry base pierced by archways, topped by a timber pavilion with tiled, upturned-eave roofs housing one or more large drums.
