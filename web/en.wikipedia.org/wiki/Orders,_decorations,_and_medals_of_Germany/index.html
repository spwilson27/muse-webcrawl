Orders, decorations, and medals of Germany

The orders, decorations, and medals of Germany comprise the honours systems of the historic German states, the German Empire, the Weimar Republic, Nazi Germany, East Germany and the modern Federal Republic of Germany. Before unification in 1871 each German state maintained its own orders, among them Prussia's Pour le Mérite and Order of the Black Eagle, Bavaria's Military Order of Max Joseph and Saxony's Military Order of St. Henry.

Federal Republic

The principal honour of the Federal Republic is the Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany (Bundesverdienstkreuz), founded in 1951 by President Theodor Heuss. It is awarded in several classes from the Medal of Merit up to the Grand Cross, for achievements in political, economic, social and intellectual fields. Other federal decorations include the Order Pour le Mérite for the Sciences and Arts (revived in 1952 as a civil order), sports and lifesaving decorations, and Bundeswehr honours such as the Cross of Honour for Bravery, instituted in 2008.

Historical systems

The Iron Cross, instituted by Prussia in 1813 and reintroduced in 1870, 1914 and 1939, became Germany's best-known military decoration; after 1945 West German veterans were permitted to wear de-Nazified 1957-pattern versions of wartime awards. The German Democratic Republic (1949–1990) maintained a separate socialist honours system, headed by the Order of Karl Marx, which lapsed at reunification. The individual Länder also confer their own orders of merit.
