Swedish Armed Forces

The Swedish Armed Forces (Swedish: Försvarsmakten) are the government agency that forms the military forces of Sweden, tasked with the defence of the country and the promotion of its security. They consist of the Swedish Army, the Swedish Navy (including the Amphibious Corps), the Swedish Air Force, and the Home Guard. The Supreme Commander (Överbefälhavaren) is the professional head of the forces, subordinate to the Swedish government. Sweden, long neutral and non-aligned, joined NATO in March 2024 following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

History

Sweden maintained a large conscript army through the Cold War despite its neutrality, built around the doctrine of total defence. After the Cold War the forces were sharply reduced and reoriented toward international missions; conscription was suspended in 2010 in favour of an all-volunteer force. Deteriorating security in the Baltic region led to the reactivation of gender-neutral conscription in 2017, a permanent garrison on Gotland, and major defence spending increases. Sweden applied for NATO membership in May 2022 and became the alliance's 32nd member on 7 March 2024.

Equipment

Sweden's defence industry produces signature systems used by the Armed Forces, including the Saab JAS 39 Gripen fighter, Stridsvagn 122 main battle tank (a Leopard 2 variant), CV90 infantry fighting vehicle, Archer artillery system, and Gotland-class submarines with air-independent propulsion.
