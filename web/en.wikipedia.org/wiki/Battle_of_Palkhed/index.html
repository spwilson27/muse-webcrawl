The Battle of Palkhed was fought on 28 February 1728 at Palkhed, near Nashik in present-day Maharashtra, between the Maratha forces of Peshwa Baji Rao I and the army of Nizam-ul-Mulk, Asaf Jah I, the Mughal viceroy of the Deccan. In a masterful campaign of manoeuvre, Baji Rao outmarched and trapped the Nizam's heavier army in waterless terrain, forcing him to negotiate. British field marshal Bernard Montgomery later cited the campaign as a masterpiece of strategic mobility.

Background

Conflict over the Maratha right to collect chauth and sardeshmukhi taxes in the Deccan, and the Nizam's support for a rival Maratha faction under Sambhaji II of Kolhapur, led to open war in 1727. The Nizam invaded Maratha territory and occupied Pune.

The campaign

Rather than defend Pune, Baji Rao struck into the Nizam's own territories toward Jalna and Burhanpur, ravaging supplies and drawing the slower, artillery-laden Hyderabad army after him. Turning suddenly, he shadowed the Nizam's march and, at Palkhed, encircled him in a rugged, water-scarce tract, cutting off forage and water while avoiding a pitched assault.

Aftermath

Starved into submission, the Nizam signed the Treaty of Mungi Shevgaon on 6 March 1728, recognising Shahu as the sole Maratha ruler and confirming Maratha tax rights in the Deccan. The victory established Baji Rao's reputation and Maratha ascendancy in the Deccan.
