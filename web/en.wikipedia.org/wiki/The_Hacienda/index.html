The Haçienda

The Haçienda (styled FAC 51, and commonly written The Hacienda) was a nightclub in Manchester, England, which operated from 1982 to 1997. Financed by Factory Records and the band New Order and designed by Ben Kelly with distinctive industrial styling and a cedilla in its name, it became the epicentre of the "Madchester" and acid house scenes.

History

Housed in a converted yacht showroom and warehouse, the club at first struggled with live gigs — Madonna made an early UK appearance there in 1984 — before DJs including Mike Pickering and Graeme Park built its reputation for house music from 1986–87. Its "Nude" nights helped launch acid house in Britain, and the club became central to the Happy Mondays / Stone Roses era.

Problems and closure

The Haçienda notoriously lost money, partly because many clubbers used ecstasy instead of buying drinks, and it suffered gang-related violence and licensing problems. It closed in 1997; the building was demolished and replaced by apartments named The Hacienda, retaining the club's industrial motifs.

Legacy

It is regularly cited among the most influential clubs in the world and is depicted in the film 24 Hour Party People (2002).
