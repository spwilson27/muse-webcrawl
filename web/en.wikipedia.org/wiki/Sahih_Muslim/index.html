Sahih Muslim

Sahih Muslim (Arabic: صحيح مسلم, Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim) is a collection of hadith compiled by the Persian scholar Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj (c. 815–875). It is one of the Kutub al-Sittah (six major hadith collections) of Sunni Islam and is regarded, together with Sahih al-Bukhari, as one of the two most authentic (sahih) collections of the sayings and actions of the Prophet Muhammad.

A manuscript of Sahih Muslim, among the most authoritative hadith collections in Sunni Islam.

Compilation

Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj travelled widely through Arabia, Egypt, Syria, and Iraq collecting traditions. According to his own account, he examined some 300,000 hadith and selected roughly 4,000 (about 7,500 including repetitions) that met his strict criteria of authenticity, requiring a continuous chain of reliable narrators. Muslim stated that he included only traditions on which there was scholarly consensus regarding their soundness.

Contents and arrangement

The collection is organised into 54 books covering topics from faith (iman) and purification, prayer, almsgiving, fasting, and pilgrimage, to transactions, marriage, penal law, and eschatology. Unlike al-Bukhari, Muslim generally avoided repeating the same hadith under multiple chapter headings, instead grouping all its chains of transmission together, a method praised for clarity. His introduction (Muqaddimah) sets out principles of hadith criticism and remains an important early work on the science of hadith.

Reception

Sahih Muslim is usually ranked second only to Sahih al-Bukhari in authenticity, though some scholars, particularly in the Maghreb, preferred Muslim's work for its arrangement. Numerous commentaries have been written on it, the most famous being that of al-Nawawi (d. 1277).
