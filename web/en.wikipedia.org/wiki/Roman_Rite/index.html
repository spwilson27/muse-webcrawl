The Roman Rite (Latin: Ritus Romanus) is the most common of the Latin liturgical rites of the Catholic Church, used by the Latin Church, the largest of the Church's twenty-four autonomous churches. Centred historically on the city of Rome, it spread through Western Europe during the early Middle Ages, largely displaced the other Western rites after the Council of Trent, and was carried worldwide by missionaries. It governs the celebration of the Mass, the Liturgy of the Hours, the sacraments and sacramentals for the great majority of the world's Catholics.

History

The liturgy of Rome developed in Latin from the fourth century, absorbing Gallican elements as it spread northward under the Carolingians and then returning south in a Franco-Roman synthesis. Medieval usage varied by diocese and religious order, producing local "uses" and distinct rites such as the Ambrosian (Milan), Mozarabic (Toledo) and the rites of orders such as the Dominicans and Carmelites — several of which survive within the Roman family. Following the Council of Trent, Pope Pius V promulgated a standardised Roman Missal in 1570 (the basis of what is now called the Tridentine Mass), suppressing most local variants less than two centuries old.

Forms of the Mass

The Second Vatican Council's constitution Sacrosanctum Concilium (1963) mandated a revision of the liturgy. Pope Paul VI promulgated a new Roman Missal in 1969–1970 — the "ordinary form", celebrated in Latin or the vernacular with the priest usually facing the people, a richer lectionary, and three additional Eucharistic Prayers beside the ancient Roman Canon. The 1962 edition of the Tridentine Missal continued in use as an "extraordinary form" under conditions set by Benedict XVI's Summorum Pontificum (2007) and restricted again by Francis's Traditionis Custodes (2021). The Roman Rite also encompasses adapted forms such as the Zaire Use and the liturgy of the Anglican ordinariates (Divine Worship).

Structure of the Mass

The Mass comprises the Liturgy of the Word — readings, psalm, homily and intercessions — and the Liturgy of the Eucharist: the presentation of gifts, the Eucharistic Prayer with the consecration, the Lord's Prayer and Communion. The Roman calendar assigns readings and prayers across a three-year Sunday cycle and seasons from Advent through Christmastide, Lent, the Easter Triduum and Eastertide to Ordinary Time.
