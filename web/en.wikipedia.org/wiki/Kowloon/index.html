Kowloon

LocationHong Kong, north of Hong Kong Island across Victoria Harbour
Name meaning"Nine dragons" (九龍)
Ceded to Britain1860 (Convention of Peking)
DistrictsIncludes Sham Shui Po, Yau Tsim Mong, Kowloon City, Wong Tai Sin, Kwun Tong

Kowloon is an urban area of Hong Kong comprising the Kowloon Peninsula and New Kowloon. It lies across Victoria Harbour from Hong Kong Island and is one of the most densely populated urban regions on Earth, home to districts such as Tsim Sha Tsui, Mong Kok, Sham Shui Po and Kwun Tong.

History

The name Kowloon means "nine dragons", referring — legend says — to the eight mountain peaks around the bay plus the Song-dynasty emperor Bing, who fled there in the thirteenth century. The southern peninsula was ceded to Britain by Qing China under the Convention of Peking in 1860, and New Kowloon and the New Territories followed under the 1898 lease.

Within Kowloon stood the Kowloon Walled City, an ungoverned enclave that became one of the densest settlements ever recorded before its demolition in 1993–94 and conversion into a park. Kowloon also hosted the old Kai Tak Airport, famous for its dramatic approach over the city, until its closure in 1998.

Today

Modern Kowloon mixes major commercial and shopping districts, temples such as Wong Tai Sin, museums along the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront, and the West Kowloon Cultural District. It is served by several MTR lines and the harbour crossings to Hong Kong Island.
