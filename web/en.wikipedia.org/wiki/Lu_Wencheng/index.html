Lu Wencheng (Chinese: 卢文成; born 10 March 1935) is a Chinese martial artist known in martial-arts circles as a senior disciple of the Natural School (Ziranmen) master Wan Laisheng (万籁声, 1903–1992), one of the most influential kung fu teachers of 20th-century China. Lu has been credited with preserving and organising Wan's teachings and with helping document the history of the Ziranmen lineage for later practitioners.

Background

Born in Shanghai in 1935, Lu studied under Wan Laisheng, who had settled in Hunan and later taught widely in southern China. Wan had learned Ziranmen from Du Xinwu, a celebrated bodyguard of the late Qing and early Republic era, and combined it with the Shaolin-derived Liuhe (Six Harmonies) boxing he had studied earlier. As a close student, Lu Wencheng absorbed both strands — the fluid, evasive movement of Ziranmen and the more structured Liuhe forms.

Teaching and writings

Lu later served as a martial-arts instructor and is described in Chinese martial-arts publications as a custodian of Wan's system, teaching Ziranmen exercises — including its characteristic "natural" stepping patterns, qinna (joint-locking) methods, and the internal-training regimen associated with Du Xinwu's school. Accounts of Lu's recollections are a frequently cited source for anecdotes about Wan Laisheng's career and the transmission of Ziranmen in the People's Republic period.
