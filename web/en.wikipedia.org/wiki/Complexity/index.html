Complexity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Complexity characterises the behaviour of a system whose components interact in multiple ways, so that the whole exhibits properties — often called emergent properties — that cannot be understood simply from the properties of the parts. The term is used across the sciences, in engineering, and in everyday language, with many distinct technical definitions; there is no single agreed measure of complexity.

Definitions and measures

  • Computational complexity — the resources (time, memory) an algorithm needs to solve a problem.
  • Kolmogorov complexity — the length of the shortest program that reproduces an object or string.
  • Effective complexity — the amount of non-random structure or regularity in a system.
  • Statistical complexity — the information needed to specify the causal states of a process.
  • Organised complexity — Jane Jacobs' and Warren Weaver's term for problems involving many interrelated variables.

Complex systems

A complex system typically has many interacting components, feedback loops, adaptation, and sensitivity to initial conditions. Examples include the brain, ecosystems, economies, the climate, and ant colonies. The interdisciplinary study of such systems — complexity science — developed through institutions such as the Santa Fe Institute (founded 1984) and uses tools including agent-based modelling, network theory, and nonlinear dynamics.

Disorganised versus organised complexity

Warren Weaver's 1948 essay distinguished problems of simplicity, problems of disorganised complexity (tractable by statistics, like gas molecules), and problems of organised complexity (many interrelated factors, as in biology and social science), arguing the latter were the great frontier for science.

Everyday usage

Outside technical contexts, "complex" usually means having many parts in intricate arrangement, or being difficult to understand. Related notions include complication, intricacy, and sophistication; complexity is not the same as chaos — a system can be complex while remaining ordered and structured.
