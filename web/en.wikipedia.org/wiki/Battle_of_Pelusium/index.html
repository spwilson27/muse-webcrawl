The Battle of Pelusium (525 BC) was fought between the Persian king Cambyses II and Pharaoh Psamtik III of Egypt at Pelusium, the fortress guarding the eastern approach to the Nile Delta. The decisive Persian victory opened Egypt to conquest: Memphis fell after a siege, Psamtik was captured, and Egypt became a satrapy of the Achaemenid Empire — the beginning of the 27th (Persian) Dynasty.

Background

Cambyses, son of Cyrus the Great, prepared the invasion of Egypt with care, securing Arab cooperation to water his army across the Sinai desert — the logistical key to the route. Egypt's long-reigning Pharaoh Amasis II died in 526 BC and was succeeded by his inexperienced son Psamtik III. The Egyptian cause was further weakened by the defection of Phanes of Halicarnassus, a Greek mercenary commander who revealed Egypt's dispositions to Cambyses, and by the failure of Egypt's Cypriot and Samian allies to resist the Persian fleet.

The battle

Psamtik deployed at Pelusium, where the two armies fought a hard, bloody battle. The Egyptians and their Greek and Carian mercenaries were overwhelmed, and the survivors fled to Memphis. Herodotus, who claimed to have visited the battlefield decades later, described heaps of bones still lying separated by side, and gave lopsided casualty figures — 50,000 Egyptians against 7,000 Persians — that modern historians treat with caution.

The cat legend

A famous later story, told by the 2nd-century AD author Polyaenus, claims Cambyses placed cats and other animals sacred to the Egyptians in front of his army (or painted Bastet on his soldiers' shields), so that the Egyptians dared not shoot for fear of injuring the animals. The tale is not in Herodotus and is generally regarded as legend, though it remains the battle's most popular anecdote.

Aftermath

Memphis was besieged — Herodotus recounts that a Mytilenean ship sent to negotiate was sunk with all hands, in vengeance for which Psamtik was forced to witness reprisals — and surrendered. Psamtik was initially treated with clemency but, implicated in revolt, was executed. Cambyses was crowned pharaoh, founding the 27th Dynasty; Egypt remained Persian, with interruptions, until Alexander the Great.
