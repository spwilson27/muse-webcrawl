Spoofing attack

In information security, a spoofing attack is an attack in which a person or program successfully masquerades as another by falsifying data, in order to gain an illegitimate advantage. Spoofing underlies many familiar attacks, from fraudulent email and caller ID scams to sophisticated network intrusions.

Types of spoofing

  • IP address spoofing — forging the source address in packet headers, used in denial-of-service attacks and to bypass address-based access controls.
  • Email spoofing — forging the sender header of an email, a basic enabler of phishing and spam.
  • Caller ID spoofing — making a phone call appear to come from another number, common in vishing and robocall fraud.
  • Website and DNS spoofing — counterfeit websites, DNS cache poisoning, and ARP spoofing redirect victims to attacker-controlled destinations.
  • GPS spoofing — broadcasting counterfeit satellite-navigation signals to mislead receivers about their position or the time.
  • Biometric spoofing — presenting fake fingerprints, faces, or voices to defeat biometric authentication, sometimes called a presentation attack.

Defences

Countermeasures centre on authentication: cryptographic protocols such as TLS, email standards including SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, ingress packet filtering against forged source addresses, and multi-factor authentication all reduce the value of a spoofed identifier by requiring proof the attacker cannot forge.
