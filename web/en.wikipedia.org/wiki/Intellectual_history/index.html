Intellectual history

Intellectual history (also the history of ideas) is a field of historical research focused on the ideas, concepts and intellectual traditions of the past, and on the thinkers, texts and contexts that produced them. It overlaps with cultural history, philosophy, and the history of science, literature and political thought.

Approaches

Early work, such as Arthur Lovejoy's "unit-ideas" approach and his Great Chain of Being (1936), traced individual concepts across centuries. The Cambridge School associated with Quentin Skinner and J. G. A. Pocock insisted instead on recovering the linguistic and political contexts in which texts were interventions. Other approaches include conceptual history (Begriffsgeschichte, associated with Reinhart Koselleck), French histoire des mentalités, and the global intellectual history that emerged in the 2000s, tracing the circulation of ideas across borders.

Institutions

The field is represented by journals such as the Journal of the History of Ideas (founded 1940), History of European Ideas and Modern Intellectual History.
