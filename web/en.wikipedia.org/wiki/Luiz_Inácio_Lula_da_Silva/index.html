Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (born 27 October 1945), known mononymously as Lula, is a Brazilian politician who has served as the 39th president of Brazil since 2023. A member of the Workers' Party, he was previously the 35th president from 2003 to 2011.

Lula, founder of the Workers' Party and twice president of Brazil

Early life and union career

Lula was born in Caetés, Pernambuco, to a poor family of agricultural workers. The family migrated to São Paulo state in search of a better life. With little formal education, Lula worked from a young age and became a metalworker in the industrial ABC Region. He rose through the metalworkers' union, leading major strikes in the late 1970s against the military dictatorship, and helped found the Workers' Party (PT) in 1980 and the Central Única dos Trabalhadores (CUT) union federation in 1983.

First presidencies (2003–2011)

After three unsuccessful bids, Lula won the presidency in 2002. His governments combined orthodox macroeconomic policy with expanded social programs, most famously Bolsa Família, a conditional cash-transfer program credited with lifting millions out of poverty and sharply reducing inequality. Brazil experienced strong growth during the 2000s commodity boom, and Lula left office with very high approval ratings, succeeded by his chosen successor, Dilma Rousseff.

Imprisonment and return

In 2017 Lula was convicted of corruption and money laundering in the Operation Car Wash investigations and was imprisoned in 2018, barring him from that year's presidential election. The convictions were annulled by the Supreme Federal Court in 2021 on jurisdictional grounds and findings of judicial bias, restoring his political rights. In the 2022 election he defeated incumbent Jair Bolsonaro and returned to the presidency on 1 January 2023, becoming the first Brazilian president elected to three terms.
