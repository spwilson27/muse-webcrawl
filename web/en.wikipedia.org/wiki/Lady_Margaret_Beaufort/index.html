Lady Margaret Beaufort

Lady Margaret Beaufort
Born: 31 May 1443, Bletsoe Castle, Bedfordshire
Died: 29 June 1509 (aged 66), Westminster Abbey, London
Spouse: John de la Pole, 2nd Duke of Suffolk (annulled); Edmund Tudor, 1st Earl of Richmond; Henry Stafford; Thomas Stanley, 1st Earl of Derby
Issue: Henry VII of England
House: Beaufort
Father: John Beaufort, 1st Duke of Somerset
Mother: Margaret Beauchamp of Bletso

Lady Margaret Beaufort (31 May 1443 – 29 June 1509) was a major figure in the Wars of the Roses and an influential matriarch of the House of Tudor. She was the mother of King Henry VII, the first Tudor monarch of England, and played an active role in securing the throne for her son after the Battle of Bosworth Field in 1485.

Early life

Margaret was the daughter and sole heiress of John Beaufort, 1st Duke of Somerset, a grandson of John of Gaunt. Through her father she carried a claim to the English throne derived from the legitimised Beaufort line. Her father died when she was an infant, and she became one of the wealthiest heiresses in England.

As a child she was married briefly to John de la Pole, but the marriage was annulled. In 1455, aged twelve, she married Edmund Tudor, 1st Earl of Richmond, half-brother of King Henry VI. Edmund died of plague in November 1456 while a prisoner of the Yorkists, leaving the thirteen-year-old Margaret a widow and seven months pregnant. On 28 January 1457 she gave birth to her only child, Henry Tudor, at Pembroke Castle.

The Wars of the Roses

Margaret later married Henry Stafford and, after his death, Thomas Stanley, later Earl of Derby. During the reign of Richard III she conspired with Elizabeth Woodville to marry her son Henry to Elizabeth of York, uniting the Lancastrian and Yorkist claims. She was placed under house arrest and her lands were confiscated, but the plot succeeded: Henry landed in Wales in August 1485 and defeated Richard at Bosworth, where Stanley's intervention proved decisive.

Role at court

As the king's mother, Margaret held a position of great influence, signing herself "Margaret R" and styling herself "My Lady the King's Mother". She administered her vast estates, oversaw the royal household, and was known for her piety and learning. She took a vow of chastity in 1499 while her husband still lived.

Patronage and legacy

Margaret was a notable patron of learning. She founded Christ's College, Cambridge (refounded 1505) and established St John's College, Cambridge, which was founded after her death under the terms of her will (charter 1511). She endowed professorships of divinity at both Oxford and Cambridge — the Lady Margaret Professorships — and supported the printers William Caxton and Wynkyn de Worde. She predeceased her son by only a few weeks in reverse order: Henry VII died in April 1509, and Margaret died on 29 June 1509, days after the coronation of her grandson, Henry VIII. She is buried in Westminster Abbey.
