The United Kingdom has a road network of roughly 262,300 miles (422,100 km), administered by National Highways and the devolved and local authorities. Roads are classified as motorways (designated M), primary and non-primary A roads, B roads, and minor C and unclassified roads. Vehicles drive on the left, and speed limits are 70 mph on motorways and dual carriageways, 60 mph on single carriageways and 30 mph in built-up areas unless signed otherwise.

History

Many routes follow Roman roads such as Watling Street and the Fosse Way. Turnpike trusts improved main roads in the 18th and 19th centuries, and the motor age brought trunk-road programmes and, from 1958, motorways, beginning with the Preston Bypass and the M1. Trunk roads in Scotland and Wales are now managed by the devolved governments.

Numbering

Great Britain's road numbering is arranged in zones radiating from London and Edinburgh: A1 to A6 run clockwise from London. Northern Ireland uses its own numbering system. Famous routes include the A1 Great North Road, the A303 past Stonehenge and Land's End to John o' Groats journeys along the network.
