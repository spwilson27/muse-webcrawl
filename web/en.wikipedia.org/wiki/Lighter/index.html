Lighter

A lighter is a portable device used to generate a flame for igniting combustible material such as cigarettes, candles, or campfires. Most modern lighters burn liquefied butane or naphtha fuel and produce ignition by a spark from a flint or by a piezoelectric crystal.

History

One of the earliest lighters was Döbereiner's lamp (1823), which generated hydrogen over a platinum catalyst. Flint-and-fuel designs spread in the early twentieth century, and the Austrian flint patent of 1903 underpinned brands such as Zippo (founded 1932), whose windproof petrol lighters became iconic. Disposable butane lighters were popularised in the 1970s by companies such as Bic, Cricket, and Tokai.

Types

Common types include disposable and refillable butane lighters, petrol (wick) lighters, torch or jet-flame lighters, and flameless electric arc and coil lighters. Safety regulations in many countries require child-resistant mechanisms, and aeroplane rules restrict certain types in baggage.
