Maria Martinez

Maria Montoya Martinez (c. 1887 – 20 July 1980), known in her Tewa language as Poveka (“Pond Lily”), was a Native American artist of San Ildefonso Pueblo in New Mexico, celebrated worldwide for her black-on-black ceramic ware. Together with her husband Julian Martinez, who painted the designs, she revived and transformed Pueblo pottery into a highly prized fine art.

Life and work

Martinez learned traditional pottery-making as a girl and was already known for her thin-walled polychrome ware when archaeologist Edgar Lee Hewett, excavating near the pueblo, asked her to attempt recreating the matte-black-on-polished-black finish of ancient sherds. After years of experiments with firing in a reduced-oxygen environment using manure and careful smudging, she and Julian perfected the black-on-black technique around 1918–1919.

Legacy

Their pottery won prizes at world’s fairs and was collected by museums and presidents; Martinez demonstrated at the White House and received numerous honorary degrees. She taught the craft widely within her family and pueblo, and several generations of her descendants continue the tradition. Her work helped create an economic foundation for Pueblo artists across the Southwest.
