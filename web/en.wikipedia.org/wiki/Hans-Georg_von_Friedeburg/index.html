Hans-Georg von Friedeburg

Hans-Georg von Friedeburg (15 July 1895 – 23 May 1945) was a German admiral, deputy commander of the U-boat arm of the Kriegsmarine in the Second World War and the last Commander-in-Chief of the Kriegsmarine. He is best known for signing, on behalf of the German armed forces, the surrender documents in northwestern Europe in May 1945.

Career

Friedeburg joined the Imperial German Navy in 1914 and served in U-boats in the First World War. Between the wars he rose through the ranks, and in 1939 he joined the U-boat command under Karl Dönitz, becoming its organisational and later deputy chief. He was responsible for U-boat training and deployment planning.

Surrender and death

After Hitler's suicide, Dönitz became head of state and promoted Friedeburg to Generaladmiral and Commander-in-Chief of the Navy. Friedeburg signed the partial surrender of German forces in the Netherlands, northwestern Germany and Denmark at Lüneburg Heath on 4 May 1945, and was present at the signing of the general surrender at Reims on 7 May. Arrested by British troops, he committed suicide on 23 May 1945 in Flensburg, the day the Dönitz government was dissolved.
