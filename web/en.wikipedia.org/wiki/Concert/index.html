Concert

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A rock concert in a large arena

A concert is a live music performance in front of an audience. The performance may be by a single musician, sometimes then called a recital, or by a musical ensemble, such as an orchestra, choir, or band. Concerts are held in a wide variety of settings and sizes, from private houses and small nightclubs to dedicated concert halls, arenas, parks, and stadiums.

History

Formal public concerts developed in the late 17th and 18th centuries: John Banister organized some of the first public concerts in London in 1672, and subscription concert series flourished in Paris, Vienna, and other European capitals. The 19th century saw the rise of the purpose-built concert hall and the virtuoso soloist. In the 20th century, amplified popular music created the modern arena and stadium concert, and multi-day music festivals became major cultural events.

Types

Classical concerts include orchestral concerts, chamber music recitals, solo recitals, and choral concerts, traditionally with seated, quiet audiences. Popular music concerts — rock, pop, jazz, hip hop, and electronic music — typically feature standing audiences and elaborate staging, lighting, and sound reinforcement. A series of concerts by an artist in different cities is called a concert tour.

Economics

Concerts are a major revenue source for performers, especially as recorded-music income has declined in the streaming era. Ticket pricing, merchandise, and sponsorships all contribute; large tours can gross hundreds of millions of dollars.
