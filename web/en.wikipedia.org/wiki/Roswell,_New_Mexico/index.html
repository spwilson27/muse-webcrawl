Roswell, New Mexico

Roswell is a city in, and the county seat of, Chaves County in the U.S. state of New Mexico. With a population of about 48,000 at the 2020 census, it is the fifth-most populous city in the state and a centre for irrigation farming, dairying, ranching, manufacturing and petroleum production in the Pecos Valley of south-eastern New Mexico.

History

The settlement was founded in 1869 by Van C. Smith, who named it after his father, Roswell Smith, and incorporated as a town in 1891. Artesian water discovered in the 1890s spurred growth. Nearby Walker Air Force Base was a major employer until its closure in 1967; the New Mexico Military Institute, founded in 1891, remains a local landmark.

The 1947 incident

Roswell is internationally famous for the Roswell incident of July 1947, when the U.S. Army Air Forces issued a press release stating it had recovered a "flying disc" from a ranch near the town, then retracted it in favour of a weather balloon. Decades later the event became the most famous case in ufology; the U.S. Air Force attributed the debris to a Project Mogul balloon train. The city embraces its notoriety with the International UFO Museum and Research Center, an annual UFO festival and alien-themed street decoration.

Geography and economy

Roswell sits on the high plains west of the Pecos River. Bottomless Lakes State Park lies to the east and Bitter Lake National Wildlife Refuge to the north-east. Agriculture, oil and gas, healthcare and education anchor the economy; the city is also known as a centre of the dairy industry and for Robert H. Goddard's rocket experiments conducted there in the 1930s.
