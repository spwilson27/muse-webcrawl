Waris Shah (1722–1798) was an 18th-century Punjabi Sufi poet, renowned for his retelling of the tragic romance Heer Ranjha, the most celebrated of the Punjabi qissa (narrative poem) tradition. He is sometimes called the Shakespeare of Punjabi literature.

Life

Born in Jandiala Sher Khan in present-day Pakistan, Waris Shah was educated in a mosque school and became associated with the Chishti Sufi order. Legend holds that his own youthful love for a woman named Bhag Bhari deepened the emotional power of his poetry. He spent much of his later life in the village of Malka Hans.

Heer Ranjha

Completed around 1766, his version of the Heer–Ranjha tale recounts the love of Heer Sial and Dheedo Ranjha, thwarted by family opposition and ending in the lovers' deaths. Far more than a romance, the poem is a rich portrait of 18th-century Punjabi society and a vehicle for Sufi reflection on divine love. Its verses are still sung and quoted throughout Punjab, and the tomb of Waris Shah in Jandiala Sher Khan remains a place of pilgrimage.
