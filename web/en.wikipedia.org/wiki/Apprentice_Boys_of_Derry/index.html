Apprentice Boys of Derry

Formation1814
TypeProtestant fraternal society
HeadquartersDerry, Northern Ireland

The Apprentice Boys of Derry is a Protestant fraternal society founded in 1814, based in Derry, Northern Ireland, with clubs throughout the United Kingdom, Ireland, and the Commonwealth. Its name commemorates the thirteen apprentice boys who, on 7 December 1688, shut the gates of the city against the Jacobite Earl of Antrim's regiment, an event that preceded the Siege of Derry of 1689, a defining episode of the Williamite War in Ireland.

Commemorations

The society's two principal annual events are the "Shutting of the Gates" ceremony on the Saturday closest to 7 December, when an effigy of the governor Robert Lundy — who had sought to surrender the city — is burned, and the Relief of Derry celebrations on the Saturday closest to 12 August, marking the lifting of the siege in 1689. The August parade is one of the largest Protestant parades in Northern Ireland and, like other loyal order parades, was historically a source of tension; in recent decades the society has cooperated with civic authorities and the annual Maiden City Festival to make the event more inclusive.
