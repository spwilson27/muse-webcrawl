Foresight (psychology)

In psychology, foresight is the ability to predict, or the action of predicting, what will happen in the future and to prepare accordingly. It involves mentally simulating future events and consequences, and is closely related to episodic future thinking, planning, delay of gratification and prospection.

Development and cognition

Foresight develops through childhood as executive function and episodic memory mature; young children initially struggle to imagine future needs (as in the classic test in which children choose an item for a future situation). In adults, foresight underlies decision-making: people with impaired foresight, for instance after frontal-lobe injury, may act impulsively despite intact knowledge of rules. The hippocampus and prefrontal cortex are implicated in constructing imagined future episodes, drawing on the same systems used to remember the past.

Clinical relevance

Diminished or distorted foresight appears in several conditions: impulsivity in ADHD and addiction (steep delay discounting of future rewards), hopelessness and negative future simulation in depression, and catastrophic anticipation in anxiety disorders. Interventions such as episodic future thinking exercises aim to strengthen vivid future simulation and thereby improve self-control and health behaviour.
