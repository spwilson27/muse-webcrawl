Insolvency Act 1986

Insolvency Act 1986
Parliament of the United Kingdom
Long title: An Act to consolidate the enactments relating to company insolvency and winding up, and related matters.
Royal assent: 25 June 1986
Commencement: 29 December 1986

The Insolvency Act 1986 (c. 45) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that provides the legal platform for all matters relating to personal and corporate insolvency in England, Wales and Scotland, with parts applying in Northern Ireland. It consolidated earlier legislation following the report of the Cork Committee, led by Sir Kenneth Cork, which proposed a comprehensive modern insolvency framework.

Contents

The Act is divided into parts dealing, among other things, with company voluntary arrangements, administration, receivership, winding up of companies, bankruptcy of individuals, preferences and transactions at an undervalue, fraudulent and wrongful trading, and the regulation of insolvency practitioners. Amendments by the Insolvency Act 2000 and the Enterprise Act 2002 reshaped administration and bankruptcy discharge.

Individual insolvency

For individuals, the Act provides for bankruptcy, individual voluntary arrangements (IVAs), and debt relief. A bankruptcy order may be made by the court on a petition, and the bankrupt's estate vests in a trustee in bankruptcy for realisation and distribution to creditors.

Corporate insolvency

For companies, the Act provides procedures intended variously to rescue the company, to achieve a better result for creditors than an immediate winding up, or to realise and distribute assets. Directors may face liability for wrongful trading under section 214 if they allowed a company to continue trading when there was no reasonable prospect of avoiding insolvent liquidation.
