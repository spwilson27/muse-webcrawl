Wikidata is a free and open knowledge base that can be read and edited by both humans and machines. Launched on 30 October 2012 and operated by the Wikimedia Foundation, it acts as a central store of structured data for Wikipedia and its sister projects, and is freely available to anyone under a CC0 public-domain dedication.

Data model

Knowledge in Wikidata is organised into items, each with a unique identifier beginning with Q (for example, Q42 for the writer Douglas Adams). Items carry multilingual labels, descriptions and aliases, and a set of statements: a property (with identifiers beginning with P, such as "date of birth") paired with a value, optionally refined by qualifiers and supported by references. Since 2018 Wikidata also stores lexemes — structured descriptions of words — for lexicographical data.

History and uses

Wikidata was developed by Wikimedia Deutschland; its initial development was funded by donations from the Allen Institute for Artificial Intelligence, Google, and the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation. It initially centralised the interlanguage links between Wikipedia editions, then took on infobox data, so that facts such as population figures can be maintained once and reused across hundreds of language editions. Data is accessible through an API and a public SPARQL query service, and is widely reused by search engines, voice assistants, researchers and third-party applications.
