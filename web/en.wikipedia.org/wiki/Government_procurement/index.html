Government procurement

Government procurement
DefinitionPurchase of goods, services and works by governments and public bodies
ScaleTypically 10–15% of GDP in developed economies
Key frameworksWTO Government Procurement Agreement; EU procurement directives; US Federal Acquisition Regulation
Common methodsOpen tender, competitive bidding, framework agreements, requests for proposals

Government procurement (also called public procurement or public tendering) is the process by which governments and state-owned enterprises purchase goods, services and construction works from private-sector suppliers. Because it is funded by taxpayers and typically represents a tenth or more of GDP, procurement is heavily regulated to ensure competition, transparency, value for money and the prevention of corruption.

Process

A typical procurement cycle runs from identification of a need and specification, through advertisement of the contract opportunity, submission and evaluation of bids, award, contract management and payment. Above defined value thresholds, contracts must generally be advertised openly and awarded by published criteria. Electronic procurement portals now host most notices in many jurisdictions.

Legal frameworks

The WTO Agreement on Government Procurement (GPA) commits its parties to open covered procurement to suppliers from other parties. The European Union's procurement directives impose competitive tendering across member states, while in the United States the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) governs federal purchases, supplemented by "Buy American" and small-business set-aside rules. Most systems balance open competition against policy goals such as supporting domestic industry, small businesses, sustainability and social value.

Challenges

Public procurement is a perennial site of concern about favoritism, bid-rigging, cost overruns on major projects, and the difficulty of specifying complex outputs — especially in defense and IT. Reform efforts emphasize e-procurement, open contracting data, and professionalized procurement workforces.
