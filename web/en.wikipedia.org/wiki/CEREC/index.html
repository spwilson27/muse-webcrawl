CEREC is a method of CAD/CAM dentistry developed by Werner Mörmann and Marco Brandestini at the University of Zurich and commercialised by Siemens and its successor Dentsply Sirona. The name is an acronym for Chairside Economical Restoration of Esthetic Ceramics and also derives from "CERamic REConstruction". The system allows a dentist to design, mill and place a ceramic crown, inlay, onlay or veneer in a single appointment.

Process

After preparing the tooth, the dentist captures a digital impression with an intraoral camera, designs the restoration on screen using the CEREC software, and sends the design to a chairside milling unit, which cuts the restoration from a solid block of ceramic or composite in a matter of minutes. The restoration is then polished, characterised or glazed as needed, and bonded into place — eliminating the conventional impression, temporary crown and second appointment required by laboratory-made work.

History and impact

The first patient was treated with a CEREC restoration in 1985, and the system was introduced commercially as CEREC 1 in 1987. Successive generations added three-dimensional imaging, colour scanning, faster milling and new materials including zirconia. CEREC pioneered chairside digital dentistry and remains one of the most widely used CAD/CAM systems in dental practice, with long-term clinical studies showing survival rates for its ceramic inlays and crowns comparable to conventional restorations.
