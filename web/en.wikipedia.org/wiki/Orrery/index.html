Orrery

A grand orrery in brass

An orrery is a mechanical model of the Solar System that illustrates the relative positions and motions of the planets and moons around the Sun. Usually driven by clockwork, an orrery moves its planets at their correct relative orbital speeds, though sizes and distances cannot both be to scale in a practical instrument.

History

The ancient Antikythera mechanism (c. 150–100 BC) computed planetary positions with geared wheels and is sometimes regarded as a precursor. The modern orrery appeared around 1704, when London instrument makers George Graham and Thomas Tompion built a model for Charles Boyle, 4th Earl of Orrery, from whom the device takes its name. Elaborate "grand orreries" showing the outer planets became prestigious scientific furniture in the 18th century, and John Winthrop lectured with one at Harvard from the 1760s.

Types

A tellurian (or tellurion) models the Earth–Moon–Sun system, demonstrating seasons, phases of the Moon, and eclipses. A planetarium in the older sense is an orrery showing only the planets. The oldest still-working planetarium of this type is Eise Eisinga's 1774–1781 ceiling orrery in Franeker, Netherlands.
