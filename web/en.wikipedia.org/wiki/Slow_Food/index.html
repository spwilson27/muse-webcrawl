Slow Food

Slow Food is an organisation that promotes local food and traditional cooking. It was founded by Carlo Petrini in 1986 and has since spread worldwide. Its goals are sustainable foods and the promotion of local small businesses. It is opposed to the process of fast food. The organisation, which began in Italy, now numbers over 100,000 members in 160 countries.

The movement's symbol is a snail, chosen because it moves slowly and calmly eats its way through life. The first Slow Food protest took place in 1986, when Petrini and a group of activists sought to prevent a McDonald's restaurant from opening near the Spanish Steps in Rome, by distributing bowls of penne pasta to passers-by.

History

Slow Food began in Italy with the founding of its forerunner organisation, Arcigola, in 1986 to resist the opening of a McDonald's restaurant near the Spanish Steps in Rome. In 1989, the founding manifesto of the international Slow Food movement was signed in Paris by delegates from 15 countries.

At its heart is the aim to promote local foods and traditional gastronomy and food production. Conversely, this means an opposition to fast food, industrial food production, and globalisation. The movement has expanded globally to over 100,000 members in 160 countries. Its goals of sustainable foods and promotion of local small businesses have been paralleled by a political agenda directed against globalisation of agricultural products.

Philosophy

Slow Food's approach to agriculture, food production and gastronomy is based on a concept of food quality with three interconnected principles, defined by its founder Carlo Petrini, who first articulated them at the movement's inception:

  • Good: quality, flavoursome and healthy food
  • Clean: production that does not harm the environment
  • Fair: accessible prices for consumers and fair conditions and pay for producers

Petrini expanded these concepts in his book Slow Food Nation and continues to articulate them in lectures, including the idea of the "gastronome" as someone engaged in the full cycle of food from production to consumption.

Activities

The movement operates through local chapters known as convivia, of which there were several hundred around the world. Slow Food organises major international events including Salone del Gusto (the "Hall of Taste") and Terra Madre, a biennial gathering of food communities in Turin. In 2004 it founded the University of Gastronomic Sciences at Pollenzo, in Piedmont.

Through its Ark of Taste programme, Slow Food catalogues endangered heritage foods — traditional varieties, breeds, cheeses, cured meats and dishes judged to be at risk of extinction. Its Presidia projects support small-scale producers of such foods. The movement also runs the "1000 Gardens in Africa" project, supporting community and school gardens.

Criticism

Critics have charged Slow Food with elitism, arguing that its preferred foods are expensive and its events inaccessible, and some have questioned the movement's occasional opposition to genetic engineering and other agricultural technologies. Petrini and other representatives answer that good, clean and fair food should be a universal right, and that the movement works to make such food accessible rather than exclusive.
