Carotid body

The carotid body is a small cluster of chemoreceptor cells located at the bifurcation of the common carotid artery, where it divides into the internal and external carotid arteries. It monitors the composition of arterial blood and helps regulate breathing.

Function

Type I (glomus) cells in the carotid body respond mainly to a fall in arterial oxygen partial pressure, and also to increases in carbon dioxide and decreases in pH. Signals travel via the carotid sinus nerve, a branch of the glossopharyngeal nerve, to the respiratory centres of the medulla, which adjust ventilation to restore blood gas levels.

Clinical significance

Tumours of the carotid body, called paragangliomas or chemodectomas, are usually benign and present as slow-growing neck masses.
