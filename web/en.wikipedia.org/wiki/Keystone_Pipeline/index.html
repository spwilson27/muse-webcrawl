The Keystone Pipeline System, operated by TC Energy (formerly TransCanada), is an oil pipeline system in Canada and the United States, commissioned in 2010 and owned by TC Energy and, as of March 2020, the Government of Alberta. It runs from the Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin in Alberta to refineries in Illinois and Texas, and also to oil tank farms and an oil pipeline distribution center in Cushing, Oklahoma.

History

The pipeline came under planned development in 2005 and the Canadian section received regulatory approval in 2007. Phase 1, from Hardisty, Alberta to Steele City, Nebraska and on to Wood River and Patoka, Illinois, was completed in 2010–2011. Phase 2, to Cushing, Oklahoma, was completed in 2011, and Phase 3, the Gulf Coast extension from Cushing to Port Arthur, Texas, began operating in 2014.

Keystone XL

The proposed fourth phase, Keystone XL, would have run from Hardisty through Montana and South Dakota to Steele City, providing a shorter route and larger-diameter pipe. The project became a major focus of environmental protest in the United States, with opponents citing risks to the Ogallala Aquifer, oil spill dangers, and the carbon intensity of Alberta oil sands crude. President Barack Obama rejected the permit in 2015; President Donald Trump revived it in 2017; President Joe Biden revoked the permit on his first day in office in January 2021, and TC Energy terminated the project in June 2021.

Spills

The pipeline has experienced several significant spills, including a 2017 spill of roughly 210,000 gallons in South Dakota and a December 2022 spill in Kansas in which approximately 14,000 barrels of crude oil were released into Mill Creek in Washington County — the largest spill in the system's history.
