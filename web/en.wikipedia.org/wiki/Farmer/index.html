Farmer

A farmer is a person engaged in agriculture, raising living organisms for food or raw materials. The term usually applies to people who grow field crops, orchards, vineyards, or raise poultry and livestock. Farmers may own the land they work or serve as laborers on land owned by others; in most developed economies, a farmer is typically a farm owner, while employees are known as farm workers or farmhands.

History

Farming dates to the Neolithic Revolution, around 10,000 BCE, when humans in the Fertile Crescent and other regions began domesticating plants and animals. For most of history, the great majority of the population worked in agriculture. The mechanization of farming from the 19th century, followed by synthetic fertilizers, pesticides and improved crop varieties in the 20th century, dramatically raised productivity and reduced agriculture's share of employment in industrialized countries to a few percent of the workforce.

Types of farmers

Farmers are often categorized by what they produce: arable farmers grow crops, pastoral or livestock farmers raise animals, and mixed farmers do both. Dairy farmers, market gardeners, fish farmers (aquaculturists), and ranchers are specialized types. Subsistence farmers produce mainly for their own households, whereas commercial farmers produce for the market.

Modern challenges

Today's farmers face pressures from volatile commodity prices, climate change, water scarcity, soil degradation and consolidation in the food supply chain. At the same time, precision agriculture — GPS-guided machinery, drones and data analytics — is reshaping the occupation, and younger farmers increasingly enter through organic and direct-to-consumer models.
