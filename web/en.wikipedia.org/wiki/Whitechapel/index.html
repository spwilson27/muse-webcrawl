Whitechapel is a district in the East End of London, within the London Borough of Tower Hamlets, just east of the City of London. It is centred on Whitechapel Road and Whitechapel High Street and takes its name from a small white chapel dedicated to St Mary, built in the area in the Middle Ages. The district has long been a gateway for immigrant communities arriving in London and is today home to a large British Bangladeshi population alongside many other communities.

History

Whitechapel grew along the Roman road from London to Colchester. By the 19th century it had become densely populated and impoverished, known for overcrowded lodging houses, sweatshops, and industries such as bell founding — the Whitechapel Bell Foundry, established in 1570, cast Big Ben and the original Liberty Bell.

In 1888 the area became infamous worldwide as the scene of the Whitechapel murders, a series of killings of women attributed in part to the unidentified murderer known as Jack the Ripper. The murders exposed Victorian London to the conditions of the East End poor and spurred social reform.

Landmarks and institutions

The Royal London Hospital, founded in 1740, stands on Whitechapel Road. The Whitechapel Gallery, founded in 1901, is a noted public art gallery. The East London Mosque, one of the largest in Europe, serves the local Muslim community. Whitechapel Market trades along the main road, and the district is served by Whitechapel station, an interchange on the London Underground and the Elizabeth line.
