Michele Sindona (8 May 1920 – 22 March 1986) was an Italian banker, lawyer and convicted felon, notorious for his ties to the Sicilian Mafia, the Vatican Bank (Istituto per le Opere di Religione) and the secret Masonic lodge Propaganda Due (P2). Known in the Italian press as "the Shark" (lo Squalo), he built a banking empire in the 1960s and early 1970s before its spectacular collapse in 1974.

Early life and career

Sindona was born in Patti, Sicily, and studied law at the University of Messina, graduating in 1942. He moved to Milan in 1946, where he built a lucrative practice as a tax lawyer, mastering the loopholes of the Italian fiscal system. During the 1950s and 1960s he acquired control of several Italian banks, including Banca Unione and Banca Privata Finanziaria, and built an international network of holding companies and offshore entities.

Banking empire and collapse

Sindona enjoyed the confidence of the Vatican, becoming a financial adviser to the Holy See and a partner of the Vatican Bank under Archbishop Paul Marcinkus. In 1972 he acquired a controlling interest in Franklin National Bank, then the twentieth-largest bank in the United States. Overextended and engaged in speculative currency dealings, his empire unravelled in 1974: Banca Privata Italiana (formed by merging his Italian banks) was placed in liquidation, and Franklin National Bank failed in what was then the largest bank failure in American history. The liquidation of Banca Privata, overseen by Giorgio Ambrosoli, exposed massive fraud and Sindona's role in recycling Mafia heroin proceeds.

Trials, staged kidnapping and murder conviction

Sindona fled to the United States, where he was convicted in New York in 1980 of fraud in connection with the Franklin collapse and sentenced to 25 years. In 1979 he had staged his own kidnapping by supposed left-wing terrorists in Sicily — in reality an attempt, organised with Mafia help, to blackmail his political protectors and recover incriminating documents. He was also convicted in Italy of ordering the 1979 murder of Giorgio Ambrosoli, the liquidator investigating his banks, and received a life sentence.

Death

On 20 March 1986, two days after being sentenced to life imprisonment by a Milan court, Sindona drank coffee laced with potassium cyanide in his cell at Voghera prison; he died on 22 March 1986. Whether his death was suicide or murder remains disputed.
