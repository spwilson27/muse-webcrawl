FGM-148 Javelin

The FGM-148 Javelin is an American-made man-portable anti-tank guided missile system developed by a joint venture between Raytheon and Lockheed Martin. It entered U.S. service in 1996, replacing the M47 Dragon. The Javelin is a "fire-and-forget" weapon: its imaging infrared seeker locks onto a target before launch and guides the missile autonomously, allowing the operator to take cover immediately after firing.

Design

The system consists of a reusable Command Launch Unit (CLU) with day and thermal sights, and a sealed launch tube containing the missile. The tandem-charge high-explosive anti-tank (HEAT) warhead is designed to defeat explosive reactive armour and penetrate main battle tank armour. The missile can attack in direct mode or in a top-attack profile, climbing steeply and diving onto the thinner roof armour of a tank. A soft-launch motor ejects the missile before the main motor ignites, permitting firing from enclosed spaces.

Service history

The Javelin has been exported to numerous allied nations and used in Iraq, Afghanistan and other conflicts. Large numbers were supplied to Ukraine from 2018 onward and were used extensively against Russian armoured vehicles following the 2022 invasion, becoming one of the emblematic weapons of that war.
