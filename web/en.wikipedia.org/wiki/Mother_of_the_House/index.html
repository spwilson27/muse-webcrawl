Mother of the House

The House of Commons chamber at Westminster

The Mother of the House is an informal title given to the longest-serving female Member of Parliament (MP) in the House of Commons of the United Kingdom. The title parallels the Father of the House, the title held by the longest-serving MP overall, which was historically always held by a man until the late 20th century.

Usage

The title is a matter of convention rather than a formal parliamentary office, and grants no official duties or privileges, although the Mother of the House, like the Father, may be consulted on matters of precedent and custom. The term came into regular use in the 2010s. Harriet Harman, MP from 1982, was frequently described as Mother of the House during her later years in Parliament, and after her retirement at the 2024 general election the description passed to Diane Abbott, first elected in 1987, who became the longest-serving female MP and the first Black woman to hold the informal title.

Equivalent titles

Similar honorifics exist in other legislatures, including the "Mother of the Parliament" in some Commonwealth countries, and in Australia the longest-serving female members of the federal parliament have occasionally been described in the same terms.
