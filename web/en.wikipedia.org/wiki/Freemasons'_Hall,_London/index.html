Freemasons' Hall, London

Freemasons' Hall in Great Queen Street, London, is the headquarters of the United Grand Lodge of England and the Supreme Grand Chapter of Royal Arch Masons, and a meeting place for many Masonic lodges in the London area. The present Art Deco building, opened in 1933, is the third Masonic hall on the site and serves as a memorial to the Freemasons who died in the First World War.

History

The site between Great Queen Street and Wild Street has been a Masonic meeting place since 1775. The current building was designed by architects Henry Victor Ashley and F. Winton Newman and constructed between 1927 and 1933 as the Masonic Peace Memorial. At its heart is the Grand Temple, seating about 1,700, with elaborate bronze doors and a celebrated organ; the building also houses the Library and Museum of Freemasonry, which is open to the public.

Use

Beyond Masonic meetings, Freemasons' Hall is a popular filming location and events venue, appearing in numerous films and television programmes, and hosts concerts, exhibitions, and London Fashion Week events. It is a Grade II* listed building.
