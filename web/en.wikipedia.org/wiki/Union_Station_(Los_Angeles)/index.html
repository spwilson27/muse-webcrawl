Union Station (Los Angeles)

Los Angeles Union Station is the main railway station in Los Angeles, California, and the largest railroad passenger terminal in the western United States. It opened in May 1939, serving the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway, Southern Pacific and Union Pacific railroads.

Architecture

The station was designed by John and Donald Parkinson with an innovative blend of Mission Revival, Spanish Colonial Revival and Streamline Moderne styles. Its grand waiting room features high beamed ceilings, tilework, travertine walls and large arched windows. The station was built partly on the site of the city's original Chinatown, whose residents were displaced.

Services

The station is served by Amtrak intercity trains (including the Coast Starlight, Southwest Chief and Pacific Surfliner), Metrolink commuter trains, Los Angeles Metro light rail and subway lines, and numerous buses. It is owned by Metro and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The station is also a popular filming location.
