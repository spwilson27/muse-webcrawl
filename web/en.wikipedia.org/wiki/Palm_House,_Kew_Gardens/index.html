Palm House, Kew Gardens

The Palm House is a large palm house in the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, in London, that houses tropical plants from around the world. Built between 1844 and 1848, it is regarded as one of the finest surviving examples of Victorian glass-and-iron architecture and one of the earliest large-scale uses of wrought iron in a curvilinear structure.

Design and construction

The Palm House was designed by architect Decimus Burton and iron-founder Richard Turner. Its central dome rises to about 19 metres, with wings extending to either side, and it is glazed with hand-blown glass panels tinted green with copper oxide to protect plants from scorching. The structure pioneered techniques later used in railway stations and exhibition halls such as the Crystal Palace.

Collections

The house is arranged geographically, with plants of the Americas in the south wing, Africa and Madagascar at the centre, and Asia, Australia and the Pacific in the north wing. Notable specimens include the world's oldest pot plant, a cycad (Encephalartos altensteinii) collected in South Africa in the 1770s. A basement marine aquarium displays algae and coastal plants.

Restoration

The Palm House was extensively restored between 1985 and 1988, when every pane of glass and iron component was cleaned, repaired or replaced, and the plants were temporarily rehoused. It is a Grade I listed building within the Kew Gardens UNESCO World Heritage Site.
