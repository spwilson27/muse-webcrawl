Naratheinkha

Naratheinkha (Burmese: နရသိင်္ခ; 1141–1174) was king of the Pagan dynasty of Burma (Myanmar) from 1171 to 1174. The eldest son of King Min Shin Saw, he came to the throne after the assassination of his father by his younger brother Narapatisithu's rivals — in the standard chronicle account, after his father was killed by the chief of a hill tribe — and his short reign ended when he was himself overthrown and killed by his brother.

Early life

Naratheinkha was the eldest son of Min Shin Saw, the eldest son of King Alaungsithu, and was thus in the direct line of succession. His younger brother was Narapatisithu, one of the most capable princes of the dynasty. When Alaungsithu fell gravely ill in 1167, Min Shin Saw attempted to seize the throne but was outmanoeuvred; he was exiled, then recalled, and after Alaungsithu's death in 1171 Min Shin Saw ascended the throne only to be assassinated within a short time, according to the chronicles by agents of a Sawlu-descended chief. Naratheinkha, then governor in the north, marched on Pagan and took the crown.

Reign

Naratheinkha's reign is poorly documented and lasted only about three years. By chronicle accounts he took as queen one of his brother Narapatisithu's wives while his brother was away on campaign, an affront that turned Narapatisithu against him. When Narapatisithu returned and advanced on the capital, the king attempted to reconcile the brothers but was seized and put to death in 1174.

Succession

He was succeeded by Narapatisithu (r. 1174–1211), whose long reign is regarded as one of the high points of the Pagan Empire, marked by extensive temple building and administrative consolidation. Little monumental building is attributed to Naratheinkha himself, and modern historians rely almost entirely on the Burmese chronicles — the Zatadawbon Yazawin, Maha Yazawin and Hmannan Yazawin — whose dates and details are treated with caution.
