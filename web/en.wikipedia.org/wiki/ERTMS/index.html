European Rail Traffic Management System

The European Rail Traffic Management System (ERTMS) is the system of standards for management and interoperation of signalling for railways by the European Union. It is conducted by the European Union Agency for Railways (ERA) and is the organisational umbrella for its principal components: GSM-R for voice and data communication, the European Train Control System (ETCS) for signalling and train protection, and traffic management layers.

Purpose

ERTMS was created to replace the many incompatible national train protection systems in Europe — historically more than twenty — so that trains can cross borders without changing locomotives or drivers and so that a competitive, interoperable railway market can develop. It is mandated for new high-speed and many conventional lines in the EU and is also adopted outside Europe, including in China, India, Australia, and Saudi Arabia.

Levels

ETCS is deployed in levels: Level 1 uses trackside signals supplemented by balises (Eurobalises); Level 2 uses continuous radio communication via GSM-R, allowing movement authorities to be displayed in the cab and lineside signals to be reduced or removed; Level 3, still largely conceptual, uses moving-block operation with train integrity confirmed on board. Baseline 3 of the specifications added features such as limited supervision modes and support for future radio migration to FRMCS.
