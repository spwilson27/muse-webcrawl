Kurchatov Institute

The Kurchatov Institute (Russian: Национальный исследовательский центр «Курчатовский институт», National Research Centre "Kurchatov Institute") is Russia's leading research and development institution in the field of nuclear energy and particle physics. Founded in 1943 in Moscow as the secret Laboratory No. 2 of the USSR Academy of Sciences, it was the birthplace of the Soviet atomic bomb project and of the first Soviet nuclear power reactor programme.

History

The institute was created in 1943 under Igor Kurchatov to develop Soviet nuclear weapons. Its F-1 reactor achieved the first self-sustaining nuclear chain reaction in Europe in December 1946, and the institute's work led to the first Soviet atomic bomb test (RDS-1) in 1949 and the first Soviet thermonuclear device in 1953. Kurchatov also directed the institute into peaceful applications: the world's first grid-connected nuclear power plant at Obninsk (1954) was planned under its auspices, as was the nuclear icebreaker Lenin.

The institute made fundamental contributions to controlled fusion: the tokamak concept was developed here under Lev Artsimovich, and the T-3 device produced landmark plasma results in the 1960s. After Kurchatov's death in 1960 the institute bore his name; Anatoly Alexandrov directed it from 1960 to 1988. In 1991 it became the Russian Research Centre "Kurchatov Institute" and in 2010 was reorganised as a national research centre, coordinating several other institutes and operating the Kurchatov synchrotron radiation source.

Research today

Current programmes include nuclear and thermonuclear energy, plasma physics, condensed matter physics, molecular physics, nanobiotechnology and cognitive sciences, as well as information technology and high-energy physics collaborations.
