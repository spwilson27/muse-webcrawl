Roth IRA

A Roth IRA is an individual retirement account under United States tax law, named after Senator William Roth. Contributions are made with after-tax dollars, but qualified withdrawals — including earnings — are tax-free.

History

Roth IRAs were established by the Taxpayer Relief Act of 1997 and first available in 1998. They contrast with a traditional individual retirement account, where contributions may be deductible but withdrawals are taxed.

Rules

Annual contribution limits apply and are set by the Internal Revenue Service, with additional catch-up contributions for those aged 50 and over. Eligibility phases out at higher incomes. Contributions (but not earnings) may be withdrawn at any time without tax or penalty; earnings may be withdrawn tax-free after age 59½ and after the account has been open five years. Roth IRAs have no required minimum distributions during the owner's lifetime.

Conversions

Funds from a traditional IRA may be converted to a Roth IRA, with tax due on the converted pre-tax amount.
