Lough Derg

Lough Derg is the name of two lakes in Ireland. The larger and better known is the third-largest lake on the island, on the River Shannon, bordered by counties Clare, Galway and Tipperary. The smaller Lough Derg in County Donegal is famous as the site of St Patrick's Purgatory, a pilgrimage destination for over a thousand years.

Lough Derg (Shannon)

The Shannon lake covers about 130 km² (13,000 ha), is roughly 35 km long, and lies at the point where the river widens before descending toward Limerick. Towns and villages on its shores include Killaloe/Ballina, Portumna, Terryglass, Mountshannon and Dromineer. It is popular for cruising, angling (pike, bream, trout), sailing and watersports, and its islands include Inis Cealtra (Holy Island) with early monastic remains. The lake forms part of the Shannon navigation and feeds the Ardnacrusha hydroelectric scheme via the headrace canal at Parteen.

Lough Derg (Donegal)

The Donegal lake, near the village of Pettigo, covers about 8.9 km². On Station Island stands St Patrick's Purgatory, where, according to tradition, St Patrick was shown an entrance to purgatory. The three-day pilgrimage — involving fasting, barefoot circuits of penitential "beds", and an all-night vigil — remains one of Europe's most austere surviving medieval pilgrimages, attracting thousands each summer season.

Ecology

The Shannon Lough Derg is a Special Protection Area for birds, supporting breeding terns and wintering wildfowl, though it suffers periodic algal blooms from nutrient enrichment.
