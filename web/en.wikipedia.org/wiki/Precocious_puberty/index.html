Precocious puberty

Precocious puberty is puberty occurring at an unusually early age: conventionally, the appearance of secondary sexual characteristics before age 8 in girls or age 9 in boys. In most cases — especially in girls — no cause is found (idiopathic), but early puberty can result from disorders of the brain, gonads or adrenal glands, or from exposure to external sex hormones, and it can compromise final adult height and psychological well-being.

Types and causes

Central precocious puberty results from early activation of the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis — the normal puberty machinery switched on too soon. It is far more common in girls and is usually idiopathic, though in boys it is more often caused by central nervous system lesions such as hypothalamic hamartomas or tumours. Peripheral precocious puberty arises from sex steroids produced independently of the axis — for example from ovarian or testicular tumours, adrenal disorders such as congenital adrenal hyperplasia, McCune–Albright syndrome, severe untreated hypothyroidism, or exogenous hormone exposure.

Evaluation and consequences

Work-up includes growth charts and bone-age X-rays (bone age is typically advanced), basal and GnRH-stimulated LH/FSH levels, sex steroid measurements, and brain MRI in central cases. Consequences of untreated precocious puberty include rapid early growth followed by premature fusion of the growth plates and short adult stature, early menarche, and psychosocial difficulties from developing out of step with peers. The reported incidence of puberty at earlier ages has risen in many countries, a trend linked in part to childhood obesity and debated environmental endocrine disruptors.

Treatment

Progressive central precocious puberty is treated with GnRH analogues (such as leuprolide or histrelin implants), which suppress the axis and pause pubertal development until an appropriate age. Peripheral forms are managed by treating the underlying cause — surgery for tumours, glucocorticoids for congenital adrenal hyperplasia, or agents that block steroid synthesis or action.
