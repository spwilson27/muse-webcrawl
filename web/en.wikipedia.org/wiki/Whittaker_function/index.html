Whittaker function

In mathematics, a Whittaker function is a special solution of Whittaker's equation, a modified form of the confluent hypergeometric equation introduced by E. T. Whittaker in 1903. The equation has the form:

d²w/dz² + ( -1/4 + κ/z + (1/4 - μ²)/z² ) w = 0

where κ and μ are parameters. The equation has a regular singular point at z = 0 and an irregular singular point at infinity.

Definitions

Two standard solutions are the Whittaker functions Mκ,μ(z) and Wκ,μ(z), expressible in terms of Kummer's confluent hypergeometric function M(a, b, z):

Mκ,μ(z) = e-z/2 zμ+1/2 M(1/2 + μ - κ, 1 + 2μ, z)

The function Wκ,μ(z) is defined by a specific linear combination of Mκ,μ and Mκ,-μ chosen so that it decays appropriately at infinity, and it can also be written using Tricomi's confluent hypergeometric function U.

Applications

Whittaker functions arise in mathematical physics as solutions of the radial Schrödinger equation for the Coulomb potential (hydrogen atom), in problems involving the Laplace equation in paraboloidal coordinates, and in the representation theory of Lie groups, where related Whittaker models and Whittaker vectors are named after the same mathematician.
