Overview

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS; CEDEAO in French) is a regional political and economic union of fifteen West African countries, established by the Treaty of Lagos in 1975 to promote economic integration — free movement of people (visa-free since 1979), a common market and eventually a single currency (the repeatedly postponed Eco). Its Commission and Parliament sit in Abuja, Nigeria, the bloc's giant (over half its population and GDP).

Politics and crisis

ECOWAS is unusual among regional blocs for its security role: its ECOMOG forces intervened in Liberia (1990) and Sierra Leone, in the Gambia (2017, enforcing an election result) and it has repeatedly suspended coup states — after the coups in Mali (2020/21), Guinea (2021), Burkina Faso (2022) and Niger (2023). The Niger coup triggered the bloc's gravest crisis when Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger formed the Alliance of Sahel States and announced withdrawal from ECOWAS in January 2024, initiating a transition dispute (extended to 2025 and beyond). The bloc's democratic credentials and integration depth remain in flux.
