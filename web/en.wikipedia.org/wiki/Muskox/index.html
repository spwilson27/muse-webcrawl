Muskox

The muskox (Ovibos moschatus) is a hoofed mammal of the family Bovidae native to the Arctic. It is noted for its thick coat and for the strong odour emitted by males during the mating season, from which its name derives. Despite its name and ox-like appearance, the muskox is more closely related to sheep and goats, being placed in the subfamily Caprinae.

Description

Muskoxen stand about 1.1 to 1.5 m (3.6 to 4.9 ft) high at the shoulder and weigh between 180 and 400 kg (400 and 880 lb). Both sexes have broad, curved horns that meet across the forehead in a "boss." Their long, shaggy outer coat of guard hairs covers a dense underlayer of fine wool called qiviut, which is among the warmest natural fibres and is shed each spring.

Behaviour and habitat

Muskoxen live in herds of 12–24 in the winter and smaller groups in summer, grazing on arctic plants such as willows, grasses and sedges. When threatened by predators such as wolves, the herd may form a defensive circle or line with calves inside and horns facing outward. During the rut in late summer, bulls compete in head-on charges after bellowing and scent-marking.

Distribution and conservation

Native populations survive in Arctic Canada and Greenland. Muskoxen were reintroduced to Alaska, Norway, Russia and Sweden during the twentieth century after earlier overhunting had eliminated them from much of their range. The species is listed as Least Concern, with a world population estimated at well over 100,000, although climate change and disease pose emerging threats.
