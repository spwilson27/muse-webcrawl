Georges Auguste Darzens (12 January 1867 – 10 September 1954) was a French organic chemist. He is best known for the Darzens reaction (also called the Darzens condensation), a method for preparing glycidic esters from ketones and alpha-chloroesters, which he reported in 1904.

Life and career

Darzens was born in Moscow, where his father was involved in trade, and was educated in France, studying at the École Polytechnique. He spent much of his career in industry, directing the research laboratories of the perfume house L.-T. Piver in Paris, while also teaching chemistry. His position in the fragrance industry shaped his interest in synthetic routes to odorant compounds.

The Darzens reaction

The Darzens reaction involves the condensation of a ketone or aldehyde with an alpha-chloroester in the presence of a base to give an alpha,beta-epoxy ester (a glycidic ester). Hydrolysis and decarboxylation of the product yields an aldehyde or ketone extended by one carbon, making the reaction a useful tool in synthesis, including in the preparation of fragrance and pharmaceutical intermediates.

Other work

Darzens also developed a method for replacing a hydroxyl group with chlorine using thionyl chloride in the presence of a tertiary amine, known as the Darzens halogenation. During the First World War he worked on chemical warfare agents and defences. He published a popular book on chemistry and remained active in the French chemical community into old age.
