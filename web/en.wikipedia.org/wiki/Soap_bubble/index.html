Soap bubble

A soap bubble is an extremely thin film of soapy water enclosing air that forms a hollow sphere with an iridescent surface. Soap bubbles usually last for only a few seconds before bursting, either on their own or on contact with another object. They are often used for children's enjoyment, but they are also used in artistic performances. Assembling several bubbles results in foam.

Physics

A soap film consists of a thin layer of water (or other liquid) sandwiched between two layers of soap molecules. The film seeks the shape of minimal surface area for the volume it encloses; for an isolated bubble this is a sphere, because a sphere has the smallest surface area for a given volume. The surface tension of the film gives the bubble its elasticity. When bubbles meet, they share walls and form angles of 120 degrees, in accordance with Plateau's laws.

Colour

The iridescent colours of a soap bubble arise from thin-film interference: light waves reflecting from the front and back surfaces of the film interfere constructively or destructively depending on the film's thickness and the wavelength of light. As the film thins by drainage and evaporation, the colours change, and when the film becomes thinner than the wavelength of visible light it appears black just before it bursts.

Formation and stability

Bubbles can be blown through a wand dipped in a solution of soap or detergent and water, often with glycerin added to slow evaporation and strengthen the film. Detergents lower the surface tension of water from about 0.072 N/m to roughly a third of that, making films easier to form. Large and long-lived bubbles can be produced with specialised mixtures and giant wands.

In science and art

Soap films have long been used as physical analogues for minimal-surface problems in mathematics; the study of soap films inspired work by Joseph Plateau and later contributions to calculus of variations. Giant bubble displays are a popular form of street performance, and bubble artists can create elaborate structures such as bubbles within bubbles.
