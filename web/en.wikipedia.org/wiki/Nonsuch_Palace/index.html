Nonsuch Palace

Nonsuch Palace
Location: Surrey, England
Built: 1538 – c. 1547
Commissioned by: Henry VIII
Demolished: 1682–83

Nonsuch Palace was a Tudor royal palace built by Henry VIII near Cheam, Surrey, England. Its name announced its ambition: there was to be "none such" — no palace to rival it. Construction began on 22 April 1538, the 29th anniversary of Henry's accession, on the site of the demolished village and church of Cuddington.

Design

The palace was arranged around two courtyards. The outer court was of stone; the inner court was timber-framed and lavishly decorated with carved and gilded slate and stucco panels depicting gods, heroes, and classical scenes, crowned by octagonal towers with onion domes. Foreign craftsmen, including the Italian artist Toto del Nunziata, worked on the decoration. Unlike most of Henry's projects, Nonsuch was built from scratch as a hunting lodge and celebration of Tudor power.

Later history

Henry died in 1547 before the palace was fully complete. Mary I sold it to the Earl of Arundel, who finished it; Elizabeth I later reacquired it and stayed often. Charles II gave Nonsuch to his mistress Barbara, Countess of Castlemaine (created Duchess of Cleveland), who had it demolished in 1682–83 to pay gambling debts, selling the materials. Excavations in 1959–60, led by Martin Biddle, revealed the palace's plan and produced important finds of glass, pottery, and decorative fragments; the site lies in Nonsuch Park.
