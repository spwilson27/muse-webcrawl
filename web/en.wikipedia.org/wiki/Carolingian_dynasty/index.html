The Carolingian dynasty was a Frankish noble family named after Charles Martel and his grandson Charlemagne. The family rose to power as hereditary mayors of the palace under the Merovingian kings, and in 751 Pepin the Short deposed the last Merovingian and took the throne himself. Under Charlemagne (r. 768–814) the Carolingians ruled an empire covering most of Western and Central Europe, and in 800 he was crowned Emperor by the Pope.

History

The dynasty's power grew from the union of the Austrasian families of the Arnulfings and Pippinids. Charles Martel won fame for halting the Umayyad advance at the Battle of Tours in 732. After Charlemagne's death, his empire was divided among his grandsons by the Treaty of Verdun (843) into West, Middle and East Francia. The Carolingians continued to rule in East Francia until 911 and in West Francia, with interruptions, until 987, when Louis V died and Hugh Capet founded the Capetian dynasty.

Carolingian Renaissance

Carolingian rule sponsored a revival of learning and culture known as the Carolingian Renaissance: monasteries copied classical texts, a standardised script (Carolingian minuscule) was developed, and scholars such as Alcuin of York reformed education at court.
