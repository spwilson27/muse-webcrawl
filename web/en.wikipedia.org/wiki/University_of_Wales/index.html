The University of Wales (Welsh: Prifysgol Cymru) was a confederal university founded in 1893 as a federal institution for Wales, modeled on the University of London. For most of its existence it was the degree-awarding body for its constituent colleges at Aberystwyth, Bangor, Cardiff, Swansea, and elsewhere, and generations of Welsh graduates received University of Wales degrees.

History

The federal university grew from the 19th-century movement for Welsh higher education, which had produced university colleges at Aberystwyth (1872), Cardiff (1883), and Bangor (1884). Its charter united these colleges, and it later included institutions such as the Welsh School of Medicine, Lampeter, and Newport.

Decline and merger

From the 1990s the member institutions progressively gained their own degree-awarding powers, and Cardiff University left the federation in 2005. In 2011, following a scandal over the validation of degrees at overseas partner colleges, the university announced it would cease validating external degrees and merge with the University of Wales Trinity Saint David. The merger was completed in 2017, bringing the federal university's independent existence to an end, though the University of Wales Press and the Gregynog conference centre continue its legacy.

Legacy

The University of Wales was a central institution of Welsh national life: its degrees were a rite of passage for Welsh professionals, and its press remains a major publisher of Welsh scholarship.
