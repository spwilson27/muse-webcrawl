Naukograd

A naukograd (Russian: наукоград, literally "science city") is a city or town in Russia with a high concentration of research and development facilities, officially recognised under Russian federal law. The term echoes the planned science towns built in the Soviet Union, many devoted to a single institute or industry.

Background

During the Soviet period, purpose-built scientific settlements grew around major research institutes — examples include Akademgorodok near Novosibirsk, Dubna (nuclear research), Zhukovsky (aviation), Obninsk (nuclear energy), and Korolyov (space flight). Many had privileged supply arrangements and some were closed cities because of defence-related work.

Legal status

The Russian federal law "On the Status of a Science City of the Russian Federation" (1999) formalised the designation. A municipality may be granted naukograd status by presidential decree, typically for a renewable term, when a large share of its employment and output is tied to a scientific-production complex of national significance. Status brings targeted federal funding for infrastructure and research. Among the first towns formally granted the status were Obninsk (2000) and Dubna and Korolyov (2001); others include Koltsovo, Pushchino, Chernogolovka, Fryazino, Michurinsk, Biysk, and Peterhof.

Purpose

The programme aims to preserve Soviet-era scientific communities through the economic transition, support innovation and high-technology production, and slow the loss of specialists abroad.
