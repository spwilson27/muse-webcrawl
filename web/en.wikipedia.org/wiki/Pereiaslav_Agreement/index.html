Pereiaslav Agreement

Signed: January 1654

Location: Pereiaslav, Ukraine

Parties: Zaporozhian Host (Bohdan Khmelnytsky); Tsardom of Russia (Vasily Buturlin)

Context: Khmelnytsky Uprising

The Pereiaslav Agreement (also called the Treaty of Pereiaslav) was concluded in January 1654 between the Cossack Hetmanate of Bohdan Khmelnytsky and the Tsardom of Russia. A council (rada) of Cossack officers and townspeople at Pereiaslav swore allegiance to Tsar Alexis of Russia, and the terms were subsequently set out in the March Articles negotiated in Moscow.

Background

Since 1648 Khmelnytsky had led a major uprising against the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, creating a Cossack polity in Ukraine. Seeking a powerful ally against Poland, he turned to the Orthodox tsar. In exchange for military support, the Hetmanate accepted the tsar's suzerainty while retaining its own administration, courts, finances and an army of 60,000 registered Cossacks.

Interpretation and legacy

What the agreement actually created has been disputed ever since: Russian and Soviet historiography traditionally presented it as the "reunification of Ukraine with Russia", whereas many Ukrainian historians regard it as a limited military alliance or protectorate whose terms Russia progressively violated as Cossack autonomy was eroded over the following century. The 300th anniversary in 1954 was marked by the Soviet transfer of Crimea to the Ukrainian SSR, and the agreement remains a contested symbol in Russian–Ukrainian relations.
