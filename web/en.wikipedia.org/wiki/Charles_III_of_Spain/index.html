Charles III of Spain

Charles III (Spanish: Carlos III; 20 January 1716 – 14 December 1788) was King of Spain from 1759 until his death. Before ascending the Spanish throne he reigned as Duke of Parma (as Charles I, 1731–1735) and as King of Naples and Sicily (as Charles VII of Naples and Charles III of Sicily, 1734–1759). He is remembered as a leading enlightened absolutist whose "Bourbon Reforms" modernised the Spanish monarchy and its American empire.

Italian years

The eldest son of Philip V and Elisabeth Farnese, Charles was sent to Italy to claim his mother's inheritances. He became Duke of Parma in 1731 and, during the War of the Polish Succession, conquered Naples and Sicily in 1734. As king in Naples he proved an active reformer, modernising administration, curbing the power of the Church, and patronising the excavations at Herculaneum and Pompeii and the building of the Palace of Caserta.

Reign in Spain

Succeeding his half-brother Ferdinand VI in 1759, Charles brought experienced Italian ministers to Spain and pursued wide-ranging reforms with advisers such as Esquilache, Aranda, Campomanes and Floridablanca. Spain entered the Seven Years' War against Britain under the Bourbon Family Compact, losing Florida but gaining Louisiana. Domestically he liberalised trade with the colonies (the comercio libre regulations of 1778), reorganised colonial government through intendancies, expelled the Jesuits from the Spanish world in 1767, promoted agriculture, industry and public works, and embellished Madrid — earning the nickname "the best mayor of Madrid". The Esquilache Riots of 1766, sparked by a clothing decree, briefly forced him to dismiss his minister and moderate his programme.

Legacy

Historians generally regard Charles III as the most successful Spanish monarch of the eighteenth century: the economy and administration of the empire were strengthened, though the reforms also tightened colonial control in ways that fed later independence movements. His reign is the high point of enlightened despotism in Spain.
