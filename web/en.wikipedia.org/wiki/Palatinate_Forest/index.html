The Palatinate Forest (German: Pfälzerwald) is a low-mountain region in southwestern Germany, in the south of the state of Rhineland-Palatinate. With about 1,771 square kilometres of almost continuous woodland, it is the largest contiguous forest area in Germany. Together with the northern Vosges in France it forms the transboundary Palatinate Forest–North Vosges Biosphere Reserve, recognised by UNESCO.

Geography and geology

The forest occupies the bunter sandstone highlands between the Rhine plain to the east and the Westrich plateau to the west, extending south to the French border. Its characteristic scenery includes deeply incised valleys, table mountains, and bizarre weathered red sandstone crags and rock towers — especially in the Dahner Felsenland in the south — many crowned by ruined medieval castles such as Trifels, where Richard the Lionheart was held in 1193, and Berwartstein.

Nature and recreation

About a third of the forest is deciduous, the rest coniferous and mixed forest; the region is a habitat for the reintroduced Eurasian lynx under a Franco-German reintroduction programme. A dense network of hiking trails, maintained by the Palatinate Forest Club (founded 1902), and numerous hikers' huts make it a popular walking area; the highest summit is the Kalmit (672.6 m) near Neustadt an der Weinstraße.
