AlphaGo

AlphaGo is a computer program developed by DeepMind Technologies (later Google DeepMind) that plays the board game Go. In March 2016 it defeated Lee Sedol, one of the world's strongest players, in a five-game match by four games to one — the first time a computer Go program had beaten a 9-dan professional without handicaps on a full-sized 19×19 board. Go had been considered a grand challenge for artificial intelligence because of its enormous search space and reliance on intuition.

How it works

AlphaGo combines Monte Carlo tree search with two deep neural networks trained on human expert games and refined by reinforcement learning from self-play: a "policy network" that selects promising moves and a "value network" that evaluates board positions. This architecture dramatically reduced the effective search depth and breadth compared with brute-force programs.

Matches and legacy

AlphaGo first defeated European champion Fan Hui 5–0 in October 2015 (announced January 2016 in Nature). The Lee Sedol match in Seoul drew worldwide attention; Lee won the fourth game after AlphaGo made errors late in the game, while AlphaGo's "Move 37" in game two became famous as a creative, apparently inhuman move. In May 2017 AlphaGo beat world number one Ke Jie 3–0 at the Future of Go Summit in Wuzhen, after which DeepMind retired the system. Its successors — AlphaGo Zero, which learned solely by self-play, and AlphaZero, which mastered chess and shogi with the same method — became landmark results in reinforcement learning.
