Nursing home

A nursing home is a residential facility that provides a high level of long-term medical care for people who are unable to live independently, typically elderly people or those with chronic illness or disability. In the United States, nursing homes are often called skilled nursing facilities (SNFs) when they provide rehabilitation and round-the-clock nursing care.

Services

Nursing homes employ registered nurses, licensed practical nurses, and nursing assistants who provide care such as medication administration, wound care, physical therapy, occupational therapy, and assistance with activities of daily living including bathing, dressing, eating, and mobility. Many facilities have specialised units for residents with dementia or Alzheimer's disease, as well as hospice and palliative care services.

Regulation and funding

In many countries nursing homes are licensed and inspected by government agencies. In the United States, facilities that accept Medicare or Medicaid funding must meet federal standards established under the Nursing Home Reform Act of 1987, which set out residents' rights and quality-of-care requirements. Standards of care, staffing levels, and costs vary widely between countries and facilities.
