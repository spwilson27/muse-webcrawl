Potager du roi

The Potager du roi (Kitchen Garden of the King) is the historic fruit and vegetable garden of the Palace of Versailles, created between 1678 and 1683 by Jean-Baptiste de La Quintinie under Louis XIV. Covering about 9 hectares (22 acres) near the palace, it supplied the royal court with out-of-season produce and remains a working garden and a masterpiece of French formal garden design, today managed by the École nationale supérieure de paysage (National School of Landscape Architecture).

Design

The garden is organized as a sunken central square — the "Grand Carré," subdivided into sixteen smaller squares around a central fountain basin — ringed by terraces and high walls. Some thirty walled enclosures ("jardins" or rooms) shelter espaliered fruit trees against the wind and create microclimates of differing warmth. Because the site was originally marshy, the ground was drained and raised with soil excavated from the nearby Pièce d'Eau des Suisses.

La Quintinie's horticulture

La Quintinie, author of the classic Instruction pour les jardins fruitiers et potagers (1690), perfected pruning and espalier techniques and built glass cloches, frames, and heated beds that produced asparagus in January, strawberries in March, and melons and figs far outside their seasons — luxuries prized at court; Louis XIV delighted in showing the garden to ambassadors. Over 300 varieties of fruit and vegetables are still grown, including many heritage varieties.

Modern use

Since 1976 the site has housed the national landscape school, whose students maintain the garden as a teaching laboratory; produce is sold to visitors, and the garden hosts horticultural heritage conservation, including a collection of some 450 fruit varieties.
