The Tenant of Wildfell Hall

Author: Anne Brontë (as Acton Bell)

Published: June 1848

Publisher: Thomas Cautley Newby

Genre: Novel, epistolary

The Tenant of Wildfell Hall is the second and final novel by the English author Anne Brontë, published in June 1848 under her pseudonym Acton Bell. It tells the story of Helen Graham, a mysterious young widow who takes up residence at the decaying Wildfell Hall with her small son and a servant, provoking gossip in the local community. The novel is structured as a series of letters from the farmer Gilbert Markham, enclosing Helen's diary.

Plot

Helen's diary recounts her disastrous marriage to Arthur Huntingdon, a charming but dissolute and alcoholic husband whose cruelty eventually drives her to leave him — taking their son and supporting herself by painting — in defiance of the law and social convention of the time. When Huntingdon falls gravely ill, Helen returns to nurse him, seeking his repentance; after his death she is free, and eventually marries Gilbert Markham.

Themes and reception

The novel is a frank portrayal of alcoholism, marital breakdown, and a woman's determination to protect her child and her own moral integrity. Its depiction of a wife leaving her husband was considered shocking and even "coarse" by many contemporary reviewers; Charlotte Brontë, after Anne's death, suppressed its republication, calling the choice of subject a mistake. Modern criticism, particularly feminist scholarship, has reclaimed it as one of the first sustained feminist novels in English.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted for television by the BBC, notably in 1968 and in a well-regarded 1996 serial starring Tara Fitzgerald as Helen.
