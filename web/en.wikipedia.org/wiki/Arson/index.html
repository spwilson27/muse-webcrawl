Arson

Arson is the crime of wilfully and deliberately setting fire to or charring property. Although the act of arson typically involves buildings, the term can also refer to the intentional burning of other things, such as motor vehicles, watercraft, or forests. The crime is typically classified as a felony, with instances involving a greater degree of risk to human life or property carrying a stricter penalty. Arson that results in death can be further prosecuted as manslaughter or murder.

Definitions and law

Common law

At common law, arson was defined as the malicious burning of the dwelling of another. Modern statutes have expanded the offence to cover many kinds of property and to include causing fires by explosion. Most jurisdictions distinguish degrees of arson according to whether the property was occupied and the value of the damage.

United States

In the United States, arson is prosecuted under both federal and state law. Federal arson statutes cover fires damaging property used in interstate commerce or property of the federal government. State laws typically grade arson in degrees; first-degree arson usually involves an occupied structure or danger to persons.

England and Wales

In England and Wales, arson is charged under the Criminal Damage Act 1971 as criminal damage by fire. Aggravated offences — where life is endangered — carry a maximum sentence of life imprisonment.

Motives

Investigators and criminologists commonly group arson motives into categories including vandalism, excitement, revenge, crime concealment, profit (such as insurance fraud), and extremism. A small proportion of arsons are associated with pyromania, an impulse-control disorder involving fascination with fire.

Investigation

Arson investigation combines fire science with forensic techniques. Investigators determine the fire's origin and cause, look for accelerant residues (often with the help of detection dogs and laboratory analysis), and eliminate accidental causes before concluding a fire was incendiary.

Notable cases

The Reichstag fire of 1933 in Berlin, blamed on a Dutch communist, was used by the Nazi regime to justify emergency decrees. In 2019, the Kyoto Animation arson attack in Japan killed 36 people and became one of the country's deadliest mass killings.
