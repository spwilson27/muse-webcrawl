SURF (Speeded Up Robust Features) is a patented local feature detector and descriptor algorithm for computer vision, presented by Herbert Bay, Tinne Tuytelaars and Luc Van Gool at the 2006 European Conference on Computer Vision. It was designed as a faster alternative to the earlier SIFT algorithm, while retaining robustness to changes in scale, rotation, illumination and viewpoint.

Algorithm

SURF detects interest points using an approximation of the determinant of the Hessian matrix, computed very efficiently with integral images and box filters. Scale space is explored by increasing the filter size rather than by repeatedly subsampling the image. For each detected keypoint, an orientation is assigned from Haar wavelet responses in a circular neighbourhood, and a descriptor (typically 64 dimensions) is built from the distributions of horizontal and vertical Haar wavelet responses in a square region aligned to that orientation. Matching descriptors between images by nearest-neighbour search then supports tasks such as object recognition, image registration, stitching and 3D reconstruction.

Use and licensing

SURF was integrated into libraries such as OpenCV, where for many years it lived in the "non-free" module because of patent restrictions, in contrast to the free SIFT implementation after that patent expired in 2020. Despite the licensing situation, SURF saw wide academic and industrial use and remains a standard baseline in feature-matching research.
