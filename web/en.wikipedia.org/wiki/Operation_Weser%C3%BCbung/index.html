Operation Weserübung

Operation Weserübung
Part of World War II
Date: 9 April – 10 June 1940
Location: Denmark, Norway
Result: German victory; occupation of Denmark and Norway

Operation Weserübung (German: Unternehmen Weserübung) was the German plan for the invasion of Denmark and Norway during the Second World War. The operation was named after the Weser River in northern Germany. The assault began on 9 April 1940 and marked the opening phase of the Norwegian Campaign.

Background

Germany depended heavily on Swedish iron ore shipped through the Norwegian port of Narvik, and the Allies had themselves considered landing troops in Norway. To pre-empt British action and secure the northern flank, German planners combined a rapid seizure of Denmark with a seaborne and airborne invasion of Norway.

The invasion of Denmark

German forces crossed the Danish border in the early hours of 9 April 1940, supported by the world's first airborne assault on airfields at Aalborg. Facing overwhelming force, the Danish government capitulated after only a few hours of fighting.

The invasion of Norway

Simultaneous landings were made at Narvik, Trondheim, Bergen, Kristiansand and Oslo. The sinking of the heavy cruiser Blücher in the Oslofjord delayed the occupation of the capital, allowing the Norwegian king and government to escape. Allied counter-landings followed, but German air superiority and the Allied withdrawal to fight in France led to Norway's capitulation on 10 June 1940.

Aftermath

Denmark and Norway remained under German occupation until 1945. The operation secured Germany's iron ore supply but cost the Kriegsmarine much of its surface fleet.
