Cortese

Cortese is a white Italian wine grape variety predominantly grown in the southeastern region of Piedmont, in the provinces of Alessandria and Asti. It is the grape behind the DOCG wine Gavi (Cortese di Gavi), and is also used in the DOC wines Colli Tortonesi and Cortese dell'Alto Monferrato.

Characteristics

Cortese is known for producing crisp, dry wines with high natural acidity and notes of lime, green apple, pear, almond and white flowers. Despite the hot summers of Piedmont, the grape retains its acidity, which makes it well suited to fresh, early-drinking white wines. DNA profiling suggests Cortese is distinct from similarly named varieties and it has been cultivated in the Alessandria hills for several centuries; the variety was already documented in the 17th century, and the wines of Gavi were celebrated at the court of Princess Cortese of Gavi, from whom the grape may take its name.

Synonyms

Cortese is also known under synonyms including Bianca Fernanda, Corteis, Cortese Bianca, Cortese d'Asti, Cortese dell'Astigliano, Fernanda Bianca, Ravanera, and Seu.
