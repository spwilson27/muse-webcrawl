Von Economo neuron

Von Economo neurons (VENs), also called spindle neurons, are a specific class of mammalian cortical neurons characterised by a large spindle-shaped soma (cell body) that tapers gradually into a single apical axon in one direction, with only a single dendrite facing opposite. They are named after the Austrian neurologist Constantin von Economo, who described them in 1929.

Distribution

VENs are found in restricted brain regions: the anterior cingulate cortex and the frontoinsular cortex (frontoinsula). They were initially reported only in humans and other great apes, but have since been found in several other large-brained, highly social mammals, including elephants, some whales and dolphins, and macaques. Their concentration in great apes and their presence in species with large brains and complex social behaviour suggest convergent evolution.

Function

Because of their large size and long axons, VENs are thought to support fast, long-distance communication between the frontoinsula and anterior cingulate — regions involved in self-awareness, empathy, emotion, error monitoring and social cognition. In humans they develop mostly after birth and increase in number during early childhood.

Clinical significance

VENs are selectively affected in behavioural variant frontotemporal dementia, in which early and severe loss of VENs and fork cells is found, correlating with loss of empathy and social conduct. Abnormalities in VEN number or distribution have also been reported in studies of autism and schizophrenia.
