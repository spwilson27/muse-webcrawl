Letter frequency

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Letter frequency is the number of times the letters of an alphabet appear on average in written language. The analysis of letter frequencies is a fundamental technique in cryptanalysis — frequency analysis allows a codebreaker to attack simple substitution ciphers by matching the most common symbols in a ciphertext to the most common letters of the underlying language. Letter frequencies also influenced practical technologies, from the arrangement of type cases to the distribution of tiles in Scrabble and the order of letters in games and keyboard design.

English letter frequencies

In English, the letter e is by far the most common, followed by t and a. A commonly cited analysis (based on a large corpus sample) gives approximate frequencies:

Frequencies vary depending on the corpus analysed, but the overall ordering is fairly stable. The twelve most frequent letters (e, t, a, o, i, n, s, h, r, d, l, u) account for roughly 80 percent of English text. The mnemonic order "ETAOIN SHRDLU" reflects the approximate frequency ranking and was famously the order of the first two rows of keys on Linotype typesetting machines.

Applications

Beyond cryptanalysis, letter frequencies are used in data compression (Huffman coding assigns shorter codes to frequent letters), stenography, information theory, and text prediction. Samuel Morse assigned the shortest Morse code symbols to the most frequent English letters — a single dot for e — after examining printers' type cases. Frequencies also differ sharply between languages, which lets software identify the language of a text from a short sample.
