Santos Basin

The Santos Basin (Portuguese: Bacia de Santos) is a large sedimentary basin offshore southeastern Brazil, extending for roughly 350,000 square kilometres beneath the Atlantic Ocean off the states of Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, Paraná, and Santa Catarina. It is one of Brazil's most important petroleum provinces.

Geology

The basin formed during the Early Cretaceous rifting that opened the South Atlantic as Gondwana broke apart. Its fill comprises rift-phase lacustrine deposits, a thick layer of Aptian evaporites (salt), and post-salt marine sediments. Hydrocarbons generated in pre-salt source rocks are trapped in microbialite and coquina reservoirs beneath the salt layer, which acts as a seal.

Pre-salt discoveries

In 2006–2007, Petrobras and partners announced giant oil discoveries in the pre-salt layer, most famously the Tupi field (later renamed Lula), among the largest oil discoveries of the decade worldwide. Subsequent finds, including Búzios — now one of the world's largest producing deepwater fields — transformed Brazil into a top-ten oil producer. Development requires wells drilled through more than 2,000 metres of water and up to 5,000 metres of rock and salt, produced via floating production, storage and offloading (FPSO) vessels.
