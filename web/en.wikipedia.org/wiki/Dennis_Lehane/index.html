Dennis Lehane (born August 4, 1965) is an American author best known for his crime novels set in Boston, several of which have been adapted into acclaimed films. His work combines the private-eye tradition with an unsparing portrait of working-class Boston neighbourhoods, especially Dorchester, where he grew up.

Career

Lehane's first novel, A Drink Before the War (1994), introduced Boston private investigators Patrick Kenzie and Angela Gennaro, who recur in five further novels including Gone, Baby, Gone (1998). His standalone novel Mystic River (2001) became a bestseller and was adapted by Clint Eastwood into an Academy Award-winning film in 2003. Shutter Island (2003), a psychological thriller set in a hospital for the criminally insane, was filmed by Martin Scorsese in 2010; Scorsese also directed the adaptation of Lehane's short work, and Ben Affleck directed Gone, Baby, Gone (2007) and Live by Night (2016), based on Lehane's Prohibition-era novel.

Later work

Lehane's historical novel The Given Day (2008) is set around the Boston police strike of 1919 and began the Coughlin family trilogy, continued in Live by Night (2012) and World Gone By (2015). Later novels include Since We Fell (2017) and Small Mercies (2023), set amid Boston's 1974 school desegregation crisis. He has also written for television, including episodes of HBO's The Wire and Boardwalk Empire, and served as a writer and producer on the series Mr. Mercedes and Black Bird.

Recognition

Lehane has won the Shamus Award, the Anthony Award, and the Barry Award, and his books have been translated into more than 30 languages.
