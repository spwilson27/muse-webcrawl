Ehresmann connection

In the mathematical field of differential geometry, an Ehresmann connection (after Charles Ehresmann, who first formalized this concept) is a version of the notion of a connection, which makes sense on any smooth fiber bundle. In particular, it does not rely on the possible vector bundle structure of the underlying fiber bundle, but nevertheless, linear connections may be viewed as a special case. Another important special case of Ehresmann connections are principal connections on principal bundles, and these are required to be equivariant in the principal bundle action.

Definition

A fiber bundle with structure group G and manifold M may be defined as a smooth surjective submersion π: E → M locally trivial with fiber F, that is, every point of M has an open neighbourhood U such that there exists a homeomorphism π⁻¹(U) → U × F, called a local trivialization. An Ehresmann connection on the fiber bundle π: E → M is a choice of a smooth distribution H on E, called the horizontal distribution, such that at each point e of E, the tangent space TeE splits as a direct sum of the vertical space Ve (the tangent space to the fiber through e) and the horizontal space He.

Parallel transport

Given a curve γ: [0,1] → M and a point e ∈ π⁻¹(γ(0)) in the fiber above the initial point of the curve, an Ehresmann connection allows one to define a horizontal lift of γ through e: a curve γ̃: [0,1] → E such that π ∘ γ̃ = γ, γ̃(0) = e, and γ̃′(t) is horizontal for all t. When such lifts exist for all curves, the connection is said to be complete, and it induces a parallel transport map between fibers.

Curvature

The curvature of an Ehresmann connection measures the failure of the horizontal distribution to be integrable. By the Frobenius theorem, the horizontal distribution is integrable if and only if the connection is flat, i.e. has vanishing curvature. The curvature may be expressed as a two-form with values in the vertical bundle, and in the theory of principal bundles it is closely related to the curvature form of a connection.
