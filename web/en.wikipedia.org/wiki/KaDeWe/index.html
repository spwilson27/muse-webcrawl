The Kaufhaus des Westens ("Department Store of the West"), usually abbreviated to KaDeWe, is a department store in Berlin, Germany. With around 60,000 square metres of selling space and more than 380,000 articles available, it is the largest department store in continental Europe, and one of Berlin's landmarks, standing on Tauentzienstraße at Wittenbergplatz in the Schöneberg district.

History

KaDeWe was founded by the merchant Adolf Jandorf and designed by architect Emil Schaudt; it opened on 27 March 1907 with 24,000 m² of space. In 1927 ownership passed to the Hermann Tietz group (Hertie); under Nazi rule the Jewish-owned company was "Aryanised" and its owners driven out. During the Second World War an American bomber crashed into the building in 1943, and most of the store burned down.

The store reopened in 1950 and was fully rebuilt by 1956, becoming a showcase of West Berlin's prosperity during the Cold War and a symbol of the "economic miracle". After reunification it was expanded again. A major burglary of the jewellery department in 2009 and the spectacular twilight robbery of the KaDeWe vault in 2023 made international headlines. The store's operator, the KaDeWe Group, passed through several owners — including Signa Holding and the Thai Central Group — with the company's 2024 insolvency proceedings following the collapse of Signa.

The store

KaDeWe has eight floors, each devoted to a category, from the ground-floor luxury hall and beauty department to fashion, accessories and homewares. Its sixth floor, Die Sechste, is one of the world's largest delicatessen departments, with some 110 chefs, 40 gourmet stands and two kilometres of food counters; the winter garden restaurant on the seventh floor overlooks Wittenbergplatz. The store receives tens of thousands of visitors daily, a substantial share of them tourists.
