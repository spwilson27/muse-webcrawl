Wealth management

Wealth management is an investment advisory and financial-planning service that combines investment management, tax guidance, estate planning, legal advice, and other financial services for affluent clients, typically high-net-worth individuals and families. A wealth manager (or a team) coordinates all aspects of a client's financial life, often acting as a single point of contact with the client's bankers, accountants, and attorneys.

Services

Services commonly include portfolio construction and asset allocation, retirement and tax planning, philanthropy and charitable-giving strategies, succession and estate planning, insurance, and family-governance advice. In private banking and family offices, wealth management may be bundled with concierge-style services. Fees are usually charged as a percentage of assets under management, though flat-fee and performance-based models exist.

Industry

The field is served by large banks' private wealth divisions (e.g., UBS, Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan), independent registered investment advisors, multi-family offices, and increasingly by digital "robo-advisor" platforms offering scaled-down versions to mass-affluent clients. Wealth managers are often subject to a fiduciary standard, depending on jurisdiction and registration.
