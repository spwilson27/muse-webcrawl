Candle in the Wind

"Candle in the Wind" is a song with music by Elton John and lyrics by Bernie Taupin. It was originally written in 1973 in honour of Marilyn Monroe, who had died 11 years earlier, and appeared on John's album Goodbye Yellow Brick Road.

Original version

The song opens with the line "Goodbye, Norma Jeane", referring to Monroe by her birth name. Its lyrics describe fame and a life cut short. A live version released as a single in 1987 reached the top ten in several countries.

1997 version

In 1997, following the death of Diana, Princess of Wales, Taupin rewrote the lyrics as "Goodbye England's rose", and John performed it at Diana's funeral. Released as a single, "Candle in the Wind 1997" became one of the best-selling singles of all time worldwide, with proceeds going to Diana's charities.

Legacy

The song is one of John's best-known works, and the metaphor of a candle in the wind has come to symbolise a fragile, celebrated life extinguished early.
