List of historical horses

This is a list of individual horses famous in history — warhorses of kings and generals, celebrated racehorses of earlier centuries, and horses otherwise notable in fact and legend. (Famous modern Thoroughbred racehorses are listed separately.)

Warhorses and horses of rulers

HorseAssociated with / notes
BucephalusHorse of Alexander the Great; tamed by the young Alexander and ridden through his campaigns until its death after the Battle of the Hydaspes (326 BC); the city of Bucephala was founded in its honour
IncitatusFavourite racehorse of Caligula; Roman tradition (probably embellished) says the emperor planned to make him consul
MengoLegendary horse of the Armenian hero David of Sassoun
MarengoWarhorse of Napoleon Bonaparte, named after the Battle of Marengo; carried Napoleon in many battles, including Austerlitz, Jena and Waterloo
CopenhagenWarhorse of the Duke of Wellington at Waterloo and afterwards
VizirOne of Napoleon's horses; his stuffed body is preserved at the Musée de l'Armée, Paris
ComancheU.S. Cavalry horse, the only member of Custer's detachment found alive by some accounts at the Little Bighorn (1876); celebrated as the battle's "sole survivor"
TravellerConfederate general Robert E. Lee's horse during the American Civil War
Little SorrelStonewall Jackson's horse; preserved and displayed at the Virginia Military Institute
CincinnatiUlysses S. Grant's favourite horse during the Civil War
Charger / various: "Old Whitey"Zachary Taylor's horse
Sergeant RecklessMongolian mare purchased by U.S. Marines; carried ammunition under fire in the Korean War (1953) and was promoted to staff sergeant
WarriorBritish cavalry horse that served throughout the First World War on the Western Front and survived; called "the horse the Germans could not kill"

RakhshLegendary horse of the Persian hero Rostam in the Shahnameh
BabiecaWarhorse of El Cid (Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar)
BayardLegendary magical bay horse of the chansons de geste, given to Renaud de Montauban
Al-BuraqThe winged steed of Islamic tradition that carried Muhammad on the Night Journey
SleipnirOdin's eight-legged horse in Norse mythology
Black Jack context — riderless horseThe riderless horse with reversed boots in the stirrups, a tradition in U.S. military funerals; Black Jack served in that role at many state funerals, including President Kennedy's
Kan-i-Gul / "Chetak"Chetak: horse of Maharana Pratap, famed for carrying his wounded rider to safety at the Battle of Haldighati (1576)
Ashva / UchchaihshravasSeven-headed flying horse of Hindu mythology
Red Hare (Chitu)Famous horse of the late Han dynasty warlords Dong Zhuo, Lü Bu, and Guan Yu in Chinese history and the Romance of the Three Kingdoms
Dilu (Hex Mark)Horse that saved Liu Bei by leaping the Tan Stream in Romance of the Three Kingdoms tradition
White Horse of Hanover / Palio horsesHorses of the Palio di Siena races in Siena, Italy, treated as the true competitors of the race

Celebrated racehorses of earlier centuries

HorseNotes
Eclipse18th-century British Thoroughbred (1764–1789), undefeated; "Eclipse first, the rest nowhere"; ancestor of the great majority of modern Thoroughbreds through the Darley Arabian line
Byerley Turk, Darley Arabian, Godolphin ArabianThe three foundation sires of the Thoroughbred breed (late 17th–early 18th century)
DiomedWinner of the first Epsom Derby (1780); exported to America and became a foundation sire there
Lexington19th-century American racehorse and the leading sire in North America a record 16 times
KingstonAmerican horse with 89 career wins, a record for a Thoroughbred
Phar LapAustralian champion of the Depression era (1926–1932); his sudden death in California remains a national legend
SeabiscuitAmerican champion (1933–1947), underdog hero of the Depression; subject of the book and film Seabiscuit
Man o' WarAmerican Thoroughbred (1917–1947), widely regarded as one of the greatest racehorses of all time; won 20 of 21 starts
War AdmiralSon of Man o' War; U.S. Triple Crown winner 1937
CitationU.S. Triple Crown winner 1948; first horse to earn $1 million
SecretariatU.S. Triple Crown winner 1973, setting records in all three races that still stand
Red RumBritish steeplechaser; won the Grand National three times (1973, 1974, 1977)
ArkleIrish steeplechaser of the 1960s, among the highest-rated of all time
Northern DancerCanadian-bred Kentucky Derby winner (1964) and the dominant sire of the late 20th century
FrankelBritish Thoroughbred (2008– ), undefeated in 14 starts and the highest-rated horse of the modern era
WinxAustralian mare (2011– ), winner of 37 races including 25 Group Ones and four Cox Plates
Old BillyBarge horse who died in 1822 aged 62 — the oldest horse reliably recorded
