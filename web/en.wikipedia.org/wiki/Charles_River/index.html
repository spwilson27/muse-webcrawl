The Charles River is an 80-mile (129 km) river in eastern Massachusetts, United States, flowing northeast from Hopkinton through 23 cities and towns to its mouth at Boston Harbor. It is best known for the stretch separating Boston from Cambridge, where it forms a broad basin lined by the campuses of Harvard University, MIT, and Boston University.

Course

The river rises at Echo Lake in Hopkinton and meanders generally northeast, receiving tributaries including the Stop River and Mill River, before passing through the suburbs of MetroWest. Below Waltham it becomes navigable in character, widening into the Charles River Basin between Watertown and the Charles River Dam at Boston Harbor. The lower basin is an artificial freshwater impoundment created in 1910, replacing tidal mudflats.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Massachusett, called the river Quinobequin, meaning "meandering." Captain John Smith mapped the coast in 1614 and named it the "Massachusetts River"; Prince Charles, later King Charles I, renamed it for himself on the published map. Early colonial industry harnessed its falls for mills, and by the 19th century the lower riverbanks were filled and developed, including the Back Bay of Boston and the Esplanade.

Recreation

The river is a centerpiece of Boston recreation. The Charles River Esplanade hosts concerts at the Hatch Memorial Shell, including the Boston Pops' Fourth of July performance. The Head of the Charles Regatta, held each October since 1965, is one of the world's largest rowing events, drawing hundreds of thousands of spectators. Paths along both banks form an 18-mile loop popular with runners and cyclists.

Water quality

Once heavily polluted by sewage and industrial discharge — the Standells' 1966 song "Dirty Water" celebrates Boston and its "dirty water" — the river has been the subject of a major cleanup since the 1990s led by the Environmental Protection Agency and the Charles River Watershed Association. Water quality grades have improved dramatically, and swimming, long banned, has returned for special events.
