Symphysodon

KingdomAnimalia
OrderCichliformes
FamilyCichlidae
GenusSymphysodon Heckel, 1840
Common nameDiscus

Symphysodon, commonly known as discus, is a genus of cichlid fish native to the Amazon River basin in South America. Discus are among the most popular freshwater aquarium fish, famous for their laterally compressed, disc-shaped bodies and vivid colouration — and for their demanding care requirements.

Species and description

Three species are generally recognised: S. discus (red discus or Heckel discus), S. aequifasciatus (blue/brown/green discus), and S. tarzoo. Adults typically reach 12–15 cm in length. Wild forms show vertical dark barring over base colours of brown, green, blue, or red; through selective breeding, aquarists have produced many cultivated varieties, including turquoise, pigeon blood, albino, and snakeskin strains.

Behaviour and breeding

Discus are social fish that live in groups in slow-moving, warm, soft, acidic waters. They are notable for their parental care: both parents guard eggs laid on vertical surfaces, and the fry feed on a nutritious mucus secretion produced on the parents' skin — a form of care unique among cichlids.

In the aquarium

Because they require warm (28–31 °C), clean, stable water and are sensitive to stress, discus are considered advanced aquarium fish and are often called the "king of the aquarium". Commercial breeding is centred in Asia as well as in Germany and the United States.
