Trombone
ClassificationBrass instrument; aerophone
RangeTenor trombone: E2 to B♭4 (approx.)
RelatedSackbut, trumpet, bass trombone, buisine

The trombone is a musical instrument in the brass family. As with all brass instruments, sound is produced when the player's vibrating lips cause the air column inside the instrument to vibrate. Nearly all trombones use a telescoping slide mechanism to change the length of the tube — and hence the pitch — rather than the valves used by most other brass instruments; the player extends the slide through seven positions. The word "trombone" derives from the Italian tromba (trumpet) plus the suffix -one (large): literally, "large trumpet".


The tenor trombone

History

The trombone descends from the Renaissance sackbut, developed in the 15th century, and for centuries was used in church and court music, valued for its ability to support voices in tune. Beethoven and the Romantic composers established it in the symphony orchestra; trombones appear in classical, brass band, jazz (in both Dixieland and big-band sections), salsa, ska and marching band contexts. In jazz the slide allows the characteristic glissando, exploited by players such as Tommy Dorsey, J. J. Johnson and Glenn Miller, who led a famous trombone-led dance band.

Types

The most common type is the tenor trombone; the bass trombone has a larger bore and additional rotary valve tubing. Many tenor instruments have an F attachment — a rotary valve adding extra tubing — extending the low range. Alto, soprano, contrabass and valve trombones also exist. Modern instruments are normally built of yellow brass with a lacquered or silver-plated finish, and are played with cup mouthpieces.
