SportThoroughbred horse racing
BasisProgeny earnings in a calendar year
Record holderLexington — 16 titles (19th century); Bold Ruler — 8 titles (modern era)

The list below shows the leading Thoroughbred sire of racehorses in North America for each year since 1830. This is determined by the amount of prize money won by the sire's progeny during the year, restricted to earnings in races in the United States, Canada and Puerto Rico. The title is a major marker of a stallion's commercial value and strongly influences stud fees.

History

The 19th-century standings were dominated by Lexington, who led the sire list a record 16 times between 1861 and 1878, and by sires such as Glencoe and Leamington. In the 20th century, Bull Lea led five times in the 1940s–50s, Nasrullah five times, and Bold Ruler a then-modern-record eight times (1963–69 and 1973). More recently the list has been headed by stallions including Storm Cat, A.P. Indy, Giant's Causeway, Tapit — who led three consecutive years in 2014–16 — and Into Mischief, the leading sire from 2019 through the mid-2020s on the strength of record progeny earnings.

Selected multiple champions

Lexington (16), Bold Ruler (8), *Sir Gallahad III, Bull Lea, Nasrullah and Mr. Prospector-era sires such as Danzig and Storm Cat (2 each for the latter pair) are among the repeat leaders. A related ranking, the leading broodmare sire list, credits stallions through the earnings of their daughters' offspring.

Methodology

Statistics are compiled by The Blood-Horse and Equibase from official race results. Rankings can change as foreign earnings are excluded and as historical records for the 19th century remain incomplete.
