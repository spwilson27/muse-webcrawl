Caernarfon is a royal town, community and port in Gwynedd, north-west Wales. It lies on the eastern shore of the Menai Strait opposite the Isle of Anglesey, at the mouth of the River Seiont. The town is dominated by Caernarfon Castle, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and is noted for its predominantly Welsh-speaking population.

History

The Romans built the fort of Segontium nearby around AD 77. After the Norman period, the town grew in importance as a Welsh stronghold before Edward I of England began building the present castle and town walls in 1283 as part of his conquest of Wales. Edward's son, the future Edward II, was born at the castle in 1284 and was later presented as Prince of Wales, a tradition that continues: in 1969 the investiture of Charles, Prince of Wales (now Charles III) took place at Caernarfon Castle, following the 1911 investiture of the future Edward VIII there.

Language and culture

Caernarfon is often cited as one of the most strongly Welsh-speaking towns in Wales, with the great majority of residents able to speak Welsh. Its inhabitants are nicknamed "Cofis" and the town has a distinct local dialect. The town square, Y Maes, fronts the castle, and the narrow streets within the medieval walls contain many listed buildings.

Transport and tourism

Caernarfon is a station on the Welsh Highland Railway from Porthmadog and a gateway to Snowdonia (Eryri). The harbour and marina on the waterfront, Victoria Dock, support leisure sailing; nearby Dinas Dinlle and the former airport host the Wales Air Ambulance.
