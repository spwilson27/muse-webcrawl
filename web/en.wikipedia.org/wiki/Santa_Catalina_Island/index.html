Santa Catalina Island

Santa Catalina Island
Location: Pacific Ocean, California
Area: about 195 km²
Largest town: Avalon

Santa Catalina Island, often just Catalina, is a rocky island in the Channel Islands archipelago off the coast of Southern California, about 35 km southwest of Los Angeles. Most of its land is protected by the Catalina Island Conservancy.

Geography and wildlife

The island is about 35 km long with rugged terrain rising to Mount Orizaba (639 m). Native wildlife includes the Catalina Island fox; introduced American bison, descendants of a herd brought for a 1924 film shoot, roam the interior. The surrounding waters are noted for kelp forests, garibaldi and clear diving conditions.

History

The island was home to the Tongva (Pimugnans) for thousands of years. Visited by Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo in 1542 and named by Sebastián Vizcaíno in 1602, it passed through Spanish, Mexican and American rule. In 1919 chewing-gum magnate William Wrigley Jr. bought a controlling interest and developed the town of Avalon as a resort, building the landmark Catalina Casino in 1929.

Tourism

Avalon and the village of Two Harbors attract about a million visitors a year, arriving by ferry from mainland ports. Cars are restricted on the island; most travel is by foot, bicycle or golf cart.
