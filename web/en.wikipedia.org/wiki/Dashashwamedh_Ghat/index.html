Dashashwamedh Ghat

Dashashwamedh Ghat during the evening aarti, Varanasi.

Dashashwamedh Ghat is the main ghat in Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh, India, located on the Ganges River close to the Vishwanath Temple. It is probably the most spectacular ghat along the riverfront and one of the oldest in the city.

Legend and history

According to Hindu mythology, the ghat was created by Brahma to welcome Shiva, and its name — "ghat of the ten horse sacrifices" — refers to the legend that Brahma sacrificed ten horses (dasha-ashwamedha) here in a yajna ritual. The present structure of the ghat was built in 1748 by Peshwa Balaji Baji Rao, and was later renovated in 1774 by Ahilyabai Holkar, the queen of Indore.

Ganga aarti

Each evening, a group of priests performs the Ganga aarti at the ghat — a choreographed fire ritual in which large flaming lamps are raised in unison toward the river, accompanied by chanting, conch shells and incense. The ceremony draws thousands of devotees and tourists, many of whom watch from boats on the river. A special aarti is held on Tuesdays and on religious festivals such as Dev Deepawali, when the ghats are illuminated with thousands of earthen lamps.

Location

The ghat sits at the southern end of Varanasi's old city, near Godowlia crossing and the Kashi Vishwanath Temple corridor. It adjoins Man Mandir Ghat to the north.
