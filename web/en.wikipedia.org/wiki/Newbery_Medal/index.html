The John Newbery Medal, frequently shortened to the Newbery Medal, is a literary award given by the Association for Library Service to Children (ALSC), a division of the American Library Association (ALA), to the author of "the most distinguished contribution to American literature for children." First awarded in 1922, it is regarded as one of the two most prestigious American children's book awards, alongside the Caldecott Medal for illustration.

History

The medal was proposed by publisher Frederic G. Melcher and named for John Newbery, the 18th-century English bookseller often called the "father of children's literature." Hendrik Willem van Loon's The Story of Mankind won the inaugural award in 1922. Each year the committee also designates several runners-up as Newbery Honor Books. To be eligible, a book must be written by a citizen or resident of the United States and published in English in the U.S. during the preceding year.

Notable winners

  • The Story of Mankind by Hendrik Willem van Loon (1922)
  • Johnny Tremain by Esther Forbes (1944)
  • Charlotte's Web by E. B. White was an Honor Book (1953)
  • A Wrinkle in Time by Madeleine L'Engle (1963)
  • Bridge to Terabithia by Katherine Paterson (1978)
  • Holes by Louis Sachar (1999)
  • The Tale of Despereaux by Kate DiCamillo (2004)
  • The Graveyard Book by Neil Gaiman (2009)
  • When You Trap a Tiger by Tae Keller (2021)

Only a few authors have won twice, among them E. B. White (honors aside), Joseph Krumgold, Elizabeth George Speare, Katherine Paterson, Lois Lowry, Kate DiCamillo, and Erin Entrada Kelly has won the medal and honors. The medal carries a guaranteed commercial effect: winners are reprinted with the gold seal and remain library staples for decades, which has also prompted periodic debate about whether committees favor literary merit over child appeal.
