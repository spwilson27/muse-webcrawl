HM Prison Pentonville

HM Prison Pentonville is a Category B/C men's prison located on the Caledonian Road in Islington, North London, England. Opened in 1842, it was the prototype of the "separate system" of prison design, with five wings radiating from a central hub so that prisoners could be kept isolated in individual cells and supervised from the centre. Its layout served as a model for many later Victorian prisons in Britain and its colonies.

Pentonville was an execution site from 1902 to 1961; 120 men were hanged there, including, in its early years elsewhere in London's system, some of the most notorious cases of the era. Among those imprisoned or executed at Pentonville were the poisoner Dr Hawley Harvey Crippen (hanged 1910, the first criminal caught with the aid of wireless telegraphy).

Today the prison holds remand and convicted adult men from the local courts. It has frequently been criticised by inspectors for overcrowding and poor conditions.
