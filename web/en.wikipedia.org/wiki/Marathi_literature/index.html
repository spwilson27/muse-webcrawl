Marathi literature

Marathi literature is the body of literature written in the Marathi language, spoken in the Indian state of Maharashtra. It is among the oldest literatures of the modern Indo-Aryan languages, with inscriptions and texts dating from around the 11th–13th centuries.

Early and medieval period

The earliest major work is the Līlācaritra (c. 1278), a biography of Chakradhara, founder of the Mahanubhava sect. The Warkari saint-poets transformed Marathi devotional writing: Dnyaneshwar's Dnyaneshwari (1290), a commentary on the Bhagavad Gita, is a foundational classic, followed by Eknath's Bhagavata and the abhanga hymns of Tukaram in the 17th century. The period also produced narrative powada ballads and prose bakhar chronicles of the Maratha era.

Modern period

Under British rule, print culture, journalism, and Western genres reshaped Marathi letters. Hari Narayan Apte pioneered the social novel; Vishnu Sakharam Khandekar won the Jnanpith Award for his novel Yayati. The Little Magazine movement of the 1950s–60s, led by poets such as B. S. Mardhekar and writers like Gangadhar Gadgil, brought modernism, while Dalit literature — including Namdeo Dhasal's poetry and Daya Pawar's autobiography Baluta — revolutionised Marathi writing from the 1970s.

Drama

Marathi theatre, from Vishnudas Bhave's mythologicals of 1843 through the sangeet natak musical tradition to Vijay Tendulkar's hard-edged modern plays such as Ghashiram Kotwal and Silence! The Court Is in Session, remains a central strand of the literature.
