Halifax is the capital and most populous municipality of the Canadian province of Nova Scotia, and the most populous municipality in Atlantic Canada. Founded by the British in 1749 on one of the world's largest natural harbours, it has long been a major naval base, port and commercial centre.

History

Halifax was established by Edward Cornwallis in 1749 as a counterweight to French Louisbourg, in the homeland of the Mi'kmaq people, who had lived in the region for millennia. The city grew as a garrison and shipbuilding town. In December 1917 the Halifax Explosion — the detonation of the munitions ship Mont-Blanc after a collision — devastated the city's north end and killed about 1,800 people. Halifax played a central role as a convoy port in both world wars.

Economy and life

Today the Halifax Regional Municipality is home to major universities (including Dalhousie), the Atlantic headquarters of the Royal Canadian Navy at CFB Halifax, a busy container port and cruise ship terminal, and a growing technology and finance sector. Landmarks include the Halifax Citadel, the Maritime Museum of the Atlantic, and the historic waterfront.
