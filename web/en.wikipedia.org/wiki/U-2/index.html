The Lockheed U-2, nicknamed the "Dragon Lady", is an American single-engine, high-altitude reconnaissance aircraft operated by the United States Air Force (USAF) and previously flown by the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA). It provides day and night, high-altitude (above 21,000 m / 70,000 ft), all-weather intelligence gathering.

Lockheed Corporation originally proposed the design in 1953. It was approved in 1954, and its first test flight was in 1955. It was flown during the Cold War over the Soviet Union, China, Vietnam, and Cuba. In 1960, Gary Powers was shot down in a CIA U-2A over the Soviet Union by a surface-to-air missile (SAM). Major Rudolf Anderson Jr. was shot down in a U-2 during the Cuban Missile Crisis in 1962.

Development

After World War II, the US military desired better strategic aerial reconnaissance to help determine Soviet capabilities and intentions. During the early 1950s, the best aircraft available to the US were bombers converted for reconnaissance duty; they were vulnerable to anti-aircraft artillery, missiles, and fighters. The USAF thought an aircraft that could fly at 70,000 feet would be beyond the reach of Soviet fighters, missiles, and even radar.

Clarence "Kelly" Johnson, head of Lockheed's Skunk Works, proposed the CL-282 design, essentially a sailplane-like aircraft with long, slender wings. The aircraft was built in great secrecy at the Groom Lake test site in Nevada, later known as Area 51, and first flew on 1 August 1955.

Design

The U-2 is a lightweight aircraft with bicycle-style landing gear and extremely long, high-aspect-ratio wings, giving it glider-like performance. Takeoffs and landings are notoriously difficult: the pilot wears a pressure suit similar to those used by astronauts, and the aircraft must be "flown" onto the runway at near-stall speed while a chase car calls out altitude. Removable pogo sticks support the wings on takeoff and fall away as the aircraft lifts off.

Operational history

U-2s have taken part in post–Cold War conflicts in Afghanistan and Iraq, and supported several multinational NATO operations. The type has also carried out electronic sensor research, satellite calibration, scientific research, and communications purposes. Despite its age, the U-2 remains in front-line service more than 60 years after its introduction, and modernized U-2S variants with powerful sensors and precision navigation continue to fly with the USAF and NASA, which operates the research variant under the designation ER-2.

On 1 May 1960, a U-2 piloted by Francis Gary Powers was shot down near Sverdlovsk by an S-75 Dvina missile, causing the U-2 crisis of 1960 and a major international incident.

Specifications (U-2S)

  • Crew: 1
  • Length: 19.2 m (63 ft)
  • Wingspan: 31.4 m (103 ft)
  • Maximum speed: 805 km/h (500 mph)
  • Service ceiling: above 21,000 m (70,000 ft)
  • Range: more than 11,000 km (7,000 mi)
