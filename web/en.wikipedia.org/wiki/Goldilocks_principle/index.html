Goldilocks principle

Goldilocks principle
Named after"Goldilocks and the Three Bears"
MeaningThe ideal amount of something lies between two extremes
FieldsAstrobiology, economics, medicine, linguistics, engineering

The Goldilocks principle is named by analogy to the children's story "Goldilocks and the Three Bears", in which Goldilocks tastes three bowls of porridge and finds the one that is neither too hot nor too cold — "just right". Applied to a range of disciplines, the principle states that something must fall within certain margins, as opposed to reaching an extreme.

Applications

In astrobiology, a "Goldilocks zone" is the habitable zone around a star where a planet is neither too hot nor too cold for liquid water to exist on its surface. In economics, a "Goldilocks economy" grows at a moderate pace — neither overheating into inflation nor slipping into recession. In medicine, the "Goldilocks dose" of a drug balances efficacy against toxicity. Cognitive science invokes a "Goldilocks effect" in infant attention, where babies preferentially attend to stimuli that are neither too simple nor too complex.

Origin of the phrase

The analogy derives from Robert Southey's 1837 telling of the Three Bears story. The phrase "Goldilocks principle" entered wide use in the 1970s and 1980s, particularly in discussions of planetary habitability and the anthropic principle — the observation that the universe's physical parameters appear "just right" for life.
