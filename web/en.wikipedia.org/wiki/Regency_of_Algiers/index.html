Regency of Algiers


Period: 1516–1830
Capital: Algiers
Status: Ottoman regency, largely autonomous

The Regency of Algiers was an early modern polity centred on Algiers in present-day Algeria, existing from 1516 until the French invasion of 1830. Nominally a province (eyalet, later regency) of the Ottoman Empire, it was in practice largely self-governing, ruled successively by beylerbeys, pashas, aghas, and, from 1671, deys elected by the janissary corps (the Odjak).

Origins

The state was founded by the corsair brothers Aruj and Hayreddin Barbarossa, who seized Algiers in 1516 and placed it under Ottoman suzerainty in exchange for support against Spain. Hayreddin consolidated the territory, annexing much of the Algerian coast and interior.

Corsair economy

Algiers became the principal base of the Barbary corsairs, whose raiding of Christian shipping and coasts—and the capture and ransoming or enslavement of prisoners—was a mainstay of the economy. European powers and the young United States paid tribute or fought wars (the Barbary Wars, 1801–05 and 1815) to protect their commerce. An Anglo-Dutch fleet under Lord Exmouth bombarded Algiers in 1816, forcing the dey to abolish Christian slavery.

Government and decline

The territory was divided into three beyliks (Constantine, Titteri, and Oran/Mascara) administered by beys under the dey of Algiers. By the early 19th century corsair revenues had collapsed, and in 1827 a diplomatic quarrel—the "fly-whisk incident," in which Dey Hussein struck the French consul—gave France a pretext to invade. Algiers fell on 5 July 1830, ending the regency and beginning French Algeria.
