Mauna Loa

Mauna Loa (Hawaiian for "Long Mountain") is one of five shield volcanoes forming the island of Hawaiʻi in the U.S. state of Hawaii. It is the largest shield volcano on Earth — and among the largest volcanoes in the solar system's terrestrial family — rising 4,169 metres (13,681 ft) above sea level, with its submerged flanks descending a further ~5 km to the ocean floor, which its mass depresses by several more kilometres. Mauna Loa is an active volcano, monitored by the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory; it most recently erupted in November–December 2022, after its longest recorded quiet period of 38 years.

Geology

Mauna Loa is built by fluid basaltic lava erupted from the Hawaii hotspot. Estimates place the start of its growth 600,000–1,000,000 years ago; it emerged above the sea roughly 400,000 years ago. Its summit caldera, Mokuʻāweoweo, is about 3 by 5 km across, and two great rift zones (northeast and southwest) extend from the summit. The volcano makes up about 51% of the island's surface and some 75,000 km³ of volume.

Eruption history

Written records since 1832 document 34 eruptions. Notable flows include those of 1859, 1880–81 (which threatened Hilo), 1926 (destroying the village of Hoʻōpūloa), 1950 (a huge southwest-rift eruption whose flows reached the sea in hours) and 1984 (again threatening Hilo). The 2022 eruption began in the summit caldera and migrated to the northeast rift zone, sending flows towards the Daniel K. Inouye Highway but stopping short; it ended after about two weeks.

Climate research

On Mauna Loa's northern flank, the Mauna Loa Observatory (NOAA) has continuously measured atmospheric carbon dioxide since 1958, producing the famous Keeling Curve, the longest direct record of atmospheric CO₂ and key evidence of human-driven climate change.
