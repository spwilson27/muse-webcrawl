Koeberg Nuclear Power Station is a nuclear power station in South Africa, currently the only one in the country — and as of the mid-2020s the only commercial nuclear power station in Africa. It is located at Duynefontein, 30 km north-west of Cape Town on the Atlantic coast, and is owned and operated by the state utility Eskom.

Design and operation

Koeberg has two pressurised water reactors of the French CP1 design supplied by Framatome, each rated at about 930 MW electrical, giving the station a total capacity of roughly 1,860 MW. The reactors use seawater for cooling, drawn from the cold Atlantic. Unit 1 entered commercial operation in 1984 and Unit 2 in 1985; the station supplies a significant share of the Western Cape's electricity and, historically, around 5% of South Africa's total generation.

History and life extension

The station was controversial from construction under apartheid-era secrecy; in December 1982, before it entered service, Umkhonto we Sizwe operatives planted limpet mines that damaged the reactor containment area, one of the most celebrated sabotage operations of the anti-apartheid struggle. In the 2020s Eskom pursued a long-term operation programme to extend Koeberg's life by 20 years, including replacement of all six steam generators, with Unit 1's extended licence granted by the National Nuclear Regulator in 2024. The surrounding land forms the Koeberg Nature Reserve, open to the public for hiking and mountain biking among fynbos and antelope.
