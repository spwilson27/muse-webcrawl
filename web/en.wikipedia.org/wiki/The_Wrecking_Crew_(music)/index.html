The Wrecking Crew was a loose collective of American session musicians based in Los Angeles whose services were in high demand during the 1960s and early 1970s. They played on thousands of recordings, including hundreds of Top 40 hits, often uncredited.

Work

The Crew formed the core of Phil Spector's "Wall of Sound" and backed artists including the Beach Boys (notably Pet Sounds), Frank Sinatra, the Monkees, the Mamas & the Papas, Sonny & Cher, the Byrds, Elvis Presley and Simon & Garfunkel, as well as countless film and television scores. Drummer Hal Blaine, who popularised the group's nickname, is among the most recorded musicians in history; bassist Carol Kaye and guitarist Tommy Tedesco were other mainstays.

Legacy

Their story was told in the 2008 documentary The Wrecking Crew!, directed by Denny Tedesco. Several members were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in the sideman category.
