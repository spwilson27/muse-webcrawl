The Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH; French: Conférence de La Haye de droit international privé) is an intergovernmental organisation in the area of private international law (conflict of laws), that develops and administers multilateral treaties — the Hague Conventions — governing cross-border legal situations. It has produced more than 40 conventions and works "for the progressive unification of the rules of private international law", its statutory purpose.

History

The Conference first met in 1893, convened by the Dutch government at the initiative of the jurist Tobias Asser (later a Nobel Peace Prize laureate), with twelve European states attending. Early sessions produced conventions on civil procedure and family matters. After interruptions by the world wars, the seventh session in 1951 adopted a statute that made the Conference a permanent organisation; the statute entered into force in 1955, with a Permanent Bureau (secretariat) in The Hague and diplomatic sessions roughly every four years.

Conventions

Among the most widely ratified HCCH instruments are:

  • The Apostille Convention (1961), abolishing consular legalisation of public documents in favour of a single certificate.
  • The Service Convention (1965) and Evidence Convention (1970), governing cross-border service of process and taking of evidence.
  • The Child Abduction Convention (1980), providing for the prompt return of children wrongfully removed across borders.
  • The Intercountry Adoption Convention (1993), setting safeguards for international adoptions.
  • The Child Support Convention (2007), the Choice of Court Convention (2005) and the Judgments Convention (2019) on recognition and enforcement of foreign judgments.

Membership and work

Membership has grown beyond its European origins to more than 90 states from all regions, plus the European Union as a member organisation; many more states are parties to individual conventions. The Permanent Bureau monitors the operation of the conventions, convenes expert groups and Special Commissions, maintains the INCADAT database on child abduction case law, and provides technical assistance to states implementing the treaties.
