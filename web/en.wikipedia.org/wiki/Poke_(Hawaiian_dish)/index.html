Poke (Hawaiian dish)

Poke (Hawaiian for "to slice or cut crosswise into pieces") is a dish of diced raw fish tossed with flavourings and served either as an appetiser or a main course. It is one of the main dishes of Native Hawaiian cuisine. Traditional forms use aku (skipjack tuna) or heʻe (octopus); the most popular modern form uses yellowfin tuna (ahi).

Ahi poke served over rice with toppings.

History

Native Hawaiians seasoned freshly caught reef fish with sea salt, seaweed, and crushed candlenuts (inamona) long before Western contact. The contemporary form of poke emerged in the 1970s, incorporating Japanese influences such as soy sauce (shoyu) and sesame oil brought by immigrants. From around 2012 the dish surged in popularity on the US mainland, spawning fast-casual "poke bowl" restaurants that serve the fish over rice with varied toppings and sauces, sometimes far removed from the Hawaiian original.

Varieties

Common styles include shoyu ahi poke, spicy ahi with mayonnaise and chilli, and limu poke with seaweed. Variants use salmon, octopus (tako), marinated tofu, or cooked seafood, and regional Hawaiian grocery stores typically sell several kinds by weight from deli counters.
