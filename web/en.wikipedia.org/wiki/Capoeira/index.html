Capoeira

Image placeholder: A capoeira roda (circle).

Capoeira (Portuguese pronunciation: [kapuˈejɾɐ]) is an Afro-Brazilian martial art and game that combines elements of combat, acrobatics, music, dance, and philosophy. It was created by enslaved Africans in Brazil and developed by their descendants, disguised historically as dance to evade prohibition.

The game

Capoeira is played in a circle called a roda, formed by participants who sing, clap, and play instruments — principally the berimbau, atabaque drum, and pandeiro. Two players enter the circle and engage in a flowing exchange of kicks, sweeps, evasions, and acrobatic movements, keeping constant motion to the rhythm set by the berimbau. Rather than a fight to injure, the game emphasises skill, deception (malícia), and control.

History

Emerging among enslaved Africans in colonial Brazil, capoeira was criminalised after abolition in the Brazilian penal code of 1890. In the 1930s Mestre Bimba developed Capoeira Regional and won official recognition, while Mestre Pastinha championed the traditional Capoeira Angola. In 2014 UNESCO inscribed the capoeira circle on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Styles

The main styles are Capoeira Angola, slower and closer to the ground, and Capoeira Regional, faster and more upright; many contemporary groups blend both. Graduations are marked by coloured cords (cordas).
