Robert Benchley


Born: September 15, 1889, Worcester, Massachusetts, U.S.
Died: November 21, 1945, New York City
Occupation: Humorist, actor, critic

Robert Charles Benchley (September 15, 1889 – November 21, 1945) was an American humorist, newspaper columnist, drama critic and actor. A founding member of the Algonquin Round Table, he was celebrated for his gentle, self-deprecating essays and for his short comedy films.

Career

Benchley wrote for Vanity Fair (where he was managing editor), Life magazine — for which he wrote the drama column under the pseudonym Guy Fawkes — and The New Yorker, where his casuals helped define the magazine's comic voice. His books, including Of All Things! (1921), The Treasurer's Report and Other Aspects of Community Singing (1930) and My Ten Years in a Quandary (1936), collected essays that turned everyday frustrations into comedy.

Film work

Reluctantly drawn into performing, Benchley became famous for his monologue short The Treasurer's Report and won the Academy Award for Best Short Subject for How to Sleep (1935), a spoof of scientific documentaries. He appeared as a character actor in dozens of films, including Foreign Correspondent (1940) and The Major and the Minor (1942).

He was the father of writer Nathaniel Benchley and grandfather of Jaws author Peter Benchley.
