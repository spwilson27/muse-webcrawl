Beyer, Peacock and Company

[Image placeholder: Beyer-Garratt locomotive]

Beyer, Peacock and Company was an English railway locomotive manufacturer with a factory in Openshaw, Manchester. Founded by Charles Beyer, Richard Peacock and Henry Robertson, it traded from 1854 until 1966. It received limited liability in 1902, becoming Beyer, Peacock & Co. Ltd.

The firm was one of the world's leading exporters of steam locomotives, supplying railways across the British Empire and beyond. It is best known for the Beyer-Garratt articulated locomotive, patented by engineer Herbert William Garratt, which allowed powerful locomotives to negotiate sharp curves on lightly laid track.

History

Beyer and Peacock established the Gorton Foundry in 1854; their first locomotive was built in 1855. The company produced more than 8,400 locomotives over its history, including condensing locomotives for London's Metropolitan Railway. After diesel and electric traction displaced steam, the works closed in 1966. Part of the site is now occupied by facilities documented at the Museum of Science and Industry in Manchester.
