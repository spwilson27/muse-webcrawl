The Godhra train burning occurred on 27 February 2002 at Godhra railway station in the Indian state of Gujarat, when coach S-6 of the Sabarmati Express, carrying Hindu pilgrims (kar sevaks) returning from Ayodhya, caught fire. Fifty-nine people died in the blaze. The incident was the immediate trigger for the 2002 Gujarat riots, in which more than a thousand people, mostly Muslims, were killed across the state.

Background

The passengers were returning from Ayodhya, site of the demolished Babri Masjid and the focus of a long-running campaign by Hindu nationalist organisations to build a temple to Rama there. The train had been halted at Godhra by a signal shortly after departing the station when the fire began.

Investigations and trials

The cause of the fire was long disputed. The Nanavati–Mehta Commission, appointed by the Gujarat government, concluded in its 2008 interim report that the fire was the result of a pre-planned conspiracy in which petrol was poured into the coach. A committee headed by Justice U. C. Banerjee, appointed by the Railway Ministry, reached the contrary conclusion that the fire was accidental; the Gujarat High Court later held the Banerjee committee's constitution unlawful. In 2011, a special trial court convicted 31 people of conspiracy and murder, sentencing 11 to death and 20 to life imprisonment; the Gujarat High Court in 2017 commuted the death sentences to life imprisonment while upholding the convictions.

Aftermath

News of the burning sparked widespread communal violence beginning the following day, including the Gulbarg Society massacre and the Naroda Patiya massacre in Ahmedabad. The events of 2002 remain politically contentious in India, including debates over the response of the state government led by Narendra Modi.
