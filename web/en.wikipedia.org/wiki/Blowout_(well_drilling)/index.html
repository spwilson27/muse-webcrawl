Blowout (well drilling)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A blowout is the uncontrolled release of crude oil or natural gas from an oil or gas well after pressure control systems have failed. In the early days of oil drilling, such uncontrolled wells were called gushers and were celebrated as signs of a rich strike; today they are recognised as dangerous and environmentally destructive events.

Causes and prevention

Blowouts usually follow a "kick", an influx of formation fluid into the wellbore, when the hydrostatic pressure of the drilling mud is insufficient to contain formation pressure. The primary defence is the blowout preventer (BOP), a stack of valves and rams at the wellhead that can seal the well. Specialist well-control companies cap runaway wells, sometimes by drilling a relief well.

Notable blowouts

Famous examples include the Lakeview Gusher in California (1910–1911), the Ixtoc I spill in the Gulf of Mexico (1979) and the Deepwater Horizon blowout of 2010, which killed eleven workers and caused the largest marine oil spill in history.
