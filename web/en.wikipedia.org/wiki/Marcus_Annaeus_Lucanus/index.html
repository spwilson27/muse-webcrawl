Lucan

Marcus Annaeus Lucanus (3 November AD 39 – 30 April AD 65), better known in English as Lucan, was a Roman poet, born in Corduba in Hispania. He is regarded as one of the outstanding figures of Imperial Latin literature. His youth and speed of composition were remarkable: he died at age 25, leaving an unfinished epic, the Pharsalia (known also as De Bello Civili), an account of the civil war between Julius Caesar and Pompey.

Life

Lucan was the son of Marcus Annaeus Mela and grandson of Seneca the Elder; his uncle was the philosopher Seneca the Younger, through whose influence he was educated in Rome and Athens. He became a friend and court poet of the emperor Nero, winning a prize at the Neronia, the emperor's artistic festival, in AD 60 with a poem in praise of the emperor. He held the quaestorship and was admitted to the augurate before the legal age.

Nero's jealousy of Lucan's poetic success, or political disagreements, led to a rupture: the emperor forbade Lucan to publish his poems or plead in court. Lucan responded with lampoons and joined the Pisonian conspiracy against Nero in AD 65. When the conspiracy was discovered, Lucan implicated his own mother, Acilia, among others, in the hope of a pardon, but was nevertheless ordered to commit suicide. Following the classical custom, he opened his veins, reciting verses of his own as he died. His father was proscribed and his widow, Polla Argentaria, was exiled.

Works

Only the Pharsalia survives. The epic, in ten books of hexameters, narrates the civil war from Caesar's crossing of the Rubicon to events in Egypt after Pompey's murder; it breaks off abruptly, probably unfinished, in Book 10. Unusually for an epic, the gods play almost no role; human will, fortune, and tyranny dominate. Lucan's poem is strongly anti-Caesarian and republican in sympathy, portraying Caesar as a destructive tyrant and Cato the Younger as a moral hero.

Lost works attributed to Lucan include the Iliacon, Catachthonion, Silvae, the tragedy Medea, and poems addressed to his wife.

Legacy

The Pharsalia was a school text in late antiquity and the Middle Ages and was admired by Dante, who places Lucan among the great poets of Limbo. Its rhetorical intensity, sententiae, and historical subject matter kept it influential through the Renaissance and beyond.
