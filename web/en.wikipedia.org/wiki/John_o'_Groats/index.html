John o' Groats

John o' Groats (Scottish Gaelic: Taigh Iain Ghròt) is a village in the historic county of Caithness, in the far north of Scotland. It is popularly regarded as the most northerly settlement on the British mainland, and together with Land's End in Cornwall — about 876 miles (1,410 km) away by road — it marks the endpoints of the longest journey in Great Britain, commemorated in the phrase "Land's End to John o' Groats".

Name and history

The village takes its name from Jan de Groot, a Dutchman who obtained a grant for the ferry from the mainland to the island of Orkney, newly acquired from Norway, around 1496. Legend says de Groot built an octagonal house with eight doors and an eight-sided table so that none of his eight sons could claim precedence at meals.

Geography and tourism

John o' Groats lies on the Pentland Firth coast. The actual northernmost point of mainland Britain is nearby Dunnet Head. The village has a harbour, from which ferries run to Orkney in summer, a famous signpost photographed by generations of "End-to-Enders" who walk, cycle or drive the length of Britain for charity or sport.
