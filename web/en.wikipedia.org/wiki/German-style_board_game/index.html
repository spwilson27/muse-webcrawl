German-style board game

A German-style board game, also called a Eurogame or designer board game, is a class of tabletop games that generally have indirect player interaction, lack player elimination, and feature abstract physical components. Eurogames emphasise strategy over luck or conflict, keep all players engaged until the end, and typically credit the designer prominently on the box.

Characteristics

Common traits include victory points rather than direct conquest, resource management, multiple paths to victory, catch-up mechanisms, and play times of 30 minutes to two hours. Themes — trading in the Mediterranean, building railways, farming — are often lightly applied over elegant mechanics.

History

Post-war Germany developed a strong family gaming culture, supported by mail-order and review media such as the journal Spielbox. The Spiel des Jahres ("Game of the Year") award, founded in 1978, rewarded accessible family games. The 1995 publication of Klaus Teuber's The Settlers of Catan launched the style internationally, followed by hits such as Carcassonne, Ticket to Ride, Puerto Rico and Agricola.

Influence

Eurogame design reshaped the global hobby market, blending with American thematic games to produce modern "hybrid" designs. Annual conventions such as the Internationale Spieltage in Essen — the world's largest board-game fair — showcase new releases each October.
