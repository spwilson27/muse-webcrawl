Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab) is a United States Department of Energy national laboratory specialising in high-energy particle physics, located in Batavia, Illinois, about 50 km west of Chicago. It is named in honour of physicist Enrico Fermi.

History

Founded in 1967 as the National Accelerator Laboratory under director Robert R. Wilson, who built it ahead of schedule and under budget, the laboratory was renamed for Fermi in 1974. Its Main Ring, and then the superconducting Tevatron (completed 1983), made it the world's highest-energy accelerator until the Large Hadron Collider: the Tevatron collided protons and antiprotons at nearly 2 TeV until its shutdown in 2011.

Discoveries

Fermilab experiments discovered the bottom quark (1977, Experiment 288 led by Leon Lederman), the top quark (1995, by the CDF and DØ collaborations) and the tau neutrino (2000, DONUT). Its neutrino programme includes the Main Injector, the NOvA long-baseline experiment, and the international Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) with its detectors in South Dakota. The laboratory also hosts major muon experiments (Muon g−2 and Mu2e), astrophysics surveys, and the PIP-II superconducting linac under construction.

Site

The 2,750-hectare site, centred on the 16-storey Wilson Hall, doubles as a tallgrass prairie restoration and wildlife area, including a herd of American bison maintained since 1969 — a symbol Wilson intended to link frontier science with the pioneer past.
