Strangers on a Train is a 1951 American psychological thriller film noir produced and directed by Alfred Hitchcock, adapted from Patricia Highsmith's 1950 novel of the same name. It stars Farley Granger as tennis player Guy Haines and Robert Walker as the charming but psychopathic Bruno Antony.

Plot

On a train, Bruno proposes an exchange of murders: he will kill Guy's unfaithful wife Miriam, who refuses Guy a divorce, if Guy will kill Bruno's hated father — each crime apparently motiveless and unsolvable. Guy dismisses the idea as a joke, but Bruno murders Miriam anyway and then blackmails Guy into holding up his end of the "bargain". The climax unfolds on a runaway merry-go-round at a fairground.

Reception and legacy

The film is celebrated for Robert Walker's performance, its set pieces (including the murder reflected in a dropped pair of glasses), and its exploration of guilt and doubling. It was added to the United States National Film Registry and is frequently ranked among Hitchcock's finest works.
