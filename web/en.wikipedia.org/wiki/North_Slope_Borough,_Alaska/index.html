The North Slope Borough is the northernmost borough (county equivalent) in the U.S. state of Alaska and, by land area, one of the largest county-level governments in the United States — larger than most U.S. states. It covers the Arctic coastal plain and Brooks Range foothills along Alaska's Arctic Ocean coast, a region known as the North Slope.

History

The borough was incorporated in 1972 by the Iñupiat people of the region, who organized the home-rule municipality in large part to gain local control and taxing authority over the development of the Prudhoe Bay oil field, discovered in 1968. It was the first time in U.S. history that a Native people incorporated an entire region as a municipal government, and the model — combining Alaska Native self-determination with American municipal law — drew wide attention. Property taxes on oil infrastructure at Prudhoe Bay, among the highest-value taxable property in any rural American jurisdiction, fund borough services.

Geography

The borough extends from the Arctic Ocean south to the Brooks Range and from the Canadian border west to the Chukchi Sea, encompassing much of the National Petroleum Reserve–Alaska and the western portion of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge. The climate is polar tundra; Utqiagvik experiences about two months of continuous darkness in winter and continuous daylight in summer.

Demographics and culture

A large majority of residents are Iñupiaq Alaska Natives, and the Iñupiaq language and subsistence hunting culture — including bowhead whaling from communities such as Utqiagvik, Point Hope and Kaktovik — remain central to life. The borough government is a major employer and funds infrastructure, schools and search-and-rescue across its eight communities, most of which are accessible only by air.

Economy

Oil and gas extraction around Prudhoe Bay and other North Slope fields dominates the economy; thousands of non-resident workers commute to the oil fields from Anchorage, Fairbanks and outside Alaska. Subsistence hunting, fishing and whaling continue alongside the cash economy.
