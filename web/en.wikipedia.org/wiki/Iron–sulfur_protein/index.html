Iron–sulfur protein

Iron–sulfur proteins (or iron–sulphur proteins) are proteins characterized by the presence of iron–sulfur clusters containing sulfide-linked di-, tri- and tetra-iron centers in variable oxidation states. Iron–sulfur clusters are found in a variety of metalloproteins, such as the ferredoxins, as well as NADH dehydrogenase, hydrogenases, coenzyme Q–cytochrome c reductase, succinate dehydrogenase and nitrogenase.

Clusters

The most common clusters are of the form [2Fe–2S] and [4Fe–4S]. Iron atoms are typically coordinated by cysteinyl sulfur atoms of the protein and by inorganic sulfide. In rubredoxins a single iron is coordinated by four cysteines without inorganic sulfide, an arrangement sometimes included among iron–sulfur proteins.

Biological roles

Iron–sulfur proteins are best known for their role in electron transfer reactions in the respiratory chain (complexes I, II and III) and photosynthesis. They also participate in catalysis, as in aconitase, in sensing of iron and oxygen, and in the biogenesis of clusters themselves. Because iron and sulfur were abundant in prebiotic environments, these clusters are thought to be among the most ancient biological cofactors.

Biosynthesis

Cells assemble clusters on scaffold proteins via dedicated systems — the ISC (iron–sulfur cluster) and SUF (sulfur mobilization) pathways — and then transfer them to apo-proteins.
