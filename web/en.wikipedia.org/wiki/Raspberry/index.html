Raspberry

The raspberry is the edible fruit of a multitude of plant species in the genus Rubus of the rose family, most of which are in the subgenus Idaeobatus. The name also applies to these plants themselves. Raspberries are perennial with woody stems.

The fruit is an aggregate of drupelets arranged around a conical receptacle; unlike the blackberry, the fruit separates from the receptacle when picked, leaving a hollow core. Raspberries are red, black, purple or golden in colour depending on the species and cultivar.

Cultivation

Raspberries are grown commercially in temperate regions worldwide. The leading producers include Russia, Mexico, Poland, Serbia and the United States. Plants are grown in rows on supports, with canes pruned annually; summer-bearing varieties fruit on second-year canes (floricanes), while autumn-bearing varieties fruit on first-year canes (primocanes).

Uses

Raspberries are eaten fresh and used in jams, purées, desserts, and flavourings. They are a good source of vitamin C, manganese and dietary fibre, and contain polyphenol antioxidants such as ellagitannins.
