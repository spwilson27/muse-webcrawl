Meal kit


The pre-portioned ingredients of a typical meal kit

A meal kit is a subscription service–foodservice business model where a company sends customers pre-portioned food ingredients and recipes in order for them to prepare home-cooked meals. Services that send pre-cooked meals are called meal delivery services. The model grew rapidly in the 2010s, led by companies such as Blue Apron and HelloFresh.

History

The concept originated in Sweden with services such as Middagsfrid (founded 2007) and Linas Matkasse. In the United States, Blue Apron was founded in 2012, and HelloFresh, founded in Berlin in 2011, expanded internationally to become the largest meal-kit provider in several markets. The COVID-19 pandemic drove a surge in demand in 2020 as restaurants closed and home cooking increased.

Business model

Customers typically subscribe to a weekly box containing ingredients for two to six meals, choosing from rotating menus. Proponents argue meal kits reduce food waste by supplying exact quantities and save time on shopping and meal planning. Critics point to the packaging waste generated by individualised portions, the higher cost per portion compared with supermarket shopping, and high customer churn rates, which have made profitability difficult for many providers.

Environmental impact

Studies have found that, despite greater packaging, meal kits can have a lower overall carbon footprint than equivalent supermarket meals, largely because of reduced food waste and streamlined supply chains, though results vary by meal type and delivery logistics.
