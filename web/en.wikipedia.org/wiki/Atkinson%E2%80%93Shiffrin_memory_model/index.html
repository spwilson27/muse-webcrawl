Atkinson–Shiffrin memory model

The Atkinson–Shiffrin model, proposed by Richard Atkinson and Richard Shiffrin in 1968, is a model of human memory that describes three structurally distinct stores: a sensory register, a short-term store, and a long-term store.

Components

Information from the environment first enters the sensory register, where it decays within a fraction of a second unless attended to. Attended information passes to the short-term store, which has limited capacity and duration and can hold items longer through rehearsal. Rehearsed information may be transferred to the long-term store, which has effectively unlimited capacity and duration.

Influence

The model became the standard "modal model" of memory and stimulated research on control processes such as rehearsal and coding. Later work, notably the working memory model of Baddeley and Hitch (1974), elaborated the short-term component.
