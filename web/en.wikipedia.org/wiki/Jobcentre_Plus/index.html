Jobcentre Plus

Formed: 2002

Parent agency: Department for Work and Pensions

Jurisdiction: Great Britain

Jobcentre Plus is a brand used by the United Kingdom's Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) for its network of local offices that administer working-age benefits and provide employment services in Great Britain. A similar service in Northern Ireland is run by the Department for Communities.

History

Jobcentre Plus was created in 2002 from the merger of the Employment Service, which ran the public jobcentre network, and the parts of the Benefits Agency dealing with working-age claimants. The merger brought benefit claims and job-search support under one roof, with new "personal adviser" interviews for claimants.

Services

Jobcentres process claims for benefits including Universal Credit, Jobseeker's Allowance and Employment and Support Allowance, and provide work coaches who agree a "claimant commitment" with each customer, review job search activity and can refer claimants to training, work experience or contracted employment programmes. Employers can advertise vacancies through the service. Claimants who fail to meet their commitments may be subject to benefit sanctions, a policy that has attracted sustained public and parliamentary debate.
