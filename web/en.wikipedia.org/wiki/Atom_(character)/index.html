Atom (character)

The Atom is the name shared by several superheroes appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. The characters are traditionally able to shrink to subatomic sizes while retaining their full strength and mass control. The best known is physicist Ray Palmer, a member of the Justice League, but the identity began in the Golden Age with Al Pratt, a short-statured brawler with no shrinking powers.

Al Pratt

The original Atom, created by writer Bill O'Connor and artist Ben Flinton, debuted in All-American Comics #19 (October 1940). Albert "Al" Pratt was a diminutive college student (5 ft 1 in) who made up in ferocity what he lacked in height, training himself into a formidable hand-to-hand fighter. He was a founding member of the Justice Society of America. In later stories he acquired superhuman strength and an "atomic punch" after exposure to radiation in a battle with the villain Cyclotron.

Ray Palmer

The Silver Age Atom was created for the superhero revival orchestrated by editor Julius Schwartz, with writer Gardner Fox and artist Gil Kane, in Showcase #34 (1961). Ray Palmer, a physics professor at Ivy University, used a lens made from a fragment of a white dwarf star to compress matter; after surviving an accident that left him uniquely able to control his own size and weight, he adopted the Atom identity. Palmer could shrink to any size — even subatomic — while exploiting the square–cube law in reverse, hitting with the force of his full-size body. He became a mainstay of the Justice League and married lawyer Jean Loring, whose later breakdown was central to the 2004 storyline Identity Crisis.

Other Atoms

Adam Cray, son of a murdered senator, briefly used Palmer's equipment in the Suicide Squad series. Ryan Choi, a Hong Kong physicist and Palmer's protégé, took over in 2006 in The All-New Atom, written by Gail Simone. The character has also appeared in animation (Justice League Unlimited, Young Justice) and in live action, played by Brandon Routh in the Arrowverse television series.
