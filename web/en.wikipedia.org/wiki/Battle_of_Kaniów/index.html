The Battle of Kaniów (Polish: Bitwa pod Kaniowem) was fought on 10–11 May 1918 near Kaniv in Ukraine between the Polish II Corps under General Józef Haller and German forces. It was one of the most famous engagements fought by Polish formations in the First World War, arising from a Polish refusal to submit to German disarmament.

Background

The Polish II Corps had been formed from Polish units of the former Russian army and auxiliary formations transferred after the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk. Ordered by the Germans to disband and lay down its arms in the spring of 1918, the corps instead marched to unite with Polish forces elsewhere, crossing near Kaniv on the Dnieper.

Battle

On the night of 10–11 May German troops attacked the Polish positions around Kaniów. The Poles defended fiercely, inflicting notable casualties, but, encircled and running short of ammunition against superior numbers supported by artillery, General Haller ordered a capitulation on 11 May to avoid annihilation. Haller himself escaped, travelling via Moscow and Murmansk to France, where he took command of the Polish Army in France (the "Blue Army").

Aftermath

Polish soldiers were interned by the Germans; many rank and file later escaped. In Polish tradition the battle stands as a symbol of national honour — a fight against overwhelming odds for the right to an independent Polish army.
