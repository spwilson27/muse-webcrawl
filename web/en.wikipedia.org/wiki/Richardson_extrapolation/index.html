Richardson extrapolation

In numerical analysis, Richardson extrapolation is a sequence acceleration method used to improve the rate of convergence of a sequence of estimates of some value. It is named after Lewis Fry Richardson, who introduced it in the early 20th century.

Method

Suppose an approximation A(h) to an exact value A depends on a step size h with an error expansion A(h) = A + c1 h^p + c2 h^(p+1) + ... . Computing A at two step sizes h and h/2 and combining them eliminates the leading error term, yielding a higher-order estimate. Repeating the process builds a Richardson table.

Applications

The technique underlies Romberg integration for numerical quadrature, the Bulirsch–Stoer algorithm for differential equations, and extrapolated finite-difference approximations to derivatives. It is also applied in practice to deferred-correction methods and in estimating discretization error.
