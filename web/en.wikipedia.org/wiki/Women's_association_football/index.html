Women's association football

Women's association football, more commonly known as women's football or women's soccer, is the team sport of association football played by women. It is played at professional level in multiple countries and 187 national teams participate internationally. The same rules, known as the Laws of the Game, apply to both men's and women's football.

History

The game was first played by women in the late 18th century, although historical instances of women participating in ball games date back much earlier. The first evidence of women playing organised football comes from Scotland in the 1790s. During the First World War, women's football in England grew enormously in popularity, with teams such as Dick, Kerr Ladies drawing crowds of over 50,000. In 1921, however, the Football Association banned women from playing on FA pitches, a prohibition that lasted until 1971. The ban had a similar chilling effect in other countries.

Governing bodies

The primary governing body of women's football is FIFA, which organises the FIFA Women's World Cup, first held in China in 1991 and won by the United States. The UEFA Women's Championship and the UEFA Women's Champions League are the principal competitions in Europe.

World Cup

The Women's World Cup has grown steadily in audience and participation. The 2019 tournament in France attracted a global audience of over one billion viewers, and the 2023 edition in Australia and New Zealand expanded to 32 teams, rivalling the men's tournament in format. The United States is the most successful team, with four titles.

Professional leagues

Professional leagues have developed in countries including the United States (the National Women's Soccer League), England (the Women's Super League), Spain, France and Germany. Attendance, broadcast deals and sponsorship have grown rapidly since the late 2010s.
