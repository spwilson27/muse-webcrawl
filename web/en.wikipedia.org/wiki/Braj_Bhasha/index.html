Braj Bhasha, also called Braj Bhakha or Brij Bhasha, is a Western Hindi language of the Central Indo-Aryan group. It is spoken in the Braj region of Uttar Pradesh, centered on the cities of Mathura, Agra, Aligarh, and Vrindavan. Along with Awadhi, it was one of the two predominant literary languages of North-Central India before the rise of standard Hindi in the 19th century.

Literature

Braj Bhasha was the principal language of devotional poetry dedicated to Krishna, including the works of Surdas, whose Sur Sagar is a landmark of Hindi literature. Amir Khusrau and many bhakti poets also composed in it. Mirza Ghalib wrote some early verse in Braj before turning to Urdu and Persian.

Status

Today Braj Bhasha is used chiefly as a regional spoken language and in religious and folk performance, while formal writing in the region uses standard Hindi.
