Context (linguistics)

In linguistics, context is the set of circumstances surrounding a word, utterance, or text that contributes to its meaning. Context includes the surrounding words and sentences (the linguistic or co-textual context), the physical and social situation in which communication occurs, and the shared knowledge and expectations of the participants. The study of how context influences meaning is central to pragmatics and sociolinguistics.

FieldPragmatics, sociolinguistics, discourse analysis
TypesLinguistic (co-text), situational, social/cultural
Key figuresBronisław Malinowski, J. R. Firth, Dell Hymes

Types of context

Anthropologist Bronisław Malinowski coined the phrase "context of situation" in 1923 to argue that utterances can only be understood within the activity and culture in which they occur; the linguist J. R. Firth developed the idea within a theory of meaning. Dell Hymes later proposed the SPEAKING model for describing the components of a communicative event. Linguistic context, or co-text, is the text surrounding a unit of analysis; words are routinely disambiguated by co-text, as with homonyms such as "bank".

Role in interpretation

Context resolves deixis (expressions such as "here", "now", and "you" that point to the speech situation), supports conversational implicature in the sense of H. P. Grice, and determines politeness and register. Modern approaches, such as relevance theory, treat context as a set of assumptions selected by the hearer during interpretation rather than as fixed background. In computational linguistics, word embeddings and transformer language models represent meaning largely through context.
