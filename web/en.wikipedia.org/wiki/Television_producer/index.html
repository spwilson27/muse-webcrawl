Television producer

A television producer is a person who oversees one or more aspects of television production. Unlike film, where the director is usually the dominant creative figure, in television the producer — particularly the executive producer/showrunner — traditionally holds ultimate creative and managerial authority over a programme.

Roles and titles

  • Executive producer — the senior producer, often the showrunner who created or runs the series; responsible for creative direction, budgets and staffing.
  • Co-executive producer, supervising producer, producer, co-producer — descending ranks typically held by senior writers on scripted series.
  • Associate producer — an entry-to-mid-level role handling segments, research, clearances or post-production tasks.
  • Line producer — manages the day-to-day physical production and budget.
  • Segment/field producer — common in news, reality and entertainment formats, responsible for individual items or shoots.

Responsibilities

Television producers develop concepts and formats, hire writers, directors and presenters, supervise scripts and edits, manage schedules and budgets, and liaise with networks and commissioners. On writer-producer series, producers write episodes and supervise the writers' room, while in factual and entertainment television they may shape stories in the edit.

Credits and awards

Producer titles are governed in the United States by Writers Guild and Producers Guild conventions and are a major currency of the industry, reflecting the writer-producer career ladder that distinguishes television from film.
