Christmas in the Philippines

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christmas in the Philippines is one of the longest and most widely observed holiday seasons in the world, reflecting the country's predominantly Roman Catholic heritage from over three centuries of Spanish colonial rule. Festivities are commonly said to begin in the "ber" months — as early as September — and continue until the Feast of the Epiphany or beyond, culminating in major celebrations from mid-December to New Year's Day.

Traditions

  • Parol: the star-shaped lantern symbolising the Star of Bethlehem is hung in homes, offices and streets; the city of San Fernando, Pampanga, famous for its Giant Lantern Festival, is styled the "Christmas Capital of the Philippines."
  • Simbang Gabi: a novena of nine dawn Masses from 16 to 24 December, ending with the Christmas Eve Misa de Gallo; after Mass, vendors sell bibingka (rice cakes) and puto bumbong.
  • Nochebuena: the midnight family feast on Christmas Eve, featuring lechón, queso de bola, ham, and pancit.
  • Caroling and aguinaldo: children sing carols house to house and visit godparents on Christmas Day to receive gifts (aguinaldo).
  • Belén: nativity scenes are displayed in homes, churches and public buildings.

Season and observances

Christmas music fills radio and malls from September, and overseas Filipino workers' remittances and homecomings mark the season. Public holidays fall on 25 December and around the New Year; many communities extend celebrations to the Feast of the Santo Niño (Sinulog) on the third Sunday of January. In a country with significant Muslim and other minorities, Christmas functions both as a religious feast and a broadly shared national cultural season.
