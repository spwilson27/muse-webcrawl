National Parks of Canada

The National Parks of Canada are protected natural spaces throughout the country administered by Parks Canada, a government agency. The system represents Canada's 39 distinct terrestrial natural regions, with the goal of protecting representative examples of each. Canada's first national park, Banff, began as a reserve in 1885 around hot springs in the Rocky Mountains and was formally established in 1887 as Rocky Mountains Park.

System

There are more than 40 national parks and national park reserves, ranging from Banff and Jasper in Alberta to vast Arctic parks such as Quttinirpaaq on Ellesmere Island. National park reserves, such as Pacific Rim and Gwaii Haanas, are areas set aside for future parks pending settlement of Indigenous land claims. The system also includes national marine conservation areas, such as Fathom Five and Saguenay–St. Lawrence.

Legislation

The parks are governed by the Canada National Parks Act of 2000, which gives priority to ecological integrity in park management. Parks Canada, established in 1911 as the Dominion Parks Branch — the world's first national park service — also manages national historic sites.

Notable parks

Well-known parks include Banff, Jasper, Yoho, and Kootenay in the Rockies; Wood Buffalo, Canada's largest, straddling Alberta and the Northwest Territories; Gros Morne in Newfoundland; and the Torngat Mountains in Labrador, managed in cooperation with Inuit.
