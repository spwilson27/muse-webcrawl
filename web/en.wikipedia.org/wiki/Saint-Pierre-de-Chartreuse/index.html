Saint-Pierre-de-Chartreuse is a commune in the Isère department in southeastern France, situated in the Chartreuse Mountains between Grenoble and Chambéry. The village is best known as the site of the Grande Chartreuse, the head monastery of the Carthusian Order, founded by Saint Bruno in 1084 in the nearby desert of Chartreuse.

Geography

The commune lies in the heart of the Chartreuse massif, a range of the French Prealps. Its territory includes high limestone peaks such as Chamechaude (2,082 m), the highest summit of the massif, and the Charmant Som. The Guiers Mort river rises within the commune. Much of the area is protected as part of the Chartreuse Regional Natural Park, established in 1995.

History

In 1084, Bruno of Cologne and six companions settled in the remote valley above the present village, founding the hermitage that became the Grande Chartreuse. The monks later developed the famous Chartreuse liqueur, distilled from a secret recipe of 130 herbs and plants; the distillery and the museum of the Grande Chartreuse are located in and near the village. The monastery itself, in the neighbouring commune area reserved for contemplative silence, is not open to visitors, but the Correrie museum nearby presents Carthusian life.

Economy and tourism

Saint-Pierre-de-Chartreuse is a centre for outdoor recreation. In winter it offers downhill skiing at the Planolet and La Scia stations and extensive cross-country skiing; in summer it is a base for hiking, mountain biking, and paragliding. The village hosts events including trail races in the Chartreuse mountains.
