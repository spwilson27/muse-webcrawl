The Concert (Dutch: Het concert) is a painting by the Dutch artist Johannes Vermeer, dating to about 1664. It depicts three musicians: a young woman playing the harpsichord, a man playing the lute, and a second woman singing, in a sunlit interior with paintings on the walls — including Dirck van Baburen's The Procuress — and a viola da gamba resting on the marble floor. The work is considered the most valuable painting ever stolen, with an estimated value exceeding US$250 million.

History

The painting was bought by collector Isabella Stewart Gardner at auction in Paris in 1892 for $5,000 and displayed in her Fenway Court museum in Boston. In the early hours of March 18, 1990, two thieves disguised as police officers entered the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum and stole The Concert together with twelve other works, including Vermeer's painting and Rembrandt's The Storm on the Sea of Galilee — the largest art theft in American history. Because the museum's will forbids altering the arrangement of the collection, the empty frames of the stolen works still hang on the gallery walls.

Investigation

The theft remains unsolved. The FBI has pursued leads involving Boston organized crime for decades, and a substantial reward — raised to $10 million — remains offered for the works' return. The statute of limitations for the theft itself has expired, but no painting from the Gardner heist has ever been recovered, making The Concert the most famous missing Vermeer of the roughly 34 paintings accepted as his.
