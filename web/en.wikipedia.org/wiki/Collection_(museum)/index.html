Collection (museum)

Most museum collections spend much of their life in curated storage, not on display.

A collection in a museum is the assembled body of objects that the institution acquires, documents, preserves and makes accessible for exhibition, research and education. Building and caring for a collection is the defining function that distinguishes a museum from a temporary exhibition venue.

Collections management

Professional practice, known as collections management, covers acquisition (by purchase, gift, bequest, fieldwork or exchange), accessioning and cataloguing, documentation and provenance research, preventive conservation and storage, loans to other institutions, and deaccessioning—the formal removal of objects, governed by ethical codes that typically restrict the use of proceeds to new acquisitions or direct collection care.

Scope and policy

Each museum adopts a collections development policy defining what it collects and why. Collections range from encyclopaedic holdings of millions of objects in national museums to highly specialised single-theme collections. Only a small fraction—often under 10%—is on display at any time; the remainder is held in study storage and increasingly published through online collection databases.

Issues

Contemporary debates concern the repatriation of cultural property (such as the Benin Bronzes and Parthenon Marbles), the provenance of objects acquired in colonial contexts, the costs and carbon footprint of indefinite storage, and digitisation priorities.
