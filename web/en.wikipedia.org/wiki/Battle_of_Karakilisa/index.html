Battle of Karakilisa

The Battle of Karakilisa was fought from 25 to 28 May 1918, during the Caucasus campaign of the First World War, near the town of Karakilisa (now Vanadzor, Armenia). Armenian forces under General Tovmas Nazarbekian made a determined stand against the advancing Ottoman army, one of three simultaneous battles — with Sardarabad and Bash Abaran — that saved Armenian-populated territory from complete occupation and made possible the proclamation of the First Republic of Armenia.

Background

After the collapse of the Russian Caucasus front and the Ottoman recovery of Erzurum, Kars and Alexandropol in the spring of 1918, Ottoman columns advanced on three axes into the Ararat plain and toward Yerevan. Nazarbekian's Armenian corps fell back to prepared positions around Karakilisa, covering the Dzoraget and Pambak valleys against the Ottoman drive from Alexandropol.

The battle

From 25 May the Armenians resisted repeated assaults on their positions for four days. On 28 May, after heavy fighting and with ammunition running short, Ottoman troops broke through and occupied Karakilisa, inflicting severe losses and visiting reprisals on the town; Armenian reports describe the killing of thousands of inhabitants in the town and surrounding villages. However, the delay, combined with the Armenian victories at Sardarabad and Bash Abaran, prevented the Ottomans from exploiting toward Yerevan.

Aftermath

On 28 May 1918 the Armenian National Council proclaimed the independence of the Republic of Armenia. By the Treaty of Batum (4 June 1918) the Ottomans kept Karakilisa and the other occupied districts, but Armenian statehood survived. In memory of the three May battles, Karakilisa's sacrifice is commemorated alongside the Sardarabad memorial complex as part of Armenia's founding struggle.
