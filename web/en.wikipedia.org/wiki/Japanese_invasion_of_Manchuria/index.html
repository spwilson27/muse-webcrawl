The Japanese invasion of Manchuria began on 18 September 1931, immediately following the Mukden Incident, a staged explosion on the Japanese-owned South Manchuria Railway that officers of Japan's Kwantung Army used as a pretext to seize the region. Within five months, Japanese forces occupied all of Manchuria in northeastern China, and in March 1932 established the puppet state of Manchukuo. The invasion is often regarded as a starting point of the Second Sino-Japanese War and, by some historians, of the Second World War in Asia.

Background

Japan had acquired railway rights and the Kwantung Leased Territory in southern Manchuria after the Russo-Japanese War. Amid the Great Depression, Japanese militarists argued that Manchuria's land and resources were a lifeline for Japan. The Chinese warlord governing Manchuria, Zhang Xueliang, nominally loyal to the Nationalist government in Nanjing, pursued a policy of non-resistance partly from military weakness.

The invasion

On the night of 18 September 1931, Kwantung Army officers detonated a small charge beside the railway near Mukden (Shenyang) and blamed Chinese troops. Acting without authorisation from Tokyo, the army attacked the Mukden garrison and swiftly took the major cities along the railway. Despite orders of non-resistance, some Chinese units fought localised actions; by February 1932, with the fall of Harbin and Jinzhou, the whole of Manchuria was under Japanese control.

Aftermath and international response

Japan installed Puyi, the last Qing emperor, first as chief executive and later as emperor of Manchukuo. China appealed to the League of Nations, whose Lytton Commission reported in 1932 that Manchuria should not remain under Japanese control; the League adopted the report in 1933, and Japan responded by withdrawing from the League. The United States issued the Stimson Doctrine of non-recognition of territorial changes made by force. "Pacification" campaigns against Chinese resistance continued for years, and the conquered region became a base for further expansion into northern China.
