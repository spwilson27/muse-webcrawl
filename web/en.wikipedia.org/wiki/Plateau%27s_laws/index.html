Plateau's laws

Plateau's laws describe the structure of soap films and foams. They were formulated by the Belgian physicist Joseph Plateau on the basis of his experimental observations in the nineteenth century, and were later proved mathematically, notably by Jean Taylor in 1976 using geometric measure theory.

The laws

The laws state that: (1) soap films are made of smooth surfaces; (2) the mean curvature of a portion of a film is constant everywhere on that portion; (3) films meet three at a time along a curve (a Plateau border) at angles of 120 degrees; and (4) these curves meet four at a time at a point, at the tetrahedral angle of about 109.47 degrees.

Explanation

The laws follow from the minimisation of surface area (and hence surface energy) under constraints. The 120° and tetrahedral angles reflect the balance of equal surface tensions meeting at edges and vertices. Closely related phenomena appear in the physics of foams, emulsions, and grain boundaries in metallurgy.
