The Battle of Arracourt took place between 18 and 29 September 1944 near the town of Arracourt in Lorraine, France, during the Second World War. It was one of the largest armoured engagements of the western front, pitting the United States 4th Armored Division against elements of the German Fifth Panzer Army.

Background

In September 1944, General George S. Patton's Third Army advanced across France but stalled in Lorraine as supplies ran short and German resistance stiffened around Metz and Nancy. Adolf Hitler ordered a counteroffensive by Hasso von Manteuffel's Fifth Panzer Army, built around new Panzer Brigades equipped with Panther tanks, to cut off the American bridgehead over the Moselle and recapture Lunéville.

The battle

German attacks began on 18 September in heavy fog. The inexperienced Panzer Brigades — many crews had barely trained together — attacked piecemeal and without adequate reconnaissance or air cover. When the fog lifted, American fighter-bombers and the well-handled Sherman tanks and M18 Hellcat tank destroyers of Combat Command A, 4th Armored Division, under Colonel Bruce C. Clarke, took a heavy toll. Fighting raged around Arracourt, Juvelize and Lezey over the following days.

Outcome

The German counteroffensive failed with the loss of well over 200 tanks and assault guns, while the 4th Armored Division lost a fraction of that number. The battle is frequently cited as an example of how training, combined-arms coordination and air support could offset the technical superiority of German armour such as the Panther.
