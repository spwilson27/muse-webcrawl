"You Really Got Me" is a song by the English rock band the Kinks, written by the group's lead singer and rhythm guitarist Ray Davies. Released as a single in August 1964, it became the band's breakthrough hit, reaching number one on the UK Singles Chart and number seven on the US Billboard Hot 100.

The song is built around a two-chord riff played on a distorted electric guitar by lead guitarist Dave Davies. Its raw, aggressive sound is widely credited with helping to invent hard rock and heavy metal guitar styles, and the track is frequently cited as an influence on punk rock and garage rock.

Background and recording

Ray Davies composed the song in early 1964, initially imagining it as a lighter, jazz-influenced number. During rehearsals the band reworked it around Dave Davies's guitar riff. According to a well-known account, Dave Davies achieved the song's distinctive distorted tone by slashing the speaker cone of his small Elpico amplifier with a razor blade and poking it with a pin; he nicknamed the damaged amp "little green". The record was produced by Shel Talmy and recorded at IBC Studios in London in July 1964.

Pye Records had given the Kinks one last chance after their first two singles failed to chart. The band insisted on re-recording "You Really Got Me" after an unsatisfying first attempt, famously funding the session themselves despite the label's reluctance.

Composition

The song is in the key of G major and is built on a repeated power-chord riff moving between G and A (with the riff later shifting up to A and B). Session musician claims have periodically surfaced regarding the guitar solo; although Jimmy Page played on other Kinks sessions of the era, the solo on "You Really Got Me" is generally credited to Dave Davies.

Reception and legacy

The single topped the UK chart for two weeks in September 1964 and launched the Kinks' career during the British Invasion. In the United States it reached No. 7. Rolling Stone placed the song on its list of the 500 Greatest Songs of All Time, and it has been inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame.

Van Halen version

American hard rock band Van Halen covered the song on their 1978 debut album, releasing it as their first single. Their version reached No. 36 on the Billboard Hot 100 and introduced the band's virtuoso guitarist Eddie Van Halen to a wide audience, helping to revive interest in the original.
