Sealaska Corporation

Sealaska Corporation is an Alaska Native regional corporation established under the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act of 1971 (ANCSA). Headquartered in Juneau, it is the regional corporation for Southeast Alaska, representing Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian shareholders.

History

Sealaska was incorporated in 1972 following the settlement of Indigenous land claims in Alaska. Under ANCSA it received land and monetary compensation; its land holdings, concentrated around the Tongass National Forest region, eventually totalled roughly 290,000 acres of surface estate, with additional subsurface and other entitlements resolved over subsequent decades.

Business

Historically, timber harvesting on its lands was Sealaska's principal business, drawing both revenue and controversy over old-growth logging and exports. The corporation has since diversified into areas including seafood, environmental services, construction and natural food products, and it advocates for shareholder priorities such as sustainable forestry and carbon offset projects on its lands.

Shareholders

Sealaska has more than 20,000 Alaska Native shareholders. It provides dividends, scholarships through the Sealaska Heritage Institute, and cultural programmes supporting Indigenous languages and arts of Southeast Alaska.
