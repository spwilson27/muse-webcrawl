Automated Clearing House

The Automated Clearing House (ACH) is a United States computer-based electronic network for processing transactions, usually domestic low-value payments, between participating financial institutions. It may support both credit transfers and direct debits. The ACH system is governed by Nacha (the National Automated Clearing House Association) and is operated by the Federal Reserve Banks (FedACH) and the Electronic Payments Network (EPN), operated by The Clearing House.

History

The ACH network grew out of regional clearing house associations formed in the early 1970s to handle the growing volume of paper cheques. The first ACH association was formed in California in 1972; Nacha was founded in 1974 to establish standardised rules, and by 1978 banks nationwide could exchange ACH payments electronically. The network expanded from payroll direct deposit to consumer bill payments, tax refunds and business-to-business transactions.

How it works

ACH payments are batch-processed: an originator instructs its bank (the ODFI), which collects transactions into batches and transmits them to an ACH operator. The operator sorts the entries and delivers them to the receiving banks (RDFIs), which post credits or debits to customers' accounts. Settlement between institutions occurs through accounts at the Federal Reserve. Because of batching, traditional ACH transfers typically take one to three business days, though same-day ACH, introduced in 2016, allows settlement within the same business day for eligible transactions.

Uses

Common uses include direct deposit of salaries and government benefits, direct payment of mortgages, utilities and insurance premiums, person-to-person payments, e-commerce purchases, and federal and state tax payments and refunds. Standard Entry Class (SEC) codes distinguish transaction types such as PPD (prearranged consumer payments) and CCD (business payments).

Comparison with other systems

ACH is slower but far cheaper than wire transfer systems such as Fedwire, which settle individually in real time. It is comparable to giro and direct debit systems in other countries, such as Bacs in the United Kingdom and the SEPA schemes in Europe. Newer instant payment systems, including the RTP network and the Federal Reserve's FedNow service (launched 2023), complement ACH with immediate settlement.
