Al-Jinn (Arabic: الجنّ, "The Jinn") is the 72nd chapter (surah) of the Qur'an, containing 28 verses (ayat). It is classified as a Meccan surah and takes its name from the jinn — beings created from smokeless fire in Islamic belief — whose speech occupies most of the chapter.

Contents

The surah opens: "Say: It has been revealed to me that a group of the jinn listened and said, 'Indeed, we have heard an amazing Qur'an. It guides to the right course, and we have believed in it. And we will never associate with our Lord anyone.'" The jinn repudiate the polytheistic beliefs attributed to them, acknowledging that they had wrongly imagined things about God, and recount that they used to sit in the heavens to eavesdrop but now find them filled with guards and flaming projectiles.

The jinn describe their own diversity — "among us are the righteous, and among us are [others] not so" — and their division into pathways and factions. They affirm that mosques belong to God and that the Prophet's call should not be combined with worship of any other. The surah closes by reserving knowledge of the unseen to God, who discloses it only to a messenger He approves, guarded by watchers before and behind him.

Context

Traditional accounts connect the revelation with Muhammad's return from Ta'if, when a group of jinn heard him reciting the Qur'an at Nakhla — echoing another passage (Al-Ahqaf 29–32) in which jinn who heard the recitation returned to their people as warners. The chapter is frequently cited in Islamic theology for its picture of the jinn as morally accountable beings, addressed by revelation alongside humankind.
