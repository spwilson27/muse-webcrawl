Colloquium

A colloquium (plural: colloquia or colloquiums) is a type of academic meeting or seminar. The word derives from the Latin colloquium, meaning "conversation" or "conference".

Academic usage

In universities, a colloquium typically denotes a regular series of research talks, in which a speaker — often a visiting scholar — presents their current work to colleagues and graduate students, followed by discussion. Unlike a large conference, a departmental colloquium is usually a single talk with substantial time for questions, and attendance is open within the institution. The term overlaps with seminar, though a seminar more often implies a smaller, recurring class-like group, while a colloquium is usually a public lecture-style event.

Other usages

Historically, "colloquium" also named formal religious conferences held to resolve doctrinal disputes, such as the Colloquy of Marburg (1529) between Lutheran and Zwinglian theologians. In some higher education systems, notably in parts of continental Europe, the colloquium is an oral examination or the public discussion component of a doctoral defence. In law, colloquium is the part of a defamation pleading that explains how the words complained of refer to the plaintiff.
