Ring road

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A ring road (also known as a beltway, beltline, loop or orbital motorway) is a road or a series of connected roads encircling a city, metropolitan area or region. Ring roads are typically designed to relieve congestion in the centre by allowing through traffic to bypass it, and to connect radial routes and suburbs to one another without entering downtown.

Purpose and effects

Ring roads improve access between suburban districts and distribute long-distance traffic around urban cores. However, they can also encourage urban sprawl and car-dependent development at their interchanges, and may fill with local traffic — a phenomenon related to induced demand. Some cities have removed or proposed removing inner ring roads built in the postwar era in order to reconnect neighbourhoods.

Notable examples

  • M25 around London — one of the world's busiest orbital motorways, 188 km long.
  • Berliner Ring (A10) — 196 km, among Europe's longest ring motorways.
  • Boulevard Périphérique in Paris and the much larger Francilienne further out.
  • Capital Beltway (I-495) around Washington, D.C., which gave American politics the phrase "inside the Beltway".
  • Beijing's numbered ring roads (2nd through 7th), the 7th extending over 900 km.
  • MKAD around Moscow, and larger outer rings built beyond it.

Variants

Some cities have multiple concentric rings (inner, middle and outer), partial arcs where geography prevents a full loop (coastal cities), or ring railways serving a similar distributing role for public transport, such as Berlin's Ringbahn and Tokyo's Yamanote Line.
