Albert Finney

Albert Finney in the 1960s

Albert Finney (9 May 1936 – 7 February 2019) was an English actor. Rising to fame in the British New Wave with Saturday Night and Sunday Morning (1960), he became one of the most acclaimed actors of his generation, receiving five Academy Award nominations for Tom Jones (1963), Murder on the Orient Express (1974), The Dresser (1983), Under the Volcano (1984) and Erin Brockovich (2000). He won Golden Globe, BAFTA and Emmy awards across film, television and stage.

Career

Finney, the son of a bookmaker, trained at the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art and worked in repertory theatre and with the Royal Shakespeare Company. His portrayal of the defiant factory worker Arthur Seaton in Karel Reisz's Saturday Night and Sunday Morning made him a star and an emblem of the "angry young man". International success followed with Tony Richardson's Tom Jones, one of the biggest hits of the 1960s. He later played Hercule Poirot in the all-star Murder on the Orient Express, Ebenezer Scrooge in Scrooge (1970), and the alcoholic consul in Under the Volcano. In his later years he gave admired performances as Winston Churchill in the television film The Gathering Storm (2002), winning Emmy, Golden Globe and BAFTA awards, and appeared in Big Fish (2003), The Bourne Ultimatum (2007) and Skyfall (2012), his final film.

Honours

Finney twice declined official honours, turning down a CBE in 1980 and a knighthood in 2000, saying he did not wish to be ranked within such a system.
