Erwin Wilhelm Müller

Born13 June 1911, Berlin, German Empire
Died17 May 1977, Washington, D.C., U.S.
FieldsSurface physics
Known forField emission microscope; field ion microscope; atom probe

Erwin Wilhelm Müller (13 June 1911 – 17 May 1977) was a German physicist who invented the field emission microscope and the field ion microscope, the first instrument able to resolve individual atoms, and who later developed the atom probe.

Field microscopes

Müller built the field emission microscope in 1936, using electrons tunnelling from a sharp metal tip under a strong electric field to project an image of the tip surface. In 1951 he introduced the field ion microscope, in which ionised gas atoms striking the tip produce the image; in 1955 he and his student Kanwar Bahadur obtained the first images showing individual atoms on a tungsten tip.

Career and the atom probe

After posts in Berlin and at the Fritz Haber Institute, Müller emigrated to the United States in 1952, joining Pennsylvania State University, where he remained for the rest of his career. In 1967, with J. A. Panitz and S. Brooks McLane, he introduced the atom-probe field ion microscope, which identifies individual imaged atoms by mass spectrometry — the ancestor of modern atom probe tomography.
