The Macartney Embassy (1792–1794), also called the Macartney Mission, was the first British diplomatic mission to China. Led by George Macartney, 1st Earl Macartney, it sailed in the 64-gun HMS Lion with a large retinue of scientists, artists and guards and a cargo of British manufactures, seeking to open new ports to trade, establish a resident ambassador in Beijing, and secure a trading island off the Chinese coast. The Qianlong Emperor refused every request.

The kowtow dispute

The mission's most famous incident concerned ceremonial. Qing officials expected Macartney to perform the kowtow — three kneelings, each with three prostrations knocking the head on the floor — as a tribute envoy. Macartney refused to do more for the emperor than he would for his own sovereign, offering to kneel on one knee as before George III. A compromise was reached and he was received in audience at the imperial summer retreat of Chengde on 14 September 1793, the emperor's birthday.

Rejection

The presents — clocks, telescopes, a planetarium, artillery and Wedgwood china — were accepted as tribute but did not persuade. Qianlong's famous edict to George III replied that the Celestial Empire possessed all things in abundance and had no need of British manufactures, that no resident envoy could be admitted contrary to precedent, and that trade would remain confined to Canton under the existing system. The mission travelled overland through China to Canton and sailed home in 1794.

Significance

Long portrayed as a clash between a dynamic free-trading Britain and a stagnant, arrogant China doomed to fall behind, the embassy has been reassessed by historians who stress Qing security concerns and the reasonableness of the refusal within the tributary framework. The failure nonetheless foreshadowed the hardening conflict that erupted in the First Opium War of 1839–42. The mission's records, including Sir George Staunton's official account and John Barrow's Travels in China, gave Europe its most detailed contemporary picture of Qing China.
