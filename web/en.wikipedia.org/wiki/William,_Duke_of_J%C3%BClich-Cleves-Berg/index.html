William, Duke of Jülich-Cleves-Berg

William of Jülich-Cleves-Berg (German: Wilhelm von Jülich-Kleve-Berg; 28 July 1516 – 5 January 1592), nicknamed William the Rich, was a Duke of Jülich-Cleves-Berg (1539–1592). He bore several other titles, including Count of Mark and Ravensberg.

Life

William was born in Düsseldorf, the only son of John III, Duke of Jülich-Cleves-Berg, and Maria, Duchess of Jülich-Berg. On his father's death in 1539 he inherited the United Duchies, one of the largest and richest principalities of the Holy Roman Empire, strategically located on the lower Rhine.

Guelders dispute

William claimed the Duchy of Guelders, leading to war with Emperor Charles V. By the Treaty of Venlo (1543) he was forced to surrender Guelders and Zutphen and to align himself with Imperial policy. The conflict also dissolved his brief marriage to Jeanne d'Albret, heiress of Navarre, which had been arranged to support a French alliance.

Family

In 1546 William married Maria of Austria, daughter of Emperor Ferdinand I. Their children included John William, who succeeded him, and Anna of Cleves should not be confused—Anna, queen consort of Henry VIII of England, was William's elder sister. William died in 1592.
