Canon EOS R

Overview
Maker: Canon Inc.
Type: Full-frame mirrorless interchangeable-lens camera
Released: October 2018
Sensor: 30.3 MP full-frame CMOS
Lens mount: Canon RF

The Canon EOS R is a full-frame mirrorless interchangeable-lens camera produced by Canon. Announced on 5 September 2018 and released the following month, it was Canon's first full-frame mirrorless camera and the launch body for the new Canon RF lens mount.

Features

The EOS R uses a 30.3-megapixel full-frame CMOS sensor closely related to that of the EOS 5D Mark IV DSLR, with a DIGIC 8 processor, an ISO range of 100–40,000 (expandable), and Dual Pixel CMOS autofocus with a claimed 5,655 selectable AF points. It offers 4K video at up to 30 frames per second (with a substantial crop), a 3.69-million-dot electronic viewfinder, a fully articulating touchscreen and a top-panel LCD. Notable design choices include a single SD card slot, no in-body image stabilisation, and a touch-sensitive "M-Fn bar" behind the shutter button, which received mixed reviews and was dropped from later models.

Reception and legacy

Reviewers praised the camera's image quality, autofocus in stills and the quality of the initial RF lenses, while criticising the video crop, single card slot and lack of in-body stabilisation. The EOS R established the RF system, which Canon expanded with bodies such as the EOS RP, R5, R6 and R3, and it was gradually succeeded in its market position by the EOS R6 Mark II.
