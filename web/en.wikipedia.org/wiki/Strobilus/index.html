Strobilus

Strobili of various plants: the reproductive cones of lycophytes and conifers

A strobilus (plural: strobili) is a structure present on many land plant species consisting of sporangium-bearing structures (sporophylls) densely aggregated along a stem. Strobili are often called cones, but some botanists restrict the term cone to the woody seed strobili of conifers.

Occurrence

Strobili are characteristic of clubmosses (lycophytes) such as Lycopodium and Selaginella, in which sporophylls are gathered into compact cones at the tips of stems. Horsetails (Equisetum) bear their sporangia in strobili at the apex of reproductive stems. Among seed plants, all living conifers produce separate pollen cones (male strobili) and seed cones (female strobili). Cycads also produce large, often massive cones, and the flowers of flowering plants are sometimes interpreted as modified strobili — the "anthostrobilus" theory of flower origins.

Structure

A typical strobilus consists of a central axis bearing modified leaves called sporophylls, each carrying one or more sporangia in its axil or on its upper surface. In heterosporous plants, such as Selaginella, a single strobilus may contain both megasporophylls and microsporophylls. Hop "cones", used in brewing, are also strobili — the female inflorescences of the hop plant.
