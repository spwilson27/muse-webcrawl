Clarinet family

The clarinet family is a large family of single-reed woodwind instruments sharing the clarinet's cylindrical bore and overblowing behaviour: a clarinet overblows at the twelfth rather than the octave, giving it a distinctive hollow timbre in its low (chalumeau) register. Members range from the tiny sopranino in A♭ to the huge contrabass instruments.

Members

The most common members are the soprano clarinets in B♭ and A, the standard instruments of the orchestra and band. Around them are arrayed the high E♭ sopranino (and rarer D, C and A♭ instruments), the alto clarinet in E♭, the basset horn in F, the bass clarinet in B♭, and the contra-alto and contrabass clarinets, which descend to depths of over two metres of tubing, often folded and made of metal or plastic.

History

The clarinet was invented around 1700 by Johann Christoph Denner in Nuremberg, adapted from the chalumeau by adding a register key. Makers such as Adolphe Sax and the firms of Buffet and Boehm developed the family during the 19th century; composers including Mozart, Weber and Brahms made the soprano clarinet a solo instrument, while Mahler, Stravinsky and the big bands exploited the extremes of the family.
