Poʻouli

The poʻouli (Melamprosops phaeosoma), also called the black-faced honeycreeper, was a species of Hawaiian honeycreeper endemic to the island of Maui. It was discovered only in 1973, in the remote rainforests of Haleakalā, and was declared extinct in 2021, the last known individual having died in captivity in 2004.

Description

The poʻouli was a stocky, short-tailed bird about 14 cm long, brown above and pale below, with a striking black face mask extending from the bill to the cheeks, a short stubby bill and large feet adapted for foraging on tree trunks and branches.

Ecology

It fed on native land snails, insects and other invertebrates gleaned from bark and foliage in wet ʻōhiʻa forest between about 1,400 and 2,100 metres elevation on the windward slopes of east Maui.

Decline and extinction

At discovery the population was estimated at roughly 200 birds; it crashed because of habitat degradation by feral pigs, predation by rats and mongooses, mosquito-borne avian malaria, and the decline of native snail prey. By 1997 only three individuals were known. Captive-breeding attempts failed, and the final bird died at the Maui Bird Conservation Center on 26 November 2004. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service formally declared the species extinct in 2021.
