Unilateral Declaration of Independence (Rhodesia)

The Unilateral Declaration of Independence (UDI) was a statement adopted by the Cabinet of Rhodesia on 11 November 1965, announcing that the self-governing British colony of Southern Rhodesia would no longer accept British rule. Prime Minister Ian Smith's government sought to preserve white minority rule rather than accept Britain's condition of progress toward majority rule ("no independence before majority African rule").

Date11 November 1965
SignatoryIan Smith and Cabinet
StatusNever internationally recognised

Background

After the Central African Federation dissolved in 1963, Northern Rhodesia (Zambia) and Nyasaland (Malawi) moved to independence under majority rule, while negotiations over Southern Rhodesia stalled. Smith's Rhodesian Front won the 1965 election on an independence platform; talks with Harold Wilson's government in London and aboard HMS Tiger and HMS Fearless failed.

Consequences

Britain declared UDI illegal, the UN Security Council called it invalid (Resolutions 216 and 217) and imposed the first mandatory UN economic sanctions. No country recognised Rhodesia, though South Africa and Portugal traded with it. The ensuing Rhodesian Bush War and international pressure led to the Lancaster House Agreement (1979) and recognised independence as Zimbabwe in 1980 under Robert Mugabe.
