Edward Carl Gaedel (June 8, 1925 – June 18, 1961) was an American baseball player who is famous for having the shortest career in Major League Baseball history — a single plate appearance — and for being, at 3 feet 7 inches tall, the shortest player in major league history. Gaedel, who had dwarfism, was sent to bat by St. Louis Browns owner Bill Veeck in a publicity stunt on August 19, 1951.

The plate appearance

Veeck, a showman who had long dreamed of sending a person of very short stature to the plate, secretly signed Gaedel to a major league contract and gave him the uniform number "⅛". Between games of a Sunday doubleheader against the Detroit Tigers at Sportsman's Park, Gaedel popped out of a papier-mâché cake wearing the uniform. In the bottom of the first inning of the second game he was announced as a pinch hitter for leadoff batter Frank Saucier.

Umpire Ed Hurley demanded to see the contract; Browns manager Zack Taylor produced a copy, and Gaedel was allowed to bat. Under instructions to crouch and on no account swing, Gaedel — whose strike zone Veeck estimated at about an inch and a half — drew a four-pitch walk from pitcher Bob Cain and trotted to first base to a standing ovation, where he was replaced by a pinch runner. The next day, American League president Will Harridge voided Gaedel's contract, declaring the stunt a mockery of the game, and Veeck was barred from such promotions.

Later life and legacy

Gaedel worked in advertising appearances and as a bartender. He died in 1961 after being beaten in a robbery near his Chicago home; Bob Cain was reportedly the only major league figure to attend his funeral. Gaedel remains the only person in major league history whose first name, size and single walk made him a folk hero: his plate appearance is commemorated in baseball lore, and Veeck recounted the story in his autobiography Veeck — as in Wreck. The Baseball Hall of Fame displays items associated with the event.
