Waldorf salad
Created1893 or 1896, Waldorf Hotel, New York
CreatorOscar Tschirky (attributed)
Main ingredientsApples, celery, grapes, walnuts, mayonnaise

A Waldorf salad is a fruit and nut salad made of fresh apples, celery and grapes dressed in mayonnaise, traditionally served on a bed of lettuce. It was created at New York's Waldorf Hotel (the predecessor of the Waldorf Astoria) in 1893 or 1896, and is generally credited to maître d'hôtel Oscar Tschirky.

History and variations

The first printed recipe (in Tschirky's The Cook Book by "Oscar" of the Waldorf, 1896) contained only apples, celery and mayonnaise; chopped walnuts, now considered integral, appeared by the 1920s, and halved grapes were added later. Modern versions substitute yogurt for some mayonnaise, or add chicken, turkey, dried fruit or different nuts. The salad became a byword for a certain era of hotel cuisine and is famously the subject of Basil Fawlty's exasperated exchange with an American guest in the Fawlty Towers episode "Waldorf Salad" (1979).
