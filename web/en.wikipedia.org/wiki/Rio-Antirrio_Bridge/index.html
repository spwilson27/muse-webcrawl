The Rio-Antirrio Bridge, officially the Charilaos Trikoupis Bridge, is a multi-span cable-stayed bridge crossing the Gulf of Corinth near Patras, Greece, linking the town of Rio on the Peloponnese with Antirrio in western mainland Greece. At its completion in 2004 it had the world's longest fully suspended cable-stayed deck (2,252 m).

Engineering

The strait posed exceptional challenges: water up to 65 m deep, a seabed of weak sediments with no bedrock within 100 m, high seismic activity, and the two shores slowly moving apart. The solution rested four pylons on large-diameter caissons sitting on the seabed, with the soil reinforced by steel inclusions, and a continuously suspended deck designed to accommodate tectonic movement, winds, and even a tanker collision.

The bridge was finished ahead of schedule and opened days before the 2004 Athens Olympics; the Olympic flame was carried across it. It cut the crossing from a 45-minute ferry ride to about five minutes by road.
