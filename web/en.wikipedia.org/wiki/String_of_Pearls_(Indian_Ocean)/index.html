String of Pearls (Indian Ocean)

The String of Pearls is a geopolitical hypothesis proposed by United States political researchers in 2004, which holds that China intends to expand its naval presence throughout the Indian Ocean region by building a network of civilian and military maritime facilities along major sea lines of communication. The "pearls" are ports and infrastructure projects in countries such as Pakistan (Gwadar), Sri Lanka (Hambantota), Bangladesh (Chittagong/Chattogram), Myanmar (Kyaukpyu), and the Maldives, extending from mainland China through the Strait of Malacca to the Persian Gulf.

Origins of the term

The term was coined in a 2005 report, Energy Futures in Asia, produced by the defence contractor Booz Allen Hamilton for the United States Department of Defense's Office of Net Assessment. The report argued that China would develop port facilities to protect its energy supply lines, especially oil shipments from the Middle East that pass through the Strait of Malacca — China's so-called "Malacca dilemma".

Chinese position and analysis

China has never formally adopted the term or an associated strategy, and Chinese officials describe the projects as commercial ventures within the Belt and Road Initiative and the Maritime Silk Road. Analysts debate whether the facilities could serve dual-use military purposes; China opened its first overseas military base at Djibouti in 2017. India, viewing the Indian Ocean as its strategic backyard, has responded with its own port and security initiatives — including the development of Chabahar port in Iran, the Security and Growth for All in the Region (SAGAR) doctrine, and the "Necklace of Diamonds" concept of countervailing partnerships.

Frequently cited "pearls"

  • Gwadar Port, Pakistan
  • Hambantota International Port, Sri Lanka
  • Kyaukpyu deep-sea port, Myanmar
  • Chittagong (Chattogram), Bangladesh
  • Facilities in the Maldives and Seychelles
  • Djibouti support base, Horn of Africa
