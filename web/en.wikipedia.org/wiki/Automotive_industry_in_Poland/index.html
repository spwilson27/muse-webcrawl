Automotive industry in Poland

Poland is one of the largest producers of light vehicles, parts and components in Central and Eastern Europe. The automotive industry in Poland accounts for a significant share of the country's industrial output and exports, and is dominated by foreign-owned plants alongside a large components sector.

History

Polish car production began in the interwar period with firms such as CWS, but the industry took its modern shape after the Second World War under state ownership. The Fabryka Samochodów Osobowych (FSO) in Warsaw built the Warszawa and later the Polski Fiat 125p under licence, while FSM in Bielsko-Biała produced the small Polski Fiat 126p — the "Maluch" — which motorised Polish society in the 1970s and 1980s and remained in production until 2000.

After 1989 the industry was privatised and integrated into global supply chains. Fiat took over FSM, General Motors built a plant at Gliwice, and Volkswagen expanded operations around Poznań. Later investors included Toyota, which produces engines and transmissions in Poland, and Mercedes-Benz, which opened an engine plant at Jawor.

StrengthsEngines, components, buses (Solaris, MAN)

Poland is particularly strong in components — engines, gearboxes, tyres, glass, batteries and upholstery — and in buses, where Solaris became one of Europe's leading makers of electric buses. Annual passenger car production has generally run between roughly 300,000 and 600,000 vehicles in recent decades, with the great majority exported, chiefly to Germany and other EU markets.
