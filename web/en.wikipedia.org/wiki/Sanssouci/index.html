Sanssouci

Location: Potsdam, Germany
Built: 1745–1747
Architect: Georg Wenzeslaus von Knobelsdorff
Style: Friderician Rococo

Sanssouci (French: sans souci, "without worries") is a historical building in Potsdam, near Berlin. Built by Prussian King Frederick the Great as his summer palace, it is often counted among the German rivals of Versailles. The palace is a single-storey villa of only ten main rooms, built on the brow of a terraced hill overlooking a garden, in the Rococo style.

History

Frederick sketched the design himself, and the palace was built between 1745 and 1747 by Georg Wenzeslaus von Knobelsdorff. The terraced vineyard below had been laid out in 1744. Sanssouci served as Frederick's private retreat, where he pursued philosophy, music, and conversation with guests such as Voltaire, away from the formality of the Berlin court. Frederick died at the palace in 1786 and was eventually buried, in 1991, beside his beloved greyhounds on the top terrace, fulfilling his own wish.

The park

Sanssouci Park covers some 290 hectares and contains numerous later buildings, including the New Palace (Neues Palais), the Orangery Palace, Charlottenhof Palace, the Chinese House, and the Picture Gallery. In 1990 the palaces and parks of Potsdam and Berlin were inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List.
