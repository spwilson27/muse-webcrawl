Skull

The skull is the bony framework of the head, comprising the cranium that encloses and protects the Brain and the facial bones including the jaw (mandible). In humans it has 22 bones.

Structure

Eight cranial bones (frontal, two parietal, two temporal, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid) are joined by fibrous sutures that fuse with age; in infants the fontanelles allow moulding during birth and brain growth. Fourteen facial bones form the orbits, nasal cavity and jaws; the mandible is the skull's only movable bone. The skull also houses the middle-ear ossicles — the body's smallest bones.

Significance

Skulls are crucial evidence in Palaeontology and forensic anthropology (age, sex, ancestry) and in tracing human evolution from Australopithecus to Homo sapiens. Trauma and congenital fusion (craniosynostosis) are major clinical topics.
