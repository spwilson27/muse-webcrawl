The River Devon (pronounced locally "Dee-von") is a small river in north-eastern Leicestershire and Nottinghamshire, England. It rises among the hills of the Vale of Belvoir near the village of Eastwell and flows generally northwards through the Vale to join the River Trent at Newark-on-Trent. Notwithstanding its modest size, it passes some notable landmarks, including Belvoir Castle, whose estate lake it supplies.

Course

From springs near Eastwell and Eaton in Leicestershire, the Devon flows north-west past Branston and Knipton, where it is impounded to form the Knipton Reservoir, built in the 1790s to supply the Grantham Canal. It then feeds the ornamental lakes in the park of Belvoir Castle, seat of the Dukes of Rutland, and passes below the castle hill through Woolsthorpe-by-Belvoir. Continuing north, it crosses into Nottinghamshire, flowing through Bottesford—where it passes the church of St Mary, burial place of several Earls and Dukes of Rutland—then Muston, and onwards past Cotham to Newark, where it meets the Trent on the eastern side of the town.

Name and character

The name Devon here is unrelated to the English county of the same name and is pronounced "Dee-von", unlike the county's name. Like other rivers on the Lias clays of the Vale of Belvoir, the Devon is slow-flowing and prone to flooding after heavy rain. It supports coarse fish and typical lowland riverbank wildlife, and the Grantham Canal, which it supplies near Knipton, runs roughly parallel to its upper valley.
