A naturalist is a person who studies or practices natural history — the observation and study of organisms, including animals, fungi, and plants, in their environment, leaning more towards observational study methods than experimental ones. Naturalists historically collected, described and classified species, laying the foundations of modern biology, geology and ecology.

History

The term dates from the 16th century, as the descriptive study of nature flourished during the Renaissance. Early naturalists such as Conrad Gessner and Ulisse Aldrovandi compiled vast encyclopedias of species. In the 18th century Carl Linnaeus formalized taxonomy, and networks of amateur collectors supplied specimens to museums worldwide. The 19th century saw naturalists such as Charles Darwin, Alfred Russel Wallace, Thomas Henry Huxley and Joseph Dalton Hooker transform natural history with the theory of evolution by natural selection, based largely on field observations made during long voyages of exploration.

Modern practice

Today the label "naturalist" is less often a formal profession and more often applied to skilled amateurs, field guides, science communicators and conservationists. Natural history societies, citizen-science platforms such as iNaturalist and eBird, and television presenters like David Attenborough have kept the tradition of close observation central to public understanding of the natural world.
