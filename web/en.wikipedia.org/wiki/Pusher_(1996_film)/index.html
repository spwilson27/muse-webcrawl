Pusher is a 1996 Danish crime thriller film directed by Nicolas Winding Refn, in his feature directorial debut, and co-written with Jens Dahl. Shot in a raw, handheld style on the streets of Copenhagen, it follows a mid-level heroin dealer whose week collapses after a police raid forces him to dump his drugs and sink deep into debt to a Serbian gangster. The film launched the careers of Refn and actor Mads Mikkelsen, and became the first entry in the Pusher trilogy.

Plot

Frank (Kim Bodnia), a small-time Copenhagen pusher, spends his days with his sidekick Tonny (Mads Mikkelsen), collecting debts and partying. When a large heroin deal with a Swedish ex-convict goes wrong and police raid the meeting, Frank throws the drugs into a lake — leaving him owing 230,000 kroner to the Balkan drug lord Milo (Zlatko Burić). Over seven days, each titled in intertitles, Frank's increasingly desperate attempts to raise the money — squeezing his own debtors, robbing, pleading with his mother — alienate everyone around him, including his girlfriend Vic. The film ends on an unresolved, ominous note as Milo's enforcers close in.

Production and reception

Made cheaply with largely unknown actors and natural locations, Pusher drew praise for its energy and realism and was compared to Mean Streets. It played at the Toronto International Film Festival and became a cult success, spawning two sequels — Pusher II (2004) and Pusher 3 (2005) — and a 2012 English-language remake set in London.
