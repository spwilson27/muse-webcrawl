The 1929 United Kingdom general election was held on Thursday 30 May 1929 and resulted in a hung parliament. The Labour Party, led by Ramsay MacDonald, won the most seats in the House of Commons for the first time, although the Conservative Party, led by Stanley Baldwin, won a slightly larger share of the popular vote. It is often called the "Flapper Election" because it was the first in which women aged 21 to 30 were entitled to vote, following the Equal Franchise Act 1928.

Campaign

Baldwin's Conservatives campaigned on the slogan "Safety First", defending their record on unemployment and opposing Labour's programme of public works. Labour promised measures to reduce unemployment, which stood at over a million, and the Liberals under David Lloyd George campaigned on the ambitious pledge "We Can Conquer Unemployment", backed by a Liberal revival assisted by John Maynard Keynes and Hubert Henderson's economic pamphlet.

Labour's 287 seats fell short of the 308 needed for a majority, and MacDonald formed his second minority government, dependent on Liberal support. Margaret Bondfield became Minister of Labour — the first woman cabinet minister in British history.

Aftermath

The government was quickly overwhelmed by the onset of the Great Depression following the Wall Street Crash of October 1929. Rising unemployment and a budget crisis split the cabinet in August 1931; MacDonald formed a National Government with the Conservatives, was expelled from the Labour Party, and the ensuing 1931 election produced a landslide for the National Government and disaster for Labour.
