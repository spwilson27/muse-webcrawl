The All-Ireland Senior Camogie Championship is the premier inter-county competition in the women's sport of camogie, organised by the Camogie Association of Ireland. First played in 1932, the championship culminates in the All-Ireland final, traditionally held at Croke Park in Dublin, with the winners receiving the O'Duffy Cup, named for association administrator Seán O'Duffy.

History

After experimental matches in the early 1930s, Dublin won the inaugural championship of 1932, beginning a period of dominance in which the capital claimed most titles through the 1940s and 1950s. Cork emerged as Dublin's great rivals and surpassed the field in later decades, while Kilkenny, Tipperary, Wexford and Galway have also enjoyed championship-winning eras. The competition's prestige grew with television coverage and the finals' move to headline billing at Croke Park.

Format

County teams compete in a group stage followed by knockout rounds, with the strongest sides seeded toward the quarter- and semi-finals. The championship is played in high summer alongside the senior hurling championship season, and the winning captain receives the O'Duffy Cup after the final.

Roll of honour

Dublin lead the roll of honour with 26 titles, followed by Cork with more than twenty. Kilkenny, Tipperary, Wexford and Galway form the next tier of winners; Antrim is the only other county to have won the senior title. Player-of-the-match awards and the annual Camogie All Stars recognise individual excellence in the competition.

Related competitions

The Camogie Association also runs intermediate and junior All-Ireland championships, the National Camogie League, and the Ashbourne Cup for universities.
