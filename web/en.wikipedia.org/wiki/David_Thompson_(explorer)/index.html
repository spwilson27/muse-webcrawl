David Thompson (explorer)

Born30 April 1770, Westminster, London, England
Died10 February 1857 (aged 86), Longueuil, Canada East
OccupationsFur trader, surveyor, cartographer, explorer

David Thompson (30 April 1770 – 10 February 1857) was a British fur trader, surveyor and cartographer who explored much of western North America. Working for the Hudson's Bay Company and later the North West Company, he travelled some 90,000 kilometres and mapped roughly 4.9 million square kilometres of the continent — about one-fifth of its landmass. He has been described as the greatest land geographer who ever lived.

Early life

Born in London to Welsh parents, Thompson was educated at the Grey Coat Hospital and apprenticed to the Hudson's Bay Company at age fourteen, sailing to Churchill on Hudson Bay in 1784. After breaking his leg in 1788, he spent a winter learning astronomy and surveying from Philip Turnor, skills that defined his career.

Explorations

In 1797 Thompson moved to the North West Company. Over the following decades he surveyed the Canada–United States border region, crossed the Rocky Mountains in 1807, established trading posts in the Columbia Basin, and in 1811 became the first European to travel the entire length of the Columbia River to its mouth at the Pacific Ocean.

Later life and legacy

His great map of northwestern North America, completed in 1814, was so accurate that it remained in use for many years. Despite his achievements, Thompson died in relative poverty and obscurity in 1857; his reputation was revived in the twentieth century, and he is now commemorated by the David Thompson Highway in Alberta, monuments, and place names across western Canada and the northwestern United States.
