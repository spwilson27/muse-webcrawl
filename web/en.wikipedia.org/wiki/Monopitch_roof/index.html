Monopitch roof
TypeRoof form
Also calledShed roof, skillion roof, lean-to roof
SurfacesOne sloping plane

A monopitch roof, also known as a shed roof, skillion roof, or lean-to roof, is a single-pitched roof surface, i.e. a single slope. It contrasts with a dual-pitched roof, whose two slopes meet at a ridge. Monopitch roofs are among the simplest roof forms to build and are traditional for additions (lean-tos) attached to a main structure, as well as for sheds and outbuildings.

Design and use

The single slope sheds rain and snow in one direction and allows tall windows or clerestories on the high side, admitting light and providing space for solar panels oriented toward the sun. Skillion roofs are prominent in modernist and contemporary architecture in Australia and elsewhere, sometimes in paired opposing slopes (a butterfly arrangement uses two inward slopes).

Because snow and water run off in one direction, drainage must be planned carefully at the lower edge. The roof's pitch is usually shallower than a gable roof's, and its covering may be metal sheeting, shingles, or membrane.
