Chronology of computation of π

The chronology of computation of π records the increasing precision with which the mathematical constant π (pi) has been computed, from ancient approximations to modern computations of over 100 trillion digits.

Ancient and classical era

Babylonian and Egyptian sources (c. 2000–1650 BC) imply values near 3.125 and 3.16. Archimedes (c. 250 BC) bounded π between 3 10/71 and 3 1/7 using polygons. Zu Chongzhi (5th century AD) gave the remarkably accurate fraction 355/113, correct to six decimal places. Madhava and the Kerala school developed infinite series around 1400, and Ludolph van Ceulen reached 35 digits by 1610.

Machine era

Machin's formula (1706) enabled hand computations reaching 100 digits; ENIAC computed 2,037 digits in 1949. The Chudnovsky algorithm (1988) powered a sequence of records: 2.7 trillion digits (2009), 10 trillion (2011), 62.8 trillion (2021, University of Applied Sciences of the Grisons), and 100 trillion digits by Emma Haruka Iwao at Google in 2022.

Why compute π?

Far fewer digits suffice for any practical purpose — 39 digits can compute the observable universe's circumference to within a hydrogen atom — but π computations serve as stress tests for supercomputers and as benchmarks for algorithms and hardware.
