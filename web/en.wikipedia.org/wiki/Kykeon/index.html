Kykeon

Kykeon (Greek: κυκεών, from κυκάω, "to stir, to mix") was a drink of ancient Greece made of water mixed with barley meal (alphita) and flavourings such as pennyroyal mint, and sometimes grated goat's cheese and wine, as in the Homeric version prepared for Nestor.

Eleusinian Mysteries

Kykeon is best known as the drink broken with at the climax of the Eleusinian Mysteries: after fasting, initiates drank the kykeon in imitation of the goddess Demeter, who, in the Homeric Hymn to Demeter, refused wine but accepted a kykeon of barley, water and pennyroyal offered by Metaneira. What initiates experienced after drinking has long been debated; R. Gordon Wasson, Albert Hofmann and Carl Ruck proposed in The Road to Eleusis (1978) that ergot, a fungus growing on barley or rye, could have given the drink psychoactive properties — a hypothesis that remains contested.
