Celebration Bowl

The Celebration Bowl is the de facto national championship game for HBCU football.

The Celebration Bowl is a postseason college football bowl game played in Atlanta, Georgia, matching the champions of the Mid-Eastern Athletic Conference (MEAC) and the Southwestern Athletic Conference (SWAC), the two NCAA Division I conferences of historically black colleges and universities (HBCUs) at the Football Championship Subdivision (FCS) level. First played in 2015, the game serves as the de facto national championship of black college football and typically opens the bowl season on the third Saturday of December.

History

The game was created by ESPN Events as a successor in spirit to the Heritage Bowl and earlier HBCU postseason matchups. The inaugural game, played on 19 December 2015 at the Georgia Dome, saw North Carolina A&T defeat Alcorn State 41–34. When the Georgia Dome closed, the bowl moved to the new Mercedes-Benz Stadium in 2017. Participating conferences receive substantial payouts that support their athletic programmes, and the MEAC champion forgoes the FCS playoffs to play in the game.

Format and significance

The MEAC and SWAC champions meet in a single game televised nationally on ABC. Beyond the football, the event is a celebration of HBCU culture, featuring the conferences' celebrated marching bands, concerts, and community events in Atlanta during bowl week. South Carolina State, North Carolina A&T, Grambling State, Jackson State, and Florida A&M are among the schools to have won the game.
