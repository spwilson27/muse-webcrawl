Heineken N.V. is a Dutch multinational brewing company, founded in 1864 by Gerard Adriaan Heineken in Amsterdam. Its flagship product, Heineken Lager Beer, is a 5% ABV pale lager sold in a distinctive green bottle with a red star, and the group produces more than 300 international, regional, and speciality beers and ciders in over 70 countries, making it one of the two largest brewers in the world by revenue alongside AB InBev.

History

In 1864 the 22-year-old Gerard Heineken bought the De Hooiberg brewery in Amsterdam. His son Henry Pierre introduced bottom-fermenting yeast and laboratory quality control; the company won prizes at the Paris expositions and expanded abroad early. Under Alfred "Freddy" Heineken (chairman 1971–1989) the brand became a global marketing icon, and his 1983 kidnapping became a famous Dutch crime case. The family retains a controlling interest through Heineken Holding.

Operations and marketing

Major regional brands include Amstel, Tiger (Singapore), Birra Moretti, Cruzcampo, Dos Equis, Tecate, and the cider brand Strongbow; Heineken 0.0 launched in 2017. The company has long sponsored sport, notably the UEFA Champions League, the Rugby World Cup, and Formula One, and the former Amsterdam brewery hosts the Heineken Experience visitor attraction.
