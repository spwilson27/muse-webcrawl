Livestock

Livestock are domesticated animals raised in an agricultural setting to provide labour and produce commodities such as meat, milk, eggs, leather and wool. The term usually excludes poultry and farmed fish, though usage varies. Livestock farming is a central component of global agriculture and has been practised since the Neolithic Revolution.

Types

The principal livestock species worldwide are cattle, sheep, goats, pigs and buffalo; in some regions horses, donkeys, camels, yaks, llamas, alpacas and reindeer are also economically important. Domestication began around 10,000 years ago in the Fertile Crescent and proceeded independently in several world regions.

Production systems

Livestock are kept in systems ranging from nomadic pastoralism and smallholder mixed farming to intensive industrial operations. Ruminants convert grass and forage from land unsuitable for crops into human food, but livestock production is also a major source of greenhouse-gas emissions, land-use change and water consumption, making it a focus of environmental and animal-welfare debate.

Global numbers

World populations include roughly 1.5 billion cattle, 1.2 billion sheep, 1 billion goats and 1 billion pigs, alongside more than 25 billion chickens (usually classed as poultry).
