University of Hamburg

University
Founded: 1919
Location: Hamburg, Germany
Students: about 44,000
Type: Public research university

The University of Hamburg (German: Universität Hamburg, UHH) is a public research university in Hamburg, Germany. It was founded on 28 March 1919 by Wilhelm Stern and others, combining the former General Lecture System, the Colonial Institute, and the Academic College. It is one of Germany's largest universities.

History

The university's creation was driven by citizens of the Hanseatic city, especially the merchant Edmund Siemers, who donated its main building, and Werner von Melle, its first rector. Physicists Otto Stern and Wolfgang Pauli worked there in the 1920s; Stern's Nobel-winning molecular beam work was done in Hamburg. Under Nazi rule many scholars were dismissed. After 1945 the university grew rapidly, merging with the University Hospital Eppendorf (UKE) as its medical faculty.

Faculties and research

UHH has eight faculties, including Law, Business/Economics/Social Sciences, Medicine, Education, Humanities, Mathematics/Informatics/Natural Sciences, Psychology, and Business Administration. It was designated a University of Excellence in 2019, with clusters in climate research, photon science (linked to DESY), quantum matter, and manuscript cultures.

Campus

The main campus lies in the Rotherbaum quarter around Von-Melle-Park, with additional sites in Bahrenfeld (science campus near DESY) and Eppendorf.
