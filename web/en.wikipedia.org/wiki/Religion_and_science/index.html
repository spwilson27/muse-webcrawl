Relationship between religion and science

The relationship between religion and science has been a subject of study since antiquity, concerning the compatibility of religious worldviews with scientific methods and findings. Scholars have proposed several models: conflict (the two fundamentally oppose each other), independence (they address separate domains, as in Stephen Jay Gould's "non-overlapping magisteria"), dialogue, and integration.

History

The nineteenth-century "conflict thesis" of John William Draper and Andrew Dickson White portrayed religion, especially the Catholic Church, as an enemy of science; modern historians of science largely reject this as oversimplified, pointing to the Church's patronage of astronomy and the devout faith of many scientists. Flashpoints have included the Galileo affair, Darwin's theory of evolution by natural selection, and debates over cosmology. Surveys show varying levels of religious belief among scientists across countries and generations, and the topic remains active in philosophy of religion.
