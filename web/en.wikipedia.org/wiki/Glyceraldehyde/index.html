Glyceraldehyde

FormulaC3H6O3
TypeTriose monosaccharide (aldose)

Glyceraldehyde (systematically 2,3-dihydroxypropanal) is a triose monosaccharide with chemical formula C3H6O3. It is the simplest of all common aldoses, containing one aldehyde group and a single stereogenic centre, and it serves as the reference compound for the D/L system of carbohydrate stereochemistry.

Stereochemistry

Glyceraldehyde exists as two enantiomers, D-glyceraldehyde and L-glyceraldehyde. In a Fischer projection with the aldehyde group at the top, the D form has its hydroxyl group on the right and the L form on the left. Emil Fischer arbitrarily assigned these configurations, and nearly all naturally occurring sugars are classified as D-sugars by comparison with D-glyceraldehyde at their highest-numbered stereocentre. D-glyceraldehyde is dextrorotatory and is sometimes written as (R)-glyceraldehyde; the L form is (S).

Biochemistry

The phosphorylated derivative glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate is a central intermediate of glycolysis and the Calvin cycle, formed from the cleavage of fructose 1,6-bisphosphate and interconverted with dihydroxyacetone phosphate by triose phosphate isomerase. Glyceraldehyde itself can be prepared by the mild oxidation of glycerol and was historically obtained from the oxidation of sugars; in the laboratory it is usually handled as its dimer or as an aqueous syrup.
