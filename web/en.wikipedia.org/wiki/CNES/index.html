CNES

French: Centre national d'études spatiales
Formed: 19 December 1961
Headquarters: Paris, France
Launch site: Guiana Space Centre, Kourou
Type: Space agency of the French government

CNES (French: Centre national d'études spatiales, lit. 'National Centre for Space Studies') is the French national space agency. Established in 1961 under President Charles de Gaulle, it is headquartered in Paris and operates under the joint authority of the French ministries of defence and of research. CNES is a major partner in the European Space Agency (ESA) and provides France's principal launch facility, the Guiana Space Centre at Kourou in French Guiana.

History

France became the third country to place a satellite in orbit with its own launcher when the Diamant rocket carried the Astérix satellite in 1965 from Hammaguir in Algeria. After Algerian independence, launch operations moved to Kourou, chosen for its equatorial latitude and safe downrange ocean. CNES led the development of the Ariane family of launch vehicles, which made Europe commercially competitive in satellite launches through Arianespace, and remains deeply involved in Ariane 6 and Vega programmes.

Programmes

CNES develops and operates Earth observation satellites (the SPOT series with Airbus, Pléiades, and the Sentinel missions under Copernicus), scientific missions (such as CoRoT for exoplanet astronomy, and contributions to ESA missions), oceanography satellites with NASA (the TOPEX/Poseidon and Jason series), and the French military space programme. Its Toulouse Space Centre is the largest space centre in Europe, employing several thousand engineers.
