Yan'an Rectification Movement

Date1942–1945
LocationYan'an Soviet, Shaanxi, China
Organised byChinese Communist Party under Mao Zedong

The Yan'an Rectification Movement (延安整风运动) was a political and ideological campaign conducted by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) at its base in Yan'an between 1942 and 1945. It was the first major ideological rectification campaign in the party's history and consolidated Mao Zedong's leadership.

Background and course

Following the Long March, the CCP leadership in Yan'an included both Mao's faction and rivals associated with the Soviet-trained "28 Bolsheviks". Beginning with Mao's lectures in early 1942, the movement called on cadres to study party documents and engage in criticism and self-criticism to eliminate "subjectivism, sectarianism, and stereotyped party writing". Mao's "Talks at the Yan'an Forum on Literature and Art" (May 1942) subordinated cultural work to politics.

Consequences

The campaign's later "rescue" phase, led by Kang Sheng, saw widespread coerced confessions and persecution of alleged spies before being curtailed. By the Seventh Party Congress in 1945, Mao Zedong Thought was written into the party constitution and Mao's supremacy was unchallenged. Historians regard the movement as the template for later CCP campaigns, including the mass movements of the Mao era.
