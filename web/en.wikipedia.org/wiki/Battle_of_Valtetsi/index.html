The Battle of Valtetsi was fought on 12 May 1821 during the Greek War of Independence, near the village of Valtetsi in Arcadia, in the central Peloponnese. Greek revolutionary forces under Theodoros Kolokotronis defeated an Ottoman column advancing from Tripolitsa, in the first major Greek victory of the war. The battle secured the revolutionary camp in the Peloponnese and set the stage for the siege and fall of Tripolitsa later that year.

Background

The Greek revolt broke out in March 1821. Kolokotronis established a fortified camp near Valtetsi, one of several camps ringing Ottoman-held Tripolitsa, the administrative capital of the Peloponnese. On 12 May an Ottoman force of several thousand under Mustafa Bey, reinforced by cavalry, marched out to destroy the Valtetsi camp.

The battle

Some 700 Greeks, fighting from stone towers and fieldworks around the village under commanders including Nikitas Stamatelopoulos ("Nikitaras", the "Turk-slayer") and Kyriakoulis Mavromichalis, held off repeated attacks for nearly a full day. When Greek reinforcements arrived in the Ottoman rear, the attackers broke and fled toward Tripolitsa, abandoning supplies, artillery and baggage.

Aftermath

Valtetsi demonstrated that Greek irregulars could defeat regular Ottoman troops in pitched battle, raised revolutionary morale enormously, and kept the blockade of Tripolitsa intact until the city's fall in September/October 1821.
