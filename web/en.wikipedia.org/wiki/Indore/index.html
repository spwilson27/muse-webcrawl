Indore (Hindi: इंदौर) is the largest and most populous city in the Indian state of Madhya Pradesh. It serves as the headquarters of both Indore District and Indore Division, and is often described as the commercial capital of the state. The city lies on the southern edge of the Malwa Plateau, on the banks of the Saraswati and Khan rivers.

Rajwada, the palace of the Holkar rulers of Indore.

History

Indore grew from a trading post on the route between the Deccan and Delhi. In the 18th century it came under the control of the Holkar dynasty of the Maratha Empire, whose most celebrated ruler was Ahilyabai Holkar (reigned 1767–1795), who moved the capital to nearby Maheshwar but developed Indore as a commercial centre. Under British rule Indore was the capital of the princely state of Indore and headquarters of the Central India Agency. After independence it served as the summer capital of the former state of Madhya Bharat.

Economy and education

Indore is a major hub for trade, textiles, pharmaceuticals and information technology, with the industrial area of Pithampur — sometimes called the "Detroit of Madhya Pradesh" — nearby. Unusually, the city hosts both an Indian Institute of Technology (IIT Indore) and an Indian Institute of Management (IIM Indore).

Culture

The city is famous for its street food, centred on the Sarafa Bazaar night market and Chappan Dukan ("56 shops"), and for signature snacks such as poha-jalebi. Major landmarks include the Rajwada palace, the glass-temple Kanch Mandir, and the Lal Bagh Palace. Indore has repeatedly ranked first in the Indian government's Swachh Survekshan cleanliness survey, earning a reputation as India's cleanest city.
