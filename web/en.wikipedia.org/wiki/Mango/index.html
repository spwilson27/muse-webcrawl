Mango

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Ripe mangoes, one cut open to show the deep orange flesh around the flat stone]

A mango is a juicy stone fruit (drupe) produced by the tree Mangifera indica, a large evergreen of the cashew family native to South Asia. Mangoes have been cultivated in India for over 4,000 years and hold deep cultural significance there; the mango is the national fruit of India, Pakistan and the Philippines. India produces roughly half the world's mangoes, most consumed at home, while Mexico, Thailand and others lead exports. Hundreds of named varieties range from the intensely aromatic Alphonso of Maharashtra — often called the "king of mangoes" — to the fibrous, shipping-hardy Tommy Atkins common in Western supermarkets.

Description

The mango tree can grow 30 metres tall and live for centuries, bearing oval to kidney-shaped fruit that ripen from green to yellow, orange or red. A single large, flat seed sits inside a fibrous husk within the flesh. The skin and sap contain urushiol-related compounds also found in poison ivy, so sensitive people may react to the peel even though the ripe flesh is widely eaten. Flavour combines sugars with turpentine-like resinous notes in some varieties — a polarising quality that mango lovers prize.

History and spread

From its homeland between northwest Myanmar, Bangladesh and northeast India, the mango travelled with traders to East Africa by around the 10th century, reached Brazil with Portuguese colonists in the 18th century, and spread through the tropics. Mango leaves and fruit feature in Hindu ritual and art; the paisley motif is said to be stylised from the mango. Breeding in Florida in the early 20th century produced varieties such as Haden, parent of most modern export cultivars, and the small golden Ataulfo ("honey mango") was selected in Mexico.

Production and use

Mangoes are eaten ripe and fresh, juiced, dried, or pickled; unripe green mangoes go into chutneys, salads and the souring powder amchoor. Mango lassi, the yoghurt drink, is a staple pairing with spiced food. Exports face strict treatment requirements — hot-water immersion or irradiation — to kill fruit-fly larvae, which historically kept prized Indian varieties out of some Western markets. Dried mango and mango pulp are major processed products.
