Stroke play

Stroke play, also known as medal play, is a scoring system in the sport of golf. In the regular form of stroke play, players compete by comparing a total number of strokes taken on each hole and over the whole round; the player with the lowest total wins. Stroke play is the most common format at professional tournaments.

Scoring

Each player (or side) records the number of strokes taken on every hole, including penalty strokes. After the round, the scores on all holes are added; the competitor with the fewest strokes is the winner. In handicap stroke play, a player's net score is computed by subtracting their handicap allowance from the gross score.

Unlike match play, where the unit of scoring is each hole, in stroke play every stroke counts, so a single disastrous hole can ruin a round. Players must hole out on every hole; there are no concessions.

In professional golf

Most tournaments on the PGA Tour, European Tour and the major championships are played over 72 holes of stroke play across four days, typically with a cut after 36 holes that eliminates the lower portion of the field. Ties for first place are usually resolved by a sudden-death or aggregate playoff. Stroke play contrasts with match play, used in events such as the Ryder Cup and the WGC Match Play.

Variants

  • Stableford — points awarded per hole based on score relative to par.
  • Par (bogey) competition — scored as match play against par of the course.
  • Maximum score — a form of stroke play in which the score on a hole is capped.
