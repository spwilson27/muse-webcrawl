Urban district (England and Wales)

In England and Wales, an urban district was a type of local government district that covered an urbanised area. Urban districts had an elected urban district council (UDC), which shared local government responsibilities with a county council. The UDC had responsibilities for housing, sanitation, roads, and other local services.

History

Urban districts were created under the Local Government Act 1894, largely succeeding the earlier local boards and urban sanitary districts. They formed the lower tier of local government in urban areas outside the larger municipal boroughs and county boroughs. Over time, many urban districts grew and were elevated to borough status, while others were merged in successive reorganisations.

Abolition

Urban districts in England and Wales were abolished under the Local Government Act 1972, which took effect on 1 April 1974, and were replaced by the new district councils within a two-tier structure of counties and districts. Some former urban district areas became successor parishes with parish councils retaining a limited range of functions.
