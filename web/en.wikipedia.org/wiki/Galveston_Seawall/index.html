Galveston Seawall


The Galveston Seawall is a 10-mile (16 km) seawall in Galveston, Texas, built after the 1900 hurricane.

The Galveston Seawall is a seawall in Galveston, Texas, built after the catastrophic Galveston hurricane of 1900 to protect the city from future storm surges. Construction began in 1902 and the original section was completed in 1904; the wall was later extended to a total length of about 10 miles (16 km).

Design and construction

The wall stands 17 feet (5.2 m) high, is 16 feet (4.9 m) thick at its base, and has a curved face designed to deflect waves upward and back to sea. Behind it, the city undertook an extraordinary grade-raising project: between 1903 and 1911, more than 500 city blocks — including thousands of buildings lifted on jackscrews — were raised by as much as 17 feet using sand dredged from the harbor, so that the city slopes gently down from the seawall to the bay.

Performance and legacy

The seawall was tested by a major hurricane in 1915, which the raised city survived with far fewer deaths than in 1900. Seawall Boulevard, atop the wall, became a famous promenade lined with hotels, murals, and attractions; a portion of the ocean-side wall was painted in 2020–2021 with the "Absolute Equality" Juneteenth mural. The seawall is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and is a designated Texas Historic Civil Engineering Landmark, though floods from Hurricane Ike (2008) showed areas behind the wall and on the unprotected bay side remain vulnerable.
