Dichroic prism

A dichroic prism is a prism that splits light into two beams of differing wavelength (colour). A trichroic prism assembly combines two dichroic prisms to split an image into red, green and blue beams, as used in some colour television cameras and digital projectors.

Operation

Dichroic prisms are manufactured from optical glass bearing dichroic thin-film coatings on their internal faces. One coated surface reflects light of one part of the spectrum while transmitting the rest; a second surface separates the remaining light further. Because the separation depends on interference coatings rather than refraction, the colour split can be made sharp, and because the light paths through the glass are matched, the beams can be recombined with high precision.

Applications

Trichroic prism assemblies were used in three-tube and later three-CCD colour television cameras and in 3LCD and some DLP projectors, directing each primary-colour beam to a separate image sensor or light valve. Dichroic prisms and cubes are also used in fluorescence microscopy, laser systems and stage lighting.
