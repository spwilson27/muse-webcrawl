The Union for Democracy and Social Progress (French: Union pour la Démocratie et le Progrès Social, UDPS) is a major political party in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Founded in 1982 by Étienne Tshisekedi and others as an opposition movement against the regime of Mobutu Sese Seko, it is one of the oldest political parties in the country.

For decades the UDPS was the principal opposition party, boycotting several elections it considered fraudulent while its supporters faced repression under Mobutu and later governments.

Rise to power

After Étienne Tshisekedi's death in 2017, his son Félix Tshisekedi became party leader. Félix Tshisekedi won the contested December 2018 presidential election and was inaugurated as President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo in January 2019, marking the country's first peaceful transfer of power and bringing the UDPS into government for the first time.
