Battles of Lexington and Concord

The Battles of Lexington and Concord were some of the initial military engagements of the American Revolutionary War, fought on April 19, 1775, in Middlesex County, Province of Massachusetts Bay, within the towns of Lexington, Concord, Lincoln, Menotomy (now Arlington), and Cambridge. They marked the outbreak of armed conflict between the Kingdom of Great Britain and its thirteen colonies in America.

Background

General Thomas Gage, the British military governor of Massachusetts, ordered troops to march from Boston to seize colonial military stores at Concord. Warned by riders including Paul Revere and William Dawes on the night of April 18, the Massachusetts militia ("minutemen") mobilised along the route.

The fighting

At dawn on Lexington Green, some 77 militiamen under Captain John Parker faced the British advance guard. A shot — its origin still disputed — was fired, and the regulars shot down eight militiamen. The British continued to Concord, where militia at the North Bridge fired on the troops in what Ralph Waldo Emerson later called the "shot heard round the world". During the long retreat to Boston, thousands of militiamen ambushed the column from behind stone walls and trees, inflicting heavy casualties before the British were rescued by reinforcements under Lord Percy.

Aftermath

The battles transformed political resistance into open war. Militia surrounded Boston, beginning the Siege of Boston, and the Second Continental Congress soon created the Continental Army under George Washington. April 19 is still commemorated in Massachusetts and Maine as Patriots' Day.
