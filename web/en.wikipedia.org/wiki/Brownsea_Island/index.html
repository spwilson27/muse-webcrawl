LocationPoole Harbour, Dorset, England

Known forSite of the first Scout camp (1907); red squirrel refuge

Brownsea Island is the largest of the islands in Poole Harbour in the county of Dorset, England. The island is owned by the National Trust, with the northern half leased to the Dorset Wildlife Trust as a nature reserve. It is famous as the site of the first experimental Scout camp in 1907.

History

The island contains Brownsea Castle, originally a blockhouse fort built by Henry VIII in 1547 to defend Poole against invasion. In 1907 Robert Baden-Powell held an experimental camp for 20 boys on the island, which led directly to the founding of the Scout Movement and the publication of Scouting for Boys in 1908.

Wildlife

Brownsea is one of the few places in southern England where red squirrels survive, protected by the island's isolation from grey squirrels. The Dorset Wildlife Trust reserve includes a lagoon attracting large numbers of wading birds and wildfowl, including avocets and common terns.
