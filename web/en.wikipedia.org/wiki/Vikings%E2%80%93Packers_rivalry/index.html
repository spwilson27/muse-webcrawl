Vikings–Packers rivalry

The Vikings–Packers rivalry is a National Football League (NFL) rivalry between the Minnesota Vikings and the Green Bay Packers. The two teams, both members of the NFC North division (formerly the NFC Central), have met twice in every regular season since the Vikings joined the league in 1961, producing one of the most closely contested rivalries in the NFL, with the all-time series having remained within a few games of even for long stretches.

History

The rivalry began on 24 September 1961, when the expansion Vikings upset the Packers in Green Bay in only their second game. Under coach Bud Grant, Minnesota dominated much of the late 1960s and 1970s, while Green Bay, led by Vince Lombardi, won early titles including the first two Super Bowls. The balance shifted in the 1990s and 2000s behind Packers quarterbacks Brett Favre and Aaron Rodgers; Favre's move to Minnesota in 2009 — after a season with the New York Jets — intensified the feud, and he led the Vikings to victory over Green Bay that year.

Memorable games

Famous meetings include the 2004 NFC wild-card game at Lambeau Field, where Randy Moss's "mooning" celebration after a Vikings touchdown became notorious; the 2012 season finale, in which Adrian Peterson fell nine yards short of the single-season rushing record as Minnesota beat Green Bay to reach the playoffs; and a 2015 finale that decided the division title. The teams have also met in the playoffs in 2004, 2012 and 2024-season play, keeping the rivalry prominent into the modern era.
