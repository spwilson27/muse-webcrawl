Georges Bouligand

Georges Bouligand, French mathematician (1889–1979).

Georges Louis Bouligand (13 October 1889 – 12 April 1979) was a French mathematician, known for his work in analysis, geometry, and mathematical physics, and for influential textbooks used by generations of French students. He studied at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris and went on to teach at Rennes and then at the University of Poitiers, where he spent most of his career as professor.

Work

Bouligand introduced the notion now called the Bouligand tangent cone, also called the contingent cone, a fundamental object in nonsmooth and variational analysis: at a point of a set, it collects the limits of directions of secants approaching the point. This concept became a cornerstone of the theory of set-valued analysis and optimization, developed further by later mathematicians. His name also attaches to the Bouligand dimension, a way of estimating the fractal dimension of a set through the volume of its parallel sets (closely related to the Minkowski dimension).

He wrote extensively on potential theory, partial differential equations, rational mechanics, and differential geometry, and edited widely used course texts, including his Leçons de géométrie vectorielle and treatises on mathematical analysis for engineers. Bouligand maintained a strong interest in applications and in the philosophy and teaching of mathematics.

Honours

He received prizes from the French Academy of Sciences and was made an officer of the Legion of Honour. His contingent cone and related "paratingent" notions remain standard references in modern variational analysis.
