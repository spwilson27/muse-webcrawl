Construction set

A construction set (also called a construction toy) is a collection of standardized pieces that can be assembled in different ways to build models, structures or machines. Construction sets are among the most enduring categories of educational toys, encouraging spatial reasoning, fine motor skills and creative play.

History

Early examples include Anchor Stones (Anker-Steinbaukästen) in late nineteenth-century Germany and Frank Hornby's Meccano (1901), a system of perforated metal strips, plates, gears and nuts and bolts. John Lloyd Wright invented interlocking Lincoln Logs in 1916, and Ole Kirk Christiansen's company produced the plastic bricks that became Lego from 1949 onwards, whose stud-and-tube coupling system (patented 1958) made it the dominant construction toy worldwide.

Types

Construction sets are classified by their connection method — bricks and blocks, rods and connectors (K'Nex), magnetic systems (Magna-Tiles, Geomag), gears, and electronic kits. Many sets are themed or licensed and include instructions for specific models, though open-ended building is a central appeal.
