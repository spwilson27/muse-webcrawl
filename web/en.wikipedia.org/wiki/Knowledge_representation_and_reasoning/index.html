Knowledge representation and reasoning

Knowledge representation and reasoning (KRR, KR&R, or KR) is a field of artificial intelligence dedicated to representing information about the world in a form that a computer system can use to solve complex tasks, and to reasoning automatically with that representation — for example, diagnosing a medical condition or having a dialogue in natural language.

Approaches

Major formalisms include first-order and description logics, semantic networks, frames, production rules, and ontologies expressed in languages such as RDF and OWL. Knowledge bases and expert systems of the 1970s and 1980s were key early applications. Key problems include the frame problem, default and non-monotonic reasoning, uncertainty, and the trade-off between expressive power and computational tractability.

Applications

KRR underlies the semantic web, question-answering systems, planning, and modern knowledge graphs used by search engines and virtual assistants.
