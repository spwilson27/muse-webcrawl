Somme (department)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Somme (French pronunciation: [sɔm]; Picard: Sonme) is a department in the Hauts-de-France region of France, in the north of the country. It is named after the river Somme, which flows through it to the English Channel at the Bay of Somme. Its prefecture is Amiens, famous for its Gothic cathedral, the largest in France.

History

The department was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790 from part of the former province of Picardy. The Somme valley has been strategically contested for centuries — the Battle of Crécy (1346) was fought in its north — but the department is known above all for the First World War. The Battle of the Somme (1916), one of the largest and bloodiest battles in history, was fought along the river east of Amiens, and the landscape around Péronne and Thiepval is filled with Commonwealth and French war cemeteries and memorials. Amiens was also the centre of the decisive Allied offensive of August 1918.

Geography and economy

The department is a chalk plateau cut by the Somme and its tributaries, opening westward to the wide, bird-rich estuary of the Bay of Somme, one of the largest nature reserves on the Channel coast. The economy mixes agriculture, food processing and industry around Amiens, and tourism — seaside resorts such as Le Crotoy and Saint-Valery-sur-Somme, and battlefield remembrance centred on the Historial de la Grande Guerre museum at Péronne.
