Polymyalgia rheumatica

Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR) is a syndrome experienced as pain or stiffness, usually in the neck, shoulders, upper arms, and hips, but which may occur all over the body. The pain can be sudden or can occur gradually over a period of time. It generally occurs in people over the age of 50, and women are affected more often than men.

Signs and symptoms

Characteristic features include bilateral aching and morning stiffness lasting more than 30 minutes in the shoulders, neck, and hip girdles, often accompanied by fatigue, low-grade fever, loss of appetite, and weight loss. Blood tests typically show raised inflammatory markers such as ESR and C-reactive protein.

Relationship with giant cell arteritis

Polymyalgia rheumatica is closely associated with giant cell arteritis (temporal arteritis), an inflammation of large blood vessels; a significant proportion of people with PMR develop giant cell arteritis, and vice versa.

Treatment

PMR responds dramatically to low-dose corticosteroids such as prednisone or prednisolone, usually started at 12.5–25 mg daily and tapered over months. Most people require treatment for one to three years.
