Anglo-Zulu War
Date11 January – 4 July 1879
LocationZululand, southern Africa
BelligerentsBritish Empire vs Zulu Kingdom
CommandersLord Chelmsford; King Cetshwayo kaMpande
ResultBritish victory; Zulu kingdom partitioned and later annexed

The Anglo-Zulu War was fought between the British Empire and the Zulu Kingdom in 1879. British officials in southern Africa, notably Sir Bartle Frere, sought a confederation of states under British control and regarded the independent, militarily strong Zulu kingdom of King Cetshwayo as an obstacle. An ultimatum demanding the disbandment of the Zulu army was designed to be rejected; British forces under Lord Chelmsford invaded Zululand in January 1879.

Course of the war

The invasion opened with disaster: on 22 January 1879 a Zulu army destroyed the British camp at Isandlwana, killing over 1,300 imperial and colonial troops in Britain's worst defeat at the hands of an indigenous force. The same night, a small garrison famously held the mission station at Rorke's Drift, where eleven Victoria Crosses were won. A second British column was besieged at Eshowe and another mauled at Hlobane, though it prevailed at Kambula.

Reinforced heavily, Chelmsford advanced again in the summer. On 4 July 1879 the British defeated the Zulu army decisively at the Battle of Ulundi and burned the royal kraal. Cetshwayo was captured weeks later, ending organised resistance.

Aftermath

Zululand was divided among thirteen appointed chiefs, a settlement that produced civil war; Cetshwayo was briefly restored in 1883 but died the following year. Zululand was annexed by Britain in 1887 and incorporated into the Colony of Natal in 1897, within what is today KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa.
