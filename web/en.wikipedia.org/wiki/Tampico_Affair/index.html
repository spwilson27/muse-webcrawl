Tampico Affair

The Tampico Affair was a minor incident in April 1914 between United States Navy sailors and Mexican forces loyal to dictator Victoriano Huerta during the Mexican Revolution. It escalated into a diplomatic crisis that led directly to the United States occupation of Veracruz.

The incident

On 9 April 1914, a whaleboat from the gunboat USS Dolphin, flying the American flag and taking on fuel at Tampico, was detained by Mexican federal troops; the sailors, two of whom were still aboard, were arrested and marched through the streets before being released. The local Mexican commander apologised, but Rear Admiral Henry T. Mayo demanded a formal apology, punishment of those responsible, and a 21-gun salute to the American flag.

Escalation

Huerta refused the salute on the demanded terms. President Woodrow Wilson backed Mayo and asked Congress for authority to use force. When news arrived that a German steamer, the Ypiranga, was delivering arms to Huerta at Veracruz, Wilson ordered the port seized. U.S. forces occupied Veracruz on 21 April 1914 and held it until November, straining relations with Mexico for years.
