Tokyo Tower

Tokyo Tower (東京タワー) is a communications and observation tower in the Shiba-koen district of Minato, Tokyo, Japan. Completed in 1958, it stands 332.9 metres (1,092 feet) tall and was the tallest structure in Japan until the completion of the Tokyo Skytree in 2012.

Construction

Modelled on the Eiffel Tower, the lattice structure was designed by Tachū Naitō and painted in international orange and white to comply with air-safety regulations. Built partly from steel recycled from American tanks damaged in the Korean War, it served as the broadcast antenna for television in the Kanto region and became a symbol of Japan's postwar rebirth and economic growth.

Observation and attractions

The tower has two observation decks: the Main Deck at 150 metres and the Top Deck at 250 metres. The building at its base, FootTown, contains museums, shops, restaurants, and an aquarium. Tokyo Tower draws millions of visitors a year and is illuminated at night in seasonal colour schemes. Although most broadcasting moved to the Skytree, the tower remains a backup transmission site and cultural landmark.
