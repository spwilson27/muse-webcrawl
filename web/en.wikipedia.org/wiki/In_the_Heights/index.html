In the Heights

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The musical is set in the Washington Heights neighbourhood of Manhattan.

In the Heights is a musical with concept, music, and lyrics by Lin-Manuel Miranda and a book by Quiara Alegría Hudes. The story is set over three days in the predominantly Dominican-American neighbourhood of Washington Heights in Upper Manhattan, New York City, and follows a community facing gentrification, a blackout, and questions of home and belonging.

Synopsis

Usnavi, a bodega owner who dreams of returning to the Dominican Republic, narrates. His friend Benny works at a taxi dispatch owned by Kevin Rosario, whose daughter Nina has dropped out of Stanford, hiding the loss of her scholarship. The much-loved Abuela Claudia holds the community together; when she discovers she holds a winning lottery ticket worth $96,000, her death the same week forces every character to weigh dreams against duty. A neighbourhood blackout during a heat wave forms the climax of the second act.

Productions

Miranda began the show as a sophomore at Wesleyan University in 1999. After workshops, it opened Off-Broadway in 2007 and transferred to Broadway in 2008, where it was nominated for thirteen Tony Awards and won four, including Best Musical and Best Original Score. The original cast featured Miranda as Usnavi, Mandy Gonzalez, Christopher Jackson, and Olga Merediz.

Film adaptation

A film adaptation directed by Jon M. Chu, with a screenplay by Hudes, was released in 2021. Anthony Ramos starred as Usnavi, with Corey Hawkins, Leslie Grace, Melissa Barrera, and Olga Merediz reprising her stage role. The film was praised for its choreography and energy, though discussion followed about colourism in casting.

Musical style

The score blends hip hop, salsa, merengue, soul, and traditional Broadway songcraft; "96,000", "Paciencia y Fe", "Carnaval del Barrio", and the opening title number are signature songs.
