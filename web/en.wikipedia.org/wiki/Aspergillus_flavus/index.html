Aspergillus flavus

KingdomFungi
PhylumAscomycota
OrderEurotiales
FamilyTrichocomaceae
GenusAspergillus
SpeciesA. flavus

Aspergillus flavus is a saprotrophic and pathogenic fungus with a cosmopolitan distribution, common in soil and decaying vegetation. It is best known for its colonisation of cereal grains, legumes and tree nuts, and for producing aflatoxins — among the most potent naturally occurring carcinogens — which contaminate food and feed stored under warm, humid conditions.

Biology

The species forms yellow-green colonies (Latin flavus, "yellow") with radiating conidial heads. Many strains produce aflatoxins B1 and B2, while close relatives produce the G toxins; atoxigenic strains of A. flavus are deliberately applied to crops (biocontrol) to out-compete toxin-producing populations.

Significance

Aflatoxin contamination of maize and groundnuts is a major public-health and trade problem, linked to liver cancer and acute aflatoxicosis outbreaks. A. flavus is also the second most common cause of invasive aspergillosis in immunocompromised patients and can infect insects and stored grain pests.
