Charles Robert Redford Jr. (August 18, 1936 – September 16, 2025) was an American actor, director and producer. He became one of the defining movie stars of the 1970s and later founded the Sundance Institute and the Sundance Film Festival, the premier showcase for independent cinema in the United States.

Early life

Redford was born in Santa Monica, California, and grew up in Los Angeles. He attended the University of Colorado Boulder on a baseball scholarship but lost it after struggles with alcohol following his mother's death. He travelled in Europe, aspiring to be a painter, before returning to New York to study acting at the American Academy of Dramatic Arts.

Acting career

Redford worked in television and on Broadway before film success came opposite Natalie Wood in Inside Daisy Clover (1965) and Jane Fonda in Barefoot in the Park (1967). His breakthrough was Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid (1969), co-starring Paul Newman, with whom he reunited in The Sting (1973), which earned Redford his only Academy Award nomination for acting.

Through the 1970s he headlined a remarkable run of films: Jeremiah Johnson (1972), The Way We Were (1973), The Great Gatsby (1974), Three Days of the Condor (1975), All the President's Men (1976) — in which he played reporter Bob Woodward — and The Electric Horseman (1979). Later performances included The Natural (1984), Out of Africa (1985), A River Runs Through It (as narrator, 1992), All Is Lost (2013) and The Old Man & the Gun (2018), after which he announced his retirement from acting.

Directing

Redford's directorial debut, Ordinary People (1980), won the Academy Award for Best Picture and earned him the Academy Award for Best Director. He went on to direct The Milagro Beanfield War (1988), A River Runs Through It (1992), Quiz Show (1994), The Horse Whisperer (1998) and The Legend of Bagger Vance (2000), among others.

Sundance and activism

In 1981 Redford founded the Sundance Institute in Utah to support independent filmmakers; its associated festival grew into the Sundance Film Festival, launching the careers of many independent directors. A lifelong environmentalist and political activist, he served as a trustee of the Natural Resources Defense Council and campaigned on climate and conservation issues. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016.

Death

Redford died at his home in Sundance, Utah, on September 16, 2025, aged 89.
