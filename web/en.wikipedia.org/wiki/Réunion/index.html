Réunion (French: La Réunion) is an island in the Indian Ocean, east of Madagascar and southwest of Mauritius, that is an overseas department and region of France. Its capital is Saint-Denis. The island is about 63 kilometres long and 45 kilometres wide, with an area of some 2,512 square kilometres, and is home to roughly 870,000 people.

Geography

Réunion is volcanic in origin and mountainous. The dormant Piton des Neiges (3,070 m) is its highest point, while the Piton de la Fournaise is one of the world's most active volcanoes, erupting frequently. Three vast natural amphitheatres, the cirques of Salazie, Cilaos and Mafate, dominate the interior; the island's peaks, cirques and ramparts were listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2010.

History

The island was uninhabited when Portuguese sailors sighted it in the early sixteenth century. France claimed it in the 1640s and settled it from the 1660s; under the name Île Bourbon it became a plantation colony worked by enslaved people from Madagascar and Africa, growing coffee and later sugar cane. Slavery was abolished in 1848, after which indentured labourers arrived from India, China and elsewhere, giving Réunion its notably mixed population of European, African, Indian, Malagasy and Chinese descent. Réunion became an overseas department of France in 1946.

Economy and culture

Sugar cane remains the principal agricultural product, and tourism is a major sector. Réunionnais culture blends French, Creole, Indian and African influences; Réunion Creole is widely spoken alongside French, and maloya music — born among enslaved people — is recognised by UNESCO. The island uses the euro and is an outermost region of the European Union.
