Temple of Jupiter (Capitoline Hill)

The Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus, also known as the Temple of Jupiter Capitolinus, was the most important temple in ancient Rome. Located on the Capitoline Hill, it was dedicated to the Capitoline Triad of Jupiter, Juno, and Minerva.

History

Tradition held that the temple was vowed by Tarquinius Priscus and built under Tarquin the Proud, the last king of Rome, and dedicated in 509 BC, the first year of the Roman Republic. It burned down several times — in 83 BC, AD 69, and AD 80 — and was rebuilt each time, the final rebuilding being under Domitian with lavish marble and gilded tiles.

Architecture

The temple stood on a high podium measuring roughly 53 by 62 metres, with three cellae side by side: Jupiter in the centre, Juno on the left, and Minerva on the right. Its cult statue of Jupiter was by the Etruscan sculptor Vulca of Veii. Roman triumphs ended at this temple, where the triumphing general laid his laurel at the feet of Jupiter.

Remains

Parts of the massive foundation survive beneath the Palazzo Caffarelli in the Capitoline Museums.
