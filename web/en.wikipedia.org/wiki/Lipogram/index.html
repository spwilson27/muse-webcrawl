Lipogram

A lipogram is a kind of constrained writing or word game consisting of writing a text while omitting a particular letter or letters — usually a common vowel such as "e", the most frequent letter in English.

History and examples

The challenge dates to antiquity — authors rewrote the Iliad and Odyssey omitting successive letters — and flourished in the twentieth century. Ernest Vincent Wright's 1939 novel Gadsby (50,000 words) avoids "e" entirely. Georges Perec's French novel La Disparition (1969) is an e-less lipogram, translated by Gilbert Adair as A Void, also without the letter e. Christian Bök's Eunoia (2001) goes further: each chapter uses only one vowel.

Analysis

Avoiding "e" forces writers to abandon common words (the, he, she, were, most past tenses in -ed), making lipograms a demonstration of how deeply a single letter structures ordinary language.
