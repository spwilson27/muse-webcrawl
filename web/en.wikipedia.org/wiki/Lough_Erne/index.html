Lough Erne (from Irish Loch Éirne) is the name of two connected lakes in County Fermanagh, Northern Ireland. It is the second-biggest lake system in Northern Ireland and Ulster, and the fourth biggest in Ireland. The lakes are widened sections of the River Erne, which flows north and then curves west into the Atlantic at Ballyshannon. The main town on the lake is Enniskillen, situated on an island between the Upper and Lower loughs.

Upper and Lower Lough Erne

Upper Lough Erne, the southern and smaller of the two, is a maze of inlets, bays and islands, joined to the River Shannon by the Shannon–Erne Waterway. Lower Lough Erne, to the north-west, is a broader body of open water some 29 km (18 mi) long, and is deeper, reaching over 60 m in places.

Islands

The loughs contain more than 150 islands, many of them of archaeological and historical interest. Devenish Island preserves a well-preserved round tower and Augustinian priory founded in the 6th century by Saint Molaise. The White Island in Castle Archdale Bay holds a group of mysterious carved stone figures of uncertain date. Boa Island is known for the two-sided Janus-like figure (the Boa Island figures) in Caldragh graveyard.

History and modern use

During the Second World War, RAF Castle Archdale on Lower Lough Erne was a base for Catalina and Sunderland flying boats patrolling the Atlantic; it is often described as the closest operational RAF station to the American continent. Today the lough is a major centre for cruising, angling and watersports, and its shores hosted the 39th G8 summit at the Lough Erne Resort in June 2013.
