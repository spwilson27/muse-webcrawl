Cardioid

A cardioid (from Greek kardia, "heart") is a plane curve traced by a point on the perimeter of a circle that is rolling around a fixed circle of the same radius. It is therefore an epicycloid with a single cusp. The cardioid is an inverse curve of the parabola and is famous in popular mathematics as the main bulb of the Mandelbrot set.

Equations

With the cusp at the origin and the curve symmetric about the x-axis, the cardioid traced by rolling a circle of radius a has polar equation r = 2a(1 − cos θ), parametric equations x = 2a cos t − a cos 2t and y = 2a sin t − a sin 2t, and the Cartesian form (x² + y² − 2ax)² = 4a²(x² + y²). In the complex plane it is the image of the unit circle under z ↦ 2z − z²-related maps and arises as the boundary of the period-1 bulb of the Mandelbrot set.

Generation

Besides its definition as a roulette, the cardioid can be generated as the envelope of circles whose centres lie on a fixed circle and which pass through a fixed point on it; as the caustic formed by light rays reflected in a circle from a source on the circle (the bright heart-shaped curve seen in a coffee cup); as the pedal curve of a circle with respect to a point on it; and as an involute-related curve. It is also the catacaustic of a circle for a radiant point on the circumference.

Properties

The area enclosed is 6πa², six times the area of the generating circle, and the total arc length is 16a. Every chord through the cusp has constant length 4a. The cardioid is a special case of the limaçon of Pascal (with b = a) and of the nephroid's family of epicycloids.

Occurrences

  • Microphones: the cardioid polar pattern — sensitive ahead, rejecting sound from behind — is the most common directional microphone response; variants include supercardioid and hypercardioid patterns.
  • Fractals: the largest bulb of the Mandelbrot set is a cardioid, with further cardioids at its tangent points.
  • Optics: cardioid caustics appear in reflected light on circular surfaces.
  • Antennas and acoustics: cardioid radiation patterns are generated by combining monopole and dipole sources.
