William John Adie


Born 31 October 1886, Geelong, Victoria, Australia
Died 17 June 1935 (aged 48)
Known for Adie's pupil; Adie syndrome
Field Neurology, ophthalmology

William John Adie (31 October 1886 – 17 June 1935) was an Australian neurologist and ophthalmologist known for describing the tonic pupil condition that bears his name.

Life and career

Adie was born in Geelong, Australia, and studied medicine at the University of Edinburgh, financing his studies in part by working as a telephone operator. During the First World War he served as a regimental medical officer and became known for saving lives during a gas attack. After the war he practised neurology in London, working at the National Hospital, Queen Square, and in ophthalmology at Moorfields Eye Hospital.

Adie's pupil

In 1931 Adie described a benign condition in which one pupil is larger than normal and reacts slowly to light but more normally to near effort, often accompanied by absent tendon reflexes. The condition, known as Adie's tonic pupil or, when combined with diminished reflexes, Holmes–Adie syndrome, results from damage to the ciliary ganglion. Adie also wrote on narcolepsy, publishing descriptions of the disorder and distinguishing it from epilepsy.

Death

Adie died prematurely in 1935 at the age of 48, following a heart attack. He is remembered as a gifted clinician and teacher.
