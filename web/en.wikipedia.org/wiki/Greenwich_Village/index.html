Greenwich Village

Greenwich Village, often simply called the Village, is a neighbourhood on the west side of Lower Manhattan in New York City. It is bounded roughly by 14th Street to the north, Broadway and the East Village to the east, Houston Street to the south and the Hudson River to the west. Washington Square Park lies at its heart.

History

The area was once a Lenape settlement and later a marshy Dutch and English colonial village — its name derives from the Dutch Groenwijck, "green district". In the nineteenth century it became a fashionable residential district, then, as wealthy residents moved uptown, an inexpensive quarter that attracted artists, writers and radicals. By the early twentieth century the Village was the centre of American bohemian culture, home to figures of the Provincetown Players and the literary salons of Mabel Dodge.

Culture

After the Second World War the Village nurtured the Beat Generation, the folk music revival — Bob Dylan and many others played its coffeehouses and clubs such as the Village Vanguard — and the Off-Broadway theatre movement. It also became a centre of gay life and activism; the Stonewall riots of 1969, at the Stonewall Inn on Christopher Street, are widely regarded as the founding event of the modern LGBT rights movement. New York University is a major presence around Washington Square. Rising property prices from the late twentieth century onwards transformed the neighbourhood into one of Manhattan's most expensive, though its winding street plan, low-rise townhouses and artistic reputation endure.
