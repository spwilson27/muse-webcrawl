Fort Ontario

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Ontario is a historic fort in Oswego, New York, situated on the east side of the Oswego River at its mouth on Lake Ontario. A fortification has stood on the site since 1755, when the British built Fort Oswego's companion works during the French and Indian War. The present star-shaped fort, of earth and masonry, dates largely from the 19th century.

History

The fort saw action in the French and Indian War, when it was captured and destroyed by the French under Montcalm in 1756, and in the American Revolutionary War and the War of 1812, when British forces attacked it in May 1814. It was garrisoned during both World Wars; from 1944 to 1946 it served as the Fort Ontario Emergency Refugee Shelter, the only refugee camp in the United States for victims of the Holocaust, housing nearly 1,000 refugees.

Today

The site is now the Fort Ontario State Historic Site, administered by New York State, and hosts tours, reenactments, and events. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1970.
