Long Beach, California

Long Beach is a coastal city in southeastern Los Angeles County, California, United States. It is the 44th-most populous city in the United States, with a population of about 450,000 as of the 2020 census. A charter city, Long Beach is the seventh-most populous city in California. The Port of Long Beach is the second-busiest container port in the Americas (with about 9 million TEU handled annually) and, combined with the neighbouring Port of Los Angeles, forms one of the largest port complexes in the world.

History

The area was originally home to the Tongva people, including the village of Puvunga. Rancho Los Alamitos and Rancho Los Cerritos were granted in the Spanish/Mexican era. The city began as Willmore City in 1882, was renamed Long Beach for its long, wide beaches, and incorporated in 1897. Oil discovered at Signal Hill in 1921 made Long Beach briefly one of the most productive oil fields per acre in the world. The city became a major US Navy port and aircraft-manufacturing centre (Douglas Aircraft) during the Second World War, and suffered serious damage in the 1933 Long Beach earthquake.

Landmarks and culture

The retired ocean liner RMS Queen Mary has been moored in Long Beach since 1967. Other attractions include the Aquarium of the Pacific, the Long Beach Museum of Art, and Naples, a neighbourhood built on canals. The city hosts the annual Grand Prix of Long Beach (IndyCar) and is home to California State University, Long Beach.

Economy

Trade, shipping, manufacturing, healthcare, and education anchor the economy; historically Douglas/McDonnell Douglas and Boeing built aircraft in the city, and the last Boeing C-17 line closed in 2015.
