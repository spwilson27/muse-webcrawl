Reported speech

Reported speech, also called indirect speech, is a way of conveying what someone said without quoting them directly, in contrast to direct speech, which reproduces the speaker's exact words, typically within quotation marks.

Grammar

In English, converting direct to reported speech usually involves "backshifting" verb tenses ("I am tired" → "She said she was tired"), changing pronouns (I → she), and adjusting time and place expressions (now → then, here → there, tomorrow → the next day). Reported questions embed interrogative clauses without inversion ("Where are you?" → "He asked where I was"), and reported commands use infinitives ("Sit down" → "She told him to sit down").

Free indirect speech

Free indirect speech (free indirect discourse) is a literary technique that blends reported speech with the character's own voice and idiom without a reporting clause, used extensively by writers such as Jane Austen and James Joyce.
