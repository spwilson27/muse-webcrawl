Prudhoe Bay Oil Field

The Prudhoe Bay Oil Field is a giant oil field on Alaska's North Slope, in the North Slope Borough. Discovered in 1968, it is the largest oil field in North America, and its development prompted construction of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline. The field is operated by Hilcorp Alaska, which took over from BP in 2020; other major owners include ExxonMobil and ConocoPhillips.

Discovery

Oil seeps near Prudhoe Bay had been known for decades, and the area lay within the Naval Petroleum Reserve. The discovery well, Prudhoe Bay State No. 1, drilled by ARCO and Exxon in 1968, struck a huge reservoir in the Triassic Ivishak sandstone. A confirmation well in 1969 established the field's scale — originally estimated at about 25 billion barrels of oil in place, with more than 9 billion recoverable.

Development

Development awaited a transport route. After years of legal and political struggle over Native land claims and environmental concerns, Congress authorised the Trans-Alaska Pipeline in 1973; oil first flowed to Valdez in June 1977. Production peaked at about 2 million barrels per day in 1988 and has since declined to a few hundred thousand barrels per day, sustained by gas reinjection and enhanced recovery.

Incidents

In 2006, corroded transit lines caused the largest oil spill on the North Slope to that date, forcing a partial shutdown of the field and leading to criminal penalties against BP. Smaller spills and a 2017 well leak have kept maintenance under scrutiny.
