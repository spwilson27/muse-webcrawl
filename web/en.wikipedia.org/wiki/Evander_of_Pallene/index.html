Evander (from Greek Ἐύανδρος, "good man" or "strong man") was a figure in Roman mythology, a culture hero from Pallene in Arcadia who was said to have migrated to Italy and founded a settlement named Pallantium on the future site of Rome, on the hill later known as the Palatine. He features prominently in Virgil's Aeneid, where he is an ally of Aeneas against the Latins.

Mythology

Evander was the son of the god Mercury (Hermes) and the nymph or prophetess Carmentis (Nicostrata). Exiled from Arcadia — in some accounts for killing his father or as the result of civil strife — he led a band of Arcadian settlers to Italy some sixty years before the Trojan War. There he founded Pallantium on the banks of the Tiber and was credited with introducing Greek customs to Italy, including the worship of Pan (identified with Faunus), the festival of the Lupercalia, and, in some traditions, the alphabet and laws.

In the Aeneid

In books 8 to 12 of the Aeneid, the elderly Evander receives Aeneas hospitably, shows him the humble settlement that will become Rome, and allies with him against Turnus and the Latins. He sends his son Pallas to fight alongside Aeneas with a contingent of Arcadian cavalry. Pallas is killed by Turnus, whose wearing of Pallas's sword-belt later seals his own fate when Aeneas kills him in the poem's final scene. Evander's lament over his son's body is one of the poem's most moving passages.

Worship

Evander was honoured at Rome as a hero and was associated with the Ara Maxima of Hercules in the Forum Boarium; tradition held that he had recognised and welcomed Hercules when the hero passed through Italy with the cattle of Geryon, and instituted his worship.
