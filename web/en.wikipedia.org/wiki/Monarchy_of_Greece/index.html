Monarchy of Greece

The monarchy of Greece was the system of government of the Kingdom of Greece, which existed from 1832 until the abolition of the monarchy in 1974. During most of this period Greece was ruled by kings from two dynasties: the Bavarian House of Wittelsbach under Otto, and, after 1863, the Danish-Greek branch of the House of Glücksburg.

History

The modern Greek state emerged from the war of independence against the Ottoman Empire, and the protecting powers installed the Bavarian prince Otto as king in 1832. Otto was deposed in 1862, and the Danish prince William ascended the throne as George I in 1863, beginning the Glücksburg line. The following century was marked by conflict between the Crown and elected governments — most famously the National Schism between King Constantine I and Prime Minister Eleftherios Venizelos during the First World War — by the Asia Minor disaster of 1922, by a republican interlude (1924–1935), by the Metaxas dictatorship, and by civil war after the Second World War.

Abolition

The last king, Constantine II, reigned from 1964. His counter-coup against the military junta in December 1967 failed and he fled into exile. The junta abolished the monarchy in 1973, and after the restoration of democracy in 1974 a referendum confirmed the republic by a two-thirds majority. Greece has remained a parliamentary republic since.
