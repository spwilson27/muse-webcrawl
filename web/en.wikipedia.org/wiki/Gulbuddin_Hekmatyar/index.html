Gulbuddin Hekmatyar (born 26 June 1949) is an Afghan politician and former mujahideen commander. He founded the Hezb-e Islami party and twice served as Prime Minister of Afghanistan during the civil wars of the 1990s.

Early life

Hekmatyar was born in Imam Saheb district, Kunduz Province, to a Pashtun family of the Kharoti tribe. He studied engineering at Kabul University, where he became involved in Islamist student politics, and was imprisoned and later exiled to Pakistan after the 1973 coup.

Soviet–Afghan War

During the Soviet–Afghan War (1979–1989), Hekmatyar's Hezb-e Islami was one of the largest recipients of American, Saudi and Pakistani support channelled through the ISI, despite criticism of his forces' attacks on rival mujahideen groups.

Civil war and aftermath

After the fall of the Najibullah government in 1992, Hekmatyar's forces repeatedly shelled Kabul during the ensuing civil war, earning him the epithet "Butcher of Kabul". He served briefly as prime minister in 1993–94 and again in 1996 before the Taliban took Kabul. Designated a terrorist by the United States in 2003, he nonetheless signed a peace agreement with the Afghan government in 2016 and returned to Kabul, where he later ran for president in 2019.
