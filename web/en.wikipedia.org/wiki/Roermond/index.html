Roermond

Roermond (Dutch pronunciation: [ruːrˈmɔnt]; Limburgish: Remunj) is a city, municipality, and diocese in the southeastern Netherlands, in the province of Limburg. It lies at the confluence of the Roer (Rur) and Meuse (Maas) rivers, near the German border, and has a population of about 57,000.

History

Roermond received city rights around 1231 and was an important trading town of the Duchy of Guelders, a member of the Hanseatic League. It passed between Spanish, Austrian, French, and Dutch rule before becoming part of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. The city is the seat of the Roman Catholic Diocese of Roermond, established in 1559. On 13 April 1992, an earthquake near Roermond — the strongest recorded in the Netherlands, magnitude 5.8 — caused significant damage.

Sights

Notable landmarks include St. Christopher's Cathedral, the Munsterkerk (a Romanesque abbey church), and the city hall on the market square. The nearby Maasplassen lakes, formed by gravel extraction, are a major water sports and recreation area, and the Designer Outlet Roermond attracts shoppers from across the region.
