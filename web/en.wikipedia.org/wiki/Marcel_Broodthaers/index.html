Marcel Broodthaers (28 January 1924 – 28 January 1976) was a Belgian poet, filmmaker, and artist. He is one of the most influential figures of twentieth-century conceptual art, celebrated for questioning the institutions, language, and commerce of art itself.

From poetry to art

Broodthaers began his career as a poet associated with the Belgian surrealists, publishing several volumes of poetry in the 1940s and 1950s and working as a journalist and photographer. In 1964, at the age of forty, he announced his conversion from poetry to visual art in a famous invitation: "Moi aussi, je me suis demandé si je ne pouvais pas vendre quelque chose et réussir dans la vie" — "I, too, wondered whether I could not sell something and succeed in life." His first exhibition embedded unsold copies of his poetry collection Pense-Bête (1964) in plaster.

Major works

Broodthaers's best-known work is the Musée d'Art Moderne, Département des Aigles (Museum of Modern Art, Department of Eagles, 1968–1972), a fictional museum that existed in various forms in his Brussels home, in galleries, and finally at documenta 5 in Kassel. Using crates, postcards, labels, and an eagle motif — a symbol of imperial power — he parodied the museum's authority to confer value and meaning on objects. Other major works include his films (for example La Pluie, 1969) and his installations using mussels, eggshells, and other everyday materials, often combined with wordplay in French, English, and German.

Legacy

Broodthaers's work anticipated and influenced institutional critique, a strand of conceptual art concerned with the workings of galleries, museums, and the art market. He died on his 52nd birthday in Cologne in 1976. His work is held by major museums, including the Museum of Modern Art in New York and the Centre Pompidou in Paris.
