Battles of Tarain
Dates1191 and 1192
LocationTarain (Taraori), near Karnal, northern India

The Battles of Tarain were two battles fought in 1191 and 1192 at Tarain (modern Taraori, in Haryana) between the invading Ghurid army of Muhammad of Ghor (Muhammad Ghori) and the Chauhan Rajput forces of Prithviraj III, king of Ajmer and Delhi. The second battle opened northern India to durable Muslim rule and is considered one of the turning points in Indian history.

First Battle (1191)

Advancing from his base in Ghazni through the Punjab, Muhammad Ghori seized the fortress of Tabarhind (Bathinda) and moved toward Prithviraj's territory. At Tarain the Rajput host met him in a frontal battle; the Ghurid army was routed, Muhammad was wounded, and he escaped only with difficulty. Prithviraj recovered the frontier fort but did not pursue the invasion into the Punjab.

Second Battle (1192)

Muhammad returned the following year with a rebuilt army built around mounted archers. According to the Persian chronicles, he divided his force, harassed the Rajput host with horse-archery, feigned retreat, and then committed a fresh division against the exhausted enemy late in the day. The Rajput army collapsed; Prithviraj was captured and executed. The Ghurids occupied Ajmer and Delhi, and Muhammad's slave-general Qutb ud-Din Aibak extended conquests across the Gangetic plain, founding the Delhi Sultanate in 1206.

Legacy

The battles live on in the epic poem Prithviraj Raso, attributed to Chand Bardai, which romanticises Prithviraj's resistance. Historians debate the scale of the armies and details of the chronicle accounts, but the strategic consequence is undisputed: the Ghurid victory of 1192 ended the era of Hindu kingdoms dominating the northwest and reshaped the political history of the subcontinent.
