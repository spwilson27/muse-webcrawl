Service à la française

Service à la française ("service in the French style") is a traditional method of serving a meal in which all the dishes of a course are placed on the table at once, and diners serve themselves or are helped by servants standing behind them. It was the dominant style of formal dining in France and much of Europe from the 17th to the early 19th century.

Structure

A formal dinner typically consisted of two or three courses, each comprising many dishes arranged symmetrically on the table — soups, roasts, entrées, vegetables, and side dishes simultaneously — followed by a dessert course. Guests took portions from the dishes nearest to them.

Successor

During the 19th century the style was gradually replaced by service à la russe, in which dishes are brought to the table sequentially in individual portions, the practice that dominates formal dining today.
