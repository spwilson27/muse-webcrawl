Same-sex marriage in the United States

Same-sex marriage has been legal nationwide in the United States since June 26, 2015, when the Supreme Court ruled in Obergefell v. Hodges that state bans violate the Fourteenth Amendment's guarantees of due process and equal protection. The Respect for Marriage Act (2022) added federal statutory protection.

History

Early litigation in the 1970s failed, but Hawaii's 1993 Baehr v. Lewin decision prompted the federal Defense of Marriage Act (1996) and many state bans. Massachusetts became the first state to legalise same-sex marriage in 2004 following Goodridge v. Department of Public Health. Other states followed by court rulings, legislation or referendums, while others adopted constitutional amendments prohibiting it.

Federal recognition

In United States v. Windsor (2013) the Supreme Court struck down DOMA's federal non-recognition provision. Two years later Obergefell required all states to license and recognise same-sex marriages. Public support rose from about 27% in 1996 to over 70% in Gallup polls of the 2020s. Following concerns after Dobbs (2022), Congress passed the Respect for Marriage Act, requiring federal and interstate recognition of valid marriages.

Demographics

The Census Bureau counts roughly three-quarters of a million married same-sex couple households in the US. Marriage confers over a thousand federal rights and responsibilities, from taxation and inheritance to hospital visitation and immigration sponsorship.
