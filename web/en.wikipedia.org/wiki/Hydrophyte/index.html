Hydrophyte

Hydrophytes are plants that have adapted to living in aquatic environments (in water or in soil saturated with water); they are also called aquatic plants or macrophytes when large enough to be seen with the naked eye. In the Raunkiær system of life forms, hydrophytes are one of the major classes, reflecting a habitat in which the perennating buds lie under water.

Categories

Hydrophytes are commonly grouped by growth habit: free-floating plants (e.g. water hyacinth, duckweed), submerged plants rooted in sediment with most tissue underwater (e.g. pondweeds, Vallisneria), floating-leaved rooted plants (e.g. water lilies, lotus), and emergent plants rooted underwater with stems and leaves above the surface (e.g. cattails, reeds, papyrus).

Adaptations

Characteristic adaptations include aerenchyma — spongy tissue with large air spaces that transports oxygen to submerged organs and provides buoyancy; reduced or absent xylem and cuticle in submerged leaves; finely dissected underwater leaves that maximise surface area for absorbing dissolved gases and nutrients; and stomata located on the upper surface of floating leaves. Many hydrophytes reproduce vigorously by fragmentation, and some floating species, such as water hyacinth and Salvinia, have become serious invasive weeds of tropical waterways.
