Yabby

Common speciesCherax destructor
FamilyParastacidae
RangeInland waterways of Australia

Yabby is a common name given to several Australian freshwater crayfish, most notably the common yabby, Cherax destructor. Yabbies inhabit rivers, dams, billabongs, and ephemeral waterholes across much of inland Australia, including the Murray–Darling Basin.

Description and ecology

Yabbies are typically blue-brown to olive in colour and grow to about 20–30 cm. They are burrowers: during drought they dig deep burrows down to the water table and can survive underground for years, emerging when rains refill the waterways. They feed on decaying plant matter, small invertebrates, and carrion, and are an important food source for fish, birds, and platypuses.

Fishing and aquaculture

Yabbies are caught recreationally with baited traps ("opera house" nets and drop nets) and are farmed commercially on a modest scale. They are eaten as a delicacy in Australia and exported live. Catch limits and trap regulations vary by state.
