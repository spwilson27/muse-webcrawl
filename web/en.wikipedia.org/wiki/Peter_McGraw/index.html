A. Peter McGraw is an American psychologist and marketing professor at the University of Colorado Boulder's Leeds School of Business. He is known for his research on humour and emotion, particularly the benign violation theory, developed with Caleb Warren, which proposes that humour arises when something is perceived as both a violation of how the world ought to be and simultaneously benign.

Research

McGraw directs the Humor Research Lab (HuRL) at the University of Colorado. His work examines what makes things funny, the role of humour in consumer behaviour and branding, and the consequences of humour in workplaces and negotiations. The benign violation theory, published in Psychological Science in 2010, has become one of the most influential scientific accounts of humour, explaining phenomena such as why tickling, insults among friends and absurd jokes can be amusing when the violation is appraised as harmless.

Publications and media

With Joel Warner, McGraw co-authored The Humor Code: A Global Search for What Makes Things Funny (2014), reporting a worldwide journey to test humour theories in stand-up clubs, cartoon studios and conflict zones. He has also written on moral judgement, decision-making and well-being, and hosts the podcast Solo: The Single Person's Guide to a Remarkable Life.
