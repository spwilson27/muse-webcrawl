Travers Stakes

Location: Saratoga Race Course, Saratoga Springs, New York
Inaugurated: 1864
Distance: 1¼ miles (10 furlongs)
Surface: Dirt

The Travers Stakes is an American Grade I Thoroughbred horse race held annually at Saratoga Race Course in Saratoga Springs, New York. First run in 1864, it is the oldest stakes race for three-year-olds in the United States and is often called the "Midsummer Derby," coming after the spring Triple Crown races.

History

The race is named for William R. Travers, a president of the Saratoga Association whose horse Kentucky won the inaugural running. The trophy, the Man o' War Cup, is a gold Tiffany cup presented since 1920; the winning owner's silks are painted on a lawn jockey outside the clubhouse, and a canoe in the infield lake is repainted in the winner's colors—a Saratoga tradition.

Notable runnings

Famous winners include Man o' War (1920), Twenty Grand, Whirlaway, Native Dancer, Buckpasser, Damascus, Alydar (awarded the 1978 race on the disqualification of Affirmed), Easy Goer, Holy Bull, Point Given, Bernardini, and Arcangelo. In 2015, Triple Crown winner American Pharoah was upset by Keen Ice, reinforcing Saratoga's nickname as the "Graveyard of Champions." In 2016 Arrogate set a track record of 1:59.36. The 2023 running was won by Arcangelo, trained by Jena Antonucci, the first woman to train a Travers winner.

Records

Run at varying distances over its history, the race settled at 1¼ miles in 1904. The purse has grown to well over one million dollars, making it one of the richest races for three-year-olds in North America.
