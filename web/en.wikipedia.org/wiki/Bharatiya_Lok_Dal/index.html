The Bharatiya Lok Dal (BLD) was an Indian political party formed in 1974 by Charan Singh through the merger of several opposition parties. Representing chiefly the interests of farmers and the rural peasantry, particularly in Uttar Pradesh and northern India, it became a major force in Indian politics in the 1970s.

History

Charan Singh founded the party after leaving the Indian National Congress and bringing together groups including his Bharatiya Kranti Dal, the Swatantra Party and others. During the Emergency of 1975–77, its leaders were jailed along with other opposition figures. In 1977 the Bharatiya Lok Dal merged with the Janata Party coalition, which won the general election that ended the Emergency; Charan Singh became Home Minister and later, in 1979, Prime Minister of India.

Legacy

After the Janata experiment collapsed, the party's tradition continued through successor organisations such as the Lokdal and later regional farmers' parties led by Charan Singh's family and followers.
