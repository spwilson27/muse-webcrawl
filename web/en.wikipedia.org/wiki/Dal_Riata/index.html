Dal Riata

Dal Riata (also written Dál Riata or Dalriada) was a Gaelic kingdom that encompassed territory in northeastern Ireland (County Antrim) and the western seaboard of Scotland (Argyll and the Inner Hebrides) from roughly the late 5th century until the mid-9th century. It was one of the principal kingdoms from which the later kingdom of Scotland emerged.

History

According to later tradition, Fergus Mór mac Eirc founded the Scottish part of the kingdom around 498 by bringing settlers from Irish Dál Riata across the North Channel. The kingdom's power peaked under Áedán mac Gabráin (r. 574–608), who campaigned widely but was checked at the Battle of Degsastan in 603. The monastery of Iona, founded by Columba in 563 under Dal Riata's protection, became a centre of Christianity for northern Britain.

Dal Riata suffered defeats by the Picts in the 8th century and pressure from Norse raiders, and in the 840s its king Kenneth MacAlpin (Cináed mac Ailpín) also became king of the Picts, an event traditionally taken as the foundation of the Kingdom of Alba — the origin of Scotland.
