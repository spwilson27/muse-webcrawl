X/Open Company, Ltd. was a consortium founded in 1984 by a group of European UNIX systems manufacturers — Bull, ICL, Siemens, Olivetti and Nixdorf — later joined by US and Japanese vendors, to promote open systems and to identify and promote standards for software portability, particularly around the UNIX operating system. Its name derived from the idea of "open" systems based on UNIX.

Portability Guide and branding

X/Open published the X/Open Portability Guide (XPG), a multi-volume specification defining a Common Applications Environment: the C language, operating-system interfaces (aligned over time with POSIX), internationalisation, and other APIs. Successive issues — XPG3 (1989) and XPG4 (1992) — became influential benchmarks. Vendors whose products passed X/Open's verification tests could use the X/Open brand; from 1993 X/Open also became the owner of the UNIX trademark, which was subsequently used for the Single UNIX Specification branding programme ("UNIX 93", "UNIX 95", and later versions).

Merger into The Open Group

In February 1996 X/Open merged with the Open Software Foundation (OSF) — its erstwhile rival in the "Unix wars" — to form The Open Group, which continues to maintain the Single UNIX Specification and the UNIX trademark. The X/Open specifications live on within that framework: the XPG lineage feeds directly into the modern POSIX/Single UNIX Specification documents.
