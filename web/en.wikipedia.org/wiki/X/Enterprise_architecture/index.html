Enterprise architecture

Enterprise architecture (EA) is a practice for conducting enterprise analysis, design, planning and implementation to guide an organisation through business, information, process and technology changes needed to execute its strategy. It applies architecture principles to align an organisation's structure and IT systems with its goals.

Overview

Enterprise architects model the current ("as-is") and target ("to-be") states of the enterprise across domains — typically business architecture, data architecture, application architecture and technology architecture — and plan the transition between them. The discipline emerged in the 1980s from efforts at IBM and the work of John Zachman, whose Zachman Framework (1987) organised architectural descriptions by stakeholder perspective and interrogative (what, how, where, who, when, why).

Frameworks

Major frameworks include TOGAF, maintained by The Open Group and structured around the Architecture Development Method (ADM); the Zachman Framework; FEAF in the US federal government; and MODAF/DoDAF in defence. Modelling is often done in the ArchiMate language, sometimes complemented by BPMN for processes and UML for software.

Criticism and evolution

EA has been criticised for producing weighty documentation disconnected from delivery. In response, modern practice emphasises lightweight, "just enough" architecture, agile collaboration, and data-driven decision support rather than centralised control.
