Open Software Foundation

The Open Software Foundation (OSF) was a software industry consortium founded in 1988 to create an open standard for the Unix operating system. It was formed largely in response to the AT&T–Sun Microsystems alliance behind Unix System V Release 4, which rival vendors feared would dominate the Unix market — an episode central to the so-called Unix wars.

Founding and members

OSF was announced in May 1988 by Apollo Computer, the Bull Group, Digital Equipment Corporation, Hewlett-Packard, IBM, Nixdorf and Siemens. It was headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and funded by member fees and technology sponsorships.

Technologies

OSF's flagship product was OSF/1, a Unix operating system built on the Mach kernel, first released in the early 1990s and adopted most notably as the basis of Digital's Tru64 UNIX. OSF also produced Motif, the widely used graphical user-interface toolkit; the Distributed Computing Environment (DCE) for distributed systems; and the Distributed Management Environment (DME).

Merger

By the mid-1990s the Unix wars were subsiding and industry consolidation followed. In 1996 OSF merged with X/Open to form The Open Group, which continues as custodian of the UNIX trademark and standards such as POSIX certification, TOGAF and ArchiMate.
