X/Open Company, Ltd. was a consortium founded by European computer vendors in 1984 to promote open systems standards, principally around the Unix operating system. It published the X/Open Portability Guide (XPG), a set of specifications defining a Common Application Environment intended to make application software portable across different vendors' Unix systems. X/Open's work on the Unix specification — culminating in the Single UNIX Specification and ownership of the UNIX trademark itself — underlies how "Unix" is defined and branded to this day.

History

X/Open was founded in 1984 by Bull, ICL, Siemens, Olivetti and Nixdorf, and grew to include most major systems vendors. Its portability guides progressed from XPG1 (1985) through XPG4 (1992), progressively incorporating POSIX, the C programming language, window systems (the X Window System), SQL databases and networking standards. In 1993, Novell transferred the UNIX trademark and the Unix systems business definitions to X/Open, allowing it to license the "UNIX" brand to conforming systems. In 1994 X/Open published Spec 1170 — so named for its 1,170 application programming interfaces — which became the basis of the Single UNIX Specification ("UNIX 95").

Merger into The Open Group

In February 1996, X/Open merged with the Open Software Foundation to form The Open Group, which continues to maintain the Single UNIX Specification, certify UNIX-branded products, and own the UNIX trademark. Products certified over the years have included IBM AIX, HP-UX, macOS and others.
