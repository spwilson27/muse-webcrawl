Single UNIX Specification

The Single UNIX Specification (SUS) is a standard for computer operating systems, compliance with which is required to qualify for using the "UNIX" trademark. It is developed and maintained by the Austin Group and is based on earlier work by X/Open, whose "Portability Guide" (XPG) lineage led to the UNIX 95 and UNIX 98 certifications. The Open Group owns the UNIX trademark and administers certification.

Contents

The specification comprises the Base Specifications — technically identical to IEEE Std 1003.1 (POSIX) since 2001 — together with the X/Open Curses (XCURSES) specification. It defines system interfaces and headers, commands and utilities, a shell, and networking interfaces. Products are certified against versions such as UNIX 03 (corresponding to SUS version 3) and UNIX V7 (SUS version 4).

Certified systems

Certified systems have included AIX, HP-UX, Solaris, Tru64 UNIX, z/OS, and macOS (certified UNIX 03 since version 10.5). Most Linux systems follow the specification closely but are generally uncertified, preferring the Linux Standard Base; one exception was Inspur K-UX, certified UNIX 03.
