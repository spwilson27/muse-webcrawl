Open Software Foundation

The Open Software Foundation (OSF) was a non-profit industry consortium founded in 1988 to develop an open standard for an implementation of the Unix operating system, in reaction to the alliance between AT&T and Sun Microsystems over System V Release 4. Its founding members included Apollo Computer, Digital Equipment Corporation, Hewlett-Packard, IBM, Bull, Nixdorf and Siemens.

Technologies

The OSF's best-known products were OSF/1, a Unix operating system based on the Mach kernel (adopted by DEC as the basis of Digital UNIX/Tru64); the Motif graphical user interface toolkit; and the Distributed Computing Environment (DCE), a framework for distributed applications that included a remote procedure call system, directory service and the DCE Distributed File System. It also sponsored the Common Desktop Environment (CDE) jointly with other vendors.

Mergers

The "Unix wars" subsided in the early 1990s, and in 1994 OSF announced a merger of its pre-structured technology and branding roles with X/Open Company; the combined organisation formally became The Open Group in 1996, custodian of the UNIX trademark and the Single UNIX Specification.
