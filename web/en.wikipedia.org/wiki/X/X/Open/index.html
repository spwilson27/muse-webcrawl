X/Open

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The X/Open Portability Guide defined a common Unix environment

X/Open Company, Ltd. was a consortium of computer manufacturers founded in 1984 to promote open standards in the Unix operating system world and to reduce fragmentation between competing Unix variants. Its founding members included major European and international vendors such as Bull, ICL, Siemens, Olivetti and Nixdorf.

Work

X/Open's best-known product was the X/Open Portability Guide (XPG), a multi-volume specification of a Common Applications Environment covering the C programming language, system calls derived from Unix and later aligned with POSIX, internationalisation, and networking. Products conforming to the guide could carry the X/Open brand, giving software developers a portable target platform. X/Open also standardised the XA interface for distributed transaction processing — the protocol by which transaction managers coordinate resource managers such as databases — which remains in use today.

Merger into The Open Group

During the "Unix wars" of the late 1980s and early 1990s X/Open acted as a neutral standards body while rival camps formed around the Open Software Foundation (OSF) and Unix International. In February 1996 X/Open merged with the Open Software Foundation to form The Open Group. The X/Open and OSF specification efforts had already converged on the Single UNIX Specification, and X/Open's branding and conformance-testing work passed to the new organisation, which continues to own the UNIX trademark.
