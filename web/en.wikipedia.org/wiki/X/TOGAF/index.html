TOGAF, an acronym of The Open Group Architecture Framework, is a framework and methodology for enterprise architecture maintained by The Open Group. It provides a structured approach for designing, planning, implementing and governing an enterprise information technology architecture, and is used by large organisations worldwide. Within the X/Open standards lineage, TOGAF forms part of the family of open architecture standards administered by The Open Group.

Architecture Development Method

The core of TOGAF is the Architecture Development Method (ADM), an iterative cycle of phases: a Preliminary phase, then Architecture Vision (A), Business Architecture (B), Information Systems Architectures (C), Technology Architecture (D), Opportunities and Solutions (E), Migration Planning (F), Implementation Governance (G), and Architecture Change Management (H), with Requirements Management running continuously at the centre. Organisations are expected to adapt the ADM rather than apply it rigidly.

Other components

The standard also includes an Architecture Content Framework (describing deliverables, artefacts and building blocks), the Enterprise Continuum (a classification of architecture and solution assets from generic foundations to organisation-specific architectures), reference models such as the Technical Reference Model and the Integrated Information Infrastructure Reference Model, and the Architecture Capability Framework covering skills, governance and the TOGAF certification programme for practitioners and tools.

History

TOGAF was first published in 1995, drawing on the U.S. Department of Defense's Technical Architecture Framework for Information Management (TAFIM). Subsequent versions progressively broadened the framework beyond IT architecture. Version 9 (2009) and 9.2 (2018) became the most widely taught editions; the 10th Edition (2022) restructured the standard into a fundamental content set plus series guides on topics such as agile architecture and digital enterprise.
