Wiener schnitzel (German: [ˈviːnɐ ˈʃnɪtsl̩], "Viennese cutlet") is a type of schnitzel made of a thin, breaded, pan-fried veal cutlet. It is one of the best-known specialities of Viennese cuisine and a national dish of Austria. The term is a protected designation in Austria and Germany: a true Wiener schnitzel must be made from veal; the identical dish made with pork is labelled "Schnitzel Wiener Art" ("Viennese-style schnitzel").

Preparation

The veal is cut from the leg, pounded thin, salted, dredged in flour, dipped in beaten egg, and coated in breadcrumbs without pressing the crumbs down, so that the coating puffs into a golden, rippled crust ("souffléd") when the cutlet is pan-fried in lard or clarified butter. It is traditionally served with a wedge of lemon, and accompaniments such as parsley potatoes (Petersilkartoffeln), cucumber salad, or Viennese potato salad.

Origins debate

The dish's origin is disputed between Austria and Italy. A popular legend credits Field Marshal Joseph Radetzky with bringing the recipe for Milan's cotoletta alla milanese to Vienna in 1857, but food historians have shown breaded veal dishes in Vienna considerably earlier, and the Radetzky story is now considered apocryphal. Similar dishes exist across central Europe and beyond, including the French escalope, the Argentine milanesa, and the Japanese tonkatsu.
