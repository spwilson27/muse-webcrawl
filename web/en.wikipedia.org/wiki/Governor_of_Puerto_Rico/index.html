Governor of Puerto Rico

The governor of Puerto Rico (Spanish: gobernador de Puerto Rico) is the head of government of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico. The governor is the chief executive of the commonwealth's executive branch and is responsible for the administration of the government, the enforcement of the Constitution and laws of Puerto Rico, and command of the Puerto Rico National Guard. Since 1948, the governor has been elected by the people of Puerto Rico; before that, governors were appointed by the president of the United States. The first native Puerto Rican appointed governor was Jesús T. Piñero (1946); the first elected governor was Luis Muñoz Marín, who took office on January 2, 1949. The governor's official residence is La Fortaleza in San Juan, the oldest executive mansion in continuous use in the Americas.

History

Puerto Rico was ruled by Spanish governors from the early 16th century until 1898, when the island was ceded to the United States after the Spanish–American War. Under the Foraker Act (1900) and the Jones–Shafroth Act (1917), the U.S. president appointed the governor, while Puerto Ricans gradually gained an elected legislature and, in 1917, U.S. citizenship. The Elective Governor Act of 1947 at last allowed Puerto Ricans to elect their governor. Luis Muñoz Marín of the Popular Democratic Party won the first election in 1948 and led the creation of the Commonwealth (Estado Libre Asociado) under the Constitution of 1952, serving four terms until 1965.

Powers and duties

Article IV of the Constitution of Puerto Rico vests executive power in the governor, who must:

  • Execute the laws and be responsible for the faithful administration of the government
  • Appoint, with the advice and consent of the Senate, the heads of executive departments, judges, and other officials
  • Present the annual budget and state of the commonwealth messages to the Legislative Assembly
  • Sign or veto bills passed by the Legislative Assembly (vetoes overridable by two-thirds of both houses)
  • Grant pardons and commutations
  • Command the militia (Puerto Rico National Guard) and call special legislative sessions

The governor serves a four-year term with no term limit and is inaugurated on January 2 following the general election. If the office becomes vacant, the Secretary of State succeeds; the vacancy crisis of 2019 — when Governor Ricardo Rosselló resigned amid mass protests (the "Ricky Renuncia" movement) and his handpicked successor Pedro Pierluisi's appointment as Secretary of State was ruled unconstitutional — resulted in Secretary of Justice Wanda Vázquez Garced becoming governor, and clarified the line of succession under Law 7 of 2005 and the Constitution.

Elected governors (selected)

Luis Muñoz Marín (1949–1965), Roberto Sánchez Vilella (1965–1969), Luis A. Ferré (1969–1973), Rafael Hernández Colón (1973–1977, 1985–1993), Carlos Romero Barceló (1977–1985), Pedro Rosselló (1993–2001), Sila María Calderón (2001–2005, the first woman elected governor), Aníbal Acevedo Vilá (2005–2009), Luis Fortuño (2009–2013), Alejandro García Padilla (2013–2017), Ricardo Rosselló (2017–2019), Wanda Vázquez Garced (2019–2021), Pedro Pierluisi (2021–2025), and Jenniffer González Colón (from 2025). Puerto Rico's governorship has alternated mainly between the Popular Democratic Party (PPD) and the New Progressive Party (PNP), divided chiefly by the island's status question.
