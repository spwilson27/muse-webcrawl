Sursock Museum

The Nicolas Ibrahim Sursock Palace, home of the museum

Location: Achrafieh, Beirut, Lebanon
Established: 1961
Type: Modern and contemporary art museum
Founder: Nicolas Ibrahim Sursock (bequest)

The Sursock Museum, officially the Nicolas Ibrahim Sursock Museum, is a modern and contemporary art museum in Beirut, Lebanon. It is housed in the opulent palace built by Nicolas Ibrahim Sursock (1875–1952), a member of one of Beirut's most prominent Greek Orthodox families, in the Achrafieh district.

History

Sursock built the villa between 1909 and 1912, blending Venetian and Ottoman architectural styles with white façades, twin staircases, and carved stone ornament. In his will he bequeathed the palace to the city of Beirut on condition that it become a museum open to the public. It opened in 1961 and for decades hosted the Salon d'Automne, a key exhibition for Lebanese artists.

Renovation and reopening

The museum closed in 2008 for an extensive renovation designed by French architect Jean-Michel Wilmotte and Lebanese architect Jacques Abou Khaled, which added underground exhibition spaces and nearly quadrupled its area. It reopened in October 2015. The building was damaged by the Beirut port explosion of 4 August 2020, which shattered windows and artwork; after restoration work it reopened again in the following years.

Collection

The permanent collection of some 1,500 works focuses on Lebanese art from the late 19th century to the present, including paintings by Georges Daoud Corm, Saliba Douaihy, and Paul Guiragossian, alongside Japanese woodblock prints and Islamic art collected by the Sursock family.
