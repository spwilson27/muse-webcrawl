Canadian cuisine

Canadian cuisine consists of the cooking traditions and practices of Canada, shaped by Indigenous peoples, French and British settlers, and waves of immigration from around the world. It varies greatly by region.

Iconic dishesPoutine, tourtière, butter tarts, Nanaimo bars, Montreal bagels, peameal bacon
Key ingredientMaple syrup

Origins

Indigenous cuisines used corn, beans, squash, wild rice, game, fish and berries. French settlers in Quebec developed hearty fare such as tourtière, pea soup and maple sugar; British and Loyalist settlers brought roasts, puddings and baking.

Regional cuisines

Quebec is known for poutine (fries, cheese curds, gravy), Montreal-style bagels and smoked meat. The Maritimes emphasise seafood, lobster and rappie pie. The Prairies feature beef, grains, Saskatoon berries and perogies from Ukrainian immigration. British Columbia highlights Pacific salmon and Asian-influenced fusion.

National symbols and products

Maple syrup — Canada produces most of the world's supply — Tim Hortons coffee culture, Canadian bacon, butter tarts, Nanaimo bars, ketchup chips and Kraft Dinner are widely recognised. See Poutine and Maple syrup.
