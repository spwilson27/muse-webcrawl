Oswego, New York

Oswego is a city in Oswego County, New York, United States, located on Lake Ontario at the mouth of the Oswego River. It is the county seat and a port city, historically nicknamed "The Port City of Central New York." Its population was about 17,000 at the 2020 census.

The site was an important trading post and military position in the colonial era; the British established Fort Ontario there, and the area saw fighting in the French and Indian War and the War of 1812. The Oswego Canal, completed in 1828, connected the city to the Erie Canal, and the harbour became a major commercial port for the Great Lakes trade.

Oswego is home to the State University of New York at Oswego (SUNY Oswego), founded in 1861 as a teacher training school by Edward Austin Sheldon. During the Second World War, Fort Ontario hosted the only refugee camp in the United States for victims of the Holocaust, sheltering nearly 1,000 European refugees. The region is famous for extremely heavy lake-effect snow.
