Apple IIGS

The Apple IIGS (1986), the most powerful model of the Apple II family.

The Apple IIGS (styled as Apple IIGS) is a personal computer released by Apple Computer on September 15, 1986. It is the fifth and most powerful model in the Apple II series, and the first computer produced by Apple to feature a colour graphical user interface, as well as "desktop" style input. The "GS" in the name stands for "Graphics and Sound", referring to its enhanced multimedia capabilities, especially its state-of-the-art sound hardware, which surpassed all other personal computers of the era by a wide margin.

Hardware

The IIGS is built around the 16-bit Western Design Center 65C816 microprocessor running at 2.8 MHz, a chip backwards-compatible with the 6502 processors used in earlier Apple II models, allowing it to run the vast majority of existing Apple II software. It shipped with either 256 KB or 512 KB of RAM, expandable to 8 MB. Unlike its predecessors, the IIGS uses a detachable keyboard and supports the Apple Desktop Bus (ADB), a peripheral interconnect that later appeared on Macintosh computers.

Its graphics were a major advance: in addition to the text and graphics modes of earlier Apple II machines, it offered "super high resolution" modes of 320×200 in 16 colours or 640×200 in 4 colours, selectable from a palette of 4,096 colours. The machine also supported the ProDOS 16 operating system and, later, GS/OS, a full graphical operating system resembling the Macintosh Finder.

Sound

The sound subsystem centres on the Ensoniq 5503 Digital Oscillator Chip, a wavetable synthesis chip with 32 oscillators supporting 15 stereo voices. This gave the IIGS musical capabilities comparable to dedicated synthesizers of the time and far beyond any competing home computer.

Legacy

Apple continued to sell the IIGS alongside the Macintosh for education markets, and it remained popular in schools into the early 1990s. Production ended in December 1992. A limited "Woz" edition, signed by Apple co-founder Steve Wozniak, was produced for the first 50,000 units.
