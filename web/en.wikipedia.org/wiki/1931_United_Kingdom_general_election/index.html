The 1931 United Kingdom general election was held on Tuesday, 27 October 1931, amid the financial crisis of the Great Depression. The recently formed National Government, a coalition led by Labour Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald but dominated by the Conservative Party, won a landslide victory — the largest in British electoral history — taking 554 of the 615 seats in the House of Commons.

Background

In August 1931, the Labour government of MacDonald split over proposed cuts to unemployment benefit intended to defend the pound sterling and balance the budget. MacDonald formed a National Government with the Conservatives and Liberals and was promptly expelled from the Labour Party. In September Britain abandoned the gold standard, and the government sought a "doctor's mandate" from the electorate to handle the crisis with a free hand.

Campaign and result

The election devastated the Labour Party under Arthur Henderson, which fell from 287 seats to just 52; Henderson himself lost his seat. The Conservatives won 470 seats, while the pro-government Liberals and National Labour also contributed to the coalition's overwhelming majority of over 490. The result remains the greatest defeat ever suffered by Labour and the largest share of the vote — about 67 per cent — won by any British government.

Aftermath

The National Government, increasingly Conservative in character, governed throughout the 1930s. MacDonald remained Prime Minister until 1935, when he was succeeded by Stanley Baldwin. The election shaped a decade of British politics dominated by economic recovery, rearmament, and the approach of the Second World War.
