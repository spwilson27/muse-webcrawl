Ferenc Puskás (1 April 1927 – 17 November 2006) was a Hungarian footballer and manager, widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time. A prolific forward famed for his devastating left foot, he captained Hungary's legendary "Mighty Magyars" of the 1950s and later became a star of the great Real Madrid side of Alfredo Di Stéfano. FIFA's award for the most beautiful goal of the year, the Puskás Award, is named in his honour.

Early career and the Mighty Magyars

Puskás was born in Budapest and grew up next door to the ground of Kispest, the club he joined as a boy and which was reorganized as the army team Budapest Honvéd. Stocky and short by forward standards, he compensated with sublime close control, vision, and a left-foot shot of extraordinary power and precision. Nicknamed "the Galloping Major" (he held an army commission) and "Öcsi" ("little brother"), he won five Hungarian league titles with Honvéd.

As captain, Puskás led Hungary through a golden age: Olympic gold at Helsinki 1952, and a four-year unbeaten run that included the famous 6–3 victory over England at Wembley in November 1953 — the first defeat of England at home by a continental side — and a 7–1 win in the return match in Budapest. In the 1954 World Cup final, Hungary led West Germany 2–0 with goals from Puskás and Czibor, only to lose 3–2 in the "Miracle of Bern"; a late Puskás equalizer was disallowed for offside. His international record of 84 goals in 85 appearances stood as a marvel of scoring consistency.

Real Madrid

After the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, Puskás did not return home from a Honvéd tour and was banned by FIFA for two years at Hungary's instigation. Signed by Real Madrid in 1958, aged 31 and overweight, he slimmed down and formed a legendary partnership with Di Stéfano. With Madrid he won five consecutive Spanish league titles (1961–65) and three European Cups (1959, 1960, 1966). In the 1960 European Cup Final at Hampden Park, a 7–3 rout of Eintracht Frankfurt often called the greatest club match ever played, Puskás scored four goals and Di Stéfano three. He finished as the Spanish league's top scorer (Pichichi Trophy) four times. He also played four times for Spain, including at the 1962 World Cup.

Managerial career and legacy

As a coach Puskás worked across the world — in Greece, where he led Panathinaikos to the 1971 European Cup Final (losing to Ajax), as well as in Spain, Chile, Egypt, Paraguay, Australia, and briefly with the Hungarian national team in 1993. Hungary's national stadium in Budapest bears his name (Puskás Aréna, opened 2019 on the site of the old Ferenc Puskás Stadium). He suffered from Alzheimer's disease in his final years and died in Budapest in 2006, receiving a state funeral.
