Étienne Pascal

Étienne Pascal (2 May 1588 – 24 September 1651) was a French tax official, lawyer, and amateur mathematician, best known as the father of the philosopher and scientist Blaise Pascal. He was a respected member of the Mersenne circle of savants in Paris, and the curve known as the limaçon of Pascal was named after him.

Life

Born in Clermont (now Clermont-Ferrand), Pascal studied law in Paris and rose in the fiscal administration of Auvergne. Widowed in 1626, he devoted himself to his three children's education and to science. In 1631 the family moved to Paris, where his salon-like household attracted leading mathematicians of the day.

Mathematical interests

Étienne Pascal participated in Marin Mersenne's informal academy, debating questions of geometry and mechanics with figures such as Roberval and Fermat. His name attaches to the limaçon, a curve he investigated; Roberval coined the name "limaçon de Pascal" in his honour.

Rouen and later years

In 1639 Pascal was appointed the king's deputy commissioner for taxes in Upper Normandy, based in Rouen — the demanding calculations of that office later inspired his son Blaise to invent the Pascaline mechanical calculator. He died in Paris in 1651.
