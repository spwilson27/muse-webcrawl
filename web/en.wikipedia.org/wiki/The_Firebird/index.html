The Firebird

The Firebird (French: L'Oiseau de feu; Russian: Жар-птица, Zhar-ptitsa) is a ballet and orchestral concert work by the Russian composer Igor Stravinsky. It was written for Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes company and first performed in Paris on 25 June 1910. The work was Stravinsky's breakthrough, launching his international career.

Synopsis

Prince Ivan, hunting at night, captures the magical Firebird in the enchanted garden of the evil Koschei. In exchange for her freedom she gives him one of her feathers, which will summon her in time of need. Ivan falls in love with one of thirteen princesses held by Koschei, who attempts to turn him to stone; Ivan waves the feather, the Firebird appears and forces Koschei and his court into an irresistible dance, then reveals the soul of Koschei hidden in an egg. Ivan smashes the egg, the spell is broken, and the ballet ends in a solemn hymn of rejoicing.

Music

The score, written for an enormous orchestra, combines the chromaticism and lush orchestration of Stravinsky's teacher Rimsky-Korsakov with folk melodies and striking innovations, notably the eerie string glissando harmonics that introduce the Firebird. Its most famous numbers include the Firebird's dance, the "Infernal Dance of King Koschei", the Berceuse, and the radiant Finale.

Suites and legacy

Stravinsky extracted three concert suites from the score, in 1911, 1919, and 1945; the 1919 suite, for reduced orchestra, is the most frequently performed. The ballet remains in the repertory, and the music is among the most popular orchestral works of the twentieth century, heralding the composer's collaborations on Petrushka and The Rite of Spring.
