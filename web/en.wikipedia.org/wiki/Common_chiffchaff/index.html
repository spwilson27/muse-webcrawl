Common chiffchaff

The common chiffchaff is named after its distinctive two-note song.

OrderPasseriformes
FamilyPhylloscopidae
GenusPhylloscopus
SpeciesP. collybita
Binomial namePhylloscopus collybita (Vieillot, 1817)

The common chiffchaff, or simply the chiffchaff (Phylloscopus collybita), is a common and widespread leaf warbler which breeds in open woodlands throughout northern and temperate Europe and the Palearctic. It is a migratory passerine, wintering in southern and western Europe, southern Asia, and north Africa. Greenish-brown above and off-white below, it is named onomatopoeically for its simple, repetitive chiff-chaff song.

Description

The common chiffchaff is a small warbler, 10–12 cm long, with olive-green upperparts, pale yellowish-white underparts, a faint pale supercilium, and dull dark legs. It is very similar in appearance to the willow warbler, from which it is best distinguished by its song, shorter wing, darker legs, and habit of frequently flicking its tail. Several subspecies are recognised, including the Siberian chiffchaff (P. c. tristis), a pale, grey-toned form that is a rare vagrant to western Europe.

Behaviour

Chiffchaffs are insectivorous, gleaning small insects and spiders from foliage and flycatching in short sallies. The female builds a domed nest of grass and leaves on or near the ground, laying five or six eggs. Northern and eastern populations are fully migratory, while populations in western Europe are increasingly short-distance migrants or resident, with growing numbers wintering in Britain and Ireland, a trend linked to climate change.

Status

With an enormous range and a global population estimated in the tens of millions, the species is classified as Least Concern by the IUCN.
