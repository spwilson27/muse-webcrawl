Speicherstadt

Speicherstadt
Location: Hamburg, Germany
Built: 1883–1927
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2015)

The Speicherstadt (German pronunciation, literally "City of Warehouses") is the largest warehouse district in the world in which the buildings stand on timber-pile foundations. It is located in the port of Hamburg, Germany, within the HafenCity quarter, and was built from 1883 to 1927.

History

The district was constructed as a free zone to transfer goods without paying customs, after Hamburg joined the German customs union. The red-brick Gothic Revival warehouse blocks, built on oak piles along canals of the Elbe, stored high-value goods such as coffee, tea, cocoa, spices, tobacco and Oriental carpets. Companies operating in the district rented warehouse space and offices in the same buildings.

Architecture and museums

The warehouses are multi-storey buildings with loading doors both to the canals and to the streets, decorated with terracotta ornamentation. Today the district houses museums including Miniatur Wunderland, the Hamburg Dungeon, the German Customs Museum and the International Maritime Museum nearby, as well as carpet dealers and creative businesses.

World Heritage

In 2015 the Speicherstadt, together with the adjacent Kontorhaus District including the Chilehaus, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, the first site in Hamburg to receive the designation.
