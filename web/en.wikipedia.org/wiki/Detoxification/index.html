Detoxification

Detoxification or detoxication (detox for short) is the physiological or medicinal removal of toxic substances from a living organism, including the human body, which is mainly carried out by the liver. Additionally, it can refer to the period of substance withdrawal during which an organism returns to homeostasis after long-term use of an addictive substance. The term is also used in alternative medicine for a variety of commercial "detox" diets and treatments whose efficacy is not supported by scientific evidence.

The liver is the principal organ of detoxification, chemically transforming toxins for excretion.

Physiological detoxification

The liver continuously detoxifies the blood: enzymes (notably the cytochrome P450 family) oxidise and conjugate foreign compounds — drugs, alcohol, metabolic waste such as ammonia — converting them into forms the kidneys or bile can excrete. The kidneys, lungs, skin and gut also contribute to the elimination of waste products. This natural process operates constantly and does not require special diets in healthy people.

Medical detoxification

In medicine, detoxification includes treatment of acute intoxication and overdose (e.g. activated charcoal, antidotes, dialysis) and the managed withdrawal from alcohol or drugs. Medically supervised withdrawal can be essential because abrupt cessation, particularly of alcohol or benzodiazepines, can cause dangerous seizures and delirium tremens.

"Detox" in alternative medicine

Commercial detox diets, cleanses, teas, foot pads and similar products claim to remove unspecified "toxins". Medical and scientific bodies have criticised these claims as vague and unsupported: they rarely identify the toxins supposedly removed, and systematic reviews find no compelling evidence that such regimens eliminate toxins or improve health beyond ordinary effects of temporary dietary change. Some products can be harmful, particularly to the liver and kidneys.
