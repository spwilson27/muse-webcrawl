BeagleBoard

Developer: Texas Instruments, BeagleBoard.org community
Released: 2008 (original); 2013 (BeagleBone Black)
Type: Single-board computers

The BeagleBoard is a low-power, open-source single-board computer family produced by Texas Instruments in association with Digi-Key and the BeagleBoard.org community. First introduced in 2008, the boards were designed as educational and development platforms for the ARM-based OMAP and Sitara processors, with hardware designs freely available.

Models

The original BeagleBoard (2008) used a TI OMAP3530 system on a chip combining an ARM Cortex-A8 core with a DSP and graphics accelerator. Successors included the smaller, cheaper BeagleBone (2011) and the widely adopted BeagleBone Black (2013), based on the Sitara AM335x with a 1 GHz Cortex-A8, 512 MB RAM, onboard flash, and two 46-pin expansion headers plus programmable real-time units (PRUs). Later entries include the BeagleBoard-X15, BeagleBone AI, the Raspberry Pi–compatible-sized PocketBeagle, and the RISC-V BeagleV.

Software

The boards run Linux distributions such as Debian (officially supported images), Ubuntu, and Ångström, as well as Android and various real-time operating systems. They are used in prototyping, robotics, 3D printers (notably driving controller boards), and embedded teaching labs.
