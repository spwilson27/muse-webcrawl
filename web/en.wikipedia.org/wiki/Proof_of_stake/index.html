Proof of stake

Proof of stake (PoS) is a class of consensus mechanisms for blockchains in which validators are chosen to create new blocks, and earn rewards, in proportion to the amount of the network's cryptocurrency they hold and lock up as collateral ("stake"). It was developed as an energy-efficient alternative to proof of work (PoW), the mining-based mechanism used by Bitcoin.

How it works

Participants who wish to validate transactions deposit a stake of the cryptocurrency. The protocol selects block proposers pseudo-randomly, weighted by stake size; other validators then attest to the validity of proposed blocks. Validators earn rewards (newly issued currency and/or transaction fees). Dishonest behaviour — such as signing conflicting blocks — can be punished by slashing, the forced loss of part or all of the stake, which is intended to make attacks economically irrational: an attacker would need to acquire a large fraction of the total staked supply and would destroy the value of their own holdings.

History

The concept was proposed on a Bitcoin forum in 2011 and first implemented by Peercoin in 2012, which used a hybrid PoW/PoS design. Pure PoS or PoS-derived systems were later adopted by networks including Cardano (Ouroboros), Polkadot, Solana (combined with proof of history) and Algorand. The most prominent adoption came in September 2022, when Ethereum — then the second-largest blockchain — completed "The Merge", switching from proof of work to proof of stake and cutting the network's estimated electricity consumption by about 99.9%.

Criticism and variants

Critics argue that PoS can entrench wealth concentration ("the rich get richer"), that large staking services centralise validation, and that problems such as the "nothing at stake" problem and long-range attacks require additional design measures (checkpointing, slashing, weak subjectivity). Variants include delegated proof of stake (DPoS), in which token holders elect a small set of delegates; nominated proof of stake; and liquid proof of stake.

Comparison with proof of work

Whereas proof of work secures a chain through the cost of computation, proof of stake secures it through the cost of capital. PoS removes the need for energy-intensive mining hardware, lowers the hardware barrier to validating, and gives the protocol an in-protocol mechanism (slashing) for penalising attackers, at the price of more complex security assumptions.
