The Pareto distribution, named after the Italian engineer, economist, and sociologist Vilfredo Pareto, is a continuous probability distribution characterised by a power-law (heavy) tail. Pareto originally used it to describe the distribution of income and wealth, observing that a large portion of wealth was held by a small fraction of the population. The distribution has since been applied to city sizes, firm sizes, file sizes, insurance losses, and many other phenomena exhibiting scale invariance.

Definition

If X follows a Pareto distribution with minimum value xm and shape parameter α, the probability that X exceeds x is P(X > x) = (xm/x)α for x ≥ xm. The mean is finite only when α > 1 (equal to αxm/(α−1)), and the variance is finite only when α > 2 — a feature typical of heavy-tailed distributions, where extreme values have a substantial effect on totals.

The Pareto principle

The distribution underlies the Pareto principle, or "80/20 rule": for many outcomes, roughly 80% of effects come from 20% of causes. Pareto's original observation (1896–97) was that about 80% of the land in Italy was owned by about 20% of the population. In economics, Pareto distributions describe the upper tail of income and wealth distributions empirically better than the log-normal distribution, which often fits the middle ranges.

Applications

  • Income and wealth distributions (upper tail)
  • Sizes of cities and of firms
  • Insurance: large claims and reinsurance pricing
  • Internet traffic and file-size distributions
  • Oil reserves in petroleum fields; sand particle and meteorite sizes
