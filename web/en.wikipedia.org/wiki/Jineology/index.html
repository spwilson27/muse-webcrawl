Jineology

Jineology (Kurdish: Jineolojî, from jin, "woman", and the suffix -lojî, "science" or "study") is a form of feminism developed within the Kurdish freedom movement, principally associated with the writings of Abdullah Öcalan, the imprisoned leader of the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK). It is often translated as the "science of women" and is a foundational ideology of the autonomous administration in north and east Syria, commonly known as Rojava.

Principles

Jineology argues that the oppression of women is the oldest and most fundamental form of domination, from which other hierarchies — class, the state, and the exploitation of nature — historically developed. It critiques both Western liberal feminism, which it regards as bound up with capitalism and individualism, and what it sees as patriarchal traditions within Kurdish and Middle Eastern society. Its advocates hold that a society cannot be free unless women are free, summarised in the slogan Jin, Jiyan, Azadî ("Woman, Life, Freedom").

Practice

Jineology is taught in academies and study groups in Kurdish-majority regions and has been put into institutional practice through measures such as co-presidency (every political office being held jointly by a woman and a man), women's quotas in councils, autonomous women's organisations, and all-women military units such as the Women's Protection Units (YPJ). It is closely linked to Öcalan's broader political framework of democratic confederalism, which emphasises grassroots democracy, gender liberation, and ecology.

Reception

Supporters credit jineology with producing one of the most far-reaching programmes of women's political participation in the Middle East. Critics note its close association with a single ideological movement and leader, and question how far autonomous women's structures operate independently of that movement's discipline.
