Institution

An institution is a humanly devised structure of rules and norms that shape and constrain social interactions. Institutions are identified with a social purpose, transcending individuals and intentions and mediating the rules that govern cooperative living behaviour. The term is used broadly across the social sciences, in two main senses: the enduring patterns of rules and practices — such as marriage, property rights, money, and the rule of law — and the organisations that embody them, such as parliaments, universities and central banks.

Definitions and scope

Historically, the term has been used to refer to any important organisation or practice. The sociologist Émile Durkheim called sociology "the science of institutions". In institutional economics, Douglass North defined institutions as "the rules of the game in a society" — the humanly devised constraints, both formal (laws, constitutions, regulations) and informal (customs, conventions, codes of conduct), that structure incentives in human exchange.

Types

Major social institutions identified by sociologists include the family, education, religion, the economy, government and law, and healthcare. In political science, institutions include legislatures, executives, bureaucracies, courts and electoral systems, and scholars study how their design affects outcomes (institutionalism and new institutionalism). Financial institutions — banks, exchanges, insurers — are organisations that channel funds between savers and borrowers.

Change and stability

Institutions are notable for their persistence, but they do change, through deliberate reform, gradual evolution, or crisis. Historical institutionalists study path dependence — how early choices lock in later development — while other scholars emphasise ideas, power and conflict in institutional change. The study of how institutions affect economic performance has become central to development economics, as recognised by the 2024 Nobel Prize in Economics awarded to Daron Acemoglu, Simon Johnson and James Robinson for work on how institutions are formed and affect prosperity.
