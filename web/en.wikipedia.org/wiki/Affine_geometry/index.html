Affine geometry

Affine geometry is the study of geometric properties that remain unchanged under affine transformations — combinations of linear transformations and translations. It can be characterised as the geometry of Euclidean space with the notions of distance and angle removed, while parallelism is retained.

Properties

Affine transformations preserve collinearity, parallelism, ratios of lengths along a line, midpoints, and ratios of areas. They do not in general preserve distances, angles, or circles, which are sent to ellipses. In the Erlangen programme viewpoint, affine geometry is the geometry associated with the affine group, intermediate between Euclidean and projective geometry.

Axiomatic treatment

Affine geometry can be developed axiomatically from a set of points and lines with a parallelism relation satisfying Playfair's axiom, or constructed from a vector space by "forgetting" the origin, yielding an affine space. It underlies computer graphics, where affine maps model translation, rotation, scaling, and shearing of objects.
