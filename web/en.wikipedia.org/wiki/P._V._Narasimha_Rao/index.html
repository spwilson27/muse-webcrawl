Pamulaparthi Venkata Narasimha Rao (28 June 1921 – 23 December 2004) was an Indian lawyer, statesman and politician who served as the ninth prime minister of India from 1991 to 1996. His premiership was pivotal in transforming India; his policies have since been credited with helping India survive and emerge from its balance-of-payments crisis. He is often referred to as the "Father of Indian Economic Reforms" for initiating the liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation programme of 1991.

Early life and education

Rao was born into a Telugu-speaking family in the village of Laknepalli near Warangal in present-day Telangana. He studied at Osmania University and later at Bombay University and Nagpur University, earning degrees in law. Fluent in many languages — Telugu, Hindi, English, Marathi, Bengali, Oriya, Tamil, Sanskrit, Urdu and others — he was unusual among Indian politicians for his literary and scholarly pursuits, translating works between languages and writing fiction and poetry. He participated in the freedom struggle and in the movement against the Nizam's rule in Hyderabad.

Political career before 1991

Rao rose through the Congress party in Andhra Pradesh, serving as Chief Minister of the state from 1971 to 1973, when he attempted land reforms that met strong resistance. Under Indira Gandhi and Rajiv Gandhi he held a string of major Union portfolios — Home Affairs, Defence, Planning and External Affairs — earning a reputation as a quiet, loyal and administratively capable minister. By 1991 he was widely believed to be on the verge of retiring from active politics.

Prime Minister (1991–1996)

After the assassination of Rajiv Gandhi during the 1991 general election campaign, the Congress party chose Rao as its leader, and he became the first prime minister from South India. India was in the grip of a severe foreign-exchange crisis, with reserves barely sufficient for a few weeks of imports. Rao appointed the economist Manmohan Singh as finance minister, and together they launched sweeping reforms: abolition of most industrial licensing, sharp reductions in tariffs and import controls, devaluation of the rupee, opening to foreign investment and reform of the financial sector. These measures are widely regarded as inaugurating modern India's economic growth trajectory.

His foreign policy reoriented India after the Cold War, strengthening ties with the United States, Israel and East Asia through the "Look East" policy. His tenure also saw grave crises: the demolition of the Babri Masjid in December 1992 and the ensuing communal violence, insurgency in Punjab and Kashmir, and allegations of corruption that followed the Congress party into its 1996 election defeat.

Later life and legacy

Rao spent his last years largely out of public life, fighting legal cases arising from his premiership, of which he was ultimately acquitted. He died of a heart attack in New Delhi in December 2004. Long under-recognised by his own party, his reputation has risen steadily since; he is now commonly ranked among the most consequential Indian prime ministers for having steered the country's economic transformation.
