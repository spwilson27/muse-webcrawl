Picture rail

A picture rail is a type of moulding installed horizontally on a wall, typically 30 cm to 60 cm below the ceiling, from which framed pictures and mirrors can be hung by wires, cords, or chains attached to specialised hooks. Also called a picture moulding, it allows heavy frames to be hung and repositioned without driving nails into plaster walls.

History and use

Picture rails were a standard feature of Victorian and Edwardian interiors, where solid masonry or lath-and-plaster walls made repeated nailing impractical. Pictures hung from rails typically tilt slightly outward at the top, a characteristic look of period hanging. Modern gallery rail systems in metal perform the same function in museums and exhibition spaces.

Related mouldings

The picture rail belongs to a family of horizontal wall mouldings that also includes the dado (chair) rail at waist height and the skirting board (baseboard) at floor level.
