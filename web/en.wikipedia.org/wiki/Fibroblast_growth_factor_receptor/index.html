The fibroblast growth factor receptors (FGFRs) are a family of receptor tyrosine kinases that bind members of the fibroblast growth factor family of signalling proteins. In humans there are four receptors, FGFR1 through FGFR4, which are expressed in different tissues and at different developmental stages.

Structure

Each FGFR consists of an extracellular ligand-binding region containing three immunoglobulin-like domains, a single transmembrane helix, and an intracellular tyrosine kinase domain. Alternative splicing of the third immunoglobulin domain produces IIIb and IIIc isoforms with different ligand specificities, greatly expanding the range of FGF–FGFR combinations. Binding of an FGF, assisted by heparan sulfate proteoglycans, induces receptor dimerisation and autophosphorylation, activating downstream pathways including RAS–MAPK, PI3K–AKT, and PLCγ.

Function

FGFR signalling regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, and survival, and is essential for embryonic development, organogenesis, wound healing, and angiogenesis.

Clinical significance

Mutations in FGFR genes cause skeletal dysplasias and craniosynostosis syndromes: activating FGFR3 mutations cause achondroplasia, while FGFR1 and FGFR2 mutations cause syndromes such as Pfeiffer, Crouzon, and Apert syndromes. Aberrant FGFR signalling through amplification, mutation, or chromosomal translocation occurs in several cancers, and FGFR inhibitors such as erdafitinib are approved for certain bladder cancers with FGFR alterations.
