Gregory Rift
Rift escarpment near Lake Baringo, Kenya
LocationKenya and northern Tanzania, East Africa
SystemEastern branch, East African Rift
Named afterJohn Walter Gregory
FeaturesRift lakes Turkana, Baringo, Naivasha, Magadi; volcanoes Suswa, Longonot, Menengai

The Gregory Rift is the eastern branch of the East African Rift System where it passes through Kenya and northern Tanzania. It is named after the geologist John Walter Gregory, who explored the region in 1892–93 and in 1896 published The Great Rift Valley, the work that gave the rift valleys of East Africa their classic description.

Geology

The rift occupies a broad arch — the Kenya dome — uplifted above a mantle plume. Rifting began in the north (Turkana) in the Oligocene–Miocene and propagated southward. Downfaulting between parallel normal faults created a graben typically 40–70 km wide, filled with enormous volumes of basaltic, trachytic and phonolitic lava and studded with caldera volcanoes such as Suswa, Longonot and Menengai. The rift floor hosts a chain of lakes: the vast, brackish Lake Turkana in the north, then Baringo, Bogoria, Nakuru, Elmenteita, Naivasha, Oloiden and the soda lake Magadi in the south.

Significance

The Gregory Rift is one of the world's classic localities for the study of continental rifting and alkaline volcanism, and its geothermal fields (notably Olkaria, by Lake Naivasha) supply a large share of Kenya's electricity. Its volcanic sediments also preserve world-famous fossil sites: the Turkana Basin at the rift's northern end and, just to the south in Tanzania, Olduvai Gorge have yielded key hominin fossils documenting human evolution, including finds by Louis and Mary Leakey.
