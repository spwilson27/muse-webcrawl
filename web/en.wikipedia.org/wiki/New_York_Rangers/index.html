New York Rangers

The New York Rangers are a professional ice hockey team based in New York City. They compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Metropolitan Division of the Eastern Conference, playing their home games at Madison Square Garden. Founded in 1926, the Rangers are one of the "Original Six" NHL franchises.

History

The team was founded by Madison Square Garden president George "Tex" Rickard and won the Stanley Cup in only its second season, 1927–28, followed by championships in 1933 and 1940. After 1940 the Rangers endured a 54-year championship drought, known as the "Curse of 1940", before winning the Stanley Cup again in 1994, defeating the Vancouver Canucks in seven games under captain Mark Messier. Notable players across the club's history include Andy Bathgate, Jean Ratelle, Rod Gilbert, Brian Leetch, Mike Richter and Henrik Lundqvist.

Identity

The Rangers wear blue, red and white; their name arose from a pun — Rickard's team was dubbed "Tex's Rangers" after the Texas Rangers law-enforcement agency. The club is among the most valuable franchises in the NHL and shares Madison Square Garden with the New York Knicks of the NBA.
