Newton-second

The newton-second (symbol N⋅s or N s) is the SI derived unit of impulse and of momentum. One newton-second is the impulse produced by a force of one newton acting for one second, and it is equivalent to one kilogram-metre per second (kg⋅m/s), the unit of momentum.

Relation to impulse and momentum

Impulse equals the change in momentum: J = F Δt = Δp. A force of 1 N applied for 1 s changes an object's momentum by 1 kg⋅m/s. The newton-second is therefore used in dynamics, rocketry (total impulse of motors), and collision analysis.

Examples

A baseball struck by a bat experiences an impulse of several newton-seconds; rocket engines are rated by total impulse in newton-seconds. The unit is dimensionally consistent with momentum, so an impulse in N⋅s can be read directly as a momentum change in kg⋅m/s.
