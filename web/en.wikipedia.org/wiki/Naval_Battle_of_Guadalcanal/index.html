Naval Battle of Guadalcanal

The Naval Battle of Guadalcanal, sometimes called the Third and Fourth Battles of Savo Island or, in Japanese sources, the Third Battle of the Solomon Sea, was a series of naval engagements fought between 12 and 15 November 1942 in the waters around Guadalcanal in the Solomon Islands during the Pacific War. It was the decisive naval confrontation of the Guadalcanal campaign.

Background

Japan sought to reinforce its troops on Guadalcanal and neutralise the American airfield, Henderson Field, by battleship bombardment, so that a convoy of transports carrying some 7,000 soldiers could land. US forces under Admiral William Halsey committed their remaining surface strength, including the battleships Washington and South Dakota, to stop them.

The battles

In the chaotic night action of 12–13 November — the "Friday the 13th battle" — a US cruiser-destroyer force under Rear Admiral Daniel Callaghan intercepted a Japanese bombardment group at close range in Ironbottom Sound. The melee cost the US two light cruisers, four destroyers and both Callaghan and Rear Admiral Norman Scott, but turned back the Japanese, whose battleship Hiei was crippled and sunk the next day by aircraft. On the night of 14–15 November the Japanese returned; in the ensuing battleship duel, Washington, guided by radar, sank the battleship Kirishima, while South Dakota was badly damaged. Japanese transports that pressed on were beached and destroyed by air attack on 15 November, and only a fraction of the troops and supplies reached the island.

Outcome

The battle was a strategic Allied victory: Japan abandoned further large-scale reinforcement attempts, its garrison on Guadalcanal was left to starve, and the initiative in the South Pacific passed to the United States. Losses were heavy on both sides — the US lost two light cruisers and seven destroyers, Japan two battleships, a heavy cruiser, three destroyers and eleven transports.
