Hire purchase

A hire purchase (HP), known in the United States as an installment plan, is a system of acquiring goods in which the buyer pays an initial deposit and the balance, plus interest, in regular instalments. Legally, the buyer hires the goods from the finance company or seller during the term and becomes the owner only when all payments — and usually a small final "option to purchase" fee — have been made.

How it works

Typically a dealer sells the goods to a finance house, which hires them to the customer. Because ownership has not passed until the end of the agreement, the customer cannot sell the goods during the term, and the owner may repossess them if payments fall into default — though in the United Kingdom, once the customer has paid one third of the total price, the Hire Purchase Act and Consumer Credit Act 1974 require the creditor to obtain a court order before repossession, and the customer has statutory rights to terminate the agreement and return the goods.

History and use

Hire purchase grew in the nineteenth century for expensive items such as sewing machines, pianos and railway wagons, and spread with the sale of furniture and motor cars; in Britain it was regulated by Hire Purchase Acts from 1938 onwards. It remains a major form of motor finance, and is closely related to conditional sale and to personal contract purchase (PCP) plans, differing chiefly in how ownership and the final payment are structured. Similar instalment arrangements exist in most common-law jurisdictions and, in adapted form, in Islamic finance as al-ijārah thumma al-bayʿ.
