Flavanol

Flavanols (also called flavan-3-ols) are a class of flavonoids based on the flavan skeleton, found in many plants and plant-derived foods including tea, cocoa, grapes, apples, and berries. The best-known flavanols are the catechins — catechin and epicatechin — and their polymers, the proanthocyanidins. Flavanols should not be confused with flavonols (with an "o"), a different flavonoid class that includes quercetin and kaempferol.

ClassFlavonoid (flavan-3-ol)
ExamplesCatechin, epicatechin, gallocatechin
SourcesTea, cocoa, grapes, apples

Chemistry

Flavan-3-ols have a saturated three-carbon chain between the two aromatic rings of the flavonoid skeleton and a hydroxyl group at the 3-position. Monomeric flavanols such as (+)-catechin and (−)-epicatechin can link together to form oligomers and polymers known as condensed tannins or proanthocyanidins, which contribute astringency to foods such as red wine and unripe fruit.

Dietary sources and research

Green and black tea, cocoa and dark chocolate, red wine, apples, pears, and berries are major dietary sources. Cocoa flavanols have been studied for effects on blood flow and cardiovascular health; in 2022 the COSMOS trial reported that a cocoa extract supplement did not significantly reduce total cardiovascular events overall, though secondary analyses suggested possible benefits for cardiovascular mortality. The European Food Safety Authority has authorized a health claim that cocoa flavanols help maintain endothelium-dependent vasodilation at intakes of 200 mg per day.
