Mi'raj

For the night journey as a whole, see Isra and Mi'raj.

The Mi'raj (Arabic: ٱلْمِعْرَاج, al-Miʿrāj, "the Ascension") is the second part of the Night Journey of the Islamic prophet Muhammad, in which, according to Islamic tradition, he ascended from Jerusalem through the seven heavens, met earlier prophets, and came into the presence of God. The first part of the journey, the Isra, took him from Mecca to "the Farthest Mosque" (al-Masjid al-Aqsa), traditionally identified with Jerusalem.

Account

According to the hadith literature, Muhammad was carried on the winged creature Buraq during the journey. In the Mi'raj he ascended through the seven heavens, encountering prophets including Adam, Jesus and John the Baptist, Abraham, Moses and Aaron. He was shown paradise and hell, reached the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary (Sidrat al-Muntaha), and spoke with God, who prescribed the fifty daily prayers — later reduced to five at Moses' urging, while retaining the reward of fifty.

Sources and observance

The event is alluded to in the Qur'an, principally in Surah An-Najm (53) and in the opening verse of Surah Al-Isra (17). Detailed narratives appear in the sira and hadith collections. The night is commemorated by many Muslims on Laylat al-Mi'raj, traditionally dated to 27 Rajab. Interpretations differ over whether the journey was bodily or spiritual, a question debated since the earliest centuries of Islam.

In art

The Mi'raj became one of the most frequently illustrated episodes in Islamic manuscript painting, especially in Persian and Turkish Mi'rajnama manuscripts, which depict Muhammad ascending through celestial spheres populated by angels.
