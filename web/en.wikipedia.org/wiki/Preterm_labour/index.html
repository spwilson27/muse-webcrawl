Preterm labour

Preterm labour is the onset of labour — regular uterine contractions accompanied by cervical change — before 37 completed weeks of gestation. Labour resulting in delivery before 37 weeks produces a preterm birth; an estimated 15 million babies are born preterm worldwide each year, and complications of prematurity are the leading cause of death in children under five. Preterm labour may be spontaneous or may follow preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM); medically indicated early delivery for conditions such as pre-eclampsia is a separate pathway to preterm birth.

Signs and diagnosis

Symptoms include regular painful contractions, pelvic pressure, low backache, increased or changed vaginal discharge and vaginal bleeding. Because many of these symptoms are nonspecific, diagnosis relies on documented cervical dilatation or effacement on examination. Transvaginal ultrasound measurement of cervical length and fetal fibronectin testing of cervicovaginal secretions are used to stratify risk: a long cervix (≥25 mm) or a negative fetal fibronectin test makes delivery in the next week unlikely.

Causes and risk factors

The mechanisms converge on premature activation of the normal pathways of parturition: intrauterine infection and inflammation (a major driver, especially at early gestations), uterine overdistension (multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios), decidual haemorrhage (abruption), cervical insufficiency, placental dysfunction, and maternal stress. The single strongest risk factor is a previous spontaneous preterm birth; others include a short cervix, multiple gestation, smoking, substance use, low body-mass index, short interpregnancy interval, periodontal disease and extremes of maternal age.

Management

  • Tocolysis — drugs such as nifedipine, atosiban, indomethacin or beta-mimetics may suppress contractions for about 48 hours, principally to allow corticosteroids to act and transfer to a centre with neonatal intensive care; they do not usually stop labour indefinitely.
  • Antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone or dexamethasone) accelerate fetal lung maturity and reduce neonatal respiratory distress syndrome, intraventricular haemorrhage and death.
  • Magnesium sulfate given before early preterm birth reduces the risk of cerebral palsy.
  • Antibiotics are given for group B streptococcus prophylaxis and where infection is suspected; they prolong pregnancy in PPROM without overt infection.

Prevention

In women with a previous preterm birth or a short cervix, vaginal progesterone reduces recurrence risk, and cervical cerclage (a stitch) benefits selected women with cervical shortening and prior preterm birth. Smoking cessation, treatment of asymptomatic bacteriuria, and single embryo transfer in IVF also reduce risk.

Outcomes

Survival and the risk of complications rise steeply with each additional week of gestation: babies born before 28 weeks ("extremely preterm") face the highest risks of death, chronic lung disease, brain injury, retinopathy of prematurity and long-term neurodevelopmental impairment, whereas "late preterm" infants (34–36 weeks) generally do well but still fare worse than term babies.
