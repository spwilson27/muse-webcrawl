Georgian Bay (French: Baie Georgienne) is a large bay of Lake Huron, in the province of Ontario, Canada. The main body of the bay lies east of the Bruce Peninsula and Manitoulin Island. To its northwest it is connected to the North Channel, and to Lake Huron itself at its western end. The bay is about 190 kilometres long by 80 kilometres wide, and covers approximately 15,000 square kilometres.

Geography

The eastern shore of Georgian Bay, known as the "30,000 Islands", forms the world's largest freshwater archipelago and is part of a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. The rugged Canadian Shield coastline is dotted with windswept white pines and granite outcrops. Georgian Bay Islands National Park protects 63 small islands, including Beausoleil Island.

History

The bay was long inhabited by Anishinaabe peoples, including the Ojibwe and Odawa. Samuel de Champlain reached it in 1615 and called it La Mer Douce, "the sweet sea". In 1822 it was renamed in honour of King George IV. In the 19th century the bay's shores saw a major lumber trade and commercial fishery.

Recreation

Georgian Bay is a popular summer destination, with cottage country along its eastern shore and sailing between its islands. Towns on the bay include Owen Sound, Midland, Penetanguishene, Collingwood and Parry Sound. The wreck of the passenger steamer Waubuno and numerous other shipwrecks make it a noted scuba-diving area.
