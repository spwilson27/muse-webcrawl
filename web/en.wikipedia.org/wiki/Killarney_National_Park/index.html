Killarney National Park

Killarney National Park (Irish: Páirc Náisiúnta Chill Airne) is a national park near the town of Killarney in County Kerry, Ireland. It was the first national park established in Ireland, created in 1932 when the Muckross Estate was donated to the Irish state. The park covers roughly 102 square kilometres of diverse ecology, including the Lakes of Killarney, extensive native oak and yew woodlands, and the mountain ranges of the MacGillycuddy's Reeks.

Features

The park contains Muckross House and gardens, the fifteenth-century Ross Castle, and Torc Waterfall. Its woodlands include one of the most extensive areas of native forest remaining in Ireland, and it is the only place in the country with a native herd of red deer, descended from a herd present since Neolithic times.

Conservation

The park is designated as a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve and a Special Area of Conservation. Management concerns include overgrazing, invasive rhododendron, and the protection of rare species such as the Killarney fern and the white-tailed eagle, which has been reintroduced there.
