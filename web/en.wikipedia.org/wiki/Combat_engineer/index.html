Combat engineer

Also called: Sapper, pioneer
Role: Military engineering in support of combat operations

A combat engineer (also called a sapper or pioneer in many armies) is a soldier who performs military engineering tasks in support of land forces, especially under or near combat conditions. Their work supports mobility, countermobility and survivability.

Tasks

Combat engineers build bridges and roads, construct fortifications and obstacles, lay and clear minefields, conduct demolitions and breaching, clear routes of improvised explosive devices, and build temporary infrastructure such as airfields and water supply. Assault engineers may accompany infantry to breach defences.

History

The term sapper derives from sapping — digging trenches toward enemy fortifications in siege warfare. Engineer troops were central to both World Wars, from trench systems to D-Day beach obstacle clearance and Bailey bridges, and remain essential in modern expeditionary operations.

Examples

Examples include the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers' combat units and Army combat engineer MOS, the British Royal Engineers, and naval construction battalions (Seabees) performing similar work.
