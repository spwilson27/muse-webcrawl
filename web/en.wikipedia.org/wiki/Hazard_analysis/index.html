Hazard analysis

Hazard analysis is the process of identifying hazards — conditions or activities that could lead to harm, damage, or loss — and evaluating the risks they pose, so that controls can be designed into a system before accidents occur. It is a foundational discipline within safety engineering and system safety, and is used across the aerospace, chemical, nuclear, medical device, defence and food industries.

Purpose

Unlike accident investigation, which reacts to failures, hazard analysis is proactive: it seeks out hazards early in a design's life cycle, when changes are cheapest, and follows them through design, operation, maintenance and disposal. The output is typically a hazard log or hazard list documenting each hazard, its causes, its effects, its assessed severity and likelihood, the controls applied, and the residual risk.

Techniques

Several structured techniques are in common use:

  • Preliminary Hazard Analysis (PHA) — a broad early screening of hazards, originating in the U.S. missile safety programmes of the 1960s.
  • HAZOP (Hazard and Operability Study) — a team-based, guideword-driven examination of process deviations, developed in the chemical industry.
  • FMEA / FMECA (Failure Modes and Effects Analysis) — a bottom-up review of component failure modes and their system-level effects.
  • Fault Tree Analysis (FTA) — a top-down deductive analysis using logic gates to show how combinations of failures can cause an undesired event.
  • HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) — the food-safety system that identifies biological, chemical and physical hazards and controls them at critical points in production.

Risk assessment

Hazards are commonly categorised by severity (from negligible to catastrophic) and probability (from improbable to frequent), often plotted on a risk matrix. Standards such as MIL-STD-882 (system safety), ISO 26262 (automotive functional safety) and IEC 61508 specify hazard analysis processes and the rigour expected at each integrity level.

Relation to risk management

Hazard analysis forms the hazard-identification and risk-assessment stage of the wider risk management process. Its findings drive design changes, protective devices, alarms, procedures, warnings and training. Because no system can be proven absolutely safe, the process aims to reduce risk to a level considered tolerable or as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP).
