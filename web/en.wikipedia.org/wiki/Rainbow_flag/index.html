Rainbow flag

The six-stripe rainbow flag, the best-known symbol of LGBTQ pride.

The rainbow flag is a multicoloured flag consisting of stripes in the colours of the rainbow. Its most famous use is as a symbol of LGBTQ pride and social movements, but rainbow flags have been used in many other contexts, including the cooperative movement, peace movements in Italy (the bandiera della Pace), Andean indigenous identity (the Wiphala), and various religious and regional traditions.

LGBTQ pride flag

The pride flag was designed by artist Gilbert Baker and first flown at the San Francisco Gay Freedom Day Parade on 25 June 1978. Baker's original design had eight stripes, each with a meaning: hot pink for sex, red for life, orange for healing, yellow for sunlight, green for nature, turquoise for art, indigo for harmony, and violet for spirit. Production constraints reduced it first to seven and then to the now-standard six stripes (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet).

Variants

Many variants exist, including the Philadelphia People of Color Pride Flag (2017), which adds black and brown stripes, and Daniel Quasar's Progress Pride Flag (2018), which adds a chevron of black, brown, light blue, pink, and white representing marginalised communities and trans people.
