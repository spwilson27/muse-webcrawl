Ambitus

Ambitus
Field: Music theory (Gregorian chant)
Meaning: the range of pitches in a chant melody

In Gregorian chant, ambitus is the range, or the distance between the highest and lowest note, of a chant or mode. The term derives from Latin, meaning a circuit or going around. Each of the church modes has a characteristic ambitus relative to its final (tonic note).

Authentic and plagal modes

Modes are classified as authentic when their ambitus lies roughly from the final up to an octave above, and as plagal when the melody ranges from about a fourth below the final to a fifth or sixth above it. Although medieval theorists described the ambitus of most modes as an octave, actual chants may exceed or fall short of this range, and theorists recognised that some melodies mix or shift between ranges.

Usage

The concept remains useful in chant scholarship and analysis for describing melodic behaviour and distinguishing modes that share the same final but differ in their melodic range and characteristic turns of phrase.
