Utrecht (province)

Utrecht (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈytrɛxt] ) is the smallest and one of the most densely populated of the twelve provinces of the Netherlands, located in the centre of the country. Its capital and largest city is also called Utrecht. Other major cities include Amersfoort, Veenendaal, Houten, Nieuwegein and Zeist.

Geography

The province covers about 1,560 square kilometres. In the east lie the wooded hills of the Utrecht Hill Ridge (Utrechtse Heuvelrug), a moraine ridge rising to about 69 metres. The south is river landscape along the Lek, while the west and north are polder meadows. The province borders Gelderland, South Holland, North Holland and Flevoland.

History

The province descends from the Bishopric of Utrecht, founded in 695 when Willibrord was consecrated bishop. In 1528 the last prince-bishop sold his temporal power to Emperor Charles V, and Utrecht became one of the Habsburg Netherlands. It later joined the Dutch Republic as one of the seven provinces, with the Union of Utrecht signed in the city in 1579.

Economy and transport

Utrecht's central position makes it a national transport hub: Utrecht Centraal is the busiest railway station in the Netherlands, and major motorways radiate from the province. The economy centres on services, finance, ICT and education, including Utrecht University.
