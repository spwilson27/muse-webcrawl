The Svalbard Global Seed Vault (Norwegian: Svalbard globale frøhvelv) is a secure backup facility for the world's crop diversity on the Norwegian archipelago of Svalbard in the remote Arctic, about 1,300 kilometres (810 mi) from the North Pole. Conservationist Cary Fowler, in association with the Consultative Group on International Agricultural Research, took the lead in establishing the vault to preserve a wide variety of plant seeds as an insurance against the loss of seeds in other gene banks through war, disaster, or mismanagement.

The vault's entrance portal on Spitsbergen

Facility

The vault is cut 130 metres into a sandstone mountain on Spitsbergen, near Longyearbyen, where permafrost keeps the storage rooms naturally cold and the site sits well above projected sea-level rise. Seeds are sealed in foil packets, stored in boxes on shelving, and held at −18 °C; depositors retain ownership and only they may withdraw their material. The vault holds well over a million seed samples from nearly every country. Its first major withdrawal came in 2015, when researchers retrieved seeds to re-establish a gene bank collection formerly based in Aleppo, Syria, relocated to Lebanon and Morocco. In 2017 meltwater from thawing permafrost entered the access tunnel (without reaching the seeds), prompting upgrades to waterproofing and cooling.
