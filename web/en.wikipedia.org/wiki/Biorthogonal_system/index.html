Biorthogonal system

A biorthogonal system pairs two families of vectors so that corresponding members are orthogonal across the families.

In mathematics, a biorthogonal system is a pair of families of vectors in a vector space equipped with a bilinear form, such that the vectors from the two families are orthogonal to each other in matched pairs. Biorthogonal systems generalize orthonormal bases, in which a single family is orthogonal to itself.

Examples and applications

A standard example is the pairing between a basis of a finite-dimensional space and its dual basis in the dual space. In signal processing, biorthogonal wavelet bases use different wavelets for decomposition and reconstruction, a freedom exploited in the JPEG 2000 image compression standard, which uses the biorthogonal CDF 9/7 wavelet for lossy compression. Biorthogonal polynomials similarly pair two families of polynomials orthogonal with respect to different weight functions.
