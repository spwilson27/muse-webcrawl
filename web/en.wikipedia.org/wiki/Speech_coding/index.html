Speech coding

Speech coding is the application of data compression to digital audio signals containing speech. By exploiting the properties of speech production and perception, speech coders achieve far greater compression than general-purpose audio codecs, enabling intelligible voice communication at bit rates from a few hundred bits per second to tens of kilobits per second.

Techniques

Waveform coders, such as the G.711 PCM standard (64 kbit/s), reproduce the signal directly. Parametric and hybrid coders model the vocal tract as a time-varying filter excited by voiced or noise-like sources; linear predictive coding (LPC) underlies standards such as GSM Full Rate, the AMR codecs used in mobile networks, and the very-low-rate MELPe military standard (600–2400 bit/s). Modern codecs such as Opus and EVS span speech and general audio at variable rates.

Applications

Speech coding is essential to mobile telephony, voice over IP, satellite and military communications, and digital radio — anywhere bandwidth is limited. Quality is commonly assessed with measures such as mean opinion score (MOS) and PESQ.
