Gasotransmitter

Gasotransmitters are a class of signalling molecules that are gases under physiological conditions. Unlike classical neurotransmitters and hormones, they are not stored in vesicles or released by exocytosis; instead they are synthesised on demand by enzymes, diffuse freely across cell membranes, and act on nearby targets before being rapidly inactivated. The three established gasotransmitters are nitric oxide (NO), carbon monoxide (CO) and hydrogen sulfide (H2S).

Nitric oxide

Nitric oxide, identified in the 1980s as the endothelium-derived relaxing factor, is produced by nitric oxide synthases from L-arginine. It activates soluble guanylate cyclase in vascular smooth muscle, raising cyclic GMP and causing vasodilation. NO also functions as a neurotransmitter in the central and peripheral nervous systems and as a cytotoxic agent of macrophages. The discovery earned the 1998 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for Robert Furchgott, Louis Ignarro and Ferid Murad.

Carbon monoxide

Carbon monoxide is generated during haem breakdown by haem oxygenase enzymes. At low physiological concentrations it is anti-inflammatory, anti-apoptotic and vasodilatory, modulating soluble guanylate cyclase and potassium channels; at high concentrations it is a well-known poison because of its tight binding to haemoglobin.

Hydrogen sulfide

Hydrogen sulfide, long known only as a toxic gas, is produced by cystathionine β-synthase, cystathionine γ-lyase and 3-mercaptopyruvate sulfurtransferase. It relaxes blood vessels, acts as a neuromodulator and protects tissues against ischaemia–reperfusion injury, partly by opening ATP-sensitive potassium channels and by persulfidation of proteins.

Criteria

The concept was formalised by Rui Wang in 2002. Proposed criteria for a gasotransmitter include: it is a small gas molecule, freely membrane-permeant, endogenously and enzymatically generated, with defined physiological functions at relevant concentrations and specific molecular targets. Other gases, including hydrogen and sulfur dioxide, have been proposed as candidate members of the family.
