Heijō-kyō

Heijō-kyō (平城京) was the capital city of Japan during most of the Nara period, from 710 to 740 and again from 745 to 784. Located at modern-day Nara, it was built by Empress Genmei and modelled on Chang'an, the capital of Tang-dynasty China, with a grid plan divided by the central Suzaku Avenue.

Layout and history

The city housed the imperial palace (Heijō Palace), government offices, aristocratic residences and great Buddhist temples such as Tōdai-ji and Kōfuku-ji. At its height its population may have reached 100,000. When the capital moved to Heian-kyō (Kyoto) in 794, Heijō-kyō declined, though its temples survived. The palace site has been excavated and reconstructed, and is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site among the Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara.
