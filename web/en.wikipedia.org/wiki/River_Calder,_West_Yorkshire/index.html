River Calder, West Yorkshire

The River Calder is a major river in West Yorkshire, England, about 72 km (45 miles) long. It rises on the Pennine moorland near Todmorden, close to the Lancashire border, and flows east through Hebden Bridge, Sowerby Bridge, Halifax (via its tributary the Hebble), Elland, Brighouse, Dewsbury, and Wakefield, before joining the River Aire at Castleford.

History and industry

The Calder valley was a heartland of the English woollen textile industry; mills lined its banks and the Calder and Hebble Navigation made the upper river navigable from the 18th century, linking the valley to the Humber ports. Industrial pollution once made stretches of the river almost lifeless, but water quality has greatly improved since the 1970s, and salmon and otters have returned.

The valley is prone to severe flooding — notably on Boxing Day 2015 and in February 2020, when Storm Ciara inundated towns including Mytholmroyd and Hebden Bridge — prompting major flood defence schemes.

Not to be confused with the River Calder in Lancashire or the River Calder in Cumbria.
