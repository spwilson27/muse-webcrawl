Frances McDormand

Frances Louise McDormand (born Cynthia Ann Smith; June 23, 1957) is an American actress and producer. In a career spanning over four decades, she has gained acclaim for her roles in small-budget independent films and blockbuster films, and is regarded as one of the finest actresses of her generation. McDormand has received numerous accolades, including four Academy Awards, two Primetime Emmy Awards, and one Tony Award, making her one of the few performers to achieve the "Triple Crown of Acting". She is one of only three women to win three Academy Awards for Best Actress.

Career

McDormand was educated at Bethany College and Yale University, and made her screen debut in Joel and Ethan Coen's neo-noir Blood Simple (1984). She married Joel Coen the same year. She won her first Academy Award for Best Actress for playing police chief Marge Gunderson in Fargo (1996). She received nominations for roles in Mississippi Burning (1988), Almost Famous (2000), and North Country (2005), and won Best Actress again for Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri (2017) and Nomadland (2020), the latter also earning her the Best Picture award as a producer.

On television, she won Emmy Awards for the miniseries Olive Kitteridge (2014); on stage she won the Tony Award for Best Actress in a Play for Good People (2011).
