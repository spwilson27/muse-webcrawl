Shrove Tuesday

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shrove Tuesday (also known in Commonwealth countries and Ireland as Pancake Tuesday or Pancake Day) is the final day of Shrovetide, marking the end of pre-Lent. It falls 47 days before Easter Sunday and is the day before Ash Wednesday, which begins the fasting season of Lent in Western Christian churches. The name derives from the practice of being "shriven" — confessing sins and receiving absolution — before Lent. Many Christians, and wider society in some countries, use the day to consume rich foods such as eggs, milk, sugar and fat that were traditionally given up for the Lenten fast; hence its association with pancakes and, in many cultures, with carnival.

Names and equivalents

  • Mardi Gras ("Fat Tuesday") in French-speaking traditions, famously in New Orleans and in France itself.
  • Faschingsdienstag in parts of Germany and Austria; Fastnacht in others.
  • Fastelavn in Scandinavia; laskiainen in Finland.
  • Maslenitsa's final days in Slavic Orthodox tradition (on the Orthodox calendar).
  • Martedì grasso in Italy, the culmination of Carnevale.

Traditions

In the United Kingdom, Ireland and parts of the Commonwealth, pancakes are eaten on Shrove Tuesday, traditionally to use up eggs and fats; pancake races — in which runners flip pancakes in frying pans while racing — are held, the most famous at Olney, Buckinghamshire, said to date from 1445. In Liberal, Kansas, the Olney race has an international counterpart run since 1950. Shrove Tuesday was also traditionally a day of football matches and other folk sports, such as the Royal Shrovetide Football at Ashbourne, Derbyshire. In many Catholic and Lutheran countries, the day closes the carnival season with parades, masquerades and feasting, while churches may burn the previous year's Palm Sunday branches to make ashes for Ash Wednesday.

Date

Shrove Tuesday falls between February 3 and March 9, depending on the date of Easter, and is the last day of the "fat eating" period of Shrovetide.
