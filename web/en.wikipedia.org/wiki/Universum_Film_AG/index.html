Universum Film AG

Universum Film AG (UFA) was a major German film studio, the country's dominant motion picture company during the Weimar Republic and the Nazi era. Founded in 1917 with covert government backing to produce propaganda and newsreels, it became one of the most powerful film companies in the world during the 1920s, rivalled only by the Hollywood majors.

Golden age

From its Babelsberg studios near Berlin, UFA produced landmarks of German Expressionism and Weimar cinema, including The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari (1920, distributed by its Decla affiliate), F. W. Murnau's The Last Laugh (1924), Fritz Lang's Metropolis (1927) and M (1931), and The Blue Angel (1930), which made Marlene Dietrich a star. Financial strain from Metropolis led to a takeover by media magnate Alfred Hugenberg in 1927.

Nazi era and aftermath

After 1933 the Nazi regime progressively absorbed UFA; it was nationalized in 1942 as UFA-Film GmbH (UFI) and served the propaganda apparatus under Joseph Goebbels. After the war its assets were divided: in East Germany the studios became DEFA, while in the West a revived UFA produced post-war entertainment films. The UFA name survives today in a German television production company within the RTL/Fremantle group.
