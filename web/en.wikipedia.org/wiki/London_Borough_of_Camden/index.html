The London Borough of Camden is an Inner London borough in north-west London. It was created in 1965 by merging the metropolitan boroughs of Hampstead, Holborn, and St Pancras, and takes its name from Camden Town, itself named after Charles Pratt, 1st Earl Camden. Camden Council is based at 5 Pancras Square beside King's Cross station. The borough stretches from Holborn and Bloomsbury in the south to Hampstead Heath in the north, and combines some of London's most expensive residential streets with areas of significant deprivation.

Districts and landmarks

Camden contains King's Cross and St Pancras International, Euston station, the British Library, and the British Museum in Bloomsbury, an area dense with University of London institutions and teaching hospitals. Camden Town and its markets around Camden Lock are among London's most visited attractions and a historic centre of alternative and music culture; Kentish Town, Gospel Oak, and West Hampstead lie further north. Hampstead is noted for its village character and Georgian streets, and the borough includes most of Hampstead Heath, an 800-acre expanse of ancient parkland ending at Parliament Hill, famous for its views over the city, as well as Primrose Hill and parts of Regent's Park and the Regent's Canal.

Camden is ethnically and economically highly diverse, with large Bangladeshi, Irish, and many other communities, and major employers in higher education, medicine, publishing, technology (the King's Cross "Knowledge Quarter"), and the creative industries. It is governed by Camden London Borough Council, which has been Labour-controlled for most of its history.
