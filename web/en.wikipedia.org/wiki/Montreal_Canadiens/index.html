The Montreal Canadiens (French: Les Canadiens de Montréal), nicknamed the Habs, are a professional ice hockey team based in Montreal, Quebec. Founded in 1909, they are the oldest continuously operating professional hockey team in the world and one of the "Original Six" franchises of the National Hockey League (NHL).

History

The club was founded by J. Ambrose O'Brien on 4 December 1909 as a francophone team in the National Hockey Association, and became a charter member of the NHL in 1917. The Canadiens have won the Stanley Cup 24 times, more than any other franchise, including a record five consecutive championships from 1956 to 1960 and four straight from 1976 to 1979.

Legendary players associated with the team include Howie Morenz, Maurice "Rocket" Richard — whose suspension in 1955 sparked the St. Patrick's Day Richard Riot at the Montreal Forum — Jean Béliveau, Guy Lafleur, Patrick Roy, and goaltender Ken Dryden. The club played at the Montreal Forum from 1926 to 1996 before moving to the Molson Centre, now the Bell Centre.

Recent years

The Canadiens' most recent Stanley Cup victory came in 1993, the last by a Canadian-based team for a long period. In 2021, the team made a surprise run to the Stanley Cup Final, defeating Toronto, Winnipeg, and Vegas before losing to the Tampa Bay Lightning.

Identity

The team's name for its fanbase derives from Les Habitants, early French settlers of Quebec, giving rise to the nickname "Habs" and the cheer "Go Habs Go". The club's motto, drawn from the poem "In Flanders Fields" — "To you from failing hands we throw the torch; be yours to hold it high" — is inscribed in its dressing room.
