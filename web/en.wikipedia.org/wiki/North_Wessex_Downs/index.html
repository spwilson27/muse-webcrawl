North Wessex Downs

The North Wessex Downs are an area of chalk downland in southern England, designated as an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty (AONB) — since 2023 styled a National Landscape — in 1972. The downs cover an expanse of approximately 670 square kilometres (260 square miles) across the counties of Berkshire, Hampshire, Wiltshire and Oxfordshire, straddling the Thames Valley to the north and the Vale of Pewsey to the south.

Geography

The North Wessex Downs form part of the chalk formation that stretches across southern England. The landscape is characterised by rolling hills, steep scarp slopes, dry valleys and beech hangers. The highest point is Walbury Hill in Berkshire, at 297 metres (974 ft), one of the highest points in south-east England. Other notable hills include Inkpen Beacon, Milk Hill and Tan Hill.

The escarpment overlooks the Vale of White Horse, famous for the Uffington White Horse, a prehistoric hill figure cut into the chalk. The downs are crossed by ancient trackways including the Ridgeway, often described as Britain's oldest road, and contain numerous archaeological sites such as Avebury, with its Neolithic stone circles, Silbury Hill and West Kennet Long Barrow.

Flora and fauna

The chalk grassland supports a rich flora including orchids, and wildlife such as the hare, skylark and various butterfly species including the chalkhill blue and Adonis blue. The area also contains chalk streams, notably the River Kennet and the River Lambourn, both Sites of Special Scientific Interest, which support water voles, otters and native brown trout.

Settlements

The principal settlements around the downs include Marlborough, Hungerford, Lambourn, Wantage and Pewsey. The area is noted for its association with racehorse training, centred on Lambourn, known as the "Valley of the Racehorse".
