Panzergrenadier

Panzergrenadier (German: [ˈpantsɐɡʁenaˌdiːɐ]) is a German term for mechanised infantry — motorised and armoured infantry formations trained to fight alongside tanks. The designation was introduced in 1942, when most of the Wehrmacht's motorised infantry regiments were renamed Panzergrenadier regiments, and divisions of this type were intended to accompany panzer divisions in mobile operations, riding in lorries or, for a minority, in armoured half-tracks such as the Sd.Kfz. 251.

Second World War

In practice, only a fraction of panzergrenadiers had armoured vehicles; most were truck-borne, and chronically short of transport as the war went on. Panzergrenadier divisions typically had two infantry regiments, a tank battalion, artillery and supporting arms. Elite formations such as the Panzergrenadier Division Großdeutschland received priority in equipment. The Waffen-SS also fielded several panzergrenadier divisions.

Bundeswehr

The term was retained by the post-war Bundeswehr, where Panzergrenadiertruppe — mechanised infantry equipped with infantry fighting vehicles such as the Marder and later the Puma — remains one of the armoured combat arms, distinguished by its green beret and its motto of fighting mounted and dismounted. Austria and Switzerland have used the term for their own armoured infantry as well.
