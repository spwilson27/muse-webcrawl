Stephen (c. 1092/96 – 25 October 1154), often called Stephen of Blois, was King of England from 1135 until his death, and Count of Boulogne from 1125. His reign was marked by the civil war known as the Anarchy, fought against his cousin and rival, the Empress Matilda.

Accession

Stephen was a grandson of William the Conqueror through his mother Adela. On the death of Henry I in 1135, despite having sworn oaths to support Henry's daughter Matilda, Stephen crossed the Channel and had himself crowned, winning initial support from the barons and the Church.

The Anarchy

Matilda landed in England in 1139, beginning a protracted civil war. Stephen was captured at the Battle of Lincoln in 1141 but was exchanged for Matilda's half-brother Robert of Gloucester; Matilda's son, the future Henry II, invaded in 1153. Exhausted, the parties settled by the Treaty of Wallingford (1153): Stephen would reign for life, and Henry would succeed him.

Death and legacy

Stephen died at Dover in October 1154 and was buried at Faversham Abbey. His reign saw the weakening of royal authority and the proliferation of adulterine (unlicensed) castles, though modern historians have qualified the traditional picture of total disorder.
