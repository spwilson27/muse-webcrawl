Mole fraction

In chemistry, the mole fraction or molar fraction, also called amount fraction, is defined as the ratio of the amount of a constituent substance (in moles) to the total amount of all constituents in a mixture. It is dimensionless, and the sum of the mole fractions of all components equals one. Mole fraction is denoted xi for liquids and solutions and yi for gas mixtures by convention in many texts.

Mole fractions in a mixture sum to one.

Properties and use

Because it is a ratio of amounts, mole fraction is independent of temperature and pressure-driven volume changes, which makes it fundamental in thermodynamics. It appears in Raoult's law, where the partial vapour pressure of a component equals its mole fraction times the vapour pressure of the pure component; in Dalton's law of partial pressures for ideal gases; and in expressions for the chemical potential, entropy of mixing, and phase diagrams (where composition axes are usually mole fractions). Mole percent is simply the mole fraction multiplied by 100.

Related quantities

Mole fraction is related to, but distinct from, mass fraction, volume fraction, molality (moles of solute per kilogram of solvent) and molarity (moles per litre of solution). Converting between them requires knowledge of molar masses and, for volume-based measures, densities.
