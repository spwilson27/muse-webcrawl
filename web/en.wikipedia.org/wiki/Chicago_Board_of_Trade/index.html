The Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT), established in 1848, is one of the world's oldest futures and options exchanges. More than 50 different options and futures contracts are traded by over 3,600 CBOT members. It is housed in the landmark Chicago Board of Trade Building, an Art Deco skyscraper at the foot of LaSalle Street crowned by a statue of Ceres, Roman goddess of grain.

History

Chicago merchants founded the exchange to standardise quantities and quality of grain traded and to create a centralised marketplace. The CBOT developed the first standardised futures contracts (1864) and pioneered "to-arrive" contracts and open-outcry trading pits. Major contracts include corn, wheat and soybeans, and US Treasury bond and note futures — for decades the world's most traded interest-rate futures.

Merger and electronic trading

In 2007 the CBOT merged with its rival across the street, the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, to form CME Group. Electronic trading on the Globex platform progressively displaced the open-outcry pits, most of which closed in 2015, though Treasury options pits survived longer.
