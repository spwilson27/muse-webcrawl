Mehmed V

Mehmed V (2 November 1844 – 3 July 1918), also known as Mehmed Reşad, was the 35th and penultimate sultan of the Ottoman Empire, reigning from 27 April 1909 until his death. He came to the throne after the deposition of his brother Abdul Hamid II in the aftermath of the 31 March Incident, and his reign coincided with the empire's final decade of crises.

Reign

Mehmed V was a figurehead for much of his reign. Real power lay first with the Committee of Union and Progress (the Young Turks), and after 1913 with the triumvirate of Enver, Talat and Cemal Pashas. His reign saw the Italo-Turkish War (1911–12), the loss of Libya, the Balkan Wars (1912–13), in which the empire lost almost all its remaining European territory, and the empire's entry into World War I on the side of the Central Powers in 1914.

As caliph, Mehmed V issued a call for jihad in November 1914, hoping to rally Muslims in Allied colonies; it had little practical effect. He died in July 1918, four months before the armistice that ended Ottoman participation in the war, and was succeeded by his brother Mehmed VI, the last sultan.

Character

Contemporaries described Mehmed V as mild, pious and fond of poetry and Persian literature. He had spent most of his life in seclusion in the palace before his accession at the age of 64, and he showed little appetite for the political struggles that consumed his reign.
