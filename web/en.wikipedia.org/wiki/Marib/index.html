Marib (Arabic: مَأْرِب, Maʾrib) is a city in central Yemen, capital of Marib Governorate, situated about 120 kilometres east of the national capital Sanaa. In antiquity it was the capital of the kingdom of Saba (Sheba), one of the great trading states of ancient South Arabia, enriched by the incense trade and by the irrigation works of the great Marib Dam.

Ancient history

Ancient Marib, whose ruins lie south of the modern city, was one of the largest cities of the ancient Near East. The Sabaeans built temples such as the Mahram Bilqis (the Awwam temple, dedicated to the moon god Almaqah) and the Barran Temple, and the monumental dam that watered an oasis of some 9,600 hectares, supporting perhaps 50,000 inhabitants. Marib controlled the overland incense route carrying frankincense and myrrh from southern Arabia to the Mediterranean. The city declined after the dam's final breach in the sixth century AD (an event remembered in the Quran's story of the flood of the dam), was largely abandoned, and its population migrated widely — an exodus remembered in Arab tradition as the dispersal after the bursting of the dam of Ma'rib.

Modern era

The modern town remained small until the discovery of oil near Marib in 1984 transformed the region into Yemen's principal oil- and gas-producing area, with pipelines to the Red Sea. During the Yemeni civil war that began in 2014–15, Marib — held by the internationally recognised government — became a refuge for hundreds of thousands of displaced Yemenis and the scene of major Houthi offensives in 2020–21 aimed at seizing the city and its energy resources. The Landmarks of the Ancient Kingdom of Saba in Marib were inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in 2023 (simultaneously placed on the danger list).
