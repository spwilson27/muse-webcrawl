Unknown (later retitled Unknown Worlds) was an American pulp fantasy magazine published by Street & Smith from 1939 to 1943. It was edited by John W. Campbell, who also edited Astounding Science Fiction, and was created as a companion magazine for fantasy fiction of the same sophistication Campbell demanded for science fiction.

History

The first issue appeared in March 1939 with a serial by Eric Frank Russell. Campbell insisted on fantasy with a logical, quasi-scientific rigor: magic had to follow consistent rules. Under the title change to Unknown Worlds in late 1940, the magazine continued until wartime paper shortages forced its cancellation with the October 1943 issue, after 39 issues in total.

Contents and contributors

Unknown published work by many of the leading speculative-fiction writers of the day, including L. Ron Hubbard, whose "Fear" and "Typewriter in the Sky" appeared there; Fritz Leiber, whose Fafhrd and the Gray Mouser stories began in its pages; L. Sprague de Camp and Fletcher Pratt, whose Harold Shea "Incompleat Enchanter" stories were a signature feature; Robert A. Heinlein ("The Devil Makes the Law"); Theodore Sturgeon; Manly Wade Wellman; and Jack Williamson, whose "Darker Than You Think" is regarded as a classic of modern horror.

Legacy

Despite its short life, Unknown is widely regarded as the finest fantasy magazine of the pulp era. Its insistence on rationally constructed fantasy helped define modern fantasy fiction, and its stories have been reprinted frequently, including in the anthologies drawn from the magazine edited in later decades.
