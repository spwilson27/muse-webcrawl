Naive Bayes spam filtering

Naive Bayes spam filtering is a statistical technique for identifying spam email, based on applying Bayes' theorem with the "naive" assumption that the presence of individual words contributes independently to the probability that a message is spam.

Method

The filter is trained on corpora of known spam and legitimate ("ham") messages, computing for each token a spam probability. For a new message, the most informative token probabilities are combined — for example in Graham's and Robinson's methods — to produce an overall score compared against a threshold.

History and limitations

Bayesian filtering was popularised by Paul Graham's 2002 essay "A Plan for Spam" and was built into mail clients such as Mozilla Thunderbird. Spammers countered with word obfuscation and "Bayesian poisoning" — padding spam with innocuous text — so modern filters combine it with other techniques.
