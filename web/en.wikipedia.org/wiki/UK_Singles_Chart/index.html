UK Singles Chart

The UK Singles Chart is a weekly chart compiled by the Official Charts Company (OCC) ranking the best-selling and most-streamed singles in the United Kingdom. The Top 100 chart is based on physical sales, digital downloads and audio and video streams, converted into chart units. It is broadcast in abridged form on BBC Radio 1 on Friday afternoons.

History

The first UK singles chart was published by the New Musical Express on 14 November 1952, with Al Martino's "Here in My Heart" as the inaugural number one. Rival charts proliferated in the 1960s until the industry adopted a single "official" chart compiled by the British Market Research Bureau from 1969. Compilation passed to Gallup in 1983 — enabling the fully electronic charts of the 1980s — and later to Millward Brown/Kantar and the OCC. Downloads were integrated from 2005, and streaming from July 2014, initially at 100 streams per sale, later adjusted to 150:1 for standard streams.

Records

Elvis Presley holds the record for the most UK number-one singles (21), followed by the Beatles (17–18 by differing counts). The longest-running number one is "I Believe" by Frankie Laine (18 weeks in 1953), with Bryan Adams's "(Everything I Do) I Do It for You" the longest of the modern era at 16 consecutive weeks (1991). Ed Sheeran's "Shape of You" (2017) was the first single to accrue over a million chart units in the streaming era, and the Christmas number one is a celebrated annual chart battle.
