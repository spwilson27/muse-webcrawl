Luton

Luton (ˈluːtən) is a town and unitary authority in Bedfordshire, England. It lies about 30 miles (48 km) north-northwest of London and had a population of about 225,000 at the 2021 census. Luton is served by London Luton Airport, one of the United Kingdom's busiest airports, and by rail services on the Midland Main Line and Thameslink route.

History

Luton began as a Saxon settlement on the River Lea in the 6th century; its name derives from "town on the river Lea." It is recorded in the Domesday Book as Loitone. From the 17th century the town became a centre of the straw-plaiting and hat-making trades, which dominated its economy through the Victorian era. In the 20th century hat-making declined and engineering took its place: Vauxhall Motors opened its Luton plant in 1905 and for a century the town was synonymous with car manufacture; van production continues at the IBC Vehicles site. Luton Borough became a unitary authority in 1997.

Airport and transport

London Luton Airport, opened in 1938, is a major base for easyJet, Wizz Air and Ryanair and handles over 16 million passengers a year in normal conditions. The Luton DART automated shuttle links Luton Airport Parkway station with the terminal. Three railway stations serve the town: Luton, Luton Airport Parkway and Leagrave, with fast trains to London St Pancras in about 25 minutes. The M1 motorway passes the town's western edge.

Demographics and culture

Luton is one of England's most ethnically diverse towns, with large British Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Irish, Polish and other communities; at the 2011 census about 55% of residents identified as White. The annual Luton International Carnival, held since 1976, is one of the largest one-day carnivals in Europe. The town's football club, Luton Town F.C., nicknamed the Hatters, plays at Kenilworth Road and in 2023 won promotion to the Premier League for the first time. The University of Bedfordshire has its main campus in the town centre.
