Tacloban

Country: Philippines

Region: Eastern Visayas

Province: Leyte (geographically only)

Population: approx. 251,000 (2020 census)

Tacloban, officially the City of Tacloban, is a highly urbanized city on the island of Leyte in the Philippines. It is the regional centre of Eastern Visayas and the largest city in the region by population. The city is located on San Juanico Strait, which separates Leyte from Samar, and is connected to Samar by the San Juanico Bridge, once the longest bridge in the Philippines.

History

Tacloban began as a fishing village called Kankabatok, a name referring to the taklub, a bamboo fish-catching device. It became the capital of Leyte in 1830. During the Second World War, the city and nearby Palo were liberated during the Leyte landings of October 1944; Tacloban briefly served as the seat of the Philippine Commonwealth government under President Sergio Osmeña. The city was also the hometown of Imelda Marcos, whose family, the Romuáldezes, long dominated local politics.

Typhoon Haiyan

On 8 November 2013, Tacloban was devastated by Typhoon Haiyan (Yolanda), one of the strongest tropical cyclones ever recorded at landfall. A storm surge several metres high inundated the coastal barangays, destroying or damaging most of the city's buildings and killing thousands of residents. The disaster drew an enormous international relief effort, and the city's recovery became a focus of disaster-resilience policy in the Philippines. Pope Francis visited Tacloban in January 2015.

Economy

Tacloban is the principal commercial, education and government centre of Eastern Visayas, served by Daniel Z. Romualdez Airport and a seaport handling inter-island trade.
