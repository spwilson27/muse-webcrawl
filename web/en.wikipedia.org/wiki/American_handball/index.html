American handball

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

One-wall American handball is a fixture of New York City playgrounds.

American handball is a sport in which players hit a small rubber ball with their hands against a wall, in singles or doubles play. It is distinct from team handball: the American game belongs to the family of wall-ball games that includes Gaelic handball, from which it directly descends.

Variants

The game is played in three principal forms. Four-wall handball is contested indoors on a court 40 feet long, 20 feet wide, and 20 feet high, using a small, hard black ball. One-wall handball, hugely popular in New York City, uses a single wall 34 feet wide and 16 feet high and a larger, softer "big blue" ball. Three-wall is played outdoors in places such as Venice Beach, California, and Toledo, Ohio.

Play

A serve must strike the front wall and rebound beyond the short line; the receiver must return the ball before its second bounce, and it may strike one or two side walls (in four-wall) before or after hitting the front wall. Games are typically played to 21 points, with points scored only by the server — or, in modern rules, by rally scoring. Skilled players use spin, ceiling shots, and "kill shots" that strike low on the front wall and roll out unreturnable.

History and organization

Irish immigrants brought handball to the United States in the 19th century. The game flourished in YMCAs, athletic clubs, firehouses, and city playgrounds. Its national governing body is the United States Handball Association (USHA), founded in 1951, which sanctions national championships in four-wall, three-wall, and one-wall play.
