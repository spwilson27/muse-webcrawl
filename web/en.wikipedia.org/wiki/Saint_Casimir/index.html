Saint Casimir

Casimir Jagiellon (Polish: Kazimierz Jagiellończyk; Lithuanian: Šventasis Kazimieras; 3 October 1458 – 4 March 1484) was a prince of the Kingdom of Poland and of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania. The second son of King Casimir IV Jagiellon and Queen Elizabeth of Austria, he was known for his piety and devotion to the Virgin Mary, and is venerated as the patron saint of Lithuania and of youth.

Life

Casimir was educated at Kraków, among his tutors the historian Jan Długosz. In 1471, as a boy of thirteen, he was offered the crown of Hungary by nobles opposed to Matthias Corvinus, but the expedition failed and he returned home. As second in line to the Polish throne during his father's absences, he administered the kingdom and showed an inclination toward prayer, fasting, and works of charity rather than court life. He is traditionally credited with composing or inspiring the hymn Omni die dic Mariae ("Daily, daily sing to Mary"), a copy of which was found in his tomb.

Death and veneration

Suffering from tuberculosis, Casimir died at Grodno in 1484 at the age of twenty-five and was buried in Vilnius Cathedral. Miracles were reported at his tomb, and he was canonized in the early 16th century. His silver sarcophagus in the ornate Casimir Chapel of Vilnius Cathedral remains a major pilgrimage site, and his feast on 4 March is celebrated in Lithuania with the Kaziukas Fair in Vilnius. In 1948, Pope Pius XII named him a special patron of all youth.
