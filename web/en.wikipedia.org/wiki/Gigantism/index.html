Gigantism, also known as giantism, is a condition characterized by excessive growth and height significantly above average. In humans, this condition is caused by overproduction of growth hormone in childhood, before the long bone epiphyses have closed. It is the childhood counterpart of acromegaly, which results from the same hormone excess beginning in adulthood.

Cause

Most cases of gigantism are caused by a benign tumour (adenoma) of the pituitary gland that secretes excess growth hormone (somatotropin). Genetic conditions associated with gigantism include multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1, McCune–Albright syndrome, Carney complex, and familial isolated pituitary adenoma, some of which involve mutations in the AIP gene.

Signs and symptoms

Affected children grow at an accelerated rate, with generalised enlargement of the body and limbs, delayed puberty, headaches, and sometimes visual field defects from pressure of the tumour on the optic chiasm. If untreated, the condition can progress to acromegalic features after the growth plates close.

Treatment

Treatment aims to reduce growth hormone secretion, most commonly by transsphenoidal surgery to remove the pituitary adenoma. Medications such as somatostatin analogues (e.g. octreotide), dopamine agonists, and the growth hormone receptor antagonist pegvisomant may be used, and radiotherapy is an option in some cases.

Notable cases

The tallest person in recorded history, Robert Wadlow (1918–1940), who reached 2.72 m (8 ft 11 in), had gigantism caused by pituitary hyperplasia. André the Giant, a professional wrestler and actor, also had gigantism that progressed to acromegaly.
