Snakebite

A snakebite is an injury caused by the bite of a snake, especially a venomous snake. Envenoming from snakebites is a major neglected public-health problem in tropical and subtropical regions: the World Health Organization estimates that about 5.4 million people are bitten by snakes each year, with 1.8 to 2.7 million cases of envenoming, between roughly 81,000 and 138,000 deaths, and several times that number of amputations and other permanent disabilities.

Signs and symptoms

Effects depend on the species, the amount of venom injected, and the bite location. Common signs include puncture marks, pain, swelling, and bruising at the site. Venoms act through different mechanisms: neurotoxins may cause descending paralysis and respiratory failure (typical of many elapids such as cobras, kraits, and mambas); haemotoxins disrupt blood clotting and damage tissue (typical of vipers); and cytotoxic venoms cause severe local swelling, blistering, and necrosis. Some bites are "dry," with no venom injected.

First aid and treatment

Recommended first aid is to move away from the snake, keep the victim still and the bitten limb immobilized and at heart level, remove jewellery and tight clothing, and transport the victim to medical care as quickly as possible. Cutting, sucking, tourniquets, electric shock, and traditional remedies are harmful or ineffective and are discouraged. The definitive treatment is antivenom — antibodies raised in animals against a species' or region's venoms — given intravenously with monitoring for allergic reactions, alongside supportive care such as ventilation, fluids, and wound management. Identifying the snake, when safely possible, helps clinicians select the appropriate antivenom.

Prevention and global response

Most victims are agricultural workers and children in rural areas of Africa, Asia, and Latin America. Prevention includes wearing boots and gloves, using lights at night, sleeping under nets, and keeping food stores sealed against rodents. The WHO, which added snakebite envenoming to its neglected tropical diseases list in 2017, adopted a strategy in 2019 aiming to halve deaths and disability from snakebite by 2030, emphasizing antivenom availability, health-system capacity, and community education.
