The Wigan Warriors are a professional rugby league club based in Wigan, Greater Manchester, England. Founded in 1872, they were founder members of the Northern Rugby Football Union in 1895 — the schism that created rugby league — and are the most successful club in the history of the British game.

History

Wigan's dominance peaked in the late 1980s and mid-1990s, when the club won the Challenge Cup a record eight times in succession (1988–1995) alongside a string of league championships, fielding celebrated players such as Ellery Hanley, Shaun Edwards, Andy Farrell, and Martin Offiah. In the Super League era the Warriors have remained perennial contenders, winning multiple Grand Finals and Challenge Cups, and World Club Challenge titles against Australian opposition. The club shares its stadium with Wigan Athletic and has a fierce local rivalry with St Helens, contested in the traditional Good Friday derby.
