Koshi Inaba

Inaba performing with B'z

Koshi Inaba (Japanese: 稲葉 浩志, Inaba Kōshi, born 23 September 1964 in Tsuyama, Okayama) is a Japanese singer, songwriter, and multi-instrumentalist, best known as the vocalist and lyricist of the rock duo B'z. With B'z he is among the best-selling music artists in Japanese history; the duo was inducted into the Hollywood RockWalk in 2007, the first Asian artists so honoured. Inaba is noted for his powerful, wide-ranging voice and for writing the lyrics to nearly all B'z songs.

Biography

Inaba graduated from Yokohama National University with a degree for teaching mathematics, but pursued music instead. After working as a session and backing vocalist, he answered an audition by guitarist Tak Matsumoto in 1988; the two formed B'z and debuted that September. Writing introspective and philosophical lyrics against Matsumoto's hard-rock guitar, Inaba became the voice of a string of record-breaking No. 1 singles through the 1990s.

Solo work

Inaba began a parallel solo career with the album Magma (1997), followed by Shian (2002), Peace of Mind (2004), Hadou (2010), and Singing Bird (2014). He has collaborated with Stevie Salas in the project INABA/SALAS (albums Chubby Groove, 2017, and Maximum Huavo, 2020), and contributed vocals to songs by artists including Steve Vai (on "Asian Sky" with Inaba's earlier band), Slash, and Larry Carlton. His solo tours are billed as "Koshi Inaba LIVE" and under the band name en-Zepp/en-ball; he is also known for marathon running and for keeping his private life out of the press.
