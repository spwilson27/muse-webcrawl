Robert Graves

BornRobert von Ranke Graves, 24 July 1895, Wimbledon, London, England
Died7 December 1985 (aged 90), Deià, Mallorca, Spain
OccupationPoet, novelist, critic
Notable worksGood-Bye to All That (1929); I, Claudius (1934); The White Goddess (1948)

Robert von Ranke Graves (24 July 1895 – 7 December 1985) was an English poet, historical novelist and critic. His father was Alfred Perceval Graves, a celebrated Irish poet and figure in the Gaelic revival. Graves served as an officer in the Royal Welch Fusiliers in the First World War, an experience he recounted in his celebrated memoir Good-Bye to All That (1929).

Work

Graves produced some 140 works, including more than 50 volumes of poetry. His historical novel I, Claudius (1934) and its sequel Claudius the God won lasting fame, later adapted by the BBC in 1976. In The White Goddess (1948) he developed an influential — if scholarly contested — theory of poetic myth centred on a matriarchal moon goddess, and in The Greek Myths (1955) he retold classical mythology in his own interpretive frame.

Life in Mallorca

After a turbulent decade in England — including a suicide attempt by his partner Laura Riding — Graves settled in Deià, Mallorca, in 1929, leaving only during the Spanish Civil War and Second World War. He lived there for the rest of his life, becoming the centre of a circle of writers and artists. He was Professor of Poetry at Oxford from 1961 to 1966.
