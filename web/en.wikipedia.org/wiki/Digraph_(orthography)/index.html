Digraph (orthography)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A digraph or digram (from the Greek: dígraphos, "double-written") is a pair of characters used in the orthography of a language to write either a single phoneme (distinct sound), or a sequence of phonemes that does not correspond to the normal values of the two characters combined.

Digraphs are often used when a language's phoneme inventory is larger than the number of letters available in its alphabet, or when a phoneme has no dedicated letter. In some orthographies digraphs are treated as independent letters for purposes of alphabetization, collation, and crossword construction.

Examples

  • English: ch (as in church), sh (ship), th (this and thin), ph (phone), ng (sing), wh (when), and vowel digraphs such as ea, ou, and ie.
  • French: ou, au, eu, ch, gn, and the trigraph eau.
  • German: sch (a trigraph), ch, ie, eu, ei.
  • Spanish: ch and ll were historically treated as separate letters in the alphabet; rr also occurs.
  • Dutch: ij, often capitalized together as IJ and sometimes treated as a single letter.
  • Welsh: ch, dd, ff, ll, ng, rh, and th are counted as single letters.
  • Hungarian: cs, dz, gy, ly, ny, sz, ty, zs, plus the trigraph dzs.

Digraphs as letters

In languages such as Welsh, Hungarian, Croatian, and Slovak, certain digraphs are letters in their own right, with their own place in alphabetical order. In Croatian and Slovak, some are even written with dedicated single characters in Unicode (for example ǆ, ǉ, ǌ).
