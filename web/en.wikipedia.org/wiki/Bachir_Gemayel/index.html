Bachir Gemayel (Arabic: بشير الجميّل; 10 November 1947 – 14 September 1982) was a Lebanese militia commander and politician. The youngest son of Pierre Gemayel, founder of the Kataeb Party (the Phalange), he rose during the Lebanese Civil War to command the Lebanese Forces, the unified military wing of the predominantly Christian Lebanese Front. In August 1982, at the height of the Israeli invasion of Lebanon, he was elected President of Lebanon, but he was assassinated three weeks later, before taking office.

Early life

Gemayel was born in the Achrafieh district of Beirut into one of Lebanon's most prominent Maronite Christian political families. He studied law at Saint Joseph University and became active in the Kataeb Party. After the civil war broke out in 1975 he emerged as a leading figure in the party's militia, the Kataeb Regulatory Forces.

Civil war and the Lebanese Forces

By 1976 Gemayel had become the principal military commander of the Lebanese Front. In 1980 he forcibly unified the rival Christian militias under the Lebanese Forces in a bloody campaign against the Tigers militia of the Chamoun family. He sought support abroad, and his alignment with Israel deepened; during Israel's 1982 invasion, the Lebanese Forces cooperated with the Israeli advance on Beirut.

Election and assassination

Following the evacuation of PLO forces from Beirut, parliament elected Gemayel president on 23 August 1982. On 14 September 1982, before his inauguration, he was killed by a massive bomb at the Kataeb headquarters in Achrafieh. The assassin, Habib Shartouni, a member of the Syrian Social Nationalist Party, was arrested; the assassination was widely attributed to Syrian direction. Days later, Christian militiamen entered the Sabra and Shatila refugee camps and massacred Palestinian and Lebanese civilians, an atrocity carried out in the shadow of his death. His elder brother Amine Gemayel succeeded him as president.
