Pokémon (TV series)

Pokémon
Genre: Anime, adventure
Country: Japan
Original run: 1997 – present
Studio: OLM

Pokémon, known in Japan as Pocket Monsters (ポケットモンスター), is a Japanese anime television series produced by OLM and based on Nintendo's Pokémon video game franchise. It premiered on TV Tokyo on April 1, 1997, and has run for more than 1,200 episodes, alongside numerous films, specials, and spin-offs, making it one of the most successful video-game adaptations of all time.

Plot

For its first 25 seasons, the series followed Ash Ketchum, a ten-year-old Pokémon Trainer from Pallet Town, and his partner Pikachu, as they travelled through the regions of the Pokémon world, catching Pokémon, winning Gym badges, and competing in regional leagues, while regularly thwarting the schemes of Team Rocket members Jessie, James, and Meowth. Ash was accompanied by a rotating cast of companions, beginning with Misty and Brock. In 2019's Pokémon Journeys, Ash finally achieved the title of world champion Monarch. The series then transitioned to new protagonists, Liko and Roy, in Pokémon Horizons (2023).

Seizure incident

The 1997 episode "Dennō Senshi Porygon" ("Electric Soldier Porygon") featured rapidly flashing red and blue lights that triggered photosensitive epileptic seizures in hundreds of Japanese children. The incident led to the episode being banned worldwide, a four-month hiatus for the series, and the adoption of broadcasting guidelines on flashing imagery in animation.

Reception and legacy

The anime was instrumental in launching "Pokémania" in North America and Europe from 1998 onward, airing in syndication and on Kids' WB in the United States in a version localised by 4Kids Entertainment, and later by The Pokémon Company International. Its theme songs, films — beginning with Pokémon: The First Movie (1998) — and merchandising made it a defining children's franchise of its era.
