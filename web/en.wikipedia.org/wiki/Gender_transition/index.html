Gender transition


Transition encompasses social, legal and medical steps

Gender transition is the process of changing one's gender presentation or sex characteristics to accord with one's internal sense of gender identity, as opposed to the gender assigned at birth. Transitioning may involve social changes (name, pronouns, clothing and hairstyle), legal changes (name and gender marker on identity documents), and medical interventions such as hormone replacement therapy and surgeries. Not all transgender people pursue every element, and the process is individual in its goals and order.

Social and legal transition

Social transition typically involves coming out to family, friends and colleagues, adopting a new name and pronouns, and presenting in a way aligned with one's gender identity. Legal recognition of the new name and gender varies greatly by jurisdiction: some allow administrative self-declaration, while others impose medical requirements or court procedures. Documentation changes can affect passports, driving licences, birth certificates and school or employment records.

Medical transition

Medical transition may include puberty blockers for adolescents, cross-sex hormone therapy (testosterone for trans men, oestrogen with androgen suppression for trans women), voice therapy, hair removal, and surgeries such as chest reconstruction, genital surgery and facial procedures. Major medical associations regard such care, provided under clinical guidelines such as the WPATH Standards of Care, as effective treatment for gender dysphoria. For young people, assessment and staged interventions are recommended; care for minors is restricted or banned in some jurisdictions, a subject of ongoing legal and political dispute. The reverse process, whether partial or complete, is called detransition.
