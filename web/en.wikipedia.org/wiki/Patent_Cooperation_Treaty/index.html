Patent Cooperation Treaty

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT), concluded in Washington in 1970 and in force from 1978, is an international patent law treaty administered by WIPO, with more than 155 contracting states as of the mid-2020s. It does not grant an “international patent” — none exists — but provides a unified procedure: a single international application, an international search and written opinion on patentability (optionally a preliminary examination), and publication at eighteen months, after which the applicant pursues grant separately before national and regional offices (the “national phase”, generally by 30 months from priority).

The system defers the heavy costs of multi-country filing (translations, local agents, fees) while applicants assess commercial prospects and search results. Roughly a quarter of a million PCT applications are filed annually (the largest filers being in China, the United States, Japan and, among companies, telecommunications and computing firms), making the PCT both a procedural utility and the statistical backbone of global patenting data. Regional systems (the European Patent Office, ARIPO, OAPI, the Eurasian office) interlock with it as “regional phases”.
