Delegate (United States Congress)

A delegate in the United States House of Representatives is a non-voting member representing a US territory or the District of Columbia. Delegates may debate, introduce bills and vote in committee, but cannot vote on final passage on the House floor.

Territories represented

Delegates serve American Samoa, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, the US Virgin Islands and Washington, D.C.; Puerto Rico elects a Resident Commissioner serving a four-year term. Delegates serve two-year terms like representatives.

History

The office dates to territorial delegates in the Confederation Congress and Northwest Ordinance era, when frontier territories sent representatives before statehood. In some periods delegates could vote in the Committee of the Whole, a practice litigated and changed by House rules.
