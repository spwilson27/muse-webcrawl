Kashan

Kashan (Persian: کاشان) is a city in Isfahan Province, Iran, lying on the edge of the central Iranian desert, the Dasht-e Kavir. It is one of Iran's oldest cities, famous for its historic merchant houses, the Fin Garden, its silk and carpet production, and its rosewater distilled from the roses of nearby Qamsar.

History

The mounds of Tepe Sialk, just outside Kashan, show settlement going back to the Neolithic period, around 6000 BC, making the area among the earliest centres of civilization in Iran. The city itself grew as an oasis on the caravan routes crossing the central desert. Kashan flourished in Seljuk and especially Safavid times as a centre of textile, lustreware pottery and carpet production; its name is often linked with kashi, the Persian word for glazed tile.

The city was devastated by an earthquake in 1778, after which many of its finest houses were rebuilt in the Qajar period. In the 19th century merchants such as the Borujerdi and Tabatabaei families built the sunken courtyard houses for which Kashan is now celebrated, designed to stay cool in the desert heat with windcatchers (badgir) and deep basements.

Sights

The Fin Garden (Bagh-e Fin), completed in the Safavid era and one of the nine Persian gardens inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is where the Qajar-era prime minister Amir Kabir was murdered in 1852. Other landmarks include the Agha Bozorg Mosque and madrasa, the Aminoddowleh caravanserai in the bazaar, and the Sultan Amir Ahmad Bathhouse. The town of Abyaneh, a village of red mud-brick houses, lies in the mountains nearby.

Economy

Kashan remains a centre of carpet weaving (now largely machine-made), silk and velvet textiles, pottery, and the production of rosewater and herbal distillates. Each spring the rose harvest around Qamsar draws visitors to the Golabgiri festival.
