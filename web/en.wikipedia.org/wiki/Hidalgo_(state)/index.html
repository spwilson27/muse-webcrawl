Hidalgo (state)

State

Capital: Pachuca de Soto
Largest city: Pachuca
Area: 20,846 km²
Population (2020): 3,082,841

Hidalgo, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Hidalgo, is one of the 32 federal entities of Mexico. It is located in east-central Mexico, bordered by San Luis Potosí and Veracruz to the north, Puebla to the east, Tlaxcala and the State of Mexico to the south, and Querétaro to the west. The state was created in 1869 under President Benito Juárez and named after Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla, initiator of the Mexican War of Independence.

Geography

The state is crossed by the Sierra Madre Oriental, with terrain ranging from high plateaus and deep barrancas to the subtropical Huasteca region in the northeast. Important sites include the Toltec ruins of Tula, the basaltic prisms of Huasca de Ocampo, and the hot springs of the Mezquital Valley.

Economy

Mining, historically of silver and gold around Pachuca and Real del Monte, has long been central to Hidalgo's economy; English miners from Cornwall in the 19th century left a lasting legacy, including the introduction of football to Mexico and the Cornish-style pastes still sold as pastes. Modern industries include cement, textiles, refining at Tula and agriculture.
