Roger Williams (c. 1603 – 1683) was an English-born New England Puritan minister, theologian and author who founded Providence Plantations, which became the Colony of Rhode Island and later the US state of Rhode Island. Banished from Massachusetts Bay Colony for his radical views, he established a settlement founded on religious liberty, the separation of church and state, and fair purchase of land from Native Americans — principles that later shaped the First Amendment to the United States Constitution.

Early life

Williams was born in London, the son of a merchant tailor, and as a boy worked as shorthand clerk to the jurist Sir Edward Coke. He studied at Charterhouse and Pembroke College, Cambridge, took holy orders in the Church of England, and became a chaplain — but his Puritan convictions led him to emigrate with his wife Mary to Boston in 1631.

Banishment and Providence

Williams preached at Salem and Plymouth but clashed with Massachusetts authorities: he denied that the king could grant Indian land without purchase, opposed compulsory church attendance and oaths, and argued that the civil magistrate had no authority over the "first table" of the Ten Commandments — matters of conscience. In October 1635 the General Court ordered his banishment. Fleeing arrest in January 1636, he was sheltered by the Narragansett people and, with their sachems Canonicus and Miantonomi granting the land, founded Providence in June 1636 as a refuge for "distressed consciences".

Rhode Island and writings

Williams travelled to England in 1643 and obtained a parliamentary patent for the Providence Plantations, publishing meanwhile A Key into the Language of America, a pioneering study of the Narragansett language, and in 1644 The Bloudy Tenent of Persecution for Cause of Conscience, his famous argument for "soul liberty". He served as president of the colony from 1654 to 1657, maintained lifelong friendships with the Narragansett (though King Philip's War devastated both peoples late in his life), and helped make Rhode Island a haven for Baptists, Quakers and Jews. He died in Providence in 1683.

Legacy

Williams is honoured as an early champion of religious freedom and church-state separation; Roger Williams University and Roger Williams Park in Providence bear his name, and his statue represents Rhode Island in the US Capitol.
