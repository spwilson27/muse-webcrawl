LGBT pride (also gay pride) is the promotion of the self-affirmation, dignity, equality and increased visibility of lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and queer people as a social group, and a movement opposing shame and stigma. Pride is expressed publicly through parades, festivals, symbols and community events, and has become a central institution of LGBTQ culture worldwide.

Origins

The modern pride movement grew from the Stonewall riots of June 1969 in New York City. On 28 June 1970, the first anniversary, the Christopher Street Liberation Day March was held in New York, with parallel marches in other US cities — the first Pride parades. Organiser Brenda Howard is often called the "Mother of Pride".

Symbols and celebrations

Gilbert Baker's rainbow flag (1978) became the dominant symbol; later designs such as the Progress Pride flag (2018) added representation for trans people and people of colour. June is widely observed as Pride Month. The largest events — in cities such as São Paulo, New York, Madrid (WorldPride/EuroPride), Toronto and Taipei — draw millions of participants. Debates within the movement address commercialisation ("rainbow capitalism"), police participation, and inclusivity.
