Herbes de Provence

Herbes de Provence (French: [ɛʁb də pʁɔvɑ̃s]) is a mixture of dried herbs considered typical of the Provence region of southeastern France. Formerly simply a descriptive term for herbs grown in the region, commercial blends began to be sold under this name in the 1970s. The blend is used to flavour grilled foods as well as stews and soups.

Composition

A typical mixture includes savory, marjoram, rosemary, thyme, and oregano. In North American blends, lavender leaves are often included, reflecting the lavender fields associated with Provence, although traditional French mixtures frequently do not contain lavender. Some blends also add fennel seed, sage, basil, or bay leaf.

History

Provencal cooking has long used the wild aromatic herbs of the Mediterranean scrubland known as the garrigue. The specific name "herbes de Provence" was popularised when spice companies, notably Ducros, began marketing standardised blends in the 1970s, spreading the mixture internationally.

Uses

The mixture is commonly used before or during cooking to season grilled meats and fish, roasted vegetables, and tomato-based dishes. Because the dried herbs can withstand cooking, they are often added earlier than fresh herbs.
