Cone (topology)

In topology, especially algebraic topology, the cone of a topological space X is the quotient space obtained from the product of X with the unit interval I = [0, 1] by collapsing the subspace X × {1} to a single point. That is,

CX = (X × [0,1]) / (X × {1}),

and it is denoted CX or, in some texts, by a join-like notation. Intuitively, CX is formed by taking a "cylinder" over X and pinching one end shut to a point called the apex (or vertex), leaving X itself embedded as the base at the other end.

Examples

  • The cone over a point is an interval.
  • The cone over a circle is a filled geometric cone, homeomorphic to a closed 2-dimensional disk.
  • The cone over an n-sphere is homeomorphic to the closed (n+1)-ball.
  • The cone over a closed interval is a triangle (a 2-simplex shape), and more generally the cone over a simplex is a higher-dimensional simplex.

Properties

Every cone CX is contractible, hence simply connected with trivial reduced homology, regardless of the topology of X. The cone construction is a functor on the category of topological spaces: a continuous map f : X → Y induces a map Cf : CX → CY. Related constructions include the reduced cone (for based spaces), the suspension, which can be described as two cones glued along their common base, and the mapping cone of a continuous map, a fundamental tool in homotopy theory and homological algebra.
