Newton (platform)

The Newton is a personal digital assistant (PDA) platform developed by Apple Computer and released in 1993. Running the Newton OS and programmed in the NewtonScript language, it powered the MessagePad line of handheld devices and a small number of licensed products by other manufacturers.

Features

Newton devices pioneered handwriting recognition on a consumer device: users wrote on the screen with a stylus, and the system converted ink to text while also recognising shapes and supporting intelligent "assistants" that could, for example, turn the written phrase "lunch with Mark tomorrow" into a calendar entry. Early recognition errors became a cultural joke — lampooned in Doonesbury and The Simpsons — but later versions improved markedly.

History

Development began in 1987 under Steve Sakoman, with the goal of reinventing personal computing; the project eventually included the ARM processor architecture, in which Apple invested. The MessagePad launched at CES in August 1993. The platform was cancelled by Steve Jobs in 1998 shortly after his return to Apple, but its handwriting and ARM legacies influenced the later iPhone and iPad era.
