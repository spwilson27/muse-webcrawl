Alutiiq

Alutiiq / Sugpiaq
Region: South-central Alaska (Kodiak Island, Alaska Peninsula, Prince William Sound, Kenai Peninsula)
Related groups: Yup'ik, Aleut (Unangax)

The Alutiiq people, also called Sugpiaq, are an Alaska Native people inhabiting the coastal regions of south-central Alaska, including Kodiak Island, the Alaska Peninsula, Prince William Sound, and the outer Kenai Peninsula. Their name for themselves, Sugpiaq, means "real person"; "Alutiiq" derives from the Russian rendering of the word "Aleut," applied historically to several Native groups of the region.

Language

The Alutiiq language (Sugcestun) is a Pacific Gulf Yupik language closely related to Central Alaskan Yup'ik. It has two main dialects: Koniag, spoken on Kodiak Island and the Alaska Peninsula, and Chugach, spoken around Prince William Sound and the Kenai Peninsula. The language is endangered, and revitalisation efforts include immersion preschools and teaching materials developed on Kodiak.

History and culture

Archaeological evidence shows human occupation of the Kodiak Archipelago for more than 7,500 years. Alutiiq society was organised into chiefdoms, with elaborate ceremonies, masks, and bentwood hunting hats used in sea-mammal hunting. Russian colonisation beginning in 1784 brought profound disruption, and Alutiiq people were later subject to American assimilation policies. Today Alutiiq cultural heritage is preserved and interpreted by institutions such as the Alutiiq Museum in Kodiak.
