Batmobile

The Batmobile is the fictional high-performance automobile driven by the DC Comics superhero Batman. Housed in the Batcave beneath Wayne Manor, the vehicle is depicted as a heavily armoured, technologically advanced car equipped for pursuit, crime-scene analysis and combat, and it has evolved continuously since Batman's earliest appearances — going from an ordinary red sedan in 1939 to the purpose-built, bat-themed machine introduced in Detective Comics #48 (1941).

The Batmobile's iconic finned silhouette from the 1966 television series.

In the comics

In modern continuity the Batmobile is a custom-built vehicle combining a racing chassis with military-grade armour, a jet or high-output engine, run-flat or self-repairing tyres, an onboard computer linked to the Batcave and police networks, non-lethal weaponry, forensic equipment and stealth capabilities. Its design varies by artist and era, but black paint, a long bonnet and bat motifs recur throughout.

In film and television

The most famous screen Batmobile is the 1966 television version, built by George Barris from the 1955 Lincoln Futura concept car. Tim Burton's films (1989–1992) used a long, turbine-nosed design; the Christopher Nolan trilogy introduced the tank-like Tumbler, a functional prototype-style vehicle; later films returned to sleeker armoured sports-car forms. In the Batman: Arkham games the car is chiefly seen in cut-scenes until it becomes drivable in Arkham Knight. Numerous full-size replicas tour museums and exhibitions, and the 1966 Barris car sold at auction in 2013 for about $4.6 million.

Cultural impact

The Batmobile is one of the most recognisable fictional vehicles in popular culture, repeatedly ranked alongside the DeLorean and James Bond's Aston Martin DB5, and its name is used generically for any heavily customised black car.
