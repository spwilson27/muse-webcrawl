Karaman

Karaman is a city in south-central Turkey, the capital of Karaman Province, located on the northern slopes of the Taurus Mountains, about 100 km south of Konya. In antiquity it was known as Laranda. The population of the city is roughly 170,000.

History

Karaman was the capital of the Karamanids (Karaman Beyliği), one of the Anatolian Turkish beyliks that emerged after the decline of the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum. In 1277, Karamanoğlu Mehmed Bey captured Konya and issued a famous decree making Turkish the official language of his state — an event commemorated annually in Karaman's Turkish Language Festival. The Karamanids were the principal rivals of the rising Ottomans in Anatolia before the region was finally absorbed into the Ottoman Empire in the late 15th century.

Sights

Notable landmarks include Karaman Castle, the Aktekke Mosque (containing the tomb of Mevlana Rumi's mother), the Hatuniye Medresesi, and, in the surroundings, the rock-cut settlements and Byzantine churches of the Binbirkilise area on Karadağ, and the ancient city of Derbe nearby.

Economy

Karaman is a significant centre of the Turkish food industry, particularly biscuit and confectionery production, as well as agriculture in the surrounding plain.
