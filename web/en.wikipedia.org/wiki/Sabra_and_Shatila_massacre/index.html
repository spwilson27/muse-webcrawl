Sabra and Shatila massacre

Location: Sabra neighbourhood and Shatila refugee camp, West Beirut, Lebanon
Date: 16–18 September 1982
Perpetrators: Lebanese Phalangist militia
Victims: Palestinian and Lebanese civilians; estimates of the dead range from about 460 to several thousand

The Sabra and Shatila massacre was the killing of civilians by a Lebanese Christian Phalangist militia in the Sabra neighbourhood and the adjacent Shatila Palestinian refugee camp in Israeli-occupied Beirut, Lebanon, between 16 and 18 September 1982.

The massacre followed the assassination of Lebanese president-elect Bachir Gemayel, leader of the Phalange party, on 14 September 1982. Israeli forces, which had invaded Lebanon in June 1982 and surrounded the camps, allowed Phalangist militiamen to enter them, ostensibly to root out remaining Palestine Liberation Organization fighters — though PLO forces had been evacuated from Beirut under international supervision weeks earlier.

Aftermath and investigations

News of the killings provoked international outrage and mass protest in Israel, where some 400,000 people demonstrated in Tel Aviv. The Israeli government established the Kahan Commission, which in 1983 found that Defence Minister Ariel Sharon bore "personal responsibility" for ignoring the danger of a massacre, and recommended his removal; Sharon resigned as defence minister but remained in the cabinet, later becoming prime minister.

In Lebanon, the 1991 amnesty law foreclosed domestic prosecution. No one has been convicted for the massacre. In 1982 the United Nations General Assembly condemned the killings as an act of genocide, a characterisation that remains debated among historians. The events are commemorated annually in Lebanon and in Palestinian communities.
