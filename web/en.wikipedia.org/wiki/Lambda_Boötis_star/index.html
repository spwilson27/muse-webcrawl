Lambda Boötis star

A Lambda Boötis star is a type of chemically peculiar star named after its prototype, Lambda Boötis. These stars are Population I, late B to early F main-sequence (or slightly evolved) stars whose spectra show a characteristic abundance pattern: carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and sulfur are roughly solar in abundance, while heavier elements — especially iron-peak elements such as iron, chromium and nickel — are strongly underabundant, in some cases by a factor of a hundred.

Characteristics

Lambda Boötis stars make up roughly 2% of stars in their spectral range. They rotate rapidly, show weak Mg II 4481 Å lines relative to hydrogen, and are often found at high galactic latitudes for their spectral type. Because the light elements remain near solar while refractory metals are depleted, the pattern resembles the composition of the interstellar gas from which dust grains have removed the heavy elements, rather than the bulk composition of a star.

Explanations

The leading explanation is selective accretion: the star accretes metal-depleted circumstellar or interstellar gas, while radiation pressure prevents dust grains — which carry the missing metals — from falling in. The underabundance is then confined to a thin outer layer that is continuously mixed and replenished, so the phenomenon may be a temporary phase. Alternative models invoke diffusion within the star or mass loss selectively removing metals. The accretion hypothesis is supported by the detection of circumstellar material around some Lambda Boötis stars and by the difficulty of explaining the light-element abundances with diffusion alone.

Members

Well-studied members include the prototype Lambda Boötis itself, Pi¹ Orionis, and a number of stars in young clusters and associations, suggesting the phenomenon can appear early in a star's main-sequence life.
