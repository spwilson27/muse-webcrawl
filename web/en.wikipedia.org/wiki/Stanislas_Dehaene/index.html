Stanislas Dehaene

Stanislas Dehaene (born 12 May 1965) is a French cognitive neuroscientist and psychologist renowned for his work on numerical cognition, reading, and the neural basis of consciousness. He holds the chair of Experimental Cognitive Psychology at the Collège de France and directs the NeuroSpin brain imaging center near Paris.

Biography

Dehaene studied mathematics at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris before turning to psychology and neuroscience. He earned his doctorate in cognitive psychology and worked with Jacques Mehler and Jean-Pierre Changeux. Early in his career his research focused on how the brain processes numbers, a field he helped establish as a rigorous experimental discipline.

Research

Dehaene is best known for the triple-code model of numerical processing, which proposes that numbers are represented in three formats: a visual Arabic form, a verbal form, and an analog magnitude representation associated with the intraparietal sulcus. His experiments demonstrated that humans and other animals share an approximate "number sense."

His work on reading led to the concept of neuronal recycling: cultural inventions such as writing reuse pre-existing cortical circuits, in this case a region of the left fusiform gyrus known as the visual word form area. With Laurent Cohen and others, he mapped how literacy reshapes the brain.

With Changeux and colleagues, Dehaene developed the global neuronal workspace theory of consciousness, proposing that conscious access occurs when information is broadcast across a network of long-range prefrontal and parietal neurons.

Books and honors

His books include The Number Sense (1997), Reading in the Brain (2009), Consciousness and the Brain (2014), and How We Learn (2020). He has received numerous awards, including the Brain Prize in 2014, shared with Giacomo Rizzolatti and Trevor Robbins.
