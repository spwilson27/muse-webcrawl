Ergogenic aid

Ergogenic aids are techniques, substances, or phenomena that are believed to increase an athlete's performance. The term derives from the Greek ergon (work) and genes (born of). Ergogenic aids may be nutritional, pharmacological, physiological, mechanical, or psychological in nature.

Categories

Nutritional aids include carbohydrate loading, caffeine, creatine, and protein supplements; pharmacological aids include anabolic steroids and stimulants, many of which are prohibited in competition; physiological aids include blood doping and altitude training; mechanical aids include equipment design such as lightweight bicycles and full-body swimsuits; and psychological aids include hypnosis and mental imagery.

Regulation

The World Anti-Doping Agency maintains a prohibited list of substances and methods; ergogenic aids not on the list, such as caffeine (removed from the prohibited list in 2004) and creatine, are legal in competition. Scientific evidence supports a genuine ergogenic effect for only a minority of marketed supplements.
