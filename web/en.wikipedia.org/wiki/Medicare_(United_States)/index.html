Medicare (United States)

Medicare is a federal health insurance program in the United States for people age 65 or older, younger people with qualifying disabilities, and people with end-stage renal disease. It was established in 1965 under Title XVIII of the Social Security Act, signed by President Lyndon B. Johnson.

Parts

Part A covers hospital insurance; Part B medical insurance; Part C Medicare Advantage plans offered by private insurers; Part D (added 2003, effective 2006) covers prescription drugs — see Medication.

Financing

Medicare is funded by payroll taxes, premiums, and general revenue, and accounts for a major share of federal spending. Long-term fiscal pressures from an ageing population are a recurring policy debate, including Medicare for All proposals.
