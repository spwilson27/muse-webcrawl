16S ribosomal RNA

16S ribosomal RNA (16S rRNA) is the RNA component of the small (30S) subunit of the prokaryotic ribosome. The gene encoding it, about 1,500 base pairs long, is the most widely used molecular marker for identifying bacteria and archaea and for reconstructing their phylogeny.

Structure and function

In Escherichia coli, the 16S rRNA is 1,542 nucleotides long. Its 3′ end contains the anti-Shine–Dalgarno sequence that helps position messenger RNA on the ribosome during translation initiation. The molecule folds into a characteristic secondary structure of helices and loops that scaffolds the small subunit's ribosomal proteins.

Use in taxonomy

Because the 16S rRNA gene combines highly conserved regions — useful for universal PCR primers — with nine hypervariable regions (V1–V9) that differ between species, sequencing it allows identification of organisms that cannot be cultured. Carl Woese's comparisons of 16S rRNA sequences led to the recognition of the Archaea and the three-domain system of life. Full-length 16S similarity above about 98.7% is commonly used as a rough criterion for assigning bacteria to the same species, though it cannot resolve all close relatives.
