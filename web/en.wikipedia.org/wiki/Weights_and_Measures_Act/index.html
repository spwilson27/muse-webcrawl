Weights and Measures Act

Weights and Measures Acts are acts of the British Parliament and legislation in other Commonwealth and common-law jurisdictions determining the regulation of weights and measures. They govern the units and standards used in trade, the verification of weighing and measuring equipment, and offences for short measure or fraudulent devices.

United Kingdom

Standardization of measures in England dates to Magna Carta (1215), which called for uniform measures of wine, ale, and corn. Successive statutes consolidated the system: the Weights and Measures Act 1824 established the imperial system of units; the Act of 1878 consolidated standards and inspection; and the Weights and Measures Act 1985 is the principal modern legislation, governing trade measurement in Great Britain, permitting prescribed imperial and metric units, and enforced locally by Trading Standards officers.

Other jurisdictions

Canada enacted a federal Weights and Measures Act administered by Measurement Canada, governing devices and quantities in trade. India historically had state and central weights and measures legislation, replaced in part by the Legal Metrology Act 2009. Several Australian states and other Commonwealth countries have their own weights and measures acts based on the British model.
