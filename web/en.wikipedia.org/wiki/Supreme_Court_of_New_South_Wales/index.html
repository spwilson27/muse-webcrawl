LocationQueens Square, Sydney

CompositionCommon Law Division; Equity Division; Court of Appeal; Court of Criminal Appeal

The Supreme Court of New South Wales sits at Queens Square, Sydney.

The Supreme Court of New South Wales is the highest court in the Australian state of New South Wales. It was established by the Third Charter of Justice in 1823 and first sat in 1824, making it one of the oldest superior courts in Australia. It has unlimited jurisdiction within the state in civil matters and hears the most serious criminal matters.

Structure

The Court comprises the Court of Appeal, which hears civil appeals; the Court of Criminal Appeal, which hears criminal appeals; and two trial divisions, the Common Law Division and the Equity Division. Judges of the Court include the Chief Justice of New South Wales, the President of the Court of Appeal, Chief Judges of each division, and puisne judges.

History

The Court's origins lie in the Supreme Court of Civil Judicature established in 1814 and the permanent court created under the New South Wales Act 1823. Its first Chief Justice was Sir Francis Forbes. Throughout the nineteenth century the Court exercised jurisdiction as the colony grew, and after Federation in 1901 it continued as the state superior court, with appeals historically lying to the Privy Council and now to the High Court of Australia.

Jurisdiction

The Court hears serious criminal offences such as murder, major civil claims, equity matters including trusts and probate, and judicial review of administrative decisions. Appeals from its decisions go to the Court of Appeal or Court of Criminal Appeal, and ultimately, by special leave, to the High Court of Australia.
