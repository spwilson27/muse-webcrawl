Bábism

Bábism (Persian: بابیه, Bâbiyye) is a religious movement founded in 19th-century Persia by the Báb (Siyyid ʻAlí Muḥammad Shírází, 1819–1850). It emerged from Twelver Shiʻi Islam and anticipated the Baháʼí Faith, into which most Bábís converted.

History

On 23 May 1844 the Báb declared himself the Promised One (the Qá'im) expected by Shiʻi Muslims, and later claimed to be a new Manifestation of God bringing a new revelation. His teachings, set out in works such as the Qayyúm al-Asmáʼ and the Persian Bayán, abrogated Islamic law and announced a new religious dispensation and the imminent coming of "Him whom God shall make manifest".

The movement spread rapidly and alarmed clerical and state authorities. After armed clashes at Shaykh Tabarsí, Zanján and Nayríz, the Báb was executed by firing squad in Tabriz on 9 July 1850. A failed Bábí attempt on the Shah's life in 1852 triggered severe persecution.

Successors

Most Bábís accepted Baháʼu'lláh's claim to be the promised one and became Baháʼís; a minority followed Mírzá Yaḥyá (Subh-i Azal), forming the Azali branch, which dwindled into a small remnant.
