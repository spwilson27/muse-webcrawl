Germany has a dense, modern transport infrastructure serving its large population and its position as Europe's biggest economy and central transit country. Motorised road transport dominates, but the country also maintains one of the world's busiest railway networks, extensive inland waterways, and major hub airports.

Road transport

The federal motorway network, the Autobahn, extends over roughly 13,200 km and is famous for stretches with no general speed limit for cars (an advisory limit of 130 km/h applies). Cars are charged no toll, while heavy goods vehicles pay a distance-based toll. Germany has one of the highest car-ownership rates in Europe and the automobile industry is a pillar of the economy.

Rail transport

The railway network of about 38,000 km is operated principally by the state-owned Deutsche Bahn. Its flagship is the Intercity Express (ICE) high-speed service, reaching 300 km/h on purpose-built lines such as Cologne–Frankfurt and Nuremberg–Ingolstadt–Munich. Dense regional services and urban rapid transit — S-Bahn and U-Bahn systems in the major cities — carry billions of passengers a year. In 2023 the country introduced the Deutschlandticket, a low-cost nationwide monthly ticket for local and regional public transport.

Air and water

Frankfurt Airport is one of Europe's busiest passenger and cargo hubs, followed by Munich, Berlin Brandenburg, Düsseldorf, and Hamburg. Lufthansa is the flag carrier. Inland navigation on the Rhine, Danube, Elbe, and the canal network carries bulk freight, and the Port of Hamburg is among Europe's largest seaports, with Rotterdam and Bremerhaven also serving German trade.
