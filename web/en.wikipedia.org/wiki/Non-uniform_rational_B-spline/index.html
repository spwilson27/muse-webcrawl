Non-uniform rational B-spline

Non-uniform rational basis spline (NURBS) is a mathematical model using basis splines (B-splines) that is commonly used in computer graphics for representing curves and surfaces. It offers great flexibility and precision for handling both analytic shapes (such as conic sections) and modelled free-form shapes. NURBS are the standard representation for geometry in most CAD, CAM, and CAE systems, and form part of industry data-exchange standards such as IGES and STEP.

Definition

A NURBS curve is determined by its degree, a set of control points, a weight for each control point, and a knot vector. "Non-uniform" means the knots need not be equally spaced, allowing sharp corners and local control. "Rational" means each control point has a weight: increasing a weight pulls the curve toward that point, and the rational form makes it possible to represent circles, ellipses, and other conic sections exactly — something polynomial B-splines cannot do. A NURBS surface is the tensor product of two NURBS curves, controlled by a grid of control points.

Properties and uses

NURBS curves are invariant under affine and perspective transformations (transforming the control points transforms the curve), have local support (moving one control point affects only nearby portions), and generalise both Bézier curves and uniform B-splines, which are special cases. Because of these properties, NURBS underpin free-form modelling in automotive and aerospace design, animation (where they were popularised in the 1990s), architecture, and 3D printing workflows.
