Matrix similarity

In linear algebra, two n-by-n matrices A and B are called similar if there exists an invertible n-by-n matrix P such that B = P−1AP. Similar matrices represent the same linear transformation under two different bases, with P being the change of basis matrix.

Properties

Similarity is an equivalence relation on the space of square matrices. Similar matrices share many properties: they have the same rank, determinant, trace, characteristic polynomial, minimal polynomial, eigenvalues (with the same algebraic multiplicities), and Jordan normal form (over an algebraically closed field). However, two matrices with the same characteristic polynomial are not necessarily similar; the Jordan form or rational canonical form provides a complete invariant.

A matrix that is similar to a diagonal matrix is said to be diagonalizable. Similarity transformations preserve the structure of a linear map independent of basis, which is why problems in linear algebra are often reduced to choosing a basis in which the representing matrix has the simplest possible form (diagonal, Jordan, or rational canonical form).

Applications

Similarity appears in the analysis of linear dynamical systems and Markov chains, in numerical algorithms such as the QR algorithm (which applies sequences of similarity transformations to converge to triangular form), and throughout representation theory, where similarity of matrices corresponds to equivalence of representations in a fixed basis.
