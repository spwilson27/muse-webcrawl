William Hillebrand (November 13, 1821 – July 13, 1886) was a German physician and botanist who became one of the most important figures in the early scientific study of the flora of the Hawaiian Islands. He lived in Hawaii for about twenty years, serving as a royal physician and helping to found medical institutions in Honolulu, and his posthumously published Flora of the Hawaiian Islands (1888) remained the standard reference on Hawaiian plants for decades.

Early life and education

Hillebrand was born in Nieheim, in the Prussian province of Westphalia. He studied medicine at the universities of Göttingen and Berlin, receiving his medical degree in 1844. After practising medicine in Paderborn, he developed an interest in travel and natural history, and in 1849 he left Germany, visiting Australia and the Philippines before arriving in Hawaii in December 1850.

Career in Hawaii

In Honolulu, Hillebrand quickly became a prominent physician. He was appointed physician to King Kamehameha IV and Queen Emma, and was a driving force behind the founding of the Queen's Hospital (now The Queen's Medical Center), which opened in 1860. He also helped establish institutions for the care of people affected by leprosy, and in 1865 he travelled to Asia to study the disease and recruit staff.

Hillebrand is also known for introducing a number of plant species to Hawaii, including the flamboyant (royal poinciana) tree from Madagascar. His Honolulu garden became one of the finest private botanical collections in the islands; after his death his widow sold the property, and it eventually became the site of Foster Botanical Garden.

Botanical work

Although poor health forced Hillebrand to leave Hawaii in 1872, he continued working on Hawaiian botany in Germany. His Flora of the Hawaiian Islands: A Description of Their Phanerogams and Vascular Cryptogams was published in 1888, two years after his death in Heidelberg, edited by his son William Francis Hillebrand, a noted chemist. The flora described hundreds of species, many new to science, and it laid the foundation for later works such as Joseph Rock's studies of Hawaiian trees.

Legacy

Numerous Hawaiian plant species commemorate Hillebrand, including species in the endemic genera Hillebrandia (a genus of begonia relatives named for him) and various species with the epithet hillebrandii. The Hillebrand Prize of the Hawaiian Medical Association is also named in his honour.
