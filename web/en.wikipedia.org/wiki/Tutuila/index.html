Tutuila is the largest and principal island of American Samoa, a territory of the United States in the South Pacific. It contains the capital, Pago Pago, and the great majority of the territory's population. The island is of volcanic origin, rugged and mountainous, with a deeply indented coastline.

Geography

Tutuila is about 33 km long and up to 10 km wide. Pago Pago Harbor, one of the finest natural deepwater harbours in the South Pacific, nearly bisects the island's southern coast. Matafao Peak, its highest summit, rises to 653 metres. Part of the island is included in the National Park of American Samoa. The small island of Aunu'u lies just off the southeastern shore.

History

Tutuila was settled by Samoans more than two thousand years ago. European contact began in the 18th century. By the Treaty of Cession of Tutuila of 1900, Samoan chiefs ceded the island to the United States, which administered it through the Navy until 1951. During the Second World War the island was a significant US Marine Corps base. In 2009 a major earthquake and tsunami struck, causing deaths and extensive damage on Tutuila.

Economy

Tuna canning at Pago Pago has long been the economic mainstay, alongside government employment and subsistence agriculture of taro, breadfruit, bananas, and coconuts.
