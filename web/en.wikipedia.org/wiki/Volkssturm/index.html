The Volkssturm (literally "people's storm") was a German national militia established by Nazi Germany in the closing months of the Second World War. Created by a decree of Adolf Hitler dated 25 September 1944 and publicly announced on 18 October 1944, it conscripted all able-bodied men between the ages of 16 and 60 who were not already serving in the military.

Organisation

Unlike the Wehrmacht, the Volkssturm was controlled by the Nazi Party: it was organised by the Party Chancellery under Martin Bormann, with local Gauleiters responsible for raising units, and Heinrich Himmler overseeing military training and equipment. Members swore an oath of loyalty to Hitler personally. Training was minimal — often only a few hours — and uniforms typically consisted of civilian clothing with a printed armband reading "Deutscher Volkssturm Wehrmacht".

Equipment and deployment

The militia was chronically short of weapons. It was issued obsolete rifles, captured foreign weapons, and large numbers of the cheap, single-shot Panzerfaust anti-tank weapon, along with hastily manufactured Volksgewehr rifles. Volkssturm units fought in the defence of East Prussia, Silesia, and Pomerania against the Red Army, on the Rhine in the west, and, most famously, in the Battle of Berlin in April–May 1945, where thousands of the militia's old men and Hitler Youth boys were killed.

Outcome

The Volkssturm had little effect on the course of the war. Poorly trained and equipped, many units disintegrated or surrendered quickly, although some fought tenaciously. Its mobilisation is widely regarded as a symbol of the Nazi regime's willingness to sacrifice Germany's remaining civilian population in a hopeless cause. The militia ceased to exist with Germany's surrender in May 1945.
