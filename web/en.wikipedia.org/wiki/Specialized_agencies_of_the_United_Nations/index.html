Specialized agencies of the United Nations

The specialized agencies of the United Nations are autonomous organisations working with the United Nations and each other through the Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC), under Articles 57 and 63 of the UN Charter. There are currently 15 specialized agencies, each with its own membership, charter, budget and headquarters, covering areas from health and labour to finance and aviation.

List of agencies

  • FAO — Food and Agriculture Organization (Rome)
  • ICAO — International Civil Aviation Organization (Montreal)
  • IFAD — International Fund for Agricultural Development (Rome)
  • ILO — International Labour Organization (Geneva) — promotes decent work and tripartism
  • IMF — International Monetary Fund (Washington, D.C.)
  • IMO — International Maritime Organization (London)
  • ITU — International Telecommunication Union (Geneva)
  • UNESCO — Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (Paris)
  • UNIDO — Industrial Development Organization (Vienna)
  • UN Tourism (formerly UNWTO) (Madrid)
  • UPU — Universal Postal Union (Bern)
  • WHO — World Health Organization (Geneva)
  • WIPO — World Intellectual Property Organization (Geneva)
  • WMO — World Meteorological Organization (Geneva)
  • World Bank Group (Washington, D.C.)

History and coordination

Some agencies predate the UN (ILO 1919, UPU 1874, ITU 1865). The first Secretary-General, Trygve Lie, oversaw early coordination through what became the UN System Chief Executives Board. Agencies are distinct from UN programmes and funds such as UNICEF and UNDP, which are subsidiary organs of the General Assembly rather than independent organisations.
