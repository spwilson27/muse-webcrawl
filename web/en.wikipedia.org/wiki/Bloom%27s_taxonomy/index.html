Bloom's taxonomy
Published1956 (original); 2001 (revised)
Editor / authorsBenjamin Bloom (1956); Lorin Anderson & David Krathwohl (2001)
Levels (revised)Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create
FieldEducation

Bloom's taxonomy is a set of three hierarchical models used for the classification of educational learning objectives into levels of complexity and specificity. The three lists cover the cognitive, affective and psychomotor domains. The cognitive domain list has been the primary focus of most traditional education and is frequently used to structure curriculum learning objectives, assessments and activities.

The cognitive domain

The original 1956 taxonomy listed six categories: Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis and Evaluation. In the 2001 revision by Anderson and Krathwohl, these became verbs — Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create — with Create placed at the top. The categories are cumulative: mastery of a higher level is taken to presuppose the levels below it.

Use and criticism

Educators use the taxonomy to write measurable learning outcomes, align assessments with objectives, and prompt higher-order thinking. Critics note that learning does not always proceed neatly up a hierarchy and that the model's categories can overlap in practice.
