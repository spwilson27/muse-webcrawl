Sabre (Semi-Automated Business Research Environment) is a computer reservation system developed by American Airlines in partnership with IBM and launched in the early 1960s. It is regarded as one of the pioneering large-scale real-time transaction processing systems: it allowed airline agents to check seat availability and create bookings instantly, replacing manual card files and telephone coordination.

Development

The project grew out of a chance 1953 conversation between American Airlines president C. R. Smith and IBM salesman R. Blair Smith, and drew on IBM's experience with the SAGE air-defence system. Development began in the late 1950s; Sabre was running experimentally by 1960 and became fully operational in 1964, running on IBM mainframes and handling thousands of terminals. It is frequently cited alongside later systems as a landmark in commercial computing and in the history of online transaction processing.

Expansion

In 1976 Sabre terminals were placed in travel agencies, transforming the system into a global distribution system (GDS) through which agents could book many airlines, hotels and car rentals. The system also underpinned innovations such as yield management and frequent-flyer programme tracking (AAdvantage). American's parent AMR spun Sabre off in 2000; today Sabre Corporation, based in Southlake, Texas, provides reservation, distribution and airline IT services worldwide, competing with Amadeus and Travelport.
