Vasoconstriction

DefinitionNarrowing of blood vessels by contraction of vascular smooth muscle
Key mediatorsNorepinephrine, angiotensin II, endothelin, vasopressin
Opposite processVasodilation

Vasoconstriction is the narrowing of the blood vessels resulting from contraction of the muscular wall of the vessels, in particular the large arteries and small arterioles. The process is the opposite of vasodilation, the widening of blood vessels. It raises blood pressure and reduces blood flow to the tissues downstream.

Mechanism and control

Vascular smooth muscle contracts when intracellular calcium rises. Vasoconstriction is driven by the sympathetic nervous system (via α1-adrenergic receptors), circulating hormones such as angiotensin II, vasopressin (ADH), and adrenaline, and local mediators such as endothelin-1. It is a central part of homeostasis: in cold conditions, constriction of skin vessels conserves core heat; after blood loss, widespread vasoconstriction helps maintain pressure to vital organs.

Clinical significance

Excessive or inappropriate vasoconstriction contributes to hypertension, Raynaud phenomenon, and the coronary spasm of variant angina. Many drugs are vasoconstrictors — decongestants such as pseudoephedrine, triptans for migraine, and pressors used in shock — while others, including calcium-channel blockers and nitrates, relieve vasoconstriction.
