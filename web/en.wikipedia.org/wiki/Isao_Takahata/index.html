Isao Takahata (October 29, 1935 – April 5, 2018) was a Japanese director, screenwriter and producer. A co-founder of Studio Ghibli, he was best known for his animated features Grave of the Fireflies (1988), regarded as one of the greatest war films of all time, Only Yesterday (1991), Pom Poko (1994), My Neighbors the Yamadas (1999), and The Tale of the Princess Kaguya (2013), which was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

Early life and career

Takahata was born in Ujiyamada (now Ise), Mie Prefecture, and narrowly survived a United States air raid on Okayama in June 1945, an experience that deeply influenced Grave of the Fireflies. After graduating from the University of Tokyo in French literature, he joined Toei Animation in 1959. His directorial debut was The Great Adventure of Horus, Prince of the Sun (1968), on which Hayao Miyazaki also worked.

Television work

At Nippon Animation and other studios, Takahata directed landmarks of television anime including Heidi, Girl of the Alps (1974), Marco (1976) and Anne of Green Gables (1979), all part of the World Masterpiece Theater series, and contributed episodes to Lupin the Third. His meticulous, realistic approach to everyday life and landscape became a signature style.

Studio Ghibli

In 1985 Takahata co-founded Studio Ghibli with Hayao Miyazaki and producer Toshio Suzuki. Grave of the Fireflies (1988), released on a double bill with Miyazaki's My Neighbor Totoro, depicts two siblings struggling to survive in wartime Japan. He later directed the nostalgic drama Only Yesterday, the ecological fable Pom Poko and the watercolour-styled family comedy My Neighbors the Yamadas. His final film, The Tale of the Princess Kaguya (2013), drew on the oldest Japanese prose narrative and was acclaimed internationally.

Style and influence

Unlike most anime directors, Takahata did not draw professionally; he worked through storyboards, layouts and close direction of animators. His films are noted for realism, attention to daily life, and experimental visual techniques. He died of lung cancer in Tokyo on April 5, 2018, aged 82.
