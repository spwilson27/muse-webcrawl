International Judo Federation

The IJF governs judo worldwide and organises the World Judo Championships and the World Judo Tour.

The International Judo Federation (IJF) is the international governing body for the sport of judo. It was founded in July 1951 and is headquartered in Lausanne, Switzerland. The IJF has more than 200 member national federations organised in five continental unions, making judo one of the most widely practised martial arts in the world.

History

The IJF was established in 1951 by members of the European Judo Union together with Argentina; its first president was the Italian Aldo Torti, succeeded in 1952 by Risei Kano, son of judo's founder Jigoro Kano. The first World Judo Championships were held in Tokyo in 1956. Judo became an Olympic sport for men at the 1964 Tokyo Games and for women at Barcelona 1992. The IJF presidency was held by Marius Vizer from 2007; under his tenure the federation launched the World Judo Tour and a global ranking system, and its headquarters moved from Budapest to Lausanne.

Competitions and rules

The IJF organises the World Judo Championships (held annually except in Olympic years), the World Masters, Grand Slam and Grand Prix events, and junior and veterans' championships. It sets the international contest rules, weight categories (seven for men and seven for women), and the refereeing standards used at Olympic judo. IJF world ranking points determine qualification for the Olympic Games.
