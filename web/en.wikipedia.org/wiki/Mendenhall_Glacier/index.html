Mendenhall Glacier

The Mendenhall Glacier is a glacier about 13.6 miles (21.9 km) long located in Mendenhall Valley, about 12 miles (19 km) from downtown Juneau, the capital of the U.S. state of Alaska. The glacier and surrounding landscape is protected as the Mendenhall Glacier Recreation Area, a federally designated unit of the Tongass National Forest.

History

The glacier was named Auke Glacier by naturalist John Muir for the Auke Tlingit people. In 1892 it was renamed in honor of Thomas Corwin Mendenhall, the physicist and superintendent of the U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey, who was responsible for defining the exact border between Canada and Alaska. At its maximum extent during the Little Ice Age, around the mid-1700s, the glacier's terminus was about 2.5 miles (4 km) downvalley from its present position; it has retreated steadily since, and retreat accelerated during the 20th and 21st centuries.

Features

The glacier flows from the Juneau Icefield into Mendenhall Lake, which formed as the glacier receded. Melting has exposed ice caves beneath the glacier that became a popular (though dangerous and now largely inaccessible) attraction. Since 2011, the melting glacier has caused periodic glacial lake outburst floods, known locally as "suicide basin" floods, from an ice-dammed side basin; major flood events in recent years have damaged homes along the Mendenhall River. The Mendenhall Glacier Visitor Center, opened in 1962, was the first U.S. Forest Service visitor center in the nation and receives hundreds of thousands of visitors per year.
