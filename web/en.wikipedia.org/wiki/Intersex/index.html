Intersex people are individuals born with sex characteristics — including chromosome patterns, gonads or genitals — that do not fit typical binary notions of male or female bodies. Intersex is an umbrella term for a wide range of congenital conditions, in medicine often grouped as differences (or disorders) of sex development (DSD). Being intersex is primarily about innate physical sex characteristics; it is distinct from gender identity and sexual orientation, though intersex people may identify with any of them.

Conditions

Conditions commonly included are congenital adrenal hyperplasia (the most common cause of atypical genitalia in XX infants), androgen insensitivity syndrome (complete and partial), Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY), Turner syndrome (45,X), ovotesticular conditions, 5-alpha-reductase 2 deficiency, hypospadias, and sex chromosome variations such as XO/XY mosaicism. Some variations are obvious at birth; others appear at puberty, during infertility investigations, or never.

Frequency

Estimates depend sharply on which conditions are counted. Biologist Anne Fausto-Sterling's widely cited figure of about 1.7% of births includes conditions such as late-onset congenital adrenal hyperplasia and Klinefelter syndrome; narrower definitions counting only ambiguous genitalia give figures around 0.02–0.05%. The definitional debate is itself a subject of scholarly and activist contention.

Medicine and human rights

From the 1950s, the standard "optimal gender" model associated with John Money promoted early "normalising" genital surgery on infants. Long-term follow-up studies and intersex advocacy from the 1990s — beginning with the Intersex Society of North America (founded 1993 by Cheryl Chase) — documented harms including loss of sexual sensation, infertility, wrong sex assignments and psychological trauma, and challenged non-consensual interventions. Since the 2010s, UN bodies, the Council of Europe and human-rights organisations have characterised medically unnecessary surgery on intersex children as a rights violation; Malta's 2015 Gender Identity, Gender Expression and Sex Characteristics Act was the first national law prohibiting such interventions on minors, followed by measures in countries including Portugal, Germany and several Australian and US jurisdictions. The 2006 Chicago consensus introduced the DSD terminology, which remains contested between clinicians, patients and advocates.

Society and law

Some jurisdictions allow non-binary sex markers on documents that also serve intersex people. Intersex status has figured in sports governance, notably sex-verification rules in athletics and cases such as that of runner Caster Semenya, leading to ongoing litigation over testosterone regulations. Intersex Awareness Day is marked on 26 October, commemorating a 1996 demonstration in Boston; Intersex Day of Solidarity falls on 8 November.
