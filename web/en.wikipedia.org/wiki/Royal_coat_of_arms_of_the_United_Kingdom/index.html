The royal coat of arms of the United Kingdom is the arms of dominion of the British monarch, used as the official coat of arms of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. Variants are used by the government, Parliament, courts and on British passports and coinage.

Description

The shield is quarterly: the first and fourth quarters bear the three lions passant guardant of England; the second, the rampant lion within a double tressure of Scotland; the third, the harp of Ireland. The shield is encircled by the garter of the Order of the Garter bearing the motto Honi soit qui mal y pense, and is supported by a crowned lion (for England) on the dexter side and a chained unicorn (for Scotland) on the sinister — the unicorn chained because in heraldic tradition a free unicorn was considered dangerous. Above is the royal helm and crest, a lion statant guardant wearing the crown. Below, the motto scroll reads Dieu et mon droit ("God and my right"), flanked by the Tudor rose, shamrock and thistle.

Scottish version

In Scotland a distinct version is used: the Scottish quarter takes precedence (first and fourth), the supporters swap order with the unicorn on the dexter side, and the Order of the Thistle replaces the Garter, with the motto Nemo me impune lacessit. The arms in their present standard form date from the accession of Queen Victoria in 1837, when the Hanoverian inescutcheon was dropped.
