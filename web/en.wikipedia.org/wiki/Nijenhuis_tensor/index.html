Nijenhuis tensor

In differential geometry, the Nijenhuis tensor is a tensor field associated with an almost complex structure J (an endomorphism of the tangent bundle with J² = −1). Introduced by Albert Nijenhuis in the early 1950s, it measures the failure of J to be integrable: an almost complex structure comes from a genuine complex structure on the manifold if and only if the Nijenhuis tensor vanishes identically.

Definition

For vector fields X and Y, the tensor is defined by N(X, Y) = [JX, JY] − J[JX, Y] − J[X, JY] − [X, Y], where brackets denote the Lie bracket of vector fields. The Newlander–Nirenberg theorem states that vanishing of N is exactly the condition for local holomorphic coordinates to exist.

Applications

Nijenhuis tensors also appear in the theory of Poisson and bi-Hamiltonian systems, where a compatible pair of Poisson structures generates a Nijenhuis operator central to the study of integrable systems.
