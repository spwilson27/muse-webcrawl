Olympias is a reconstruction of an ancient Athenian trireme, built in Greece between 1985 and 1987 by the Trireme Trust, founded by historian John Sinclair Morrison, naval architect John F. Coates and writer Frank Welsh, and funded largely by banker Frank Welsh and the Hellenic Navy. She was commissioned into the Hellenic Navy as a commissioned vessel and is the only reconstructed trireme of her type.

Construction

The ship was built using the shell-first, mortise-and-tenon "sewn" construction described in ancient sources, with a hypozomata tensioning cable running the length of the hull. Her design drew on literary evidence, the Lenormant relief and the remains of the Zea ship sheds at Piraeus. She is 36.9 m long and carries 170 oars in three files, replicating the thranite, zygian and thalamian arrangement.

Sea trials

Trials between 1987 and 1994 with volunteer crews demonstrated that a trireme could reach about 9 knots in short bursts and cruise at 4 knots, sustain turns, and accelerate rapidly, confirming ancient accounts of trireme performance that had previously been doubted. The trials transformed scholarly understanding of ancient naval warfare.

Later use

Olympias carried the Olympic flame into Piraeus for the Athens 2004 Games. She is now preserved as a dry-dock exhibit at the Naval Tradition Park at Palaio Faliro, Athens.
