Circadian rhythm sleep disorder

Circadian rhythm sleep disorders (CRSD), also known as circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders (CRSWD), are a family of sleep disorders that affect the timing of sleep. People with circadian rhythm sleep disorders are unable to sleep and wake at the times required for normal work, school, and social needs. They are generally able to get enough sleep if allowed to sleep and wake at the times dictated by their internal body clocks.

The human circadian cycle governs the timing of sleep and wakefulness over roughly 24 hours.

Types

The principal intrinsic disorders include delayed sleep phase disorder (sleep onset and waking persistently late), advanced sleep phase disorder (sleep onset and waking persistently early), non-24-hour sleep-wake disorder — common in totally blind people, whose free-running clock cycles longer than 24 hours — and irregular sleep-wake rhythm. Extrinsic disorders include jet lag and shift work sleep disorder, caused by misalignment between the internal clock and the environment.

Causes and treatment

The disorders arise when the endogenous circadian pacemaker in the suprachiasmatic nucleus is misaligned with the light-dark cycle or social schedule, sometimes due to genetic variants in clock genes. Diagnosis uses sleep diaries and actigraphy. Treatment combines timed bright-light exposure, exogenous melatonin, structured sleep-wake schedules, and in some cases chronotherapy or medication such as tasimelteon for non-24-hour disorder.
