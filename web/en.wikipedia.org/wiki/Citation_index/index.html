Citation index

A citation index is a kind of bibliographic index, an index of citations between publications, allowing the user to easily establish which later documents cite which earlier documents. A form of citation index is first found in 19th-century legal literature with citators such as Shepard's Citations. The first modern citation indexes for scientific literature were developed by Eugene Garfield and the Institute for Scientific Information (ISI) in the 1960s.

History

Garfield's Science Citation Index (SCI), first published in 1964, was followed by the Social Sciences Citation Index and the Arts and Humanities Citation Index. These were later incorporated into the Web of Science database. Major competing services include Scopus, launched by Elsevier in 2004, and Google Scholar, which offers free citation searching.

Uses

Citation indexes support literature searching by following chains of references forward and backward in time, and they provide the raw data for bibliometric indicators such as citation counts, the impact factor, and the h-index. Critics note that citation counts can be distorted by self-citation, disciplinary differences, and gaming, and should not be the sole measure of research quality.
