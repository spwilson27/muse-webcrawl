Stock route

A stock route, also known as a travelling stock route (TSR), is a road or track used for moving livestock ("stock") on foot, typically by drovers, from place to place. Stock routes are a feature of several countries with large pastoral industries, notably Australia.

Australian stock routes developed from the tracks blazed by early explorers and drovers in the 19th century, linking waterholes and grazing land across the interior. They form a network of corridors of public land that connect properties, towns and railheads, with reserves at intervals for overnight camping, grazing and watering. Famous routes include the Canning Stock Route in Western Australia — one of the longest and most remote stock routes in the world at about 1,850 km — the Birdsville Track, the Strzelecki Track, the Tanami Track and the Murranji Track.

Although motor transport has largely replaced droving, stock routes remain in use in drought years, when the grazing along the "long paddock" is valuable, and they are also valued today for biodiversity conservation, Indigenous heritage and tourism.
