Stilt

Stilts are poles or pillars that provide a platform above ground level, used either to raise a structure, as in stilt houses, or to elevate a person who walks upon them. This article concerns stilts for walking.

Types

Hand-held (peg) stilts have foot platforms partway up the pole and straps or hand-grips above; the walker holds the poles and lifts them with each step. Strap-on stilts are fastened to the legs, freeing the hands; spring stilts ("power bocks" or jumping stilts) use leaf springs to store energy and allow running and leaping. Drywall stilts, used by construction workers to reach ceilings while plastering, are a practical strap-on form sold under names such as Dura-Stilts.

Uses

Stilt walking has practical origins: shepherds in the Landes region of south-western France famously watched their flocks from tall échasses, crossing marshy ground and covering long strides; the town of Namur in Belgium has held jousts on stilts since the fifteenth century. Elsewhere stilts were used for fording rivers, fruit picking, hop-stringing in Kentish gardens, and crossing flooded streets in Venice. Today stilt walkers are fixtures of parades, circuses and street festivals worldwide, and stilt dances survive in folk traditions from West Africa to South America.

Stilt structures

Beyond walking, the word denotes any supporting post that raises a building above ground or water — pile dwellings, stilt houses on floodplains and coasts from Southeast Asia to the Amazon, and the stilted legs of water towers and some machinery.
