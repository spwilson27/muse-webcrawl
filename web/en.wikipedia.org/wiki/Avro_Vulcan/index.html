The Avro Vulcan (later Hawker Siddeley Vulcan) is a jet-powered, tailless, delta-wing, high-altitude, strategic bomber, which was operated by the Royal Air Force (RAF) from 1956 until 1984. Aircraft manufacturer A.V. Roe and Company (Avro) designed the Vulcan in response to Specification B.35/46. Of the three V bombers produced to equip the RAF's nuclear deterrent force, the Vulcan was the most technically advanced.

A Vulcan B.2 in flight; the type was famous for its delta wing and howling engines.

Service

Vulcans formed the backbone of Britain's airborne nuclear deterrent during much of the Cold War, initially carrying free-fall bombs such as Blue Danube and Yellow Sun at high altitude, then switching to low-level penetration with the WE.177 and the Blue Steel stand-off missile. The Vulcan's only combat use came in the 1982 Falklands War, when Vulcans flew the Black Buck raids from Ascension Island against Port Stanley — at the time the longest bombing missions in history. A few aircraft were converted to tankers (K.2) before final retirement in 1984.

The last airworthy Vulcan, XH558, flew in civilian display hands until 2015 and became one of Britain's most popular airshow aircraft.
