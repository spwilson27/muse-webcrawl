Bettis Atomic Power Laboratory is a U.S. Government-owned research and development laboratory in West Mifflin, Pennsylvania, near Pittsburgh, dedicated exclusively to the design and development of nuclear propulsion for the United States Navy. It is operated under contract by Bechtel Marine Propulsion Corporation for the Naval Reactors program.

History

The laboratory was established in 1949 on the site of the former Bettis Field airfield, with Westinghouse Electric Corporation as its first contractor, to develop the pressurized water reactor plant for the world's first nuclear-powered submarine, USS Nautilus (SSN-571), launched in 1954. Bettis subsequently designed the reactor plants for generations of U.S. submarines and surface ships, including the Shippingport Atomic Power Station (1957), the first full-scale atomic electric power plant in the United States devoted exclusively to peacetime uses.

Work

Together with its sister site, the Knolls Atomic Power Laboratory in New York, Bettis develops advanced reactor designs, materials, and fuel systems and supports the operation and refuelling of the Navy's nuclear fleet. The laboratory's work is largely classified.
