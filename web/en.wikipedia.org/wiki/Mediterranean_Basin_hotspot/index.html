Mediterranean Basin biodiversity hotspot

The Mediterranean Basin is the second-largest of the world's biodiversity hotspots and the largest of the world's five Mediterranean-climate regions. It stretches across more than two million square kilometres, from Portugal and the Cape Verde islands in the west to Jordan and Turkey in the east, and from Italy in the north to Morocco and Tunisia in the south, encompassing the lands around the Mediterranean Sea.

Biodiversity

The hotspot harbors roughly 22,500 vascular plant species, of which about 11,700 (52 percent) are endemic — a level of plant endemism among the highest in the world, driven by its complex topography, islands, and climate history. Its fauna includes endemic amphibians, reptiles such as tortoises and chameleons, and mammals including the Barbary macaque and the critically endangered Mediterranean monk seal.

Threats

The region has been inhabited and transformed by agriculture, grazing, and urbanization for millennia; only a small fraction of its original vegetation remains intact. Current threats include coastal tourism development, water abstraction, overgrazing, wildfire, invasive species, and climate change. Conservation is coordinated in part through the Critical Ecosystem Partnership Fund, which has invested in civil-society-led conservation across the hotspot since 2012.
