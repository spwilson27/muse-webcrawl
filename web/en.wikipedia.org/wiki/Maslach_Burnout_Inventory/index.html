Maslach Burnout Inventory

The Maslach Burnout Inventory (MBI) is a psychological assessment instrument used to measure occupational burnout. It was developed by Christina Maslach and Susan E. Jackson and first published in 1981. The MBI is the most widely used measure of burnout in research and practice and is a proprietary, licensed instrument published by Mind Garden, Inc.

Structure

The inventory consists of statements about job-related feelings that respondents rate according to how often they experience them, typically on a scale from "never" to "every day". The items load onto three subscales corresponding to Maslach's three-dimensional model of burnout:

  • Emotional exhaustion — feeling emotionally overextended and depleted by one's work.
  • Depersonalization (or cynicism) — a detached, callous, or negative response toward the people with whom one works.
  • Personal accomplishment (or professional efficacy) — feelings of competence and successful achievement in one's work; low scores indicate burnout.

Versions

Several versions exist, including the MBI–Human Services Survey (MBI-HSS) for health and social-service workers, the MBI–Educators Survey (MBI-ES), and the MBI–General Survey (MBI-GS), which adapts the instrument to occupations outside the helping professions and uses the labels exhaustion, cynicism, and professional efficacy.

Criticism

The MBI has been criticised for its proprietary cost, for questions about whether burnout should be treated as three separate dimensions rather than a single syndrome, and for the fact that it was designed as a research tool rather than a diagnostic test for individuals — Maslach herself has cautioned against using cut-off scores to diagnose burnout in individual employees.
