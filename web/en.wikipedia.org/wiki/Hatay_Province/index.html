Hatay Province

Hatay Province is the southernmost province of Turkey, on the eastern shore of the Mediterranean Sea, bordering Syria to the south and east. Its capital is Antakya — ancient Antioch — and its largest city and port is İskenderun. The province is known for its religious and ethnic diversity, its cuisine, and its contested twentieth-century history.

History

After the First World War the Sanjak of Alexandretta was administered as part of the French Mandate for Syria. Under a League of Nations statute it became the autonomous Hatay State in 1938, and in 1939, following a referendum and negotiations between France and Turkey, it was annexed by Turkey — a move Syria never formally recognised, and the territory long appeared on Syrian maps as Syrian land.

Geography and economy

The province surrounds the eastern end of the Gulf of İskenderun. The Amik plain is an important agricultural area producing cotton, grains and citrus fruit, while İskenderun hosts one of Turkey's largest ports and major steel plants. The Nur (Amanos) Mountains separate the coast from the interior.

2023 earthquakes

Hatay was among the provinces devastated by the earthquakes of 6 February 2023. Antakya in particular suffered catastrophic destruction, with much of its historic centre destroyed and very heavy loss of life, prompting a vast national and international relief and reconstruction effort.
