Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey Circus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey Circus was an American traveling circus billed as The Greatest Show on Earth. It was created in 1919 by the merger of the Ringling Brothers' circus (founded 1884 in Baraboo, Wisconsin) with P. T. Barnum and James Anthony Bailey's Barnum & Bailey Circus. For most of the 20th century it was the dominant big-top circus in the United States, famous for its three rings, spectacle acts, menagerie and mile-long circus trains.

History

The five Ringling brothers began their circus in 1884; by 1907 they had also acquired the Barnum & Bailey show, operating the two separately until merging them in 1919. The combined show wintered in Sarasota, Florida, which became closely identified with the circus. It survived the Depression under John Ringling North and passed through corporate ownership, being bought by the Feld family in 1967.

Amid changing economics and controversy over animal acts, the circus retired its elephants in 2016 and Feld Entertainment announced the show's closure in 2017; the final performance took place on 21 May 2017 at Nassau Veterans Memorial Coliseum in New York. In 2023 the circus was relaunched as an animal-free, reimagined production.

The show

At its height the Greatest Show on Earth employed hundreds of performers and animals, presented aerialists, clowns, jugglers, animal trainers and novelty acts in three rings under an enormous big top (later in arenas), and travelled in two unit trains — the Red and Blue Units — each touring its own route.

Legacy

The circus shaped American popular entertainment and vocabulary ("bread and circuses", "the greatest show on Earth"), and its history is preserved at the Ringling Museum complex in Sarasota, including the Ca' d'Zan mansion and the Circus Museum.
