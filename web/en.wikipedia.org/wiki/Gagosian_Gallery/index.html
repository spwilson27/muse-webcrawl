Gagosian Gallery

Gagosian is a contemporary art gallery empire founded and owned by Larry Gagosian. From its beginnings in Los Angeles in the late 1970s it has grown into the largest gallery enterprise in the world, operating some nineteen exhibition spaces across the United States, Europe and Asia — including multiple locations in New York, London and Paris, as well as Beverly Hills, Rome, Athens, Geneva, Basel and Hong Kong — and representing many of the most commercially successful living artists and estates.

History

Larry Gagosian began by selling posters near UCLA before opening a gallery in Los Angeles in 1980 and expanding to New York in the mid-1980s, in partnership at times with dealer Leo Castelli. His Chelsea spaces, opened from 1999, set new standards of scale for commercial galleries, mounting museum-sized exhibitions of artists including Richard Serra and Damien Hirst. Gagosian became the archetype of the "mega-gallery", with a global footprint mirroring the geography of art fairs and billionaire collectors.

Role in the art market

The gallery handles both the primary market for living artists and secondary-market dealing in blue-chip modern and contemporary works, and is known for organising large historical loan exhibitions with scholarly catalogues. Its dominance — along with rivals such as Hauser & Wirth, Pace and David Zwirner — has prompted debate about the concentration of power in the commercial art world and the blurring line between gallery and museum.
