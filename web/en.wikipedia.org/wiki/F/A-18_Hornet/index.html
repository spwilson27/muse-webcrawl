McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet

The McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet is an all-weather supersonic, twin-engine, carrier-capable, multirole combat aircraft, designed as both a fighter and attack aircraft (hence the F/A designation). Designed by McDonnell Douglas and Northrop, the F/A-18 was derived from Northrop's YF-17 that lost the USAF Lightweight Fighter program to the F-16. The United States Navy and Marine Corps use the aircraft as a carrier-based strike fighter; the type has also been exported to several nations, including Australia, Canada, Finland, Kuwait, Malaysia, Spain and Switzerland.

Development and service

The Hornet first flew on 18 November 1978 and entered fleet service in 1983, replacing the A-7 Corsair II and the F-4 Phantom II. It was the first tactical aircraft designed from the outset for both fighter and attack missions, and it pioneered features such as hands-on-throttle-and-stick controls, a digital fly-by-wire system, and strakes (leading-edge extensions) giving exceptional high angle-of-attack performance. Hornets saw extensive combat from the 1986 strikes on Libya and the 1991 Gulf War through operations in Iraq, Afghanistan and Syria.

Variants and successors

The original single-seat F/A-18A and two-seat F/A-18B were followed by the improved F/A-18C/D with upgraded radar, night-attack capability and AMRAAM integration. A larger redesign, the Boeing F/A-18E/F Super Hornet, entered service in 1999 and progressively replaced the "legacy" Hornets in U.S. Navy service; the U.S. Navy retired its last legacy Hornets from fleet squadrons in 2019, while the Marine Corps continued operating upgraded F/A-18C/Ds into the 2020s pending replacement by the F-35. The Blue Angels flight demonstration squadron flew the Hornet from 1986 until transitioning to the Super Hornet in 2021.

Specifications

The F/A-18C is powered by two General Electric F404-GE-402 turbofans producing 17,700 lbf each with afterburner, giving a maximum speed of Mach 1.8, a combat range over 500 nautical miles, and a service ceiling of 50,000 ft. Armament includes a 20 mm M61 Vulcan cannon and up to 13,700 lb of stores on nine hardpoints.
