Fair Park

The Esplanade and Hall of State at Fair Park

Fair Park is a 277-acre (112 ha) recreational and educational complex in Dallas, Texas, United States, located just east of downtown. It is best known as the home of the State Fair of Texas, held there annually since 1886. Fair Park contains one of the largest collections of Art Deco exhibition architecture in the United States and is a designated National Historic Landmark.

History

The site was established in 1886 as the grounds of the Dallas State Fair. In 1904 the City of Dallas purchased the grounds as a park. For the Texas Centennial Exposition of 1936 the park was comprehensively rebuilt in Art Deco style under architect George Dahl, producing the ensemble of halls, murals and sculpture — including the Hall of State — that survives today.

Attractions

Fair Park includes the Cotton Bowl stadium, the Music Hall at Fair Park, the African American Museum, the Texas Discovery Gardens, the Children's Aquarium and a number of museums and performance venues. The 212-foot (65 m) Texas Star Ferris wheel, built for the 1985 fair, is among the tallest in North America. During the State Fair the park hosts "Big Tex", the fair's 55-foot cowboy mascot, along with the Red River Showdown football game between Texas and Oklahoma at the Cotton Bowl.
