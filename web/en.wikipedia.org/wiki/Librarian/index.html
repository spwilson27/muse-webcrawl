Librarian
Occupation type: Profession
Fields: Libraries, archives, information science
Education: Often MLIS or equivalent

A librarian is a person who works professionally in a library, providing access to information and sometimes social or technical programming to users. In addition, librarians provide instruction on information literacy to users as part of their education packages.

Roles and responsibilities

The role of a librarian has evolved over time, particularly in the past century, with much media now concentrated in digital form. Librarians select, acquire, catalogue and classify materials, manage collections and budgets, answer reference enquiries, run reader development and literacy programmes, and curate digital resources and databases. Specialisations include public, academic, school and special librarianship, as well as archivists, systems librarians, cataloguers and children's librarians.

Education and professional bodies

In many countries, professional librarian posts require a master's degree in library and information science (MLIS) or an accredited equivalent. Professional associations such as the American Library Association (ALA), founded in 1876, and CILIP in the United Kingdom accredit courses and advocate for libraries and intellectual freedom.

History

Librarianship dates to the ancient world — the Library of Ashurbanipal and the Great Library of Alexandria had scholarly keepers of collections — and developed through monastic, university and royal libraries before the public library movement of the 19th century professionalised the role, with figures such as Melvil Dewey reshaping training and classification.
