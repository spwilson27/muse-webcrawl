Vizier

A vizier (Arabic: وزير, wazīr) is a high-ranking political advisor or minister in the lands of the Islamic world. Historically, the vizier was the chief minister of a caliph, sultan or other monarch, often functioning as the effective head of government. The institution appears in the Abbasid Caliphate, in later Islamic empires such as the Ottoman and Safavid states, and in the administration of ancient Egypt, where the pharaoh's chief official bore a title conventionally translated as vizier.

Etymology

The Arabic word wazīr derives from the root w-z-r, meaning "to bear a burden", so that the vizier is the one who carries the burdens of rule for the sovereign. The term entered European languages via Turkish vezir and French vizir. The rendering of the ancient Egyptian title tjaty as "vizier" is a modern convention based on the similar functions of the office.

Islamic history

Under the Abbasid caliphs, the vizierate developed into the central office of the bureaucracy; the Barmakid family under Harun al-Rashid made the post famous, and later viziers such as Nizam al-Mulk, vizier of the Seljuk sultans and author of the Siyasatnama ("Book of Government"), exemplified the vizier as statesman-administrator. In the Ottoman Empire, the Grand Vizier (sadrazam) was the sultan's absolute deputy, holder of the imperial seal and head of the Imperial Council; the office lasted until 1922. Figures such as Sokollu Mehmed Pasha and the Köprülü family wielded near-royal power.

Ancient Egypt

In ancient Egypt, the vizier (tjaty) was the highest official under the pharaoh, overseeing the administration, judiciary, treasury, and royal works. From the New Kingdom there were usually two viziers, for Upper and Lower Egypt. The "Duties of the Vizier", recorded in the tomb of Rekhmire, describes the office in detail.

Modern usage

In several modern states the word survives: in Pakistan and parts of South Asia, ministers may be styled wazir, and the prime minister is the wazir-e-azam ("grand vizier"). The term is also familiar from literature and games, from the scheming viziers of the Arabian Nights to chess, in which the queen's predecessor in shatranj was the vizier, or counsellor.
