Max Q

In aerospace engineering, Max Q is the point of maximum dynamic pressure during a rocket's ascent through the atmosphere — the moment at which the vehicle experiences its greatest aerodynamic stress. Dynamic pressure q is given by q = ½ρv², where ρ is air density and v is the vehicle's velocity.

Explanation

As a launch vehicle climbs, its speed increases while the surrounding air density decreases. Dynamic pressure first rises as velocity grows faster than density falls, reaches a peak (max Q), and then declines toward zero as the vehicle leaves the atmosphere. For the Space Shuttle, max Q occurred roughly a minute after launch at an altitude of about 11 km, with a dynamic pressure around 33–35 kPa. Rockets are often throttled down shortly before max Q to reduce structural loads, then throttled back up afterward — a "thrust bucket" profile.

Significance

Max Q is a critical design constraint: the vehicle's structure, aerodynamic surfaces, and abort criteria must all withstand the peak loading. Launch commentary traditionally announces "the vehicle is going through max Q" as a milestone of ascent. For crewed vehicles, exceeding design dynamic pressure can be catastrophic, making max Q one of the most closely monitored phases of flight.
