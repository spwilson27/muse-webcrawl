Touch (sport)

Touch, also known as touch football or touch rugby, is a limited-contact team sport derived from rugby league, in which players tackle opponents by touching them with one hand rather than bringing them to the ground. It is played in more than fifty countries and governed internationally by the Federation of International Touch (FIT).

Rules

Teams of six players attack by running and passing the ball backwards; a "touch" on the ball carrier counts as a tackle. After a touch, play restarts with a "rollball" — the tackled player places the ball on the ground and steps over it while a teammate (the dummy half) picks it up. The attacking side has six touches to score a touchdown by grounding the ball over the scoreline; possession then changes. There is no kicking in general play except at the start. Matches are usually 40 minutes, and mixed-gender competition is a prominent feature of the sport.

History

Touch emerged in Australia in the 1960s as a training method for rugby league and a social game, with the first formal competitions in Sydney in 1968. It is now particularly popular in Australia and New Zealand and is played widely in schools in the United Kingdom, South Africa, and Asia.
