Galactorrhea (also spelled galactorrhoea) is the spontaneous flow of milk from the breast, unassociated with childbirth or nursing. It can occur in both women and men and is usually caused by elevated levels of the hormone prolactin (hyperprolactinaemia), which stimulates milk production. Galactorrhea is a symptom rather than a disease in itself.

Causes

The most common pathological cause is a prolactinoma, a benign prolactin-secreting tumour of the pituitary gland. Other causes include medications that block dopamine — notably antipsychotics, some antidepressants, metoclopramide and certain antihypertensives — primary hypothyroidism (in which raised TRH stimulates prolactin release), chronic kidney disease, chest-wall stimulation or injury, and, rarely, other pituitary or hypothalamic lesions. In a proportion of cases no cause is found (idiopathic galactorrhea).

Diagnosis and treatment

Evaluation includes a medication review, measurement of serum prolactin and thyroid function, pregnancy testing, and, when prolactin is markedly elevated or no cause is apparent, MRI of the pituitary. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause: offending drugs are withdrawn where possible, hypothyroidism is treated, and prolactinomas usually shrink with dopamine agonist drugs such as cabergoline or bromocriptine; surgery is reserved for resistant or very large tumours.
