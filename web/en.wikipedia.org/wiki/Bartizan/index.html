Bartizan

A bartizan (also spelled bartisan) is an overhanging turret projecting from the walls or towers of a fortification or castle. Bartizans generally arise from a corner and are supported on corbels, allowing a sentry or defender to observe and fire along the face of the wall while remaining under cover.

Origins and use

The feature developed in medieval military architecture and became common on late medieval and early modern castles and fortified houses, especially in Scotland, France, and Spain. Unlike a full turret rising from the ground, a bartizan is carried on corbelling at wall-head level, often at the corners of a tower house, and frequently capped with a conical or pyramidal roof. Openings in the floor (machicolations) could allow missiles to be dropped on attackers below.

Later use

With the decline of fortification, bartizans survived as decorative elements in Scottish baronial and other revival styles, where they ornament the corners of Victorian country houses and civic buildings. In modern usage the word is also applied to small projecting watch turrets on prisons and secure buildings.
