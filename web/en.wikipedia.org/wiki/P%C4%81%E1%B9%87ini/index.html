Pāṇini (Devanagari: पाणिनि) was an ancient Indian grammarian and scholar who composed the Aṣṭādhyāyī ("Eight Chapters"), a comprehensive and scientific grammar of Sanskrit. Probably living between the 6th and 4th centuries BCE in the Gandhara region, he is widely regarded as the founder of linguistics, and his work is often called the earliest surviving complete generative grammar of any language.

The Aṣṭādhyāyī

The grammar consists of nearly 4,000 aphoristic rules (sūtras) that describe the morphology, phonetics and syntax of Sanskrit with extraordinary economy. Using auxiliary markers, ordered rule application and technical conventions, Pāṇini's system generates all and only the correct forms of the language — an achievement that modern scholars have compared to a formal algorithm or computer program. His analysis of compound formation, case relations (kāraka) and phonetic classes, organized in the Śiva-sūtras, influenced grammar-writing for millennia.

Legacy

Kātyāyana and Patañjali expanded Pāṇini's work in the centuries after him, and the grammatical tradition he founded preserved Sanskrit in a standardized form. When European scholars encountered the text in the 19th century, it helped launch comparative and structural linguistics; in the 20th, figures such as Leonard Bloomfield praised it as "one of the greatest monuments of human intelligence," and its formal devices anticipate concepts in modern computation and generative grammar.
