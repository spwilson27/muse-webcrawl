Spectral sequence

The second page of a spectral sequence, with differentials d2 drawn between entries.

In homological algebra and algebraic topology, a spectral sequence is a technique used in computing (co)homology groups by approximating them through successive stages, called pages, each obtained from the previous one by taking homology with respect to a differential. Spectral sequences were introduced by Jean Leray in the 1940s, initially while a prisoner of war, and were refined by many others into one of the central computational tools of modern algebraic topology.

Informal description

A spectral sequence is a collection of pages Er, each consisting of objects arranged in a grid, together with differentials dr of some fixed bidegree on each page. The next page is the homology of the current one: Er+1 = H(Er, dr). In many examples the pages eventually stabilize: for large r the differentials vanish and the sequence is said to converge to the (co)homology of some filtered object, with the limit page giving the associated graded groups. Extension problems — reassembling the desired group from its graded pieces — often remain.

Examples

Leray–Serre spectral sequence

For a fibration F → E → B, the Serre spectral sequence relates the homology of the total space E to that of the base B and fibre F, with E2 page Hp(B; Hq(F)). It was used by Serre in his thesis to compute homology groups of spheres and underlies much of the subsequent development of homotopy theory.

Grothendieck spectral sequence

In homological algebra, the Grothendieck spectral sequence composes two derived functors, expressing the derived functors of the composition in terms of those of the parts. Important special cases include the local-to-global Ext spectral sequence and the Leray spectral sequence for sheaf cohomology.

Others

Other widely used examples are the Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence for generalized (co)homology theories, the Adams spectral sequence for stable homotopy groups of spheres, the Bockstein spectral sequence relating mod-p and integral homology, and the Frölicher spectral sequence in complex geometry.

History

Jean Leray introduced the sheaf-theoretic ideas leading to spectral sequences in 1945. Roger Godement and Henri Cartan gave early systematic treatments; the theory of exact couples, due to William Massey, supplied a clean algebraic framework. Alexander Grothendieck's 1957 Tôhoku paper recast homological algebra in abelian categories and made spectral sequences standard in algebraic geometry.
