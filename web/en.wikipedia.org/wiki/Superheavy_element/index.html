Superheavy element

Superheavy elements occupy the heaviest positions in the periodic table.

Superheavy elements, also known as transactinide elements, are the chemical elements with atomic numbers greater than 103. These elements are all synthetic and highly radioactive, with the known isotopes having extremely short half-lives measured in milliseconds to minutes. They are created in laboratories by bombarding heavy target nuclei with lighter ions in particle accelerators.

Known elements

Elements 104 (rutherfordium) through 118 (oganesson) complete the seventh period of the periodic table. Their syntheses were accomplished by research groups at JINR Dubna in Russia, Lawrence Berkeley and Livermore laboratories in the United States, GSI Darmstadt in Germany, and RIKEN in Japan. Element 118, oganesson, was synthesized in 2002 at Dubna and formally recognized in 2015; its name honours the physicist Yuri Oganessian.

Island of stability

Nuclear shell theory predicts an "island of stability" around certain proton and neutron numbers (notably neutron number 184), where superheavy nuclei might have much longer half-lives than their neighbours. Reaching this island is a central goal of superheavy element research, motivating attempts to synthesize elements 119 and 120 and to produce more neutron-rich isotopes.

Chemistry

Because only a few atoms are ever produced, chemical experiments on superheavy elements are performed one atom at a time. Studies suggest relativistic effects significantly alter their chemistry; for example, copernicium behaves like a volatile noble metal, and flerovium may be unusually inert.
