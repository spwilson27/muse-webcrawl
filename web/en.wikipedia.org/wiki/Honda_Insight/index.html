Honda Insight

The Honda Insight is a hybrid electric vehicle that was manufactured and marketed by Honda in three generations: 1999–2006, 2009–2014, and 2018–2022. The first-generation Insight was a two-seat hatchback notable for its extreme aerodynamic efficiency and lightweight aluminum construction; it was the first hybrid sold in North America, reaching the market in December 1999, several months before the Toyota Prius.

First generation (1999–2006)

The original Insight used Honda's Integrated Motor Assist (IMA) system, pairing a 1.0-liter three-cylinder gasoline engine with a thin electric motor mounted on the crankshaft. With an EPA highway rating of about 70 mpg (U.S.) for the manual-transmission version, it was the most fuel-efficient gasoline-powered car ever sold in the United States. Its teardrop shape, covered rear wheels, and flat underbody gave it a drag coefficient of 0.25. The two-seat layout and small size limited its appeal, however, and production ended in 2006 after roughly 17,000 cars were sold worldwide.

Second generation (2009–2014)

Honda revived the Insight name in 2009 for a five-door, five-seat hatchback designed to be the most affordable hybrid on the market. It used a 1.3-liter engine with an updated IMA system but could not match the sales or efficiency of the contemporary Prius, and it was discontinued in 2014.

Third generation (2018–2022)

The third-generation Insight, launched for the 2019 model year, was a sedan based on the tenth-generation Civic platform. It used a two-motor hybrid system with a 1.5-liter Atkinson-cycle engine, achieving an EPA combined rating of 52 mpg, and was praised for its conventional styling and driving dynamics. Production ended in June 2022 as Honda consolidated its hybrid offerings around hybrid versions of the Accord and CR-V.
