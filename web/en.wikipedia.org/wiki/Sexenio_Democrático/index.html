Sexenio Democrático

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Sexenio Democrático ("Democratic Six Years"), also known as the Sexenio Revolucionario, is the period of Spanish history between the Glorious Revolution of September 1868, which deposed Queen Isabella II, and the pronunciamiento of December 1874 that restored the Bourbon monarchy under Alfonso XII. It is commonly divided into three phases: the provisional government and the reign of Amadeo I (1868–1873), the First Spanish Republic (1873–1874), and the transitional period of 1874.

The revolution and the Constitution of 1869

The revolution began with the naval uprising at Cádiz led by Admiral Juan Bautista Topete and generals Juan Prim and Francisco Serrano. Isabella II fled to France, and a provisional government drafted the Constitution of 1869, the most liberal Spain had known: it proclaimed national sovereignty, universal male suffrage, freedom of religion and of the press, and a democratic monarchy as the form of government.

Amadeo I

After a search for a suitable king, the Cortes elected Amadeo of Savoy, who reigned from 1871 to 1873. His reign was undermined by the assassination of his chief supporter Prim, the outbreak of the Third Carlist War and the Ten Years' War in Cuba, and constant party conflict. Amadeo abdicated in February 1873.

First Republic

The First Spanish Republic was proclaimed on 11 February 1873. It had four presidents in eleven months — Estanislao Figueras, Francesc Pi i Margall, Nicolás Salmerón and Emilio Castelar — and was consumed by the Cantonal Rebellion, in which federalist cities declared themselves independent cantons. General Pavía's coup in January 1874 dissolved the Cortes, and the government of Serrano effectively ended the republic before the Bourbon restoration coup of General Martínez Campos at Sagunto in December 1874.
