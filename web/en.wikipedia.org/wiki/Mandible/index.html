In jawed vertebrates, the mandible (from Latin mandibula, "jawbone") is the lower jaw: in humans and other mammals it is a single large bone — the largest and strongest bone of the face — that holds the lower teeth and forms the lower part of the skull's jaw structure. It is the only movable bone of the skull (apart from the ossicles of the ear), articulating with the temporal bones at the temporomandibular joints.

Structure

The human mandible consists of a horizontal body and two vertical rami (singular ramus). The body bears the alveolar process, a ridge of bone containing the sockets (alveoli) of the lower teeth, and ends at the front in the mental protuberance of the chin. Each ramus rises from the back of the body at the angle of the mandible and ends in two processes: the anterior coronoid process, to which the temporalis muscle attaches, and the posterior condylar process, whose head forms the lower half of the temporomandibular joint. The mandibular foramen on the inner surface of each ramus admits the inferior alveolar nerve and vessels, which supply the lower teeth and emerge at the mental foramen.

Muscles and movement

The mandible is moved by the muscles of mastication — the masseter, temporalis and medial and lateral pterygoids — which elevate, protrude, retract and side-shift the jaw in chewing and speech. It is the strongest bone of the face and can withstand considerable force generated in biting.

Development and variation

The mandible develops by intramembranous ossification around Meckel's cartilage of the first pharyngeal arch. At birth it consists of two halves joined at the mandibular symphysis, which usually fuse during the first year of life. With age and tooth loss the alveolar bone is resorbed, changing the profile of the lower face.

Clinical significance

The mandible may be fractured by trauma — commonly at the angle, condyle or symphysis — dislocated at the temporomandibular joint, or affected by disorders such as temporomandibular joint dysfunction, osteomyelitis and tumours. In forensic anthropology and archaeology it is used in estimating age, sex and ancestry. In arthropods, mandibles are paired mouthpart appendages used for cutting and grinding food; in birds the lower jaw is part of the beak.
