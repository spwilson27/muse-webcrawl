Women's basketball is a team sport played by women, with rules closely following the men's game. It began within a year of basketball's invention and is now played worldwide at school, collegiate, professional and Olympic levels.

Early history

Basketball was invented by James Naismith in December 1891; in 1892 Senda Berenson, a physical education instructor at Smith College, adapted the rules for women, restricting movement and dividing the court into zones — a version that persisted in parts of the United States into the 1960s in the form of six-on-six basketball. The first public women's game is generally dated to 1893 at Smith. The sport spread internationally, and the first FIBA Women's World Cup was held in 1953 in Chile.

Growth and Title IX

In the United States, the 1972 passage of Title IX, which barred sex discrimination in federally funded education, transformed women's college basketball. The NCAA began sponsoring a women's championship in 1982, supplanting the AIAW tournament. Women's basketball became an Olympic sport at the 1976 Montreal Games, and the United States has dominated the Olympic tournament, winning a long string of consecutive gold medals.

Professional game

After earlier short-lived leagues (including the WBL of 1978–1981 and the ABL of 1996–1998), the Women's National Basketball Association (WNBA), launched by the NBA in 1997, became the leading professional league in the United States. Strong professional leagues also exist in Europe (notably the EuroLeague Women), Australia (WNBL) and China (WCBA). The college game surged in popularity in the 2020s, with stars such as Caitlin Clark driving record television audiences and attendance.

Rules differences

The women's game uses a slightly smaller ball (size 6) than the men's. College women's basketball is played in four 10-minute quarters, a format adopted by the NCAA in 2015 to align with FIBA and WNBA play.
