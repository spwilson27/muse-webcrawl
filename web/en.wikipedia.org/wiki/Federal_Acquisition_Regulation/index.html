Federal Acquisition Regulation

The Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) is the principal set of rules governing how executive agencies of the United States federal government purchase goods and services. Codified at Title 48 of the Code of Federal Regulations, it implements procurement statutes and policies and is the foundation of the world's largest public purchasing system.

Structure

Established in 1984 to consolidate the separate procurement regulations of the civilian agencies and the Department of Defense, the FAR is organised into 53 parts covering such subjects as acquisition planning, competition requirements, contracting methods, contract types, cost principles, socioeconomic programmes, contract administration and termination. Individual agencies supplement it — most notably the Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement (DFARS) — but may not contradict it. Standard FAR clauses are incorporated into government contracts and bind both officials and contractors.

Administration

The FAR is maintained jointly by the Department of Defense, the General Services Administration and NASA, acting through the FAR Council with the Office of Federal Procurement Policy. Amendments are issued as Federal Acquisition Circulars, usually after public notice and comment. Its guiding vision statement calls for delivering the best value product or service to the customer on a timely basis while maintaining the public's trust and fulfilling public policy objectives.
