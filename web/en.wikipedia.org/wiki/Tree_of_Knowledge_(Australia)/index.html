Tree of Knowledge (Australia)

LocationBarcaldine, Queensland, Australia
SpeciesGhost gum (Corymbia aparrerinja)
DiedOctober 2006 (poisoned)
SignificanceSymbolic birthplace of the Australian Labor Party

The Tree of Knowledge memorial in Barcaldine, opened in 2009.

The Tree of Knowledge was a ghost gum tree in Barcaldine, Queensland, Australia, under which striking shearers gathered during the 1891 Australian shearers' strike. It is regarded as the symbolic birthplace of the Australian Labor Party and of the Australian labour movement more broadly.

History

During the 1891 strike, striking shearers camped and held meetings beneath the tree outside the Barcaldine railway station. The defeat of the strike led unionists to pursue political representation, and in 1892 the first Labor candidate, Tommy Ryan, was elected to the Queensland parliament. According to legend, the party's founding deliberations took place in the tree's shade.

Poisoning and memorial

In 2006 the tree was discovered to have been poisoned with glyphosate herbicide; despite treatment, it died in October 2006. The perpetrator was never identified. After conservation, the preserved trunk and branches were incorporated into a striking timber memorial — an 18-metre-high canopy structure designed by Brian Hooper Architect and m3architecture — which opened on 2 May 2009. The site is heritage-listed and remains a place of pilgrimage for the labour movement. Cuttings and clones of the tree survive at other locations, including a descendant grown in Barcaldine.
