Problem of Apollonius

In Euclidean plane geometry, Apollonius's problem is to construct circles that are tangent to three given circles in a plane. The problem is named after Apollonius of Perga (c. 200 BC), who posed and — according to later commentators such as Pappus — solved it in his lost work Tangencies (Ἐπαφαί). In general there are eight solution circles: for each of the three given circles, a solution circle may touch it externally or internally (enclose it), giving 23 combinations.

History

The original Greek solution is lost. In the 16th century the problem was revived: François Viète published a reconstruction and a straightedge-and-compass solution in his Apollonius Gallus (1600), and Adriaan van Roomen gave a solution using hyperbolas. Isaac Newton also solved the problem geometrically. The special case of three mutually tangent circles connects with Descartes' theorem on circle curvatures and with Apollonian gaskets (Ford circles, Soddy circles).

Methods of solution

Standard approaches include:

  • Shrinking/growing circles: reduce the problem by adjusting radii until one given circle becomes a point, then apply circle inversion to turn circles into lines.
  • Inversion: invert in a suitable circle so that two given circles become concentric (or into lines), making the solution circles easy to draw, then invert back.
  • Algebraic method: tangency gives three quadratic equations in the unknown centre and radius; subtracting pairs yields linear equations, reducing the system to solving one quadratic — hence up to eight solutions.
  • Lie sphere geometry: treat circles as points in a higher-dimensional space where tangency becomes incidence.

Generalizations

The construction generalises: circles tangent to any combination of points, lines, and circles (the ten "Apollonian problems"), and in three dimensions to spheres tangent to four given spheres, which have up to 16 solutions. It has applications in trilateration and navigation, where the analogous problem underlies hyperbolic positioning systems.
