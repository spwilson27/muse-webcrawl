Tetrabiblos

The Tetrabiblos (Greek: Τετράβιβλος, "Four Books"), also known in Latin as the Quadripartitum, is a treatise on astrology written in the 2nd century CE by the Alexandrian scholar Claudius Ptolemy. Together with his astronomical work the Almagest, it formed the authoritative foundation of Western and Islamic astrology for well over a thousand years.

Contents

Ptolemy presents astrology as a natural, physical science: the heavens influence earthly affairs through the qualities of the planets, the Sun, and the Moon. Book I defends the study philosophically and sets out the nature and powers of the planets, the signs of the zodiac, and concepts such as aspects and dignities. Book II treats mundane astrology — the effects of eclipses and planetary configurations on regions, nations, weather, and prices. Books III and IV concern genethlialogy, the casting of an individual's nativity, covering conception and birth, length of life, bodily form and temperament, wealth, rank, marriage, children, travel, and the manner of death.

Transmission and influence

The work was translated into Arabic in the 9th century (as the Kitāb al-arbaʿa) and commented on in the Islamic world, notably by Abu Ma'shar's predecessors and by Ali ibn Ridwan. A Latin translation from Arabic was made in the 12th century by Plato of Tivoli, and the Tetrabiblos became a standard university text in medieval and Renaissance Europe, printed from 1484 onwards. Ptolemy's rationalising presentation — grounding astrological effects in Aristotelian physics — distinguished the work from more magical or divinatory astrological texts and contributed to its long authority. Although astrology's scientific standing collapsed in the 17th century, the Tetrabiblos remains a primary source for the history of astrology and for Ptolemaic natural philosophy.

Editions

The standard English translation is that of F. E. Robbins in the Loeb Classical Library (1940), based on the Greek text edited by Franz Boll and Emilie Boer.
