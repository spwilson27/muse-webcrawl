Missing person

A missing person is a person who has disappeared and whose status as alive or dead cannot be confirmed, whose whereabouts are unknown, and who has not contacted known associates for an unusual period of time. Missing-person cases are investigated by law enforcement agencies, and many countries maintain national databases and clearinghouses to help locate them. People go missing for many reasons, including voluntary disappearance, accidents, foul play, mental illness, and natural disasters.

Missing person cases are investigated by police and supported by public appeals for information.

Reasons for disappearance

People may disappear voluntarily to escape debt, an abusive relationship, or criminal prosecution, or to start a new life. Others disappear involuntarily: children may be abducted by a parent or a stranger; adults may suffer accidents, become lost, develop conditions such as dementia that cause wandering, or fall victim to homicide or human trafficking. Some disappearances remain unexplained for decades and become famous cold cases.

Investigation

Investigations typically begin with a report to police, who collect identifying information, photographs, dental and medical records, and details of the person's last known movements. Depending on the jurisdiction, there may be no mandatory waiting period before a report is accepted, despite the popular myth of a 24- or 48-hour rule. Modern investigations use mobile-phone records, financial transactions, CCTV footage, DNA databases, and social media appeals. In the United States, the National Missing and Unidentified Persons System (NamUs) and the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children support case work; internationally, Interpol issues Yellow Notices for missing persons.

Statistics

Hundreds of thousands of missing-person reports are filed each year in large countries, though the great majority are resolved quickly, often within days. A small proportion remain missing for years. Unidentified remains are sometimes matched to missing persons long after death through forensic genealogy and DNA comparison.

Notable cases

Famous unresolved disappearances include aviator Amelia Earhart (1937), the crew of the Mary Celeste (found abandoned in 1872), and Madeleine McCann (2007). Some long-missing people, such as Jaycee Dugard and the Cleveland kidnapping survivors, have been found alive after many years.
