Ducal Prussia

Ducal Prussia (German: Herzogtum Preußen; Polish: Prusy Książęce) was a duchy in the eastern part of Prussia, existing from 1525 to 1657/1701. It was established when Albert of Brandenburg-Ansbach, Grand Master of the Teutonic Order, secularised the Monastic State of the Teutonic Knights, converted to Lutheranism, and — by the Treaty of Kraków of 1525 — received the territory as a hereditary duchy held as a fief of the Kingdom of Poland. Its capital was Königsberg.

History

Ducal Prussia was the first Protestant state in Europe, and Albert founded the University of Königsberg (the Albertina) in 1544 as a centre of Lutheran learning for the region. Because Albert's line faced succession problems — his son Albert Frederick suffered mental illness and died without male heirs — regency and inheritance passed to the Brandenburg Hohenzollerns through marriage. In 1618 John Sigismund, Elector of Brandenburg, inherited the duchy, creating the composite state of Brandenburg-Prussia, though the duchy remained a Polish fief.

During the Northern War, the Treaties of Wehlau and Bromberg (1657) released the Hohenzollerns from Polish suzerainty, making the duchy fully sovereign; the Peace of Oliva (1660) confirmed this internationally. In 1701 Elector Frederick III crowned himself in Königsberg as Frederick I, "King in Prussia" — a title chosen because the western part of Prussia (Royal Prussia) remained Polish. The old duchy thereafter formed the core of the province of East Prussia within the Kingdom of Prussia.

Geography and population

The duchy comprised the eastern portion of the former Teutonic state, west of Lithuania and east of Polish Royal Prussia. Its population was mixed: German settlers and townspeople, Polish-speaking Masurians in the south, and Lithuanian speakers in the northeast (Lithuania Minor), alongside descendants of the indigenous Old Prussians, whose language died out in the early 18th century, hastened by the plague of 1709–1711.
