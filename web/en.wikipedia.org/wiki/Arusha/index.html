Arusha is a city in northeastern Tanzania and the capital of the Arusha Region. It lies below Mount Meru, on the eastern edge of the eastern branch of the Great Rift Valley, at an elevation of about 1,400 metres, which gives it a temperate climate despite its proximity to the equator. Arusha is a major centre of international diplomacy in East Africa and the principal gateway for tourists visiting the national parks of northern Tanzania.

History

The area was settled by Maasai and Arusha (Wa-Arusha) peoples before German colonial administration established a military post and administrative centre around 1900, after which the modern town developed. Under British administration following the First World War, Arusha grew with coffee and sisal estates in the surrounding highlands. The town gained international prominence in 1967 with the Arusha Declaration, President Julius Nyerere's statement of Tanzanian socialist policy (ujamaa). In 1993 the Arusha Accords, intended to end the Rwandan civil war, were signed here, and from 1994 to 2015 the city hosted the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda.

Diplomacy and institutions

Arusha is the headquarters of the East African Community, whose secretariat, Legislative Assembly, and Court of Justice are based in the city. It also hosts the African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights and the residual mechanism of the Rwanda tribunal. For this reason it is sometimes styled the "Geneva of Africa," a phrase associated with Bill Clinton's visit in 2000. The city also hosts the Nelson Mandela African Institution of Science and Technology, in nearby Tengeru, and a campus of the University of Arusha.

Economy and tourism

Arusha is the hub of the "northern circuit" of Tanzanian safari tourism, serving Arusha National Park, Mount Kilimanjaro, Tarangire, Lake Manyara, the Ngorongoro Conservation Area, and the Serengeti. Agriculture—especially coffee, flowers, and seed production—remains important to the regional economy. Tanzania's tanzanite, a gemstone found only near the Mererani hills southeast of the city, is traded through Arusha. The city is served by Kilimanjaro International Airport and by Arusha Airport for domestic flights.

Geography and landmarks

The city spreads beneath Mount Meru (4,566 m), an active stratovolcano about 70 km west of Kilimanjaro. Notable landmarks include the Clock Tower—said to stand at the midpoint between Cairo and Cape Town on the old Great North Road—the Arusha Declaration Monument, the Natural History Museum in the old German fort (the Boma), and the Cultural Heritage Centre gallery. The surrounding districts include Usa River and the coffee estates of the Meru foothills.
