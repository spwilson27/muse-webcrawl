The Dauphiné Alps are a group of mountain ranges in the southwestern part of the Alps, in southeastern France. They form the western section of the Cottian and Maritime chains' northern neighbours and take their name from the historic province of the Dauphiné. Their highest summit is the Barre des Écrins at 4,102 metres, the southernmost 4,000-metre peak in the Alps.

Geography

The range is bounded by the Romanche and Durance valleys and includes the Écrins massif, protected by the Écrins National Park, together with the ranges around Briançon and the upper Durance. Important peaks besides the Barre des Écrins include La Meije (3,984 m), Mont Pelvoux and Ailefroide. The area is a major centre for mountaineering, skiing — at resorts such as Les Deux Alpes, Alpe d'Huez and Serre Chevalier — and for the Tour de France's famous Alpine stages.

History

The Barre des Écrins was first climbed in 1864 by Edward Whymper's party, and La Meije, one of the most storied peaks in alpinism, was first ascended in 1877 by Emmanuel Boileau de Castelnau with the guide Pierre Gaspard.
