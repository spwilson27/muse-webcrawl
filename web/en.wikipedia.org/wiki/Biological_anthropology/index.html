Biological anthropology

Biological anthropology, also known as physical anthropology, is a scientific discipline concerned with the biological and behavioral aspects of human beings, their extinct hominin ancestors, and related non-human primates, particularly from an evolutionary perspective.

Subfields

Subfields include primatology, paleoanthropology and human fossil studies, human biology and adaptation, bioarchaeology, forensic anthropology, skeletal biology, and molecular anthropology using genetics to trace population history.

History

Early physical anthropology involved anthropometry and typological race science, now repudiated. In the mid-20th century the "new physical anthropology" of Sherwood Washburn refocused the field on evolution, adaptation and population variation; later work integrated genetics following the modern synthesis and DNA discoveries.

Methods

Researchers study fossils, skeletal remains, living primate behaviour, physiology, ancient DNA and modern human variation to understand evolution, migration, health and adaptation to environments.
