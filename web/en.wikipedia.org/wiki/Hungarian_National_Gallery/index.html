Hungarian National Gallery

Established1957
LocationBuda Castle, Budapest, Hungary
TypeArt museum

The Hungarian National Gallery (Hungarian: Magyar Nemzeti Galéria) is the largest public collection of Hungarian fine art. It was established in 1957 and has occupied the main wings of Buda Castle in Budapest since 1975. Its collections cover the history of art in Hungary from the medieval period to the present day.

The Gallery occupies the central wings of Buda Castle overlooking the Danube.

Collections

The Gallery's holdings include medieval panel paintings and wooden sculptures, late Gothic winged altarpieces, Renaissance and Baroque art, and extensive 19th- and 20th-century painting and sculpture. Highlights include works by Mihály Munkácsy, László Paál, Pál Szinyei Merse, József Rippl-Rónai, and Tivadar Csontváry Kosztka, whose monumental canvases form one of the most popular displays. The collection of Hungarian sculpture includes the equestrian statue studies of the 19th century and modernist works.

History

The National Gallery was created in 1957 from the Hungarian material of the Museum of Fine Arts, together with other collections. It was first housed in the former Palace of Justice on Kossuth Square before moving to Buda Castle in 1975. In 2012 the Gallery was organisationally reunited with the Museum of Fine Arts, while continuing to exhibit in the Castle. Plans under the Liget Budapest Project envision a new building for the Gallery in City Park, with the castle wings eventually vacated, although the timetable has repeatedly changed.
