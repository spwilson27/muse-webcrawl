Highway Revenue Act

The Highway Revenue Act of 1956, passed together with the Federal-Aid Highway Act of 1956, created the Highway Trust Fund and the system of federal excise taxes that financed construction of the Interstate Highway System in the United States.

Provisions

The Act raised the federal gasoline tax from two to three cents per gallon and increased taxes on diesel fuel, tires, trucks, trailers and buses. Revenues were earmarked — credited to the Highway Trust Fund rather than the general fund — under a "pay-as-you-go" user-fee philosophy championed by Senator Harry F. Byrd, ensuring the highways would be paid for by road users rather than by deficit spending. The federal share of Interstate construction costs was set at 90 per cent.

Later changes

A subsequent Highway Revenue Act of 1982 raised the fuel tax to nine cents and created a Mass Transit Account within the Trust Fund, dedicating one cent per gallon to public transportation. The federal gasoline tax was last increased in 1993, to 18.4 cents per gallon, and the Trust Fund has required repeated general-fund transfers since 2008 as revenues fell short of spending.
