Pillage

Pillage, also called looting or plundering, is the act of stealing goods by force, typically during war or civil disorder. In international humanitarian law, pillage is expressly prohibited and constitutes a war crime.

History

Throughout much of history, plunder was an accepted part of warfare: soldiers were often paid in booty, and the sack of a stormed city was regarded as a customary reward. The spoils of conquered peoples funded armies and states from antiquity through the early modern period. Attitudes changed as professional standing armies and codified laws of war developed in the seventeenth to nineteenth centuries.

Legal status

The Hague Regulations of 1899 and 1907 formally prohibited pillage, and the Fourth Geneva Convention of 1949 protects the property of civilians in occupied territory. The Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court lists "pillaging a town or place, even when taken by assault" as a war crime in both international and non-international armed conflicts. Convictions related to pillage were among the charges in post-Second World War trials, and the prohibition extends to organized, large-scale looting of natural resources and cultural property.
