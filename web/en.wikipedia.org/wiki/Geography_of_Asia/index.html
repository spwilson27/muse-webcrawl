Geography of Asia

Asia, the largest continent

Area: 44.6 million km²

Highest point: Mount Everest, 8,849 m

Lowest point: Dead Sea shore, −430 m

Asia is the largest and most populous continent, covering about 44.6 million square kilometres — roughly 30% of Earth's land area. It stretches from the Arctic Ocean in the north to the Indian Ocean in the south, and from the Ural Mountains and Suez in the west to the Pacific Ocean in the east.

Regions

The United Nations geoscheme divides Asia into Central Asia, East Asia, Southeast Asia, South Asia, and West Asia (the Middle East), with North Asia (Siberia) forming the Asian part of Russia.

Physical geography

Asia contains the world's highest mountain system, the Himalayas, including Mount Everest, and vast lowlands drained by great rivers: the Yangtze, Yellow, Mekong, Ganges, Indus, Ob, Yenisei and Lena. Major deserts include the Arabian, Gobi, Taklamakan and Thar. The Tibetan Plateau — the "Roof of the World" — feeds most of Asia's great rivers. Lake Baikal in Siberia is the world's deepest and oldest freshwater lake.

Climate

Climates range from polar tundra in Siberia to equatorial rainforest in Indonesia and Malaysia. South and Southeast Asia are dominated by the monsoon, whose seasonal rains sustain the continent's densest agricultural populations. Central and West Asia are largely arid.

Boundaries

The boundary with Europe is conventionally drawn along the Ural Mountains, the Ural River, the Caspian Sea, the Caucasus and the Turkish straits; the boundary with Africa is the Isthmus of Suez.
