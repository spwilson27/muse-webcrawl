Peruvian political crisis

The term Peruvian political crisis refers to the prolonged period of institutional instability in Peru characterised by repeated clashes between the presidency and Congress, frequent changes of government, corruption scandals, and mass protests. Commentators most often apply it to the period beginning in 2016 or 2017 and intensifying from 2021 onward.

Background and course

Instability deepened during the presidency of Pedro Pablo Kuczynski, who resigned in 2018 amid the Odebrecht bribery scandal; his successor Martín Vizcarra dissolved Congress in 2019 and was himself removed in 2020, triggering the short-lived presidency of Manuel Merino and street protests in which two demonstrators were killed. Pedro Castillo, a rural teachers' union leader, narrowly won the 2021 election but faced three impeachment attempts amid continuous conflict with a hostile Congress and allegations of corruption.

The 2022 crisis and aftermath

On 7 December 2022, facing a third impeachment vote, Castillo announced the dissolution of Congress and rule by decree in what was widely described as an attempted self-coup. Congress removed him the same day; he was arrested, and Vice President Dina Boluarte succeeded him. Her inauguration sparked nationwide protests, particularly in the southern Andes, demanding her resignation, early elections, and a constituent assembly. The government's crackdown killed dozens of protesters — including massacres at Ayacucho in December 2022 and Juliaca in January 2023 — drawing condemnation from international human rights bodies, while approval ratings for both the president and Congress fell to historically low levels.
