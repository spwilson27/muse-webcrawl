Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street is a 1979 musical thriller with music and lyrics by Stephen Sondheim and a book by Hugh Wheeler. It tells the story of Benjamin Barker, a barber exiled to Australia on a trumped-up charge by the corrupt Judge Turpin, who returns to Victorian London under the name Sweeney Todd to exact revenge.

Synopsis

Todd sets up a barber shop above the pie shop of the widowed Mrs Lovett. Unable at first to reach the Judge, he begins murdering customers with his razor; Mrs Lovett, in a burst of practicality, bakes the victims into her meat pies, which become a sensation in London. Numbers include "The Ballad of Sweeney Todd", "The Worst Pies in London", "Pretty Women", "A Little Priest", "By the Sea" and "Not While I'm Around". The tale ends in tragedy as Todd's obsession consumes nearly everyone, including his own long-lost wife.

Reception and adaptations

The original Broadway production, directed by Harold Prince and starring Len Cariou and Angela Lansbury, won eight Tony Awards including Best Musical. The show is widely regarded as Sondheim's masterpiece. Tim Burton directed a 2007 film adaptation starring Johnny Depp and Helena Bonham Carter. Major revivals have scaled the production from grand opera-house spectacle to intimate actor-musician stagings.
