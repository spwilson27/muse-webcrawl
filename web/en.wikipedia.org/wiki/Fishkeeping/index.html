Fishkeeping

Fishkeeping is a popular hobby, practised by aquarists, concerned with keeping fish in a home aquarium or garden pond. Fish have been raised as food in pools and ponds for thousands of years, and ornamental fishkeeping developed with the breeding of colourful varieties of carp, such as goldfish in China and koi in Japan. The modern aquarium hobby expanded greatly in the nineteenth century and is supported by an industry supplying tanks, filtration, lighting, foods and livestock.

Types of systems

Freshwater aquaria are the most common, divided broadly into tropical (heated) and coldwater setups. Popular tropical species include tetras, guppies, angelfish and cichlids.

Marine (saltwater) aquaria keep species from the oceans, including reef tanks with corals and other invertebrates; they are generally more demanding and expensive.

Brackish aquaria imitate estuarine environments where fresh and salt water mix.

Garden ponds typically house goldfish and koi outdoors.

Aquarium maintenance

Successful fishkeeping depends on managing water chemistry. Fish waste produces ammonia, which is toxic; in an established aquarium, nitrifying bacteria in the filter convert ammonia to nitrite and then to the much less harmful nitrate — the nitrogen cycle. New aquariums must be "cycled" before heavy stocking. Regular partial water changes, testing of pH, ammonia, nitrite and nitrate, and appropriate feeding and stocking levels keep the system stable.

History

Keeping fish in glass aquaria became a Victorian craze in the 1850s after displays at the Great Exhibition of 1851; Philip Henry Gosse popularised the "aquarium" in Britain. Air travel and plastic-bag shipping in the twentieth century made tropical species widely available, and the hobby now counts millions of participants worldwide.
