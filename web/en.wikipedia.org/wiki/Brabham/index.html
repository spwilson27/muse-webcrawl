Brabham was a British racing car manufacturer and Formula One racing team. Founded in 1960 by Australian driver Jack Brabham and designer Ron Tauranac as Motor Racing Developments, the team competed in Formula One from 1962 to 1992, winning four Drivers' Championships and two Constructors' Championships.

History

Jack Brabham, already a double world champion with Cooper, left to build his own cars with Tauranac. The team scored its first Grand Prix victory at the 1964 French Grand Prix with Dan Gurney. In 1966, with the new 3-litre formula and reliable Repco V8 engines, Brabham won both championships, making Jack Brabham the first — and still only — driver to win the World Championship in a car of his own construction and bearing his own name. Denny Hulme repeated the drivers' title for the team in 1967 as Brabham again took the constructors' crown.

Brabham sold his interest to Tauranac in the early 1970s, and in 1971 the team was bought by Bernie Ecclestone. Under designer Gordon Murray, Brabham pioneered innovations including the radical 1978 BT46B "fan car", which won its only race before being withdrawn. Nelson Piquet won the Drivers' Championship for Brabham in 1981 and again in 1983 with the turbocharged BMW-powered BT52 — the first turbo-era title. The team's fortunes declined in the late 1980s through a succession of owners, and it folded during the 1992 season.

Legacy

Brabham cars also competed successfully in Formula Two, Formula Three, sports car racing, and at the Indianapolis 500. The Brabham name was briefly revived for an abortive 2010 Formula One entry, and the Brabham Automotive company launched the BT62 track car in 2018.
