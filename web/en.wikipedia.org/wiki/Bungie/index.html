Bungie

Bungie headquarters in Bellevue, Washington

Bungie, Inc. is an American video game developer based in Bellevue, Washington. The company was founded in May 1991 by Alex Seropian, who later brought in programmer Jason Jones; the two had previously collaborated on Jones's game Minotaur: The Labyrinths of Crete. Bungie initially focused on games for the Macintosh platform, among them the first-person shooter series Marathon.

History

Microsoft acquired Bungie in 2000, and its project Halo: Combat Evolved became a launch title and flagship franchise for the Xbox console in 2001. The Halo series made Bungie one of the world's most prominent developers. In 2007 Bungie split from Microsoft and became an independent, privately held company. It later signed a publishing agreement with Activision and released the shared-world shooter Destiny in 2014, followed by Destiny 2 in 2017, which Bungie self-published after parting with Activision in 2019.

Sony acquisition and later work

In 2022, Sony Interactive Entertainment acquired Bungie for $3.6 billion; Bungie continues to operate as an independent subsidiary developing multiplatform games. The studio has also been developing Marathon, a revival of its 1990s series as an extraction shooter. Bungie's community hub, Bungie.net, has long served as the centre of its player community and statistics tracking.

Selected games

Key releases include Pathways into Darkness (1993), the Marathon trilogy (1994–1996), Myth: The Fallen Lords (1997), Oni (2001), the Halo series (2001–2010), and the Destiny series (2014–present).
