GameStop short squeeze

In January 2021, a short squeeze of the stock of the American video game retailer GameStop (NYSE: GME) and other securities took place, causing major financial consequences for certain hedge funds and large losses for short sellers. Approximately 140 percent of GameStop's public float had been sold short, and the rush to buy shares to cover those positions as the price rose caused it to rise even further. The short squeeze was initially and primarily triggered by users of the subreddit r/wallstreetbets, an Internet forum on the social news website Reddit, although a number of hedge funds also participated.

At its height, on January 28, the short squeeze caused the retailer's stock price to reach a pre-market value of over US$500 per share ($125 split-adjusted), nearly 30 times the $17.25 valuation at the beginning of the month. The price of many other heavily shorted securities and cryptocurrencies also increased.

Background

GameStop, an American chain of brick-and-mortar video game stores, had struggled in recent years due to competition from digital distribution services and the economic effects of the COVID-19 pandemic, which reduced the number of customers who shopped in person. As a result, GameStop's stock price declined, leading many institutional investors to short the stock. By January 2021, however, commentators on r/wallstreetbets, most famously Keith Gill (known as "Roaring Kitty" on YouTube), argued the stock was undervalued.

Trading restrictions and reaction

On January 28, several brokerages, including Robinhood, halted the buying of GameStop and other securities, citing volatility. The decision attracted criticism and accusations of market manipulation from prominent politicians and businesspeople, and prompted class action lawsuits. The episode led to congressional hearings and renewed debate about market structure, payment for order flow, and the role of social media in investing.
