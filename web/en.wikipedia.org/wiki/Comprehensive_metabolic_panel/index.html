Comprehensive metabolic panel

The comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP) is a blood test consisting of a group of 14 individual tests that provide information about the body's metabolism, including kidney function, liver function, acid–base balance, and levels of blood glucose and blood proteins. It is a commonly ordered panel in clinical medicine and is often performed as part of a routine physical examination or hospital admission.

Liver functionAlkaline phosphatase (ALP), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), bilirubin

Comparison with the basic metabolic panel

The CMP includes all the tests of the basic metabolic panel (BMP) plus the liver function tests and proteins (albumin and total protein). Abnormalities in the panel can point to kidney disease, liver disease, diabetes, and disorders of fluid and electrolyte balance, and results are interpreted alongside the patient's history and other investigations.
