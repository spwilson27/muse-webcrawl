Egill Skallagrímsson (Modern Icelandic: Egill Skallagrímsson; c. 904 – c. 995) was a Viking Age war poet, sorcerer and berserker, and the central character of Egils saga, one of the best known of the Icelandic sagas. He is regarded as one of the greatest skalds of the Norse world.

Egill as depicted in a 17th-century Icelandic manuscript.

Life

Egill was the son of Skalla-Grímr Kveldúlfsson, a Norwegian settler who founded the farm of Borg in western Iceland. The saga portrays Egill as composing his first poem at the age of three and committing his first killing at seven. In Norway he fell into a deadly feud with King Eric Bloodaxe, who sentenced him to death; according to the saga, Egill saved his life by composing the praise poem Höfuðlausn ("Head-Ransom") overnight and reciting it before the king at York. He fought at the Battle of Brunanburh in the service of King Æthelstan of England, in which his brother Þórólfr was killed. In old age at Mosfell, blind and infirm, he composed Sonatorrek ("The Loss of Sons"), a lament for his drowned son Böðvarr, considered one of the masterpieces of Old Norse poetry.

Works

Poems attributed to Egill include Höfuðlausn, Sonatorrek and Arinbjarnarkviða, along with numerous loose verses (lausavísur) preserved within the saga. His verse is notable for its complex kennings and intensely personal voice, unusual in skaldic poetry.

Legacy

Egill remains a central figure of Icelandic cultural identity; his farm district around Borg á Mýrum and the settlement exhibition devoted to him draw on his saga, whose authorship is often attributed to Snorri Sturluson.
