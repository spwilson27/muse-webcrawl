Keith Jarrett

Jarrett performing solo

Keith Jarrett (born May 8, 1945) is an American jazz and classical pianist and composer. Regarded as one of the most important pianists of his generation, he is known for his fully improvised solo concerts, his long-running "Standards Trio" with Gary Peacock and Jack DeJohnette, and acclaimed recordings of classical repertoire.

Early career

Born in Allentown, Pennsylvania, Jarrett was a child prodigy who gave his first formal recital at age seven. After studying briefly at Berklee College of Music, he joined Art Blakey's Jazz Messengers in 1966, then played with Charles Lloyd's popular quartet, and briefly with Miles Davis, appearing on electric keyboards during Davis's early fusion period around 1970–1971.

Solo improvisations

Beginning with Facing You (1971) for the ECM label, Jarrett developed the solo improvised piano concert as his signature form. The Köln Concert (1975), recorded live in Cologne under difficult conditions, became the best-selling piano album and the best-selling solo jazz album in history. Other landmark solo recordings include the Sun Bear Concerts (1976) and the Vienna Concert (1991).

Groups and classical work

In the 1970s Jarrett led both an American quartet (with Dewey Redman, Charlie Haden and Paul Motian) and a European quartet (with Jan Garbarek, Palle Danielsson and Jon Christensen). From 1983, his Standards Trio with Peacock and DeJohnette recorded dozens of albums of jazz standards and played together for three decades. He has also recorded Bach, Handel, Shostakovich and Mozart on piano and harpsichord.

Later life

Jarrett suffered two strokes in 2018 that left his left hand largely paralysed, effectively ending his performing career; he discussed his condition publicly in 2020. His influence on jazz piano and on improvised music generally remains profound.
