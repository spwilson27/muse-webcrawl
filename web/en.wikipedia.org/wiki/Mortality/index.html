Mortality

Mortality is the state of being subject to death; the term is also used in demography and medicine for the incidence of death in a population over a given period, commonly expressed as a mortality rate. Mortality stands in contrast to immortality, the ability to live indefinitely.

Mortality in demography

Demographers measure mortality using indicators such as the crude death rate, age-specific mortality rates, infant mortality, and life expectancy at birth. Mortality patterns vary by age, sex, geography, and socioeconomic conditions, and they change over time through the epidemiologic transition from infectious to chronic diseases as the leading causes of death.

Causes of death

Globally, the leading causes of death include cardiovascular diseases, cancers, respiratory diseases, and infections. Mortality data are collected through civil registration systems and surveys and are used in public health planning, actuarial science (in the form of life tables), and epidemiology.

Mortality in religion and philosophy

Awareness of mortality — sometimes called mortality salience — has been a central theme in philosophy, religion, and art, informing doctrines about the afterlife, practices of memento mori, and existential reflection on the meaning of a finite life.
