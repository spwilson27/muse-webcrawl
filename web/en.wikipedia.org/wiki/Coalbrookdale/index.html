Coalbrookdale

Coalbrookdale is a village in the Ironbridge Gorge in Shropshire, England, widely regarded as one of the birthplaces of the Industrial Revolution. It was here that Abraham Darby I first smelted iron ore with coke in 1709, a breakthrough that freed iron production from dependence on charcoal and made large-scale, inexpensive cast iron possible.

History

The gorge offered coal, ironstone, limestone, and the River Severn for transport—an ideal combination for industry. In 1708 Abraham Darby leased a disused blast furnace and the following year successfully smelted iron using coke made from local coal. His Coalbrookdale Company became famous for cast-iron cooking pots, cylinders for steam engines, and later rails and structural ironwork.

Under Abraham Darby II and III, the works expanded with new furnaces and the Upper Works. In 1779 Darby III cast the ribs of the Iron Bridge, the world's first major cast-iron bridge, erected over the Severn in 1779–1781 and still standing. The Coalbrookdale Company also built early steam locomotives, including an engine for Richard Trevithick in 1802. Philip James de Loutherbourg's 1801 painting Coalbrookdale by Night captured the furnaces' dramatic glow.

Today

The valley is now a museum landscape administered by the Ironbridge Gorge Museum Trust. Sites include the Museum of Iron, the Darby Houses, the preserved Old Furnace, and the Coalbrookdale Museum of Iron, while the area forms part of the Ironbridge Gorge World Heritage Site inscribed by UNESCO in 1986.
