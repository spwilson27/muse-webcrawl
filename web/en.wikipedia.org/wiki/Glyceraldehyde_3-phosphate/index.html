Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate, also known as triose phosphate or 3-phosphoglyceraldehyde (abbreviated G3P, GAP or PGAL), is a metabolite that occurs as an intermediate in several central metabolic pathways in all organisms. It is the phosphate ester of the 3-carbon sugar glyceraldehyde and has the chemical formula C3H7O6P.

Role in glycolysis

In glycolysis, fructose 1,6-bisphosphate is split by aldolase into dihydroxyacetone phosphate and glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate; the two triose phosphates are interconverted by triose-phosphate isomerase, and only the glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate proceeds onward. Its oxidation by glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate is the first energy-yielding step of the payoff phase of glycolysis, generating NADH and enabling subsequent substrate-level phosphorylation.

Role in photosynthesis

In the Calvin cycle of photosynthesis, G3P is the principal product of carbon fixation: for every three CO2 molecules fixed, one net molecule of G3P leaves the cycle to be used in the synthesis of sugars, starch and other organic compounds, while the rest is recycled to regenerate the CO2 acceptor ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate. G3P is also an intermediate in gluconeogenesis and the pentose phosphate pathway.
