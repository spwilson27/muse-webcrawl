Virtue

Virtue (Latin: virtus) is a trait or quality that is deemed morally good and thus valued as a foundation of principle and good moral being. In a given culture, virtues are shared character traits that promote individual and collective well-being.

Ancient traditions

Plato and Aristotle identified four cardinal virtues: prudence (wisdom), justice, temperance, and courage (fortitude). Aristotle in the Nicomachean Ethics described virtue as a habit and a mean between excess and deficiency, central to eudaimonia. Stoics held virtue is the only true good.

Religious traditions

Christianity adds the theological virtues of faith, hope and charity (love). Buddhism cultivates paramitas; Confucianism stresses ren, li and filial piety; Hinduism values dharma-related virtues such as ahimsa.

Modern virtue ethics

Revived by Elizabeth Anscombe and Alasdair MacIntyre, virtue ethics focuses on character rather than rules or consequences. See Classical republicanism, where civic virtue sustains a republic.
