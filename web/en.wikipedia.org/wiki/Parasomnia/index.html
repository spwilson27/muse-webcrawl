Parasomnia

Parasomnia
SpecialtySleep medicine, psychiatry
SymptomsAbnormal behaviours during sleep: sleepwalking, night terrors, acting out dreams
TypesNREM-related, REM-related, other
Diagnostic methodClinical history; polysomnography

Parasomnias are a category of sleep disorders that involve abnormal movements, behaviours, emotions, perceptions, and dreams that occur while falling asleep, sleeping, between sleep stages, or during arousal from sleep. Parasomnias are dissociated sleep states: partial arousals during transitions between wakefulness, non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep, and REM sleep. The word derives from the Greek para ("beside") and Latin somnus ("sleep").

Classification

NREM-related parasomnias

These arise from slow-wave (stage N3) sleep, typically in the first third of the night, and are common in children:

  • Confusional arousals — disorientation on awakening
  • Sleepwalking (somnambulism) — walking or performing complex behaviours while asleep
  • Sleep terrors (night terrors) — episodes of screaming, intense fear, and autonomic arousal
  • Sleep-related eating disorder — recurrent episodes of eating during partial arousals

REM-related parasomnias

  • REM sleep behaviour disorder — loss of normal muscle atonia in REM sleep, causing dream enactment; strongly associated with later development of synucleinopathies such as Parkinson's disease
  • Recurrent isolated sleep paralysis — inability to move at sleep onset or upon awakening
  • Nightmare disorder — frequent, vivid, disturbing dreams causing distress

Other parasomnias

These include exploding head syndrome, sleep-related hallucinations, sleep enuresis (bedwetting), and sleep talking (somniloquy).

Causes and triggers

Parasomnias often have a genetic component and are frequently triggered by sleep deprivation, fever, stress, alcohol, and certain medications. NREM parasomnias are understood as disorders of arousal: the brain is simultaneously in states of sleep and wakefulness. REM sleep behaviour disorder, by contrast, frequently reflects underlying neurological disease.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies primarily on a detailed history from the patient and bed partner; video polysomnography is used when episodes are atypical, injurious, or suggestive of nocturnal epilepsy. Treatment focuses on safety measures (securing the sleep environment), regular sleep hygiene, avoiding triggers, and, in selected cases, medication such as clonazepam or melatonin for REM sleep behaviour disorder.

Forensic aspects

Parasomnias have occasionally been raised as a legal defence ("sleepwalking defence") in cases of violence committed during confusional arousals, famously in the 1987 Canadian case R v Parks, in which the accused was acquitted of murder committed while sleepwalking.
