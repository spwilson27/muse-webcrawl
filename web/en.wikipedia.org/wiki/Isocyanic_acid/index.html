Isocyanic acid

Isocyanic acid
Chemical formula: HNCO
Molar mass: 43.03 g/mol
Appearance: Colorless gas or liquid
Melting point: −86 °C
Boiling point: 23.5 °C
Related: Cyanic acid (HOCN), fulminic acid

Isocyanic acid is a chemical compound with the formula HNCO, the simplest stable member of the isocyanate class. It is a tautomer of cyanic acid (HOCN), and is by far the more stable and abundant of the two in the gas phase. The discovery of isocyanic and cyanic acids played an important historical role: Jöns Jacob Berzelius coined the term "isomerism" in part because silver cyanate and silver fulminate — and Wöhler's and Liebig's studies of these related acids — showed that compounds with the same composition could have different properties.

Structure and properties

The molecule has the connectivity H–N=C=O, with a nearly linear N=C=O skeleton and the hydrogen on nitrogen. In condensed phases it polymerizes readily, notably trimerizing to cyanuric acid. In aqueous solution it hydrolyzes to ammonia and carbon dioxide, and it is a weak acid.

Production and sources

Isocyanic acid is formed on heating urea and cyanuric acid; it is released during urea thermolysis (relevant to diesel exhaust after-treatment with urea-SCR) and is present in biomass-burning smoke and cigarette smoke. It has also been detected in interstellar clouds.

Health effects

Like other isocyanates, HNCO can carbamylate proteins; atmospheric scientists have studied its role in smoke exposure. The related compound methyl isocyanate was responsible for the Bhopal disaster of 1984.
