Moynihan Train Hall

Moynihan Train Hall is an expansion of Pennsylvania Station, the main intercity and commuter rail station in New York City, located in the James A. Farley Building, the former main post office building, across Eighth Avenue from the existing station in Midtown Manhattan. The 486,000-square-foot (45,200 m²) hall, designed by Skidmore, Owings & Merrill, opened on January 1, 2021.

The hall serves Amtrak and Long Island Rail Road passengers (with New Jersey Transit as a tenant in earlier plans) and features a 92-foot-high (28 m) main concourse built within the Farley Building's former mail-sorting room, lit by a vast skylight supported by the building's original steel trusses. The project, costing about $1.6 billion, includes a smaller glass-vaulted Midblock Entrance Hall and art installations including Kehinde Wiley's stained-glass triptych Go.

An expanded Penn Station in the Farley Building was championed for decades by Senator Daniel Patrick Moynihan, for whom the hall is named; the Beaux-Arts Farley Building (1913–14) was designed by McKim, Mead & White as a companion to their demolished original Pennsylvania Station. The opening of Moynihan Train Hall relieved congestion in the much-criticised 1960s Penn Station concourses below Madison Square Garden.
