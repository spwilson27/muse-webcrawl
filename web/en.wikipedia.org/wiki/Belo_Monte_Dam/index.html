Belo Monte Dam

The Belo Monte Dam is a hydroelectric dam complex on the northern part of the Xingu River in the state of Pará, Brazil. With an installed capacity of 11,233 MW, it is the fourth-largest hydroelectric dam in the world by capacity.

CountryBrazil
LocationPará, Xingu River
StatusOperational (completed 2019)
Installed capacity11,233 MW
OperatorNorte Energia

History

Plans date to the 1970s. Construction began in 2011 amid major protests. The first turbines came online in 2016 and the plant was fully inaugurated in November 2019.

Controversy

Critics cite displacement of Indigenous peoples and riverine communities, deforestation, reduced flow on the Volta Grande bend of the Xingu, harm to fish species, and lower-than-promised output during dry seasons. Supporters cite renewable energy for millions of homes.

Design

The complex uses two powerhouses — the main Belo Monte powerhouse and the smaller Pimental powerhouse — and a canal diverting water from the Xingu.
