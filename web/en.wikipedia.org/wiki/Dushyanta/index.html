Dushyanta

Dushyanta (Sanskrit: दुष्यन्त, Duṣyanta) is a king in Hindu mythology, best known as the husband of Shakuntala and the father of the emperor Bharata, from whom, according to tradition, India takes its name Bhārata. He belongs to the lunar dynasty (Chandravamsha) and appears in the Mahabharata as an ancestor of the Kuru kings.

Legend

In the Mahabharata's Adi Parva, Dushyanta, while hunting in the forest, came upon the hermitage of the sage Kanva and met his foster daughter Shakuntala, daughter of the sage Vishvamitra and the apsara Menaka. The two fell in love and married by the gandharva rite. Dushyanta returned to his capital, promising to send for her, and gave her his signet ring as a token.

Through the curse of the sage Durvasa — incurred when Shakuntala, lost in thought, failed to greet him — Dushyanta forgot her. When Shakuntala arrived at court pregnant, the king did not recognise her, and the ring had been lost in a river. It was later recovered from the belly of a fish, restoring the king's memory; the couple were reunited, and their son Bharata became a celebrated universal emperor (chakravartin).

In literature

The story was most famously dramatised by the poet Kalidasa in the Sanskrit play Abhijñānaśākuntalam ("The Recognition of Shakuntala", c. 4th–5th century CE), regarded as one of the masterpieces of classical Sanskrit literature. Kalidasa's version introduced the curse and the ring motif into the tale, softening the king's conduct as it appears in the epic.
