Ralph Johnson Bunche (7 August 1904 – 9 December 1971) was an American political scientist, diplomat, and a leading figure in the early United Nations. In 1950 he received the Nobel Peace Prize for his mediation of the 1949 Armistice Agreements between Israel and the Arab states — the first African American and the first person of African descent to receive a Nobel Prize.

Career

Bunche graduated valedictorian from UCLA and earned a doctorate in political science from Harvard (1934) — the first PhD in the field awarded to an African American — after which he taught at Howard University and contributed to Gunnar Myrdal's landmark study An American Dilemma. During World War II he worked for the US Office of Strategic Services and the State Department, helping draft the trusteeship chapters of the UN Charter at San Francisco in 1945, and then joined the UN Secretariat, heading its Trusteeship Division.

Palestine mediation and later work

Appointed to the UN Special Committee on Palestine and then as principal secretary to mediator Count Folke Bernadotte, Bunche took over the negotiations after Bernadotte's assassination in September 1948. From Rhodes he brokered, between January and July 1949, the armistice agreements between Israel and Egypt, Lebanon, Jordan, and Syria. As UN Under-Secretary-General for Special Political Affairs he later oversaw UN peacekeeping operations in the Suez (1956), the Congo (1960), and Cyprus, and was a prominent supporter of the American civil rights movement, marching with Martin Luther King Jr. at Selma in 1965.
