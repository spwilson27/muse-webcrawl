Jacobian matrix and determinant

A nonlinear map distorts a grid; the Jacobian describes its best local linear approximation
FieldMultivariable calculus
Named afterCarl Gustav Jacob Jacobi

In vector calculus, the Jacobian matrix of a vector-valued function of several variables is the matrix of all its first-order partial derivatives. When this matrix is square, its determinant is called the Jacobian determinant. Both are named after the mathematician Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi (1804–1851).

Definition

Suppose f : ℝn → ℝm is a function such that each of its first-order partial derivatives exists. The Jacobian matrix J of f is the m × n matrix whose (i, j) entry is the partial derivative of the i-th component function with respect to the j-th variable. The Jacobian represents the best linear approximation to the function near a point: f(x) ≈ f(p) + Jf(p)(x − p).

Jacobian determinant

If m = n, the Jacobian matrix is square and its determinant, denoted det J or sometimes |J|, is a real-valued function. Geometrically, the absolute value of the Jacobian determinant at a point gives the factor by which the function scales volumes near that point: it is the ratio of the volume of an infinitesimal region after transformation to its volume before. If the determinant is zero at a point, the function is not locally invertible there (by the inverse function theorem).

Applications

Jacobians appear in the change-of-variables formula for multiple integrals — famously in the conversion to polar coordinates, where the Jacobian determinant is r — in implicit and inverse function theorems, in Newton's method for systems of equations, in robotics (relating joint velocities to end-effector velocities), and in statistics when transforming probability densities of random variables.
