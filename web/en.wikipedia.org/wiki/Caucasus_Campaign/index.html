Caucasus Campaign

Date: 24 October 1914 – 30 October 1918
Location: Caucasus, Eastern Anatolia
Belligerents: Russian Empire vs Ottoman Empire (later others)

The Caucasus Campaign is a name that describes the armed engagements between the Russian Empire and the Ottoman Empire during the First World War, later involving Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, Persia and the British Empire as part of the Middle Eastern theatre, across the mountainous terrain of the Caucasus and eastern Anatolia.

Background

The Ottoman Empire aimed to recover territory lost to Russia in the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78 and to advance into the Russian Caucasus, while Russia sought to secure its southern frontier and relieve pressure elsewhere. The Ottoman war minister Enver Pasha personally led the opening offensive in December 1914.

Course of the campaign

The Ottoman Third Army's winter offensive ended in disaster at the Battle of Sarikamish (December 1914 – January 1915), where much of the army was destroyed by Russian forces and the severe cold. Russian forces under General Nikolai Yudenich then advanced into eastern Anatolia, capturing Erzurum, Trabzon and Erzincan in 1916. The campaign unfolded against the backdrop of the Armenian genocide, carried out by the Ottoman government against its Armenian population.

After the Russian Revolution of 1917, Russian forces withdrew, and the front collapsed. Ottoman forces advanced in 1918 to Baku before the Armistice of Mudros ended hostilities in October 1918.
