The Boeing 737 Next Generation, commonly abbreviated 737NG, is the third generation of the Boeing 737. Produced from 1997 to 2019, it comprises the -600, -700, -800 and -900 series, seating 108 to 215 passengers.

Development

Launched in 1993 in response to the Airbus A320 family, the NG features a redesigned larger wing, upgraded CFM56-7 engines, a glass cockpit and larger overhead bins. The -800, launched by Hapag-Lloyd in 1994, became the best-selling variant.

Variants

The -600 (108–130 seats), -700 (126–149), -800 (162–189) and stretched -900/-900ER (180–215). Military variants include the C-40 Clipper, P-8 Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft and E-7 Wedgetail. The BBJ business jet is derived from the -700.

Successor

The fourth-generation 737 MAX, with CFM LEAP engines, was introduced in 2017. See also Airbus A320 family.

Specifications (737-800)

Length 39.5 m, wingspan 35.8 m with winglets, range about 5,436 km (2,935 nmi), cruise Mach 0.785, engines 2× CFM56-7B.
