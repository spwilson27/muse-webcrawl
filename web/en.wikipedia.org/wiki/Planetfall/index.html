Planetfall

Developer: Infocom
Author: Steve Meretzky
Released: 1983
Genre: Interactive fiction
Mode: Single-player

Planetfall is a science-fiction interactive fiction video game written by Steve Meretzky and published by Infocom in 1983, his first published game. The player takes the role of a lowly Ensign Seventh Class aboard the Stellar Patrol ship Feinstein, who is stranded on a deserted alien planet after the ship explodes, and must explore its abandoned complexes to find a way to survive and escape.

Gameplay

Like other Infocom titles, Planetfall is played entirely through typed commands interpreted by a sophisticated text parser. The player must manage hunger, thirst, and sleep while solving puzzles involving alien machinery, a library computer, and a mutant-infested bio-lab. The game's best-remembered character is Floyd, a cheerful, childlike robot who becomes the player's companion; his heroic self-sacrifice late in the game — entering a radiation-filled room to fetch a vital card — is frequently cited as one of the earliest moments in gaming to provoke a genuine emotional response from players.

Reception and legacy

Planetfall was a commercial and critical success, selling over 74,000 copies, and Meretzky's humour — from the player's humble job scrubbing decks to the game's bureaucratic forms — won wide praise. A novelisation by Arthur Byron Cover appeared in 1988. A sequel, Stationfall (1987), also by Meretzky, reunited the player character, now promoted, with Floyd. Planetfall remains one of the best-regarded works of the text-adventure era and is regularly included in lists of the greatest video games of all time.
