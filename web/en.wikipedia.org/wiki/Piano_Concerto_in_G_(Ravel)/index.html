The Piano Concerto in G major, M. 83, is a piano concerto by Maurice Ravel, composed from 1929 to 1931. The concerto is in three movements and is noted for its wit, jazz influences, and lyrical slow movement. It is not to be confused with Ravel's other piano concerto, the Piano Concerto for the Left Hand, written around the same time for Paul Wittgenstein.


Maurice Ravel in 1930, during the period he composed the two piano concertos.

Background

After his successful American tour in 1928, Ravel planned a concerto for himself to perform on a world tour. Health problems prevented him from preparing the solo part, and the premiere was instead given by Marguerite Long, to whom the score is dedicated, with Ravel conducting the Orchestre Lamoureux at the Salle Pleyel on 14 January 1932. Ravel said of the work: "The Concerto in G is a concerto in the strict sense of the term: it is written very much in the spirit of Mozart and Saint-Saëns."

Movements

  1. Allegramente — Opening with a whip-crack, the movement combines Basque and Spanish-flavoured themes with jazz elements, including blue notes and a blues-inflected trombone and piano writing. Material Ravel had sketched for an earlier, abandoned Basque-themed work (Zaspiak bat) found its way into the concerto.
  2. Adagio assai — A long, serene E-major melody for the solo piano, unfolding in a single extended phrase over a gently flowing accompaniment; Ravel joked that he wrote it "two bars at a time, with the help of Mozart's Clarinet Quintet". A solo English horn takes up the melody before the movement dissolves.
  3. Presto — A whirlwind finale of less than five minutes, brilliant and sardonic, full of fanfare figures, wrong-note wit and virtuoso passagework.

Influences

The concerto reflects Ravel's admiration for jazz, which he had heard in Paris and on his American tour — including performances associated with George Gershwin — as well as for Mozart's and Saint-Saëns's clarity of form. Its scoring is light: a single flute doubling piccolo, oboe doubling English horn, clarinets, two bassoons, two horns, trumpet, trombone, percussion, harp, and strings.
