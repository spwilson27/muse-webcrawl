A digital video recorder (DVR), also called a personal video recorder (PVR), is an electronic device or software that records video in a digital format to a disk drive, USB flash drive, memory card, or other local or networked storage. DVRs include set-top boxes with direct recording to disk, portable media players, TV gateway devices, and software for personal computers that enables video capture and playback to and from a disk.

History

Consumer DVRs arrived in 1999 with TiVo and ReplayTV, demonstrated at the Consumer Electronics Show that year. They transformed television viewing by letting users pause live TV, schedule season-long recordings from an electronic programme guide, and skip advertising — functions that became central to broadcaster-supplied boxes such as Sky+ in the UK and cable-company DVRs. Litigation over features such as ReplayTV's automatic commercial skipping and, later, remote-storage ("cloud") DVRs, tested copyright limits; the US Cablevision case (2008) upheld network DVRs under certain conditions.

Types and evolution

Beyond television recording, the term covers CCTV/security DVRs (digitising camera feeds, now largely supplanted by network video recorders for IP cameras) and software DVRs on computers. As streaming and catch-up services grew in the 2010s, standalone DVR use declined, though broadcasters integrated cloud DVR recording into their apps and set-top DVRs remain widespread, especially for live sport and in markets with limited broadband.
