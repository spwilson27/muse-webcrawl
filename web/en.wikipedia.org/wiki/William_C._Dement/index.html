William C. Dement

BornJuly 29, 1928, Wenatchee, Washington, U.S.
DiedJune 17, 2020 (aged 91), Stanford, California, U.S.
OccupationPhysician, sleep researcher
Known forFounding sleep medicine; REM sleep research

William Charles Dement (July 29, 1928 – June 17, 2020) was an American sleep researcher and physician, widely regarded as the father of sleep medicine. As a medical student at the University of Chicago in the 1950s he worked with Nathaniel Kleitman on the newly discovered phenomenon of rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, helping to establish the connection between REM sleep and dreaming.

Career

Dement joined the faculty of Stanford University in 1963. In 1970 he founded the Stanford Sleep Disorders Clinic — the first clinic in the United States devoted to the diagnosis and treatment of sleep disorders — and helped pioneer the use of the overnight sleep study (polysomnography) in clinical practice. He was involved in the description of narcolepsy and in characterising obstructive sleep apnea as a major clinical problem.

Advocacy and teaching

Dement spent decades campaigning for public recognition of sleep deprivation as a health and safety hazard, testifying before Congress and helping to prompt the creation of the National Commission on Sleep Disorders Research in the late 1980s. At Stanford his undergraduate course "Sleep and Dreams" became one of the university's most popular classes. His books include The Promise of Sleep (1999), a widely read survey of sleep science for general readers.
