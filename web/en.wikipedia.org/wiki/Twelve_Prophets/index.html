Twelve Prophets

The Twelve Prophets, also called the Minor Prophets, are the twelve short prophetic books that conclude the Nevi'im (Prophets) section of the Hebrew Bible: Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah and Malachi. The term "minor" refers to the brevity of the books, not their importance.

Contents

In the Hebrew Bible the twelve are counted as a single book, the Trei Asar ("the Twelve"), and were traditionally written on one scroll. They span a period from the 8th century BCE to the post-exilic era, addressing themes of covenant faithfulness, social justice, divine judgement and restoration. Hosea and Amos prophesied to the northern kingdom of Israel; Haggai and Zechariah encouraged the rebuilding of the Second Temple.

Order

The order of the books differs slightly between the Masoretic Text and the Greek Septuagint, in which the first six are arranged as Hosea, Amos, Micah, Joel, Obadiah and Jonah. In Christian Old Testaments the twelve individual books close the canon, ending with Malachi before the New Testament.

Scholarship

Modern scholarship studies the Twelve both as individual compositions and as a redacted collection, noting editorial links — such as shared catchwords and thematic framing — that bind the books into a larger literary whole.
