Arsinoe II

Arsinoe II Philadelphus (Greek: Ἀρσινόη ἡ Φιλάδελφος; 316 BC – between 270 and 268 BC) was a Ptolemaic queen of Egypt, first married to King Lysimachus of Thrace, then to her half-brother Ptolemy Ceraunus, and finally to her full brother Ptolemy II, with whom she ruled as a deified co-monarch. She was a powerful political figure in her own right: she influenced foreign policy, patronised cult and scholarship, and may have accompanied military campaigns.

Life

Arsinoe was the daughter of Ptolemy I, founder of the Ptolemaic dynasty. Around 300 BC she married the elderly Lysimachus, king of Thrace and Macedon; court intrigue — she accused her stepson Agathocles of treason, leading to his execution — contributed to Lysimachus's defeat and death at Corupedium (281 BC). She briefly married her half-brother Ptolemy Ceraunus, who murdered two of her sons by Lysimachus, and fled to Egypt, where she married her brother Ptolemy II (c. 274 BC). The sibling marriage, shocking to Greek sensibilities but in line with pharaonic tradition and the myth of Isis and Osiris, earned the pair the cult title Theoi Philadelphoi ("Sibling-loving Gods").

Rule and deification

Arsinoe appears in Egyptian temple reliefs as a goddess in her own right with her own crown (combining the Lower Egyptian crown, ram horns, and solar disk), received a widespread cult after her death, lent her name to cities (including Arsinoe in the Fayum), and appears on coinage for generations. A famous monumental stele records Ptolemy II's grief and her deification; the Alexandrian festival of the Arsinoeia honoured her, and objects such as a magnetic-levitation votive statue were described by ancient authors.
