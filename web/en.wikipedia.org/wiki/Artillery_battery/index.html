Artillery battery

A battery of field howitzers in firing position

In military organizations, an artillery battery is a unit or system of artillery guns, mortars, rocket launchers or surface-to-surface missiles grouped for command and control by a single commander. Historically the term also denoted a fortified position — a set of guns mounted in a fort or coastal defence work, as in a "shore battery".

Organization

A modern field battery typically consists of six to eight guns, their crews, towing vehicles, fire-direction personnel and communications, and is commanded by a major or captain. Several batteries form a battalion (in Commonwealth usage, a regiment). Shipboard and air-defence usage follow similar principles: all guns controllable as a single fire unit constitute a battery.

The usage dates to the 18th century, when artillery was massed under one commander to concentrate fire. Napoleon famously employed "grand batteries" of dozens of guns to break enemy lines, notably at Wagram and Borodino.

Other meanings

"Battery" can also refer to the group of weapons themselves — for example a ship's main battery — and historically to any emplacement of artillery in siege works. In naval architecture, the term survives in "battery deck".
