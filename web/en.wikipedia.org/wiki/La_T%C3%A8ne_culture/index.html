La Tène culture

The La Tène culture was a European Iron Age culture, named after the archaeological site of La Tène on the northern shore of Lake Neuchâtel in Switzerland. It flourished from about 450 BC until the Roman conquest in the 1st century BC and is associated with the Celts.

Development

La Tène culture developed out of the earlier Hallstatt culture under Mediterranean influence from the Greeks and Etruscans. It spread across much of Europe, from Ireland and Iberia to Anatolia, where Galatian Celts settled.

Art and material culture

La Tène art is famous for its elegant curvilinear ornament, found on fine metalwork such as torcs, brooches, swords with decorated scabbards, and objects like the Gundestrup cauldron. Settlements included large fortified towns known as oppida. Burials included rich chariot graves and deposits of metalwork in lakes and rivers.
