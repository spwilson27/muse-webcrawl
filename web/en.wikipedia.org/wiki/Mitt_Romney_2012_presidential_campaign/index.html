The 2012 presidential campaign of Mitt Romney, the 70th Governor of Massachusetts, began on June 2, 2011, when Romney formally announced his candidacy for the Republican nomination in the 2012 United States presidential election. Romney had previously run in the 2008 Republican primaries. He secured his party's nomination at the Republican National Convention in Tampa in August 2012, choosing Representative Paul Ryan of Wisconsin as his running mate.

Primaries

Romney entered the race as the presumptive front-runner but faced a rotating cast of conservative challengers, including Rick Perry, Herman Cain, Newt Gingrich and Rick Santorum, through a long primary season. He prevailed on the strength of fundraising, organization and strong debate and primary performances in states such as Florida and Ohio, and his rivals withdrew by April 2012.

General election

Against incumbent President Barack Obama, Romney campaigned on the weak economic recovery, jobs, and repeal of the Affordable Care Act — awkwardly, given that Massachusetts health care reform under Romney had been its model. The campaign was damaged by the release in September of a secretly recorded fundraiser video in which Romney referred to "47 percent" of Americans as dependent on government and unlikely to vote for him. Romney performed strongly in the first presidential debate in Denver, briefly taking the lead in national polls. On election day, November 6, 2012, Obama won re-election with 332 electoral votes to Romney's 206, and 51.1% of the popular vote to Romney's 47.2%.
