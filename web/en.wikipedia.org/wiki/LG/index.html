LG Corporation (Korean: 주식회사 엘지), formerly Lucky-Goldstar, is a South Korean multinational conglomerate holding company headquartered in Seoul. It is one of the country's largest chaebol, with major subsidiaries in electronics (LG Electronics), chemicals and batteries (LG Chem and LG Energy Solution), displays (LG Display) and telecommunications (LG Uplus). The name "LG" comes from the merger of the chemical firm Lucky and the electronics firm GoldStar.

History

Founder Koo In-hwoi established Lak-Hui (pronounced "Lucky") Chemical Industrial Corp in 1947, producing Korea's first cosmetics cream and later pioneering plastics. In 1958 he founded GoldStar, which produced South Korea's first radio, and went on to make the country's first telephones, refrigerators and televisions. Lucky and GoldStar brands coexisted within the group until 1995, when the whole conglomerate rebranded globally as LG, adopting the stylised "Life's Good" identity. The group split in 2021 when the GS and LS families' long-separated branches were joined by a spin-off of some affiliates into a new LX group led by a branch of the founding family.

Major subsidiaries

LG Electronics is a top global maker of televisions — a leader in OLED panels through LG Display — and home appliances. LG Chem is one of the world's largest chemical companies, and its spun-off battery unit, LG Energy Solution, is among the biggest manufacturers of electric-vehicle batteries, supplying carmakers worldwide. Other affiliates include LG CNS (IT services), LG Uplus (mobile and broadband) and LG Household & Health Care (cosmetics and consumer goods). The group also owns the LG Twins baseball team.
