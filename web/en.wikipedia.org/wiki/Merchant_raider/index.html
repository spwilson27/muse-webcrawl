A merchant raider is a merchant ship converted into a warship and disguised as a neutral or friendly merchantman for the purpose of commerce raiding — attacking enemy shipping while avoiding stronger naval forces.

History

Germany employed merchant raiders in both world wars. In the First World War, ships such as SMS Möwe and SMS Wolf made long independent cruises, laying mines and capturing prizes. In the Second World War the Kriegsmarine's Hilfskreuzer (auxiliary cruisers) — including Atlantis, Pinguin and Kormoran — used concealed guns and torpedoes behind hinged flaps and false superstructure. The Kormoran famously sank the Australian cruiser HMAS Sydney in 1941 before sinking herself from damage sustained.

Distinctions

Merchant raiders differ from armed merchant cruisers, which were openly commissioned auxiliaries used for patrol and convoy escort, and from Q-ships, the disguised anti-submarine decoys operated by the Allies.
