Sassicaia is an Italian red wine produced by Tenuta San Guido in the Bolgheri area of the Tuscan coast. It is regarded as one of the finest Cabernet-based wines in the world and is the original and best-known of the "Super Tuscan" wines — innovative Tuscan reds made outside the traditional DOC rules.

History

The wine was created by Marchese Mario Incisa della Rocchetta, who planted Cabernet Sauvignon vines at his San Guido estate in the 1940s, inspired by the great wines of Bordeaux. For two decades the wine was made only for family consumption; with the help of the eminent oenologist Giacomo Tachis, the first commercial vintage was released in 1968. The 1985 vintage received a celebrated 100-point score from critic Robert Parker, cementing Sassicaia's international reputation.

Appellation

Sassicaia is unique in Italian wine law: since 2013 it has had its own DOC, Bolgheri Sassicaia, the only Italian appellation named for a single estate's wine. The name derives from the Italian word sassi, meaning stones, reflecting the stony soils of the vineyards.
