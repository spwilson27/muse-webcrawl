Leave of absence

A leave of absence (LOA) is a period of time that an employee, student, or officeholder is permitted to be away from their primary duties while retaining their position or status. Leaves may be paid or unpaid, voluntary or mandatory, and are granted for reasons such as illness, family care, education, military service, or personal matters.

Types

Common categories include medical leave, parental leave, bereavement leave, sabbatical leave in academia, and statutory family leave. In the United States, the Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 (FMLA) entitles eligible employees to up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave for specified family and medical reasons. Universities also grant students leaves of absence for health, financial, or personal reasons, preserving their enrollment status.

Administration

Employers typically require notice and documentation, and policies define how pay, benefits, and seniority are treated during the absence. Some jurisdictions mandate paid leave, and short-term disability insurance is often used to replace income during medical leaves.
