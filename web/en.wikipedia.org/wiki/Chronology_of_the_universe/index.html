Chronology of the universe

The chronology of the universe describes the history of the universe according to Big Bang cosmology, from the Planck epoch about 13.8 billion years ago through inflation, nucleosynthesis, recombination, and the formation of stars, galaxies, the Solar System, and life on Earth.

Early universe

After the Planck epoch came grand unification, inflation, quark and hadron epochs, and Big Bang nucleosynthesis in the first minutes, forming hydrogen, helium, and traces of lithium. At about 380,000 years, recombination made the universe transparent, releasing the cosmic microwave background.

Later evolution

The first stars ignited after a few hundred million years (cosmic dawn), galaxies assembled, and about 9 billion years later the Solar System formed (4.6 billion years ago). The universe's expansion is now accelerating under dark energy.
