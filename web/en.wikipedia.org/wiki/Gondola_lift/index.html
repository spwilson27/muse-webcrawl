Gondola lift

A gondola lift is a type of aerial lift transport in which enclosed cabins are suspended from a continuously circulating cable. Cabins are attached to the cable by grips that can be released in the stations, allowing the cabins to slow for boarding and alighting while the cable keeps moving — these are called detachable-grip gondola lifts, or monocable detachable gondolas.

Design

A modern gondola cabin typically carries 4 to 15 passengers; the largest systems use cabins for 30 or more on two cables (bicable and tricable gondolas). Gondolas differ from aerial tramways, whose two large cabins simply shuttle back and forth, and from chairlifts, which carry passengers on open chairs. Major manufacturers include Doppelmayr, Leitner, and Poma.

Uses

Gondola lifts are common at ski resorts, where they serve as principal mountain access lifts protected from the weather, and at scenic attractions such as the Ngong Ping 360 in Hong Kong and the Sugarloaf cable car in Rio de Janeiro. Since the 2000s they have also been adopted as urban public transport, notably the Metrocable systems of Medellín, Colombia, and the networks of La Paz, Bolivia (Mi Teleférico), because they can cross steep or congested terrain cheaply and quickly.
