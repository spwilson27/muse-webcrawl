Brazil v Germany (2014 FIFA World Cup)

The first semi-final of the 2014 FIFA World Cup, played on 8 July 2014 at the Estádio Mineirão in Belo Horizonte, saw host nation Brazil lose 1–7 to Germany — the worst defeat in Brazil's World Cup history and one of the most shocking results in the tournament's history. In Brazil the match is remembered as the Mineirazo, echoing the Maracanazo of 1950. Germany scored five goals in the first 29 minutes, four of them in a seven-minute spell, on the way to winning the World Cup days later.

Background

Brazil reached the semi-final weakened: star forward Neymar had fractured a vertebra in the quarter-final against Colombia, and captain Thiago Silva was suspended. Germany, coached by Joachim Löw, had progressed steadily through the tournament. The teams had last met in the 2002 World Cup final, which Brazil won.

Match

Thomas Müller opened the scoring in the 11th minute. Miroslav Klose made it 2–0 in the 23rd minute, in the process scoring his 16th World Cup goal and breaking Ronaldo's all-time tournament record. Toni Kroos then scored twice in two minutes (24' and 26'), and Sami Khedira added a fifth at 29 minutes. Substitute André Schürrle scored twice in the second half (69' and 79') before Oscar scored a late consolation for Brazil in the 90th minute.

Aftermath

The result provoked national mourning in Brazil, wide media criticism of coach Luiz Felipe Scolari — who resigned after Brazil also lost the third-place playoff to the Netherlands — and became a lasting cultural reference point, with the phrase "7–1" (sete a um) entering everyday Brazilian speech. Germany went on to beat Argentina 1–0 in the final to win their fourth World Cup.
