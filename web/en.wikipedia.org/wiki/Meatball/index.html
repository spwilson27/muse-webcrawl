Meatball

A meatball is ground meat rolled into a small ball, sometimes along with other ingredients, such as bread crumbs, minced onion, eggs, butter, and seasonings. Meatballs are cooked by frying, baking, steaming, or braising in sauce. There are many types of meatballs using different types of meat and spices, found in cuisines around the world.

History

The ancient Roman cookbook Apicius included recipes for meatballs made from peacock, pheasant, rabbit, chicken, and pork. The Persian kofta (from "to pound" or "to grind") spread through the Middle East, South Asia, and Central Asia. Chinese meatballs such as the large steamed pork "lion's head" (shizi tou) of Huaiyang cuisine date back centuries, as do Japanese tsukune, Indonesian bakso, and Turkish köfte.

Varieties by region

  • Italy – Polpette, typically made of beef and/or pork with bread crumbs, Parmesan, and herbs, served in tomato sauce or soup. The famous spaghetti and meatballs combination is an Italian-American creation of the early 20th century.
  • Sweden – Köttbullar, small beef-and-pork meatballs served with cream sauce, mashed potatoes, and lingonberry jam; internationally popularized by IKEA stores.
  • Middle East and South Asia – Kofta, spiced balls or loaves of minced lamb or beef, grilled, fried, or curried.
  • China – Lion's head meatballs, beef balls in Cantonese soups and hot pot, and Fuzhou fish balls.
  • Spain – Albóndigas, often served in almond or tomato sauce as tapas.
  • United States – Cocktail meatballs in grape jelly and chili sauce; meatball submarine sandwiches.

Preparation

Ground meat is combined with a binder (bread crumbs soaked in milk—panade—egg, or rice) and seasonings, formed into balls, and cooked. Panade keeps the texture tender by preventing meat proteins from forming a dense network. Sizes range from bite-size cocktail balls to large lion's head meatballs of 8–10 cm.

Records

The largest meatball ever recorded weighed over 500 kg (about 1,100 lb), made in New Hampshire in 2009, and meatball-eating contests and an annual National Meatball Day (March 9) are celebrated in the United States.
