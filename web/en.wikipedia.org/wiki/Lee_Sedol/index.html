Lee Sedol

Lee Sedol (Korean: 이세돌; born 2 March 1983), or Lee Se-dol, is a South Korean former professional Go player of 9 dan rank. Nicknamed "The Strong Stone" (Ssen-dol), he is widely regarded as one of the greatest Go players of all time. In February 2016 he ranked second in international titles (18), behind only Lee Chang-ho.

Lee is best known internationally for his five-game match against the computer program AlphaGo in March 2016, which he lost 1–4. His single victory in game four — achieved with a celebrated "divine move" (move 78) — made him the only human player to defeat AlphaGo under match conditions, and the match is widely credited with transforming public understanding of artificial intelligence.

Early life and career

Lee was born in Sinan County, South Jeolla Province, and learned Go at a young age. He turned professional in 1995 at the age of 12. Known in his youth for an aggressive, fighting style and exceptional reading ability in complicated positions, he rose rapidly through the professional ranks and won his first international title, the Fujitsu Cup, in 2002.

Playing style

Commentators describe Lee's style as creative and unpredictable, favouring sharp fighting and complex ko battles. He was famous for the "broken ladder" game against Hong Chang-sik in the 2003 LG Cup, in which he deliberately allowed a ladder — normally a beginner's trap — to run across the board because he had read out that the position worked in his favour.

The AlphaGo match

In March 2016 Lee faced DeepMind's AlphaGo in Seoul in a best-of-five match with a prize of US$1 million. AlphaGo won the first three games, securing the match, but Lee won the fourth game after nine hours of play, eliciting an emotional response from commentators. He lost the fifth game and the series 1–4.

Retirement

Lee announced his retirement from professional play in November 2019, stating that the rise of Go AI had made it impossible for a human to be "at the top" even with superhuman effort. His farewell match against the Korean AI program HanDol in December 2019 ended with a 1–2 loss, though he won the first game.
