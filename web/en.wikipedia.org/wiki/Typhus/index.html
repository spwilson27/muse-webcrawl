SpecialtyInfectious disease
SymptomsFever, headache, rash, muscle pain, delirium

Typhus, also known as typhus fever, is a group of infectious diseases caused by bacteria of the genus Rickettsia and transmitted to humans by arthropod vectors. The name derives from the Greek typhos, meaning smoky or hazy, describing the state of mind of those affected. Typhus should not be confused with typhoid fever, a different disease caused by Salmonella bacteria.

Types

Epidemic typhus, caused by Rickettsia prowazekii and spread by the human body louse, is the most severe form and historically devastated armies and crowded populations. Murine (endemic) typhus, caused by Rickettsia typhi, is spread by fleas. Scrub typhus, caused by Orientia tsutsugamushi, is transmitted by chigger mites in parts of Asia and the Pacific.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms generally begin one to two weeks after exposure and include high fever, severe headache, muscle aches, and a rash that starts on the trunk and spreads outward. Epidemic typhus can progress to delirium, stupor, and, untreated, death in a substantial fraction of cases.

History

Typhus epidemics repeatedly decided the course of wars: it decimated Napoleon's Grande Armée during the 1812 retreat from Russia and killed millions on the Eastern Front during and after the First World War. The introduction of DDT delousing and effective antibiotics — doxycycline is the treatment of choice — has greatly reduced its impact, though outbreaks still occur in conditions of war, displacement, and poverty.
