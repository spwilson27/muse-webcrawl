The Association for Psychological Science (APS), previously the American Psychological Society, is an international non-profit organisation dedicated to the advancement of scientific psychology and its representation at national and international level. Founded in 1988, it has grown to more than 35,000 members — researchers, practitioners, and students — in over 80 countries.

History

APS was founded by a group of researchers who felt that the American Psychological Association had become dominated by clinical practitioners and was neglecting the scientific wing of the discipline. The new society positioned itself as a voice for experimental and applied science, launched the journal Psychological Science in 1990, and adopted its present name in 2006 to reflect its international membership. Its founding president was Janet Taylor Spence.

Publications and activities

The association publishes several influential journals, including Psychological Science, Current Directions in Psychological Science, Psychological Science in the Public Interest, Perspectives on Psychological Science, Clinical Psychological Science, and Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science. Its monthly magazine, the Observer, carries news and commentary for members. APS organises an annual convention, advocates for research funding, promotes open and replicable science practices — including registered reports and replication studies prompted by the replication crisis — and honours achievement through awards such as the William James Fellow Award.
