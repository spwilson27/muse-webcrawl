Daulat Beg Oldi (also spelled Daulat Beg Oldie) is an Indian military base and advanced landing ground in the Karakoram mountains of northern Ladakh, close to the Line of Actual Control with China and just south of the Karakoram Pass. At an elevation of about 5,065 metres (16,614 feet), its airstrip is among the highest in the world. The name, in a Turkic language, is traditionally rendered "where the rich man died", after a legend about a wealthy caravan merchant who perished there on the old silk-road route between Yarkand and Leh.

History

The site lay on the historic caravan trade route over the Karakoram. During the Sino-Indian War of 1962 the Indian Army established a post there, and the airstrip was first used by an Indian Air Force transport shortly afterwards. Operations lapsed for decades until the strip was reactivated on 31 May 2008, when an An-32 transport landed — then the highest airfield operation of its kind. In August 2013 a C-130J Super Hercules landed at Daulat Beg Oldi, a world record for the type at such altitude.

Strategic significance

The base anchors India's presence in the "sub-sector north", the northernmost part of the disputed border, near China's Xinjiang region and the China–Pakistan Karakoram Highway. In April–May 2013 a Chinese platoon camped some 19 km inside the Indian-claimed line near Daulat Beg Oldi, producing a three-week standoff resolved diplomatically. India has since built the 255 km Darbuk–Shyok–DBO road to supply the sector, completed in 2019, making the outpost strategically and symbolically important in the ongoing border dispute.
