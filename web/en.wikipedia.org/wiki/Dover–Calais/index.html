Dover–Calais

Dover–Calais refers to the short sea crossing of the Strait of Dover between Dover in Kent, England, and Calais in Hauts-de-France, France — at about 33.3 km (20.7 miles) the shortest sea route between Great Britain and mainland Europe, and one of the busiest international ferry routes in the world.

RouteDover, England — Calais, France
WaterwayStrait of Dover, English Channel
Distance~33 km (21 mi)
Crossing time~90 minutes by ferry

The crossing

Ferries have plied between Dover and Calais for centuries. Modern roll-on/roll-off services carry cars, coaches and freight lorries, with dozens of sailings a day operated by companies such as P&O Ferries, DFDS and Irish Ferries. The Port of Dover is the busiest passenger port in the United Kingdom, and Calais is France's leading passenger port.

Channel Tunnel

Since 1994 the fixed link of the Channel Tunnel has offered an alternative route, with Eurotunnel vehicle shuttles running between Folkestone and Coquelles near Calais and Eurostar passenger trains passing through the tunnel beneath the strait.

History

The strait has been swum, rowed and flown across in famous feats: Matthew Webb made the first recorded unaided Channel swim in 1875, and Louis Blériot flew from Calais to Dover in 1909. During the Second World War the route was central to the Dunkirk evacuation of 1940, when troops were brought back across the strait to Dover and other ports.
