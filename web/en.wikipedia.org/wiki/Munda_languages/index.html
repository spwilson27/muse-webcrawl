Munda languages

The Munda languages are a group of languages spoken by ethnic Munda peoples in the eastern and central regions of India and in parts of Bangladesh. They form one of the two primary branches of the Austroasiatic language family, the other being the Mon–Khmer languages of mainland Southeast Asia. Altogether the Munda languages have roughly nine to ten million speakers, and they are believed to have been spoken in the region for several thousand years, possibly arriving with rice farmers migrating from Southeast Asia.

Languages and classification

The largest Munda language is Santali, with over seven million speakers and official status in India; it is written in the Ol Chiki script devised by Raghunath Murmu in 1925. Other significant languages include Mundari, Ho, Korku, Kharia, Sora and Kherwarian and Koraput varieties. Scholars typically divide the family into North Munda, spoken in the Chota Nagpur plateau, and South Munda, spoken further south in Odisha and Andhra Pradesh.

Features

Unlike most other Austroasiatic languages, which are analytic, the Munda languages are notably agglutinative, with complex verb morphology marking subject, object, tense, aspect and mood, and they are typically subject–object–verb in word order — traits often attributed to long contact with neighbouring Indo-Aryan and Dravidian languages of the South Asian linguistic area.
