Fujiwara no Kintō

Fujiwara no Kintō (Japanese: 藤原 公任, 966 – 4 February 1041), also known as Shijō-dainagon, was a Japanese poet, courtier, and bureaucrat of the Heian period. A member of the powerful Northern Fujiwara clan, he was the son of the regent Fujiwara no Yoritada and the great-grandson of Fujiwara no Tadahira. He rose to the rank of dainagon (major counsellor) and was famed at the imperial court for his learning, calligraphy, and musical skill as much as for his verse.

Poetic legacy

Kintō is remembered above all as a compiler and arbiter of poetic taste. He is credited with the Shūi Wakashū tradition of anthologies, compiled the influential Wakan rōeishū (Collection of Japanese and Chinese Poems for Singing, c. 1013), which paired Japanese waka with Chinese verse, and selected the celebrated list of the Thirty-six Poetry Immortals (Sanjūrokkasen), which fixed the canon of classical Japanese poets for centuries. His critical judgement shaped the standards of Heian court poetry, and his own poems appear in imperial anthologies including the Shūi Wakashū and later collections; he is also included in the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu.

The poet and diarist Murasaki Shikibu served at the same court, and anecdotes about Kintō's sharp ear for poetry — including his correction of a single character in a poem, earning him the nickname "Sir One-Character" — are preserved in later tale literature.
