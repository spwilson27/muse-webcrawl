Dihedral (aeronautics)

Dihedral angle is the upward angle from horizontal of the wings or tailplane of a fixed-wing aircraft. "Anhedral angle" is the name given to negative dihedral angle, that is, when there is a downward angle from horizontal of the wings or tailplane.

Effect on stability

Dihedral angle has a strong influence on lateral stability. When an aircraft with dihedral rolls or sideslips, the lower wing meets the airflow at a higher angle of attack and generates more lift than the raised wing, producing a restoring rolling moment — known as the dihedral effect. It is one of the main contributors to stability in the roll axis and to the aircraft's spiral stability characteristics.

Design practice

High-wing aircraft often need little geometric dihedral because the fuselage position adds effective dihedral, while low-wing airliners typically use several degrees, also improving ground clearance for engines. Swept wings add effective dihedral, so designs with strong sweep — and many military aircraft — use neutral dihedral or anhedral to avoid excessive roll stability and Dutch roll tendencies.
