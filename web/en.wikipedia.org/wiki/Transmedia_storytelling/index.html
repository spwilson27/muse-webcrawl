Transmedia storytelling

Transmedia storytelling (also known as transmedia narrative or multiplatform storytelling) is the technique of telling a single story or story experience across multiple platforms and formats using current digital technologies. Rather than simply adapting a story from one medium to another, a transmedia production develops the story world across media — for example film, television, comics, novels, video games, websites and alternate reality games — such that each medium makes a distinctive and valuable contribution to the whole, and each entry can be enjoyed independently while rewarding engagement with the others.

Origins and theory

The term was popularised by media scholar Henry Jenkins, notably in his 2003 MIT Technology Review article and his book Convergence Culture (2006), which analysed franchises such as The Matrix, whose narrative was distributed across films, animated shorts, comics and the game Enter the Matrix. Jenkins identified principles including spreadability, drillability, continuity and multiplicity, subjectivity, and worldbuilding. Earlier and parallel examples include the Star Wars expanded universe and the marketing campaigns for The Blair Witch Project.

Practice

Transmedia techniques are used in entertainment franchises, marketing ("transmedia branding"), education and documentary. The Producers Guild of America recognised a "transmedia producer" credit in 2010. Alternate reality games such as I Love Bees (for Halo 2) are an intensive form, blending fiction with real-world interaction.
