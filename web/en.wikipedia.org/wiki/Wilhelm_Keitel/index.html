Wilhelm Bodewin Johann Gustav Keitel (22 September 1882 – 16 October 1946) was a German field marshal who held office as chief of the Oberkommando der Wehrmacht (OKW), the high command of Nazi Germany's armed forces, during World War II.

An artillery officer from a Hanoverian landowning family, Keitel served in the First World War and remained in the Reichswehr. In February 1938 Adolf Hitler appointed him chief of the newly created OKW. Keitel became one of Hitler's most loyal subordinates, signing or transmitting criminal orders including the Commissar Order and the Commando Order, and he was derisively nicknamed "Lakeitel" — a play on Lakai, "lackey" — by fellow officers for his subservience.

Second World War

Keitel was promoted to Generalfeldmarschall in July 1940 after the fall of France. He countersigned the instrument of unconditional surrender at Berlin-Karlshorst on 8 May 1945, following the Reims signing by Alfred Jodl. He also signed orders that authorised the murder of Soviet political commissars, downed Allied airmen, and commandos — decisions that would define his trial.

Trial and execution

Arrested after the war, Keitel was tried by the International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg. He was convicted on all four counts — conspiracy, crimes against peace, war crimes, and crimes against humanity — and sentenced to death. His plea to be shot rather than hanged as a soldier was denied. He was executed by hanging on 16 October 1946.
