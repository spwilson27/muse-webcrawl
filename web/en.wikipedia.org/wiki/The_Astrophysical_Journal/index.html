The Astrophysical Journal (often abbreviated ApJ) is a peer-reviewed scientific journal covering astronomy and astrophysics. It was founded in 1895 by the American astronomers George Ellery Hale and James Edward Keeler and has been one of the principal journals of the field ever since, publishing much of the landmark research of modern astrophysics, including many Nobel Prize–winning papers on topics from the cosmic microwave background to exoplanets and gravitational waves.

Structure

The journal is published on behalf of the American Astronomical Society by IOP Publishing. It comprises the main journal; The Astrophysical Journal Letters (ApJL), for rapid publication of short, significant results; and The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series (ApJS), for extensive data sets, catalogues and long articles. Since 2009 the journal has been published only in electronic form.

Notable editors and papers

Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar served as editor for nearly two decades from 1952, raising the journal's scientific standards. A large fraction of the most-cited papers in astronomy have appeared in the journal, and its name is conventionally shortened in references simply to "ApJ".
