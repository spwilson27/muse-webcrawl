NORAID
Full name: Irish Northern Aid Committee
Founded: 1970
Founder: Martin Galvin and others
Location: United States

The Irish Northern Aid Committee, commonly known as NORAID, is an Irish American membership organization founded after the start of the Troubles in Northern Ireland. It raised funds in the United States ostensibly for the families of Irish republican prisoners and for humanitarian relief.

History

NORAID was founded in 1970 by Irish immigrants and Irish American activists, including Martin Galvin, who became its public spokesman. The organization maintained that its funds went to charitable relief through the Green Cross in Ireland. Critics, including the British and Irish governments, alleged that money was diverted to the Provisional Irish Republican Army (IRA) for weapons; NORAID denied this.

Legal issues

In 1981, a U.S. federal court required NORAID to register as a foreign agent of the IRA under the Foreign Agents Registration Act, identifying the IRA as its foreign principal. Fundraising declined after the Northern Ireland peace process and the 1998 Good Friday Agreement. The organization continued in reduced form, associated with support for republican causes.
