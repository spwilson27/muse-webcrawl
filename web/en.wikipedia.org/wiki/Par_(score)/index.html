Par (score)

In golf, par is the predetermined number of strokes that a scratch (or zero-handicap) golfer should require to complete a hole, a round, or a tournament. Par is primarily determined by the distance of the hole from the tee to the green, taking elevation, terrain, and hazards into account. Nearly all holes in modern golf are par 3, par 4, or par 5, and a typical 18-hole course has a total par of 70 to 72.

Scoring relative to par

Scores on each hole are described by terms comparing them to par:

  • Albatross (double eagle) — three strokes under par
  • Eagle — two strokes under par
  • Birdie — one stroke under par
  • Par — equal to par
  • Bogey — one stroke over par
  • Double bogey — two strokes over par
  • Triple bogey — three strokes over par

In professional tournaments, cumulative scores are reported relative to par (e.g., "ten under par"), and the winner is typically the player with the lowest total relative to par over the event.

Determining par

Guideline yardages for par depend on the tees used and differ slightly for men's and women's play. As a rough convention for men, holes up to about 260 yards are par 3; holes from roughly 240 to 490 yards are par 4; and longer holes are par 5. A hole's par may also be adjusted for significant uphill or downhill slopes or forced carries. The governing bodies (the USGA and The R&A) publish par guidelines, but course owners ultimately set the par printed on the scorecard.

History

The word "par" entered golf from finance, where it denoted the normal or expected value of a stock. In the 19th century the expected score for a hole was commonly called its "bogey score," standardized by Colonel Bogey; "par" as the target for expert play gained currency in the 1890s and 1900s, with the first recorded use in the modern sense dated to 1898.

Other uses

Variants of the concept appear in disc golf, footgolf, and miniature golf, where each hole likewise has an assigned par. The phrase "par for the course" has passed into general English as a metaphor for something normal or expected.
