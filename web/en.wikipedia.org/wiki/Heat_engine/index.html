Heat engine

Heat engine
FunctionConverts heat into work
Efficiency limitCarnot efficiency
ExamplesSteam engine, internal combustion engine, Stirling engine
[Image: Diagram of a heat engine taking heat from a hot reservoir and exhausting to a cold sink]

A heat engine is a system that converts thermal energy into mechanical work by exploiting a temperature difference between a hot reservoir and a cold sink. Heat engines power most of the world's electricity generation and transport, from steam turbines to the internal combustion engine.

Carnot limit

Sadi Carnot showed in 1824 that no heat engine can exceed the efficiency of an ideal reversible cycle operating between the same two temperatures; efficiency depends only on those temperatures. The concept of entropy, developed later, explains why waste heat is unavoidable. Refrigerators and heat pumps are heat engines run in reverse.
