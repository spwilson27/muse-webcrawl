Artin L-function

In mathematics, an Artin L-function is a type of Dirichlet series associated to a linear representation of a Galois group G. Introduced by Emil Artin in 1923, these functions are fundamental objects in algebraic number theory and form a central part of the Langlands program.

Definition

Given a finite-dimensional complex representation ρ of the Galois group of a Galois extension of number fields, the Artin L-function L(s, ρ) is defined by an Euler product over prime ideals, in which the local factor at each prime is built from the characteristic polynomial of a Frobenius element acting on the inertia-invariant subspace of the representation. For the trivial representation it reduces to the Dedekind zeta function of the base field.

Properties and conjectures

Artin L-functions satisfy a functional equation relating values at s and 1 − s. The Artin conjecture asserts that the L-function of any nontrivial irreducible representation is holomorphic (entire) on the whole complex plane; it remains open in general, though it is known for one-dimensional representations (via class field theory and Dirichlet/Hecke L-functions) and in many two-dimensional cases through work connected with modularity. Artin L-functions also factor the Dedekind zeta functions of Galois extensions, generalising the factorisation of the Riemann zeta function into Dirichlet L-functions over cyclotomic fields.
