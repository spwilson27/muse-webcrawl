Overview

The West Wing is an American political drama television series created by Aaron Sorkin that aired on NBC from September 22, 1999, to May 14, 2006. Set in the West Wing of the White House, it depicts the administration of fictional Democratic President Josiah 'Jed' Bartlet (Martin Sheen), a Nobel laureate economist and former governor of New Hampshire, and the work of his senior staff.

The series is renowned for Sorkin's rapid-fire dialogue and the 'walk and talk' tracking shots following staffers through White House corridors. Its main cast includes Allison Janney as press secretary C.J. Cregg, Rob Lowe as Sam Seaborn, Bradley Whitford as Josh Lyman, Richard Schiff as Toby Ziegler, and John Spencer as chief of staff Leo McGarry.

Reception and influence

The West Wing won 26 Emmy Awards, including Outstanding Drama Series four consecutive times (2000-2003), and three Golden Globes. Praised by critics and by many political professionals for its idealistic depiction of public service, it also drew criticism for its liberal slant. After Sorkin's departure at the end of season four, the show continued under John Wells until 2006, concluding with the election of Bartlet's successor, Matt Santos. A reunion special was staged in 2020 to benefit voter-participation efforts.
