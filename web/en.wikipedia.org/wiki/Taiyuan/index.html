Taiyuan

Taiyuan is the capital and largest city of Shanxi province in North China. Located on the Fen River in the fertile Taiyuan Basin, it has long been a strategic and industrial centre, historically known by names including Bingzhou, Jinyang, and Longcheng. The prefecture-level city has a population of over five million.

History

The area was the fief of the ancient state of Jin and later a frontier stronghold guarding northern China. Taiyuan served as a secondary capital under several dynasties and as the seat from which Li Yuan launched the Tang dynasty in 617. In the early 20th century the warlord Yan Xishan ruled Shanxi from Taiyuan for decades. The city later developed into a major centre of coal, steel, and heavy industry.

Landmarks and economy

Notable sites include the Jinci Temple, the Twin Pagoda Temple, and the Shanxi Museum. Taiyuan remains a hub of China's coal and metallurgical industries, with growing aerospace and equipment manufacturing, and is served by Taiyuan Wusu International Airport and high-speed rail links.
