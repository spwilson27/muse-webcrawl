The Houston Astros sign stealing scandal was a major scandal in Major League Baseball (MLB) in which members of the Houston Astros were found to have illegally stolen signs using technology during the 2017 and 2018 seasons. The scandal broke in November 2019, when former Astros pitcher Mike Fiers told journalists Ken Rosenthal and Evan Drellich of The Athletic that the team had used a camera in center field to read the catchers' signs, relaying the type of upcoming pitch to batters by banging on a trash can in the dugout tunnel.

Investigation

MLB's Department of Investigations interviewed dozens of witnesses and reviewed thousands of documents and messages. Commissioner Rob Manfred's report, issued on January 13, 2020, confirmed that the Astros used a video camera and monitor to decode signs in real time during the 2017 season — including the postseason, in which Houston won the 2017 World Series — and that the scheme continued, in modified form, into 2018 using the replay room. The report described the scheme as player-driven, with bench coach Alex Cora identified as an active participant.

Penalties and fallout

MLB fined the Astros $5 million, the maximum permitted under the league constitution, and stripped the club of its first- and second-round draft picks in 2020 and 2021. General manager Jeff Luhnow and manager A. J. Hinch were each suspended for the 2020 season; the Astros fired both men the same day. Cora parted ways with the Boston Red Sox, whom he had managed to the 2018 title, and Carlos Beltrán, a 2017 Astros player who had just been hired to manage the New York Mets, resigned before managing a game. No players were disciplined, the commissioner citing the practical difficulty of punishing individual players and a grant of immunity in exchange for testimony, and the Astros' 2017 championship was not vacated — a decision that generated sustained controversy among fans, players, and media.
