Al-Ashraf Sayf ad-Din Qa'it Bay (c. 1416/1418 – 8 August 1496), known as Qaitbey, was the eighteenth Burji Mamluk Sultan of Egypt, ruling from 1468 to 1496. A Circassian purchased as a slave-soldier by Sultan Barsbay, he rose through the Mamluk military hierarchy, was manumitted, and after a succession of short-lived sultans was elevated to the throne at about fifty years of age — unusually old for a new sultan.

Reign

Qaitbey's twenty-eight-year reign was the longest of the Burji period and brought a measure of stability. He fought two wars against the rising Ottoman Empire in Anatolia (1485–1491), ending in stalemate, campaigned in the Hijaz and Syria, reformed the administration, and stabilised the currency, although the spice trade through Egypt was beginning to be threatened by Portuguese expansion into the Indian Ocean.

Architecture

Qaitbey is remembered above all as the greatest architectural patron of the late Mamluk era. His buildings, in a refined style of carved stone domes and minarets, survive across Cairo, Alexandria, Mecca, Medina, Jerusalem and Syria. Most famous is the Citadel of Qaitbey in Alexandria (1477), a fortress built on the island of Pharos over the ruins of the ancient Lighthouse designed by Sostratus of Cnidus, using some of its fallen stone. His funerary complex in Cairo's City of the Dead is considered a masterpiece of Mamluk architecture and appeared on the Egyptian one-pound note.
