Tri-state water dispute

The tri-state water dispute is a long-running legal and political conflict among the U.S. states of Georgia, Alabama, and Florida over the allocation of water in the Apalachicola–Chattahoochee–Flint (ACF) River Basin and the Alabama–Coosa–Tallapoosa (ACT) basin in the southeastern United States. The dispute, sometimes called the "water wars," has continued since about 1990.

Background

Metropolitan Atlanta depends on Lake Lanier and the Chattahoochee River for much of its water supply, while downstream users in Alabama (including farmers and a nuclear plant) and Florida (including the ecologically and commercially important Apalachicola Bay oyster fishery) depend on adequate flows in the same river system. Federal dams operated by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers sit at the center of the conflict.

Litigation

The states have fought through interstate compacts that expired without agreement, Corps of Engineers litigation, and an original-jurisdiction case in the U.S. Supreme Court, Florida v. Georgia, which the Court decided in Georgia's favor in 2021 after finding Florida had not proved that Georgia's consumption caused the collapse of the Apalachicola oyster fishery. Related disputes over the Corps' water control manuals continued in lower federal courts.
