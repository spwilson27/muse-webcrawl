Energy in Australia

Major sourcesCoal, natural gas, oil, renewables
StatusMajor global energy exporter

Energy in Australia is dominated by fossil fuels. Australia is one of the world's largest exporters of coal and liquefied natural gas, and historically generated most of its electricity from coal. Since the 2010s, wind and especially rooftop solar have grown rapidly, and Australia has among the highest per-capita uptake of household photovoltaic systems in the world.

Electricity

The National Electricity Market interconnects the eastern and southern states, while Western Australia and the Northern Territory operate separate grids. Coal-fired generation has declined from around three quarters of supply toward roughly half, with closures of ageing plants announced across New South Wales, Victoria, and Queensland, offset by growth in wind, solar, hydroelectricity, and battery storage.

Exports and policy

Energy exports — coal, LNG, and uranium — are a major contributor to export earnings. Climate and energy policy has been politically contested for decades; Australia has committed to net-zero emissions by 2050 and to a legislated target of reducing emissions 43% below 2005 levels by 2030.
