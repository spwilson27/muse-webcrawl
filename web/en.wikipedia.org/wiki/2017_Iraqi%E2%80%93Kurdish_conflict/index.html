The 2017 Iraqi–Kurdish conflict was a brief confrontation between the Iraqi federal government and the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG) that followed the Kurdish independence referendum of 25 September 2017, in which voters overwhelmingly endorsed independence despite Baghdad's opposition.

Background

During the war against the Islamic State, Kurdish Peshmerga forces had taken control of Kirkuk and other disputed territories in 2014 after the Iraqi army withdrew. The referendum, championed by KRG president Masoud Barzani, was rejected as unconstitutional by Iraq's federal government and supreme court, and opposed by Turkey, Iran and the United States.

Operation

On 15–16 October 2017, Iraqi security forces and Popular Mobilization Forces advanced on Kirkuk. Much of the Peshmerga withdrew following an agreement between Baghdad and the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan, and federal forces retook the city, its oil fields and military bases with limited fighting. Within days, federal control extended across most of the disputed territories.

Aftermath

Barzani announced his resignation effective 1 November 2017. The loss of Kirkuk's oil revenues dealt a major blow to the KRG's finances and to the near-term independence project, and relations between Baghdad and Erbil were gradually renegotiated over budgets and oil exports.
