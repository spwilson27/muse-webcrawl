Smithy

A smithy is the workshop of a smith, especially a blacksmith. The term derives from Old English smiþþe. A smithy is centred on a forge or hearth where metal is heated, together with an anvil, hammers, tongs, a quench trough and a bellows or blower.

History

Smithies were once found in nearly every village, producing and repairing tools, horseshoes, nails, hinges and weapons. Industrial mass production from the 19th century reduced their number, though farriers and artisan blacksmiths still work from smithies today, and restored examples are common features of open-air museums.

Equipment

Traditional equipment includes the forge fire (fuelled by coal or coke), anvil, leg vice, quench bucket, and hand tools such as cross-peen and ball-peen hammers. Modern smithies may add power hammers, gas forges and welding equipment.
