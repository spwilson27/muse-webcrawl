Raymond Bernard Cattell (1905–1998) was a British-American psychologist known for his psychometric research into personality and intelligence. Using factor analysis on an unprecedented scale, he sought to discover the basic dimensions of human individuality empirically rather than by intuition.

Contributions

Cattell's factor analyses of personality descriptors led to the Sixteen Personality Factor Questionnaire (16PF), one of the most widely used personality tests. In intelligence research he distinguished fluid intelligence (novel reasoning, peaking in early adulthood) from crystallised intelligence (accumulated knowledge, maintained or growing with age) — a distinction central to later theories such as the Cattell–Horn–Carroll model. He also devised culture-fair intelligence tests intended to reduce linguistic and educational bias.

Controversy

Cattell's writings on eugenics and his Beyondism movement drew lasting controversy, and a planned lifetime-achievement award from the American Psychological Association in 1997 was withdrawn amid protest, though his scientific contributions to trait psychology remain foundational.
