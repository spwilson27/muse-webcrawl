Matched filter

In signal processing, a matched filter is obtained by correlating a known delayed signal, or template, with an unknown signal to detect the presence of the template in the unknown signal. This is equivalent to convolving the unknown signal with a conjugated time-reversed version of the template. The matched filter is the optimal linear filter for maximizing the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in the presence of additive stochastic noise.

Overview

Matched filters are commonly used in radar, in which a known signal is sent out, and the reflected signal is examined for common elements of the out-going signal. Pulse compression is an example of matched filtering. It is so called because the impulse response is matched to input pulse signals. Two-dimensional matched filters are commonly used in image processing, for example, to improve the SNR for X-ray observations.

Derivation

Given an observed signal consisting of a known waveform plus additive white Gaussian noise, the filter that maximizes the output signal-to-noise ratio is the one whose impulse response is a time-reversed, conjugated copy of the known waveform. In the frequency domain, the matched filter's transfer function is proportional to the complex conjugate of the signal's Fourier transform divided by the noise power spectral density; for white noise this reduces to the conjugate spectrum of the signal.

Applications

  • Radar and sonar: pulse compression to increase range resolution and SNR.
  • Digital communications: optimal detection of known pulse shapes in noisy channels.
  • Gravitational-wave astronomy: template banks of matched filters are used to search detector data for known chirp waveforms, as in the detections by LIGO.
  • Medical imaging and astronomy: detection of faint known structures in noise.
