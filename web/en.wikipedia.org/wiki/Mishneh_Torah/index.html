Mishneh Torah

A medieval manuscript of the Mishneh Torah

The Mishneh Torah (Hebrew: מִשְׁנֵה תּוֹרָה, "Repetition of the Torah"), subtitled Sefer Yad ha-Hazaka ("Book of the Strong Hand"), is a code of Jewish law (halakha) compiled by Moses ben Maimon (Maimonides) between 1170 and 1180 in Egypt. Written in clear Mishnaic Hebrew, it is one of the most influential works in Jewish legal history.

Structure

The work comprises fourteen books ("Yad" = 14 in Hebrew numerals), covering all areas of Jewish law, including laws no longer applicable after the destruction of the Temple. The first book, Sefer Madda (Book of Knowledge), includes Maimonides' philosophical foundations and his Thirteen Principles of Faith in the Laws of Repentance and the Laws of the Foundations of the Torah.

Influence

The Mishneh Torah was controversial in its day for presenting rulings without sources and including philosophy, but it became a central authority, shaping later codes such as the Shulchan Aruch, and is studied daily by many through study cycles instituted by the Lubavitcher Rebbe.
