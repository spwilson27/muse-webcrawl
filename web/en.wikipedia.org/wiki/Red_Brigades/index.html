Red Brigades

The Red Brigades (Italian: Brigate Rosse [briˈɡaːte ˈrosse], often abbreviated BR) was an Italian Marxist–Leninist armed organisation, founded in 1970 and active during Italy's Years of Lead. The group sought to create a revolutionary state through armed struggle, and to remove Italy from the Western Alliance, particularly NATO. It is best known internationally for the 1978 kidnapping and murder of former prime minister Aldo Moro.

The Red Brigades' symbol was an asymmetrical five-pointed star. [Image placeholder]

History

The Red Brigades were formed in 1970 by Renato Curcio, Margherita Cagol, and Alberto Franceschini, drawing members from university sociology circles in Trento and from radicalised factory workers in Milan and Turin. Early actions were factory-focused: arson attacks on managers' cars, sabotage, and brief kidnappings of executives.

From the mid-1970s the group escalated to assassinations of police officers, magistrates, journalists, and politicians. In 1974 Curcio and Franceschini were arrested; Cagol freed Curcio from prison in 1975 but was killed by carabinieri later that year.

Kidnapping of Aldo Moro

On 16 March 1978, a BR unit led by Mario Moretti ambushed the motorcade of Aldo Moro, president of the Christian Democracy party and architect of the "historic compromise" with the Italian Communist Party, killing his five bodyguards. Moro was held for 55 days while the group demanded an exchange of prisoners. The government refused to negotiate, and Moro's body was found on 9 May 1978 in the boot of a car in Via Caetani in Rome, symbolically equidistant between the headquarters of the two parties.

Decline

During the 1980s the organisation fractured into factions and was progressively dismantled by mass arrests, aided by legislation granting reduced sentences to collaborators (pentiti). The kidnapping of US general James L. Dozier in 1981 ended with his rescue by Italian special forces. By 1988 the original BR had effectively ceased to operate, though small successor groups styling themselves the New Red Brigades claimed attacks into the early 2000s.
