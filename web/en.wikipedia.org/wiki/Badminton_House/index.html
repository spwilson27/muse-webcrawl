Badminton House

Badminton House is a large country house and Grade I listed building in Badminton, Gloucestershire, England. It has been the principal seat of the Dukes of Beaufort since the late 17th century, when Henry Somerset, later 1st Duke of Beaufort, built a new house on the site in 1682, incorporating parts of an earlier Raglan Castle-derived gabled manor. Successive architects — including William Kent, who designed the north front in the Palladian style in the 1740s — enlarged and remodelled it.

History

The estate passed to the Somerset family in the 17th century. The house gave its name to the sport of badminton, which was popularised there in the 19th century from the game of battledore and shuttlecock played in the Great Hall. Badminton also hosts the famous Badminton Horse Trials, one of the world's leading three-day eventing competitions, first held in 1949. During the Second World War, Queen Mary, widow of George V, lived at Badminton as the guest of her niece, the Duchess of Beaufort.

The estate

The house stands in a large deer park landscaped in the 18th century. It remains a private residence of the 12th Duke of Beaufort and is occasionally opened for events, including concerts and equestrian competitions.
