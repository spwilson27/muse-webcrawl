Date22 September 1980 – 20 August 1988
LocationIran–Iraq border, Persian Gulf

The Iran–Iraq War was an armed conflict between Iran and Iraq that lasted from September 1980 to August 1988. It began with Iraq's invasion of Iran, driven by Saddam Hussein's desire to annex the oil-rich Khuzestan province, prevent the spread of Iran's Islamic Revolution, and establish Iraq as the dominant power in the Persian Gulf.

Course of the war

After initial Iraqi gains, Iran recovered its territory by 1982 and carried the war into Iraq, demanding Saddam's removal. The conflict settled into attritional trench warfare reminiscent of the First World War, including Iraqi use of chemical weapons and the "War of the Cities" missile exchanges. The Tanker War in the Gulf drew in international navies, including the United States.

Aftermath

The war ended in a stalemate under UN Security Council Resolution 598, with borders essentially unchanged and casualties estimated in the hundreds of thousands to over a million. It devastated both economies and set the stage for Iraq's invasion of Kuwait in 1990.
