Alan Sillitoe

Alan Sillitoe, chronicler of working-class Nottingham

Alan Sillitoe (4 March 1928 – 25 April 2010) was an English writer. Associated with the "angry young men" of 1950s British literature, he is best known for his novel Saturday Night and Sunday Morning (1958) and the short story "The Loneliness of the Long Distance Runner" (1959), both of which were adapted into landmark films of the British New Wave starring Albert Finney and Tom Courtenay respectively.

Life

Sillitoe was born into a poor working-class family in Nottingham and left school at 14 to work in a bicycle factory. He joined the RAF at 18 and served as a wireless operator in Malaya; there he contracted tuberculosis and spent 18 months in hospital, where he began reading and writing seriously, before being pensioned out of the service. Living in France and Mallorca with the poet Ruth Fainlight, encouraged by Robert Graves, he reworked his Nottingham material into the stories and novels that made his name.

Work

Saturday Night and Sunday Morning follows factory worker Arthur Seaton, whose defiant creed is "don't let the bastards grind you down", through weekend drinking, affairs and eventual grudging settlement. The story collection The Loneliness of the Long Distance Runner, whose title story is narrated by a Borstal boy who throws a race he could win as an act of rebellion, became key texts of kitchen sink realism. Sillitoe went on to write more than 50 books, including the Frank Dawley trilogy, poetry, travel writing and the memoir Life Without Armour (1995).
