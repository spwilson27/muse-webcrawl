Podcast

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Photograph of a podcaster speaking into a studio microphone with pop filter]

A podcast is a programme made available in digital format for download over the Internet, typically a series of audio episodes to which users can subscribe. The medium is built on RSS: a podcast is fundamentally an RSS feed whose entries carry audio file enclosures, so any podcast app can fetch new episodes automatically — an open distribution system no single company owns.

The technology assembled in 2003–2004: RSS enclosures (Dave Winer), the first podcatcher software, and Adam Curry's promotion of the format; journalist Ben Hammersley coined 'podcasting' in a 2004 Guardian article, blending 'iPod' and 'broadcasting'. Apple's addition of podcasts to iTunes in 2005 brought the medium to the mainstream. It grew slowly as a niche of talk and comedy, then boomed in the 2010s — Serial (2014) proved the narrative form, smartphones and cars made listening effortless, and Spotify's entry (with nine-figure exclusive deals, most famously for Joe Rogan's show) pulled the open ecosystem toward platform exclusivity.

Podcasting's open RSS roots remain its defining tension: a medium born decentralised, now contested between the open feed and the walled garden — a small-scale replay of the web's own history.
