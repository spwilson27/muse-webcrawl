Denizli Province (Turkish: Denizli ili) is a province of Turkey in the Aegean Region, in the southwest of the country. Its capital is the city of Denizli. The province is bordered by Uşak to the north, Burdur, Isparta and Afyon to the east, Muğla to the south, and Aydın and Manisa to the west. Since 2012 the whole province has been administered as a metropolitan municipality, with nineteen districts.

Geography

The province lies largely in the basin of the Büyük Menderes River (the ancient Maeander) and includes fertile plains, high plateaus, and mountains such as Mount Honaz (2,571 m), which is protected as a national park. The climate is typically Aegean-continental: hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters, colder in the uplands.

History and sights

The region was inhabited in antiquity by Phrygians, Lydians, Greeks, and Romans. Its most famous site is Pamukkale ("Cotton Castle"), where mineral-rich thermal waters have formed dazzling white travertine terraces beside the ruins of the Greco-Roman spa city of Hierapolis; together they are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Other ancient cities in the province include Laodicea on the Lycus — one of the Seven Churches of Revelation — Tripolis, and Colossae. The area later came under Seljuk and then Ottoman rule.

Economy

Denizli is one of Turkey's notable industrial and export centres, nicknamed one of the "Anatolian Tigers". Its economy is led by textiles — especially towels, bathrobes, and home textiles woven in the province for export worldwide — alongside marble and travertine quarrying, cable and wire manufacture, agriculture (grapes, figs, cotton), and tourism drawn by Pamukkale and the province's thermal springs. The Denizli rooster, famous for its long crow, is a provincial symbol.
