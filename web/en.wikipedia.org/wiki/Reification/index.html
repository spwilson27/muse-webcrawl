Reification

Reification is the fallacy of treating an abstraction (a belief, concept, or hypothetical construct) as if it were a concrete, real event or physical entity. In other words, it is the error of treating something that is not concrete, such as an idea, as a concrete thing.

In Marxism

In Marxist theory, reification (German: Verdinglichung, "making into a thing") is a concept developed by Georg Lukács in History and Class Consciousness (1923): under capitalism, social relations between people acquire the character of things, and human labour and its products appear as commodities governed by apparently objective laws independent of human will. The concept is closely related to Marx's theory of commodity fetishism.

Other uses

In computer science, reification is the process by which an abstract idea about a program is turned into an explicit data model or object; in statistics and philosophy of science, Alfred North Whitehead's term for a related error was the "fallacy of misplaced concreteness".
