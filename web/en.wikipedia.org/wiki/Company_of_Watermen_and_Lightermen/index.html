Company of Watermen and Lightermen

The Company of Watermen and Lightermen is a City of London company without livery, historically the guild responsible for licensing and regulating the watermen and lightermen who worked on the River Thames.

History

Watermen ferried passengers across and along the Thames, while lightermen moved cargo between ships and wharves. An Act of 1555 established a company to regulate watermen, and lightermen were incorporated into the company in the early eighteenth century.

Role

For centuries the Company controlled apprenticeships and licensing for Thames boatmen. Licensing functions largely passed to public authorities and eventually the Port of London Authority, but the Company continues as a professional and charitable body.

Doggett's Coat and Badge

The Company administers Doggett's Coat and Badge, the oldest continuing rowing race in the world, founded in 1715 by the actor Thomas Doggett for young watermen in their first year of freedom.
