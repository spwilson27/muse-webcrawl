Demand

In economics, demand is the quantity of a good that consumers are willing and able to purchase at various prices during a given period of time. The relationship between price and quantity demanded is described by the law of demand: other things equal, as the price of a good increases, the quantity demanded decreases, and vice versa. The relationship is depicted graphically by a downward-sloping demand curve.

Determinants

Besides the good's own price, demand depends on consumer income, prices of related goods (substitutes and complements), tastes and preferences, expectations, and the number of buyers. A change in price causes a movement along the demand curve; a change in any other determinant shifts the whole curve.

Elasticity

The price elasticity of demand measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price. Demand is elastic when quantity responds strongly and inelastic when it responds weakly, as with necessities such as basic foodstuffs or insulin.

Aggregate demand

In macroeconomics, aggregate demand is the total demand for final goods and services in an economy at a given overall price level and time period, central to Keynesian analysis of business cycles.
