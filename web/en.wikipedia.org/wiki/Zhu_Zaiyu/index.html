Zhu Zaiyu

Born: 1536, Qinyang, Henan, China

Died: 1611

Known for: Mathematical theory of equal temperament

Zhu Zaiyu (1536–1611) was a Chinese prince of the Ming dynasty, mathematician, physicist and musicologist, best known for publishing the first complete mathematical description of twelve-tone equal temperament.

Work on temperament

In his treatise Lülü Jingyi (1584), Zhu calculated the string and pipe lengths for equal temperament using the twelfth root of two, computed to high precision with an abacus of his own design. His results anticipated by decades the comparable European work of Simon Stevin and Marin Mersenne.

Other work

Zhu also wrote on astronomy and calendar reform, dance notation, and the theory of pitch pipes, and he famously renounced his claim to the princely succession. His writings were largely overlooked in China and had to be rediscovered by later scholars.
