National Congress of the Chinese Communist Party

National Congress
Body: Chinese Communist Party
Frequency: every five years
Venue: Great Hall of the People, Beijing
Delegates: over 2,000

The National Congress of the Chinese Communist Party (Chinese: 中国共产党全国代表大会) is the highest organ of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), convened every five years. Nominally it elects the Central Committee and the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and approves amendments to the party constitution; in practice, its leadership selections and political report ratify decisions shaped in advance by the party's top leadership.

Function

The Congress hears and adopts the political report delivered by the General Secretary, setting the party's line for the following five years. The Central Committee elected by the Congress then, at its first plenum, formally selects the Politburo, the Politburo Standing Committee and the General Secretary, and endorses the composition of the Central Military Commission.

History

The 1st National Congress met clandestinely in Shanghai and aboard a boat on South Lake in Jiaxing in July 1921, founding the party. Congresses during the revolutionary years were irregular; since the 9th Congress (1969), and especially after the 12th Congress (1982), the five-year cycle has been regular. The 20th National Congress was held in October 2022, electing a Central Committee that returned Xi Jinping for a third term as General Secretary.
