Daclatasvir, sold under the brand name Daklinza, is an antiviral medication used in combination with other medications to treat hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection. It was among the first direct-acting antivirals targeting the HCV NS5A protein.

Medical uses

Daclatasvir is used in combination regimens, most commonly with sofosbuvir, for chronic hepatitis C genotypes 1 through 4, and in some settings genotype 5 or 6. Treatment regimens typically last 12 to 24 weeks and achieve high sustained virologic response rates.

Mechanism of action

The drug inhibits the HCV nonstructural protein 5A (NS5A), a phosphoprotein essential for viral replication and assembly. Daclatasvir is a potent inhibitor active at picomolar concentrations.

History

It was developed by Bristol-Myers Squibb after being discovered by scientists at the company (originally code-named BMS-790052). It was approved in the European Union in 2014, and by the US FDA in 2015. The World Health Organization included it on its List of Essential Medicines for hepatitis C treatment combinations. Bristol-Myers Squibb withdrew Daklinza from the US market in 2019 for commercial reasons; generic versions remain widely available.
