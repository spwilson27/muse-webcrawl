Temple (Paris)

TypeFortress and commandery
LocationLe Marais, Paris (3rd arrondissement)

The Temple was a fortified complex in the Marais district of Paris, built by the Knights Templar from the mid-twelfth century. It served as the order's European headquarters and treasury, and later as a royal fortress and prison. The site gave its name to the surrounding Quartier du Temple, the Rue du Temple, and the Temple Paris Métro station, though almost nothing of the medieval buildings survives.

History

The Templars established their Paris house around 1140 and expanded it into a walled enclave — effectively a town within the city — containing a church modelled on the Holy Sepulchre, a great keep (the Grosse Tour), palaces, gardens, and workshops. The Temple functioned as a bank: kings deposited treasure there, and the French crown used its vaults and financial services throughout the thirteenth century.

After the suppression of the Templars in 1307–1312 — launched by dawn arrests ordered by Philip IV on Friday 13 October 1307 — the complex passed to the Knights Hospitaller. During the French Revolution it became a prison. Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette were held in the keep after the storming of the Tuileries in August 1792; the king left the Temple for his execution in January 1793, and the young Louis XVII died there in 1795.

Demolition and site

Napoleon ordered the great keep demolished in 1808 to prevent it becoming a royalist shrine; the remaining structures were cleared in the nineteenth century. The site is now occupied by the Square du Temple, the Mairie of the 3rd arrondissement, and the Carreau du Temple market hall.
