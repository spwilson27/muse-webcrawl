Curie–Weiss law

The Curie–Weiss law describes the magnetic susceptibility χ of a ferromagnet in the paramagnetic region above the Curie point: χ = C / (T − TC), where C is the material-specific Curie constant, T is the absolute temperature, and TC is the Curie temperature.

Derivation

The law is a modification of Curie's law, which works well for simple paramagnets without interactions between magnetic moments. Curie–Weiss law accounts for interactions by replacing the temperature T with T − θ, where θ is the Weiss constant. When θ is positive, ferromagnetic interactions dominate; when negative, antiferromagnetic interactions dominate.

Validity

The law holds well at temperatures well above the Curie point but fails near and below it, where mean-field approximations break down and critical fluctuations become important. In many materials the susceptibility follows the law with a Curie constant determined by the effective magnetic moment of the ions.
