The Harvesters

The Harvesters is an oil painting by the Flemish Renaissance artist Pieter Bruegel the Elder, painted in 1565. It is one of a series of works depicting the seasons, five of which survive.

Pieter Bruegel the Elder, 1565
ArtistPieter Bruegel the Elder
Year1565
MediumOil on wood
Dimensions119 cm × 162 cm
LocationMetropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Description

The painting shows peasants harvesting wheat in high summer, probably representing the months of July and August. In the foreground, workers cut and bind sheaves while others rest, eat and drink beneath a pear tree. A vast landscape unfolds beyond: golden fields, a church, a distant village and a glimpse of the sea. As in many of Bruegel's works, the scene combines close observation of everyday labour with an immense, almost cosmic sense of the natural world.

The series

The series of the seasons (or months of the year) was commissioned by the Antwerp banker Niclaes Jonghelinck. Five panels survive: The Hunters in the Snow, The Gloomy Day, The Hay Harvest, The Harvesters and The Return of the Herd. Three are in the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna, one in Prague, and The Harvesters in New York.

Provenance

The painting passed through the collections of Jonghelinck, the city of Antwerp, Archduke Ernest of Austria and Emperor Rudolf II, among others, before being acquired by the Metropolitan Museum of Art in 1919 with the Rogers Fund.

Legacy

The Harvesters is regarded as a foundational work of Western landscape painting — one of the first in which the landscape itself, animated by ordinary human activity, is the true subject. It is among the most celebrated works in the Met's European paintings collection.
