Kurt Jooss

Born12 January 1901, Wasseralfingen, German Empire
Died22 May 1979 (aged 78), Heilbronn, West Germany
OccupationDancer, choreographer
Known forThe Green Table (1932)

Kurt Jooss (12 January 1901 – 22 May 1979) was a German dancer and choreographer who mixed classical ballet with theatre, and is regarded as one of the founders of German expressionist dance theatre (Tanztheater). His most famous work is The Green Table (1932), an anti-war ballet that remains in the repertory of companies worldwide.

Life and career

Jooss studied music in Stuttgart and dance with Rudolf Laban, whose assistant and leading dancer he became; he also adopted Laban's system of dance notation. In 1927 he co-founded the Folkwang School in Essen and became dance director of the Essen Opera's ballet company, which developed into the Folkwang Tanztheater.

The Green Table (Der grüne Tisch), subtitled "A Dance of Death in Eight Scenes," premiered in Paris in 1932 and won first prize at an international choreography competition. It opens and closes with the "Gentlemen in Black" — diplomats arguing fruitlessly around a green table — framing scenes in which Death appears as a skeleton figure collecting victims of war.

In 1933 Jooss refused a Nazi demand to dismiss the Jewish members of his company, including the composer Fritz Cohen, and fled Germany with his dancers. The company, Ballets Jooss, settled at Dartington Hall in Devon, England, and toured the world. After the war Jooss returned to Essen in 1949, rebuilt the Folkwang dance department, and continued to teach and choreograph until his retirement. His pupils included Pina Bausch, who later transformed Tanztheater at Wuppertal.

Legacy

Jooss's synthesis of ballet technique, Laban's movement analysis and dramatic content laid the foundations of modern German dance theatre. The Green Table is widely considered the first ballet to address political and social themes directly.
