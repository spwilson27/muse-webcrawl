Taz River

Taz River
Native name: Таз
Location: Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug, Russia
Length: 1,401 km (871 mi)
Basin size: 150,000 km²
Mouth: Taz Estuary, Kara Sea

The Taz (Russian: Таз) is a river in the Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug of western Siberia, Russia. At about 1,401 kilometres (871 mi) long, it is one of the longest Russian rivers draining into the Arctic Ocean between the basins of the Ob and the Yenisei. Its drainage basin covers roughly 150,000 square kilometres.

Course

The Taz rises in the Siberian Uvaly hills and flows generally northward across the West Siberian Plain through flat, swampy taiga and tundra terrain. It empties into the Taz Estuary (Tazovskaya Guba), a long inlet of the Gulf of Ob on the Kara Sea. The estuary is about 250 kilometres long, running from the river's mouth near the settlement of Tazovsky northward to its junction with the Gulf of Ob.

History

In the early 17th century, Russian traders and explorers from Mangazeya — a trading colony founded in 1601 on the lower Taz — used the river as part of a route into Siberia. A portage connected the Taz basin via the Turukhan River to the Yenisei, allowing movement between the Ob and Yenisei systems. The Taz also gave its name to the Tashtyp and to the Taz Selkups, an Indigenous people of the middle course of the river.

Settlements and economy

The principal settlement on the river is Krasnoselkup, and Tazovsky lies near the mouth. The basin contains significant natural gas fields, and the river region is home to Selkup and Nenets communities engaged in fishing, reindeer herding, and hunting.
