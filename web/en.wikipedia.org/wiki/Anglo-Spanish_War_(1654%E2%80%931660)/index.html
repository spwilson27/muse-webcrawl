Anglo-Spanish War (1654–1660)

Date1654 – 1660
LocationCaribbean, Spain, Spanish Netherlands
ResultEnglish acquisition of Jamaica and Dunkirk
BelligerentsCommonwealth of England (later allied with France) vs. Spain

The Anglo-Spanish War of 1654–1660 was a conflict between the Commonwealth of England under Oliver Cromwell and Spain, fought in the Caribbean, at sea, and in the Spanish Netherlands.

Western Design

Cromwell's "Western Design" aimed to break Spanish monopoly in the Americas. An expedition failed to take Hispaniola in 1655 but captured Jamaica, which remained an English (later British) colony. Admiral Robert Blake destroyed a Spanish treasure fleet at Cádiz in 1656 and won a complete victory at the Battle of Santa Cruz de Tenerife in 1657.

War in Flanders

Allied with France from 1657, English troops under Sir William Lockhart fought alongside the French at the Battle of the Dunes in 1658, after which Dunkirk was handed to England.

Aftermath

The war formally ended after the Restoration of Charles II in 1660. Spain recognised England's possession of Jamaica in the Treaty of Madrid (1670).
