Antonio Tejero Molina (born 30 April 1932) is a Spanish former Civil Guard lieutenant colonel who led the failed coup d'état of 23 February 1981, known in Spain as 23-F or El Tejerazo. His armed storming of the Congress of Deputies during the investiture vote of Leopoldo Calvo-Sotelo was broadcast on television and remains one of the most famous episodes of Spain's transition to democracy.

The 1981 coup attempt

On the afternoon of 23 February 1981, Tejero entered the Congress chamber with some 200 Civil Guards, fired shots into the ceiling and ordered the deputies to the floor — "¡Quieto todo el mundo!" ("Everybody stay still!"). Only Prime Minister Adolfo Suárez, Santiago Carrillo and General Manuel Gutiérrez Mellado remained standing or refused to obey fully. The coup, coordinated with General Jaime Milans del Bosch, who declared a state of emergency in Valencia, collapsed after King Juan Carlos I refused to support it and addressed the nation on television in the early hours of 24 February, ordering the armed forces to uphold the constitutional order. Tejero surrendered that day.

Tejero had previously been implicated in the 1978 plot known as Operation Galaxia and had received only a minimal punishment. For the 1981 coup he was sentenced to 30 years' imprisonment for military rebellion. He was released on parole in 1996, having served his sentence in military prisons. From prison he founded a far-right party, Spanish Solidarity, which won no seats in the 1982 elections and soon dissolved.

Later life

Tejero has lived quietly since his release, never expressing regret for the coup attempt. His appearance at public events connected with the Franco era has periodically returned him to the news, including a controversial send-off attended by serving officers decades later. The events of 23-F are commemorated in Spain as a moment when the young democracy defended itself; the bullet holes in the Congress ceiling have been preserved.
