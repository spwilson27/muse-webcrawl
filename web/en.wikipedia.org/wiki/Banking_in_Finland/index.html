Banking in Finland

Banking in Finland is a concentrated, technologically advanced sector supervised, since Finland's membership of the euro area and the European banking union, principally by the European Central Bank together with the Finnish Financial Supervisory Authority (FIN-FSA). The central bank is the Bank of Finland (Suomen Pankki), founded in 1811 and one of the world's oldest central banks.

Market structure

Three groups dominate Finnish retail and corporate banking. The largest is OP Financial Group, a cooperative federation of local cooperative banks and the central institution OP Corporate Bank. Second is the Finnish business of Nordea, the Nordic banking group, which moved its headquarters from Stockholm to Helsinki in 2018 to sit inside the banking union. Third is S-Bank (S-Pankki), owned by the S Group cooperative and its regional cooperatives, which grew rapidly on the back of the S Group's retail loyalty scheme. Smaller players include Aktia, the Savings Banks group, Ålandsbanken, Danske Bank's Finnish branch, and Handelsbanken (whose Finnish operations were agreed for sale in 2023–2024).

History

Finland's modern banking history is marked by the severe banking crisis of 1991–1995, triggered by financial deregulation, a credit boom, and the collapse of Soviet trade. The crisis forced state intervention, the rescue and restructuring of the savings bank sector, and mergers that produced today's consolidated market (including the merger of Union Bank of Finland and Kansallis-Osake-Pankki into Merita, a predecessor of Nordea). Since the euro's introduction in 1999, Finnish banking has been characterised by early adoption of electronic payments, near-universal online banking, and a steady decline in branch numbers and cash use.
