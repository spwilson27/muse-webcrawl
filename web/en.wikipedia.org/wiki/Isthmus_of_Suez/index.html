Isthmus of Suez

The Isthmus of Suez is the narrow land bridge, about 125 kilometres (78 miles) wide, that lies between the Mediterranean Sea and the Red Sea, connecting the continents of Africa and Asia. It is located within Egypt and is traversed by the Suez Canal.

Geography

The isthmus is a low, largely desert region of sand and gravel plains, interrupted by a chain of lakes — Lake Manzala, Lake Timsah, and the Great and Little Bitter Lakes — which the canal incorporated into its course. The city of Suez lies at its southern end, Ismailia near its centre, and Port Said at its northern end.

History

Ancient canals linking the Nile to the Red Sea were dug under pharaohs and later rulers. The modern sea-level canal, designed under Ferdinand de Lesseps, opened in 1869, transforming world shipping by eliminating the voyage around the Cape of Good Hope. The isthmus was a frontline in the Arab–Israeli wars, and the canal was closed from 1967 to 1975 following the Six-Day War.
