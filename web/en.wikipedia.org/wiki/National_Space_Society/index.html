The National Space Society (NSS) is an American international nonprofit educational and scientific organisation specialising in space advocacy. It supports human spaceflight, space exploration and the eventual settlement of space beyond Earth.

History

The NSS was formed in 1987 by the merger of the National Space Institute, founded in 1974 by rocketry pioneer Wernher von Braun, and the L5 Society, founded in 1975 to promote the space colony ideas of physicist Gerard K. O'Neill. The merger united a Washington-based policy organisation with a grassroots activist network, adopting the L5 goal of human communities in space.

Activities

The society publishes the quarterly magazine Ad Astra, holds the annual International Space Development Conference (ISDC), organises local chapters, and engages in legislative advocacy in Washington. It presents awards including the Gerard K. O'Neill Memorial Award and administers student competitions such as the NSS/NASA Ames Space Settlement Contest. Prominent supporters and governors have included figures from the aerospace industry, science fiction authors, and former astronauts.
