National Employment Standards

CountryAustralia
LegislationFair Work Act 2009

The National Employment Standards (NES) are a set of eleven minimum employment entitlements that apply to all employees covered by the national workplace relations system in Australia. Established under the Fair Work Act 2009 and in force from 1 January 2010, they form a safety net that cannot be undercut by an award, enterprise agreement or employment contract.

The standards

The eleven standards cover: maximum weekly hours of work (38 hours plus reasonable additional hours); the right to request flexible working arrangements; offers and requests to convert from casual to permanent employment; parental leave and related entitlements (12 months unpaid, with a right to request a further 12); annual leave (four weeks paid); personal/carer's leave, compassionate leave and family and domestic violence leave (10 days paid personal leave, plus unpaid and, since 2023, 10 days paid family and domestic violence leave); community service leave; long service leave; public holidays; notice of termination and redundancy pay; and the requirement to provide a Fair Work Information Statement (and Casual Employment Information Statement) to new employees.

Application

The NES apply to full-time, part-time and (for most standards, with some pro-rated or excluded) casual employees in the federal system — that is, most private-sector employees in Australia. Some standards, such as redundancy pay and long service leave, have qualifying periods or exclusions for small businesses. The Fair Work Ombudsman enforces compliance, and the standards have been amended several times, notably to add paid family and domestic violence leave and casual conversion rights.
