Milinda Panha

Milinda Pañha
Author: attributed to Nagasena (dialogue)
Language: Pali
Subject: Buddhist doctrine
Form: Dialogue between King Milinda (Menander I) and the monk Nagasena

The Milinda Panha (Pali: Milindapañha, "The Questions of Milinda") is a Buddhist text that records a dialogue between the Indo-Greek king Milinda — identified with Menander I, who ruled in the northwest of the Indian subcontinent in the 2nd century BCE — and the Buddhist monk Nagasena. It is one of the most celebrated works of early Buddhist literature outside the Pali Canon proper, and it is included in the canon of Burmese Buddhism as part of the Khuddaka Nikaya.

Contents and structure

The text is arranged as a series of questions posed by the king, who is portrayed as intelligent, inquisitive, and well versed in Greek philosophy, and the answers given by Nagasena, who resolves apparent contradictions in Buddhist teaching through parables, similes, and careful analysis. The dialogue ranges over the nature of the self, rebirth, karma, the problem of evil, and the path to awakening.

The most famous passage is Nagasena's simile of the chariot. Asked how a person can be reborn if there is no permanent soul, Nagasena asks the king whether a chariot is identical with its pole, axle, wheels, or reins; the king concedes that "chariot" is a mere designation for the assemblage of parts. Likewise, "Nagasena" is a designation for the five aggregates, and rebirth is explained as continuity without transmigration of a soul — famously compared to a flame passed from one lamp to another.

Dating and transmission

Scholars generally date the core of the work to around the beginning of the Common Era, with additions over subsequent centuries. The text was originally composed in a northwestern Prakrit or possibly in Sanskrit; the Pali version survives, together with an older Chinese translation (the "Nagasena Sutra") that preserves a shorter form of the dialogue in three chapters, as against seven in the Pali. The work was translated into English by T. W. Rhys Davids in the Sacred Books of the East series under the title The Questions of King Milinda.

Significance

The Milinda Panha is valued both as literature and as philosophy: it is an early example of a cross-cultural philosophical dialogue between Hellenistic and Indian traditions, and it remains a standard introduction to central Buddhist doctrines such as anattā (no-self), dependent origination, and the mechanics of karma and rebirth.
