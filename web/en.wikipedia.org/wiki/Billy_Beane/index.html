William Lamar Beane III (born March 29, 1962) is an American former professional baseball player and executive. He became famous as the general manager of the Oakland Athletics, where his data-driven approach to building competitive teams on a small budget — popularised by Michael Lewis's 2003 book Moneyball and its 2011 film adaptation, in which Beane was portrayed by Brad Pitt — transformed player evaluation across baseball.

Playing career

A highly touted prospect out of Mt. Carmel High School in San Diego, Beane was selected by the New York Mets in the first round of the 1980 draft, forgoing a scholarship to Stanford. Despite his physical tools, he never established himself in the major leagues, batting .219 over parts of six seasons (1984–1989) with the Mets, Twins, Tigers, and Athletics, spending most of his career in the minor leagues.

Front office career

Beane joined Oakland's front office as a scout in 1990 and succeeded Sandy Alderson as general manager after the 1997 season. Constrained by one of the lowest payrolls in Major League Baseball, he applied sabermetric principles — emphasising on-base percentage and other undervalued skills — to assemble winning rosters. The 2002 Athletics won 103 games including an American League-record 20 consecutive victories, the season chronicled in Moneyball. In 2002 he turned down a lucrative offer to become general manager of the Boston Red Sox. He was promoted to executive vice president of baseball operations in 2015, with David Forst taking over the GM role, and later became a minority owner and senior advisor. Beane also held involvement in soccer analytics through a stake in Dutch club AZ Alkmaar and a role with English club Barnsley.

Legacy

Beane is widely credited with accelerating the analytics revolution in professional sports, influencing front offices in baseball and other sports to adopt statistical decision-making.
