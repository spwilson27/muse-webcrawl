Tod Browning (born Charles Albert Browning Jr.; July 12, 1880 – October 6, 1962) was an American film director, film actor, screenwriter, vaudeville performer and carnival sideshow performer. He is best remembered as the director of the landmark horror films Dracula (1931) and Freaks (1932), and for his fruitful collaboration with actor Lon Chaney during the silent era.

Early life

Browning was born in Louisville, Kentucky. Fascinated by carnival life, he ran away as a teenager to join travelling circuses and sideshows, where he worked as a talker, contortionist and clown — including a burial act billed as "The Living Corpse". His sideshow experience deeply influenced the themes of his later films.

Career

After acting in motion pictures, including for D. W. Griffith, Browning turned to directing in 1917. At MGM he formed a celebrated partnership with Lon Chaney, "the Man of a Thousand Faces", making a series of dark melodramas and thrillers including The Unholy Three (1925), The Unknown (1927) and London After Midnight (1927).

Following Chaney's death, Browning directed Dracula (1931) for Universal, launching the classic Universal horror cycle with Bela Lugosi's iconic performance. His next film, Freaks (1932), which cast real carnival performers with disabilities, was a commercial disaster that effectively ended his career as a major director; heavily cut and banned in several countries, it is now regarded as a cult classic and a deeply humane work.

Legacy

Browning has been called the "Edgar Allan Poe of cinema" for his fascination with the macabre, outsiders and the grotesque. His work is now seen as foundational to the horror genre.
