Italian language

Italian (italiano) is a Romance language of the Indo-European family, descended from the Vulgar Latin of the Roman Empire. It is the official language of Italy, Switzerland (with German, French, and Romansh), San Marino, and Vatican City, and is spoken by roughly 65 million native speakers and some 85 million people in total.

History

Modern standard Italian derives principally from the Tuscan dialect of Florence, elevated by the prestige of Dante Alighieri, Petrarch, and Boccaccio in the 14th century. At the unification of Italy in 1861 only a small minority spoke the standard language; mass schooling, military service, and later radio and television spread it nationwide during the 20th century.

Features

Italian is noted for its phonetic spelling, seven-vowel system, double consonants, and a grammar retaining two genders and extensive verb conjugation. It is the Romance language closest to Latin in vocabulary, and it has contributed many words to music, art, food, and finance in other languages.
