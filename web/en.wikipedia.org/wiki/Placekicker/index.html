Placekicker

A placekicker, or simply kicker (abbreviated K or PK), is the player in gridiron football who is responsible for the kicking duties of field goals and extra points. In many cases, the placekicker also serves as the team's kickoff specialist or punter, though at the professional level the roles are usually separated.

Role

On a field goal or extra-point attempt, the placekicker works in a unit with a long snapper, who snaps the ball seven or eight yards back, and a holder, who catches the snap and holds the ball upright — traditionally with its laces facing away from the kicker ("laces out"). The kicker, who begins his approach as the snap is made, then strikes the ball between the goalposts. A successful field goal scores three points; an extra point after a touchdown scores one. Placekickers are almost always involved in kickoffs as well, booting the ball deep into the opponent's territory at the start of each half and after scores.

Technique

The modern placekicker uses the "soccer style" of kicking, approaching the ball at an angle and striking it with the instep of the foot. Before its adoption in the 1960s — pioneered in the NFL by the Hungarian-born Gogolak brothers, Pete and Charlie — kickers used a straight-on "toe style," striking the ball with the toe of a stiffened shoe. Soccer-style kicking proved far more accurate and powerful, and produced record-setting kickers such as Morten Andersen, Adam Vinatieri, and Justin Tucker, whose career field-goal percentage and 66-yard record field goal in 2021 are among the highest marks in NFL history.

Specialisation

Although a placekicker may touch the ball only a handful of times per game, his kicks frequently decide outcomes, and the position is among the most specialised in sport: kickers rarely tackle or are tackled, train on separate schedules, and are judged almost entirely on accuracy statistics. Because they can play into their forties, placekickers include some of the longest-serving and highest-scoring players in football history.
