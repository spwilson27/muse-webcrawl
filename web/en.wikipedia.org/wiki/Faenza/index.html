Faenza

Faenza is a city and comune in the province of Ravenna, Emilia-Romagna, Italy, located southeast of Bologna near the Lamone river. It has a population of about 59,000. The city is world-famous for its glazed earthenware pottery, maiolica, which gave its name to "faience", the French term for tin-glazed pottery used across Europe.

History

Faenza was the Roman Faventia, favourably placed on the Via Aemilia. In the Middle Ages it was a free commune and later ruled by the Manfredi family. Its ceramic industry flourished from the Renaissance, when potters developed the white tin-glaze that made Faenza ware celebrated at courts throughout Europe. The International Museum of Ceramics (MIC), founded in 1908, holds one of the world's largest ceramics collections.

Main sights and events

Landmarks include the cathedral (begun 1474), the clock tower and the arcaded Piazza del Popolo. Faenza hosts the Palio del Niballo, a medieval-style tournament held each June, and the biennial Argillà pottery festival. The city is also the birthplace of racing driver and the Scuderia Toro Rosso/AlphaTauri Formula One team was based nearby; Dino Dini and evangelist Torricelli — the physicist Evangelista Torricelli was born in Rome but raised partly in Faenza.
