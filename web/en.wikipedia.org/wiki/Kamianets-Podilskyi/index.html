Kamianets-Podilskyi

Kamianets-Podilskyi (Ukrainian: Кам'янець-Подільський) is a city in Khmelnytskyi Oblast, western Ukraine, and a historic centre of the Podolia region. It is famous for its medieval old town and fortress, dramatically situated on a rocky island encircled by the canyon of the Smotrych River, and is counted among the "Seven Wondrous Castles of Ukraine".

History

The site was settled from early times and the city is first mentioned in the 14th century, when Podolia passed between Lithuania and Poland. Under the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, Kamianets ("Kamieniec Podolski") became one of the Commonwealth's strongest fortresses, guarding its southeastern frontier. The Ottomans captured it in 1672 (Treaty of Buchach) and held it until the Treaty of Karlowitz returned it to Poland in 1699. After the Second Partition of Poland in 1793 it passed to the Russian Empire.

In 1919–1920 the city briefly served as a seat of the government of the Ukrainian People's Republic. In August 1941, in one of the first large massacres of the Holocaust, some 23,000 Jews, many deported from Hungary, were murdered near the city by German SS units and auxiliaries.

Sights

The Kamianets-Podilskyi Castle, built and rebuilt between the 14th and 18th centuries, stands at the entrance to the old town across the Turkish Bridge. The old town preserves the churches and town houses of its historically Polish, Armenian, Jewish, and Ukrainian communities, including the Armenian cathedral ruins, the Dominican and Franciscan churches, and one of Ukraine's oldest town halls. The Smotrych canyon, up to 50 m deep, is a protected geological monument.

Today

The modern city is a regional centre for education, light industry, and tourism, anchored by Ivan Ohienko National University. Its old town and castle form a national historical-architectural reserve.
