Music cognition

Music cognition is an interdisciplinary field concerned with the cognitive processes involved in musical behaviours such as listening, performing, composing, reading, writing, and ancillary activities. It draws on methods from musicology, psychology, neuroscience, cognitive science, linguistics, anthropology, and education.

Scope

Researchers in music cognition study how listeners perceive pitch, rhythm, metre, timbre and melody; how musical expectations are formed and violated; how music is remembered; and how musical training changes the brain. The field also investigates the emotional effects of music, the development of musical ability in children, and disorders such as amusia, in which musical perception is selectively impaired.

History

Although interest in the psychology of music reaches back to figures such as Hermann von Helmholtz, whose On the Sensations of Tone appeared in 1863, and Carl Seashore in the early twentieth century, music cognition emerged as a distinct field in the 1980s with the founding of the journal Music Perception in 1983 and the first International Conference on Music Perception and Cognition in Kyoto in 1989.

Topics

Central topics include absolute pitch, tonality and key perception, rhythm and entrainment, musical memory, performance expression, and the neural basis of musical processing revealed by neuroimaging studies. Computational modelling of listeners has become an important tool, with statistical models of melodic expectation used to predict how listeners will respond to unfamiliar music.
