Ganoid scale

Ganoid scales are a type of fish scale found in some species of fish. The scales are rhomboid in shape and often have serrated edges, and are joined by peg-and-socket articulations. They are covered by a layer of ganoine, a substance resembling enamel, over a middle layer of dentine-like material and an inner layer of bone (isopedine). Ganoid scales are found in the so-called ganoid fishes, including gars (family Lepisosteidae), bichirs, and, in modified forms, sturgeons and paddlefish.

Structure

The distinctive feature of a ganoid scale is the ganoine layer, an inorganic bone salt apatite with an organic component similar to enamel. This gives the scales a hard, shiny surface. Unlike the overlapping arrangement of the more flexible cycloid and ctenoid scales of most bony fishes, ganoid scales fit together edge to edge, forming a nearly impenetrable armor.

Evolution

Ganoid scales are regarded as an ancestral character state for bony fishes. They were widespread among Palaeozoic and Mesozoic fishes, including the early ray-finned fishes (palaeoniscoids), before being largely replaced by thinner, more flexible scale types in modern teleosts.
