Subiaco is a town and comune in the Metropolitan City of Rome, in Lazio, central Italy, situated in the valley of the Aniene river at the foothills of the Monti Simbruini, about 70 kilometres east of Rome. Its ancient name, Sublaqueum, refers to its position below the artificial lakes built for the Emperor Nero's villa.

History

Subiaco is renowned as the place where Saint Benedict of Nursia began his monastic life around the year 497, living for three years as a hermit in a cave — the Sacro Speco — above the Aniene. He went on to found twelve monasteries in the valley before leaving for Monte Cassino. Two Benedictine monasteries survive at Subiaco: the Monastery of Saint Scholastica, the only one of the original twelve still standing, and the Monastery of Saint Benedict, built around the Sacro Speco and decorated with medieval frescoes.

Subiaco also holds a place in the history of printing: in 1465, the German printers Conrad Sweynheym and Arnold Pannartz set up at the monastery of Saint Scholastica the first printing press in Italy, producing some of the earliest books printed in the country.

Main sights

The town's landmarks include the Rocca Abbaziale, a fortress-palace of the abbots, the medieval bridge of Saint Francis over the Aniene, and the two monasteries above the town, which remain major pilgrimage destinations. The surrounding Simbruini mountains are protected as a regional park popular for hiking.
