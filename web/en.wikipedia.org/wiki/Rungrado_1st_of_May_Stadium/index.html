The Rungrado 1st of May Stadium is a multi-purpose stadium occupying an area of 20.7 hectares (51 acres) on Rungra Island, Pyongyang, North Korea. It opened on 1 May 1989, in time for the 13th World Festival of Youth and Students, and is by most measures the largest stadium in the world by seating capacity; original North Korean figures claimed 150,000 seats, while post-renovation estimates suggest roughly 114,000.

Design

The stadium's scalloped roof, formed of 16 arches arranged in a ring like the petals of a magnolia blossom, spans more than 60 metres in places and gives the building its distinctive appearance. The main pitch is surrounded by a running track, and there are additional training pitches, halls and facilities within the complex.

Events

The stadium is best known as the principal venue for the Arirang Mass Games, the enormous synchronised gymnastics and artistic performances. It also hosts North Korea national football team matches and athletics events. In 1995 it hosted the Collision in Korea professional wrestling event, a joint WCW/NJPW show that drew one of the largest attendances in wrestling history. The stadium was closed for remodelling in 2013 and reopened in 2015 with new seats and facilities.
