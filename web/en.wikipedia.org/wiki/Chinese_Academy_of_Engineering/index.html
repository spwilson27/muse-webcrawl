The Chinese Academy of Engineering (CAE; Chinese: 中国工程院) is the national engineering academy of the People's Republic of China. Founded in 1994, it is the country's highest honorary and advisory body in engineering and technological sciences, functioning alongside the Chinese Academy of Sciences as one of China's two national academies.

Organization

The CAE is organized into nine divisions: mechanical and vehicle engineering; information and electronic engineering; chemical, metallurgical and materials engineering; energy and mining engineering; civil, hydraulic and architectural engineering; light industry and environmental engineering; agriculture; medicine and health; and engineering management. New members (院士, yuànshì, "academicians") are elected every two years from senior engineers and scientists of distinguished achievement.

Functions

The academy provides strategic consulting to the state on major engineering and technology policy, sponsors research programmes, evaluates major national engineering projects, and promotes international scientific cooperation. It publishes academic journals and hosts conferences. Its members have included the chief engineers of landmark Chinese projects in high-speed rail, nuclear power, spaceflight and bridge construction.
