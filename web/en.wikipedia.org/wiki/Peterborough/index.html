Peterborough is a cathedral city in the East of England, in the ceremonial county of Cambridgeshire, on the River Nene about 30 miles (48 km) north-west of Cambridge and 76 miles north of London. Historically part of Northamptonshire (the Soke of Peterborough), it is now a unitary authority within Cambridgeshire and one of England's fastest-growing cities.

History

The settlement grew around a Benedictine abbey founded in the seventh century (as Medeshamstede) and refounded in the tenth; the present Peterborough Cathedral, begun in 1118, is one of the finest Norman churches in England, noted for its unique painted wooden nave ceiling and its great west front. Catherine of Aragon is buried in the cathedral, and Mary, Queen of Scots was first buried there before removal to Westminster Abbey. The town became a railway and engineering centre in the nineteenth century and a major centre of brickmaking. Designated a New Town in 1967, Peterborough expanded rapidly with the development of township suburbs, parkways and a new shopping centre (Queensgate).

Economy and transport

Peterborough is a logistics, food-processing and service centre with major employers in insurance, publishing and engineering, and hosts the UK headquarters of several national firms. The city is a major rail junction on the East Coast Main Line, with fast services to London King's Cross, Leeds, York and Edinburgh, and sits at the junction of the A1(M) and A47 roads.

Culture

Local attractions include the cathedral, Burghley House (just outside the city), Flag Fen Bronze Age archaeological site, the Nene Valley Railway heritage line and the Key Theatre. The city is home to Peterborough United Football Club ("The Posh"), playing at London Road.
