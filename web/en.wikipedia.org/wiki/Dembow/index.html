Dembow

Dembow is a staple of Dominican street-party culture

Dembow is a Dominican musical genre and rhythmic pattern that emerged in the Dominican Republic in the late 2000s. It developed out of reggaeton, Jamaican dancehall riddims and local street-party culture, and is characterized by a fast, insistent beat, sparse arrangements and call-and-response vocals.

Origins and rhythm

The dembow rhythm descends from the 3+3+2 "dembow" riddim popularized by Jamaican producers in the early 1990s — notably the track "Dem Bow" by Shabba Ranks — which also underpins much reggaeton. Dominican artists accelerated the tempo (typically to around 110–115 BPM) and built tracks around short looping beats, giving the genre its raw, high-energy sound.

Artists and spread

Pioneers and popular acts include El Alfa, who brought dembow to international audiences, along with artists such as Bulin 47, Rochy RD and Tokischa. The genre has been criticized in the Dominican Republic for explicit lyrics, but it has grown from a marginalized barrio sound into one of the dominant styles of Latin urban music, influencing global pop collaborations.
