Doctrine

Doctrine (from Latin doctrina, "teaching, instruction") is a codification of beliefs or a body of teachings or instructions, taught principles or positions, as the essence of teachings in a given branch of knowledge or in a belief system.

Uses

In religion, doctrine refers to the authoritative teachings of a faith, such as Christian doctrine concerning the Trinity or incarnation. In law, a doctrine is an established legal principle developed through precedent, such as the doctrine of stare decisis. Military doctrine is the official body of concepts guiding armed forces in operations, while political doctrines — such as the Monroe Doctrine or the Truman Doctrine — state principles of government policy, especially in foreign affairs.

The term is sometimes contrasted with dogma, doctrine being regarded as teachings that may be examined and debated, whereas dogma demands assent.
