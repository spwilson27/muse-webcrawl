Iverson bracket

In mathematics, the Iverson bracket, named after Kenneth E. Iverson, is a notation that generalises the Kronecker delta, which is the Iverson bracket of the statement of equality. It maps any statement to a function of the free variables in that statement, equal to 1 if the statement is true and 0 if false:

[P] = 1 if P is true; 0 otherwise,

where P is a statement that can be evaluated to true or false.

Uses

The notation, introduced by Iverson in his programming language APL and popularized in mathematics by Donald Knuth, simplifies manipulations of sums and other expressions, for example by encoding conditions directly in summations and by expressing the Kronecker delta, the sign function, and indicator functions in a uniform way.

Properties

Brackets obey rules analogous to logic: the bracket of a conjunction is the product of the brackets, and related identities hold for negation and disjunction under suitable conditions.
