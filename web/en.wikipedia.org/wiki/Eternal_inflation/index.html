Eternal inflation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eternal inflation is a scenario within cosmic inflation theory in which the inflationary phase of the universe, once begun, continues forever in at least some regions of space. Because quantum fluctuations can locally keep the inflaton field high on its potential — or because new inflating regions are continually produced faster than old ones thermalise — inflation ends only in patches, each of which becomes a "pocket universe" with its own hot Big Bang, while the inflating background expands without end and spawns further pockets.

Development

The idea emerged in the early 1980s from the work of Alexander Vilenkin, who showed that in many inflationary models quantum fluctuations make inflation future-eternal, and was developed by Andrei Linde in the framework of chaotic inflation as "eternal chaotic inflation". Alan Guth and others extended the picture; in Linde's self-reproducing universe, the global structure resembles a fractal multiverse in which pocket universes may differ in their low-energy constants and even effective laws.

Implications and criticism

Eternal inflation underlies many modern multiverse proposals and interacts with string theory's landscape of vacua and with anthropic reasoning about the cosmological constant. It raises the measure problem: in an infinite, eternally inflating spacetime, comparing probabilities of different observations requires a prescription for regulating infinities, and different measures give different predictions. Critics, including some founders of inflationary theory, question the scenario's testability and whether the required potentials are plausible; the Borde–Guth–Vilenkin theorem shows that inflationary spacetimes of this kind remain past-incomplete — eternal into the future but not, in general, into the past.
