Bookland

Bookland is the informal name for the fictional "country" used in the EAN barcode system to identify books. When publishers began printing EAN-13 barcodes on books, the International ISBN Agency arranged for the prefixes 978 and later 979 to be reserved for ISBNs, even though no such country exists in the EAN system. In the terminology of the GS1 organisation, which administers EAN codes, these prefixes constitute "Bookland", and the joke name has become standard in the book trade.

Background

The 10-digit ISBN predated machine-readable barcodes. When European Article Number (EAN) barcodes became standard for retail in the 1970s and 1980s, books needed to participate so that bookshops could scan them at the till. Rather than give books a real country's prefix, the EAN authority allocated 978 as a pseudo-country prefix, allowing an ISBN-10 to be converted into an EAN-13 by prepending 978 and recalculating the check digit. Books also typically carry a five-digit add-on code (an "EAN-5") encoding the price or currency.

Expansion under ISBN-13

On 1 January 2007 the ISBN system itself moved to 13 digits aligned exactly with the Bookland EAN: all ISBNs now begin with 978, and as the 978 range fills, new ISBNs are issued from a 979 block (979-8 being reserved for music, the ISMN). In barcode terms, books are thus "citizens" of Bookland, an illustration of how a catalogue standard can acquire the trappings of a place — Bookland even appears in humorous maps and in discussions of the country codes of the world as the most populous fictional country on Earth.
