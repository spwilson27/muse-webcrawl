Junagadh rock inscription of Rudradaman

The Junagadh rock inscription of Rudradaman, also known as the Girnar rock inscription of Rudradaman, is a Sanskrit prashasti (eulogy) inscription composed around 150 CE by the Western Satrap ruler Rudradaman I. It is carved on a great rock near Girnar hill, close to Junagadh in present-day Gujarat, India — the same rock that also bears inscriptions of Ashoka and of the Gupta emperor Skandagupta.

Content

Written in refined classical Sanskrit prose in the Brahmi script, the inscription is the earliest surviving long inscription in chaste Sanskrit and a landmark in the history of Sanskrit kavya (court poetry). It records the repair of the embankment of Sudarshana lake, an artificial reservoir originally built under Chandragupta Maurya and improved under Ashoka, after it was damaged by a storm. Rudradaman's governor Suvishakha carried out the repairs. The text also celebrates Rudradaman's conquests, his patronage of learning and his mastery of grammar, music and logic.

Significance

The inscription is a key source for the political geography of western India in the second century CE, for the history of the Sudarshana lake over some eight centuries, and for the rise of Sanskrit as the language of royal epigraphy.
