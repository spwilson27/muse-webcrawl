Outer core

Earth's outer core is a fluid layer about 2,260 km thick, composed of mostly iron and nickel, that lies above Earth's solid inner core and below its mantle. Its outer boundary lies about 2,890 km beneath Earth's surface.

Temperatures in the outer core range from about 3,000 °C near the top to over 5,000 °C near the inner core. Unlike the inner core, the outer core is liquid because pressures are not high enough to solidify it despite the high temperatures.

Convection and rotation in the liquid outer core generate Earth's magnetic field through the geodynamo process. Seismic wave studies — in particular the absence of S-waves and the refraction of P-waves — provided the evidence for its liquid state.
