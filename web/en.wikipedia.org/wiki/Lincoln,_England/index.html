Lincoln is a cathedral city and the county town of Lincolnshire in the East Midlands of England. It grew from the Roman town of Lindum Colonia and is dominated by its medieval cathedral and castle, which stand on a hill above the River Witham.

History

The Romans established a legionary fortress at the site around AD 48, which became the colonia of Lindum. In the Middle Ages Lincoln was one of England's most prosperous towns, a centre of the wool trade and the site of two battles (1141 and 1217) during civil wars. Lincoln Cathedral, rebuilt in the Gothic style after an earthquake in 1185, was for centuries among the tallest buildings in the world; the city also preserves one of the four surviving exemplars of Magna Carta, kept at Lincoln Castle.

The Victorian era brought heavy engineering — firms such as Ruston & Hornsby made Lincoln a centre for agricultural and industrial machinery, and the first tanks were designed and tested here during the First World War.

Geography and the city today

The historic "uphill" quarter around the cathedral and castle contrasts with the commercial "downhill" city along the High Street, connected by the aptly named Steep Hill. The University of Lincoln, founded in 1996 beside the Brayford Pool marina, has driven modern growth.
