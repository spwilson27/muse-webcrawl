Nuclear power in Sweden

Nuclear power in Sweden supplies roughly 30% of the country's electricity from six reactors at three plants: Forsmark, Oskarshamn, and Ringhals. Most of the remainder of Swedish electricity comes from hydropower and wind. Sweden has a long and politically contested history with nuclear energy.

History

Sweden built twelve commercial reactors from the 1970s, mostly boiling water reactors of domestic ASEA-Atom design plus three Westinghouse pressurized water reactors at Ringhals. A 1980 referendum, held after the Three Mile Island accident, set a course to phase out nuclear power by 2010 — a deadline later abandoned. Two reactors at Barsebäck were closed in 1999 and 2005 under political pressure, and four further units (Oskarshamn 1 and 2, Ringhals 1 and 2) were retired between 2016 and 2020 for economic reasons.

Policy reversal

In 2010 the Swedish parliament voted to allow replacement reactors, ending the phase-out policy in practice. The government elected in 2022 went further, actively promoting new nuclear build — including large reactors and small modular reactors — as necessary for electrification, industry, and climate goals, with state credit guarantees and removal of the cap on reactor numbers and sites.

Waste management

Spent fuel is stored at the CLAB interim facility at Oskarshamn. In 2022 the government approved a deep geological repository at Forsmark using the KBS-3 method of copper canisters in bentonite clay, alongside an encapsulation plant.
