Yue opera (Chinese: 越劇; pinyin: Yuèjù), also known as Shaoxing opera, is a form of Chinese opera that originated in Shengzhou, Zhejiang province, in the early 20th century. It is the second most popular opera genre in China after Peking opera, and is distinctive for its lyrical, gentle singing style and its tradition of all-female casts, in which women perform both female and male roles.

History

Yue opera developed from the folk narrative-singing traditions of the Shengzhou area, where rice-planting songs and storytellers' ballads were staged as simple plays from around 1906. Early troupes were all male, but in the 1920s and 1930s, as the form flourished in Shanghai, all-female troupes came to dominate and defined the genre's soft, elegant aesthetic. The reformer Yuan Xuefen, a leading performer, modernised Yue opera in the 1940s, introducing new scripts, staging, and costume design.

Characteristics

Performances emphasise singing in a melodic, sentimental style suited to romantic storylines, most famously the tragic love story The Butterfly Lovers and episodes from Dream of the Red Chamber. The principal role types include the young scholar (xiaosheng), usually played by a woman, and the young lady (huadan). Orchestras combine traditional Chinese instruments with some Western strings.

Legacy

The 1950s film version of The Butterfly Lovers by a Shanghai Yue opera troupe was a landmark of Chinese cinema and carried the genre to audiences across China and overseas. Yue opera remains centred on Shanghai and Zhejiang, with major companies including the Shanghai Yue Opera House, and it is inscribed on China's national list of intangible cultural heritage.
