Stibnite

Category: Sulfide mineral

Formula: Sb2S3

Color: Lead-grey, tarnishing blackish

Hardness: 2 (Mohs scale)

Stibnite, sometimes called antimonite, is a sulfide mineral with the formula Sb2S3 (antimony(III) sulfide). This soft grey material is the principal ore of antimony. The name comes from Latin stibium, the old name for the element antimony.

Structure and properties

Stibnite crystallizes in the orthorhombic system and commonly forms elongated, bladed or acicular crystals with a lead-grey metallic luster. It is very soft, with a Mohs hardness of 2, and has a high density of about 4.6. It melts readily in a candle flame and is identified by its softness, cleavage, and habit.

Occurrence

Stibnite occurs in hydrothermal veins and hot-spring deposits, associated with minerals such as realgar, orpiment, cinnabar, galena, pyrite, and quartz. Large deposits have been mined in Hunan province, China (notably the Xikuangshan mine, the world's largest antimony deposit), as well as in Japan, Romania, and the United States. Exceptionally large and perfect crystals came from the Ichinokawa mine on Shikoku, Japan.

Uses

Stibnite is the most important source of antimony, which is used in flame retardants, alloys, and semiconductors. In ancient times, powdered stibnite was used as a cosmetic (kohl) for darkening the eyelids and eyebrows in the Middle East and Mediterranean world.
