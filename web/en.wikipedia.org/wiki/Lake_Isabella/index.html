Isabella Lake (also called Lake Isabella) is a large reservoir in Kern County, California, in the southern Sierra Nevada. It was created in 1953 by the earthen Isabella Dam on the Kern River, built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers for flood control and irrigation storage.

History

Filling the reservoir inundated the former townsites of Isabella and Kernville; the town of Kernville was relocated upstream to its present site. The lake is named after Isabella, continuing the name of the old settlement. For many years the reservoir's capacity was restricted because Isabella Dam was found to lie near an active fault and was judged at risk from seismic activity and seepage; a major dam-safety modification project, including raising and strengthening the main and auxiliary dams, was completed in the early 2020s.

Recreation

Surrounded by the Sequoia National Forest, Isabella Lake is a major recreation area for boating, water skiing, windsurfing, camping and fishing — noted for largemouth bass, crappie, catfish and stocked trout. Communities around its shores include Lake Isabella, Wofford Heights, Kernville, Bodfish and South Lake. The area is also known for the annual Whiskey Flat Days festival in Kernville.
