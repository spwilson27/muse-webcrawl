Zhengyangmen

Zhengyangmen (正陽門)
Location: South of Tiananmen Square, Beijing
Built: 1419 (Ming dynasty)
Also known as: Qianmen ("Front Gate")

Zhengyangmen (lit. "Gate of the Zenith Sun"), colloquially known as Qianmen ("Front Gate"), is a historic gate in Beijing's former city wall, standing at the southern end of Tiananmen Square on the city's central north-south axis. It consists of two structures: the gate tower to the north and the archery tower to the south, once joined by a barbican.

History

The gate was built in 1419 during the reign of the Yongle Emperor, when the Ming capital's walls were rebuilt; it was originally named Lizhengmen and renamed Zhengyangmen in 1436. As the main southern gate of the Inner City, it was reserved on ceremonial occasions for the emperor's passage to the Temple of Heaven. The gate was damaged during the Boxer Rebellion in 1900 and rebuilt in the following years; in the 1910s the barbican was demolished under the architect Yang Shichao to improve traffic, separating the two towers.

The towers today

The gate tower, about 42 metres high, is the largest of Beijing's surviving city gates and has housed exhibitions on the city's history. The archery tower to the south long sheltered shops and a teahouse quarter in the surrounding Qianmen commercial district, which was redeveloped in a traditional style for the 2008 Olympics. Zhengyangmen, along with the nearby Arrow Tower and the redeveloped Qianmen Street, forms one of the capital's best-known historic landmarks, and the "Zhengyangmen" brand of Beijing cigarettes takes its name from it.
