Graeco-Arabic translation movement

[Image placeholder: Arabic translation of Dioscorides' Materia Medica]

The Graeco-Arabic translation movement was a large, well-funded and sustained effort in Baghdad and elsewhere in the Abbasid Caliphate, lasting from the mid-eighth century to the late tenth century, to translate Greek — and, via Syriac and Middle Persian intermediaries, other — works of science, medicine and philosophy into Arabic. It transferred much of the intellectual inheritance of antiquity into the Islamic world and laid the foundations for the flourishing of Arabic science and philosophy.

Background

The movement grew under the early Abbasid caliphs, especially al-Mansur, Harun al-Rashid and al-Ma'mun, whose court patronised scholars and collected manuscripts from Byzantium and Persia. Translation was supported by wealthy patrons such as the Banu Musa brothers and was carried out largely by Christian scholars fluent in Syriac and Arabic.

Translators and works

The most famous translator was Hunayn ibn Ishaq (809–873), who with his son Ishaq ibn Hunayn and nephew Hubaysh translated the medical corpus of Galen and Hippocrates, works of Plato and Aristotle, and mathematical texts. Other major figures include Thabit ibn Qurra, Qusta ibn Luqa and al-Kindi, the first major philosopher to build on the translated material. Works rendered into Arabic included Euclid's Elements, Ptolemy's Almagest, Dioscorides' Materia Medica, and most of Aristotle and his commentators.

Methods

Translators developed increasingly literal and philologically rigorous methods, revising earlier versions, collating Greek manuscripts and creating a technical Arabic vocabulary. Many translations passed through Syriac versions, a heritage of the schools of Edessa and Nisibis.

Legacy

The movement supplied the textual basis for original work by al-Khwarizmi, al-Farabi, Avicenna and Averroes. From the twelfth century, Arabic versions were in turn translated into Latin in Spain and Sicily, reintroducing Greek learning to Western Europe.
