Mopti

CountryMali
RegionMopti Region
Populationapproximately 115,000 (2009 census)

Mopti (Bambara: ߡߏߕߌ, romanized: Moti) is a town and an urban commune in the Inner Niger Delta region of Mali. The town is the capital of the Mopti Region and lies at the confluence of the Niger River and the Bani River, making it the country's most important river port.

Geography and economy

Mopti is built on three islands linked by causeways and is sometimes nicknamed the "Venice of Mali". Its harbour handles dried and smoked fish, rice, livestock, salt, and other goods traded across the Inner Niger Delta. The town is a transport hub between southern Mali, Timbuktu, and Gao, and serves as a gateway for visitors to the Dogon Country.

Landmarks

The Grand Mosque of Mopti (also known as the Komoguel Mosque), built in the Sudano-Sahelian mud-brick style between 1933 and 1935, dominates the old town. The busy Marché Souguiba and the port market are centres of commerce.
