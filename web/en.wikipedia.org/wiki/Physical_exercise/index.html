Physical exercise is any bodily activity that enhances or maintains physical fitness and overall health and wellness. It is performed for various reasons, to aid growth and improve strength, develop muscles and the cardiovascular system, hone athletic skills, improve health, or simply for enjoyment.

Classification

Exercise is commonly grouped into aerobic exercise (such as running, cycling and swimming, which improves cardiovascular endurance), anaerobic exercise (such as weight training and sprinting, which builds strength and muscle mass), and flexibility and balance training (such as stretching and yoga). Many activities combine these modes.

Health effects

Regular exercise reduces the risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, several cancers, depression and cognitive decline, and improves bone density, sleep and immune function. It is considered one of the most effective preventive health measures. Conversely, excessive training without recovery can cause injury and overtraining syndrome, and sudden vigorous exertion carries a small transient cardiac risk in sedentary individuals.

History

Deliberate exercise for health dates to antiquity — in China, India, Greece and Rome — but declined in parts of the post-classical West. It revived with 19th-century gymnastics movements in Germany, Sweden and Britain, and became a mass phenomenon in the 20th century with jogging, aerobics and commercial fitness industries.

Guidelines

The World Health Organization recommends that adults accumulate 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity, or 75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity, aerobic activity weekly, plus muscle-strengthening activities on two or more days. Children and adolescents should average at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity daily.
