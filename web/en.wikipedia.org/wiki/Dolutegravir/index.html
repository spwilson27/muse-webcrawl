Dolutegravir

Dolutegravir (DTG), sold under the brand name Tivicay among others, is an antiretroviral medication used, together with other medications, to treat HIV/AIDS. It is also used, as a fixed-dose combination with lamivudine, as a complete two-drug regimen for treatment of HIV. It works as an integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI), blocking the HIV integrase enzyme that inserts viral DNA into the host cell genome.

Medical uses

Dolutegravir is used in combination therapy for adults and children with HIV infection. Because of its potency, high barrier to resistance, tolerability and once-daily dosing, the World Health Organization recommends dolutegravir-based regimens — most commonly the fixed-dose combination of tenofovir, lamivudine and dolutegravir (TLD) — as the preferred first-line treatment for adults and adolescents with HIV. It is also used in two-drug combinations (with lamivudine, marketed as Dovato, or with rilpivirine, marketed as Juluca).

History

Dolutegravir was developed by ViiV Healthcare (with Shionogi) and approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration in August 2013. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. Patent arrangements through the Medicines Patent Pool made inexpensive generic versions widely available in low- and middle-income countries, enabling tens of millions of people to receive dolutegravir-based therapy.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include insomnia, headache and fatigue; increases in weight have been reported in some studies. A Botswana surveillance study (Tsepamo) in 2018 initially suggested a possible increased risk of neural tube defects when dolutegravir was taken around conception, but the estimated risk fell as more data accumulated, and the WHO continues to recommend the drug for all adults and adolescents, including women of childbearing potential, with informed choice. Dolutegravir chelates polyvalent cations, so antacids and iron or calcium supplements must be spaced away from dosing, and it raises levels of metformin by inhibiting renal transporters.
