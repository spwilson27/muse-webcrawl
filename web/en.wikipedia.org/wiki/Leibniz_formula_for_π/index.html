Leibniz formula for π

In mathematics, the Leibniz formula for π, named after Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, states that

π/4 = 1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − 1/7 + 1/9 − … = Σk=0∞ (−1)k / (2k + 1)

It is the Taylor series for the arctangent function evaluated at x = 1. The series is also known as the Madhava–Leibniz series, after the Indian mathematician Madhava of Sangamagrama, who discovered it some 250 years before Leibniz, along with faster-converging variants used by the Kerala school.

Convergence

The series converges extremely slowly: by the alternating series error bound, the error after n terms is about 1/(2n), so some five billion terms would be needed to compute π to ten correct decimal places. For this reason it is of theoretical rather than practical interest, although acceleration methods such as Euler summation can transform it into rapidly convergent forms.

Proof sketch

Integrating the geometric series for 1/(1 + x2) term by term from 0 to 1 gives the arctangent series; substituting x = 1, where arctan(1) = π/4, yields the formula.
