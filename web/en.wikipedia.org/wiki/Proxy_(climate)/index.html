Proxy (climate)

In the study of past climates, climate proxies are preserved physical characteristics of the natural environment that can be measured and used to reconstruct climatic conditions over periods before instrumental records existed. A proxy substitutes for direct temperature or precipitation measurements: scientists calibrate the proxy against the instrumental record and then apply the relationship to older material.

Types of proxy

  • Ice cores — trapped air bubbles and isotopic ratios in Greenland and Antarctic ice record temperature and atmospheric composition back hundreds of thousands of years.
  • Tree rings — annual ring widths and densities reflect growing-season temperature and moisture (dendroclimatology).
  • Marine and lake sediments — foraminifera shells, pollen and diatoms record ocean and catchment conditions.
  • Corals and speleothems — growth bands and isotope ratios record sea-surface temperature and rainfall.
  • Boreholes and documents — subsurface temperature profiles and historical records such as harvest dates supplement natural archives.

Reconstructions

Combining many proxies allows reconstructions of hemispheric temperature over the last two millennia, including the so-called "hockey stick" graphs first produced by Mann, Bradley and Hughes in 1998–1999. Such reconstructions carry uncertainties from dating, calibration and the spatial coverage of proxy networks, and they are cross-checked against each other and against climate models.
