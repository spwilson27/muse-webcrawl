The Los Angeles Police Department (LAPD), officially the City of Los Angeles Police Department, is the primary law enforcement agency of Los Angeles, California, United States. With roughly 9,000 sworn officers serving a population of about 4 million across 470 square miles, it is the third-largest municipal police department in the United States, after the New York City and Chicago police departments.

History

The LAPD was founded in 1869, replacing volunteer and ranger forces of the frontier era. Under Chief William H. Parker (1950–1966) it was professionalised and became famous through the television series Dragnet, whose creator Jack Webb popularised the department's image — and its motto, adopted in 1955 from an internal magazine contest. The department's history also includes major controversies: the 1965 Watts riots, the 1991 beating of Rodney King and the ensuing 1992 riots, and the Rampart Division CRASH scandal of the late 1990s, which led to a federal consent decree (2001–2013) imposing reforms. The LAPD pioneered SWAT teams (under Daryl Gates in the late 1960s) and was among the first departments to employ women officers and a policewoman, Alice Stebbins Wells, in 1910.

Organisation

The department is headed by the Chief of Police, appointed by the civilian Board of Police Commissioners. It is organised into geographic bureaus (Central, South, Valley and West), 21 community police stations, and specialised units including Metropolitan Division (which includes SWAT), the Air Support Division, Robbery-Homicide Division and the counter-terrorism bureau.
