Generalized Riemann hypothesis

The generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) extends the Riemann hypothesis from the Riemann zeta function to Dirichlet L-functions. It states that for every Dirichlet character χ and every zero ρ of the associated L-function L(s, χ) lying in the critical strip 0 < Re(s) < 1, the real part of ρ equals 1/2. The hypothesis was first formulated by Adolf Piltz in 1884 and remains unproven; it is one of the most important open problems in analytic number theory.

GRH asserts that all nontrivial zeros of Dirichlet L-functions lie on the critical line Re(s) = 1/2.

Statement

For a Dirichlet character χ modulo q, the Dirichlet L-function L(s, χ) is defined for Re(s) > 1 by an infinite series and continued analytically to the whole complex plane. GRH asserts that every zero of this continuation inside the critical strip lies on the critical line Re(s) = 1/2. The case of the principal character recovers the original Riemann hypothesis for the zeta function.

Consequences

Many results in number theory are proved conditionally on GRH. These include strong forms of Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions with tight error terms; the statement that the least prime in an arithmetic progression a mod q (coprime) is at most about q² (Linnik's theorem with exponent 2); efficient deterministic primality testing — Miller's test is polynomial-time assuming GRH; the weak Goldbach conjecture for sufficiently large numbers (before its unconditional proof); and bounds on class numbers of number fields. GRH also implies sharp estimates for character sums and for the distribution of primes in short intervals.

Extended Riemann hypothesis

A further extension, the extended Riemann hypothesis, makes the same claim for Dedekind zeta functions of number fields; the broadest versions apply to automorphic L-functions (the grand Riemann hypothesis).
