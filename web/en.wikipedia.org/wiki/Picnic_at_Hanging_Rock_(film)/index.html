Picnic at Hanging Rock is a 1975 Australian mystery film directed by Peter Weir and based on the 1967 novel of the same name by Joan Lindsay. Cliff Green adapted the novel into a screenplay. The film stars Rachel Roberts, Dominic Guard, Helen Morse, and Jacki Weaver. It was a critical and commercial success, helping draw international attention to the Australian New Wave of cinema.

Plot

On St Valentine's Day, 1900, a group of schoolgirls from Appleyard College, a private girls' school near Mount Macedon in Victoria, set out on a picnic to Hanging Rock. During the afternoon, four of the girls venture up the rock; three of them — Miranda, Marion, and Irma — along with a teacher, Miss McCraw, disappear without a trace. Irma is later found alive, with no memory of what happened, but the others are never found. The unresolved mystery destabilizes the school and its formidable headmistress, Mrs Appleyard, and haunts the local community.

Production

The film was shot on location at Hanging Rock and in South Australia, with cinematographer Russell Boyd using soft-focus photography, achieved in part by stretching bridal veil fabric over the lens, to create the film's dreamlike atmosphere. The score by Bruce Smeaton prominently features the pan flute of Gheorghe Zamfir playing traditional Romanian melodies. The deliberately ambiguous ending, faithful to Lindsay's novel, left audiences debating whether the story was based on real events — it is fictional, though Lindsay's coy presentation encouraged speculation.

Reception and legacy

The film is widely regarded as one of the greatest Australian films ever made and a landmark of the Australian New Wave. A "director's cut" released in 1998 removed about seven minutes of footage. In 2018 the story was adapted as a television miniseries. The film was selected for preservation in Australia's National Film and Sound Archive registry.
