The Camp Fire was the deadliest and most destructive wildfire in California history. Ignited on November 8, 2018, in Butte County near Pulga, it destroyed the town of Paradise and killed 85 people.

Cause

Investigators determined the fire was ignited by electrical transmission lines owned by Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E) near the Poe Dam. PG&E later pleaded guilty to 84 counts of involuntary manslaughter and filed for bankruptcy under the weight of wildfire liabilities.

Impact

The fire destroyed 18,804 structures, displaced tens of thousands of residents, and blanketed Northern California in hazardous smoke for weeks. Paradise has partially rebuilt under new fire-safety standards, and the disaster reshaped California utility regulation and wildfire policy.
