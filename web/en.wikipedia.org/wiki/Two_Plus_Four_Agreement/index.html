Two Plus Four Agreement

The Treaty on the Final Settlement with Respect to Germany, commonly called the Two Plus Four Agreement, was negotiated in 1990 between the Federal Republic of Germany and the German Democratic Republic (the "two") and the four powers that occupied Germany after the Second World War — France, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom and the United States (the "four").

Terms

Signed in Moscow on 12 September 1990, the treaty restored full sovereignty to a united Germany, confirmed the Oder–Neisse line as Germany's eastern border, set a ceiling on German armed forces, banned nuclear, biological and chemical weapons on German soil, and provided for the withdrawal of Soviet troops by the end of 1994. It functioned as the peace settlement for Germany that had never been concluded after 1945, and cleared the way for German reunification on 3 October 1990.
