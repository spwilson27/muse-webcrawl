Union of South Africa

The Union of South Africa (Dutch: Unie van Zuid-Afrika; Afrikaans: Unie van Suid-Afrika) was the historical predecessor to the present-day Republic of South Africa. It came into existence on 31 May 1910 with the unification of the British colonies of the Cape and Natal and the Boer republics of the Transvaal and Orange Free State, which had become colonies after the Second Boer War.

History

The Union was formed under the South Africa Act 1909 passed by the British Parliament. It was a dominion of the British Empire, with the British monarch as head of state, represented by a governor-general, and Louis Botha as its first prime minister. The Statute of Westminster 1931 increased its legislative independence.

South Africa participated in both world wars on the Allied side. In 1948 the National Party came to power and formally instituted apartheid. After a referendum among white voters in 1960, South Africa became a republic on 31 May 1961 and left the Commonwealth of Nations.
