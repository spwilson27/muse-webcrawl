Palm Sunday

Palm Sunday
Observed byChristians worldwide
DateSunday before Easter; first day of Holy Week
SignificanceCommemorates Jesus's triumphal entry into Jerusalem

Palm Sunday is a Christian feast falling on the Sunday before Easter, the first day of Holy Week. It commemorates the triumphal entry of Jesus into Jerusalem, described in all four Gospels, when crowds spread palm branches and their cloaks on the road before him, crying "Hosanna".

Liturgy and customs

In many churches the liturgy begins with a procession in which the faithful carry palm branches, or in colder climates branches of native trees such as willow, yew, or olive; hence local names such as Willow Sunday. Palms are blessed and distributed, and in Catholic and some other traditions the previous year's palms are burned to make the ashes for Ash Wednesday. The Mass or service typically includes a long reading of the Passion narrative.

Observance worldwide

Elaborate processions are held in Spain and Latin America during Semana Santa, in the Philippines (where woven palm fronds called palaspas are brought to church), and in Ethiopia and the Eastern Orthodox world, where the feast is one of the Twelve Great Feasts.
