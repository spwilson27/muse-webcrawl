Caxixi

Caxixi
Type: idiophone (shaken rattle)
Origin: West Africa / Brazil
Used in: capoeira, Afro-Brazilian music

The caxixi (pronounced ka-shee-shee) is a percussion instrument consisting of a closed basket with a flat bottom filled with seeds or other small particles. It is an indirectly struck idiophone, like a maraca. Originating in West Africa, it was brought to Brazil, where it is an essential accompaniment to the berimbau in capoeira music.

Construction and playing

Traditional caxixi are woven from rattan or wicker with a bottom cut from a calabash gourd, filled with seeds, shells, or small stones. The player holds it in the same hand as the berimbau stick, shaking it to produce a sharp, rattling accent that varies depending on whether the seeds strike the woven side or the hard gourd bottom. Sizes range from small children's versions to large bass caxixi.

Use

Beyond capoeira, the caxixi appears in Afro-Brazilian religious music, samba, and contemporary world percussion, and it is widely used in music education and drum circles.
