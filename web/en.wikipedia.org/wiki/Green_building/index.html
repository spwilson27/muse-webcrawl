Green building

Green building (also known as sustainable building) refers to both the structure and the application of processes that are environmentally responsible and resource-efficient throughout a building's life cycle: from planning to design, construction, operation, maintenance, renovation, and demolition. Its goals include efficient use of energy, water, and materials; protection of occupant health; and reduction of waste and environmental degradation.

Principles

Green design typically begins with siting and orientation to exploit daylight and natural ventilation, a high-performance building envelope, and efficient heating, cooling, and lighting systems. Materials are selected for low embodied carbon, durability, recycled content, and low toxicity; construction waste is diverted from landfill; and water use is reduced through efficient fixtures, rainwater harvesting, and greywater recycling. Because buildings account for roughly a third of global energy-related emissions, operational energy savings are usually the largest single benefit, though embodied emissions from materials such as concrete and steel are a growing focus.

Certification

Voluntary rating systems give designers and owners a framework and a market signal. LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design), administered by the U.S. Green Building Council since the 1990s, awards Certified, Silver, Gold, and Platinum levels. BREEAM, launched in the United Kingdom in 1990, is the oldest such scheme. Other systems include Germany's DGNB, Australia's Green Star, and the WELL Building Standard, which emphasizes health. Critics note that certification can reward design intent rather than measured performance, prompting a shift toward performance-based verification and disclosure laws.

Costs and benefits

Studies generally find a modest construction cost premium — often 0–4% — offset by lower operating costs, higher rents and resale values, and improved occupant productivity and health. Retrofitting existing buildings, which will constitute most of the building stock for decades, is increasingly the sector's priority.
