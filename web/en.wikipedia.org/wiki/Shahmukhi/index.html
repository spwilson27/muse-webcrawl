Shahmukhi

Shahmukhi (شاہ مکھی, "from the mouth of the Shah") is a Perso-Arabic script used to write the Punjabi language, primarily in the Punjab province of Pakistan. It is one of the two standard scripts for Punjabi, the other being Gurmukhi, used mainly in India. Shahmukhi is written right-to-left in a style closely related to the Urdu Nastaʿlīq tradition and shares most of its letter inventory with Urdu.

Characteristics

Shahmukhi uses the Urdu alphabet with additional letters for Punjabi sounds, including retroflex consonants marked with the ṭoʾe (ط) diacritic above letters — for example ڑ for the retroflex flap /ɽ/ — and letters for the nasalised and aspirated consonants of Punjabi. As in Urdu, short vowels are usually unwritten, and the script is normally printed in Nastaʿlīq style. Punjabi's tonal contrasts are not directly represented in Shahmukhi (nor fully in Gurmukhi), being inferred from consonant choice and context, with the letter ھ used to mark tone in some positions.

History and use

Punjabi has been written in Perso-Arabic characters since the medieval period; Shahmukhi carried the classical Sufi poetry of Punjab — the qissas and kafis of Shah Hussain, Bulleh Shah, and Waris Shah (whose Heer is the best-known Punjabi epic). Today Shahmukhi is the script of Punjabi newspapers, books, and schooling in Pakistani Punjab, though Urdu dominates formal education and administration. The Partition of 1947 entrenched the script divide: Shahmukhi in the west, Gurmukhi in the east.

Literature

Modern Shahmukhi literature includes the poetry of Ustad Daman and Shiv Kumar Batalvi's Pakistani readership, fiction, and a growing digital presence, though Punjabi in Pakistan is often written informally in Urdu script conventions without strict Shahmukhi orthography.
