House music

House
Stylistic origins: Disco, post-disco, electronic, soul, funk
Cultural origins: Early 1980s, Chicago, United States
Typical tempo: 115–130 BPM
Key figures: Frankie Knuckles, Larry Levan, Marshall Jefferson, Mr. Fingers
Subgenres: Deep house, acid house, tech house, Chicago house

House is a genre of electronic dance music characterised by a repetitive four-on-the-floor beat and a typical tempo of 115 to 130 beats per minute. It was created by DJs and music producers from Chicago's underground club culture — notably at the Warehouse club, from which the genre takes its name — in the early to mid-1980s.

Characteristics

House tracks typically feature a kick drum on every beat, off-beat hi-hats, synthesized basslines (often from the Roland TB-303 in acid house), sampled or soulful vocals, and piano or string stabs. Producers such as Frankie Knuckles, known as the "Godfather of House," Ron Hardy, Marshall Jefferson, and Larry Heard (Mr. Fingers) defined the early sound using drum machines like the TR-808 and TR-909.

Spread and influence

By the late 1980s house had spread to New York, London, and Ibiza, fuelling the UK's acid house and rave scenes. It gave rise to countless subgenres and profoundly shaped later dance music, including techno (in parallel in Detroit), trance, garage, and modern EDM.
