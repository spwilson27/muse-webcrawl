Crisis negotiation

Crisis negotiation is a law enforcement technique used to communicate with people in crisis — including hostage-takers, barricaded suspects, and persons threatening suicide — with the goal of resolving incidents peacefully. It was pioneered in the 1970s, notably by the New York City Police Department following the Attica prison riot and the Munich massacre.

Methods

Negotiators build rapport through active listening, empathy, and patience, following models such as the Behavioral Change Stairway Model developed by the FBI: active listening leads to empathy, rapport, influence, and finally behavioural change. Time is treated as an ally, allowing emotions to subside.

Organisation

Specialised units such as the FBI's Crisis Negotiation Unit and Hostage Rescue Team support agencies in high-risk incidents. Negotiation teams typically include a primary and secondary negotiator, a team leader, and intelligence and mental-health support.
