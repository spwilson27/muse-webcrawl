509th Composite Group

Active1944–1952
CountryUnited States
BranchUnited States Army Air Forces
CommanderCol. Paul W. Tibbets

The 509th Composite Group was a group of the United States Army Air Forces created during World War II and charged with the operational deployment of nuclear weapons. It achieved fame as the unit that carried out the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945.

Organization

Activated in December 1944 under Colonel Paul W. Tibbets, the group was deliberately self-contained, with its own transport, engineering, and security elements, and trained at Wendover Field, Utah, before deploying to Tinian in the Mariana Islands. Its 393rd Bombardment Squadron flew specially modified B-29 Superfortress bombers, the "Silverplate" aircraft.

Atomic missions

On 6 August 1945 the B-29 Enola Gay, piloted by Tibbets, dropped the "Little Boy" bomb on Hiroshima. Three days later, Bockscar dropped "Fat Man" on Nagasaki. The group's lineage continues today in the 509th Bomb Wing, which operates B-2 Spirit bombers from Whiteman Air Force Base.
