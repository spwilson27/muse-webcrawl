Minsmere is a nature reserve owned and managed by the Royal Society for the Protection of Birds (RSPB) on the Suffolk coast of eastern England, between Southwold and Aldeburgh. Covering about 1,000 hectares of reedbed, shallow lagoons, lowland wet grassland, heathland, woodland and shingle beach, it is one of the RSPB's flagship reserves and one of the richest bird sites in Britain, with more than 340 species recorded. The nearby coastal area is dominated by the Sizewell nuclear power stations, visible from the reserve.

The Scrape, dug in the 1960s, made Minsmere famous for its breeding avocets.

History

The Minsmere marshes were drained for grazing in the nineteenth century after earlier medieval reclamation, and the area was reflooded in 1940 as an anti-invasion defence. The RSPB took a lease in 1947 and purchased the land in 1977. In 1947 avocets bred in Britain for the first time in over a century at Minsmere and nearby Havergate Island; the avocet became the logo of the RSPB. In the 1960s warden Bert Axell pioneered habitat management at the reserve, creating the Scrape — a shallow lagoon with islands for nesting waders and terns — and a network of hides and trails that became a model for reserve design.

Wildlife

Breeding species include bitterns, marsh harriers, bearded tits, avocets, Mediterranean gulls and Dartford warblers; the reserve is also notable for otters, water voles, red deer, and insects such as the Norfolk hawker dragonfly. The reedbeds are among the largest in England and are managed on rotation for bitterns. Minsmere hosted the BBC's Springwatch from 2014 to 2016, bringing it to a wide television audience.
