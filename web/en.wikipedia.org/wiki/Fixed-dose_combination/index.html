Fixed-dose combination

A fixed-dose combination (FDC) is a medicine that includes two or more active ingredients combined in a single dosage form, such as a tablet or capsule, at fixed doses. FDCs are widely used in the treatment of HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria, hypertension, and diabetes.

Advantages

Combining medicines into one pill reduces pill burden, simplifies regimens, and improves patient adherence, which is critical for preventing drug resistance in anti-infective therapy. Single-pill antiretroviral combinations transformed HIV treatment, and FDCs are included on the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines for several conditions.

Criticism

FDCs limit dose titration of individual components, can complicate management of side effects, and some marketed combinations—particularly the many FDCs sold in India—have been criticised as irrational, combining drugs without proven benefit or with incompatible pharmacokinetics. Regulators in several countries have moved to ban or restrict such products.
