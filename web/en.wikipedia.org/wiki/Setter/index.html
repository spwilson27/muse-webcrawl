Setter

A setter is a type of gun dog used most often for hunting game birds such as quail, pheasant and grouse. Setters take their name from their distinctive behaviour on finding game: the dog crouches (or "sets") low and freezes, indicating the location of hidden birds to the hunter.

History

Setters were developed in Britain from setting spaniels used with nets in medieval hunting. With the advent of shooting flying birds in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, setters were refined into fast, wide-ranging dogs with keen noses that quarter ground methodically and point scent once located. John Caius described setting dogs in his Of English Dogs (1576).

Breeds

The setter group recognised by major kennel clubs comprises four breeds: the English Setter, with its speckled "belton" coat; the Gordon Setter, a black-and-tan Scottish breed; the Irish Setter, renowned for its rich red coat; and the Irish Red and White Setter, the older Irish form from which the solid red dog was derived. In the United States, the Llewellin setter, a strain of English setter, is also highly regarded in field trials.

Temperament and care

Setters are energetic, affectionate dogs that require substantial exercise and mental stimulation. Their long, silky coats need regular grooming to prevent matting.
