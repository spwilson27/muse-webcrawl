Porridge

Porridge is a dish made by heating or boiling ground, crushed or chopped starchy plants — typically grains — in water or milk. It is usually served hot in a bowl, often as a breakfast food, and may be sweetened or flavoured. Oat porridge is the most common type in the English-speaking world, but porridges are made worldwide from rice, maize, wheat, barley, sorghum, millet and many other staples.

History

Porridge is among the oldest prepared foods: evidence of oat-based porridge-like meals dates to the Neolithic era, and heated grain gruels were staples in ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome. In medieval Europe, coarse porridges and pottages were the everyday food of peasants, cooked in a large pot over an open fire. In Scotland, oat porridge became a national staple, traditionally stirred with a wooden stick called a spurtle and sometimes eaten standing, with each spoonful dipped into a separate bowl of cold milk.

Types

  • Oat porridge — rolled or steel-cut oats cooked in water or milk.
  • Congee — rice porridge eaten across East and Southeast Asia, often savoury.
  • Grits and polenta — coarse maize porridges of the American South and Italy respectively.
  • Kasha — buckwheat porridge of Eastern Europe.
  • Uji and ugali — millet, sorghum or maize porridges of East Africa.

Nutrition

Grain porridges are a source of dietary fibre and slow-release carbohydrate. Oat porridge in particular is rich in beta-glucan, a soluble fibre associated with reduced cholesterol absorption.
