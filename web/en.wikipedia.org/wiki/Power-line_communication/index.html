Power-line communication

Power-line communication (PLC) is the carrying of data on a conductor that is also used simultaneously for AC electric power transmission or distribution. Because it reuses existing wiring, PLC avoids installing new cables. Systems range from narrowband signals used by utilities for metering and control to broadband home networking.

Applications

Utilities have used PLC since the early 20th century for telemetry and remote switching, and narrowband PLC (standardized in protocols such as G3-PLC and PRIME) now supports smart meters and smart grid functions. Broadband over power lines (BPL) was trialled as an internet access technology in the 2000s but largely lost out to cable and DSL. Within buildings, standards such as HomePlug and ITU-T G.hn let adapters send Ethernet data over household wiring, and PLC is also used inside vehicles and aircraft to reduce wiring weight.

Technical challenges

Power lines are a noisy, harsh channel: impedance varies with connected loads, appliances inject interference, and signals attenuate through distribution transformers. Unshielded wiring also radiates radio-frequency energy, which has led to long-standing interference disputes with amateur radio operators and corresponding emissions regulations.
