Hackers on Planet Earth (HOPE) is a series of hacker conferences sponsored by the magazine 2600: The Hacker Quarterly, first held in 1994. One of the longest-running hacker conventions in the United States, it typically features talks on computer security, privacy, technology, politics and hacker culture, alongside workshops, film screenings and hardware-hacking areas.

History

The first HOPE conference took place in 1994 at the Hotel Pennsylvania in Manhattan, marking the first decade of 2600 magazine. The venue hosted most subsequent conferences, with editions given playful names such as "Beyond HOPE" (1997), "H2K" (2000), "The Fifth HOPE" (2004), "The Next HOPE" (2010), "HOPE X" (2014) and "A New HOPE" (2022), the latter held at St. John's University after the Hotel Pennsylvania's closure. A virtual conference, HOPE 2020, was held online.

Content

Speakers have included security researchers, journalists, lawyers and whistleblowers; keynotes have been given by figures such as Steve Wozniak, Jello Biafra, Edward Snowden (by video link) and Chelsea Manning. HOPE is associated with the hacker ethic of open access to information, and its talks are recorded and published freely online.
