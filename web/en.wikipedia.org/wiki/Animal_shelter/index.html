Animal shelter

An animal shelter or pound is a place where stray, lost, abandoned, or surrendered animals—mostly dogs and cats—are housed. The term "pound" derives from the agricultural pounds of the 18th and 19th centuries, where stray livestock were impounded until claimed by their owners. In modern usage, shelters are typically operated by municipalities, humane societies, or private non-profit organisations, and are staffed by paid employees and volunteers.

Functions

Shelters take in stray animals found by the public or seized by animal control officers, hold them for a legally specified period to allow owners to reclaim them, and offer unclaimed animals for adoption. Most shelters provide veterinary care, spaying and neutering, vaccinations, and microchipping. Many also run foster programmes, behaviour rehabilitation, and trap–neuter–return schemes for feral cats.

Euthanasia and no-kill

Where intake exceeds capacity and adoptions, shelters may euthanise animals. In the United States, shelter euthanasia has fallen dramatically since the 1970s—when estimates exceeded ten million animals annually—as spay/neuter programmes and adoption promotion expanded. The "no-kill" movement seeks to end the euthanasia of healthy or treatable animals; a shelter is commonly described as no-kill if it saves at least 90 percent of the animals it takes in.

Related organisations

Breed-specific and species-specific rescue groups, sanctuaries for animals that cannot be adopted, and large charities such as the RSPCA in England and Wales and the ASPCA in the United States complement municipal shelters.
