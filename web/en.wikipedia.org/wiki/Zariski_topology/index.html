Zariski topology

The Zariski topology is a topology defined on algebraic varieties and, more generally, on the spectrum of a commutative ring. It was introduced by Oscar Zariski and is the standard topology of algebraic geometry. Unlike the familiar topologies of analysis, it is extremely coarse: its open sets are large and few, and the space is almost never Hausdorff. Its closed sets are precisely the affine algebraic sets — the sets of common zeros of collections of polynomials.

Definition on affine space

On affine n-space over a field k, the closed sets are the sets V(S) of common zeros of sets S of polynomials. The empty set and the whole space are closed; finite unions and arbitrary intersections of algebraic sets are again algebraic, so these sets satisfy the axioms for the closed sets of a topology. On the affine line over an algebraically closed field, the proper closed sets are exactly the finite sets, so the Zariski topology there is the cofinite topology.

The spectrum of a ring

For a commutative ring R, the spectrum Spec(R) is the set of prime ideals of R with the topology whose closed sets are the sets of prime ideals containing a given ideal. This construction turns commutative algebra into geometry: points of Spec(ℤ), for example, correspond to the prime numbers together with a generic point. Equipped with its structure sheaf, the spectrum becomes the basic building block of the modern theory of schemes.

Properties

Zariski spaces are quasi-compact and possess a basis of quasi-compact open sets. Every irreducible closed subset has a unique generic point whose closure is the whole subset. The topology is T0 but, except in trivial cases, not T1 on the spectrum and not Hausdorff. Polynomial functions are continuous between varieties in the Zariski topology, and a map between varieties that is continuous in this topology need not be continuous in the classical topology — indeed the Zariski topology is far weaker, which is why results such as the Zariski main theorem are striking.

Contrast with analytic topologies

When the ground field is ℂ, a variety also carries the classical (analytic) topology inherited from complex space. That topology is much finer, and algebraic geometers pass between the two according to need. Because Zariski-closed sets are so large, Zariski-open conditions, such as smoothness at a generic point, express properties holding "almost everywhere" on a variety.
