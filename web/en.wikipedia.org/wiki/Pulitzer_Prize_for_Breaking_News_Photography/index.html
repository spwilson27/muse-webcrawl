Pulitzer Prize for Breaking News Photography

The Pulitzer Prize for Breaking News Photography is one of the American Pulitzer Prizes annually awarded for distinguished journalism. It recognizes a distinguished example of breaking news photography in black and white or color, which may consist of a photograph or photographs, a sequence, or an album.

The category was established in 2000, when the former Pulitzer Prize for Spot News Photography was renamed. The spot news category itself dated to 1968, when the original photography prize was split into two awards: one for spot news and one for feature photography.

History

The Pulitzer photography prizes were first awarded in 1942. In 1968 the single photography category was divided into Spot News Photography and Feature Photography. The spot news award honored immediate coverage of unfolding events, from fires and disasters to assassinations and street violence. In 2000 the Board renamed it Breaking News Photography to reflect the broader range of newsworthy events covered, including planned events of great significance.

Notable winners

Early winners in the breaking news category included the staff of the Rocky Mountain News for coverage of the Columbine High School massacre (2000), the staff of The New York Times for coverage of the September 11 attacks (2002), and photographers of the Associated Press for images of the Iraq War. In 2016 the award went jointly to The New York Times and Thomson Reuters for photographs of refugees crossing into Europe. In 2019 the Reuters staff won for images of migrants on the journey to the United States border, and in 2021 the Associated Press won for coverage of the protests following the murder of George Floyd. In 2023 the Associated Press staff won for images from the war in Ukraine, including the siege of Mariupol.
