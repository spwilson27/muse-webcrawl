Marc Léopold Benjamin Bloch (6 July 1886 – 16 June 1944) was a French historian, a founder of the Annales School of historical writing and one of the most influential historians of the 20th century. A specialist in medieval French history, Bloch, with Lucien Febvre, founded the journal Annales d'histoire économique et sociale in 1929, championing an interdisciplinary "total history" that drew on geography, economics, sociology and anthropology. A decorated veteran of the First World War, he fought again in 1940, joined the French Resistance, and was captured, tortured and shot by the Gestapo in 1944.

Life

Bloch was born in Lyon into an Alsatian Jewish family; his father Gustave Bloch was a professor of ancient history. Educated at the École Normale Supérieure, he studied in Berlin and Leipzig, taught in lycées, and served through the First World War as an infantry officer, winning the Croix de Guerre. In 1919 he joined the refounded University of Strasbourg, where he met Febvre, and in 1936 he moved to the Sorbonne. As a Jew he was excluded from his chair under the Vichy racial laws, though an exceptional dispensation allowed him to teach at Montpellier for a time. In 1943 he went underground in Lyon, working for the Resistance movement Franc-Tireur under the pseudonym "Narbonne". Arrested in March 1944, he was tortured by the Gestapo — in the Lyon headquarters directed by Klaus Barbie — and shot with other prisoners at Saint-Didier-de-Formans on 16 June 1944.

Works

His first major book, The Royal Touch (1924; Les Rois thaumaturges), studied the belief that the kings of France and England could heal scrofula by touch, and became a classic of the history of mentalities. Feudal Society (1939–40) surveyed the social order of medieval Europe between the 9th and 13th centuries. Strange Defeat (written 1940, published 1946) is a searing first-hand analysis of France's collapse. His unfinished methodological testament, The Historian's Craft (Apologie pour l'histoire), edited by Febvre and published posthumously in 1949, remains one of the most widely read reflections on the practice of history, famous for its plea that the historian, "like the ogre of the fairy tale, must scent human flesh" wherever the past is concerned.

Legacy

The Annales approach reshaped historiography worldwide, influencing Fernand Braudel and the later generations of the school. The humanities university of Strasbourg bore Bloch's name (Université Marc Bloch) until a merger in 2009.
