Dunkelweizen

Dunkelweizen (German for "dark wheat") is a dark wheat beer style originating in Bavaria, southern Germany. It combines the character of a wheat beer (Weizenbier or Weissbier) — brewed with a large proportion of wheat malt and fermented with a distinctive ale yeast — with the darker colour and richer malt flavours of a dark lager such as a Dunkel. Typical examples are mahogany-brown, hazy, and have an alcohol content of about 4.5–5.5%.

Characteristics

The style's flavour comes from two sources: the wheat and dark barley malts, which contribute bready, caramel, chocolate, and sometimes toffee notes; and the Bavarian weizen yeast, which produces esters and phenols perceived as banana and clove. Dunkelweizen is usually unfiltered, retaining a yeast haze, and is served in the tall, vase-shaped Weizen glass to support its large, rocky head. Hop bitterness is low, and carbonation is high.

Dunkelweizen is generally considered the older, more traditional form of wheat beer, predating the pale Hefeweizen that became dominant in the twentieth century. Stronger dark wheat variants include the Weizenbock. Well-known commercial examples are produced by breweries such as Weihenstephaner, Erdinger, Paulaner, and Franziskaner.
