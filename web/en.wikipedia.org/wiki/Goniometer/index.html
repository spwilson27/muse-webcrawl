Goniometer

A goniometer is an instrument for measuring angles, or for positioning an object at a precisely known angle. The name derives from the Greek gōnia (angle) and metron (measure). Goniometers have long been used in crystallography, surveying, medicine, and physics.

Applications

In crystallography, contact and reflecting goniometers measure the angles between crystal faces, data fundamental to identifying minerals and determining crystal structure; the optical reflecting goniometer developed in the 19th century enabled accurate measurement on small crystals. In medicine and physical therapy, simple two-armed goniometers measure the range of motion of joints. In audiology and radio work, a goniometer is an electronic direction-finding instrument. Position goniometers are also used in X-ray diffraction and in laboratories to rotate samples with fine angular control.

Types

Types range from the simple contact goniometer of Carangeot (c. 1780) to precision optical instruments with graduated circles read by microscopes or encoders, and modern digital inclinometers used in rehabilitation and ergonomics.
