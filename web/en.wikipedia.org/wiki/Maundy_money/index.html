Maundy money

Maundy coins are specially minted silver coins presented by the British monarch at the Royal Maundy service.

Maundy money is specially minted silver money distributed by the monarch of the United Kingdom at the Royal Maundy service on Maundy Thursday, the day before Good Friday. The ceremony commemorates Jesus washing the feet of the Apostles at the Last Supper, and traces its origins in England to at least the reign of John (1199–1216).

The ceremony

In the traditional service, the monarch personally presents a small purse to each recipient. Each recipient — one man and one woman for each year of the monarch's age — receives two purses: a red purse containing ordinary coinage as an allowance for clothing and provisions, and a white purse containing specially struck silver Maundy coins. The denominations are 1p, 2p, 3p and 4p, and the total value in pence equals the monarch's age in years.

History

In medieval times monarchs themselves washed the feet of the poor and distributed alms. The foot-washing was discontinued by James II in 1685, and the distribution of money by the monarch or a representative continued. In modern practice the ceremony moves each year to a different cathedral or abbey around the country, and the recipients are chosen from the local community in recognition of service to their churches and communities. The coins, struck in sterling silver by the Royal Mint, are legal tender but are rarely spent, and are prized by collectors.
