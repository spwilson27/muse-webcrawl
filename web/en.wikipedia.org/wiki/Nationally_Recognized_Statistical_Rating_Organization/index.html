Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organization

Abbreviation: NRSRO
Regulator: U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission
Term coined: 1975 (SEC net capital rule)
Statutory basis: Credit Rating Agency Reform Act of 2006

A Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organization (NRSRO) is a credit rating agency registered with and recognised by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), whose ratings may be used for regulatory purposes in the United States, such as determining capital requirements and permissible investments.

History

The SEC introduced the term in 1975 in amendments to its net capital rule for broker-dealers, relying on ratings from agencies it deemed "nationally recognised" — initially Moody's, Standard & Poor's and Fitch. Because the designation was granted informally and new entrants faced a chicken-and-egg problem (they needed market recognition to gain the status, and the status to gain recognition), the field remained dominated by the "Big Three" for decades.

The Credit Rating Agency Reform Act of 2006 created a formal registration regime, and the SEC's Office of Credit Ratings now oversees registered NRSROs, which number about ten, including Morningstar DBRS, Kroll Bond Rating Agency, Egan-Jones, A.M. Best and Japan Credit Rating Agency. After the 2008 financial crisis, the Dodd-Frank Act ordered references to credit ratings to be removed from federal regulations where possible, though NRSRO ratings remain deeply embedded in financial practice.
