Extreme metal
Stylistic originsHeavy metal, hardcore punk
Cultural originsEarly 1980s, Europe and North America
SubgenresThrash metal, death metal, black metal, doom metal, grindcore

Extreme metal is a loosely defined umbrella term for a number of related heavy metal subgenres developed since the early 1980s. It generally involves a more aggressive, harsher sound and style than traditional heavy metal, drawing on speed, distortion, extreme vocal techniques such as growling and screaming, and transgressive lyrical themes.

History

Bands such as Venom, Motörhead, and the New Wave of British Heavy Metal laid the groundwork, alongside hardcore punk. Thrash metal (Metallica, Slayer) emerged in the early 1980s, followed by death metal (Death, Morbid Angel) and black metal (Bathory; the Norwegian scene of the early 1990s). Doom metal took the opposite route of slow, heavy tempos, while grindcore fused metal with hardcore at very short song lengths.

Characteristics

Common traits include fast tempos and blast beats, downtuned and heavily distorted guitars, complex or atonal riffing, and vocals ranging from deep growls to shrieks. Lyrics often address death, war, horror, religion, and the occult.

Influence

Extreme metal developed largely underground, supported by tape trading, fanzines, and independent labels. Subgenres have continued to hybridise, producing styles such as blackened death metal, sludge metal, deathcore, and metalcore offshoots.
