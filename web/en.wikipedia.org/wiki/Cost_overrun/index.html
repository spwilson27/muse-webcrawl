Cost overrun

A cost overrun, also known as a cost increase or budget overrun, involves unexpected incurred costs in excess of budgeted amounts due to an underestimation of the actual cost during budgeting. Cost overrun should be distinguished from cost escalation, which is an anticipated growth in the budgeted cost of a project component due to factors such as inflation.

Causes

Research by Bent Flyvbjerg and colleagues on thousands of major projects identifies three main explanations:

  • Technical — imperfect forecasting techniques, inadequate data, and honest mistakes.
  • Psychological — optimism bias and the planning fallacy: planners underestimate times and costs and overestimate benefits.
  • Political-economic — strategic misrepresentation: promoters deliberately lowball estimates to get projects approved and funded.

Flyvbjerg's data show overruns are the rule rather than the exception: roughly nine out of ten megaprojects experience them, with average overruns (in constant prices) of about 20% for roads, 34% for bridges and tunnels, and 45% for rail projects. Famous examples include the Sydney Opera House (estimated at A$7 million, completed at A$102 million), Denver International Airport, the Channel Tunnel, and Boston's Big Dig.

Countermeasures

Proposed remedies include reference class forecasting — basing estimates on the outcomes of similar completed projects — independent audits of business cases, skin-in-the-game arrangements for forecasters, and budgeting with explicit contingency uplifts derived from historical overrun distributions.
