My Neighbor Totoro (Japanese: となりのトトロ, Tonari no Totoro) is a 1988 Japanese animated fantasy film written and directed by Hayao Miyazaki and produced by Studio Ghibli. It tells the story of two young sisters who move to the countryside while their mother recovers from an illness, and who befriend the gentle forest spirits living nearby.

Plot

In 1950s rural Japan, university professor Tatsuo Kusakabe moves with his daughters Satsuki and Mei to an old house near the hospital where their mother Yasuko is recovering. The sisters discover that the surrounding forest is home to spirits: tiny soot sprites in the house, and in a great camphor tree, the large, sleepy, rabbit-like creature Mei names Totoro, along with his smaller companions. When Mei runs away to take corn to her mother and becomes lost, Satsuki asks Totoro for help, and the magical Catbus carries them to find her. The film closes with the family reassured that their mother's health is improving.

Production and release

The film was released in Japan on a double bill with Isao Takahata's Grave of the Fireflies. Although initially only a modest commercial success, it became a cultural phenomenon through television broadcasts and merchandise — Totoro became Studio Ghibli's mascot and logo. An English dub produced for Streamline Pictures was released in 1993, and Disney released a new dub in 2006 featuring Dakota and Elle Fanning as the sisters.

Reception and legacy

My Neighbor Totoro is consistently ranked among the greatest animated films ever made; it appeared in the top tier of the 2012 Sight & Sound critics' poll of the best films of all time. The film is praised for its depiction of childhood, its animist reverence for nature, and its gentle, low-conflict storytelling. The "Totoro Forest" in Sayama Hills near Tokorozawa, the landscape that inspired the film, is preserved in part through a conservation fund named after the character.
