Vasari Corridor

LocationFlorence, Italy
ArchitectGiorgio Vasari
Built1565
Lengthc. 760 m
Commissioned byCosimo I de' Medici

The Vasari Corridor (Italian: Corridoio Vasariano) is an elevated enclosed passageway in Florence, built in 1565 by Giorgio Vasari for Grand Duke Cosimo I de' Medici. It connects the Palazzo Vecchio with the Palazzo Pitti, passing through the Uffizi Gallery and over the Ponte Vecchio across the Arno River, so that the ruler could move between his residence and the seat of government in safety and privacy.

Construction

Vasari completed the corridor in about five months for the wedding of Cosimo's son Francesco to Joanna of Austria. Where it crosses the Ponte Vecchio, panoramic windows — enlarged in 1939 for a visit by Adolf Hitler — overlook the Arno; the butcher shops on the bridge were expelled in favour of goldsmiths, a trade that remains there today.

Contents and access

From the eighteenth century the corridor housed part of the Uffizi's collections, most famously its unrivalled series of artists' self-portraits, as well as later portraits and paintings. Access was long restricted to guided groups, and the corridor closed for restoration and safety works in 2016; it reopened to visitors in late 2024 after renovation.
