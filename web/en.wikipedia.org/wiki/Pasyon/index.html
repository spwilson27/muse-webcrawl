Pasyon

Placeholder image

The Pasyon (Spanish: Pasión) is a Philippine epic narrative of the life of Jesus Christ, focused on his Passion, Death, and Resurrection. Written in stanzas of five lines of eight syllables each, it is traditionally sung or chanted during Holy Week in the Philippines.

History

The most influential version is the Casaysay nang Pasiong Mahal ni Jesucristong Panginoon Natin na Sucat Ipag-alab nang Puso nang Sinomang Babasa ("Story of the Sacred Passion of Jesus Christ Our Lord That Rightly Should Inflame the Heart of Whosoever Readeth"), commonly called the Pasyong Genesis, published in 1814 and attributed to the priest Gaspar Aquino de Belén, whose earlier version dates to 1704.

Practice

The uninterrupted chanting of the Pasyon, known as pabasa, may last for days during Holy Week, with chanters taking turns. The narrative became deeply woven into Filipino folk religiosity and was read by nineteenth-century reformists and revolutionaries as commentary on their own society.
