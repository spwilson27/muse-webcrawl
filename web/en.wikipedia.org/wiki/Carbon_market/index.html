Carbon market

Carbon market
InstrumentsEmission allowances, carbon credits/offsets
Largest schemeEU Emissions Trading System
BasisCap-and-trade; baseline-and-credit

The carbon market is a trading system through which countries, companies and other entities buy and sell allowances or credits representing the right to emit greenhouse gases, chiefly carbon dioxide. Carbon markets are a central market-based instrument of climate policy, intended to achieve emissions reductions at the lowest overall cost by putting a price on carbon.

Types

Compliance markets are created by regulation. In cap-and-trade systems, such as the European Union Emissions Trading System (EU ETS, launched 2005) and China's national ETS (launched 2021, the world's largest by covered emissions), a regulator sets a declining cap on emissions and issues allowances that firms can trade. Under the Kyoto Protocol's flexible mechanisms, project-based credits (CERs from the Clean Development Mechanism and ERUs from Joint Implementation) were traded internationally.

Voluntary carbon markets allow companies and individuals to buy carbon offsets — credits from projects such as reforestation or renewable energy — outside any legal obligation. These markets have grown rapidly but face persistent criticism over the additionality, permanence and verification of credits.

Criticism

Debates over carbon markets concern over-allocation of allowances (which collapsed EU prices in the scheme's early phases), carbon leakage to unregulated jurisdictions, the integrity of offsets, price volatility, and whether trading delays direct regulation. Supporters counter that well-designed caps guarantee the emissions outcome while trading minimises cost.
