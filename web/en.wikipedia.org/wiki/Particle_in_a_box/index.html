Particle in a box

[Image: Energy levels and wavefunctions of a particle in a one-dimensional box]

Overview

In quantum mechanics, the particle in a box model (also known as the infinite potential well or infinite square well) describes a particle free to move in a small space surrounded by impenetrable barriers. It is the canonical first example in quantum mechanics courses because it can be solved exactly and displays quantisation in its purest form.

Solution and significance

For a box of width L, the allowed energies are E_n = n²h²/8mL² for n = 1, 2, 3, …, and the wavefunctions are standing sine waves vanishing at the walls. The lowest energy (the zero-point energy) is not zero — a direct consequence of the uncertainty principle: confining a particle costs energy. The model explains, qualitatively, the colours of conjugated dyes (π electrons in a molecular "box") and the physics of quantum dots, and extends to finite wells, boxes in 2D and 3D, and periodic (Kronig–Penney) potentials.
