The Provisional Government of the Republic of Korea (Korean: 대한민국 임시정부) was a Korean government in exile based in Shanghai, China, and later in Chongqing, during the period of Japanese colonial rule over Korea. It was proclaimed on 11 April 1919 in the wake of the March 1st Movement, the mass demonstrations for independence that swept Korea that spring.

History

Several provisional governments were declared by Korean independence activists in 1919; these were consolidated in September of that year into a single Provisional Government in Shanghai, with Syngman Rhee elected as its first president. The government maintained a provisional assembly, published the newspaper The Independent (Dongnip Sinmun), and conducted diplomacy in China, the United States and Europe, though it never gained formal recognition from the major powers.

After the outbreak of the Second Sino-Japanese War, the government moved inland, eventually settling in Chongqing, the wartime capital of the Republic of China, in 1940. That year it organised the Korean Liberation Army, which cooperated with Allied forces and planned operations alongside the American OSS.

Kim Ku and later years

Under the leadership of Kim Ku, who headed the government from 1927 and again from 1940, the Provisional Government directed resistance activities, including the 1932 Hongkou Park bombing by Yun Bong-gil, which killed senior Japanese officials in Shanghai. In 1941 it issued a declaration of war against Japan and the Axis powers.

Legacy

Following Japan's defeat in 1945, members of the Provisional Government returned to Korea, but the country was divided between American and Soviet occupation zones. The Republic of Korea, established in 1948 in the south, regards the Provisional Government as its legal predecessor; its constitution's preamble states that the Republic is founded upon the spirit of the Provisional Government established through the March 1st Movement.
