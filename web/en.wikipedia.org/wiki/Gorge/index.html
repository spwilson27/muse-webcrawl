Gorge

A gorge is a narrow valley with steep, rocky walls located between hills or mountains. The term comes from the French word gorge, which means throat or neck. A gorge is often smaller than a canyon, and although a gorge and a canyon are similar, a gorge is not necessarily carved by a river, whereas a canyon generally is.

Formation

Gorges are formed by fluvial erosion, the wearing away of rock by water flowing over it, often accelerated where a river crosses resistant rock bands or where tectonic uplift steepens the river's gradient. They can also form through the collapse of underground river systems in limestone (karst) terrain, or by catastrophic flooding.

Notable gorges

  • Tiger Leaping Gorge, China
  • Cheddar Gorge, England
  • Iron Gates, between Serbia and Romania
  • Columbia River Gorge, United States
  • Gorges du Verdon, France
