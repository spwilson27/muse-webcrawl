Banger racing

Banger racing is a British and Irish motorsport discipline in which drivers race old, often scrap-value cars on short oval tracks, with deliberate contact between vehicles permitted and expected. Unlike most forms of circuit racing, damaging opponents' cars is part of the spectacle: races frequently end with only a handful of the starting field still mobile, and meetings are often capped by a demolition derby in which the last car able to move wins.

Format

Meetings are held at stadiums and short ovals — many of them also used for stock car racing and speedway — including venues such as King's Lynn, Wimbledon (until its closure), Arena Essex, and Hednesford Hills Raceway. Cars are stripped of glass and flammable trim, fitted with a roll cage and harness, and painted in lurid colours. Classes range from "National Bangers" for large saloons and hearses to "Micro Bangers" for small hatchbacks, plus novelty classes for caravans, coaches, and Reliant three-wheelers.

Culture

Banger racing grew out of 1950s and 1960s dirt-oval racing and became a working-class Saturday-night institution. The sport has an ambivalent relationship with classic car enthusiasts: once-common models, notably Ford Granadas and Volvo estates, became scarce partly through banger consumption, though the "Pre-70" and classic banger classes now mandate period cars chosen from more plentiful models. Championships such as the Banger World Final at Ipswich and the World Championship at Wimbledon/Plough Lane drew fields of several hundred cars in their heyday.
