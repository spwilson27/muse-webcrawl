Scientific misconduct

Scientific misconduct is the violation of standard codes of scholarly conduct and ethical behavior in professional scientific research. The U.S. federal definition covers fabrication (making up data), falsification (manipulating research materials, equipment, or processes, or changing or omitting data), and plagiarism — collectively "FFP" — while excluding honest error and differences of opinion.

Related practices

"Questionable research practices" include p-hacking, selective reporting, salami slicing, gift authorship, and failure to disclose conflicts of interest. Retractions, tracked by resources such as Retraction Watch, have risen sharply as detection tools and scrutiny improve.

Notable cases

Famous cases include Jan Hendrik Schön (fabricated physics results), Hwang Woo-suk (fabricated stem-cell claims), Andrew Wakefield (retracted vaccine paper), Diederik Stapel (fabricated social-psychology data), and the Piltdown Man hoax.

Consequences and prevention

Sanctions range from retraction and loss of funding to criminal charges. Institutions, funders, and journals pursue prevention through data management, preregistration, image screening, and research-integrity offices.
