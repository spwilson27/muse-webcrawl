The Reiwa era (Japanese: 令和時代, Reiwa jidai) is the current era of the official calendar of Japan. It began on 1 May 2019, the day on which Emperor Akihito's eldest son, Naruhito, ascended the throne following his father's abdication — the first abdication by a Japanese emperor in over 200 years. The era name was announced a month earlier, on 1 April 2019, by Chief Cabinet Secretary Yoshihide Suga, who displayed the characters 令和 on a framed calligraphy board in a nationally televised press conference.

Meaning and origin

The government officially glossed Reiwa in English as "beautiful harmony". Unusually, the name was not drawn from Chinese classics but from the Man'yōshū, the oldest anthology of Japanese poetry: it combines rei (令), "auspicious/beautiful", and wa (和), "harmony/peace", from a verse about plum blossoms in early spring. Reiwa is the 248th era name in Japanese history. Because the characters could be read with a more authoritarian nuance ("command/order"), the choice prompted public discussion, and translations in the foreign press varied ("auspicious harmony", "order and harmony").

Events of the era

The Reiwa era opened with national celebration and an extended Golden Week holiday. Its early years included the 2019 Rugby World Cup in Japan, the COVID-19 pandemic and the postponed Tokyo 2020 Olympic and Paralympic Games (held in 2021), the assassination of former prime minister Shinzo Abe in 2022, and Japan's response to inflation and demographic decline. Under the era-name (gengō) system, years are counted from the emperor's accession, so 2019 is Reiwa 1.
