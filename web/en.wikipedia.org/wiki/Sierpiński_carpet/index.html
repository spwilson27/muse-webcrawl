Sierpiński carpet

The Sierpiński carpet, iterated through several stages.

The Sierpiński carpet is a plane fractal introduced by the Polish mathematician Wacław Sierpiński in 1916. It is a two-dimensional analogue of the Cantor set and a generalisation that leads, in three dimensions, to the Menger sponge. The carpet is constructed by recursively subdividing a square and removing its centre.

Construction

Start with a filled square. Divide it into nine congruent subsquares in a 3×3 grid and remove the central one. Repeat the same procedure for each of the eight remaining subsquares, and continue indefinitely. After n iterations there are 8n squares, each of side 3−n times the original. The limiting set is the Sierpiński carpet.

Properties

The carpet has Lebesgue measure (area) zero, since the fraction of area remaining after n steps is (8/9)n, which tends to zero. Its Hausdorff dimension is log 8 / log 3 ≈ 1.8928. Topologically, the carpet is remarkable: Sierpiński showed that it is a universal curve — every one-dimensional planar continuum is homeomorphic to a subset of the carpet. It is self-similar, connected and locally connected, yet encloses infinitely many holes.

Variations and applications

Removing different subsquares, or using random choices at each stage, gives variants of the carpet. Fractal antennas based on the carpet's self-similar geometry exploit its multi-band behaviour, and the construction appears frequently in discussions of recursion and iterated function systems.
