United States Capitol rotunda

Rotunda
Location: United States Capitol, Washington, D.C.
Height: 180 ft (55 m)
Diameter: 96 ft (29 m)

The United States Capitol rotunda is the tall central rotunda of the United States Capitol in Washington, D.C. It is the symbolic and physical heart of the Capitol, built beneath the Capitol dome, and has been described as the "symbolic center of the United States of America."

Description

The rotunda is 96 feet (29 m) in diameter and rises 180 feet (55 m) to the canopy. Its sandstone walls are divided by fluted Doric pilasters. Eight large historical paintings hang in niches around the room, four of them by John Trumbull depicting scenes from the American Revolution, including the Declaration of Independence.

The Apotheosis of Washington

The fresco painted on the canopy of the dome, The Apotheosis of Washington, was completed by Constantino Brumidi in 1865. It depicts George Washington rising to the heavens surrounded by allegorical figures. Below it runs the Frieze of American History, begun by Brumidi and completed by Filippo Costaggini and Allyn Cox.

Lying in state

The rotunda is used for the ceremonial lying in state of eminent citizens, including presidents, members of Congress, and military heroes. The catafalque built for Abraham Lincoln in 1865 remains in use for these ceremonies, which are authorized by concurrent resolution of Congress.
