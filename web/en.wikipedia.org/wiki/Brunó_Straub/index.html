Brunó Ferenc Straub (5 January 1914 – 15 February 1996) was a Hungarian biochemist and politician. As a scientist he worked in the school of Albert Szent-Györgyi at Szeged on muscle biochemistry and oxidation, and he founded and directed the Institute of Biochemistry of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences, also serving as the Academy's president.

Political role

Straub entered politics late in life as a respected, relatively independent figure. In June 1988 he became Chairman of the Presidential Council, Hungary's collective head of state, succeeding Károly Németh. He thus presided over the final phase of the Hungarian People's Republic during the negotiated transition to democracy, and formally proclaimed the Republic of Hungary on 23 October 1989, handing over to acting president Mátyás Szűrös. He retired from public life thereafter.
