Mithridates VI

King of Pontus
Reign: c. 120 – 63 BC
Born: c. 134 BC, Sinope
Died: 63 BC, Panticapaeum

Mithridates VI Eupator (c. 134–63 BC) was the last and most famous king of Pontus, in northern Anatolia. He is remembered as one of the Roman Republic's most formidable and determined enemies, fighting Rome in the three Mithridatic Wars over several decades.

Early life

Mithridates came to the throne as a child after the assassination of his father, Mithridates V. According to later accounts, fearing poisoning, he spent years in the wilderness and deliberately built up an immunity to poisons by taking small doses — the practice from which the word mithridatism derives.

Wars with Rome

Expanding across the Black Sea region and into Asia Minor, Mithridates clashed with Rome in 88 BC, ordering the massacre of Roman and Italian residents in Asia — the so-called Asiatic Vespers. In the ensuing wars he faced Roman generals including Sulla, Lucullus, and finally Pompey, who defeated him decisively. Fleeing to his Bosporan kingdom, Mithridates died in 63 BC, by suicide or assassination, after a revolt led by his son Pharnaces.

Legacy

Mithridates was famed for his knowledge of languages — he was said to speak the tongues of all the nations he ruled — and for his interest in poisons and antidotes, including the universal antidote known as mithridatium.
