The Dassault/Dornier Alpha Jet is a light attack jet and advanced jet trainer developed jointly by Dassault Aviation of France and Dornier of Germany. It first flew in 1973 and entered service with the French and German air forces in 1979.

Development and service

France adopted the type primarily as a trainer (Alpha Jet E), while Germany used a close air support variant (Alpha Jet A). German aircraft were retired in the 1990s, but France retained the type for training and for the Patrouille de France aerobatic team, whose Alpha Jets are famous for their red, white and blue smoke displays.

Operators

The Alpha Jet was exported widely, serving with Belgium, Portugal, Morocco, Nigeria, Cameroon, Togo, Egypt, Qatar, and Thailand, among others. Many retired aircraft continue flying with private contractors and aerobatic teams.

Specifications

The aircraft is powered by two SNECMA Turbomeca Larzac turbofans, carries a crew of two in tandem, and has a maximum speed of about Mach 0.86.
