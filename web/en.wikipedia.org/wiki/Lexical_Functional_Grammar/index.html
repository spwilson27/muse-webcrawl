Lexical Functional Grammar

Lexical Functional Grammar (LFG) is a constraint-based grammar framework in theoretical linguistics. It is a type of phrase structure grammar, as opposed to a dependency grammar. The theory was developed by Joan Bresnan and Ronald Kaplan in the 1970s in reaction to transformational grammar.

Overview

LFG posits two separate levels of syntactic structure: constituent structure (c-structure), a phrase structure tree representing word order and constituency, and functional structure (f-structure), an attribute-value matrix representing grammatical functions such as subject and object and features such as tense and number. The lexicon plays a central role, and grammatical constraints are expressed as equations over f-structures.

Applications

LFG has been used for the description of a wide range of languages, especially morphologically rich and non-configurational languages, and in computational linguistics, notably the ParGram project for parallel grammar development.
