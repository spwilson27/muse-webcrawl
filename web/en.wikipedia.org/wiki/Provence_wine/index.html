Provence wine

Provence is the world's leading source of dry rosé.

Provence wine comes from the French wine-producing region of Provence in southeast France. The Romans called the area provincia nostra ("our province"), giving Provence its name. Wine has been made here for at least 2,600 years, since the ancient Greeks founded Marseille around 600 BC and brought grapevines with them.

Rosé

Provence is above all rosé country: the great majority of its production — around 85–90% in the largest appellation, Côtes de Provence — is pale, dry rosé made chiefly from Grenache, Cinsault, Mourvèdre, Syrah and the local Tibouren. The wines are typically light salmon-pink, with aromas of red berries, citrus and Provençal herbs, intended for early drinking.

Appellations

The region's AOCs include Côtes de Provence (with the Sainte-Victoire and Fréjus sub-appellations), Coteaux d'Aix-en-Provence, Coteaux Varois en Provence, Bandol (renowned for structured Mourvèdre reds and rosés), Cassis (mainly white), Bellet near Nice, Les Baux de Provence, and Palette. Most Provence wine is consumed within France, though rosé exports, especially to the United States, have grown steeply.

Climate and grapes

The Mediterranean climate gives abundant sunshine tempered by the mistral wind, which keeps vines dry and disease-free. Beyond the rosé varieties, white wines use Rolle (Vermentino), Ugni blanc, Clairette and Sémillon. The food the wines accompany is Provençal cuisine — olive oil, garlic, tomatoes and herbs.
