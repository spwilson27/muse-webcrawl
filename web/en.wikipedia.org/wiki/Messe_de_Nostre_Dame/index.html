The Messe de Nostre Dame (Mass of Our Lady) is a polyphonic mass composed before 1365 by the French poet and composer Guillaume de Machaut (c. 1300–1377). It is one of the masterpieces of medieval music and, as the earliest complete setting of the Mass Ordinary attributable to a single composer, one of the most famous and influential works of the ars nova.

Music

The Mass is scored for four voices — triplum, motetus, tenor, and contratenor. Its six movements set the complete Ordinary: Kyrie, Gloria, Credo, Sanctus, Agnus Dei, and the dismissal Ite missa est. The Kyrie, Sanctus, Agnus Dei, and Ite missa est are built isorhythmically over chant-derived tenor melodies associated with the Virgin Mary, while the Gloria and Credo are written in a simpler, largely syllabic style with simultaneous declamation of the long texts, though they share a concluding "Amen" technique. The movements are unified by the Marian chant basis and consistent style, suggesting Machaut intended them as a single cyclic work.

Context and performance

Machaut was a canon of Reims Cathedral, and the Mass is thought to have been composed for performance there — possibly for the Saturday Lady Mass, or as a memorial mass for Machaut and his brother Jean, also a canon, whose endowment provided for its continued performance; the brothers' tombstone in the cathedral recorded their wish that the Mass be sung after their deaths. The work survives in the collected-works manuscripts of Machaut's music, which he supervised.
