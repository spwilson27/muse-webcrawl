The Imaginary (psychoanalysis)

The Imaginary is one of the three registers of human psychic life in the theory of French psychoanalyst Jacques Lacan, alongside the Symbolic and the Real.

Mirror stage

The Imaginary is rooted in the mirror stage, in which the infant, between roughly six and eighteen months, identifies with its reflection or image and thereby forms the ego. This identification is a misrecognition (méconnaissance): the ego is constituted through an alienating image of wholeness that contrasts with the child's actual motor uncoordination.

Characteristics

The register of the Imaginary is the domain of images, fantasy, dual relations, rivalry, and narcissism. Intersubjective relationships structured purely in the Imaginary tend toward aggressivity, since each subject experiences the other as a rival for the same object. In clinical practice, analysis aims to move beyond Imaginary identifications through the Symbolic register of speech and language.
