Real Time Streaming Protocol

The Real Time Streaming Protocol (RTSP) is an application-level network protocol designed for multiplexing and packetizing multimedia transport streams (such as video and audio) over the Internet Protocol networks. It is used in entertainment and communications systems to control streaming media servers. The protocol is intended for multiple data delivery sessions, provides an extensible framework, is built for client–server operation, and is intended to facilitate real-time control of playback of media files.

RTSP was developed by RealNetworks, Netscape and Columbia University, and standardised by the IETF in RFC 2326 (1998). RTSP 2.0 was published as RFC 7826 in 2016.

Operation

RTSP acts as a "network remote control": the client issues VCR-like commands such as OPTIONS, DESCRIBE, SETUP, PLAY, PAUSE, TEARDOWN and RECORD. The media data itself is usually carried by a separate transport, most commonly RTP over UDP or interleaved in the RTSP TCP connection. RTSP servers commonly listen on port 554.

Uses

RTSP is widely used for IP cameras and surveillance systems, and by media players and streaming servers, although consumer streaming has largely moved to HTTP-based adaptive streaming (HLS, DASH) and WebRTC.
