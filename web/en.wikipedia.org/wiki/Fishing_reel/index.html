A fishing reel is a cranking device attached to a fishing rod used in winding and stowing a fishing line. Modern fishing reels usually have fittings aiding in casting for distance and accuracy, as well as retrieving line under tension. Fishing reels are traditionally used in angling and competitive casting, and are sited at the near end of the rod close to the angler's hand.

History

The earliest known depiction of a fishing reel comes from a Chinese painting of about 1195 AD by Ma Yuan, showing an angler with a rod fitted with a wheel-like reel. In England, reels appeared in print in the mid-17th century, and the multiplying reel — geared so that one turn of the handle produces several turns of the spool — was developed in Britain and the United States in the 18th and 19th centuries. George Snyder of Kentucky is credited with the first American-made baitcasting reel around 1810. The fixed-spool spinning reel was popularised in the early 20th century, with designs such as the Mitchell 300 (1948) becoming classics.

Types

Spinning reel

A spinning (fixed-spool) reel hangs below the rod; the spool does not rotate when casting. Line peels off the spool when the bail arm is opened, and is rewound around the spool by the rotating bail when the handle is turned. Spinning reels are valued for ease of use and for casting light baits and lures.

Baitcasting reel

In a baitcasting reel the spool rotates during the cast and is mounted above the rod. The angler controls the spool with the thumb to prevent overruns (“bird's nests”). Modern baitcasters use magnetic or centrifugal braking systems and are favoured for accuracy and for heavier lines and lures.

Other types

Spincast reels enclose the spool in a nose cone and are operated by a push button. Fly reels are simple large-arbor reels designed mainly to store fly line and provide drag. Centrepin reels, which rotate almost frictionlessly, are used for float fishing on rivers. Large multiplier and conventional reels are used in sea fishing for powerful species.

Drag systems

Most reels incorporate a drag — a friction brake on the spool that allows line to be pulled off under a set tension when a hooked fish runs, preventing the line from breaking. Drags may be front- or rear-mounted on spinning reels and are typically made from stacks of metal and fibre washers.
