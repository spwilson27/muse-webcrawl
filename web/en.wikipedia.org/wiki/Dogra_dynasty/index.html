Dogra dynasty

The Dogra dynasty was a Hindu dynasty of the Rajput clan that ruled the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir from 1846 to 1947. The Dogras are an ethnolinguistic community of the Dogri-speaking Jammu region of northern India. The dynasty was founded by Gulab Singh, a Dogra nobleman in the service of Maharaja Ranjit Singh of the Sikh Empire.

History

After the First Anglo-Sikh War, the British, by the Treaty of Amritsar (1846), transferred Kashmir to Gulab Singh for 7.5 million rupees, making him the first Maharaja of Jammu and Kashmir. His successors — Ranbir Singh, Pratap Singh and Hari Singh — expanded and administered a vast Himalayan state encompassing Jammu, Kashmir, Ladakh, Gilgit and Baltistan.

Hari Singh, the last ruling Maharaja, acceded to the Dominion of India in October 1947 amid the tribal invasion from Pakistan that began the First Kashmir War, ending Dogra rule. His son Karan Singh later served as regent, Sadr-i-Riyasat and governor of the state.

Legacy

The Dogra period saw the building of palaces such as Amar Mahal and Mubarak Mandi in Jammu, the establishment of schools and courts, and the codification of the Ranbir Penal Code. Dogra rule remains a subject of historical debate, particularly regarding administration in the Kashmir Valley.
