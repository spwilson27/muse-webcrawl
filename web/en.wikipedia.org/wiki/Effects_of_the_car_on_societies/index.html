Effects of the car on societies

Since the twentieth century the role of the car has become highly important, though controversial. Cars are used worldwide and have become the most common mode of transport in the developed world. Their widespread adoption has transformed patterns of work, settlement, leisure and social life, while also imposing significant costs in terms of pollution, accidents, land use and resource consumption.

Mass motorisation reshaped cities and daily life during the twentieth century.

History

In the 1920s the automobile began to change life in the United States, giving ordinary people a degree of personal mobility previously available only to the rich. Mass production, pioneered by Henry Ford's Model T, made cars affordable to the middle class. After the Second World War, motorway building programmes and suburban development made car ownership near-universal in North America and increasingly common in Europe and elsewhere.

Benefits

Cars provide flexible, door-to-door transport on the user's own schedule, access to jobs and services spread over wide areas, and freedom of movement for people in rural regions poorly served by public transport. The motor industry is also a major employer and a driver of manufacturing output in many economies.

Costs and criticism

  • Deaths and injuries: road traffic crashes kill well over a million people worldwide each year.
  • Pollution: exhaust emissions contribute to urban air pollution and to greenhouse gas emissions.
  • Land consumption: roads and parking occupy a large share of urban land, and car-dependent suburbs encourage sprawl.
  • Congestion: in dense cities, mass car use produces chronic traffic jams and long journey times.
  • Social effects: car dependence can reduce street life, walking and cycling, and disadvantage people too young, old, poor or disabled to drive.

Future trends

Electric vehicles, car-sharing, ride-hailing and experiments with self-driving cars may change the car's role, while many cities are investing in public transport, cycling and pedestrian infrastructure to reduce car dependence.
