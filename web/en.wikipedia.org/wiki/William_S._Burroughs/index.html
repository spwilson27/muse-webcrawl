William Seward Burroughs II (5 February 1914 – 2 August 1997) was an American writer and visual artist, a primary figure of the Beat Generation and a major postmodern author. He wrote eighteen novels and novellas, six short-story collections and four essay collections, including the confessional Junkie (1953) and the experimental, controversial Naked Lunch (1959).

Life

Burroughs was born in St. Louis to a wealthy family — his grandfather, William Seward Burroughs I, founded the Burroughs Corporation — and graduated from Harvard in 1936. Drawn to the criminal and drug underworlds, he gravitated to New York in the 1940s, where he befriended Allen Ginsberg and Jack Kerouac, and became addicted to heroin. In 1951, in Mexico City, he accidentally shot and killed his wife, Joan Vollmer, during a drunken "William Tell" stunt — an event he said convinced him he was possessed by an "Ugly Spirit" and drove his writing.

Naked Lunch and the cut-up

Living in Tangier's International Zone, Burroughs assembled the fragmentary manuscript published in Paris in 1959 as Naked Lunch. The book's hallucinatory satire of addiction, control systems and sexuality made it the target of a landmark 1966 Massachusetts obscenity trial, which ended in its favour and effectively ended literary censorship in the United States. With Brion Gysin he developed the cut-up technique — slicing and randomly reassembling text — applied across the "Nova Trilogy": The Soft Machine (1961), The Ticket That Exploded (1962) and Nova Express (1964).

Later years

Burroughs lived in London and New York (where his "Bunker" on the Bowery became a salon for artists and musicians) before settling in Lawrence, Kansas in 1981. The Red Night trilogy (Cities of the Red Night, 1981; The Place of Dead Roads, 1983; The Western Lands, 1987) was widely considered his finest late work. He collaborated with artists from David Cronenberg — who filmed Naked Lunch in 1991 — to Kurt Cobain, and took up painting, including "shotgun art". He died of a heart attack in 1997.

Influence

Burroughs's themes — language as a virus, addiction as a model of control, the subversion of narrative — influenced writers such as J. G. Ballard and Kathy Acker, and musicians including David Bowie, Patti Smith, the punk and industrial scenes; the band Steely Dan took its name from a dildo in Naked Lunch, and the term "heavy metal" is popularly traced to his writing. His friend and sometime lover Neal Cassady linked his circle to Kerouac's On the Road.
