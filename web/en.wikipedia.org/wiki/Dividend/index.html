Dividend

A dividend is a distribution of profits by a corporation to its shareholders, after which the stock exchange decreases the price of the stock by the dividend to remove volatility. When a corporation earns a profit, that profit can be put to two uses: it can either be re-invested in the business (called retained earnings), or it can be distributed to shareholders as a dividend. Most dividends are paid in cash, but they may also be issued as shares of stock (a stock dividend) or other property.

History

The Dutch East India Company (VOC) was the first recorded (public) company ever to pay regular dividends, beginning in 1602, as it distributed profits to shareholders in both cash and spices. Dividend payments became a central feature of publicly traded companies in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Payment procedure

The board of directors declares a dividend, setting the amount per share, the record date (shareholders recorded on that date receive the dividend), the ex-dividend date (the first date the stock trades without the right to the dividend), and the payment date. In the United States and Canada, dividends are typically paid quarterly.

Taxation

Dividends are taxed in most jurisdictions, though rates vary. In the United States, qualified dividends are taxed at the lower capital gains rate, while non-qualified dividends are taxed as ordinary income. Many European countries operate imputation systems designed to reduce double taxation of corporate profits.

Dividend yield and payout ratio

The dividend yield is the dividend per share divided by the share price, expressed as a percentage. The dividend payout ratio is the proportion of earnings paid out as dividends. Companies that pay reliable dividends over long periods are sometimes called dividend aristocrats.
