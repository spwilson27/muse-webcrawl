The Group of 24 (G-24), formally the Intergovernmental Group of Twenty-Four on International Monetary Affairs and Development, is a grouping of developing countries established in 1971 to coordinate the positions of developing nations on international monetary and development finance issues and to ensure their interests are adequately represented in negotiations on the international monetary system.

Role and structure

The G-24 is a chapter of the larger Group of 77 at the United Nations. Its members are drawn from the African, Asian-Pacific and Latin American and Caribbean regional groups, and its membership has grown beyond the original twenty-four countries. China participates as a "special invitee". The group is not an organ of the IMF or World Bank but works closely with them, meeting at ministerial level in connection with the spring and annual meetings of the Bretton Woods institutions.

Activities

The G-24 issues communiqués and supports research on issues such as reform of IMF and World Bank governance and quotas, debt sustainability, climate finance, special drawing rights and the voice of emerging markets and developing economies in the global financial architecture.
