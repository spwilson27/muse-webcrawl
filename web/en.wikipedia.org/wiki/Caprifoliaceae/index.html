Caprifoliaceae

Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Dipsacales
Family: Caprifoliaceae Juss.
Type genus: Lonicera
Genera: Around 40 (in the broad sense); about 860 species

The Caprifoliaceae or honeysuckle family is a family of flowering plants in the order Dipsacales, consisting of shrubs and vines, rarely herbs, found mainly in temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere, with some representatives in tropical mountains. The family takes its name from Caprifolium, an older name for honeysuckles, ultimately from Latin words meaning "goat leaf".

Description

Members of the family typically have opposite leaves without stipules, tubular or bell-shaped flowers with a five-lobed corolla, and fruits that are berries, drupes or capsules. The flowers are often strongly scented and adapted to pollination by bees, hawkmoths and hummingbirds; several species are popular garden ornamentals, including honeysuckles (Lonicera), Weigela, Abelia and the snowberry (Symphoricarpos). Some honeysuckle species, such as Japanese honeysuckle, have become invasive weeds outside their native range.

Classification

The circumscription of the family has changed considerably under modern phylogenetic classification. In the APG IV system the family is taken in a broad sense, absorbing the former families Dipsacaceae (teasels and scabiouses) and Valerianaceae (valerians), along with smaller groups such as Linnaeaceae and Morinaceae. In this broad sense the family contains roughly 40 genera and about 860 species.

Well-known genera include:

  • Lonicera — honeysuckles
  • Symphoricarpos — snowberries and coralberries
  • Viburnum is sometimes included, but in the APG system it is placed in the related family Adoxaceae (Viburnaceae)
  • Dipsacus — teasels (in the broad circumscription)
  • Valeriana — valerians (in the broad circumscription)
  • Linnaea — twinflower, named for Carl Linnaeus

The family is closely related to the Adoxaceae within the Dipsacales, an order of asterid flowering plants that also includes the bellflower relatives.
