Weipa is a mining town on the western coast of the Cape York Peninsula in Far North Queensland, Australia. It is the largest town on the peninsula and exists largely because of the enormous bauxite deposits along the coast, among the largest in the world. The town sits on Albatross Bay, on the Gulf of Carpentaria.

History

The area has been home to Aboriginal peoples for thousands of years. The Dutch explorer Willem Janszoon's 1606 landing near the Pennefather River, close to modern Weipa, was the first recorded European landing in Australia. The modern town was established in the 1960s after Comalco (later Rio Tinto) began developing the bauxite deposits; a Presbyterian mission station had existed at the site since 1898. The removal of Aboriginal residents and disputes over land associated with the mining leases made Weipa a significant site in the history of Australian Indigenous land rights.

Mining

Bauxite mining around Weipa is operated by Rio Tinto, shipping ore from the port at Weipa to alumina refineries at Gladstone and overseas. The operation includes mines at Andoom and East Weipa, and the Amrun project south of the Embley River, which opened in 2018.

Geography and climate

Weipa has a tropical savanna climate with a pronounced wet season from roughly December to April. The surrounding area is known for recreational fishing, particularly for barramundi, and for its birdlife and crocodile-inhabited waterways. The town is connected by road to the rest of Queensland via the Peninsula Developmental Road, which is unsealed in sections and may be cut during the wet season; Weipa Airport provides regular flights.
