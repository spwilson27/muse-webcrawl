Emmylou Harris (born April 2, 1947) is an American singer, songwriter, and musician. Renowned for her crystalline soprano and her interpretations of other writers' songs, she has released dozens of albums across a career spanning more than five decades and is a central figure in country rock and Americana music.

Early career and Gram Parsons

Harris grew up in Virginia, the daughter of a Marine Corps officer, and began performing folk music in Greenwich Village in the late 1960s, releasing her debut album Gliding Bird in 1969. In 1971 she was discovered by members of the Flying Burrito Brothers, who introduced her to Gram Parsons. She sang harmony on Parsons's solo albums GP (1973) and Grievous Angel (1974), a partnership that defined country rock before Parsons's death in 1973.

Solo success

Her major-label debut, Pieces of the Sky (1975), produced by Brian Ahern and backed by her Hot Band (which at times included Rodney Crowell and Ricky Skaggs), launched a run of acclaimed albums including Elite Hotel (1975), Luxury Liner (1977), and the traditional country collection Blue Kentucky Girl (1979). Her 1987 collaboration with Dolly Parton and Linda Ronstadt, Trio, was a major commercial success, followed by Trio II (1999).

In the 1990s Harris reinvented her sound with the atmospheric Wrecking Ball (1995), produced by Daniel Lanois, and turned increasingly to her own songwriting on Red Dirt Girl (2000), which won a Grammy for Best Contemporary Folk Album. She contributed prominently to the O Brother, Where Art Thou? soundtrack (2000) and has collaborated with artists from Bob Dylan to Mark Knopfler and, later, Rodney Crowell.

Honours

Harris has won 14 Grammy Awards and was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 2008. She is also known for animal-welfare activism, founding the Bonaparte's Retreat dog rescue in Nashville.
