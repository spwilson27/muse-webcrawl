Fantasy television

Fantasy television is a genre of television programming featuring elements of the fantastic, often including magic, supernatural forces, or exotic fantasy worlds. Fantasy television programmes are often based on tales from mythology and folklore, or are adapted from fantasy stories in other media. The boundaries for fantasy television often overlap with those of science fiction and horror, although fantasy is distinguished from the other two genres by its reliance on imaginary phenomena that do not have any scientific or macabre explanation.

History

Fantasy elements appeared on television almost from the medium's beginnings. Early examples in the United States included anthology series and children's programming, while situation comedies with fantasy premises such as Bewitched (1964–1972) and I Dream of Jeannie (1965–1970) brought supernatural characters into suburban domestic settings. Sword-and-sorcery and adventure series followed in later decades, including Hercules: The Legendary Journeys and its spin-off Xena: Warrior Princess in the 1990s.

The genre expanded dramatically in the 2000s and 2010s. Series such as Charmed, Buffy the Vampire Slayer, Supernatural, and Once Upon a Time attracted large audiences, while HBO's Game of Thrones (2011–2019), adapted from George R. R. Martin's A Song of Ice and Fire novels, became a global phenomenon and demonstrated that big-budget epic fantasy could succeed on television. Streaming services subsequently invested heavily in the genre, producing series such as The Witcher, The Wheel of Time, and The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power.

Subgenres

Fantasy television encompasses several subgenres, including high fantasy set in fully imagined secondary worlds, urban fantasy set in the contemporary world, dark fantasy blending fantasy with horror, historical fantasy, and comic fantasy. Animated fantasy series form a substantial part of the genre, particularly in children's programming and anime.
