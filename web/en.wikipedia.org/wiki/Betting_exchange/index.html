Betting exchange

An exchange market for a horse race, showing matched and unmatched bets.

A betting exchange is a marketplace for customers to bet on the outcome of discrete events. Unlike a traditional bookmaker, which sets odds and accepts bets against its own book, a betting exchange matches bets between customers: one customer "backs" an outcome (betting that it will happen) while another "lays" it (betting that it will not happen, effectively acting as the bookmaker). The exchange operator does not take a position on the event but charges a commission on net winnings, typically between 2% and 5%.

Operation

Exchanges display available prices and liquidity in a manner similar to a stock exchange, and customers may request their own odds, which remain as unmatched offers until another customer takes them. Because margins are smaller than those of bookmakers, exchanges generally offer better prices, and they allow customers to trade positions during an event ("in-play") and to lay outcomes, a function previously available only to bookmakers.

History

The concept emerged with the internet in the late 1990s. Flutter.com and Betfair, both founded in 2000, pioneered the model in the United Kingdom; the two merged in 2001 under the Betfair name, with Betfair becoming the dominant global exchange. Competitors have included Betdaq and Matchbook. Exchanges have been controversial with traditional bookmakers and racing authorities, who argued that the ability to lay horses to lose facilitates corruption, and their legality varies by jurisdiction.
