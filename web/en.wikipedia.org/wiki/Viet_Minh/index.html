Viet Minh

The Việt Minh (Vietnamese: [vîət mīŋ]; abbreviated from Việt Nam Độc lập Đồng minh, "League for the Independence of Vietnam") was a national independence coalition formed at Pác Bó by Hồ Chí Minh on 19 May 1941. It sought independence for Vietnam from French colonial rule and Japanese occupation, and later fought the French Union in the First Indochina War.

Formation and Second World War

Led in practice by the Indochinese Communist Party, the Viet Minh built a broad front, guerrilla bases in the Việt Bắc mountains, and in 1944 its armed propaganda unit under Võ Nguyên Giáp, nucleus of the People's Army. It cooperated at times with the American OSS against Japan.

August Revolution and First Indochina War

In August 1945 the Viet Minh seized Hanoi during the power vacuum after Japan's surrender; Hồ Chí Minh proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam on 2 September 1945. War with France broke out in December 1946. After years of guerrilla and then conventional warfare, the Viet Minh's victory at Điện Biên Phủ (May 1954) led to the Geneva Accords, partitioning Vietnam at the 17th parallel, with the Viet Minh government in the North.

Legacy

The organisation was superseded in the North by the Fatherland Front, while in the South the Việt Cộng (National Liberation Front, founded 1960) inherited its mantle during the Vietnam War.
