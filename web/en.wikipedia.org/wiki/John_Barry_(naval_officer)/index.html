John Barry (25 March 1745 – 13 September 1803) was an Irish-born American naval officer who served in the Continental Navy during the American Revolutionary War and later became the first commissioned officer of the United States Navy. He is often called the "Father of the American Navy," a title he shares with John Paul Jones and John Adams. Barry received the first commission issued to a naval officer under the new Constitution, signed by President George Washington, and held the rank of commodore with precedence over all other captains.

Early life

Barry was born in 1745 at Tacumshane, County Wexford, Ireland, the son of a poor tenant farmer evicted by his English landlord. As a youth he went to sea, sailing in merchant vessels and rising through the ranks from cabin boy to master mariner. He settled in Philadelphia in the 1760s, where he prospered as captain of fast merchant ships trading with the West Indies. His final merchant command was the Black Prince, one of the swiftest vessels sailing from the port of Philadelphia; when the Revolutionary War began, the ship was purchased by Congress and converted into the warship Alfred.

Revolutionary War service

Barry's first naval command was the brig Lexington. On 7 April 1776 he captured the British sloop HMS Edward off the Virginia Capes, achieving the first capture of a British warship by a commissioned American naval vessel. He later commanded the frigates Raleigh and Alliance. Aboard Alliance in May 1781, Barry defeated the British sloops Atalanta and Trepassey in a hard-fought action. On 10 March 1783, commanding Alliance in company with Duc de Lauzun, he fought the last naval engagement of the war against HMS Sybil off Cape Canaveral while carrying a cargo of Spanish silver dollars from Havana to Congress.

Later service and the new Navy

After the war Barry returned to the merchant service and in 1787–88 commanded the ship Asia on a pioneering trade voyage to China. When the United States Navy was created in 1794, Barry was appointed its senior captain. He supervised the construction of the frigate USS United States and commanded her during the Quasi-War with France, cruising in the West Indies. He remained on active duty until his death in Philadelphia on 13 September 1803; his seniority made him, in effect, the first head of the United States Navy.

Legacy

Several U.S. Navy destroyers have borne his name, including USS Barry (DDG-52). A statue of Barry stands behind Independence Hall in Philadelphia, and 13 September is observed as John Barry Day. Barry's reputation as a skilled seaman, a successful commerce raider and convoy escort, and the founding senior officer of the U.S. Navy has endured for more than two centuries.
