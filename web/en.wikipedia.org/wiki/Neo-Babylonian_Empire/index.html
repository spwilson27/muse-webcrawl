The Neo-Babylonian Empire, also known as the Second Babylonian Empire and historically as the Chaldean Empire, was the last Mesopotamian great power of the ancient Near East, ruling from the coronation of Nabopolassar in 626 BC until the Persian conquest of Babylon by Cyrus the Great in 539 BC. Though it lasted less than a century, it is remembered for the splendor of its capital and for its role in biblical history.

Rise

Nabopolassar, a Chaldean leader in southern Babylonia, revolted against the Neo-Assyrian Empire and was crowned king of Babylon in 626 BC. In alliance with the Medes, he destroyed Nineveh in 612 BC, extinguishing Assyrian power. His son Nebuchadnezzar II (r. 605–562 BC) secured the empire's western frontiers by defeating Egypt at the Battle of Carchemish (605 BC) and campaigned repeatedly in the Levant. He captured Jerusalem in 597 BC and destroyed it, including Solomon's Temple, in 586 BC, deporting much of Judah's population to Babylonia — the Babylonian captivity of the Hebrew Bible.

Babylon under Nebuchadnezzar II

Nebuchadnezzar transformed Babylon into one of the wonders of the ancient world, rebuilding the great ziggurat Etemenanki, the temple of Marduk (Esagila), the Processional Way, and the Ishtar Gate with its glazed-brick reliefs of bulls and dragons. The Hanging Gardens of Babylon, counted among the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, were attributed to him by later tradition. The empire grew rich on trade and agricultural administration, and Babylonian astronomy and mathematics flourished.

Decline and fall

After Nebuchadnezzar, a rapid succession of kings ended with Nabonidus (r. 556–539 BC), an antiquarian devotee of the moon god Sin whose long absence at Tayma in Arabia and promotion of Sin over Marduk alienated the Babylonian priesthood. In 539 BC Cyrus the Great of Persia defeated the Babylonian army at Opis and entered Babylon, according to tradition, without a battle; the empire was absorbed into the Achaemenid Empire, and the Jewish exiles were permitted to return home.
