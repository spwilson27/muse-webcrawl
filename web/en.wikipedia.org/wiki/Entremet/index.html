Entremet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An entremet or entremets (French pronunciation: [ɑ̃tʁəmɛ]; from Old French, literally meaning "between servings") in French cuisine originally referred to a dish served between courses of a meal. Over time the term came to denote, first, a small side dish served alongside the roast, and later, in modern usage, a sweet dish — typically a layered mousse cake — served as dessert.

A modern entremet: a multi-layered mousse cake, here with a mirror glaze.

History

In the elaborate banquets of medieval and Renaissance France, entremets were far more than food. Between the main courses, hosts presented spectacular interludes: edible and inedible tableaux ranging from sculpted sugar and pastry creations — castles, ships, fountains, and animals — to live entertainment. Some entremets were purely theatrical, featuring musicians, dancers, or even live animals concealed inside pastry structures, a tradition echoed in the nursery rhyme image of "four and twenty blackbirds baked in a pie".

By the 17th century, the culinary meaning had narrowed: in the classical French service codified by writers such as La Varenne, the entremets were a course of smaller dishes served after the roast, including vegetables, eggs, and — increasingly — sweet preparations. In the 19th century, under service à la russe and the reforms of chefs such as Marie-Antoine Carême, sweet entremets became the standard closing course of a formal meal.

Modern usage

In contemporary French pâtisserie, an entremet is a layered dessert, usually built in a mold or ring, combining several textures and flavors: a base of sponge cake (such as joconde or dacquoise), mousse or bavarois, a creamy insert (crémeux), a crunchy layer (for example feuilletine praline), and often a bright fruit gelée, all finished with a glossy glaze. Because the components must be prepared in sequence and frozen between steps, the entremet is considered a showcase of the pastry chef's technique, and it features prominently in professional competitions.
