Brownlow Medal

The Brownlow Medal, officially the Charles Brownlow Trophy, is the most prestigious individual award in Australian rules football. It is presented each year to the fairest and best player in the Australian Football League (AFL) during the home-and-away season, as judged by the field umpires.

Voting

After each match, the field umpires award three votes to the best player, two to the second-best, and one to the third-best. Votes are kept secret and counted on Brownlow Medal night, held on the Monday before the AFL Grand Final, in a televised ceremony famous for its round-by-round count. Players suspended during the season are ineligible, even if they poll the most votes.

History

The medal was first awarded in 1924 and named for Charles Brownlow, a Geelong footballer and administrator who died that year. A handful of players have won three medals, including Haydn Bunton, Dick Reynolds, Bob Skilton, and Ian Stewart. The count night, with partners' fashion on the red carpet, is a fixture of Australian sporting culture.
