Hans Langsdorff

Died20 December 1939, Buenos Aires, Argentina

Hans Wilhelm Langsdorff (20 March 1894 – 20 December 1939) was a German naval officer best known as the commander of the pocket battleship Admiral Graf Spee during the Battle of the River Plate in December 1939.

Career

Langsdorff joined the Imperial German Navy in 1912 and served at the Battle of Jutland. Between the wars he worked in torpedo warfare and naval administration. In 1938 he took command of Admiral Graf Spee, which sailed before the outbreak of the Second World War to raid Allied merchant shipping in the South Atlantic.

Battle of the River Plate

Langsdorff's ship sank nine merchant vessels without loss of life among their crews, earning him a reputation for chivalrous conduct. On 13 December 1939 his ship engaged the British cruisers Exeter, Ajax, and Achilles off the River Plate. Believing a superior force awaited him and with his ship damaged, Langsdorff scuttled Graf Spee off Montevideo rather than risk his crew in a further battle. Days later he died by suicide in Buenos Aires.

Legacy

Langsdorff was buried with full military honours in Buenos Aires, attended by British as well as German officers. His decision to spare his crew is still widely discussed in naval histories of the war.
