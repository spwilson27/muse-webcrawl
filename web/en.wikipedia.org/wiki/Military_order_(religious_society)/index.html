A military order is a Christian religious society whose members combine monastic vows with military service. Emerging during the Crusades, the orders fielded armies of monk-knights, built castles, ran hospitals and commanderies across Europe and the Holy Land, and became some of the wealthiest and most powerful institutions of medieval Christendom.

Origins

The first and most famous order, the Poor Knights of Christ and of the Temple of Solomon — the Knights Templar — was founded around 1119 to protect pilgrims in the Kingdom of Jerusalem, and received its rule with the support of Bernard of Clairvaux. The Knights Hospitaller, originating in a hospital for pilgrims in Jerusalem, militarised over the twelfth century. The Teutonic Order, founded during the Third Crusade, later carved out a monastic state in Prussia. Numerous regional orders arose in Iberia — Calatrava, Santiago, Alcántara and Avis among them — to fight in the Reconquista, and others on the Baltic and Balkan frontiers.

Organisation and decline

Members took vows of poverty, chastity and obedience and were divided into knights, sergeants and chaplains. The orders amassed land, banking functions (the Templars famously lent to kings) and naval power. The Templars were suppressed in 1312 amid charges trumped up by Philip IV of France. The Hospitallers, after losing the Holy Land, conquered Rhodes and then Malta, surviving as the Sovereign Military Order of Malta. The Teutonic Order's state was secularised in the Reformation; several orders survive today as orders of chivalry or charitable bodies.
