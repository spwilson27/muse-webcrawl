Mensalão

The Mensalão scandal (Portuguese: Escândalo do Mensalão) was a major political scandal in Brazil that erupted in 2005. It involved members of the Brazilian Congress allegedly receiving monthly payments (mensalão, "big monthly payment") from the governing Workers' Party (PT) in exchange for their votes in support of government legislation. The scandal severely damaged the administration of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, although Lula himself was not charged.

Background

The scandal broke in June 2005 when federal deputy Roberto Jefferson, leader of the Brazilian Labour Party (PTB), alleged in a newspaper interview that the PT was paying deputies 30,000 reais per month to vote with the government. Jefferson himself admitted involvement and named Marcos Valério, a businessman whose advertising agencies were said to channel the money through loans from banks including Banco Rural.

Investigation and trial

A congressional inquiry (CPI) investigated the scheme, and in 2006 the Supreme Federal Court accepted charges against 40 defendants. The trial, known as Criminal Action 470 (Ação Penal 470), began in August 2012 and became one of the most prominent corruption trials in Brazilian history. Reporting justice Joaquim Barbosa led the proceedings, which were televised. Key figures convicted included José Dirceu, former chief of staff to President Lula; PT president José Genoíno; treasurer Delúbio Soares; and Marcos Valério, who received a sentence of over 37 years.

Consequences

The convictions were seen as a landmark in Brazilian jurisprudence because senior political and business figures were imprisoned for corruption. Sentences were largely imposed in 2013, though some defendants later benefited from pardons, sentence reductions, and legal revisions. The scandal cast a long shadow over Brazilian politics and preceded later investigations, including Operation Car Wash.
