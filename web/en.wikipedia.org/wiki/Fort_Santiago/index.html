Fort Santiago

LocationIntramuros, Manila, Philippines
Built1571; rebuilt in stone 1590–1593
BuilderSpanish colonial government
TypeCitadel / defense fortress

Fort Santiago (Spanish: Fuerte de Santiago; Filipino: Moóg ng Santiago) is a citadel built by Spanish navigator and governor Miguel López de Legazpi for the newly established city of Manila in the Philippines. The defense fortress is located in Intramuros, the walled city of Manila.

History

The site was once the seat of the palace of Rajah Matanda, a Muslim rajah of pre-colonial Manila, before it was destroyed and replaced by the Spanish fort. The first fort was built of earth and timber in 1571; a stone citadel followed between 1590 and 1593. Fort Santiago served as the premier defense fortress of the Spanish government in the Philippines and figured in the British occupation of 1762, American and Japanese occupations, and the Battle of Manila in 1945, which heavily damaged it.

Rizal Shrine

National hero José Rizal was imprisoned in the fort before his execution in 1896. The fort now houses the Rizal Shrine museum, and brass footprints mark Rizal's final walk from his cell to the execution ground at Bagumbayan.
