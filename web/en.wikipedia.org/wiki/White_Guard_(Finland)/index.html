White Guard (Finland)

The White Guard or Civil Guard (Finnish: Suojeluskunta; Swedish: Skyddskår) was a Finnish voluntary national defence organisation formed in 1918 from the "Whites" who fought in the Finnish Civil War. It existed as the paramilitary arm of White Finland until its dissolution in 1944 under the terms of the Moscow Armistice.

Civil War

After Finland declared independence from Russia in December 1917, local White Guards formed to uphold order and oppose the socialist Red Guards. In January 1918 the Finnish Senate recognised the White Guards as the troops of the government, under the command of C. G. E. Mannerheim. They formed the core of the White Army, which defeated the Reds by May 1918 with German assistance.

Interwar period

Between the wars the Civil Guard grew into a mass organisation of up to 120,000 members, providing voluntary military training, marksmanship, and civic education, with the women's auxiliary Lotta Svärd working alongside it. Guardsmen staffed local defence during the Winter War and Continuation War.

Dissolution

The 1944 armistice with the Soviet Union required the dissolution of the Civil Guard, which Mannerheim, by then president, ordered in the autumn of 1944.
