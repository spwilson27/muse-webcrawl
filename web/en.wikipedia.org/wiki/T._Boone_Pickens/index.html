Thomas Boone Pickens Jr. (May 22, 1928 – September 11, 2019) was an American business magnate and financier. Pickens chaired the hedge fund BP Capital Management and was a well-known corporate takeover operator during the 1980s. As of November 2016, Pickens had a net worth of US$500 million.

Early life and career

Pickens was born in Holdenville, Oklahoma, the son of a landman and oil lease broker. He delivered newspapers as a boy and studied geology at Texas A&M before transferring to Oklahoma Agricultural and Mechanical College (now Oklahoma State University), graduating in 1951. After working for Phillips Petroleum, he founded the company that became Mesa Petroleum in 1956 with only $2,500 in borrowed money.

Corporate raids

Under Pickens, Mesa grew into one of America's largest independent oil companies. In the 1980s he gained fame — and the label "corporate raider" — through attempted takeovers of much larger companies including Cities Service, Gulf Oil, Phillips Petroleum and Unocal. Though most bids failed, they often earned Mesa large profits when the target companies bought back their shares or were acquired by others, and Pickens became a prominent voice for shareholder rights.

Later ventures and philanthropy

Pickens later founded BP Capital, focused on energy investments, and Clean Energy Fuels, promoting natural gas vehicles. In 2008 he launched the "Pickens Plan", a campaign to reduce US dependence on foreign oil through wind power and natural gas. A major benefactor of Oklahoma State University, he gave hundreds of millions of dollars to its athletic and academic programmes, and to medical and research institutions. He died in Dallas on 11 September 2019, aged 91.
