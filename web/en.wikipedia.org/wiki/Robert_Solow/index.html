Robert Solow


Born: August 23, 1924, Brooklyn, New York, U.S.
Died: December 21, 2023, Lexington, Massachusetts
Occupation: Economist (MIT)
Awards: Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences (1987)

Robert Merton Solow (August 23, 1924 – December 21, 2023) was an American economist who taught at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and won the 1987 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for his work on the theory of economic growth.

Work

In two celebrated papers of 1956 and 1957, Solow developed the neoclassical growth model — the Solow–Swan model — in which long-run growth depends on capital accumulation, labour-force growth and, crucially, technological progress, which the model treats as exogenous. Applying the framework to United States data, he estimated that most of the growth in output per worker came not from more capital but from technical change — the "Solow residual".

Solow also contributed to the economics of natural resources and exhaustible resources (the Hartwick rule and related work), served on President Kennedy's Council of Economic Advisers, and was a central figure — with his colleague Paul Samuelson — in the Cambridge capital controversies with economists at the University of Cambridge, England. He received the National Medal of Science (1999) and the Presidential Medal of Freedom (2014).
