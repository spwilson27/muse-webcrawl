Chess prodigy

A young player competing against adult opponents (image placeholder)

A chess prodigy is a young child who plays chess at a level far above what is typical for their age, often reaching master strength before adolescence and defeating experienced adult players.

History

Famous historical prodigies include Paul Morphy, José Raúl Capablanca, and Samuel Reshevsky, who gave simultaneous exhibitions as a child. In the modern era, faster access to engines and online play has lowered the age of achievement dramatically. Sergey Karjakin became the youngest grandmaster in history in 2002 at 12 years and 7 months, a record broken in 2021 by Abhimanyu Mishra at 12 years, 4 months, and 25 days. Magnus Carlsen, Judit Polgár, Hou Yifan, Praggnanandhaa Rameshbabu, and Gukesh Dommaraju were all celebrated prodigies; Gukesh became the youngest undisputed World Chess Champion in 2024 at age 18.

Measuring prodigiousness

Comparisons usually consider the age at which a player earned the grandmaster title, broke rating thresholds (such as 2500 or 2600 Elo), or defeated reigning champions. Record ages have fallen steadily over the decades, a trend attributed to computer training tools, abundant online competition, and professional coaching from an early age.
