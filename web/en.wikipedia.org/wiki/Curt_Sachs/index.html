Curt Sachs (29 June 1881 – 5 February 1959) was a German-American musicologist and one of the founders of modern organology, the study of musical instruments. With Erich Moritz von Hornbostel he devised the Hornbostel–Sachs system for classifying musical instruments, first published in 1914 and still the most widely used scheme in ethnomusicology and museum cataloguing.

Life

Sachs studied art history, music history and composition in Berlin, taking his doctorate in 1904 with a dissertation on the paintings of Veronese. He became curator of the Berlin collection of musical instruments at the Staatliche Musikinstrumenten-Sammlung and a professor at Berlin University, building a reputation through works on the instruments of India and of the Baroque era. Dismissed from his posts by the Nazi regime in 1933 because of his Jewish ancestry, he emigrated first to Paris — where he worked at the Musée de l'Homme and the Sorbonne — and in 1937 to New York. There he taught at New York University and served as consultant to the Metropolitan Museum of Art, remaining in the United States for the rest of his life.

Work

The Hornbostel–Sachs system divides instruments by the physical means of sound production into idiophones, membranophones, chordophones and aerophones (later supplemented by electrophones), subdivided by a decimal scheme analogous to library classification. Sachs's later English-language books, above all The History of Musical Instruments (1940) and The Rise of Music in the Ancient World (1943), surveyed instruments from prehistory to the modern orchestra and remain standard references. He also wrote extensively on dance history, including World History of the Dance (1933).

Legacy

The American Musical Instrument Society awards a Curt Sachs Award for distinguished work in organology, and the Galpin Society likewise honours his contribution to the field he helped create. His classification, repeatedly revised and extended, underlies the catalogues of instrument collections worldwide and the MIMO (Musical Instrument Museums Online) database.
