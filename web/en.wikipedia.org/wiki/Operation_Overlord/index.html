Operation Overlord was the codename for the Battle of Normandy, the Allied operation that launched the successful liberation of German-occupied Western Europe during the Second World War. The operation was launched on 6 June 1944 — D-Day — with the Normandy landings (Operation Neptune). Nearly 160,000 troops crossed the English Channel on the first day, supported by the largest seaborne invasion fleet in history and extensive airborne operations the preceding night.

Planning and deception

Planning for a cross-Channel invasion began years earlier and was assigned in 1943 to the staff of COSSAC under Lieutenant-General Frederick Morgan; General Dwight D. Eisenhower became Supreme Allied Commander, with Bernard Montgomery commanding the ground forces. The assault plan targeted five beaches — Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno, and Sword. An elaborate deception, Operation Fortitude, convinced German commanders that the main invasion would come at the Pas-de-Calais, and held their reserves away from Normandy even after the landings.

The campaign

After securing the beachheads, Allied forces fought a grinding campaign through the Normandy bocage. Key battles included the capture of Cherbourg, the battle for Caen, the American breakout in Operation Cobra in late July, and the encirclement of German forces in the Falaise pocket in August. Paris was liberated on 25 August 1944, and by the end of August Allied armies had crossed the Seine, concluding Overlord.

Significance

Overlord established the long-demanded Western ("Second") Front in France, from which Allied forces advanced into Germany in 1944–45. The operation remains the largest seaborne invasion in history and a landmark of coalition logistics and planning.
