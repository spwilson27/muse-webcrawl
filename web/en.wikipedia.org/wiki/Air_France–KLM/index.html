Air France–KLM

Founded: 5 May 2004 (merger)
Headquarters: Tremblay-en-France, France
Subsidiaries: Air France, KLM, Transavia
Alliance: SkyTeam

Air France–KLM S.A. is a Franco-Dutch airline holding company created by the merger of Air France and KLM in 2004. One of the world's largest airline groups by revenue, it operates a dual-hub network at Paris–Charles de Gaulle and Amsterdam Schiphol, and is a founding member of the SkyTeam alliance.

History

The merger, announced in September 2003 and completed in May 2004, combined the two flag carriers under a single holding company while preserving both brands and national identities. The group later took stakes in other carriers and developed the low-cost subsidiary Transavia. Both the French and Dutch states hold significant shareholdings, and the group has periodically been the subject of tension between Paris and The Hague over governance and strategy. Air France–KLM suffered heavy losses during the COVID-19 pandemic and received multi-billion-euro state support packages from both governments.

Operations

The group carries on the order of 100 million passengers in strong years and operates cargo services, engineering and maintenance (Air France Industries KLM Engineering & Maintenance), and the Flying Blue frequent-flyer programme shared by both airlines.
