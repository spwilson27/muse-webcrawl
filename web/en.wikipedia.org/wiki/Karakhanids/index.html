Karakhanids

Period840–1212
CapitalsBalasagun, Kashgar, Samarkand, Uzgen
ReligionIslam (Sunni)

The Karakhanids, also spelled Qarakhanids and known in Chinese sources as the Black Khan dynasty, were a Turkic khanate that ruled Transoxiana and parts of the Tarim Basin in Central Asia from 840 to 1212. They were the first Turkic dynasty to convert to Islam and to rule a predominantly Muslim Turkic state.

History

The Karakhanids arose from a confederation of Karluks and other tribes after the collapse of the Uyghur Khaganate in 840. Under Satuq Bughra Khan they adopted Islam in the 10th century, and in 999 they took Bukhara from the Samanids, dividing Perso-Islamic Central Asia with the Ghaznavids along the Oxus (Amu Darya). The khanate later split into western (Samarkand) and eastern (Balasagun/Kashgar) branches, came under Seljuk suzerainty after the Battle of Qatwan (1141) passed to the Qara Khitai, and was extinguished by the Khwarazmshahs in 1212.

Culture

The Karakhanid era produced foundational works of Turkic-Islamic literature in the Karakhanid (Hakaniyya) language: Yusuf Khass Hajib's mirror for princes Kutadgu Bilig (1070, dedicated at Kashgar) and Mahmud al-Kashgari's Dīwān Lughāt al-Turk, a comparative dictionary of Turkic dialects. Cities such as Kashgar, Balasagun, and Samarkand flourished on Silk Road trade, with notable architecture including minarets and rabbats.
