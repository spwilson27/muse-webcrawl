Garden of the Master of the Nets


The central pond of the Garden of the Master of the Nets.

The Garden of the Master of the Nets (Chinese: 网师园; pinyin: Wǎngshī yuán) in Suzhou, Jiangsu, China, is a renowned Chinese garden and one of the Classical Gardens of Suzhou inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. Though the smallest of Suzhou's residential gardens, it is widely considered the finest, praised for the artistry with which its compact site — about 5,400 square metres — is made to feel spacious.

History

The garden was first laid out in the Song dynasty (from 1140) by Shi Zhengzhi, a government official, who named it the Hall of Ten Thousand Volumes. After falling into ruin, it was rebuilt in 1785 during the Qing dynasty by Song Zongyuan, a retired official, who gave it the name Master of the Nets — "Master of the Nets" referring to the simple life of a fisherman, in humble contrast to a court career. The garden later passed to the Qu family and was restored in the 1940s before becoming a public heritage site.

Design

The garden is divided into a residential quarter in the east and the garden proper in the west. At its heart lies a pond surrounded by pavilions, covered walkways, rockeries and the Peony Study, designed so that views unfold in sequence. Sightlines, borrowed scenery and varied rooflines create the impression of a much larger space.
