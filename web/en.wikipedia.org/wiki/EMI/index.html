EMI

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Abbey Road Studios, EMI's famous London recording studios.

EMI Group Limited (originally Electric and Musical Industries, also known as EMI Records) was a British transnational conglomerate founded in March 1931 in London. For most of the 20th century it was one of the dominant companies in the recorded music business — for decades counted among the "Big Four" record labels — with interests that at various times also included electronics, defence equipment and broadcasting technology. Its recorded music division was sold to Universal Music Group in 2012, ending EMI's existence as an independent company; the EMI name survives as a label imprint.

History

EMI was formed by the merger of the British Columbia Graphophone Company and the Gramophone Company, whose "His Master's Voice" label and Nipper dog trademark were famous worldwide. The Gramophone Company had opened the recording studios at 3 Abbey Road, London, in 1931; the studios became synonymous with EMI through the work of The Beatles, Pink Floyd, Cliff Richard and many others. EMI's Parlophone, Columbia and Capitol labels (the latter acquired in 1955) signed artists including Frank Sinatra, The Beach Boys, Queen, Duran Duran, Iron Maiden, Radiohead, Coldplay and Kylie Minogue.

Beyond music, EMI's electronics arm made the first commercial television system used by the BBC, developed radar equipment, and built the first commercially available CT scanner under Godfrey Hounsfield, who won a Nobel Prize for the work. The Thorn EMI merger of 1979 combined the company with Thorn Electrical Industries; the two demerged in 1996.

Decline and breakup

Hit by the collapse of recorded-music revenues after 2000, EMI was bought in 2007 by the private equity firm Terra Firma for about £4.2 billion, in a heavily leveraged deal that soon struggled. Citigroup seized the company in 2011 after Terra Firma defaulted, and in 2012 EMI was broken up: its recorded music business went to Universal Music Group for £1.2 billion, and its music publishing arm to a Sony-led consortium. Regulatory conditions forced Universal to sell Parlophone and other assets, which were acquired by Warner Music Group.
