Demotic (from Greek δημοτικά, "popular") refers both to a stage of the ancient Egyptian language and to the highly cursive script in which it was written. Demotic emerged around 650 BC in the Late Period, developing out of the hieratic script used for everyday writing, and remained in use for roughly a thousand years — for legal contracts, administrative documents, letters, literature and magical texts — until the mid-fifth century AD, longer than any other native Egyptian script. The language stage immediately precedes Coptic.

Development

Demotic arose in the Delta region during the Twenty-sixth (Saite) Dynasty and spread throughout Egypt under Persian rule, replacing abnormal hieratic in the south. Egyptologists divide it into Early Demotic (to c. 300 BC), Middle or Ptolemaic Demotic, and Roman Demotic (to c. AD 450). Even after Greek became the main language of government under the Ptolemies, Demotic remained the medium of Egyptian law and temple administration; native Egyptian notaries drafted contracts in it well into the Roman period.

Script

The Demotic script is an extreme cursive: signs are heavily ligatured and abbreviated, individual hieroglyphic origins are often unrecognisable, and the same sign can have many contextual forms. It is written right to left. Because Demotic preserves the writing system of hieratic while the spoken language changed markedly, spelling is conservative and often difficult; the authoritative reference is the Chicago Demotic Dictionary, completed by the Oriental Institute over several decades.

Literature

Beyond tens of thousands of documents — marriage contracts, tax receipts, temple ostraca — Demotic literature includes wisdom instructions (the Instructions of Ankhsheshonq, the Papyrus Insinger), narrative tales (the Setne Khaemwaset cycle, stories of magician-princes), the mythological manual of the Book of the Temple tradition, prophetic texts such as the Demotic Chronicle and the Oracle of the Potter, and the bilingual decree texts — most famously the Rosetta Stone (196 BC) — inscribed in hieroglyphs, Demotic and Greek.

Decipherment

The middle register of the Rosetta Stone is Demotic, and work on it by Silvestre de Sacy, Johan David Åkerblad and Thomas Young — who correctly identified some Demotic groups and saw that Demotic derived from hieratic — paved the way for Jean-François Champollion's decipherment of the hieroglyphs in 1822. The last dated Demotic graffito, scratched at the temple of Isis at Philae, dates to AD 452 — shortly before the last hieroglyphic inscription (AD 394) at the same temple closed Egypt's three-thousand-year scribal tradition.
