Fen River

LocationShanxi, China
Length694 km (431 mi)
MouthYellow River, near Hejin

The Fen River (Chinese: 汾河; pinyin: Fén Hé) is the longest river in Shanxi Province, China, and the second-longest tributary of the Yellow River. It rises in the Guancen Mountains of Ningwu County, flows southward through the provincial capital Taiyuan and the fertile Fen valley, and joins the Yellow River at Hejin after a course of about 694 kilometres.

Geography and history

The Fen valley is one of the cradles of early Chinese civilisation; Neolithic and Bronze Age sites cluster along its banks, and the ancient state of Jin took its name from the region. The river passes through the loess highlands, carrying a heavy sediment load, and drains most of central and southern Shanxi, including the cities of Linfen and Yuncheng.

Pollution and restoration

Industrialisation and coal mining made the Fen one of China's most polluted rivers by the early 2000s, with long stretches classified as unfit even for agricultural use. Provincial restoration programmes since the 2010s have closed polluting plants, built wastewater treatment works and restored wetlands along the Taiyuan section, substantially improving water quality.
