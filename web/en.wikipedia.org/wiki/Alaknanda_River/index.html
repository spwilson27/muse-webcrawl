Alaknanda River

Alaknanda
Country: India
State: Uttarakhand
Source: Satopanth and Bhagirathi Kharak glaciers
Mouth: Confluence with Bhagirathi at Devprayag (forming the Ganga)
Length: about 195 km

The Alaknanda is a Himalayan river in the Indian state of Uttarakhand and one of the two headstreams of the Ganga (Ganges), alongside the Bhagirathi. In Hindu tradition and in terms of length and discharge, the Alaknanda is regarded as the source stream of the Ganga; the two rivers meet at Devprayag, from where the combined river takes the name Ganga.

Course

The Alaknanda rises at the confluence of the Satopanth and Bhagirathi Kharak glaciers near the Tibetan border. It flows past the pilgrimage town of Badrinath and through a chain of sacred confluences known as the Panch Prayag — Vishnuprayag, Nandaprayag, Karnaprayag, Rudraprayag and finally Devprayag — receiving tributaries including the Dhauliganga, Nandakini, Pindar and Mandakini.

Significance

The Alaknanda valley forms the main pilgrim route to Badrinath, one of the Char Dham temples. The river is also the site of numerous hydroelectric projects, and its upper reaches were devastated by the June 2013 Uttarakhand floods, triggered in part by the bursting of Chorabari lake above Kedarnath in the Mandakini valley.
