The Grolier Club is a private club and society of bibliophiles in New York City, founded in 1884. It is the oldest existing bibliophilic club in North America. The club is named for the French bibliophile Jean Grolier de Servières, a Renaissance collector famed for his motto that his books were "of Grolier and his friends." Its stated objective is "the study, collecting, and appreciation of books and works on paper."

Activities

The Grolier Club maintains a research library of more than 100,000 volumes on bibliography, bookbinding, printing history, and the book arts, open to qualified researchers. It mounts free public exhibitions on book-related subjects and publishes catalogues and scholarly works on printing, binding, and collecting.

Clubhouse

After earlier homes in Midtown Manhattan, the club moved in 1917 to its current clubhouse at 47 East 60th Street, designed by architect Bertram Goodhue. The building contains exhibition galleries, the library, and rooms for members' meetings and dinners.

Membership

Membership, numbering about 800, is by invitation and includes collectors, librarians, dealers, printers, and scholars. Women were first admitted in 1976.
