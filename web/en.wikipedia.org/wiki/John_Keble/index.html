John Keble (25 April 1792 – 29 March 1866) was an English Anglican priest, poet and theologian, one of the leaders of the Oxford Movement. His sermon "National Apostasy", preached at Oxford on 14 July 1833, is traditionally taken as the beginning of that movement to renew the Church of England. Keble College, Oxford, founded in his memory in 1870, is named for him.

Early life and Oxford

Keble was born at Fairford, Gloucestershire, son of a vicar, and won a scholarship to Corpus Christi College, Oxford, achieving a rare double first and a fellowship at Oriel College. He was ordained in 1816 and served as a tutor and examiner at Oxford, forming close friendships with Richard Hurrell Froude, John Henry Newman and Edward Bouverie Pusey.

The Christian Year

In 1827 Keble anonymously published The Christian Year, a volume of devotional poems for the Sundays and feasts of the Anglican calendar. Immensely popular — it ran through nearly a hundred editions in his lifetime — it shaped Victorian Anglican piety and made Keble the most beloved religious poet of his age.

Oxford Movement and parish life

Keble's 1833 Assize Sermon denounced Parliament's suppression of Irish bishoprics as national apostasy. With Newman and others he issued the Tracts for the Times advocating apostolic succession and sacramental worship. From 1836 until his death he was vicar of Hursley, Hampshire, where he rebuilt the church and lived a model parochial life, remaining loyal to the Church of England even after Newman's conversion to Rome in 1845.
