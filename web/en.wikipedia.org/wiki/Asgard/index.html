Asgard

Mythology: Norse

Inhabitants: The Æsir gods

In Norse mythology, Asgard (Old Norse: Ásgarðr, "enclosure of the Æsir") is a realm inhabited by the Æsir tribe of gods, one of the Nine Worlds connected by the world tree Yggdrasil. Asgard is surrounded by an incomplete wall attributed to a nameless hrimthurs who rode the stallion Svaðilfari, and is associated with the god Odin.

Attestations

Asgard is attested in the Poetic Edda, compiled in the 13th century from earlier traditional sources, and in the Prose Edda and Heimskringla of Snorri Sturluson. Within its walls stand the halls of the gods: Odin's Valhalla, where half of those who die in battle gather as einherjar; Gladsheim; Vingólf; and the hall Bilskirnir belonging to Thor. The well of Urd lies beneath one root of Yggdrasil in Asgard, where the gods hold their courts and the Norns tend the tree.

Features

  • Bifröst — the burning rainbow bridge connecting Asgard to Midgard, guarded by Heimdall.
  • Valhalla — Odin's hall of the chosen slain, roofed with spears and shields.
  • Idavoll — the plain where the gods assemble and, after Ragnarök, meet again.
  • Fólkvangr — Freyja's field, receiving the other half of the battle dead.

Etymology and legacy

The name combines áss ("god") and garðr ("enclosure, yard"). In Snorri's euhemerising prologue, Asgard is reimagined as a city in Asia — Troy. Asgard features prominently in modern popular culture, including Marvel Comics and its film adaptations, where it is depicted as a floating realm in space.
