Affective turn
Field: Humanities and social sciences
Period: From the mid-1990s
Key figures: Eve Sedgwick, Brian Massumi, Sara Ahmed
Focus: Affect, embodiment, emotion in culture

The affective turn is a shift in the humanities and social sciences, beginning in the mid-1990s, toward studying affect, emotion and embodied experience as central to social and cultural life rather than as secondary to language and representation.

Origins

The term was popularised by Patricia Clough's introduction to the 2007 collection The Affective Turn. Influential early interventions included Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick and Adam Frank's work on Silvan Tomkins's affect theory and Brian Massumi's writings on affect in Deleuze and Spinoza.

Themes

Scholars of the affective turn examine how feelings circulate through media, politics and everyday life, how atmospheres and moods shape collectives, and how bodies affect and are affected by one another.
