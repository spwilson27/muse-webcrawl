MAFFT (Multiple Alignment using Fast Fourier Transform) is a multiple sequence alignment program for amino acid or nucleotide sequences. It was developed by Kazutaka Katoh, initially at Osaka University, and was first described in 2002.

Method

MAFFT identifies homologous regions between sequences by converting them into numerical signals and applying the fast Fourier transform to detect matching segments quickly. This allows it to align large numbers of sequences far faster than earlier dynamic-programming-heavy methods while retaining high accuracy.

The software offers a family of algorithms trading speed against accuracy, including the fast FFT-NS-1 and FFT-NS-2 progressive strategies, the more accurate iterative refinement methods (such as FFT-NS-i), and the consistency-based L-INS-i and structure-aware methods for RNA sequences. The widely recommended "auto" mode selects a strategy based on the size of the input.

Use

MAFFT is one of the most widely used alignment tools in computational biology and phylogenetics, and it is available as a standalone program and through web servers and workflow platforms such as Galaxy.
