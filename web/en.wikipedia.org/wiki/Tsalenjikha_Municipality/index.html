Tsalenjikha Municipality (Georgian: წალენჯიხის მუნიციპალიტეტი) is a municipality in western Georgia, in the region of Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti. Its administrative centre is the town of Tsalenjikha. The municipality covers about 647 square kilometres of the Colchic lowland and foothills, stretching from the Enguri river in the north-east toward the Black Sea plain, and had a population of roughly 26,000 at the 2014 census.

Geography

The territory is drained by rivers flowing from the foothills of the Greater Caucasus toward the Black Sea, and its lowland climate is humid subtropical, supporting tea plantations, citrus, hazelnuts, and maize. The municipality borders Zugdidi Municipality to the south and west, Mestia Municipality to the north-east, and Chkhorotsku Municipality to the east. Part of its north-eastern area lies close to the administrative boundary with Abkhazia, along the Enguri river; the Enguri hydroelectric dam, one of the largest arch dams in the world, lies just beyond the municipality's north-eastern corner.

Settlements and history

Besides the town of Tsalenjikha, the municipality includes the town of Jvari and numerous villages. The area is part of the historical region of Samegrelo (Mingrelia). Its best-known monument is the Tsalenjikha Cathedral of the Saviour, a medieval church (built in the 14th century under the Dadiani princes) notable for its cycle of Byzantine-style wall paintings executed by the Constantinopolitan artist Manuel Eugenikos, with donor portraits of the Dadiani family.

During the Soviet period the district was known for collective tea farming. After the Georgian civil conflicts of the early 1990s, the municipality hosted many internally displaced people from Abkhazia, and its proximity to the Enguri crossing point keeps it significant in Georgia's post-conflict geography.
