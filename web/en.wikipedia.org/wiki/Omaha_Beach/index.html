Omaha Beach

Omaha Beach was one of five landing sectors of the Allied invasion of German-occupied France in the Normandy landings on 6 June 1944 during the Second World War. It is located on the coast of Normandy, France, facing the English Channel, and is about 8 kilometres (5 miles) long, from east of Sainte-Honorine-des-Pertes to west of Vierville-sur-Mer.

The landing

Assault troops of the US 1st and 29th Infantry Divisions landed at Omaha beginning at 06:30 under heavy fire from German strongpoints overlooking the beach. Pre-landing air and naval bombardment largely missed the defences, and many landing craft were swamped or destroyed. The beach was heavily mined and obstructed, and the high bluffs gave defenders commanding fields of fire. Casualties were far heavier at Omaha than at the other beaches—estimates of American casualties are around 2,000–3,000 killed, wounded, and missing—but by the end of the day small groups of infantry had scaled the bluffs, opened exits from the beach, and secured a fragile lodgment.

Memorials

The beach is today the site of the Normandy American Cemetery at Colleville-sur-Mer, where nearly 9,400 American war dead are buried, as well as museums and memorials at Vierville-sur-Mer and Saint-Laurent-sur-Mer. The sector is known by the codename "Omaha", sometimes given the grim nickname "Bloody Omaha".
