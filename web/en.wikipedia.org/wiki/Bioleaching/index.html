Bioleaching

Bioleaching is the extraction of metals from their ores through the use of living organisms. Bioleaching is one of several applications within biohydrometallurgy and several methods are used to treat ores or wastes to extract valuable metals. Bioleaching can involve numerous ferrous iron- and sulfur-oxidizing bacteria, including Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans and Acidithiobacillus thiooxidans. As a general principle, Fe3+ ions are used to oxidize the ore, and the bacteria regenerate the oxidant and acid that drive the process.

Process

In commercial practice, low-grade ore is stacked in large heaps or dumps on impermeable pads. The heap is irrigated with acidic bacterial cultures, and the metal-rich solution (the pregnant leach solution) is collected and processed, for example by solvent extraction and electrowinning for copper. Stirred-tank bioleaching, using aerated bioreactors, is used for refractory gold ores and concentrates, where the bacteria oxidise the sulfide matrix that locks up the gold before cyanidation.

Advantages and disadvantages

Bioleaching is generally simpler and therefore cheaper to operate than traditional smelting, and it can profitably treat low-grade ores and mine wastes that would otherwise be uneconomic. It is also considered more environmentally friendly because it avoids the sulfur dioxide emissions of smelters. Its disadvantages are slowness — processes can take months — and sensitivity to temperature, acidity and toxic substances.

Applications

Bioleaching accounts for a significant fraction of world copper production and is used for uranium, nickel, cobalt, zinc and gold. The unintentional microbial generation of acid mine drainage is essentially the same process occurring naturally.
