The Pro Football Hall of Fame is the hall of fame for professional American football, located in Canton, Ohio. Opened on September 7, 1963, the Hall of Fame enshrines exceptional figures in the sport of professional football, including players, coaches, franchise owners, and front-office personnel, almost all of whom made their primary contributions to the game in the National Football League (NFL).

History

Canton was chosen as the site because the NFL was founded there in 1920 (as the American Professional Football Association), the Canton Bulldogs were an early professional power, and local citizens campaigned successfully for the honour. The original building, with its distinctive American-football-shaped dome, has been expanded several times, and the surrounding campus now includes Tom Benson Hall of Fame Stadium and the Hall of Fame Village development.

Selection process

Inductees are selected by a 50-member committee of media representatives. Any fan may nominate a person who has been retired for at least five seasons (players and coaches) for consideration. A seniors committee and a coach/contributor committee surface additional candidates from earlier eras. Each new class is announced during Super Bowl week, and the enshrinement ceremony takes place in Canton in early August, preceded by the Hall of Fame Game, the first preseason game of the NFL season.

Exhibits

The museum's galleries trace the history of professional football from its 19th-century origins through the modern NFL, displaying bronze busts of every enshrinee, historic uniforms and equipment, and interactive exhibits on the game's evolution. Unlike the inductees of some other sports halls of fame, Hall of Famers are not formally associated with a single team; their busts record all the teams with which they were connected.
