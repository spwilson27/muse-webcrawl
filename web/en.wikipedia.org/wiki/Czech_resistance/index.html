Czech resistance

The Czech resistance (Czech: český odboj) was the resistance movement in the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia and the Slovak State against Nazi German occupation during World War II, along with the Czechoslovak government-in-exile in London led by Edvard Beneš. It comprised domestic underground networks, intelligence work, sabotage, and Czechoslovak military units fighting alongside the Allies.

Domestic resistance

After the Munich Agreement (1938) and the German occupation of March 1939, resistance organizations formed, including Obrana národa (Defence of the Nation), composed largely of former army officers, and the coordinating ÚVOD council. The intelligence network of the "Three Kings" (Josef Mašín, Václav Morávek, and Josef Balabán) passed valuable information to the Allies. Gestapo repression was severe, especially after Reinhard Heydrich became Acting Reich Protector in 1941.

Operation Anthropoid

In May 1942, Czechoslovak agents Jozef Gabčík and Jan Kubiš, trained by the British SOE and sent by the government-in-exile, attacked Heydrich in Prague; he died of his wounds on 4 June 1942. The Nazis retaliated with the destruction of the villages of Lidice and Ležáky and the murder of thousands of Czechs. The assassins died in a gun battle at the Saints Cyril and Methodius Cathedral.

Slovak National Uprising and liberation

In August 1944 the Slovak National Uprising rose against the Tiso regime and German forces. In May 1945, the Prague uprising liberated the capital days before the war's end. Czechoslovak forces also fought on the Eastern Front (including at Sokolovo and the Dukla Pass) and in the West (RAF squadrons and the armoured brigade at Dunkirk).
