Moridunum (Carmarthen)

TypeRoman fort and civitas capital
LocationCarmarthen, Carmarthenshire, Wales
Foundedc. AD 75
TribeDemetae

Moridunum was a Roman fort and town in the Roman province of Britannia, on the site of present-day Carmarthen. It served as the civitas capital of the Demetae people of south-west Wales. (For the general overview of the name, see Moridunum.)

Fort and town

The fort was established around AD 75 on the north bank of the River Towy (Afon Tywi). By the early second century the fort had been given up and the civilian vicus had developed into a defended town laid out on a grid of streets, with timber and later stone buildings, a forum-basilica complex, baths, and temples. Excavations in the 1970s revealed substantial parts of the town's buildings, making Carmarthen one of the better understood Roman towns in Wales.

Amphitheatre

To the east of the town, at Priory Street, stands the Roman amphitheatre, one of only a handful known in Wales and among the westernmost amphitheatres in the Roman Empire. Its surviving elliptical earthworks measure roughly 46 by 27 metres in the arena.

Later history

Occupation of the town continued into the late Roman period and possibly beyond; the site underlies the eastern part of modern Carmarthen, and many finds are displayed at the Carmarthenshire County Museum.
