Og (Hebrew: עוֹג, romanized: ʿŌg) was, according to the Hebrew Bible, an Amorite king of Bashan who was slain along with his army by Moses and his men at the battle of Edrei. In Arabic, he is sometimes called ʿŪj ibn ʿAnāq (عوج بن عنق, "Og son of Anaq").

The Bible presents Og as a figure of enormous size. Deuteronomy describes his iron bedstead (or sarcophagus), preserved at Rabbah of the Ammonites, as nine cubits long and four cubits wide — roughly 13.5 by 6 feet (about 4 by 1.8 metres). He is called the last survivor of the remnant of the Rephaim, a race of giants remembered in Israelite tradition.

In the Bible

According to Numbers 21, the Israelites, having already defeated King Sihon of the Amorites, turned toward Bashan. Og came out against them at Edrei. Moses was reassured by God — "Fear him not: for I have delivered him into thy hand" — and the Israelites destroyed Og, his sons and his entire army, taking possession of his sixty fortified cities in the region of Argob. Deuteronomy 1–3 retells the battle, and the conquest of Og's kingdom is recalled in Psalms 135 and 136 alongside that of Sihon: "Og the king of Bashan: for his mercy endureth for ever."

Og's territory east of the Jordan was allotted to the half-tribe of Manasseh.

Later tradition

Rabbinic literature greatly expands Og's legend: he is said to have survived the Flood by clinging to the roof of Noah's Ark, to have lived into the time of Abraham, and to have attempted to crush the Israelite camp by hurling a mountain at it. In Islamic tradition, ʿŪj ibn ʿAnāq, a giant of prodigious lifespan, was killed by Moses, who struck his ankle — in some tales Moses leapt into the air and still barely reached it.
