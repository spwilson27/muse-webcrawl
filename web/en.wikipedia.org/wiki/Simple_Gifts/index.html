"Simple Gifts" is a Shaker song written and composed in 1848 by Elder Joseph Brackett (1797–1882) at the Shaker community in Alfred, Maine. Originally a quick dance song for Shaker worship — whose members practised dancing as a form of devotion — it remained little known outside the Shakers until the twentieth century, when it became one of the best-known folk hymns in America.

The melody of "Simple Gifts," as preserved in Shaker manuscripts.

Lyrics

'Tis the gift to be simple, 'tis the gift to be free,
'Tis the gift to come down where we ought to be;
And when we find ourselves in the place just right,
'Twill be in the valley of love and delight.
When true simplicity is gain'd,
To bow and to bend we shan't be asham'd,
To turn, turn will be our delight,
Till by turning, turning we come 'round right.

Revival and adaptations

The song was brought to wide attention when Aaron Copland used its melody in his ballet score Appalachian Spring (1944), commissioned by Martha Graham; Copland later incorporated it into his Old American Songs (1950). Copland had encountered the tune in Edward Deming Andrews's 1940 book The Gift to Be Simple. Sydney Carter's hymn "Lord of the Dance" (1963) was written to the same melody, carrying it into churches worldwide, and the tune has since appeared in countless arrangements, from orchestral works to folk and popular recordings. It was performed at the inaugurations of several U.S. presidents, and John Williams's "Air and Simple Gifts" (2009) was composed for Barack Obama's first inauguration.
