West Lothian

West Lothian (Scots: Wast Lowden; Scottish Gaelic: Lodainn an Iar) is one of the 32 council areas of Scotland and a historic county. It lies between Edinburgh and Glasgow, with Livingston as its largest town and administrative centre; other towns include Linlithgow, Bathgate, Whitburn, Broxburn and Armadale.

History

The historic county of West Lothian was also known as Linlithgowshire after its county town. Linlithgow Palace was a favoured residence of the Stewart monarchs and the birthplace of Mary, Queen of Scots, in 1542. In the 19th century the area industrialised around shale oil — James "Paraffin" Young established the world's first commercial oil industry here — along with coal mining and iron working.

Modern area

Livingston was designated a new town in 1962 and grew into a centre for electronics and retail. The famous "West Lothian question", posed by MP Tam Dalyell in 1977, concerns the constitutional anomaly of Scottish MPs voting on English matters at Westminster.
