Gabbro

Gabbro is a phaneritic (coarse-grained), mafic intrusive igneous rock formed from the slow cooling of magnesium- and iron-rich magma deep beneath the Earth's surface. It is chemically equivalent to basalt, its fine-grained extrusive counterpart, and makes up much of the oceanic crust.


Type: Mafic intrusive igneous rock
Main minerals: Plagioclase feldspar, pyroxene, olivine

Composition and formation

Gabbro consists mainly of calcium-rich plagioclase and clinopyroxene, with variable olivine and amphibole. It crystallises in large intrusions, dikes and layered complexes such as the Bushveld and Skaergaard intrusions, and in the lower oceanic crust at mid-ocean ridges.

Uses

Polished gabbro is sold as "black granite" for countertops, tiles and monuments, and crushed gabbro is used as aggregate. Layered gabbros can host ores of chromium, nickel and platinum.
