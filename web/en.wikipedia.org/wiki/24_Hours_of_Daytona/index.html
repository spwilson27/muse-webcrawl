The 24 Hours of Daytona, currently known as the Rolex 24 at Daytona, is a 24-hour sports car endurance race held annually at Daytona International Speedway in Daytona Beach, Florida. Run since 1962 in shorter formats and as a full 24-hour race from 1966, it opens the IMSA SportsCar Championship season each January and is one of the premier endurance races in the world, alongside the 24 Hours of Le Mans and the 12 Hours of Sebring.

History

The event began in 1962 as a three-hour race on the speedway's road course, part of NASCAR founder Bill France Sr.'s plan to bring international sports car racing to Daytona. It grew to 2,000 kilometres and then, in 1966, to a full 24 hours. The race became a round of the World Sportscar Championship and later the IMSA GT Championship. After a period under Grand-Am sanction as part of the Rolex Sports Car Series, it returned to unified IMSA competition in 2014.

Format

The race uses the speedway's 3.56-mile (5.73 km) road course, combining the banked oval with an infield section. Fields of 40–60 cars compete across prototype and GT classes, with each car driven by three to five drivers. Winning drivers traditionally receive a Rolex Cosmograph Daytona watch. The race is notorious for cold night temperatures, heavy traffic and long caution periods.

Notable records

Hurley Haywood and Scott Pruett share the record of five overall victories. Porsche is the most successful manufacturer overall. The 2023 race introduced the new GTP hybrid prototype class under the LMDh regulations.
