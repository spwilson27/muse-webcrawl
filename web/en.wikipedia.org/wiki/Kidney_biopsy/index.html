Kidney biopsy

A kidney biopsy (renal biopsy) is a medical procedure in which a small sample of kidney tissue is removed, usually with a needle under ultrasound or CT guidance, for microscopic examination. It is a key diagnostic tool in nephrology.

Indications

Biopsy is performed to diagnose unexplained acute kidney injury, nephrotic syndrome, glomerulonephritis, significant proteinuria or haematuria of uncertain cause, and to assess rejection or disease in a transplanted kidney.

Examination

The tissue core is studied by light microscopy, immunofluorescence and electron microscopy, which together can identify the pattern and cause of glomerular, tubular or interstitial disease and guide treatment.

Risks

The main risk is bleeding, including perirenal haematoma; less commonly, infection or damage to adjacent structures. Patients are monitored with bed rest and haemoglobin checks after the procedure.
