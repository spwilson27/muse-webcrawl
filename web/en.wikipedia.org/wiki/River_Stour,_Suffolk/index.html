River Stour, Suffolk

The River Stour in Suffolk and Essex, England, rises near Kedington and flows generally east for about 76 km (47 mi), forming most of the historic border between the two counties, before reaching the North Sea at Harwich. It should not be confused with the other English rivers of the same name in Kent, Dorset, Warwickshire and Worcestershire.

Course

From its headwaters near the Essex–Suffolk–Cambridgeshire border the Stour flows through Clare, Cavendish, Long Melford and Sudbury — the market town where the artist Thomas Gainsborough was born — then through Bures and the Dedham Vale. At Manningtree it becomes tidal and widens into an estuary flanked by mudflats and the Royal Hospital School at Holbrook, joining the Orwell estuary between Harwich and Felixstowe.

Constable Country

The Dedham Vale, through which the middle Stour flows, is known as "Constable Country" after the landscape painter John Constable, who was born at East Bergholt and painted the river valley throughout his life. His subjects included Flatford Mill, Willy Lott's Cottage, the lock and barges at Flatford, Dedham Vale and the hay wain crossing a ford beside the mill — the scene of The Hay Wain (1821). The valley is protected as part of the Dedham Vale National Landscape (formerly the Dedham Vale Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty).

Navigation and industry

The Stour was made navigable from Sudbury to Manningtree by an Act of 1705, among the earliest statutory river navigations in England; horse-drawn lighters carried grain, coal and bricks, and the basin at Sudbury served its mills and maltings. Commercial traffic ended in the early 20th century, but the River Stour Trust works to restore navigation, and the upper river is popular for rowing — Sudbury Rowing Club holds a regatta on the meadows each summer — and for the annual Sudbury-to-the-Sea canoeing event.
