Cambrian

The Cambrian is the first geological period of the Paleozoic Era, lasting from about 538.8 million years ago to 485.4 million years ago. It succeeded the Ediacaran and was followed by the Ordovician. It is named after Cambria, the Latin name for Wales, where rocks of this age were first studied.

Chronology538.8 – 485.4 Ma
EtymologyCambria (Wales)
Proposed byAdam Sedgwick, 1835

Subdivisions

The Cambrian is divided into four epochs/series: Terreneuvian, Series 2, Miaolingian and Furongian, comprising ten stages. Its base is defined by the first appearance of the trace fossil Treptichnus pedum.

Life

The Cambrian witnessed the Cambrian explosion, a rapid diversification of multicellular animals. Trilobites, brachiopods, molluscs, echinoderms and early chordates such as Pikaia appeared. Famous fossil sites include the Burgess Shale in Canada and Chengjiang in China.

Geography and climate

The supercontinent Rodinia had broken up; Gondwana dominated the south. Sea levels rose, flooding continents with shallow seas. The climate was generally warm with no permanent polar ice.
