The Macedonia naming dispute was a political dispute over the use of the name "Macedonia" between Greece and the Republic of Macedonia (now North Macedonia) that lasted from 1991 until its resolution with the Prespa agreement in 2018. The dispute arose after the Yugoslav republic of Macedonia declared independence in 1991 under the name "Republic of Macedonia". Greece objected that the name implied territorial claims on the Greek region of Macedonia, appropriated Greek history and symbols (notably Alexander the Great and the Vergina Sun), and could fuel irredentism.

Background and interim phase

Under a 1995 Interim Accord, the country removed the Vergina Sun from its flag and was admitted to the United Nations in 1993 under the provisional reference "the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia". Greece lifted a trade embargo it had imposed in 1994, and agreed not to block the country's membership in international organisations under the provisional name. Nevertheless, at the 2008 NATO summit in Bucharest, Greece effectively vetoed an invitation for the country to join NATO pending a settlement — a move the International Court of Justice later (2011) found breached the Interim Accord, though it ordered no remedy.

The Prespa agreement

After years of UN-mediated talks under envoy Matthew Nimetz, prime ministers Zoran Zaev and Alexis Tsipras reached agreement on 12 June 2018 at Lake Prespa. The country would rename itself the Republic of North Macedonia (erga omnes — for all uses), amend its constitution, and accept clarifications on language and identity, while Greece would ratify its NATO accession and unblock EU talks. A referendum in September 2018 produced an overwhelming "yes" among voters but turnout below the 50% threshold; parliament nonetheless passed the constitutional amendments in January 2019, and the Greek parliament ratified the agreement the same month. The name change took effect on 12 February 2019, North Macedonia joined NATO in March 2020, and the agreement was widely hailed as a model of diplomacy — Tsipras and Zaev were nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize — though it remained politically contentious in both countries.
