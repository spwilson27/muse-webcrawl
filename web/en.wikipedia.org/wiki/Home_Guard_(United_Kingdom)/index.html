Home Guard (United Kingdom)

The Home Guard (originally the Local Defence Volunteers, LDV) was an armed citizen militia raised in the United Kingdom during the Second World War as a secondary line of defence against German invasion. Between 1940 and its stand-down in 1944, some 1.5 million men served, mostly those too old, too young, or in reserved occupations for regular military service.

Formation

On 14 May 1940, Secretary of State for War Anthony Eden broadcast an appeal for volunteers between 17 and 65 to enrol as Local Defence Volunteers; a quarter of a million came forward within a day. The force was renamed the Home Guard in July 1940 at the suggestion of Winston Churchill. Initially short of uniforms and weapons — armed in some cases with shotguns, sporting rifles, and improvised weapons — it was progressively equipped with British and American-supplied arms, including P17 rifles and specialised weapons such as the Northover projector and sticky bomb.

Role

The Home Guard manned observation posts, guarded factories, airfields and coasts, operated anti-aircraft and coastal batteries in later years, and prepared to delay an invasion through roadblocks and demolitions. Members trained in the evenings while holding civilian jobs. The force was stood down on 3 December 1944 and formally disbanded in 1945. A new Home Guard was briefly re-raised during the Cold War (1951–1957).

In popular culture

The Home Guard is affectionately remembered through the BBC sitcom Dad's Army (1968–1977), whose Walmington-on-Sea platoon reflected the age and spirit of the original volunteers.
