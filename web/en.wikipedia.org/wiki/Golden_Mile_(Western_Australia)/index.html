Golden Mile (Western Australia)

LocationKalgoorlie-Boulder, Western Australia
CommodityGold
Discovered1893
CentrepieceSuper Pit gold mine

The Golden Mile is a gold-mining district at Kalgoorlie-Boulder in Western Australia, long described as the richest square mile of gold-bearing ground in the world. Following the discovery of gold by Paddy Hannan and his companions in 1893, prospectors and mining companies found that the area's gold occurred not only in quartz reefs but in unusual telluride ores within a dense cluster of lodes along a strip of land roughly a mile long, from which the district took its name.

History

In the 1890s boom, dozens of mines — including the Great Boulder, Lake View, Ivanhoe and Perseverance — operated side by side along the Mile, drawing investment from London and producing enormous wealth amid bitter disputes over the telluride ores, which were at first discarded as worthless. Through the twentieth century the individual underground mines were progressively consolidated, principally under Kalgoorlie Consolidated Gold Mines. In the late 1980s entrepreneur Alan Bond assembled the leases that allowed the underground workings to be merged into a single open pit.

The Super Pit

The result, the Super Pit (formally the Fimiston Open Pit), is one of Australia's largest open-cut gold mines, roughly 3.5 kilometres long, 1.5 kilometres wide and more than 600 metres deep, visible from space and a major tourist attraction with a public lookout. It produces on the order of 800,000 ounces of gold in a good year and has been operated since 2019 by Northern Star Resources. The Golden Mile remains the heart of Australia's gold industry more than 130 years after its discovery.
