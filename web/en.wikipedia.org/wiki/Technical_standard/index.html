Technical standard

A technical standard is an established norm or requirement for a repeatable technical task, expressed in a formal document that lays down uniform engineering or technical criteria, methods, processes, and practices. Standards may govern dimensions, tolerances, materials, safety requirements, test methods, terminology, or interoperability between products and systems. They are typically developed by consensus within standards organisations and are usually voluntary, though governments may incorporate them into regulation.

Interchangeable standard parts, such as screw threads, are a classic result of technical standardisation.

Standards organisations

  • International: the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC), and the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) develop global standards; ISO alone has published tens of thousands of standards, including management standards such as ISO 9001.
  • Regional: bodies such as CEN/CENELEC in Europe produce harmonised standards supporting the European single market.
  • National: examples include ANSI (United States), BSI (United Kingdom), DIN (Germany), and JIS (Japan).
  • Industry and consortia: organisations such as the IEEE, ASTM International, IETF (for Internet standards), and W3C (for web standards) produce standards outside the formal national system.

Types of standards

Standards may be de jure (formal, published by recognised bodies), de facto (widely adopted by market practice), or open standards developed through public, transparent processes. They range from fundamental metrology and terminology standards to product specifications, codes of practice, and test methods.

Purposes and effects

Standardisation enables interchangeability and mass production (a drive famously advanced by interchangeable-parts manufacturing in the 19th century), assures minimum safety and quality, reduces transaction costs and trade barriers, and allows components from different suppliers to work together — as with shipping containers, USB connectors, and Internet protocols. Standards can also entrench incumbent technologies and, when encumbered by patents, raise competition concerns addressed through FRAND licensing commitments for standard-essential patents.
