Howards End

AuthorE. M. Forster
Published1910
GenreNovel

Howards End is a novel by E. M. Forster, published in 1910, about social relations, class and the condition of England in the Edwardian era. Its epigraph, "Only connect…", expresses the novel's plea to bridge the divisions between inner and outer life and between social classes.

Plot

The story interweaves three families: the cultured, idealistic Schlegel sisters, Margaret and Helen; the wealthy, pragmatic Wilcoxes, whose country house, Howards End, becomes the novel's symbolic centre; and Leonard Bast, a poor clerk whose life is damaged by his contact with both. After Margaret marries Henry Wilcox, conflicts over the house and its intended inheritance from the first Mrs Wilcox reach a tragic climax.

Themes and reception

The novel explores the clash between materialism and humanism, the precariousness of the lower middle class, and the spiritual significance of place. It is frequently ranked among the greatest English novels of the 20th century.

Adaptations

The Merchant Ivory film of 1992, starring Emma Thompson and Anthony Hopkins, won three Academy Awards, and a BBC/Starz television adaptation followed in 2017.
