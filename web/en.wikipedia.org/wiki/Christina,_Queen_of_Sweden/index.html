Christina, Queen of Sweden

Christina (1626–1689) was Queen of Sweden from 1632 until her abdication in 1654. Daughter of Gustavus Adolphus, she succeeded at age six, ruled in her own right from 1644, became celebrated as a patron of learning — sometimes called the "Minerva of the North" — then abdicated, converted to Catholicism, and moved to Rome.

Christina of Sweden
Reign: 1632–1654
Born: 1626, Stockholm
Died: 1689, Rome
Father: Gustavus Adolphus

Reign

Regents under Axel Oxenstierna governed during her minority. Christina ended Swedish participation in the Thirty Years' War (Peace of Westphalia, 1648), invited René Descartes to her court (he died in Stockholm in 1650), and lavishly patronized scholars and artists, straining finances.

Abdication and Rome

Refusing marriage, she abdicated in favour of her cousin Charles X Gustav, secretly converted to Catholicism, and settled in Rome, where she remained a notable cultural figure until her death.
