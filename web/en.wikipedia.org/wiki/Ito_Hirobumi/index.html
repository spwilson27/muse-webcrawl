Itō Hirobumi

Born16 October 1841, Chōshū Domain, Japan
Died26 October 1909, Harbin, Manchuria (assassinated)
Office1st Prime Minister of Japan (four terms)

Prince Itō Hirobumi (1841–1909) was a Japanese statesman who served as the first Prime Minister of Japan (1885–1888, and three further terms) and was the principal architect of the Meiji Constitution of 1889. A samurai of Chōshū, he was one of the genrō oligarchs who built modern Japan.

Early life

Born to a low-ranking samurai family, Itō studied under Yoshida Shōin, joined the sonnō jōi movement, and in 1863 secretly travelled to London with Inoue Kaoru, returning convinced Japan must modernise. He served on the Iwakura Mission (1871–73) and rose through the Meiji government in finance and home affairs.

Constitution and premiership

After studying constitutional systems in Berlin and Vienna under Rudolf Gneist and Lorenz von Stein, Itō drafted a constitution modelled partly on Prussia's, creating a strong emperor-centred state with a Diet. As first prime minister under the new cabinet system (1885), he steered the constitution's promulgation in 1889 and the opening of the Imperial Diet in 1890. He later founded the Seiyūkai party (1900).

Korea and assassination

After Japan's victory over Russia, Itō became first Resident-General of Korea (1905), forcing the protectorate treaty. He resigned in 1909 and was assassinated at Harbin railway station on 26 October 1909 by Korean independence activist An Jung-geun — an act that hastened Japan's annexation of Korea in 1910.
