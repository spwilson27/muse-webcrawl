Racquetball is a racquet sport and ball game played by two (singles) or four players (doubles) on a four-walled court with a short racquet and a hollow rubber ball. It was invented in 1949 in Greenwich, Connecticut, by tennis and handball player Joseph Sobek, who combined elements of squash and handball and designed the first strung paddle-racquet and ball.

Gameplay

Players alternate hitting the ball to the front wall; the ball may strike the side walls, floor and ceiling on its way but must reach the front wall before bouncing twice on the floor. A rally is won when the opponent fails to make a legal return. Matches are generally best of three games, to 15 points (11 in the deciding game in some formats), with points scored only by the server in traditional scoring or on every rally under rally scoring.

Popularity and organisation

The sport boomed in the United States in the 1970s and 1980s, with millions of recreational players at YMCAs and purpose-built clubs, and professional tours for men and women. The International Racquetball Federation, founded in 1979, organises world championships, and racquetball is a regular event at the Pan American Games. It is similar to, but distinct from, British racketball (now called squash 57) and American handball.
