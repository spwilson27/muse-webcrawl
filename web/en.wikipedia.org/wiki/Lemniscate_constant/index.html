Lemniscate constant

The lemniscate constant is a transcendental mathematical constant, usually denoted G or ϖ (varpi), that arises in connection with the lemniscate of Bernoulli and the elliptic integrals associated with it. It is the analogue of the constant π for the lemniscate curve, in much the same way that π measures the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter.

Definition

The lemniscate constant can be defined as the ratio of the period of the lemniscate sine function to its amplitude, or equivalently through the complete elliptic-like integral ∫₀¹ dt/√(1 − t⁴) multiplied by a factor of 2, giving ϖ = 2.6220575542921198104648395…

History

The constant was studied by Carl Friedrich Gauss, who investigated the arithmetic-geometric mean and its relation to lemniscatic arcs in the late eighteenth century. Gauss computed the constant to many decimal places and recognised its deep connection with elliptic functions and the theory of complex multiplication.

Properties

The lemniscate constant is known to be transcendental, a fact that follows from the work of Theodor Schneider on values of elliptic functions. It also appears in Viète-like infinite products and in the evaluation of the Gamma function at one quarter: ϖ is related to Γ(1/4) by a closed formula involving π.
