The PalmPilot is a line of personal digital assistants (PDAs) produced by Palm Computing, a division of U.S. Robotics and later 3Com. The original models, the Pilot 1000 and Pilot 5000, launched in 1996; the PalmPilot Personal and PalmPilot Professional followed in March 1997 and gave the line its best-known name.

Features

PalmPilots ran Palm OS and centred on four core applications — calendar, address book, to-do list, and memo pad — synchronised with a desktop computer via a cradle and HotSync software. Text was entered with a stylus using Graffiti, a simplified single-stroke character-recognition alphabet. The Professional model added TCP/IP networking and an email client.

History

The devices were designed under Jeff Hawkins, who had earlier developed the GRiDPad and the unsuccessful Zoomer. Hawkins famously carried a block of wood the size of the intended device to test its pocketability. The PalmPilot line was a major commercial success, creating the handheld PDA market category that Palm's successors — the Palm III, V, and later Treo smartphones — continued through the 2000s. After a trademark dispute with the Pilot Pen Corporation, later devices dropped "Pilot" from their names.
