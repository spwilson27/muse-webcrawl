Cyprian

Born: c. 210 AD, North Africa
Died: 14 September 258 AD, Carthage
Feast day: 16 September
Known for: Bishop of Carthage; Church Father; martyr

Cyprian (Latin: Thascius Caecilius Cyprianus; c. 210 – 14 September 258) was a bishop of Carthage and an early Christian writer of Berber descent, many of whose Latin works are extant. He is recognised as a saint in the Catholic Church, Orthodox Church, and Anglican Communion. He was born around the beginning of the 3rd century in North Africa, perhaps at Carthage, where he received a classical education.

Life

Cyprian was a wealthy pagan rhetorician and teacher who converted to Christianity around 246 and gave much of his fortune to the poor. His rapid rise — he became Bishop of Carthage in 249 — caused some resentment among clergy who had longer service. His episcopate was dominated by crisis: the Decian persecution (250) forced him into hiding, from which he governed by letter, and left the problem of the lapsi, Christians who had lapsed under persecution and later sought readmission.

Controversies

Cyprian steered a middle course on the lapsi between laxity and the rigorism of Novatian, requiring penance before reconciliation. He later clashed with Pope Stephen I over whether baptism administered by heretics was valid — Cyprian held it was not and required rebaptism — a dispute left unresolved at his death but formative for sacramental theology. His treatise On the Unity of the Church contains the famous maxim "outside the Church there is no salvation" (extra ecclesiam nulla salus) and the declaration that one "cannot have God for his Father who has not the Church for his mother".

Martyrdom and writings

Under the Valerian persecution, Cyprian was exiled in 257 and, refusing to sacrifice to the Roman gods, was beheaded at Carthage on 14 September 258. His writings include treatises on unity, prayer (a commentary on the Lord's Prayer), patience, and mortality — the last written during a plague — and a large correspondence of 81 letters that is a major source for the third-century Church. A near-contemporary Life by his deacon Pontius is among the earliest Christian biographies.
