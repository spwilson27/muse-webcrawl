Dodecanese
Country: Greece
Region: South Aegean
Capital: Rhodes
Largest islands: Rhodes, Kos, Kalymnos, Leros, Karpathos

The Dodecanese (Greek: Δωδεκάνησα, 'twelve islands') are a group of Greek islands in the southeastern Aegean Sea, off the coast of Turkey. Despite the name, the group comprises about 15 large islands and more than 150 smaller ones, of which 26 are inhabited.

Geography

Rhodes is the largest island and historical capital; other major islands include Kos, Patmos, Symi, and Kastellorizo, the easternmost Greek island.

History

Inhabited since prehistory, the islands were ruled successively by Greek city-states, Rome, Byzantium, the Knights Hospitaller, and the Ottoman Empire. Italy seized them in the Italo-Turkish War of 1912 and held them as the Italian Aegean Islands until 1947, when the Treaty of Paris ceded them to Greece.

Culture and tourism

The islands are known for medieval castles, ancient sites such as the Asklepieion of Kos, sponge diving traditions on Kalymnos, and major tourism on Rhodes and Kos.
