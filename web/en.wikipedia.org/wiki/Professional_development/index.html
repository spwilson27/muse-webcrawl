Professional development, also known as professional education, is learning to earn or maintain professional credentials such as academic degrees to formal coursework, attending conferences, and informal learning opportunities situated in practice. It has been described as intensive and collaborative, ideally incorporating an evaluative stage. There are a variety of approaches to professional development that include consultation, coaching, communities of practice, lesson study, mentoring, reflective supervision and technical assistance.

Participants

A wide variety of people, such as teachers, military officers and non-commissioned officers, health care professionals, lawyers, accountants and engineers engage in professional development. Many professional bodies require members to complete a set amount of continuing professional development (CPD) each year to maintain licensure or membership, measured in hours or credits.

Approaches

Professional development ranges from single workshops to semester-long academic courses, and may be delivered face-to-face, online, or in blended formats. Research on teacher professional development emphasises sustained duration, active learning, coherence with teachers' existing practice, and collective participation. Reflective practice, as described by Donald Schön, is a recurring theme. See also Continuing education and Mentorship.
