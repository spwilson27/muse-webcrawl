Dunkery Beacon

Dunkery Beacon, at the summit of Dunkery Hill in Exmoor National Park, Somerset, England, is the highest point in Somerset at 519 metres (1,703 ft) above sea level, and the highest point in southern England outside Dartmoor. The summit is marked by a large Bronze Age cairn and a toposcope, and has long served as a beacon site for signalling.

Geography

Dunkery Hill is a long sandstone ridge covered in heather moorland, part of the Exmoor SSSI. On a clear day the summit commands views over the Bristol Channel to Wales, across Exmoor, and as far as Dartmoor and the Quantock Hills. The hill is owned by the National Trust as part of its Holnicote Estate.

History

The large cairn at the summit dates to the Bronze Age. As its name suggests, the hilltop was used as a beacon — part of a chain of warning fires, including in the era of the Spanish Armada. In 2016 a "Dunkery Beacon" lighting marked the Queen's 90th birthday celebrations.
