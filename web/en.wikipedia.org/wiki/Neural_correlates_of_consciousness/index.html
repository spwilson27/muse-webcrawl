Neural correlates of consciousness

The neural correlates of consciousness (NCC) are the minimal neural mechanisms jointly sufficient for any one specific conscious percept. The term was developed by Francis Crick and Christof Koch, who argued that identifying the neuronal basis of consciousness is a tractable scientific programme even while the philosophical "hard problem" remains debated.

Approaches

Researchers contrast conscious and unconscious processing of identical stimuli using binocular rivalry, masking and attentional blink paradigms, and study states such as sleep, anaesthesia and disorders of consciousness. Findings implicate recurrent processing in sensory cortex, frontoparietal networks, and the thalamocortical system.

Theories

Major accounts include global workspace theory (Bernard Baars, Stanislas Dehaene), which holds that information becomes conscious when broadcast widely across cortex; integrated information theory (Giulio Tononi), which associates consciousness with a system's integrated information (Φ); and higher-order and predictive-processing theories.
