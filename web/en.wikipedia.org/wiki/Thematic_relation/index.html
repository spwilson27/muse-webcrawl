Thematic relation

In linguistics, thematic relations (also called theta roles, semantic roles, or in their original formulation cases) are labels for the roles that the arguments of a predicate play in the situation the predicate describes. Typical thematic relations include the agent (the doer of an action), the patient (the entity changed by an action), the theme (the entity moved or located), the experiencer, the goal, the source, the instrument and the beneficiary. Thematic relations mediate between the semantics of a verb and the syntactic positions its arguments occupy, and they are a central tool in theories of argument structure and linking.

History

The modern notion grew out of Charles Fillmore's case grammar (1968), which proposed that the deep structure of a sentence consisted of a verb plus a set of "deep cases" such as Agentive, Instrumental, Dative, Locative and Objective. Jeffrey Gruber's work on prelexical structure and Ray Jackendoff's Semantic Interpretation in Generative Grammar (1972) developed "thematic relations" as part of the internal structure of lexical entries. In government and binding theory these were recast as theta roles governed by the Theta Criterion, which requires each argument to bear one and only one theta role, and each theta role to be assigned to one and only one argument.

Common roles

Agent — the initiator or doer of the action (Maria broke the vase). Patient — the entity undergoing a change of state (the vase in the same sentence). Theme — the entity whose location or possession is described (the book in "She put the book on the shelf"). Experiencer — the sentient being who perceives or feels (John in "John fears dogs"). Stimulus — the object of perception or emotion. Goal, Source and Location — endpoints and settings of motion or transfer. Instrument — the means used (with a hammer). Beneficiary/Recipient — the entity for whom or to whom something is done or given.

Problems and approaches

There is no universally agreed inventory of roles, and theorists debate whether thematic relations are discrete primitives, clusters of entailments (as in David Dowty's proto-agent and proto-patient proposal of 1991), or descriptions of positions in structured event representations, as in decompositional approaches where roles are read off configurations like [x ACT], [x GO TO y] or [x CAUSE [BECOME]]. Despite this, role labels remain indispensable shorthand in syntax, computational linguistics (for example in PropBank and FrameNet annotation) and language teaching.
