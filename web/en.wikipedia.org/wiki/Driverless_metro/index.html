Driverless metro

A driverless metro is a metro (rapid transit) system whose trains operate automatically without a driver on board. Such systems use automatic train operation (ATO) combined with automatic train protection (ATP) and automatic train supervision (ATS). In the classification of the International Association of Public Transport (UITP), the highest level, Grade of Automation 4 (GoA4), describes trains capable of fully unattended operation, including door closing, obstacle detection, and handling of emergency situations.

History

The first fully automated metro line in regular passenger service is generally considered to be the Port Liner in Kobe, Japan, opened in 1981, followed by the SkyTrain in Vancouver (1985) and the Lille Metro in France (1983), the first fully automated large-profile metro. The SkyTrain network in Vancouver remains one of the longest fully automated systems in the world.

Notable systems

  • Copenhagen Metro — fully driverless since its opening in 2002, with trains running at short headways around the clock.
  • Dubai Metro — its Red and Green lines form one of the longest fully automated networks, opened from 2009.
  • Singapore MRT — several lines, including the North East Line (2003), Circle Line, and Downtown Line, are fully driverless.
  • Paris Métro — Line 1 was converted to driverless operation, completed in 2012; Line 14 was driverless from its opening in 1998.
  • Riyadh Metro — opened in 2024–2025 as one of the largest driverless networks built at once.
  • Sydney Metro — Australia's first fully automated metro, opened in 2019.

Advantages and disadvantages

Automation permits very short headways — some systems run trains every 75–90 seconds — increasing capacity without new tunnels. It also improves punctuality and energy efficiency and allows service hours to be extended, since staffing a driver's cab is not required. Against this, the capital cost of signalling, platform screen doors, and control systems is high; retrofitting existing lines is difficult; and some passengers and unions raise concerns about the absence of staff on board during emergencies.

Safety

Driverless lines are usually built with platform screen doors, intrusion detection, and extensive CCTV and intercom coverage. Their accident records are generally comparable to or better than conventionally driven metros, since most metro accidents historically involve human error.
