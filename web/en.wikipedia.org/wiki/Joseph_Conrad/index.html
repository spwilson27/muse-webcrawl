Joseph Conrad (born Józef Teodor Konrad Korzeniowski; 1857–1924) was a Polish-British novelist, considered one of the greatest writers in the English language — a language he did not learn fluently until his twenties. His experience as a merchant seaman in the French and British merchant navies supplied the settings for much of his fiction.

Works

Heart of Darkness (1899), drawn from his voyage to the Congo, is his most famous work. Other major novels include Lord Jim (1900), Nostromo (1904), The Secret Agent (1907), and Under Western Eyes (1911). His prose, with its sceptical view of human nature and the civilizing mission of empire, anticipated modernism and influenced generations of writers.
