Ruth Gordon Jones (October 30, 1896 – August 28, 1985) was an American actress, playwright, and screenwriter. In a career spanning seven decades, she achieved particular fame late in life for her film roles as the eccentric Minnie Castevet in Rosemary's Baby (1968), for which she won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress, and as Maude in the cult classic Harold and Maude (1971).

Early life and stage career

Gordon was born in Quincy, Massachusetts, and enrolled at the American Academy of Dramatic Arts in New York despite her father's skepticism. She made her Broadway debut in 1915 in Peter Pan and became an established stage actress in the 1920s and 1930s, appearing in productions including The First Man, Seventeen, and Maxwell Anderson's Saturday's Children. She was also acclaimed for her performances in Ethan Frome (1936) and in Thornton Wilder's The Matchmaker (1955), in which she created the role of Dolly Levi.

Writing

With her second husband, Garson Kanin, Gordon co-wrote the screenplays for the Katharine Hepburn and Spencer Tracy films Adam's Rib (1949) and Pat and Mike (1952), both of which received Academy Award nominations. The couple also wrote The Rat Race and collaborated on several other projects. Gordon additionally wrote plays including Over 21 (1944), drawn from her wartime experiences, and later published three volumes of memoirs.

Late film career

After early screen appearances in the 1940s, including portraying Mary Todd Lincoln in Abe Lincoln in Illinois (1940), Gordon returned to films in the 1960s with roles in Inside Daisy Clover (1965) and Rosemary's Baby (1968). Her performance as Harold's free-spirited 79-year-old love interest in Harold and Maude (1971) became one of her most beloved roles. She later appeared in Every Which Way but Loose (1978) and its sequel, and won an Emmy Award for a guest appearance on the sitcom Taxi.

Death and legacy

Gordon died of a stroke on August 28, 1985, at her summer home in Edgartown, Massachusetts, at the age of 88. A theatre at the Westport Country Playhouse is named in her honor, and her Academy Award acceptance remark — "I can't tell you how encouraging a thing like this is" — remains one of the ceremony's most quoted lines.
