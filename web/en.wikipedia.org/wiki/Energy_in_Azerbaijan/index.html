Energy in Azerbaijan

Energy in Azerbaijan describes the energy and electricity production, consumption, export and import in Azerbaijan, a country whose economy has been shaped for more than a century by its petroleum wealth. Azerbaijan is one of the world's oldest oil-producing regions: commercial drilling around Baku began in the 1840s–1870s, and at the start of the twentieth century the Absheron Peninsula supplied roughly half of the world's oil.

Oil

Most of Azerbaijan's crude oil comes from offshore fields in the Caspian Sea, above all the Azeri–Chirag–Gunashli complex, developed by a consortium led by BP under a 1994 production-sharing agreement often called the "Contract of the Century". Crude is exported chiefly through the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline to the Turkish Mediterranean port of Ceyhan, with additional volumes moving via the Baku–Supsa pipeline to Georgia and by rail.

Natural gas

The Shah Deniz gas field, one of the largest gas discoveries of its era, anchors Azerbaijan's gas sector. Shah Deniz Stage 2 supplies the Southern Gas Corridor — the South Caucasus Pipeline, the Trans-Anatolian Pipeline (TANAP) across Turkey, and the Trans Adriatic Pipeline (TAP) into Greece, Albania and Italy — which began delivering gas to Europe at the end of 2020.

Electricity

Electricity generation is dominated by natural-gas-fired thermal plants, with hydropower contributing a modest share and solar and wind still small but growing. The state utility Azerenerji operates the grid, and Azerbaijan intermittently exports electricity to neighbours including Georgia, Turkey, Russia and Iran.

Renewables

The government has set targets to raise the renewable share of installed capacity, citing strong wind potential in the Caspian and solar resources in the Nakhchivan exclave and the liberated territories of Karabakh, where "green energy" zones have been declared.
