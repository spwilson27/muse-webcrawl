Galois group

In mathematics, in the area of abstract algebra known as Galois theory, originally developed by Évariste Galois, a Galois group is the group of field automorphisms of a field extension that fix the base field. Given a field extension E/F that is normal and separable — a Galois extension — its Galois group Gal(E/F) consists of all automorphisms of E that fix every element of F.

Examples

The Galois group of ℚ(√2)/ℚ has order 2: the nontrivial automorphism sends √2 to −√2. The Galois group of the splitting field of x³ − 2 over ℚ is isomorphic to the symmetric group S₃ of order 6. Cyclotomic extensions ℚ(ζn)/ℚ have Galois group isomorphic to the multiplicative group (ℤ/nℤ)×.

The fundamental theorem

The fundamental theorem of Galois theory establishes an inclusion-reversing bijection between intermediate fields of a Galois extension E/F and subgroups of Gal(E/F):

H ↦ EH = { x ∈ E : σ(x) = x for all σ ∈ H },    K ↦ Gal(E/K).

Normal subgroups correspond to intermediate fields that are themselves Galois over F. The theorem converts questions about fields and polynomial roots into questions about finite groups.

Solvability by radicals

Galois's celebrated application: a polynomial is solvable by radicals if and only if its Galois group is a solvable group. Since S₅ is not solvable, there is no general formula by radicals for quintic equations — the group-theoretic explanation behind the Abel–Ruffini theorem. The absolute Galois group of ℚ, the Galois group of the algebraic closure of ℚ over ℚ, is a profinite group central to modern number theory and the Langlands programme.
