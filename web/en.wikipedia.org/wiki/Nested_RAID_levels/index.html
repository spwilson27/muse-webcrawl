Nested RAID levels

Nested RAID levels, also known as hybrid RAID, combine two or more of the standard RAID levels to gain performance, redundancy, or both. They are typically implemented by using multiple hardware or software RAID controllers, where one level is built on top of arrays created at another level.

Common combinations

Well-known nested levels include RAID 10 (1+0), a stripe of mirrors; RAID 01 (0+1), a mirror of stripes; RAID 50 (5+0), striping across RAID 5 arrays; and RAID 60 (6+0), striping across RAID 6 arrays. RAID 10 is the most widely deployed because it offers fast rebuilds and good random I/O performance.

Notation

The two numbers in a nested level's name are sometimes read in different orders by different vendors, so "RAID 10" and "RAID 1+0" are used to distinguish the stripe-of-mirrors arrangement from a mirror of stripes.
