Em dash

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The em dash (—)

The em dash is the long horizontal dash used in writing, typically represented as "—". Its name derives from its traditional width of one em, a unit equal to the point size of the typeface (for example, 12 points in 12-point type). The em dash is distinguished from the shorter en dash (–) and from the hyphen (-).

Usage

Em dashes are used in several ways in English prose:

  • Interruptions and parenthetical statements: An em dash or pair of em dashes can set off an explanatory or parenthetical element, often with a sharper break than commas or parentheses.
  • Abrupt change in thought: A single em dash can mark a sudden break or interruption in dialogue or narration.
  • Attribution: In quotations, an em dash (or two-em dash) may precede the name of the person quoted.
  • Redaction: Dashes are sometimes used to censor or omit parts of words.

Spacing and style

Style guides differ on whether em dashes should be surrounded by spaces. The Chicago Manual of Style and most American publishers set em dashes closed (without spaces), while many British and newspaper styles use spaced en dashes for the same function. In informal digital writing, two hyphens (--) are often typed as a substitute, and many word processors automatically convert them to an em dash.

Related marks

  • En dash – roughly half the width of the em dash, used for ranges.
  • Hyphen – used in compound words and line breaks.
  • Dash – the general class of horizontal punctuation marks.
