The Isshi Incident (乙巳の変, Isshi no Hen) was a successful coup d'état carried out at the Japanese imperial court in 645. Led by Prince Naka no Ōe (later Emperor Tenji) and Nakatomi no Kamatari (later Fujiwara no Kamatari), the plot culminated in the assassination of Soga no Iruka before Empress Kōgyoku, destroying the dominance of the main line of the Soga clan.

Background

By the early seventh century the Soga clan dominated the court through marriage ties to the imperial family and control of the treasury. Soga no Emishi and his son Iruka were widely regarded as wielding power exceeding that of the sovereign; Iruka had ordered the destruction of Prince Yamashiro no Ōe and his family in 643, removing a rival claimant to the throne.

The assassination

On 10 July 645, during a court ceremony at which a memorial from the Korean kingdoms was to be read, Naka no Ōe and his allies closed the palace gates and struck down Iruka in the presence of the Empress. Wounded, Iruka begged for an investigation; the Empress withdrew, and Iruka was killed. His father Emishi burned his own mansion and died in the fire the following day, along with many of the Soga clan records.

Aftermath

Empress Kōgyoku abdicated in favour of her brother, who reigned as Emperor Kōtoku, with Naka no Ōe as crown prince. The new regime proclaimed the Taika Reforms in 646, a sweeping programme to centralise government on the Chinese Tang model, including public ownership of land and a new administrative code. The incident takes its name from Isshi (乙巳), the sexagenary designation of the year 645.
