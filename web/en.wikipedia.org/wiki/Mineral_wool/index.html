Mineral wool

Mineral wool is any fibrous material formed by spinning or drawing molten mineral or rock materials such as slag, basalt and other stone. Products include stone wool (rock wool), slag wool and, often grouped with them, glass wool. Mineral wool is used principally for thermal insulation, soundproofing and fire protection in buildings and industry, and as a growing medium in hydroponics.

Manufacture

Stone wool is made by melting basaltic rock and recycled slag at around 1,500 °C and spinning the melt into fine fibres, which are bound with a resin binder and formed into batts, rolls, boards or pipe sections. The resulting material consists mostly of trapped air, giving it a low thermal conductivity.

Properties and uses

Mineral wool is non-combustible, with a melting point above 1,000 °C, and provides both thermal and acoustic insulation. It is used in walls, roofs, floors, ships, offshore platforms and industrial plant, and for passive fire protection. In horticulture, stone wool cubes and slabs are a common hydroponic substrate for tomatoes, peppers and other crops.

Health considerations

Like other man-made vitreous fibres, mineral wool can irritate the skin, eyes and respiratory tract during installation, so protective equipment is recommended. Modern products use biosoluble fibres that dissolve in lung fluid, and international agencies classify current insulation wools as not classifiable as to carcinogenicity.
