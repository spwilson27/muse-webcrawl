The Direct Action Network (DAN) was a North American confederation of anti-corporate and anti-authoritarian affinity groups, collectives, and organizations, best known for coordinating the mass protests that disrupted the World Trade Organization Ministerial Conference in Seattle on 30 November 1999 (N30).

The Seattle protests

DAN was formed in Seattle in 1999 by activists from groups including Art & Revolution and the Ruckus Society. Using a decentralized "spokescouncil" structure in which affinity groups sent rotating spokespeople, DAN organized blockades of the intersections around the WTO meeting venue, preventing delegates from entering on the opening day. The protests involved tens of thousands of participants, helped catalyse the broader anti-globalization movement, and influenced later mobilizations in Washington, D.C. (A16, 2000) and elsewhere; local DAN chapters persisted for several years afterwards.

Principles and legacy

DAN operated on principles of non-hierarchy, consensus-based decision-making, and a pledge of nonviolence toward people (while accepting property damage by autonomous groups). Its spokescouncil model and emphasis on affinity groups were widely adopted by subsequent movements, and retrospectives credit it with helping to inspire the structure of later networked protest movements.
