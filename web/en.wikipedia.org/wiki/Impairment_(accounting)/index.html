Impairment (accounting)

Impairment reduces the carrying amount of an asset to its recoverable amount

In accounting, an impairment is a permanent reduction in the value of a company asset. It may relate to a fixed asset (capital asset), an inventory asset, or an intangible asset. The logic behind recording an impairment is that the current market value or the future cash flows expected from the asset are less than the carrying amount (the amount recorded in the accounts), and this shortfall is not expected to be temporary.

Recognition and measurement

Under International Financial Reporting Standards (IAS 36), an entity must assess at each reporting date whether there is any indication that an asset may be impaired. If so, the entity estimates the asset's recoverable amount — the higher of its fair value less costs of disposal and its value in use — and, if the carrying amount exceeds that recoverable amount, recognises an impairment loss in profit or loss, reducing the carrying amount accordingly. Goodwill and intangible assets with indefinite useful lives must be tested for impairment at least annually regardless of indicators.

Reversal

Under IFRS, an impairment loss on assets other than goodwill may be reversed if the estimates used to determine the recoverable amount have changed, though the carrying amount may not exceed what it would have been had no impairment been recognised. Impairment of goodwill can never be reversed. US GAAP similarly prohibits reversals of impairment for held-and-used assets.
