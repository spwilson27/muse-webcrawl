The Primeira Liga (officially known as Liga Portugal Betclic for sponsorship reasons) is the top level of the Portuguese football league system. Contested by 18 clubs, it operates on a system of promotion and relegation with the Liga Portugal 2. Founded in 1934 as an experimental league before becoming the official national championship in 1938, the Primeira Liga is ranked among Europe's top leagues by UEFA coefficients and is famous for developing and exporting world-class talent.

The "Big Three"

Portuguese football is dominated by the Três Grandes ("Big Three"): Benfica, FC Porto, and Sporting CP. Together they have won virtually every league title — only Belenenses (1945–46) and Boavista (2000–01) have ever broken the monopoly — and they have never been relegated. Benfica leads with 38 championships, followed by Porto with 30 and Sporting with 21 (figures as of the mid-2020s). The fiercest rivalries are the Lisbon derby (Benfica–Sporting) and O Clássico (Benfica–Porto). Porto achieved European glory by winning the European Cup/Champions League in 1987 and 2004 and the UEFA Cup/Europa League in 2003 and 2011.

Format

Each club plays the others twice (home and away) for 34 matches. The bottom two teams are relegated, while the 16th-placed team enters a play-off with the third-placed team of the second tier. The champion and runner-up qualify for the UEFA Champions League league phase or qualifying rounds depending on Portugal's coefficient, with further places in the Europa League and Conference League, and the Portuguese Cup (Taça de Portugal) winner also qualifying for Europe.

Notable players

The league has showcased stars from Eusébio (Benfica's all-time great of the 1960s) to modern exports such as Cristiano Ronaldo (Sporting), Bernardo Silva, Rúben Dias, João Félix, and Bruno Fernandes, with Portuguese clubs renowned for profitable player trading.
