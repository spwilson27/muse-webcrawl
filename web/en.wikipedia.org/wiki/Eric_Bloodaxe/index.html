Eric Bloodaxe

Reign: King of Norway c. 932–934; King of Northumbria 947–948, 952–954
Died: 954, Stainmore, England
Spouse: Gunnhild, Mother of Kings

Eric Haraldsson (Old Norse: Eiríkr Haraldsson, Norwegian: Eirik Haraldsson; c. 885–954), nicknamed Bloodaxe (Old Norse: blóðøx), was a 10th-century Norwegian ruler. He was the second king of Norway (c. 932–934) and twice king of Northumbria (947–948 and 952–954). He was a favourite son of Harald Fairhair, and according to later saga tradition earned his nickname by murdering several of his brothers.

Life

Eric succeeded his father as king of Norway but quickly lost support to his younger half-brother Haakon the Good, who arrived from England with backing around 934. Eric withdrew to Britain, where English sources record him as king in Viking York (Jórvík). Expelled and restored once, he was finally driven out in 954 and killed at Stainmore, in what contemporaries viewed as the end of the independent Viking kingdom of York, after which Northumbria was absorbed by the English kingdom.

Sources and legacy

Eric's career is known from English chronicles and coinage as well as from Icelandic sagas and skaldic poetry, including the praise poem Eiríksmál said to have been composed by his widow Gunnhild. The reliability of the saga accounts, written centuries later, is debated, but his Northumbrian kingship is firmly attested by coins minted at York in his name.
