Mare Fecunditatis

Mare Fecunditatis, southeast of Mare Tranquillitatis

Mare Fecunditatis ("Sea of Fertility" or "Sea of Fruitfulness") is a lunar mare, a large dark basaltic plain on the Moon's near side. It lies to the southeast of Mare Tranquillitatis, to which it is joined by a narrow "strait" near the crater Torricelli. The mare is roughly 840 kilometres (520 miles) in its longest dimension.

Geology

The Fecunditatis basin was formed in the Pre-Nectarian epoch, while the basaltic lavas that fill it date chiefly from the early Imbrian period, later than the basalts of the neighbouring Tranquillitatis basin. The mare material overlaps ejecta from the younger Crisium basin to the northeast. Around its margins lie several notable craters, including Langrenus and Petavius to the southeast and the paired craters Messier and Messier A in its western part, famous for their bright twin rays.

Exploration

The Soviet Luna 16 probe landed in northeastern Mare Fecunditatis in September 1970 and returned about 101 grams of lunar soil to Earth — the first successful robotic sample-return mission to the Moon. Luna 20 landed in the nearby highlands in 1972 and Luna 23 in 1974. Because of its relatively smooth surface and proximity to the equator, the region has also featured in studies of possible future landing sites.
