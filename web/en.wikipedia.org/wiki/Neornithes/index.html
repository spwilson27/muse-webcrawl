Neornithes

Neornithes is the subclass comprising all modern birds, the crown group of Aves. The name means "new birds". Neornithes includes every living bird species — over 11,000 — as well as their extinct close relatives, and is defined as the clade consisting of the most recent common ancestor of all living birds and all of its descendants.

Classification

Neornithes divides into two superorders: Palaeognathae, the ratites (ostriches, rheas, cassowaries, emus, kiwis) together with the tinamous; and Neognathae, containing all other modern birds. Within Neognathae, the Galloanserae (gamebirds and waterfowl) branch off first, followed by the enormous radiation Neoaves.

Evolution

Fossil evidence, including the Late Cretaceous Vegavis iaai from Antarctica, indicates that the modern bird lineages originated before the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago, though the timing of their diversification remains debated between molecular-clock and fossil-based estimates. Neornithines differ from earlier avialans such as Enantiornithes in features including a beak lacking teeth (in most) and a shortened, fused tail ending in a pygostyle.
