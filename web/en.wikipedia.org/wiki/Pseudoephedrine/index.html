Pseudoephedrine

Pseudoephedrine
Class: Sympathomimetic; decongestant
Common brand: Sudafed
Formula: C10H15NO
Legal status: Restricted (precursor chemical)
Routes: Oral

Pseudoephedrine is a sympathomimetic drug of the phenethylamine and amphetamine chemical classes. It is widely used as a nasal and sinus decongestant, shrinking swollen nasal mucous membranes by constricting blood vessels. It occurs naturally as an alkaloid in plants of the genus Ephedra, and is a diastereomer of ephedrine — hence the prefix "pseudo".

Medical use

Pseudoephedrine is sold, alone and in combination products, for relief of nasal congestion from colds, allergies and sinusitis; a well-known brand is Sudafed. As a stimulant it can cause insomnia, nervousness and raised blood pressure, and it appears on the World Anti-Doping Agency's list of substances prohibited in competition above a threshold dose.

Regulation

Because pseudoephedrine can be readily converted into methamphetamine, it is regulated internationally as a drug precursor. In the United States, the Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act of 2005 moved pseudoephedrine products behind the pharmacy counter, with identification and purchase limits; many manufacturers reformulated over-the-counter products with phenylephrine instead — a substitute later judged ineffective at oral doses by an FDA advisory committee in 2023. Similar restrictions exist in many other countries, and in some it is prescription-only.
