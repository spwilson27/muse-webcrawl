Laplace operator

The Laplace operator, or Laplacian, is a differential operator given by the divergence of the gradient of a scalar function on Euclidean space. It is usually denoted by the symbols ∇·∇, ∇² or Δ, and is named after the French mathematician Pierre-Simon Laplace. The Laplacian of a function f at a point measures the extent to which the average value of f over small spheres around the point deviates from f at the point itself.

Δf = ∇²f = ∇·∇f = ∂²f/∂x² + ∂²f/∂y² + ∂²f/∂z²  (in three Cartesian dimensions)

Motivation and interpretation

If Δf is positive at a point, the function tends to be lower there than at surrounding points (a local "valley" in the average sense); if negative, the point is on average higher than its neighbours. This underlies its physical meaning as a measure of net flux: in diffusion, Δf times a diffusivity constant gives the rate of accumulation of the diffusing quantity, since material flows from regions where the function is above its local average towards regions where it is below.

Equations involving the Laplacian

  • Laplace's equation Δf = 0, whose solutions are the harmonic functions, central to potential theory, electrostatics and fluid flow.
  • Poisson's equation Δf = g, governing, for example, the gravitational and electrostatic potentials due to a given mass or charge distribution.
  • Heat equation ∂u/∂t = αΔu, describing conduction and diffusion.
  • Wave equation ∂²u/∂t² = c²Δu.
  • Schrödinger equation, in which the Laplacian supplies the kinetic-energy operator.

Coordinate expressions

The Laplacian has explicit forms in cylindrical and spherical polar coordinates, where it acquires additional first-derivative terms. In two dimensions it is Δf = ∂²f/∂x² + ∂²f/∂y². It can also be generalized: to Riemannian manifolds (the Laplace–Beltrami operator), to differential forms (the Hodge Laplacian), to graphs (the graph Laplacian, important in spectral graph theory and machine learning) and to discrete settings.

Spectral theory

The eigenvalue problem −Δf = λf on a bounded domain with boundary conditions underlies the theory of vibrating membranes, quantum mechanics in confined geometries and the modern field of spectral geometry, which studies how the eigenvalues ("spectrum") of the Laplacian reflect the shape and geometry of a domain.
