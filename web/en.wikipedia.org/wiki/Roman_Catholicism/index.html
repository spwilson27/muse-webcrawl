Roman Catholicism

Roman Catholicism is the system of beliefs, practices, and organisation of the Catholic Church, the world's largest Christian church, which understands itself to be in communion with the Bishop of Rome, the Pope. The term "Roman Catholic" came into popular use during and after the Protestant Reformation to distinguish the church in communion with Rome from other Christian bodies that also claim the title "catholic".

Beliefs

Roman Catholic teaching is based on the Nicene Creed, the sacraments — of which there are seven, including Baptism, the Eucharist, and Confirmation — and the doctrines of apostolic succession, papal primacy, and the communion of saints. Catholic doctrine holds that the Church is the original church founded by Jesus Christ and that its bishops are the successors of the apostles.

Organisation

The church is governed by the Pope and the College of Bishops and is organised into dioceses and particular churches, both the Latin Church and 23 Eastern Catholic Churches in full communion with Rome. With roughly 1.4 billion baptised members worldwide, it is the largest single religious denomination.
