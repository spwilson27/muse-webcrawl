Baltimore riot of 1861

The attack on the 6th Massachusetts on Pratt Street, 19 April 1861

The Baltimore riot of 1861 (also called the Pratt Street Riot or Pratt Street Massacre) occurred on 19 April 1861, a week after the start of the American Civil War, when a pro-Confederate mob attacked Union troops of the 6th Massachusetts Militia as they transited through Baltimore, Maryland on their way to defend Washington, D.C.

Events

Because Baltimore's rail lines were not directly connected, troops had to march between President Street Station and Camden Station along Pratt Street. Confederate sympathisers blocked the route, tore up track, and assaulted the soldiers with bricks, paving stones and pistols; shots were fired on both sides. Four soldiers and twelve civilians were killed — the soldiers among the first Union combat deaths of the war — and many more were wounded. Maryland's divided loyalties and Baltimore's strategic position led to the federal occupation of the city in May 1861, the arrest of secessionist officials, and the suspension of habeas corpus in the case of Ex parte Merryman. The riot inspired James Ryder Randall's poem "Maryland, My Maryland", long the state song.
