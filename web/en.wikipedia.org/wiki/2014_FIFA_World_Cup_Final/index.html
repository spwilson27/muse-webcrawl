2014 FIFA World Cup Final

The 2014 FIFA World Cup Final was the final match of the 2014 FIFA World Cup, the 20th edition of FIFA's competition for national football teams. The match was played at the Maracanã Stadium in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, on 13 July 2014, and was contested by Germany and Argentina. Germany won the match 1–0 after extra time, claiming their fourth World Cup title.

Background

The final was the third meeting between Germany and Argentina in a World Cup final, following 1986 (won by Argentina) and 1990 (won by West Germany) — a record pairing for the fixture. Germany had reached the final after a famous 7–1 semi-final victory over hosts Brazil in the Mineirazo, while Argentina advanced past the Netherlands on penalties after a goalless draw. Germany were managed by Joachim Löw and captained by Philipp Lahm; Argentina, led by Alejandro Sabella, relied heavily on captain Lionel Messi, who was named the tournament's best player and awarded the Golden Ball.

Match

Argentina had the clearer chances in normal time: Gonzalo Higuaín put a one-on-one wide and had a goal disallowed for offside, while Messi dragged a second-half shot past the post. Germany's Benedikt Höwedes headed against the post just before half-time. With the match goalless after 90 minutes, it went to extra time. In the 113th minute, substitute André Schürrle crossed from the left and fellow substitute Mario Götze controlled the ball on his chest before volleying left-footed past Sergio Romero for the only goal of the game. Germany saw out the remaining minutes to win 1–0.

Aftermath

Germany became the first European team to win a World Cup held in the Americas. Goalkeeper Manuel Neuer won the Golden Glove, and Götze became the first substitute to score a World Cup-winning goal. The victory completed a project of rebuilding German football that had begun after Euro 2000, and the team were received by hundreds of thousands of fans in Berlin. Argentina's defeat was their third loss in a World Cup final.
