George I (George Louis; 28 May 1660 – 11 June 1727) was King of Great Britain and Ireland from 1 August 1714 and ruler of the Electorate of Hanover within the Holy Roman Empire from 23 January 1698 until his death in 1727. He was the first British monarch of the House of Hanover.

Early life and succession

Born in Hanover, George was the eldest son of Ernest Augustus, Elector of Hanover, and Sophia of Hanover, a granddaughter of James VI and I. Under the Act of Settlement 1701, which excluded Catholics from the succession, Sophia was heir presumptive to Queen Anne; she died weeks before Anne, so George succeeded on Anne's death in 1714. He arrived in England speaking little English and governed with the help of ministers, surviving an early Jacobite rising in 1715.

Reign

George's reign saw the growing dominance of the Whigs, the South Sea Bubble crisis of 1720, and the rise of Robert Walpole, often regarded as Britain's first prime minister, whose influence grew partly because of the king's limited English and long absences in Hanover. The Septennial Act 1716 extended the maximum life of Parliament to seven years. George died of a stroke while visiting Hanover and was buried there; he remains the most recent British monarch to be buried outside the United Kingdom. He was succeeded by his son, George II.
