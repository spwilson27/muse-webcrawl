Silver nitrate

Silver nitrate is an inorganic compound with chemical formula AgNO3. It is a versatile precursor to many other silver compounds, such as those used in photography. It is far less sensitive to light than the halides. It was once called lunar caustic because silver was called luna by ancient alchemists, who associated silver with the moon. In solid silver nitrate, the silver ions are three-coordinated in a trigonal planar arrangement.

Synthesis

Silver nitrate can be prepared by reacting silver, such as silver bullion or silver foil, with nitric acid, resulting in silver nitrate, water, and oxides of nitrogen. The reaction may be carried out with concentrated or dilute acid, with heating used to drive it in the latter case.

Reactions

A typical reaction with silver nitrate is to suspend a rod of copper in a solution of silver nitrate and leave it for a few hours. The silver nitrate reacts with copper to form hairlike crystals of silver metal and a blue solution of copper nitrate. Silver nitrate also precipitates silver halides on treatment with halide salts, a reaction once central to film photography. In analytical chemistry, silver nitrate is used in the Volhard method and in Mohr's method for the determination of chloride ions.

Applications

Medicine

Silver nitrate is used to cauterize infected tissues around a skin wound, to remove granulation tissue, and in the form of applicator sticks ("caustic pencils") to treat warts and other skin lesions. Dilute solutions were historically instilled into newborns' eyes to prevent gonococcal ophthalmia neonatorum, though this practice has largely been replaced by antibiotic ointments. Silver nitrate has antiseptic properties, and silver compounds were widely used before the advent of antibiotics.

Photography and other uses

Silver nitrate is the starting material for preparing silver halides used in photographic emulsions. It is also used in mirror manufacturing (the Tollens reaction deposits metallic silver), in indelible inks and hair dyes, in the manufacture of other silver compounds, and as a stain in histology and in scanning electron microscopy.

Safety

Silver nitrate is a strong oxidizer and is corrosive. Contact with skin leaves black stains of reduced metallic silver and can cause burns; ingestion is toxic. Long-term exposure to silver compounds can cause argyria, a permanent bluish-gray discoloration of the skin.
