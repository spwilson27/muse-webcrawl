Old Firm

LocationGlasgow, Scotland
TeamsCeltic and Rangers
First meeting28 May 1888 (friendly)
StadiumsCeltic Park, Ibrox Stadium

The Old Firm is the collective name for the Scottish football clubs Celtic and Rangers, who are both based in Glasgow. The two clubs are the most successful and popular in Scotland and their rivalry — the Old Firm derby — is one of the most famous in world football.

History

Celtic and Rangers first played on 28 May 1888, Celtic winning 5–2 in a friendly. The term "Old Firm" is commonly said to derive from commentators describing the clubs as "old, firm friends", though it may also refer to the commercial benefits of their meetings. Together the clubs have dominated Scottish football for over a century, winning the overwhelming majority of league championships, Scottish Cups and League Cups.

Rivalry and culture

The rivalry has been fuelled by religious and political divisions: Celtic are historically associated with Glasgow's Irish Catholic immigrant community, Rangers with the Protestant, unionist community. Sectarianism linked to the fixture has been the subject of legislation, including the Offensive Behaviour at Football and Threatening Communications (Scotland) Act 2012, later repealed. The clubs meet at least four times per season in the Scottish Premiership, plus cup ties.

Head-to-head

The fixture has been played more than 440 times in major competitions, with the win totals of the two clubs historically very close. Rangers' liquidation in 2012 and subsequent re-entry at the bottom tier of Scottish league football interrupted the league fixture until 2016.
