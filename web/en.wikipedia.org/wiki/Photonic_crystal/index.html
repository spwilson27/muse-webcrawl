Photonic crystal

A photonic crystal is an optical nanostructure in which the refractive index changes periodically. This periodicity affects the motion of photons in much the same way that the periodic potential in a semiconductor crystal affects electrons, giving rise to photonic band gaps — ranges of wavelength that cannot propagate through the structure.

ConceptsPhotonic band gap; Bloch waves
Key figuresEli Yablonovitch; Sajeev John (1987)
Examples in natureOpals; butterfly wing scales; peacock feathers

History

The concept was developed independently in 1987 by Eli Yablonovitch and Sajeev John. Natural photonic crystals produce the structural colour of opals and many animals; artificial ones are fabricated by drilling, etching, or self-assembly at scales comparable to the wavelength of light.

Applications

Photonic crystals are used in low-loss optical waveguides and fibres, high-efficiency LEDs and lasers, sensors, and optical computing components, and form the basis of photonic-crystal fibres with unusual guiding properties.
