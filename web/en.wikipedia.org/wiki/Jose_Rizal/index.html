Jose Rizal


José Protacio Rizal Mercado y Alonso Realonda
Born: 19 June 1861, Calamba, Laguna, Captaincy General of the Philippines
Died: 30 December 1896 (aged 35), Bagumbayan, Manila
Occupation: Writer, ophthalmologist, polymath
Known for: Noli Me Tángere, El filibusterismo

José Protacio Rizal Mercado y Alonso Realonda (19 June 1861 – 30 December 1896) was a Filipino nationalist, writer and polymath active at the end of the Spanish colonial period of the Philippines. He is considered the national hero (pambansang bayani) of the Philippines.

An ophthalmologist by profession, Rizal became a writer and a key member of the Filipino Propaganda Movement, which advocateded political reforms for the colony under Spain. His novels Noli Me Tángere (1887) and El filibusterismo (1891) exposed the abuses of Spanish colonial rule and the friars, and are now considered classics of Filipino literature.

Life

Rizal was born in Calamba, Laguna, to a prosperous family. He studied at the Ateneo Municipal de Manila and the University of Santo Tomas, then in Madrid, Paris and Heidelberg, qualifying as an ophthalmologist. Fluent in many languages, he wrote poetry, essays and novels, sculpted, painted and corresponded with scholars across Europe.

Execution and legacy

After returning to the Philippines he founded La Liga Filipina. He was exiled to Dapitan, then arrested during the outbreak of the Philippine Revolution, tried for rebellion, sedition and conspiracy, and executed by firing squad at Bagumbayan field in Manila on 30 December 1896. His death helped galvanise the revolution. Rizal Park in Manila is named in his honour, and 30 December is a public holiday in the Philippines.
