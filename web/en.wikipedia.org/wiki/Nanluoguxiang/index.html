Nanluoguxiang

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nanluoguxiang (Chinese: 南锣鼓巷; pinyin: Nánluógǔxiàng, "South Gong and Drum Lane") is a well-preserved historic neighbourhood and a famous hutong (alley) in Dongcheng District, Beijing, China. It runs about 786 metres from Gulou Dongdajie in the north to Di'anmen Dongdajie in the south.

History

The lane dates to the Yuan dynasty, when the layout of Dadu (Beijing) was established around 1267; it appears in Ming and Qing records as Luogu Xiang, split into north and south sections in the Qing dynasty. Eight parallel hutongs branch off on each side in a pattern likened to a centipede or fishbone, and many of the courtyard houses (siheyuan) once belonged to officials, artists, and notables. The Qing empress dowager's birthplace is traditionally identified in the lane, and figures such as the painter Qi Baishi and the writer Mao Dun had residences in its side alleys.

Preservation and tourism

Listed among Beijing's 25 municipal historical and cultural protection areas, Nanluoguxiang largely retained its traditional fabric while much of old Beijing was redeveloped. From the mid-2000s, and especially around the 2008 Beijing Olympics, the lane was renovated and filled with cafés, bars, souvenir shops, and boutique hotels, becoming one of the city's most popular tourist streets. Its commercialization has also drawn criticism, and in 2016 the lane's managers voluntarily requested its removal from China's national AAAA tourist attraction rating to help reduce visitor numbers and improve quality.

Transport

The Nanluoguxiang station of Beijing Subway, serving Lines 6 and 8, opened in 2012 at the southern end of the lane and takes its name from it.
