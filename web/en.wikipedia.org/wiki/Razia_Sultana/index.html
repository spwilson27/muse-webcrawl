Razia Sultana, the only woman to rule the Delhi Sultanate.

Razia Sultana (c. 1205 – 15 October 1240), often styled Raziya Sultan, was the ruler of the Delhi Sultanate from 1236 to 1240. The daughter of Sultan Iltutmish, she was the first and only woman to ascend the throne of Delhi, and one of the few female sovereigns in the history of the medieval Islamic world. She issued coins in her own name bearing the title "pillar of women, queen of the times, Sultan Razia, daughter of Shamsuddin Iltutmish".

Accession

Judging his sons incompetent, Iltutmish nominated Razia as his heir — an unprecedented step that the court initially resisted. On his death in 1236 the nobles instead installed her half-brother Ruknuddin Firuz, whose misrule and his mother Shah Turkan's cruelty provoked a popular revolt in Delhi. Razia appealed directly to the public at Friday prayers, was proclaimed sultan with popular support, and Ruknuddin and Shah Turkan were executed.

Reign

Razia proved an able administrator: she restored order, patronized learning, founded schools, and personally led military campaigns. Contemporary chronicler Minhaj-i Siraj praised her as "sagacious, just, beneficent... endowed with all the admirable attributes and qualifications necessary for a king" — while adding that she was, in his view, disqualified by her sex. She discarded the veil, adopted male attire and a turban, rode out in public, and hunted, scandalizing conservative nobles. Opposition crystallized among the Turkish slave-officers known as the Chihilgani ("the Forty"), who resented her promotion of non-Turks, particularly the Abyssinian officer Jamaluddin Yaqut.

Downfall and death

In 1240 the governor of Bathinda, Malik Altunia, rebelled; Yaqut was killed and Razia imprisoned. The nobles placed her brother Muizuddin Bahram on the throne. Razia married Altunia and marched on Delhi to recover her kingdom, but their forces were defeated near Kaithal, and both were killed on 15 October 1240. Her modest tomb, the Tomb of Razia Begum, stands in Bulbuli Khana near Turkman Gate in Old Delhi.
