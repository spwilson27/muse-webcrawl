Tobacco smoking is the practice of burning tobacco and inhaling the resulting smoke. The smoke may be inhaled, as is done with cigarettes, or simply released from the mouth, as is generally done with pipes and cigars. The practice is believed to have begun as early as 5000–3000 BC in Mesoamerica and South America. Tobacco was introduced to Eurasia in the late 17th century by European colonists and spread along Western trade routes.

History

Tobacco was used ceremonially and medicinally by Indigenous peoples of the Americas long before European contact. After Columbus's voyages, smoking spread through Europe in the 16th and 17th centuries, initially as a supposed medicine, then as a social custom. The invention of mechanized cigarette manufacture (notably James Bonsack's machine of 1880) and mass marketing in the early 20th century made smoking ubiquitous; consumption peaked in many Western countries in the mid-20th century.

Health effects

Smoking is the leading preventable cause of death worldwide, killing more than 8 million people each year according to the World Health Organization, including over a million non-smokers exposed to second-hand smoke. Tobacco smoke contains thousands of chemicals, dozens of them known carcinogens. Smoking causes lung cancer, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), heart disease, stroke, and many other cancers, and harms nearly every organ of the body. Nicotine is highly addictive. The causal link to lung cancer was established by epidemiological studies in the 1950s (notably by Richard Doll and Austin Bradford Hill) and affirmed by the 1964 US Surgeon General's report.

Prevalence and regulation

Smoking rates have declined in most high-income countries since the 1960s, in response to taxation, advertising bans, smoke-free laws, and health warnings, coordinated internationally by the WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (2003). Roughly 1.25 billion adults still use tobacco, with the burden increasingly concentrated in low- and middle-income countries. Cessation aids include nicotine replacement therapy, medications such as varenicline, and behavioural support; quitting at any age yields substantial health benefits.
