The Air Force Cross is the second-highest military decoration that can be awarded to a member of the United States Air Force or United States Space Force, ranking just below the Medal of Honor. It is awarded for extraordinary heroism in combat against an armed enemy force that does not quite justify the Medal of Honor. It is the Air Force's equivalent of the Army's Distinguished Service Cross and the Navy and Marine Corps' Navy Cross.

History

Before the Air Force became a separate service in 1947, airmen received the Army's Distinguished Service Cross. The Air Force Cross was established by Congress in 1960 and first awarded for actions in the Vietnam War era. The medal, designed by Eleanor Cox and Thomas Hudson Jones of the Institute of Heraldry, is a bronze cross with an eagle superimposed, suspended from a ribbon of Brittany blue with red and white edge stripes.

Recipients

Fewer than 200 awards have been made. The majority were for the Vietnam War, including awards to pararescuemen and pilots recognised for rescue and combat missions under fire. Notable recipients include pararescueman William H. Pitsenbarger (whose award was later upgraded to the Medal of Honor), and airmen honoured for actions in Laos, at Kham Duc, and in later conflicts in Iraq and Afghanistan. The first Space Force recipient era followed the creation of that service in 2019, with eligibility extended accordingly.

Criteria

The cross may be awarded to any person serving in any capacity with the Air Force or Space Force who distinguishes themselves by extraordinary heroism involving one of the following: action against an enemy of the United States; military operations involving conflict with an opposing foreign force; or serving with friendly foreign forces engaged in armed conflict against an opposing armed force in which the United States is not a belligerent party.
