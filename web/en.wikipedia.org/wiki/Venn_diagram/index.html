Venn diagram

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Two overlapping circles showing a shaded intersection region]

A Venn diagram is a diagram that shows all possible logical relations between a finite collection of different sets. Two or three overlapping circles (or other closed curves) divide the plane into regions, one for each combination of the sets; regions may be shaded to highlight intersections, unions, or complements.

The diagrams were introduced by John Venn in a paper dated 1880 and published in 1881, and popularised in his Symbolic Logic (1881). Venn himself called them 'Eulerian circles', refining Leonhard Euler's earlier diagrams, which show only actual relations rather than all possible ones.

Properties and extensions

With circles, at most three sets can be fully represented (8 regions); four or more sets require ellipses or other curves, and elegant constructions are a small research topic. Venn diagrams remain standard teaching tools in set theory, probability (events as regions), and logic (testing syllogisms).

In popular usage the two-circle Venn has become a general icon of overlapping categories, often drawn loosely without regard to region completeness.
