Kaesong (Korean: 개성; pronounced [kɛsʌŋ]) is a special city in the southern part of North Korea, near the border with South Korea and the Korean Demilitarized Zone. It was the capital of the Goryeo dynasty (918–1392), and its historic monuments are inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. The city is also known for the Kaesong Industrial Region, a joint inter-Korean industrial park operated with South Korean companies until 2016.

History

Kaesong rose to prominence as the home of Wang Geon, who founded the Goryeo dynasty in 918 and made the city — then called Songdo — his capital. For nearly five centuries it was the political and commercial heart of Korea, a thriving centre of trade famed for its ginseng (the prized "Korea ginseng" of Kaesong), ceramics, and printing. Because the Joseon dynasty that followed moved the capital to Hanyang (Seoul), and because later rulers restricted redevelopment of the old city, an unusual amount of Goryeo-era fabric survived.

After the division of Korea in 1945, Kaesong lay just south of the 38th parallel and was administered by the South; during the Korean War it changed hands repeatedly and, under the 1953 armistice, ended up in North Korea, very close to the DMZ. The "Historic Monuments and Sites in Kaesong" — including the Manwoldae palace site, the Kaesong Namdae gate, Koryo Songgyungwan (the dynasty's highest educational institution), the Sungyang Sowon Confucian academy, and the tombs of Kings Kongmin and Wang Geon — were made a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013.

Kaesong Industrial Region

Following the inter-Korean summit of 2000, South Korea's Hyundai Asan and the Korea Land Corporation developed an industrial park just outside the city, where South Korean firms employed tens of thousands of North Korean workers — at its peak around 54,000 — in light manufacturing such as textiles, shoes, and watches. The complex became the foremost symbol of inter-Korean economic cooperation. Operations were suspended several times amid political tensions, and in February 2016, after a North Korean nuclear test and rocket launch, South Korea closed the park entirely; North Korea expelled the South Korean managers and seized assets. In 2020 North Korea demolished the inter-Korean liaison office in the city. Kaesong also featured in inter-Korean tourism, with South Korean tours to the old city running briefly in 2007–2008.

Geography and economy

Kaesong sits on the Ryesong River plain amid low hills, about 70 kilometres northwest of Seoul. Apart from the industrial region, its economy rests on agriculture, light industry, and its famous ginseng cultivation, which dates back centuries and remains a prized export product of North Korea.
