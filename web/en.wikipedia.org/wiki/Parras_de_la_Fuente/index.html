Parras de la Fuente

Parras de la Fuente is a city in the south of the Mexican state of Coahuila. Founded in 1598, it is one of Mexico's oldest wine-producing centres and a designated Pueblo Mágico. Its name, meaning "vines of the fountain", reflects the springs and vineyards that sustain an oasis in the surrounding desert.

Wine

Parras is home to Casa Madero, established in 1597 and regarded as the oldest winery in the Americas. Vines thrive in the valley's high-altitude desert climate, irrigated by abundant spring water. Wine and brandy production remain central to the local economy alongside pecans and textiles — the city is also known for denim manufacturing.

History

The settlement was founded by Captain Antón Martín de Zapata and Jesuit missionaries among the Lagunero peoples. Francisco I. Madero, leader of the Mexican Revolution and president of Mexico, was born in Parras in 1873 into the wine-making Madero family.
