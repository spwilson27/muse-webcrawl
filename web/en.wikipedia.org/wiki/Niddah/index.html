Niddah

Niddah (Hebrew: נִדָּה, "separation") is a category of Jewish law (halakha) concerning menstruation and related states of ritual impurity (tumah), derived principally from the Book of Leviticus. A woman in the state of niddah is separated from marital physical relations until the prescribed period ends and she immerses in a ritual bath, the mikveh.

Biblical and rabbinic sources

Leviticus 15 prescribes seven days of impurity for menstruation. Rabbinic law, elaborated in the Mishnah and Talmud tractate Niddah, combined this with rules about irregular bleeding (zavah), so that in common Orthodox practice a minimum of five days of menstruation is followed by seven "clean days" with examinations, after which immersion takes place.

Practice

The laws of family purity (taharat hamishpacha) govern physical contact between spouses during the niddah period and are centrally observed in Orthodox Judaism; Conservative and Reform movements vary in teaching and practice. Related rules apply after childbirth and to certain other discharges.
