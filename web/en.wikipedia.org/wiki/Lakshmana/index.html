Lakshmana

Lakshmana (Sanskrit: लक्ष्मण, Lakṣmaṇa) is a principal character of the Hindu epic Ramayana. He is the younger brother and devoted companion of Rama, the prince of Ayodhya, and is regarded as an incarnation of the serpent god Shesha. Lakshmana is revered as an embodiment of loyalty, selflessness, and service.

Life in the epic

Lakshmana was born to King Dasharatha and Queen Sumitra, and is the twin brother of Shatrughna. He married Urmila, the younger sister of Rama's wife Sita. When Rama was exiled to the forest for fourteen years, Lakshmana insisted on accompanying him and Sita, guarding them throughout their exile and famously remaining sleepless in his vigilance.

Role in the war

During the search for the abducted Sita, Lakshmana fought alongside Rama and the vanara army against the rakshasa king Ravana of Lanka. He killed Ravana's son Meghanada (Indrajit) after a fierce battle, and in many versions was gravely wounded and revived by the sanjeevani herb brought by Hanuman.

Worship and retellings

Lakshmana is worshipped together with Rama and Sita in temples across India and Southeast Asia, and he appears in the many regional retellings of the Ramayana, as well as in Thai, Cambodian, and Indonesian traditions.
