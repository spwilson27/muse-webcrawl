Civil disorder

Civil disorder, also known as civil disturbance, civil unrest, or social unrest, is a general term used by law enforcement to describe situations in which public order breaks down, typically involving groups of people engaging in protests, rioting, strikes, vandalism, or other disruptive behaviour that may overwhelm normal police capacity. It is distinguished from civil disobedience, which is characteristically nonviolent and deliberately symbolic.

Causes

Civil disorder commonly arises from grievances such as economic inequality, racial or ethnic tensions, unpopular government policies, disputed elections, or the perceived illegitimacy of state actions. It may be spontaneous — triggered by a single incident, such as a police shooting — or organised. Crowd psychologists emphasise that behaviour in such events is shaped by group identity and intergroup dynamics rather than simple irrational contagion.

Response

Responses by authorities range from dialogue and negotiated management of protest to riot police deployments, curfews, mass arrests, and in extreme cases military intervention or declarations of emergency. Legal regimes differ: many jurisdictions have specific offences of riot, violent disorder, and affray, and some permit restrictions on assembly under defined conditions.
