Grigory Potemkin

Prince Grigory Aleksandrovich Potemkin-Tauricheski (11 October 1739 – 16 October 1791) was a Russian statesman, field marshal and the favourite of Catherine the Great. As governor-general of "New Russia" he directed the annexation of Crimea (1783), founded cities including Kherson, Nikolayev, Yekaterinoslav (now Dnipro) and Sevastopol, built the Black Sea Fleet, and prosecuted the war of 1787–92 against the Ottoman Empire. The phrase "Potemkin village" — a sham façade built to deceive — entered the language from hostile accounts of Catherine's 1787 Crimean journey, though historians regard the story of cardboard villages as largely a myth.

Rise

A guards officer who helped in the coup of 1762, Potemkin caught Catherine's eye in the Turkish war of 1768–74 and became her lover in 1774 and — many historians believe — her secret husband. Even after the romance cooled (he effectively chose her later favourites), he remained her indispensable partner in power for seventeen years, in a political intimacy she described in thousands of letters.

New Russia and Crimea

Potemkin governed the vast southern steppe won from the Ottomans and the Crimean Khanate. He abolished the Zaporozhian Cossack Host (1775), planted colonies of settlers, founded shipyards, and in 1783 proclaimed the annexation of Crimea — celebrated on Catherine's progress of 1787, when she travelled to meet Emperor Joseph II and view her new fleet at Sevastopol. In the renewed war he took Ochakov (1788) and directed Alexander Suvorov's storming of Izmail (1790). He died of fever in 1791 while negotiating the peace of Jassy.

Legacy

The battleship Potemkin, named for him, became famous through its 1905 mutiny and Eisenstein's film. His remains were removed from Kherson cathedral during the 2022 invasion of Ukraine — a measure of how alive his Black Sea legacy remains.
