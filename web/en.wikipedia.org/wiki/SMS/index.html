Short Message Service, commonly abbreviated as SMS, is a text messaging service component of most telephone, Internet and mobile device systems. It uses standardised communication protocols that let mobile phones exchange short text messages, typically 160 characters in length when using the GSM 7-bit default alphabet. Although the term SMS technically refers to one type of service, it is commonly used for text messaging generally.

History

The SMS concept was developed in the Franco-German GSM cooperation in 1984 by Friedhelm Hillebrand and Bernard Ghillebaert, who proposed carrying short messages on the signalling pathways of the GSM network. The first SMS message — "Merry Christmas", typed on a computer — was sent by engineer Neil Papworth to Richard Jarvis of Vodafone on 3 December 1992. Commercial services launched in the mid-1990s, and texting grew explosively in the late 1990s and 2000s, reaching trillions of messages per year globally.

Technical details

Messages are carried as control-channel signalling rather than voice traffic and are stored and forwarded by a Short Message Service Centre (SMSC). A single segment carries 140 bytes; encoding in UCS-2 for non-Latin scripts reduces the limit to 70 characters, and longer texts are split into concatenated segments. SMS is widely used for notifications, marketing and two-factor authentication codes, though in many markets person-to-person texting has declined in favour of internet-based messengers such as WhatsApp and iMessage, and RCS is its designated successor standard.
