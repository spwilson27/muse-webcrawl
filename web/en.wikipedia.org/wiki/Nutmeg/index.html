Nutmeg

Nutmeg is the seed of the evergreen tree Myristica fragrans, native to the Banda Islands of Indonesia, used ground as a spice. The same fruit yields a second spice, mace, from the bright red lacy covering (aril) of the seed. Nutmeg was for centuries among the most valuable commodities in world trade.


Nutmeg fruit: the seed (nutmeg) wrapped in its red aril (mace).

History

Nutmeg reached Europe through Arab and Venetian traders and commanded enormous prices. The Portuguese seized the Banda trade after 1512, but the Dutch East India Company (VOC) conquered the islands in 1621, massacring or expelling much of the Bandanese population to enforce a monopoly. The tiny island of Run, held by the English, was exchanged for New Amsterdam (Manhattan) under the 1667 Treaty of Breda. The British later transplanted nutmeg to Grenada in the Caribbean, now a major producer known as the "Isle of Spice."

Production and uses

The tree takes about twenty years to reach full bearing. The spice is used in baking, in dishes such as béchamel sauce and potato preparations, in drinks like eggnog, and in cuisines from India (garam masala) to the Netherlands. Nutmeg essential oil and nutmeg butter are used in food and cosmetics. In large doses the seed is toxic and psychoactive because of compounds such as myristicin, and it has a history of dangerous recreational misuse.
