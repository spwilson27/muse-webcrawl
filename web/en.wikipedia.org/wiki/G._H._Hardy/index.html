Godfrey Harold Hardy (7 February 1877 – 1 December 1947) was an English mathematician known for his achievements in number theory and mathematical analysis. In biology he is known for the Hardy–Weinberg principle, a basic principle of population genetics. To the general public he is best known as the mentor of the Indian mathematician Srinivasa Ramanujan, whose genius he recognised at once, and for his 1940 essay A Mathematician's Apology, a celebrated defence of the pursuit of pure mathematics for its own sake.

Life

Hardy was educated at Winchester College and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he was elected a fellow in 1900. From 1919 he held the Savilian Chair of Geometry at Oxford, returning to Cambridge in 1931 as Sadleirian Professor. With J. E. Littlewood he formed one of the most productive collaborations in mathematical history, producing around one hundred joint papers; their partnership prompted the jest that there were four great English mathematicians — "Hardy, Littlewood, Hardy–Littlewood, and Ramanujan".

Work

With Littlewood and Ramanujan, Hardy developed the circle method for problems in additive number theory such as Waring's problem and the partition function. He proved infinitely many zeros of the Riemann zeta function lie on the critical line, founded (with Littlewood) systematic Tauberian theory, and worked extensively on divergent series, inequalities, and Diophantine approximation. Independently of Wilhelm Weinberg, he derived in 1908 the Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium law of population genetics in a brief letter to Science, reportedly after a conversation about Mendelian inheritance at a cricket match discussion.

A Mathematician's Apology

Written when Hardy felt his creative powers fading, A Mathematician's Apology (1940) argues that pure mathematics is a creative art justified by beauty rather than utility, and famously declares "there is no permanent place in the world for ugly mathematics". C. P. Snow wrote the foreword to the 1967 edition. Hardy was a lifelong atheist, a passionate cricket enthusiast, and an eccentric who compiled lists of personal "resolutions" and disliked mirrors and photographs.
