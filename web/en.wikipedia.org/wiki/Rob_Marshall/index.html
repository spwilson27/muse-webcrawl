Robert Doyle Marshall Jr. (born October 17, 1960) is an American film and theater director, producer, and choreographer. He is best known for directing the film version of the Broadway musical Chicago (2002), which won the Academy Award for Best Picture and earned Marshall nominations for Best Director at the Academy Awards and Golden Globes, and won him the Directors Guild of America Award. His subsequent films include Memoirs of a Geisha (2005), Nine (2009), Pirates of the Caribbean: On Stranger Tides (2011), Into the Woods (2014), Mary Poppins Returns (2018), and the live-action The Little Mermaid (2023).

Early life and career

Marshall was born in Madison, Wisconsin, and raised in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, where his father was a professor and dean at the University of Pittsburgh. He graduated from Carnegie Mellon University and began his career in Pittsburgh theatre before moving to New York, where he performed in Broadway productions including Cats and worked as a choreographer. He gained acclaim co-choreographing and co-directing the 1998 Broadway revival of Cabaret with Sam Mendes, and choreographed the television film Annie (1999), which he also directed.

Film career

Chicago, his feature directorial debut, grossed over $300 million worldwide and won six Academy Awards. Marshall went on to become a frequent collaborator with Disney, directing the fourth Pirates of the Caribbean film—the first in the series to gross over $1 billion—Stephen Sondheim's Into the Woods, the sequel Mary Poppins Returns, and the 2023 remake of The Little Mermaid. With his partner John DeLuca, he runs the production company Lucamar Productions.
