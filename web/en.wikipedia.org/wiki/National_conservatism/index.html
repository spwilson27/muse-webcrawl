National conservatism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

National conservatism is a nationalist variant of conservatism that concentrates on upholding national and cultural identity, sovereignty and social order. National conservatives favour an assertive nation-state, controlled immigration, law and order policies, and the preservation of traditional institutions and values; on economics they are often more sceptical of globalisation and free trade than liberal conservatives, though positions vary.

Parties described as national conservative have governed or contended in several European states in the twenty-first century, and the tendency has gained an organised intellectual form, including National Conservatism conferences held since 2019. Its adherents distinguish it from ethnic or racial nationalism, defining the nation in cultural and civic terms; critics argue that in practice the boundary is unstable and that national conservatism shades into the radical right. The classification of particular parties is contested by scholars and by the parties themselves.

Ideas

Core themes include the nation as the indispensable unit of democratic self-government; scepticism toward supranational authority (notably the European Union) and toward international courts; cultural continuity against rapid social change; and a political economy protective of domestic industry and workers, sometimes called conservative protectionism. Philosophical influences cited by the movement include Edmund Burke, and more recently the Israeli-American theorist Yoram Hazony, whose The Virtue of Nationalism (2018) argues for an order of independent nations against liberal internationalism.
