Title II (Communications Act)

Title II of the Communications Act of 1934 is the portion of United States federal law governing common carriers in telecommunications. It authorizes the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) to regulate providers of telecommunications services as common carriers, requiring just and reasonable rates and forbidding unjust discrimination.

Provisions

Key sections include Section 201 (service and charges upon reasonable request), Section 202 (prohibition of unjust or unreasonable discrimination), and Section 222 (customer privacy). Title II carriers must provide service on reasonable request and may not make unjust differences in charges or facilities.

Net neutrality

Title II became central to the net neutrality debate when the FCC's 2015 Open Internet Order reclassified broadband Internet access as a telecommunications service under Title II, giving the agency authority to ban blocking, throttling, and paid prioritization. The 2017 Restoring Internet Freedom Order reversed that classification, and the question continued through litigation and subsequent rulemakings, including the FCC's 2024 vote to restore Title II classification and its later judicial setback.
