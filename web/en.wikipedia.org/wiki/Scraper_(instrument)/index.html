Scraper (instrument)

A scraper is a musical instrument that produces sound by scraping a stick, plectrum or similar object across a notched or ridged surface. Scrapers belong to the idiophone family in the Hornbostel–Sachs classification (class 112.2) and are among the oldest known types of instrument, with examples made of bone, wood, gourd and shell found in many cultures.

Examples

The güiro of Latin American music is a hollow gourd with parallel notches played with a stick. The washboard, used in skiffle, zydeco and jug bands, is a metal scraper played with thimbles. The reco-reco of Brazil, the Vietnamese sinh tien (coin clapper), and the notched "frog" instruments of East and Southeast Asia are other members of the family. Scraped instruments appear in ritual and dance music worldwide, including the Aztec omichicahuaztli, a notched human bone.
