Rhondda

Rhondda is a former coal mining area in South Wales, historically in the county of Glamorgan and now within the county borough of Rhondda Cynon Taf. It comprises two valleys, the larger Rhondda Fawr and the smaller Rhondda Fach, which converge at Porth. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries the Rhondda became one of the most famous coal-producing districts in the world.

History

Before industrialisation the Rhondda was a sparsely populated upland farming area. From the 1860s, exploitation of its high-quality steam coal drew a huge influx of workers; the population rose from a few thousand to over 160,000 by the 1920s. The valleys became known for their chapels, male voice choirs, miners' institutes and strong labour politics.

The Tonypandy riots of November 1910, during a miners' strike, saw troops deployed to the valley and became a lasting symbol of industrial conflict. Coal production declined through the 20th century, and the last deep mine, Maerdy Colliery, closed in 1990.

Culture

The Rhondda has a rich musical tradition, exemplified by choirs such as the Treorchy Male Voice Choir, and produced the songwriters and performers of the Welsh hymn-singing tradition. Rugby union has played a central part in valley life.
