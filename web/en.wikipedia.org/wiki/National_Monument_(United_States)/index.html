National Monument (United States)

In the United States, a national monument is a protected area that can be created from any land owned or controlled by the federal government by proclamation of the President of the United States under the Antiquities Act of 1906. National monuments protect a wide range of natural and historic resources, including objects of historic and scientific interest.

History

The Antiquities Act was signed by President Theodore Roosevelt, who proclaimed the first national monument, Devils Tower in Wyoming, in 1906. Unlike national parks, which are created by Congress, national monuments can be established quickly by the president without congressional approval, though their size and any later reductions have sometimes been politically controversial. Well-known monuments include Grand Staircase–Escalante and Bears Ears in Utah, both designated by Democratic presidents and subsequently reduced and restored by their successors.

Administration

National monuments are managed by one of several federal agencies, principally the National Park Service, the Bureau of Land Management, the U.S. Forest Service, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Many monuments, including the Grand Canyon's first federal protection, have later been redesignated as national parks by Congress.
