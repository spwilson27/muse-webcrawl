Elderly care

Elderly care, or eldercare, is the fulfilment of the special needs and requirements of senior citizens. It encompasses a broad range of services including assisted living, adult day care, long-term care, nursing homes, hospice care and home care. Because of the wide variety of needs and cultural perspectives on ageing, elderly care differs greatly between and within countries.

Forms of care

Home care allows older people to remain in their own homes with support ranging from domestic help and personal care to skilled nursing visits. Assisted living facilities provide housing, meals and help with activities of daily living while preserving a degree of independence. Nursing homes (skilled nursing facilities) provide 24-hour medical and personal care for people with significant health needs. Hospice care focuses on comfort and quality of life for people nearing the end of life. Adult day care offers daytime supervision, social activity and respite for family carers.

Activities of daily living

Eligibility for care is often assessed against activities of daily living (ADLs) — bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, transferring and continence — and instrumental activities such as cooking, cleaning, shopping and managing medication and finances.

Challenges

Population ageing is increasing demand for elderly care in most countries, raising questions about funding models, workforce shortages, carer burden and the quality and safety of institutional care. Policy debates commonly contrast institutional provision with ageing in place supported by community services and assistive technology.
