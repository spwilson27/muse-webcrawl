BRM

British Racing Motors (BRM) was a British Formula One motor racing team. Founded in 1945 by Raymond Mays and Peter Berthon, it was based at Bourne in Lincolnshire and raced as a constructor from 1951 to 1977, competing in 197 Grands Prix and winning seventeen. BRM won the Formula One World Constructors' Championship in 1962, the same year its driver Graham Hill won the Drivers' Championship.

History

The team began with the ambitious BRM Type 15, powered by a supercharged 1.5-litre V16 engine of great complexity and unreliability. Through the 1950s the team's fortunes improved, and Jo Bonnier gave BRM its first World Championship victory at the 1959 Dutch Grand Prix. In 1962, with the 1.5-litre V8 Type 56 and Graham Hill driving, BRM took both world titles.

BRM later built the H16-engined P83 and the V12-engined P153 and P160, scoring its final victories in the early 1970s, including Jean-Pierre Beltoise's celebrated win in the rain at Monaco in 1972. The team declined through the mid-1970s and withdrew after 1977.

Legacy

The BRM name became closely associated with the Owen Organisation and with British motor racing of the post-war era. The original cars remain prized historic racing machines, and the marque has been revived in limited form for continuation models.
