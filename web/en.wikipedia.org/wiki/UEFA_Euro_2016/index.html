The 2016 UEFA European Football Championship, commonly referred to as UEFA Euro 2016, was the 15th edition of the UEFA European Championship, the quadrennial international men's football championship of Europe. It was held in France from 10 June to 10 July 2016 and was the first European Championship contested by 24 teams, expanded from the 16-team format used since 1996.

Tournament summary

France, as hosts, were favourites and reached the final at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis. The group stage and knockouts produced several famous upsets: debutants Iceland defeated England in the round of 16 and reached the quarter-finals, while Wales, also debutants, reached the semi-finals. Iceland's "Viking clap" celebrations became an emblem of the tournament.

Final

In the final on 10 July 2016, Portugal defeated France 1–0 after extra time. Portugal captain Cristiano Ronaldo was substituted off injured in the first half, but substitute Éder scored the only goal in the 109th minute, giving Portugal their first major international trophy. Antoine Griezmann of France was named player of the tournament and won the Golden Boot with six goals.

Venues

Matches were played in ten stadiums across France, including the Stade de France (Saint-Denis), Stade Vélodrome (Marseille), Parc Olympique Lyonnais (Lyon), Stade Pierre-Mauroy (Lille) and Parc des Princes (Paris).
