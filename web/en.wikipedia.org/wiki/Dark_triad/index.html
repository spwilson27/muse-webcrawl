The dark triad is a psychological theory of personality that describes three aversive but non-pathological personality traits: narcissism, Machiavellianism, and psychopathy. The name derives from the perception of the traits as having dark or malevolent qualities — a callous, manipulative approach to other people combined with a grandiose sense of self-importance.

Research on the dark triad is used in applied psychology, especially in law enforcement, clinical psychology, and business management. People scoring high on these traits are statistically more likely to commit crimes, cause social distress, and create severe problems in an organisation, especially if they are in leadership positions.

Components

Narcissism is characterised by grandiosity, pride, egotism, and a lack of empathy. Machiavellianism is characterised by manipulation and exploitation of others, indifference to morality, unemotional callousness, and a higher level of self-interest. Psychopathy is characterised by continuous antisocial behaviour, impulsivity, selfishness, callous and unemotional traits, and remorselessness.

All three traits share characteristics such as a lack of empathy, interpersonal hostility, interpersonal offensiveness, and impulsivity, and are moderately to highly correlated with one another. Factor analysis carried out at the Glasgow Caledonian University found that among the Big Five personality traits, low agreeableness is the strongest correlate of the dark triad, while neuroticism and a lack of conscientiousness were associated with some of its members.

History

In 1998, McHoskey, Worzel, and Szyarto provoked a debate by claiming that narcissism, Machiavellianism, and psychopathy are more or less interchangeable in normal samples. Delroy Paulhus and Kevin Williams published research in 2002 arguing the traits were distinct enough to be investigated separately, while sharing a common core of disagreeableness.

Measurement

Researchers have sought to measure the three traits simultaneously, most commonly with the Short Dark Triad (SD3) questionnaire and the earlier 'Dirty Dozen' 12-item measure. Clinical instruments such as the Hare Psychopathy Checklist are used for psychopathy specifically.

Dark tetrad and light triad

An extension, the dark tetrad, adds everyday sadism. Conversely, researchers have proposed a light triad of benevolent traits: faith in humanity, humanism, and Kantianism.
