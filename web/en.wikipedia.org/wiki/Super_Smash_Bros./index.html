Super Smash Bros.

The series pits Nintendo characters — and later third-party guests — against each other in platform-fighting matches.

Super Smash Bros. is a series of crossover fighting video games published by Nintendo, featuring characters from the company's franchises alongside guests from third-party series. The gameplay differs from most fighting games: instead of depleting an opponent's health bar, players build up opponents' damage percentages to knock them off the edges of the stage.

The series was created by Masahiro Sakurai, who has directed every instalment. The first game, released for the Nintendo 64 in 1999, began as a prototype called Dragon King: The Fighting Game at HAL Laboratory before Nintendo characters were added. It was followed by Super Smash Bros. Melee (2001, GameCube), Brawl (2008, Wii), Super Smash Bros. for Nintendo 3DS and Wii U (2014), and Super Smash Bros. Ultimate (2018, Nintendo Switch).

Gameplay

Matches take place on stages drawn from Nintendo games. Fighters use normal and special moves tied to simple directional inputs, making the games accessible to newcomers while retaining depth in movement, spacing, and edge-guarding. Items such as the Poké Ball, Assist Trophies, and the Smash Ball add variability. In addition to versus modes, the games include single-player campaigns, minigames, and extensive collectible content celebrating Nintendo history.

Reception and legacy

The series is among Nintendo's best-selling franchises, with total sales of more than 70 million copies. Melee and Ultimate in particular sustained long-running competitive scenes. Ultimate launched with every playable character from the series' history and expanded its roster to 89 fighters through downloadable content, including characters from Minecraft, Final Fantasy, Persona, and Kingdom Hearts.
