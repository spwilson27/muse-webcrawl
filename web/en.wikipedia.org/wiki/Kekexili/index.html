Kekexili

Hoh Xil (Kekexili)
Location: Qinghai, Tibetan Plateau, China
Area: nature reserve about 45,000 km²
Elevation: average over 4,600 m
UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2017

Kekexili (Mongolian for "beautiful girl"), in Chinese Hoh Xil (可可西里), is a remote high-altitude region in the north-eastern Tibetan Plateau in Qinghai province, China. One of the least populated places on earth, it is protected as the Hoh Xil National Nature Reserve and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2017 as "Qinghai Hoh Xil".

Wildlife

The alpine steppe of Kekexili supports Tibetan antelope (chiru), wild yak, Tibetan wild ass (kiang), Tibetan gazelle, wolves and brown bears. The reserve is the principal calving ground of the Tibetan antelope, whose herds migrate hundreds of kilometres each summer. Antelope were poached massively for shahtoosh wool in the 1980s and 1990s; volunteer patrols such as the "Wild Yak Brigade" — whose leader Sonam Dargye was murdered by poachers in 1994, inspiring the 2004 film Kekexili: Mountain Patrol — fought the trade, and numbers have since partly recovered.

Geography

The region lies between the Kunlun and Tanggula mountain ranges at an average elevation above 4,600 metres, with a harsh, arid continental climate. The Qinghai–Tibet railway and highway cross its eastern edge, where wildlife underpasses and tunnels were built for migrating antelope.
