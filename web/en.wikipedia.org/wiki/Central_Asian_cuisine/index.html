Central Asian cuisine comprises the food traditions of Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan, shaped by the region's steppe nomadism, Silk Road trade and settled oasis agriculture. Meat (especially mutton), wheat, rice, noodles and fermented dairy products dominate, and the cuisines of the five countries share many dishes while differing in proportions and detail.

Characteristic dishes

Plov (pilaf; Uzbek osh, Tajik palav) — rice cooked in a cauldron (qazan) with meat, carrots, onions and oil, often flavoured with cumin and barberries — is the region's ceremonial centrepiece, inscribed by UNESCO on its Intangible Cultural Heritage list for Uzbekistan and Tajikistan. Hand-pulled noodles appear in laghman, a Uyghur-influenced dish of noodles with meat and vegetables in sauce or broth. Steamed dumplings (manti), baked pastries (samsa from the tandoor), grilled skewers (shashlik) and flatbreads (non/naan) are universal. In Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, beshbarmak — boiled meat served over broad noodles with onion broth — is the national festive dish, and horse meat and fermented mare's milk (kumis) remain honoured foods of the nomadic heritage.

Influences and drinks

The cuisine blends Turkic and Mongol nomadic traditions with Persianate oasis cooking and, along the Silk Road, Chinese techniques such as pulled noodles and steamed breads; the Russian and Soviet periods added dishes such as borscht and Korean-influenced salads (via the Koryo-saram). Tea — green in Uzbekistan, black with milk in Kazakhstan — is the universal drink of hospitality, served throughout the day. Melons, dried fruits, nuts and sweets such as halva accompany it.
