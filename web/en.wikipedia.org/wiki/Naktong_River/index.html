The Naktong River or Nakdong River (Korean: 낙동강, Nakdong-gang) is the longest river in South Korea, flowing 521 kilometres (324 mi) from the Taebaek Mountains to the Korea Strait near Busan. It drains most of the southeastern part of the Korean Peninsula, including the Gyeongsang provinces, and its basin covers roughly a quarter of South Korea's territory.

Course

The river rises in the Taebaek Mountains and flows generally southward through the cities of Andong, Sangju, Daegu and Miryang before turning east and then south again to reach the sea at Busan. Major tributaries include the Geumho, Nam and Hwang rivers. The lower reaches form a broad delta near Busan, an important habitat for migratory birds.

History

The Naktong valley has been a centre of Korean civilisation since prehistoric times, and the river's name is traditionally explained as "the river east of Garak". During the Korean War in 1950, the river formed the western edge of the Pusan Perimeter, the last defensive line of United Nations and South Korean forces. Fierce fighting took place around the river crossings near Daegu and at the Naktong Bulge.

Environment and development

The river supplies water for agriculture, industry and drinking water to millions of people in southeastern Korea. A series of dams and weirs, including those built under the Four Major Rivers Restoration Project (2008–2011), regulate its flow. The Nakdong River Estuary and Eulsukdo Island at Busan are noted bird sanctuaries.
