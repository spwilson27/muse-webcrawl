Ľudovít Štúr

Ľudovít Velislav Štúr (28 October 1815 – 12 January 1856) was a Slovak politician, writer, and linguist, the leader of the Slovak national revival in the 19th century and the codifier of the modern Slovak literary language.

Language reform

In 1843, at Hlboké, Štúr and his collaborators Jozef Miloslav Hurban and Michal Miloslav Hodža agreed to base the standard Slovak language on the central Slovak dialects, replacing the earlier Bernolák standard and the Czech used by Slovak Protestants. Štúr set out the system in his works Slovak Speech and the Need to Write in This Speech and The Slovak Dialect (both 1846). Though modified by the Hodža–Hattala reform, his standard remains the basis of written Slovak today.

Political activity

Elected to the Hungarian Diet in 1847, Štúr demanded Slovak language rights and the abolition of serfdom. During the revolutions of 1848 he helped draft the Demands of the Slovak Nation and led Slovak volunteer campaigns alongside the imperial forces. Disappointed by the outcome, he spent his final years under police surveillance at Modra, where he died from a hunting accident wound in 1856.
