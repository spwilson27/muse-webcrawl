Binomial type

Binomial type
FieldCombinatorics, umbral calculus
Defining identitypₙ(x + y) = Σ (n choose k) pₖ(x) pₙ₋ₖ(y)
ExamplesMonomials xⁿ, Abel polynomials, Touchard polynomials

In mathematics, a polynomial sequence p₀(x), p₁(x), p₂(x), … is said to be of binomial type if it satisfies the binomial identity: pₙ(x + y) equals the sum over k of the binomial coefficient "n choose k" times pₖ(x) times pₙ₋ₖ(y), for all n.

Examples

The simplest example is the sequence of monomials xⁿ, for which the identity is the ordinary binomial theorem. Other examples include the lower factorials, the Abel polynomials, and the Touchard (Bell) polynomials, which arise in the combinatorics of set partitions.

Theory

The modern theory of binomial-type sequences is a central part of the umbral calculus developed by Gian-Carlo Rota and collaborators. A key result is that a polynomial sequence is of binomial type if and only if it is the sequence of basic polynomials for some delta operator — a shift-invariant operator that reduces the degree of a polynomial by one. This correspondence links binomial-type sequences to formal power series and generating functions.
