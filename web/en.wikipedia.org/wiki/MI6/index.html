The Secret Intelligence Service (SIS), commonly known as MI6, is the foreign intelligence service of the United Kingdom. It is responsible for collecting intelligence overseas — principally through human intelligence (HUMINT) — in support of British national security. Its chief, traditionally signing documents in green ink with the letter "C", reports to the Foreign Secretary.

History

MI6 originated in the Secret Service Bureau founded in 1909, whose foreign section was led by Mansfield Smith-Cumming, the first "C". The service operated in both world wars, running agent networks in occupied Europe. During the Cold War it was penetrated by the Cambridge Five, most notoriously Kim Philby. The British government did not formally acknowledge SIS's existence in law until the Intelligence Services Act 1994. Since 1995 the service has been headquartered in the distinctive SIS Building at Vauxhall Cross on the River Thames, a location popularised worldwide by the James Bond films, in which MI6 is the employer of the fictional agent 007.

Role

Under the Intelligence Services Act 1994, SIS functions include obtaining and providing information relating to the actions of persons outside the British Islands, and performing other tasks relating to such persons, in the interests of national security, the economic well-being of the UK, and the prevention or detection of serious crime. It works alongside the domestic security service MI5 and the signals intelligence agency GCHQ.
