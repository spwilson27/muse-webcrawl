Cannes

Cannes (French pronunciation: [kan]) is a city located on the French Riviera. It is a commune in the Alpes-Maritimes department of France, known worldwide as the host city of the annual Cannes Film Festival, the Midem music market and the Cannes Lions International Festival of Creativity.

History

The area was first settled by Ligurian tribes and later became a Roman outpost known as Aegitna. In the Middle Ages the hilltop quarter of Le Suquet grew around a watchtower built to guard against Saracen raiders. Cannes remained a small fishing village until the 19th century, when the British statesman Lord Henry Brougham, detained there by a cholera quarantine in 1834, built a villa and promoted the town as a health resort. Aristocrats from Britain and Russia followed, transforming Cannes into a fashionable winter retreat.

Geography and climate

Cannes lies on the Mediterranean coast between Nice and Saint-Tropez. It enjoys a Mediterranean climate with mild, sunny winters and hot, dry summers. Offshore lie the Lérins Islands — Île Sainte-Marguerite, where the Man in the Iron Mask was imprisoned, and Île Saint-Honorat, home to Cistercian monks.

Culture

The Cannes Film Festival, founded in 1946, is held each May at the Palais des Festivals et des Congrès on the Boulevard de la Croisette. Its top prize, the Palme d'Or, is among the most prestigious awards in cinema. The city's luxury hotels, beaches and marinas make it a major destination for tourism and conferences.
