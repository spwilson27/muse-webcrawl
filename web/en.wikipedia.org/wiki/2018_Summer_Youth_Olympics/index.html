The 2018 Summer Youth Olympics, officially the III Summer Youth Olympic Games, were held in Buenos Aires, Argentina, from 6 to 18 October 2018. They were the first Youth Olympic Games held in the Americas and the first Olympic event staged in Argentina. Nearly 4,000 athletes aged 14 to 18 from 206 National Olympic Committees took part. The Games were notable for innovations including a street-party opening ceremony at the Obelisco — the first opening ceremony held outside a stadium — full gender equality in athlete numbers, and the debut of sports such as breaking (sport dance), sport climbing, 3x3 basketball variants, beach handball, futsal and roller speed skating.

Bidding and preparation

Buenos Aires was elected host in July 2013, defeating Medellín and Glasgow. Venues were clustered in four zones, centred on the Youth Olympic Park in the south of the city, where a purpose-built Youth Olympic Village housed the athletes. Organisers emphasised low-cost, sustainable facilities integrated into urban parks.

Sports programme

The programme comprised 32 sports with mixed-gender and mixed-nation events designed to encourage cultural exchange. Karate and sport climbing appeared ahead of their senior Olympic debuts at Tokyo 2020, and breaking made its first appearance at any Olympic event, foreshadowing its inclusion at Paris 2024. Hockey5s, futsal and beach handball replaced their traditional formats. Esports were showcased in demonstration form alongside the Games.

Medal table and legacy

Russia topped the medal table, followed by China and Japan; host Argentina enjoyed its best Youth Olympic performance, with notable golds in sports including beach volleyball and sailing. The Games were praised for enthusiastic local crowds — total attendance exceeded 600,000, with many events free — and are credited with renewing Argentine interest in Olympic sport. Buenos Aires' urban, festival-style model influenced later Youth Olympic planning.
