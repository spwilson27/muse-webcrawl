DC Extended Universe

The DC Extended Universe (DCEU) is an American media franchise and shared universe centered on a series of superhero films distributed by Warner Bros. Pictures and produced by DC Films, based on characters from DC Comics. The franchise began in 2013 with Man of Steel and concluded in 2023 with Aquaman and the Lost Kingdom, comprising fifteen films.

Development

Following the success of Marvel Studios' shared cinematic universe, Warner Bros. launched its own interconnected slate starting with Zack Snyder's Man of Steel (2013), a reboot of the Superman film series starring Henry Cavill. Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice and Suicide Squad followed in 2016, and Wonder Woman (2017) became one of the franchise's best-reviewed and highest-grossing entries.

Films

The series includes Justice League (2017), Aquaman (2018) — the franchise's only billion-dollar film — Shazam! (2019), Birds of Prey (2020), Wonder Woman 1984 (2020), The Suicide Squad (2021), Black Adam (2022), Shazam! Fury of the Gods (2023), The Flash (2023), Blue Beetle (2023), and Aquaman and the Lost Kingdom (2023). The director's cut Zack Snyder's Justice League was released on HBO Max in 2021.

Reception and succession

The DCEU grossed over $7 billion worldwide but was noted for an uneven critical reception and repeated changes of creative direction. In 2022, James Gunn and Peter Safran were appointed co-heads of the newly formed DC Studios and announced a rebooted continuity, the DC Universe, superseding the DCEU.
