National Historic Preservation Act

The National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA) is a United States federal law enacted in 1966 and signed by President Lyndon B. Johnson. It established national policy for historic preservation and created the framework that still governs preservation at the federal level.

Provisions

The Act created the National Register of Historic Places, the list of National Historic Landmarks, the Advisory Council on Historic Preservation, the Historic Preservation Fund, and the system of State Historic Preservation Offices. Its best-known requirement, Section 106, requires federal agencies to consider the effects of their undertakings on historic properties and to give the Advisory Council an opportunity to comment. Section 110 sets out agency responsibilities for identifying and protecting historic properties under their control.

Background

The NHPA responded to widespread destruction of historic buildings and neighborhoods by urban renewal and highway construction in the post-war decades, and to the growing preservation movement documented in the 1966 report With Heritage So Rich.
