Out-of-home advertising

Digital out-of-home displays in an urban centre

Out-of-home advertising (abbreviated OOH) is advertising experienced outside of the home. This includes billboards, wallscapes, bus shelters, benches, and other street furniture, transit advertising on buses, trains and in stations and airports, and place-based media in venues such as offices, gyms, bars and retail stores. It contrasts with broadcast, print and internet media consumed at home or at work.

Formats

Traditional OOH formats include static billboards (including large "bulletins" and smaller "posters"), street furniture, and transit media. Digital out-of-home (DOOH) media uses networked digital screens whose content can be changed remotely and scheduled by time of day or audience data; digital billboards and screens in malls, airports and elevators are common examples.

Industry

The OOH industry is dominated by large operators such as JCDecaux, Clear Channel Outdoor, Lamar Advertising and Outfront Media, which hold concessions for street furniture and transit sites in many cities, often providing the furniture itself in exchange for advertising rights. Audience measurement systems estimate impressions from traffic counts and travel surveys. OOH is often considered resistant to ad-blocking and audience fragmentation because the media cannot be switched off, skipped or fast-forwarded by the viewer.

Regulation

Outdoor advertising is regulated in many jurisdictions for aesthetic, safety and land-use reasons; some places, such as the US state of Vermont and the city of São Paulo, have imposed broad bans on billboards.
