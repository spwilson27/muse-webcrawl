The Goal introduced the Theory of Constraints through a fictional story.

The Goal: A Process of Ongoing Improvement is a management book by Eliyahu M. Goldratt and Jeff Cox, published in 1984. Written as a novel, it presents Goldratt's Theory of Constraints (TOC) through the story of a plant manager struggling to save his factory, and it remains one of the best-selling business books ever written.

Plot

Alex Rogo, manager of a struggling UniCo manufacturing plant, is given ninety days to turn the plant around or face closure. Guided by his former physics teacher, Jonah, through Socratic questioning, Alex redefines the goal of the plant — to make money — and develops new measures: throughput, inventory, and operational expense. On a hiking trip with his son's scout troop, he grasps the interplay of dependent events and statistical fluctuations, symbolized by the slowest boy, Herbie, as the bottleneck. Back at the plant, he identifies constraints, subordinates everything else to them (the drum-buffer-rope system), and transforms the plant into the division's best performer, saving it and his marriage in the process.

Legacy

The book popularized bottleneck analysis and the five focusing steps of TOC: identify the constraint, exploit it, subordinate everything else, elevate it, and repeat. It is widely used in business schools and operations management courses. Goldratt wrote sequels including It's Not Luck (1994) and Critical Chain (1997), applying TOC to marketing, distribution, and project management.
