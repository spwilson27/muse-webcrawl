Dame Judith Olivia Dench (born 9 December 1934) is an English actress, widely regarded as one of the greatest of her generation. She built her reputation in classical theatre with the Royal Shakespeare Company and the National Theatre before becoming an international film star in her sixties, notably as M in the James Bond films.

Theatre career

Dench trained at the Central School of Speech and Drama and made her professional debut with the Old Vic in 1957 as Ophelia. She joined the Royal Shakespeare Company in 1961 and became one of its defining performers, acclaimed in roles from Lady Macbeth to Cleopatra, and won multiple Olivier Awards. She also starred in the musical Cabaret in its 1968 London premiere.

Television and film

On television she starred in the sitcom A Fine Romance (1981–1984) opposite her husband Michael Williams, and in As Time Goes By (1992–2005) with Geoffrey Palmer. Her film career surged late: she played Queen Victoria in Mrs Brown (1997), won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress for her brief appearance as Elizabeth I in Shakespeare in Love (1998), and earned further Oscar nominations for Chocolat, Iris, Mrs Henderson Presents, Notes on a Scandal, Philomena and Belfast.

As M — head of MI6 — in seven James Bond films from GoldenEye (1995) to Skyfall (2012), she brought gravitas to the franchise; her character's death in Skyfall was a landmark of the series. Despite macular degeneration affecting her eyesight, she has continued to act, memorizing scripts read aloud to her.
