Ellsworth Raymond "Bumpy" Johnson (October 31, 1905 – July 7, 1968) was an American crime boss in the Harlem neighbourhood of New York City. He was a central figure in Harlem's illegal numbers game and became a folk legend in the community, remembered for both his criminal career and acts of conspicuous generosity.

Early life

Johnson was born in Charleston, South Carolina, and acquired the nickname "Bumpy" from a bump on the back of his head. After his brother was accused of killing a white man, his parents sent him north for safety; he moved to Harlem in 1919. He accumulated a long record of arrests for burglary and assault in his youth.

Criminal career

In the early 1930s Johnson became the chief enforcer and lieutenant of the Harlem numbers queen Stephanie St. Clair, and was a principal in her war against Bronx beer baron Dutch Schultz, who was trying to muscle into Harlem's lucrative policy racket. After Schultz's murder in 1935, Johnson negotiated a settlement with Lucky Luciano's organisation that let him keep control of Harlem numbers in exchange for a share of profits, a truce that lasted decades.

Johnson was convicted of drug conspiracy in 1951 and served most of a 15-year sentence, much of it at Alcatraz, returning to Harlem in 1963 to find his empire diminished. He died of a heart attack in 1968, while under indictment on another narcotics charge.

In popular culture

Johnson has been portrayed in the films Shaft (1971), Hoodlum (1997, played by Laurence Fishburne) and American Gangster (2007), and is the central character of the television series Godfather of Harlem (2019–), played by Forest Whitaker.
