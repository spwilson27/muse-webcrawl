The 2005 Jilin chemical plant explosions were a series of explosions that occurred on 13 November 2005 at the No. 101 petrochemical plant in Jilin City, Jilin Province, China, operated by Jilin Petrochemical Company, a subsidiary of PetroChina. Six people were killed and dozens injured. The disaster is remembered chiefly for the massive benzene spill it released into the Songhua River, which contaminated drinking water supplies for millions of people downstream, including the city of Harbin.

The explosions and fire at the Jilin aniline plant released a toxic slick into the Songhua River.

The explosions

The blasts occurred in a benzene plant's nitration unit producing aniline and nitrobenzene. Investigators found that a tower had become blocked and workers had mishandled the unit, allowing temperatures to rise until the equipment exploded. Fires burned for hours and more than 10,000 nearby residents were evacuated.

Songhua River contamination

An estimated 100 tonnes of benzene, nitrobenzene and related pollutants flowed into the Songhua River, forming an 80-kilometre toxic slick. Authorities initially did not disclose the spill; Harbin, a city of several million people downstream, shut off its water supply for several days from 23 November, officially for "maintenance", before officials confirmed the contamination. The slick passed through Heilongjiang into Russia's Amur River, straining relations between Beijing and Moscow and prompting emergency water monitoring in Khabarovsk.

Aftermath

The director of China's State Environmental Protection Administration, Xie Zhenhua, resigned over the handling of the incident — the highest-ranking official then to fall over an environmental disaster. The disaster became a landmark case in Chinese environmental governance, exposing failures in emergency reporting and intensifying public debate about industrial safety and government transparency.
