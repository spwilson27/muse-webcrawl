Streptococcus pyogenes

Domain:Bacteria
Phylum:Bacillota
Class:Bacilli
Order:Lactobacillales
Family:Streptococcaceae
Genus:Streptococcus
Species:S. pyogenes
S. pyogenes forms chains of spherical cocci and is classified as a group A streptococcus.

Streptococcus pyogenes is a species of Gram-positive, aerotolerant bacteria in the genus Streptococcus. These bacteria are extracellular and made up of non-motile and non-sporing cocci (spherical cells) that tend to link in chains. They are clinically important for humans, as they are an infrequent but usually pathogenic part of the skin flora and can cause group A streptococcal infection.

S. pyogenes is the principal species that carries the Lancefield group A antigen, and is often called group A Streptococcus (GAS). However, both Streptococcus dysgalactiae and the Streptococcus anginosus group can also possess group A antigen.

Diseases

S. pyogenes causes a wide range of diseases, from mild localized infections such as strep throat (pharyngitis) and impetigo to severe invasive conditions including scarlet fever, necrotizing fasciitis, and streptococcal toxic shock syndrome. Post-infectious complications include acute rheumatic fever and post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, autoimmune sequelae that can follow untreated infection.

Virulence factors

Key virulence factors include the M protein, which resists phagocytosis; a hyaluronic acid capsule that mimics host tissue; streptolysins O and S, which lyse red blood cells; and pyrogenic exotoxins (such as SpeA) that act as superantigens and are implicated in scarlet fever and toxic shock.

Treatment

S. pyogenes remains universally susceptible to penicillin, which is the treatment of choice for most infections. Macrolides and clindamycin are used for patients with penicillin allergy or as adjuncts in severe invasive disease.
