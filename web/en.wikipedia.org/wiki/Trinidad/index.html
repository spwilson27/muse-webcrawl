Trinidad

Trinidad is the larger and more populous of the two major islands of the Caribbean nation of Trinidad and Tobago. Lying just off the northeastern coast of Venezuela in the southern Caribbean Sea, it covers about 4,768 km² and is home to the capital, Port of Spain, and the great majority of the country's population of about 1.4 million.

Geography

The island is crossed by three east–west hill ranges, the Northern Range — with El Cerro del Aripo (940 m) the country's highest point — the Central Range, and the Southern Range. Notable natural features include the Pitch Lake at La Brea, the world's largest natural deposit of asphalt, and extensive wetlands such as the Caroni Swamp.

History

Trinidad was inhabited by Arawak and Carib peoples before Columbus sighted it in 1498 and named it for the Holy Trinity. It was held by Spain until Britain captured it in 1797. Enslaved Africans, and later indentured labourers from India, China, and elsewhere, shaped its society; it united administratively with Tobago in 1889 and became independent as Trinidad and Tobago in 1962.

Economy and culture

The economy rests on petroleum and natural gas — Trinidad hosts major LNG and petrochemical industries — rather than the tourism that dominates much of the Caribbean. Culturally the island is the birthplace of calypso, soca, and the steelpan, and its Carnival is among the world's most famous.
