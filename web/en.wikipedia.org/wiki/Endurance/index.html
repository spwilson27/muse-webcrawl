Endurance

Endurance (also related to suffering, resilience, constitution, fortitude, and hardiness) is the ability of an organism to exert itself and remain active for a long period of time, as well as its ability to resist, withstand, recover from and have immunity to trauma, wounds or fatigue. It is usually used in aerobic or anaerobic exercise. The definition of "long" varies according to the type of exertion — minutes for high intensity anaerobic exercise, hours or days for low intensity aerobic exercise.

Physiology

Aerobic endurance depends on the capacity of the cardiovascular and respiratory systems to supply oxygen to working muscles and on the muscles' ability to use that oxygen to produce energy. Key determinants include maximal oxygen uptake (VO2 max), lactate threshold, and movement economy. Endurance exercise causes adaptations such as increased stroke volume, greater capillarisation of muscle, and higher mitochondrial density.

Training

Endurance training typically involves sustained, repetitive exercise at moderate intensity — such as long-distance running, cycling and swimming — often combined with interval training at higher intensities. Training over time causes a reduction in resting heart rate and improved fatigue resistance. Concepts such as periodisation and progressive overload are used to structure endurance programmes.

Psychological endurance

The term is also applied to mental stamina: the capacity to persist with a task or endure hardship over time. Research on mental toughness overlaps significantly with the study of physical endurance, and perceived exertion plays a central role in both.
