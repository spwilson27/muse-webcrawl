Portable water purification

A hiker using a portable pump filter.

Portable water purification devices are self-contained, easily transported units used to purify water from untreated sources (such as rivers, lakes, and wells) for drinking purposes. Their main function is to eliminate pathogens, and often also suspended solids and some unpalatable or toxic compounds. They are widely used by hikers and campers, military personnel, and aid workers in disaster relief.

Techniques

  • Boiling — the most reliable method; vigorously boiling water kills bacteria, viruses and protozoa.
  • Filtration — pump, gravity, squeeze and straw filters with pore sizes around 0.1–0.2 micrometres remove bacteria and protozoan cysts such as Giardia and Cryptosporidium, but generally not viruses.
  • Chemical disinfection — chlorine, chlorine dioxide tablets, or iodine kill bacteria and viruses; effectiveness against Cryptosporidium varies.
  • Ultraviolet light — portable UV pens and bottles inactivate a broad range of microorganisms in clear water.
  • Solar disinfection (SODIS) — exposing water in clear plastic bottles to sunlight for several hours.

Limitations

Most portable methods do not remove dissolved chemicals, heavy metals or salts. Filters clog in silty water, and chemical treatments require contact time and can leave tastes. In areas with viral contamination, treatment methods effective against viruses must be chosen or combined with fine filtration.

Applications

Beyond recreation, portable purification is central to humanitarian response: organisations distribute family-scale filters and purification units after earthquakes, floods and conflicts where municipal water systems fail.
