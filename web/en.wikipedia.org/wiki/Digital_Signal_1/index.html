Digital Signal 1

Digital Signal 1 (DS1, sometimes T1) is a T-carrier signalling scheme devised by Bell Labs and widely used in telecommunications in North America and Japan. A DS1 circuit carries 1.544 megabits per second.

Structure

A DS1 frame consists of 24 time-division multiplexed voice channels (DS0s) of 64 kbit/s each, plus a framing bit, giving 193 bits transmitted 8,000 times per second. Signalling was historically carried in-band by robbed-bit signalling, later replaced by out-of-band common-channel signalling such as ISDN PRI, which uses the 23B+D configuration.

Use

T1 lines were widely used for business telephone trunks, PBX connections, and early internet access, and remain in some legacy and rural deployments. Higher-order signals (DS3, at 44.736 Mbit/s) multiplex 28 DS1s.
