Noah Webster

Noah Webster Jr.
Born: 16 October 1758, West Hartford, Connecticut
Died: 28 May 1843, New Haven, Connecticut
Occupation: Lexicographer, educator, writer, political activist
Notable work: An American Dictionary of the English Language (1828); the "Blue-backed Speller" (1783)
[Image: A portrait engraving of Noah Webster in old age, seated, with high collar and spectacles.]

Noah Webster Jr. (1758–1843) was an American lexicographer, textbook pioneer and spelling reformer whose name became synonymous with dictionaries in the United States. Convinced that the young republic needed its own cultural independence from Britain, he set out to create a distinctly American standard for English in spelling, grammar and pronunciation.

Works

His Grammatical Institute of the English Language (1783–85) — whose first part, the "Blue-backed Speller," sold on the order of a hundred million copies over the following century — taught generations of American schoolchildren. It introduced spellings such as color for colour, center for centre, traveled with a single l, and defense for defence, many of them adopted to simplify or Americanise the language. His life's work, An American Dictionary of the English Language (1828, two volumes, about 70,000 entries), was learned in method — Webster taught himself some 28 languages to pursue etymologies — and patriotic in purpose. He also championed copyright law, helped found Amherst College and served in Connecticut politics.

Legacy

After Webster's death, George and Charles Merriam bought the rights to his dictionary; the Merriam-Webster company still publishes under his name. "Webster's" became so generic in American usage that courts eventually ruled the name public domain for dictionaries. See Samuel Johnson and the Oxford English Dictionary.
