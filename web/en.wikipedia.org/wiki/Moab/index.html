Moab was an ancient kingdom located in the region of the modern state of Jordan, east of the Dead Sea, on a plateau bordered by the deep canyon of the Arnon River (Wadi Mujib) to the north and the Zered (Wadi Hasa) to the south. Its people, the Moabites, spoke the Moabite language, closely related to Hebrew, and worshipped the national god Chemosh.

History

Moabites appear in Egyptian inscriptions and settled the region in the early Iron Age. In the biblical account they are presented as kin of the Israelites, descended from Lot, and relations between the two peoples alternated between trade, intermarriage — Ruth the Moabitess is an ancestress of King David — and war. The principal historical source for Moab itself is the Mesha Stele (c. 840 BC), in which King Mesha recounts throwing off Israelite domination under the house of Omri and rebuilding Moabite towns. Moab later paid tribute to Assyria, and the kingdom disappears from the record by the Persian period, its territory absorbed by Nabataeans and others.

Geography and economy

The Moabite plateau, well watered by Mediterranean standards, supported wheat, barley, vines, and sheep herding; the King's Highway trade route crossing the plateau brought customs revenue. Major sites include Dibon (Dhiban), Madaba, Kerak, and Mount Nebo, from whose summit, according to Deuteronomy, Moses viewed the Promised Land.
