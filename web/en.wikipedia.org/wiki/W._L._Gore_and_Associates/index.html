W. L. Gore and Associates, Inc. is an American multinational manufacturing company specializing in products derived from fluoropolymers, best known as the maker of Gore-Tex, the waterproof, breathable fabric membrane.

History

The company was founded in 1958 by Wilbert Lee "Bill" Gore and his wife Genevieve (Vieve) in the basement of their home in Newark, Delaware. Bill Gore had worked at DuPont, where he saw potential in polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE). In 1969 their son Bob Gore discovered that PTFE could be stretched to create expanded PTFE (ePTFE), a microporous material that became the basis of Gore-Tex, commercialized from 1976.

Products

Beyond fabrics, Gore makes medical implants, vascular grafts, cables, filtration media, sealants and venting products, sold in more than 100 countries.

Corporate culture

Gore is famous for its flat "lattice" organization: no formal hierarchy or job titles, small teams, leaders chosen by followership, and associates (not employees) who commit to projects. It has frequently appeared on best-workplace lists.
