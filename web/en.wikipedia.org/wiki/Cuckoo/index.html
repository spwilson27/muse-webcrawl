Cuckoo

Cuckoos are birds of the family Cuculidae, the sole taxon in the order Cuculiformes. The family includes the common cuckoo, roadrunners, koels, malkohas, couas, coucals and anis — about 150 species found worldwide, most of them arboreal and insectivorous.

Characteristics

Cuckoos are generally medium-sized, slender birds with long tails and zygodactyl feet (two toes forward, two backward). Many species are famous for brood parasitism: the common cuckoo lays its eggs in the nests of other birds, and the newly hatched chick ejects the host's eggs or young. Other species, including coucals and anis, build their own nests and raise their own young.

In culture

The two-note call of the male common cuckoo gave the family its name and inspired the cuckoo clock. The greater roadrunner of the American Southwest is famed as a ground-dwelling predator of snakes and lizards. Many cuckoos feed on hairy caterpillars avoided by other birds.
