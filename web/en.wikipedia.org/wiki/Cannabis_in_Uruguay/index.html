Cannabis in Uruguay

Cannabis in Uruguay is fully legal for recreational use, making Uruguay the first country in the world to completely legalise the production, sale and consumption of cannabis. The law was signed by President José Mujica on 20 December 2013.

History

Personal possession of drugs was decriminalised in Uruguay in 1974, though production and sale remained illegal for decades. In 2012 the Mujica government proposed state regulation of cannabis as a measure to undercut drug trafficking. The Cannabis Regulation and Control Law passed the Senate on 10 December 2013 by a vote of 16–13.

The regulatory system

The system is overseen by the Institute for the Regulation and Control of Cannabis (IRCCA). Adult residents of Uruguay may obtain cannabis through three channels, after registering with the state:

  • Home cultivation — up to six plants per household.
  • Cannabis clubs — non-profit collectives of 15 to 45 members growing up to 99 plants.
  • Pharmacy sales — up to 40 grams per month of state-supervised cannabis, which began in July 2017.

Prices were initially set at roughly one US dollar per gram to compete with the illicit market. Tourists and non-residents are not permitted to purchase cannabis legally.

Impact

Uruguay's model has been studied internationally as an alternative to both prohibition and fully commercial legalisation, such as the systems later adopted in Canada and several US states. Registered users numbered in the tens of thousands within a few years, though a significant share of consumers continued to obtain cannabis outside the legal channels.
