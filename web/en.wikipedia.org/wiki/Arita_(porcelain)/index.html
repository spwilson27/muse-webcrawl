Arita (porcelain)

Arita is a town in Saga Prefecture, Japan, famous as the birthplace of Japanese porcelain. In the early 17th century, porcelain stone was discovered at Izumiyama in Arita, and Korean potters brought to Japan during the invasions of Korea in the 1590s established the first kilns.

History

Arita ware, shipped abroad from the nearby port of Imari, became known in Europe as Imari ware. When Chinese porcelain exports were disrupted by the Ming–Qing transition in the 1650s, the Dutch East India Company turned to Arita, and its blue-and-white and enamelled wares (including Kakiemon-style pieces) were exported worldwide and widely imitated, notably at Meissen and Delft.

Styles

Principal styles include Ko-Imari (old Imari), Kakiemon, with milky-white nigoshide bodies and sparse enamel designs, and Nabeshima ware, produced for the local ruling clan.
