Four Great Inventions

The Four Great Inventions (四大發明, sì dà fāmíng) are inventions from ancient China that are celebrated in Chinese culture for their historical significance and as symbols of China's advanced science and technology. They are the compass, gunpowder, papermaking, and printing. These four inventions had a profound effect on the development of civilisation throughout the world.

The inventions

  • Papermaking — traditionally credited to the Han dynasty eunuch Cai Lun in AD 105, though paper fragments predate him; paper spread through the Islamic world to Europe.
  • Printing — woodblock printing was developed by the Tang dynasty (the Diamond Sutra of 868 is the earliest dated printed book); Bi Sheng invented movable type in the 1040s during the Song dynasty.
  • Gunpowder — discovered by Tang-dynasty alchemists in the 9th century and soon used in fireworks, bombs, rockets and firearms.
  • Compass — lodestone compasses were used for geomancy in Han China; by the Song dynasty the magnetic compass was used for navigation, transforming seafaring.

Origins of the concept

The idea of the four inventions was popularised by the sinologist Joseph Needham, whose Science and Civilisation in China documented China's technological achievements; the concept echoes Francis Bacon's observation that printing, gunpowder and the compass "changed the whole face and state of things throughout the world." In modern China the Four Great Inventions are a staple of school textbooks and patriotic education.
