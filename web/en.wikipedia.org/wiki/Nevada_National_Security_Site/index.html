Nevada National Security Site

The Nevada National Security Site (N2S2 or NNSS), known until 2010 as the Nevada Test Site (NTS), is a United States Department of Energy reservation located in southern Nye County, Nevada, about 65 miles (105 km) northwest of Las Vegas. Established in 1951, the site covers approximately 1,350 square miles (3,500 km2) of desert terrain.

Nuclear testing

Between 1951 and 1992, the site hosted 928 announced nuclear tests, more than anywhere else in the world: 100 atmospheric tests (ending in 1962) and 828 underground tests. Iconic images of mushroom clouds were visible from Las Vegas in the early years. The last underground test, "Divider," took place on 23 September 1992, shortly before the United States entered a testing moratorium. Landmarks include the Sedan Crater, formed by a 104-kiloton excavation experiment in 1962, and the mock towns of "Survival Town" used in civil defense tests.

Current activities

Today the NNSS supports stockpile stewardship through subcritical experiments, nuclear nonproliferation work, emergency response training, and environmental remediation. The site also hosts the Area 5 Radioactive Waste Management Complex. Public tours of portions of the site are offered periodically.
