Aladdin is a 1992 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Feature Animation and released by Walt Disney Pictures. The 31st Disney animated feature film, it is based on the Arabic folktale of the same name from One Thousand and One Nights. It was directed by John Musker and Ron Clements, and features the voices of Scott Weinger, Robin Williams, Linda Larkin, Jonathan Freeman and Gilbert Gottfried.

Plot

In the fictional city of Agrabah, the street urchin Aladdin is recruited by the royal vizier Jafar to retrieve a magic lamp from the Cave of Wonders. Trapped inside, Aladdin befriends a magic carpet and releases the lamp's Genie, who grants three wishes. Disguised as "Prince Ali", Aladdin courts Princess Jasmine, who longs for freedom from palace life; Jafar's schemes to seize the lamp and the throne culminate in a confrontation in which Aladdin uses his final wish to free the Genie.

Production and reception

Lyricist Howard Ashman conceived the film and wrote several songs with composer Alan Menken before his death in 1991; Tim Rice completed the score. Robin Williams's largely improvised performance as the Genie was a landmark in celebrity voice casting. The film was the highest-grossing of 1992, won Academy Awards for Best Original Score and Best Original Song ("A Whole New World"), and spawned two direct-to-video sequels, a television series, a Broadway musical (2014) and a live-action remake (2019).
