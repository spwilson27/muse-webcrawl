The Consolidated PB4Y-2 Privateer is an American maritime patrol bomber developed during the Second World War from the B-24 Liberator. The US Navy ordered the type to meet its requirement for a dedicated long-range patrol aircraft, and 739 were built at Consolidated's San Diego plant between 1943 and 1945.

Design

Compared with the B-24/PB4Y-1, the Privateer had a lengthened forward fuselage (by about 7 feet) to accommodate flight-engineer positions, a single large vertical tail for better low-altitude, low-speed stability, and turbosupercharger-less Pratt & Whitney R-1830-94 engines optimised for low-level work. Defensive armament grew to twelve .50-calibre machine guns in six powered turrets, including two deck turrets and an ERCO ball turret in the nose; it carried up to about 12,800 lb of bombs, mines or depth charges, plus search radar in a retractable housing.

Service

Privateers entered Pacific service in early 1945, flying very-long-range patrol, anti-shipping and reconnaissance missions. In the Korean War, PB4Y-2s flew flare-dropping "Firefly" night interdiction missions and signals-intelligence flights — one Privateer on an ELINT mission was shot down by Soviet fighters over the Baltic Sea in April 1950, an early Cold War flashpoint. The US Coast Guard operated the PB4Y-2G for search and rescue into the early 1960s. Retired airframes later flew as firefighting airtankers into the 2000s; a number survive in museums.
