Ferguson rifle

DesignerPatrick Ferguson

The Ferguson rifle was a .65-calibre flintlock breech-loading rifle designed by Major Patrick Ferguson of the British Army and patented in 1776. It was one of the first breech-loading rifles to see military service, used by Ferguson's experimental rifle corps during the American Revolutionary War.

Design

The rifle used a vertical screw breech plug operated by rotating the trigger guard one full turn, lowering the plug so a ball and powder charge could be inserted directly into the chamber. This allowed a trained soldier to fire six to ten aimed shots per minute — two or three times the rate of a muzzle-loading musket — and to reload while kneeling or prone.

Service

About one hundred rifles were made for Ferguson's corps, which fought at the Battle of Brandywine in 1777, where Ferguson was wounded. After his recovery he was killed at the Battle of Kings Mountain in 1780. The rifles were withdrawn and the concept shelved, largely because of cost and manufacturing complexity, and breech-loading did not return to general British service for decades.
