Sandwich, Kent

Sandwich is a historic town and civil parish in the Dover District of Kent, England. It lies on the River Stour and was once one of the Cinque Ports and a major English Channel harbour; although the river has silted and the sea has retreated about two miles, the town preserves one of the most complete medieval street plans in England, with many timber-framed buildings.

History

The name derives from the Old English Sandwic, "sandy trading place". Nearby Richborough was the Roman landing place for the invasion of AD 43. Sandwich flourished as a port in the Middle Ages, was attacked repeatedly during the Hundred Years' War, and received Flemish settlers in the sixteenth century who introduced market gardening to Kent. The silting of the Wantsum Channel and the River Stour ended its career as a major port by the seventeenth century, leaving the town a quiet market centre remarkably preserved from later development.

The sandwich

The town gave its name — via John Montagu, 4th Earl of Sandwich (1718–1792) — to the sandwich. Popular legend holds that the Earl, a keen gambler, asked for meat served between slices of bread so that he could eat without leaving the gaming table; his title attached itself to the snack, which became famous worldwide.

Golf

Just outside the town are two famous links golf courses: Royal St George's, a regular host of The Open Championship, and Prince's. Sandwich Bay, a nature reserve and bird observatory, lies between the town and the sea.

Notable sights

St Peter's, St Clement's and St Mary's churches, the old town walls and Fisher Gate, and the Guildhall (1579) are among the principal historic buildings.
