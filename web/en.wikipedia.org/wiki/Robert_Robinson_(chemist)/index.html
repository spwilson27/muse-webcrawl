Robert Robinson (chemist)

Sir Robert Robinson (13 September 1886 – 8 February 1975) was a British organic chemist. He received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1947 for his investigations on plant products of biological importance, especially the alkaloids.

Career

Robinson held chairs at the Universities of Sydney, Liverpool, St Andrews, Manchester, University College London, and Oxford, where he was Waynflete Professor of Chemistry from 1930 to 1955. His research included the synthesis of natural pigments (anthocyanins and flavones), the structures of morphine and strychnine, and the landmark one-pot synthesis of tropinone in 1917, a classic of biomimetic synthesis. He also developed electronic theories of organic reaction mechanisms, including the curly arrow notation for electron flow.

Honours

Robinson was president of the Royal Society from 1945 to 1950, was knighted in 1939, and received the Davy, Royal, and Copley medals. Mount Robinson in Antarctica and the Robert Robinson Award of the Royal Society of Chemistry commemorate him. The Robinson annulation reaction in organic synthesis also bears his name.
