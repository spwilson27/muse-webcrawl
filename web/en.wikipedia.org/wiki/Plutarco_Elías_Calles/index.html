Plutarco Elías Calles

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Plutarco Elías Calles (born Francisco Plutarco Elías Campuzano; 25 September 1877 – 19 October 1945) was a Mexican politician and military leader who served as President of Mexico from 1924 to 1928. After leaving office he remained the dominant figure in Mexican politics as Jefe Máximo ("Maximum Chief") of the Revolution during the period known as the Maximato (1928–1935), ruling through puppet presidents until his break with Lázaro Cárdenas, who exiled him in 1936.

Presidency

A former schoolteacher and Constitutionalist general from Sonora, Calles succeeded Álvaro Obregón. His government promoted public education, road and irrigation works, land reform and the creation of the Bank of Mexico (1925). His enforcement of anticlerical provisions of the 1917 Constitution — including the 1926 "Calles Law" penalising religious offences — provoked the Cristero War (1926–1929), a Catholic uprising in west-central Mexico suppressed at great human cost before a negotiated settlement.

Maximato and exile

After Obregón's assassination in 1928, Calles founded the National Revolutionary Party (PNR, ancestor of the PRI) in 1929, institutionalising the revolutionary regime. As Jefe Máximo he dominated the presidencies of Portes Gil, Ortiz Rubio and Rodríguez. President Lázaro Cárdenas, elected in 1934, gradually asserted independence and in April 1936 had Calles arrested and exiled to the United States. Calles was allowed to return in 1941 under President Ávila Camacho and died in Mexico City in 1945.
