Sejong City

Sejong City (Korean: 세종시), officially the Sejong Special Self-Governing City, is a special self-governing city in South Korea, established in 2012 as the country's de facto administrative capital. It was created from territory formerly part of South Chungcheong Province and the city of Cheongju, and is named after Sejong the Great, the fifteenth-century king who promulgated the Korean alphabet.

History

The project originated in President Roh Moo-hyun's 2002 campaign pledge to build a new administrative capital to relieve congestion in Seoul and promote balanced regional development. In 2004 the Constitutional Court ruled that relocating the capital required a constitutional amendment, after which the plan was revised: most government ministries and agencies would move, while the presidency, National Assembly, and foreign ministry would remain in Seoul.

Construction began in 2007, and the city officially launched on 1 July 2012. Government ministries relocated in phases from 2012 to 2014, and the Government Complex Sejong now houses the Prime Minister's office and numerous ministries. The population grew rapidly from about 100,000 at founding to well over 350,000 within a decade.

Geography and planning

Sejong lies about 120 kilometres south of Seoul, near Daejeon, and is centred on a ring-shaped urban plan organised around the Geum River and a large central park and lake. The master plan targeted a population of 500,000 by 2030, with districts arranged along a circular public-transport spine. The city hosts research institutes, the National Library of Sejong, and campuses of Korea University and Hongik University.

Debate

The move has been debated for cost, administrative inefficiency from the split between Seoul and Sejong, and repeated proposals — including approved plans in 2021 — to build a National Assembly branch in Sejong and to grant the city a fuller capital role.
