The Königsallee (German for "King's Avenue"), known locally as the "Kö", is an urban boulevard in Düsseldorf, Germany. It is one of Germany's most famous luxury shopping streets, lined with flagship stores of international fashion and jewellery brands, banks, hotels and cafés.

Layout

The boulevard runs roughly north–south for about one kilometre through the Stadtmitte district. Its defining feature is a landscaped canal some 31 metres wide running down its centre, fed by the Düssel river and crossed by small bridges. Chestnut trees line both banks of the canal; the eastern side concentrates luxury retailers, while the western side is dominated by banks and offices.

History

The avenue was laid out at the beginning of the 19th century on the site of Düsseldorf's demolished eastern fortifications, to designs involving court architect Kaspar Anton Huschberger, landscape architect Maximilian Friedrich Weyhe and hydraulic engineer Wilhelm Gottlieb Bauer. It received the name Königsallee in 1851 — by tradition as an apology from the city after an incident in 1848 in which horse manure was thrown at King Friedrich Wilhelm IV of Prussia during a visit; the street had previously been called Kastanienallee ("Chestnut Avenue").

At the canal's southern end stands the Triton Fountain (Tritonengruppe, 1902) by sculptor Fritz Coubillier, one of the city's landmarks. The boulevard was rebuilt after Second World War bombing and remains the centrepiece of Düsseldorf's identity as a fashion and trade-fair city.
