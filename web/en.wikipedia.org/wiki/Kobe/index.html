Kobe (神戸市, Kōbe-shi) is the capital city of Hyōgo Prefecture, Japan, and one of the country's principal port cities. With a population around 1.5 million, it forms the core of the Keihanshin metropolitan area together with Osaka and Kyoto.

History

The natural harbour at the foot of Mount Rokkō was a trading port from early times; under the name Ōwada Anchorage and later Hyōgo port it handled trade with China and domestic shipping through the medieval and Edo periods. Kobe was opened to foreign trade in 1868, in the first days of the Meiji era, and a foreign settlement grew up whose Western-style residences (ijinkan) still distinguish the Kitano district. The modern city was founded in 1889 and grew into one of Japan's busiest ports.

On 17 January 1995 the Great Hanshin earthquake (magnitude 6.9) devastated the city, killing more than 6,400 people, destroying tens of thousands of buildings and wrecking the port and the elevated Hanshin Expressway. Kobe rebuilt over the following decade; the annual Kobe Luminarie light festival, begun in 1995, commemorates the victims.

Economy and culture

The Port of Kobe remains among Japan's top container ports, and the city hosts shipbuilding, steel, and a biomedical cluster on Port Island. Kobe is internationally famous for Kobe beef — marbled wagyu from Tajima cattle raised in Hyōgo — and for the Nada district's sake breweries, as well as the hot-spring resort of Arima Onsen in the city's north. Landmarks include Kobe Port Tower, Meriken Park, the Kitano ijinkan, and the Akashi Kaikyō Bridge, the world's longest suspension bridge span when it opened in 1998, linking Kobe to Awaji Island.
