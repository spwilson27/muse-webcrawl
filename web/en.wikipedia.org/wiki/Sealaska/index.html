Sealaska

Sealaska Corporation is one of thirteen Alaska Native Regional Corporations created under the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act of 1971 (ANCSA). It was incorporated in Alaska on June 16, 1972, and is headquartered in Juneau. Sealaska represents the Tlingit, Haida, and Tsimshian peoples of Southeast Alaska and has more than 22,000 tribal member shareholders.

Under ANCSA, Sealaska received land selections of about 290,000 acres of surface estate and 600,000 acres of subsurface estate in Southeast Alaska, along with a share of the monetary settlement.

Business

Sealaska's businesses have historically centred on timber harvesting on its lands in the Tongass region, though the corporation has moved toward sustainable land management, natural foods and seafood, environmental services, and other ventures through its subsidiaries. It also supports cultural preservation through the Sealaska Heritage Institute, which works to perpetuate Tlingit, Haida, and Tsimshian cultures and languages.
