"Moon River" is a song composed by Henry Mancini with lyrics by Johnny Mercer. It was originally performed by Audrey Hepburn in the 1961 film Breakfast at Tiffany's, winning the Academy Award for Best Original Song. The song also won the 1962 Grammy Awards for Record of the Year and Song of the Year. Mancini's own orchestral recording, released as a single in 1961, reached number 11 on the Billboard Hot 100, and a version by Jerry Butler also charted.

Composition

Mercer and Mancini wrote the song for Hepburn's limited vocal range, keeping it within an octave plus a note. The lyrics evoke Mercer's childhood in Savannah, Georgia: the "huckleberry friend" recalls a childhood playmate, and the "river" a real waterway near his home — a back river later officially renamed Moon River in the song's honour. Hepburn sings it in the film seated on her apartment's fire escape, accompanying herself on guitar, in a scene that nearly was cut from the film.

Recordings and legacy

Andy Williams recorded the song in 1962 and made it his signature tune, performing it at the Academy Awards and naming his television variety show and his Moon River Theatre in Branson, Missouri, after it. Hundreds of artists have covered it, including Frank Sinatra, Judy Garland, Aretha Franklin, Barbra Streisand, Elton John, REM, and Jacob Collier. In 2004 the film version finished at number 4 on the American Film Institute's 100 Years...100 Songs list.
