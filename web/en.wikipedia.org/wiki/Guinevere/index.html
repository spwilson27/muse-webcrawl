Guinevere

Guinevere (Welsh: Gwenhwyfar) is the legendary queen and wife of King Arthur in Arthurian legend. She is best known for her love affair with Arthur's chief knight Lancelot, which in many versions precipitates the fall of Camelot.

SpouseKing Arthur
FatherKing Leodegrance (in later tradition)
First appearanceEarly Welsh tradition; Geoffrey of Monmouth, Historia Regum Britanniae

Legend

Geoffrey of Monmouth (c. 1136) made Guanhumara Arthur's queen, who betrayed him with Mordred. Chrétien de Troyes introduced her abduction by Meleagant and rescue by Lancelot (Lancelot, the Knight of the Cart). In the Vulgate Cycle and Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur, her adultery with Lancelot, their rescue from burning, and the resulting civil war destroy the Round Table; she ends her life in a convent at Amesbury.

Interpretations

Modern retellings — from Tennyson's Idylls to Marion Zimmer Bradley's The Mists of Avalon — re-examine her as victim, agent, or pagan priestess. See King Arthur and Lancelot.
