Central Papua

Central Papua (Indonesian: Papua Tengah) is an Indonesian province located in the central highlands of Western New Guinea. It was formally established on 25 July 2022, when it was split from Papua province along with the new provinces of Highland Papua and South Papua. Its capital is Nabire, on the north coast at Cenderawasih Bay.

Geography

The province covers about 61,000 square kilometres and spans environments from the mangrove-lined northern coast to the rugged Sudirman Range, which contains Puncak Jaya (Carstensz Pyramid), at 4,884 metres the highest peak in Indonesia and Oceania. Major settlements include Nabire, Timika, Enarotali, and Ilaga. The Grasberg mine near Timika, one of the world's largest gold and copper mines, lies within the province.

Population and culture

Central Papua's population is roughly 1.3–1.4 million. Indigenous Papuan peoples of the province include the Mee, Moni, Dani (in the western highlands), Amungme, Kamoro, and Ekari, alongside migrants from elsewhere in Indonesia. Languages spoken include Ekari (Mee), Moni, and Indonesian, widely used in administration and education. The coastal town of Nabire is a trading centre for the highland region served by roads and air links.

Administration

The province is divided into eight regencies: Nabire, Paniai, Dogiyai, Deiyai, Intan Jaya, Puncak, Puncak Jaya, and Mimika. It was created under Law No. 15 of 2022 as part of a broader reorganization of Papua's provincial administration intended to accelerate development and bring government services closer to local communities.
