The Serbian Empire (Serbian: Српско царство, Srpsko carstvo) was a medieval Serbian state that existed from 1346 to 1371. It was established when King Stefan Dušan, who had greatly expanded Serbia into Byzantine territory in Macedonia, Albania, and Greece, had himself crowned "Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks" at Skopje in 1346.

Expansion under Dušan

Dušan (reigned 1331–1355) exploited Byzantine civil wars to conquer much of the southern Balkans, nearly doubling Serbian territory; at its height his empire stretched from the Danube to the Gulf of Corinth and from the Adriatic nearly to the Aegean. In 1349 and 1354 he promulgated Dušan's Code, one of the most important legal compilations of medieval Serbia, meant to govern the multi-ethnic empire.

Decline and fall

Dušan died suddenly in 1355 while marching toward Constantinople. His son Stefan Uroš V, called "the Weak", could not hold the empire together against rising regional lords such as Vukašin Mrnjavčević and against the advancing Ottoman Turks. The Serbian defeat at the Battle of Maritsa in 1371, the year of Uroš's death, is conventionally taken as the end of the empire; its lands fragmented into competing lordships.

Legacy

The empire's age under Dušan is remembered as the apex of medieval Serbian statehood, leaving a rich legacy of monasteries and frescoes, including the Patriarchate of Peć and the Visoki Dečani monastery, now UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
