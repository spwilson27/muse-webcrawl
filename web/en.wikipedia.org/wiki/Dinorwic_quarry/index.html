Dinorwic quarry

Dinorwic quarry is a former slate quarry near Llanberis in Gwynedd, North Wales. At its height in the late 19th century it employed more than 3,000 men and was the second-largest slate quarry in Wales and the world, after the neighbouring Penrhyn quarry. It sprawls across the slopes of Elidir Fawr above the village of Llanberis and the lake Llyn Peris.

Slate was worked on the site from the late 18th century; the quarry was developed from 1809 by Thomas Assheton Smith and grew through the Victorian building boom, its output carried to the coast at Port Dinorwic (Y Felinheli) first by the Dinorwic Railway and then by the Padarn Railway. The rock was extracted in a vast series of stepped terraces or "galleries", each with its own tramways, inclines, and dressing sheds.

Production declined through the 20th century and the quarry closed in 1969. Much of the site is now part of the National Slate Museum at Gilfach Ddu, and the lower terraces overlook the Dinorwig pumped-storage hydroelectric power station inside Elidir Fawr. In 2021 the quarry was inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "The Slate Landscape of Northwest Wales". The terraces are also a celebrated venue for rock climbing.
