Veld

The veld (also spelled veldt) is a type of wide open rural landscape in Southern Africa, particularly flat grassland or scrubland. The term, from Afrikaans and Dutch for "field", is used for the largely treeless plains of South Africa, Zimbabwe, and neighbouring countries.

Different types are distinguished by altitude and vegetation: the high-elevation Highveld around Johannesburg, the lower Lowveld toward the Kruger National Park, the Bushveld with denser trees and shrubs, and the Thornveld and Sandveld. The veld supports grazing wildlife and livestock, and its grasses are adapted to periodic fire, grazing, and seasonal drought.

Ecology

Veld ecosystems include many endemic grasses, forbs, and antelope species. Land use includes ranching, game reserves, and crop farming on the Highveld's maize belt. "Veld" also appears in the military phrase "in the veld" for being in the field, dating to the Boer Wars.
