The BP Pedestrian Bridge, or simply BP Bridge, is a girder footbridge in the Loop community area of Chicago, Illinois, United States. It spans Columbus Drive to connect Millennium Park with Maggie Daley Park (formerly Daley Bicentennial Plaza), both in Grant Park.

Designed by Pritzker Prize-winning architect Frank Gehry, it opened along with the rest of Millennium Park on July 16, 2004. It was funded by the energy company BP, which donated $5 million toward its construction, and is the first bridge Gehry designed to be built.

Design

The bridge is known for its aesthetic, which uses broad, sweeping, curved forms, clad in brushed stainless steel panels. Its snaking, curvilinear form was intended to evoke a river, and its deck is faced in Brazilian hardwood planks. The bridge slopes gently at a 5 percent grade, making it barrier-free and accessible, while its parapets double as sound barriers that muffle traffic noise from Columbus Drive below.

Because the bridge passes over the Monroe Street parking garage as well as Columbus Drive and a rail corridor, its 935-foot (285 m) length was dictated by the need to span the roadway without intermediate supports that would obstruct traffic.

History

Gehry was commissioned to design the bridge as part of the Millennium Park project under Mayor Richard M. Daley. Construction began in 2002 and the bridge was completed for the park's grand opening in July 2004. The bridge and the nearby Jay Pritzker Pavilion, also designed by Gehry, are the architect's first completed works in Chicago.

Reception

The bridge has been praised as one of the park's architectural highlights and a popular vantage point for photographs of the Chicago skyline. Critics noted that the handsome but costly structure exemplified both the ambitions and the budget controversies of the Millennium Park project, which was completed years late and far over its original budget.
