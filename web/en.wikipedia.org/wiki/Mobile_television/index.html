Mobile television

Mobile television is television watched on a small handheld or mobile device, either received through dedicated broadcast networks designed for mobile reception or streamed over cellular and Wi-Fi data connections. It includes programming normally watched on a television set as well as content made specifically for mobile viewing.

History

Handheld television began with pocket-sized analogue receivers in the 1970s and 1980s, such as Sony's Watchman. In the 2000s, digital broadcast standards aimed at handheld devices were launched in several regions, including DVB-H in Europe, DMB in South Korea, ISDB-T's 1seg service in Japan, and MediaFLO in the United States. Many of these dedicated services were later discontinued as smartphones and IP streaming applications became dominant.

Technology

Broadcast mobile TV standards were designed to save battery power by time-slicing transmissions and to cope with the Doppler effects of reception in moving vehicles. Modern mobile video is delivered mainly over the top via streaming platforms and social media apps using adaptive bitrate protocols over 4G and 5G networks.
