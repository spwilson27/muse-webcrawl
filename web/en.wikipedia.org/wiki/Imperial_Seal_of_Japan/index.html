Imperial Seal of Japan

The Chrysanthemum Seal, emblem of the Emperor of Japan

The Imperial Seal of Japan or National Chrysanthemum Seal is the seal used by the Emperor of Japan and members of the Imperial Family. It depicts a stylised chrysanthemum blossom with sixteen petals in front and sixteen behind. Under the Meiji Constitution it was forbidden for anyone other than the Emperor and the Imperial Family to use the seal, and it remains closely associated with the imperial institution.

History

The chrysanthemum emblem came into use during the Kamakura period; Emperor Go-Toba was particularly fond of the flower and adopted it as his personal emblem. Over time it became the crest of the imperial house. In 1869 the Meiji government regulated the imperial crests, reserving the sixteen-fold chrysanthemum for the Emperor, and in 1871 a proclamation prohibited its use by others.

Use

The seal appears on the covers of Japanese passports (in a simplified form), on imperial orders and decorations, on documents issued by the Emperor, and at imperial shrines and properties. A stylised version crowns the Order of the Chrysanthemum, Japan's highest order. The chrysanthemum crest appears on the bows of Japanese warships and on the coins and gates of imperial residences.
