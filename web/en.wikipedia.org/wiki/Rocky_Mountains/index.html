Rocky Mountains

[Image: Moraine Lake and the Canadian Rockies]
Moraine Lake and the Canadian Rockies

The Rocky Mountains, also known as the Rockies, are a major mountain range and the largest mountain system in North America, stretching about 4,800 kilometres from northern British Columbia in Canada to New Mexico in the United States. They form part of the North American Cordillera and the Continental Divide of the Americas.

The Rockies were formed roughly 80 to 55 million years ago in the Laramide orogeny. Their highest peak is Mount Elbert in Colorado at 4,401 metres. Famous protected areas include Banff, Jasper, Yellowstone and Rocky Mountain National Park. Major rivers rising in the Rockies include the Colorado, Rio Grande, Missouri, Columbia and Fraser.

Geography

The range varies from the snow-capped Canadian Rockies, with their glaciers and turquoise lakes, to the high ranges of Wyoming, Colorado's 'fourteeners', and the southern Rockies of New Mexico. The Continental Divide follows the crest, separating waters flowing to the Pacific from those flowing to the Atlantic and Arctic.
