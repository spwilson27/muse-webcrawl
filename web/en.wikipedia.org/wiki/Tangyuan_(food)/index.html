Tangyuan (food)

Tangyuan (simplified Chinese: 汤圆; traditional Chinese: 湯圓; pinyin: tāngyuán, "soup balls") are Chinese glutinous rice balls served in hot broth or syrup. Made from glutinous rice flour mixed with water, they may be plain or filled with sweet pastes such as black sesame, peanut, red bean or jujube, and are sometimes coloured.

Festivals

Tangyuan are traditionally eaten during the Lantern Festival (Yuanxiao), which closes the Chinese New Year celebrations on the 15th day of the first lunar month — hence their alternative northern name, yuanxiao, though northern yuanxiao are made by a different dry-rolling method. In southern China tangyuan are also eaten at the winter solstice (Dongzhi) and at weddings and family reunions, their round shape symbolising wholeness, unity and completeness.

Preparation and variations

Southern tangyuan are formed by wrapping filling in a flattened disc of rice dough, then boiling until they float; they are served in the cooking water, a clear sweet soup, or fermented rice wine soup. Modern variations include savoury meat-filled versions from Ningbo, fried tangyuan, and commercial frozen products with fillings such as chocolate or fruit.
