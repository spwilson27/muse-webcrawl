Table d'hôte

In restaurant terminology, a table d'hôte (French pronunciation: [tablə dot]; lit. "host's table") menu is a menu where multi-course meals with only a few choices are charged at a fixed total price. Such a menu may be called prix fixe ("fixed price"). The terms set meal and set menu are also used.

The cutlery on the table may also be set for all of the courses. Food is served by a waiter or waitress. The opposite of table d'hôte is à la carte, where the guest orders individually priced dishes from a menu.

Origin

The term derives from the practice of innkeepers in France serving a single communal meal at a fixed time and price to all guests seated together at the host's own table. In early nineteenth-century Paris, eating establishments offered such set meals at specified hours; diners who arrived late risked missing courses.

Usage

Table d'hôte menus are common in hotels, traditional restaurants and at banquets and special occasions, where a fixed price covers several courses with a limited choice at each course. Early-bird specials and theatre menus are modern forms of the practice. In some countries the term is also used for a set meal included in the price of accommodation, as in the half-board tariff of a hotel.
