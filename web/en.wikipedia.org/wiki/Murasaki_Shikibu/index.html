Murasaki Shikibu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Murasaki Shikibu at her desk in Ishiyama Temple, in a later Japanese painting: the court lady writing by moonlight]

Murasaki Shikibu (c. 973 – c. 1014 or 1025) was a Japanese novelist, poet and lady-in-waiting at the Heian imperial court, author of The Tale of Genji, widely recognised as a masterpiece of world literature and often called the world's first novel. Her real name is unknown — 'Murasaki' comes from a character in her own tale, 'Shikibu' from her father's ministry post.

Born into the Fujiwara clan's scholarly branch, she learned Chinese — the forbidden language of male learning — by listening at her brother's lessons, to her father's regret that she was not a son. Widowed young, she was summoned around 1005 to serve Empress Shōshi, in whose salon she wrote much of Genji and kept the diary (The Murasaki Shikibu Diary) that records court life and her cool view of her rival Sei Shōnagon, author of The Pillow Book.

Works and standing

Her poems (some 100 in her collection) and the Diary show the same eye as the novel: exact about dress, weather and feeling, laced with melancholy about impermanence. Nothing certain is known of her last years or the date of her death — a vanishing typical of Heian women writers, whose works outlived their names.

Legacy

Murasaki is a national icon in Japan — her image appeared on the 2,000-yen note — and Genji's millennium in 2008 was celebrated worldwide. Feminist and literary histories alike place her among the founders of the novel, centuries before the form's European career.
