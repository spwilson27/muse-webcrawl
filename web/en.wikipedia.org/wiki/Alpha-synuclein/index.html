Alpha-synuclein (α-synuclein) is a protein that, in humans, is encoded by the SNCA gene. It is abundant in the brain, concentrated at the tips of neurons in specialized structures called presynaptic terminals, and is also found in smaller amounts in the heart, muscle, and other tissues. Although its normal function is not fully understood, it is thought to play a role in regulating synaptic vesicle trafficking and neurotransmitter release.

Structure

Alpha-synuclein is a small protein of 140 amino acids. It is intrinsically disordered in solution but adopts an alpha-helical structure when bound to membranes. Its name derives from its original identification in synaptic vesicles and the cell nucleus ("syn" + "nuclein"). The protein has three regions: an amphipathic N-terminal region that binds lipids, a central hydrophobic region (the NAC domain) that promotes aggregation, and an acidic C-terminal tail.

Role in disease

Alpha-synuclein is the principal component of Lewy bodies, the abnormal protein aggregates that are the pathological hallmark of Parkinson's disease and dementia with Lewy bodies; related inclusions in glial cells characterize multiple system atrophy. Collectively these disorders are called synucleinopathies. Point mutations in SNCA and multiplications of the gene cause rare inherited forms of Parkinson's disease. Aggregated alpha-synuclein is thought to spread between neurons in a prion-like manner, and the protein is a major target for experimental disease-modifying therapies and for diagnostic seed-amplification assays.
