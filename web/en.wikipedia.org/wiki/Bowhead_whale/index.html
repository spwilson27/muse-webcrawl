Bowhead whale

Bowhead whale
Balaena mysticetus
Baleen whale of Arctic waters
Lifespan: potentially over 200 years

The bowhead whale (Balaena mysticetus) is a species of baleen whale belonging to the family Balaenidae, the only living member of its genus. Named for its massive bow-shaped skull, which can be used to break through Arctic sea ice, it lives year-round in Arctic and subarctic waters — unique among baleen whales, which otherwise migrate.

Biology

Bowheads have the largest mouth of any animal, with baleen plates up to about 4 metres long used to filter krill and other invertebrates. Their blubber, the thickest of any whale, insulates them in icy water. They are also remarkable for longevity: estimates from recovered stone harpoon points and amino-acid racemisation suggest individuals may live over 200 years, making them among the longest-lived mammals. Bowheads are highly vocal, producing complex, varied songs during the breeding season.

Conservation

Intensive commercial whaling from the 1600s to the early 1900s devastated populations, especially in the North Atlantic, where stocks remain small. Western Arctic populations have recovered substantially under protection, and the species is listed as Least Concern globally, while some subpopulations remain endangered. Alaska Native communities hunt bowheads under a regulated subsistence quota of great cultural importance.
