The United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, branded as Team GB, has competed at every modern Olympic Games, beginning with the first Games in Athens in 1896, and is one of only a handful of nations to have done so. The team is organized by the British Olympic Association (BOA), founded in 1905.

History

Great Britain is the only team to have won at least one gold medal at every Summer Olympics. London has hosted the Summer Games three times — in 1908, 1948, and 2012 — more than any other city. The 2012 London Games saw Britain finish third in the medal table with 29 gold medals, and at Rio 2016 the team finished second overall, its best result of the modern era, with 27 golds.

Medal record

Across the Summer Olympics, British athletes have won around 900 medals, with particular strength historically in athletics, rowing, sailing, cycling, and boxing. In track cycling and rowing, Team GB dominated several recent Games. At the Winter Olympics, Britain's medal count is far smaller, with successes concentrated in curling, skeleton, and figure skating.

Composition

Athletes from England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland compete together as Great Britain, although in some sports (notably football outside the Olympics) the home nations compete separately. Northern Irish athletes may choose to represent either Great Britain or Ireland.
