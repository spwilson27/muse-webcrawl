Chain Home Low (CHL), officially known as AMES Type 2, was a British early-warning radar system of the Second World War, developed to complement the original Chain Home stations by detecting low-flying aircraft that the earlier system could not see.

Development

Chain Home operated at low frequencies (20–30 MHz), which gave it a blind zone near the ground: aircraft flying below a few thousand feet could approach under its coverage. To close this gap, British scientists led by teams at Bawdsey Research Station and later the Telecommunications Research Establishment adapted techniques from naval and airborne radar work. CHL operated at about 200 MHz (1.5-metre wavelength), using a rotating antenna array mounted on a gantry or tower, which allowed operators to determine an aircraft's range and bearing, and roughly estimate its height.

Service

The first CHL stations entered service around 1939–1941 and eventually ringed much of the British coast. CHL became the backbone of low-altitude reporting during the Battle of Britain period and afterwards, feeding plots into the Fighter Command control system. It was progressively supplemented and replaced by the more advanced Chain Home Extra Low (CHEL) and centimetric sets such as the AMES Type 7 ground-controlled interception radar. Many CHL sites were later reused for postwar ROTOR air-defence radar installations.
