Long gun

A long gun is a category of firearm with a long barrel. In small arms, a long gun or longarm is generally designed to be held by both hands and braced against the shoulder, in contrast to a handgun, which can be fired while being held with a single hand. In the context of cannons and mounted firearms, an artillery long gun is contrasted with a howitzer or carronade.

Small arms

Long guns include rifles, shotguns, carbines, muskets and personal defence weapons with stocks. The long barrel gives the projectile more time to be accelerated by the propellant gases, generally producing higher muzzle velocity and a longer sight radius for accuracy, while the stock allows the weapon to be steadied against the shoulder. Historically the archetypal long gun was the smoothbore musket carried by infantry from the 17th to the mid-19th century, succeeded by the rifled musket and then the bolt-action and semi-automatic rifle.

Legal definitions

Many legal systems define long guns by minimum barrel and overall lengths. In the United States, for example, federal law generally requires rifle barrels of at least 16 inches (41 cm) and shotgun barrels of at least 18 inches (46 cm), with an overall length of at least 26 inches (66 cm); shorter weapons fall into regulated categories such as short-barrelled rifles and shotguns under the National Firearms Act.

Naval and artillery use

In naval terminology a "long gun" was a standard cannon with a long barrel relative to its bore, valued for range and accuracy, as against the short-barrelled carronade used at close quarters.
