Monograph

A monograph is a specialist work of writing (in contrast to reference works) or exhibition on a single subject or an aspect of a subject, often by a single author or artist, and usually on a scholarly subject. In library cataloguing, a monograph is a non-serial publication complete in one volume or in a finite set of volumes, as distinct from a serial publication such as a magazine or journal.

In academia

In the humanities and some social sciences, the research monograph is a key form of scholarly communication and a milestone in an academic career: tenure review at many universities weighs a peer-reviewed monograph published by a university press heavily. Unlike a journal article, a monograph develops an extended argument across book-length chapters and is typically aimed at specialist readers. Since the late 20th century, scholars have debated a perceived "crisis of the monograph", as library purchases of individual titles have declined and print runs have shortened, with open-access publishing increasingly proposed as a remedy.

In biology

In taxonomy, a monograph is a comprehensive treatment of a taxon — for example of a genus or family — revising all known species, with keys, descriptions, and discussions of relationships and distribution.

In pharmacology and regulation

A drug monograph is a standardised document describing a medicine's properties, indications, dosage, and safety information; regulators such as Health Canada and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration publish monographs that define the conditions under which over-the-counter drug products may be marketed.
