Mignonette is a classic French condiment traditionally served with raw oysters. It consists of minced shallots macerated in vinegar — most often red wine vinegar or Champagne vinegar — seasoned with coarsely cracked black pepper. The sauce provides a sharp, acidic counterpoint to the briny sweetness of raw oysters on the half shell.

The name derives from the French word mignonnet, referring to the finely crushed peppercorns used in the original preparation; the term sauce mignonnette originally described a pepper seasoning rather than the vinegar-based oyster accompaniment known today.

Preparation

A basic mignonette is made by combining finely minced shallots with vinegar and freshly cracked black pepper, then allowing the mixture to rest for at least an hour — and preferably several — so the shallots soften and their flavour infuses the vinegar. The sauce is spooned in small amounts over each oyster immediately before eating.

Variations substitute rice wine vinegar, sherry vinegar, or citrus juice for part of the vinegar, and may add chopped herbs such as tarragon or chives, diced cucumber, or a pinch of sugar to soften the acidity. A granita-style frozen mignonette is sometimes scraped over oysters as a chilled garnish.

History

The pairing of vinegar and pepper with oysters dates back centuries in French coastal cookery, where acidic condiments were used both for flavour and, historically, as a presumed safeguard against spoilage. The modern shallot-based mignonette became standard in French and American oyster bars during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries and remains the default accompaniment to raw oysters in French bistros and seafood restaurants.
