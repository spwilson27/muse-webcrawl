Kiyonao Ichiki

Kiyonao Ichiki (16 October 1892 – 21 August 1942) was a colonel in the Imperial Japanese Army during the Second World War. He is best known for commanding the Ichiki Detachment in the Battle of the Tenaru on Guadalcanal, the first major Japanese land offensive against Allied forces in the Pacific.

Career

Ichiki had served in China and was an instructor at the Army Infantry School before his detachment — built around the 28th Infantry Regiment — was earmarked for the recapture of Midway and then diverted to Guadalcanal after the American landings of August 1942. On the night of 20–21 August 1942 his roughly 900 men launched frontal attacks across the Ilu River sandbar against entrenched U.S. Marines and were virtually annihilated. Ichiki died in the battle, reportedly by suicide as his command was destroyed.
