People smuggling

People smuggling (also called migrant smuggling) is the procurement, in order to obtain a financial or other material benefit, of the illegal entry of a person into a state of which the person is not a national or a permanent resident. The definition is drawn from the Protocol against the Smuggling of Migrants by Land, Sea and Air (2000), supplementing the United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime.

Distinction from human trafficking

People smuggling is distinguished from human trafficking in that smuggling is generally consensual — the smuggled person contracts with the smuggler for transport across a border — whereas trafficking involves coercion, deception, or exploitation of the victim. In practice the two can overlap when smuggled migrants are extorted, held for ransom, or forced into labour during or after the journey.

Scale and routes

Smuggling is a large transnational business operating along major migration routes, including across the Mediterranean Sea, through the Balkans, across the Mexico–United States border, and along maritime routes in Asia and the Pacific. Fees vary by route from hundreds to tens of thousands of dollars. Smuggling journeys are frequently dangerous: overcrowded and unseaworthy boats, sealed vehicles, and desert crossings cause thousands of deaths.

Enforcement

States criminalise the smugglers while the smuggling protocol provides that migrants themselves are not to be criminally liable merely for having been smuggled. Enforcement responses include border patrols, naval operations, intelligence-led disruption of smuggling networks, and international cooperation on returns and prosecutions.
