Franz von Waldeck

Franz von Waldeck (c. 1491 – 15 July 1553) was a German nobleman and Catholic cleric who became Prince-Bishop of Münster, Osnabrück and Minden in the early 1530s. His episcopate is chiefly remembered for his ambiguous early dealings with the Reformation and for the suppression of the radical Anabaptist regime in the Münster rebellion of 1534–35.

Born: c. 1491, Sparrenberg Castle
Died: 15 July 1553, Wolbeck, Münster
Offices: Prince-Bishop of Münster, Osnabrück and Minden
Known for: Role in the Münster rebellion

Life

Waldeck was a younger son of Count Philip II of Waldeck-Eisenberg. He pursued an ecclesiastical career, accumulating canonries and provostships. In 1530 he became Bishop of Minden, and in 1532 he was elected Bishop of Münster and Osnabrück, accumulating three north-west German prince-bishoprics. Early in his rule he tolerated evangelical preaching in Münster, perhaps hoping to secularise his territories, but pressure from the Emperor, his cathedral chapter and neighbouring princes forced a reversal.

The Münster rebellion

From 1534 the city of Münster fell under the control of radical Anabaptists led by Bernhard Rothmann and later Jan Matthys and John of Leiden, who proclaimed the city the "New Jerusalem". Waldeck laid siege to the city with imperial support. After a blockade of more than a year, his troops retook Münster on the night of 24–25 June 1535. The Anabaptist leaders were tortured and executed, their bodies displayed in iron cages hung from St. Lambert's Church — cages that still hang there today.

Later years

Waldeck thereafter pursued an oscillating religious policy, at times flirting with introducing the Reformation in his territories during the Schmalkaldic War era, before the Catholic restoration prevailed. His personal life was unconventional for a bishop: he lived openly with Anna Polmann, with whom he had several children. He died in 1553 and was buried in Münster Cathedral.
