Ampicillin/sulbactam is a fixed-dose combination medication of the common penicillin-derived antibiotic ampicillin and sulbactam, an inhibitor of bacterial beta-lactamase. It is sold under the brand name Unasyn among others. It is used to treat infections caused by susceptible bacteria, including those that have become resistant to ampicillin alone because they produce beta-lactamase enzymes.

Medical uses

The combination is used for skin and soft-tissue infections, intra-abdominal infections, gynaecological infections (including pelvic inflammatory disease), community-acquired pneumonia, diabetic foot infections, and animal or human bite wounds. It provides coverage against many Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria and anaerobes such as Bacteroides fragilis, though it is not active against Pseudomonas aeruginosa or MRSA.

Mechanism

Ampicillin kills bacteria by inhibiting cell-wall synthesis. Many bacteria resist it by producing beta-lactamases that hydrolyse the beta-lactam ring. Sulbactam irreversibly inhibits many of these enzymes, protecting ampicillin and restoring its activity; sulbactam also has intrinsic antibacterial activity against Acinetobacter species.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include pain at the injection site, diarrhoea, nausea and rash. As with other penicillins, serious allergic reactions including anaphylaxis can occur, and use can predispose to Clostridioides difficile infection.

Formulations

The parenteral product is supplied as a powder for injection in a 2:1 ratio of ampicillin to sulbactam. Sultamicillin is an orally administered mutual prodrug that is hydrolysed after absorption to release ampicillin and sulbactam.
