Amoxicillin

Class: Aminopenicillin antibiotic
Related drug: Ampicillin
Routes: Oral, intravenous
Status: WHO List of Essential Medicines

Amoxicillin is a widely used penicillin-class (aminopenicillin) antibiotic for treating bacterial infections. Closely related to ampicillin — the two drugs have nearly the same spectrum of activity — amoxicillin differs chiefly in being much better absorbed from the gut, so it is the preferred oral agent, while ampicillin is commonly used intravenously and in combination with sulbactam. Amoxicillin was developed by Beecham in the 1960s and marketed from 1972; it appears on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Amoxicillin and ampicillin treat infections caused by susceptible bacteria, including streptococcal pharyngitis, otitis media, sinusitis, pneumonia, urinary tract infections, and Helicobacter pylori infection (in combination therapy). Amoxicillin combined with the beta-lactamase inhibitor clavulanic acid (co-amoxiclav) extends cover to many beta-lactamase-producing organisms. The drugs act by inhibiting bacterial cell-wall synthesis and are bactericidal against dividing bacteria.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include nausea, diarrhoea and rash; a non-allergic rash is particularly associated with ampicillin/amoxicillin given during infectious mononucleosis. True penicillin allergy can cause anaphylaxis and is the major contraindication. Widespread use has driven resistance, including MRSA and beta-lactamase-producing strains against which plain aminopenicillins are ineffective.
