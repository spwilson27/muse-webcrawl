Amoxicillin/clavulanic acid, also known as co-amoxiclav, is a combination antibiotic medication used to treat a range of bacterial infections. It consists of amoxicillin, a penicillin-family antibiotic, together with clavulanic acid, a beta-lactamase inhibitor that protects the amoxicillin from destruction by bacterial enzymes. It is sold under brand names including Augmentin and appears on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

The combination is used for infections in which beta-lactamase-producing bacteria are suspected, including sinusitis, otitis media, pneumonia and other respiratory tract infections, urinary tract infections, skin and soft-tissue infections, dental infections and animal or human bites. Adding clavulanate extends amoxicillin's spectrum to many strains of Staphylococcus aureus, Haemophilus influenzae, Moraxella catarrhalis and anaerobes such as Bacteroides.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting and candidiasis (thrush). The combination is associated more often than amoxicillin alone with cholestatic liver injury, which is usually reversible but can be serious, and like other penicillins it can cause allergic reactions ranging from rash to anaphylaxis in sensitised patients.

History

Clavulanic acid was isolated from the bacterium Streptomyces clavuligerus by scientists at Beecham in the 1970s, and the combination was launched in 1981, becoming one of the most widely prescribed oral antibiotics in the world.
