Duchy of Cleves

The Duchy of Cleves (German: Herzogtum Kleve; Dutch: Hertogdom Kleef) was a state of the Holy Roman Empire emerging from the medieval county of Cleves and encompassing territories on both sides of the Lower Rhine, in present-day northwestern Germany and the eastern Netherlands. It was ruled by the House of La Marck, later passing to the Hohenzollern margraves of Brandenburg, and ultimately forming part of Prussia.

History

The Counts of Cleves are documented from the 11th century. In 1417, at the Council of Constance, Count Adolf II was raised to the rank of duke by Emperor Sigismund. Through the marriage of Duke John III to Maria of Jülich-Berg, the duchies of Cleves, Jülich and Berg and the counties of Mark and Ravensberg were united in 1521 in the personal union known as the United Duchies, under William V "the Rich" the most powerful Lutheran-influenced imperial state in northwestern Germany; William famously married—and rapidly divorced—Anne of Cleves, sister of his fourth wife's predecessor, becoming brother-in-law to Henry VIII of England.

When Duke John William died childless in 1609, the War of the Jülich Succession followed. By the Treaty of Xanten (1614), Cleves passed to the Elector of Brandenburg, an outcome definitively confirmed by the Treaty of Cleves of 1666. As part of Brandenburg-Prussia, Cleves was among the earliest Prussian possessions in the Rhineland.

Geography

The duchy straddled the Rhine around the towns of Cleves, Wesel, Kalkar, Emmerich and Xanten, with the county of Mark later associated. Its flat Lower Rhine landscape, cattle farming and trade along the river shaped its economy.
