Sonogashira coupling

The Sonogashira coupling is a cross-coupling reaction used in organic synthesis to form carbon–carbon bonds between a terminal alkyne and an aryl or vinyl halide (or triflate). It employs a palladium catalyst, typically with a copper(I) co-catalyst and an amine base, under mild conditions. The reaction was reported by Kenkichi Sonogashira and Nobue Hagihara in 1975 and has become one of the most widely used methods for constructing enynes and arylalkynes.

General scheme of the Sonogashira coupling.

Mechanism

The mechanism involves two interconnected catalytic cycles. In the palladium cycle, oxidative addition of the aryl halide to Pd(0) gives a Pd(II) intermediate. In the copper cycle, the terminal alkyne is deprotonated by the base in the presence of copper(I) to form a copper acetylide, which transfers the alkynyl group to palladium by transmetalation. Reductive elimination from the resulting cis-diorganopalladium species releases the coupled product and regenerates Pd(0). Copper-free variants exist and avoid the Glaser-type homocoupling of alkynes that copper and oxygen can promote.

Conditions and scope

Typical conditions use PdCl2(PPh3)2 or Pd(PPh3)4 with CuI in triethylamine or another amine, which acts as both base and solvent. Aryl iodides react most readily, followed by bromides and triflates; aryl chlorides require more active catalyst systems. The reaction tolerates many functional groups and proceeds at or near room temperature, making it valuable in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals, natural products, conjugated polymers, and molecular materials.

Applications

Sonogashira coupling has been used in the syntheses of numerous natural products and drugs, including the anticancer agent enediyne antibiotics chemistry, and in the preparation of compounds for materials science such as phenylene-ethynylene oligomers and polymers. A notable application is the synthesis of tazarotene, a retinoid drug, and of intermediates toward dynemicin.
