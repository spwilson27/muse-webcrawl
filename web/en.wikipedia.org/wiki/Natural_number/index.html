Natural number

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Counting objects: a row of apples numbered 1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

Natural numbers are the numbers used for counting and ordering: 1, 2, 3, and so on. Whether 0 is included varies by field — set theorists and computer scientists often include it, while number theorists traditionally start at 1.

Leopold Kronecker is supposed to have said that God made the natural numbers and all the rest is the work of man. Giuseppe Peano's axioms of 1889 gave them a rigorous foundation from which their arithmetic can be derived.

Foundations

In modern set theory natural numbers are constructed as sets: 0 is the empty set and each number is the set of all previous ones (the von Neumann construction). The principle of mathematical induction follows from their defining properties.

Natural numbers are the most basic number system, extended by the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers as new equations demand new solutions.
