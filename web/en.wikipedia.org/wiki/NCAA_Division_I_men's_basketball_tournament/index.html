NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament

The NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament, commonly branded March Madness, is a single-elimination tournament played each spring in the United States to determine the national champion of NCAA Division I men's college basketball. Featuring 68 teams, it is one of the most watched sporting events in the country and a fixture of American popular culture.

Format

Thirty-two teams qualify automatically by winning their conference tournaments; a selection committee awards the remaining 36 at-large bids and seeds all teams into four regions. Four play-in games — the "First Four" — reduce the field to 64. Teams then advance through the first and second rounds, the regional semifinals ("Sweet Sixteen") and finals ("Elite Eight") to the national semifinals and championship game, known collectively as the Final Four. A single loss eliminates a team, producing frequent upsets of highly seeded sides by "Cinderella" teams; in 2018 UMBC became the first 16-seed to defeat a 1-seed, Virginia.

History

The tournament was first held in 1939 with eight teams, an idea credited to Ohio State coach Harold Olsen, and Oregon won the inaugural title. It gradually eclipsed the rival National Invitation Tournament (NIT), expanding to 16 teams in 1951, 32 in 1975, 64 in 1985 and 68 in 2011. UCLA under John Wooden won ten championships between 1964 and 1975, including seven in a row; UCLA's eleven titles remain the record, followed by Kentucky with eight.

Culture and economics

"Selection Sunday", when the bracket is revealed, launches an annual national ritual of office bracket pools; the American Gaming Association routinely estimates billions of dollars wagered legally and informally. Television rights are correspondingly valuable: CBS and Turner extended their agreement through 2032 in a deal worth roughly $1.1 billion per year. The song "One Shining Moment" traditionally closes the championship broadcast.
