XTS

XTS most commonly refers to the XEX-based tweaked-codebook mode with ciphertext stealing, a mode of operation for block ciphers used for encrypting data on storage devices. The name may also refer to several other topics listed below.

Cryptography

XTS mode, standardized as IEEE Std 1619-2007 and approved by NIST in SP 800-38E, is designed for disk encryption. It combines the XEX (XOR–Encrypt–XOR) construction with ciphertext stealing, which allows data units that are not a multiple of the cipher block size to be handled. Each block is encrypted with a "tweak" derived from its logical position (such as sector number), so identical plaintext blocks at different locations produce different ciphertext. XTS-AES uses two independent AES keys. It is the mode used by most full-disk encryption systems, including BitLocker, FileVault, and dm-crypt/LUKS.

Other uses

XTS may also refer to the Cadillac XTS, a full-size luxury sedan produced by General Motors from 2012 to 2019; to XTS (band) and other musical or commercial names; and to the ICAO code for various aviation-related entities.
