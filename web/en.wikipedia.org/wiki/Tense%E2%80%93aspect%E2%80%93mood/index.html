Tense–aspect–mood

Tense–aspect–mood (abbreviated TAM) is a group of grammatical categories that, taken together, are common ways languages mark the time, duration, completion and factuality of an event described by a verb.

The three categories

Tense locates an event in time (past, present, future). Aspect describes the internal temporal structure of the situation — whether it is completed (perfective), ongoing (imperfective/progressive), habitual and so on. Mood (modality) expresses the speaker's attitude toward the proposition — whether it is presented as real (indicative), desired or commanded (imperative, subjunctive), possible or conditional.

In language typology

Languages differ in which TAM distinctions they grammaticalise and how they fuse them into single markers. In many languages a single affix expresses a combination of tense, aspect and mood, making TAM a standard framework in comparative linguistics.
