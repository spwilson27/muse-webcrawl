Squib load

A squib load, also known as a squib round, pop and no kick, or simply a squib, is a firearm malfunction in which a fired projectile does not carry enough force to exit the barrel and thus becomes stuck. This type of malfunction can be extremely dangerous, because failing to notice that the projectile has become stuck in the barrel may result in another round being fired directly into the obstructed barrel, resulting in a catastrophic failure of the weapon's structural integrity.

Causes

Squib loads occur when the propellant charge in a cartridge is insufficient, absent, or degraded. A common cause is a cartridge loaded with a primer but little or no powder — the primer alone may generate just enough pressure to drive the bullet partway into the barrel. Contaminated powder, improper handloading, and deteriorated ammunition can also produce squibs.

Signs and response

A squib often produces a noticeably weaker report (a soft "pop"), reduced or absent recoil, and may fail to cycle a semi-automatic action. Shooters are trained to stop firing immediately when a shot sounds or feels abnormal, keep the muzzle pointed in a safe direction, unload the firearm, and check the bore for an obstruction. A lodged bullet must be removed, typically by a gunsmith or with a suitable rod driven carefully from the muzzle end, before the firearm is used again.

Firing a subsequent round into an obstructed barrel can cause the barrel to bulge or burst, potentially injuring the shooter and bystanders.
