The Basilica of the National Shrine of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary, also called the Baltimore Basilica, was the first Roman Catholic cathedral built in the United States after the country's founding. Designed by Benjamin Henry Latrobe, architect of the United States Capitol, it is considered a masterpiece of early American neoclassical architecture.

History

Construction began in 1806 under John Carroll, the first American bishop, and the cathedral was dedicated in 1821. Latrobe's design features a domed cruciform plan, an Ionic portico, and innovative double-shell dome construction with a lantern admitting light to the sanctuary. The basilica served as the cathedral of the Archdiocese of Baltimore until the Cathedral of Mary Our Queen opened in 1959; it remains a co-cathedral.

Significance

The basilica hosted historic events in American Catholicism, including the funeral of Charles Carroll of Carrollton, the last surviving signer of the Declaration of Independence. It was designated a minor basilica in 1937 and a National Historic Landmark in 1969. A major restoration completed in 2006 returned the interior to Latrobe's original vision, reopening clerestory windows that had been blocked for decades.
