Walpi, Arizona

Walpi is a Hopi village on First Mesa in Navajo County, Arizona, United States. Situated on a narrow promontory at the southern end of the mesa, at an elevation of about 6,200 feet (1,890 m), it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited villages in the United States, with a history of occupation extending over a thousand years.

History

The Hopi people had lived at the base of First Mesa before moving to the mesa top around 1700, following the Pueblo Revolt of 1680 against the Spanish, to gain a more defensible position. The present village of Walpi was built on the rocky spur after that move; its stone houses rise in tiers, at points several stories high, directly from the bedrock. The older settlement at the foot of the mesa is known as Old Walpi.

Community

Walpi and its neighbouring First Mesa villages — Sichomovi and Hano (Tewa Village) — retain a strongly traditional Hopi way of life. For much of the 20th century, Walpi had no running water or electricity, and it remains among the most traditional of the Hopi villages. Access is restricted, and photography is not permitted.

Ceremonies

First Mesa is noted for its katsina dances and for the winter Kachina ceremonies held in its villages. Walpi also has a tradition of pottery, most famously associated with the potter Nampeyo (c. 1860–1942) of Hano, whose revival of ancient Sikyatki polychrome designs influenced Pueblo pottery across the Southwest.
