The People's Party, also known as the Populist Party, was a left-wing agrarian political party in the United States in the late 19th century. It grew out of the Farmers' Alliance and the Knights of Labor, and campaigned against the power of railroads, banks, and monopolies on behalf of farmers and labourers.

Omaha Platform

The party's 1892 Omaha Platform called for the free and unlimited coinage of silver, a graduated income tax, government ownership of railroads and telegraphs, the direct election of senators, and an eight-hour workday. Its presidential candidate James B. Weaver won over a million votes and 22 electoral votes in 1892.

Fusion and decline

In 1896 the Populists backed the Democratic nominee William Jennings Bryan, whose "Cross of Gold" campaign centred on free silver. Bryan's defeat and the party's fusion with the Democrats split the movement, and the People's Party declined rapidly, formally dissolving in 1909. Many of its proposals were later enacted during the Progressive Era and the New Deal.
