Clickwrap

A typical clickwrap "I agree" prompt.

A clickwrap agreement (also called a clickthrough agreement) is a digital prompt that gives a user the opportunity to accept or decline a digitally mediated policy, such as terms of service or a software license, typically by clicking an "I agree" button after the terms are presented. The name derives from shrinkwrap licenses, in which accepting terms was implied by tearing the plastic wrap on a software box.

Enforceability

Courts in the United States have generally upheld clickwrap agreements where the user had reasonable notice of the terms and manifested assent by an affirmative click, for example in ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg (1996) for shrinkwrap terms and subsequent cases applying similar reasoning online. By contrast, browsewrap arrangements—where terms are merely posted via a hyperlink without any required act of assent—have fared worse, as in Specht v. Netscape, because users may lack notice. Hybrid "scrollwrap" and "sign-in wrap" designs fall between these poles, and their enforceability turns on the conspicuousness of the notice and the clarity of the assent mechanism.

Design considerations

Best practice includes presenting terms before the click, requiring the user to scroll through them or at least making the full text easily accessible, using explicit assent language adjacent to the button, and recording evidence of acceptance (version, timestamp, and user identifier). Consumer-protection and privacy laws in various jurisdictions may restrict certain clauses regardless of assent.
