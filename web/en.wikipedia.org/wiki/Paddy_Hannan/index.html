Patrick "Paddy" Hannan (1840 – 4 November 1925) was an Irish-born Australian prospector whose discovery of gold near Mount Charlotte in Western Australia in 1893 triggered the Kalgoorlie gold rush and led to the founding of the town of Kalgoorlie, one of the richest goldfields in the world.

Life

Hannan was born in Quin, County Clare, Ireland, and emigrated to Australia in 1863, working as a labourer and prospector in Victoria, New South Wales, New Zealand, and eventually Western Australia. By the early 1890s he was prospecting in the Coolgardie area, then the scene of a major rush.

The Kalgoorlie discovery

In June 1893, according to the traditional account, Hannan and his partners Thomas Flanagan and Daniel Shea found gold near Mount Charlotte after one of their horses cast a shoe and the party stopped to search for it. Hannan travelled to Coolgardie to register the reward claim on 17 June 1893, triggering a rush of thousands of prospectors to the field. The claim lay at the northern end of what became known as the Golden Mile, considered the richest square mile on Earth, which produced enormous quantities of gold over the following century. The townsite was named Kalgoorlie; its main street is Hannan Street.

Later life and legacy

Hannan left the goldfields within a few years and retired to Brunswick, Melbourne, on a government pension of £150 per year granted in recognition of his discovery. He died there in 1925. A statue of Hannan, erected in 1929, stands on Hannan Street in Kalgoorlie and is a well-known landmark; a replica water bag hangs from it, commemorating the water scarcity that plagued the early field.
