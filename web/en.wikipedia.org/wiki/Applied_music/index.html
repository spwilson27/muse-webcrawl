Applied music

Applied music (also called applied lessons or applied study) is a term used in music education, particularly in colleges, conservatories, and universities, for the individual or small-group study of a musical instrument, voice, composition, or conducting with a specialist teacher. In degree programmes such as the Bachelor of Music, applied music forms the core performance component: students receive regular one-to-one lessons on their principal instrument, prepare repertoire, develop technique and musicianship, and are assessed in performances and end-of-term juries before a faculty panel.

Applied music lessons are the individual, studio-based core of conservatory training.

In degree programmes

Applied study is distinguished from academic coursework such as music theory, musicology, and aural skills: where those classes are taught in lecture or seminar formats, applied lessons are studio based and credit-bearing, typically meeting weekly for 30–60 minutes over a term. Students are usually required to give recitals — junior and senior recitals are milestones in many performance degrees — and to participate in ensembles such as orchestras, bands, choirs, or chamber groups as a complement to individual instruction. Entrance to applied study normally requires an audition, and continuation depends on jury examinations each semester.

Content and approach

Lessons address technique (scales, études, exercises), repertoire appropriate to the student's level, sight-reading, interpretation and style, and performance practice. Teachers assign practice goals and repertoire lists; students are expected to practise daily. The tradition descends from the master-apprentice model of European conservatories founded in the nineteenth century, and the phrase "applied" reflects its original contrast with theoretical or "speculative" music study — the practical doing of music rather than its abstract study.

Related usage

In some contexts, "applied music" also denotes music courses designed for non-majors (applied lessons open to the wider student body) or, outside academia, music composed or used for a specific function, though the educational meaning is the dominant one in North American higher education. Adjunct and studio faculty who teach applied lessons are often professional performers engaged specifically for their instrumental or vocal expertise.
