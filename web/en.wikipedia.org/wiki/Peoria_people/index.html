Peoria people

Peoria (Peoria: Piimiyaki)
Regions: Illinois (historically); Oklahoma (present)
Languages: Miami–Illinois (historically), English
Related groups: Miami, Kaskaskia, Cahokia, Tamaroa

The Peoria were one of the peoples of the Illinois Confederacy

The Peoria are a Native American people, historically one of the constituent nations of the Illinois Confederacy (Illiniwek), who inhabited the Illinois River valley in what is now the US state of Illinois. The city of Peoria, Illinois, takes its name from them. Today their descendants are enrolled in the federally recognised Peoria Tribe of Indians of Oklahoma, headquartered in Miami, Oklahoma.

History

French explorers and missionaries, including Jacques Marquette and Louis Jolliet in 1673, encountered the Peoria near the Illinois River, where the large village of Kaskaskia (the Grand Village of the Illinois) stood near present-day Starved Rock. The Illinois peoples were farmers, hunters of bison, and traders, but their numbers were devastated by disease and by wars with the Iroquois and other nations during the Beaver Wars of the late 17th century.

Through a series of treaties in the early 19th century, the Peoria ceded their lands in Illinois and were removed westward — first to Missouri and Kansas, and finally, in the 1860s, to Indian Territory (Oklahoma), where they consolidated with the Kaskaskia, Cahokia, Tamaroa, and the allied Wea and Piankeshaw. The Peoria language, a dialect of Miami–Illinois, ceased to be spoken fluently in the 20th century but is the subject of community revitalisation efforts.
