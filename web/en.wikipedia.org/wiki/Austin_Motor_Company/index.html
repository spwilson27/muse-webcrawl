Austin Motor Company

The Austin Motor Company Limited was a British manufacturer of motor vehicles, founded in 1905 by Herbert Austin in Longbridge, Birmingham. It merged with Morris Motors Limited in 1952 to form the British Motor Corporation (BMC), becoming one of Britain's largest car makers. The Austin name was used on cars until 1987, after the marque passed through British Leyland and the Rover Group. The Austin marque and its Longbridge factory played a central role in the British motor industry for most of the twentieth century.

History

Early years

Herbert Austin had managed the Wolseley Sheep Shearing Machine Company's car division before setting up on his own at Longbridge in 1905. Early Austins were large, expensive cars, but after the First World War the company moved downmarket.

The Austin Seven

The Austin Seven, launched in 1922, was a milestone: an affordable small car that brought motoring to a mass market in Britain and was built under licence abroad, including in Germany (by BMW) and Japan. It saved the company and defined its interwar success.

BMC and after

Austin merged with its great rival Morris in 1952 to form BMC. Under BMC and its successors, Austin models included the A30 and A35, the 1100/1300 range — Britain's best-selling car of the 1960s — and later the Allegro, Maxi, Metro, Maestro and Montego. The famous Mini was sold under both Morris and Austin names. The Austin badge was retired in 1987.
