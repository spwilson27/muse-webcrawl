Goddess of Liberty (Texas State Capitol)

The Goddess of Liberty is the statue crowning the dome of the Texas State Capitol in Austin, Texas. Standing nearly 16 feet (about 4.9 m) tall and holding a gilded Lone Star aloft in her raised hand, she has surmounted the Capitol since 1888. The figure holds a sword in her other hand and wears a classical gown and a crown of stars.

History

The statue was installed in February 1888 as the Capitol neared completion. The original figure was cast in zinc — a common and economical material for statuary at the time — and painted white. Over nearly a century the metal corroded and the statue became structurally unsound.

In June 1985 the original was removed by helicopter in a widely watched operation. A replica, cast in aluminium from moulds taken from the original and funded partly by public donations — schoolchildren across Texas contributed pennies to the restoration campaign — was hoisted into place on 14 June 1986. The replica weighs far less than the original and carries a new coat of white paint with a gilded star.

The original Goddess of Liberty was conserved and is now displayed indoors at the Bullock Texas State History Museum in Austin, where visitors can view her at close range.

Symbolism

The figure represents Liberty, consistent with the Capitol's classical iconography, and the star in her hand invokes the Lone Star of Texas. Together with the height of the dome — deliberately built taller than the United States Capitol — the statue is a well-known symbol of Texas pride and statehood.
