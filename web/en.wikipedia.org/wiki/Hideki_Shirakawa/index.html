Hideki Shirakawa

Hideki Shirakawa (born August 20, 1936) is a Japanese chemist best known for the discovery and development of conductive polymers. He shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2000 with Alan MacDiarmid and Alan Heeger "for the discovery and development of conductive polymers."

Work

At the Tokyo Institute of Technology, Shirakawa developed a method of synthesising polyacetylene as free-standing films; famously, an accidental thousand-fold excess of catalyst produced silvery, lustrous films rather than an ill-defined powder. In 1976–77, working with MacDiarmid and Heeger in the United States, he showed that doping polyacetylene with iodine vapour increased its electrical conductivity by many orders of magnitude, opening the field of plastic electronics.

Career

Shirakawa spent most of his career at the University of Tsukuba, where he became professor in 1982 and later professor emeritus. Conductive polymers now underpin technologies such as organic light-emitting diodes, flexible displays, and antistatic coatings.
