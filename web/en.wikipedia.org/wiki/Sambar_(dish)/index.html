Sambar (dish)

TypeLentil stew
Place of originIndia
RegionSouth India
Main ingredientsToor dal, tamarind, vegetables, sambar powder
ServedHot, with rice, idli, dosa or vada

Sambar is a lentil-based vegetable stew, cooked with pigeon pea (toor dal) and tamarind broth, originating from South India. It is popular in the cuisines of Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, and is also widely eaten in Sri Lanka and in Indian diaspora communities worldwide.

Preparation

Sambar is made with one or more vegetables such as drumstick, carrot, pumpkin, okra, brinjal (eggplant), tomato, shallots and radish, cooked with boiled and mashed pigeon peas and a souring agent, usually tamarind pulp. The dish is flavoured with sambar powder, a coarse spice mix of roasted lentils, dried red chillies, fenugreek, coriander seeds, turmeric and curry leaves. It is finished with a tempering (tadka) of mustard seeds, cumin, dried chillies, curry leaves and asafoetida fried in oil or ghee.

History and legends

One popular legend attributes the dish to the Maratha court of Thanjavur in the 17th or 18th century, prepared for or named after Sambhaji, son of Shivaji. Whatever its precise origin, lentil-and-tamarind stews have a long history in Tamil cuisine, and sambar became a defining element of the South Indian meal, served at breakfast with idli, dosa and vada, and at lunch poured over rice.

Variations

Regional varieties include Tamil Nadu sambar made with freshly ground paste, Kerala sambar with coconut, Karnataka's sweeter huli with a hint of jaggery, and Udupi sambar served in temple cuisine. Tiffin sambar, served in hotels with breakfast items, is typically thinner and sometimes made with moong dal.

Nutrition

Sambar combines protein-rich lentils with a variety of vegetables and is generally regarded as a nutritious dish, providing fibre, vitamins and plant protein with modest fat when tempering is restrained.
