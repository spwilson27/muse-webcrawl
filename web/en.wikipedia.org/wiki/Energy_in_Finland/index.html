Energy in Finland

Olkiluoto Nuclear Power Plant

Energy in Finland describes energy production, consumption and policy in Finland. Because of its cold climate, energy-intensive industry (pulp and paper, metals, chemicals) and dispersed population, Finland has high per-capita energy consumption. It has no domestic fossil fuel reserves; its resources are nuclear power, hydropower, wind, and biomass — above all wood fuels from its extensive forests — supplemented by peat.

Electricity

Finland's electricity comes mainly from nuclear power, hydropower, wind and biomass-based combined heat and power. The nuclear fleet consists of two units at Loviisa (VVER reactors) and three at Olkiluoto; Olkiluoto 3, a 1,600 MW EPR, entered regular operation in 2023 after long delays, substantially raising nuclear's share to around 40 percent of generation. Wind power has grown rapidly to become one of the largest sources. Finland is part of the Nord Pool electricity market and is interconnected with Sweden, Norway, Estonia and (until imports ceased in 2022) Russia.

Heat

District heating covers about half of the heating market, increasingly fuelled by biomass and waste heat rather than coal and peat; Helsinki's Helen utility has closed its coal plants as the city moves away from fossil heating. A statutory ban on coal in energy production takes effect in 2029.

Policy

Finland's Climate Change Act sets a target of carbon neutrality by 2035, among the most ambitious in the world. Historically a large share of Finland's oil and gas came from Russia; after 2022 Finland rapidly diversified supplies, and an LNG terminal at Inkoo (the FSRU Exemplar) began operating in 2023. Finland also hosts the world's first deep geological repository for spent nuclear fuel, Onkalo, near Olkiluoto.
