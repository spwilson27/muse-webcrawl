Castlevania: Symphony of the Night is a 1997 action role-playing game developed and published by Konami for the PlayStation. Directed by Toru Hagihara, with Koji Igarashi acting as producer and assistant director, it is a direct sequel to Castlevania: Rondo of Blood and one of the most acclaimed entries in the Castlevania series.

Gameplay

The player controls Alucard, the half-vampire son of Dracula, as he explores Dracula's castle, which has reappeared five years after Richter Belmont defeated Dracula. Departing from the linear stage-based design of earlier Castlevania games, Symphony of the Night features a large, interconnected castle that the player explores freely, gaining new abilities — such as transforming into a bat, a wolf, or mist — that open previously unreachable areas. This exploration-driven structure, combined with role-playing elements such as experience points, levels, equipment, and spells, helped define the genre later known as "Metroidvania". A famous twist occurs halfway through the game: an inverted copy of the entire castle, which the player must also explore to reach the true ending.

Plot

Four years after Richter Belmont's victory over Dracula, Richter has mysteriously vanished, and Dracula's castle has returned far sooner than its usual hundred-year cycle. Alucard awakens from a self-imposed slumber to investigate. Inside the castle he encounters Maria Renard, who is searching for Richter, and eventually discovers that Richter has been bewitched by the dark priest Shaft, who intends to resurrect Dracula. Alucard frees Richter, confronts Shaft in the inverted castle, and destroys Dracula once more before departing as the castle crumbles.

Development and reception

The game featured detailed hand-drawn sprite artwork by Ayami Kojima and a celebrated orchestral and eclectic score by Michiru Yamane. Although it was initially a modest commercial success — 2D games were falling out of fashion in the early 3D era — it received near-universal critical acclaim and is now routinely ranked among the greatest video games ever made. It has been re-released on numerous platforms, including Xbox 360, PlayStation Portable, PlayStation 4, and mobile devices.
