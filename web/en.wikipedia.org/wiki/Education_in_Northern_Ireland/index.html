Education in Northern Ireland

Education in Northern Ireland differs from systems in other parts of the United Kingdom in structure, examinations and administration, education being a devolved matter for the Northern Ireland Assembly. It is overseen by the Department of Education and administered through the Education Authority, created in 2015.

Structure

Compulsory schooling begins at age 4 — the youngest starting age in Europe — and continues to 16. Primary education runs from Year 1 to Year 7 (ages 4–11). Secondary education runs from Year 8 to Year 12 (ages 11–16), with many pupils continuing to Years 13–14 for A-levels. Pupils take GCSEs at 16 and AS/A-levels at 17–18, mostly through the local exam board CCEA, though schools may use English boards.

Selection and school types

Northern Ireland retains a largely selective system: although the official 11-plus transfer test was abolished in 2008, most grammar schools admit pupils using unregulated entrance tests (such as the SEAG test introduced in 2023). Schools are divided principally into:

  • Controlled schools, managed by the Education Authority, historically attended mainly by Protestant children;
  • Catholic maintained schools, managed through the Council for Catholic Maintained Schools;
  • Voluntary grammar schools;
  • Integrated schools, which deliberately educate Catholic and Protestant children together — still a minority (around 7–8%) of pupils;
  • Irish-medium schools, teaching through the Irish language.

Curriculum

The Northern Ireland Curriculum, revised in 2007, organises learning into areas including language and literacy, mathematics and numeracy, the world around us, and personal development and mutual understanding.
