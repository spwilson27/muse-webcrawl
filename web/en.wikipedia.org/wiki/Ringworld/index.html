Ringworld

Ringworld is a 1970 science fiction novel by Larry Niven, set in his Known Space universe. It tells the story of Louis Wu, recruited on his 200th birthday for an expedition with the alien Nessus, a Pierson's Puppeteer, the Kzin speaker-to-animals Chmeee, and the human Teela Brown, to explore a vast artificial ring around a distant star.

The Ringworld

The Ringworld itself is a megastructure: a ring about 150 million kilometres in radius and 1.6 million kilometres wide, rotating to provide gravity, with inner surface area equal to some three million Earths, walled by thousand-mile-high rim walls and given day and night by shadow squares. It was built by the Pak Protectors and is made of the super-material scrith.

Reception and sequels

The novel won the Hugo, Nebula, and Locus Awards and became one of the most celebrated works of hard science fiction, though readers famously noted stability problems that Niven addressed in the sequel The Ringworld Engineers (1979). Further sequels include The Ringworld Throne and Ringworld's Children, plus prequels co-written with Edward M. Lerner. The concept has influenced later megastructures in fiction, from Iain M. Banks's Orbitals to the Halo game franchise.
