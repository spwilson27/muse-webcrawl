Lord's Cricket Ground, commonly known as Lord's, is a cricket venue in St John's Wood, London. Named after its founder, Thomas Lord, it is owned by Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC) and is the home of Middlesex County Cricket Club, the England and Wales Cricket Board and, until 2005, the International Cricket Council. It is widely referred to as the "Home of Cricket".

History

The present ground is the third established by Thomas Lord; the first two, opened in 1787 and 1811, were built over. The current site on the former Eyre Estate opened in 1814. The Victorian pavilion, completed in 1890, and the Grace Gates, erected in 1923 in memory of W. G. Grace, are among its best-known features. Lord's hosted its first Test match in 1884, between England and Australia.

Ground and museum

The ground has a pronounced slope of about 2.5 metres from north to south. Stands include the Grand Stand, the Compton and Edrich stands, and the futuristic Media Centre, opened in 1999. The MCC Museum at Lord's is one of the oldest sporting museums in the world and holds the original Ashes urn. Lord's has hosted matches at several Cricket World Cups, including the finals of 1975, 1979, 1983, 1999 and 2019.
