Harness racing

Harness racing is a form of horse racing in which horses race at a specific gait — the trot or the pace — pulling a two-wheeled cart called a sulky, occupied by a driver.

Breeds and gaits

Most harness races feature Standardbred horses, bred for either trotting (diagonal gait) or pacing (lateral gait). Pacers often wear hobbles to maintain gait. In continental Europe trotting predominates, sometimes under saddle in trot monté races in France.

Major races

Prominent events include the Hambletonian (United States), the Prix d'Amérique (France) and the Elitloppet (Sweden). Races are usually run over one mile (1.6 km) on oval tracks.
