Soviet (council)

A soviet (Russian: совет, literally "council") is a workers' council that originated in the Russian Empire in 1905 and became the foundational unit of government in the Soviet Union. Soviets were elected bodies of workers, peasants and soldiers, theorised by revolutionaries as a form of direct democracy and the dictatorship of the proletariat.

Origins

The first soviet was formed during the 1905 Revolution, notably the St. Petersburg Soviet of Workers' Deputies, chaired for a time by Leon Trotsky. In 1917 soviets sprang up across Russia alongside the Provisional Government, creating a situation of dual power. The Bolshevik slogan "All Power to the Soviets" preceded their seizure of power in the October Revolution, after which the All-Russian Congress of Soviets became nominally the supreme authority.

In the USSR

Soviets at local, regional and republican levels were organised in a pyramid topped by the Supreme Soviet of the Soviet Union. The 1936 and 1977 constitutions described the USSR as a state governed through soviets of people's deputies; in practice, real power lay with the Communist Party, and soviet elections offered a single approved candidate until competitive elections were introduced in 1989.
