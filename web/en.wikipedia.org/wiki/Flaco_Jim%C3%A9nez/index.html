Leonardo "Flaco" Jiménez (March 11, 1939 – July 31, 2025) was an American singer, songwriter and accordionist from San Antonio, Texas. He is regarded as the most celebrated practitioner of conjunto music, a traditional form of Tex-Mex music, and brought the diatonic button accordion to wide audiences through collaborations across genres.

Career

Jiménez came from a musical family: his father, Santiago Jiménez Sr., was a pioneering conjunto accordionist. By his twenties he was performing with Los Caminantes and recording extensively. In the 1970s he worked in New York and performed with artists such as Ry Cooder, whose album Chicken Skin Music (1976) featured him prominently. He later recorded with the Rolling Stones, Bob Dylan, Dwight Yoakam and many others, and was a member of the supergroups Texas Tornados and Los Super Seven.

Honours

Jiménez won multiple Grammy Awards, including a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2015, and was named a National Heritage Fellow by the National Endowment for the Arts. He remained an emblem of San Antonio's West Side musical heritage throughout his life.
