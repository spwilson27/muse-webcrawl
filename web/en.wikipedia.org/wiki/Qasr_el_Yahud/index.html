Qasr el Yahud

Qasr el Yahud
Location: Jordan River, near Jericho
Significance: Traditional site of the Baptism of Jesus
Access: Opened to pilgrims in 2011

Qasr el Yahud (Arabic: قصر اليهود, "Castle of the Jews") is the western, West Bank side of the traditional site associated with the Baptism of Jesus on the Jordan River, near Jericho and the Allenby Bridge crossing into Jordan. In Christian tradition the area is also identified with the Israelites' crossing of the Jordan into the Promised Land and with the ascension of the prophet Elijah.

History

Monasteries and churches were built around the site from the Byzantine period onward, and the Greek Orthodox Monastery of St John the Baptist nearby was given its castle-like appearance — hence the name "castle". After the 1967 Six-Day War the area became a closed military zone littered with mines; pilgrims were restricted for decades to the Israeli-administered site at Yardenit, further north. Qasr el Yahud was officially opened for visits in 2011 under Israeli military escort arrangements, and a major de-mining and restoration project, completed around 2020–2021, cleared thousands of mines from the church lands of the area.

Pilgrimage

The site, facing the Jordanian site of Al-Maghtas (Bethany Beyond the Jordan) across the river, now receives large numbers of pilgrims, especially around Epiphany and Theophany celebrations, when processions from rival denominations descend to the river.
