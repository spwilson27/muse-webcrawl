Ocean quahog

Scientific nameArctica islandica
FamilyArcticidae
ClassBivalvia
HabitatNorth Atlantic Ocean
Maximum ageOver 500 years

The ocean quahog (Arctica islandica) is a species of edible clam in the family Arcticidae. It is native to the North Atlantic Ocean and is harvested commercially as a food source. The species is one of the longest-living marine animals and the longest-living non-colonial animal known to science.

Individual ocean quahogs can live for several centuries. A specimen nicknamed Ming, dredged off the coast of Iceland in 2006, was initially calculated to be 405 years old; later analysis revised its age to approximately 507 years, making it the oldest non-colonial animal ever recorded. Ming was born around 1499, during the Ming dynasty of China, which gave the clam its nickname.

Distribution and habitat

The ocean quahog is found in the boreal waters of the North Atlantic, from Newfoundland and the eastern United States to Iceland, the British Isles, and the Baltic approaches. It lives buried in sandy or muddy seabeds at depths typically between about 8 and 400 metres, filtering plankton from the water column.

Longevity

Like other bivalves, the ocean quahog lays down annual growth rings in its shell, allowing researchers to determine its age by counting the rings, a technique called sclerochronology. The exceptional lifespan of Arctica islandica is attributed to its slow metabolism, low rate of cellular damage, and cold-water habitat. The species has become an important model organism for the study of ageing.

Fishery

The ocean quahog supports a significant commercial fishery in the United States and Iceland, where it is used in clam chowder, fried clam strips, and other processed seafood products. Because individuals grow very slowly and reproduce late in life, the fishery is carefully managed with quotas to avoid overexploitation.
