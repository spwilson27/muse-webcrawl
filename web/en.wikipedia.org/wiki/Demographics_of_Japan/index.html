Japan has a population of roughly 124 million, making it one of the most populous countries in the world, though its population has been declining since it peaked at about 128 million in 2008. Japan is the world's most aged society: more than 29 per cent of its population is aged 65 or over, the highest share of any country.

Population change

Japan's total fertility rate, about 1.2 to 1.3 children per woman, has been below replacement level since the mid-1970s. Deaths have outnumbered births every year since 2007, and annual population losses have reached several hundred thousand. The government projects continued decline through the 21st century, with the population possibly falling below 100 million by the 2050s.

Distribution and urbanisation

More than 90 per cent of Japanese live in urban areas. The population is heavily concentrated in the Pacific coastal belt from Tokyo through Nagoya to Osaka and Fukuoka, while rural prefectures face severe depopulation, with thousands of abandoned houses (akiya) and shrinking villages.

Ethnicity and immigration

Japan is ethnically homogeneous: ethnic Japanese form the overwhelming majority. Indigenous minorities include the Ainu of Hokkaido, recognised as an indigenous people in 2019, and the Ryukyuan peoples of Okinawa. Foreign residents number roughly 3 million (about 2.5 per cent of the population), the largest groups being Chinese, Koreans (including long-settled zainichi communities), Vietnamese, and Filipinos. Immigration has expanded gradually through technical trainee and specified skilled worker programmes in response to labour shortages.

Language and religion

Japanese is spoken by virtually the entire population. Shinto and Buddhism are the two major religions, though most Japanese describe themselves as non-religious while participating in rituals of both traditions.
