Kingdom of Navarre

The Kingdom of Navarre straddled the western Pyrenees between France and Spain.

The Kingdom of Navarre was a Basque kingdom that occupied lands on both sides of the western Pyrenees, alongside the Atlantic Ocean between present-day Spain and France. It emerged around 824 as the Kingdom of Pamplona, traditionally said to have been established by Íñigo Arista, who led a revolt against Frankish authority in the region.

History

The kingdom reached its greatest extent under Sancho III the Great in the early 11th century, when Navarre briefly dominated much of Christian Iberia. After Sancho's death in 1035, the realm was divided among his sons, and Navarre shrank to a modest inland kingdom wedged between the growing powers of Castile, Aragon, and France.

In 1512, Ferdinand II of Aragon invaded and conquered the Iberian portion of Navarre south of the Pyrenees, incorporating it into the Spanish crown, though it retained its own laws (fueros) and institutions for centuries. The smaller northern portion, Lower Navarre, remained independent and eventually passed to Henri IV of France, who united the Navarrese crown with the French monarchy in 1589–1620.

Legacy

Navarre survives today as an autonomous community of Spain, the Chartered Community of Navarre, with its capital at Pamplona. The chain of gold links on its coat of arms, said to derive from the Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa in 1212, also appears on the coat of arms of Spain.
