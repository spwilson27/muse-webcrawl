Digital Cinema Initiatives

Image: Digital cinema projection

Digital Cinema Initiatives, LLC (DCI) is a joint venture of major Hollywood motion picture studios, formed to establish a standard architecture for digital cinema systems. Its member studios are Disney, Paramount, Sony Pictures, Universal, and Warner Bros. (20th Century Fox was also a founding member).

History and specifications

DCI was founded in March 2002. In July 2005 it published the first version of the Digital Cinema System Specification, defining the Digital Cinema Package (DCP): a set of files containing image (JPEG 2000 codestreams in an X'Y'Z' colour space at 2K or 4K resolution), audio (uncompressed PCM), subtitle, and composition playlist data, wrapped in MXF files. The specification also mandates strong content encryption and forensic watermarking, with decryption keys delivered to exhibitors as Key Delivery Messages (KDMs).

Impact

The DCI specification became the worldwide basis for the conversion of cinemas from 35 mm film prints to digital projection, a transition substantially complete in most markets by the mid-2010s. SMPTE later standardised the DCP formats (the SMPTE DCP) based on DCI's work.
