Fuqi feipian

Fuqi feipian (Chinese: 夫妻肺片; pinyin: fūqī fèipiàn) is a popular Sichuan dish, served cold or at room temperature, consisting of thinly sliced beef and offal — typically beef tendon, tripe, tongue, and sometimes other cuts — dressed with a spicy, numbing sauce of chili oil, Sichuan pepper, and other seasonings, and garnished with peanuts, sesame seeds, and coriander.

Name and origin

The name literally means "husband-and-wife lung slices". The dish is associated with Chengdu in the 1930s, where a married couple, Guo Chaohua and Zhang Tianzheng, became famous for the beef offal slices they sold from a street cart. The character fei (“lung”) in the name is often explained as a substitution for a homophonous word meaning "waste" or discarded cuts, since beef lung itself is rarely used; the name celebrates the couple (fuqi, husband and wife) who transformed cheap scraps into a celebrated dish.

Preparation

The meats are braised in a seasoned master stock until tender, chilled, and sliced paper-thin. They are then tossed with a dressing of chili oil, Sichuan pepper oil or ground pepper, soy sauce, black vinegar, sugar, and garlic, and finished with crushed roasted peanuts, toasted sesame, and fresh coriander. The dish exemplifies the Sichuan flavour profile known as mala — hot and numbing — combined with savoury and aromatic notes.

Recognition

Fuqi feipian is one of the best-known Sichuan cold dishes and is served in Sichuan restaurants worldwide. In 2017 it was named a top appetizer in an American restaurant-industry award for dish of the year, bringing it wider attention in the United States.
