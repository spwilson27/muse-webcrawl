Co-op Food is a brand used for the grocery retail businesses of a number of British consumer co-operatives, principally The Co-operative Group, the UK's largest co-operative, together with independent societies such as Midcounties, Scotmid, Lincolnshire, and Central England. The Co-operative Group's food business operates around 2,400–2,600 stores, mostly small convenience stores, making Co-op the United Kingdom's sixth-largest grocery retailer by market share and its largest convenience operator.

History

Co-operative food retailing descends from the Rochdale Pioneers (1844) and the hundreds of local societies that once ran shops in nearly every British town. After decades of decline and consolidation, the movement unified branding under "The Co-operative Food" with the cloverleaf logo, and relaunched in 2016 with the revived 1968 "Co-op" cloverleaf identity and a membership scheme returning a percentage of own-brand spending to members and their communities.

Operations

Co-op stores emphasize convenience locations, extended hours, and own-brand ranges with prominent fairtrade and British-sourcing commitments; the Co-op was the first UK retailer to sell Fairtrade products widely and switched all its own-brand fresh meat to British-only. The Group supplies many independent societies through its buying arrangements and also wholesales to independent Co-op-branded stores (FRTS), and it operates rapid delivery partnerships and its own online service from local stores.
