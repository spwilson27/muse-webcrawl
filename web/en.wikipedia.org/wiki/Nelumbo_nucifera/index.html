Nelumbo nucifera, also known as the sacred lotus, Indian lotus, or simply lotus, is one of two extant species of aquatic plant in the family Nelumbonaceae. It is adapted to grow in the flood plains of slow-moving rivers and delta areas, and stands have survived in sediments for thousands of years. It is native to East Asia, South Asia and Southeast Asia, and is widely cultivated as an ornamental plant in water gardens.

The lotus is often confused with the water lilies of the genus Nymphaea, particularly Nymphaea caerulea, the "blue lotus." In fact, several older systems call the lotus a member of the Nymphaeaceae family. Botanically, N. nucifera is characterized by its distinctive receptacle — the flat-topped central "showerhead" containing the seeds.

Uses

All parts of the plant are edible. The seeds can be eaten raw or dried and popped like popcorn; in China they are made into a paste used in pastries such as mooncakes. The rhizomes, sold as lotus root, are a common vegetable in Asian cuisines, used in soups, deep-fried, stir-fried and braised dishes. The leaves are used as a flavouring and as a wrap for rice. Lotus seeds are famously long-lived: viable seeds more than 1,000 years old have been germinated.

Cultural and religious significance

The sacred lotus is the national flower of India and Vietnam. It holds deep significance in Hinduism and Buddhism, where it symbolizes purity, beauty, fertility, prosperity and spiritual awakening: the flower rises unstained from muddy water. In Hindu iconography, deities such as Lakshmi and Brahma are depicted seated on lotus flowers. In Buddhist art, the Buddha is often shown on a lotus pedestal, and the mantra "Om mani padme hum" invokes the jewel in the lotus.

Botany

The plant has round, waxy leaves up to 60 cm across that rise above the water on long stalks. Its surface exhibits extreme hydrophobicity — the "lotus effect" — owing to microscopic papillae coated in wax crystals, which has inspired biomimetic self-cleaning surfaces. Flowers are large, pink or white, and thermogenic, able to regulate their temperature to attract pollinating beetles.
