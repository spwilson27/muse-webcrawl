Grand National

The Grand National is a National Hunt horse race held annually at Aintree Racecourse near Liverpool, England. First run in 1839, it is the most famous steeplechase in the world, run over a distance of about 4 miles 2½ furlongs, with horses jumping 30 large fences over two laps.

The race

The course features formidable fences such as Becher's Brook, The Chair, and the Canal Turn. The race is a handicap, and its long distance and large field make it a severe test of stamina and jumping; famous winners include Red Rum, who won three times in the 1970s, and Tiger Roll, a back-to-back winner in 2018–19. The event draws an enormous television audience and is a centrepiece of the British sporting calendar.

Safety

The race has long attracted criticism from animal-welfare groups over horse fatalities. Following reviews, fences were modified, the start moved, and the maximum field reduced from 40 to 34 runners for the 2024 race.
