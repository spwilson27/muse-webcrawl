Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde

Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr HydeAuthor: Robert Louis Stevenson
Published: 5 January 1886
Publisher: Longmans, Green & Co.
Genre: Gothic novella

Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde is an 1886 Gothic novella by the Scottish author Robert Louis Stevenson. It follows Gabriel John Utterson, a London legal practitioner who investigates a series of strange occurrences between his old friend, Dr Henry Jekyll, and a murderous criminal named Edward Hyde.

Plot

Utterson is disturbed by Jekyll's will, which leaves everything to the repellent Hyde, who has assaulted a young girl and later murders the MP Sir Danvers Carew. After Jekyll secludes himself in his laboratory, his servant Poole seeks Utterson's help; breaking in, they find Hyde dead by suicide, dressed in Jekyll's clothes. Documents left by Jekyll and the late Dr Lanyon reveal the truth: Jekyll, seeking to separate his respectable self from his darker impulses, devised a potion that transformed him into Hyde, the embodiment of his evil side. The transformations grew spontaneous and uncontrollable, and, unable to reproduce the original impure salt used in the potion, Jekyll took his own life.

Themes and influence

The novella explores the duality of human nature, respectability and repression in Victorian society, addiction, and the divided self; it is often read through Freudian ideas of the id and superego, though it predates psychoanalysis. Stevenson conceived the story in a dream and wrote the first draft in a matter of days. It was an immediate success and has never been out of print. "Jekyll and Hyde" has entered the language as a byword for a person with sharply contrasting moral character, and the tale has been adapted scores of times for stage and screen, including the 1931 film for which Fredric March won an Academy Award.
