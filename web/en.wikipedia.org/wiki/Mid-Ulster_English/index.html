Mid-Ulster English

Mid-Ulster English is spoken across the central part of Ulster

Mid-Ulster English is the variety of English spoken in the central part of the province of Ulster in Ireland, including most of County Armagh, County Tyrone, County Londonderry/Derry and parts of neighbouring counties. It is the baseline dialect from which the other regional varieties of Ulster English — such as Ulster Scots-influenced speech in Antrim and Down — diverge. Mid-Ulster English derives principally from the English brought by settlers during the Plantation of Ulster in the 17th century, predominantly from the north of England, blended with Irish and Scots influences.

Features

Phonologically, Mid-Ulster English is rhotic (the /r/ is pronounced in all positions) and shows features such as the raising of /ɛ/ towards /ɪ/ before velars (so "beg" may sound like "big" to outsiders) and distinctive intonation patterns. Grammar retains older English forms and Irish-influenced constructions, including the "after" perfect ("I'm after eating my dinner" for "I have just eaten"), use of "whenever" for single past events, and habitual constructions in some areas.

Vocabulary

Characteristic vocabulary includes words from Irish and Scots such as "wee" (small), "craic" (fun, news), "messages" (groceries/errands), and "oxter" (armpit). The dialect was famously catalogued in dialect dictionaries and is regarded by linguists such as those working on the Tape-recorded Survey of Hiberno-English Speech as the most conservative mainstream variety of Hiberno-English.
