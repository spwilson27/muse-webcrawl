Tunisian Revolution

The Tunisian Revolution, also called the Jasmine Revolution or the Dignity Revolution, was an intensive campaign of civil resistance in Tunisia in 2010–2011 that led to the ousting of longtime president Zine El Abidine Ben Ali and sparked the wider Arab Spring uprisings across the Middle East and North Africa.

Background and spark

Ben Ali had ruled Tunisia since 1987 in an authoritarian system marked by corruption, unemployment, and political repression. On 17 December 2010, Mohamed Bouazizi, a street vendor in Sidi Bouzid, set himself on fire after police confiscated his cart and produce. His act ignited protests that spread from the impoverished interior to the capital, Tunis, drawing trade unions, lawyers, and young people mobilised partly through social media.

Fall of Ben Ali

Despite crackdowns that killed more than 300 people, protests intensified. On 14 January 2011, Ben Ali fled to Saudi Arabia after 23 years in power. An interim government followed, and in October 2011 Tunisia held free elections for a Constituent Assembly, won by the moderate Islamist Ennahda party.

Significance

Tunisia became the only Arab Spring country to complete a transition to a democratic constitution, adopted in 2014; its National Dialogue Quartet won the 2015 Nobel Peace Prize. The country nevertheless continued to face economic hardship and political instability in later years.
