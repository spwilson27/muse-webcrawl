Estádio das Antas

Estádio das Antas
Location: Porto, Portugal
Opened: 28 May 1952
Closed: 2003
Demolished: 2004
Capacity: up to c. 95,000 (original); about 50,000 at closure
Tenants: FC Porto (1952–2003)

The Estádio das Antas was a football stadium in Porto, Portugal, and the home ground of FC Porto from 1952 until 2003. It was officially known as the Estádio do Futebol Clube do Porto. At its peak it was the largest stadium in Portugal, before capacity reductions for safety in later decades.

History

The stadium was inaugurated on 28 May 1952 with a match against Benfica. Over the following half-century it staged Porto's greatest triumphs, including the 1987 European Cup final run (the final itself was played in Vienna) and numerous domestic titles. It also hosted matches of the 1991 FIFA World Youth Championship. A third tier added in the 1980s briefly made it one of Europe's largest grounds.

Replacement

FC Porto's last match at the Antas was played in 2003, after which the club moved to the newly built Estádio do Dragão, constructed nearby for UEFA Euro 2004. The old stadium was demolished in 2004, and its site was redeveloped with housing and the club's museum complex nearby.
