Albert of Prussia (German: Albrecht von Brandenburg-Ansbach; 1490–1568) was the 37th Grand Master of the Teutonic Order, who converted to Lutheranism and secularised the order's Prussian territory into the Duchy of Prussia, becoming its first duke in 1525. A member of the House of Hohenzollern, he ruled Prussia as a fief of the Polish Crown for over four decades.

Grand Master and war with Poland

Elected Grand Master in 1510, Albert sought to free the Order from its obligations to Poland, culminating in the Polish–Teutonic War of 1519–21, which ended inconclusively. On Martin Luther's advice, Albert resolved the impasse radically: by the Treaty of Kraków (1525) he dissolved the monastic state, paid homage to Sigismund I of Poland in the famous Prussian Homage, and received the territory as a hereditary secular duchy.

Duke of Prussia

Albert made the Duchy of Prussia the first state to adopt Lutheranism officially, reformed its church and schools, and in 1544 founded the University of Königsberg (the Albertina). He patronised printing, learning and theologians such as Andreas Osiander, whose doctrines later caused bitter controversy in the duchy. Albert died at Tapiau in 1568 and was succeeded by his son Albert Frederick.
