Luigi (Japanese: ルイージ, Hepburn: Ruīji) is a character from the Mario franchise, a video game series created by Japanese game designer Shigeru Miyamoto. Luigi is portrayed as a kind-hearted, cowardly Italian plumber, and the younger fraternal twin brother and sidekick of Mario. Luigi first appeared in the 1983 arcade game Mario Bros. as the character controlled by the second player, and he retains this role in many games.

Luigi, Mario's younger fraternal twin brother

Concept and creation

Luigi was created for the 1983 arcade game Mario Bros. as a palette swap of Mario, wearing green instead of red. The name "Luigi" is said to have been inspired by a pizza parlor near Nintendo of America's headquarters called "Mario & Luigi's". His name is also a pun: the Japanese word ruiji means "similar".

Characteristics

Luigi is physically taller and thinner than Mario, and wears a green shirt and hat with blue overalls. Although originally equal to Mario in ability, in later games he developed a distinct personality: nervous, cowardly and self-conscious, but brave when it counts. Luigi is shown to jump higher and farther than Mario, but with less traction. He is the protagonist of the Luigi's Mansion series, in which he must capture ghosts in haunted mansions using the Poltergust vacuum created by Professor E. Gadd.

Appearances

Luigi has appeared in nearly every Mario game, including mainline platformers such as Super Mario Bros. 2 and New Super Luigi U, spin-offs like Mario Kart, Mario Party and Super Smash Bros., and the Luigi's Mansion series. The year 2013 was promoted by Nintendo as the "Year of Luigi" to mark the character's 30th anniversary.
