Ronald Bilius "Ron" Weasley is a fictional character in J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter fantasy novel series. He is one of the three protagonists of the series, alongside Harry Potter and Hermione Granger, and a member of the Weasley family, a pure-blood wizarding family. Ron is first introduced in the first book, Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone (1997), as Harry's best friend and loyal companion throughout his years at Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry.

Character development

Rowling has described Ron as one of the characters she created "on the very first day". He is inspired in part by Rowling's own best friend from school. Ron is the sixth of seven children, and grows up feeling overshadowed by the achievements of his older brothers and by his famous best friend. His insecurities — about money, talent and recognition — are a recurring source of tension in the series, most notably in Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire and Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows.

Appearances

Ron meets Harry on the Hogwarts Express in Philosopher's Stone, and the two become fast friends after Ron's pet rat Scabbers and Harry's sweets break the ice in their compartment. Ron helps Harry through the challenges protecting the Philosopher's Stone, winning a game of wizard's chess at great personal risk. In later books he joins Dumbledore's Army, plays Keeper for the Gryffindor Quidditch team, becomes a prefect, and accompanies Harry and Hermione on the hunt for Voldemort's Horcruxes during Deathly Hallows. In the epilogue, set nineteen years after the Battle of Hogwarts, Ron is married to Hermione and works as an Auror before helping George run Weasleys' Wizard Wheezes.

Characterisation

Ron is characterised by his loyalty, humour and emotional perceptiveness, which often balance Harry's impulsiveness and Hermione's bookishness. He is afraid of spiders, a trait stemming from a childhood prank by his brother Fred, which becomes significant in Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets. Rowling has said that Ron's swearing and comic relief made him "very fun to write".

Reception

Ron is widely regarded as one of the most popular characters in the series. Rupert Grint's portrayal in the film adaptations was praised for capturing Ron's warmth and comic timing, although some readers felt the films transferred several of Ron's best moments to Hermione.
