Acoustic resonance

Acoustic resonance is a phenomenon in which an acoustic system amplifies sound waves whose frequency matches one of its own natural frequencies of vibration (its resonance frequencies). The term "acoustic resonance" is sometimes used to narrow mechanical resonance to the frequency range of human hearing, but in general acoustics is a theoretical and practical field covering vibration of all frequencies and media.

Resonant tubes

A tube of air is a classic example of an acoustic resonator. For a tube open at both ends, the resonant frequencies are harmonics of a fundamental whose wavelength is twice the length of the tube. For a tube closed at one end, only odd harmonics resonate, with the fundamental wavelength equal to four times the tube length. These principles govern the behaviour of wind instruments: an organ pipe, for example, sounds at the pitch determined by its effective length.

Resonance in musical instruments

Musical instruments exploit acoustic resonance in many ways. The body of a guitar or violin acts as a resonating cavity that amplifies the vibration of the strings. A tuning fork vibrates at a single sharp resonance, producing a nearly pure tone. The Helmholtz resonator — a vessel with a short neck, like an empty bottle — resonates at a frequency determined by the volume of the cavity and the dimensions of the neck; blowing across the mouth of a bottle produces this resonance.

Breaking a glass with sound

A well-known demonstration of acoustic resonance is breaking a wine glass with sound alone. If a sufficiently intense sound is sustained at the natural resonant frequency of the glass, the amplitude of its vibration grows until the stresses exceed the strength of the material and the glass shatters. The feat requires amplification and precise tuning to the individual glass's resonant frequency.

Applications

Acoustic resonance finds applications in ultrasonic cleaning, medical imaging, acoustic levitation, and nondestructive testing, where resonance frequencies reveal the internal structure or defects of an object. Acoustoelastic and resonant ultrasound spectroscopy are used to characterize the elastic properties of materials.
