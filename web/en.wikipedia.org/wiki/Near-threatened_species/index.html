Near-threatened species

A near-threatened species is a species which has been categorized as "Near Threatened" (NT) by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) as it is close to, or is likely to qualify for, a threatened category in the near future. Near Threatened is one of the categories of the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species, sitting between Least Concern and Vulnerable.

Criteria

Under the IUCN Red List Categories and Criteria (version 3.1), a taxon is Near Threatened when it has been evaluated against the criteria for Critically Endangered, Endangered and Vulnerable but does not qualify for any of them at present, while being close to qualifying or likely to qualify in the near future. Assessors are expected to give reasons for the classification, such as ongoing population decline, dependence on conservation measures, or a small or restricted population falling just short of the Vulnerable thresholds.

Context

Near-threatened species are not formally classified as "threatened" (a term reserved for Critically Endangered, Endangered and Vulnerable species), but the category serves as an early-warning flag for conservation planning. Taxa may move into or out of the category at each reassessment as new data become available or conditions change. Older Red List versions used the term "Lower Risk/near threatened" (LR/nt) within the Lower Risk category, which was replaced in the 2001 framework.

Well-known near-threatened species have included the jaguar, the plains zebra, and various species of albatross and penguin at different times, though listings change with each assessment cycle.
