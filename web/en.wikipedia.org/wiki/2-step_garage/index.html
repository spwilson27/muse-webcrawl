2-step garage, or simply 2-step, is a subgenre of UK garage electronic dance music that emerged in London in the late 1990s. Its name comes from its distinctive rhythm: a broken, syncopated drum pattern — originally produced by removing some of the four-to-the-floor kick drum beats of standard garage — giving the music a skipping, off-kilter groove.

Characteristics

Where UK garage typically kept a house-style four-on-the-floor kick with shuffled hats and snares, 2-step producers such as El-B and the Ghost collective dropped kicks to two per bar or dispensed with regular kicks entirely, letting the bassline and percussive swing carry the track. Songs often feature pitched-up, time-stretched R&B vocal fragments, deep sub-bass and tempos around 130–135 BPM. The sound flourished on pirate radio stations and in clubs such as the Velvet Rooms and at nights in venues across London and the South East.

Commercial peak and legacy

Around 1999–2001, 2-step crossed over to the charts: Artful Dodger's "Re-Rewind" featuring Craig David reached number 2 in the UK, and MJ Cole's "Sincere" was a top-20 hit and Mercury Prize-nominated album track. As the sound darkened, younger producers stripped it down into what became dubstep, while its MC-led London offshoot fed into grime. In later years, "future garage" artists such as Burial revived the 2-step rhythm, and the genre has enjoyed periodic revivals in UK club music.
