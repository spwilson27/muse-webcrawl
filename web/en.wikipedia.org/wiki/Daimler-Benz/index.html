Daimler-Benz AG was a German automotive and industrial company formed in 1926 by the merger of Daimler Motoren Gesellschaft (founded by Gottlieb Daimler) and Benz & Cie. (founded by Karl Benz). Its cars were marketed under the Mercedes-Benz brand, combining the "Mercedes" name of DMG's cars with Benz's surname.

History

The merged company became Germany's leading luxury car maker and a major producer of trucks, buses, aircraft engines and industrial powerplants. During the Second World War it built aero engines (such as the DB 601 and DB 605) and military vehicles, making extensive use of forced labour, for which it later paid compensation. Post-war production resumed with cars such as the 300SL "Gullwing" and the S-Class line, and Daimler-Benz built a dominant position in the world luxury car and heavy truck markets. It diversified in the 1980s into aerospace (Daimler-Benz Aerospace) and services. In 1998 Daimler-Benz merged with the American Chrysler Corporation to form DaimlerChrysler; after Chrysler was sold in 2007, the company became Daimler AG, renamed Mercedes-Benz Group in 2022.
