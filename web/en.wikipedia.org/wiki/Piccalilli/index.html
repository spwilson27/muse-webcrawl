Piccalilli, or mustard pickle, is a British interpretation of Indian pickles: a relish of chopped pickled vegetables and spices, characteristically cauliflower, onion, and gherkins in a thick, bright-yellow sauce of vinegar, mustard, turmeric, and sugar. It is a staple of the ploughman's lunch, eaten with cheese, cold ham, and pork pies.

History

Recipes appear in English cookbooks from the mid-eighteenth century — Hannah Glasse's era — under picturesque names such as "Paco-Lilla" and "India Pickle," reflecting returning East India Company officers' taste for the fiery oil pickles of India (see achar). The name is probably a fanciful blend of "pickle" and "chilli." By the Victorian period, commercial producers had standardised the crunchy, sweet-sharp, mustard-laden style recognised today.

Varieties

The British version uses chunky vegetables in a cooked mustard sauce. The American version, popular in the Midwest and on Chicago-style hot dogs, is a finely chopped, sweet, neon-green relish based on green tomatoes and cucumbers — a very different condiment bearing the same name.

Preparation

Vegetables are brined overnight to keep them crisp, then briefly cooked in a sauce of vinegar thickened with flour or cornstarch and flavoured with mustard powder, turmeric, ginger, and sugar. Properly made, the vegetables remain crunchy and the sauce sharply tangy — a balance of preservation technique inherited from Indian pickling and British store-cupboard tastes.
