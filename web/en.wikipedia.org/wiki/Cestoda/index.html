Cestoda

Cestoda is a class of parasitic flatworms of the phylum Platyhelminthes, commonly known as tapeworms. The roughly 6,000 described species are endoparasites of vertebrates, living as adults in the digestive tract of their definitive hosts and absorbing nutrients directly through their body surface.

Anatomy

An adult tapeworm consists of a scolex (head) bearing suckers and often hooks for attachment, a short neck, and a strobila — a ribbon of segments called proglottids, each containing a complete set of reproductive organs. Tapeworms have no mouth or digestive tract. Some species, such as the beef tapeworm Taenia saginata and the pork tapeworm Taenia solium, can grow several metres long inside the human intestine.

Life cycle and disease

Most cestodes require one or more intermediate hosts, in which larval stages form cysts in tissues. Humans acquire intestinal infections by eating raw or undercooked meat or fish containing larvae; larval infection of human tissues (as in cysticercosis or hydatid disease caused by Echinococcus) can be far more serious. Treatment typically uses anthelmintics such as praziquantel or niclosamide.
