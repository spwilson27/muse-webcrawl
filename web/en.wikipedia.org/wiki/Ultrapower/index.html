Ultrapower

In model theory, a branch of mathematical logic, the ultrapower construction is a method of producing new structures from a given structure using an ultrafilter. Given a structure M and an ultrafilter U on an index set I, the ultrapower MI/U consists of equivalence classes of sequences of elements of M, where two sequences are identified if they agree on a set belonging to U.

Łoś's theorem

The fundamental result about ultrapowers is Łoś's theorem, which states that a first-order sentence holds in the ultrapower exactly when the set of indices at which it holds in the original structure belongs to the ultrafilter. Consequently, an ultrapower is elementarily equivalent to the original structure, while typically being much larger and non-isomorphic to it.

Applications

Ultrapowers give an algebraic construction of the hyperreal numbers used in nonstandard analysis: the hyperreals are an ultrapower of the real numbers over the natural numbers with respect to a non-principal ultrafilter. Ultrapowers also play a central role in set theory, where iterated ultrapowers by measures are used in the study of large cardinals, and in the model-theoretic proofs of compactness and completeness theorems.
