Headache

A headache is pain in any region of the head. Headaches may occur on one or both sides of the head, be isolated to a certain location, radiate across the head from one point, or have a viselike quality. A headache can appear as a sharp pain, a throbbing sensation or a dull ache, and may develop gradually or suddenly, lasting from less than an hour to several days. It is one of the most commonly reported symptoms worldwide.

Classification

The International Classification of Headache Disorders (ICHD), published by the International Headache Society, divides headaches into primary and secondary types.

Primary headaches are not caused by another condition. The main types are:

  • Migraine — typically a moderate-to-severe throbbing pain, often one-sided, accompanied by nausea and sensitivity to light and sound, sometimes preceded by aura.
  • Tension-type headache — the most common type, a mild-to-moderate pressing or tightening pain on both sides of the head.
  • Cluster headache — severe, strictly one-sided pain around the eye, occurring in bouts ("clusters") with tearing and nasal congestion.

Secondary headaches arise from an underlying cause such as head injury, infection (e.g. meningitis), stroke, tumour, or medication overuse.

Causes and mechanisms

The brain tissue itself has no pain receptors; headache pain arises from sensitive structures including blood vessels, meninges, muscles and nerves of the head and neck. Triggers for migraine and tension headaches include stress, lack of sleep, dehydration, alcohol, and certain foods. Sudden, severe "thunderclap" headaches, headaches with fever and neck stiffness, or headaches following head injury are warning signs ("red flags") that require urgent medical evaluation.

Treatment

Most headaches respond to simple analgesics such as paracetamol, ibuprofen or aspirin, together with rest and hydration. Migraine-specific drugs include the triptans and newer CGRP-targeting therapies. Frequent use of acute medication can itself cause medication-overuse headache. Preventive treatment — including certain antidepressants, beta blockers, anticonvulsants and lifestyle measures — is used when attacks are frequent or disabling.

Epidemiology

Headache disorders are among the most prevalent conditions globally; roughly half of adults report a headache in a given year, and migraine alone affects more than a billion people and is a leading cause of disability, particularly in women and in people under 50.
