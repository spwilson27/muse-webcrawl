Japanese Instrument of Surrender

The Japanese Instrument of Surrender was the written agreement that formalised the surrender of the Empire of Japan, marking the end of World War II. It was signed by representatives of Japan and of the Allied nations aboard the US Navy battleship USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay on 2 September 1945.

Terms

By the document, Japan accepted the provisions of the Potsdam Declaration, proclaimed the unconditional surrender of the Imperial Japanese Army and all armed forces under Japanese control wherever situated, ordered Japanese forces to cease hostilities and surrender their arms, and subjected the authority of the Emperor and the Japanese Government to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

Signatories

Foreign Minister Mamoru Shigemitsu signed for the Japanese government and General Yoshijirō Umezu for the Imperial General Headquarters. General of the Army Douglas MacArthur, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, signed for all the Allied nations at war with Japan, followed by representatives of the United States, China, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, Australia, Canada, France, the Netherlands and New Zealand. MacArthur used five pens to sign his name in portions, giving them to generals Wainwright and Percival and others.

Aftermath

The ceremony, broadcast worldwide, formally ended the war. The original document is held by the US National Archives. The surrender was followed by the Allied occupation of Japan (1945–1952).
