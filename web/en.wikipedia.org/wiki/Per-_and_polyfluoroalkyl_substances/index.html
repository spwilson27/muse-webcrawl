Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are a group of synthetic organofluorine chemical compounds that have multiple fluorine atoms attached to an alkyl chain. There are thousands of distinct PFAS compounds. They have been used since the 1940s in products such as water-repellent fabrics, non-stick cookware coatings (Teflon), food packaging, cosmetics, firefighting foams and stain-resistant carpets, owing to the exceptional strength of the carbon–fluorine bond, which makes them resistant to heat, water and oil.

Persistence and exposure

The same bond strength makes PFAS extremely resistant to environmental degradation, earning them the nickname "forever chemicals". Long-chain PFAS such as perfluorooctanoic acid (PFOA) and perfluorooctane sulfonic acid (PFOS) bioaccumulate in blood and organs and have multi-year half-lives in humans. They are now detected nearly ubiquitously in water, soil, wildlife and human blood serum worldwide, with exposure mainly through drinking water, food and consumer products.

Health concerns

Epidemiological studies — notably the C8 Health Project following contamination near a DuPont plant in West Virginia — have linked PFOA exposure to kidney and testicular cancer, ulcerative colitis, thyroid disease, pregnancy-induced hypertension and high cholesterol. PFAS exposure has also been associated with reduced vaccine antibody response in children, low birth weight and liver effects. Regulatory agencies differ in the strength of conclusions for individual compounds, but the class as a whole has become a major environmental-health concern.

Regulation and litigation

ActionDetail
Stockholm ConventionPFOS listed 2009; PFOA listed 2019; PFHxS listed 2022 for global restriction/elimination.
United States3M and DuPont phased out US PFOA/PFOS production in the 2000s–2010s; EPA set enforceable drinking-water limits (4 ppt for PFOA and PFOS) in 2024. 3M announced exit from PFAS manufacture by end of 2025 and settled water-system claims for over $10 billion.
European UnionA universal PFAS restriction proposal covering the whole class was submitted to ECHA in 2023.
