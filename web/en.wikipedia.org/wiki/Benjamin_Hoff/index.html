Benjamin Hoff (born 1946) is an American author best known for his books The Tao of Pooh (1982) and The Te of Piglet (1992), which use A. A. Milne's Winnie-the-Pooh characters to explain the principles of Taoist philosophy to Western readers. The Tao of Pooh was an international bestseller and spent 49 weeks on The New York Times bestseller list.

Life and career

Hoff grew up in Sylvan Grove, Oregon, and studied Asian art, architecture, and music at Portland's Museum Art School and the School for American Craftsmen. He earned a bachelor's degree from The Evergreen State College in Olympia, Washington, in 1973. Before publishing his famous Pooh books, he worked in a variety of fields and published The Way to Life: At the Heart of the Tao Te Ching (1981), an interpretive translation of selections from the Tao Te Ching.

The Tao of Pooh and The Te of Piglet

In The Tao of Pooh, Hoff presents Winnie-the-Pooh as an exemplar of the Taoist principle of wu wei — effortless doing and living in harmony with one's nature — contrasting Pooh with characters such as the fretful, knowledge-chasing Owl, the anxious Rabbit, and the gloomy Eeyore. The sequel, The Te of Piglet (1992), focuses on Piglet as an embodiment of Te, the virtue of inner power found in the small and humble. Both books became popular introductions to Taoism for general readers.

Later work and dispute

Hoff later became disillusioned with the publishing industry. In 2006 he published an essay, "Farewell to Authorship," on his official website announcing that he was quitting writing, citing disputes with publishers over rights and royalties. His later books include The Singing Creek Where the Willows Grow (1986), a biography of the American nature diarist Opal Whitely, which won the American Book Award in 1988.
