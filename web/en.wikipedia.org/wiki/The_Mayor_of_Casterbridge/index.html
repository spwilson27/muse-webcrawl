The Mayor of Casterbridge: The Life and Death of a Man of Character is an 1886 novel by the English author Thomas Hardy. One of Hardy's Wessex novels, it is set in the fictional market town of Casterbridge, based on Dorchester in Dorset. It opens with one of the most famous scenes in English fiction: the drunken hay-trusser Michael Henchard auctions his wife Susan and their baby daughter to a sailor for five guineas at a country fair.

Plot

Sober the next morning, Henchard swears an oath not to drink for twenty-one years and sets out to remake himself. Twenty years later he is the prosperous mayor of Casterbridge — when Susan returns, with a young woman she presents as their daughter Elizabeth-Jane (in fact the sailor's child; Henchard's own daughter died). Henchard remarries Susan, hires the innovative young Scotsman Donald Farfrae as his manager, and then quarrels with him; Farfrae sets up a rival business and outstrips him in trade, love, and civic honour. After Susan's death Henchard learns Elizabeth-Jane is not his daughter and turns cold toward her. His fortunes collapse through speculation on the harvest weather, he loses the mayoralty to Farfrae, and a public scandal — the reading in court of his youthful wife-sale by the "furmity woman" — completes his ruin. The sailor Newson returns, Henchard lies that Elizabeth-Jane is dead, then loses her to Newson and to her marriage with Farfrae. He dies alone in a hut on Egdon Heath, leaving a will that asks to be forgotten: "that no man remember me."

Themes

The novel is a tragedy of character in changing times: Henchard's impulsive, proud temperament, the tension between old agricultural ways and Farfrae's modern methods, the role of chance and weather, and Hardy's deterministic view that "character is fate". Elizabeth-Jane's quiet endurance provides the novel's moral counterweight.

Background and adaptations

The novel was serialised in The Graphic in 1886; Hardy drew the wife-sale from documented cases in rural England and the setting from his native Dorchester. It is now regarded as one of his greatest novels. Adaptations include a 1978 BBC television serial starring Alan Bates, a 2001 film, and a 2003 television film with Ciarán Hinds as Henchard.
