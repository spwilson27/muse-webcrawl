Endoxyla leucomochla

FamilyCossidae
GenusEndoxyla
DistributionAustralia

Endoxyla leucomochla is a species of large moth in the family Cossidae, found in Australia. It is notable because its larvae are one of the insects known as witchetty grubs, a traditional food of Aboriginal Australians.

Life cycle

The larvae bore into the roots of the witchetty bush (Acacia kempeana) and related acacias, feeding within the root system. Fully grown larvae are large, pale, and soft-bodied. After pupation, the adult moth emerges; adults are heavy-bodied and do not feed, living only long enough to mate and lay eggs.

As food

Witchetty grubs from this species have been an important source of protein in the diets of desert Aboriginal peoples, eaten raw or lightly cooked in hot ashes. The grub's name in the Arrernte language and other Central Australian languages reflects its association with the witchetty bush.
