Salpingography

Salpingography is a radiographic imaging technique used to visualise the fallopian tubes by injecting a contrast medium, allowing assessment of tubal patency and shape. Most commonly it is performed as part of a hysterosalpingogram (HSG), in which contrast injected through the cervix outlines both the uterine cavity and the tubes under fluoroscopy. In selective salpingography, a fine catheter is advanced directly into the ostium of an individual tube to evaluate or treat a proximal blockage.

Clinical use

The principal indication is the investigation of female infertility: free spillage of contrast from the fimbrial end into the peritoneal cavity indicates a patent tube, while failure to fill, dilation (hydrosalpinx) or abrupt cut-off suggests obstruction or disease. The technique can also demonstrate congenital anomalies, tubal occlusion after sterilisation, and peritubal adhesions. Selective salpingography combined with tubal cannulation can open some proximal obstructions without surgery.

Procedure and considerations

The examination is usually scheduled in the first half of the menstrual cycle, after menstruation and before ovulation, to avoid irradiating an early pregnancy. Either water-soluble or oil-based contrast media are used; use of oil-based contrast has been associated in studies with a modest increase in pregnancy rates in the months following the procedure. Risks include pelvic infection, contrast reaction and discomfort. Ultrasound-based alternatives such as HyCoSy avoid ionising radiation.
