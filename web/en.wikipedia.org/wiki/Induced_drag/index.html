Induced drag

In fluid dynamics, induced drag (also called vortex drag or, historically, drag due to lift) is drag that occurs as the result of the creation of lift on a three-dimensional lifting body, such as the wing or rotor of an aircraft. It arises from the deflection of air downward by the wing and the formation of wingtip vortices.

Mechanism

A wing generates lift by creating lower pressure above and higher pressure below; air spills around the wingtips, forming vortices that induce a downward flow (downwash) behind the wing. This tilts the local relative airflow and the lift vector backward, so a component of lift acts opposite to the direction of motion — that component is induced drag.

Characteristics

Induced drag is inversely proportional to the square of airspeed, so it dominates at low speeds and high angles of attack, and it decreases with greater wing span and aspect ratio. It can be reduced by winglets, careful planform design and formation flying. It combines with parasitic drag to form the total drag curve used in aircraft performance analysis.
