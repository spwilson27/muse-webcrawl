Hydatidiform mole

A hydatidiform mole (molar pregnancy) is a form of gestational trophoblastic disease in which a fertilised egg develops into an abnormal mass of cystic chorionic villi rather than a viable embryo. It occurs when fertilisation errors produce abnormal chromosomal complements.

Types

A complete mole usually arises when an egg lacking maternal DNA is fertilised by one (duplicated) or two sperm, giving a 46,XX or 46,XY karyotype entirely of paternal origin, with no foetal tissue. A partial mole is typically triploid (69 chromosomes), with some foetal tissue present.

Signs and management

Signs may include vaginal bleeding, a uterus large for dates, hyperemesis, very high hCG levels, and a "snowstorm" or cluster-of-grapes appearance on ultrasound. Treatment is evacuation of the uterus, usually by suction curettage, followed by serial hCG monitoring because of the risk of persistent trophoblastic disease or choriocarcinoma.
