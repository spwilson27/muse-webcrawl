Place Ville Marie is a landmark office and shopping complex in downtown Montreal, Canada. Its cruciform main tower, completed in 1962, was one of the first major works of architect I. M. Pei and a defining project of Montreal's modernist downtown redevelopment.

Design

The 47-storey tower is built on a cross-shaped plan so that no office is far from a window, and is clad in a grid of glass and aluminium. Because it was erected over a sunken railway cutting, the tower rises from an elevated plaza above the tracks. Atop the roof sits a rotating beacon whose beams are a fixture of the Montreal night skyline. Developers led by William Zeckendorf built the complex for tenants including the Royal Bank of Canada, long the tower's principal occupant.

Underground city

Beneath the plaza-level shopping concourse, Place Ville Marie became a founding node of Montreal's Underground City (RÉSO), the network of tunnels linking offices, metro stations, and shops — a response to the city's harsh winters.

Significance

The development shifted Montreal's commercial centre westward and set the height benchmark for the downtown core: by convention, later towers (including 1000 de La Gauchetière) match its elevation relative to Mount Royal.
