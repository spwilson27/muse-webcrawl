Potamoi

The Potamoi (Greek: Ποταμοί, "rivers") are the gods of rivers and streams of the earth in Greek mythology. They were the sons of the great earth-encircling river Oceanus and his sister-wife Tethys, and brothers of the Oceanids.

Mythology

Hesiod's Theogony counts some 3,000 Oceanids and as many river gods. Individual Potamoi included Achelous, the greatest of them; Alpheus, who pursued the nymph Arethusa; Scamander, who fought Achilles in the Iliad; and Nilus, god of the Nile. River gods were typically depicted as vigorous men with bull's horns, or as reclining elders pouring water from an urn.

Worship

Rivers were worshipped locally throughout Greece: youths dedicated locks of hair to them, and sacrifices were made before crossing or in times of drought.
