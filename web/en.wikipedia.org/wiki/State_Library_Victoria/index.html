State Library Victoria

State Library Victoria is the main public library for the Australian state of Victoria and one of the oldest public libraries in the world. Located on Swanston Street in Melbourne, it was established in 1854 as the Melbourne Public Library and has always been free and open to the public.

History

The library was founded under the patronage of Lieutenant-Governor Charles Joseph La Trobe and Redmond Barry, who served as its first president and was a driving force behind its creation. The complex grew over time, most notably with the construction of the great domed reading room, opened in 1913, which was for a time the largest reinforced-concrete dome in the world.

Collections and spaces

The La Trobe Reading Room, under the dome, is the library's centrepiece. The library holds millions of books, photographs, manuscripts, newspapers and artworks, including major collections relating to Victorian history, the diaries of Melbourne's founders, and the folios of John James Audubon's Birds of America. A major redevelopment, Vision 2020, reopened previously closed spaces including the Ian Potter Queen's Hall in 2019.
