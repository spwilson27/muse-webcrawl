Paraná (Portuguese pronunciation: [paɾaˈna]) is one of the 26 states of Brazil, in the south of the country, bordered on the north by São Paulo state, on the east by the Atlantic Ocean, on the south by Santa Catarina state and the province of Misiones, Argentina, and on the west by Mato Grosso do Sul and Paraguay. Its capital is Curitiba, and its largest city is Curitiba as well. Other major cities include Londrina, Maringá, Ponta Grossa, Cascavel, São José dos Pinhais and Foz do Iguaçu.

The state is home to the Iguaçu National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the Itaipu Dam, one of the largest hydroelectric power plants in the world, shared with Paraguay on the Paraná River, from which the state takes its name.

History

The region was originally inhabited by Indigenous peoples including the Guarani and Kaingang. In the 16th and 17th centuries, Jesuit missions were established in the west, and Portuguese bandeirantes raided them for enslaved labour. The area was part of the captaincy of São Paulo; the territory of Paraná was separated to form a new province in 1853, in part as a response to the Farroupilha Revolution further south. European immigration in the late 19th and 20th centuries — Germans, Poles, Ukrainians, Italians and others — strongly shaped the state's culture.

Geography

Paraná is crossed by rivers flowing generally westward to the Paraná River, including the Iguaçu, Ivaí, Piquiri and Tibagi. The coastal plain gives way to the Serra do Mar and then to a series of plateaus. The climate ranges from subtropical along the coast to temperate in the highlands; frosts are common in winter in the south and centre, and Curitiba is known as one of Brazil's coldest capitals. The araucaria (Paraná pine) forests that once covered much of the state have been greatly reduced by logging and agriculture.

Economy

Paraná is one of Brazil's leading agricultural states, producing soybeans, maize, wheat, coffee, sugarcane and yerba mate, and it is a major producer of poultry, pork and dairy products. Industry is concentrated in the Curitiba metropolitan area (automotive, machinery) and in agro-processing elsewhere. The Port of Paranaguá is one of Brazil's largest grain-exporting ports.

Demographics

Most of the population is of European descent, with significant Afro-Brazilian, mixed-race and Indigenous minorities, and communities of Japanese, Arab and other immigrant origin. The majority religion is Roman Catholicism, with a growing Evangelical Protestant minority.
