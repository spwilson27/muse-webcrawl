The Downtown Eastside (DTES) is a neighbourhood in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. One of the city's oldest districts, it is the site of a complex set of social issues including disproportionately high levels of drug use, homelessness, poverty, crime, mental illness and sex work. It is also known for its strong community of residents and history of social activism.

Around 6,000–10,000 people live in the wider Downtown Eastside, many in single-room occupancy (SRO) hotels and social housing. The neighbourhood includes the historic areas of Chinatown, Gastown (partly), Strathcona and Victory Square.

History

The Downtown Eastside grew up around the original Granville townsite and Hastings Mill in the late 19th century and was Vancouver's early commercial and civic centre. As the city's business district shifted west in the 20th century, the area became a working-class and resource-worker district of residential hotels and beer parlours. Deinstitutionalization of psychiatric patients in the 1980s, the loss of low-cost housing around Expo 86, and the arrival of crack cocaine and later fentanyl concentrated poverty and addiction in the area.

Social issues and responses

The neighbourhood experienced an HIV epidemic in the 1990s among people who inject drugs. In 2003 the supervised injection site Insite opened on East Hastings Street — the first sanctioned facility of its kind in North America — and its operation was upheld by the Supreme Court of Canada in 2011. The community is also associated with the crisis of missing and murdered Indigenous women; serial killer Robert Pickton preyed on women from the area, and annual Women's Memorial Marches have been held there since 1992. Since British Columbia declared a public-health emergency in 2016, the opioid overdose crisis has killed thousands in the province, with the Downtown Eastside among the hardest hit places.

Alongside these challenges, the DTES has a dense network of community organisations, harm-reduction services, art spaces and activist groups, including the Vancouver Area Network of Drug Users (VANDU) and the Downtown Eastside Residents Association.
