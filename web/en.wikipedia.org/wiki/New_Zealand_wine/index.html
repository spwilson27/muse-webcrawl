New Zealand wine is produced in several of the nation's winegrowing regions, mainly in the North and South Islands. New Zealand, the world's southernmost wine-producing country, is best known internationally for Marlborough Sauvignon blanc, whose pungent, intensely aromatic style — passionfruit, gooseberry and fresh-cut grass — transformed global expectations of the variety after its breakthrough in the 1980s. Pinot noir, especially from Central Otago and Martinborough, is the second flagship variety, alongside Chardonnay, Pinot gris and Riesling, and Bordeaux-style red blends from Hawke's Bay.

History

The first vines were planted by the missionary Samuel Marsden at Kerikeri in 1819, and James Busby, British Resident in the 1830s, established a vineyard at Waitangi, making the country's first recorded wine. Croatian (Dalmatian) immigrants founded enduring family wineries in West Auckland in the early twentieth century. For much of its history the industry relied on hybrid varieties and fortified wines for a small domestic market, constrained by temperance sentiment and restrictive licensing. The modern era began in the 1970s with plantings in Marlborough (from 1973, led by Montana) and a shift to Vitis vinifera varieties; a government vine-pull scheme in 1986 removed poor-quality vines and accelerated replanting with quality varieties.

Regions and style

New Zealand's vineyards stretch some 1,600 km from subtropical Northland to Central Otago in the south, the world's southernmost wine region, at about 45°S. The maritime climate, long sunshine hours, cool nights and free-draining stony soils favour slow ripening and retention of acidity. Marlborough accounts for the great majority of production, dominated by Sauvignon blanc. Hawke's Bay, the oldest region, is noted for Chardonnay and Syrah and Bordeaux blends from the Gimblett Gravels. Central Otago, a continental-climate inland region, produces Pinot noir of international reputation. Sustainable Winegrowing New Zealand certifies the great majority of vineyards.

Exports

Wine is one of New Zealand's major export earners, worth around NZ$2 billion annually in recent years, with the United States, United Kingdom and Australia the main markets. Exports are dominated by Sauvignon blanc, which makes up the large majority of shipments.
