Zhang Heng

Zhang Heng (Chinese: 張衡; AD 78–139), courtesy name Pingzi, was a Chinese polymathic scientist and official of the Han dynasty. An astronomer, mathematician, inventor, geographer, artist and poet, he is best known for inventing the world's first seismoscope and for his work as Imperial Astronomer at the court in Luoyang.

Inventions

In AD 132 Zhang presented his seismoscope (houfeng didong yi), an urn-shaped bronze vessel with eight dragon heads around its rim, each holding a ball above a toad's open mouth. An earthquake's tremor disturbed an internal pendulum, releasing a ball into the mouth of the toad facing the direction of the quake — famously detecting a distant earthquake in Gansu that had not been felt at the capital. He also improved the water-powered armillary sphere by adding a ring for the ecliptic, and is credited with an odometer-like device and a south-pointing chariot.

Astronomy and mathematics

As Chief Astronomer, Zhang catalogued some 2,500 stars and wrote the Ling Xian, summarising contemporary cosmology. He approximated pi as 3.1724 (and as √10) and improved formulae for the volume of a sphere. He correctly explained lunar eclipses and recognised that the Moon shines by reflected sunlight.

Literature

Zhang was also one of the great poets of the Han, writing elaborate fu rhapsodies on the eastern and western capitals (Two Metropolises Rhapsody) and lyrical poems that influenced later Chinese literature.
