The Governor of Gibraltar is the representative of the British monarch in the British Overseas Territory of Gibraltar. The governor is appointed by the monarch on the advice of the British Government, and acts as the de facto head of state, responsible for defence, foreign affairs, internal security, and the appointment of senior officials. Most executive authority over domestic matters is exercised by the elected Government of Gibraltar, headed by the Chief Minister.

Role

Under the Gibraltar Constitution Order 2006, the governor retains responsibility for external affairs, defence, internal security (including the Royal Gibraltar Police, in consultation with the Gibraltar Police Authority), and public appointments. The governor formally appoints the Chief Minister after general elections and assents to legislation passed by the Gibraltar Parliament. The governor is also commander-in-chief of British Forces Gibraltar; holders of the post have typically been senior officers of the Royal Navy, Army, or Royal Marines.

History

Gibraltar came under British control in 1704 during the War of the Spanish Succession, when an Anglo-Dutch fleet under Admiral Sir George Rooke captured the Rock. Early governors were military commanders of a garrison town, and the office was at times combined with that of the military commander. The governor's residence since 1728 has been The Convent, a former Franciscan friary on Main Street.

Notable governors

Distinguished holders include General James Oglethorpe, founder of Georgia; Lord Napier of Magdala; and Field Marshal Lord Harding. The governor during the Second World War directed a fortress of great strategic importance guarding the entrance to the Mediterranean.
