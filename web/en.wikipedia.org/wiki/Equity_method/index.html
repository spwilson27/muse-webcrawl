Equity method

The equity method is an accounting treatment for an investment in another company when the investor has significant influence over the investee but does not control it. Significant influence is generally presumed when the investor holds between 20% and 50% of the voting stock of the investee. The investee in such a relationship is typically an associate or a joint venture.

Treatment

Under the equity method, the investment is initially recorded at cost. In subsequent periods, the carrying amount is increased by the investor's share of the investee's profits (or decreased by its share of losses), reported as a single line in the investor's income statement. Dividends received from the investee are not recognised as income; instead they reduce the carrying amount of the investment.

Standards

The method is prescribed under International Financial Reporting Standards by IAS 28, Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures, and under US GAAP by ASC 323. When ownership rises to the point of control, the investor switches to full consolidation; when significant influence is lost, fair value or cost methods may apply.
