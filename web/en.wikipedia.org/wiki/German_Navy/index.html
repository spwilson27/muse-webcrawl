The German Navy (German: Deutsche Marine) is the naval arm of the Bundeswehr, the armed forces of the Federal Republic of Germany. It was established in 1956, during the Cold War, as the navy of West Germany (then formally the Bundesmarine, "Federal Navy"). Following German reunification in 1990 it absorbed elements of the former East German Volksmarine and adopted its present name.

History

German naval forces have existed in several forms since 1848, including the Imperial Navy (Kaiserliche Marine), the Reichsmarine and the Kriegsmarine of the Second World War. After 1945 Germany was initially demilitarised, but the worsening Cold War led the western allies to approve West German rearmament. The Bundesmarine was officially founded on 2 January 1956, initially built around minesweeping formations and personnel who had served in wartime and immediate post-war mine clearance units.

During the Cold War the navy's central task was deterrence in cooperation with NATO: guarding the Baltic approaches against the Soviet Baltic Fleet and helping to secure the North Atlantic sea lanes. After reunification, the navy's mission widened to international crisis management, counter-piracy and expeditionary operations, including deployments off the Horn of Africa, in the Mediterranean and in the Indo-Pacific.

Organisation

The navy is commanded by the Inspector of the Navy through the Navy Command (Marinekommando) in Rostock. Its operational forces are organised in two flotillas: Operational Flotilla 1 in Kiel, covering corvettes, mine warfare forces, submarines and support units, and Operational Flotilla 2 in Wilhelmshaven, covering the frigates and replenishment ships. Naval aviation, including maritime patrol aircraft and helicopters, is concentrated at Nordholz Naval Airbase. The main naval bases are Wilhelmshaven, Kiel, Eckernförde, Rostock-Warnemünde and Nordholz.

Fleet

The fleet includes Brandenburg-, Sachsen- and Baden-Württemberg-class frigates, Braunschweig-class corvettes, Type 212A submarines, mine countermeasures vessels, fleet service ships and auxiliary replenishment vessels such as the Berlin-class combat support ships. Germany has no aircraft carrier; naval aviation operates fixed-wing patrol aircraft and helicopters from shore bases and from frigates.

Missions

Peacetime tasks include protection of German waters and sea lanes, contributions to NATO standing naval forces, embargo and sanctions enforcement, counter-piracy, mine clearance of wartime ordnance in the Baltic and North Seas, and support of civil authorities in search and rescue.
