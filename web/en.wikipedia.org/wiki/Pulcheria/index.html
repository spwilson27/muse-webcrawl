Pulcheria

Aelia Pulcheria (19 January 398/399 – July 453) was an Eastern Roman empress and one of the most powerful women of the fifth century. Daughter of Emperor Arcadius and sister of Theodosius II, she effectively ruled the empire as regent from 414 and shaped its religious politics; after her brother's death in 450 she became empress in her own right and chose Marcian as husband and emperor. She is venerated as a saint in the Orthodox and Catholic churches.

Regency

Declared Augusta in 414 at about fifteen, Pulcheria took charge of Theodosius's upbringing and court, taking a vow of virginity together with her sisters and reorganising the palace along near-monastic lines. She promoted devotion to the Virgin Mary as Theotokos and clashed with Theodosius's wife Eudocia, who eventually withdrew to Jerusalem. Her relations with the eunuch chamberlain Chrysaphius, who rose in the 440s, were hostile, and she was for a time pushed from power.

Empress and the Council of Chalcedon

When Theodosius died childless in a riding accident in 450, Pulcheria returned, married the senator Marcian in a nominal union respecting her vow, and secured his recognition as emperor — a key moment in the transmission of imperial legitimacy through a woman of the Theodosian house. Together they convened the Council of Chalcedon (451), which defined Christ as one person in two natures; Pulcheria was acclaimed there as "the new Helena". She built churches in Constantinople, including the Blachernae church of the Theotokos, and died in July 453.
