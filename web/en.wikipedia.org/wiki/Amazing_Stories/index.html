Amazing Stories

Amazing Stories is an American science fiction magazine launched in April 1926 by Hugo Gernsback's Experimenter Publishing. It was the first magazine devoted solely to science fiction. Before Amazing, science fiction stories had appeared in other magazines, but Amazing helped define and launch a new genre of popular fiction — Gernsback called it "scientifiction".

History

The first issue (cover dated April 1926) reprinted stories by Poe, Verne and Wells alongside new work. Amazing published early stories by writers including Jack Williamson, John W. Campbell, and E. E. "Doc" Smith, whose The Skylark of Space appeared in 1928; the August 1928 issue also carried Philip Francis Nowlan's first Buck Rogers story. The magazine passed through many publishers and editors — including T. O'Conor Sloane, Raymond A. Palmer, Cele Goldsmith (who championed new writers in the late 1950s and early 1960s), and Ted White — and survived repeated cancellations and revivals over nearly eight decades, ceasing regular print publication in the 2000s before later revival attempts.

Influence

The Hugo Awards, science fiction's most famous honours, are named for Gernsback. Amazing Stories helped create organised science fiction fandom through its letter columns and the Science Fiction League. The title was also used for Steven Spielberg's 1985 television anthology series Amazing Stories.
