The Fox Broadcasting Company, commonly known simply as Fox and stylized in all caps, is an American commercial broadcast television network serving as the flagship property of the Fox Corporation. Launched as a competitor to the Big Three television networks (ABC, CBS and NBC), Fox went on air on 9 October 1986, assembled from Metromedia stations purchased by Rupert Murdoch's News Corporation.

History

The network began prime time programming in April 1987 with shows such as Married... with Children and The Tracey Ullman Show — the latter spawning The Simpsons in 1989. Fox expanded to seven nights a week in 1993 and gained major sports rights, acquiring NFL coverage in 1994, which raised its profile decisively. In 2019, following Disney's acquisition of 21st Century Fox, the network became part of the newly formed Fox Corporation.

Programming

Fox is known for animated comedies (The Simpsons, Family Guy, Bob's Burgers), dramas, reality competitions such as American Idol (2002–2016) and sports broadcasts under the Fox Sports banner, including the NFL, Major League Baseball and previously the FIFA World Cup.
