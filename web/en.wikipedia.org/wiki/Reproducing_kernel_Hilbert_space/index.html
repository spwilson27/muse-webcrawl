Reproducing kernel Hilbert space

In functional analysis, a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) is a Hilbert space of functions in which point evaluation is a continuous linear functional. Equivalently, for each point x in the domain there exists a function kx in the space such that the value of any function f at x is given by the inner product ⟨f, kx⟩. The function K(x, y) = ⟨ky, kx⟩ is called the reproducing kernel of the space.

History and examples

The theory was developed in the first half of the 20th century, notably by Nachman Aronszajn, whose 1950 paper systematised the subject. Familiar examples include the Hardy space H² and the Bergman space of analytic functions, as well as spaces of functions on finite sets, where the kernel is a positive-definite matrix.

Moore–Aronszajn theorem

The Moore–Aronszajn theorem states that every symmetric, positive-definite kernel function on a set defines a unique RKHS for which it is the reproducing kernel, and conversely every RKHS has such a kernel. This correspondence underlies the "kernel trick" in machine learning: algorithms such as support vector machines can be expressed entirely in terms of inner products, which are replaced by a kernel evaluated on the data.

Applications

RKHS theory is central to statistical learning theory, where the representer theorem shows that the minimiser of a regularised risk functional over an RKHS lies in the finite span of kernel functions centred at the training points. It is also used in Gaussian process regression, signal processing and the theory of integral operators.
