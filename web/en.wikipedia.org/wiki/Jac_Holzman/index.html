Jac Holzman (born 15 September 1931) is an American music industry executive best known as the founder of Elektra Records and its classical subsidiary Nonesuch Records, and for signing acts including the Doors, Love, Judy Collins and Queen (for the US).

Elektra Records

Holzman founded Elektra in New York in 1950 while a student at St. John's College, Annapolis, borrowing $600 and releasing a first LP of art song. Through the 1950s the label specialised in folk music, recording Josh White, Theodore Bikel and the young Judy Collins, and became noted for its adventurous catalogue and high production values. In the 1960s Holzman expanded into rock, signing Paul Butterfield's Blues Band, Love and — in 1966 — the Doors, whose producer Paul A. Rothchild became the label's key creative partner. Holzman also founded Nonesuch (1964) as a budget classical label, commissioning landmark recordings including the early synthesizer hit Switched-On Bach (via licensing) and new-music commissions.

Later career

Elektra was sold to Kinney National in 1970; Holzman stayed on briefly, helped assemble the Warner–Elektra–Atlantic (WEA) group, and later led Warner's technology initiatives, including early work on cable television and the CD. He chronicled the label's history in Follow the Music (1998, with Gavan Daws). In 2011 he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in the non-performer category.
