Lakshmi is the Hindu goddess of wealth, fortune, prosperity (material and spiritual), beauty and grace, and the consort of Vishnu, whom she accompanies in his avatars — as Sita beside Rama and Rukmini (in other tellings, Radha's tradition) beside Krishna — hence her standing prayer is to be where Vishnu is: seated on his chest as Śrīvatsa, or massaging his feet as he reclines on the cosmic ocean.

Origins and character

Her most famous origin is the churning of the ocean of milk, from which she emerged seated on a lotus, radiant, and chose Vishnu as her husband; the occasion is depicted everywhere in Hindu art, with elephants showering her with water (Gaja Lakshmi). She is depicted with four arms (the four aims of life), holding lotuses, gold coins cascading from one palm in blessing. Lakshmi is famously fickle — fortune deserts the lazy and the impure — and her worship joins prosperity firmly to dharma, cleanliness and generosity: houses are cleaned and lit for her visit at Diwali.

Worship

Lakshmi Puja on the third day of Diwali is among the most widely observed Hindu observances, in homes, shops and ledgers opened fresh for the year; merchants worship account books and tools in her name. As one of the Tridevi with Parvati and Saraswati, and in her eight forms (Ashtalakshmi), she is worshipped across traditions; her name is a common blessing word for wealth itself, and "Lakshmi" remains a popular girl's name and the brand of countless businesses.
