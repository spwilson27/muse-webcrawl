Red ribbon

The red ribbon, as an awareness ribbon, is a symbol of solidarity with people living with HIV/AIDS and of support for the fight against the disease. It is worn especially on World AIDS Day (1 December) and at AIDS-related fundraising and memorial events.

History

The red ribbon was created in 1991 by Visual AIDS, a New York-based artists' collective, modelled in part on the yellow ribbons worn in support of American troops during the Gulf War. Its designers chose red for its association with blood and love, and kept the project deliberately un-copyrighted so the symbol could be freely reproduced. It gained international prominence when worn by attendees at the 1992 Tony Awards and by celebrities at other televised events.

Other meanings

A red ribbon is also traditionally the prize for second place in competitions in the United States (where blue is first), and the phrase "red ribbon" appears in other contexts, including anti-drug campaigns such as the Red Ribbon Week observed in American schools.
