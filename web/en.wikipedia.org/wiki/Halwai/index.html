Halwai

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Halwai (Hindi: हलवाई) is a traditional confectioner and sweet-maker caste and occupational community of India, Nepal, Pakistan and Bangladesh. The name derives from halwa, a sweet dish, reflecting the community's hereditary occupation of preparing sweets (mithai), snacks and festive foods in milk, sugar, ghee and flour.

Occupation

Halwai sweet shops are a fixture of bazaars across South Asia, selling confections such as jalebi, laddu, barfi, gulab jamun, rasgulla and peda, as well as savoury snacks like samosas and pakoras. The trade traditionally expanded during festivals and wedding seasons, when large orders of sweets were prepared by families of halwais. Famous sweet houses in many Indian cities were founded by Halwai families, and some have grown into national restaurant and packaged-sweets chains.

Community

The Halwai community is found among Hindus and Muslims across northern and eastern India, including Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, West Bengal and Odisha, and in the Terai region of Nepal, where Halwai is also known as Madheshi-associated sweet-making community. Subgroups and surnames include Gupta, Modak (in Bengal), and others. In modern India the community is generally classified among the Other Backward Classes in several states.
