Vitoria-Gasteiz (Spanish: Vitoria; Basque: Gasteiz) is the seat of government of the Basque Country autonomous community and the capital of the province of Álava in northern Spain. It is home to the Basque Parliament and the headquarters of the Basque Government, and has a population of roughly 255,000.

History

The town was founded in 1181 by Sancho VI, king of Navarre, as a defensive outpost on the site of the village of Gasteiz; he named the new town Nueva Victoria. In 1200 it passed to the Crown of Castile. In 1813 the Battle of Vitoria, a decisive engagement of the Peninsular War, was fought nearby, in which the Duke of Wellington's allied army defeated the French.

Green capital

Vitoria-Gasteiz was designated European Green Capital for 2012, in recognition of its high proportion of green public areas — the "Green Belt" (Anillo Verde), a ring of parks and wetlands around the city — its recycling rates, and its sustainable mobility policies, including an extensive cycling network.

Sights

The medieval old quarter, built on a hill, retains much of its almond-shaped street plan and Gothic buildings, including the Cathedral of Santa María, whose restoration inspired Ken Follett's novel World Without End. The city also hosts the Artium museum of contemporary art and the annual Azkena Rock Festival and jazz festival.
