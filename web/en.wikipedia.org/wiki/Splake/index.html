Splake

Hybrid: Salvelinus namaycush × Salvelinus fontinalis
Family: Salmonidae

The splake (Salvelinus namaycush × Salvelinus fontinalis) is a hybrid fish produced by crossing a male brook trout (Salvelinus fontinalis) with a female lake trout (Salvelinus namaycush). The name is a portmanteau of "speckled" (from speckled trout, a common name for brook trout) and "lake trout". The hybrid was first produced in the nineteenth century and has been widely stocked in lakes in Canada and the northern United States since the mid-twentieth century.

Characteristics

Splake combine features of both parents: they have the light spots on a dark background characteristic of the Salvelinus (char) genus, a moderately forked tail (intermediate between the deeply forked tail of the lake trout and the squarer tail of the brook trout), and body markings and coloration reminiscent of the brook trout, especially in spawning season. They grow faster than either parent species in many stocked lakes and are easier to catch than lake trout, making them popular with anglers. The world record splake weighed about 9.3 kg (20 lb 11 oz), caught in Ontario.

Reproduction and management

Although splake are fertile, backcrossing in the wild is rare, and most populations are maintained by hatchery stocking. Fisheries managers use them to provide sport fisheries in lakes where neither parent species performs well, and because they are largely hatchery-controlled, they can be managed more predictably than self-sustaining wild fish. There is some concern that stocked splake may interbreed with wild brook trout or lake trout populations where the two co-occur.
