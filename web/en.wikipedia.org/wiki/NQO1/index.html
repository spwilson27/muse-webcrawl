NQO1

NQO1 (NAD(P)H quinone dehydrogenase 1, also known as DT-diaphorase) is a cytosolic enzyme that catalyses the two-electron reduction of quinones to hydroquinones, using NADH or NADPH as the electron donor. It is encoded by the NQO1 gene in humans.

Function

By reducing quinones in a single two-electron step, NQO1 prevents the formation of reactive semiquinone radicals and reactive oxygen species, acting as an antioxidant and detoxification enzyme. It also stabilises the tumour suppressor protein p53.

Clinical significance

A common polymorphism (NQO1*2, Pro187Ser) produces an unstable protein with little activity; people homozygous for it may be more susceptible to benzene toxicity and certain cancers. NQO1 is overexpressed in many tumours and is a target for enzyme-activated anticancer prodrugs such as mitomycin C.
