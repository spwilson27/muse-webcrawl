Dayan Khan

Reign1479/1480 – 1517 (Northern Yuan)
Born1473 or 1474
Died1517
SpouseMandukhai Khatun

Dayan Khan (1473/74–1517), born Batu Möngke, was a khan of the Northern Yuan dynasty in Mongolia. A descendant of Kublai Khan, he is regarded as the restorer of Mongol unity after a period of fragmentation, ruling with the support of his wife and regent Mandukhai Khatun.

Rise to power

Orphaned as a child and raised at the court of Manduul Khan, Batu Möngke was adopted by Manduul's widow Mandukhai, who had him proclaimed khan as a boy. Mandukhai governed as regent and later married him, campaigning alongside him against the Oirats and rival Mongol factions.

Reunification of the Mongols

Taking the title Dayan Khan — from the Chinese "Great Yuan" — he subdued the Oirats, incorporated the Uriankhai, and defeated the rebellious right-wing tümens, bringing most of the Mongol plateau under one ruler for the first time in generations. He reorganised the eastern Mongols into six tümens, a structure that endured for centuries.

Relations with Ming China

Dayan Khan repeatedly raided the Ming frontier to press for trade, at times reaching the outskirts of Beijing's hinterland. His descendants, including his grandson Altan Khan, continued to shape Mongol–Ming relations throughout the sixteenth century.
