Aaron Kwok Fu-shing (born 26 October 1965) is a Hong Kong singer, dancer, and actor. Active since the 1980s, Kwok is regarded as one of the "Four Heavenly Kings" of Cantopop, alongside Jacky Cheung, Andy Lau, and Leon Lai. He is noted for his energetic dance performances and has been called the "God of Dance" in Chinese media.

Early life and career

Kwok was born in Hong Kong to a family that ran a gold and jewellery business. He joined the television station TVB as a dancer in 1984, appearing in music videos and variety shows. After a period working in Taiwan, where a motorcycle commercial made him famous, he released his debut Mandarin album Loving You Endlessly in 1990, which sold over a million copies across Asia.

Music career

Returning to Hong Kong, Kwok became a central figure of the dance-pop wave of the early 1990s. His signature song "I Can't Help Myself in Loving You" and his distinctive hairstyle made him a youth idol. He has won numerous music awards and holds records for large-scale concert productions, including a 450-degree rotating stage.

Acting career

Kwok reinvented himself as a serious actor in the 2000s. He won the Golden Horse Award for Best Actor twice consecutively, for Divergence (2005) and After This Our Exile (2006). His later acclaimed roles include the films Port of Call (2015), for which he won the Hong Kong Film Award for Best Actor, and the crime thriller Project Gutenberg (2018).

Personal life

Kwok married model Moka Fang in 2017; they have two daughters. He is also known as an avid collector of sports cars and a race car enthusiast.
