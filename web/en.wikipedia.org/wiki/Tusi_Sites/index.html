Tusi Sites

The Tusi Sites (Chinese: 土司遗址) are a group of archaeological sites in China inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2015. They comprise the ruins of Hailongtun, Tangya and Laosicheng, representing the seats of hereditary native chieftains (tusi) who governed ethnic minority regions of southwest China under imperial China's tusi system.

The tusi system

From the 13th to the early 20th century, the Yuan, Ming and Qing courts appointed local hereditary chiefs as tusi to administer frontier regions, combining central authority with local custom. The three sites — Laosicheng in Hunan (seat of the Peng clan for over 800 years), Tangya in Hubei, and the fortified Hailongtun in Guizhou, destroyed in the Bozhou rebellion of 1600 — together illustrate the system's governance, architecture and cultural exchange between the Han Chinese centre and minority peoples.
