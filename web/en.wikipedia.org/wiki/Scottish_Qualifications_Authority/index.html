Scottish Qualifications Authority

Abbreviation: SQA
Formed: 1997
Type: Non-departmental public body
Purpose: Awarding and accreditation of qualifications in Scotland
Headquarters: Glasgow (Optima Building); offices in Dalkeith

The Scottish Qualifications Authority (SQA) is the national awarding and accreditation body for qualifications in Scotland, a non-departmental public body of the Scottish Government. It develops, assesses, and certificates school and vocational qualifications — including National Qualifications (National 1–5), Highers, and Advanced Highers sat by Scottish school pupils, as well as Higher National Certificates and Diplomas (HNCs and HNDs) taken in colleges.

History

The SQA was formed in 1997 by merging the Scottish Examination Board and SCOTVEC, unifying academic and vocational qualifications. It oversaw the introduction of Curriculum for Excellence qualifications in the 2010s. In 2000 the organisation faced a major crisis when thousands of Higher results were issued late or incorrect, leading to a parliamentary inquiry and leadership changes. In 2020, after exams were cancelled due to COVID-19, the SQA's standardisation algorithm downgraded many teacher estimates — disproportionately in deprived areas — provoking protests and a government reversal; the episode led to the 2021 decision to replace the SQA with a new body, Qualifications Scotland, under Scottish education reform.

Qualifications

School qualifications are pegged to the Scottish Credit and Qualifications Framework (SCQF): National 5 sits at level 5, Higher at level 6 (the main university entrance qualification), and Advanced Higher at level 7. The SQA also accredits qualifications from other awarding bodies operating in Scotland and works internationally, advising governments and awarding qualifications abroad.
