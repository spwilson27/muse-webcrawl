Golden age of arcade video games

The golden age of arcade video games was a period of rapid growth, technological innovation, and cultural influence for arcade games, generally dated from the release of Space Invaders in 1978 to the video game crash of 1983 in North America. During these years arcade video games became a major entertainment industry and a fixture of popular culture.

Rise

Taito's Space Invaders (1978) triggered the boom, reportedly causing a shortage of 100-yen coins in Japan. Atari's Asteroids (1979) and Namco's Pac-Man (1980) — the best-selling arcade game of the era — followed, along with Galaxian, Defender, Missile Command, Centipede, and Nintendo's Donkey Kong (1981), which introduced Mario. Colour raster graphics replaced monochrome displays, high-score tables fostered competition, and arcades proliferated in shopping malls and dedicated venues. By 1982 the American arcade industry's revenue from quarters famously rivalled or exceeded that of Hollywood films and pop music.

Decline

Market saturation, a flood of low-quality home console software, and the rise of home computers contributed to the video game crash of 1983, which sharply reduced the arcade industry in North America. Arcades revived in the late 1980s and early 1990s with fighting games such as Street Fighter II, but the golden age is remembered as the medium's formative, quarter-driven era.
