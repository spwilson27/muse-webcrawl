Thomas Kendall

Thomas Kendall (13 December 1778 – 6 May 1832) was a New Zealand missionary, linguist and teacher, among the first Europeans to reside in New Zealand. He is remembered for his pioneering work recording the Māori language and for his controversial dealings with Māori chiefs.

Mission to New Zealand

Recruited by the Church Missionary Society under Samuel Marsden, Kendall sailed to New Zealand in 1814 and helped establish the first mission station at Rangihoua in the Bay of Islands. He founded a school for Māori children and worked closely with the Ngāpuhi chiefs Hongi Hika and Waikato.

Linguistic work

Kendall produced the first systematic book on the Māori language, A Grammar and Vocabulary of the Language of New Zealand (1820), prepared in collaboration with Hongi Hika, Waikato and Professor Samuel Lee at Cambridge during a visit to England. His orthography laid the groundwork for the written form of te reo Māori, though his understanding of its grammar was imperfect.

Controversy and death

Kendall's involvement in the musket trade between Māori and European traders, his purchase of land, and his relationship with a Māori woman led the CMS to dismiss him in 1822. He later settled in New South Wales and drowned when his vessel was wrecked off the Australian coast in 1832.
