Pyrogallol

IUPAC nameBenzene-1,2,3-triol
FormulaC6H6O3
Molar mass126.11 g/mol
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid
Melting point131–134 °C

Pyrogallol is an organic compound with the formula C6H3(OH)3. It is a white, water-soluble solid, although samples are often brownish because of its sensitivity to oxygen. It is one of three isomers of trihydroxybenzene. Pyrogallol was first obtained by Carl Wilhelm Scheele in 1786 by heating gallic acid, a process reflected in its name (pyro-, from heat).

Production

Pyrogallol is produced industrially by the thermal decarboxylation of gallic acid, which is itself obtained from tannins. It can also be prepared by hydrolysis of tannic acid or by other routes from phenolic precursors.

Uses

Pyrogallol is a strong reducing agent. Alkaline solutions of pyrogallol absorb oxygen from the air, a property once widely used in gas analysis (for example in the Orsat apparatus) to measure oxygen content. It was historically important in photography as a developing agent ("pyro"), still favoured by some film photographers for the staining, fine-grain negatives it produces. It has also been used in hair dyes, as an antiseptic in ointments for skin conditions such as psoriasis (though it is toxic and has largely been replaced), and in the manufacture of dyes and pharmaceutical intermediates.

Safety

Pyrogallol is harmful by ingestion, inhalation and skin contact, and can cause dermatitis and damage to the liver and kidneys.
