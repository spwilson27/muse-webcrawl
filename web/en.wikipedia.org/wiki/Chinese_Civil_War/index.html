Chinese Civil War

The Chinese Civil War was a civil war in China fought between the Kuomintang (KMT)-led government of the Republic of China (ROC) and the forces of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP). It is conventionally divided into two main phases: 1927–1936 (interrupted by the Second United Front) and 1946–1949, separated by the Second Sino-Japanese War. The war ended with the Communists controlling mainland China and the Republic of China retreating to Taiwan.

First phase (1927–1936)

After the death of Sun Yat-sen, Chiang Kai-shek purged the Communists from the First United Front in the Shanghai massacre of April 1927. The CCP retreated to rural base areas; the encirclement campaigns of the Nationalists forced the Communists onto the Long March (1934–1935), during which Mao Zedong rose to leadership. The Xi'an Incident of December 1936 pushed the two parties into a Second United Front against Japan.

Second phase (1946–1949)

Full-scale war resumed after Japan's defeat. After early Nationalist successes, the People's Liberation Army won decisive campaigns at Liaoshen, Huaihai, and Pingjin in 1948–49. On 1 October 1949 Mao proclaimed the People's Republic of China in Beijing. Chiang and the ROC government evacuated to Taiwan, along with around two million refugees. No armistice or peace treaty was ever signed, a division that underlies the ongoing political status of Taiwan.
