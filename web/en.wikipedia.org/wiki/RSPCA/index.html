The Royal Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (RSPCA) is a charity operating in England and Wales that promotes animal welfare. Founded in 1824 as the Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals — the world's first animal welfare charity of its kind — it received royal patronage from Queen Victoria in 1840.

History

The society grew out of the campaign for Martin's Act of 1822, the first British law against cruelty to cattle, championed by the Irish MP Richard Martin. At a meeting of campaigners including the abolitionist William Wilberforce and the Reverend Arthur Broome, held at Old Slaughter's Coffee House in London in June 1824, the society was founded to inspect slaughterhouses and enforce the new law. Its uniformed inspectorate became the model for humane societies around the world, and similar SPCAs were later established in Scotland, Ireland, the United States, Australia and elsewhere — though most are independent organisations.

Work

The RSPCA operates animal centres, hospitals and clinics; rescues animals in distress; rehomes pets; and runs education and campaigning programs on issues from farm animal welfare (through its RSPCA Assured labelling scheme) to wildlife and animals in research. Its inspectors investigate complaints of cruelty and neglect and bring private prosecutions under animal welfare legislation — a role that has periodically attracted debate about the combination of campaigning, investigation and prosecution in one charity.
