Acoustical engineering

Acoustical engineering is the branch of engineering dealing with sound and vibration. It applies the science of acoustics to the design of buildings, vehicles, audio equipment, medical devices and environments, and to the control of unwanted noise.

[Image placeholder: an anechoic chamber]

Branches

  • Architectural acoustics — the sound quality of concert halls, theatres, classrooms and open-plan offices, including reverberation control and sound insulation between spaces.
  • Noise control — reducing environmental and industrial noise from transport, machinery and building services.
  • Electroacoustics — microphones, loudspeakers, headphones and hearing aids; closely tied to acousmatic and recorded sound.
  • Musical acoustics — the physics of instruments such as the acoustic guitar.
  • Underwater acoustics — sonar, ocean measurement and marine communication.
  • Medical acoustics — ultrasound imaging and lithotripsy.
  • Vibration engineering — isolation and analysis of structural vibration.

Practice

Acoustical engineers use measurement microphones, analysers and simulation tools (ray tracing, finite-element and boundary-element modelling) and work to standards set by bodies such as ANSI, ISO and national building codes. Professional societies include the Acoustical Society of America and the Institute of Acoustics in the UK. The field is often traced to Wallace Clement Sabine, whose work in the 1890s founded architectural acoustics and produced the reverberation-time formula still in use.
