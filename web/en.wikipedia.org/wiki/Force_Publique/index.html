The Force Publique (French: [fɔʁs pyblik], "Public Force") was the military and police force of the Congo Free State and later of the Belgian Congo, in existence from 1885 to 1960. It was composed of Congolese enlisted men (askaris) led by white, initially mostly Belgian, officers and NCOs, and it served as both the colony's army and its internal security and enforcement apparatus. The Force Publique is closely associated both with the rubber terror of the Congo Free State era and with notable Congolese military service in the two World Wars.

Under the Congo Free State

Leopold II of Belgium created the force in 1885, recruiting soldiers locally and from other parts of Africa, including Zanzibar, Nigeria, and Liberia. Under the Free State, the Force Publique enforced the rubber quota system in the Congo Basin; its soldiers and sentries were implicated in the atrocities of the period, including hostage-taking, punitive expeditions, and the practice of severing the hands of victims — abuses documented by the Casement Report of 1904 and the Congo Reform movement. After Belgium annexed the Congo in 1908, the force was reorganised as a colonial gendarmerie and territorial army.

World Wars

During the First World War, the Force Publique campaigned in German Kamerun and German East Africa, contributing to the capture of Tabora in 1916 under General Charles Tombeur. In the Second World War, Belgian Congo forces served in East Africa against Italian forces, taking part in the campaign in Ethiopia including the siege of Saïo in 1941, and Congolese units and medical personnel also served in the Middle East and Burma.

Mutiny and independence

Despite its long history, the officer corps remained entirely white up to independence: in 1960 there was not a single Congolese commissioned officer. Days after the Republic of the Congo became independent on 30 June 1960, the Force Publique mutinied against its Belgian officers (5 July 1960), an event that triggered the Congo Crisis. The force was renamed the Armée Nationale Congolaise (ANC).
