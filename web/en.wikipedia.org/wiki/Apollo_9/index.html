Apollo 9

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Apollo 9
CrewJames McDivitt, David Scott, Rusty Schweickart
Launch3 March 1969
Duration10 days
SpacecraftCSM Gumdrop; LM Spider
Spider in Earth orbit.

Apollo 9 was the third crewed mission of the Apollo program and the first flight of the complete Apollo spacecraft — command/service module and lunar module together — in Earth orbit in March 1969. James McDivitt, David Scott and Rusty Schweickart tested the LM's systems, rendezvous and docking, and the portable life-support backpack, in the rehearsal that made Apollo 10's lunar dress rehearsal and Apollo 11's landing possible.

Schweickart and Scott performed spacewalks; the LM Spider flew free of the CSM Gumdrop for over six hours, firing both descent and ascent engines — the first flight of a crewed vehicle that could not return through an atmosphere, a psychological Rubicon for the programme.

Significance

Apollo 9 was the quiet hinge of Apollo: every later mission depended on its proof that the fragile lunar module worked. Schweickart's EVA was curtailed by space sickness (he had vomited), which led NASA to build adaptation days into later flight plans.
