VT100

The VT100 is a video terminal introduced by Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC) in August 1978. It was one of the first terminals to support ANSI escape codes for cursor control and other functions, and DEC's implementation of the emerging ANSI X3.64 standard became the de facto model that virtually all later terminals and terminal emulators followed. The VT100's escape sequences remain in daily use today inside terminal emulators such as xterm and the consoles of modern operating systems.

Features

The VT100 displayed 24 lines of 80 or 132 columns of text on a 12-inch monochrome screen, generated from an Intel 8080-based design with a separate keyboard. It supported smooth scrolling, double-width and double-height characters, blinking, bold, reverse video, and underlined text, as well as a special graphics character set for drawing simple forms. Configuration was handled through on-screen "set-up" menus stored in non-volatile memory — an innovation at the time. An optional add-on board, the AVO (Advanced Video Option), extended its capabilities.

Legacy

The VT100 established the template for the entire VT series (VT220, VT320, VT420, VT520) and for the ANSI.SYS driver in MS-DOS. Its control sequences, descended from the ANSI X3.64/ECMA-48 standards, are still the vocabulary spoken between computers and terminal windows, and "vt100" remains a standard setting of the TERM environment variable on Unix-like systems.
