World Bank

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The World Bank is an international financial institution that provides loans and grants to the governments of low- and middle-income countries for the purpose of pursuing capital projects and development programmes. It comprises five organisations, principally the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD, founded 1944) and the International Development Association (IDA, 1960), together the "World Bank"; the wider World Bank Group adds the IFC, MIGA and ICSID.

History and work

Created at Bretton Woods alongside the IMF, the Bank first lent for Europe's post-war reconstruction, then pivoted to decolonising Asia and Africa: infrastructure, agriculture, health and education. From the 1980s it was central to structural-adjustment lending and the "Washington Consensus" policy package; criticism of social and environmental impacts spurred safeguards, debt-relief initiatives (HIPC), and a stated twin mission of ending extreme poverty and boosting shared prosperity. The Bank is also a major research and data producer — the World Development Report, Doing Business (discontinued after data controversies), and global poverty statistics.

Governance

Member countries own the Bank through shareholding weighted towards large economies; its president has by convention been a US nominee. Presidents have included Robert McNamara, who massively expanded lending, and reformers who refocused the institution on governance and climate; climate finance and the balance between lending volume and development outcomes remain central debates.
