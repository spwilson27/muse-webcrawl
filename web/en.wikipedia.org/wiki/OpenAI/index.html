OpenAI

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The OpenAI logo, a geometric knot-like floral mark]

OpenAI is an American artificial intelligence research organisation founded in December 2015 in San Francisco by Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Ilya Sutskever, Greg Brockman, and others, with a mission to ensure artificial general intelligence benefits humanity. It began as a non-profit and added a capped-profit subsidiary (OpenAI LP) in 2019.

OpenAI is best known for the GPT series of large language models and ChatGPT, launched on 30 November 2022, which reached an estimated 100 million users within about two months — the fastest-growing consumer application in history to that point — and ignited a global race in generative AI.

History and structure

Early work included Gym (2016), research on reinforcement learning and robotics, and increasingly large language models: GPT (2018), GPT-2 (2019, initially withheld over misuse concerns), GPT-3 (2020), and GPT-4 (2023), plus the DALL-E image models and Sora video model (2024). Microsoft invested heavily (2019, and a much larger deal in 2023) and integrates OpenAI models into Azure and Copilot products.

In November 2023 the board abruptly fired CEO Sam Altman, triggering an employee revolt and his reinstatement within days, with a new board — a governance crisis that exposed tension between the non-profit mission and commercial scale. OpenAI has since moved toward converting to a public-benefit structure amid litigation and debate.

Impact

OpenAI's products shifted AI from research to mass experience, reshaping software, search, education, and creative industries, and intensifying policy debate over safety, copyright in training data, and concentration of AI capability.
