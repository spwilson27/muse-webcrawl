Scouting for Boys

Scouting for Boys: A Handbook for Instruction in Good Citizenship is a book on Boy Scout training, published in various editions since 1908. Early editions were written and illustrated by Robert Baden-Powell, with later editions extensively rewritten by others. The book was originally a series of six fortnightly instalments issued from January to March 1908, and is regarded as the founding text of the Scout Movement.

Contents

The book sets out the Scout method: learning by doing in small groups (patrols), with instruction in woodcraft, observation, tracking, first aid, lifesaving, patriotism and chivalry. Baden-Powell drew heavily on his military experiences, particularly the Siege of Mafeking during the Second Boer War, and on Ernest Thompson Seton's Woodcraft Indians in North America.

Publication and influence

The first edition appeared amid great public enthusiasm, and boys across Britain spontaneously formed Scout patrols using the handbook. The book has been translated into many languages and is said to be one of the best-selling books of the 20th century, with estimated sales of 100 to 150 million copies. A companion volume, Scouting for Girls-style guidance, followed with The Handbook for Girl Guides in 1912.

Editions

Subsequent editions were revised by The Scout Association in the United Kingdom; the last edition overseen closely in Baden-Powell's spirit appeared in 1940. The book's text has since been adapted to modern editions in many national Scout associations.
