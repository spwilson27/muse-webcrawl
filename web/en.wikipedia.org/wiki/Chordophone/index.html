Chordophone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A chordophone is a musical instrument that makes sound by way of a vibrating string or strings stretched between two points. It is one of the five main divisions of instruments in the Hornbostel–Sachs system of musical instrument classification, along with idiophones, membranophones, aerophones and electrophones.

Classification

Hornbostel–Sachs subdivides chordophones into simple chordophones (zithers), where the strings run over a basic string bearer, and composite chordophones, where a resonator is integral to the instrument. Composite chordophones include lutes (such as the guitar and violin family), harps and lyres.

How they work

Strings may be set in vibration by plucking (guitar, harp), bowing (violin, cello), striking (piano, hammered dulcimer) or by wind (aeolian harp). The pitch depends on the string's length, tension and mass per unit length; shorter, tighter, lighter strings sound higher notes.

Examples

The chordophones include most instruments of the Western orchestra's string section, the piano, guitars and banjos, harps and lyres, zithers, dulcimers, and instruments from many other traditions such as the sitar, erhu, koto and oud.
