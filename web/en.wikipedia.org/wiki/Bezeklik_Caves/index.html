Bezeklik Caves

LocationFlaming Mountains, near Turpan, Xinjiang, China
Periodc. 5th–14th centuries
TypeBuddhist cave temples (grottoes)

The Bezeklik Caves (also Bezeklik Thousand Buddha Caves) are a complex of Buddhist cave temples dating from the 5th to the 14th centuries, cut into the cliffs of the Mutou Valley in the Flaming Mountains near Turpan in Xinjiang, China. They were an important religious centre of the Uyghur kingdom of Qocho on the northern Silk Road.

Art and exploration

The caves — 77 rock-cut grottoes survive — were decorated with murals depicting the Buddha, bodhisattvas, donors, and scenes of monastic life, including notable images of Uyghur princes and Tocharian monks. The name "Bezeklik" derives from a Uyghur word meaning "place of paintings".

In the early 20th century, expeditions led by Albert von Le Coq and other European and Japanese explorers removed large sections of the murals, many of which were taken to Berlin, where some were destroyed during the Second World War. The site suffered further damage over the centuries, including during the Cultural Revolution, but remains a significant monument of Silk Road Buddhism.
