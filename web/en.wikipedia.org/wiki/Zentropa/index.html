Zentropa, officially Zentropa Entertainments, is a Danish film production company founded in 1992 by director Lars von Trier and producer Peter Aalbæk Jensen. Named after the sinister railway company in von Trier's film Europa (released as Zentropa in North America), it became the largest film production company in Scandinavia and the institutional home of the Dogme 95 movement.

History

Zentropa's first production was von Trier's television series The Kingdom (1994), followed by Breaking the Waves (1996), which won the Grand Prix at Cannes. In 1995 von Trier and Thomas Vinterberg launched the Dogme 95 manifesto from within the company, and Zentropa produced the movement's defining works, including Vinterberg's The Celebration (1998) and Lone Scherfig's Italian for Beginners (2000). In 1997 the company moved into the former army barracks at Hvidovre, developing them into the Filmbyen studio complex.

Productions and influence

Zentropa has produced or co-produced hundreds of films and television series, including Dancer in the Dark (2000, Palme d'Or), Dogville (2003), After the Wedding (2006), The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo (2009), Melancholia (2011), A Royal Affair (2012), The Hunt (2012), Nymphomaniac (2013) and Another Round (2020, Academy Award for Best International Feature). It built international co-production and sales networks that reshaped the economics of European art cinema.

Controversies

The company has periodically attracted controversy, including over the pornography produced in the late 1990s under its Puzzy Power imprint — an attempt to make explicit films from a female perspective — and, during the #MeToo revelations of 2017, over accounts of a harsh workplace culture around co-founder Peter Aalbæk Jensen, who stepped back from daily management.
