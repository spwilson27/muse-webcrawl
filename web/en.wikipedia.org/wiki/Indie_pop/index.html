Indie pop

Indie pop is a music genre and subculture that combines guitar pop with a DIY ethic and an opposition to mainstream commercial pop music. It originated in the United Kingdom in the early 1980s from the post-punk independent scene, drawing on the melodic guitar music of 1960s pop and the jangly sound of bands such as the Byrds. Indie pop is generally more melodic, less abrasive and relatively angst-free compared with its sibling genre, indie rock.

Jangly guitar textures are a hallmark of the indie pop sound.

History

The roots of indie pop lie in the Scottish post-punk scene, particularly Orange Juice on the Postcard Records label, and in the self-released singles of bands like Television Personalities. A landmark moment was the NME magazine's C86 cassette (1986), which showcased jangly, melodic guitar bands including Primal Scream, the Pastels and the Wedding Present, and gave its name to a whole scene.

Twee pop and Sarah Records

A more deliberately innocent and lo-fi offshoot, twee pop, centred on Bristol's Sarah Records (home of the Field Mice and Heavenly) and, in the United States, Olympia's K Records, run by Calvin Johnson of Beat Happening. The Scottish band Belle and Sebastian carried the twee sensibility to wide acclaim in the late 1990s with albums such as If You're Feeling Sinister (1996).

Later developments

In the 2000s indie pop broadened to encompass synth-led and folk-influenced acts, and in the 2010s a softer, bedroom-recorded variant — sometimes called bedroom pop — emerged through artists recording at home and distributing music online. Indie pop aesthetics (melody, intimacy, amateur charm) have repeatedly fed back into mainstream pop.
