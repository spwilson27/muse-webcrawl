Canadian heraldry

Canadian heraldry is the cultural tradition and style of coats of arms and other heraldic achievements in both modern and historic Canada. It developed from British and French heraldic traditions and, since 1988, has been administered at home through the Canadian Heraldic Authority, one of the few national heraldic authorities in the world outside Europe.

History

Heraldry came to the territory of modern Canada with French and British colonists. The arms granted to Nova Scotia in 1625 are among the oldest in the Commonwealth outside Britain. For most of Canada's history, Canadians seeking arms petitioned the College of Arms in London or the Court of the Lord Lyon in Scotland.

On June 4, 1988, Queen Elizabeth II authorized the exercise of her heraldic prerogative in Canada by the Governor General, and the Canadian Heraldic Authority was created within the Chancellery of Honours at Rideau Hall in Ottawa. Canada thereby became the first Commonwealth realm to establish a domestic heraldic authority.

Characteristics

Canadian heraldry is notable for incorporating distinctively Canadian elements: maple leaves, ravens, narwhals, polar bears, and other northern fauna, as well as symbols drawn from Indigenous cultures, used with the permission of the communities concerned. The authority grants arms to Canadian citizens and organizations, and women receive arms on the same terms as men. Blazons are written in English or French, and grants are recorded in the Public Register of Arms, Flags, and Badges of Canada.

National symbols

The arms of Canada, adopted in 1921 and revised in 1994 to include the ribbon of the Order of Canada, combine the arms of England, Scotland, Ireland, and France with a sprig of three maple leaves. Each province and territory also bears arms granted or confirmed through Canadian authority.
