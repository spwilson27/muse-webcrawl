Optimal control

Optimal control theory is a branch of mathematical optimisation concerned with finding a control for a dynamical system over time such that an objective function — for example cost, energy or time — is optimised. It grew out of the calculus of variations and was formalised in the mid-twentieth century.

Key results include Pontryagin's maximum principle, which gives necessary conditions for optimality, and Bellman's dynamic programming and the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation. For linear systems with quadratic costs, the optimal controller is the linear-quadratic regulator (LQR).

Applications include aerospace guidance, robotics, chemical process control, economics and epidemiology. Numerical methods, including direct collocation and model predictive control, are widely used when analytical solutions are unavailable.
