Stimulant psychosis

Stimulant psychosis is a psychotic disorder induced by stimulant drugs such as cocaine, methamphetamine, and amphetamine, characterised by paranoia, delusions, hallucinations, and disorganised behaviour that typically emerges during intoxication or binge use and usually resolves with abstinence.

Symptoms

Persecutory delusions are the most common feature, often accompanied by auditory or tactile hallucinations (including formication, the sensation of insects crawling on the skin), hypervigilance, and agitation. Symptoms can closely mimic paranoid schizophrenia.

Causes and course

Excess dopaminergic activity is thought to underlie the condition. Risk rises with dose, sleep deprivation, and route of administration. Most episodes remit within days to weeks of abstinence, but a minority persist or recur, and stimulant use can trigger a chronic psychotic disorder in vulnerable individuals.

Treatment

Management involves stopping the stimulant, a calm environment, benzodiazepines for agitation, and antipsychotics when needed, along with treatment of the underlying substance use disorder.
