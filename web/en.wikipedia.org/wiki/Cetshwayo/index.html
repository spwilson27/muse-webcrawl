Cetshwayo kaMpande (c. 1826 – February 8, 1884) was the king of the Zulu Kingdom from 1872 to 1879 and again, briefly, from 1883. He led the Zulu nation during the Anglo-Zulu War of 1879, in which his army inflicted on Britain one of its most famous colonial defeats at the Battle of Isandlwana, before being defeated in turn at Ulundi.

Reign and war

Son of King Mpande, Cetshwayo secured the succession by defeating his brother Mbuyazi at the Battle of Ndondakusuka in 1856. As king he rebuilt Zulu military strength and sought to balance the pressures of the British in Natal and the Boers of the Transvaal. In December 1878 the British high commissioner Sir Bartle Frere issued an ultimatum designed to be rejected; British forces invaded in January 1879. After Isandlwana and the defence of Rorke's Drift, the British regrouped, burned the royal kraal at Ulundi, and captured Cetshwayo.

Exile, restoration and death

Exiled to Cape Town and then London, where he was received by Queen Victoria in 1882, Cetshwayo campaigned successfully for restoration. He returned in 1883 to a kingdom partitioned among thirteen chiefs, was attacked by his rival Zibhebhu, and fled to Eshowe, where he died in February 1884, possibly by poisoning. He was the last king of an independent, united Zulu kingdom.
