Ghostbusters II is a 1989 American supernatural comedy film directed by Ivan Reitman and written by Harold Ramis and Dan Aykroyd. It is the sequel to Ghostbusters (1984) and the second film in the Ghostbusters franchise, reuniting the original cast.

Plot

Five years after saving New York, the Ghostbusters have been driven out of business by lawsuits and restraining orders, and the team has drifted apart. Investigating a mysterious force that attacks Dana Barrett's baby, they discover a river of psychoreactive pink slime flowing beneath the city, fed by New Yorkers' negativity, and learn that the spirit of Vigo the Carpathian, a 16th-century tyrant imprisoned in a painting at the Manhattan Museum of Art, plans to possess Dana's son and return to life on New Year's Eve. The Ghostbusters reform, animate the Statue of Liberty with positively charged slime, and defeat Vigo with the help of the city's goodwill.

Reception

The film was a commercial success, grossing over $215 million worldwide, but received mixed reviews and was generally considered inferior to its predecessor. Its soundtrack featured Bobby Brown's "On Our Own". In later years the film was reassessed somewhat more warmly, and the franchise eventually continued with Ghostbusters: Afterlife (2021).
