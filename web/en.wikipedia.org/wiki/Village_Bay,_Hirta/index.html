Village Bay, Hirta

Village Bay (Scottish Gaelic: Bàgh a' Bhaile) is the main bay and settlement site on Hirta, the largest island of the St Kilda archipelago, Scotland. The sheltered, curving bay on Hirta's south-east coast was the home of St Kilda's island community for centuries until the evacuation of the islands in 1930.

The village

Above the shore runs the arc of "The Street", a row of cottages built in the 1860s to replace older blackhouses; some have been restored by the National Trust for Scotland, which owns the archipelago. Behind the village are hundreds of cleitean — small drystone storage structures unique to St Kilda — scattered over the slopes, used for storing seabirds, eggs, peat, and other goods.

The islanders lived from seabird harvesting (fulmars, gannets, and puffins), crofting, and weaving. Hardship, emigration, and the First World War reduced the population, and in August 1930 the remaining 36 islanders were evacuated to the mainland at their own request.

Today

St Kilda is a double UNESCO World Heritage Site, for both its culture and its natural environment. Village Bay is now occupied seasonally by National Trust work parties, scientists, and a small Ministry of Defence radar station staff. The bay is the usual landing place for visitors arriving by boat from the Outer Hebrides.
