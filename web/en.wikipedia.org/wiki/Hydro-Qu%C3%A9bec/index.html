Hydro-Québec

Hydro-Québec is a Canadian state-owned enterprise that manages the generation, transmission and distribution of electricity in Quebec. Established by the Government of Quebec in 1944 from the expropriation of several private firms, it was fully nationalized in 1963 during the Quiet Revolution under the slogan "Maîtres chez nous" ("Masters in our own house"). Hydro-Québec operates the largest power system in Canada, with an installed capacity of more than 37,000 megawatts, overwhelmingly from hydroelectric generating stations, and serves over four million customer accounts.

History

The company was created in 1944 when the province took over Montreal Light, Heat and Power. In 1963, under premier Jean Lesage and minister René Lévesque, Quebec nationalized the remaining private electricity distributors. During the 1970s Hydro-Québec built the massive James Bay Project in northern Quebec, including the Robert-Bourassa generating station, one of the world's largest underground powerhouses. The 1998 ice storm caused extensive damage to its transmission network in southern Quebec.

Operations

Hydro-Québec operates about 60 hydroelectric stations, a 735-kilovolt transmission backbone—technology it pioneered—and exports electricity to Ontario, New Brunswick and the northeastern United States. Its research institute, IREQ, is based in Varennes. Churchill Falls power, purchased under a long-term 1969 contract with Newfoundland, supplies a significant share of its energy.

Rates

Because of its abundant low-cost hydroelectricity, Quebec enjoys some of the lowest residential electricity rates in North America, a fact often cited in debates over the company's dividend payments to the provincial government.
