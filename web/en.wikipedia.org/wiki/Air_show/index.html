An air show (or airshow) is a public event at which aircraft are shown, typically including aerobatic flying displays, military demonstrations, and static exhibitions of aircraft on the ground. Air shows range from small local fly-ins to enormous international gatherings that combine trade exhibitions with flying programs and draw hundreds of thousands of spectators.

History

The first international aviation meet was the Grande Semaine d'Aviation held at Reims, France, in August 1909, which drew huge crowds and competing aviators from several countries. Air racing and "flying circuses" popularized aviation between the wars, including barnstorming troupes in the United States. After 1945, military display teams — such as the RAF Red Arrows, the U.S. Navy Blue Angels, the USAF Thunderbirds, and France's Patrouille de France — became the centerpiece of many shows.

Major air shows

The Paris Air Show at Le Bourget (founded 1909) and the Farnborough International Airshow in England are primarily aerospace trade fairs with flying displays. The Royal International Air Tattoo at RAF Fairford is the world's largest military air show. EAA AirVenture Oshkosh in Wisconsin is the largest gathering of aviation enthusiasts, attracting over 600,000 visitors and 10,000 aircraft annually. Other prominent events include the Dubai Airshow, the Singapore Airshow, MAKS in Russia, and Airshow China at Zhuhai.

Safety

Air shows are tightly regulated: display routines must be rehearsed and authorized, aerobatics are confined to a display box away from the crowd line, and minimum altitudes and distances are enforced. Accidents have nonetheless occurred, most infamously the 1988 Ramstein air show disaster in Germany, when three Italian jets collided and one crashed into the crowd, killing 70 people; the 2015 Shoreham Airshow crash in England killed 11 people on a road outside the airfield and led to major regulatory changes in the United Kingdom. Modern rules in many countries prohibit aerobatics over populated areas and require energy vectors to be directed away from spectators.
