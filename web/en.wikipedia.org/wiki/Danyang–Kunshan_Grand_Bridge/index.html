The Danyang–Kunshan Grand Bridge is a 164.8-kilometre-long (102.4 mi) viaduct on the Beijing–Shanghai High-Speed Railway in China. It is the world's longest bridge, a record recognised by Guinness World Records since its completion. The bridge runs between Danyang and Kunshan in Jiangsu province, roughly parallel to the Yangtze River, passing through the low-lying rice-paddy and lake country of the Yangtze Delta.

Construction

Construction began in 2006 and the bridge opened with the high-speed line on 30 June 2011. Rather than crossing a single obstacle, most of the structure is a long elevated viaduct carrying the railway over soft delta soils, rivers, canals, lakes and farmland; a section of about 9 kilometres (5.6 mi) crosses open water over Yangcheng Lake near Suzhou. Building on a viaduct avoided the need to acquire and sever large areas of valuable agricultural land and protected the line from flooding.

At its peak the project employed around 10,000 workers. The structure used enormous quantities of concrete and steel — on the order of 450,000 tonnes of steel — and consists largely of standardised prestressed-concrete box girder spans erected by launching gantries.

Setting

The line carried by the bridge connects Shanghai with Nanjing and ultimately Beijing, serving some of the most densely populated and economically productive territory in China. Trains crossing the bridge travel at speeds of up to 300 km/h (186 mph), so a full traversal of the bridge takes more than half an hour.
