Big Tree Records was an American record label founded in 1970 by Doug Morris and Dick Vanderbilt. Initially distributed by Ampex and later by Bell Records, the label was acquired by Atlantic Records in 1974, with Morris continuing to run it as an Atlantic subsidiary until it was folded into Atlantic in 1980.

[Image: A stack of 1970s Big Tree Records 45 rpm singles]

History and artists

Big Tree scored a series of 1970s pop hits. Its roster and releases included Brownsville Station ("Smokin' in the Boys Room"), Lobo, England Dan & John Ford Coley ("I'd Really Love to See You Tonight" and "Nights Are Forever Without You"), Hot Chocolate's U.S. releases ("Emma," "You Sexy Thing," "Every 1's a Winner"), Orleans, and the one-hit wonder group Dave and Ansil Collins in some territories. The label also released records by LeBlanc & Carr ("Falling") and by the Canadian band April Wine in the United States.

Legacy

Doug Morris went on to lead Atlantic Records and later became one of the most powerful executives in the music industry, heading Universal Music Group and Sony Music. Big Tree's catalogue is now administered by Atlantic/Rhino.
