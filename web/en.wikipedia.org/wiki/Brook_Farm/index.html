Brook Farm

Brook Farm, formally the Brook Farm Institute of Agriculture and Education, was a utopian experimental community in West Roxbury, Massachusetts, founded in 1841 by George Ripley and his wife Sophia Ripley. It was shaped by Transcendentalist ideals and sought to combine intellectual labor with manual work so that members could live simply, equally, and with time for culture and thought.

Founding and life

Members bought shares, worked on the farm or in the school, and shared in the proceeds. The community's school was financially important and attracted students from prominent families. Writers and thinkers associated with the circle included Nathaniel Hawthorne, an early member and trustee, and Margaret Fuller, a frequent visitor. Daily life mixed farming, teaching, publishing, music, and conversation.

Fourierism and decline

In 1844 Brook Farm reorganized along the lines of Charles Fourier's phalanx system, hoping a more systematic socialist model would secure its future. Debts mounted, and in 1846 a newly built Phalanstery building burned before completion. The community dissolved in 1847. Hawthorne later drew on his experience, critically, in The Blithedale Romance.

The site is now a historic landscape and burial ground, remembered as one of the best-known American intentional communities of the nineteenth century.
