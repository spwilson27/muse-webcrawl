Rickson Gracie (born 21 November 1958) is a Brazilian retired mixed martial artist and Brazilian jiu-jitsu practitioner, a member of the Gracie family. Regarded within the family as its greatest champion, he was the dominant Gracie fighter of the 1980s vale tudo scene in Brazil and won the Japan Open tournaments of 1994 and 1995, retiring undefeated in professional MMA with all of his victories by submission.

Early life

Rickson was born in Rio de Janeiro, the third son of Hélio Gracie, co-founder of Gracie jiu-jitsu. He began competing at age six and received his black belt at eighteen. At twenty he defeated the much larger Brazilian luta livre fighter Hugo Duarte's predecessor in fame — the 230-pound Rei Zulu — a victory he repeated in a famous 1984 rematch before a huge crowd at Maracanãzinho, cementing his status as the family's flagship fighter.

Fighting career

Rickson won the Vale Tudo Japan tournaments in 1994 and 1995, defeating all opponents by submission, including Nobuhiko Takada. He fought in Pride FC's first event in 1997, again submitting Takada. His final fight came in May 2000 at the Colosseum event in Japan, where he defeated Masakatsu Funaki by rear-naked choke. His documented MMA record is 11–0, all wins by submission; family lore credits him with hundreds of victories across jiu-jitsu, sambo (in which he also won competition honours), freestyle wrestling and challenge matches, a claim impossible to verify and sometimes debated. He was expected to fight Kazushi Sakuraba — the "Gracie Hunter" — but the match never took place, partly following the death of Rickson's son Rockson in late 2000.

Legacy and later life

Rickson has taught jiu-jitsu around the world, appeared in films (including a cameo in The Incredible Hulk, 2008), and in 2017 was promoted to red belt, the highest rank in Brazilian jiu-jitsu. His memoir Breathe: A Life in Flow (2021) describes his life, philosophy and grief for his son. His son Kron Gracie has competed in MMA, including in the UFC. Within the martial arts world Rickson is frequently cited by peers as the purest exponent of Gracie jiu-jitsu technique.
