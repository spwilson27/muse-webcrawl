Lancer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Overview
Type:Cavalry soldier
Primary weapon:Lance
Era:Ancient times – early 20th century; ceremonial today

A lancer is a cavalry soldier armed with a lance. Lances — long thrusting spears — were first used for mounted warfare by the Assyrians as early as the 7th century BC, and lance-armed cavalry remained a feature of warfare across Eurasia for well over two millennia. Lancers of the modern era — from the 18th to the early 20th century — were especially celebrated in Polish service, and regiments titled "lancers" or their equivalents (uhlans, szwoleżers) were raised by many European armies.

History

Medieval knights fought as heavy cavalry with the couched lance, but the type declined with the rise of pike-and-shot infantry. It was revived in the 18th century, strongly influenced by Polish cavalry practice. During the Napoleonic Wars, Polish lancers in French service — notably the Vistula Legion and the Imperial Guard's Polish chevau-légers — proved so effective, particularly at Albuera in 1811, that France, Britain, Prussia, Austria and Russia all converted or raised lancer regiments, typically dressed in the Polish style with the square-topped czapka helmet and a short pennon on the lance.

Later use

Lancers fought through the 19th century, including in the Crimean War (the Charge of the Light Brigade faced Russian lancer regiments) and the American Civil War in small numbers. In the First World War lancers served largely as mounted infantry once trench warfare was established. Between the wars most lancer regiments were mechanised, though several British, Indian and other Commonwealth regiments retain the lancer title and ceremonial lance today. Polish uhlans of 1939 became part of the (largely mythical) story of cavalry charging tanks during the invasion of Poland.
