Singles' Day

Also calledDouble 11 (双十一)
Date11 November
ObservancesShopping, entertainment, dating
OriginNanjing University, China (1993)

Singles' Day (Chinese: 光棍节; pinyin: Guānggùn Jié), also called Double 11 (双十一), is a Chinese unofficial holiday and shopping season that celebrates people who are not in romantic relationships. The date, 11 November (11/11), was chosen because the numeral 1 resembles an individual who is alone. The holiday originated among students at Nanjing University in 1993 and spread through Chinese universities during the 1990s.

Shopping festival

In 2009, the e-commerce company Alibaba turned Singles' Day into a shopping festival on its Tmall platform, offering steep discounts for 24 hours beginning at midnight on 11 November. The event grew rapidly into the largest physical retail and online shopping day in the world, surpassing Black Friday and Cyber Monday in the United States. Alibaba reported sales of about US$1 billion within the first minutes of the 2019 event and a gross merchandise volume exceeding US$84 billion across the extended 2021 campaign. Competing platforms such as JD.com and Pinduoduo run their own Double 11 promotions, and the festival has spread to Southeast Asia and other markets through Lazada and AliExpress.

Celebration

Beyond commerce, the day retains its original meaning for many young Chinese people: single people treat friends to meals, attend parties, or celebrate their independence; others use the date — with its four ones symbolising "one person" finding another — for dates, engagements, and weddings. A traditional food associated with the day is youtiao, deep-fried dough sticks whose shape resembles the numeral 1.

Criticism

The festival has been criticised for promoting materialism and excessive consumption, generating packaging waste, and using misleading discount practices. In recent years Chinese regulators have pressed platforms for clearer pricing and "rational consumption", and Alibaba and JD.com stopped publishing live sales totals after 2022 amid slower growth.
