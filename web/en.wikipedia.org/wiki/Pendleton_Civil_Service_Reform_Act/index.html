Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act is a United States federal law passed by Congress and signed by President Chester A. Arthur on 16 January 1883, which established that positions within the federal government should be awarded on the basis of merit instead of political affiliation (the spoils system). It created the bipartisan Civil Service Commission to administer competitive examinations and made it unlawful to fire or demote covered employees for political reasons.

The Act followed the assassination of President Garfield (1881) by a disappointed office-seeker, the scandal that turned reform sentiment into law. Initially it covered only about a tenth of federal positions, but presidents could (and did) extend coverage by executive order, “blanketing in” their appointees; by the mid-twentieth century the merit system covered the large majority of the federal workforce. Later landmarks include the Civil Service Reform Act of 1978, which replaced the Commission with the Office of Personnel Management and the Merit Systems Protection Board. The Pendleton Act remains the founding statute of the professional, non-partisan American civil service that reformers contrast with patronage systems elsewhere.
