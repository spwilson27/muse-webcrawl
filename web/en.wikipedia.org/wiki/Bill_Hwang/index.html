Bill Hwang (born Sung Kook Hwang, Korean: 황성국, in 1964) is a Korean-American investor who founded Archegos Capital Management, a family office whose sudden collapse in March 2021 caused tens of billions of dollars in losses at major global banks and became one of the largest trading blow-ups in modern financial history.

Early career

Hwang immigrated to the United States from South Korea as a teenager. He worked at Hyundai Securities in New York and later joined Julian Robertson's Tiger Management, becoming one of the "Tiger Cubs". In 2001 he founded Tiger Asia Management, a hedge fund focused on Asian equities. In 2012, Tiger Asia pleaded guilty to insider trading in Chinese bank stocks and paid a large fine; Hwang settled related SEC charges and was barred from the investment advisory industry in the United States.

Archegos

In 2013 Hwang converted Tiger Asia into the Archegos family office. Using total return swaps, Archegos built enormous, highly leveraged, and largely undisclosed concentrated positions in companies including ViacomCBS, Discovery, Baidu, and several Chinese education and tech firms. When ViacomCBS shares fell in March 2021 after a share offering, Archegos could not meet margin calls; its brokers — including Credit Suisse, Nomura, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley — liquidated positions, wiping out roughly $20 billion of Hwang's wealth in days and costing the banks more than $10 billion combined.

Prosecution

Hwang was arrested in April 2022 and charged with racketeering conspiracy, securities fraud, wire fraud and market manipulation. In July 2024 a federal jury in Manhattan convicted him on ten of eleven counts. In November 2024 he was sentenced to 18 years in federal prison.
