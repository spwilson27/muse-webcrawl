Iranian cuisine

Iranian cuisine is renowned for its saffron rice, kebabs, and aromatic stews.

Iranian cuisine or Persian cuisine comprises the culinary traditions of Iran. The country's food is characterised by fragrant rice dishes, grilled meats, thick aromatic stews (khoresh), and the interplay of sweet and sour flavours, with heavy use of herbs, saffron, dried fruits, pomegranate, and nuts.

Staples

Rice is the central staple, prepared as fluffy long-grained chelow, served with a crisp golden crust called tahdig, or cooked together with other ingredients as polo. Bread varieties include lavash, barbari, sangak, and taftoon. Chelow kebab — saffron rice with grilled lamb, beef, or chicken kebab, grilled tomato, and butter — is often called the national dish.

Stews and soups

Celebrated stews include ghormeh sabzi (herbs, kidney beans, and lamb with dried lime), fesenjān (chicken or duck in walnut and pomegranate molasses), and gheimeh (split peas and tomato with dried lime). Āsh reshteh is a thick noodle and herb soup, and abgoosht (dizi) is a lamb and chickpea stew mashed and eaten with bread.

Sweets and drinks

Desserts include saffron and rosewater ice cream (bastani sonnati, often served as a faloodeh, a frozen noodle sorbet), gaz nougat from Isfahan, and sohan brittle from Qom. Tea (chai) is the ubiquitous drink, brewed strong and taken with sugar cubes; doogh is a salted yogurt drink.
