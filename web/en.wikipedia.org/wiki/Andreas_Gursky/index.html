Andreas Gursky (born 15 January 1955) is a German photographer and professor at the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf. He is known for his large-format architecture and landscape colour photographs, often employing a high point of view and digital manipulation to create images of immense scale and detail. Gursky's works are among the most expensive photographs ever sold at auction.

Life and education

Gursky was born in Leipzig and grew up in Düsseldorf, where his father ran a commercial photography studio. He studied visual communication at the Folkwang School in Essen under Otto Steinert before attending the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf, where he was a student of Bernd Becher and joined the circle of photographers later known as the Düsseldorf School, including Thomas Struth, Candida Höfer, and Thomas Ruff.

Work

From the early 1990s Gursky produced enormous prints — often more than two metres high — of stock exchanges, supermarkets, factories, apartment blocks, raves, and natural landscapes. His elevated, distant viewpoint turns crowds and structures into patterns in which individual detail and overall order coexist. Works such as Paris, Montparnasse (1993), 99 Cent (1999), and the Ocean series made him one of the defining photographers of globalisation.

In November 2011 his Rhein II (1999), a digitally cleaned view of the river Rhine, sold at Christie's for US$4.34 million, then a world record for a photograph. His 99 Cent II Diptychon (2001) had earlier set auction records, selling for over $3.3 million in 2007.

Technique

Gursky was an early adopter of digital post-production, compositing multiple negatives or exposures and removing distracting elements to build images that are documentary in appearance but heavily constructed — a method he describes as serving a "vision" of the world rather than a record of a single moment.
