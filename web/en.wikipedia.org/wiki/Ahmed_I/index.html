Ahmed I (Ottoman Turkish: احمد اول; 18 April 1590 – 22 November 1617) was Sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1603 until his death in 1617. He acceded to the throne at the age of thirteen and is remembered both for breaking with the practice of royal fratricide and for commissioning the Sultan Ahmed Mosque — the "Blue Mosque" — in Istanbul.

Reign

Ahmed inherited wars on two fronts: against Habsburg Austria in Hungary and against Safavid Persia, which under Shah Abbas I had recovered Tabriz and much of the Caucasus. The Peace of Zsitvatorok (1606) ended the Long Turkish War with Austria on terms that recognized the Habsburg emperor as an equal, while the war with Persia ended unfavourably with the Treaty of Nasuh Pasha (1612), ceding territory in the Caucasus. Domestically he faced the Celali revolts in Anatolia, suppressed with severity by his grand viziers, and campaigns against Druze rebels in Lebanon.

Succession reform

Unlike his predecessors, Ahmed did not execute his younger brother Mustafa, instead confining him in the palace — the origin of the kafes ("cage") system of princely seclusion and the shift from fratricide toward succession by seniority among the sultan's male relatives.

Building and death

Ahmed ordered the construction of the Sultan Ahmed Mosque opposite Hagia Sophia; begun in 1609 and designed by Sedefkâr Mehmed Ağa, it was completed shortly before his death and remains one of Istanbul's defining monuments. He died of typhus at the Topkapı Palace in 1617, aged 27, and was buried in a mausoleum beside his mosque. His consort Kösem Sultan became one of the most powerful women in Ottoman history as mother and grandmother of later sultans.
