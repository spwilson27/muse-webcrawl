Journey to the Center of the Earth


Journey to the Center of the Earth
Author: Jules Verne
Original title: Voyage au centre de la Terre
Language: French
Published: 1864 (Hetzel)
Series: Voyages extraordinaires

Journey to the Center of the Earth (French: Voyage au centre de la Terre) is an 1864 science fiction novel by Jules Verne. Considered one of the founding works of the subterranean fiction genre, it follows a party who descend into the Earth through an Icelandic volcano.

Plot

Professor Otto Lidenbrock of Hamburg deciphers a runic manuscript by the 16th-century Icelandic alchemist Arne Saknussemm claiming a passage to the centre of the Earth via the crater of Snæfellsjökull in Iceland. Accompanied by his reluctant nephew Axel, the narrator, and their Icelandic guide Hans Bjelke, Lidenbrock descends into vast caverns, crosses a subterranean ocean, encounters prehistoric creatures and a giant humanoid, and is finally expelled through the erupting volcano of Stromboli in Italy.

Context

Verne drew on the geology of his day, including debates over the Earth's interior and the hollow Earth theories of earlier writers, and on Charles Lyell's geological writings. The novel was part of his Voyages extraordinaires series for the publisher Pierre-Jules Hetzel.

Adaptations

The story has been adapted many times, notably the 1959 film starring James Mason and Pat Boone, and the 2008 film starring Brendan Fraser, as well as for radio, television and the stage.
