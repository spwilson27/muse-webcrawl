Cardiff Castle

Cardiff Castle (Welsh: Castell Caerdydd) is a medieval castle and Victorian Gothic revival mansion located in the city centre of Cardiff, Wales. The original motte and bailey castle was built in the late 11th century by Norman invaders on top of a 3rd-century Roman fort.

History

The Roman fort at Cardiff was probably established around AD 55 and occupied until the end of the 4th century. After the Norman conquest, a motte was raised within the Roman walls, and the stone keep followed in the 12th century, promoted by Robert FitzHamon and later held by the de Clare family. The castle was repeatedly contested during the Welsh wars and was attacked by Owain Glyndŵr in 1404.

Victorian transformation

In the 19th century the castle passed to the Marquesses of Bute, whose coal wealth funded an extraordinary Gothic revival remodelling by architect William Burges for John Crichton-Stuart, 3rd Marquess of Bute. Burges created lavish interiors including the Arab Room, the Banqueting Hall, and the famous Clock Tower completed in 1873.

Today

The castle was given to the city of Cardiff in 1947 by the 5th Marquess and is now a major tourist attraction and venue, set within Bute Park. Its grounds also house the regimental museum of the Royal Welsh.
