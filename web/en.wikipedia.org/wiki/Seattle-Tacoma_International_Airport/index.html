Seattle–Tacoma International Airport (IATA: SEA), commonly called Sea-Tac Airport, is the primary international airport serving the Seattle metropolitan area in the U.S. state of Washington. It is located in the city of SeaTac, roughly 14 miles (23 km) south of downtown Seattle and 13 miles (21 km) north of Tacoma, and is owned and operated by the Port of Seattle. The airport is a hub for Alaska Airlines, its largest tenant, and a hub and international gateway for Delta Air Lines.

History

The airport was built in 1944 by the Port of Seattle with the support of the Civil Aeronautics Administration after the U.S. military took control of Seattle's Boeing Field during the Second World War. Commercial service began in 1947, and the airport was formally dedicated in 1949; the cost of the original construction was about $11 million. The name was changed from Seattle International Airport to Seattle–Tacoma International Airport in 1951 at the urging of Tacoma interests. A third runway opened in 2008 after a long and litigated construction process, giving the airport three parallel runways.

Terminals and facilities

Sea-Tac has one main terminal divided into check-in and baggage areas, with four concourses (A, B, C, D) and two satellite terminals (North and South) reached by an underground automated people mover, the SEA Underground. The South Satellite handles most international arrivals, which are processed through a newer International Arrivals Facility opened in 2022, connected to Concourse A by a long aerial walkway passing over the taxiway. The Central Terminal, renovated in 2005, is known for its large curtain-wall window overlooking the airfield and the Cascade Range.

Ground transportation

The airport is served by Interstate 5 and State Route 99, and since 2009 by the Link light rail 1 Line, whose SeaTac/Airport station connects the airport to downtown Seattle and, following extensions, to Lynnwood and Federal Way. A consolidated rental car facility opened in 2012 a short shuttle ride from the terminal.

Traffic

In recent years the airport has handled on the order of 50 million passengers annually, ranking among the ten busiest airports in the United States. Alaska Airlines and its regional partners account for roughly half of all passengers, followed by Delta. International services link Seattle with destinations in Asia, Europe, and the Middle East.
