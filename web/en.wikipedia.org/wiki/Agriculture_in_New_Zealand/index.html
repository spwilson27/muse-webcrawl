Agriculture in New Zealand

Agriculture in New Zealand is a cornerstone of the national economy. New Zealand is a major exporter of dairy products, meat, wool, fruit, and wine, and its pastoral farming systems — based on year-round grazing of ryegrass and clover pastures — are internationally noted. Agriculture, forestry, and fishing together employ a significant share of the workforce, and primary products make up a large majority of merchandise exports.

History

Māori cultivated kūmara, taro, and other crops introduced from Polynesia. European settlement from the nineteenth century transformed the landscape: forests were cleared for pasture, sheep numbers boomed to supply wool and, after the first refrigerated shipment in 1882, frozen meat to Britain. Refrigerated shipping also enabled the great dairy export trade. In 1984 the Fourth Labour Government abruptly removed nearly all farm subsidies and price supports, making New Zealand farming one of the least subsidised in the developed world and forcing rapid restructuring toward efficiency and market signals.

Sectors

Dairy is the largest sector, dominated by the cooperative Fonterra; New Zealand produces a substantial share of world traded dairy products. Sheep and beef farming remain major land uses, though sheep numbers have fallen from a 1982 peak of about 70 million. Horticulture — notably kiwifruit and apples — viticulture, arable cropping, and deer farming are also significant. Key issues include greenhouse-gas emissions from livestock, water quality, and land-use change.
