Menander I

Menander I Soter (Greek: Μένανδρος Σωτήρ, Ménandros Sōtḗr, "Menander the Saviour"; Pali: Milinda) was an Indo-Greek King who ruled the Indo-Greek Kingdom from about 165/155 BC to 130 BC. He is widely considered the most famous and successful of the Indo-Greek kings, extending Greek rule deep into northern India, with his capital at Sagala (modern Sialkot).

Reign

Menander's extensive coinage, found from Afghanistan to the Gangetic plain, testifies to the scale of his realm and the prosperity of Indo-Greek trade. Greek and Roman sources, including Strabo and Plutarch, remembered him as a just and popular ruler. He was a patron of Buddhism: in the Buddhist text Milinda Pañha ("The Questions of King Milinda"), he debates philosophy with the monk Nāgasena and is portrayed as converting to the Buddhist faith. His coins sometimes bear the dharmacakra (wheel of dharma) and the epithet dharmikasa ("follower of the Dharma") in Kharoshthi. Plutarch relates that after his death his ashes were distributed among his cities and honoured with monuments, in the manner of the Buddha's relics.
