Mnemiopsis

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Ctenophora
Class: Tentaculata
Order: Lobata
Family: Bolinopsidae
Genus: Mnemiopsis

Mnemiopsis is a genus of comb jellies (ctenophores) in the family Bolinopsidae. Its best-known species, Mnemiopsis leidyi — the warty comb jelly or sea walnut — is native to the eastern coasts of North and South America and has become a notorious invasive species in European and Asian waters.

Description

Mnemiopsis has an oval, transparent, lobed body typically 7–10 cm long. It swims using eight rows of fused cilia (comb rows) that refract light into iridescent colors, and it is bioluminescent. It is a simultaneous hermaphrodite capable of self-fertilization and extremely rapid reproduction.

Invasion of the Black Sea

Introduced into the Black Sea in the early 1980s, apparently in ships' ballast water, M. leidyi exploded in numbers, consuming zooplankton and fish eggs and larvae and contributing to the collapse of the anchovy fishery. It later spread to the Caspian Sea, the Baltic Sea, and the North Sea. Its impact in the Black Sea was partly checked by the arrival of another comb jelly, Beroe ovata, which preys on it.

Research

The genome of Mnemiopsis leidyi has been sequenced and is of great interest for understanding the early evolution of animals, including the origins of nervous systems and muscles.
