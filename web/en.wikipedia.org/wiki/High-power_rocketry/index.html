High-power rocketry

High-power rocketry is a hobby and technical pursuit similar to model rocketry but involving rockets flown with motors exceeding model-rocket limits: in United States practice, rockets using motors with more than 160 newton-seconds of total impulse (H class and above), average thrust above 80 newtons, or containing more than 125 grams of propellant, as well as rockets built from heavier materials or weighing more than 1,500 grams at liftoff.

Practice

High-power flyers typically build rockets from fibreglass, carbon fibre, plywood and aluminium rather than the balsa and cardboard of model rockets, and use commercially made reloadable solid motors based on ammonium perchlorate composite propellant (APCP). Recovery is usually by dual-deployment parachute systems with electronic altimeters: a small drogue parachute at apogee and a main parachute at low altitude.

Certification

In the United States, flyers must be certified by the National Association of Rocketry (NAR) or the Tripoli Rocketry Association (TRA) to purchase and fly high-power motors: Level 1 (H–I motors), Level 2 (J–L, requiring a written examination), and Level 3 (M and above, requiring a documented project reviewed by senior members). Launches are held under safety codes and, in the US, FAA waivers for flights into controlled airspace. Major launch events include LDRS and BALLS in the United States.

Safety

The hobby has a strong safety record under its certification regime; motors are certified by testing, and spark-producing metal components and guidance systems that would constitute missiles are prohibited.
