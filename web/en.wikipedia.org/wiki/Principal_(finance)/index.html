Principal (finance)

In finance, the principal is the original sum of money lent, invested, or borrowed, separate from any interest, dividends, or other earnings that accrue on it. The term is used across lending, bonds, mortgages, and investment accounting to distinguish the initial capital from the returns or charges applied to it.

[Image: Amortising principal balance]

Loans and mortgages

For a loan, the principal is the amount originally advanced to the borrower. Each periodic payment on an amortising loan is divided between interest (charged on the outstanding balance) and repayment of principal, so the outstanding principal declines over the life of the loan — typically slowly at first, when the balance and hence the interest charge is largest, and faster towards the end. Borrowers may make additional principal payments ("prepayments" or, on mortgages, overpayments) to shorten the term and reduce total interest.

Bonds

For a bond, the principal — also called the face value, par value, or nominal amount — is the sum the issuer promises to repay at maturity, and on which the bond's coupon interest payments are calculated. Bonds may trade at a premium or a discount to principal depending on market interest rates.

Investments

In an investment context, the principal is the original capital committed. Rules and products that promise to "protect the principal" (such as principal-protected notes) guarantee the return of this initial sum at maturity, subject to the issuer's creditworthiness, though not necessarily any positive return.
