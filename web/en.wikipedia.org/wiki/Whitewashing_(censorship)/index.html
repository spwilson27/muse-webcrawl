Whitewashing (censorship)

Whitewashing
TypeCensorship / reputation management
MeaningGlossing over or suppressing damaging information

Whitewashing is the practice of glossing over, concealing, or censoring unfavourable information — crimes, scandals, or historical facts — in order to present a subject in a better light. The term is a metaphor from whitewash, a cheap white paint used to cover up defects on walls. An official inquiry or campaign that exonerates wrongdoers despite evidence is often dismissed as a "whitewash".

Uses of the term

The figurative use dates to the 18th century; in politics it was popularised in the United States in the 1800s. The term is applied to historical revisionism (the sanitising of colonial or wartime records), corporate public relations, and edited media. In a separate, newer sense in film and media studies, "whitewashing" also describes the casting of white actors in roles of characters who are people of colour — a usage that has generated substantial debate in the entertainment industry.
