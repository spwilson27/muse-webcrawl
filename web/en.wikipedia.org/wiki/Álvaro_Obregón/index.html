Álvaro Obregón Salido (19 February 1880 – 17 July 1928) was a Mexican military leader and politician who served as the President of Mexico from 1920 to 1924. A brilliant strategist of the Mexican Revolution, he was the last great caudillo of the revolutionary era and presided over the beginning of the institutional reconstruction of the country.

Obregón was born in Siquisiva, Sonora, the youngest of eighteen children. Orphaned young, he worked as a mechanic, farmer and schoolteacher before entering politics. He rose to prominence during the revolution as the most successful general of the Constitutionalist army of Venustiano Carranza, defeating the forces of Pancho Villa in the decisive battles of Celaya in 1915, where he lost his right arm to an artillery shell.

Presidency

After breaking with Carranza and helping to overthrow him in 1920, Obregón won the presidential election of that year. His administration focused on rebuilding the economy and education system. Under his Secretary of Public Education, José Vasconcelos, Mexico experienced a cultural renaissance, with muralists such as Diego Rivera commissioned to paint public buildings and literacy campaigns extended into rural areas. Obregón also secured diplomatic recognition from the United States through the Bucareli Treaties of 1923.

Death

In 1928 Obregón was re-elected president but was assassinated on 17 July 1928, before taking office, by José de León Toral, a Catholic militant angered by the government's anti-clerical policies during the Cristero War. His death marked the end of the era of revolutionary caudillos and led to the founding of the National Revolutionary Party, the ancestor of the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI).
