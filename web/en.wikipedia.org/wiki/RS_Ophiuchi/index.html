RS Ophiuchi is a recurrent nova in the constellation Ophiuchus, roughly 5,000 light-years away. It is a symbiotic binary system consisting of a massive white dwarf accreting material from a red giant companion, and it erupts roughly every 15 to 20 years when the accreted hydrogen layer on the white dwarf ignites in a thermonuclear runaway.

Eruptions

At quiescence the system is around 12th magnitude, but in outburst it brightens to about magnitude 4.5–5, becoming visible to the naked eye within roughly a day before fading over a few months. Eruptions have been recorded in 1898, 1933, 1958, 1967, 1985, 2006 and most recently on 8 August 2021. The 2006 eruption was observed across the spectrum, from radio to X-rays, and showed a shock wave ploughing into the red giant's wind; the Fermi satellite detected gamma rays in 2021, confirming RS Ophiuchi as a gamma-ray source.

Significance

Because the white dwarf is believed to be near the Chandrasekhar limit (about 1.35 solar masses) and is gaining rather than losing mass on balance, RS Ophiuchi is a leading candidate progenitor of a future Type Ia supernova. Its rapid recurrence — among the shortest known — makes it a key laboratory for nova theory and shock physics.
