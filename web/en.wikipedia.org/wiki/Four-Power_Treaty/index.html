Four-Power Treaty

The Four-Power Treaty was a treaty signed by the United States, Great Britain, Japan, and France on December 13, 1921, during the Washington Naval Conference. Under its terms, the four signatories agreed to respect one another's rights regarding insular possessions and dominions in the Pacific region, and to consult jointly should a controversy arise between any of them over Pacific questions.

Terms and significance

The treaty provided that if a dispute threatened the harmonious accord of the parties, all would be invited to a joint conference to consider and adjust the matter. If their rights were threatened by the aggressive action of any other power, the signatories would communicate fully and frankly to arrive at an understanding of the most efficient measures to meet the situation.

Importantly, the treaty also provided for the termination of the Anglo-Japanese Alliance of 1902, which the United States had long viewed with suspicion. The alliance formally ended when the Four-Power Treaty's ratifications were exchanged in August 1923. The treaty was one of several concluded at the Washington Conference, alongside the Five-Power Treaty limiting naval armaments and the Nine-Power Treaty concerning China.

United States ratification

The United States Senate consented to ratification with a reservation stating that the treaty created no commitment to armed force, no alliance, and no obligation to join in defence. The treaty remained in force until it was effectively superseded by events of the 1930s; Japan gave notice of withdrawal, and the treaty lapsed in 1936.
