Hygroscopy

Hygroscopy is the phenomenon of attracting and holding water molecules via absorption or adsorption from the surrounding environment, usually at normal or room temperature. Materials that do this are described as hygroscopic.

Examples

Common hygroscopic substances include salts such as calcium chloride and sodium hydroxide, silica gel, honey and other sugar-rich materials, glycerol, ethanol, and many natural fibres. Some substances are deliquescent — they absorb so much water that they dissolve in it — while others, like silica gel, are used as desiccants to keep environments dry.

Applications

Hygroscopic behaviour matters in food storage, pharmaceuticals, building materials, and electronics packaging. Hygroscopic expansion and contraction of wood and paper with humidity must be allowed for in design. The property is distinct from capillarity and from hydrophilicity, though they are related.
