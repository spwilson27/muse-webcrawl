Jacques Cathelineau

Jacques Cathelineau (January 5, 1759 – July 14, 1793) was a French Vendéan counter-revolutionary leader during the French Revolution. Known as the "Saint of Anjou" for his piety, he led peasant forces in the War in the Vendée against the revolutionary government.

Life

Cathelineau was born in Le Pin-en-Mauges in Anjou. A peddler and former soldier, he was a devout Catholic churchwarden of his parish. When the revolutionary government ordered mass conscription in March 1793, resistance erupted in the Vendée; peasants of his district chose Cathelineau as their leader. His insurgents, armed with farm tools and hunting guns, won early victories at Jallais and Chemillé and captured Saumur in June 1793, in alliance with other Vendéan leaders such as Maurice d'Elbée and the marquis de Bonchamps.

After the capture of Saumur, Cathelineau was proclaimed generalissimo of the Catholic and Royal Army, the first commoner to hold supreme command. During the assault on Nantes on 29 June 1793 he was shot and seriously wounded; the attack failed, and he died of his wounds at Saint-Florent-le-Vieil on 14 July 1793. He was succeeded by d'Elbée.

Legacy

Cathelineau remains a hero of royalist and Catholic memory in the Vendée. In the 19th century his family was ennobled by the restored Bourbon monarchy in recognition of his service.
