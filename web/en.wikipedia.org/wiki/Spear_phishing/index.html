Spear phishing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Placeholder image — spear phishing messages are tailored to a specific target.

Spear phishing is a targeted form of phishing in which an attacker sends fraudulent messages designed for a specific individual or organisation, using information gathered about the target to make the message appear convincing. Unlike bulk phishing, which is sent indiscriminately to many recipients, spear phishing messages often reference the victim's name, employer, colleagues or current projects.

Techniques

Attackers research targets using social media, company websites and data breaches, then craft emails that appear to come from a trusted sender — a colleague, a bank or a software provider. The message typically urges the victim to open a malicious attachment, follow a link to a credential-harvesting page, or transfer money. Attacks on senior executives are sometimes called "whaling".

Notable incidents

Spear phishing has been the entry point for many high-profile intrusions attributed to state-sponsored groups, including the 2016 compromise of email accounts connected with the Democratic National Committee in the United States and numerous espionage campaigns against government agencies and defence contractors.

Defences

Defences include multi-factor authentication, email authentication protocols such as DMARC, user training and simulated phishing exercises, and technical filtering of attachments and links.
