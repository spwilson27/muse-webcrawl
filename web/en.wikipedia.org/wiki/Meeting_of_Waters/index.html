The Meeting of Waters (Portuguese: Encontro das Águas) is the confluence between the dark (blackwater) Rio Negro and the pale sandy-colored Solimões River, near Manaus in the Brazilian Amazon. For about 6 km (3.7 miles) the two rivers' waters run side by side without mixing, forming one of the main tourist attractions of Manaus.

Cause

The phenomenon results from the differences in temperature, density, and speed between the two rivers. The Rio Negro flows slowly (about 2 km/h) at around 28 °C, its water darkened by humic acids from flooded forests, while the Solimões flows faster (4–6 km/h) at a cooler 22 °C, laden with sediment from the Andes. The sharp boundary between them persists until turbulence finally merges the waters to form the lower Amazon River.

Protection

The Meeting of Waters was listed as protected natural heritage by Brazil's IPHAN in 2010, a step taken in response to controversy over a planned port development at the site. Boat tours from Manaus visit the confluence daily, and local legend explains the phenomenon through a story of two star-crossed lovers turned into rivers.
