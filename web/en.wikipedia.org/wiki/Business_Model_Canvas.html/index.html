Business Model Canvas

The Business Model Canvas is a strategic management template used for developing new business models and documenting existing ones. It offers a visual chart with elements describing a firm's or product's value proposition, infrastructure, customers, and finances, assisting businesses to align their activities by illustrating potential trade-offs.

The Business Model Canvas was initially proposed in 2005 by Swiss business theorist Alexander Osterwalder, based on his earlier PhD work on business model ontology, and was popularised in the 2010 book Business Model Generation, co-authored with Yves Pigneur. The nine "building blocks" of the model are mapped onto a single-page canvas.

  1. Customer segments — the different groups of people or organisations an enterprise aims to reach and serve.
  2. Value propositions — the bundle of products and services that create value for a specific customer segment.
  3. Channels — how a company communicates with and reaches its customer segments to deliver a value proposition.
  4. Customer relationships — the types of relationships a company establishes with specific customer segments.
  5. Revenue streams — the cash a company generates from each customer segment.
  6. Key resources — the most important assets required to make a business model work.
  7. Key activities — the most important things a company must do to make its business model work.
  8. Key partnerships — the network of suppliers and partners that make the business model work.
  9. Cost structure — all costs incurred to operate a business model.

Applications and criticism

The canvas is widely used by start-ups and established companies alike, and underpins related tools such as the Lean Canvas. Critics note that it says little about competition, strategy dynamics, and the external environment, and that its static snapshot format can oversimplify how businesses evolve.
