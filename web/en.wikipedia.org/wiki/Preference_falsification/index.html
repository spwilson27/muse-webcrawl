Preference falsification is the act of misrepresenting one's genuine wants or beliefs under perceived social pressures. It involves making choices that diverge from one's true preferences under the influence of monitoring by peers or authority figures. The concept was developed by social scientist Timur Kuran in his 1995 book Private Truths, Public Lies: The Social Consequences of Preference Falsification.

Kuran distinguishes between private preferences, what an individual truly wants, and public preferences, what the individual expresses in public. Because individuals value both expressive honesty and social acceptance or material rewards, the preferences they express are often a compromise weighted toward whatever opinion appears safe or advantageous to display.

Consequences

Unanticipated revolutions

Widespread preference falsification can conceal the true distribution of public opinion: even members of a silent majority may believe they are isolated. As a result, a regime or social norm that appears stable can collapse suddenly when a triggering event lowers the perceived cost of dissent and private preferences are revealed in cascade. Kuran applied this argument to the unexpected revolutions of 1989 in Eastern Europe and to the Iranian Revolution of 1979, arguing that revolutions surprise even their participants because the underlying discontent was hidden.

Distorted public knowledge

Because public discourse reflects falsified preferences, societies may preserve inefficient or unpopular policies for long periods. The stock of publicly expressed ideas — what Kuran calls public knowledge — can differ sharply from the broader store of private knowledge, constraining political and intellectual debate and discouraging innovation.

Preference cascades

When some individuals begin expressing previously hidden preferences, they lower the expressive cost for others whose thresholds for dissent are slightly higher. The resulting chain reaction, or preference cascade, can rapidly flip the apparent majority opinion, a mechanism related to the spiral of silence and to pluralistic ignorance.
