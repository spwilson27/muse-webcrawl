Jan Dirk Breytenbach (14 July 1932 – June 2024) was a South African military officer and author. He is best known as the founder and first commanding officer of 32 Battalion, one of the most decorated units of the South African Defence Force during the South African Border War, and as the founder of 44 Parachute Brigade.

Early life and career

Breytenbach was born in Bonnievale in the Cape Province. He joined the South African Army in 1950 and served in a variety of infantry and special forces roles. His younger brother, Breyten Breytenbach, became a renowned poet and anti-apartheid activist — a contrast frequently noted in accounts of the family. Jan Breytenbach undertook specialist training with foreign forces, including airborne and unconventional warfare instruction, which shaped his later approach to unit formation and training.

32 Battalion

In 1975, during the early phase of South Africa's intervention in the Angolan civil war (Operation Savannah), Breytenbach was tasked with organising former FNLA guerrillas — largely Portuguese-speaking Angolan soldiers — into a conventional battalion. The result was 32 Battalion, nicknamed the "Buffalo Battalion". The unit became known for its aggressive bush warfare tactics and fought extensively in southern Angola and northern Namibia. Breytenbach's leadership emphasised rigorous selection, decentralized initiative and deep familiarity with the operational area.

Later career and writing

After leaving 32 Battalion, Breytenbach held command and staff posts, including command of 44 Parachute Brigade, before retiring as a colonel. In retirement he wrote several memoirs and military histories drawing on his operational experience, including The Buffalo Soldiers, Forged in Battle, Eden's Exiles and The Plunderers. His works are widely cited by historians of the Border War, though his role in the apartheid-era military remains a subject of debate.

Death

Breytenbach died in June 2024, shortly before his 92nd birthday. Tributes from former soldiers and military historians marked him as one of the SADF's most influential unconventional commanders.
