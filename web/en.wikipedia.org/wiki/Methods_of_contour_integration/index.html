Methods of contour integration

Contour integration is a technique of complex analysis for evaluating certain integrals along paths in the complex plane. Methods of contour integration — choosing a suitable closed contour, indenting around singularities or branch cuts, and applying the residue theorem or Jordan's lemma — allow many difficult real integrals, improper integrals, and inverse Laplace transforms to be evaluated in closed form.

Core tools

  • Cauchy's integral theorem: the integral of a holomorphic function around a closed contour is zero.
  • Residue theorem: for a meromorphic function, the contour integral equals 2πi times the sum of enclosed residues.
  • Jordan's lemma: controls integrals over large arcs for functions with exponential factors.
  • ML inequality: bounds an integral by the maximum of the integrand times the path length.

Standard contours

Semicircular contours

Rational functions and products with eiaz are integrated over the real axis closed by a semicircle in the upper or lower half-plane, the choice depending on the sign of the exponential's exponent so that Jordan's lemma applies.

Keyhole contours

Integrands with branch points, such as xa/(1+x) for non-integer a, are handled with a keyhole contour that runs around a branch cut placed along the positive real axis.

Indented contours

When a pole lies on the path, small semicircular detours ("indentations") exclude it, contributing half-residues via the Sokhotski–Plemelj formulae; this is used for integrals interpreted as Cauchy principal values.

Rectangular and wedge contours

Rectangular contours exploit periodicity (e.g. for integrals of e−x²-type or hyperbolic functions), while wedge contours of angle π/n suit integrands involving xn.

Applications

Contour methods evaluate integrals such as ∫0∞ sin x / x dx = π/2, sums of infinite series via residues of π cot πz, and Bromwich integrals in the inversion of Laplace transforms used throughout physics and engineering.
