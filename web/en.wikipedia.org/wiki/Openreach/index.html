Openreach Limited is a company wholly owned by BT Group plc, that maintains the telephone cables, ducts, cabinets and exchanges that connect nearly all homes and businesses in the United Kingdom to the national broadband and telephone network. It was established in 2006 following an agreement between BT and the telecoms regulator, Ofcom, to implement undertakings pursuant to the Enterprise Act 2002, to ensure that rival telecom operators have equality of access to BT's local network.

History

Openreach was created following Ofcom's 2005 strategic review of telecommunications, which found that BT had an enduring economic bottleneck in the local access network. BT gave undertakings to create a functionally separate access services division.

In March 2017, BT agreed with Ofcom to legally separate Openreach into a distinct company with its own board and greater independence, while remaining wholly owned by BT. The separation was completed in 2018, and Openreach Limited became a separately registered company with around 31,000 staff transferred from BT.

Operations

Openreach does not sell services directly to the public; instead, communications providers such as BT Consumer, Sky, TalkTalk, and Vodafone buy wholesale access to its network and sell phone and broadband services to end customers. Openreach engineers install and repair lines on behalf of these providers. The company has been rolling out fibre-to-the-premises (FTTP) broadband across the UK, aiming to reach 25 million premises by the end of 2026.
