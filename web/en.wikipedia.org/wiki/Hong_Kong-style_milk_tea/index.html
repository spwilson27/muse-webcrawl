Hong Kong-style milk tea

Hong Kong-style milk tea, often called pantyhose tea or stocking tea, is a tea drink from Hong Kong consisting of black tea sweetened with evaporated milk or condensed milk. It is a signature item of cha chaan teng (Hong Kong diners) and a symbol of Hong Kong food culture.

Preparation

The tea is brewed from a blend of black teas — often Ceylon and Pu'er — and strained through a long cotton sackcloth filter resembling a stocking, which gives it an exceptionally smooth texture. The tea is "bumped" — poured repeatedly between vessels — to develop flavour and remove bitterness, then mixed with evaporated milk.

History

The drink developed during British colonial rule, adapting British afternoon tea to local tastes with stronger tea and preserved milk, since fresh milk was scarce. In 2017 the technique of making Hong Kong-style milk tea was inscribed on Hong Kong's first Intangible Cultural Heritage inventory list.

Variations

Common variants include yuanyang (a mix of milk tea and coffee), iced milk tea, and "cha jau" made with condensed milk only. Annual Hong Kong-style milk tea making competitions ("Kam Cha") crown champion tea masters.
