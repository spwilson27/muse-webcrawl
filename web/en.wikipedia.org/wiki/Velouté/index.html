Velouté
Velouté: a light stock thickened with a blond roux
TypeSauce
Place of originFrance
Main ingredientsLight stock (chicken, veal or fish), blond roux
ClassificationOne of the five French mother sauces

A velouté sauce (French pronunciation: [və.lu.te]) is a savoury sauce made from a roux and a light stock. It is one of the "mother sauces" of French cuisine listed by chef Auguste Escoffier in the early 20th century, along with espagnole, tomato, béchamel and hollandaise. The term velouté is French for "velvety," describing the sauce's smooth, pale finish.

Preparation

In preparing a velouté sauce, a light stock (one in which the bones of the base animal were not previously roasted, such as chicken, veal or fish stock) is thickened with a blond roux. A chicken velouté, for instance, uses chicken stock thickened with roux and served as the base for secondary ("daughter") sauces. The three main types are chicken, veal and fish velouté, depending on the base stock used.

Daughter sauces

Well-known sauces derived from velouté include allemande (finished with egg yolk and cream liaison), suprême (chicken velouté enriched with cream and mushroom liquor), and white wine sauce (fish velouté with white wine and cream). Blended with cream alone, a velouté also forms the basis of many cream soups in classical French cooking.
