St Briavels

St Briavels is a medium-sized village and civil parish in the Forest of Dean District of Gloucestershire, England. It lies approximately 8 miles (13 km) south of Coleford, close to the Wye Valley, on the border with Wales.

History

The village is dominated by St Briavels Castle, a Norman stronghold built around 1075 by William FitzOsbern, 1st Earl of Hereford, to defend the Welsh border. The castle was later administered by the Constable of St Briavels, a Crown office, and became a centre for the iron industry of the Forest of Dean: crossbow quarrels and other iron goods were produced in large numbers for the Crown in the 13th century.

The village church, St Mary's, dates largely from the 12th century. An annual "Bread and Cheese Dole" ceremony is held on Whit Sunday, in which pieces of bread and cheese are thrown from a wall to local "Dole Claimers" — a custom said to date from a charter granted by Milo of Gloucester in the 12th century in return for payment for wood taken from the surrounding woods.

Geography

St Briavels stands on a limestone plateau above the River Wye, at an elevation of around 200 metres (660 ft). It lies just outside the Wye Valley Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty boundary, with views towards Monmouthshire. Nearby villages include Brockweir, Hewelsfield and Clearwell.

Facilities

The village has a primary school, a post office and store, a pub (The George), and a recreation ground. The George Inn has served as a meeting place for the village's ancient Forest courts in past centuries.
