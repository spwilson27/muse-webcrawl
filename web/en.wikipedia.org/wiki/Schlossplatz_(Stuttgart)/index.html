Schlossplatz (Stuttgart)

LocationStuttgart-Mitte, Stuttgart, Germany
Named forThe New Palace (Neues Schloss)
Key featuresJubilee Column (1841), bandstand, fountains

The Schlossplatz (Palace Square) is the largest square in Stuttgart-Mitte, the central district of Stuttgart, capital of Baden-Württemberg. It takes its name from the Neues Schloss (New Palace), the Baroque residence of the kings of Württemberg, which dominates its eastern side. With its lawns, fountains and tree-lined walks, it functions as the city's green living room and a favourite meeting place.

Layout and monuments

The square was laid out in its present form in the 19th century. At its centre stands the Jubiläumssäule (Jubilee Column), erected in 1841 to mark the silver jubilee of King Wilhelm I, crowned by a figure of Concordia. The square also contains an ornate cast-iron bandstand (1871), two fountain basins added for the 1878 horticultural exhibition, and monuments to King Wilhelm II and the poet Friedrich Schiller nearby on Schillerplatz. The Kunstmuseum Stuttgart, the Königsbau shopping arcade and the main shopping street Königstraße border the square.

Events

The Schlossplatz hosts open-air concerts, the Stuttgart wine village's overflow events, public viewing for major football tournaments and the Christmas market. The adjacent New Palace now houses state government ministries, while the Old Palace (Altes Schloss) across the square contains the Württemberg State Museum.
