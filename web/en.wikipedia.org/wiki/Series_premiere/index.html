Series premiere

A series premiere is the first-ever episode of a television series. In the United States, many series premieres are broadcast in the fall, as part of the September premiere week, although new series can debut at any time of year, including as mid-season replacements.

Characteristics

The series premiere is often, though not always, the pilot episode — the standalone episode produced to demonstrate the concept, cast, and tone of a proposed series to network executives before a full season is ordered. A premiere typically introduces the main characters, establishes the setting and premise, and sets up the central conflicts or story arcs of the series.

Series premieres are heavily promoted because their ratings performance is an early indicator of a show's viability; a strong premiere audience can help sustain a series through its first season, while a weak debut may lead to quick cancellation. In the streaming era, premieres may consist of several episodes released simultaneously, and global day-and-date launches have become common.

The term is also used by analogy for the first release in other serialized media, though "premiere" in that context more often applies to films and theatrical productions.

Related terms

  • Season premiere — the first episode of a subsequent season of an established series.
  • Series finale — the final episode of a series.
  • Pilot episode — a test episode produced before the series is commissioned.
