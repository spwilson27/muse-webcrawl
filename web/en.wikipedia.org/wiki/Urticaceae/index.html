Urticaceae

Urticaceae

Order: Rosales

Genera: approx. 50–60

Species: approx. 2,600

Includes: Nettles, ramie, pellitory

The Urticaceae, the nettle family, is a family of flowering plants in the order Rosales. It comprises roughly 50 to 60 genera and about 2,600 species, ranging from herbs and shrubs to lianas and, rarely, trees. Its best-known members are the stinging nettles of the genus Urtica.

Characteristics

Many members bear characteristic stinging hairs, though some genera lack them. Flowers are small and unisexual, usually wind-pollinated, and the leaves frequently contain cystoliths — deposits of calcium carbonate visible as streaks or dots.

Economic importance

Ramie (Boehmeria nivea) is an important fibre crop in Asia, and several Pilea and Pellionia species are grown as ornamentals and houseplants. In modern classifications the family has been expanded to include genera formerly placed in the Cecropiaceae, such as Cecropia.

Distribution

The family is cosmopolitan but reaches its greatest diversity in the tropics, especially in Southeast Asia and the Neotropics, where many species grow as understorey plants in wet forests.
