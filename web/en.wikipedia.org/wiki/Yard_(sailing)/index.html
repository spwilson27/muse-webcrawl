Yard (sailing)

Yard
TypeSpar (rigging component)
OrientationHorizontal, crossing a mast
FunctionSupports and extends a square sail

A yard is a spar on a mast from which sails are set. It may be constructed of timber or steel or from more modern materials such as aluminium or carbon fibre. Although some types of fore-and-aft rigs have yards, the term is usually used to describe the horizontal spars on a square-rigged vessel, from which the square sails are hung.

Description

On a square rigger, each mast carries a series of yards, the largest and lowest being the main yard or course yard, with topsail, topgallant and royal yards above. The head (top edge) of a square sail is bent (lashed) to its yard, and the sail's shape and angle are controlled by lines — braces, lifts, sheets and clewlines — worked by the crew. Yards are hoisted, lowered and braced around to catch the wind; sailors climbed out along the yards, standing on footropes, to make or furl sail aloft.

Other rigs

Lateen-rigged vessels carry a single long, slung yard crossing the mast at an angle, and lug sails are set from a yard as well. On gaff-rigged vessels the spar at the head of the sail is a gaff rather than a yard, though the two are sometimes confused.
