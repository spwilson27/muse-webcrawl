The 21st Army Group was a British-led headquarters formed during the Second World War to command the ground forces of the United Kingdom and Canada in Northwest Europe. Commanded by Bernard Montgomery, it played a central role in the Normandy landings and the subsequent campaign through France, Belgium, the Netherlands and into northern Germany.

Formation and Normandy

The headquarters was formed in London in July 1943 to plan and execute the Allied landings in Normandy under Operation Overlord. For the assault phase, it controlled the British Second Army and the First Canadian Army, as well as the US First Army until American forces were transferred to the newly activated 12th Army Group in August 1944.

Campaign in Northwest Europe

After breaking out of Normandy, the 21st Army Group liberated much of Belgium, including Brussels and Antwerp, in September 1944. It mounted Operation Market Garden, the attempt to cross the Lower Rhine at Arnhem, which ended in failure. In 1945 it cleared the Rhineland in Operations Veritable and Grenade, crossed the Rhine in Operation Plunder, and advanced across the North German Plain, taking Hamburg and reaching the Baltic at Lübeck. German forces in the Netherlands, northwest Germany and Denmark surrendered to Montgomery at Lüneburg Heath on 4 May 1945.

Aftermath

In August 1945 the headquarters became the British Army of the Rhine, responsible for the British occupation zone in Germany.
