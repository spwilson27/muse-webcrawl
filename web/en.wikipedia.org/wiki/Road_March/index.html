Road March

OriginTrinidad and Tobago Carnival
First awarded1930s (title formalised in 1950s)
CriterionSong played most often at judging points

The Road March is a title and competition associated with the Trinidad and Tobago Carnival. The title is awarded to the song — traditionally a calypso, and in recent decades most often a soca song — that is played most frequently as costumed bands ("mas bands") cross the judging points during the Carnival Monday and Tuesday street parade.

History

The concept derives from the songs that revellers sang and bands played "on the road" during Carnival. From the mid-20th century the title became the most prestigious song award of the Carnival season, rivalled only by the Calypso Monarch crown. Winning the Road March can make an artist's career, and repeat winners such as the Mighty Sparrow, Lord Kitchener, Super Blue, Machel Montano and Fay-Ann Lyons are central figures in Carnival history.

Selection

Unlike judged competitions, the Road March is determined by the bands themselves: the tune the mas bands choose to play most often at the judging points wins. In some years the title has been decided by formal counts of plays at the Queen's Park Savannah and other judging venues.

Other carnivals

Road March competitions are also held at Caribbean carnivals elsewhere, including in Antigua, Barbados (Crop Over), St. Kitts, Grenada and at Caribbean carnival celebrations in Toronto, London and New York.
