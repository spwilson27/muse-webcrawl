Pedestrian street

Other namesPedestrianised street, car-free street
PurposeRetail and walking priority
Famous examplesStrøget (Copenhagen), Buchanan Street (Glasgow)

A pedestrian street is a street from which most or all vehicular traffic is excluded, so that the full width of the street is available for walking, shopping and street life. Pedestrian streets are commonly found in the commercial centres of towns and cities.

History

Although many streets in ancient and medieval cities were effectively pedestrian, deliberate pedestrianisation is largely a post-Second World War phenomenon. Copenhagen's Strøget, converted in 1962, is among the most famous early examples and demonstrated that pedestrian streets could increase retail trade rather than harm it. In Britain, streets such as London's Carnaby Street and Glasgow's Buchanan Street followed.

Design and effects

Pedestrian streets are usually paved across their whole width, with seating, planting and street furniture, and often permit access for deliveries at restricted hours, bicycles, and emergency vehicles. Supporters argue they improve safety, air quality and economic vitality; critics raise concerns about accessibility and the displacement of traffic to neighbouring streets.
