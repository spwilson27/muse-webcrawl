Sport in the Gambia

Sport in the Gambia is dominated by football, as in much of West Africa, with wrestling the most popular traditional spectator sport. The Gambia, the smallest country on mainland Africa, participates in the Olympic Games, the Commonwealth Games and the Africa Cup of Nations, and its athletes compete internationally in athletics, beach volleyball and judo despite limited facilities and funding.

Football

The Gambia national football team, nicknamed the Scorpions and governed by the Gambia Football Federation, long struggled to qualify for major tournaments before a breakthrough run to the quarter-finals on their Africa Cup of Nations debut at the 2021 tournament (held in 2022 in Cameroon) under coach Tom Saintfiet. The domestic top flight is the GFA League First Division; clubs such as Real de Banjul and Wallidan have historically dominated. Street and beach football are widespread, and local nawettan summer leagues draw large crowds.

Wrestling and other sports

Traditional wrestling (boreh do) is deeply rooted in Serer and other cultures and remains a major spectator attraction, with champions fêted like football stars. The Gambia has competed at every Summer Olympics since 1984 without winning a medal; sprinter Gina Bass became one of the country's best-known athletes, reaching World Championship finals in the 200 metres. The national beach volleyball teams have won African titles, and judoka Faye Njie became the first Gambian to win an African championship medal in his sport.
