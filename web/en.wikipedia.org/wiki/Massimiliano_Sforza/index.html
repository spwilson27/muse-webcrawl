Massimiliano Sforza, Duke of Milan 1512–1515.

Massimiliano Maria Sforza (25 January 1493 – 4 June 1530) was Duke of Milan from 1512 until his death... more precisely, until his abdication in 1515. He was the eldest son of Ludovico Sforza ("il Moro") and Beatrice d'Este, and a member of the Sforza family that had ruled Milan since 1450. His brief reign fell during the Italian Wars, when Milan was a prize contested by France, Spain, the Swiss Confederation, and the Papal States.

Early life

Maximilian was named after the Holy Roman Emperor Maximilian I, his father's ally. When the French invaded Lombardy in 1499–1500 and captured Ludovico, the young Massimiliano and his brother Francesco fled to the Imperial court at Innsbruck, where they were raised under the Emperor's protection.

Reign

After the French defeat in Italy in 1512, the Swiss restored Massimiliano as Duke of Milan. In reality he was largely a puppet of the Swiss cantons, who garrisoned the duchy and extracted heavy payments. His troops, together with the Swiss, won the Battle of Novara (1513) against the French. However, after Francis I of France defeated the Swiss at the Battle of Marignano in September 1515, Massimiliano was besieged in the Castello Sforzesco and agreed to abdicate in return for an annual pension of 30,000 ducats.

Later life and death

Massimiliano spent the rest of his life in France, living comfortably but under supervision, first at Amboise and then in Paris. He never married and died in Paris on 4 June 1530. The duchy of Milan eventually passed in 1521 to his younger brother, Francesco II Sforza, the last Sforza duke.
