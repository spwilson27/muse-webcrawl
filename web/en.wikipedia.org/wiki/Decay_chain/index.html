Decay chain

The uranium-238 decay series, a chain of alpha and beta decays ending at stable lead-206.

In nuclear science, a decay chain is a sequence of radioactive decays in which an unstable nucleus transforms into another unstable nucleus, and so on, until a stable nucleus is reached. Each step in the chain is characterised by its mode of decay — usually alpha decay or beta decay — and its half-life. Decay chains are of practical importance in nuclear power, radiometric dating, medicine and radiation protection.

The four classical series

Heavy nuclei decay mainly by alpha emission, which reduces the mass number by four; consequently all heavy decay chains fall into four families distinguished by the remainder of the mass number modulo 4. Three of them still occur in nature because their parent isotopes have half-lives comparable to the age of the Earth:

  • The thorium series (4n), beginning with thorium-232 and ending at lead-208
  • The radium or uranium series (4n+2), beginning with uranium-238 and ending at lead-206
  • The actinium series (4n+3), beginning with uranium-235 and ending at lead-207
  • The neptunium series (4n+1), whose parent, neptunium-237, has a half-life of only about two million years, so the chain is extinct in nature and ends at thallium-205

Behaviour and equilibrium

The amounts of the members of a chain are governed by the Bateman equations, which balance production by decay of the parent against loss by decay of the member itself. When a long-lived parent feeds a much shorter-lived daughter, the two eventually reach secular equilibrium, in which their activities become equal. This principle underlies radiometric dating methods such as uranium–lead dating and the operation of radionuclide generators in nuclear medicine, in which a longer-lived parent is "milked" for a short-lived daughter such as technetium-99m.

Decay chains also determine the long-term radiotoxicity of spent nuclear fuel: much of the residual activity of high-level waste over thousands of years comes from the decay chains of actinides such as plutonium-239 and americium-241.
