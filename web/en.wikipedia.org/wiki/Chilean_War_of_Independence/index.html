The Chilean War of Independence (Spanish: Guerra de la Independencia de Chile) was a military and political process that liberated Chile from Spanish colonial rule and established the Republic of Chile. It developed in the context of the Spanish American wars of independence sparked by Napoleon's invasion of Spain. The conflict is traditionally divided into three stages: the Patria Vieja (Old Homeland, 1810–1814), the Spanish Reconquista (1814–1817), and the Patria Nueva (New Homeland, 1817–1823), with royalist resistance in the south continuing until 1826.

Patria Vieja (1810–1814)

On 18 September 1810, Chilean criollo leaders formed a Government Junta in Santiago in the name of the captive Ferdinand VII — a date now celebrated as the start of independence. Under figures such as José Miguel Carrera, the patriots enacted reforms including freedom of the press and the first national flag and coat of arms. Royalist forces from Peru counterattacked, and after the patriot disaster at Rancagua (1–2 October 1814), Spanish forces retook control.

Reconquista and the Army of the Andes

During the harsh Reconquista, patriot leaders including Bernardo O'Higgins and Manuel Rodríguez fled across the Andes to Mendoza, Argentina, where with Governor José de San Martín they organised the Army of the Andes. In January–February 1817 this army made the celebrated Crossing of the Andes and defeated the royalists at the Battle of Chacabuco (12 February 1817). O'Higgins became Supreme Director, and Chilean independence was formally declared on 12 February 1818.

Patria Nueva (1817–1823)

After a royalist surprise victory at Cancha Rayada, San Martín's and O'Higgins's forces decisively defeated the Spanish at the Battle of Maipú (5 April 1818), securing the independence of central Chile. The new Chilean navy, commanded by Lord Thomas Cochrane, seized Valdivia (1820) and carried the liberation expedition to Peru. Royalist forces under Vicente Benavides waged guerrilla war (the Guerra a Muerte) in the south until 1822, and the last royalist stronghold, Chiloé, fell in 1826. O'Higgins abdicated in 1823 amid political conflict, ending the Patria Nueva period.
