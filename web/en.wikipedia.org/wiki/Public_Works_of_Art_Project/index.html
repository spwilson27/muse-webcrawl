Public Works of Art Project

The Public Works of Art Project (PWAP) was a New Deal work-relief program in the United States that employed artists during the Great Depression. Administered by George Biddle under the Treasury Department and funded through the Civil Works Administration, it operated from December 1933 to June 1934 — the first of the New Deal art programs.

Activities

The PWAP employed some 3,700 artists, who produced nearly 16,000 works of art — murals, paintings, sculptures, prints, and crafts — for public buildings such as schools, hospitals, libraries, and government offices. Artists were paid a weekly wage and were encouraged to take the "American scene" as their theme. Works were allocated to public institutions across the country; a major exhibition of PWAP art was held at the Corcoran Gallery in Washington, D.C., in 1934.

Legacy

Although short-lived, the PWAP set the precedent for later federal art programs: the Treasury Department's Section of Painting and Sculpture, the WPA's Federal Art Project, and the Treasury Relief Art Project. Participating artists included many who later became prominent, and the program demonstrated that government patronage of the arts could be part of economic recovery.
