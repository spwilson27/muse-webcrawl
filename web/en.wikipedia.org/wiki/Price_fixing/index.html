Price fixing

Price fixing is an agreement among market participants — typically competitors — to buy or sell a product, service, or commodity at a fixed price, or to maintain prices at a certain level, rather than allowing them to be set by competition. It is a form of collusion and is illegal under the antitrust and competition laws of most jurisdictions.

Legal treatment

In the United States, price fixing among competitors is a per se violation of Section 1 of the Sherman Antitrust Act, punishable by fines and imprisonment. The European Union prohibits it under Article 101 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Vertical price fixing between manufacturers and retailers (resale price maintenance) is assessed under varying standards.

Examples

Notable cases include the lysine and vitamins cartels of the 1990s, the LIBOR manipulation scandal, and the DRAM and LCD price-fixing conspiracies, which produced billions of dollars in fines. The OPEC oil cartel is a prominent example of intergovernmental price coordination generally exempt from domestic antitrust law.
