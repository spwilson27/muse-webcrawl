Sabor

Sabor may refer to several places and things, most notably the Sabor River (Portuguese: Rio Sabor) in northeastern Portugal. Other uses include a Croatian word for a parliament or assembly (the Croatian Parliament is called the Hrvatski sabor), and a Spanish/Portuguese word meaning "flavour" or "taste".

Sabor River

The Sabor is a river in the district of Bragança, in the Trás-os-Montes region of northeastern Portugal. It rises in the Sierra de la Culebra in the Spanish province of Zamora and flows generally southwest for about 120 km through Portuguese territory before joining the Douro near Torre de Moncorvo. Its valley is one of the most remote and least populated in Portugal, noted for rugged schist and granite landscapes, olive and almond groves, and rich wildlife, including golden eagles and Iberian ibex reintroduced to the area.

Baixo Sabor dam

The lower course of the river is impounded by the Baixo Sabor hydroelectric scheme, composed of two dams (the montante and jusante barrages) built by EDP and completed in the mid-2010s, which flooded part of the lower valley. The project was controversial because the valley hosted protected habitats under the EU Natura 2000 network; a conservation fund (the Fundo Baixo Sabor) was created to finance environmental mitigation in the region.

Other uses

  • Sabor — in Croatian political vocabulary, an assembly or diet; the national parliament is formally the Croatian Sabor.
  • Sabor — a locality and former municipality names in the Iberian Peninsula.
  • In fiction, Sabor is the name given to the leopard (in the original novels, a generic term for leopardess) in Edgar Rice Burroughs's Tarzan books, later used for the leopard antagonist in Disney's Tarzan (1999).
