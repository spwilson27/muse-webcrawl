The Brit Awards (often simply called the Brits) are the British Phonographic Industry's annual popular music awards. They are the United Kingdom's most high-profile music awards ceremony, broadly equivalent to the American Grammy Awards. The name was originally a shortened form of "British", "Britain" or "Britannia", and the trophy depicts Britannia.

History

The awards began in 1977 as the BPI Awards, held as part of the Silver Jubilee celebrations, when The Beatles won multiple honours. They became an annual event from 1982, and were first broadcast live in 1989 — a famously chaotic edition hosted by Samantha Fox and Mick Fleetwood. The ceremony has been held at London venues including Earls Court and, since 2011, The O2 Arena.

Categories and notable winners

Major categories include British Album of the Year, British Artist of the Year, British Song of the Year, International Artist of the Year and the Rising Star award. Robbie Williams is the most successful artist in Brits history, and Adele, Coldplay, Blur and Arctic Monkeys are among the acts with multiple Album of the Year wins. In 2022 the awards abolished gendered artist categories in favour of unified awards. In 2023 Harry Styles won all four categories in which he was nominated.

Memorable moments

The ceremony is known for flamboyant live performances and controversies, including Jarvis Cocker's stage invasion during Michael Jackson's performance in 1996 and Geri Halliwell's Union Jack dress in 1997.
