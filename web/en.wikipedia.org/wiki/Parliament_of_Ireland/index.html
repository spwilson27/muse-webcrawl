Parliament of Ireland

The Parliament of Ireland was the legislature of the Lordship, and later Kingdom, of Ireland, existing from 1297 until the end of 1800. It comprised the King of Ireland, a House of Lords of spiritual and temporal peers, and a House of Commons. It met at various places in Dublin, finally in the purpose-built Parliament House on College Green.

History

Created in the thirteenth century within the English Lordship of Ireland, the parliament was long subordinate to England: Poynings' Law (1494) required English approval for Irish legislation. The Constitution of 1782, won by Henry Grattan's Volunteers, granted legislative independence, giving rise to "Grattan's Parliament".

Abolition

After the failed rebellion of 1798, the Acts of Union 1800 abolished the Irish Parliament and united Ireland with Great Britain; Irish MPs thereafter sat at Westminster. The Parliament House was sold to the Bank of Ireland.
