Molding (decorative)

Molding (American English) or moulding (British English) is a strip of material with various profiles used to cover transitions between surfaces or for decoration. It is traditionally made from solid milled wood or plaster, but may be of plastic or reformed wood. In classical architecture and interior design, molding profiles follow a vocabulary of curves refined since antiquity.

Profiles

Common molding profiles include the cyma (a double curve, S-shaped profile, divided into cyma recta and cyma reversa), the ovolo (a convex quarter-round), the cavetto (a concave quarter-round), the scotia (a deeper concave curve), the torus (a large convex semicircular profile), the astragal (a small half-round bead), and flat elements such as the fillet and fascia. Combinations of these form the profiles of the classical orders.

Types by location

Moldings are named for their position: cornice or crown molding at the top of a wall, chair rail at dado height, baseboard (skirting board) at the floor, casing around doors and windows, and picture rail near the ceiling. In classical entablatures, moldings articulate the architrave, frieze and cornice.

History and manufacture

Stone moldings appear in Egyptian and Greek architecture; Roman, Renaissance and Neoclassical architects codified their profiles. Traditionally, wood moldings were shaped with molding planes, and plaster cornices were run in place with templates or cast in moulds. Modern moldings are milled by machine or extruded in MDF, polyurethane or PVC, and are often purely decorative rather than structural.
