Kensington Palace is a royal residence set in Kensington Gardens, in the Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea in London. It has been a residence of the British royal family since the 17th century and is the official London residence of the Prince and Princess of Wales, among other royals. The State Rooms are open to the public and are managed by Historic Royal Palaces.

History

The original building, Nottingham House, was a Jacobean mansion built in 1605. In 1689 William III and Mary II bought the house, seeking a home away from the river air of Whitehall that aggravated William's asthma, and had Sir Christopher Wren and Nicholas Hawksmoor enlarge it into a palace. Queen Anne later had the Orangery built (1704) and the gardens laid out. Kensington was the favourite residence of successive monarchs until the death of George II there in 1760, after which it became home to junior members of the royal family.

Queen Victoria was born at Kensington Palace in 1819 and lived there until she acceded to the throne in 1837; she famously learned of her accession in the palace. In the late 20th century the palace was the London home of Charles and Diana, Prince and Princess of Wales, and after Diana's death in 1997 the palace gates became a focal point for public mourning. Prince William and Catherine, Prince and Princess of Wales, use Apartment 1A as their official London residence, and the palace also houses the offices of several royal households.
