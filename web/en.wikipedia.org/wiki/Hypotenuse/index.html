Hypotenuse

In geometry, a hypotenuse is the side of a right-angled triangle opposite the right angle. It is the longest side of the triangle, and one of the two sides adjacent to the right angle is called a leg (or cathetus). The length of the hypotenuse follows from the Pythagorean theorem: the square of its length equals the sum of the squares of the lengths of the two legs.

A right triangle with hypotenuse c and legs a and b.

Calculating the hypotenuse

If the legs have lengths a and b, the hypotenuse has length c = √(a² + b²). Conversely, a leg can be found from the hypotenuse and the other leg: a = √(c² − b²). Ratios involving the hypotenuse define the trigonometric functions sine and cosine: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse and cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse.

Properties

The midpoint of the hypotenuse is equidistant from all three vertices of the right triangle; it is the circumcentre of the triangle, and the hypotenuse is the diameter of the circumcircle (an instance of Thales's theorem). The word hypotenuse comes from the Greek hypoteinousa, "stretching under" (the right angle).
