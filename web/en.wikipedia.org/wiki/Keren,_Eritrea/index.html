Keren, Eritrea

Placeholder image: Keren, in the Anseba region.

Keren (Tigrinya: ከረን; Arabic: كرن) is the second-largest city in Eritrea and the capital of the Anseba Region. It lies about 91 kilometres northwest of the capital Asmara at an elevation of roughly 1,390 metres, in a basin surrounded by mountains. Its population is on the order of 80,000–120,000, drawn largely from the Bilen and Tigre peoples.

History

Keren grew as a trading town on routes between the Red Sea coast and the Sudanese lowlands, and expanded under Egyptian and then Italian colonial rule. It was the site of the Battle of Keren in 1941, a hard-fought engagement of the East African Campaign in which British and Commonwealth forces broke Italian defences in the surrounding hills. During the Eritrean War of Independence, control of the town changed hands several times, notably in 1977–78.

Landmarks and culture

Notable sites include the Mariam Da'arit shrine set inside a large baobab tree, the Italian and Commonwealth war cemeteries, and the town's lively livestock market and camel market. The old railway station recalls the era of the Eritrean Railway, which once connected Keren to Asmara and Massawa.

Climate

Keren has a semi-arid climate moderated by its altitude, with a short rainy season in summer and mild, dry winters.
