Music industry

The music industry consists of the individuals and organisations that earn money by creating new songs and musical recordings, selling and distributing recorded music, presenting live concerts, and managing the rights of musical works. Its main sectors are the recorded music business (record labels and streaming services), music publishing (songwriters, publishers and collecting societies administering copyright), and the live music business (promoters, venues and ticketing).

Structure

Three major label groups — Universal Music Group, Sony Music and Warner Music Group — together account for the majority of global recorded music revenue, alongside thousands of independent labels. According to the IFPI, global recorded music revenues were about US$28.6 billion in 2023, with streaming contributing roughly two-thirds of the total. Major live-music companies include Live Nation and AEG Presents.

History

The industry grew from 19th-century sheet music publishing (Tin Pan Alley) and the phonograph, through radio, vinyl LPs, cassettes and the compact disc, which peaked around 1999. Widespread unauthorised file sharing after the launch of Napster halved recorded revenues over the following decade and a half, before licensed downloads and then subscription streaming services such as Spotify and Apple Music returned the industry to growth from 2015 onward. The vinyl revival of the 2010s–2020s made LPs the leading physical format again.
