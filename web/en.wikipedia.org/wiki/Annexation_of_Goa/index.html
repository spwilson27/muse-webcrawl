Annexation of Goa

The Annexation of Goa was the process in which the Republic of India annexed the State of Portuguese India, the then Portuguese Indian territories of Goa, Daman and Diu, starting with the armed action carried out by the Indian Armed Forces in December 1961. In India, this action is referred to as the "Liberation of Goa", while in Portugal it is referred to as the "Invasion of Goa".

Background

After India gained independence from British rule in 1947, Portugal refused to negotiate the transfer of its Indian enclaves, which it had held since 1510. The Portuguese dictator António de Oliveira Salazar insisted that the territories were integral parts of Portugal. Diplomatic efforts and a largely non-violent satyagraha movement in the 1950s failed to dislodge the colonial administration.

Operation Vijay

On 17 December 1961, India launched Operation Vijay, involving air, sea and land strikes. Portuguese forces, numbering roughly 3,300, faced some 45,000 Indian troops. After brief fighting, the Portuguese Governor-General Manuel António Vassalo e Silva surrendered on 19 December 1961.

Aftermath

Goa, Daman and Diu were incorporated as a Union Territory of India. Goa later attained full statehood in 1987, while Daman and Diu remained a separate union territory. Portugal refused to recognise Indian sovereignty until 1974, following the Carnation Revolution.
