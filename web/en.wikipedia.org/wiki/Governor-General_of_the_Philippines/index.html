Governor-General of the Philippines

Style: His Excellency
Residence: Malacañang Palace, Manila (from 1863)
First holder: Miguel López de Legazpi (1565, as governor)
Last holder: Frank Murphy (1935)
Abolished: 15 November 1935 (replaced by High Commissioner)

The governor-general of the Philippines (Gobernador-General de Filipinas) was the chief executive of the Philippines during its colonial period, first under Spain (1565–1898) and then under the United States (1898–1935). The office represented the Spanish Crown and, later, the US President, and combined executive, legislative and judicial-supervisory authority over the archipelago. The title was also, in Spanish usage, Captain-General in his military capacity.

Spanish period (1565–1898)

The Spanish colonial government was established by Miguel López de Legazpi, who became the first governor in 1565. For its first decades the Philippines were ruled from New Spain (Mexico), to which the islands were administratively attached until Mexican independence in 1821; thereafter the governor-general reported directly to Madrid through the Ministry of Overseas Territories. Spanish governors-general — 122 men served in the Spanish period — exercised sweeping powers, tempered by the residencia (an official review at the end of a term) and by the influence of the Church and the Royal Audiencia of Manila. Notable holders included Miguel López de Legazpi, Basco José de Basco y Vargas (who promoted economic reforms in the 18th century), Narciso Clavería (who systematized Filipino surnames in 1849), and, during the Philippine Revolution, Camilo de Polavieja and Fernando Primo de Rivera.

American period (1898–1935)

Following the Spanish–American War, the United States first governed through military governors (1898–1901), then through a civil governor-general appointed by the US President, beginning with William Howard Taft (1901–1903), who became the first civil Governor-General. Successors included Luke Edward Wright, Francis Burton Harrison (who advanced Filipinization of the civil service under the Jones Law of 1916), Leonard Wood, Henry L. Stimson, Dwight F. Davis, and Frank Murphy, the last holder of the title.

With the inauguration of the Commonwealth of the Philippines on 15 November 1935, under President Manuel L. Quezon, executive authority passed to the Filipino government and the governor-generalship was replaced by the office of United States High Commissioner to the Philippines. Full independence followed on 4 July 1946.
