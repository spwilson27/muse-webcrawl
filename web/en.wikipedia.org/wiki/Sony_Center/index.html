Sony Center
Location: Potsdamer Platz, Berlin, Germany
Architect: Helmut Jahn
Completed: 2000
Contents: Offices, cinemas, museum, apartments

The Sony Center is a complex of buildings at Potsdamer Platz in Berlin, Germany, designed by architect Helmut Jahn and completed in 2000. It was built on land that had been divided by the Berlin Wall and was developed as Sony's European headquarters and an entertainment quarter.

Design

The complex consists of seven buildings arranged around a central oval forum covered by a distinctive tent-like glass-and-fabric roof, said to be inspired by Mount Fuji. The roof is illuminated at night and changes colour. The forum is a public space containing cafés and restaurants.

Contents

The Sony Center includes office space, luxury apartments, the Cinestar cinema complex (a venue of the Berlin International Film Festival), a film museum, and a Legoland Discovery Centre. The historic Kaisersaal and parts of the former Grand Hotel Esplanade were preserved and integrated into the new construction — one section of the hotel was famously moved on rollers.

Ownership

Sony sold the complex in 2008 to a Morgan Stanley real-estate fund and partners; it was later resold, and the Sony Center name was retained for a time before rebranding plans emerged.
