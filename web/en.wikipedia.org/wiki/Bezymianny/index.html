Bezymianny (Russian: Безымянный, meaning "unnamed") is an active stratovolcano in Kamchatka, Russia, part of the Klyuchevskaya group of volcanoes. It was considered extinct until its catastrophic eruption of 1955–56.

The 1956 eruption

After roughly a thousand years of quiet, Bezymianny reawakened in October 1955. On 30 March 1956 the volcano's flank collapsed in a massive landslide and a directed lateral blast devastated some 500 km² of forest, in an event closely analogous to the 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens. The eruption, rated VEI 5, blasted away the summit and created a large horseshoe-shaped crater. Remarkably, there were no casualties, as the area was unpopulated.

Subsequent activity

A lava dome has been growing in the crater ever since, accompanied by frequent explosive eruptions, pyroclastic flows and ash plumes monitored by the Kamchatka Volcanic Eruption Response Team. Bezymianny remains one of the most active volcanoes of Kamchatka.
