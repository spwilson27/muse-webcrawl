Heisei era

The Heisei era (平成) is the period of Japanese history corresponding to the reign of Emperor Akihito, from 8 January 1989—the day after the death of his father, Emperor Shōwa (Hirohito)—until his abdication on 30 April 2019. It was preceded by the Shōwa era and succeeded by the Reiwa era. The name Heisei, drawn from classical Chinese histories, connotes "peace everywhere" or "peace and success."

Overview

The era began almost exactly with the collapse of Japan's asset price bubble (1990–91), which ended the country's spectacular post-war growth and ushered in the prolonged economic stagnation known as the "Lost Decades": deflation, slow growth, rising public debt and labour-market change. Against this, the period saw Japan's population peak and begin to decline, two great natural disasters—the Great Hanshin earthquake of 1995 and the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami, which caused the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster—and the continued globalisation of Japanese popular culture, from anime and video games to cuisine. Politically, the Liberal Democratic Party lost power for extended periods for the first time (1993–94 and 2009–12). The era closed with the first imperial abdication in Japan in about two centuries, permitted by special legislation in 2017; Akihito was succeeded by his son Naruhito on 1 May 2019.

Conversion

To convert a Gregorian year between 1989 and 2019 to the Heisei year, subtract 1988 (e.g., 2000 = Heisei 12).
