Activision

Activision Publishing, Inc. is an American video game publisher based in Santa Monica, California. Founded in 1979 by former Atari programmers David Crane, Larry Kaplan, Alan Miller and Bob Whitehead, together with Jim Levy, it was the world's first independent developer and distributor of video games for home consoles — the first third-party game developer.

History

Activision found early success with Atari 2600 titles such as Pitfall! (1982). After the video game crash of 1983 it diversified onto home computers, and under CEO Bobby Kotick (from 1991) it was rebuilt into a major publisher, acquiring studios and hit franchises including Tony Hawk's, Guitar Hero and Call of Duty. In 2008 it merged with Vivendi Games to form Activision Blizzard, which Microsoft acquired in 2023 for $68.7 billion; Activision continues as a publishing unit of Microsoft Gaming.
