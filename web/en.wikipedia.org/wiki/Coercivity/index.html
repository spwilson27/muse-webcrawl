Coercivity
Common symbolHc
SI unitampere per metre (A/m)
Other unitoersted (Oe)

Coercivity, also called the magnetic coercivity, coercive field or coercive force, is a measure of the ability of a ferromagnetic material to withstand an external magnetic field without becoming demagnetised. Coercivity is normally measured in oersted or ampere/metre units and is denoted Hc.

Definition

On a hysteresis loop, the coercive field is the applied field required to reduce the magnetisation (or the magnetic flux density, for the related quantity intrinsic coercivity) of a material to zero after it has been driven to saturation.

Soft and hard magnets

Materials with low coercivity, such as silicon steel, are called magnetically soft and are used in transformer cores and inductors. Materials with high coercivity, such as neodymium magnets and alnico, are magnetically hard and used to make permanent magnets. Magnetic recording media have intermediate coercivities chosen to balance writability against data stability.
