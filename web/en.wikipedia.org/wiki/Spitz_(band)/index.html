Spitz (スピッツ, Supittsu) is a Japanese rock band formed in 1987 by four art-school students in Shizuoka. The group — vocalist and guitarist Masamune Kusano, guitarist Tetsuya Miwa, bassist Akihiro Tamura, and drummer Tatsuo Sakiyama — took its name from the dog breed, suggested by Kusano because the word sounded sharp and memorable. They made their major-label debut in 1991.

Career

Spitz achieved commercial breakthrough in the mid-1990s with the singles "Sora mo Toberu Hazu" (1994) and "Robinson" (1995), the latter selling over a million copies and remaining one of the band's signature songs. The albums Hachimitsu (1995) and Indigo Chiheisen (1996) consolidated their popularity, and later hits such as "Cherry" became karaoke standards in Japan.

The band's sound combines melodic guitar pop with folk and alternative-rock influences, distinguished by Kusano's high, clear voice and lyrical songwriting. Despite changes in the Japanese music industry, Spitz has maintained a stable line-up and a loyal audience for more than three decades.
