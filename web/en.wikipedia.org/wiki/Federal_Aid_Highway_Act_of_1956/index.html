The Federal Aid Highway Act of 1956, officially the National Interstate and Defense Highways Act, authorised the construction of 41,000 miles of the Interstate Highway System in the United States. Signed into law by President Dwight D. Eisenhower on 29 June 1956, it was the largest public works programme in American history up to that time, with the federal government bearing 90% of construction costs.

Background

Eisenhower's support drew on his experience of the 1919 Army convoy across the United States and his admiration for the German autobahn network seen during the Second World War. National defence — rapid troop movement and urban evacuation — featured prominently in arguments for the system.

Implementation

The act established the Highway Trust Fund, supplied by fuel taxes, and set uniform Interstate design standards. Construction proceeded for more than three decades; the system was formally declared complete in 1992, though new segments are still added.
