Self-archiving

Self-archiving is the act of depositing a free copy of an electronic document on the World Wide Web in order to provide open access to it. The term usually refers to the self-archiving of peer-reviewed research journal articles and conference papers, as well as theses, book chapters and other scholarly texts, in an institutional repository or subject repository such as arXiv, bioRxiv or PubMed Central.

Green open access

Self-archiving is the publishing model termed "green" open access, in contrast to "gold" open access, in which the journal itself makes articles freely available. Authors may archive a preprint (before peer review), a postprint (the accepted manuscript after peer review), or, where licences permit, the publisher's version of record. The SHERPA/RoMEO database summarises publishers' policies on which versions may be archived and under what embargo.

Mandates

Many universities and research funders now require self-archiving through open-access mandates, following early policies such as the University of Liège model and the 2008 Harvard Faculty of Arts and Sciences policy, which secured institutional rights through a prior non-exclusive licence.

Impact

Studies of the open-access citation advantage find that self-archived articles tend to be downloaded more and, in many fields, cited more than paywalled articles, though the size and cause of the advantage are debated.
