Museo Nacional Centro de Arte Reina Sofía

The Museo Nacional Centro de Arte Reina Sofía ("Queen Sofía National Museum Art Centre"), often called the Reina Sofía, is Spain's national museum of 20th- and 21st-century art. Located in Madrid near Atocha station, it forms the "Golden Triangle of Art" with the Prado and Thyssen-Bornemisza museums. Its most famous work is Picasso's Guernica (1937).

History

The main building was an 18th-century hospital designed under Charles III by José de Hermosilla and Francisco Sabatini. It opened as an art center in 1986 and as a national museum in 1990, named for Queen Sofía. Glass elevator towers by Ian Ritchie were added, and a major extension by Jean Nouvel opened in 2005.

Collection

The collection centers on Spanish modern art: Picasso's Guernica, transferred from the Prado in 1992, Dalí's The Great Masturbator, and works by Miró, Juan Gris, Ángeles Santos, and contemporary artists, plus international figures. Exhibitions and a major library support research in modern and contemporary art.
