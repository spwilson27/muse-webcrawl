The Ajoie is a historic region and plateau in the northwestern corner of Switzerland, in the canton of Jura, forming roughly the district of Porrentruy. It lies at around 400 metres above sea level, drained by the river Allaine, and borders France to the north and west.

Geography and history

The chief town is Porrentruy, historically the residence of the Prince-Bishops of Basel, whose castle overlooks the old town. The Ajoie belonged to the Prince-Bishopric of Basel, was annexed by France in the 1790s as part of the department of Mont-Terrible, and was assigned to the canton of Bern at the Congress of Vienna in 1815. In 1979 it became part of the newly created canton of Jura.

Economy

The region is known for agriculture, orchards producing damson plums used for the eau-de-vie Damassine, watchmaking, and dinosaur fossil sites near Porrentruy excavated during construction of the A16 motorway.
