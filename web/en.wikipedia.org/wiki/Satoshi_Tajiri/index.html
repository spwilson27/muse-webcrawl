Satoshi Tajiri

Satoshi Tajiri (Japanese: 田尻 智, born 28 August 1965) is a Japanese video game designer best known as the creator of the Pokémon franchise and the founder of Game Freak. Born in Setagaya, Tokyo, Tajiri grew up in Machida, where as a child he was fascinated by collecting insects — a hobby that earned him the nickname "Dr. Bug" and later inspired the collecting and trading mechanics at the heart of Pokémon.

Early life and Game Freak

As a teenager, Tajiri became absorbed by arcade games such as Space Invaders. In 1982 he co-founded Game Freak, a hand-made fan magazine devoted to arcade gaming, with artist Ken Sugimori among its contributors. After studying electronics, Tajiri decided to move from writing about games to making them, and in 1989 Game Freak was re-established as a game development company. Its early titles included Quinty (released as Mendel Palace in North America) and Yoshi (1991).

Pokémon

Tajiri conceived Pokémon after noticing the Game Boy's Game Link Cable, imagining insects crawling between two connected consoles. He pitched the idea to Nintendo; Shigeru Miyamoto became his mentor on the project. Development of Pocket Monsters Red and Green took six difficult years, nearly bankrupting Game Freak, before the games were released in Japan in 1996. They became a phenomenon, launching one of the highest-grossing media franchises of all time. The protagonist of the Pokémon anime, Ash Ketchum, is named Satoshi in Japanese in Tajiri's honour.

Later work

Tajiri remains an executive director at Game Freak and has served as executive producer on subsequent Pokémon titles, while also working on smaller projects such as Drill Dozer (2005), which he directed.
