Carolinas campaign

The Carolinas campaign (January 1 – April 26, 1865) was the final campaign of the American Civil War in the Carolinas theater. Union Major General William Tecumseh Sherman marched his army north from Savannah, Georgia, through South Carolina and North Carolina, defeating Confederate forces under General Joseph E. Johnston, who surrendered near Durham, North Carolina, on April 26, 1865 — the largest surrender of Confederate troops in the war.

Background

After capturing Savannah in December 1864 at the end of his March to the Sea, Sherman turned north to unite with Ulysses S. Grant's armies besieging Petersburg and Richmond in Virginia, destroying Confederate infrastructure and supplies in his path. His forces of about 60,000 men faced a much smaller patchwork Confederate army.

The march

Sherman's troops cut a swath through South Carolina — the first state to secede, and singled out for especially destructive treatment. Columbia, the state capital, was captured on February 17, 1865, and much of the city burned that night, an event whose responsibility remains disputed. The campaign's principal battles were fought at Averasborough (March 16) and Bentonville (March 19–21), the largest battle ever fought in North Carolina, where Johnston failed to destroy part of Sherman's army before the Union wings united at Goldsboro.

Surrender

After the fall of Richmond and Lee's surrender at Appomattox on April 9, and the assassination of Abraham Lincoln on April 14, Sherman and Johnston negotiated at Bennett Place near Durham Station. Their first terms, signed April 18, were rejected in Washington as too generous; Johnston surrendered on revised, purely military terms on April 26, 1865, ending the war for some 89,000 Confederate soldiers in the Carolinas, Georgia and Florida.
