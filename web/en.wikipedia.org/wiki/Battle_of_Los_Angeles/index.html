The Battle of Los Angeles, also known as the "Great Los Angeles Air Raid", was a false alarm on the night of 24–25 February 1942 in which U.S. anti-aircraft batteries fired over 1,400 shells at supposed enemy aircraft over Los Angeles. No Japanese planes were present.

Background

Coming weeks after Pearl Harbor and a day after a Japanese submarine shelled the Ellwood oil field near Santa Barbara, the incident reflected intense invasion fears on the West Coast.

The incident

Radar reports and a balloon sighting triggered air-raid sirens and a citywide blackout. From about 3 a.m., artillery barraged the skies for an hour amid reports of planes that were never confirmed. Five civilians died, from heart attacks and traffic accidents during the blackout, and buildings were damaged by falling shells and shrapnel.

Aftermath

The Navy called it a false alarm caused by war nerves and possibly weather balloons; press speculation about a cover-up helped make the event a famous case in UFO lore.
