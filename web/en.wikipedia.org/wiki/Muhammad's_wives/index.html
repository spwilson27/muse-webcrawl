Muhammad's wives

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The wives of Muhammad, the Islamic prophet, are known in Islamic tradition as the Mothers of the Believers (Arabic: Ummahāt al-Muʾminīn), a title drawn from the Quran (33:6). Muhammad married eleven women over the course of his life, according to the majority of traditional sources; two — Khadija and Maria al-Qibtiyya — bore him children.

Maria al-Qibtiyya, an Egyptian woman sent by the ruler of Egypt, is often counted as a wife or concubine in the sources; she was the mother of Muhammad's son Ibrahim, who died in infancy.

Context

Traditional accounts describe Muhammad's first marriage, to Khadija — a wealthy merchant some years his senior — as monogamous for 25 years until her death in 619. His later marriages in Medina are presented in Islamic scholarship as having social, political and charitable purposes: caring for widows, cementing tribal alliances, and integrating former adversaries. The wives lived in apartments adjoining the Prophet's Mosque in Medina and are revered in Islam as models of piety and as transmitters of his teaching; Aisha and Umm Salama in particular are credited with narrating many hadith.
