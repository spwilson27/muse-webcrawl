FreeCell

FreeCell is a solitaire card game played with a single 52-card deck. Unlike most solitaire games, nearly all deals are solvable, and all cards are dealt face-up from the start, making it a game of strategy rather than luck.

Rules

Cards are dealt into eight tableau cascades. Four "free cells" serve as temporary storage for single cards, and four foundation piles are built up by suit from ace to king. Within the tableau, cards are built down in alternating colours. Empty cascades can hold any card. The number of cards that can be moved at once depends on the number of empty free cells and cascades.

History

FreeCell derives from earlier games such as Eight Off. Paul Alfille implemented it for the PLATO computer system in 1978, and it became famous through Jim Horne's version bundled with Microsoft Windows from 1991 onwards. The Windows version numbers its deals; of the original 32,000 deals, only deal #11982 is generally accepted as unsolvable. Computer solvers have confirmed that the overwhelming majority of random deals can be won.
