O'Leno State Park

O'Leno State Park is a 790-acre Florida State Park located on the banks of the Santa Fe River in Alachua and Columbia counties, about six miles north of High Springs, Florida. It is one of Florida's first state parks, developed by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s, and its name derives from the old town of Leno that once stood nearby.

Features

The park's most distinctive geological feature is the place where the Santa Fe River disappears underground through a sinkhole known as the River Sink, re-emerging about three miles away at River Rise Preserve State Park. A suspension bridge built by the CCC crosses the river, and the park preserves examples of rust-coloured rustic park architecture from the New Deal era, including a recreation hall and cabins.

Activities

Visitors can hike and bike miles of trails through hardwood hammock, sandhill, and sinkhole ponds, swim and paddle near the river rise, and camp at two campgrounds. The park also hosts a camp for park ranger training and interpretive events about CCC history.
