Belém Tower

Belém Tower (Portuguese: Torre de Belém), officially the Tower of Saint Vincent, is a 16th-century fortification located in the civil parish of Santa Maria de Belém in Lisbon, Portugal. It is a prominent example of the Portuguese Manueline style and served as a point of embarkation and disembarkation for Portuguese explorers and as a ceremonial gateway to Lisbon.

History

The tower was commissioned by King John II of Portugal as part of a defence system at the mouth of the Tagus river and was built between 1514 and 1519 under King Manuel I, designed by military architect Francisco de Arruda. It once stood on an island in the river, but the shifting shoreline after the 1755 Lisbon earthquake left it on the north bank.

Architecture

The four-storey limestone tower is decorated with Manueline motifs including armillary spheres, crosses of the Order of Christ, and carved ropes. A notable feature is the carved rhinoceros gargoyle, one of the earliest depictions of a rhinoceros in Western European art.

Heritage

Since 1983 the tower has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site, listed together with the nearby Jerónimos Monastery.
