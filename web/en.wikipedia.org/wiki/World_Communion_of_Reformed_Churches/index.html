The World Communion of Reformed Churches (WCRC) is an international fellowship of Reformed, Presbyterian, Congregational, United and Waldensian churches. Formed in 2010 by the merger of the World Alliance of Reformed Churches and the Reformed Ecumenical Council, it brings together more than 230 member churches in over 100 countries, representing an estimated 100 million Christians, making it one of the largest Protestant communions in the world.

History

The WCRC's oldest predecessor, the Alliance of the Reformed Churches holding the Presbyterian System, was founded in London in 1875 and later became the World Alliance of Reformed Churches (WARC). The Reformed Ecumenical Council (REC) was founded in 1946 in Grand Rapids, Michigan, as a more confessionally conservative body. After decades of growing cooperation, the two organisations agreed to merge, and the Uniting General Council held at Calvin College, Grand Rapids, in June 2010 formally created the World Communion of Reformed Churches. The communion moved its world headquarters from Geneva to Hanover, Germany, in 2014 for cost reasons.

Beliefs and work

Member churches stand in the tradition of the sixteenth-century Reformation associated with figures such as John Calvin and Huldrych Zwingli, holding to historic Reformed confessions including the Westminster Confession and the Heidelberg Catechism. The WCRC is known for linking confessional faith with social justice: its 2004 Accra Confession, adopted by the WARC at Accra, Ghana, criticised the global economic order as a matter of faith. The communion works in areas of theology, mission, gender justice, care for creation and interreligious dialogue, and in 2017 it formally associated itself with the Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification at a ceremony in Wittenberg during the 500th anniversary year of the Reformation.

Organisation

The General Council, meeting roughly every seven years, is the highest governing body; between councils an executive committee governs. The presidency has been held by the South African theologian Jerry Pillay (2010–2017) and, since the 2017 General Council in Leipzig, by the Lebanese pastor Najla Kassab, the first woman and first Middle Eastern church leader to hold the office. Regional councils organise work in Africa, Asia, Europe, Latin America, the Caribbean and North America, and the Pacific.
