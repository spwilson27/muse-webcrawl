John Jacob Astor (born Johann Jakob Astor; July 17, 1763 – March 29, 1848) was a German-American businessman, merchant, real-estate mogul, and investor who made his fortune in the fur trade and in New York City real estate. He was the first multi-millionaire in the United States and the founder of the Astor fortune.

From Walldorf to New York

Astor was born in Walldorf near Heidelberg, the son of a butcher. After working in London selling musical instruments, he emigrated to the United States in 1784 and built a fur business in New York, travelling into the interior to trade with Native American nations and eventually dominating the Great Lakes and Canadian trade.

American Fur Company

In 1808 he chartered the American Fur Company. His Pacific Fur Company founded Fort Astoria at the mouth of the Columbia River in 1811 — the first American settlement on the Pacific coast — though the venture failed in the War of 1812, when Astoria was sold to the North West Company. Astor also profited handsomely from the opium trade to China, shipping Turkish opium aboard his vessels.

Real estate and legacy

From the 1830s Astor withdrew from furs and invested his profits in Manhattan real estate, buying farms and lots ahead of the city's growth. At his death in 1848 his estate was estimated at $20–30 million, an enormous share of American GDP at the time. He left $400,000 to found the Astor Library in New York, which later became part of the New York Public Library. The Waldorf-Astoria hotels and many Astor place names descend from his family.
