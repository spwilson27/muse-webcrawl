Fasil Ghebbi

Location: Gondar, Amhara Region, Ethiopia
Founded: 17th century
Built by: Fasilides and successors
UNESCO World Heritage Site: inscribed 1979

Fasil Ghebbi (the "Royal Enclosure") is a fortress-city in Gondar, Ethiopia, founded in the 17th century by Emperor Fasilides and enlarged by his successors. It served as the residence of Ethiopian emperors and the centre of government for more than two centuries. In 1979 the site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Description

The walled compound covers about 70,000 square metres and contains a distinctive group of stone castles and palaces unlike any other architecture in Africa of the period. The oldest and most imposing structure is Fasilides' Castle, a fortified palace with square corner towers capped by domes, built of basalt rubble with lime mortar in a style blending Ethiopian, Portuguese, Moorish, and Indian influences — a legacy of the Jesuit presence at court. Other major buildings include the palace of Iyasu I, the library and chancellery of Yohannes I, the castle of Empress Mentewab, banqueting halls, stables, and the sunken Fasilides' Bath, a pavilion in a large rectangular pool that is still filled annually for the Timkat (Epiphany) celebrations.

History

Before Fasilides made Gondar his fixed capital around 1636, Ethiopian emperors had ruled from mobile tented courts. Gondar remained the imperial capital until the mid-19th century, when Emperor Tewodros II moved the court, and the palaces declined. The complex suffered damage from earthquakes, from the Mahdist invasion in the 1880s, and from British bombing during the Second World War, but major structures survived and have been restored. The site should not be confused with the emperor who founded it: Fasil Ghebbi is the place; Fasilides is the man.
