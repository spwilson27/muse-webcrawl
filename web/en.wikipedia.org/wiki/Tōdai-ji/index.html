Tōdai-ji

Tōdai-ji (東大寺, "Great Eastern Temple") is a Buddhist temple complex in Nara, Japan, once one of the Seven Great Temples. Its Great Buddha Hall (Daibutsuden) houses the world's largest bronze statue of the Buddha Vairocana, known in Japanese as Daibutsu.

LocationNara, Japan
DenominationKegon
Founded8th century (752 dedication)
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site

History

Emperor Shōmu commissioned the temple as head of a network of provincial temples (kokubunji) and ordered the colossal Buddha, cast with enormous effort and dedicated in 752 with the famous eye-opening ceremony attended by envoys from across Asia. The statue stands about 15 metres tall. Fires and earthquakes repeatedly damaged the complex; the present Daibutsuden, rebuilt in 1709, is about two-thirds the width of the original yet remains among the world's largest wooden buildings.

Treasures

The Shōsō-in repository preserves 8th-century imperial treasures, including objects from the Silk Road. The Nigatsu-dō hall hosts the Omizutori ceremony held continuously since 752. Sacred sika deer roam Nara Park around the temple.
