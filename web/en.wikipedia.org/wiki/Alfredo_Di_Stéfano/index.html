Alfredo Di Stéfano

Alfredo Stéfano Di Stéfano Laulhé
Born: 4 July 1926, Buenos Aires, Argentina
Died: 7 July 2014 (aged 88), Madrid, Spain
Position: Forward
Clubs: River Plate, Millonarios, Real Madrid, Espanyol
National teams: Argentina, Colombia (unofficial), Spain

Alfredo Stéfano Di Stéfano Laulhé (4 July 1926 – 7 July 2014) was an Argentine-born professional footballer and coach who played as a forward, widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time. Nicknamed "Saeta rubia" ("Blond Arrow"), he was a powerful, quick, skilful and prolific goalscorer with great stamina, tactical versatility and vision, able to play almost anywhere on the pitch.

Career

Di Stéfano began at River Plate, winning Argentine titles, then starred for Millonarios of Bogotá during Colombia's El Dorado era. His contested 1953 transfer — fought over by Barcelona and Real Madrid — ended with him joining Madrid, a decision said to have shifted the balance of Spanish football.

Real Madrid dominance

With Ferenc Puskás, Raymond Kopa, Paco Gento and José Santamaría, Di Stéfano led Real Madrid to eight La Liga titles and five consecutive European Cups (1956–1960), scoring in each of the five finals, including a hat-trick in the famous 7–3 win over Eintracht Frankfurt in 1960. He won the Ballon d'Or in 1957 and 1959 and was twice top scorer in Spain.

International

He played for Argentina (winning the 1947 Copa América) and Spain, but remarkably never appeared at a World Cup — Argentina withdrew, Colombia was unaffiliated, Spain failed to qualify in 1958 and he was injured in 1962.

Later life

After retiring at Espanyol, he coached clubs including Valencia (winning the 1980 Cup Winners' Cup) and Real Madrid, and became Madrid's honorary president in 2000. He died in Madrid in 2014.
