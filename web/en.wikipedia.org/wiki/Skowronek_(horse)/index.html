Skowronek (1908–1930) was a grey Arabian stallion bred in Poland and imported to England, where he became one of the most influential sires in the history of the Arabian breed. His name means "skylark" in Polish. Bred by Count Józef Potocki at the Antoniny stud, he was sired by the desert-bred Ibrahim out of the Polish mare Jaskoulka.

Racing and sale to England

Skowronek raced in Poland as a young horse. In 1913 he was sold and brought to England, eventually being purchased by H. V. Musker and then by Lady Wentworth of the Crabbet Arabian Stud, where he stood for the rest of his life.

Influence

At Crabbet, Skowronek sired numerous champions, including the stallions Raktha and Naseem; Naseem became a foundation sire in Russian breeding at Tersk. In the United States his grandson *Raffles was a major sire. Skowronek is celebrated for his classic Arabian type — a dished head, large eyes and high tail carriage — and his bloodline appears in a large proportion of modern Arabians worldwide. He died in 1930.
