Surge protection

Surge protection is the practice of protecting electrical and electronic systems from transient overvoltages — brief, high-energy voltage spikes ("surges") caused by lightning strikes, utility switching, or the operation of large inductive loads. A surge protective device (SPD), also called a transient voltage surge suppressor, limits the voltage supplied to equipment by blocking or shorting unwanted voltages above a safe threshold.

Devices and technologies

Common protective components include metal-oxide varistors (MOVs), which clamp voltage by becoming conductive above a threshold; gas discharge tubes, which handle high energies but respond more slowly; avalanche (TVS) diodes for fast, low-energy clamping; and spark gaps. Devices are specified by clamping voltage, surge current rating (often in kiloamperes), and energy absorption in joules.

Standards and installation

Standards such as UL 1449 in North America and the IEC 61643 series classify SPDs by location: Type 1 devices at the service entrance, Type 2 in distribution panels, and Type 3 point-of-use devices such as plug strips. Effective protection is coordinated in layers and depends critically on short, direct earthing connections. Plug-in surge protectors protect connected equipment but not a building's wiring, and their MOVs degrade with each surge event, so many include an indicator showing when protection has expired.
