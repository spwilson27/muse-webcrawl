Pipilotti Rist

Pipilotti Rist (born Elisabeth Charlotte Rist, 21 June 1962) is a Swiss visual artist best known for immersive video installations and single-channel works that use saturated color, music, and the female body. Her first name is a childhood nickname combining Pippi Longstocking and Lotti. She is regarded as a pioneer of video art whose playful, sensuous projections transformed gallery spaces from the 1980s onward.

Career

Rist studied commercial art and photography in Vienna and video at the School of Design in Basel. Her breakthrough single-channel video I'm Not the Girl Who Misses Much (1986) shows her dancing in a distorted, skipping image to a warped version of John Lennon's "Happiness Is a Warm Gun." In the 1980s she was also a member of the Swiss band Les Reines Prochaines.

Major works

Ever Is Over All (1997), a two-channel projection of a woman gleefully smashing car windows with a flower-shaped iron — while a policewoman smiles and passes by — won the Premio 2000 at the Venice Biennale and entered the collection of the Museum of Modern Art, New York. Large immersive installations followed, including Himalaya Goldsteins Stube, Pour Your Body Out at MoMA (2008), and sweeping retrospectives such as Pixel Forest at the New Museum (2016) and exhibitions at the Louisiana Museum and the Museum of Contemporary Art Australia. Her work recurrently explores nature, the body, technology, and a feminist reclaiming of pleasure.

Style

Rist's installations often require visitors to lie on cushions or beds, dissolving the boundary between viewer and image. Saturated hues, underwater footage, macro shots of skin and fruit, and her own music-video pacing create what she has described as visual "vitamins" for the senses.
