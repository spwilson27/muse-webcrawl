Janis Lyn Joplin (January 19, 1943 – October 4, 1970) was an American singer-songwriter, regarded as one of the most influential rock vocalists of her era. Known for her powerful, raspy mezzosoprano voice and electrifying stage presence, she first rose to fame as the lead singer of the San Francisco band Big Brother and the Holding Company.

Career

Joplin's breakthrough came at the Monterey Pop Festival in 1967, where her rendition of "Ball and Chain" stunned the audience. With Big Brother she recorded the album Cheap Thrills (1968), featuring "Piece of My Heart". As a solo artist she released I Got Dem Ol' Kozmic Blues Again Mama! (1969) and performed at Woodstock. Her final album, Pearl, was released posthumously in 1971 and included the number-one hit "Me and Bobby McGee" and "Mercedes Benz".

Death and legacy

Joplin died of a heroin overdose in Hollywood on 4 October 1970, aged 27, becoming part of the so-called "27 Club". She was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1995 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2005.
