Samarium–cobalt magnet

Type: Rare-earth permanent magnet

Composition: Alloy of samarium and cobalt (SmCo5, Sm2Co17)

Developed: Early 1970s

Maximum operating temperature: 250–350 °C

A samarium–cobalt (SmCo) magnet is a type of rare-earth magnet, a strong permanent magnet made of an alloy of the rare-earth element samarium with the transition metal cobalt. Developed in the early 1970s, SmCo magnets were the first commercial rare-earth magnets and remain in use where high temperature stability and corrosion resistance are required.

Types

Two compositions are common. SmCo5 (series 1:5) has energy products of roughly 18–23 MGOe, while Sm2Co17 (series 2:17), often alloyed with iron, copper and zirconium, achieves higher energy products of about 24–32 MGOe and superior temperature performance.

Properties

Samarium–cobalt magnets have high remanence and coercivity, an excellent resistance to demagnetisation, and a low temperature coefficient of remanence, allowing them to retain strength at temperatures up to about 300 °C. They are naturally corrosion-resistant and usually require no protective coating, though they are brittle and expensive because of the cost of samarium and cobalt.

Applications

Applications include high-performance motors and generators, aerospace and defence equipment, travelling-wave tubes, sensors, medical devices and precision instruments operating in harsh or high-temperature environments. Neodymium magnets, which are cheaper and stronger at room temperature, have displaced SmCo in many uses, but SmCo remains preferred where temperature stability is critical.
