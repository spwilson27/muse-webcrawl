Goliath heron

The Goliath heron (Ardea goliath), also known as the giant heron, is a very large wading bird of the heron family, Ardeidae. It is found in sub-Saharan Africa, with a small number in Southwest and South Asia. It is the largest living heron, standing 120–152 cm (47–60 in) tall with a wingspan of 185–230 cm (73–91 in).

Description

The Goliath heron has slate-grey upperparts and flight feathers, a chestnut head, neck and underparts, a white chin and throat streaked with black, and a massive dagger-like bill. In flight it is slow and laboured, with legs held below the horizontal.

Behaviour

A solitary and territorial hunter of shallow water, the Goliath heron stands motionless for long periods before spearing large fish — often of 15–50 cm — with its bill; it also takes frogs, reptiles, small mammals and carrion. It is largely sedentary and crepuscular, often feeding at dusk. Breeding is usually solitary or in small colonies, with a platform nest of sticks built in trees, reeds, or on the ground near water. The species is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN, though it is sensitive to disturbance and wetland drainage.
