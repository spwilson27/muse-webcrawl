Homotopy equivalence

In algebraic topology, a homotopy equivalence is a pair of continuous maps f : X → Y and g : Y → X between topological spaces such that g∘f is homotopic to the identity on X and f∘g is homotopic to the identity on Y. Spaces joined by such maps are said to have the same homotopy type.

Examples

A solid disc is homotopy equivalent to a point; a cylinder and a circle share a homotopy type; a Möbius strip is homotopy equivalent to a circle. A deformation retraction gives a strong form of homotopy equivalence onto a subspace.

Invariants

Homotopy equivalent spaces share fundamental groups, homology and cohomology groups and higher homotopy groups, though the converse fails — spaces can share all these invariants without being homotopy equivalent.

References: Hatcher, Algebraic Topology.
