Seven Species

The Seven Species (Hebrew: שבעת המינים, Shiv'at haMinim) are seven agricultural products named in the Hebrew Bible as special products of the Land of Israel: wheat, barley, grapes, figs, pomegranates, olives, and dates. They hold a special status in Jewish law and tradition.

Biblical source

Deuteronomy 8:8 describes the Promised Land as "a land of wheat and barley, of vines and fig trees and pomegranates, a land of olive trees and honey." The "honey" of the verse is traditionally understood as date honey (silan), making the date the seventh species. In the Temple period, the first-fruits offering (bikkurim) brought to Jerusalem could be made only from these seven species.

In Jewish tradition

The seven species are eaten with special celebration on the festival of Tu BiShvat, the "New Year of the Trees," when a blessing is recited over them. Jewish law gives them precedence in the order of blessings over other foods, ranked by their position in the biblical verse. Products of the seven species grown in the Land of Israel are also subject to the agricultural commandments, such as tithes (terumah and ma'aser) and the sabbatical year (shmita).

Symbolism

The species symbolise the fertility and blessing of the land and appear frequently in Jewish and Israeli art, coinage, and decoration. Wheat and barley represent staple grains; the vine, fig, pomegranate, and olive, perennial fruits; and the date, sweetness and endurance.
