Cultural ecology

Cultural ecology is the study of human adaptations to social and physical environments — how culture interacts with the natural world. It was developed by the American anthropologist Julian Steward in the mid-twentieth century, notably in his Theory of Culture Change (1955).

Steward's method

Steward proposed analysing the "culture core": the features of a society most closely tied to subsistence and economic arrangements, produced by the interplay of environment and technology. Comparing similar environments across cultures, he developed the idea of multilinear evolution, against older unilinear schemes. His comparative studies of irrigation societies and the Basin-Plateau peoples of the American West are classic examples.

Later developments

Cultural ecology influenced political ecology, human behavioural ecology and environmental anthropology, though later scholars criticised its environmental determinism and inattention to power and history. The field continues in research on traditional ecological knowledge and human responses to climate change.
