Japanese poetry

Japanese poetry is poetry typical of Japan, whether written in Japanese, by Japanese poets, or in earlier periods in classical Chinese. Its best-known forms are the short haiku and the older waka (including tanka), counted in sound units called on.

History

The earliest anthology, the Man'yōshū (c. 759), contains about 4,500 waka. The imperial Kokin Wakashū (905) codified court poetry. Linked verse (renga) flourished in the medieval period.

Haiku

From renga's opening verse (hokku) Matsuo Bashō (1644–1694) created a profound art form; Masaoka Shiki in the Meiji era coined the term haiku and promoted realism. A haiku is traditionally 5-7-5 on, with a seasonal reference (kigo) and a cutting word (kireji).

Modern poetry

Meiji poets introduced free verse (shintaishi) under Western influence. Modern and contemporary poets write tanka, haiku and free verse; haiku has become a worldwide form.
