Author: Pramoedya Ananta Toer
Original title: Bumi Manusia
Published: 1980
Series: Buru Quartet (book 1)

This Earth of Mankind (Indonesian: Bumi Manusia) is a 1980 historical novel by Indonesian author Pramoedya Ananta Toer, the first volume of the Buru Quartet. Set in the Dutch East Indies at the end of the 19th century, it is widely regarded as a masterpiece of Indonesian literature.

Background

Pramoedya composed the story while a political prisoner on Buru Island (1969–1979), initially telling it aloud to fellow inmates before being allowed to write it down. Published in Indonesia in 1980, it was a bestseller before being banned by the Suharto government in 1981. The English translation by Max Lane appeared in 1982.

Plot

The novel follows Minke, a Javanese priyayi youth — the only native student at an elite Dutch-language secondary school in Surabaya — who falls in love with Annelies Mellema, the daughter of the powerful Nyai Ontosoroh, a native concubine who has become the capable manager of a large Dutch-owned business. Through Minke's education and the tragic legal struggle over Annelies, the novel exposes the racial hierarchies and injustices of colonial society and traces the birth of Indonesian national consciousness.

Reception and adaptation

The Buru Quartet has been translated into dozens of languages. A film adaptation, Bumi Manusia, directed by Hanung Bramantyo, was released in 2019.
