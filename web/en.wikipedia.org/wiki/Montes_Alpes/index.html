Montes Alpes

LocationMoon, between Mare Imbrium and Mare Frigoris
Lengthabout 280 km
Highest peakMons Blanc, about 3.6 km
Named afterThe Alps

Montes Alpes is a mountain range in the northern part of the Moon's near side. It was named after the Alps in Europe by the German astronomer Johannes Hevelius. The range extends roughly 280 kilometres from northwest of the crater Cassini toward the east-northeast.

Description

The range forms part of the outer rim of the Imbrium impact basin, separating Mare Imbrium to the southwest from Mare Frigoris to the north. About a third of the way along the range it is cut by the Vallis Alpes, a broad graben valley some 130 km long, with a narrow central rille running along its floor.

Peaks

The tallest peak in the range is Mons Blanc, at about 3.6 km above the surrounding terrain. Other named peaks include Mons Piton and Mons Pico, which lie as isolated mountains in the mare to the south.
