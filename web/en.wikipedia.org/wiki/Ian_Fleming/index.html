Ian Lancaster Fleming (28 May 1908 – 12 August 1964) was a British author best known for creating the fictional British Secret Service agent James Bond and the series of twelve novels and two short-story collections about him. The Bond books rank among the best-selling book series in the world, and spawned one of the most successful film franchises in history.

Fleming at work; his wartime intelligence career supplied much of Bond's background detail.

Life

Fleming, grandson of a Scottish banker and son of Valentine Fleming MP (killed in the First World War), was educated at Eton and Sandhurst, and studied languages in Munich and Geneva before working for Reuters and as a stockbroker. In the Second World War he served as personal assistant to the Director of Naval Intelligence, Rear Admiral John Godfrey, rising to commander. He was involved in deception planning, including conceiving the trope-laden Operation Goldeneye and helping lay groundwork that influenced Operation Mincemeat, and he proposed the unit that became 30 Assault Unit.

The Bond books

At his Jamaican estate, Goldeneye, Fleming wrote Casino Royale (1953), the first Bond novel, drawing the name from ornithologist James Bond, author of a Caribbean bird guide. Eleven more novels followed, including From Russia, with Love (1957), Goldfinger (1959), and Thunderball (1961). The film series began with Dr. No in 1962. Fleming also wrote the children's story Chitty-Chitty-Bang-Bang (1964). A heavy smoker and drinker, he died of a heart attack in 1964 at age 56. The Bond series was continued after his death by several authorised authors, beginning with Kingsley Amis.
