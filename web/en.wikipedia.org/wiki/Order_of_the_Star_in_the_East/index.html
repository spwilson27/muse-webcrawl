The Order of the Star in the East was an organisation founded in 1911 by leaders of the Theosophical Society to prepare for the expected imminent arrival of the "World Teacher", a messianic figure identified with the young Jiddu Krishnamurti. The Order grew into an international movement with tens of thousands of members before Krishnamurti himself dissolved it in 1929.

History

In 1909, the Theosophist Charles Webster Leadbeater encountered the fourteen-year-old Krishnamurti on the beach at Adyar, Madras, and declared that the boy had an extraordinary aura and would become a great spiritual teacher. The Society's president, Annie Besant, became his legal guardian and promoted him as the vehicle of the Lord Maitreya. The Order of the Rising Sun, founded in India in 1911, was soon reconstituted internationally as the Order of the Star in the East, with Krishnamurti as its head.

Dissolution

Over the 1920s Krishnamurti grew increasingly uncomfortable with the role prepared for him. On 3 August 1929, at the opening of the annual camp at Ommen in the Netherlands, before some 3,000 members, he formally dissolved the Order, declaring that "Truth is a pathless land" and that no organisation or creed could lead human beings to truth. He returned donated property and went on to teach independently for the rest of his life.
