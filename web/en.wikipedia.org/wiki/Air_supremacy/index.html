Air supremacy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fighter aircraft of the type used to contest control of the air (image placeholder)

Air supremacy (or air superiority) is the degree of dominance of one side's air power over the other's in a conflict. NATO and U.S. doctrine distinguish degrees: air supremacy means the opposing air force is incapable of effective interference; air superiority means a degree of dominance permitting operations without prohibitive interference; and a favourable air situation is a lesser condition still. Control of the air is usually a prerequisite for successful land and naval operations in modern warfare.

History

Contest for the air began in the First World War with "Fokker scourge" periods of German advantage and Allied recovery. In the Second World War, the Battle of Britain (1940) demonstrated that air superiority over the battlefield had to be won before an invasion could be attempted, and by 1944 the Allies enjoyed near-supremacy over Western Europe, permitting the Normandy landings and unrestricted daylight bombing. In the Pacific, the destruction of Japanese naval air power at Midway and the Philippine Sea shifted supremacy decisively.

Postwar conflicts

Israel's pre-emptive destruction of Arab air forces on the ground in the Six-Day War (1967) is a classic case of gaining instant supremacy. Conversely, dense surface-to-air missile defences complicated air operations in Vietnam and in the 1973 Yom Kippur War. The 1991 Gulf War saw a coalition achieve supremacy over Iraq through stealth aircraft, suppression of air defences (SEAD), and precision weapons; similar conditions prevailed over Kosovo in 1999 and Iraq in 2003. In the Russo-Ukrainian War from 2022, neither side achieved supremacy, with dense ground-based air defences denying both air forces freedom of action — a state analysts call air denial or mutual air parity.

Means of achieving control

Air supremacy is pursued through counter-air operations: attacks on airfields and air defence networks, air-to-air combat by fighter aircraft, electronic warfare, and suppression or destruction of enemy air defences. Stealth, airborne early warning, and aerial refuelling extend the reach of these operations.
