Mount Etna

[Image: Mount Etna erupting at night above Catania]
Mount Etna erupting at night above Catania

Mount Etna is an active stratovolcano on the east coast of Sicily, Italy, between Catania and Messina. It is one of the most active volcanoes in the world, in an almost constant state of activity, and at about 3,357 metres (changing with eruptions) it is the tallest peak in Italy south of the Alps and the largest volcano in Europe by volume.

Mount Etna has one of the longest documented records of eruptions, dating to 1500 BCE. In Greek mythology it was the forge of Hephaestus and the prison of the monster Typhon. Its fertile volcanic soils support vineyards (Etna DOC wines), apple orchards and citrus, while its slopes hold ski resorts with sea views. UNESCO listed Etna in 2013 for its exceptional level of volcanic activity and scientific documentation.
