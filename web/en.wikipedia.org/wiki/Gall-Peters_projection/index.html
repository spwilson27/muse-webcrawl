Gall–Peters projection

TypeCylindrical equal-area map projection
Described byJames Gall (1855); promoted by Arno Peters (1973)
Standard parallels45° N and S

The Gall–Peters projection is a cylindrical equal-area map projection that represents regions of the Earth in their correct relative sizes. It was described by the Scottish clergyman and geographer James Gall in 1855 and championed in the 1970s by the German historian Arno Peters, after whom it is also called the Peters projection.

Properties

As an equal-area projection, it avoids the gross size inflation of high-latitude regions produced by the Mercator projection: on Gall–Peters maps Africa and South America appear at their true size relative to Europe and North America. The cost is severe distortion of shapes, especially near the equator, where landmasses appear vertically stretched.

Controversy

Peters promoted the map as a corrective to Eurocentric cartography, generating heated debate in the 1970s and 1980s; cartographers pointed out that equal-area projections had existed for over a century and that no flat map can preserve both area and shape. Nevertheless the projection was adopted by some UN agencies and schools, including Boston Public Schools in 2017.
