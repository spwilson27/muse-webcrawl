Forum shopping

Forum shopping is a colloquial term for the practice of litigants seeking to have their legal case heard in the court they believe most likely to deliver a favourable judgement. Some jurisdictions attract particular kinds of cases because of their reputation for sympathetic judges, generous juries, or convenient procedural rules.

Overview

Where more than one court has jurisdiction over a dispute, a claimant may choose among them on grounds such as the applicable law, likely damages, speed, or jury composition. The term carries a pejorative connotation: courts and commentators often view deliberate forum shopping as an abuse that undermines the orderly administration of justice.

Examples

In the United States, patent cases were long concentrated in the Eastern District of Texas, perceived as plaintiff-friendly, until the Supreme Court's decision in TC Heartland v. Kraft Foods (2017) restricted venue. Plaintiffs in mass torts may seek state courts thought less strict than federal courts. In international litigation, London has been nicknamed the "libel capital of the world" for claimant-friendly defamation law, a reputation curtailed by the Defamation Act 2013.

Legal responses

Doctrines such as forum non conveniens allow a court to decline jurisdiction where another forum is clearly more appropriate. Venue and jurisdictional rules, anti-suit injunctions, and international conventions on recognition of judgments all operate to limit abusive forum shopping, while respecting a claimant's legitimate right to choose among competent courts.
