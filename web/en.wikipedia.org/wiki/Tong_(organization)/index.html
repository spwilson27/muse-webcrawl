Tong (organization)

A tong (Chinese: 堂; pinyin: táng, literally "hall") is a type of organisation found among Chinese immigrant communities in the United States, Canada and other countries. Tongs began as benevolent and fraternal associations but some became notorious for involvement in organised crime.

Origins

Tongs emerged in North American Chinatowns in the mid-nineteenth century, modelled partly on Chinese secret societies and partly on Western fraternal orders. They provided social services, dispute resolution, lodging and protection for immigrants who were excluded from mainstream institutions.

Tong wars

Rivalries over territory, gambling, opium and vice rackets produced the violent "tong wars" that erupted periodically in cities such as San Francisco, New York and Boston from the late nineteenth into the early twentieth century. Hired gunmen known as "hatchet men" carried out assassinations for competing tongs.

Modern role

Most tongs today function as legitimate community and business associations, though law enforcement agencies have linked some to gangs such as the Ghost Shadows and Flying Dragons.
