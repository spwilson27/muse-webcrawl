Beef cattle are cattle raised for beef production, as distinct from dairy cattle raised for milk. Beef breeds have been selectively bred for rapid growth, efficient conversion of feed into muscle, carcass yield, and meat quality traits such as marbling. Beef production is a major agricultural industry worldwide and a significant source of protein in many diets.

Breeds

British breeds such as the Aberdeen Angus and Hereford are prized for maternal qualities, early maturity, and marbling. Continental European breeds — including Charolais, Limousin, and Simmental — are larger-framed and leaner, and are frequently used as terminal sires in crossbreeding programs. In hot climates, zebu (Bos indicus) breeds such as the Brahman contribute heat tolerance and parasite resistance, and composites such as Brangus and Beefmaster combine zebu and European ancestry. Japanese Wagyu breeds are renowned for intensely marbled beef.

Production systems

Most beef production begins in cow–calf operations, in which a breeding herd grazes pasture or rangeland and calves are weaned at roughly six to ten months. Weaned cattle may be "backgrounded" on forage before finishing. Grain-finished cattle spend their final months in feedlots on energy-dense rations to promote rapid gain and marbling, the dominant system in the United States. Grass-finished cattle remain on forage until slaughter, producing leaner beef; such systems predominate in countries such as Argentina, Uruguay, and New Zealand. Cattle are typically slaughtered at 12–30 months of age.

Environmental and health issues

Beef production is associated with substantial greenhouse-gas emissions — chiefly enteric methane — and with significant land and water use; it is among the most resource-intensive common foods. The industry is also scrutinized for antibiotic use, animal welfare, and deforestation linked to pasture expansion in regions such as the Amazon. Efforts to reduce impacts include improved breeding, feed additives that reduce methane, rotational grazing, and alternative protein products.
