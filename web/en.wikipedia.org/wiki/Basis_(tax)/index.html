Basis (tax)

In United States tax law, basis is generally the amount of a taxpayer's economic investment in property. When property is sold, the taxpayer's taxable gain (or loss) is the difference between the amount realised and the taxpayer's basis in the property. The initial basis is usually the property's cost, but basis can be acquired in other ways and is adjusted over time for events such as depreciation and capital improvements.

Initial basis

Property purchased by the taxpayer takes a cost basis equal to its purchase price plus certain acquisition costs. Property received by gift generally takes a carryover basis — the donor's basis — for computing gain, with special rules for loss. Property inherited from a decedent generally receives a stepped-up basis equal to its fair market value at death, a rule of major significance in estate planning. Property received in a tax-free exchange takes a substituted basis derived from the property given up.

Adjusted basis

Basis is not static. It is increased by capital improvements and certain assessments, and decreased by depreciation deductions, casualty losses, and returns of capital. The resulting figure is the adjusted basis used when the property is sold or depreciated. Partners and S corporation shareholders likewise maintain basis in their ownership interests, which limits the losses they may deduct.
