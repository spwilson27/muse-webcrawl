Free module

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a free module is a module that has a basis — a linearly independent spanning set — generalising the notion of a vector space to modules over an arbitrary ring. Free modules are the free objects in the category of modules: a module F over a ring R is free if it is isomorphic to a direct sum of copies of R, or equivalently if it satisfies the universal property that any map from its basis to another module extends uniquely to a module homomorphism.

Definition

A subset B of an R-module M is a basis if it is linearly independent (no nontrivial finite R-linear combination of distinct basis elements equals zero) and spans M (every element is a finite linear combination of basis elements). The module is then free with basis B. Unlike vector spaces, not every module has a basis — modules with torsion, such as ℤ/2ℤ over ℤ, are not free — and over noncommutative rings questions of basis size require the invariant basis number property.

Examples

Every vector space is a free module over its field of scalars. The ring R itself is free of rank one over itself, and Rn is free of rank n. Over the integers, free modules are exactly the free abelian groups. Polynomial rings are free modules over their coefficient ring with basis given by the monomials.

Properties

Free modules are projective, and every module is a quotient of a free module — a fact used to build free resolutions, central to homological algebra. Submodules of free modules need not be free over a general ring (over a principal ideal domain they are), and a submodule of a free module can require more generators than the module itself. Direct sums of free modules are free; direct products need not be. The rank of a free module is well defined for commutative rings and more generally for rings with invariant basis number.
