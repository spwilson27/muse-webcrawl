Sedna (mythology)

Sedna (Inuktitut: ᓴᓐᓇ, Sanna) is the goddess of the sea and marine animals in Inuit mythology, also known as the Mother of the Sea or Mistress of the Sea. She is a central figure in the creation myth of the Inuit peoples, and the animals she governs — seals, walruses, fish, and whales — are the foundation of Inuit subsistence hunting.

AbodeAdlivun, the underworld beneath the sea
DomainThe sea and sea animals
Other namesSanna, Nerrivik, Arnakuagsak, Sassuma Arnaa

The legend

Versions of the Sedna story vary across the Arctic, but the core narrative concerns a young woman who refuses her suitors, or is tricked into marrying a fulmar (or raven, or dog), and is taken across the sea. When her father attempts to rescue her by kayak, the fulmar raises a storm. In terror, the father throws Sedna overboard to save himself. As she clings to the side of the kayak, he cuts off her fingers, joint by joint. Her severed fingers fall into the sea and become the sea animals — the first joints becoming seals, the next walruses, and the last whales. Sedna sinks to the bottom of the ocean, where she rules over Adlivun.

Role in hunting ritual

Because Sedna controls the sea animals, hunters must keep her pleased. When hunting is poor, it is said that Sedna's hair has become tangled and dirty from the transgressions of humans, and because she has no fingers she cannot comb it herself. A shaman (angakkuq) must then journey in spirit to the bottom of the sea to comb her hair and appease her, after which she releases the animals to the hunters.

Namesakes

The trans-Neptunian dwarf planet candidate 90377 Sedna is named after the goddess, in recognition of its discovery in a cold, distant region of the Solar System.
