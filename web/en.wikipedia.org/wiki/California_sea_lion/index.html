The California sea lion (Zalophus californianus) is a coastal eared seal native to western North America. It is one of six species of sea lion. Its natural habitat ranges from the coast of southeastern Alaska to central Mexico, including the Gulf of California. Males are considerably larger than females and have a thick neck and a crested forehead; adults range from chocolate brown to tan.

Behaviour and ecology

California sea lions are highly social and intelligent, gathering in large colonies on beaches, rocks, piers and buoys. They feed on squid, anchovies, sardines, rockfish and other schooling fish, and are preyed upon by killer whales and great white sharks. During the breeding season (June to August) males establish territories and maintain harems; pups are born in early summer, mainly on the Channel Islands of California and islands in Mexico. Their trainability has made them familiar performers in zoos and aquariums, and they have been trained by the US Navy for underwater retrieval and harbour protection.

Population

Protected under the US Marine Mammal Protection Act of 1972, the population recovered to several hundred thousand animals and is listed as Least Concern, although strandings during warm-water (El Niño) events and domoic-acid poisoning from algal blooms periodically cause mass mortalities.
