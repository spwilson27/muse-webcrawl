Pain threshold

The pain threshold is the point along a curve of increasing perception of a stimulus at which pain begins to be felt. It is an entirely subjective phenomenon: the intensity at which a stimulus — heat, cold, pressure, or electrical current — is first reported as painful varies between individuals and with context.

Measurement

Thresholds are commonly measured with thermal probes, pressure algometers, or electrical stimulation, and expressed in the units of the stimulus applied. Pain threshold should not be confused with pain tolerance, which is the maximum level of pain a person is willing or able to endure; threshold is generally more stable than tolerance, which is strongly influenced by psychological and cultural factors.

Factors affecting threshold

Genetics, sex, age, fatigue, prior experience, and certain drugs can raise or lower the threshold. Conditions such as congenital insensitivity to pain represent an extreme failure of pain perception, while hyperalgesia involves an abnormally lowered threshold.
