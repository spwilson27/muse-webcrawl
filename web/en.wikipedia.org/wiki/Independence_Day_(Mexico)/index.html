Independence Day (Mexico)

Independence Day in Mexico (Día de la Independencia) is celebrated on 16 September, commemorating the start of the Mexican War of Independence from Spain in 1810. It is Mexico's most important national holiday and is the centrepiece of the Fiestas Patrias. (It is distinct from Cinco de Mayo, which marks the 1862 Battle of Puebla and is celebrated more prominently in the United States.)

The president re-enacts the Grito de Dolores from the National Palace balcony.

The Grito de Dolores

In the early hours of 16 September 1810, the parish priest Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla rang the church bell in the town of Dolores, Guanajuato, and issued a call to arms against Spanish rule — the Grito de Dolores ("Cry of Dolores"). The exact words are not documented, but the event ignited an eleven-year war that ended with Mexican independence in 1821.

Celebrations

Festivities begin on the evening of 15 September, when the President of Mexico, from the balcony of the National Palace overlooking the Zócalo in Mexico City, rings Hidalgo's original bell and delivers the Grito — shouting "¡Viva!" to Hidalgo and the other heroes of independence and three times "¡Viva México!" — before a crowd of hundreds of thousands, with fireworks following. Governors and mayors perform the same ritual in cities and towns across the country, and Mexican embassies hold ceremonies abroad. On 16 September there are military parades, school celebrations, concerts, and traditional foods such as chiles en nogada, prepared in the green, white, and red of the flag.
