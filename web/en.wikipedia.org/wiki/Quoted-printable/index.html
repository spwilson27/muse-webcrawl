Quoted-printable

Quoted-printable (or QP encoding) is a binary-to-text encoding defined in the MIME standard (RFC 2045) for representing 8-bit data as printable ASCII characters in email message bodies. It is designed for data that is mostly ASCII text, so that the encoded form remains largely readable by humans.

Encoding rules

Printable ASCII characters (except "=") may represent themselves. Any other byte is encoded as "=" followed by two uppercase hexadecimal digits; for example, "=" itself becomes "=3D". Spaces and tabs may appear literally but must be encoded at the end of a line. Long lines are wrapped with "soft line breaks": an "=" at the end of a line indicates that the break is an artefact of encoding, and lines are limited to 76 characters.

Comparison with Base64

Unlike Base64, which expands all data by about a third uniformly, quoted-printable adds little overhead when the input is plain English text but triples the size of non-ASCII bytes, making it inefficient for binary data or text in languages using many non-ASCII characters. It remains common in email for bodies in European languages and in combination with charsets such as ISO-8859-1 and UTF-8.
