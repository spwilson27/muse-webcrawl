Pyrosomes are free-floating colonial tunicates that live in warm seas. Each colony is a hollow, gelatinous tube — usually a few centimetres to several metres long, and in rare giant forms reportedly far longer — made up of hundreds to thousands of tiny individual animals called zooids embedded in a common tunic. The name means "fire body", for the colony's vivid bioluminescence.

Biology

Each zooid draws in water through its own oral siphon, filters out plankton, and expels the water into the shared central cavity of the tube; the combined current propels the colony slowly through the water. Zooids are connected by tissue and can coordinate pulses of light: a flash started at one end ripples along the colony. Colonies reproduce both sexually and by budding of new zooids.

Distribution and blooms

Pyrosomes are normally creatures of tropical and temperate open ocean, but they occasionally appear in enormous blooms; in 2017, millions of Pyrosoma atlanticum appeared along the Pacific coast of North America, far north of their usual range, clogging fishing gear and washing ashore — an event attributed to unusually warm ocean conditions.
