United States Transportation Command

The United States Transportation Command (USTRANSCOM) is one of eleven US unified combatant commands, headquartered at Scott Air Force Base, Illinois. As the Department of Defense's global mobility and logistics command, it provides air, land and sea transportation for US forces, moving personnel, equipment and supplies anywhere in the world. Its creed centres on delivering the joint force "anytime, anywhere".

History

USTRANSCOM was established on 1 July 1987, uniting for the first time under one commander the military's strategic lift organisations — a reform spurred by Goldwater–Nichols. It proved itself in Operations Desert Shield and Desert Storm (1990–91), which required the largest airlift and sealift since the Second World War. In 2003 the command was also designated the Defense Department's Distribution Process Owner, coordinating the entire military supply chain.

Components

The command works through three service component commands: the Air Force's Air Mobility Command (airlift and aerial refuelling, including the C-17, C-5 and KC-135 fleets), the Navy's Military Sealift Command (government-owned and chartered cargo ships and the surge sealift fleet), and the Army's Military Surface Deployment and Distribution Command (ports, rail and trucking). Together they manage an enterprise of roughly 120,000 military and civilian personnel, hundreds of aircraft and ships, and extensive partnerships with commercial carriers through programmes such as the Civil Reserve Air Fleet.

USTRANSCOM has supported every major US operation since 1987, including the drawdown and reinforcement of forces in Iraq and Afghanistan, humanitarian airlifts after earthquakes and hurricanes, the 2021 Kabul evacuation, and large-scale deliveries of military aid to Ukraine from 2022.
