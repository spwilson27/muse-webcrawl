Pseudo-random number sampling

Pseudo-random number sampling, or non-uniform pseudo-random variate generation, is the numerical practice of generating pseudo-random numbers that follow a given probability distribution. Such methods are a foundation of Monte Carlo simulation, statistics and computer graphics.

Basic methods

Most methods start from a source of uniform random numbers on (0,1). The inverse transform method applies the inverse of the target distribution's cumulative distribution function to a uniform variate; it is exact whenever the inverse CDF can be computed. Rejection sampling, introduced by John von Neumann, samples from an easier proposal distribution that envelopes the target density and accepts points under the target curve. The acceptance–complement and ratio-of-uniforms methods are refinements of the same idea.

Common distributions

The Box–Muller transform and the polar (Marsaglia) method convert pairs of uniform numbers into normally distributed pairs. The ziggurat algorithm samples efficiently from distributions such as the normal and exponential by precomputed layered rectangles. Discrete distributions are commonly sampled with the alias method, which allows O(1) sampling after O(n) preprocessing.

Software

Functions for non-uniform sampling are built into statistical environments such as R, NumPy and SciPy, and into the GNU Scientific Library. The choice of algorithm trades setup cost, speed, memory and numerical accuracy in the tails of the distribution.
