Hugh Clowers Thompson Jr. (15 April 1943 – 6 January 2006) was a United States Army helicopter pilot who, with his crew Glenn Andreotta and Lawrence Colburn, intervened to stop the Mỹ Lai massacre during the Vietnam War on 16 March 1968.

Mỹ Lai

Flying a low-level reconnaissance mission in an OH-23 Raven helicopter, Thompson saw dead and wounded civilians everywhere around the village of Sơn Mỹ and realised American troops of Charlie Company were killing unarmed villagers. He landed repeatedly to question officers, marked the locations of wounded civilians with smoke, and — finding troops advancing on a group of villagers sheltering in a bunker — landed his helicopter between the soldiers and the villagers, ordering his crew to train their guns on the Americans and to fire on them if they shot the civilians. He then persuaded the villagers to come out and had them evacuated by larger helicopters, and pulled a surviving child from an irrigation ditch filled with bodies.

Thompson reported the massacre up the chain of command, helping to halt the operation. His testimony was central to later investigations, though he was vilified by some at the time. Thirty years later, in 1998, Thompson, Andreotta (posthumously) and Colburn received the Soldier's Medal, the U.S. Army's highest award for bravery not involving direct contact with the enemy. Thompson returned to Vietnam in 1998 and met survivors he had rescued. He is widely cited in military ethics training as an exemplar of moral courage.
