Wilson Dam is a dam spanning the Tennessee River between Florence and Muscle Shoals in the U.S. state of Alabama. Completed in 1924, it is the largest conventional hydroelectric facility of the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) in terms of generating capacity and one of nine TVA dams on the main stem of the Tennessee River. The dam was named for U.S. President Woodrow Wilson.

History

The site at Muscle Shoals was long recognized as a major obstacle to navigation on the Tennessee River because of a series of rapids and shoals, but also as a site of enormous hydroelectric potential. In 1916, as part of war preparedness under the National Defense Act, President Wilson authorized construction of a dam and two nitrate plants at Muscle Shoals to produce nitrates for munitions. Construction of the dam began in 1918 under the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, employing at its peak some 18,000 workers. The First World War ended before the project was finished, but construction continued and the dam began generating power in 1924.

During the 1920s the fate of the Muscle Shoals facilities became a major political controversy, with Senator George Norris of Nebraska championing public operation against proposals, including one associated with Henry Ford, to lease the works to private interests. Presidential vetoes blocked Norris's bills in 1928 and 1931. In 1933 President Franklin D. Roosevelt signed the Tennessee Valley Authority Act, and Wilson Dam became the cornerstone asset of the new public corporation.

Navigation and locks

Wilson Dam has two locks. The main lock, completed in 1959, measures 110 by 600 feet (34 by 183 m) and has a lift of about 94 feet (29 m)—among the highest single-lift locks in the eastern United States. An older auxiliary lock, dating from the dam's original construction, is also maintained. The dam raised the water level over the Muscle Shoals rapids, making year-round barge navigation possible.

Power and reservoir

The powerhouse contains 21 generating units with a net dependable capacity in excess of 650 megawatts. The dam impounds Wilson Lake, which extends about 15 miles (24 km) upstream and provides recreation, water supply, and flood-control benefits in conjunction with the wider TVA reservoir system.
