Ian Hickson is a software engineer and web standards editor, best known as the long-time editor of the HTML specification at the Web Hypertext Application Technology Working Group (WHATWG) — the HTML Living Standard — and for his work at Google, where he later became one of the creators of the Flutter user-interface toolkit.

Career

Hickson was born in Geneva and studied physics at the University of Bath in England. He worked at Netscape and then at Opera Software, where he contributed to the CSS specifications and co-authored the Acid2 (2005) and Acid3 (2008) browser conformance tests with Håkon Wium Lie and others. In 2004 he was a founding figure of the WHATWG, formed by browser vendors to continue the evolution of HTML after the W3C turned toward XHTML. Hickson joined Google in 2005 and edited the HTML5 specification through its development; his exacting, single-editor style of spec writing, and his famous habit of resolving thousands of feedback bugs, shaped the modern HTML standard and related specifications such as the original drafts of Web Workers, Web Sockets, and Server-Sent Events.

In the mid-2010s Hickson moved within Google to the team that created Flutter, a cross-platform UI framework written around the Dart language, where he was a prominent engineer and community voice. He stepped down as editor of the HTML Living Standard in the early 2020s, and in 2024 announced his departure from Google after nearly two decades.

Contributions

  • Editor of HTML5 / the HTML Living Standard for nearly two decades.
  • Co-author of the Acid2 and Acid3 tests for web standards compliance.
  • Early editor of CSS specifications during his time at Opera.
  • Core contributor to Flutter at Google.
