Peltast

A peltast (Greek: πελταστής) was a type of light infantry in ancient Greek warfare, named for the pelta, a light crescent-shaped wicker shield covered with hide. Peltasts typically carried several javelins and a short sword, wore little or no armor, and fought as skirmishers.

Role

Originally associated with Thrace, peltasts harassed enemy formations with volleys of javelins, screened the heavy hoplite phalanx, protected flanks, and pursued broken enemies. They were far more mobile than hoplites and could operate in rough terrain where the phalanx could not.

Importance

Their value grew in the Peloponnesian War — most famously at Sphacteria in 425 BC, where Athenian peltasts and light troops defeated Spartan hoplites. In the fourth century BC the Athenian general Iphicrates reformed peltast equipment with longer spears and swords, and peltasts became a standard component of Hellenistic armies.
