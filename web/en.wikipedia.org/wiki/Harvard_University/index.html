Harvard University

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Harvard Yard in autumn: red-brick buildings, the white steeple of Memorial Church and students crossing the green]

Harvard University is a private Ivy League research university in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Founded in 1636, it is the oldest institution of higher learning in the United States and one of the world's most prestigious universities. It is organised into a dozen schools, including Harvard College, the Graduate School of Arts and Sciences, and the professional schools of law, medicine, business and government.

Harvard's endowment — the largest of any academic institution, in the tens of billions of dollars — supports its libraries (the largest academic library system in the world), museums and financial aid. Alumni include eight US presidents, numerous heads of state, and well over a hundred Nobel laureates affiliated with the university.

History

Named for the clergyman John Harvard, its first benefactor, the college trained Puritan clergy before broadening into a research university under President Charles Eliot in the late 19th century. Harvard Yard, Widener Library and the Harvard Crimson (founded 1873) are fixtures of its identity.

People

Mark Zuckerberg and Bill Gates are famous former students who left without graduating to found Facebook and Microsoft; the university also counts more billionaires among alumni than most institutions.
