Kaufhaus des Westens

Kaufhaus des Westens (KaDeWe)
Location: Tauentzienstraße, Schöneberg, Berlin, Germany
Opened: 27 March 1907
Founder: Adolf Jandorf
Sales area: about 60,000 m²

The Kaufhaus des Westens ("Department Store of the West"), usually abbreviated KaDeWe, is a department store on Tauentzienstraße in Berlin, Germany. With some 60,000 square metres of sales space and more than 380,000 items, it is one of the largest department stores in Europe and a landmark of luxury retail, famous especially for its sixth-floor food hall, the "Feinschmeckeretage".

History

The store was founded by Adolf Jandorf and designed by architect Johann Emil Schaudt, opening in 1907. In 1927 it passed to the Hertie group of Hermann Tietz. During the Nazi period the store was "aryanised" and its Jewish owners and staff driven out. An American bomber crashed into the building in November 1943 and it largely burned down. KaDeWe reopened in 1950 in the western sector of the divided city and became a showcase of West Berlin consumer abundance, later expanding after reunification.

The store today

KaDeWe's food hall offers tens of thousands of products, around 3,400 wines and hundreds of cheeses, with gourmet bars serving oysters, champagne and regional dishes. The store was part of the KaDeWe Group with Alsterhaus in Hamburg and Oberpollinger in Munich; it continued trading through the insolvency proceedings affecting its parent groups in 2024.
