The Isère is a river in southeastern France, 286 kilometres (178 mi) long, and a left tributary of the Rhône. It rises in the Graian Alps near the Col de l'Iseran and the Italian border, in the Savoie department, and flows through the Tarentaise valley past Albertville, then southwest through Grenoble in the Grésivaudan valley, before joining the Rhône just north of Valence.

Course and uses

The river's name gave the modern Isère department its name. Its valley carries major road and rail links toward the alpine passes, and the river is heavily developed for hydroelectric power, with numerous dams in the Tarentaise including Tignes. Grenoble, the largest city on the river, historically used its gravel banks for industry, and white-water reaches of the upper river hosted events linked to the 1992 Winter Olympics in Albertville.
