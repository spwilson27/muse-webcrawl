Macadamia nut

The macadamia nut is the edible seed of trees of the genus Macadamia in the family Proteaceae, native to eastern Australia. Only two species, Macadamia integrifolia and Macadamia tetraphylla, are of commercial importance. The kernel is exceptionally rich, creamy and high in monounsaturated fat, and is sold as a premium snack nut.

GenusMacadamia (Proteaceae)
OriginQueensland & New South Wales, Australia
Major producersAustralia, South Africa, Kenya, Hawaii

Cultivation

Macadamias were first commercialised in Hawaii in the late 19th century, where the industry was pioneered by growers such as William Purvis in 1881. Trees take seven to ten years to bear a full crop, and the nuts have one of the hardest shells of any commercial nut, requiring considerable force to crack.

Uses

The nuts are eaten roasted and salted, coated in chocolate, or pressed for oil used in cooking and cosmetics. Macadamia foliage and husks contain cyanogenic compounds, and the nuts are toxic to dogs.
