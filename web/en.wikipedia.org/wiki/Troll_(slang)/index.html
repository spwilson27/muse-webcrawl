Troll (slang)

Troll
DefinitionA person who posts deliberately provocative or off-topic messages online to elicit emotional responses or disrupt discussion
OriginUsenet, late 1980s–1990s; from "trolling" for fish, later associated with the mythological troll
Related termsFlaming, griefing, concern troll, patent troll

In slang, a troll is a person who posts deliberately offensive, extraneous or inflammatory messages in an online community—such as a forum, chat room or social media platform—with the intent of provoking readers into displaying emotional responses, or manipulating others' perception. The act is called trolling.

Etymology

The verb "to troll" originally referred to a fishing technique of trailing a baited line behind a slow-moving boat; early Usenet users "trolled for newbies" by posting naive bait questions. The word's resemblance to the mythological troll—a creature that lurks under bridges to ambush travellers—reinforced the term, though the fishing sense came first.

Usage and response

The conventional advice is "do not feed the troll": responding rewards the behaviour with attention. Platforms combat trolling with moderation, rate-limiting and reputation systems. The term has expanded to cover state-sponsored manipulation campaigns run from "troll farms", organised political provocation, and, in unrelated commercial usage, the "patent troll" that enforces patents without producing goods.
