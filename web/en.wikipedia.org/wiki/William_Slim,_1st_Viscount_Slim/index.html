William Slim, 1st Viscount Slim


Born 6 August 1891, Bristol, England
Died 14 December 1970 (aged 79), London
Rank Field Marshal
Commands Fourteenth Army; Chief of the Imperial General Staff

Field Marshal William Joseph Slim, 1st Viscount Slim (6 August 1891 – 14 December 1970) was a British military commander, best known for leading the Fourteenth Army in the reconquest of Burma from the Japanese during the Second World War.

Early career

Slim, from a modest background in Bristol and Birmingham, was commissioned during the First World War and wounded at Gallipoli. He served in Mesopotamia and India, transferring to the Indian Army and the Gurkhas, and rose steadily through staff and command appointments. In 1940–41 he fought in the East African and Middle East campaigns.

Burma

In March 1942 Slim took command of Burma Corps during the long retreat from Rangoon to India, the longest retreat in British military history. He rebuilt the defeated forces into the Fourteenth Army — the "Forgotten Army" — restoring morale and training it for jungle warfare. In 1944 his army withstood the Japanese offensives at Imphal and Kohima, and in 1945 he executed a brilliant offensive down the Irrawaddy, capturing Mandalay and Rangoon, one of the great land victories of the war.

Later life

Slim served as Chief of the Imperial General Staff (1948–1952) and as Governor-General of Australia (1953–1960). His memoir Defeat into Victory (1956) is widely regarded as one of the finest military memoirs in English. In a 2013 poll of historians he was voted Britain's greatest general.
