Luxor Obelisks

Luxor Obelisks
MaterialRed granite (Aswan)
HeightAbout 23 metres (75 ft)
CreatedReign of Ramesses II, 13th century BC
Present locationLuxor Temple, Egypt; Place de la Concorde, Paris

The Luxor Obelisks are a pair of ancient Egyptian obelisks, more than 3,000 years old, carved during the reign of Ramesses II (c. 1250 BC). They originally stood at the entrance to Luxor Temple in the southern Egyptian city of Luxor. The right-hand (western) obelisk remains in Egypt, while the left-hand obelisk was moved to Paris in the 1830s and now stands in the centre of the Place de la Concorde.

Description

Both obelisks are carved from a single block of red granite quarried at Aswan. They are covered with hieroglyphic inscriptions celebrating the military victories and reign of Ramesses II. The obelisks were originally flanked by statues of the king and stood before the first pylon of the temple, where an avenue of sphinxes led toward Karnak.

The Paris obelisk

In 1829 the Ottoman viceroy of Egypt, Muhammad Ali Pasha, offered both obelisks to France. Only one was transported: a specially built ship, the Louxor, carried it from Egypt to France, arriving in Paris in December 1833. King Louis Philippe I decided to place it in the Place de la Concorde, and it was erected there on 25 October 1836, in the presence of a huge crowd, using elaborate lifting machinery designed by the engineer Apollinaire Lebas. The obelisk's original pyramidion was later capped with a gold-leafed pyramid cap, installed in 1998.
