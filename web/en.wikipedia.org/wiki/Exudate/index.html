Exudate

An exudate is a fluid emitted by an organism through pores or a wound, a process known as exuding or exudation. In medicine, exudate is fluid that leaks out of blood vessels into surrounding tissues as a result of inflammation or injury. It is rich in protein and cellular elements, in contrast to a transudate, which has low protein content and forms mainly due to pressure imbalances.

Medical exudates

Exudative fluids collect in body cavities and wounds. A pleural effusion, for instance, is classified as an exudate or transudate using Light's criteria, based on protein and lactate dehydrogenase ratios. Purulent exudate (pus) contains large numbers of neutrophils and dead cells and indicates infection; fibrinous exudate is rich in fibrinogen and may form adhesions; serous exudate is watery and typical of early inflammation or blistering.

Plant exudates

Plants exude substances such as sap, resins, latex, gums, and nectar. Root exudates — sugars, amino acids, and organic acids released into the rhizosphere — shape the soil microbiome and nutrient availability. Amber is fossilized tree resin exudate.
