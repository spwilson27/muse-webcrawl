Lock picking

Lock picking is the practice of unlocking a lock by manipulating the components of the lock device without the original key. It is performed with tools such as tension wrenches, hook picks, and rakes, and is used legitimately by locksmiths, security researchers, and hobbyists (locksport), as well as unlawfully by criminals.

Pin tumbler picking

In a pin tumbler lock, key pins of varying lengths must be raised to align the shear line between plug and housing. Applying light rotational tension with a wrench causes pins that are set correctly to bind and stay in place; the picker lifts each pin stack in turn (single-pin picking) until all are set and the plug rotates. Raking instead scrubs a waved pick across the pins to set them randomly.

Other methods and tools

Related techniques include bump keys, pick guns, impressioning, decoding, and bypassing the lock mechanism entirely. Manufacturers counter picking with security pins (spool, serrated, mushroom), tighter tolerances, sidebars, and additional locking elements.

Legality

The legality of possessing lock picks varies by jurisdiction. In many places possession is legal absent intent to commit a crime, while some jurisdictions treat picks as burglary tools or restrict them to licensed locksmiths.

Locksport

Organised hobbyist lock picking, called locksport, is promoted by groups such as TOOOL (The Open Organisation of Lockpickers), which hold competitions and argue that open discussion of lock weaknesses improves security.
