The Rann of Kutch is a large area of salt marshes in the Thar Desert region of Kutch district in Gujarat, India, and the Sindh province of Pakistan. It is divided into the Great Rann of Kutch and the Little Rann of Kutch, and is reputed to be one of the largest salt deserts in the world.

Geography and climate

The Rann was once a shallow arm of the Arabian Sea; seismic uplift and the silting of the Indus delta cut it off, leaving a vast seasonal wetland. During the monsoon it floods ankle-to-waist deep; in the dry season it becomes a blinding white crust of salt. Summer temperatures exceed 44 °C.

Ecology

The region hosts the Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary in the Little Rann and the flamingo breeding grounds of the Great Rann ("Flamingo City"), as well as caracal, desert foxes, and seasonal wetlands used by migratory birds.

People and culture

Kutchi communities herd livestock on the marsh islands (bets) and produce salt by traditional methods. The annual Rann Utsav festival near Dhordo celebrates Kutchi music, crafts, and the full-moon landscape.

Border disputes

The inhospitable terrain along the India–Pakistan border has been contested, notably in the 1965 Rann of Kutch skirmishes before the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965, and the Sir Creek dispute at the marsh's western edge.
