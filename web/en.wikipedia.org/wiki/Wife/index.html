Wife

A wife is a woman in a marital relationship. A woman becomes a wife when she marries; the male counterpart is a husband. The rights and obligations of a wife regarding her spouse and her status in the community and in law vary between cultures and have varied over time.

Etymology

The word derives from Old English wīf, meaning "woman", cognate with German Weib. In earlier usage, "wife" could simply mean a woman, a sense preserved in words such as "midwife", "fishwife", and "housewife". A woman on her wedding day is commonly described as a bride, and after the wedding she is referred to as a wife.

Legal and social aspects

Historically, in many legal systems a wife's legal identity was subsumed under that of her husband under the doctrine of coverture, limiting her ability to own property or enter contracts; these rules were progressively reformed from the 19th century onward. Historically, the exchange of goods or money associated with marriage—such as dowry, dower, and bride price—has defined obligations between the wife's and husband's families in different societies. Today, legal systems generally recognize spouses as equal partners, though customs, religious law, and family expectations continue to shape the role. A woman whose spouse has died is a widow; a woman who has divorced or whose marriage has been annulled is an ex-wife.
