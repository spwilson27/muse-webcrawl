Tarabai

Tarabai Bhonsle (1675 – 9 December 1761) was the regent of the Maratha Empire in India from 1700 until 1708. She was the queen of Rajaram I and daughter-in-law of the empire's founder Shivaji. Widely praised for her role in keeping alive the resistance against Mughal occupation of Maratha territories after the death of her husband, she led Maratha forces personally during the final years of the long Mughal–Maratha war.

Early life

Tarabai was the daughter of Hambirrao Mohite, commander-in-chief of Shivaji's armies. She married Rajaram, Shivaji's younger son, and was known from an early age for her intelligence and knowledge of military and administrative affairs.

Regency

When Rajaram died in 1700 during the siege of Jinji, Tarabai proclaimed her infant son Shivaji II as king and herself as regent. Under her leadership the Marathas, though driven from much of their territory by the Emperor Aurangzeb's armies, carried the war into Mughal provinces, raiding Malwa and Gujarat and exhausting the imperial forces. The Mughal historian Khafi Khan acknowledged her abilities as a ruler and military organiser.

Later years

After Aurangzeb's death in 1707, the released Maratha prince Shahu claimed the throne, and Tarabai was defeated and sidelined in the ensuing civil war; she established a rival court at Kolhapur for her son. She lived until 1761, long enough to reconcile with Shahu and to play a final political role in the affairs of Satara.
