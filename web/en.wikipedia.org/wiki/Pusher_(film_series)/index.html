Pusher (film series)

Pusher

FilmsPusher (1996), Pusher II (2004), Pusher 3 (2005)

The Pusher films are a Danish crime trilogy written and directed by Nicolas Winding Refn, set in the criminal underworld of Copenhagen. Shot in a raw, documentary-influenced style with largely non-professional supporting casts drawn from the milieu itself, the films follow different protagonists through the same interconnected world of drug dealers, enforcers and addicts, each story structured around a desperate countdown as a character tries to raise money owed to a supplier.

Films

Pusher (1996)

Refn's feature debut, made when he was 26, follows mid-level dealer Frank (Kim Bodnia) over one disastrous week: a heroin deal raided by police forces him to dump the drugs, leaving him owing a fortune to the Serbian kingpin Milo (Zlatko Burić). The film's escalating, real-time desperation launched Refn's career and became a landmark of Danish cinema.

Pusher II: With Blood on My Hands (2004)

The sequel centres on Tonny (Mads Mikkelsen), Frank's former partner, released from prison to discover he has a child, an ailing father who despises him, and an unwanted place in a stolen-car and drug operation. Widely regarded as the strongest of the trilogy, it deepened the series' themes of masculinity, fathers and sons, and inescapable criminal cycles.

Pusher 3: I'm the Angel of Death (2005)

The third film follows Milo, now an ageing middle manager of crime, juggling a Narcotics Anonymous meeting, his daughter's 25th birthday party and a botched shipment of ecstasy he must unload — while younger Albanian gangsters close in on his territory.

Remakes and legacy

An Indian remake, Pusher (2010), and a British remake, Pusher (2012) directed by Luis Prieto and executive-produced by Refn, transplanted the first film's plot to London. The trilogy established Refn's international reputation, preceding Bronson, Drive and The Neon Demon, and gave early major roles to Mads Mikkelsen and Kim Bodnia.
