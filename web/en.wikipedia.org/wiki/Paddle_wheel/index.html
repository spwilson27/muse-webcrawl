Paddle wheel

A paddle wheel is a form of waterwheel or impeller in which a number of paddles are set around the periphery of a wheel. It has multiple uses, most famously as the propulsion device of paddle steamers, where the rotating paddles push against the water to drive the vessel forward.

Design

In marine propulsion, paddle wheels were mounted either on the sides of the hull (side-wheelers) or at the stern (stern-wheelers). Early wheels had fixed radial paddles; later "feathering" wheels used a linkage to keep each paddle near-vertical as it passed through the water, improving efficiency. Paddle wheels are efficient in shallow water but lose efficiency as a vessel's draft changes and are vulnerable to damage in rough seas.

Other uses

Paddle wheels are also used to aerate fish ponds and wastewater treatment basins, to measure water flow, and historically to power machinery as a type of waterwheel.
