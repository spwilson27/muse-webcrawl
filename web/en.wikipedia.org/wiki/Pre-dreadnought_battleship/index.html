Pre-dreadnought battleship

Pre-dreadnought battleships were sea-going battleships built from the mid- to late-1880s to the early 1900s, before the launch of HMS Dreadnought in 1906. The pre-dreadnoughts replaced the ironclad warships of the 1870s and 1880s.

Characteristics

They characteristically carried a mixed-calibre armament: a main battery of four heavy guns (typically 12-inch) in two twin turrets fore and aft, a secondary battery of intermediate-calibre guns, and a tertiary battery for defence against torpedo boats. They were powered by coal-fired triple-expansion steam engines and protected by steel armour.

Obsolescence

The all-big-gun Dreadnought rendered the type obsolete almost overnight, though pre-dreadnoughts still saw extensive service in the First World War, including at Gallipoli, and as training and depot ships thereafter. The only surviving example is the Japanese battleship Mikasa, preserved as a museum ship at Yokosuka.
