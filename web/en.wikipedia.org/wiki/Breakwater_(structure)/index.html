Breakwater (structure)

[Image placeholder: Breakwater (structure)]

A breakwater is a permanent structure constructed at a coastal or offshore location, in the sea or a river, to protect against the action of weather and longshore drift; it usually serves to calm the waters and provide safe anchorage for ships in a harbour.

Types

Rubble-mound breakwaters are heaped piles of rock or concrete armour units (such as tetrapods); vertical caisson breakwaters are large concrete boxes sunk onto the seabed; floating breakwaters attenuate waves in marinas. Design must balance wave reflection and overtopping against cost and environmental effects such as altered sediment transport.
