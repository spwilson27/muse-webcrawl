Leah (Hebrew: לֵאָה, Lēʼā) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, the elder daughter of Laban and the first wife of the patriarch Jacob. She is the mother of six of Jacob's sons — Reuben, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun — and of his daughter Dinah, and is thus an ancestress of six of the twelve tribes of Israel, including the priestly tribe of Levi and the royal tribe of Judah.

Biblical narrative

Jacob, fleeing to Haran, fell in love with Leah's younger sister Rachel and agreed to work seven years for her hand. At the wedding, Laban substituted the veiled Leah; when Jacob protested, Laban pleaded local custom that the elder must marry first, and gave him Rachel a week later in exchange for seven further years of labour. Genesis describes Leah's eyes as "tender" (or "weak/delicate") in contrast to Rachel's beauty, and notes that Leah was "unloved" — yet God, seeing this, opened her womb while Rachel remained barren.

The naming of Leah's children records her hope that her fertility would win Jacob's love: Reuben ("See, a son"), Simeon, Levi, and Judah ("This time I will praise the LORD"). In the episode of the mandrakes, Leah bargains with Rachel for a night with Jacob, from which Issachar is conceived; she later bears Zebulun and Dinah.

Interpretation

Commentators have read Leah's story as a portrait of the unloved wife vindicated by fruitfulness: from her line come Moses (through Levi) and David and the Messiah (through Judah) in Jewish and Christian tradition alike. Leah is traditionally said to be buried with Jacob in the Cave of Machpelah at Hebron. In Dante's Purgatorio, Leah and Rachel symbolise the active and contemplative lives.
