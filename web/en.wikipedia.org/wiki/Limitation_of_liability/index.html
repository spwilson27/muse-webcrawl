Limitation of liability

Limitation of liability
Legal areaContract law, maritime law, tort
FormsStatutory caps; contractual limitation clauses; limitation funds

Limitation of liability refers to legal rules and contractual provisions that restrict the amount of damages or other liability that a party may be required to pay. Limitations appear in statutes, in international conventions, and in private contracts, where a limitation clause caps a party's exposure at an agreed sum, excludes certain categories of loss (such as consequential damages), or both.

Contractual limitation clauses

Parties to commercial contracts commonly agree clauses limiting liability to a fixed sum or a multiple of the contract price, or excluding liability for indirect losses. Courts may refuse to enforce such clauses where they are unreasonable, unconscionable, or prohibited by statute; consumer-protection and unfair-terms legislation in many jurisdictions (for example the UK Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 and the Consumer Rights Act 2015) restricts exclusion of liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence and subjects other exclusions to a reasonableness test. Liability for fraud and certain wilful misconduct often cannot be limited at all.

Maritime limitation

Shipowners have long enjoyed a statutory right to limit liability for maritime claims. Under the United States Limitation of Liability Act of 1851, a vessel owner may petition to limit liability to the value of the vessel and pending freight after a casualty, provided the loss occurred without the owner's privity or knowledge. Internationally, the Convention on Limitation of Liability for Maritime Claims (LLMC) of 1976, as amended by its 1996 Protocol, sets tonnage-based limits and a procedure under which a limitation fund is constituted for distribution among claimants. Limitation is typically lost where the loss resulted from the liable person's personal act or omission committed with intent to cause loss, or recklessly with knowledge that such loss would probably result.

Other statutory schemes

Analogous caps exist elsewhere: airline liability under the Montreal Convention, nuclear liability conventions channelling and capping operators' liability, and statutory limits on professional or governmental liability in some jurisdictions. Distinct from these, limited liability describes the protection of company shareholders, whose exposure is limited to their investment.
