The World War I Victory Medal is a United States service medal created in 1919 and awarded to military personnel who served in the First World War. It was designed by James Earle Fraser and features a winged Victory, standing, holding a sword and shield. The medal was awarded to all members of the armed forces who served between 6 April 1917 and 11 November 1918, with extensions for service in European Russia (1918–1919) and with the American Expeditionary Forces in Siberia (1918–1920).

Battle clasps

Personnel who took part in designated battles and campaigns could wear clasps on the medal's suspension ribbon naming the engagement, such as Cambrai, Somme, Ypres-Lys, St. Mihiel and Meuse-Argonne, along with country clasps for France, Italy, England, Russia and Siberia for service not involving battle. Corresponding small battle stars were worn on the service ribbon.

Design and ribbon

The rainbow-coloured ribbon, symbolising the coalition of Allied nations, was a feature shared in some form by the victory medals of many Allied and associated nations after the war, giving the series the informal name of the Allied Victory Medals.
