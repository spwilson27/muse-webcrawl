The Iona Community is an ecumenical Christian community founded in 1938 by the Church of Scotland minister George MacLeod on the island of Iona in Scotland. It is a dispersed community of members, associates and friends committed to working for peace and social justice, the rebuilding of community, and the renewal of worship.

History

MacLeod, a minister in Govan, Glasgow, founded the community during the Great Depression, bringing together trainee ministers and unemployed craftsmen from Glasgow to rebuild the ruined medieval Iona Abbey together — a deliberate symbol of joining the spiritual and material sides of life. Wartime interrupted the work, but the community resumed restoring the abbey, completing the church in 1965. MacLeod later became Moderator of the General Assembly of the Church of Scotland and a peer.

Work

The community runs residential centres on Iona (the Abbey and the MacLeod Centre) and the Camas adventure centre on Mull, welcoming thousands of guests each year for weeks of worship, discussion and work. Its Wild Goose Publications publishes influential liturgical and musical resources, including hymns by John L. Bell and Graham Maule that have spread worldwide. Members observe a common rule of daily prayer, Bible reading, accounting for use of time and money, and action for justice and peace. The Iona Community is distinct from the abbey's historic fabric, which is cared for by Historic Environment Scotland, though the community staffs and worships in the abbey.
