A Balrog is a powerful fictional demon in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. Balrogs were Maiar — angelic spirits of the same order as Sauron and the Wizards — who were seduced into the service of the dark lord Morgoth in the earliest ages. They appear as towering beings of shadow and flame, wielding whips and swords of fire.

In the legendarium

In The Silmarillion, Balrogs fought in Morgoth's armies throughout the First Age, led by their lord Gothmog, who slew Fëanor and Fingon and was himself killed by Ecthelion at the Fall of Gondolin. Most Balrogs perished in the War of Wrath, but at least one escaped and hid deep beneath the Misty Mountains.

In The Fellowship of the Ring, the Dwarves of Khazad-dûm, delving too deep for mithril, awoke this survivor — "Durin's Bane." It drove the Dwarves from Moria and, in January 3019 of the Third Age, confronted the Fellowship on the Bridge of Khazad-dûm, where it fought the wizard Gandalf. Both fell into the abyss; their duel continued to the peak of Zirakzigil, where Gandalf slew the Balrog and died of his own wounds before being sent back to Middle-earth.

Interpretation

The name derives from Sindarin Balrog, "demon of power" (Quenya Valarauko). Tolkien's depiction evolved: in early writings Balrogs were more numerous and less mighty. Whether Balrogs have wings — from the description of Durin's Bane's shadow spreading "like two vast wings" — is a famously debated question among readers.
