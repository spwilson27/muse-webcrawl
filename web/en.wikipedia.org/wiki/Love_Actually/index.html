Love Actually is a 2003 Christmas romantic comedy film written and directed by Richard Curtis. An ensemble production filmed primarily in London, it interweaves nine separate stories examining the complexities of love in the weeks leading up to Christmas.

Plot

The film opens at Heathrow Airport as the narrator reflects that "love actually is all around." Its storylines include the new Prime Minister (Hugh Grant) falling for a junior staff member; a widowed stepfather (Liam Neeson) helping his young stepson pursue his first crush; a writer (Colin Firth) who falls for his Portuguese housekeeper despite the language barrier; a married couple (Alan Rickman and Emma Thompson) strained by temptation; and aging rock star Billy Mack (Bill Nighy) chasing a Christmas number one with a cynical cover single.

Reception and legacy

Released in November 2003, the film grossed roughly $250 million worldwide. Initial critical response was mixed, but the film has since become a staple of Christmas television, frequently cited among the most popular holiday films, though its storylines have also drawn retrospective debate.

Sequel

In 2017, much of the cast reunited for Red Nose Day Actually, a short television sequel made for Comic Relief's Red Nose Day telethon.
