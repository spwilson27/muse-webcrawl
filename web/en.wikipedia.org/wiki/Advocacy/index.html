Advocacy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia. This article is about public advocacy in politics and civil society.


Advocacy at a public hearing (placeholder image).

Advocacy is an activity by an individual or group that aims to influence decisions within political, economic and social institutions. It includes activities and publications to influence public policy, laws and budgets by using facts, their relationships, the media and messaging to educate government officials and the public. Advocacy can include many activities that a person or organisation undertakes, including media campaigns, public speaking, commissioning and publishing research.

Forms

Lobbying is a form of advocacy in which a direct approach is made to legislators on a specific issue. Other forms include legal advocacy, in which lawyers plead the cause of a client; self-advocacy by marginalised groups speaking on their own behalf; health advocacy assisting patients; and budget advocacy directed at public spending decisions. Advocacy groups (also called interest groups, pressure groups or special interests) organise to promote causes ranging from human rights and environmental protection to industry interests.

Approaches

Advocacy strategies include insider approaches (consultation, briefing decision-makers, participation in committees), outsider approaches (demonstrations, petitions, media campaigns), and coalition building among organisations with shared goals. Modern advocacy makes extensive use of digital media, data and storytelling to mobilise supporters.

Ethics and regulation

Many jurisdictions regulate lobbying through registration and disclosure requirements, and professional advocates may be bound by codes of conduct. Critics raise concerns about unequal access to decision-makers, while defenders regard advocacy as an essential element of pluralist democracy.
