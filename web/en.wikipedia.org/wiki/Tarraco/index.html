Tarraco

Tarraco is the ancient name of the modern city of Tarragona in Catalonia, Spain. Founded as a Roman military base during the Second Punic War, it became the capital of the province of Hispania Citerior and later of the vast province of Tarraconensis, one of the most important cities of Roman Hispania.

History

Scipio Africanus fortified the site from 218 BCE on an earlier Iberian settlement. Julius Caesar granted it colonial status as Colonia Iulia Urbs Triumphalis Tarraco. Augustus wintered there in 27–25 BCE while directing campaigns in northern Spain, making it effectively a seat of imperial government; the city honoured him with an altar and later a great temple. Tarraco flourished with a forum, circus, amphitheatre by the sea, theatres, and an aqueduct, the Pont del Diable.

Archaeology

The Archaeological Ensemble of Tárraco, including its walls, amphitheatre, circus, and the nearby Centcelles villa and Scipions Tower, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000.
