Reaction time

FieldPsychology, neuroscience, human factors
Typical values~200–250 ms (visual), ~140–160 ms (auditory)
Key lawHick's law

Reaction time (RT) is the length of time between the presentation of a stimulus and the initiation of a motor response. It is a basic measure in experimental psychology and neuroscience of the speed of mental processing.

Types

In a simple reaction time task, one stimulus maps to one response. In choice reaction time tasks, each stimulus requires a different response; RT rises with the number of alternatives, a relationship described by Hick's law. Recognition tasks require responding to only one of several stimuli.

Factors

Reaction time depends on stimulus modality and intensity, practice, fatigue, age, and expectations. Elite sprinters, for example, react to the starting gun in about 120–180 ms, and starts faster than 100 ms are ruled false starts.

History

Franciscus Donders pioneered mental chronometry in the 1860s, using differences between task variants to estimate the duration of individual mental operations — an approach still fundamental to cognitive psychology.
