Quartic equation

A quartic equation is a polynomial equation of degree four, of the form ax⁴ + bx³ + cx² + dx + e = 0 with a ≠ 0. The corresponding function is called a quartic function. Quartics are the highest-degree polynomial equations solvable in radicals in general: the Abel–Ruffini theorem shows that no such formula exists for degree five and above.

History

The general solution was discovered by Lodovico Ferrari around 1540 and published in Gerolamo Cardano's Ars Magna (1545). Ferrari's method reduces the quartic to a resolvent cubic equation: after depressing the quartic (removing the cubic term), one completes the square with an auxiliary parameter chosen so that the remaining quadratic in x becomes a perfect square, which requires solving a cubic.

Properties

A quartic function has up to four real roots and up to three critical points; its graph is roughly W- or M-shaped when it has three real turning points. Biquadratic equations (containing only even powers) reduce to quadratics by substituting y = x². Descartes' and Euler's alternative methods also solve the general quartic.
