Sian Heder

Sian Heder (born June 23, 1977) is an American filmmaker. She is known for writing and directing the comedy-drama film Tallulah (2016) and the coming-of-age drama film CODA (2021), which won three Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Adapted Screenplay for Heder.

Early life

Heder was born in Cambridge, Massachusetts, the daughter of Welsh artist Sandor Antal and Hungarian artist Mags Harries. Her interest in deaf culture began when she worked as a babysitter for a deaf couple. She graduated from the Carnegie Mellon School of Drama.

Career

Heder's short film Mother (2006) won the Cinéfondation prize at Cannes. She wrote for the Netflix series Orange Is the New Black and created the series Little America for Apple TV+. CODA, an English-language remake of the French film La Famille Bélier set in Gloucester, Massachusetts, premiered at the 2021 Sundance Film Festival, where it won the Grand Jury Prize and was acquired by Apple for a festival-record sum. Its cast included deaf actors Marlee Matlin, Troy Kotsur, and Daniel Durant; Kotsur became the first deaf man to win an acting Oscar.
