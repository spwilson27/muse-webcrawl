Liège–Bastogne–Liège, also known as La Doyenne ("The Old Lady"), is a one-day professional road cycling race held annually in the Ardennes region of Wallonia, Belgium. First run in 1892, it is the oldest of the five Monuments of the European professional road cycling calendar — the most prestigious one-day races — and is the traditional finale of the spring "Ardennes classics" week, following the Amstel Gold Race and La Flèche Wallonne. The race is organised by the Amaury Sport Organisation and forms part of the UCI World Tour.

Course

The race runs roughly 255 km from Liège south to Bastogne and back, over the steep, short, forested hills (côtes) of the Ardennes. There is no single decisive climb; instead the cumulative effect of about a dozen categorised ascents — including the Côte de Stockeu, the Côte de la Redoute, the Côte de la Roche-aux-Faucons, and the Côte de Saint-Nicolas — wears the field down. The finish returned to a city-centre location in Liège in 2019 after decades of finishing in the suburb of Ans. Cold, wet weather is frequent, and the 1980 edition, raced through snow, was won by Bernard Hinault with a famous solo attack; several riders finished with frostbitten fingers.

History

Liège–Bastogne–Liège was created in 1892 by the Liège cycling club as an amateur event of 250 km, intended to rival contemporary long-distance races. It turned professional in 1908 and survived interruptions during the two world wars. Over the twentieth century it shifted to a demanding hilly parcours suiting climbers and all-rounders rather than pure sprinters. Eddy Merckx holds the record with five victories (1969, 1971, 1972, 1973, 1975). Other multiple winners include Moreno Argentin and Alejandro Valverde with four each. A women's race, Liège–Bastogne–Liège Femmes, was first held in 2017 as part of the UCI Women's World Tour.

Character of the race

Of the Ardennes classics, Liège is the longest and the most selective — usually described as a race for grand-tour climbers rather than puncheurs. The typical winning move comes on the final climbs in the last 20 km, from a small reduced group. Tadej Pogačar and Remco Evenepoel have been prominent winners in the 2020s, each winning the race twice in succession with long-range attacks.
