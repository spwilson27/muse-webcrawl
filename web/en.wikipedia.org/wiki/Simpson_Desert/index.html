Simpson Desert

The Simpson Desert is a large area of dry, red sandy plain and dunes in the Northern Territory, South Australia and Queensland in central Australia. It is the fourth-largest Australian desert, with an area of about 176,500 km² (68,100 sq mi). The desert is underlain by the Great Artesian Basin, one of the largest inland drainage areas in the world. It is famous for its long, parallel red sand dunes — some of the longest in the world — which run roughly north-northwest to south-southeast for hundreds of kilometres.

Geography

The Simpson Desert occupies the driest part of central Australia. Rainfall averages less than 200 mm per year and is highly erratic; summer temperatures can exceed 50 °C. The dune fields are composed of long, straight, parallel ridges typically 10–30 metres high — the largest, known as Big Red (Nappanerica), near Birdsville, rises about 40 metres — separated by interdunal swales covered with spinifex grassland. There are no permanent rivers, though the Georgina and Diamantina systems border the desert, and claypans and ephemeral lakes fill after rare heavy rain.

History

Aboriginal peoples, including the Wangkangurru and Arrernte, have lived in and around the desert for thousands of years, sustained by its soaks and "mikiri" wells. The explorer Charles Sturt entered its margins in 1845, and David Lindsay crossed part of it in 1886. The first complete motorised crossing was made in 1936 by pastoralist and aviator Ted Colson, and the first vehicle crossing from west to east was achieved by the geologist Reg Sprigg and his family in 1962. The desert was named in 1929, after an aerial survey, for Alfred Allen Simpson, a South Australian industrialist and president of the Royal Geographical Society of Australasia's South Australian branch.

Conservation and tourism

Large parts of the desert are protected in reserves, including the Munga-Thirri—Simpson Desert National Park in Queensland (proclaimed in 2021, one of Australia's largest national parks), the Witjira National Park in South Australia (home to the Dalhousie Springs mound springs complex) and regional reserves crossed by popular four-wheel-drive routes such as the French Line and the Rig Road. The township of Birdsville, on the desert's eastern edge, is a well-known outback destination.

Fauna and flora

Despite its aridity, the desert supports specialised wildlife, including the crest-tailed mulgara, Eyrean grasswren, sand goannas, thorny devils and feral camels. Vegetation is dominated by spinifex (Triodia) hummock grassland, with sandhill cane-grass (Zygochloa paradoxa) stabilising the dune crests.
