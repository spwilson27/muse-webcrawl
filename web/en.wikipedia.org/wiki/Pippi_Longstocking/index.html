Pippi Longstocking

Pippi Longstocking (Swedish: Pippi Långstrump) is the fictional main character in an eponymous series of children's books by Swedish author Astrid Lindgren. Pippi — full name Pippilotta Delicatessa Windowshade Mackrelmint Ephraim's Daughter Longstocking — is a supernaturally strong, red-haired nine-year-old girl who lives alone in Villa Villekulla with her monkey, Mr. Nilsson, and her horse, while her sea-captain father sails the world.

Books and character

The first book appeared in 1945, after Lindgren invented the stories for her daughter Karin, who coined the name. Pippi is cheerful, unconventional, and fiercely independent: she can lift her horse one-handed, mocks pompous adults, and dispenses gold coins from a suitcase. Her neighbours and friends are the well-behaved Tommy and Annika. Three original novels, picture books, and later adaptations made Pippi one of the most translated and beloved characters in children's literature.

Adaptations and legacy

The 1969 Swedish television series, starring Inger Nilsson, fixed Pippi's image for generations, and numerous films — including the 1988 American film The New Adventures of Pippi Longstocking — followed. Pippi has been interpreted as a symbol of children's autonomy and nonconformity, and Lindgren's manuscripts of the books are inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register.
