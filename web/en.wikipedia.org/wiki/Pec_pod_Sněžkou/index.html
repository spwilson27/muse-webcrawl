Pec pod Sněžkou

Country: Czech Republic
Region: Hradec Králové
District: Trutnov
Elevation: 769 m (2,523 ft)
Population: approx. 700

Pec pod Sněžkou (German: Petzer) is a town in Trutnov District in the Hradec Králové Region of the Czech Republic. It lies in the valley of the Úpa River in the Krkonoše (Giant Mountains), directly below Sněžka — at 1,603 metres the highest mountain in the Czech Republic — whose name the town bears: "Pec below Sněžka". Despite a small permanent population of only several hundred, it is one of the country's best-known mountain resorts.

History

The settlement grew around mining and forestry; iron, copper and arsenic ores were mined in the surrounding mountains from the 16th century, and the oldest mining settlement in the area, Obří důl, dates to 1511. The first written mention of Pec itself is from 1790. Tourism developed in the 19th and 20th centuries as hiking and skiing in the Giant Mountains grew in popularity, and the town was granted town status in the 20th century.

Tourism and transport

Pec pod Sněžkou is a major centre for skiing, with a network of slopes and lifts, and in summer it is the principal starting point for ascents of Sněžka. A chairlift — first opened in 1949 and rebuilt as a modern gondola lift in 2012–2013 — runs from the town via Růžová hora to the summit of Sněžka. The town lies within the Krkonoše National Park, and its traditional mountain chalets and newer hotels serve visitors year-round.
