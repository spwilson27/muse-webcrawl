Intaglio

Intaglio is a term with several related meanings in the arts, most of them describing designs cut or incised into a surface:

  • Intaglio (printmaking) — the family of printing and printmaking techniques in which the image is incised into a surface and the sunken areas hold the ink: engraving, etching, drypoint, aquatint and mezzotint.
  • Intaglio (gemstone) — a small carved or engraved gem, often used as a seal, in which the design is cut into the stone rather than raised above it; the opposite of a cameo.
  • Intaglio (burial mound) — a type of effigy mound, a shallow concave pit shaped by Native American mound builders in the upper Mississippi valley.
  • Intaglio (jewellery and metalwork) — designs carved below the surface of metal, glass or stone generally.

The word derives from the Italian intagliare, "to cut into". In each of its senses the defining feature is the same: the image lies below the surface of the material rather than standing out from it.
