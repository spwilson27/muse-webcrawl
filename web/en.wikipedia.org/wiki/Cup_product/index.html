Cup product

[Image: Schematic of two cochains combining on a triangulated surface to form a cup product]

In mathematics, specifically in algebraic topology, the cup product is a method of combining two cohomology classes to produce a new class of higher degree. It endows the total cohomology of a space with the structure of a graded-commutative ring, the cohomology ring, which is a much finer invariant than the cohomology groups alone.

Definition

For a topological space X, the cup product is a family of bilinear maps Hp(X) × Hq(X) → Hp+q(X), written α ⌣ β. At the level of singular cochains it can be defined by evaluating one cochain on the front face and the other on the back face of a simplex. The product is associative and graded-commutative: α ⌣ β = (−1)pq β ⌣ α.

Example

For the two-dimensional torus, each of the two one-dimensional classes corresponding to its circles squares to zero, but their cup product is the generator of H² — geometrically, the two circles intersect in a point. More generally, the cup product mirrors the intersection of the cycles that, by Poincaré duality, correspond to the classes: on a closed orientable n-manifold, the cup product pairing with the fundamental class reproduces intersection numbers.

Applications

Cohomology rings distinguish spaces that have identical cohomology groups: for instance, the ring structure tells apart the torus from a wedge of spheres, and complex projective space has a polynomial ring structure generated by a single class in degree 2. Related constructions include the cap product, Massey products, and the cup product in sheaf and group cohomology.
