Battle of Konya

The Battle of Konya was fought on 21 December 1832 near Konya in central Anatolia, during the Egyptian–Ottoman War of 1831–1833. The Egyptian army of Ibrahim Pasha, son of Muhammad Ali of Egypt, decisively defeated the Ottoman army of Grand Vizier Reşid Mehmed Pasha, opening the road to Constantinople and forcing the Ottoman Sultan to seek Russian protection.

Background

Muhammad Ali, the ambitious viceroy of Egypt, had demanded Syria as his price for aiding the Sultan against Greece. When refused, his son Ibrahim invaded Syria in 1831, taking Acre, Damascus, Aleppo and the passes into Anatolia after victories at Homs and Belen. Sultan Mahmud II sent the Grand Vizier Reşid Mehmed Pasha with an army of perhaps 50,000–60,000 men to stop Ibrahim's roughly 15,000–25,000 well-drilled Egyptian regulars at Konya.

The battle

The armies met on a foggy plain north of Konya. Ibrahim deployed his European-trained infantry, cavalry and artillery in coordinated formations, while a thick fog disrupted Ottoman movements. Egyptian artillery fire and disciplined infantry volleys broke the Ottoman attack; in the confusion, Reşid Mehmed Pasha rode into the Egyptian lines and was captured. Leaderless, the Ottoman army dissolved, losing several thousand killed and wounded and thousands more taken prisoner, against a few hundred Egyptian casualties.

Aftermath

With no army between Ibrahim and Constantinople, Mahmud II accepted Russian military aid under the ensuing Treaty of Hünkâr İskelesi (1833), alarming Britain and France. The Convention of Kütahya (May 1833) ended the war, leaving Muhammad Ali in control of Syria and Adana. Konya is regarded as the supreme demonstration of the superiority of Muhammad Ali's reformed army over the Sultan's forces, and it set the stage for a second Egyptian–Ottoman war in 1839.
