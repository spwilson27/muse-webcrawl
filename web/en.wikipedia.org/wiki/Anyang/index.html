Anyang (Chinese: 安阳; pinyin: Ānyáng) is a prefecture-level city in the north of Henan province, China, bordering Hebei and Shanxi. It is one of the cradles of Chinese civilisation: the nearby site of Yinxu, the ruins of Yin, the last capital of the Shang dynasty (c. 1300–1046 BC), lies within its territory and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Anyang is often counted among the Eight Ancient Capitals of China.

History

The area has been inhabited since the Neolithic period. Under the Shang king Pan Geng the capital was moved to Yin around 1300 BC, and it remained the Shang capital for some 255 years under twelve kings. Excavations at Yinxu, begun in 1928, uncovered palace-foundations, royal tombs, and vast numbers of oracle bones — inscribed ox scapulae and turtle plastrons bearing the earliest significant corpus of Chinese writing, the oracle bone script. The site also yielded the Houmuwu ding, the heaviest known ancient Chinese bronze vessel. In later centuries Anyang (historically Zhangde) was a regional centre and, in the modern era, an industrial city with steel and textile production.

Geography and administration

The prefecture-level city administers four districts, one county-level city (Linzhou), and four counties, covering about 7,400 km² at the eastern foot of the Taihang Mountains. The Huan River flows through the urban area. The region has a warm temperate monsoon climate with hot summers and cold, dry winters.
