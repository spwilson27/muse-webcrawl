Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan

The Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan, also known as the Constitution of 1973 (Urdu: آئینِ پاکستان), is the supreme law of Pakistan. Drafted by the National Assembly elected in 1970 and promulgated on 14 August 1973, it established Pakistan as a federal parliamentary republic with Islam as the state religion. It is the country's longest-lasting constitution, succeeding the abrogated constitutions of 1956 and 1962, though it has been suspended or held in abeyance during periods of military rule and amended many times.

History

Following the secession of East Pakistan (Bangladesh) in 1971, the government of Zulfikar Ali Bhutto convened a constitutional committee representing all parties. The resulting document, a rare consensus achievement, was passed unanimously in April 1973. The constitution was suspended in 1977 by General Zia-ul-Haq, restored in amended form in 1985, held in abeyance by General Pervez Musharraf in 1999, and restored again thereafter.

Main features

The constitution declares Islam the state religion and incorporates the Objectives Resolution of 1949 as a substantive article (Article 2-A). It provides for a bicameral federal legislature, a prime minister as head of government and a largely ceremonial president, an independent judiciary headed by the Supreme Court, and a chapter of fundamental rights (Articles 8–28). Urdu is the national language and English remained in official use; Islam is protected by the "Islamic provisions," including the Council of Islamic Ideology and the Federal Shariat Court (added later). The Fourth Schedule and Articles 141–159 divide legislative powers between the federation and the provinces.

Amendments

More than twenty amendments have been made. The Eighth Amendment (1985) validated martial-law measures and gave the president power to dissolve the National Assembly; the Thirteenth (1997) removed that power; the Seventeenth (2003) revived elements of Musharraf's Legal Framework Order; and the Eighteenth Amendment (2010) restored the parliamentary character of the constitution, renamed the North-West Frontier Province as Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and devolved wide powers to the provinces. The Twenty-fifth Amendment (2018) merged the Federally Administered Tribal Areas into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
