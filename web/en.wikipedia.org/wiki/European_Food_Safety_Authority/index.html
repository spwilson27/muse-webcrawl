European Food Safety Authority

Image: EFSA headquarters, Parma (placeholder)

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) is an agency of the European Union that provides independent scientific advice and communicates on existing and emerging risks associated with the food chain. It was established in 2002 under the General Food Law Regulation, following a series of food crises in the 1990s, including BSE. Its headquarters are in Parma, Italy.

Role

EFSA's scientific panels assess risks in areas such as food and feed safety, animal health and welfare, plant protection, nutrition, and genetically modified organisms. Its opinions inform EU legislation — for example, the approvals of pesticides, food additives, and novel foods — but EFSA itself makes no regulatory decisions; risk management is the responsibility of the European Commission and member states.

Structure and criticism

The authority employs several hundred staff and draws on a network of national experts. It has periodically faced criticism and debate over potential conflicts of interest of panel members and over controversial assessments, notably its evaluation of the herbicide glyphosate.
