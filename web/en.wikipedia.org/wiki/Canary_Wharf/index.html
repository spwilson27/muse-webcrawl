Canary Wharf is a privately owned financial district in London, England, located in the London Borough of Tower Hamlets on the Isle of Dogs in the West India Docks. Alongside the City of London, it is one of the main financial centres of the United Kingdom and the world, containing many high-rise buildings including the third-tallest in the UK, One Canada Square, which opened in 1991.

History

The West India Docks were once among the busiest in the Port of London, handling trade including imports from the Canary Islands — the origin of the name. The docks declined after the 1960s as container shipping moved downstream, and closed in 1980. The area was designated an enterprise zone in 1981, and the London Docklands Development Corporation promoted its redevelopment.

Construction of the office district began in 1988 under the Canadian developers Olympia & York, led by the Reichmann family. The project's first phase was hit by the early-1990s property crash, and Olympia & York entered administration in 1992, but Canary Wharf recovered and expanded rapidly in the late 1990s as major banks relocated from the City. Over 300,000 square metres of retail space and more than 16 million square feet of offices now occupy the site, with around 120,000 people working there.

Transport

Canary Wharf is served by the Jubilee line station of the same name, the Docklands Light Railway (Heron Quays and Canary Wharf stations), and the Elizabeth line station which opened in 2022.
