Soweto (/səˈwɛtoʊ, -ˈweɪt-, -ˈwiːt-/) is a township of the City of Johannesburg Metropolitan Municipality in Gauteng, South Africa, bordering the city's mining belt in the south. Its name is an English syllabic abbreviation for South Western Townships, and it is home to more than a million people, making it one of the largest Black urban settlements in Africa.

History

Black South Africans were first removed to the area south-west of Johannesburg in the early twentieth century, after the outbreak of bubonic plague in the inner-city suburb of Newtown led to clearances in 1904. Successive settlements — Klipspruit, Pimville, Orlando (1932), Moroka, Meadowlands and others — were built as dormitory townships for Black labour under segregation and, after 1948, apartheid. In 1963 the separate townships were administratively united under the name Soweto.

Soweto became a byword for resistance to apartheid. On 16 June 1976, thousands of school students marched in protest against a decree making Afrikaans a medium of instruction; police opened fire, killing a number of students including 13-year-old Hector Pieterson, whose photograph — carried in the arms of Mbuyisa Makhubo, with his sister Antoinette at his side — became an icon of the struggle. The Soweto uprising spread across the country and marked a turning point in the anti-apartheid movement. 16 June is now commemorated as Youth Day, a South African public holiday.

Soweto today

Since the end of apartheid, Soweto has seen major development, with shopping centres such as Maponya Mall, improved infrastructure, and a growing middle class, though unemployment and inequality remain severe. Landmarks include Vilakazi Street in Orlando West — the only street in the world to have housed two Nobel Peace Prize laureates, Nelson Mandela and Archbishop Desmond Tutu — the Hector Pieterson Memorial and Museum, Regina Mundi church, the Chris Hani Baragwanath Hospital (one of the largest hospitals in the world) and the Orlando Towers, painted cooling towers of a decommissioned power station.

Soweto is also a cradle of South African popular culture, from kwaito music and the township jazz of earlier decades to the football clubs Kaizer Chiefs and Orlando Pirates, whose derby is one of the most watched fixtures in African football.
