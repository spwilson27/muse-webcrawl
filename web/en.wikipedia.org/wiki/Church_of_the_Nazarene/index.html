Church of the Nazarene

A typical Nazarene church building.

The Church of the Nazarene is an evangelical Christian denomination that emerged in North America from the nineteenth-century Wesleyan-Holiness movement and Methodist, Presbyterian, Congregationalist, and Brethren traditions. It is the largest denomination in the Wesleyan-Holiness tradition and one of the largest international Holiness bodies, reporting roughly 2.6–2.7 million members in about 30,000 congregations in more than 160 world areas. Its global headquarters are in Lenexa, Kansas, United States.

History

The church was formed by a series of mergers. Phineas F. Bresee founded the first Church of the Nazarene in Los Angeles in 1895, choosing the name because he believed the name "Nazarene" represented Christ's identification with the poor and lowly. In 1907 and 1908 the Los Angeles body united with groups in the eastern United States (the Association of Pentecostal Churches of America) and the south (the Holiness Church of Christ) at assemblies in Chicago and Pilot Point, Texas; 1908 is commonly taken as the denomination's founding year. Further mergers and international expansion followed throughout the twentieth century.

Beliefs

The Church of the Nazarene identifies its primary mission as making "Christlike disciples in the nations". Its theology is Arminian and Wesleyan: it teaches sixteen Articles of Faith, emphasising prevenient grace, justification by faith, and the doctrine of entire sanctification — a second work of grace, also called Christian holiness, in which the believer is cleansed from original sin and filled with perfect love. The church practises two sacraments, baptism (by sprinkling, pouring, or immersion) and the Lord's Supper, and licenses and ordains women as well as men as ministers.

Organisation

Governance is a blend of episcopal and congregational polity: local churches call their own pastors, districts are overseen by elected superintendents, and the whole body is led by a Board of General Superintendents elected by the quadrennial General Assembly. The denomination supports universities and colleges in several countries, the Nazarene Publishing House, and extensive missionary and compassionate ministries.
