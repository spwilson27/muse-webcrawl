The Governor of Tokyo (東京都知事, Tōkyō-to chiji) is the head of government of Tokyo Metropolis, Japan's capital prefecture, home to roughly 14 million residents in its 23 special wards, cities, towns and villages. The governor heads the Tokyo Metropolitan Government, one of the largest subnational administrations in the world, with a budget comparable to that of a mid-sized nation state.

Role and powers

The governor is directly elected by Tokyo residents for a four-year term, with no limit on the number of terms. As chief executive, the governor submits budgets and ordinances to the Tokyo Metropolitan Assembly, appoints senior officials (including vice governors, subject to assembly consent), and oversees metropolitan services: waterworks, sewerage, transport (including the Toei subway and bus networks), hospitals, fire and disaster management (through the Tokyo Fire Department), and urban development. The governor also plays a central role in Tokyo's hosting of national and international events, notably the 1964 and 2020 Summer Olympic and Paralympic Games.

History

Tokyo City and Tokyo Prefecture were merged into Tokyo Metropolis (Tokyo-to) in 1943, during the Second World War, with an appointed governor-general (chōkan) at its head. Under the post-war Local Autonomy Law, the office became elective, and Seiichirō Yasui won the first gubernatorial election in 1947. The long 1967–1979 tenure of Ryōkichi Minobe, a socialist-backed reformist, brought free medical care for the elderly and aggressive pollution controls. Shunichirō Suzuki served four terms (1979–1995), presiding over the bubble economy and the construction of the Shinjuku government complex.

Novelist Yukio Aoshima (1995–1999) won on a promise to cancel a costly world city exposition, and Shintarō Ishihara (1999–2012), another novelist and former cabinet minister, led Tokyo's unsuccessful 2016 Olympic bid and its successful 2020 bid before resigning to enter national politics. After the short, scandal-shortened terms of Naoki Inose (2012–2013) and Yōichi Masuzoe (2014–2016), Yuriko Koike, a former defence minister, was elected in July 2016 as Tokyo's first woman governor, winning re-election in 2020 and 2024; she led the metropolitan response to the COVID-19 pandemic and the postponed Tokyo 2020 Games.
