Ethiopian Rift Valley lakes

The Ethiopian Rift Valley lakes are a group of lakes in the Great Rift Valley of Ethiopia, lying in the basin formed between the Ethiopian Highlands to the north and the Somali Plateau to the southeast. They are the northernmost of the African Rift Valley lakes and occupy the floor of the Ethiopian Rift, which is slowly splitting the African Plate into the Nubian and Somali plates.

The lakes

From north to south the principal lakes are:

  • Lake Ziway — a freshwater lake fed by the Meki and Katar rivers
  • Lake Shala — the deepest of the group, an alkaline lake within Abijatta-Shala National Park
  • Lake Abijatta — a shallow alkaline lake famous for its flamingos
  • Lake Langano — a brown-water lake popular for recreation, free of schistosomiasis
  • Lake Hawassa (Awasa) — a freshwater lake beside the city of Hawassa
  • Lake Abaya — the largest of the Rift Valley lakes by surface area
  • Lake Chamo — known for its large Nile crocodiles and hippos

Further south the basin continues toward Lake Turkana on the Kenyan border. Unlike the lakes of the Western Rift, most Ethiopian Rift lakes have no outlet; their waters are consequently alkaline to varying degrees, though Ziway, Langano and Hawassa are comparatively fresh.

Ecology

The lakes support rich birdlife, including great white pelicans and vast flocks of lesser and greater flamingos, and fisheries based on tilapia and catfish. Several are protected within national parks, notably Abijatta-Shala and Nechisar. Water abstraction for irrigation, soda ash extraction at Lake Abijatta, and shoreline development have placed increasing pressure on lake levels and ecosystems.
