Birthday Honours

Hero image: an investiture at Buckingham Palace, where Birthday Honours recipients are presented with their awards.

The Birthday Honours, in some Commonwealth realms officially called the King's Birthday Honours, are part of the British honours system awarded by the monarch on the celebration of their official birthday. The honours are published in the London Gazette and awarded primarily in the United Kingdom and in Commonwealth realms that retain the British honours system, with ceremonies and lists traditionally released in June.

Practice

Birthday Honours have been awarded since 1860, during the reign of Queen Victoria; the custom became annual from 1885 under the authority of the sovereign's official birthday. (British monarchs with winter birthdays adopted an "official birthday" in summer; since 1974 it has been fixed to a Saturday in June, marked by Trooping the Colour.) The lists are drawn up twice yearly alongside the New Year Honours, following nominations reviewed by honours committees and submitted through the Prime Minister and, for realm lists, through governors-general.

Content of the lists

Recipients include civil servants, military personnel, charity workers, community volunteers, and figures from public life, arts, sport, and business. Awards range from life peerages and knighthoods (KBE/DBE) through appointments to the Order of the British Empire (CBE, OBE, MBE) and the British Empire Medal, which was revived in 2012 for local community service. Gallantry and service medals may also appear. In several realms — including Australia (since 1975) and New Zealand — parallel national orders now supply most Birthday Honours, with Australians celebrating the King's Birthday in June with their own list.

Refusals and controversies

Some nominees decline honours for political or personal reasons; the list of known refusers includes figures from L. S. Lowry to David Bowie. The system has periodically faced criticism over honours perceived as rewards for political donors or establishment figures, prompting reforms to emphasize community and voluntary service.
