Lex fori (Latin: "the law of the forum") is a concept in the conflict of laws: the law of the jurisdiction in which a legal action is brought. When a court hears a case with foreign elements, matters of procedure are generally governed by the lex fori, even where the substantive rights of the parties are governed by a foreign law (the lex causae).

Scope

The traditional rule is that the lex fori governs such matters as the mode of trial, admissibility and manner of proof in practice, remedies and their enforcement, limitation periods (historically treated as procedural in common-law systems, though many jurisdictions now treat them as substantive by statute), and the form of the judgment. The maxim reflects both practicality — a court cannot readily operate foreign procedures — and the public nature of court administration.

Characterisation problems

The distinction between substance and procedure is itself decided by the lex fori, a process called characterisation. Courts sometimes recharacterise apparently procedural rules (for example, a foreign rule extinguishing a right rather than merely barring the remedy) to avoid results that would defeat the applicable foreign substantive law.
