The Master is a 2012 American psychological drama film written and directed by Paul Thomas Anderson. It stars Joaquin Phoenix as Freddie Quell, a traumatised World War II naval veteran struggling to adjust to post-war society, who meets Lancaster Dodd (Philip Seymour Hoffman), the charismatic leader of a quasi-religious movement called "The Cause". Amy Adams plays Dodd's formidable wife, Peggy.

Plot

Freddie Quell, an alcoholic drifter who brews toxic moonshine, drifts from job to job after the war until he stows away on the yacht of Lancaster Dodd, founder of The Cause, a movement based on therapies that purport to recover memories of past lives spanning trillions of years. Dodd takes Freddie as a protégé and subjects him to "processing", including an intense, unblinking interrogation in which Freddie confesses painful truths. As Freddie becomes both enforcer and intimate of the movement, doubts about Dodd's claims—championed by sceptics and by Peggy's tight control—culminate in Freddie's departure and a final, ambiguous farewell between the two men in England.

Production

The film was Anderson's first since There Will Be Blood (2007) and was partly inspired by stories of L. Ron Hubbard and the early days of Scientology, though Anderson described the relationship as loose and emphasised that the film is primarily about post-war America and veterans. It was shot largely on 65mm film, giving it an unusually rich, period-appropriate image, and features a score by Jonny Greenwood dominated by strings and big-band-era arrangements.

Reception

The film premiered at the Venice Film Festival, where Phoenix and Hoffman shared the Volpi Cup for Best Actor and Anderson won the Silver Lion for Best Director. Critics praised its performances and cinematography, and at the 85th Academy Awards the three leads received acting nominations: Phoenix for Best Actor, Hoffman for Best Supporting Actor, and Adams for Best Supporting Actress. Despite modest box-office returns, The Master has been ranked among the greatest films of the 2010s and of the 21st century in polls by critics and publications.
