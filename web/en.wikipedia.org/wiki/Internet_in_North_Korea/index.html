Internet in North Korea


Country: North Korea (DPRK)
ccTLD: .kp
Domestic network: Kwangmyong

The Internet in North Korea is among the most restricted in the world. Access to the global Internet is limited to a small number of senior officials, researchers, and foreign residents with special permission, while the general population instead uses Kwangmyong, a state-run domestic intranet established around 2000 that offers email, news, and approved websites hosted inside the country.

Access and infrastructure

North Korea's international connectivity has historically run through a small number of links, including a connection via China Unicom and, from 2017, an additional link through the Russian provider TransTelekom. The country's .kp domain, delegated in 2007, hosts only a few dozen websites, including the official KCNA news agency and the Naenara portal. Broadband and mobile data are largely unavailable to ordinary citizens; the domestic mobile network Koryolink provides limited intranet services to subscribers.

Kwangmyong

Kwangmyong ("Bright Star") is accessible from universities, libraries, government offices, and some public institutions. It is estimated to host between roughly 1,000 and 5,500 websites, including university pages such as that of Kim Il Sung University, science and technology libraries, and state media. Users access it through computers running the Red Star OS operating system, and content is filtered and monitored by state authorities.

Regulation and surveillance

Unauthorized access to foreign media or the global Internet is a serious offence. Devices sold in North Korea are registered, and operating systems are designed to watermark and track files. Despite the restrictions, a small elite in Pyongyang, foreign diplomats, and aid workers have comparatively open access, and some North Koreans near the Chinese border illicitly connect to Chinese mobile networks.
