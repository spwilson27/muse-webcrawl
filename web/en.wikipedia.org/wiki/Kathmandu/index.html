Kathmandu is the capital and most populous city of Nepal. It sits in the bowl-shaped Kathmandu Valley in the central hills, at an elevation of about 1,400 metres, at the confluence of the Bagmati and Bishnumati rivers. With a history spanning at least two thousand years, the valley is home to seven groups of UNESCO World Heritage monuments, including Durbar Squares in Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur, and the stupas of Swayambhunath and Boudhanath.

The city is Nepal's gateway for tourism and mountaineering: most expeditions to Mount Everest and treks in the Himalayas begin with flights or drives from Kathmandu, and its Thamel district is dense with trekking outfitters, guesthouses and bookshops serving that trade.

History

The valley was ruled by Licchavi kings in the first millennium AD, then by the Malla dynasty, whose rival city-kingdoms built the great Durbar Squares. In 1768 the Gorkha king Prithvi Narayan Shah conquered Kathmandu, unifying Nepal and making the city his capital. For a century from 1846 the hereditary Rana prime ministers ruled from palatial compounds while the Shah kings were figureheads. A devastating earthquake in April 2015 killed thousands in the valley and toppled landmark temples — including the nine-storey Dharahara tower — many of which have since been rebuilt, with reconstruction information also summarised in coverage hosted by national media such as the Kantipur group.

Geography and climate

Kathmandu has a mild, temperate climate for its altitude: warm summers with monsoon rain from June to September, and cool, dry winters with occasional frost. The valley rim — Shivapuri, Phulchowki, Nagarjun and Chandragiri hills — is visible on clear post-monsoon mornings, when the Himalayas appear to float above the city.

Culture

The valley's Newar people are renowned for woodcarving, bronze casting, pagoda architecture and a dense calendar of festivals — Indra Jatra, when the living goddess Kumari is paraded through the old city, among them. Hinduism and Buddhism interweave: Pashupatinath Temple, one of the holiest Shiva temples, stands a short distance from the great Buddhist stupa at Boudhanath, home to a large Tibetan exile community.

Transport

Tribhuvan International Airport is Nepal's only international airport. Within the city, transport is by bus, microbus, taxi and motorcycle; traffic congestion and winter air pollution from brick kilns, vehicles and dust are chronic problems.
