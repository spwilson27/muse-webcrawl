B vitamins

B vitamins are a class of water-soluble vitamins that play important roles in cell metabolism and the synthesis of red blood cells. Though these vitamins share similar names, they are chemically distinct compounds that often coexist in the same foods. Generally, dietary supplements containing all eight are referred to as a vitamin B complex.

The eight B vitamins

B2RiboflavinPrecursor of FAD and FMN
B3NiacinPrecursor of NAD and NADP; deficiency causes pellagra
B5Pantothenic acidComponent of coenzyme A
B6Pyridoxine (group)Coenzyme in amino-acid metabolism and neurotransmitter synthesis
B7BiotinCoenzyme for carboxylases
B9FolateOne-carbon metabolism; deficiency causes neural tube defects and megaloblastic anaemia
B12CobalaminRed-cell formation and nervous system function; deficiency causes pernicious anaemia

The numbering gaps reflect substances once thought to be vitamins — such as "vitamin B4" (adenine) and "B8" (inositol) — that were later found not to be essential nutrients because the body synthesises them or they are not required in the diet.

Sources and deficiency

B vitamins are widely distributed in whole grains, meat, eggs, dairy products, legumes, seeds, nuts, and dark leafy vegetables. Because they are water-soluble they are not stored to a great extent (vitamin B12, stored in the liver, is an exception), and deficiency states can develop within weeks to months of inadequate intake. Individual B-vitamin deficiencies produce distinctive syndromes and are covered in the articles for each vitamin.
