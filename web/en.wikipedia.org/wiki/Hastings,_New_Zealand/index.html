Hastings, New Zealand

Hastings (Māori: Heretaunga) is an inland city in the Hawke's Bay region of New Zealand's North Island. It is one of two major urban centres in Hawke's Bay, together with the coastal port city of Napier, about 18 kilometres to the northeast; the two are often called "the Twin Cities". Hastings is the administrative centre of Hastings District and has an urban population of roughly 50,000 (district population about 90,000).

Geography and economy

The city sits on the fertile alluvial Heretaunga Plains, one of New Zealand's premier food-producing areas. Its warm, dry, Mediterranean-like climate supports extensive orchards, vineyards and wineries; the Gimblett Gravels sub-region is noted for red wines. Food processing — including Wattie's, founded in Hastings in 1934 — is a major employer. The surrounding district contains the settlements of Havelock North and Flaxmere.

History

The Heretaunga area was settled by Māori for centuries before European purchase in the 1860s–70s; the town was founded in 1873 and named after Warren Hastings, first Governor-General of India. The 1931 Hawke's Bay earthquake devastated Hastings and Napier, and Hastings was rebuilt partly in Spanish Mission and Art Deco styles, giving the city centre notable interwar architecture. Attractions include the Hawke's Bay Opera House, the municipal theatre precinct and Splash Planet water park, successor to the earlier Fantasy Land.
