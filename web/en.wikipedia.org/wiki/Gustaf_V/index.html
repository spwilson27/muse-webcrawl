Gustaf V

Gustaf V (Oscar Gustaf Adolf; 16 June 1858 – 29 October 1950) was King of Sweden from 1907 until his death in 1950. He was the eldest son of Oscar II and Sophia of Nassau, and the last Swedish monarch to exercise substantial direct political influence, intervening in the defence debate of 1914 (the Courtyard Speech) and in foreign policy during both world wars.

His long reign oversaw Sweden's definitive transition to parliamentary democracy — after the 1917 elections he accepted a liberal-social democratic government — and he kept Sweden neutral through both world wars, meeting the leaders of Denmark and Norway at Malmö in 1914 and again at the three-kings meetings. Gustaf V was an accomplished tennis player, competing under the pseudonym "Mr G", and helped popularise the sport in Sweden. He was succeeded by his son, Gustaf VI Adolf.
