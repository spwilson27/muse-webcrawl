Jephthah

Jephthah was a judge of Israel in the Book of Judges (chapters 11–12), a military leader of the tribe of Gilead who delivered Israel from the Ammonites.

Story

Jephthah, the son of Gilead and a prostitute, was expelled by his half-brothers but recalled to lead the fight against Ammon. Before battle he vowed that if victorious he would sacrifice whatever first came out of his house to meet him — and his only child, his daughter, came out dancing. The text says he fulfilled his vow, and Israelite women commemorated her four days a year.

Interpretation

The story has been widely discussed by scholars and theologians, who debate whether the vow was fulfilled literally, whether the daughter was devoted rather than killed, and what it reveals about ancient vows. See also Book of Judges.
