Lady Margaret Hall (LMH) is one of the constituent colleges of the University of Oxford in England, located on a bank of the River Cherwell at the end of Norham Gardens in north Oxford. The college was founded in 1878 as the first women's college in Oxford, closely associated with the Church of England; in 1979 it began admitting men as well. It is named after Lady Margaret Beaufort, patron of scholarship and mother of Henry VII.

History

The college was founded in 1878 by Edward Stuart Talbot, Warden of Keble College, and his wife Lavinia, opening in October 1879 with its first principal, Elizabeth Wordsworth, great-niece of the poet William Wordsworth. The first nine students lived in a house at 38 Norham Gardens; the college soon grew, acquiring its present riverside site and building its neo-Jacobean main buildings, designed by Basil Champneys.

LMH became a full college of the university in 1960. Men were first admitted in 1979. In 2016 the college established a Foundation Year for students from disadvantaged backgrounds, the first scheme of its kind at Oxford or Cambridge, and it has a particular reputation for access and outreach work.

Grounds and buildings

The college occupies an unusually spacious site of about 12 acres stretching down to the River Cherwell, with extensive gardens, a fellows' garden and meadow, and punt moorings. Buildings include the original Old Old Hall, Wolfson Quadrangle, the Leatare Quadrangle, and the chapel, designed by Giles Gilbert Scott, consecrated in 1933. The library holds around 90,000 volumes.

Student life and people

LMH has an undergraduate and graduate community of about 650 students. Notable alumni include the authors Antonia Fraser and Eglantyne Jebb (founder of Save the Children), the former prime minister of Pakistan Benazir Bhutto, the broadcaster Zeinab Badawi, the writer Sarah Waters and the geneticist Richard Gardner. Among its fellows and principals have been the theologian and novelist C. S. Lewis's contemporary Elizabeth Wordsworth, and the economist and principal Frances Lannon.
