Hull speed

Hull speed
QuantitySpeed limit of a displacement hull
Depends onWaterline length
Typical formula1.34 × √(waterline length in feet) knots

Hull speed or displacement speed is the speed at which the wavelength of a vessel's bow wave is equal to the waterline length of the vessel. As a boat accelerates, the bow wave grows and the hull settles between the bow and stern wave crests; at hull speed the boat becomes effectively trapped in the trough of its own wave system, so that greatly increased power yields only marginal increases in speed unless the hull is driven up onto a plane or is very slender.

Formula and theory

The classic rule of thumb is:

Hull speed (knots) ≈ 1.34 × √LWL (feet) ≈ 2.43 × √LWL (metres)

The limit arises because the speed of a deep-water wave depends on its wavelength: a wave travels at about 1.34 × √(wavelength in feet) knots. When the bow wave's wavelength reaches the waterline length, the stern falls into the bow wave's trough. In modern naval architecture the concept is expressed through the Froude number; hull speed corresponds to a Froude number of about 0.4. Multihulls, very narrow hulls, and planing and semi-displacement designs can substantially exceed the nominal limit, which is why the simple formula is treated as an approximation rather than a hard physical barrier.
