StatusInvestigational; prohibited in sport (WADA)

Ligandrol, also known as LGD-4033 and developmentally as VK5211, is a nonsteroidal selective androgen receptor modulator (SARM) investigated for the treatment of muscle wasting, osteoporosis and cancer cachexia. It was originally developed by Ligand Pharmaceuticals and later licensed to Viking Therapeutics.

Pharmacology

Ligandrol binds the androgen receptor with high affinity and selectivity, producing anabolic effects in muscle and bone with reduced activity in androgen-sensitive tissues such as the prostate compared with testosterone. In phase I trials it increased lean body mass dose-dependently in healthy volunteers and was generally well tolerated, though it suppressed endogenous testosterone and HDL cholesterol.

Clinical development

A phase II trial in patients recovering from hip fracture reported gains in lean body mass. The drug has not been approved for any medical use by any regulator, and its long-term safety is unknown.

Misuse in sport

Despite its investigational status, ligandrol is widely sold online as a "research chemical" and bodybuilding supplement. It is prohibited by the World Anti-Doping Agency as an anabolic agent, and a number of athletes have been sanctioned after testing positive for it, sometimes attributing the result to contaminated supplements. Regulators including the US FDA have warned of risks including liver toxicity, heart attack and stroke.
