Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry

Active: 1914–present
Country: Canada
Branch: Canadian Army
Type: Line infantry
Nickname: "The Patricias", "VP"
Colonel-in-Chief: (historically) Countess Mountbatten

Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (PPCLI, generally referred to as the Patricias) is a Canadian Army infantry regiment, one of three Regular Force infantry regiments. It was founded in 1914 by Captain Andrew Charles Hamilton Gault at the outbreak of the First World War, and named for Princess Patricia of Connaught, daughter of the Governor General.

First World War

The regiment was raised largely at Gault's own expense and recruited experienced soldiers, many of them British-born veterans. It was the first Canadian infantry unit to reach France, landing in 1914–15, and fought at Frezenberg, Mount Sorrel, the Somme, Vimy Ridge and Passchendaele. The originals were nearly wiped out at Frezenberg in May 1915, where the regiment held its ground at heavy cost.

Later service

The PPCLI served in the Second World War in Sicily, Italy and Northwest Europe; in Korea at the Battle of Kapyong (1951), where its stand earned a United States Presidential Unit Citation; in Cyprus and the Balkans on peacekeeping duties; and in Afghanistan, where Corporal Rob Furlong's record sniper shot in 2002 was made by a member of 3 PPCLI. The regiment's home station is Edmonton, Alberta.
