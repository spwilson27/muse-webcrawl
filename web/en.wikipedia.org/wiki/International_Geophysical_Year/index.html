International Geophysical Year

IGY
Dates: 1 July 1957 – 31 December 1958
Participants: 67 countries
Fields: Earth science, space science

The International Geophysical Year (IGY) was an international scientific project that lasted from 1 July 1957 to 31 December 1958. It marked the end of a long period during the Cold War when scientific interchange between East and West had been seriously curtailed, and involved some 67 countries in coordinated study of the Earth and its environment.

Activities and discoveries

IGY activities spanned eleven Earth science disciplines, including aurora, cosmic rays, geomagnetism, glaciology, gravity, meteorology, oceanography, seismology and solar activity. The programme confirmed the existence of mid-ocean ridges, advanced understanding of Antarctica — research stations were established across the continent — and led directly to the Antarctic Treaty of 1959.

Satellites

Both superpowers announced plans to launch artificial satellites for the IGY. The Soviet Union surprised the world with Sputnik 1 in October 1957, and the United States followed with Explorer 1 in January 1958, whose data led to the discovery of the Van Allen radiation belt. The IGY thus opened the Space Age.
