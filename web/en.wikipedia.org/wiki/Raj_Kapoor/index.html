Raj Kapoor

Raj Kapoor (born Shrishti Nath Kapoor; 14 December 1924 – 2 June 1988) was an Indian actor, film director and producer, regarded as one of the greatest and most influential figures in the history of Indian cinema. Often referred to as the "greatest showman of Indian cinema" and the "Charlie Chaplin of Indian cinema", Kapoor's films were noted for their striking visuals, emotional depth and memorable music, and he won immense international popularity, particularly in the Soviet Union and the Middle East.

Early life

Raj Kapoor was born in Peshawar (now in Pakistan) into the Kapoor family, the eldest son of the actor Prithviraj Kapoor. As a child he appeared in films, making his debut in Inquilab (1935). He worked as an assistant and clapper boy at Bombay Talkies and later at his father's Prithvi Theatre before receiving his first lead role in Neel Kamal (1947) opposite Madhubala.

Career

In 1948 Kapoor founded his own studio, R. K. Films, and directed its first production, Aag. His breakthrough came with Barsaat (1949), and Awaara (1951) established his enduring screen persona: the lovable tramp, modelled partly on Charlie Chaplin's Little Tramp, who combined innocence, humour and social conscience. The title song "Awaara Hoon" became hugely popular across Asia, Africa and the Soviet Union, where Kapoor was adored by millions.

Through the 1950s and 1960s he starred in and directed a string of classics, including Shree 420 (1955), Chori Chori (1956), Jagte Raho (1956) and Jis Desh Mein Ganga Behti Hai (1960). His on-screen pairing with actress Nargis in sixteen films became legendary. His ambitious magnum opus Mera Naam Joker (1970) was a commercial failure on release but was later re-evaluated as a classic. Kapoor bounced back as a director with the teenage romance Bobby (1973), which launched his son Rishi Kapoor, and continued with Satyam Shivam Sundaram (1978), Prem Rog (1982) and Ram Teri Ganga Maili (1985).

Death and legacy

Kapoor suffered from asthma in his later years and died in New Delhi on 2 June 1988, shortly after receiving the Dadasaheb Phalke Award, Indian cinema's highest honour. He had earlier been awarded the Padma Bhushan in 1971. His sons Randhir, Rishi and Rajiv Kapoor became actors, and his grandchildren include stars Karisma Kapoor, Kareena Kapoor and Ranbir Kapoor, making the Kapoors one of Indian cinema's most enduring dynasties.
