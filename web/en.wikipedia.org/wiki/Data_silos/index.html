Data silos

A data silo is a repository of fixed data that remains under the control of one department of an organization and is isolated from the rest of the organization, much like grain in a farm silo is closed off from the rest of the farm. Data silos arise when departments or business units acquire and manage their own information systems without coordination, so that data cannot easily be shared or combined across the organization.

Causes

Silos commonly result from organizational structure, differing priorities between departments, mergers and acquisitions that bring incompatible systems together, and the use of separate software applications that store data in proprietary formats.

Problems

Data silos lead to duplicated and inconsistent data, incomplete views of customers and operations, difficulty in producing organization-wide reports and analytics, and wasted storage and licensing costs. They are a frequently cited obstacle to digital transformation, data-driven decision-making, and unified customer experience initiatives.

Remedies

Approaches to breaking down data silos include enterprise data warehouses and data lakes, application programming interfaces (APIs) and integration platforms, master data management, common data governance policies, and cloud-based data platforms that centralize access while leaving operational systems in place.
