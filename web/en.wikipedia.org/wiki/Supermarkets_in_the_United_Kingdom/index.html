Supermarkets in the United Kingdom

Supermarkets account for the great majority of grocery sales in the United Kingdom. The market is dominated by a small number of large chains, historically led by Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, and Morrisons — collectively long known as the "big four".

Market structure

Tesco became the largest UK retailer after its growth in the 1990s and holds roughly a quarter of the grocery market. Since the 2000s the German discount chains Aldi and Lidl have expanded rapidly, overtaking Morrisons in market share, while premium grocers such as Waitrose and Marks & Spencer serve the higher end of the market. Co-op operates a large network of smaller convenience stores.

History

Self-service grocery stores spread in Britain after the Second World War; Sainsbury's opened its first self-service store in Croydon in 1950, and purpose-built supermarkets proliferated from the 1960s as car ownership and out-of-town shopping centres grew. Online grocery delivery, pioneered in Britain by firms such as Ocado, later became a significant channel.
