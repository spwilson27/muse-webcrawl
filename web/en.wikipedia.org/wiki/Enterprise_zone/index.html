Enterprise zone

An enterprise zone is a designated geographic area in which governments offer tax incentives, reduced regulation and sometimes infrastructure support to encourage business investment and job creation in economically distressed communities. The concept was popularised in the late 1970s and 1980s and has been adopted, under various names, in the United Kingdom, the United States, France and elsewhere.

Origins

The idea is usually credited to the British geographer Peter Hall, who in 1977 proposed "freeport" experiments modelled loosely on Hong Kong for derelict inner-city districts. Chancellor Geoffrey Howe championed the scheme, and the Local Government, Planning and Land Act 1980 enabled the first UK enterprise zones — among them Swansea, Gateshead and Corby — offering ten years of rate relief, exemption from development land tax and simplified planning. US congressman Jack Kemp and others promoted a parallel American version, and by the 1990s most US states had zone programmes; the federal government created "empowerment zones" in 1993 and, later, "opportunity zones" in 2017.

Typical incentives

Zone benefits commonly include property tax abatements, credits against income or payroll taxes for hiring local residents, sales tax exemptions on equipment, capital allowances, and fast-tracked planning or permitting. Programmes differ in whether they target places, businesses or people, and in the scale of their areas, from single industrial estates to whole city districts.

Evaluation and criticism

Economists remain divided on effectiveness. Studies find that zones often displace activity from neighbouring areas rather than creating wholly new jobs, that much of the benefit accrues to landowners and firms that would have invested anyway, and that costs per job created can be high. Supporters counter that zones can tip location decisions, accelerate regeneration of derelict land and signal long-term public commitment to neglected areas. The UK revived the policy in 2011 with a new generation of enterprise zones focused on sector clusters and business-rate retention.
