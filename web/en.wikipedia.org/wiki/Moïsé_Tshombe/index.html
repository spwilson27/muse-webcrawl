Moïse Kapenda Tshombe (November 10, 1919 – June 29, 1969) was a Congolese businessman and politician. He served as president of the secessionist State of Katanga from 1960 to 1963 and later, briefly, as prime minister of the whole Republic of the Congo.

Katanga secession

Tshombe founded the CONAKAT party, representing the interests of Katanga province and its Lunda people. Days after Congolese independence on June 30, 1960, he declared mineral-rich Katanga independent, with tacit Belgian support and Belgian troops on the ground. The secession precipitated the Congo Crisis and a United Nations military intervention. Patrice Lumumba, the Congo's first prime minister, was transferred to Katanga and murdered there in January 1961, an act for which Tshombe's government bore responsibility. UN forces ended the secession in January 1963 and Tshombe went into exile in Spain.

Prime minister and death

In 1964 he returned as prime minister of a unified Congo, employing white mercenaries to suppress the Simba rebellion, but was dismissed by President Joseph Kasa-Vubu in October 1965. After Mobutu's coup he was sentenced to death in absentia for treason; in 1967 his aircraft was hijacked to Algeria, where he remained under house arrest until his death, officially of a heart attack, in 1969.
