Law of total variance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In probability theory, the law of total variance or variance decomposition formula, also known as Eve's law, states that if X and Y are random variables on the same probability space, and the variance of Y is finite, then the variance of Y can be decomposed into the expected value of the conditional variance and the variance of the conditional expectation:

Var(Y) = E[Var(Y | X)] + Var(E[Y | X])

Interpretation

The first term, the expected conditional variance, measures the average spread of Y within groups defined by X (the "unexplained" or within-group variance); the second term, the variance of the conditional expectation, measures how much the group means vary (the "explained" or between-group variance). The decomposition underlies analysis of variance (ANOVA) and the notion of explained variation.

Proof

The result follows by expanding the conditional variance and conditional expectation using the law of total expectation: Var(Y) = E[Y²] − (E[Y])², and conditioning each term on X yields the two components, with cross-terms cancelling.
