Trial balance

A trial balance is a list of all the general ledger accounts contained in the ledger of a business. The list includes the name of the nominal ledger account and the value of that nominal ledger balance at a particular point in time, presented as either a debit or a credit balance. The main purpose of preparing a trial balance is to demonstrate that the total debits equal the total credits in the recording process.

Purpose

Under double-entry bookkeeping, every transaction is recorded with equal debits and credits, so the sum of all debit balances must equal the sum of all credit balances. If the totals differ, an error has been made somewhere in the recording process. The trial balance is also the first step toward preparing the financial statements — the balance sheet and income statement — because it gathers all account balances in one place.

Example

AccountDebitCredit
Cash15,000
Accounts receivable8,500
Accounts payable6,000
Owner's capital17,500
Total23,50023,500

Limitations

A balanced trial balance does not prove that the accounts are correct. Several classes of error leave the totals equal: errors of omission (a transaction never recorded), errors of commission (posted to the wrong account of the same class), errors of principle, compensating errors, and complete reversals of entries. Such mistakes are found only by checking individual entries.
