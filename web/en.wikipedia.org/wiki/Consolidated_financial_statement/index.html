Consolidated financial statement

Consolidation combines a parent and its subsidiaries into one set of accounts.

Consolidated financial statements are the financial statements of a group of entities presented as those of a single economic entity. They are prepared by a parent company that controls one or more subsidiaries, combining the assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses of the parent and its subsidiaries after eliminating intra-group balances and transactions.

Under International Financial Reporting Standards, IFRS 10 requires a parent to consolidate entities it controls, where control means power over the investee, exposure to variable returns, and the ability to use that power to affect returns. Under U.S. GAAP, ASC 810 provides similar consolidation guidance, including the variable interest entity model. Ownership of more than 50% of a subsidiary's voting shares is the usual indicator of control.

Presentation

A consolidated statement of financial position and statement of profit or loss present the group's results as a whole; the portion of equity and profit attributable to outside shareholders is shown as non-controlling interest. Subsidiaries' accounts are adjusted to uniform accounting policies and reporting dates, and goodwill arising on acquisition is recognised under the acquisition method.
