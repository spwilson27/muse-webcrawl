Centennial Hall

The Centennial Hall (Polish: Hala Stulecia; German: Jahrhunderthalle) is a historic building in Wrocław, Poland. It was constructed according to the plans of architect Max Berg in 1911–1913, when the city was part of the German Empire, to commemorate the centennial of the 1813 Battle of Leipzig and the Wars of Liberation against Napoleon.

Architecture

The hall was a landmark achievement of early reinforced-concrete construction. Its ribbed dome is 69 metres in diameter — exceeding the span of the Pantheon in Rome — and the building rises 42 metres high, seating some 7,000 people. At its completion it was the largest structure of its kind in the world, and it remains a pioneering work of modern engineering and architecture, notable for its interplay of functional design and expressive form.

History and use

Built as part of a fairground complex that includes a pergola, fountain, and exhibition pavilions, the hall has hosted exhibitions, concerts, sporting events, and political gatherings. It housed one of the largest pipe organs in the world, later moved to Wrocław Cathedral. In 2006 the hall was listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site and is officially one of Poland's national Historic Monuments.
