Rainer 'Rai' Weiss (29 September 1932 – 26 August 2025) was a German-born American physicist, known for his pioneering work on laser interferometric gravitational-wave detection. His 1972 internal MIT report, analysing every significant noise source, became the blueprint for LIGO; he shared the 2017 Nobel Prize in Physics with Kip Thorne and Barry Barish 'for decisive contributions to the LIGO detector and the observation of gravitational waves'.

Born in Berlin, Weiss fled the Nazis with his family (via Prague) to New York in 1939. He also led the design of the COBE satellite's FIRAS instrument, which measured the Cosmic microwave background spectrum as a near-perfect black body.

Career

Weiss dropped out of MIT, returned, and took his PhD there (1964) in atomic physics. Teaching a relativity course led him to design a practical gravitational-wave interferometer and to solve, one by one, its problems of seismic, thermal and quantum noise — the design LIGO still follows. Patient through decades of scepticism and prototype-building, he lived to hear the first chirp in 2015 at age 84, and died in 2025.
