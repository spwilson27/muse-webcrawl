D'Artagnan

Charles de Batz de Castelmore (c. 1611 – 25 June 1673), also known as d'Artagnan and later Count d'Artagnan, was a French Musketeer who served Louis XIV as captain of the Musketeers of the Guard. He was killed at the siege of Maastricht in 1673. A fictionalised account of his life by Gatien de Courtilz de Sandras formed the basis for Alexandre Dumas's The Three Musketeers (1844) and its sequels, in which d'Artagnan is the protagonist.

Life

D'Artagnan was born at the Château de Castelmore near Lupiac in Gascony, the son of Bertrand de Batz. He took his mother's family name, d'Artagnan, on entering the Guards. He served with distinction in campaigns of the 1640s and 1650s, acted as a trusted agent of Cardinal Mazarin during the Fronde, and in 1661 was given the delicate mission of arresting the finance minister Nicolas Fouquet. He later served as governor of Lille. As captain of the King's Musketeers he was shot in the throat and killed during the assault on Maastricht in the Franco-Dutch War.

In fiction

Courtilz de Sandras's Mémoires de M. d'Artagnan (1700), a semi-fictionalised biography, supplied Dumas with his hero. In The Three Musketeers the young Gascon travels to Paris with a letter of introduction, befriends Athos, Porthos and Aramis — "all for one, and one for all" — and foils the plots of Cardinal Richelieu and Milady de Winter. The sequels Twenty Years After and The Vicomte of Bragelonne follow him to old age and a death closely modelled on the real man's. The character has been played in film and television by actors including Douglas Fairbanks, Gene Kelly, Michael York, Chris O'Donnell and Logan Lerman.
