Royal yacht

A royal yacht is a ship used by a monarch, a member of a royal family, or a head of state for official travel, state visits, receptions and leisure. Royal yachts may be operated by a navy and crewed by naval personnel, and have historically served as floating symbols of state.

History

The tradition of royal vessels extends back centuries; purpose-built royal yachts became prominent in the 17th century, when Charles II of England received a Dutch yacht (the Dutch word jacht giving English the word "yacht"). Successive British monarchs maintained yachts used for travel to royal residences and for fleet reviews.

Notable royal yachts

The most famous modern royal yacht was HMY Britannia, in service from 1954 until 1997, which carried Queen Elizabeth II and other members of the royal family on more than 600 foreign trips. Denmark's Dannebrog remains in active service, as do the royal yachts of Norway (Norge) and the Netherlands (Groene Draeck, a sailing yacht).
