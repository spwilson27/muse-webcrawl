Usk

Usk (Welsh: Brynbuga) is a small town and community in Monmouthshire, Wales, located on the River Usk, about 10 miles northeast of Newport. It is known for its historic castle, its flowers — the town has won prizes in Britain in Bloom competitions — and its role in the history of the Welsh Marches.

History

The Romans established a legionary fortress at nearby Caerleon and a smaller fort at Usk, known as Burrium, around AD 55. The Norman lord Richard fitz Gilbert de Clare founded Usk Castle in the twelfth century; the town was repeatedly fought over during the Welsh wars and was burned by Owain Glyndŵr's forces in 1405 after the Battle of Pwll Melyn, fought nearby. Adam of Usk, a chronicler of the court of Richard II, was born in the town.

Geography

The town stands in the valley of the River Usk, a noted salmon and trout river designated a Special Area of Conservation. The Usk Valley Walk and the surrounding countryside of the Wye Valley and Brecon Beacons attract visitors, and the town has many pubs, antique shops, and a small museum.

Landmarks

Usk Castle, still privately owned, overlooks the town and hosts events in its grounds. The Priory Church of St Mary dates in part to the twelfth century. The town square features a clock tower erected for Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee. Sessions House, the former courthouse, now houses the town council and a museum dedicated in part to Alfred Russel Wallace, who was born at Kensington House near the town.
