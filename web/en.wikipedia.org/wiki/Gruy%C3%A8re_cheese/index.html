Gruyère cheese

Gruyère (French: [ɡʁɥijɛʁ]) is a hard Swiss cheese named after the town of Gruyères in the canton of Fribourg. Made from raw cow's milk, it is sweet, slightly salty and nutty, with flavour that deepens with age, and it melts exceptionally well — essential for fondue and French onion soup.

CountrySwitzerland
RegionGruyères, Fribourg
MilkRaw cow's milk
TextureHard, dense, few small holes
Aging5–12+ months
StatusAOP (protected designation)

Production

Milk is curdled, cut, cooked and pressed into wheels of 25–40 kg, brined, and matured in cellars where a brown rind forms. Swiss Gruyère AOP, protected since 2001, must be made in designated cantons; a long dispute with French Gruyère ended in compromise labelling. Young Gruyère (5 months) is mild; réserve (10+ months) is intense and may develop crunchy crystals.

Uses

Gruyère is the classic cheese for fondue (moitié-moitié with Vacherin), croque-monsieur, quiche and gratin toppings. See Swiss cheese.
