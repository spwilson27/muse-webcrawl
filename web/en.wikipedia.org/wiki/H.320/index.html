H.320

H.320 is an umbrella Recommendation from the ITU-T for running multimedia (audio/video/data) over ISDN-based networks. First issued in 1990, it was the first widely adopted standard for videoconferencing and defined how terminals from different vendors could interoperate over circuit-switched digital lines.

Components

The H.320 suite specifies a set of component standards, including H.221 for frame structure and multiplexing of audio, video and data into a single bitstream; H.230 and H.242 for control and call procedures; H.261 (and later H.263) for video coding; and G.711, G.722 and G.728 for audio coding. Terminals typically connected over one or more ISDN basic-rate or primary-rate channels, using bonding to aggregate bandwidth in multiples of 64 kbit/s up to 1.5–2 Mbit/s.

H.320 was later complemented by H.323 for packet-based (IP) networks and H.324 for the public switched telephone network, and ISDN videoconferencing declined as IP systems became dominant.
