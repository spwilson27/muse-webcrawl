Cellulose

AppearanceWhite solid

Cellulose is an organic compound with the formula (C6H10O5)n, a polysaccharide consisting of a linear chain of several hundred to many thousands of β(1→4) linked D-glucose units. Cellulose is the most abundant organic polymer on Earth and the main structural component of the primary cell wall of green plants, many algae and some bacteria.

Structure and properties

Straight, unbranched cellulose chains pack into microfibrils held together by hydrogen bonds, giving high tensile strength. Unlike starch, cellulose cannot be digested by humans, though it serves as dietary fibre; ruminants and termites digest it with the help of symbiotic microorganisms producing cellulase enzymes.

Uses

Cellulose is the principal constituent of paper and cardboard, of cotton and linen textiles, and of wood. Derivatives include cellophane, rayon, cellulose acetate (photographic film), nitrocellulose (gun-cotton and early plastics) and microcrystalline cellulose, a common pharmaceutical excipient and food additive (E460).
