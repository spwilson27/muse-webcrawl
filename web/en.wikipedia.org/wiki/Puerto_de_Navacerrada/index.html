Puerto de Navacerrada

The Puerto de Navacerrada is a mountain pass in the Sierra de Guadarrama, in central Spain, on the border between the Community of Madrid and the province of Segovia in Castile and León. At an elevation of about 1,860 metres (6,100 ft) above sea level, it carries roads connecting Madrid with Segovia and is the site of one of Spain's oldest ski resorts.

Ski resort

The Navacerrada ski area, developed from the early twentieth century onward, is one of the closest ski resorts to Madrid and has historically been a popular day-trip destination for residents of the capital. Its slopes lie on the northern and southern flanks of the pass, near the Bola del Mundo peak. In recent decades the resort has faced declining snowfall and environmental debate about the future of skiing in the Guadarrama, including the removal of lifts on the Segovia side within the Sierra de Guadarrama National Park, established in 2013.

Access

The pass is reached by the M-601 road from the Madrid side and the CL-601 from Segovia. Navacerrada station, on the C-9 line of Cercanías Madrid, provides rail access via a narrow-gauge rack-assisted line from Cercedilla, one of the oldest mountain railways in Spain.
