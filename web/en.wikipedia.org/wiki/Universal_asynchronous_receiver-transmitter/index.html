Universal asynchronous receiver-transmitter

A universal asynchronous receiver-transmitter (UART) is a hardware circuit that translates data between parallel and serial forms for asynchronous serial communication. UARTs were a standard feature of computer serial ports and remain common in microcontrollers and embedded systems.

Operation

The transmitter shifts out a start bit, data bits (usually 8), an optional parity bit and one or more stop bits at an agreed baud rate; no shared clock is sent. The receiver samples the line, often at 16 times the baud rate, to recover the data.

History

The 8250 UART powered the original IBM PC serial port; the 16550 added FIFOs that enabled reliable higher speeds. Modern uses include GPS modules, Bluetooth modules, debugging consoles and RS-232, RS-422 and RS-485 interfaces via transceivers.
