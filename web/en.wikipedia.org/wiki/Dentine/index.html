Dentine

Dentine (American English: dentin) is a calcified tissue forming the bulk of a tooth, lying beneath the enamel of the crown and the cementum of the root and surrounding the pulp. It is about 70% mineral by weight, less hard than enamel but more resilient.

Structure

Dentine is permeated by microscopic tubules radiating from the pulp, which contain processes of the odontoblasts that secrete it. Because it is living and sensitive, exposed dentine — from wear, gum recession or decay — can transmit stimuli to the pulp and cause pain. Primary dentine forms before eruption; secondary and reparative dentine form later in life or in response to injury.
