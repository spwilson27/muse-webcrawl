Ranibizumab, sold under the brand name Lucentis, is a monoclonal antibody fragment (Fab) that inhibits vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A). It is injected into the vitreous of the eye to treat retinal diseases characterized by abnormal blood-vessel growth and leakage, principally the "wet" form of age-related macular degeneration (AMD), and also diabetic macular edema, diabetic retinopathy, and macular edema following retinal vein occlusion.

Development and use

Ranibizumab was developed by Genentech as an affinity-matured fragment derived from the same parent antibody as bevacizumab (Avastin), optimized for penetration of the retina. Approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2006, it was the first treatment shown not merely to slow vision loss in wet AMD but to improve vision in a substantial fraction of patients. It is administered as a 0.5 mg intravitreal injection, typically monthly or on individualized regimens.

Comparisons and cost

Because ranibizumab is far more expensive than off-label bevacizumab, its cost-effectiveness has been extensively studied; large trials such as CATT in the United States and IVAN in the United Kingdom found similar visual outcomes between the drugs for AMD. Biosimilar versions of ranibizumab have been approved since 2021. Common adverse effects relate mainly to the injection procedure, including conjunctival haemorrhage, eye pain, and a small risk of endophthalmitis.
