Coffeehouse

A coffeehouse, coffee shop, or café is an establishment that serves various types of coffee, espresso, latte, and cappuccino, as well as teas and light refreshments. Coffeehouses have historically served as important centres of social interaction, conversation, business, and intellectual exchange — in the words of one historian, places where people could gather, talk, read, write, and entertain one another for the price of a cup of coffee.

History

Coffeehouses first appeared in the Ottoman world: establishments are documented in Mecca and Medina in the fifteenth century, in Cairo and Damascus soon after, and in Constantinople (Istanbul) by the 1550s, where the kahvehane became a fixture of urban life. Coffee reached Europe through trade with the Levant; the first European coffeehouses opened in Venice, Oxford (1650), London (1652), and Paris (1672).

London's coffeehouses became known as "penny universities" because, for the price of a penny, a customer could buy a cup of coffee and engage in conversation. They were seedbeds of commerce and ideas: Lloyd's of London grew out of Edward Lloyd's coffee house, the London Stock Exchange began in Jonathan's Coffee-House, and coffeehouses were closely associated with the journalism, science, and politics of the Enlightenment. In Vienna, the coffee house culture that followed the Ottoman siege of 1683 became famous for its newspapers, marble tables, and literary clientele, and was inscribed by UNESCO on its list of intangible cultural heritage.

The modern coffee shop

In the twentieth century, the Italian espresso bar spread worldwide, and chains such as Starbucks (founded in Seattle in 1971) popularised a model of the coffee shop as a comfortable "third place" between home and work, offering speciality drinks, pastries, and space to work or socialise. Independent and specialty coffee shops emphasise bean origin, roasting, and brewing craft.

Social role

Coffeehouses continue to function as meeting places, workspaces, and cultural venues hosting readings, music, and exhibitions. Debates about their role include their effect on neighbourhood gentrification and the ethics of sourcing. In many Middle Eastern and Balkan communities, traditional coffeehouses remain largely male social clubs anchored by coffee and games such as backgammon.
