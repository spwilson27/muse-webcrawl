Overview

Vilnius University is the oldest university in the Baltic states and one of the oldest in Northern Europe, founded in 1579 when Stephen Báthory chartered the Jesuit Academy of Vilnius. Closed by the Russian Empire in 1832 after the November Uprising, it reopened after the First World War and again, as a Lithuanian institution, during the Soviet period and independence.

Campus and alumni

Its Old Town campus of interconnected courtyards, the baroque Church of St Johns and the Presidential Palace (once the university's observatory quarter) form an architectural ensemble; the library holds one of the region's great rare-book collections. Alumni and teachers include the poet Adam Mickiewicz (a student), the historian Joachim Lelewel and Nobel laureate Czesław Miłosz, who studied law here.
