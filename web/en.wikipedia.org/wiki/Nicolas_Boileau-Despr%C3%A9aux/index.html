Nicolas Boileau-Despréaux

Born1 November 1636, Paris, France
Died13 March 1711, Paris
OccupationPoet, critic
Notable workL'Art poétique (1674)

Nicolas Boileau-Despréaux (1 November 1636 – 13 March 1711), often known simply as Boileau, was a French poet and critic who became the leading literary legislator of French classicism. His verse treatise L'Art poétique (1674) codified rules of taste, clarity and decorum that influenced French — and European — literature for a century.

Works

Boileau first gained fame with his Satires (from 1666), mocking bad writers and social follies. His mock-heroic poem Le Lutrin burlesqued a trivial quarrel among churchmen. In the "Quarrel of the Ancients and the Moderns" he championed the ancients against Charles Perrault.

Influence

Appointed historiographer to Louis XIV and admitted to the Académie française in 1684, Boileau shaped the classical doctrine later echoed in English by Alexander Pope's Essay on Criticism.
