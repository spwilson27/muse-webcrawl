Ratnik (program)

Ratnik (Russian: Ратник, "warrior") is a Russian future infantry combat system, part of the "soldier of the future" programme of the Russian Armed Forces. It integrates modernised small arms, body armour, communications and navigation equipment into a single kit for individual servicemen. The system was developed by TsNIITochMash and entered service around 2015.

Components

The Ratnik kit comprises around 40–60 items depending on configuration, including:

  • The 6B47 helmet and 6B45 ballistic vest (rated against rifle rounds), plus protective goggles, knee and elbow pads.
  • Modernised AK-12 rifles and Pecheneg machine guns with rails for optics and accessories.
  • The Strelets command-and-control system: a tablet/GPS-GLONASS unit, radio and push-button controls linking soldiers to their commanders.
  • Thermal imaging sights, night-vision monoculars, and an arctic and standard uniform ensemble in EMR camouflage.

Development

Deliveries to the Russian Ground Forces began in the mid-2010s, with hundreds of thousands of sets delivered by the early 2020s. Ratnik equipment has been used in the war in Ukraine. A successor system, Sotnik, has been announced as under development, potentially including powered elements and advanced materials.
