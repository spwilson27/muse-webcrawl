The Sega Genesis, known as the Mega Drive outside North America, is a 16-bit fourth-generation home video game console developed and sold by Sega. It was Sega's third console and the successor to the Master System. Sega released it in Japan in 1988, in North America in 1989 (renamed Genesis because Sega could not obtain legal rights to the Mega Drive name in that region), and in Europe and other territories in 1990.

The console's library of over 900 games was created by Sega and a wide array of third-party publishers and delivered on ROM cartridges. The console is particularly well known for the Sonic the Hedgehog series, which was launched to compete with Nintendo's Mario franchise, as well as the Madden NFL and other Electronic Arts sports titles that helped establish it in North America.

History

Designed by a team led by Masami Ishikawa, the Mega Drive adapted Sega's System 16 arcade board, built around the Motorola 68000 processor. In North America, Sega of America's aggressive marketing under Tom Kalinske — price cuts, the "Genesis does what Nintendon't" campaign, and a push for older-skewing sports and arcade titles — let the Genesis lead the Super Nintendo Entertainment System for several years in the early 1990s "console war".

Add-ons and decline

Sega released two add-ons: the Sega CD (Mega-CD, 1991), which added CD-ROM games, and the 32X (1994), which added 32-bit capability; both underperformed and fragmented the market. Attention shifted to the Sega Saturn in 1994–95, and the Genesis was discontinued by Sega in 1997, though licensed variants from Majesco and later AtGames sold for many years afterwards.

Legacy

The Genesis is regarded as one of the most influential consoles of its era; Sega re-released it as the dedicated Genesis Mini in 2019. Its library and mascots remain central to retro gaming culture.
