W. R. Case & Sons Cutlery Company is an American manufacturer of traditional pocketknives, hunting knives, and collectible cutlery, headquartered in Bradford, Pennsylvania. The company traces its roots to 1889, when William Russell Case and his brothers began selling knives made for them by Job Case's earlier cutlery ventures; the firm was formally established in its present form in the early 1900s and moved its manufacturing to Bradford.

Products and collecting

Case is renowned for patterns such as the Trapper, Stockman, Canoe, and Peanut, for handle materials ranging from jigged bone to stag and synthetic Delrin, and for its tang stamps and dot-dating system, which allow collectors to date knives by year. Case knives are among the most collected American pocketknives, supported by the Case Collectors Club, founded in 1981. The company produced knives for the U.S. military in both World Wars, including the V-42 stiletto made for the First Special Service Force.

Ownership

After periods under the ownership of American Brands and the Alcas Corporation, Case was acquired in 1993 by Bradford's Zippo Manufacturing Company, which returned the brand to local ownership. The Zippo/Case Museum in Bradford exhibits the history of both companies and serves as the brand's flagship visitor attraction.
