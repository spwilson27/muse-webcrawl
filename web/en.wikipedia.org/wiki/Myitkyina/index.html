Myitkyina is the capital city of Kachin State in northern Myanmar (Burma). It lies on the west bank of the Ayeyarwady River, about 1,480 kilometres north of Yangon, and is the northernmost river port and railway terminus in the country. The name comes from the Jingpho language and means "near the big river".

History

Myitkyina has long been an important trading town between China and Burma. During the Second World War it was the scene of the prolonged Siege of Myitkyina in 1944, when Allied forces under Joseph Stilwell, including Merrill's Marauders and Chinese troops, captured the town and its strategically vital airfield from Japanese forces after months of fighting in difficult jungle conditions.

Geography and culture

The city has a humid subtropical climate strongly influenced by the monsoon. It is a centre of Kachin culture and hosts the Manau festival celebrating Jingpho traditions. The surrounding region is known for jade and gold mining, agriculture, and the confluence of the Mali and N'mai rivers nearby, where the Ayeyarwady proper begins. Myitkyina is home to several universities and theological colleges, and has significant Kachin, Shan, Bamar, and Chinese communities.
