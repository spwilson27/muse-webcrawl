Gilberto Passos Gil Moreira (born 26 June 1942) is a Brazilian singer-songwriter and guitarist, and a former politician. Alongside Caetano Veloso, he was a founder of the Tropicália movement in the late 1960s, which fused Brazilian traditions with rock and avant-garde influences and brought him into conflict with Brazil's military government, leading to exile in London.

Music

Gil's catalogue spans bossa nova, samba, forró, funk, and reggae; songs such as "Aquele Abraço", "Expresso 2222", and "Refazenda" are standards of Brazilian popular music. He has won multiple Grammy and Latin Grammy awards.

Politics

A founding member of Brazil's Green Party, Gil served as Minister of Culture from 2003 to 2008 under President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, promoting digital culture and creative-commons licensing.
