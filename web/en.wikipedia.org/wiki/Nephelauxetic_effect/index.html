Nephelauxetic effect

The nephelauxetic effect is a term used in the inorganic chemistry of transition metals. It refers to the reduction in the Racah interelectronic repulsion parameter (B) for a metal ion when it forms a complex, compared with the free ion. The name comes from the Greek for "cloud-expanding", reflecting the interpretation that the metal d-electron cloud expands over the ligands as covalency increases.

Nephelauxetic series

Ligands can be ordered by the size of the effect they produce, giving the nephelauxetic series: fluoride produces the smallest reduction, while iodide, sulfide and phosphine ligands produce the largest. Likewise, metal ions have their own ordering. The ratio β = B(complex)/B(free ion) is less than 1 and decreases as covalent character grows.

Significance

The effect provides experimental evidence for covalency in metal–ligand bonding, going beyond the purely electrostatic assumptions of basic crystal field theory, and is reflected in red shifts of d–d absorption bands in the spectra of complexes.
