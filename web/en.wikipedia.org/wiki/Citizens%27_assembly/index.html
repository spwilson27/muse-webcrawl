Citizens' assembly

A citizens' assembly is a body formed from citizens or generally people to deliberate on an issue or issues of local, national or international importance. The membership of a citizens' assembly is randomly selected, as in a jury, aiming to be demographically representative of the wider population.

Process

Assemblies typically involve a learning phase with expert testimony, a listening phase consulting affected communities, and a deliberation phase in which members discuss options and produce recommendations, often requiring supermajority support.

Examples

Well-known examples include the Citizens' Assembly on Electoral Reform in British Columbia (2004), the Irish Citizens' Assembly (2016–2018), whose recommendations led to referendums on marriage equality follow-up issues and abortion, and the French Citizens' Convention on Climate (2019–2020).
