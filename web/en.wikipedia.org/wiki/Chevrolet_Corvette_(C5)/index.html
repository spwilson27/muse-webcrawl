Chevrolet Corvette (C5)

Chevrolet Corvette (C5)
Production: 1997–2004
Engine: 5.7 L LS1 / LS6 V8
Body styles: coupé, convertible, hardtop

The Chevrolet Corvette (C5) is the fifth generation of the Corvette sports car, produced by the Chevrolet division of General Motors for the 1997 to 2004 model years. It was a substantially redesigned car with a new chassis using hydroformed frame rails, a rear-mounted transaxle, and the new LS1 V8 engine producing 345 hp.

Variants

The high-performance Z06 model, introduced for 2001 with the LS6 engine (385 hp, later 405 hp), revived a name first used in 1963. The C5 also served as the basis for the C5-R racing car, which won its class at the 24 Hours of Le Mans.

Reception

The C5 was praised for greatly improved structural rigidity, refinement and everyday usability compared with the C4, while remaining relatively affordable.
