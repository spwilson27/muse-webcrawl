Maximilian Bircher-Benner

Maximilian Oskar Bircher-Benner
Born: August 22, 1867, Aarau, Switzerland
Died: January 24, 1939, Zurich
Known for: Inventing muesli; raw-food diet advocacy

Maximilian Oskar Bircher-Benner (22 August 1867 – 24 January 1939) was a Swiss physician and dietitian best known as the inventor of muesli. After recovering from jaundice during his studies — which he attributed to eating raw apples — he developed theories about the healing power of raw fruits and vegetables and energy derived from sunlight stored in plants.

The sanatorium

In 1904 he opened the sanatorium "Lebendige Kraft" ("Vital Force") on the Zürichberg in Zurich, where patients followed strict regimes of raw-food diet, exercise, and regulated daily routine. His signature dish, originally called Apfeldiätspeise, mixed grated apple, rolled oats, lemon juice, condensed milk and nuts — the original Birchermüesli.

Ideas and influence

Though regarded with scepticism by much of the medical establishment of his day, Bircher-Benner's emphasis on whole foods, vegetables and dietary prevention influenced twentieth-century nutrition and the modern wholefood movement. Muesli became a staple breakfast across Europe and beyond.
