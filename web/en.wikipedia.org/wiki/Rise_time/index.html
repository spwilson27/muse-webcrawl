Rise time

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In electronics, when describing a voltage or current step function, rise time is the time taken by a signal to change from a specified low value to a specified high value. These values can be expressed as ratios or, equivalently, as percentages with respect to a given reference value. In analog circuits, these values are most often 10% and 90% of the step height; in digital circuits, thresholds are frequently defined relative to logic levels.

Overview

Rise time is a key parameter in both the digital and analog domains because it bounds how fast a system can respond to a step input. For a simple first-order RC or LR system, the 10–90% rise time is approximately 2.2 time constants, giving the useful rule of thumb that bandwidth (in hertz) is roughly 0.35 divided by the rise time (in seconds). Measuring rise time on an oscilloscope is a standard way to characterise amplifiers, oscilloscope probes and transmission channels.

Related concepts

The corresponding downward transition is the fall time. Together with propagation delay and settling time, rise time describes the step response of a system. In control theory the rise time of an underdamped second-order system is related to its natural frequency and damping ratio.
