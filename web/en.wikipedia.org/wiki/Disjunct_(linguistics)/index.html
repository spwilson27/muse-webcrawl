Disjunct (linguistics)

FieldGrammar
CategorySentence adverbial
Examples"Frankly, …", "Fortunately, …", "Regrettably, …"

In linguistics, a disjunct is a type of sentence adverbial that expresses the speaker's attitude toward, or evaluation of, the content of the sentence rather than modifying the verb directly. Examples include frankly in "Frankly, the report is wrong" and fortunately in "Fortunately, no one was hurt".

Distinguishing features

Disjuncts are loosely attached to the clause: they can usually be moved to the beginning or end of the sentence, are set off by pauses or commas, and cannot be questioned, negated or emphasised in the way ordinary manner adverbs can. "Frankly" in the disjunct sense means "I tell you frankly", not "in a frank manner" — contrast the adjunct use in "She spoke frankly".

Types

Grammarians commonly distinguish style disjuncts, which comment on how the utterance is presented (frankly, briefly, literally), from content disjuncts, which evaluate the truth or character of the proposition (certainly, fortunately, regrettably, wisely). Disjuncts contrast with adjuncts, which are integrated into the clause structure, and with conjuncts, which link clauses together.
