Jefferson County is a county in the US state of Colorado, lying immediately west of Denver along the foothills of the Front Range of the Rocky Mountains. Its county seat is Golden, and its largest city is Lakewood. With roughly 580,000 residents at the 2020 census, it is the fourth-most populous county in Colorado.

History

The county was created in 1861 as one of the original counties of Colorado Territory and is named for Thomas Jefferson. Golden served as the territorial capital from 1862 to 1867. Gold was discovered along Clear Creek, and the area developed with mining, agriculture and later suburban growth as part of the Denver metropolitan area.

Geography and communities

The county spans from the plains of the Denver suburbs — including Lakewood, Arvada, Wheat Ridge, Westminster (partly) and Littleton (partly) — westward into mountain communities such as Evergreen and Conifer. It is home to Red Rocks Park and Amphitheatre (in neighbouring usage near Morrison), the Coors brewery in Golden, the Colorado School of Mines, and the National Renewable Energy Laboratory.
