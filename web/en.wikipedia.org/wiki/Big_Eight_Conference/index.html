The Big Eight Conference was a National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA)-affiliated Division I-A college athletic association that sponsored football. It was formed in January 1907 as the Missouri Valley Intercollegiate Athletic Association (MVIAA) by its charter schools and remained one of the premier college athletic conferences in the United States until its dissolution in 1996, when its members joined with four Texas schools of the Southwest Conference to form the Big 12 Conference.

History

The Missouri Valley Intercollegiate Athletic Association was founded on January 12, 1907, by five charter schools: the University of Kansas, the University of Missouri, the University of Nebraska, Iowa State University (then Iowa State College), and Washington University in St. Louis. Over time the membership shifted: Drake University and Grinnell College were early members, and Oklahoma, Kansas State, Colorado, and Oklahoma State joined in later decades.

In 1928 the conference split, with six schools forming a conference that retained the MVIAA name while Drake, Grinnell, and others formed the Missouri Valley Conference. The larger group was informally known as the "Big Six," becoming the "Big Seven" with Colorado's arrival in 1948 and the "Big Eight" when Oklahoma State joined in 1958. The conference formally adopted the Big Eight name in 1964.

Members at dissolution

At the time of its dissolution in 1996, the Big Eight consisted of Colorado, Iowa State, Kansas, Kansas State, Missouri, Nebraska, Oklahoma, and Oklahoma State. Oklahoma and Nebraska dominated the conference's football history; their annual rivalry game frequently determined the conference champion and often had national title implications. Nebraska won or shared 31 Big Eight football titles, while Oklahoma claimed the conference crown 26 times.

Football

The Big Eight was renowned for its powerful running attacks, popularized by the wishbone offenses of Oklahoma under Barry Switzer and the option attacks of Nebraska under Tom Osborne. The conference sent its champion to the Orange Bowl for most of its existence, maintaining a tie-in with the Miami bowl game from 1968 until the conference's end. Members of the Big Eight won multiple national championships, including Nebraska's titles in 1970, 1971, 1994, and 1995, and Oklahoma's championships in 1974, 1975, and 1985. Colorado shared the 1990 national title.

Dissolution

In 1994, after years of realignment maneuvering, it was announced that the Big Eight would merge with four Texas members of the collapsing Southwest Conference — Texas, Texas A&M, Texas Tech, and Baylor — to form the new Big 12 Conference. Competition in the Big 12 began in the 1996–97 academic year. The Big Eight played its final championship events in the spring of 1996.
