Badrinath is a Hindu holy town in the Chamoli district of Uttarakhand, India. It is one of the four sites of the Char Dham pilgrimage and is dedicated to the god Vishnu, worshipped here as Badrinarayan. The town lies on the banks of the Alaknanda River, between the Nar and Narayana mountain ranges, at an elevation of about 3,100 metres.

Badrinath Temple

The temple, attributed in its origins to the philosopher Adi Shankara in the 8th century, has been rebuilt several times after avalanches and earthquakes; its present colourful stone facade dates to the 19th century under the kings of Garhwal. The shrine opens for roughly six months a year, from late April or May to November, closing in winter when the deity is moved to Joshimath.

Legends

Tradition holds that Vishnu meditated here beneath a badri (jujube) tree, and that the sage Narada and the twin sages Nara and Narayana are associated with the site. The nearby Tapt Kund hot springs and the village of Mana, often called the last village before the Tibetan border, are also pilgrimage stops.
