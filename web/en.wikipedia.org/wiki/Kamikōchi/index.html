Kamikōchi

Kamikōchi (上高地, "upper highland") is a remote mountain valley in the Hida Mountains of Nagano Prefecture, Japan, within Chūbu-Sangaku National Park. About 15 km long and lying at roughly 1,500 metres elevation along the Azusa River, it is celebrated as one of Japan's most scenic alpine areas and is often called the birthplace of Japanese mountaineering tourism.

Geography

The valley runs beneath the jagged Hotaka range — including Mount Okuhotaka (3,190 m), Japan's third-highest peak — and the active volcano Mount Yake. Sights include the Tashiro and Taishō ponds, formed by Yake's 1915 eruption, and the wooden Kappa Bridge over the Azusa River, the valley's emblematic viewpoint.

History

Once used by lumbermen, the valley was introduced to the wider world in the writings of English missionary and alpinist Walter Weston, who popularised Japanese mountain climbing in the 1890s; a monument and annual Weston Festival commemorate him. Novelist Ryūnosuke Akutagawa's 1927 story "Kappa" is set at the bridge that now bears the creature's name.

Access and conservation

To protect the environment, private cars have been banned from the valley since 1975; visitors arrive by bus or taxi from Matsumoto or Takayama. The valley is open only from mid-April to mid-November and is an important habitat for Japanese macaques, serow and black bears, with strict rules against feeding wildlife and removing natural material.
