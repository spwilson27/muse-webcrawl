Abraham Darby III (24 April 1750 – 25 March 1791) was an English ironmaster, the third of the Darby family of Coalbrookdale in Shropshire. He is best known for overseeing the construction and casting of the world's first major bridge made of cast iron, the Iron Bridge over the River Severn, completed in 1779 and opened in 1781.

Early life

Darby was born at Coalbrookdale, the son of Abraham Darby II and grandson of Abraham Darby I, who had pioneered smelting iron with coke. Like his family, he was a member of the Society of Friends (Quakers). He took control of the family's Coalbrookdale ironworks while still young.

The Iron Bridge

In 1776 Darby was commissioned to build a bridge across the Severn at what is now Ironbridge. Designed by architect Thomas Farnolls Pritchard, the bridge's ribs were cast at Coalbrookdale; the largest components weighed several tons each. The bridge has a span of about 100 feet (30 metres) and uses roughly 378 tons of iron. Darby personally bore much of the financial risk, underwriting the cost overruns when the project exceeded its estimates.

Later work

Under Darby III the Coalbrookdale works expanded, producing cylinders for steam engines, iron rails and other castings, and he promoted the use of iron in construction. He died in 1791, aged 40, leaving debts partly incurred from the bridge project, though the works continued under his family and associates.
