Speech from the throne

A speech from the throne, or throne speech, is an event in certain monarchies — and in some republics with analogous ceremonies — in which the reigning sovereign, or a representative, reads a prepared speech to a meeting of the nation's legislature. The speech sets out the government's legislative programme and policy priorities for the coming session. Because it is written by the government of the day rather than by the monarch personally, the speech is a key expression of the constitutional principle that the sovereign reigns but does not rule.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, the speech is the centrepiece of the State Opening of Parliament. The King travels to the Palace of Westminster, summons the House of Commons to the Lords chamber (with the door symbolically slammed in the face of Black Rod and reopened after knocking, asserting the Commons' independence), and reads the speech — formerly called the King's Speech or Queen's Speech — from the throne in the House of Lords. The ceremony is preceded by traditions such as the searching of the cellars by the Yeomen of the Guard, a legacy of the Gunpowder Plot of 1605, and the holding of an MP as a ceremonial "hostage" at Buckingham Palace to guarantee the sovereign's safe return. Parliament then debates the contents of the speech for several days, voting on an "Address in Reply".

Other countries

  • Canada: the Speech from the Throne opens each session of Parliament and is usually delivered by the governor general as the sovereign's representative.
  • Australia and New Zealand: governors-general deliver throne speeches written by their respective governments.
  • Netherlands: on Prinsjesdag, the monarch reads the Troonrede (throne speech) to a joint session of the States General, coinciding with the presentation of the budget.
  • Norway, Denmark, and Sweden retain comparable ceremonies at the opening of their parliaments, though Sweden's monarch no longer delivers the government's programme.

Republican analogues

Similar functions are performed in some republics by an annual address of the head of state, such as the State of the Union address in the United States, in which the president presents the administration's agenda to Congress.
