Disability Discrimination Act 1995

The Disability Discrimination Act 1995 (c. 50) was an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom which made it unlawful to discriminate against people in respect of their disabilities in relation to employment, the provision of goods and services, education and transport. It was the first legislation of its kind in the UK and followed years of campaigning by disability rights organisations, including civil disobedience protests in the early 1990s.

Provisions

The Act defined a disabled person as someone with a physical or mental impairment which has a substantial and long-term adverse effect on their ability to carry out normal day-to-day activities. It imposed duties on employers to make reasonable adjustments, and — in stages, with key duties taking effect in 1999 and 2004 — on service providers to adjust policies and physical features of premises to improve access. The Disability Rights Commission was established by later legislation in 1999 to enforce and promote the Act.

Replacement

In Great Britain the Act was repealed and replaced by the Equality Act 2010, which consolidated disability discrimination law with other equality legislation. In Northern Ireland the 1995 Act (as amended) remains in force.
