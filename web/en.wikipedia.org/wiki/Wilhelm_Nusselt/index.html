Wilhelm Nusselt

Born: November 25, 1882, Nuremberg

Died: September 1, 1957, Munich

Field: Mechanical engineering, thermodynamics

Ernst Kraft Wilhelm Nusselt (November 25, 1882 – September 1, 1957) was a German engineer best known for his pioneering work in heat transfer. The dimensionless Nusselt number, central to the study of convective heat transfer, is named after him.

Nusselt studied mechanical engineering at the Technical Universities of Munich and Berlin and earned his doctorate in Munich in 1907. He worked on the thermal conductivity of insulating materials and, applying dimensional analysis, in 1915 published his foundational paper on the dimensionless numbers governing convection, giving heat-transfer engineering a rigorous theoretical basis.

Contributions

In 1916 Nusselt derived the classic theory of film condensation on a vertical surface, still known as the Nusselt theory of condensation. He also developed the theory of regenerative heat exchangers and cross-flow heat exchange, work with lasting importance for power stations and chemical plants. He held professorships at Karlsruhe and, from 1925, at the Technical University of Munich, where he built a leading school of thermodynamics and heat transfer.

Legacy

The Nusselt number (Nu = hL/k), the ratio of convective to conductive heat transfer across a boundary, remains the standard correlating quantity in convection research and practice, and Nusselt is regarded as a founder of modern heat-transfer science.
