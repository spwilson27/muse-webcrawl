Consumer credit

Consumer credit refers to debt created by an individual to finance the purchase of goods and services, or to refinance existing debt. Consumer credit is extended by banks, retailers and other lenders in the form of loans, credit cards, lines of credit and instalment plans. It is distinct from credit extended to businesses or governments.

Types

Consumer credit is commonly divided into revolving credit and instalment credit. Revolving credit, such as a credit card account, allows a borrower to draw repeatedly up to a credit limit and to repay in variable amounts. Instalment credit, such as an auto loan or a personal loan, is repaid in fixed amounts over a set term.

Other common forms include:

  • Home equity lines of credit (HELOCs)
  • Student loans
  • Buy now, pay later arrangements
  • Payday loans and other short-term, high-cost credit
  • Retail store cards and charge cards

Regulation

In the United States, consumer credit is regulated under laws including the Truth in Lending Act, the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, the Fair Credit Reporting Act and the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), created by the Dodd–Frank Act of 2010, supervises many consumer lenders. Lenders typically evaluate applicants using a credit score, which summarises the borrower's history of repayment.

Economics

Consumer credit supports consumption smoothing, allowing households to make large purchases before they have accumulated sufficient savings. However, excessive household debt is associated with financial fragility and can amplify economic downturns. The cost of consumer credit is expressed as an annual percentage rate (APR), which includes interest and certain fees.

History

Instalment selling grew rapidly in the United States in the early twentieth century, particularly for automobiles and household appliances. General-purpose credit cards emerged in the 1950s with the Diners Club card and the BankAmericard (later Visa), and revolving credit became a mass-market product in subsequent decades.
