Baijiu

Baijiu (Chinese: 白酒; pinyin: báijiǔ), also known as shaojiu, is a colourless Chinese distilled alcoholic beverage traditionally made from grain. It typically has an alcohol content between 35% and 60% by volume. Baijiu is by volume the most consumed distilled spirit in the world, with most sales in China.

Production

Baijiu is produced from fermented grains, most commonly sorghum, though wheat, barley, millet, rice, and corn are also used. Fermentation relies on qū, a preparation of grain cultured with yeasts, moulds, and bacteria, which simultaneously saccharifies and ferments the mash. Unusually among major spirits, baijiu is typically fermented in solid state, in pits or jars, before distillation in pot stills. The distillate is aged in ceramic vessels and blended before bottling.

Flavour categories

Baijiu is classified by aroma style. The four major styles are strong aroma (nóngxiāng), exemplified by Wuliangye and Luzhou Laojiao; light aroma (qīngxiāng), such as Fenjiu from Shanxi; sauce aroma (jiàngxiāng), represented by Maotai from Guizhou; and rice aroma (mǐxiāng), common in the south. Maotai is served at Chinese state banquets and is one of the most prestigious brands.

Cultural significance

Baijiu is deeply embedded in Chinese social and business culture, drunk in small cups at banquets with frequent toasts. Distilling in China has a history of more than a thousand years, and baijiu consumption exceeds that of whisky, vodka, gin, rum, and tequila combined.
