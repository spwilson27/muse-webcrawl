Vomeronasal organ

The vomeronasal organ (VNO), or Jacobson's organ, is the paired auxiliary olfactory sense organ located in the soft tissue of the nasal septum, in the nasal cavity just above the roof of the mouth of many animals. It is the first stage of the accessory olfactory system and contains sensory neurons that detect certain non-volatile chemicals, especially pheromones.

Function

In snakes and lizards the forked tongue delivers scent molecules to the VNO; many mammals display the flehmen response, curling the lip to expose the organ's ducts. VNO signals project to the accessory olfactory bulb and amygdala, influencing mating, aggression, and prey detection rather than conscious smell.

In humans

Although a vomeronasal structure is present in human embryos and pit-like openings are found in many adult nasal septa, the human VNO lacks functional sensory neurons and its receptor genes are largely pseudogenized. Claims that it mediates human pheromone responses remain scientifically controversial.
