Midpoint method

Midpoint method
Field: Numerical analysis
Type: One-step method for ODEs
Order: 2

In numerical analysis, the midpoint method is a one-step method for solving the initial value problem for ordinary differential equations, y′ = f(t, y), y(t0) = y0. It is a second-order method and a member of the Runge–Kutta family.

Method

Given a step of size h, the method first takes a half-step using Euler's method to estimate the value at the midpoint, yn + 1/2 ≈ yn + (h/2) f(tn, yn), and then uses the slope at that midpoint for the full step: yn+1 = yn + h f(tn + h/2, yn + 1/2). Evaluating the slope at the centre of the interval cancels the leading error term of Euler's method, giving a global error of order h².

Related methods

The midpoint method is the simplest of the two-stage Runge–Kutta methods; Heun's method is another second-order variant that averages slopes at the start and end of the step.
