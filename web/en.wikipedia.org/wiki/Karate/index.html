Karate (空手, "empty hand") is a martial art developed in the Ryukyu Kingdom (present-day Okinawa, Japan) from indigenous Okinawan fighting methods (te) combined with Chinese martial arts brought by trade and migration. Karate is primarily a striking art using punches, kicks, knee and elbow strikes, and open-hand techniques such as knife-hand strikes, supplemented by forms (kata), pre-arranged sparring, free sparring (kumite), and in some schools grappling and throws. Practitioners are called karateka, and training takes place in a dojo. Karate is one of the most widely practised martial arts in the world. [Image: Karate practitioners performing kata]

History

The Ryukyu Kingdom's position in the China trade brought Fujianese martial arts — especially White Crane kung fu — to Okinawa, where they merged with local te (which took regional names: Shuri-te, Naha-te, Tomari-te). Weapons bans imposed under the kingdom and later under Satsuma domination are traditionally said to have encouraged empty-hand methods, though modern historians treat this as only part of the story. In the early 20th century masters such as Ankō Itosu introduced karate into Okinawan schools, and Gichin Funakoshi brought the art to mainland Japan in the 1920s, changing the written characters from "Chinese hand" to "empty hand" and adding the suffix -dō ("way") in keeping with Japanese budō. After the Second World War, servicemen stationed in Japan and Okinawa carried karate worldwide, and university clubs, movies, and tournaments made it a global phenomenon.

Training

Training is conventionally divided into kihon (basics), kata (formal solo exercises encoding techniques and principles), and kumite (sparring, from pre-arranged drills to free fighting). Students wear a white uniform (gi) and progress through coloured belts (kyū grades) to black belt (dan grades). Many schools emphasise etiquette and maxims such as Funakoshi's Twenty Precepts, including "karate begins and ends with courtesy" and "there is no first attack in karate" (karate ni sente nashi). Conditioning traditionally uses Okinawan implements such as the makiwara striking post and chiishi stone weights.

Styles

The four styles recognised by the World Karate Federation are Shotokan (founded by Funakoshi), Shito-ryu (Kenwa Mabuni), Goju-ryu (Chōjun Miyagi), and Wado-ryu (Hironori Ōtsuka). Other major schools include Kyokushin, the full-contact style founded by Mas Oyama; Shorin-ryu, among the oldest Okinawan lineages; and Uechi-ryu, closely tied to Chinese Pangai-noon. Styles differ in stance depth, breathing methods, the balance of hard and soft technique, and kata repertoire.

Sport karate

The World Karate Federation governs international competition in kata and point-scoring kumite, with weight classes and protective equipment. Karate made its Olympic debut at the Tokyo 2020 Games (held in 2021) with events in kata and kumite, though it was not retained for Paris 2024. Full-contact "knockdown" karate, kickboxing offshoots such as K-1, and karate-derived competitors in mixed martial arts represent other branches of the art's competitive life.
