The First Epistle to Timothy is one of three letters in the New Testament of the Christian Bible known as the pastoral epistles — the others being the Second Epistle to Timothy and the Epistle to Titus. It is addressed to Timothy, a young associate of Paul the Apostle, left in charge of the church at Ephesus, and gives instructions on church order, leadership qualifications, and conduct.

Content

The letter warns against false teachers, gives qualifications for overseers (bishops) and deacons, regulates prayer, widows, and elders, and includes famous sayings: "the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil" (6:10) and "Godliness with contentment is great gain" (6:6). The passage in chapter 2 instructing women to learn in silence has been widely debated in discussions of women's roles in ministry.

Authorship

Tradition ascribes the letter to Paul, but since the early 19th century many scholars have argued on grounds of vocabulary, style, and church structure that the pastorals are pseudepigraphical, written in Paul's name in the late first or early second century. Other scholars defend Pauline authorship, sometimes positing an amanuensis.
