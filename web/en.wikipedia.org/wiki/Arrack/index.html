Arrack

Arrack is a distilled alcoholic drink produced in South and Southeast Asia, typically made from the fermented sap of coconut flowers, sugarcane, grain or fruit. The name, from Arabic araq ("sweat" or "juice"), is applied to several distinct spirits.

Varieties

Coconut arrack, made in Sri Lanka and parts of India from toddy drawn from coconut palms, is pot- or column-distilled and often matured in wood. Batavia arrack, from Java, is distilled from sugarcane and red rice and was historically a prized ingredient in European punch; related drinks include Indonesian arak and the anise-flavoured arak of the Levant.

History

Arrack is among the oldest distilled spirits in Asia, described by travellers such as Marco Polo, and it became an important trade commodity of the Dutch East India Company.
