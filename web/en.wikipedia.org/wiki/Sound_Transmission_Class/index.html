Sound Transmission Class

Sound Transmission Class (STC) is an integer rating of how well a building partition attenuates airborne sound. In the United States it is widely used to rate interior partitions, ceilings, floors, doors, windows and exterior wall configurations. Introduced in 1961 and defined by the ASTM E413 standard, the rating condenses laboratory transmission-loss measurements (per ASTM E90) across sixteen third-octave bands from 125 Hz to 4000 Hz into a single number.

Method

Measured transmission loss values are plotted and compared with a standard reference contour. The contour is shifted until the sum of deficiencies below it does not exceed 32 dB and no single deficiency exceeds 8 dB; the STC is read from the contour's value at 500 Hz. Higher numbers indicate better sound insulation.

Interpretation

Roughly, at STC 25 normal speech can be understood through a wall; at STC 35 loud speech is audible but not intelligible; at STC 45 loud speech is faintly audible; at STC 50 loud sounds are barely heard; and STC 60 and above provides excellent isolation, typical of studio construction. Building codes in the US commonly require STC 50 (or field-measured FSTC 45) for walls and floors separating dwelling units.

Limitations

STC does not account for low-frequency sounds such as bass music or mechanical rumble, nor for impact noise, which is rated separately by Impact Insulation Class (IIC). Ratings for exterior envelopes use Outdoor–Indoor Transmission Class (OITC), which weights lower frequencies more heavily.
