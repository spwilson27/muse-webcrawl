World economy

[Image: planet Earth at night showing illuminated city networks, representing the integrated world economy]

The world economy (global economy) is the economy of all human societies — the aggregate of national economies linked by international trade, Investment, finance, migration and information flows. Its gross product (GWP) is roughly US$105–110 trillion at market exchange rates in the mid-2020s, and around US$180+ trillion at purchasing power parity.

Composition and governance

Services account for about two-thirds of world output, industry a quarter, agriculture a few percent. The largest economies — the United States (~26% of world GDP), China (~17%), then Germany, Japan, India and the United Kingdom — dominate, though growth has shifted toward Asia for four decades. There is no world government for this economy; coordination runs through the International Monetary Fund, World Bank, World Trade Organization, the G20 and central-bank cooperation, tested by the 2008 crisis, the COVID-19 shock and the 2021–23 inflation surge. Long-run questions — convergence of poor countries, demographic divergence, decarbonisation, debt — frame the study of the world economy in Macroeconomics and development economics.
