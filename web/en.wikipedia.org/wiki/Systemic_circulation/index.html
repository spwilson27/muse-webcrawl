Systemic circulation

Systemic circulation is the circuit of the cardiovascular system that carries oxygenated blood away from the heart to the body, and returns deoxygenated blood back to the heart. It is distinguished from the pulmonary circulation, which carries blood between the heart and the lungs.

Course

Oxygenated blood leaves the left ventricle through the aorta, the body's largest artery, and is distributed via progressively smaller arteries and arterioles to capillary beds in all tissues. After delivering oxygen and nutrients and collecting carbon dioxide and waste products, blood returns through venules and veins — ultimately the superior and inferior venae cavae — to the right atrium. Notable subdivisions include the coronary circulation to the heart muscle itself, the cerebral circulation to the brain, and the hepatic portal system, which directs blood from the digestive organs to the liver.

Physiology

The systemic circuit operates at much higher pressure than the pulmonary circuit because it must supply the entire body against greater resistance; systemic vascular resistance is the main determinant of arterial blood pressure.
