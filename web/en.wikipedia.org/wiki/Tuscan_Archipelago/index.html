Tuscan Archipelago

The Tuscan Archipelago is a chain of islands between the Ligurian Sea and the Tyrrhenian Sea, west of the Italian region of Tuscany. It comprises seven main islands — Elba, Giglio, Capraia, Montecristo, Pianosa, Giannutri and Gorgona — together with several smaller islets. The islands are protected as the Tuscan Archipelago National Park, one of the largest marine parks in Europe.

Geography

Elba is by far the largest island, with an area of about 224 square kilometres, and is famous as the place of Napoleon Bonaparte's first exile in 1814–1815. Montecristo, a granite island rising steeply from the sea, is a strict nature reserve; Pianosa is notably flat; Gorgona is the northernmost island and hosts an agricultural penal colony.

History

The islands were known to the Etruscans and Romans, who mined iron on Elba and granite on Giglio and Montecristo. In legend, the archipelago was formed from a necklace of Venus that fell into the Tyrrhenian Sea. During the medieval and early modern periods the islands passed among Pisa, Genoa, Spain and the Grand Duchy of Tuscany, and suffered frequent pirate raids.

Nature

The national park protects Mediterranean scrub, pine forests and rich marine habitats. Wildlife includes mouflon, wild boar, peregrine falcons, Audouin's gulls and monk seals, and the surrounding waters are part of the Pelagos Sanctuary for Mediterranean marine mammals.
