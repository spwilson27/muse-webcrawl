Vallis Alpes

Vallis Alpes (Latin for "Alpine Valley") is a lunar valley feature that bisects the Montes Alpes range on the Moon. It extends about 166 kilometres from the Mare Imbrium basin eastward toward the Mare Frigoris, and was discovered telescopically in 1727 by the Italian astronomer Francesco Bianchini.

Description

The valley is a graben — a flat-floored trough formed by faulting — roughly 10 kilometres wide at its broadest, with terraced walls. Along its centre runs a narrow, winding rille, a favourite but challenging target for amateur astronomers. The valley floor was flooded by basaltic lava, which also partially filled the great crater Plato at the western end of the Alps.

Setting

The Montes Alpes form part of the outer ring of the Imbrium impact basin. Vallis Alpes cuts directly through the range, and its orientation points roughly toward the basin's interior, consistent with formation by fractures radial to the impact.
