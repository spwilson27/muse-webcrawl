Emotional support animal

An emotional support animal (ESA) is an animal that provides relief to individuals with a mental or emotional disability through companionship. Unlike service animals, emotional support animals require no specialised task training, and in the United States their legal recognition is narrower than that of service dogs.

Legal status

Under the Americans with Disabilities Act, only dogs trained to perform specific tasks related to a disability qualify as service animals; ESAs are not covered. The Fair Housing Act has historically required landlords to make reasonable accommodations for assistance animals, including ESAs, supported by documentation from a licensed mental health professional. In January 2021, the U.S. Department of Transportation revised its rules so that airlines are no longer required to treat ESAs as service animals, following controversies over unusual animals — including peacocks, ducks and pigs — being brought into aircraft cabins.

Debate

Supporters argue that ESAs measurably reduce symptoms of anxiety, depression and PTSD. Critics point to online services selling ESA letters with minimal screening, fraudulent claims, and risks posed by untrained animals in public spaces, and argue that loose rules undermine access for people with trained service animals.
