Barrio Chino, Lima

Barrio Chino is the Chinatown of Lima, Peru, located in the historic center of the city around Calle Capón, near the Central Market. Founded in the mid-19th century, it is one of the oldest Chinatowns in the Americas, established after the arrival of Chinese indentured laborers (coolies) in Peru beginning in 1849.

History

Chinese workers arrived in Peru to work in guano extraction, railways, and coastal plantations after the abolition of slavery. As their contracts ended, many settled in Lima around the Calle Capón area, opening shops, restaurants, and import businesses. In 1909 the community erected a decorative Chinese arch; the present monumental arch (paifang), a gift from Taiwan, was inaugurated in 1999 as part of efforts to revitalize the district. The neighborhood is the center of Peru's large Chinese-Peruvian community, known locally as tusán.

Culture and cuisine

Barrio Chino is famous for its chifas — restaurants serving Chifa cuisine, the Chinese-Peruvian fusion that produced dishes such as lomo saltado's soy-sauce variants, arroz chaufa (fried rice), and sopa wantán. The district is decorated with red lanterns and lion statues, and hosts celebrations for Chinese New Year. Nearby streets are lined with shops selling Asian ingredients, medicines, and goods, and the area draws large crowds of shoppers and diners daily.
