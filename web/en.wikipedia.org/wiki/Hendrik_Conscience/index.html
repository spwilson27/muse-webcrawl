Hendrik Conscience

Hendrik Conscience
Born: 3 December 1812, Antwerp
Died: 10 September 1883, Ixelles (Brussels)
Notable work: The Lion of Flanders (1838)

Hendrik Conscience (3 December 1812 – 10 September 1883) was a Belgian author, widely considered the pioneer of Dutch-language literature in Flanders. He is credited with awakening Flemish national consciousness and is famed as "the man who taught his people to read".

Life and work

Born in Antwerp to a French father and Flemish mother, Conscience served in the Belgian revolutionary army of 1830. His breakthrough was the historical novel De Leeuw van Vlaanderen (The Lion of Flanders, 1838), celebrating the Flemish victory at the Battle of the Golden Spurs in 1302. He went on to write some 100 novels and novellas — peasant tales such as The Conscript (1850) as well as historical romances — translated into many languages.

Legacy

Conscience's works shaped 19th-century Flemish identity and Dutch prose writing in Belgium. Statues and squares bear his name across Flanders, including the Hendrik Conscienceplein in Antwerp.
