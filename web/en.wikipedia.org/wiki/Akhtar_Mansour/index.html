Akhtar Mohammad Mansour (c. 1968 – 21 May 2016) was an Afghan militant who served as the second supreme leader of the Taliban, succeeding the movement's founder Mullah Mohammed Omar. He had been Omar's deputy and, after Omar's death in 2013 — a fact concealed for two years — effectively directed the movement before being formally appointed leader in July 2015. He was killed in a United States drone strike in Pakistan's Balochistan province in May 2016.

Early life and Taliban government

Mansour was an ethnic Pashtun of the Ishaqzai tribe from the Kandahar region. He fought in the Soviet–Afghan War with a mujahideen faction and joined the Taliban when it emerged in 1994. Under the Islamic Emirate government (1996–2001) he served as minister of civil aviation and tourism, overseeing Ariana Afghan Airlines. After the fall of the Taliban regime in 2001 he was appointed the movement's shadow governor of Kandahar and later became Mullah Omar's deputy and a senior figure in the Quetta Shura.

Leadership

When Omar's death was publicly confirmed in July 2015, Mansour was chosen as leader at a shura meeting in Pakistan. His accession was disputed: several senior Taliban figures, including Omar's son and brother, initially refused allegiance, and a splinter faction broke away. Mansour publicly accepted responsibility for having concealed Omar's death, saying it had been necessary to preserve unity. During his tenure the Taliban made major battlefield gains, briefly capturing the northern city of Kunduz in September 2015, their first seizure of a provincial capital since 2001.

Mansour was involved in preliminary contacts about peace talks, yet also authorised continued offensives. Analysts reported substantial personal wealth derived from the drug trade and other enterprises.

Death

On 21 May 2016 Mansour was travelling by car in Balochistan, Pakistan, reportedly returning from Iran under a false identity as "Muhammad Wali", when a US drone strike destroyed the vehicle near the town of Ahmad Wal, killing him and the driver. President Barack Obama confirmed the strike, saying Mansour had rejected peace efforts. Pakistan protested the violation of its sovereignty. The Taliban's leadership council selected Hibatullah Akhundzada, Mansour's deputy, as his successor.
