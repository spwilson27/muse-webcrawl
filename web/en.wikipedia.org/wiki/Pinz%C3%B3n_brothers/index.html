Pinzón brothers

The Pinzón brothers were Spanish navigators and explorers from Palos de la Frontera, in the province of Huelva, Andalusia. The three brothers — Martín Alonso Pinzón, Vicente Yáñez Pinzón, and Francisco Martín Pinzón — played a major role in Christopher Columbus's first voyage of 1492, supplying ships, crews, and local credibility that made the expedition possible.

The voyage of 1492

Martín Alonso, an experienced and respected shipowner, captained the Pinta; his brother Francisco sailed as her master. Vicente Yáñez captained the Niña. Martín Alonso's support persuaded reluctant sailors of Palos to enlist. During the crossing he at one point separated from Columbus in search of gold, rejoining before the return journey; he died within days of reaching Spain in March 1493, possibly in part from disgrace after Columbus's accusations of desertion.

Later voyages

Vicente Yáñez Pinzón went on to lead his own expeditions: in 1499–1500 he reached the coast of Brazil and explored the mouth of the Amazon, and in 1508 he explored the Caribbean coast of Central America with Juan Díaz de Solís. In 1501 he was made a knight by Ferdinand II.
