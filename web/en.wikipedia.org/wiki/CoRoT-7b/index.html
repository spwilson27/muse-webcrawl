CoRoT-7b (formerly CoRoT-Exo-7b) is an exoplanet orbiting the star CoRoT-7, a Sun-like star about 490 light-years away in the constellation Monoceros. Announced on 3 February 2009 by the French-led CoRoT space mission, it was the smallest exoplanet known at the time and the first strong candidate for a rocky, terrestrial exoplanet.

Characteristics

CoRoT-7b orbits its star in about 20.5 hours at a distance of only about 0.017 astronomical units — roughly one-seventieth of the Earth–Sun distance. Its radius is about 1.6 times that of Earth. Radial velocity measurements of its mass proved difficult because the parent star is unusually active, and published estimates have ranged widely, from under three to about eight Earth masses. Even so, its small size implies a composition dominated by rock and metal rather than gas or ice.

The planet is tidally locked, always showing the same face to its star. Surface temperatures on the dayside are estimated at 2,000–2,600 °C, hot enough to melt rock, so the dayside may be covered by an ocean of lava, while the permanent nightside could be as cold as −240 °C. These extremes have led to CoRoT-7b being described as a "lava planet" and a possible remnant core of an evaporated gas giant, sometimes called a chthonian planet.

System

A second planet, CoRoT-7c, a super-Earth that does not transit, was identified by radial velocity measurements in 2009, and a third candidate (CoRoT-7d) has been debated. The system remains a benchmark for the study of ultra-short-period rocky planets.
