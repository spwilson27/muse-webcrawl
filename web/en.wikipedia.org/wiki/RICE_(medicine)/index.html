RICE (medicine)

Placeholder image: ice and compression applied to an injured ankle.

RICE is a mnemonic acronym for the four elements of a treatment regimen that was once widely recommended for soft tissue injuries such as sprains and strains: rest, ice, compression, and elevation. It was commonly regarded as a first-aid treatment rather than a cure, intended to manage swelling and pain during the first 24 to 48 hours after injury.

Components

Rest

Rest is a key component of repairing the body. Without rest, continual strain is placed on the injured area, leading to increased inflammation, pain, and possible further injury. However, prolonged complete rest is now generally discouraged in favour of early, gentle mobilisation as pain allows.

Ice

Ice, typically in the form of crushed ice or a cold pack applied through a barrier, constricts blood vessels and helps limit swelling and numb pain after an acute injury. Applications of about 15 to 20 minutes at a time were traditionally recommended.

Compression

Compression aims to reduce oedema by applying an elastic bandage or similar wrap to the injured area, limiting the accumulation of fluid in surrounding tissues.

Elevation

Elevation aims to reduce swelling by encouraging venous and lymphatic drainage of fluid from the injured area, keeping the injured part raised above the level of the heart where practical.

Variations and criticism

Variants of the acronym include PRICE (adding "protection"), POLICE (protection, optimal loading, ice, compression, elevation), and more recently PEACE & LOVE, proposed in the British Journal of Sports Medicine in 2019, which de-emphasises ice and anti-inflammatory medication in favour of progressive loading, optimism, vascularisation and exercise. Dr Gabe Mirkin, who coined the term RICE in his 1978 Sportsmedicine Book, later wrote that ice and complete rest may delay healing rather than promote it, and research evidence for the protocol as a whole remains limited.
