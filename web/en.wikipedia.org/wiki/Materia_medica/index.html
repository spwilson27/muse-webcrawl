Materia medica

Materia medica (from Latin, "medical material") is a term from the history of pharmacy for the body of collected knowledge about the therapeutic properties of any substance used for healing. Historically it denoted the substances themselves — mainly plant-derived drugs, but also animal and mineral materials — and the books describing them. In modern usage, materia medica has largely been superseded by pharmacology.

History

The most famous early work of materia medica is De materia medica by Pedanius Dioscorides, a Greek physician in the Roman army who wrote in the first century AD. His five-volume encyclopedia described around 600 plants and some 1,000 simple drugs, and remained an authoritative reference for more than 1,500 years, influencing Western and Islamic medicine.

Throughout the Middle Ages and the early modern period, herbals and pharmacopoeias compiled materia medica knowledge. In the 19th century, the active constituents of medicinal plants began to be isolated — morphine from opium, quinine from cinchona bark, and others — marking the transition to modern pharmacology.

In alternative medicine

The term remains in use in homeopathy, where a "materia medica" is a reference work listing homeopathic remedies and the symptoms associated with them, and in Ayurvedic and traditional Chinese medicine, which maintain their own classical materia medica texts such as the Shennong Bencaojing.
