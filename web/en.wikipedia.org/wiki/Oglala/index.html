The Oglala (Lakota: Oglála) are one of the seven subdivisions of the Lakota people, who, along with the Dakota, make up the Oceti Sakowin (Seven Council Fires). The Oglala were historically a powerful band of mounted buffalo hunters on the northern Great Plains, and today the federally recognised Oglala Sioux Tribe is based at the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation in southwestern South Dakota.

The name Oglala is usually translated "those who scatter their own," or "dust scatterers," and is often explained by a tradition of a quarrel within the band long ago.

History

By the early nineteenth century the Oglala ranged across what are now South Dakota, Nebraska, Wyoming and Montana, following the buffalo herds and trading at posts on the North Platte River. They were central to Lakota resistance to United States expansion: Red Cloud's War (1866–1868) forced the U.S. to abandon its forts along the Bozeman Trail and sign the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868), which established the Great Sioux Reservation. In 1876 Crazy Horse and Oglala warriors fought at the Battle of the Little Bighorn. After the killing of Crazy Horse in 1877 and years of pressure, the Oglala settled at the Pine Ridge Agency.

In December 1890, soldiers of the U.S. 7th Cavalry massacred Lakota — mostly Miniconjou, with Hunkpapa refugees among them — at Wounded Knee Creek on the Pine Ridge Reservation. In 1973 the reservation again became a focus of national attention during the 71-day occupation of Wounded Knee by members of the American Indian Movement and Oglala residents protesting tribal government and federal policy.

Today

The Oglala Sioux Tribe operates its own government, schools and college (Oglala Lakota College) at Pine Ridge, one of the largest and poorest reservations in the United States. Lakota language revitalisation, land and treaty-rights claims — including the Black Hills claim, for which the Sioux won a monetary judgment they have refused in favour of the land's return — remain central concerns.
