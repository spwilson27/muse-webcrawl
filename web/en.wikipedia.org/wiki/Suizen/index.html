Suizen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A komusō monk playing the shakuhachi as blowing meditation.

Suizen (吹禅, "blowing Zen") is a form of Zen Buddhist meditation consisting of playing the shakuhachi, a Japanese bamboo flute. The practice was central to the Fuke sect of Zen, whose monks, the komusō, wandered Japan playing the instrument, wearing woven baskets (tengai) over their heads to symbolise the detachment of ego.

History

The Fuke sect traced its lineage to the Chinese master Fuke (Puhua), a contemporary of Linji (Rinzai). During the Edo period the komusō were granted official status and exclusive rights to play the shakuhachi as a spiritual practice; in return, some acted as informants for the Tokugawa shogunate. The sect was abolished in 1871 during the Meiji Restoration, but the repertoire survived through secular teachers and lay lineages.

Practice and repertoire

The core repertoire of suizen consists of solo pieces called honkyoku ("original pieces"), transmitted within particular lineages and temples, such as the pieces of Myōan-ji and Ichigetsu-ji. The pieces are characterised by free rhythm, phrases shaped to the length of the breath, extensive use of timbral effects and microtonal inflections, and long passages of near-silence (ma). The aim is not performance but meditative absorption — the player concentrates the mind through breath, in a state sometimes described as ichion jōbutsu, "enlightenment through a single tone".

Suizen is contrasted with zazen (sitting meditation): where zazen empties the mind in stillness, suizen does so through sustained, disciplined blowing.
