Theo de Raadt

Theo de Raadt (born May 19, 1968) is a South African-born Canadian software engineer living in Calgary, Alberta. He is the founder and leader of the OpenBSD and OpenSSH projects and was a founding member of the NetBSD project.

Career

De Raadt co-founded NetBSD in 1993 but was forced out in late 1994 after internal disputes; in 1995 he forked the codebase to create OpenBSD, an operating system renowned for its emphasis on security, code auditing and correctness, with the motto "Only two remote holes in the default install, in a heck of a long time!" Under his leadership the OpenBSD project also produced OpenSSH — now the standard implementation of the SSH protocol worldwide — as well as OpenBGPD, OpenNTPD, the pf firewall and LibreSSL. De Raadt is known for his uncompromising advocacy of free software licences, open documentation for hardware, and software security; he led the successful campaign for freely redistributable firmware and open hardware specs from wireless chipset vendors. In 2003, a U.S. DARPA grant supporting OpenBSD was controversially cancelled after de Raadt publicly criticised the Iraq War. He received the Free Software Foundation's Award for the Advancement of Free Software in 2004.

Style

De Raadt runs OpenBSD with final authority over code commits and project direction, releasing two versions per year with accompanying songs and artwork. His blunt communication style is famous in the open-source world.
