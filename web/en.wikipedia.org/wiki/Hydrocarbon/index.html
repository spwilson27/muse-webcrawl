A hydrocarbon is an organic compound consisting entirely of hydrogen and carbon. Hydrocarbons are the principal constituents of fossil fuels — petroleum, natural gas and coal — and the feedstock of the petrochemical industry.

They are classified by bonding: alkanes (single bonds, saturated), alkenes and alkynes (double and triple bonds, unsaturated), and aromatics such as benzene with delocalised rings. Chain length and branching control boiling point and use, from methane gas to bitumen.

Reactions

Hydrocarbons combust to CO₂ and water (exothermic — the basis of their use as fuels), undergo cracking (large molecules split into smaller, more useful ones over catalysts), and are functionalised into the alcohols, acids, polymers and pharmaceuticals of organic chemistry.

Sources

Crude oil is separated by fractional distillation into fractions (refinery gases, petrol, kerosene, diesel, fuel oil, bitumen). Supply and climate concerns drive interest in biofuels and green hydrogen as hydrocarbon alternatives.
