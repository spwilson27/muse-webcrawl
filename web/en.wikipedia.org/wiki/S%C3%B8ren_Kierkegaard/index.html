Søren Kierkegaard

Born5 May 1813, Copenhagen, Denmark
Died11 November 1855, Copenhagen
Era19th-century philosophy
Main interestsFaith, anxiety, subjectivity, the individual

Søren Aabye Kierkegaard (1813–1855) was a Danish philosopher, theologian and author widely regarded as the first existentialist philosopher. Writing largely under pseudonyms, he explored how individuals live and choose, emphasising subjective truth, anxiety, despair and the "leap of faith".

Life

Born into a wealthy Copenhagen family, Kierkegaard studied theology at the University of Copenhagen. His broken engagement to Regine Olsen in 1841 profoundly shaped his writing. Supported by an inheritance, he published an extraordinary series of works in the 1840s, including Either/Or (1843), Fear and Trembling (1843), The Concept of Anxiety (1844) and Concluding Unscientific Postscript (1846).

Thought

Kierkegaard described three stages of existence — the aesthetic, the ethical and the religious — and attacked Hegelian system-building and the complacency of the Danish state church. In his final years he launched a fierce public campaign against Christendom in his journal The Moment.

Influence

Rediscovered in the 20th century, Kierkegaard influenced existentialism, modern theology and literary figures from Kafka to Ibsen.
