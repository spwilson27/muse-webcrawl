Dance-punk

Dance-punk is a post-punk subgenre that emerged in the late 1970s and reached renewed popularity in the early 2000s. It fuses the energy and angular guitars of punk rock and post-punk with the rhythms of disco, funk and dance music, producing music that is aggressive yet aimed at the dancefloor.

History

In the late 1970s, bands such as Gang of Four, Talking Heads, Public Image Ltd and A Certain Ratio combined punk's abrasiveness with funk basslines and disco rhythms; New York's no wave and mutant disco scenes produced acts like ESG and Liquid Liquid. The style faded in the mid-1980s but was revived around 2001–2004 by New York's DFA Records scene — LCD Soundsystem, The Rapture and !!! — and by British bands such as Franz Ferdinand, whose success briefly made dance-punk a mainstream indie style.
