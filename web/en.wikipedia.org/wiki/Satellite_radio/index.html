Satellite radio

Satellite radio is a broadcasting service in which digital radio signals are transmitted from satellites directly to subscribers' receivers, offering continent-wide coverage of many channels from a single frequency allocation. The best-known service is Sirius XM in North America, formed by the 2008 merger of the rival Sirius and XM satellite radio companies.

History

The US Federal Communications Commission auctioned satellite digital audio radio service (SDARS) licences in 1997. XM Satellite Radio launched in 2001 and Sirius in 2002, each spending billions on geostationary and highly elliptical orbit satellites and terrestrial repeater networks to fill urban gaps. Both services grew through car-manufacturer deals and exclusive content — Sirius's 2004 signing of Howard Stern was a landmark — but accumulated heavy losses, leading to their merger, approved in 2008.

Technology

Services broadcast in the S band around 2.3 GHz. Receivers use small patch antennas; signals are digitally compressed and error-corrected, and satellite diversity plus ground repeaters maintain reception in cities and tunnels. Unlike terrestrial radio, satellite radio is subscription-funded and largely commercial-free on music channels.

Other services

WorldSpace, founded by Noah Samara, served parts of Africa and Asia from 1999 until its bankruptcy in 2008. In Europe and elsewhere, satellite radio remained niche, with digital terrestrial systems such as DAB more prominent. Satellite-delivered content also continues through television audio channels and internet streaming companions to satellite subscriptions.
