Sardonyx

Sardonyx is a banded variety of the mineral chalcedony (a form of quartz, silicon dioxide, SiO2) in which the bands are straight and roughly parallel, combining layers of sard — a translucent reddish-brown chalcedony — with layers of onyx, typically white, black or grey. Like all chalcedony it has a Mohs hardness of about 6.5–7 and a waxy lustre.

Properties and varieties

Sardonyx bands range from white through shades of red, orange and brown to black. The contrast between the layers makes the stone ideal for cameo carving, in which a raised design is cut into the upper layer against a differently coloured background layer. The finest material historically came from India and Arabia; today it is sourced from Brazil, India, Madagascar and elsewhere.

History and uses

Sardonyx was prized in the ancient world. Roman soldiers wore sardonyx talismans engraved with heroes such as Hercules or Mars, believing the stone imparted courage. It was used for signet rings and seals because hot wax does not stick to its surface. In the Middle Ages it was associated with eloquence and was one of the stones of the biblical breastplate of Aaron and the foundations of the New Jerusalem. Sardonyx is the traditional birthstone for August (alongside peridot and spinel in modern lists).

Famous examples include the Gemma Augustea and other large imperial cameos, whose layered compositions exploit the stone's natural banding.
