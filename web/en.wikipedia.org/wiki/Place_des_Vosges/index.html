Place des Vosges

The Place des Vosges in the Marais district of Paris

The Place des Vosges, originally the Place Royale, is the oldest planned square in Paris, France. It is located in the Marais district, straddling the dividing line between the 3rd and 4th arrondissements of Paris.

Commissioned by Henry IV and built between 1605 and 1612, the square measures 140 metres by 140 metres and consists of red brick houses with vaulted arcades at ground level, steep slate roofs, and dormer windows. It was inaugurated in 1612 with a grand carousel to celebrate the engagement of Louis XIII and Anne of Austria.

History

The square was built on the site of the Hôtel des Tournelles, a royal residence demolished after Henry II was mortally wounded there in a tournament in 1559. Henry IV intended the square partly as a centre for the silk industry, with workshops above the arcades. The identical façades, all of brick over stone arcades, were imposed by royal decree – an early example of urban planning applied to residential architecture.

During the Revolution the square was renamed several times before receiving its present name in 1800, honouring the Vosges department, the first to pay its taxes under the new regime. The writer Victor Hugo lived at No. 6 from 1832 to 1848; the house is now a museum, the Maison de Victor Hugo.

Layout

The square is a true square of identical houses, broken only by the Pavillon du Roi on the south side and the Pavillon de la Reine opposite, which are taller than the other façades. In the centre are lawns, rows of lime trees, and an equestrian statue of Louis XIII, the original by Pierre Biard having been destroyed in the Revolution and replaced in 1825.
