Malvasia is a group of wine grape varieties grown historically in the Mediterranean region, including the Balearic Islands, the Canary Islands, Madeira, Greece, Italy, Croatia, Slovenia, and Portugal. The grapes are used to produce a wide range of wines, from dry table wines to rich sweet and fortified wines.

History

The name derives from Monemvasia, a medieval fortress town on the southern Peloponnese coast of Greece, from which sweet wines were exported across Europe during the Middle Ages. In England the wine was known as "Malmsey", famously associated with the legend that George, Duke of Clarence, was drowned in a butt of Malmsey wine in 1478.

Varieties

Numerous distinct varieties carry the Malvasia name, including Malvasia Bianca in Italy, Malvasia Istriana in Friuli and Istria, Malvasia de Sitges in Catalonia, and Malvasia Candida, a parent of the Malmsey grapes of Madeira. Many are aromatic, producing wines with notes of peach, apricot and honey.
