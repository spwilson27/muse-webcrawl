Nimitz class: ten nuclear-powered aircraft carriers of the United States Navy, in service from 1975. Displacement about 97,000–104,000 long tons; length 1,092 ft (333 m); two A4W reactors; crew of roughly 5,000–5,200 with air wing; up to 90 aircraft.

Nimitz-class aircraft carrier

The Nimitz class is a class of ten nuclear-powered aircraft carriers in service with the United States Navy. Named for Second World War fleet admiral Chester W. Nimitz, the ships were for decades the largest warships ever built and remain the backbone of US carrier strike groups, being progressively succeeded by the Gerald R. Ford class.

Ships

All ten were built by Newport News Shipbuilding in Virginia: Nimitz (CVN-68, commissioned 1975), Dwight D. Eisenhower (CVN-69), Carl Vinson (CVN-70), Theodore Roosevelt (CVN-71), Abraham Lincoln (CVN-72), George Washington (CVN-73), John C. Stennis (CVN-74), Harry S. Truman (CVN-75), Ronald Reagan (CVN-76) and George H.W. Bush (CVN-77, 2009). Later ships incorporate design changes such as bulbous bows and, on Reagan and Bush, a redesigned island.

Design

Each carrier is powered by two Westinghouse A4W pressurised-water reactors driving four steam turbines and four shafts, giving speeds above 30 knots and effectively unlimited range for a designed service life of about 50 years, with one mid-life refuelling and overhaul. The angled flight deck carries four steam catapults and can operate an air wing of 60–90 aircraft, including F/A-18 Super Hornets and F-35C Lightning IIs. Defensive armament includes Sea Sparrow and Rolling Airframe missiles and Phalanx close-in weapon systems. Air wings, escorts and support ships together form a carrier strike group of some 7,500 personnel.
