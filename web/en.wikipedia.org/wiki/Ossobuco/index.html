Ossobuco

Ossobuco (Italian: [ˌɔssoˈbuːko]; Lombard: oss bus) is a specialty of Lombard cuisine of cross-cut veal shanks braised with vegetables, white wine and broth. The name means "bone with a hole", from the marrow hole at the centre of the cross-cut shank. The marrow, prized as a delicacy, is traditionally eaten with a small spoon or fork after the meat.

Preparation

In the classic ossobuco alla milanese, veal shank slices about 3–5 cm thick are tied to keep the meat on the bone, dredged in flour, browned in butter, and braised slowly with onions, carrots, celery, white wine and stock — in the traditional Milanese version, without tomato. Cooking takes around two hours, until the meat is fork-tender. The dish is finished with gremolata, a pungent garnish of lemon zest, garlic and parsley added at the end of cooking or at serving.

Serving and variations

Ossobuco is traditionally served with risotto alla milanese (saffron risotto) or polenta. Modern versions often include tomato, and variants are made with pork or beef shanks. The dish, first documented in Milan in the late 19th century, has become a classic of Italian restaurants worldwide; it must be made with veal shanks, which are also used in stocks and other braises.
