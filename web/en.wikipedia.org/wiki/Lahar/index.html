Lahar

Lahar
Type: Volcanic mudflow / debris flow
Word origin: Javanese (Indonesian)
Deadliest modern example: Armero, Colombia, 1985 (c. 23,000 deaths)

A lahar is a violent type of mudflow or debris flow composed of a slurry of pyroclastic material, rocky debris and water flowing down from a volcano, typically along a river valley. The term is of Javanese origin.

Causes

Lahars form when water — from a crater lake, glacier melt during an eruption, or intense rainfall on loose ash — mobilises volcanic sediment. They move at tens of kilometres per hour, can grow to ten times their initial volume by eroding material, and set almost like concrete when they stop.

Notable disasters

In 1985 an eruption of Nevado del Ruiz in Colombia melted its ice cap; lahars buried the town of Armero, killing about 23,000 people. Lahars destroyed settlements at Mount Pinatubo for years after its 1991 eruption, and monitoring systems now guard valleys below Mount Rainier and Ruapehu. See Volcano and Armero tragedy.
