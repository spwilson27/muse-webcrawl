Peninsula Shield Force

Peninsula Shield Force (Arabic: قوة درع الجزيرة)
Founded: 1984
Country: Gulf Cooperation Council member states
Type: Joint military force
Role: Collective defence of GCC states

Emblem of the Peninsula Shield Force

The Peninsula Shield Force (or Peninsula Shield) is the joint military force of the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC). It was established in 1984, three years after the GCC's founding, to deter and repel aggression against any member state of the council — Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates.

History

The force was created against the backdrop of the Iran–Iraq War, which raised fears among the Gulf monarchies of the conflict spilling over. Its headquarters were established at Hafar al-Batin in Saudi Arabia, near the borders with Iraq and Kuwait. Saudi Arabia has consistently contributed the largest contingent, and the force has historically numbered around 5,000–10,000 personnel drawn from the member states' armies.

Operations

Elements of the Peninsula Shield deployed to Kuwait during the 1990–91 Gulf crisis following Iraq's invasion. The force's most prominent operation came in March 2011, when Peninsula Shield units — primarily Saudi and Emirati troops — crossed the King Fahd Causeway into Bahrain at the request of the Bahraini government during the 2011 Bahraini uprising, guarding key installations while Bahraini security forces suppressed the protests.

In 2018, GCC leaders agreed to transform and expand the joint command structure, establishing a unified military command headquartered in Riyadh, reflecting ambitions for deeper defence integration.
