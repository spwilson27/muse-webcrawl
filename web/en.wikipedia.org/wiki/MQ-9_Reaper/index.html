The General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper (sometimes called Predator B) is an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV, or "drone") capable of remotely controlled or autonomous flight operations, developed by General Atomics Aeronautical Systems (GA-ASI) primarily for the United States Air Force (USAF). The MQ-9 and other UAVs are referred to as Remotely Piloted Vehicles/Aircraft (RPV/RPA) by the USAF to indicate ground control by humans.

The MQ-9 is a larger, heavier, and more capable aircraft than the earlier General Atomics MQ-1 Predator, and it can be controlled by the same ground systems. The Reaper has a 950-shaft-horsepower (712 kW) turboprop engine (compared to the Predator's 115 hp piston engine) and can carry 15 times more ordnance payload at nearly three times the cruise speed.

Design and operation

The aircraft is monitored and controlled, including weapons employment, by an aircrew in a Ground Control Station (GCS), communicating mainly via Ku-band satellite link, which permits operations flown by crews on the other side of the world from the theatre. Its sensor suite combines electro-optical/infrared cameras, a laser designator and rangefinder, and the Lynx II synthetic-aperture radar. Armament can include AGM-114 Hellfire missiles, GBU-12 Paveway II laser-guided bombs, and GBU-38 JDAMs, carried on seven hardpoints.

Typical endurance exceeds 27 hours for surveillance missions, with a service ceiling around 50,000 ft and a maximum speed of about 240 KTAS. The type is designed for long-endurance intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance (ISR), close air support, and precision strike.

Operational history

The Reaper entered US service in 2007 and has been deployed extensively in Afghanistan, Iraq, Syria, Yemen, Libya and Somalia. It was used for the January 2020 strike at Baghdad airport that killed Iranian general Qasem Soleimani and the July 2022 strike that killed al-Qaeda leader Ayman al-Zawahiri in Kabul. Export operators include the United Kingdom (as Protector in its successor variant), France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, India and others. A specialised variant, the MQ-9B SkyGuardian/SeaGuardian, underlies several follow-on programs.
