The National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR) is the United Kingdom's largest funder of health and social care research, financed by the Department of Health and Social Care. Its mission, summarised as "improving the health and wealth of the nation through research", is to fund, enable and deliver research that improves treatment and care in the NHS and public health and social care systems.

Work

The NIHR funds research programmes — including Health Technology Assessment, Efficacy and Mechanism Evaluation, and Health and Social Care Delivery Research — and supports infrastructure across England: Biomedical Research Centres at major teaching hospitals, Clinical Research Facilities, the Clinical Research Network that recruits patients into trials in NHS settings, and dedicated units for patient safety, blood and transplant, and health protection research. It also trains researchers through fellowships and the NIHR Academy. During the COVID-19 pandemic it funded and delivered landmark platform trials such as RECOVERY, which identified dexamethasone as a life-saving treatment.

Name change

Founded in 2006 as the National Institute for Health Research following the Cooksey review of UK health research, it adopted its present name in 2022, adding "and Care" to underline its remit across social care as well as medicine. It works in partnership with the devolved administrations' research funders in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
