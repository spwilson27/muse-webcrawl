Engineering tolerance

Engineering tolerance is the permissible limit of variation in a physical dimension, a measured value, or a property of a manufactured object. Tolerances are specified by designers so that parts fit and function correctly while recognising that no manufacturing process achieves perfect dimensions.

Principles

A dimension is usually given as a nominal value plus an allowable deviation, such as 25.00 ± 0.05 mm, or by upper and lower limits. Tighter tolerances generally increase cost sharply because they demand slower machining, better machines, and more inspection. The international framework for fits between shafts and holes is set by the ISO limits and fits system (ISO 286), which defines tolerance grades (IT grades) and fundamental deviations.

Tolerance stack-up

When assemblies combine many parts, individual tolerances accumulate — the tolerance stack. Engineers analyse worst-case and statistical (root-sum-square) accumulation to guarantee clearance and function.

Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing

Beyond size, geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T, standardized in ASME Y14.5 and ISO GPS standards) controls form, orientation, location, and runout of features using symbols such as flatness, perpendicularity, and position, referenced to datums. Closely related is the idea of an engineering fit — clearance, transition, or interference — chosen according to how mating parts must move or be fixed.
