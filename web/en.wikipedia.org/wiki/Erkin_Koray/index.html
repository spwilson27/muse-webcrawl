Erkin Koray

Erkin Koray (24 June 1941 – 7 August 2023) was a Turkish singer-songwriter and guitarist, a pioneer of Anatolian rock.

Erkin Koray (24 June 1941 – 7 August 2023) was a Turkish rock guitarist, singer and composer widely regarded as the father of Turkish rock music. A central figure of Anatolian rock, he fused Turkish folk melodies and Arabic-influenced scales with psychedelic rock, helping to pioneer the use of the electric bağlama and pioneering fuzz and phase effects in Turkish music.

Career

Born in Istanbul, Koray formed his first band as a schoolboy in the late 1950s, playing covers of Elvis Presley and Fats Domino. His early singles in the 1960s were among the first Turkish-language rock recordings. In the 1970s he released landmark work including the album Elektronik Türküler (1974), "Electronic Folk Songs", and songs such as "Şaşkın", "Fesuphanallah", "Estarabim", "Sevince" and "Çöpçüler". His adaptation of the Indian-flavoured "Şaşkın" and his experiments with tape echo and feedback made him a countercultural icon, though his long hair and dress drew hostility, and he later recalled being attacked and stabbed because of it.

Later life and legacy

Koray's popularity surged again in the 1980s and 1990s, and his 1970s recordings were rediscovered internationally by collectors of psychedelic music, leading to reissue compilations. He emigrated to Canada, living in Vancouver, and died there in August 2023 aged 82. He is remembered alongside Barış Manço and Cem Karaca as one of the founding figures of Turkish rock.
