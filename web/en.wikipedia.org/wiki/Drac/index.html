Drac

This article is about the river in France. For other uses, see Drac (disambiguation).

The Drac is a 130-kilometre (81 mi) long river in southeastern France. It is a left tributary of the Isère, which it joins at Grenoble. Its drainage basin is about 3,350 square kilometres (1,290 sq mi).

Course

The Drac rises in the Écrins massif in the French Alps, in the département of Hautes-Alpes. It flows generally northwest, passing through the Champsaur valley and the Lac du Sautet reservoir, created by the Sautet Dam. It continues through a deep gorge, receives its main tributary the Romanche near Champ-sur-Drac, and joins the Isère at Grenoble in the département of Isère.

Name and folklore

In local folklore, the "Drac" is also a mischievous water spirit or dragon of Occitan legend, a shape-shifting creature said to inhabit rivers and lure the unwary — tales of the drac are widespread in the folklore of Languedoc and Provence.
