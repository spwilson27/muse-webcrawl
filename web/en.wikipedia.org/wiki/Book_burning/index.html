Book burning

Book burning is the deliberate destruction by fire of books or other written materials, usually carried out in a public context. The burning of books represents an element of censorship and usually proceeds from a cultural, religious, or political opposition to the material in question. Book burning can be an act of contempt for the author or the contents of a book, or it can be an act of religious, cultural, or political censorship.

History

Book burning has a long history. In 213 BC, the Chinese emperor Qin Shi Huang ordered the burning of books and burying of scholars, an episode remembered as a notorious act of suppression intended to consolidate ideological control. In the ancient and medieval worlds, libraries such as the Library of Alexandria suffered destruction, though often through warfare rather than deliberate censorship decrees.

In modern times, the Nazi book burnings of 1933, in which works by Jewish, pacifist, and other authors deemed "un-German" were burned in public squares, became a symbol of totalitarian censorship. Book burnings and bans continue to be reported in various countries, often sparking debates about freedom of expression. The practice has also extended symbolically to the destruction of other media, such as records, videotapes, and digital files.
