Police misconduct

Police misconduct is inappropriate conduct and illegal actions taken by police officers in connection with their official duties. Types of misconduct include wrongful arrests, false confessions, coerced testimony, perjury, evidence tampering, police brutality, corruption, racial profiling and sexual offences. Police misconduct can generate false leads, wrongful convictions and a loss of public trust in law enforcement.

Causes

Researchers attribute misconduct to a range of factors: weak oversight, a "code of silence" among officers, stressful working conditions, recruitment and training failures, and, in some forces, institutional cultures tolerant of corruption. The Knapp Commission, which investigated corruption in the New York City Police Department in the 1970s, famously distinguished between "grass-eaters", who passively accepted bribes, and "meat-eaters", who aggressively sought corrupt opportunities.

Oversight

Oversight mechanisms include internal affairs departments, civilian review boards, independent complaints bodies such as the Independent Office for Police Conduct in England and Wales, body-worn cameras, and criminal prosecution. In the United States, federal pattern-or-practice investigations and consent decrees have been used to reform departments found to engage in systemic misconduct.
