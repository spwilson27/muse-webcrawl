Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti (Georgian: სამეგრელო-ზემო სვანეთი) is a region (mkhare) in western Georgia, created in 1995 by joining the historic province of Samegrelo (Mingrelia) with Zemo Svaneti (Upper Svaneti) in the high Caucasus. Its capital and largest city is Zugdidi; other significant towns include the Black Sea port of Poti, Senaki, and Mestia, the administrative centre of mountainous Upper Svaneti.

The region extends from the Black Sea coast at Poti to the main ridge of the Greater Caucasus in Upper Svaneti.

Geography

The region spans strikingly varied terrain: the low, humid Kolkheti plain of Samegrelo along the Black Sea, rising through forested foothills to the alpine valleys of Upper Svaneti, which include some of the highest inhabited settlements in Europe, such as Ushguli — a UNESCO World Heritage site noted for its medieval defensive towers. The Enguri river, with its great arch dam and hydroelectric station near the administrative boundary with Abkhazia, is a major feature of the region.

Subdivisions

The region is divided into the city of Poti and eight municipalities: Abasha, Zugdidi, Martvili, Mestia, Senaki, Chkhorotsku, Tsalenjikha, and Khobi. Regional administration is headed by a state representative (governor) appointed by the central government.

Demographics and culture

The population is predominantly ethnic Georgian, comprising Mingrelians in the lowlands and Svans in the mountains; both speak distinct Kartvelian languages (Mingrelian and Svan) alongside Georgian. The region hosts large numbers of internally displaced persons from Abkhazia. Notable sights include the Dadiani palace museum in Zugdidi, the Martvili canyon and monastery, Kolkheti National Park, and the towers and churches of Mestia and Ushguli.
