Spatha

The spatha was a type of straight and long sword used in the Roman Empire from the 1st to the 6th centuries AD, and later by the peoples of the Migration Period. Longer than the gladius, the spatha typically measured 0.5 to 1 metre, with the blade accounting for most of its length.

History

The spatha was originally a cavalry weapon, adapted from the long swords of the Celts. Its extra reach suited mounted combat, and during the later Roman period it was adopted by infantry as well, gradually replacing the shorter gladius as the standard Roman sidearm. The changing style of Roman warfare, with greater emphasis on cavalry and on fighting enemies with longer weapons, favoured the longer blade.

Design

Spathae had a double-edged blade, a short hilt, and were worn on the left or right side, suspended from a baldric or belt. Surviving examples are found across the former empire and in Germanic graves beyond the frontier, where the spatha became the prestige sword of the early Middle Ages and an ancestor of the medieval knightly sword.
