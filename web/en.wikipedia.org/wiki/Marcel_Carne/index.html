Marcel Carné (18 August 1906 – 31 October 1996) was a French film director. A key figure of the poetic realism movement of the 1930s, he is best known for his collaborations with screenwriter Jacques Prévert, including Port of Shadows (1938), Le Jour se lève (1939) and Children of Paradise (1945), the latter frequently ranked among the greatest films ever made.

Career

Carné began as a film critic and assistant to Jacques Feyder. His early successes, Jenny (1936) and Drôle de drame (1937), established the poetic realist style. During the German occupation of France he made Les Visiteurs du soir (1942) and Children of Paradise (1945), shot under difficult conditions in Nice and Paris.

After the war his work fell out of fashion with the critics of the French New Wave, who dismissed his studio-bound craftsmanship as the "tradition of quality", although films such as Thérèse Raquin (1953) and Les Tricheurs (1958) were popular with audiences.
