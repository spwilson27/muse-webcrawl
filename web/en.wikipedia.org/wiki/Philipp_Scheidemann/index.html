Philipp Scheidemann

Born: July 26, 1865, Kassel, Electorate of Hesse
Died: November 29, 1939, Copenhagen, Denmark
Office: Chancellor of Germany (1919)
Party: Social Democratic Party (SPD)

Scheidemann proclaimed the German republic on November 9, 1918.

Philipp Heinrich Scheidemann (26 July 1865 – 29 November 1939) was a German politician of the Social Democratic Party of Germany (SPD) who proclaimed the German republic on 9 November 1918 and in 1919 briefly served as the country's first chancellor under the Weimar constitution's forerunner government.

Proclamation of the republic

A printer by trade and a longtime Reichstag deputy and SPD leader, Scheidemann served as a minister without portfolio in Prince Max of Baden's government in October 1918. As revolution swept Germany at the end of the First World War, and fearing a communist seizure of power, he stepped onto a balcony of the Reichstag in Berlin on 9 November 1918 and, without authorisation, proclaimed Germany a republic — hours before Karl Liebknecht proclaimed a rival "free socialist republic" from the Berlin Palace.

Chancellorship and later life

After elections to the National Assembly, Scheidemann became Minister President (Chancellor) of the coalition government in February 1919. He resigned in June of that year in protest at the Treaty of Versailles, famously declaring that the hand should wither that signed such a treaty. He later served as mayor of Kassel and as an SPD elder statesman, fleeing to Denmark after the Nazi takeover in 1933, where he died in 1939.
