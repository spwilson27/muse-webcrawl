Leaf blower

A leaf blower, commonly known as a blower, is a device that propels air out of a nozzle to move debris such as leaves and grass cuttings from lawns, paths and driveways. Leaf blowers are powered by electric or gasoline motors and exist as hand-held units, backpack-mounted units, and larger wheeled "walk-behind" models.

History

The leaf blower was developed from agricultural crop-dusting equipment; the Japanese company Echo is credited with introducing a backpack power blower in the 1970s, followed by hand-held models later that decade. The devices spread quickly in North America and elsewhere because they clear large areas faster than raking.

Criticism and regulation

Leaf blowers, especially two-stroke gasoline models, have been criticized for noise, air pollution and the dispersal of dust and allergens. Many municipalities have restricted their hours of use, noise levels or banned gasoline-powered models entirely in favour of electric versions.
