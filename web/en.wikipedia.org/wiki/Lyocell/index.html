Lyocell

Lyocell
Type: Regenerated cellulose fibre
Best known brand: Tencel (Lenzing)
Source material: Wood pulp
First commercial production: 1992

Lyocell is a form of regenerated cellulose fibre made from dissolving wood pulp using dry jet-wet spinning. It was developed beginning in 1972 and first commercialized in 1992. The best-known brand name is Tencel, produced by the Austrian company Lenzing AG.

Production

In the lyocell process, wood pulp is dissolved directly in N-methylmorpholine N-oxide (NMMO), a comparatively benign organic solvent, without the chemical derivatization used in the older viscose process. The solution is extruded through spinnerets into a water bath, where the cellulose regenerates as filament. The solvent is recovered in a closed loop at rates above 99%, giving lyocell a reputation as one of the more environmentally friendly manufactured fibres.

Properties and uses

Lyocell fibres are strong — stronger than viscose rayon, especially when wet — absorbent, smooth, and comfortable next to the skin. They are biodegradable and used in clothing, denim, bedding, towels, medical dressings, and nonwoven products. A tendency to fibrillate can be exploited for peach-skin finishes or controlled by enzyme treatments.
