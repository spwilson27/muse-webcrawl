Duckweed

Duckweed is the common name for the smallest flowering plants, the subfamily Lemnoideae of the arum family Araceae. The plants float on or just below the surface of still and slow-moving fresh water worldwide. Genera include Lemna (common duckweeds), Spirodela (greater duckweeds), Landoltia, Wolffia (watermeals, the smallest flowering plants known, about a millimetre across and lacking roots), and Wolffiella.

Biology

A duckweed plant consists of a flattened thallus or frond, often with a single simple root hanging in the water. They reproduce mostly vegetatively, by budding, and can double in biomass in a few days under warm, nutrient-rich conditions, quickly forming dense mats. Flowering is rare; the flowers are minute and unisexual. Duckweeds survive winter by sinking or as turions on the bottom.

Ecology and uses

Duckweed mats shelter aquatic invertebrates and are eaten by waterfowl and fish, but excessive growth can shade out submerged plants and deplete oxygen. Because of their fast growth and high protein content (up to roughly 40% dry weight in good conditions), duckweeds are used and researched for wastewater treatment, animal and fish feed, biofuel feedstock, phytoremediation, and even as a human food (e.g. Wolffia eaten in parts of Southeast Asia). They are also standard model organisms in ecotoxicology tests, and Lemna growth tests are widely used to assess water quality.
