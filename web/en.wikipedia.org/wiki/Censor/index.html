Censor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A censor is an official or office responsible for examining books, films, plays, letters, news reports and other material before publication or distribution, and for suppressing or removing anything considered obscene, politically unacceptable, or a threat to security. The practice of such an office is known as censorship.

Origins of the term

The word derives from the censor of ancient Rome, a magistrate who conducted the census and supervised public morality (the regimen morum); the censor could note a mark of disgrace (the nota censoria) against a citizen's name. From this supervisory function over morals, the term passed to officials charged with oversight of the press and stage.

Historical offices

In early modern Europe, pre-publication censorship was exercised by church and state officials; in England the Master of the Revels licensed plays, while printing was controlled through bodies such as the Stationers' Company and licensing acts. In Imperial China, the Censorate was an agency of inspectors charged with monitoring officials and reporting misconduct to the emperor. In the twentieth century, state censors operated extensively in wartime and in authoritarian states, reviewing mail, journalism, film and literature.

Modern usage

Where formal offices of censor no longer exist, the term is still used for officials who classify or restrict media, such as film classification boards, and more loosely for any person or institution that suppresses expression. In psychoanalysis, "censor" is used metaphorically for the agency that disguises repressed material in dreams.
