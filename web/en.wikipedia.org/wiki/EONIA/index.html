EONIA

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

EONIA tracked the average rate on unsecured overnight euro lending between banks.

EONIA (Euro Overnight Index Average) was a reference interest rate representing the weighted average of all unsecured overnight lending transactions in the euro area interbank market. It was published from 1999 and for two decades served as the euro area's standard overnight benchmark, underpinning a large volume of interest-rate swaps and other derivatives. Following benchmark reforms after the LIBOR scandal, EONIA was reformed in 2019 and then discontinued, its last publication being on 3 January 2022; it was replaced by the euro short-term rate (€STR).

Calculation and administration

EONIA was calculated by the European Central Bank on the basis of lending data reported by a panel of banks, and published by the European Money Markets Institute (EMMI) as administrator. In the years before its retirement the underlying market thinned considerably: volumes fell and contributions became concentrated among fewer banks, one of the reasons regulators judged the rate unsustainable under the EU Benchmarks Regulation.

Replacement by €STR

The ECB began publishing the euro short-term rate (€STR) on 2 October 2019. €STR is based on actual transaction data from the ECB's money market statistical reporting, covering both bank and non-bank counterparties, and is designed to be a more robust, regulation-compliant benchmark. From October 2019 until its discontinuation, EONIA was recalibrated as €STR plus a fixed spread of 8.5 basis points, to give markets a smooth transition. Contracts referencing EONIA were encouraged to migrate to €STR, and the overnight indexed swap market largely switched during 2021.
