Snaefell Mountain Railway

The Snaefell Mountain Railway is an electric mountain railway on the Isle of Man, connecting the village of Laxey with the summit of Snaefell, at 2,036 feet (621 m) the island's highest point. Opened in 1895, it is the only electric mountain railway in the British Isles and remains a popular tourist attraction operated as part of the Isle of Man's heritage railways.

Route and operation

The line runs for about 5.5 miles (8.9 km) from Laxey, where it connects with the Manx Electric Railway, climbing steadily to the Snaefell summit station. Its gauge is 3 ft 6 in (1,067 mm), wider than the island's other railways, chosen to accommodate the Fell centre rail used for braking on the steep gradients — the cars are equipped with a Fell braking system engaging a central rail as a safety measure. The line is electrified at 550 volts DC from overhead wires, and the journey takes about 30 minutes. On a clear day, passengers at the summit are traditionally said to see seven kingdoms: England, Scotland, Wales, Ireland, the Isle of Man, and the kingdoms of heaven and the sea (Neptune).

Rolling stock

The railway operates six wooden-bodied tram cars built for the line's opening in 1895 by George F. Milnes & Co., numbered 1 to 6. Remarkably, the original cars remain in service, extensively rebuilt over their long lives. A seventh vehicle, a diesel railcar and works unit, supports maintenance.

History

The line was promoted by the Snaefell Mountain Railway Association and built quickly in 1895, opening on 21 August of that year. It later passed to the Manx Electric Railway company and, after that company's collapse, into public ownership in 1957. Operation is seasonal, typically from spring to autumn. The railway was the first mountain line in the British Isles to climb with electric traction throughout.
