Albert Campion

Albert Campion
Created by: Margery Allingham
First appearance: The Crime at Black Dudley (1929)
Occupation: Adventurer, detective
Family: Of aristocratic, deliberately obscure background

Albert Campion is a fictional character in a series of detective novels and short stories by Margery Allingham. He first appeared as a supporting character in The Crime at Black Dudley (1929) and became the protagonist of 18 novels and many short stories.

Character

Campion is a thin, blond young man with large horn-rimmed glasses and a deceptively blank, amiable expression that conceals a sharp intelligence. His full background is intentionally mysterious: he was born in 1900 into an aristocratic family, educated at Rugby and (possibly) St John's College, Cambridge, and uses several aliases. He lives at 17A Bottle Street, Piccadilly, London, attended by his manservant Magersfontein Lugg, a hulking reformed burglar.

Development

Originally a comic, Bertie Wooster-like figure modelled in part as a parody of Dorothy L. Sayers's Lord Peter Wimsey, Campion matured across the series into a more serious and psychologically complex detective, especially in novels such as The Tiger in the Smoke (1952). He marries Amanda Fitton, an aircraft engineer, and has a son, Rupert.

After Allingham's death in 1966, her husband Philip Youngman Carter completed and wrote further Campion books; Mike Ripley later continued the series with Allingham estate approval.

Adaptations

Campion was adapted by the BBC in 1959–1960 (played by Bernard Horsfall and Brian Smith?) and most famously in the 1989–1990 series Campion starring Peter Davison, with Brian Glover as Lugg.
