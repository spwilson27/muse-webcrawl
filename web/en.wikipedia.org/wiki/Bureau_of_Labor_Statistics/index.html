The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) is a unit of the United States Department of Labor and the principal fact-finding agency for the U.S. federal government in the broad field of labor economics and statistics. It collects, processes, analyzes, and disseminates statistical data on employment, unemployment, prices and inflation, wages, productivity, and occupational safety and health.

History

The bureau was founded in 1884 as the Bureau of Labor within the Department of the Interior, championed by Commissioner Carroll D. Wright. It became an independent department in 1888, and in 1913 it was transferred to the newly created Department of Labor and renamed the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

Major statistical programs

  • Consumer Price Index (CPI) — the most widely used measure of inflation in the United States.
  • Employment Situation — the monthly report containing the unemployment rate (from the Current Population Survey) and nonfarm payroll employment (from the Current Employment Statistics survey).
  • Producer Price Index (PPI) — prices received by domestic producers.
  • Employment Cost Index (ECI) — wages, salaries, and benefits.
  • Current Population Survey (CPS) — conducted jointly with the Census Bureau.
  • JOLTS — Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey.
  • Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) and the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW).

Commissioners

The Commissioner of Labor Statistics serves a four-year term and is appointed by the President with Senate confirmation. Notable commissioners have included Carroll D. Wright, Ethelbert Stewart, Janet Norwood, Katharine Abraham, Keith Hall, Erica Groshen, William Beach, and Erika McEntarfer.
