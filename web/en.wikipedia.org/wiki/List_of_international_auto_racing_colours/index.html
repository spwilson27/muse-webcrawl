List of international auto racing colours

International auto racing colours are the traditional national paint schemes that racing cars were required to carry in international competition. From the early 20th century until the late 1960s, cars competing in events such as the Gordon Bennett Cup and later Grands Prix were painted in colours assigned to their country of origin rather than to their sponsors. The system was formalised by the Association Internationale des Automobile Clubs Reconnus (AIACR), the forerunner of the FIA.

The practice began with the 1900 Gordon Bennett Cup, in which Count Eliot Zborowski suggested that each competing nation be assigned a distinct colour. Although sponsorship liveries replaced national colours in Formula One in 1968, the traditional schemes remain widely recognised and are still used in some series.

History

The national colour scheme was introduced at the first Gordon Bennett Cup in 1900, with France in blue, Germany in white, Britain in green (initially olive) and the United States in red (later changed). Belgium raced in yellow. Italy adopted red after Count Vincenzo Florio's car, painted red for the 1907 Peking to Paris race, proved popular.

Britain's green is often linked to the 1903 race held in Ireland because racing was banned on public roads in Great Britain; the cars were painted shamrock green in gratitude. German teams famously stripped the white paint from their Mercedes-Benz W25 in 1934 to meet a weight limit, revealing the aluminium bodywork and beginning the Silver Arrows legend.

The FIA formally abandoned compulsory national colours in Formula One for the 1968 season, allowing teams to use sponsor liveries. National colours survived longest in sports car racing and are still referenced in modern design.
