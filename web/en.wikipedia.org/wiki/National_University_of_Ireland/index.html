National University of Ireland

The National University of Ireland (NUI) (Irish: Ollscoil na hÉireann) is a federal university system in Ireland, headquartered at 49 Merrion Square, Dublin. Established under the Irish Universities Act 1908, it comprises four constituent universities — University College Dublin, University College Cork, the University of Galway, and Maynooth University — together with recognised colleges including the Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland.

Structure

The Chancellor of the NUI is its ceremonial head; since 2013 the office has been held by Maurice Manning. The constituent universities are autonomous institutions with their own governing authorities, but degrees are awarded by the NUI and the degrees of its constituent universities carry the NUI designation. The NUI Senate, which includes elected graduates, oversees matriculation requirements and the awarding of degrees and honorary degrees.

History

The NUI succeeded the Royal University of Ireland, an examining body on the model of the University of London. It was formed to provide university education accessible to Ireland's Catholic majority, who had largely been excluded from Trinity College Dublin in practice. Its graduates have played major roles in Irish public life, and the NUI's graduate electorate elects four members of Seanad Éireann, the upper house of the Irish parliament, alongside three elected by the University of Dublin.
