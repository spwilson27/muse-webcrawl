Blood Brothers is a musical with book, lyrics and music by Willy Russell. It is one of the longest-running musical productions in the history of London's West End, where its revival ran for more than 24 years, from 1988 until its closure in November 2012, playing over 10,000 performances. It originated as a school play written by Russell in the early 1980s before being developed into a full musical.

Synopsis

The story is set in Liverpool and follows twin brothers, Mickey and Edward (Eddie), who are separated at birth. Their mother, Mrs Johnstone, is a working-class woman who, already struggling to support her existing children, is persuaded by her wealthy employer, Mrs Lyons, to give one of the twins to her. Mickey is raised in poverty by Mrs Johnstone, while Edward grows up in the comfortable Lyons household. Despite the adults' efforts to keep them apart, the boys meet by chance as children and, unaware of their relationship, become best friends — "blood brothers". As they grow up, the differences in their upbringings drive a wedge between them: Edward goes to university and becomes a councillor, while Mickey loses his job, turns to crime and serves a prison sentence. Both fall in love with the same woman, Linda. The tragedy culminates when the truth of their relationship is revealed, and the twins die on the same day.

Themes

The musical is widely read as a commentary on the British class system and on the influence of nature versus nurture. It asks whether the brothers' fates are determined by superstition and destiny or by the social circumstances into which they are born. A Narrator presides over the action, frequently returning to the theme of superstition and the idea that a debt must eventually be paid.

Productions

The original production opened at the Liverpool Playhouse in 1983 and transferred to London's Lyric Theatre the same year, winning the Olivier Award for Best New Musical. A revival produced by Bill Kenwright opened in the West End in 1988 and ran at the Phoenix Theatre until 2012. A Broadway production opened in 1993 and ran for over 800 performances. The show has toured extensively in the United Kingdom and internationally, and remains a staple of regional and school productions.

Musical numbers

Well-known songs from the score include "Marilyn Monroe", "Easy Terms", "Bright New Day", "I'm Not Saying a Word" and the closing number "Tell Me It's Not True".
