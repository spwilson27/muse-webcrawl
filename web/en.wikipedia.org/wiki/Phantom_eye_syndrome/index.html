Phantom eye syndrome

Phantom eye syndrome is a condition in which patients experience sensations — including pain, visual hallucinations, or the feeling that the eye is still present — after removal of an eye (enucleation, evisceration, or exenteration). It is analogous to phantom limb pain after amputation.

Symptoms

Studies report that a substantial proportion of patients experience phantom phenomena: non-painful sensations of the removed eye, visual phenomena such as flashes or formed images, and phantom pain in the empty socket. Symptoms often begin soon after surgery and may lessen over time, though pain can persist and is associated with pre-operative eye pain, anxiety, and depression.

Mechanisms and treatment

The syndrome is thought to arise from changes in the visual and somatosensory pathways and cortical reorganisation following loss of input. Management focuses on reassurance, treatment of socket problems, prosthesis fitting, and, for pain, analgesics and therapies used for other neuropathic and phantom pain conditions.
