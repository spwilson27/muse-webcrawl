Blue wildebeest

Scientific nameConnochaetes taurinus
FamilyBovidae
RangeSouthern and eastern Africa
Conservation statusLeast Concern

The blue wildebeest (Connochaetes taurinus), also called the common wildebeest, white-bearded wildebeest or brindled gnu, is a large antelope native to eastern and southern Africa. It is one of two wildebeest species, the other being the black wildebeest. Adults stand about 1.15–1.45 m at the shoulder and weigh 118–290 kg. The coat is bluish-grey with darker vertical stripes on the neck and flanks, and both sexes carry curved horns.

Migration

The species is famous for the great migration of the Serengeti–Masai Mara ecosystem, in which roughly 1.5 million blue wildebeest, accompanied by zebras and gazelles, move in a vast annual circuit following seasonal rains and grazing, crossing crocodile-infested rivers such as the Mara. Other populations migrate in the Kalahari and around Liuwa Plain in Zambia, while some herds are resident year-round.

Ecology

Blue wildebeest are grazers specialised on short grasses, and they in turn are principal prey for lions, spotted hyenas, leopards and crocodiles. During the rut, males establish temporary territories and display to attract females; calves are born in a synchronised pulse at the start of the rains and can stand within minutes. Although local declines have occurred through fencing, habitat loss and poaching, the species remains abundant and is classified as Least Concern.
