Day of Remembrance (Japanese Americans)

The Day of Remembrance is an annual commemoration held by Japanese American communities in the United States on or near 19 February, the anniversary of the signing of Executive Order 9066 by President Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1942. The order authorised the forced removal and incarceration of some 120,000 people of Japanese ancestry — two-thirds of them US citizens — from the West Coast during the Second World War.

Origins

The first Days of Remembrance were organised in Seattle and Portland in November 1978 by the Seattle Evacuation Redress Committee and local activists, several of them Sansei (third-generation) organisers such as Frank Chin and Henry Miyatake, who had been pressing the Japanese American Citizens League to campaign for redress. The Seattle event drew more than 2,000 people to the Puyallup fairgrounds — the site of the wartime "Camp Harmony" assembly centre — from which a caravan drove to the state capitol to demand reparations. The commemoration moved to February in subsequent years to mark the date of the executive order itself.

Role in the redress movement

Days of Remembrance became organising engines of the redress movement: occasions to educate the public, collect testimony from former incarcerees, and press Congress for an official apology and payments. The campaign culminated in the Civil Liberties Act of 1988, which apologised for the incarceration and authorised $20,000 payments to surviving incarcerees. After redress, the events continued as acts of memory and as platforms for solidarity — in recent years often linking the incarceration to the treatment of Muslim, Arab and immigrant communities after 9/11 and to contemporary detention policies, under the banner "Never Again Is Now".

Observance

Observances range from solemn ceremonies at former camp sites such as Manzanar and Tule Lake — also the destinations of annual pilgrimages — to lectures, film screenings, taiko performances and interfaith services at universities, museums and state capitols. Several states and cities formally recognise the day, and presidents have issued Day of Remembrance messages; in 2022, on the 80th anniversary, President Joe Biden issued a formal proclamation honouring the day.
