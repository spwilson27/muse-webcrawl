The First Canadian Army (French: 1re Armée canadienne) was a field army and the senior formation of the Canadian Army that served in northwest Europe during the Second World War. Formed in England in 1942, it was the largest army ever commanded by a Canadian officer, and at its peak included British, Polish, American, Belgian, and Dutch units alongside its two Canadian corps.

Operations

The army became operational in Normandy in July 1944 under General Harry Crerar. It fought in the closing of the Falaise Pocket, cleared the Channel ports, and undertook the bitter Battle of the Scheldt to open the port of Antwerp. In 1945 it led Operation Veritable into the Rhineland and liberated much of the northern Netherlands before the German surrender; it was disbanded in 1946.

Composition

Its principal formations were I Canadian Corps (transferred from Italy in early 1945) and II Canadian Corps, together comprising five Canadian divisions plus attached Allied formations.
