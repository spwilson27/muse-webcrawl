Wiesbaden is the capital and second-largest city of the German state of Hesse, after Frankfurt am Main, and lies on the east bank of the Rhine opposite Mainz. It is one of Europe's oldest spa towns, with 26 hot springs known since Roman times, when a fort and settlement called Aquae Mattiacorum stood here.

History

Wiesbaden flourished in the 19th century as a fashionable spa and gambling resort for European nobility and wealthy visitors; Fyodor Dostoevsky gambled at its casino and drew on the experience for his novella The Gambler. The grand Kurhaus (1907) remains a landmark. The city survived the Second World War with relatively little damage and became capital of the newly formed state of Hesse in 1945. It hosts the Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA) and the Federal Statistical Office, and a significant US Army garrison (Lucius D. Clay Kaserne).
