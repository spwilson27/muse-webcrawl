Jane Addams

Laura Jane Addams (September 6, 1860 – May 21, 1935) was an American settlement activist, reformer, social worker, sociologist, public administrator, philosopher and author. She was a leader in the history of social work and women's suffrage, and an advocate for world peace. She co-founded Hull House, one of America's most famous settlement houses, in Chicago in 1889. In 1910 Addams was awarded an honorary master of arts degree from Yale University, becoming the first woman to receive an honorary degree from the school. In 1931 she became the first American woman to be awarded the Nobel Peace Prize.

Early life

Born in Cedarville, Illinois, Addams graduated from Rockford Female Seminary (now Rockford University) in 1881. Travels in Europe, including a visit to Toynbee Hall in London's East End, inspired her to establish a similar settlement in an immigrant neighbourhood of Chicago.

Hull House

In 1889 Addams and Ellen Gates Starr rented the Charles Hull mansion on Halsted Street. Hull House provided kindergarten and day care, adult education, an art gallery, a public kitchen, baths, and meeting space for unions and clubs, serving thousands of mostly immigrant neighbours weekly. Residents of Hull House included future reform leaders such as Florence Kelley, Alice Hamilton and Julia Lathrop, and its work informed campaigns for child-labour laws, factory inspection, juvenile courts and eight-hour workdays.

Peace activism and later career

Addams was a founder of the Woman's Peace Party and the Women's International League for Peace and Freedom (1919), and she presided over the 1915 International Congress of Women at The Hague. Her pacifism during the First World War cost her popularity at home but she continued relief work through the 1920s. Her books include Democracy and Social Ethics (1902), Twenty Years at Hull-House (1910) and Peace and Bread in Time of War (1922).

Legacy

Addams is regarded as a founder of the social-work profession in the United States and a pioneer of applied sociology; Hull House's remaining buildings are preserved as the Jane Addams Hull-House Museum at the University of Illinois Chicago.
