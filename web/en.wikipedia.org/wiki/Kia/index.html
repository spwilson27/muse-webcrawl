Kia

Kia Corporation (Korean: 기아), formerly Kia Motors, is a South Korean multinational automobile manufacturer headquartered in Seoul. Founded in 1944 as a maker of steel tubing and bicycle parts, it is South Korea's second-largest carmaker after Hyundai Motor Company, which holds a major stake in it.

History

Kia produced Korea's first domestic bicycle in 1951 and later built motorcycles, trucks and cars. It entered the United States market in the 1990s. After bankruptcy during the 1997 Asian financial crisis, Kia was acquired in part by Hyundai, forming the Hyundai-Kia group. The brand undertook a major design-led revival in the 2000s under chief design officer Peter Schreyer and rebranded in 2021 with a new logo and the name Kia Corporation, emphasising electric vehicles such as the EV6 and EV9.

Operations

Kia sells several million vehicles annually through a global network, with factories in South Korea, the United States, Slovakia, Mexico, India, China and elsewhere. Its line-up spans small cars, sedans, SUVs, and a growing range of battery-electric models.
