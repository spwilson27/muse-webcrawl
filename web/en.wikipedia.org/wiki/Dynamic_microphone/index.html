Dynamic microphone

A handheld dynamic microphone, the workhorse of live sound reinforcement.

A dynamic microphone is a type of microphone that converts sound into an electrical signal using electromagnetic induction. A lightweight coil of wire attached to the diaphragm is suspended in the field of a permanent magnet; when sound waves vibrate the diaphragm, the coil moves within the magnetic field and generates a corresponding voltage. The design is sometimes called a moving-coil microphone.

Characteristics

Dynamic microphones need no external power, are mechanically rugged, tolerate very high sound-pressure levels and resist moisture and rough handling. Their moving mass gives them a somewhat limited high-frequency response and lower sensitivity than condenser microphones, but this robustness makes them the standard choice for live vocals, guitar amplifiers and drums. Classic examples include the Shure SM57 and SM58 (the latter long a standard for stage singing) and large broadcast models such as the Electro-Voice RE20.

Ribbon microphones

Ribbon microphones are a related dynamic design: a thin corrugated metal ribbon itself vibrates in the magnetic field and generates the signal. Traditional ribbon microphones are fragile and prized for a warm, smooth sound in studio recording; modern versions are considerably more robust.
