Thomas DeCarlo Callaway-Burton (born 30 May 1975), known professionally as CeeLo Green (or simply CeeLo), is an American singer, songwriter, rapper, record producer and actor. He first came to prominence as a member of the Atlanta hip hop group Goodie Mob, part of the Dungeon Family collective, before achieving international fame as one half of the duo Gnarls Barkley with producer Danger Mouse.

Career

With Goodie Mob, CeeLo released the albums Soul Food (1995), Still Standing (1998) and World Party (1999), and contributed the distinctive hook to several Southern hip hop tracks. After solo albums in 2002 and 2004, he formed Gnarls Barkley; their 2006 single "Crazy" topped charts worldwide and was named by Rolling Stone among the greatest songs of the decade. Gnarls Barkley's albums St. Elsewhere (2006) and The Odd Couple (2008) followed.

His 2010 solo album The Lady Killer produced the hit single "Fuck You" (also released in a censored version as "Forget You"), which won the Grammy Award for Best Urban/Alternative Performance and was nominated for Record and Song of the Year. From 2011 to 2014 he was a coach on the American television singing competition The Voice. He co-wrote the Pussycat Dolls' hit "Don't Cha" and has contributed songs and voice acting to films and animation.
