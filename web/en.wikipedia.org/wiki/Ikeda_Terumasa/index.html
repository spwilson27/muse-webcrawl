Ikeda Terumasa (池田 輝政; 31 January 1565 – 16 March 1613) was a Japanese daimyō of the early Edo period. A veteran commander who served Oda Nobunaga, Toyotomi Hideyoshi and Tokugawa Ieyasu in turn, he is best remembered as the builder of the great keep of Himeji Castle, the finest surviving example of Japanese castle architecture.

Career

Terumasa was the second son of Ikeda Nobuteru, a senior Oda retainer. He distinguished himself in the campaigns of the 1580s and 1590s, including the Battle of Komaki and Nagakute (1584) and Hideyoshi's invasions of Korea. After his father's death he inherited the family lands, and in 1590 he was granted Yoshida in Mikawa Province. Although he had close ties to the Toyotomi — his wife Toku-hime was a daughter of Tokugawa Ieyasu — he sided with Ieyasu in the crisis of 1600.

Himeji

After the Battle of Sekigahara, Ieyasu rewarded Terumasa with the province of Harima and the castle of Himeji, a domain assessed at 520,000 koku. Between 1601 and 1609 Terumasa rebuilt Himeji on a monumental scale, creating the multi-storey white-plastered keep complex — nicknamed the "White Heron Castle" — that survives today and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. He also developed the castle town and its waterworks.

Death

Terumasa died in 1613 and was succeeded by his son Toshitaka. The Ikeda remained one of the great tozama houses of the Edo period, later ruling Okayama and Tottori as well.
