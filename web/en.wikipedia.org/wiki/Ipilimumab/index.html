Ipilimumab

Ipilimumab, sold under the brand name Yervoy, is a monoclonal antibody medication used in the treatment of cancer. It is a fully human IgG1 antibody that blocks CTLA-4, an immune checkpoint molecule that normally restrains T-cell activation. By releasing this "brake" on the immune system, ipilimumab enhances the immune response against tumours.

Medical uses

Ipilimumab was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2011 for the treatment of unresectable or metastatic melanoma — the first immune checkpoint inhibitor to reach the market — after a phase III trial showed improved overall survival. Its indications later expanded, including adjuvant treatment of melanoma, and combinations with the PD-1 inhibitor nivolumab for certain lung cancers, renal cell carcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, colorectal cancer, and malignant pleural mesothelioma.

Adverse effects

Because ipilimumab activates the immune system, it frequently causes immune-related adverse events such as colitis and diarrhoea, dermatitis, hepatitis, and endocrinopathies including hypophysitis. Severe toxicities are managed by stopping the drug and giving corticosteroids or other immunosuppressants. Treatment-related deaths occurred in roughly 1–2% of patients in early trials, requiring specialist management.

History

The drug arose from research by James Allison and colleagues on CTLA-4 blockade in the 1990s, work that helped found modern cancer immunotherapy and contributed to Allison's share of the 2018 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. It was developed by Medarex and later Bristol-Myers Squibb.
