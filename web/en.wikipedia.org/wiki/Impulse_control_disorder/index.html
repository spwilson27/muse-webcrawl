Impulse control disorder

Impulse control disorder
FieldPsychiatry
ExamplesIntermittent explosive disorder, kleptomania, pyromania, trichotillomania
ClassificationDSM-5: "Disruptive, impulse-control, and conduct disorders"
TreatmentCognitive behavioural therapy; SSRIs and other medication

An impulse control disorder is a class of psychiatric disorders characterised by impulsivity: the failure to resist a temptation, urge or impulse that may harm oneself or others. Affected individuals typically experience mounting tension or arousal before the act and a sense of relief or gratification during it, sometimes followed by regret or guilt.

Types

Recognised impulse control disorders include intermittent explosive disorder (recurrent aggressive outbursts), kleptomania (compulsive stealing of unneeded items), pyromania (deliberate fire-setting), and trichotillomania (hair-pulling, grouped in DSM-5 with related obsessive–compulsive spectrum conditions). Problem gambling was classified as an impulse control disorder in DSM-IV and moved to the addictive disorders in DSM-5. Impulse control problems also arise as side effects of dopaminergic medication in Parkinson's disease, manifesting as gambling, hypersexuality or compulsive shopping.

Causes and treatment

The disorders are thought to involve dysfunction in serotonergic and dopaminergic circuits of the frontal cortex and striatum, with genetic and environmental contributions. Treatment commonly combines cognitive behavioural therapy — including habit-reversal and exposure techniques — with medication such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors; opioid antagonists such as naltrexone have been used for gambling and kleptomania.
