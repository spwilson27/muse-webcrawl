Edward Vernon

Edward Vernon (12 November 1684 – 30 October 1757) was a Royal Navy officer and politician. He became famous during the War of Jenkins' Ear for capturing the Spanish town of Porto Bello in Panama in 1739 with only six ships, a feat celebrated throughout Britain and its American colonies.

Career

Vernon entered the navy in 1700 and fought in the War of the Spanish Succession. Promoted to vice-admiral at the outbreak of war with Spain, he fulfilled his boast to take Porto Bello with six ships in November 1739. His subsequent expedition against Cartagena de Indias in 1741, one of the largest fleets yet assembled, failed disastrously amid disease and disputes with the army commander. He later commanded in the North Sea against the threat of a French invasion in support of the Jacobite rising of 1745, but was dismissed from the service in 1746 after publishing pamphlets criticising the Admiralty.

"Old Grog" and legacy

Vernon is remembered for his 1740 order that sailors' rum be diluted with water, a mixture nicknamed "grog" after his grogram boat cloak. Lawrence Washington, who served under him, named his Virginia estate Mount Vernon after the admiral; the estate later passed to his half-brother George Washington.
