Aridity index

An aridity index (AI) is a numerical indicator of the degree of dryness of the climate at a given location. A number of aridity indices have been proposed; these indicators serve to identify, locate or delimit regions that suffer from a deficit of available water, a condition that can severely affect the effective use of the land for activities such as animal husbandry or irrigation.

Definition

The index most widely used by the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) is defined as the ratio of mean annual precipitation (P) to mean annual potential evapotranspiration (PET):

AI = P / PET

where potential evapotranspiration is the amount of water that would evaporate and transpire from a surface fully covered with water or vegetation if enough water were available.

Historical background

Early in the 20th century Wladimir Köppen and Rudolf Geiger developed climate classifications based in part on aridity. In 1948 C. W. Thornthwaite proposed an AI calculated as 100 times the surplus of precipitation over evapotranspiration needs, and in 1962 Mikhail Ivanovich Budyko proposed an index based on potential evaporation using net radiation. The UNEP ratio-based index became standard in assessments of desertification.

Applications

Aridity indices are used in classifying drylands, monitoring desertification and land degradation, planning irrigation, and studying how climate change may shift climatic zones. Trends in AI computed from climate model output are used to project future expansion or contraction of arid regions.
