Slab City, also called The Slabs, is an unincorporated, off-the-grid alternative community in the Sonoran Desert of Imperial County, California. It occupies the concrete foundations ("slabs") of Camp Dunlap, a United States Marine Corps barracks built during the Second World War and later dismantled, leaving only the slabs behind. The site lies just outside the small town of Niland, east of the Salton Sea.

Community

Slab City is inhabited by a shifting population of snowbirds who arrive in winter to escape cold climates, along with a smaller number of year-round residents who endure summer temperatures that can exceed 120 °F (49 °C). Residents live in recreational vehicles, trailers, buses, and improvised shelters. There is no rent, no running water, no electricity grid, and no sewerage or rubbish collection; residents rely on solar panels, generators, and supplies brought in from Niland and Brawley. Many residents describe the settlement as one of the "last free places" in America.

Attractions

The entrance to Slab City is marked by Salvation Mountain, a brightly painted adobe hillside artwork created by Leonard Knight and decorated with Bible verses and religious messages; it is maintained after Knight's death by a charitable organisation. Nearby stands East Jesus, an experimental and habitable art installation featuring large-scale sculptures made from discarded materials. The community also hosts an outdoor music venue known as The Range, which stages weekly open-mic concerts, and a lending library and social clubs run informally by residents.

Land status

The land is owned by the State of California and administered as part of the school lands trust. Its long-term future has periodically been debated, as the state could in principle sell or lease the parcel, but as of the early 2020s the community remained in place.
