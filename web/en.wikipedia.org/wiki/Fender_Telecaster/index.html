Fender Telecaster

The Telecaster, introduced in 1951, was the first commercially successful solid-body electric guitar.

The Fender Telecaster, colloquially known as the Tele, is an electric guitar produced by Fender. Together with its sister model, the Esquire, it was the world's first mass-produced, commercially successful solid-body electric guitar. Its simple yet effective design and revolutionary sound broke ground and set trends in electric guitar manufacturing and popular music. Introduced for national distribution as the Broadcaster in the autumn of 1950 as a two-pickup model, the guitar was renamed Telecaster in 1951 after a trademark dispute over the Broadcaster name.

Design

The Telecaster features a single-cutaway slab body, typically made of ash or alder, with a bolt-on maple neck—a construction chosen by Leo Fender for ease of manufacture and repair. It has two single-coil pickups: a bridge pickup mounted in a steel bridge plate, responsible for the model's bright, cutting "twang", and a chrome-covered neck pickup. Controls consist of volume and tone knobs and a three-way selector switch.

History

Leo Fender developed the design in Fullerton, California, drawing on the lap steel guitars his company already produced. A single-pickup version, the Esquire, appeared first in 1950; the two-pickup Broadcaster and the briefly nameless "Nocaster" followed before the Telecaster name was settled. The design has remained in continuous production ever since, with countless variations including the Telecaster Custom, Deluxe, and Thinline semi-hollow models.

Players and influence

The Telecaster became foundational in country music and is closely associated with players such as James Burton, Albert Collins, and Brad Paisley, but it has been used across genres: Bruce Springsteen, Keith Richards, Jimmy Page (notably on early Led Zeppelin recordings), Joe Strummer, and Prince all played Telecasters or Tele-style guitars. Its plain, workmanlike aesthetic and distinctive tone have made it one of the most enduring guitar designs.
