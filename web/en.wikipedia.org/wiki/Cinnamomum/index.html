Cinnamomum is a genus of evergreen trees and shrubs in the laurel family, Lauraceae, native to tropical and subtropical Asia and Australasia. Its aromatic bark, leaves and wood yield some of the world's oldest spices and medicines.

Notable species

  • Cinnamomum verum — "true" or Ceylon cinnamon, from Sri Lanka;
  • C. cassia — Chinese cassia cinnamon, the most common commercial cinnamon;
  • C. camphora — the camphor tree, source of camphor;
  • C. burmanni (Indonesian cinnamon) and C. loureiroi (Saigon cinnamon), other commercial cinnamons;
  • C. tamala — Indian bay leaf (tejpat).

Uses

Cinnamon bark is harvested by coppicing the stems and peeling the inner bark, which curls into quills as it dries. Cinnamon is used worldwide in cooking and traditional medicine; coumarin content (high in cassia types, low in Ceylon cinnamon) is a consideration at heavy intakes. Camphor from C. camphora has been used in medicine, mothproofing and celluloid manufacture, and several species are valued as shade and street trees.
