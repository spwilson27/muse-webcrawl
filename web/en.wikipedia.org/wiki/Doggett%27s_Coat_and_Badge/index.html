Doggett's Coat and Badge

Doggett's Coat and Badge is the prize and name of the oldest continuous rowing race in the world, held annually on the River Thames in London between London Bridge and Cadogan Pier, Chelsea. The race is contested by apprentice watermen who have recently completed their apprenticeships.

History

The race was founded in 1715 by the Irish actor and comedian Thomas Doggett, in honour of the accession of George I and the Hanoverian succession. Doggett left money in his will to fund the race in perpetuity, and it is now administered by the Fishmongers' Company. Winners receive the traditional prize of a scarlet coat and a large silver badge bearing the white horse of Hanover — the "coat and badge" of the title.

Course

The course is 4 miles 5 furlongs (about 7.4 km) and passes under eleven bridges. The race has been held almost every year since 1715, making it the oldest rowing race and one of the oldest sporting contests in the world.
