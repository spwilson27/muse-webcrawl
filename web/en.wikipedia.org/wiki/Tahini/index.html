Tahini

Tahini (from Arabic: طحينة, ṭaḥīna) is a Middle Eastern condiment made from toasted ground sesame seeds. It is served by itself (as a dip), or as a major ingredient in hummus, baba ghanoush, and halva. Tahini is used in the cuisines of the Levant and Eastern Mediterranean, the South Caucasus, and parts of North Africa, and has parallels in East Asian sesame pastes and sauces.

Preparation

Tahini is made from sesame seeds that are soaked in water and then crushed to separate the bran from the kernels. The crushed seeds are soaked in salt water, causing the bran to sink. The floating kernels are skimmed off, toasted, and ground to produce an oily paste. Because of its high oil content, the paste separates on standing and is usually stirred before use; some commercial products include emulsifiers to prevent this. Whole-seed ("whole grain") tahini, ground with the hulls, is darker and more bitter than tahini made from hulled seeds.

Culinary uses

Whisked with water, lemon juice, salt and garlic, tahini becomes tarator, a sauce served with falafel, shawarma, grilled meats and fish throughout the Levant. It is the base of hummus and of sweet halva, and appears in dishes and dressings from Turkey and Armenia to Israel, Palestine, Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Egypt and Cyprus. In Greek cuisine it is used in soups during Lent, when animal products are avoided. In the 21st century tahini has become popular in Western cooking as an ingredient in dressings, baked goods and desserts.

Nutrition

Tahini is rich in fat (mostly unsaturated), protein, B vitamins, vitamin E, and minerals such as calcium, iron and magnesium. It contains roughly 590 calories per 100 grams. Sesame is a recognised food allergen, and labelling of sesame as an allergen became mandatory in the United States in 2023.
