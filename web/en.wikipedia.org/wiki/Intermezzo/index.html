Intermezzo


A comic intermezzo performed between acts of a serious opera

In music and theatre, an intermezzo (Italian, literally "halfway"; plural intermezzi or intermezzos) is a composition which fits between other musical or dramatic entities, such as acts of a play or movements of a larger musical work. Historically, the term covers several distinct genres in opera and instrumental music.

Opera

In the 18th century, intermezzi were short comic operas performed in one or two acts between the acts of a serious opera (opera seria) or play. The most famous example is Pergolesi's La serva padrona (1733), originally an intermezzo to his opera seria Il prigionier superbo. When performed in Paris in 1752 it helped spark the Querelle des Bouffons, the pamphlet war between supporters of French and Italian opera. The comic intermezzo, with its small cast and naturalistic plots, was an important ancestor of opera buffa.

Instrumental music

In the 19th century the term was applied to a movement that was either in the middle of a larger work or a character piece in its own right. Schumann used intermezzo as a movement title, and Brahms wrote many piano pieces called Intermezzo, mostly in his later collections (Opp. 76, 116–119) — lyrical, introspective pieces that he described as "lullabies of my sorrows".

Operatic interludes

The term is also used for orchestral interludes within operas, such as the famous Intermezzo from Mascagni's Cavalleria rusticana (1890), the Intermezzo from Puccini's Manon Lescaut, and the interludes in Britten's Peter Grimes. Richard Strauss titled one of his operas Intermezzo (1924), a domestic comedy drawn from an episode in his own marriage.

Other uses

In theatre more broadly, an intermezzo can be any short entertainment inserted between the acts of a longer play, a practice going back to the intermedi of Renaissance courts, which featured elaborate music, dance and spectacle at Medici weddings and festivals.
