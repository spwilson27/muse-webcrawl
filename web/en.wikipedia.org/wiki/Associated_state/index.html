Associated state

Freely associated states in the Pacific

An associated state is a political entity in a formal association with another, usually larger, state, under which the smaller state retains a high degree of self-government while the larger partner assumes responsibility for matters such as defence and, often, foreign relations. The arrangement most commonly arises from a compact of free association or an act of association agreed between the two parties.

Examples

The Cook Islands and Niue are self-governing states in free association with New Zealand: their people hold New Zealand citizenship, and New Zealand acts on their behalf in defence and some foreign affairs, though both conduct increasingly independent foreign policies and belong to international bodies in their own right. Under Compacts of Free Association with the United States, the Marshall Islands, the Federated States of Micronesia and Palau are sovereign states that grant the US defence rights in return for economic assistance and the right of their citizens to live and work in the United States. Tokelau, though a New Zealand territory, has considered associated-state status in referendums.

Characteristics

Associated states are generally recognised as sovereign under international law, may join the United Nations (as the Compact states have), and can usually terminate the association unilaterally. The status differs from that of a protectorate or a dependent territory in that it rests on a freely negotiated agreement between formally equal partners.
