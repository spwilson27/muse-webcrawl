Machiya

Machiya (Japanese: 町家) are traditional wooden townhouses found throughout Japan, especially in historic city centres such as Kyoto. Developed from the medieval period onward, they served as the combined homes and workplaces of merchants and craftsmen (chōnin), with a shop fronting the street and living quarters behind.

Design

Machiya are typically long and narrow — a form sometimes called "eel's nests" (unagi no nedoko) — because street frontage was taxed. Behind a lattice-fronted shop (mise) lie a passageway leading to inner rooms, an internal courtyard garden (tsuboniwa), and a fireproof storehouse (kura) at the rear. Construction is timber-framed with earth walls and tiled roofs; in Kyoto the classic form is known as the kyō-machiya.

Preservation

Although many machiya were demolished in the postwar decades, preservation movements since the 1990s have restored them as homes, cafes, galleries, and guesthouses, and several districts containing machiya are designated preservation areas for groups of traditional buildings.
