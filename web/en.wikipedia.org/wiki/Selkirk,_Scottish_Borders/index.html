Selkirk, Scottish Borders

Selkirk is a town and historic royal burgh in the Scottish Borders, Scotland, lying on the Ettrick Water, a tributary of the River Tweed. It was formerly the county town of Selkirkshire. The town's population is around 6,000. Selkirk's people are traditionally known as "Souters" (shoemakers), from the town's historic trade supplying shoes and boots — famously, according to legend, to Bonnie Prince Charlie's army in 1745.

Selkirk's Market Place; Sir Walter Scott served as Sheriff-Depute of Selkirkshire from 1799.

History

Selkirk is one of the oldest royal burghs in Scotland. William Wallace was proclaimed Guardian of Scotland in the kirk at Selkirk after his victory at Stirling Bridge in 1297, and the town suffered heavily in the Anglo-Scottish wars and the Rough Wooing. Selkirk men fought at Flodden in 1513; only one, Fletcher, is said to have returned, carrying a captured English banner — an event commemorated every year in the town's Common Riding, one of the largest equestrian festivals in Scotland, in which the standard bearer casts the flag in the Market Place. The town's woollen mills, built up in the nineteenth-century tweed trade, have declined, though tweed and tartan production continue.

People and culture

Sir Walter Scott was Sheriff of Selkirkshire and held court in the town; his statue and the courthouse stand in the Market Place. The explorer Mungo Park was born nearby, and the town is associated with the ballad tradition of the Borders. Selkirk's Common Riding, held in June, is the centerpiece of local identity, recalling both Flodden and the ancient riding of the burgh's boundaries.
