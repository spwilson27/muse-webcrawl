Donner Pass is a 2,151-metre (7,056 ft) mountain pass in the northern Sierra Nevada of California, above Donner Lake near the town of Truckee. It carries the former US Route 40 and the transcontinental railroad across the Sierra crest, and is named for the Donner Party, the emigrant group snowbound nearby in the catastrophic winter of 1846–47.

History

Native Washoe people used routes over the crest for millennia. The first wagon train to cross was the Stephens–Townsend–Murphy Party in 1844. Two winters later the Donner Party, delayed by shortcuts and early snow, was trapped at the lake and pass; of 87 members, 48 survived, some through cannibalism — the most notorious episode of the overland emigration era. In the 1860s the Central Pacific Railroad forced the first transcontinental railroad over the pass with tunnels and miles of snowsheds, using largely Chinese labour.

Transport and recreation

Today Interstate 80 crosses slightly north of the old pass; the Union Pacific (former Southern Pacific) line still uses the summit tunnel route. The area is a centre for skiing (Sugar Bowl, Boreal), climbing on Donner Summit's granite, and hiking on the Pacific Crest Trail. It is among the snowiest inhabited places in the United States, averaging around ten metres of snow a year.
