Three Sisters (Australia)

Three Sisters
Location: Blue Mountains, New South Wales, Australia
Nearest town: Katoomba
Elevation: 922 m (Meehni), 918 m (Wimlah), 906 m (Gunnedoo)
Geology: Sandstone

The Three Sisters are an unusual rock formation in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, Australia, on the north escarpment of the Jamison Valley near the town of Katoomba. The three weathered sandstone peaks, named Meehni, Wimlah, and Gunnedoo, are among the most famous landmarks in Australia and attract millions of visitors to the Echo Point lookout each year.

The Three Sisters from Echo Point, Katoomba, with the Jamison Valley beyond.

Formation

The Sisters were formed by erosion of the soft sandstone of the Blue Mountains plateau. Wind, rainwater and the rivers of the valley gradually wore away the cliff face; the harder, iron-stained rock of the peaks resisted, leaving three pinnacles standing proud of the escarpment. The formations continue to erode and will eventually collapse into the valley.

Aboriginal legend

A well-known Aboriginal Dreaming story of the Gundungurra people tells of three sisters, Meehni, Wimlah and Gunnedoo, who fell in love with three brothers from the neighbouring Nepean tribe, whom tribal law forbade them to marry. A battle ensued, and a witchdoctor turned the sisters to stone to protect them, intending to reverse the spell afterwards; but he was killed in the fighting, and the sisters remain as stone to this day.

Tourism and protection

The Three Sisters form part of the Greater Blue Mountains World Heritage Area. Floodlighting illuminates the formation at night, and the nearby Giant Stairway descends to the valley floor and the Federal Pass walking track. In 2014 the Three Sisters were declared an Aboriginal Place under New South Wales law, recognising their cultural significance.
