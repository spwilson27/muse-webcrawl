A xenon arc lamp is a highly specialised type of gas discharge lamp that produces extremely bright, near-daylight white light by passing electricity through ionised xenon gas at high pressure. First developed in Germany in the 1940s and introduced widely in the 1950s, xenon lamps largely replaced carbon arc lamps in film projection because of their stability, colour fidelity and convenience.

Construction

In a short-arc lamp, xenon is sealed in a fused quartz envelope at pressures of several atmospheres, between two tungsten electrodes only a few millimetres apart. A high-voltage pulse ionises the gas, after which a direct-current supply maintains the arc. The plasma reaches very high luminance concentrated near the cathode, making the lamp an almost ideal point source for optical systems.

Applications

Xenon short-arc lamps are the standard light source for cinema projectors, including IMAX systems that use lamps of up to 15 kW, and are also used in searchlights, follow spots, solar simulators, fluorescence microscopy and fibre-optic illuminators. Long-arc variants have been used for studio and architectural lighting.

Safety

Because the envelope is under high pressure even when cold, xenon lamps carry an explosion risk and must be handled with protective equipment; they also emit significant ultraviolet radiation and generate ozone in some configurations.
