Leopard 1

The Leopard 1 is a main battle tank designed by Porsche and manufactured by Krauss-Maffei in West Germany, first entering service in 1965. It became the standard tank of the Bundeswehr and was exported widely to NATO and allied armies, remaining in service — in upgraded forms — with several countries well into the 21st century.

Design philosophy

West German doctrine of the early 1960s held that advancing anti-tank weapons — in particular shaped-charge (HEAT) warheads — had made heavy armour of diminishing value. The Leopard 1 therefore traded armour thickness for outstanding mobility, with a powerful MTU diesel engine, low silhouette, and the excellent British-designed 105 mm L7 gun. Its emphasis on cross-country speed and firepower reflected an expectation of fluid warfare on the North German Plain.

Variants and service

Successive marks (1A1 through 1A5) added thermal sleeves, stabilisation, add-on turret armour, and modern fire-control systems with thermal sights. Specialised variants include the Gepard self-propelled anti-aircraft gun, armoured recovery and engineer vehicles, and the Biber bridgelayer. More than 6,400 Leopard 1 gun tanks were built; operators have included Germany, Italy (which licence-built it), the Netherlands, Belgium, Norway, Denmark, Canada, Australia, Turkey, Greece, Brazil, Chile, and Ukraine, which received donated 1A5s beginning in 2023.
