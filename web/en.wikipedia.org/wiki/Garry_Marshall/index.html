Garry Kent Marshall (born Garry Kent Masciarelli; November 13, 1934 – July 19, 2016) was an American film director, film producer, screenwriter, and actor. He is best known for creating the television sitcom Happy Days and its various spin-offs, and for directing the romantic comedy films Pretty Woman (1990), Runaway Bride (1999), and The Princess Diaries (2001).

Early life

Marshall was born in the Bronx, New York City, the son of Marjorie Irene, a tap dance teacher, and Anthony W. Masciarelli (later Anthony Wallace Marshall), a director of industrial films. His father was of Italian descent and his mother had German, English, and Scottish ancestry. His younger sister is the television producer and director Penny Marshall. Marshall graduated from De Witt Clinton High School and Northwestern University, where he wrote a sports column for The Daily Northwestern.

Career

Marshall began his career as a joke writer for comedians such as Joey Bishop and Phil Foster, then became a writer for The Tonight Show with Jack Paar. In the 1960s he moved to Hollywood and, with Jerry Belson, wrote for The Dick Van Dyke Show, The Danny Thomas Show, and I Spy. The pair adapted Neil Simon's play The Odd Couple into a successful television series in 1970.

In 1974 Marshall created Happy Days, which ran for eleven seasons and spawned the spin-offs Laverne & Shirley (starring his sister Penny), Mork & Mindy, and Joanie Loves Chachi. Throughout the 1970s and 1980s he was one of the most successful producers in American television.

Marshall's film career began with Young Doctors in Love (1982) and The Flamingo Kid (1984). His biggest success was Pretty Woman (1990), starring Richard Gere and Julia Roberts, one of the highest-grossing romantic comedies of all time. Later films included Frankie and Johnny (1991), Exit to Eden (1994), Dear God (1996), The Other Sister (1999), Runaway Bride (1999), The Princess Diaries (2001) and its 2004 sequel, Raising Helen (2004), Georgia Rule (2007), and the ensemble holiday films Valentine's Day (2010), New Year's Eve (2011), and Mother's Day (2016).

As an actor, Marshall appeared in films such as Soapdish (1991) and A League of Their Own (1992), and provided the voice of Buck Cluck in Disney's Chicken Little (2005).

Death

Marshall died on July 19, 2016, at a hospital in Burbank, California, from complications of pneumonia following a stroke, at the age of 81.
