A macron (from Greek μακρόν makrón, "long") is a diacritical mark consisting of a short horizontal line placed above a letter, usually a vowel. It is most often used to indicate a long vowel, as in Latin, Ancient Greek, and many modern romanization and orthographic systems; in some languages it marks tone or other distinctions instead.

Uses

In the romanization of Japanese (Hepburn), a macron marks long vowels: Tōkyō (とうきょう, properly とうきょう with long o and u sounds). The International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration uses macrons for long vowels (ā, ī, ū), as does scholarly transliteration of Arabic. Latin dictionaries conventionally mark vowel length with macrons (and breves for short vowels) although classical Latin was written without them. Several modern orthographies use macrons as regular letters: Māori (tohutō), Hawaiian (where the mark is called the ʻokina-equivalent kahakō), Latvian, Lithuanian (in some positions), and various Polynesian and indigenous languages; Japanese kana use a similar horizontal bar (the chōonpu) to extend vowel sounds in katakana.

Technical notes

In Unicode, the combining macron is U+0304 and combines with a preceding character; precomposed characters exist for most Latin vowels (Ā ā, Ē ē, Ī ī, Ō ō, Ū ū, and also Ȳ ȳ, Ǖ etc.). The standalone macron character ¯ (U+00AF) originated as a spacing mark. The macron is distinct from the breve (˘), which indicates shortness, and from the macron below (◌̱, U+0331), used in phonetic transcription and some orthographies. A line above can also be an overline, used in mathematics and elsewhere with different meanings.
