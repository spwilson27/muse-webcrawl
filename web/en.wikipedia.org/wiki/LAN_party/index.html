A LAN party is a gathering of people with computers or compatible game consoles, who connect their devices to each other via a local area network (LAN) primarily for the purpose of playing multiplayer video games together. LAN parties range from small, informal gatherings of friends in a home to very large organised events with thousands of participants in exhibition halls.

History

Multiplayer gaming on local networks grew with the spread of Ethernet networking in universities and offices in the early 1990s; games such as Doom (1993) and Quake (1996) made "deathmatch" sessions a cultural phenomenon. In the era before widespread broadband internet, bringing computers together physically was the only way to enjoy low-latency multiplayer and to exchange large files. Participants traditionally brought their own computer, monitor, and peripherals ("BYOC") and connected them through switches and long runs of cable.

The culture peaked in the 2000s with mega-events: Sweden's DreamHack grew into the world's largest LAN, drawing over 20,000 attendees; Norway's The Gathering and the bring-your-own-computer section of QuakeCon in Texas became institutions, combining gaming with demo parties, case-modding, and file sharing.

Decline and legacy

Ubiquitous broadband, online matchmaking, and digital distribution removed the technical need for LAN parties, and many modern games dropped LAN support entirely. Small private LANs nevertheless persist as social events, and large festivals such as DreamHack Summer and Winter continue the tradition as broader digital festivals with esports stages and streaming.
