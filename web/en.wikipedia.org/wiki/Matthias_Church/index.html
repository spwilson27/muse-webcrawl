Matthias Church

Matthias Church
Location: Castle Hill, Budapest, Hungary
Denomination: Roman Catholic
Founded: c. 1015 (original); current church 13th–15th centuries
Style: Gothic / Gothic Revival
Status: Parish church

Matthias Church (Hungarian: Mátyás-templom), formally the Church of the Assumption of the Buda Castle, is a Roman Catholic church located in Holy Trinity Square, in front of the Fisherman's Bastion in the heart of the Buda Castle District in Budapest, Hungary. It is one of the finest churches in Budapest and one of the most significant tourist attractions in the city.

History

According to church tradition, the original church was founded by Saint Stephen, the first king of Hungary, around 1015. The current building was constructed in the florid late Gothic style in the second half of the 14th century and was extensively restored in the late 19th century under architect Frigyes Schulek, who added the distinctive diamond-patterned Zsolnay ceramic roof tiles and Gothic Revival ornamentation.

The church is named after King Matthias Corvinus (r. 1458–1490), who ordered the transformation of its original southern tower and celebrated both of his weddings here. During the roughly 150 years of Ottoman rule, the church was converted into Buda's main mosque; it returned to Christian use after the recapture of Buda in 1686. It served as the coronation church for Hungarian monarchs, including Franz Joseph I in 1867 and Charles IV in 1916.

Features

The interior is decorated with colorful frescoes by Károly Lotz and Bertalan Székely. The church houses an ecclesiastical art museum with medieval stone carvings, reliquaries, and replicas of the Hungarian royal crown and coronation jewels. The Béla Tower contains the tombs of King Béla III and his wife, Agnes of Antioch.
