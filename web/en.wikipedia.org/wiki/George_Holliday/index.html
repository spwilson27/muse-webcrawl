George Holliday

George Holliday is an American plumber and amateur videographer who filmed the 1991 Rodney King beating in Los Angeles.

Born: 1959 (Buenos Aires, Argentina)
Known for: Videotaping the Rodney King beating

George Holliday (born 1959) is an Argentine-born American plumber who, on March 3, 1991, videotaped the beating of motorist Rodney King by Los Angeles Police Department officers from the balcony of his apartment in Lake View Terrace, Los Angeles. The footage became one of the most famous pieces of citizen journalism of the twentieth century.

The video

Woken by sirens and helicopter noise in the early hours of March 3, 1991, Holliday took his recently purchased Sony Handycam to his balcony and recorded about nine minutes of footage as officers struck King dozens of times with batons, kicked him, and shocked him with a Taser after a high-speed chase. After the police declined his offer of the tape, Holliday sold it to local television station KTLA for $500. Broadcast worldwide, the video provoked outrage and led to criminal charges against four officers.

Aftermath

The officers' acquittal by a jury in Simi Valley in April 1992 sparked the 1992 Los Angeles riots; two officers were later convicted in a federal civil rights trial. Holliday's recording is widely cited as a landmark in the history of citizen witnessing and video evidence. He later sued over the use of his footage and worked to protect his rights in it; the tape itself became the subject of legal disputes over copyright and licensing.

Later life

Holliday continued to work as a plumber in the Los Angeles area and largely avoided the spotlight, though he occasionally spoke about the video's role in debates over policing and, decades later, the smartphone era of bystander recordings.
