Leo IV the Khazar (Byzantine emperor)

Leo IV the Khazar (25 January 750 – 8 September 780) was Byzantine emperor from 775 to 780. He was the son of Constantine V and his first wife, Tzitzak, a daughter of the Khagan of the Khazars, from whom Leo derived his epithet. Leo continued his father's iconoclast policies, though in a somewhat milder form, and his brief reign was marked by campaigns against the Abbasid Caliphate in the east and the Bulgars in the Balkans. He died suddenly in 780, reportedly after developing a fever while wearing a crown taken from a church, and was succeeded by his young son Constantine VI under the regency of his wife Irene, who would later restore the veneration of icons and depose her own son.

Pope Leo IV

Pope Leo IV (790 – 17 July 855) was bishop of Rome from 847 until his death. A Benedictine, he is remembered for fortifying Rome against Saracen raids after the sack of 846: he built the Leonine Wall around the Vatican, creating the Leonine City, and organized the naval league that defeated a Saracen fleet at the Battle of Ostia in 849, an event depicted in Raphael's frescoes in the Vatican. Tradition also associates him with extinguishing a fire in the Borgo by making the sign of the cross. He is venerated as a saint.

Leo IV of Armenia

Leo IV (1309 – 28 August 1341) was the last Hethumid king of Cilician Armenia, reigning from 1320 until his assassination in 1341. His pro-Western and pro-Catholic policies alienated the Armenian nobility and clergy, and his murder brought his cousin Guy de Lusignan to the throne as Constantine II.
