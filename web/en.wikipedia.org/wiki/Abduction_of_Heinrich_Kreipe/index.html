Abduction of Heinrich Kreipe

Date26 April – 14 May 1944
LocationCrete, occupied Greece
ParticipantsSOE officers Patrick Leigh Fermor and William Stanley Moss; Cretan resistance
OutcomeGeneral Heinrich Kreipe captured and evacuated to Egypt

The abduction of Heinrich Kreipe was a Second World War operation carried out in April–May 1944 by Britain's Special Operations Executive (SOE) together with the Cretan resistance. Major Patrick Leigh Fermor and Captain William Stanley Moss kidnapped Major-General Heinrich Kreipe, commander of German forces on Crete, and smuggled him off the island to Egypt.

Background

Crete had been under German occupation since 1941. The original target was General Friedrich-Wilhelm Müller, known for reprisal atrocities, but before the operation he was replaced by Kreipe. The team decided to proceed; the object was to damage German morale and raise Cretan spirits rather than to kill.

The kidnapping

On the night of 26 April 1944, Leigh Fermor and Moss, disguised as German military police, stopped Kreipe's car near his quarters at Knossos. They overpowered the driver, bound the general, and drove through 22 checkpoints in Heraklion before abandoning the car with misleading documents suggesting a submarine escape.

Escape across Crete

Guided by Cretan partisans, the party crossed Mount Ida — where Leigh Fermor famously exchanged Horace quotations with the captive general — and reached the south coast after an 18-day pursuit by thousands of German troops. A British motor launch collected them on 14 May and took Kreipe to Egypt. German commander Bruno Bräuer issued a reprisal threat, but the threatened wholesale destruction of villages was not carried out on this occasion.

Legacy

Moss's diary became the bestselling book Ill Met by Moonlight (1950), filmed in 1957 with Dirk Bogarde as Leigh Fermor. The operation remains one of the most celebrated SOE exploits of the war.
