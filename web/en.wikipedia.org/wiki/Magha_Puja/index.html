Magha Puja

Magha Puja (Pali: Māghapūjā), also written Makha Bucha, is an important Buddhist festival celebrated on the full moon day of the third lunar month (Magha) in Thailand, Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar. It commemorates an event in the life of the Buddha nine months after his enlightenment.

The event

According to tradition, on this day 1,250 of the Buddha's disciples — all arahants ordained by the Buddha himself — assembled spontaneously, without prior arrangement, at the Veluvana monastery in Rajgir. The Buddha addressed them with the Ovada Patimokkha, a summary of his teaching: to cease from evil, to do good, and to purify the mind. Four remarkable features of the gathering (the "fourfold assembly") are traditionally listed.

Celebration

In Thailand, where Magha Puja is a public holiday, devotees visit temples to make merit, listen to sermons, observe the Five Precepts and join the evening candlelit procession (wian thian), circling the ordination hall three times with candles, incense and lotus flowers. Abstention from alcohol is encouraged, and in Thailand alcohol sales are prohibited on the day.
