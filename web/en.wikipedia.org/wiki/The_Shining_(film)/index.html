The Shining (film)

The Shining is a 1980 psychological horror film produced and directed by Stanley Kubrick, co-written with Diane Johnson, and based on Stephen King's 1977 novel. It stars Jack Nicholson, Shelley Duvall, Danny Lloyd and Scatman Crothers.

Plot

Writer Jack Torrance accepts a job as winter caretaker of the isolated Overlook Hotel in the Colorado Rockies, bringing his wife Wendy and young son Danny, who possesses a psychic gift — "the shining" — that shows him the hotel's violent past, including caretaker Delbert Grady's murder of his family. Snowed in and manipulated by the hotel's spirits, Jack descends into madness, types page after page of a single sentence, and pursues his family with an axe. The cook Dick Hallorann, who shares Danny's gift, returns to help but is murdered; Danny escapes through the hedge maze while Jack freezes to death in it. A closing photograph shows Jack at the hotel's July 4th ball in 1921.

Production

Kubrick shot at EMI Elstree Studios with pioneering Steadicam work by Garrett Brown. The production was famously gruelling, with extreme numbers of takes that distressed Duvall. Stephen King disliked the adaptation and later wrote a 1997 television miniseries closer to his novel; his sequel novel Doctor Sleep was filmed in 2019.

Reception and legacy

Initial reviews were mixed — it was even nominated for two Razzies — but the film's reputation grew enormously; it is now routinely ranked among the greatest horror films and greatest films overall. Images such as the blood elevator, the Grady twins, "REDRUM" and "Heeeere's Johnny!" are embedded in popular culture. It was selected for the National Film Registry in 2018.
