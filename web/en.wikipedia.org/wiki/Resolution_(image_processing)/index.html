Resolution (image processing)

Image resolution is the detail an image holds. The term applies to digital images, film images, printers and other types of images, and higher resolution means more image detail. Resolution can be measured in several ways: for a digital image, most directly as the number of pixels along its width and height (for example 1920 × 1080), and for printed output as dots per inch (DPI) or pixels per inch (PPI).

Pixel resolution

The pixel count of a sensor or display is commonly summarised in megapixels. Standard display resolutions include HD (1280 × 720), Full HD (1920 × 1080), QHD (2560 × 1440) and 4K UHD (3840 × 2160). Pixel count alone does not determine sharpness: the physical size of the display (hence pixel density), viewing distance, lens quality and sensor noise all affect the detail actually perceived.

Spatial resolution

In imaging science, spatial resolution is the ability to distinguish two closely spaced objects, limited by the optics (diffraction), sampling (the Nyquist limit) and the modulation transfer function of the system. In remote sensing, "resolution" often means ground sample distance — the size of one pixel on the ground — as in satellite imagery resolving features of 30 m (Landsat) down to tens of centimetres (commercial imaging satellites).

Other senses

For printers and scanners resolution is quoted in dots per inch; for audio and instrumentation the analogous concepts are sampling rate and bit depth. "Temporal resolution" describes how frequently a system samples a changing scene, as in a camera's frame rate.
