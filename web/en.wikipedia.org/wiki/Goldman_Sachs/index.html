The Goldman Sachs Group, Inc. is an American multinational investment bank and financial services company founded in 1869 by German immigrant Marcus Goldman, joined by his son-in-law Samuel Sachs. Headquartered in New York City, it is one of the world's largest investment banks, advising on mergers and acquisitions, underwriting securities, trading, and managing assets for corporations, governments and wealthy individuals.

History

The firm pioneered commercial paper, grew through the 20th century into a leading underwriter and adviser, and went public in 1999. During the 2008 financial crisis it converted to a bank holding company in September 2008 to gain Federal Reserve support, received $10 billion under TARP (repaid in 2009), and was criticised for selling mortgage securities it allegedly expected to fall, leading to a $550 million SEC settlement in 2010. It was a central bank in the subprime crisis era and a contrast to Lehman Brothers, which failed.

Business

Goldman's main segments are Global Banking & Markets and Asset & Wealth Management. It is a primary dealer in US Treasuries and is designated a global systemically important bank, subject to Basel III surcharges. Its consumer ventures (Marcus, the Apple Card partnership) were scaled back in the 2020s as the firm refocused on its core institutional and wealth franchises.

Criticism

The firm, sometimes nicknamed "Government Sachs" for its alumni in public office, has faced controversies including the 1MDB scandal, for which it paid billions in penalties in 2020.
