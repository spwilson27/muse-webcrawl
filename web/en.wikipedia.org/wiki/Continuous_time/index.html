Continuous time

Continuous time is a model in which time is treated as a continuous variable rather than a discrete sequence of steps. It is contrasted with discrete time, and appears in physics, signal processing, and economics.

A continuous-time signal takes values at every instant; samples are discrete-time.

Signal processing

A continuous-time signal is defined for every real time t (e.g. an audio waveform from a microphone). Digital processing requires sampling it at discrete instants; the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem gives the conditions under which the original can be recovered exactly.

Physics

Classical physics models time as a continuum. In relativity, the time coordinate is continuous, while some quantum gravity programmes consider discreteness at the Planck scale.

Finance

In continuous-time finance, models such as Black–Scholes use stochastic calculus (Itô calculus) to describe asset prices evolving at every instant.
