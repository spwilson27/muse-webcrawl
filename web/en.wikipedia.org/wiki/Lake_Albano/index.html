Lake Albano (Italian: Lago Albano) is a volcanic crater lake in the Alban Hills of Lazio, Italy, about 25 km southeast of Rome. The towns of Castel Gandolfo and Albano Laziale overlook its steep shores. It is the deepest volcanic lake in Italy, reaching a depth of about 170 metres.

Geology and history

The lake occupies a complex crater (a maar system) formed by explosive eruptions of the Alban Hills volcano. In antiquity it was known as Albanus Lacus. Roman tradition told that during the siege of Veii in 398 BC an oracle demanded the lake's waters be drained; the Romans built an emissary tunnel through the crater rim, a remarkable feat of hydraulic engineering that still regulates the lake's outflow today.

On the shore stood Alba Longa, the legendary mother city of Rome, according to tradition destroyed by King Tullus Hostilius. The emperors built villas along the lake, including Domitian's Alban villa. Castel Gandolfo, on the western rim, has been the summer residence of the popes since the 17th century.

Modern use

Lake Albano is popular for swimming, rowing and sailing. For the 1960 Rome Olympics, the rowing and canoeing events were held on the lake, and temporary grandstands and courses were installed. Because the lake has no natural surface outlet, water levels are controlled by the ancient emissary and modern pumping; fluctuations in level related to water extraction and seismic and volcanic-gas monitoring are periodically studied by Italian geologists.
