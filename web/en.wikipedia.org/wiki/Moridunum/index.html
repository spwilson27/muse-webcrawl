Moridunum

Moridunum was a Roman fort and town in Roman Britain, located on the site of the modern town of Carmarthen in south-west Wales. It was the civitas capital of the Demetae tribe and is generally identified with the Moridunum listed in the Antonine Itinerary and Ptolemy's Geography.

This article is a general overview of the name and its identifications; see Moridunum (Carmarthen) for the archaeological site in detail.

Name

The name Moridunum is of Celtic origin, commonly interpreted as "sea fort", from mori- ("sea") and dunum ("fort"). Carmarthen's Welsh name, Caerfyrddin, is traditionally — though probably incorrectly — derived from the name of Myrddin (Merlin).

History

A Roman auxiliary fort was founded at Carmarthen around AD 75 during the campaigns of Sextus Julius Frontinus. A civilian settlement grew up around the fort, and after the fort was abandoned in the early second century the town was provided with defences, public buildings including a forum and an amphitheatre — among the most westerly in the Roman Empire. Moridunum became the administrative capital of the Demetae.
