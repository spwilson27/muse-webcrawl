Portuñol

Portuñol (Spanish spelling portuñol; Portuguese: portunhol) is the name given to mixed varieties of Portuguese and Spanish spoken in the border regions between Portuguese-speaking Brazil and its Spanish-speaking neighbours, notably Uruguay, Argentina, Paraguay, Bolivia, Peru, Colombia and Venezuela. The name is a blend of português/portugués and español/espanhol.

Varieties

The best-known variety is the Riverense Portuñol (fronteiriço) spoken along the Brazil–Uruguay border, in the twin cities of Rivera and Santana do Livramento, where it has been spoken for generations and studied by linguists as a stable contact variety. In other settings, "Portuñol" refers to the improvised mixture used by speakers of one language attempting to communicate in the other, exploiting the considerable mutual intelligibility between Spanish and Portuguese.

Characteristics

Portuñol mixes Portuguese and Spanish vocabulary, pronunciation and grammar in varying proportions according to the speaker and context. Because both languages descend from Vulgar Latin and share much of their lexicon, mixed speech is easily understood on both sides of the border, though it has no official status and is often stigmatised as "broken" language.

Cultural recognition

Border writers and musicians have used Portuñol as a literary language, and in Uruguay the variety has been the subject of campaigns for recognition as part of the region's intangible cultural heritage.
