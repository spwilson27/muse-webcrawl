Treasure Coast

The Treasure Coast is a region of the U.S. state of Florida, comprising the Atlantic coastal counties of Indian River, St. Lucie and Martin, and sometimes including Palm Beach County to the south or Brevard County (the Space Coast) to the north. The name derives from the Spanish Treasure Fleet that was wrecked along the coast by a hurricane in 1715, scattering gold and silver coins that salvors — and, for centuries afterwards, beachcombers — have recovered.

History

On 31 July 1715 a hurricane sank eleven of the twelve ships of the Spanish plate fleet returning from Havana to Spain laden with New World treasure. Roughly 1,500 sailors died and millions of gold and silver coins were spilled into the sea along the coast between present-day Sebastian and Stuart. Modern salvage operations from the 1960s onwards, led by figures such as Kip Wagner and Mel Fisher, recovered large quantities of the treasure and popularised the region's name, which was adopted in the early twentieth century by local boosters seeking a distinctive identity to rival the Gold Coast to the south.

Geography and communities

The region's principal cities include Port St. Lucie, one of Florida's fastest-growing cities, along with Fort Pierce, Stuart, Vero Beach and Sebastian. The Indian River Lagoon runs parallel to the coast behind barrier islands, and the area is known for citrus growing, beaches and a more relaxed pace than metropolitan South Florida. The McLarty Treasure Museum and the Mel Fisher's Treasure Museum display artefacts of the 1715 fleet.
