Quantum information

Quantum information is the information of the state of a quantum system. It is the basic entity of study in quantum information theory, and can be manipulated using engineering techniques known as quantum information processing. Quantum information refers to both the technical definition in terms of von Neumann entropy and the general field of study, which includes theoretical and experimental investigation of quantum computers and related devices.

Basics

The basic unit of quantum information is the qubit, the quantum analogue of the classical bit. Whereas a classical bit is either 0 or 1, a qubit can exist in a superposition of both states. Two or more qubits may be entangled, sharing correlations with no classical counterpart. Quantum information cannot be copied perfectly (the no-cloning theorem), and measuring a quantum system generally disturbs its state.

Processing and communication

Quantum information underlies a set of protocols with no classical equivalent or with substantial quantum advantages:

  • Quantum teleportation — transferring an unknown quantum state using entanglement and classical communication
  • Superdense coding — sending two classical bits by transmitting one qubit of an entangled pair
  • Quantum key distribution — cryptographic key exchange whose security rests on physical law, such as the BB84 protocol
  • Quantum computing — algorithms such as Shor's factoring algorithm and Grover's search algorithm that exploit superposition and interference

Entropy and measures

The von Neumann entropy quantifies the information content of a quantum state, playing the role of Shannon entropy in classical information theory. Related measures include entanglement entropy, quantum mutual information, and fidelity between states, which guide the design of quantum communication protocols and error-correcting codes.
