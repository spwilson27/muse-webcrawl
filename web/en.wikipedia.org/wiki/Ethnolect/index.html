Ethnolect

An ethnolect is a variety of a language associated primarily with speakers of a particular ethnic group or with descendants of members of that group. The term, from ethnic and -lect (as in dialect), was coined in the sociolinguistic literature to describe speech varieties whose defining feature is the ethnic identity of their speakers rather than a geographic region.

Ethnolects may be full heritage languages maintained by an immigrant community, or they may be distinctive varieties of the majority language that retain features — in pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar, or discourse style — inherited from an ancestral language. A variety can persist for several generations after the community has shifted to the dominant language, serving as a marker of in-group identity.

Characteristics

Ethnolects are typically characterised by substrate influence: features of the ancestral language are carried over into the adopted language. Examples include distinctive intonation patterns, loanwords for culturally specific items (especially food, kinship, and religious terms), and discourse markers. Because speakers often move between the ethnolect and the standard variety according to audience and setting, ethnolect use is closely tied to code-switching and style-shifting.

Scholars distinguish between the ethnolect as spoken by first-generation immigrants, which may reflect second-language acquisition, and the ethnolect of later generations, for whom the variety is a native dialect learned in the community. The latter can remain stable and socially meaningful long after fluent knowledge of the heritage language has faded.

Examples

Well-studied ethnolects include Chicano English in the United States, Jewish American English, Italo-Australian English in Australia, and varieties associated with Greek, Polish, and Vietnamese communities in various anglophone cities. In Germany, multiethnic urban youth varieties such as Kiezdeutsch have been analysed as ethnolects or multiethnolects, since they are used by young people of several ethnic backgrounds in multiethnic neighbourhoods. The related concept of the multiethnolect was developed to capture varieties that emerge in such super-diverse urban settings rather than within a single ethnic group.
