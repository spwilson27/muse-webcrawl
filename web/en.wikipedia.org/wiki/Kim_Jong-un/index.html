Kim Jong-un (Korean: 김정은; born 8 January 1982, 1983 or 1984) is a North Korean politician who has been the supreme leader of North Korea since the death of his father Kim Jong-il in December 2011. He is the third generation of the Kim family to rule the country, after his grandfather Kim Il-sung, the state's founder.

Early life and rise

Little about Kim's early life is officially confirmed. He is believed to have been educated in Switzerland under an assumed name in the 1990s, and later at Kim Il-sung University and the Kim Il-sung Military University in Pyongyang. He was publicly revealed as heir apparent in 2010, when he was made a four-star general and vice chairman of the party's Central Military Commission despite no prior military service.

Leadership

Kim consolidated power through purges, most notoriously the 2013 execution of his uncle Jang Song-thaek. His reign has pursued the byungjin policy of simultaneous nuclear weapons and economic development: North Korea conducted nuclear tests in 2013, twice in 2016 and in 2017, and tested intercontinental ballistic missiles capable of reaching the United States. His half-brother Kim Jong-nam was assassinated in Malaysia in 2017.

Diplomacy

In 2018–2019 Kim held unprecedented summits with South Korean president Moon Jae-in and three meetings with US president Donald Trump—the first between North Korean and sitting American leaders—including the June 2018 Singapore summit. Negotiations over denuclearisation stalled after the failed Hanoi summit of February 2019. Since then Kim has expanded missile programmes and, from 2023–24, deepened ties with Russia, including a mutual defence treaty.

Personal life

Kim is married to Ri Sol-ju, who has appeared publicly more often than previous leaders' spouses; they are reported to have three children. His sister Kim Yo-jong is a senior party official and frequent spokesperson.
