Oxfordshire

Oxfordshire (abbreviated Oxon, from the Latin Oxonia) is a ceremonial county in South East England. It is bordered by Northamptonshire, Buckinghamshire, Berkshire, Wiltshire, Gloucestershire and Warwickshire. The county town and only city is Oxford, famous worldwide for its university. The county has a population of roughly 725,000 and covers about 2,605 km² (1,006 sq mi). The River Thames flows through the county for some 95 miles, and the landscape includes parts of three Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty: the Cotswolds in the north-west, the Chiltern Hills in the south-east and the North Wessex Downs in the south.

Geography

The county is drained by the Thames and its tributaries, the Cherwell, Evenlode, Windrush, Leach and Thame. In the north lie the ironstone hills around Banbury; the centre is a clay vale around Oxford; and the south is crossed by two chalk escarpments — the Chilterns and the Berkshire Downs, whose White Horse Hill, crowned by the prehistoric Uffington White Horse, is the county's highest point. The largest towns after Oxford are Banbury, Bicester, Abingdon-on-Thames (historically the county town of Berkshire until 1974), Witney, Didcot, Carterton (home to RAF Brize Norton, the Royal Air Force's largest station) and Henley-on-Thames, famed for its Royal Regatta.

History

The area was settled from prehistoric times — the Ridgeway, sometimes called Britain's oldest road, runs along the southern downs, past the Uffington White Horse and Wayland's Smithy. Roman villas dotted the county, and in the Anglo-Saxon period the region lay between the kingdoms of Mercia and Wessex. Oxford grew into a major town and university centre in the Middle Ages. During the Civil War, Oxford was the royalist capital. The historic county also included territory south of the Thames until 1974, when the Vale of White Horse (including Abingdon) was transferred from Berkshire — a change that still rankles with some. The modern county council is based in Oxford, with five district councils: Oxford City, Cherwell, South Oxfordshire, Vale of White Horse and West Oxfordshire.

Economy

Oxfordshire's economy is dominated by the "knowledge spine" running from Bicester through Oxford to Didcot and Harwell, taking in the University of Oxford, Oxford Brookes University, the science parks at Harwell Campus (home to the Diamond Light Source synchrotron and the Rutherford Appleton Laboratory), Culham (site of the UK's fusion research, including the Joint European Torus), and Milton Park. The county has consistently had one of the highest rates of business formation and lowest unemployment rates in Britain. Car manufacturing at the BMW Mini plant in Cowley, publishing in Oxford, and the motorsport cluster around the Silverstone border ("Motorsport Valley") are other significant employers.
