Ambrogio Spinola Doria, 1st Marquis of Los Balbases (1569 – 25 September 1630) was an Italian condottiero and general who served the Spanish crown. He is best known as commander of the Army of Flanders in the Eighty Years' War against the Dutch Republic, where he won great fame for the sieges of Ostend (1601–1604) and Breda (1624–1625).

Early life

Spinola was born in Genoa into the wealthy Spinola banking family. Rather than entering the family bank, he and his brother Federico raised troops at their own expense for Spanish service in Flanders.

Career in Flanders

Spinola arrived in Flanders in 1602 and took command of the long Siege of Ostend, capturing the ruined city in 1604. His manoeuvres forced Maurice of Nassau onto the defensive, and Spanish arms recovered strongly. Financial exhaustion on both sides led to the Twelve Years' Truce of 1609. When war resumed, Spinola captured Breda in 1625 after a celebrated siege — the victory immortalised in Velázquez's painting The Surrender of Breda (Las Lanzas).

Later years and death

Spinola fell from favour, lost control of the war's finance and strategy, and in 1629 was sent to Italy during the War of the Mantuan Succession. He died at the siege of Casale in 1630, reportedly broken by disgrace and the loss of his son Filippo at Casale.
