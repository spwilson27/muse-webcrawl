Zoology

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zoology is the branch of biology that studies the animal kingdom — the structure, physiology, development, classification, behaviour and distribution of animals, living and extinct. Its name derives from the Greek zōion (animal) and logos (study). Aristotle's History of Animals (4th century BC) was the first systematic work in the field.

Animals are multicellular, heterotrophic eukaryotes that typically digest food internally and can move at some life stage. Zoologists study them from sponges and jellyfish to insects — the majority of described species — and vertebrates including humans.

Subdisciplines

Entomology (insects), ornithology (birds), ichthyology (fish), herpetology (reptiles and amphibians), mammalogy, primatology, marine biology, ethology (behaviour) and palaeozoology. Much modern zoology is integrative, combining field observation with genetics and computational phylogenetics.

Methods and institutions

Field surveys, specimen collections in natural history museums, captive observation in zoos (from which the discipline took public form), tracking technology and environmental DNA sampling. Zoological nomenclature is governed by the ICZN code founded on Linnaeus.
