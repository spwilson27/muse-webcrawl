Congress of Paris (1856)

The Congress of Paris was a diplomatic conference held in Paris from February to April 1856 to negotiate the settlement ending the Crimean War. Representatives of France, the United Kingdom, the Ottoman Empire, Russia, Austria, Prussia, and Sardinia took part. The congress produced the Treaty of Paris, signed on 30 March 1856, and the separate Declaration of Paris on maritime law of 16 April 1856. It was presided over by the French foreign minister, Count Alexandre Walewski.

Treaty terms

The Treaty of Paris neutralized the Black Sea: its waters and ports were opened to merchant shipping but closed to warships of all nations, and Russia and the Ottoman Empire were forbidden to maintain arsenals on its coasts. Russia returned southern Bessarabia (the mouths of the Danube) to Moldavia, relinquished its claim to a protectorate over the Danubian Principalities (Moldavia and Wallachia) and over Ottoman Christians, and restored Kars to the Ottomans in exchange for the return of Sevastopol and other captured places. The Danube was placed under an international commission guaranteeing freedom of navigation. The Ottoman Empire was formally admitted to the European concert and its independence and territorial integrity guaranteed — while a firman promising reforms for its Christian subjects was noted but not made an enforceable condition.

Declaration of Paris

The Declaration Respecting Maritime Law, adopted by the plenipotentiaries and later acceded to by most states, laid down four rules: privateering is abolished; a neutral flag covers enemy goods except contraband of war; neutral goods except contraband are not liable to capture under an enemy flag; and blockades, to be binding, must be effective. It is regarded as the first modern multilateral treaty of the law of naval warfare.

Significance

The congress marked the high point and the beginning of the decline of the Concert of Europe; it humiliated Russia, checked its expansion toward the Balkans and the Mediterranean for a generation, and raised the prestige of Napoleon III's France. Sardinia's participation, earned by its contingent in the Crimea, allowed Cavour to raise the Italian question before Europe. Russia repudiated the Black Sea clauses in 1870 during the Franco-Prussian War, the first of the settlement's provisions to collapse.
