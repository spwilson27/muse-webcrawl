Antonio José de Sucre y Alcalá (3 February 1795 – 4 June 1830), known as the Gran Mariscal de Ayacucho (Grand Marshal of Ayacucho), was a Venezuelan independence leader who served as the fourth president of Peru and the second president of Bolivia. He was one of Simón Bolívar's closest friends and most important generals in the Spanish American wars of independence.

Early life

Sucre was born in Cumaná, in the Captaincy General of Venezuela, into a wealthy family of Belgian and Spanish descent. Educated as an engineer, he joined the patriot cause in 1811 after the Venezuelan declaration of independence and rose rapidly through the ranks for his tactical skill and organisational ability.

Military career

Sucre commanded patriot forces in the south, liberating Ecuador with victory at the Battle of Pichincha on 24 May 1822, which secured the independence of the territory that became Ecuador. His greatest triumph came on 9 December 1824 at the Battle of Ayacucho in Peru, where his army decisively defeated the last major Spanish royalist force in South America, effectively ending Spanish rule on the continent.

Presidency of Bolivia

After helping create the Republic of Bolivia in 1825, Sucre served as its president from 1826 to 1828, drafting a constitution and attempting liberal reforms. A mutiny and invasion pressure forced his resignation in 1828, and he retired to Quito.

Death and legacy

Sucre was assassinated on 4 June 1830 in the mountains near Pasto while travelling to Quito, amid the collapse of Gran Colombia. He is remembered as one of the most capable and humane commanders of the independence era: the Bolivian city of Sucre, its constitutional capital, bears his name, as do a Venezuelan state and numerous institutions across South America.
