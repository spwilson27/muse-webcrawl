Preparatory school (United Kingdom)

In the United Kingdom, a preparatory school (or prep school) is a fee-charging independent school that educates children up to the age of 11 or 13, preparing them — historically especially boys — for entry to senior independent schools, traditionally known as public schools.

Overview

Prep schools are part of the primary sector of British independent education. Pupils typically leave at 11+ for girls' schools or at 13+ for many boys' and co-educational schools, historically sitting the Common Entrance examination at 13+. Many prep schools are affiliated with particular senior schools, acting as "feeder" schools, though pupils increasingly transfer at 11 as more senior schools have lowered their entry points.

History

Preparatory schools developed in the 19th century as the major public schools raised their entry ages and entrance standards. They expanded greatly in the Victorian and Edwardian eras, often as small boarding institutions run by a headmaster and his wife. Boarding was once the norm; today most pupils are day pupils, though many schools retain boarders.

Organisation

Many prep schools belong to the Incorporated Association of Preparatory Schools (IAPS) or the Independent Schools Council. A school educating pupils only to age 11 is sometimes termed a "pre-prep" for its youngest pupils (ages 4–7 or 8).
