Women's Institute
Founded: 1897 (Canada); 1915 (UK)
Type: Women's voluntary organisation

The Women's Institute (WI) is a community-based organisation for women, founded in Stoney Creek, Ontario, Canada, in 1897, and established in the United Kingdom in 1915. The National Federation of Women's Institutes (NFWI) in England, Wales, Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man is one of the largest voluntary women's organisations in the UK, with thousands of local institutes.

History

The first Women's Institute was founded in Canada by Adelaide Hoodless to improve women's knowledge of domestic science and child care. The movement spread to Britain during the First World War, where institutes encouraged rural women to grow and preserve food. The WI played a notable role in organising evacuee care and food production during the Second World War.

Activities

Local WIs hold monthly meetings with speakers, demonstrations and social activities, and the federation campaigns on issues ranging from rural services and food labelling to climate change and violence against women. The WI is also popularly associated with jam-making, baking and the hymn "Jerusalem", sung at meetings.
