Free Church of Scotland

The Free Church of Scotland is a Presbyterian denomination that traces its origins to the Disruption of 1843, when more than 450 ministers broke away from the established Church of Scotland over the issue of patronage and the spiritual independence of the Church. The present-day Free Church is the continuation of that portion which did not join the union of 1900.

History

Led by Thomas Chalmers, the evangelical party left the General Assembly of 1843 and formed a fully independent church, building hundreds of churches, schools, and a theological college (New College, Edinburgh) within a few years. In 1900 most of the Free Church united with the United Presbyterian Church to form the United Free Church, but a minority — concentrated in the Highlands — remained outside and retained the Free Church name, winning legal recognition of its claim to the church's property in a famous House of Lords judgment of 1904.

Present day

The Free Church of Scotland today has around one hundred congregations, mainly in Scotland with a small presence elsewhere, and is known for its conservative evangelical theology, psalm singing, and its seminary, the Edinburgh Theological Seminary. A further division in 2000 produced the Free Church of Scotland (Continuing).
