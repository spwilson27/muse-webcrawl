Minolta X-700

The Minolta X-700 is a 35 mm single-lens reflex film camera introduced by Minolta in 1981. It was the flagship of the X-series manual-focus SLRs and remained in production, with manufacture later moving outside Japan, into the late 1990s.

Features

The X-700 offered programmed autoexposure (P mode) in addition to aperture-priority and full manual metering, using Minolta's MD lens mount. Through-the-lens flash metering, an acute-matte focusing screen, exposure compensation and a depth-of-field preview were among its features aimed at advanced amateurs.

It formed part of Minolta's MPS (Minolta Program System) with compatible program flashes and motor drives. The camera won the European Camera of the Year award in 1981.

Legacy

Alongside the cheaper X-570 and X-370, the X-700 was among the last manual-focus SLRs Minolta made before the autofocus Maxxum line, and it remains popular with film photographers for its bright viewfinder and program mode.
