Clodia

Clodia (born Claudia, c. 95 BC, sometimes identified as Clodia Pulchra) was a Roman noblewoman of the late Republic, sister of the tribune Publius Clodius Pulcher and wife of Quintus Caecilius Metellus Celer. She is famous through Cicero's hostile portrait of her and as the probable model for 'Lesbia', the beloved in the poems of Catullus.

Life

Born into the patrician Claudii, Clodia adopted the plebeian spelling of her name in solidarity with her brother Clodius. After her husband Metellus Celer died in 59 BC — amid rumours of poisoning — she was linked by gossip with Marcus Caelius Rufus. When Caelius was tried in 56 BC, Cicero, defending him, demolished Clodia's credibility in the Pro Caelio, portraying her as a promiscuous poisoner; the speech is a masterpiece of innuendo and a notoriously biased source.

Lesbia

Apuleius names Catullus's Lesbia as a Clodia; most scholars identify her with this Clodia, whose affair with the poet would explain the cycle of love and bitter recrimination in his verses. The identification, like much of her biography, remains debated.
