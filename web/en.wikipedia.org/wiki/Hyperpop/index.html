Hyperpop

Hyperpop is a loosely defined electronic music movement and microgenre characterized by an exaggerated, maximalist take on popular music. Artists in the style typically integrate pop, electronic dance music, hip hop, and avant-garde sensibilities, using heavily processed, pitch-shifted vocals, abrasive metallic synths, and frenetic tempos. The genre emerged in the mid-2010s around the British label and collective PC Music, founded by A. G. Cook, and was popularized in the late 2010s and early 2020s by artists such as 100 gecs, Charli XCX, and Sophie.

Characteristics

Hyperpop songs often juxtapose sugary melodies with distorted bass, glitch effects, and abrupt transitions, drawing on bubblegum pop, Eurodance, trance, emo, nu metal, and chiptune. Vocals are frequently Auto-Tuned and pitch-shifted to extremes, blurring gender presentation — a feature linked to the genre's strong association with LGBTQ and internet-native communities. Critics describe the sound as both a sincere embrace and an ironic critique of commercial pop excess.

History

The term circulated informally before being applied to a Spotify editorial playlist in 2019, which cemented "hyperpop" as a category and spotlighted artists like 100 gecs, whose album 1000 gecs (2019) became a breakout for the style. Charli XCX's How I'm Feeling Now (2020), produced with A. G. Cook during COVID-19 lockdowns, brought the sound to wider audiences. The death of Sophie in 2021 prompted widespread tributes to her pioneering production work. By the mid-2020s the label was contested — some key artists rejected the term — and its aesthetics had diffused into mainstream pop, digicore, and internet rap scenes.
