Vostok Station (Russian: Станция Восток, "Station East") is a Russian research station in inland Princess Elizabeth Land, Antarctica. Founded by the Soviet Union on 16 December 1957 during the International Geophysical Year, it sits near the south geomagnetic pole, some 1,300 kilometres from the geographic South Pole and about 1,260 kilometres from the nearest coast, atop approximately 3,700 metres of ice.

Climate

Vostok is one of the coldest and most isolated places on Earth. On 21 July 1983 it recorded −89.2 °C (−128.6 °F), the lowest directly measured natural air temperature on the planet. Winter temperatures routinely fall below −70 °C, and the station experiences nearly four months of polar night. The air is extremely dry, winds are katabatic, and the high elevation means the oxygen level is comparable to about 5,000 metres at mid-latitudes.

Research: ice cores and Lake Vostok

Drilling at Vostok produced one of the most important ice cores in climate science: by 1998 a joint Russian–French–American project had reached 3,623 metres, recovering ice about 420,000 years old and revealing four full glacial–interglacial cycles, with temperature and greenhouse-gas records that demonstrated the tight coupling of carbon dioxide and climate. Beneath the station lies Lake Vostok, one of the largest subglacial lakes in the world, sealed under nearly 4 kilometres of ice; in February 2012 Russian scientists drilled into the lake's surface water for the first time. The station also conducts geomagnetic, meteorological, and upper-atmosphere observations.
