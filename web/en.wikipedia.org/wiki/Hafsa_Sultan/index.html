Hafsa Sultan (Ottoman Turkish: حفصه سلطان; died 19 March 1534) was the wife of Sultan Selim I and the mother of Suleiman the Magnificent of the Ottoman Empire. She was the first imperial consort to be formally styled "Valide Sultan" — sultan's mother — and exercised considerable influence during the first part of her son's reign, from his accession in 1520 until her death in 1534. She is often regarded as the first powerful figure of the period historians call the Sultanate of Women.

Origins and life

Hafsa's origins are debated: Ottoman tradition and some historians suggest she may have been a daughter of Meñli I Giray, Khan of Crimea, while others hold that she was a convert of Christian background taken into the imperial harem like other consorts of the period. She bore Suleiman in Trabzon in 1494, where Selim was provincial governor, and accompanied her son to his princely governorships at Kefe and then Manisa. Following Ottoman custom she oversaw Suleiman's household in Manisa, where she founded a major charitable complex.

Valide Sultan

When Suleiman ascended the throne in 1520, Hafsa moved to the Old Palace in Istanbul as the highest-ranking woman of the dynasty. She acted as her son's adviser and intercessor, corresponded with foreign rulers — including the king of Poland — and received petitions from officials and subjects. Her standing is reflected in her generous allowance and in the deference paid to her by the court. She died in 1534 and was buried beside Selim I at the Yavuz Selim Mosque complex in Istanbul; Suleiman succeeded her in influence first by his chief consort Hürrem Sultan.

Foundations

In Manisa, Hafsa built the Sultaniye complex (1522) — mosque, primary school, hospice and a hospital (darüşşifa) — whose mental-health and medical services became famous, and the town's Mesir Macunu festival, distributing a spiced paste said to have restored her health, is still celebrated and inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list.
