OASIS

OASIS (Organization for the Advancement of Structured Information Standards) is a global nonprofit consortium that works on the development, convergence, and adoption of open standards for cybersecurity, blockchain, Internet of things, emergency management, cloud computing, legal data exchange, energy, and other areas. Founded in 1993, it was originally known as the SGML Open consortium before adopting its current name in 1998.

Standards

Well-known specifications produced or hosted by OASIS include the DocBook documentation format, the OpenDocument office file format (ODF), the SAML security standard, the AMQP messaging protocol, the MQTT IoT protocol, and the Common Alerting Protocol. OASIS standards are developed through technical committees whose work is open to members and, for many specifications, publicly reviewed.

Membership

The consortium's members include technology vendors, government agencies, universities, and individual experts. Its standards process emphasises royalty-free or reasonable licensing terms, and many OASIS specifications have gone on to be adopted by other standards bodies such as ISO.
