Dall sheep

Scientific name: Ovis dalli
Family: Bovidae
Range: Alaska and northwestern Canada
Status: Least Concern

The Dall sheep (Ovis dalli), also known as the thinhorn sheep, is a species of wild sheep native to the mountain ranges of northwestern North America, from British Columbia and the Mackenzie Mountains to Alaska. It is named for the American naturalist William Healey Dall.

Description

Dall sheep are distinguished by their white or near-white coat, which sets them apart from the brown bighorn sheep to the south. Rams carry massive, curled amber horns that grow throughout life; ewes have shorter, slightly curved horns. Animals range from about 60 to 90 kg, with rams considerably larger than ewes.

Behaviour and habitat

They inhabit arid alpine country, grazing sedges, grasses and lichens and retreating to steep, rugged terrain to escape wolves and bears. During the autumn rut, rams clash head-on in charges whose crack can carry for long distances. Two subspecies are usually recognised: the snowy-white Dall's sheep proper and the darker Stone's sheep of British Columbia and the southern Yukon.
