Eminönü

Eminönü is a predominantly commercial waterfront area of central Istanbul, Turkey, located within the Fatih district on the historical peninsula. Until 2009 it formed a separate district of the city. Eminönü occupies the point where the Golden Horn meets the Sea of Marmara, opposite Galata across the Galata Bridge.

History

The shoreline of Eminönü has been a harbour since the founding of Byzantium; the Neorion and Prosphorion harbours served the ancient city, and the area remained the maritime commercial heart of Constantinople through the Byzantine and Ottoman periods. Venetian and Genoese merchants had quarters nearby. After the conquest of 1453, the bazaars and markets around the port expanded, and the district became the great wholesale trading hub of the empire.

Landmarks and transport

Eminönü is dominated by the New Mosque (Yeni Cami), completed in 1665, the Spice Bazaar (Egyptian Bazaar), and the large ferry terminals from which boats depart for Üsküdar, Kadıköy, and the Bosphorus. The Galata Bridge, Sirkeci station—historic terminus of the Orient Express—and bustling markets make it one of the busiest transport interchanges in Istanbul.

Administration

In 2009 the district of Eminönü was abolished and merged into Fatih. The name continues to denote the neighbourhood around the ferry piers and bazaars at the mouth of the Golden Horn.
