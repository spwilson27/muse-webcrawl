Prosecco

Prosecco is an Italian DOC and DOCG white wine, almost always sparkling or semi-sparkling, produced in a large area spanning nine provinces in the Veneto and Friuli-Venezia Giulia regions of north-eastern Italy. It is made principally from the Glera grape (historically itself called Prosecco), and takes its name from the village of Prosecco near Trieste. Prosecco is the main ingredient of the Bellini and Aperol spritz cocktails and, by production volume, has become the world's best-selling sparkling wine, ahead of Champagne.

Production

Unlike Champagne, Prosecco is produced mainly by the Charmat (Martinotti) method, in which the second fermentation takes place in large pressurised stainless-steel tanks rather than in the bottle. This preserves the grape's fresh, aromatic character — typical notes are white flowers, apple, pear, and citrus — and reduces cost. Styles range from fully sparkling spumante to lightly sparkling frizzante (sometimes closed with a tied cork, spago) and a small production of still tranquillo. Sweetness levels run brut, extra dry (the classic style, in fact slightly sweeter than brut), and dry.

Appellations and history

The historic heartland is the steep hills between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene, granted DOCG status in 2009 (as was the smaller Asolo zone) and inscribed by UNESCO as a World Heritage cultural landscape in 2019; the broader Prosecco DOC was created the same year, and the grape's name was changed to Glera so that "Prosecco" could be protected as a geographical designation. Antonio Carpenè pioneered its sparkling production in the 19th century, and Carpenè Malvolti (founded 1868) is often credited with the first spumante Prosecco. Global demand surged in the 2000s–2010s, and production now runs to several hundred million bottles a year.
