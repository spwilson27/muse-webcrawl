Individualism

Individualism
AntonymCollectivism
Related ideasLiberalism, individual rights, self-reliance, autonomy

Individualism is the moral stance, political philosophy, ideology, and social outlook that emphasises the intrinsic worth of the individual. Individualists promote realising one's goals and desires, valuing independence and self-reliance, and advocate that the interests of the individual should gain precedence over the state or a social group, while opposing external interference upon one's own interests by society or institutions such as the government.

History

Although ideas of individuality appear in many traditions, individualism as a named doctrine emerged in 19th-century Europe and America. The word itself was first used, often pejoratively, in post-revolutionary France; it was popularised in English partly through translations of Alexis de Tocqueville's Democracy in America, which described a new American disposition to withdraw into private life. Philosophical roots are traced to Enlightenment thinkers such as John Locke, who grounded property and government in individual rights, and to John Stuart Mill, whose On Liberty (1859) defended individuality against the tyranny of custom and majority opinion.

Varieties

Political individualism underlies classical liberalism and libertarianism, holding that individuals possess natural rights and that government's role should be limited. Methodological individualism in the social sciences holds that social phenomena must be explained by the actions of individuals. Ethical individualism and the ideal of self-reliance, celebrated by Ralph Waldo Emerson, stress personal development and nonconformity. Critics from communitarian, socialist, and conservative traditions argue that individualism understates human social dependence and corrodes communal bonds. In cross-cultural psychology, individualism is contrasted with collectivism as a dimension along which societies differ.
