Blood orange

Blood orange
SpeciesCitrus × sinensis
OriginMediterranean, probably Sicily or Spain
Main cultivarsMoro, Tarocco, Sanguinello
Distinctive pigmentAnthocyanins

The blood orange is a variety of the sweet orange (Citrus × sinensis) with crimson, almost blood-coloured flesh. The distinctive dark red colour comes from anthocyanin pigments, which develop when the fruit ripens in a climate with warm days and cool nights.

Cultivars

The three principal types are the deeply coloured Moro, grown largely in California and Sicily; the Tarocco, a large, sweet, half-blood orange prized in Italy; and the Sanguinello, from Spain, with red-streaked skin and flesh. Blood oranges have been cultivated around the Mediterranean since the 18th century and are a signature crop of eastern Sicily, where "Arancia Rossa di Sicilia" holds protected status.

Flavour and uses

The flesh is sweet with a distinctive tang often compared to raspberry or strawberry. Blood oranges are eaten fresh, juiced (the juice is dark red and must be consumed promptly), and used in salads, desserts, marmalades, and cocktails. The anthocyanins that colour the fruit are antioxidants also found in berries and red wine.
