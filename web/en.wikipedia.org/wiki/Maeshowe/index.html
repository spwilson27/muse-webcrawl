Maeshowe

Maeshowe (also spelled Maes Howe) is a Neolithic chambered cairn and passage grave situated near Loch of Stenness on Mainland, Orkney, Scotland. Built around 2800 BCE, it is one of the finest Neolithic monuments in Britain and forms part of the Heart of Neolithic Orkney UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Architecture

The mound, about 35 metres in diameter, covers a stone-built chamber reached by a low passage some eleven metres long. The passage is aligned so that the setting sun at the winter solstice shines along it into the chamber. Inside, the main chamber rises to a corbelled roof (the present roof is modern) and contains three side cells raised above floor level, with four great standing stones at its corners.

Viking runes

In the twelfth century, Norse visitors — probably sheltering from a storm, as the inscriptions themselves record — broke into the tomb and carved more than thirty runic inscriptions on its walls, the largest collection of runes in situ anywhere. The inscriptions include boasts, verses, a famous lion carving and references to figures from the Orkneyinga saga era.
