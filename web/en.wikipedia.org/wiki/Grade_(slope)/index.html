Grade (slope)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Grade is the ratio of vertical rise to horizontal run.

The grade (also called slope, incline, gradient, mainfall, pitch, or rise) of a physical feature, landform, or constructed line refers to the tangent of the angle that the surface makes with the horizontal. It is most often expressed as a percentage: the amount of vertical rise over a horizontal distance of 100 units.

Expression

Grade can be stated as a percentage (rise/run × 100), as an angle in degrees, as a ratio (for example 1 in 20), or in per mille, which is common in railway practice. A 100% grade corresponds to a 45° angle. The steepest streets in the world, such as Baldwin Street in Dunedin, New Zealand, and Ffordd Pen Llech in Wales, have grades of roughly 35–37%.

Applications

Roads

Road designers limit grades to ensure vehicles can climb and descend safely; interstate highways in the United States generally keep grades to about 6%. Grade affects braking distance, fuel consumption, and the need for runaway truck ramps on mountain descents. Road signs warn of steep grades with percentage figures.

Railways

The ruling grade of a railway line determines the load a locomotive can haul. Mainline railways typically keep grades under about 2–2.5%; steeper inclines historically required banking engines, rack systems, or cable haulage.

Accessibility

Building codes regulate the slope of ramps for wheelchair access; the Americans with Disabilities Act standards specify a maximum running slope of 1:12 (about 8.33%).
