Constitutional Court of Spain

The Constitutional Court of Spain (Spanish: Tribunal Constitucional de España) is the supreme interpreter of the Spanish Constitution, with the power to determine the constitutionality of acts and statutes of the Spanish Government and of the autonomous communities. It is defined in Part VI, articles 159 to 165, of the Constitution of Spain of 1978.

Composition

The Court is composed of twelve magistrates appointed by the King of Spain: four on the proposal of the Congress of Deputies, four on the proposal of the Senate, two on the proposal of the Government, and two on the proposal of the General Council of the Judiciary. Magistrates serve a single nine-year term and must be jurists of recognised competence with more than fifteen years of professional experience.

Powers

The Court hears appeals of unconstitutionality (recurso de inconstitucionalidad) against laws, questions of unconstitutionality raised by ordinary courts, conflicts of competence between the State and the autonomous communities, and the individual recurso de amparo for the protection of fundamental rights. Its judgments are final and binding on all public powers.

History

The Court began functioning in 1980, following the Organic Law on the Constitutional Court of 1979. It has played a central role in Spanish public life, including its landmark 2010 judgment on the Statute of Autonomy of Catalonia, which had significant political repercussions.
