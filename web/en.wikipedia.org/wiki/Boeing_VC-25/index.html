The Boeing VC-25 is a military version of the Boeing 747 airliner, modified for presidential transport. Two VC-25A aircraft, based on the 747-200B, serve with the United States Air Force as the primary transport of the president of the United States; when the president is aboard, the aircraft uses the call sign Air Force One. They are operated by the Presidential Airlift Group of the 89th Airlift Wing at Joint Base Andrews, Maryland.

Design and capabilities

The VC-25A has three decks and about 4,000 square feet (372 m²) of floor space. The presidential suite at the front includes an office, bedroom, shower and dressing room; the aircraft also has a medical facility with an operating table, a conference/dining room, two galleys capable of preparing 100 meals, communications equipment allowing the president to command the armed forces from the air, and quarters for staff, Secret Service and press. The aircraft can be refuelled in flight, giving it effectively unlimited range, and its wiring is hardened against the electromagnetic pulse of a nuclear explosion. Defensive systems include radar jammers and flare dispensers. The aircraft generally flies with an identical decoy/escort schedule, the two jets being SAM 28000 and SAM 29000 (tail numbers 82-8000 and 92-9000).

History

The VC-25As were ordered in 1985 under President Ronald Reagan and entered service in 1990 under George H. W. Bush, replacing the Boeing VC-137s. Memorable appearances include flying President George W. Bush on 11 September 2001, and carrying the bodies of former presidents, including Ronald Reagan and Gerald Ford, to their burial places.

Replacement

The successor VC-25B programme, based on two Boeing 747-8 airframes originally built for a defunct Russian airline, was contracted in 2018 with a fixed-price agreement. The heavily modified aircraft, with new electrical power systems, communications and interiors, were originally expected in the mid-2020s but have been delayed, with delivery projected later in the decade. They will retain the famous livery designed under Jacqueline Kennedy and updated with Raymond Loewy for the VC-137 era.
