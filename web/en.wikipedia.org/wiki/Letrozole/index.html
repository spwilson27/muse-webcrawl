Letrozole

Trade nameFemara, others
ClassAromatase inhibitor
RoutesBy mouth
FormulaC17H11N5

Letrozole, sold under the brand name Femara among others, is an aromatase inhibitor medication used in the treatment of hormone receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women. It is also used off-label for ovulation induction in women with infertility, including polycystic ovary syndrome.

Medical uses

Letrozole is approved as adjuvant treatment of early breast cancer and as first-line treatment of advanced breast cancer in postmenopausal women. By blocking the enzyme aromatase, it prevents the conversion of androgens into estrogens, sharply reducing estrogen levels that fuel estrogen receptor-positive tumours. Trials such as BIG 1-98 compared letrozole with tamoxifen as adjuvant therapy.

Ovulation induction

Since the early 2000s letrozole has been used off-label to induce ovulation, and several trials found live-birth rates equal or superior to clomiphene citrate in women with polycystic ovary syndrome, with a lower rate of multiple gestation.

Side effects

Common side effects include hot flashes, joint pain (arthralgia), fatigue, and nausea. Long-term estrogen suppression can reduce bone mineral density, increasing fracture risk, and may raise cholesterol. Letrozole must not be used during pregnancy.

Pharmacology

Letrozole is a non-steroidal, reversible competitive inhibitor of aromatase (CYP19A1). It is metabolised in the liver, mainly by CYP2A6 and CYP3A4, and has an elimination half-life of about two days.
