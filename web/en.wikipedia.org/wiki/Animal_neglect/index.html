Animal neglect

Animal neglect
Failure to provide basic care to an animal
FieldAnimal welfare, law
Related toCruelty to animals, animal hoarding

Animal neglect is a form of cruelty to animals in which a person fails to provide an animal in their care with the basic necessities of food, water, shelter, sanitation and veterinary attention. Unlike active abuse, neglect is usually a failure to act, but it can cause prolonged suffering and death, and it is among the most common types of animal cruelty reported to authorities.

Forms

Neglect ranges from simple neglect — for example a dog left without adequate food or water, or an untreated wound — to severe cases involving starvation, dehydration and untreated disease. Animal hoarding, in which a person keeps far more animals than they can care for, is a recognised pattern often associated with mental illness. Neglect also occurs in farming, breeding establishments and laboratories.

Law

Most jurisdictions criminalise neglect under animal-protection statutes: examples include the UK's Animal Welfare Act 2006, which imposes a duty of care on keepers, and state cruelty laws in the United States, which typically classify serious neglect as a misdemeanour or felony. Enforcement is carried out by animal-control officers, police and inspectorates of bodies such as the RSPCA and ASPCA; penalties can include fines, seizure of animals, bans on keeping animals and imprisonment.

Responses

Because neglect often stems from ignorance, poverty or illness rather than malice, many agencies combine prosecution with education, support and, in hoarding cases, mental-health intervention. Studies have also noted links between animal neglect and neglect of children or elderly people in the same household, prompting cross-reporting schemes.
