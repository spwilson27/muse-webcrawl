Gap year

A gap year, also known as a sabbatical year, is a period, typically a year, taken by a student as a break between secondary school and higher education, or between university and the start of a career. During a gap year, students may engage in travel, work, volunteering, internships, or advanced courses.

History

The gap year concept developed in the United Kingdom in the 1960s and 1970s, as post-war affluence and cheap air travel made extended travel accessible to young people. It was partly popularised by the travels of the "hippie trail" generation and later institutionalised through organisations offering structured volunteer placements abroad. The practice spread to the United States, Australia, and other countries; in the US, some universities actively encourage admitted students to defer enrolment for a year.

Activities

Common gap year activities include:

  • Travel and backpacking — often in Southeast Asia, Australia, South America or Europe.
  • Volunteering — with charities, conservation projects, or teaching programmes overseas.
  • Paid work — saving money for university or gaining experience.
  • Internships and work experience related to a future career.
  • Adventure and skills courses — such as ski instructing, sailing, or language study.

Benefits and criticism

Research suggests gap year participants often report increased maturity, independence, and clarity about their studies and careers, and some studies associate gap years with improved academic performance at university. Critics note that unstructured gap years can be expensive, that some commercial programmes have been accused of "voluntourism" with limited benefit to host communities, and that a poorly planned year may simply delay entry into education or work.
