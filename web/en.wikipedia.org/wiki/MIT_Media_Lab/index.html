The MIT Media Lab is an interdisciplinary research laboratory at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), growing out of MIT's Architecture Machine Group in the School of Architecture. Its research does not fit into traditional disciplinary categories, drawing instead from technology, media, science, art, and design. Media Lab researchers include engineers, computer scientists, physicists, biotechnologists, artists, and musicians; as of 2024, the lab was home to some 25 research groups and roughly 650 people, including faculty, staff, graduate students, and visiting researchers.

The lab was founded in 1985 by Nicholas Negroponte and former MIT president Jerome B. Wiesner, and is known for an unconventional, "antidisciplinary" culture and for spinning off influential companies and technologies, including electronic ink (E Ink), LEGO Mindstorms, and the Guitar Hero and Rock Band video games developed by spin-off Harmonix.

History

Negroponte, a faculty member in architecture, had founded the Architecture Machine Group in 1967 to explore human–computer interaction. When the Media Lab opened in 1985 in the Wiesner Building (E15, designed by I. M. Pei), it was one of the first laboratories explicitly devoted to the digital convergence of computing, communications, and media. Negroponte served as director until 2000, when Walter Bender took over; later directors included Frank Moss (2006–2011) and Joi Ito (2011–2019).

In 2009–2010 the lab expanded into a new six-story building, E14, designed by Pritzker Prize-winning architect Fumihiko Maki, connected to the original Wiesner Building. In 2019, director Joi Ito resigned after revelations about donations accepted from convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein; the ensuing scandal prompted a wider institutional review. Dava Newman, a former NASA deputy administrator, became director in 2021.

Research

Research groups at the Media Lab include Affective Computing (founded by Rosalind Picard), Tangible Media (Hiroshi Ishii), Biomechatronics (Hugh Herr), Personal Robots (Cynthia Breazeal), Responsive Environments, Viral Communications, Lifelong Kindergarten (Mitchel Resnick, origin of the Scratch programming language), and the Center for Bits and Atoms (Neil Gershenfeld), which pioneered the fab lab movement. Projects have included wearable computing, human augmentation, prosthetics, ubiquitous computing, and "smart cities" research.

Funding and spin-offs

The lab is funded largely by corporate consortium sponsorships; member companies pay annual fees in exchange for access to research and intellectual property. Notable spin-offs include E Ink Corporation, Harmonix, Oblong Industries, Formlabs, Affectiva, and the One Laptop per Child association, launched by Negroponte in 2005 to produce low-cost laptops for children in developing countries.
