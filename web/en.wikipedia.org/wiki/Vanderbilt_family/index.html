Vanderbilt family

The Vanderbilt family is an American family who gained prominence during the Gilded Age. Their success began with the shipping and railroad empires of Cornelius Vanderbilt, and the family expanded into various other areas, including the construction of grand mansions such as the Biltmore Estate and The Breakers.

History

Cornelius "Commodore" Vanderbilt built a fortune first in steamboats and then in railroads, consolidating the New York Central Railroad. At his death he was among the richest Americans in history. His son William Henry Vanderbilt doubled the fortune and expanded the rail system.

Gilded Age prominence

Later generations became leading figures in New York society, building palatial Fifth Avenue houses and summer "cottages" in Newport, Rhode Island. Family members included Alva Vanderbilt, a suffragist; William Kissam Vanderbilt, a racing enthusiast; and George Washington Vanderbilt II, creator of Biltmore in Asheville, North Carolina.

Decline of the fortune

By the mid-20th century the great fortune had been dispersed through inheritance, spending, taxes and the demolition of many mansions, though descendants remain notable in arts, media and business — including journalist Anderson Cooper, a descendant through Gloria Vanderbilt.
