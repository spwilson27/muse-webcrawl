Gender apartheid

Gender apartheid (or sexual apartheid) is the institutionalised separation and discrimination of people on the basis of sex or gender, enforced through law and state policy. The term draws an analogy with racial apartheid in South Africa.

Usage

The term has been applied most prominently to Afghanistan under the Taliban, where women and girls have been barred from secondary and higher education, most employment, and many public spaces, and to aspects of the legal system of Iran. United Nations experts and human rights organisations have called for gender apartheid to be recognised as a crime against humanity in international law.

Debate

Supporters argue the term captures the systematic, state-enforced nature of such regimes of exclusion. Critics debate whether the analogy to racial apartheid is exact, while campaigns continue to press for its codification in a future crimes-against-humanity treaty.
