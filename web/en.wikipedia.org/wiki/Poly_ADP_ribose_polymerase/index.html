Poly ADP ribose polymerase

Poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) is a family of proteins involved in a number of cellular processes such as DNA repair, genomic stability, and programmed cell death. The PARP family comprises 17 members. They vary greatly in structure and function within the cell. PARP1, PARP2, VPARP (PARP4), Tankyrase-1 and -2 (PARP-5a or TNKS, and PARP-5b or TNKS2) have a confirmed poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase activity. Others have been shown to have only mono ADP-ribose transferase activity.

Function

The main role of PARP (discovered due to its role in the cellular response to DNA damage) is to detect and initiate an immediate cellular response to metabolic, chemical, or radiation-induced single-strand DNA breaks (SSB). Once activated, PARP transfers ADP-ribose units from NAD+ onto target proteins — including itself — building poly(ADP-ribose) chains that recruit DNA repair machinery to the site of damage.

PARP inhibitors

Inhibitors of PARP, such as olaparib, rucaparib, niraparib, and talazoparib, are a class of anticancer drugs. They exploit the concept of synthetic lethality: cancer cells with defects in homologous recombination repair (for example, BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutations) are especially sensitive to PARP inhibition, because blocking single-strand break repair leads to lethal double-strand breaks in those cells.
