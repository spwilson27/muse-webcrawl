Gabby Pahinui

Charles Philip "Gabby" Pahinui (April 22, 1921 – October 13, 1980) was a Hawaiian slack-key guitarist and singer. He is considered one of the most influential musicians in Hawaiian music history, frequently called the "godfather of modern Hawaiian music".

Pahinui was a master of the Hawaiian slack-key guitar style.

Life and career

Pahinui was born in Honolulu and grew up in the Kaimuki district. Largely self-taught, he began playing professionally as a teenager. He recorded for the Bell Records label in the 1940s, making some of the first widely heard slack-key guitar recordings, including "Hi'ilawe". He performed with the Sons of Hawaii, led by Eddie Kamae, and with his own group, the Gabby Pahinui Hawaiian Band.

In the 1970s his recordings for Panini Records, and the celebrated album recorded with a young Ry Cooder (1976's Chicken Skin Music featured him as a guest), brought slack-key guitar to a worldwide audience and helped fuel the Hawaiian music renaissance. His backyard jam sessions in Waimanalo became legendary gatherings for Hawaiian musicians.

Legacy

His sons Cyril, Bla, and Martin Pahinui all became notable Hawaiian musicians. The annual Gabby Pahinui Waimanalo Kanikapila festival honors his memory, and he was inducted into the Hawaiian Music Hall of Fame.
