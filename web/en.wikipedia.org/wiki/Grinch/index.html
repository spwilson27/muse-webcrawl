The Grinch is a fictional character created by Dr. Seuss, best known as the protagonist of the 1957 children's book How the Grinch Stole Christmas! A grouchy, green, pot-bellied creature with a heart "two sizes too small", he lives in a cave atop Mount Crumpit overlooking Whoville with his dog, Max. Annoyed by the Whos' Christmas celebrations, he disguises himself as Santa Claus and steals all their presents and decorations — only to discover that Christmas "doesn't come from a store", whereupon his heart grows three sizes and he returns everything and joins the feast.

Adaptations

The character was popularised by Chuck Jones's 1966 animated television special, narrated by Boris Karloff (who also voiced the Grinch) and featuring the song "You're a Mean One, Mr. Grinch", sung by Thurl Ravenscroft. Ron Howard's live-action film How the Grinch Stole Christmas (2000) starred Jim Carrey under heavy prosthetic make-up and expanded the character's backstory. Illumination's animated film The Grinch (2018), with Benedict Cumberbatch in the title role, again retold the story for a new generation. The Grinch has also appeared in stage musicals and in Seuss works including Halloween Is Grinch Night (1977) and The Grinch Grinches the Cat in the Hat (1982).

Cultural impact

"Grinch" has entered English as a common noun for a spoilsport or a person who hates Christmas festivities. The character is one of the most recognised in American Christmas culture, appearing annually in broadcasts, parades (including a balloon in the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade), advertising and merchandise.
