The cylindrical equal-area projection is a family of cylindrical, equal-area map projections: the sphere is projected onto a cylinder tangent or secant to it, and then unrolled, with the vertical scale compressed so that areas are represented correctly everywhere. Meridians and parallels form a rectangular grid.

Forms

The family is parameterised by the standard parallels at which the projection is true to scale. Johann Heinrich Lambert introduced the version with the equator as standard parallel in 1772. Setting the standard parallels at 45° gives the Gall–Peters projection, publicised by James Gall in the 19th century and by Arno Peters in 1973 amid controversy over the political implications of map choice. Other members include the Behrmann projection (standard parallels 30°) and the Hobo–Dyer projection (37.5°).

Properties

Because the projection is equal-area, countries and continents appear in their true relative sizes — Africa, for instance, appears far larger relative to Europe and North America than on the Mercator projection. The trade-off is distortion of shape, which grows severe toward the poles, where features are compressed vertically and stretched horizontally at the top and bottom edges.
