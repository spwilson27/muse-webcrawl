Yoshitomo Nara

Yoshitomo Nara (奈良 美智, Nara Yoshitomo, born 5 December 1959) is a Japanese contemporary artist. He is best known for his paintings and sculptures of children and animals rendered in a deceptively simple, cartoon-like style—wide-eyed girls and dogs whose expressions range from dreamy and vulnerable to defiant and menacing. Nara rose to prominence during the Japanese Pop art movement of the 1990s and is associated with the Superflat movement of Takashi Murakami. His works are among the most valuable of any living Japanese artist at auction.

Life and education

Nara was born in Hirosaki, Aomori Prefecture, and raised largely alone while both parents worked, an experience of childhood solitude that pervades his art. He studied at the Aichi University of the Arts, earning his BFA in 1985 and MFA in 1987, then moved to Germany to study at the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf under A. R. Penck. He lived in Cologne from 1988 to 2000 before returning to Japan.

Work

Nara's signature figures—children with oversized heads, piercing eyes and subtle emotional ambiguity—draw on manga, anime, ukiyo-e, children's book illustration and the punk rock music he loves. Works such as Sleepless Night (Sitting) (1997) and Knife Behind Back (2000), which sold for about HK$195 million at Sotheby's Hong Kong in 2019, exemplify his blend of cuteness and menace. He also produces sculpture, including large dog figures, ceramic works and the collaborative "A to Z Cubes" installations, and extensive drawings on paper, envelopes and found materials. Following the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami, near his home region, his work took on themes of prayer, loss and anti-nuclear protest.

Exhibitions and legacy

Major solo exhibitions include a retrospective at the Los Angeles County Museum of Art (2020–2021), which travelled to the Yuz Museum Shanghai, Museo Guggenheim Bilbao and Kunsthal Rotterdam. The Nara-inspired café culture and merchandise market in Asia has made his imagery ubiquitous. He lives and works in Tochigi Prefecture, Japan.
