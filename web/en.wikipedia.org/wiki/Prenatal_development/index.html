Prenatal development

Prenatal development is the process in which an embryo and later fetus (or foetus) develops during gestation from fertilisation until birth. It is conventionally divided into three stages: the germinal stage, the embryonic stage, and the fetal stage. Human prenatal development lasts about 38 weeks from fertilisation (about 40 weeks from the last menstrual period).

Germinal stage

The germinal stage (weeks 1–2) begins at fertilisation, when the sperm and egg unite to form a zygote. The zygote undergoes rapid cell division (cleavage) as it travels down the fallopian tube, becoming a blastocyst that implants in the uterine wall around the end of the second week.

Embryonic stage

During the embryonic stage (weeks 3–8), the three germ layers — ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm — form and give rise to all organs in a process called organogenesis. The neural tube closes, the heart begins to beat, and limb buds appear. This is the period of greatest vulnerability to teratogens.

Fetal stage

From week 9 until birth the developing human is called a fetus. Growth and maturation dominate: the brain develops rapidly, the lungs mature, fat is deposited, and by about 23–24 weeks the fetus may be viable outside the womb with intensive care. Birth normally occurs at around 40 weeks.
