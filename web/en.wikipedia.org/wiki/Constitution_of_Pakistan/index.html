Constitution of Pakistan

[Hero image placeholder: Parliament House, Islamabad]

The Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan (Urdu: Ā'īn-e-Pākistān) is the supreme law of Pakistan. It establishes the framework of the Pakistani state as a federal parliamentary republic with Islam as the state religion. The current constitution, the country's third, was adopted by the National Assembly on 10 April 1973 and promulgated on 14 August 1973, during the government of Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto.

Previous constitutions

Pakistan's earlier constitutions were those of 1956 and 1962, both abrogated following martial law. The 1973 constitution restored a parliamentary system after the secession of East Pakistan (Bangladesh) in 1971 and the 1971 war with India.

Features

The constitution has 280 articles and 12 schedules. It provides for a bicameral parliament — the Senate and the National Assembly — a President as ceremonial head of state, and a Prime Minister as head of government. It guarantees fundamental rights, including equality of citizens, freedom of speech and religion, and safeguards against discrimination. The Objectives Resolution of 1949 forms Article 2A and a substantive part of the constitution. The Council of Islamic Ideology advises the legislature on conformity of laws with the Qur'an and Sunnah.

Amendments

The constitution has been amended more than twenty times. The Eighth Amendment (1985) granted the presidency sweeping powers, including the power to dissolve the National Assembly; these were partly reversed by the Thirteenth Amendment (1997), restored in modified form under Pervez Musharraf, and largely repealed by the landmark Eighteenth Amendment (2010), which also renamed the North-West Frontier Province as Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and strengthened provincial autonomy. The constitution was twice held in abeyance under martial law (1977 and 1999) but never fully abrogated after 1973.
