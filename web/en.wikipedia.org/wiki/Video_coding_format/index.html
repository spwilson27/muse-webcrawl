Video coding format

A video coding format (or sometimes video compression format) is a content representation format of digital video content, such as in a data file or bitstream. It typically uses a standardized video compression algorithm, most commonly based on discrete cosine transform (DCT) coding and motion compensation. A specific software, firmware, or hardware implementation capable of compression or decompression to a specific video coding format is called a video codec.

Some video coding formats are documented by a detailed technical specification document known as a video coding specification. Some such formats are also standardized by standardization organizations as technical standards. A standard based on a specific video coding format is sometimes referred to simply by the same name, which can cause confusion because the standard and format may evolve independently of an implementation.

History

The first digital video coding standard was H.120, created by the CCITT (now ITU-T) in 1984. H.120 used differential PCM coding and had poor performance, but it was the beginning of practical digital video compression technology. In 1988, the ITU-T published H.261, the first practically successful digital video coding standard, which introduced concepts such as DCT-based coding and motion compensation that have been used in nearly all subsequent standards.

In 1991, the Moving Picture Experts Group (MPEG) published MPEG-1, which popularized digital video through the Video CD format and later MP3 audio. MPEG-2 followed in 1994 and enabled digital television broadcasting and DVDs. MPEG-4 Part 2, standardized in 1999, provided greater compression efficiency for low-bitrate applications.

Major formats

Modern video coding formats include H.264/AVC (2003), which became the most widely used format for internet video streaming and Blu-ray discs; H.265/HEVC (2013), which roughly doubles compression efficiency; VP9, developed by Google as a royalty-free alternative; and AV1 (2018), developed by the Alliance for Open Media as a successor to VP9. In 2020, H.266/VVC was finalized, promising further efficiency gains.

Patents and licensing

Many video coding formats are covered by patents owned by various companies, and implementations often require royalties paid to patent pools such as those administered by MPEG LA and Access Advance. This has motivated the development of royalty-free formats such as VP9 and AV1.
