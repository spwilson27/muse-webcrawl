The Women's Super League (WSL) is the highest league of women's football in England. Established in 2010 by the Football Association and first played in 2011, it replaced the FA Women's Premier League National Division as the top tier. The league is contested by twelve professional clubs and operates on a promotion and relegation system with the Women's Championship.

A WSL fixture between two professional clubs

History

The WSL began in 2011 as an eight-team, summer-season league without relegation. It expanded over the following years, moving to a winter calendar in 2017 and becoming fully professional from the 2018–19 season, when all clubs were required to offer full-time contracts. A landmark domestic broadcast deal with Sky Sports and the BBC began in 2021, greatly increasing visibility, and attendances grew rapidly — boosted by England's victory at UEFA Women's Euro 2022. In 2024, governance of the league passed from the FA to Women's Professional Leagues Limited, an independent, club-owned company.

Competition format

Each club plays the others home and away, for 22 matches. The champions and leading clubs qualify for the UEFA Women's Champions League, and the bottom club is relegated to the Women's Championship. Arsenal won the inaugural titles; Chelsea became the dominant side of the 2020s under manager Emma Hayes, while Manchester City and Arsenal remained their principal challengers.
