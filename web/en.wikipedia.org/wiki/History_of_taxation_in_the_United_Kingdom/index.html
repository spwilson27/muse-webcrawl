History of taxation in the United Kingdom

JurisdictionUnited Kingdom
MilestonesDanegeld; income tax 1799/1842; PAYE 1944; VAT 1973

The history of taxation in the United Kingdom spans Anglo-Saxon land taxes, medieval feudal dues, Pitt's first income tax, and the modern system of income tax, VAT and national insurance.

Medieval and early modern

Anglo-Saxon kings levied the Danegeld and geld on land; Norman and Plantagenet finance drew on feudal incidents, customs and tallages recorded by the Exchequer on tally sticks. The poll taxes of 1377–1381 provoked the Peasants' Revolt. In the 17th century, ship money and Civil War assessments contributed to constitutional conflict; after 1688 Parliament controlled taxation, funding the national debt through customs, excise and the land tax.

Income tax and reform

William Pitt the Younger introduced income tax in 1799 to fund the Napoleonic Wars; repealed in 1816, it was restored by Peel in 1842 and became permanent. Gladstonian finance lowered tariffs toward free trade. The "People's Budget" of 1909 added graduated rates and land taxes.

Modern system

Two world wars made income tax a mass tax, collected by PAYE from 1944. Purchase tax gave way to VAT in 1973 on EEC entry; corporation tax, capital gains tax (1965) and inheritance tax complete the modern structure administered by HM Revenue and Customs.
