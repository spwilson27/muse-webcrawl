Air charter

Air charter is the business of renting an entire aircraft — as opposed to purchasing individual seats on a scheduled flight — for the carriage of passengers or cargo. Charter flights operate on the customer's schedule rather than a published timetable, and range from small air-taxi hops to large airliners hired by tour operators, sports teams, governments and corporations.

Types of charter

Passenger charters include business aviation (executive jets hired by companies and individuals), inclusive-tour charters contracted by holiday companies, and ad hoc charters for groups such as orchestras and sports teams. Cargo charters carry outsized, urgent or specialised freight, from humanitarian relief supplies to racing cars. The sector also covers air ambulance flights and government or military charter contracts.

Regulation and market

Charter operators must hold an air operator's certificate and commercial licences for public transport operations. Much capacity is sold through air charter brokers, who match customers with operators. Demand peaks around holidays, major sporting events and emergencies; "empty leg" repositioning flights are often sold at a discount. The market includes very light jets and turboprops for short routes through to wide-body airliners for long-haul group travel.

History

The charter industry grew after the Second World War using surplus military transports, and inclusive-tour charters underpinned the growth of the Mediterranean package holiday in the 1960s and 1970s. Business aviation expanded with the arrival of dedicated jets such as the Learjet in the 1960s.
