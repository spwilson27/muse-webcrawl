Xerox

Xerox Holdings Corporation is an American corporation that sells print and digital document products and services. Founded in 1906 in Rochester, New York, as the Haloid Photographic Company, a manufacturer of photographic paper, the company became famous for the Xerox 914 plain-paper photocopier (1959), whose runaway success made "Xerox" a genericised term for photocopying in many countries — a usage the company has long campaigned against to protect its trademark.

History

Haloid licensed Chester Carlson's xerography patents in 1947, became Haloid Xerox in 1958 and Xerox Corporation in 1961. Copier revenues drove rapid growth in the 1960s. The company's Palo Alto Research Center (PARC, founded 1970) pioneered key elements of modern computing — the graphical user interface, the laser printer, Ethernet and the Alto personal computer — many of which were commercialised by other firms, a story often told as Xerox "fumbling the future".

Recent history

Xerox restructured repeatedly in the digital era: in 2017 it spun off its business-process outsourcing arm as Conduent; in 2018 Fujifilm agreed to take control, a deal abandoned after activist investor opposition, and in 2019 Xerox sold its stake in Fuji Xerox. A hostile bid for HP Inc. in 2019–20 was withdrawn during the COVID-19 pandemic. In 2023–24 Xerox announced a major "reinvention" reorganisation, and agreed to acquire printer maker Lexmark.
