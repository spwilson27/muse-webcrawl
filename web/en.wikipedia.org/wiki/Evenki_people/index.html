Evenki people

Total population: approx. 70,000 (Russia and China)

Regions: Siberia; Inner Mongolia and Heilongjiang, China

Language: Evenki (Tungusic)

Religion: Shamanism, Tibetan Buddhism, Orthodox Christianity

The Evenki (also spelled Ewenki, formerly known in Russian sources as Tungus) are a Tungusic people of Northern Asia, among the most widely distributed Indigenous groups in the world. About 38,000 live in Russia, spread across an enormous area of Siberia from the Ob River in the west to Sakhalin and the Sea of Okhotsk in the east, and from the Arctic Ocean south to the Amur; some 30,000 more live in China, mainly in the Evenki Autonomous Banner of Inner Mongolia and in Heilongjiang, where they are recognised as one of China's 56 ethnic groups, with a further small population in Mongolia.

Culture and livelihood

Traditionally the Evenki were hunters and reindeer herders of the taiga, living in conical tents (chum) and moving seasonally with their herds. Reindeer provided transport, milk, meat and hides. Shamans mediated with the spirit world, and clan organisation was egalitarian. The Evenki language, the largest of the northern Tungusic languages, gives the world the word "shaman" itself (via Russian from Evenki šamán) and is the source of the name of the Tunguska region, site of the 1908 Tunguska event.

History

Russian expansion into Siberia from the seventeenth century brought the fur tribute (yasak), disease and administration, while in China Evenki groups were incorporated into the Qing banner system, notably the Solon. Soviet collectivisation, boarding schools and sedentarisation campaigns in the twentieth century transformed Evenki life, and the Evenki language is now considered endangered in Russia, though revival efforts continue in both countries.
