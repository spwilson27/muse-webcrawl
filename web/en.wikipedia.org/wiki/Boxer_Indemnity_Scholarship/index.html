Boxer Indemnity Scholarship

[Image: Tsinghua University, founded with Boxer Indemnity funds]

The Boxer Indemnity Scholarship (Chinese: 庚子賠款獎學金) was a scholarship program for Chinese students to study in the United States, funded by the remitted portion of China's Boxer Indemnity payments to the United States. Established after President Theodore Roosevelt's administration agreed in 1908 to remit a substantial part of the American share of the indemnity imposed by the Boxer Protocol of 1901, the program became one of the most significant early channels of educational exchange between China and the United States.

Background

Following the suppression of the Boxer Rebellion, the Boxer Protocol required Qing China to pay 450 million taels of silver in indemnities to the Eight-Nation Alliance. The American share was about $24.4 million. Influenced by missionaries and educators, and by a 1907 memorandum from University of Illinois president Edmund James arguing that educating China's future leaders in America served American interests, the United States remitted the portion of the indemnity exceeding actual claims — roughly $12 million initially, with a further remission in 1924.

The program

Competitive examinations were held in China beginning in 1909, and the first group of 47 students sailed for the United States that year. Altogether some 1,300 Chinese students were educated under the program through 1929. A preparatory school established in Beijing in 1911 with indemnity funds evolved into Tsinghua University, one of China's leading universities. Alumni included prominent scientists, engineers, and educators who shaped modern Chinese higher education and research.

Other nations followed with similar remission schemes: Britain, Japan, France, Belgium, Italy, and the Netherlands each applied portions of their indemnity shares to educational and cultural programs in China, though on varying terms.
