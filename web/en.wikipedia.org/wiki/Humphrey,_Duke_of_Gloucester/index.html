Humphrey, Duke of Gloucester

Humphrey of Lancaster, Duke of Gloucester
Born: 3 October 1390
Died: 23 February 1447, Bury St Edmunds
House: Lancaster
Father: Henry IV of England
Known as: "Good Duke Humphrey"

Humphrey of Lancaster, Duke of Gloucester (3 October 1390 – 23 February 1447), was an English prince, soldier and literary patron. He was the fourth son of King Henry IV and the youngest brother of King Henry V. During the minority of his nephew Henry VI he served as Lord Protector and Defender of the Realm, and was a central, controversial figure in the politics of the Hundred Years' War era.

Career

Humphrey campaigned in France with Henry V and was wounded at Agincourt in 1415. After Henry's death in 1422 he claimed the regency but was constrained by the royal council and his rivalry with his uncle Cardinal Henry Beaufort and his brother John, Duke of Bedford. His marriage to Jacqueline of Hainaut and failed expedition to the Low Countries (1424–25) damaged his standing; his later marriage to Eleanor Cobham ended in scandal when she was convicted of treasonable necromancy in 1441.

Patronage and death

Known popularly as "Good Duke Humphrey", he was a major patron of learning: his donations of hundreds of manuscripts to the University of Oxford form the core of Duke Humfrey's Library in the Bodleian. Arrested for treason at the Parliament of Bury St Edmunds in February 1447, he died a few days later — officially of a stroke, though contemporaries suspected foul play. Shakespeare later treated him sympathetically in the Henry VI plays.
