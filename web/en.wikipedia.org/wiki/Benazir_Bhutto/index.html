Benazir Bhutto (21 June 1953 – 27 December 2007) was a Pakistani politician who served twice as Prime Minister of Pakistan, from 1988 to 1990 and again from 1993 to 1996. She was the first woman to head a democratic government in a majority-Muslim country, and led the Pakistan People's Party (PPP), founded by her father Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, for most of her life. She was assassinated in December 2007 while campaigning for a third term.

Early life

Bhutto was born in Karachi to Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, a Sindhi landowner who became Pakistan's president and prime minister, and Nusrat Ispahani, of Iranian-Kurdish origin. She studied at Radcliffe College, Harvard University, and then read Philosophy, Politics and Economics at Lady Margaret Hall, Oxford, where she became president of the Oxford Union. After her father was overthrown in General Zia-ul-Haq's 1977 coup and executed in 1979, Bhutto endured years of house arrest and detention before going into exile in London in 1984, where she became joint leader of the PPP.

Prime Minister

Returning to Pakistan in 1986 to large crowds, Bhutto led the PPP to victory in the 1988 elections held after Zia's death, becoming prime minister at 35. Her first government was hampered by conflict with the military establishment and President Ghulam Ishaq Khan, who dismissed it in 1990 on corruption charges. After a period in opposition, she won the 1993 election and formed a second government, which pursued economic liberalisation and privatisation but was again dismissed, in 1996 by President Farooq Leghari, amid corruption allegations involving her husband Asif Ali Zardari.

Exile and return

Facing prosecution, Bhutto lived in self-imposed exile in Dubai and London from 1999, while remaining chairperson of the PPP. In 2007, under a US- and UK-brokered understanding with President Pervez Musharraf, she returned to Pakistan in October 2007; her homecoming procession in Karachi was attacked by suicide bombers, killing around 180 people.

Assassination

On 27 December 2007, after addressing an election rally at Liaquat National Bagh in Rawalpindi, Bhutto was shot by an assassin who then detonated a suicide vest as she stood through her vehicle's sunroof to wave to supporters. She died in hospital the same day. The Pakistani government blamed Taliban commander Baitullah Mehsud; a 2010 United Nations inquiry concluded the assassination could have been prevented and that the subsequent investigation was deliberately obstructed. Her death triggered nationwide rioting; the PPP won the February 2008 elections on a sympathy vote, and Zardari became president later that year. Bhutto is buried in the family mausoleum at Garhi Khuda Bakhsh, Sindh, beside her father.

Legacy

Bhutto remains a polarising figure: admired internationally as a trailblazer for women in the Muslim world and a symbol of resistance to military rule, criticised at home for corruption scandals and dynastic politics. Her son Bilawal Bhutto Zardari succeeded her as PPP chairperson. Her books include the autobiography Daughter of Destiny (1989) and Reconciliation (2008), published posthumously.
