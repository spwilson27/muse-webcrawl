Danish Estonia

Danish Estonia (Danish: Dansk Estland) was the territory in present-day northern Estonia ruled by Denmark during the 13th and 14th centuries, and again in the 16th and 17th centuries on the island of Saaremaa (Øsel).

History

Danish involvement in Estonia began with the crusades against the pagan Estonians. According to legend, the Danish national flag, the Dannebrog, fell from the sky during the Battle of Lyndanisse (present-day Tallinn) in 1219, securing a Danish victory. King Valdemar II of Denmark established Danish rule over northern Estonia, founding the city of Reval (Tallinn) and building the castle of Toompea.

The territory, centred on the Duchy of Estonia, was administered by vassals of the Danish crown, many of them German. Danish rule in mainland Estonia ended in 1346, when King Valdemar IV sold the territory to the Teutonic Order's Livonian branch for 19,000 marks, following the St. George's Night Uprising of 1343.

Later Danish rule

Denmark later regained a foothold in the region when it acquired the island of Øsel (Saaremaa) in 1559, which it held until 1645, when the island was ceded to Sweden under the Treaty of Brömsebro.
