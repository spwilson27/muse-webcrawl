Schwarz lemma

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the Schwarz lemma, named after Hermann Amandus Schwarz, is a result in complex analysis about holomorphic functions on the open unit disc. It gives a sharp bound on how much such a function can move points, provided it fixes the origin, and it is a cornerstone application of the maximum modulus principle.

Statement

Let D = {z ∈ C : |z| < 1} be the open unit disc and let f : D → D be a holomorphic function with f(0) = 0. Then

|f(z)| ≤ |z|   for all z ∈ D,    and    |f′(0)| ≤ 1.

Moreover, if equality holds in the first inequality for some z ≠ 0, or if |f′(0)| = 1, then f is a rotation: f(z) = eiθz for some real θ.

Proof

The proof applies the maximum modulus principle to the auxiliary function g(z) = f(z)/z for z ≠ 0, with g(0) = f′(0). Since f has a zero at the origin, g has a removable singularity there and is holomorphic on the disc. On the circle |z| = r < 1 one has |g(z)| ≤ 1/r, so by the maximum principle |g| ≤ 1/r inside; letting r → 1 gives |g| ≤ 1 throughout, which is exactly the conclusion. If equality holds anywhere inside, the maximum principle forces g to be a constant of modulus 1, i.e. a rotation.

Related results

  • Schwarz–Pick lemma: Carathéodory and Pick generalised the result to holomorphic maps between unit discs without the fixed-point assumption, expressed in terms of the Poincaré (hyperbolic) metric: such maps are contractions in hyperbolic distance.
  • Automorphisms of the disc: the lemma implies that every automorphism of the unit disc is a Möbius transformation of the form eiθ(z − a)/(1 − āz).
  • Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma: a far-reaching generalisation by Lars Ahlfors relating the lemma to negative curvature and pseudo-Riemannian metrics.
  • The lemma is a first step toward the Riemann mapping theorem and the study of hyperbolic geometry in complex analysis.

History

Schwarz published the result in 1869 in connection with his work on conformal mapping. It has since become one of the most frequently taught theorems in a first course on complex analysis, because its short proof displays the power of the maximum modulus principle.
