Alignment (typesetting)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In typesetting and page layout, alignment refers to the way text is arranged relative to the margins of a page or column. The four principal alignments are flush left, flush right, centred and justified; the choice affects the evenness of the margins and the spacing between words and letters.

Flush left

Text is aligned along the left margin, and letter- and word-spacing are left unmodified, giving a ragged right edge. This is the default alignment for languages written left to right.

Left alignment (sometimes "rag left" or "ragged right") leaves the right edge uneven. Because word spacing is constant, the text avoids the uneven gaps that justification can create and is generally considered the most legible setting for body text in Western languages.

Flush right

Right alignment ("ragged left") aligns text along the right margin. It is used for small blocks such as captions, side notes and some poetry, and is the natural alignment for right-to-left scripts such as Arabic and Hebrew.

Centred

Centred text is aligned along an imaginary vertical axis; both edges are ragged. It is used for titles, headings, invitations and short displays, but is hard to read in long passages.

Justified

Justified text is spaced so that the left and right edges of the block are both aligned with the margins. The last line of a justified paragraph is typically left-aligned. Achieving an even colour requires variable word spacing, and in narrow columns it can produce conspicuous gaps known as "rivers".

Justification is traditional in printed books and newspapers. Typesetters mitigate rivers and loose lines by hyphenation, letter-spacing adjustments and rewriting. In CSS, justification is controlled by the text-align: justify property, optionally combined with automatic hyphenation.

History

Medieval manuscripts were usually justified, as alignment of both margins suited the columnar layout of the codex. Movable type preserved the practice. The typewriter era made ragged-right typing normal, and modern word processors and web browsers default to left alignment for screen reading.
