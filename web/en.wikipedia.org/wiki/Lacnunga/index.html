Lacnunga


An Anglo-Saxon medical manuscript

The Lacnunga ("Remedies") is a collection of medical texts and prayers in Old English and Latin, compiled in England around the late tenth or early eleventh century. It survives in a single manuscript, British Library Harley MS 585, and is one of the principal sources for Anglo-Saxon medicine, alongside Bald's Leechbook and the Herbarium Apuleii.

Contents

The collection contains nearly two hundred remedies for ailments ranging from headaches and eye infections to elf-shot and flying venom. Many entries combine herbal preparations with charms and ritual instructions. The Lacnunga preserves some of the most famous Old English metrical charms, including the Nine Herbs Charm, which invokes the god Woden, and Wið færstice ("Against a sudden stitch"), a dramatic charm describing an attack by supernatural female riders and elves.

Significance

Because its remedies draw promiscuously on Mediterranean learned medicine, folk practice and pre-Christian Germanic tradition, the Lacnunga has been central to scholarly debate about the character of Anglo-Saxon healing — whether it represented a rational empirical tradition or a largely magical one. The text was first edited and translated by Oswald Cockayne in the nineteenth century.
