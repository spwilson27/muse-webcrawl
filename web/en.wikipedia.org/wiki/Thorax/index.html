The thorax (from Greek θώραξ, "breastplate, cuirass") or chest is a part of the anatomy of mammals and other tetrapod animals located between the neck and the abdomen. In insects, crustaceans and trilobites the word denotes a quite different body segment — the middle tagma bearing the walking legs and wings. The human thorax includes the thoracic cavity and the thoracic wall. It contains organs including the heart and lungs and many blood vessels; its bony framework, the ribcage, protects these organs and provides attachment for the muscles of breathing, the upper limbs and the trunk.

Structure

The thoracic cavity is bounded above by the thoracic inlet (superior thoracic aperture), through which pass the trachea, oesophagus and great vessels of the neck, and below by the thoracic diaphragm, which separates it from the abdominal cavity. The thoracic wall consists of twelve pairs of ribs joined to the twelve thoracic vertebrae behind and, for the upper ten pairs, to the sternum (breastbone) in front by costal cartilages. The seven "true ribs" attach directly to the sternum; ribs 8–10 attach indirectly, and ribs 11–12 are "floating".

Within the cavity, the two pleural sacs contain the lungs; between them lies the mediastinum, containing the heart in its pericardium, the oesophagus, trachea and main bronchi, the thymus, and the great vessels — aorta, pulmonary artery and veins, and the venae cavae — together with nerves such as the vagus and phrenic nerves and the thoracic duct.

Function

The thorax is central to breathing: movements of the ribs (the "pump-handle" and "bucket-handle" actions) and the diaphragm change the volume of the thoracic cavity, driving ventilation of the lungs. The compliant chest wall also transmits the heartbeat and protects the vital organs from trauma and infection.

Clinical significance

Conditions of the thorax include rib fractures and flail chest, pneumothorax and pleural effusion (treated by procedures such as thoracentesis), chest pain of cardiac, pleural or musculoskeletal origin, and deformities such as pectus excavatum and pectus carinatum. Thoracic surgery (cardiothoracic surgery) encompasses operations on the heart, lungs and oesophagus; opening the chest is called a thoracotomy.

In other animals

In most terrestrial vertebrates the thorax is the rib-bearing region of the trunk. In arthropods the thorax is the middle of the three body sections: in insects the three thoracic segments bear six legs and (in winged insects) two pairs of wings; in crustaceans the thorax bears the walking legs; in trilobites it is the articulated middle section between head and pygidium.
