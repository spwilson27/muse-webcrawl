Dulwich Picture Gallery

Dulwich Picture Gallery
Opened: 1817
Location: Dulwich, London
Architect: Sir John Soane
Collection: Old Master paintings

Dulwich Picture Gallery is an art museum in Dulwich, south London. It opened to the public in 1817 and was England's first purpose-built public art gallery, designed by Sir John Soane in a pioneering style with top-lit galleries that influenced museum architecture worldwide.

Collection

The core collection was assembled by the art dealer Noël Desenfans and his partner Sir Francis Bourgeois for the King of Poland; when the Polish project collapsed, Bourgeois bequeathed the pictures to Dulwich College in 1811, together with funds for a gallery and mausoleum. The collection is especially rich in 17th- and 18th-century European painting, with major works by Rembrandt, Rubens, Van Dyck, Poussin, Claude Lorrain, Murillo, Canaletto, Gainsborough and Watteau.

Building

Soane's spare brick building, with its integrated mausoleum for the founders, is a landmark of Regency architecture. It was extended in the 20th century, bomb-damaged in the Second World War, and modernised with a Millennium extension. The gallery made headlines in the 1960s–80s for repeated thefts of Rembrandt's small Portrait of Jacob de Gheyn III, stolen and recovered four times.
