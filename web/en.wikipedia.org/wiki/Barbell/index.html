A barbell is a long metal bar to which weight plates are attached at each end, used in strength training and in the sports of weightlifting and powerlifting. It consists of a central shaft with knurled grip sections and rotating sleeves at the ends that hold the plates, which are secured by collars. Barbells allow heavier loading and more precise progression than dumbbells and are the basis of the fundamental compound lifts.

Types

Olympic barbells follow IWF specifications: the men's bar weighs 20 kg and is 2.2 m long with a 28 mm shaft; the women's bar weighs 15 kg, is 2.01 m long with a 25 mm shaft; sleeves are 50 mm in diameter and spin on bearings or bushings so the plates do not twist the bar during the snatch and clean and jerk. Powerlifting bars are stiffer, with aggressive knurling and a centre knurl; competition deadlift and squat specialty bars also exist. Standard (non-Olympic) bars with 25–28 mm plate holes are common in home gyms. Variants include the EZ curl bar (angled for curls), the trap/hex bar (for deadlifts), the safety squat bar, and fixed-weight barbells in gyms.

Exercises

Barbells are used for the squat, bench press, deadlift, overhead press and barbell row (the powerlifting and general strength repertoire), and for the Olympic lifts, the snatch and the clean and jerk. Weight is adjusted by loading bumper plates (for Olympic lifting, so the bar can be dropped) or iron plates in standard increments marked in kilograms or pounds, counted including the bar itself (e.g. "two plates" = bar + 2 × 20 kg per side = 100 kg).

History

Fixed globe barbells and shot-loading bars appeared in 19th-century strongman culture; the modern plate-loading barbell with rotating sleeves was developed in the early 20th century, and standardised equipment underpinned the growth of competitive weightlifting and, later, powerlifting and modern strength training.
