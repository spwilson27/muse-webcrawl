Bulk carrier

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A large bulk carrier being loaded with iron ore by conveyor at a terminal]
Loading a Capesize bulker.

A bulk carrier is a merchant ship specially designed to transport unpackaged bulk cargo — iron ore, coal, grain, bauxite, cement and similar commodities — in its cargo holds. Bulk carriers range from small \'handysize\' ships to the giant Valemax ore carriers of about 400,000 deadweight tons, the largest ships by tonnage class in service. They carry roughly half of the world\'s seaborne trade by tonnage and are grouped by size: Handysize, Handymax/Supramax, Panamax, Capesize and Valemax.

Design

Bulkers have a single deck over large box-like holds with wide hatch openings, raised hatch coamings and hopper tanks for self-trimming; geared ships carry their own cranes for ports without equipment. Double-side-skin construction and, on ore carriers, concentrated loads demand careful stress management — a string of 1990s bulker losses (notably Derbyshire, 1980, found later) drove tougher survey and construction rules.

Trades

Iron ore (Australia/Brazil to China), coal, grain (US Gulf, Black Sea, South America) and minor bulks define the market; Capesize ships, too big for Panama or (laden) Suez shortcuts, round the Capes of Good Hope and Horn. Charter rates are famously volatile, tracked by the Baltic Dry Index.

History

The type evolved from colliers and tramp steamers; specialised ore carriers and the self-unloading Great Lakes \'lakers\' (with conveyor belts) pioneered modern bulk handling. The largest, China\'s/Brazil\'s Valemax fleet, serves dedicated deep-water terminals.
