NIMBY
Acronym for"Not in my back yard"
UsagePeJORATIVE term for local opposition to development
Related termsYIMBY, NIMTOO, BANANA

NIMBY (an acronym for the phrase "not in my back yard") is a pejorative characterisation of opposition by residents to proposed developments in their local area, even when those developments are widely considered necessary or beneficial, such as housing, infrastructure, energy projects, waste facilities or shelters. The term implies that opponents would accept the development elsewhere but resist its costs and nuisances near their own homes.

Origin and use

The phrase came into use in the United States in the late 1970s and early 1980s, first in debates over hazardous-waste siting, and spread widely through journalism and planning literature. It has since been applied to wind farms, airport expansion, mobile phone masts, railway lines such as HS2, homeless shelters, prisons and dense housing.

Debate

Critics of the label note that local objections may rest on legitimate environmental, heritage or safety concerns, and that dismissing residents as "NIMBYs" can delegitimize democratic participation. Conversely, economists and housing advocates argue that organised local veto power is a major cause of housing shortages in high-demand cities, giving rise to the pro-development "YIMBY" ("yes in my back yard") movement. Variants include NIMTOO ("not in my term of office") and BANANA ("build absolutely nothing anywhere near anything").
