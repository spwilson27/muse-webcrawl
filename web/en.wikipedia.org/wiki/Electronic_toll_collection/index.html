Electronic toll collection


An overhead gantry used for electronic toll collection

Electronic toll collection (ETC) is a wireless system to automatically collect the usage fee or toll charged to vehicles using toll roads, HOV lanes, toll bridges, and toll tunnels. It is a faster alternative which is replacing toll booths, where vehicles must stop and the driver manually pays the toll with cash or a card; with electronic toll collection, vehicles pass at normal driving speeds under a gantry or through a toll point without stopping.

Technology

Most systems use radio-frequency identification (RFID): a transponder or tag in the vehicle communicates with roadside antennas using dedicated short-range communications, and the toll is charged to the user's account. Other systems use automatic number-plate recognition (ANPR) cameras to bill the registered owner, and some, such as satellite-based schemes for heavy trucks in Germany, use GNSS positioning.

Well-known systems include E-ZPass in the eastern United States, FasTrak in California, the ETC system in Japan, and city congestion-charging systems such as those in London, Stockholm, and Singapore — Singapore's Electronic Road Pricing, launched in 1998, was the world's first urban electronic congestion-pricing system.

Benefits and concerns

ETC reduces congestion at toll plazas, lowers operating costs, cuts emissions from idling vehicles, and enables distance-based or time-varying tolls. Criticisms include privacy concerns over the tracking of vehicle movements, billing errors, and the burden of fees on unbanked drivers in cashless systems.

Interoperability

Because regions adopted incompatible systems, efforts such as the European Electronic Toll Service and U.S. national interoperability initiatives aim to let a single account or transponder work across many toll networks.
