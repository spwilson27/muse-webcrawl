Tampere

Tampere (Swedish: Tammerfors) is a city in the Pirkanmaa region of Finland, located between lakes Näsijärvi and Pyhäjärvi, whose waters are connected by the Tammerkoski rapids that run through the city centre. It is the third most populous city in Finland, with a population of roughly 260,000, and the largest inland city in the Nordic countries.

History

Tampere was founded in 1779 by King Gustav III of Sweden. In the 19th century it grew into Finland's foremost industrial centre — sometimes called the "Manchester of Finland" — powered by the rapids and known for textile mills such as Finlayson, engineering works and shoe factories. During the Finnish Civil War of 1918, the Battle of Tampere was one of the largest and bloodiest engagements.

Economy and culture

As heavy industry declined, Tampere reinvented itself around technology, universities and services, hosting Tampere University and a major Nokia research presence. Landmarks include the Näsinneula observation tower, the Moomin Museum, Tampere Cathedral, and the old red-brick factory districts along the rapids, now converted to museums, restaurants and offices. The city is also fondly associated with the black sausage mustamakkara.
