The Cordillera Central is the highest of the three branches of the Colombian Andes. It extends roughly 1,023 km from the Colombian Massif in the south, in the department of Cauca, northward to the Serranía de San Lucas in Bolívar. It is separated from the Cordillera Oriental by the valley of the Magdalena River and from the Cordillera Occidental by the valley of the Cauca River.

Geography

The range contains Colombia's most active volcanic belt. Its highest summits include Nevado del Huila (5,364 m), the highest volcano in Colombia, Nevado del Ruiz (5,321 m), Nevado del Tolima (5,216 m) and Nevado de Santa Isabel. On the western slopes lies the Colombian coffee-growing region (the Eje Cafetero), with the cities of Manizales, Pereira and Armenia, while Medellín sits in the Aburrá Valley at the range's northern end.

Volcanism

On 13 November 1985, an eruption of Nevado del Ruiz melted its ice cap and generated lahars that destroyed the town of Armero, killing about 23,000 people — one of the deadliest volcanic disasters of the 20th century. The range's volcanoes are monitored by the Colombian Geological Service from observatories in Manizales, Pasto and Popayán.

Ecology

The cordillera's slopes host Andean cloud forests and, above the treeline, páramo grasslands with their distinctive frailejón plants. Los Nevados National Natural Park protects the high volcanic region. The range is an important watershed: the Magdalena and Cauca rivers and many tributaries originate in its massifs.
