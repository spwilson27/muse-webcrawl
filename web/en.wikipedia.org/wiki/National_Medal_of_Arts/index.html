National Medal of Arts

The National Medal of Arts is an award and title created by the United States Congress in 1984 and administered by the National Endowment for the Arts (NEA). It is the highest honour given by the US government to artists and arts patrons.

Selection

Each year the NEA solicits nominations from the public and a committee recommends candidates to the President, who selects the recipients — normally up to twelve per year — and presents the medals at a White House ceremony.

Recipients

Honorees have included painters, musicians, writers, architects, dancers and arts philanthropists, among them Georgia O'Keeffe, Aaron Copland, Ralph Ellison, Frank Lloyd Wright (posthumously recognised in the medal's early years of eligibility debates), and Aretha Franklin.
