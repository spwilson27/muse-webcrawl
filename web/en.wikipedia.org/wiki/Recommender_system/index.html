Recommender system

A recommender system (or recommendation system) is a subclass of information filtering system that seeks to predict the "rating" or "preference" a user would give to an item. They are primarily used in commercial applications, where they suggest products, movies, music, news, books and other content to users of platforms such as Amazon, Netflix, YouTube and Spotify.

Approaches

Collaborative filtering

Collaborative filtering makes recommendations based on the past behaviour of users: if two users agreed in the past, they are likely to agree in the future. Methods include user-based and item-based nearest-neighbour approaches and latent-factor models such as matrix factorisation, which were popularised by the Netflix Prize competition (2006–2009), won by improvements on matrix factorisation with temporal dynamics.

Content-based filtering

Content-based filtering recommends items similar to those a user has liked, using features of the items themselves — genres, keywords, artists or text embeddings. It avoids dependence on other users but can overspecialise, producing a narrow "filter bubble".

Hybrid and modern methods

Most production systems combine several approaches. Deep learning models, sequence-aware recommenders and bandit algorithms that balance exploration with exploitation are widely used. Key challenges include the cold start problem for new users and items, data sparsity, scalability, and evaluating recommendation quality beyond accuracy, including diversity, novelty and fairness.

Evaluation

Systems are evaluated offline with metrics such as RMSE for rating prediction and precision, recall and normalised discounted cumulative gain (NDCG) for ranking, and online with A/B testing of engagement and revenue outcomes.
