Proliferating cell nuclear antigen

Proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) is a DNA sliding clamp protein that acts as a processivity factor for DNA polymerase δ in eukaryotic cells. Encoded in humans by the PCNA gene on chromosome 20, PCNA forms a ring-shaped homotrimer that encircles double-stranded DNA and tethers the polymerase to the template, greatly increasing the length of DNA that can be synthesised without dissociation. Beyond replication, PCNA serves as a central scaffold — sometimes described as a "toolbelt" — that recruits and coordinates dozens of proteins involved in DNA replication, DNA repair, chromatin assembly and cell-cycle control.

Structure and loading

Each PCNA monomer has two similar domains; three monomers associate head-to-tail to form a closed ring with a central hole about 35 Å across, lined with positively charged residues that allow it to slide along DNA without binding tightly. PCNA cannot load itself onto DNA: the clamp-loader complex replication factor C (RFC) uses ATP to open the ring and place it at primer–template junctions. The clamp is unloaded by a variant RFC complex containing Elg1/ATAD5. Many PCNA-binding proteins interact through a short conserved motif called the PIP box.

Function in replication

On the lagging strand, PCNA coordinates Okazaki fragment maturation: it activates the flap endonuclease FEN1 to remove RNA–DNA primers and stimulates DNA ligase I to seal the nicks. On both strands it links the polymerases to the replication fork, interacts with the replicative helicase, and helps recruit chromatin assembly factors such as CAF-1 so that newly replicated DNA is promptly packaged into nucleosomes — coupling replication to epigenetic inheritance.

DNA repair and modification

PCNA is a hub for DNA repair pathways including nucleotide excision repair, mismatch repair and base excision repair. Its function is regulated by post-translational modifications: monoubiquitination of lysine 164 by the RAD6–RAD18 complex during replication stress recruits translesion synthesis polymerases that can bypass DNA lesions (at the cost of fidelity), while polyubiquitination promotes error-free template switching. SUMOylation of the same residue discourages unwanted recombination. The cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p21 binds PCNA strongly and blocks replication while permitting repair — one mechanism by which the p53 tumour suppressor pathway halts the cell cycle after DNA damage.

Clinical relevance

Because PCNA accumulates in the nucleus during S phase, immunostaining for PCNA has been widely used as a marker of cell proliferation in pathology, complementary to Ki-67. PCNA was originally identified as an autoantigen in some patients with systemic lupus erythematosus. Its many interactions make it an attractive, if challenging, target for anticancer drug development.
