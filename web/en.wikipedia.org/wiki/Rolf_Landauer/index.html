Rolf Landauer

Rolf William Landauer (February 4, 1927 – April 27, 1999) was a German-American physicist who worked at IBM on the thermodynamics of information processing.

Born in Stuttgart, Landauer emigrated to the United States in 1938. After a Harvard PhD (1950) he joined IBM in 1952, where he spent his career. In 1961 he formulated Landauer's principle: erasing one bit of information necessarily dissipates at least kBT ln 2 of heat into the environment, linking information theory to thermodynamics and showing computation can in principle be thermodynamically reversible if no information is erased.

With Charles Bennett he developed the theory of reversible computing. The Landauer formula for electrical conductance in mesoscopic systems, expressing conduction as transmission, is another of his contributions. He received the Oliver E. Buckley Prize in 1995.
