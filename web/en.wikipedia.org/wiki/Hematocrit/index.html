The hematocrit (from Greek haima, blood, and kritēs, judge) is the volume percentage of red blood cells in blood, measured as part of a complete blood count. It is normally about 40–50% for adult men and 36–46% for adult women. In children it varies with age, and newborns have notably high values.

Measurement

Classically the hematocrit was measured by centrifuging a capillary tube of anticoagulated blood and reading the height of the packed red-cell column against the total column — hence the older term "packed cell volume" (PCV). Modern automated analysers compute it from red-cell count and mean corpuscular volume.

Clinical significance

A low hematocrit indicates anaemia, whether from blood loss, reduced production, or haemolysis; it is also lowered by overhydration and pregnancy. A high hematocrit occurs in polycythemia (including polycythemia vera), dehydration, chronic hypoxia (for example at altitude or in lung disease), and erythropoietin abuse. Because it determines much of blood viscosity, hematocrit affects oxygen delivery and cardiovascular risk; endurance athletes' values are monitored in anti-doping "blood passport" programmes.
