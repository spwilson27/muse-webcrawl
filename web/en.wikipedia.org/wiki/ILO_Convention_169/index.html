Indigenous and Tribal Peoples Convention, 1989

Signed27 June 1989
LocationGeneva
Effective5 September 1991
DepositaryInternational Labour Organization

The Indigenous and Tribal Peoples Convention, 1989 is an International Labour Organization convention, numbered C169. Adopted in 1989, it is the principal binding international instrument concerning the rights of indigenous and tribal peoples, revising the earlier assimilation-oriented Convention 107 of 1957.

Contents

The convention requires ratifying states to recognize indigenous peoples' rights to their traditional lands, to consultation and participation in decisions affecting them, to maintain their own customs and institutions, and to control their own economic, social and cultural development.

Ratification

The convention has been ratified by roughly two dozen countries, concentrated in Latin America, together with states such as Norway, Denmark, Spain and Nepal. It strongly influenced the later UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (2007).
