The Smiths were an English rock band formed in Manchester in 1982, composed of singer Morrissey, guitarist Johnny Marr, bassist Andy Rourke and drummer Mike Joyce. Critics have called them the most important British alternative rock band to emerge from the 1980s indie scene. The partnership between Morrissey's literate, witty and melancholic lyrics and Marr's jangling guitar work defined the band's sound.

History

The band signed to the independent label Rough Trade Records and released their debut album, The Smiths, in 1984. Albums Meat Is Murder (1985), The Queen Is Dead (1986) and Strangeways, Here We Come (1987) all reached the top of the UK charts or near it, and singles such as "This Charming Man", "How Soon Is Now?", "There Is a Light That Never Goes Out" and "Panic" became defining songs of the era.

Internal tensions, disputes over business matters and exhaustion led Marr to leave in 1987, and the band split soon after. Legal disputes over royalties followed in the 1990s. The Smiths have consistently refused lucrative reunion offers; Morrissey and Marr both pursued successful solo and collaborative careers.

Legacy

The Smiths' influence on British guitar music — from Oasis and Radiohead to countless indie bands — is widely acknowledged. The Queen Is Dead is frequently ranked among the greatest albums of all time.
