Kars

Kars (Armenian: Կարս; Kurdish: Qers) is a city in north-eastern Turkey and the capital of Kars Province. It stands on a high plateau near the border with Armenia, overlooked by the medieval Kars Castle. Because of its strategic position on the routes between Anatolia, the Caucasus and Iran, Kars changed hands many times among Armenian, Byzantine, Seljuk, Mongol, Ottoman and Russian rulers.

History

Kars was a capital of the Bagratid Armenian kingdom in the 10th century before passing to Byzantium, the Seljuks and others. Captured by the Ottomans in the 16th century, its fortress was besieged repeatedly in the Russo-Turkish wars; the defence of Kars in 1855, aided by British officers, became famous in Britain. Russia annexed Kars in 1878 and ruled it for forty years, building the gridded Russian quarter with Baltic-style stone buildings that still distinguishes the city. After the First World War and the Turkish War of Independence, the Treaties of Moscow and Kars (1921) confirmed Turkish sovereignty and fixed the border with Soviet Armenia.

The ruined medieval Armenian city of Ani, once a Bagratid capital, lies about 42 km east of Kars on the closed Armenian border and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Geography and culture

At roughly 1,768 metres above sea level, Kars has a humid continental climate with very cold, snowy winters. The region is famous for its aged kaşar cheese (Kars gravyeri), honey, and goose dishes. Orhan Pamuk set much of his novel Snow in the city, drawing on its wintry isolation and political history. Landmarks include Kars Castle, the Church of the Holy Apostles (a 10th-century Armenian church, now the Kümbet Mosque), the stone bridge over the Kars River, and the Russian-era Fethiye Mosque.
