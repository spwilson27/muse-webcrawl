Nolan Ryan

Lynn Nolan Ryan Jr. (born January 31, 1947), nicknamed "the Ryan Express", is an American former professional baseball pitcher who played 27 seasons in Major League Baseball for the New York Mets, California Angels, Houston Astros and Texas Rangers. He is regarded as one of the greatest power pitchers in baseball history.

Career

Ryan's fastball was regularly clocked above 100 mph, and his longevity was extraordinary: he pitched in four different decades, from 1966 to 1993, retiring at age 46. He holds the major league records for career strikeouts (5,714), no-hitters (seven, three more than any other pitcher), and strikeouts per nine innings among long-career pitchers. He also holds the unenviable records for walks allowed and wild pitches. Ryan won a World Series with the 1969 "Miracle Mets" and threw his seventh no-hitter with Texas in 1991 at age 44.

Legacy

Ryan was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1999, his first year of eligibility. After retirement he served as CEO of the Texas Rangers and as an executive advisor to the Houston Astros, and built a business career including beef production and minor-league team ownership.
