Vocational education

Vocational education is education that prepares people to work directly in a trade, craft or occupation, as a technician or in a professional position in fields such as engineering, accountancy, nursing, medicine, architecture or law. It is often called career and technical education (CTE) in the United States, or technical and vocational education and training (TVET) internationally. It may occur at the secondary, post-secondary or further-education level and includes apprenticeships.

Systems by country

Germany's dual system combines paid apprenticeship in a company with part-time study at a vocational school (Berufsschule), and is widely credited with low youth unemployment. Switzerland and Austria run similar systems. In the United States, vocational preparation is offered by high schools, community colleges and technical institutes, with federal support under the Perkins Acts. The United Kingdom offers apprenticeships, BTECs and T Levels; Australia uses the TAFE system; India has the Industrial Training Institutes.

Characteristics and debates

Vocational curricula combine classroom theory with workshop practice and workplace placements. Advocates stress employability, skills shortages and routes into good jobs without a university degree. Critics warn of early "tracking" that can entrench social inequality, and of narrow training that ages quickly as technology changes; reformers therefore emphasise transferable skills and pathways from vocational qualifications into higher education.

History

Guild apprenticeship was the medieval norm. The industrial revolution created mechanics' institutes and trade schools in the 19th century; the 20th century saw national systems codified, and the late 20th and 21st centuries brought renewed policy interest as economies shifted to services, green technology and digital skills.
