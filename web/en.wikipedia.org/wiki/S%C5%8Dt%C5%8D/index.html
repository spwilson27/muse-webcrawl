Sōtō

Founder (Japan)Dōgen Zenji (1200–1253)
Head templesEihei-ji; Sōji-ji
Core practiceShikantaza ("just sitting")

Sōtō Zen (Sōtō-shū) is the largest of the three main schools of Zen Buddhism in Japan, alongside Rinzai and Ōbaku. It descends from the Chinese Caodong school and was transmitted to Japan by the monk Dōgen, who founded the temple Eihei-ji in Fukui Prefecture.

Practice

Sōtō emphasises shikantaza — "just sitting" meditation without objects or goals — reflecting Dōgen's teaching that practice and enlightenment are one. Unlike Rinzai, it places less stress on kōan introspection, favouring gradual cultivation and everyday activity as practice.

History

Dōgen's writings, especially the Shōbōgenzō, are central texts. Keizan Jōkin, regarded as the school's second founder, spread Sōtō widely among laypeople and founded Sōji-ji in present-day Yokohama. Today the school counts over ten thousand temples in Japan and a substantial international following, especially in North America and Europe.
