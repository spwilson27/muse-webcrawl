Youghal

Youghal (Irish: Eochaill, meaning 'yew wood') is a seaside resort town in County Cork, Ireland. Situated on the estuary of the River Blackwater, it was historically an important port and military garrison, and it preserves substantial stretches of its medieval town walls, among the best-surviving in Ireland. The town has a population of roughly 8,000.

History

Youghal developed from a settlement founded in the Norse period and was incorporated in the thirteenth century following the Anglo-Norman invasion. It became one of Ireland's principal ports for trade with England and the continent. The collegiate church of St Mary's dates to the thirteenth century. In the late sixteenth century the town and its lands were granted to Sir Walter Raleigh, who is said to have planted Ireland's first potatoes in his garden at Myrtle Grove, his residence there; Edmund Spenser is also associated with the district. The Cromwellian and Williamite wars saw the town change hands several times. Its harbour later declined in importance as trade shifted to Cork and Waterford.

Tourism and landmarks

Landmarks include the town walls, the Clock Gate tower on the main street, Tynte's Castle, and the long sandy beaches at nearby Claycastle and Redbarn that made Youghal a popular Victorian holiday resort. The 1956 film Moby Dick, directed by John Huston, was partly filmed in the town, which doubled for New Bedford.
