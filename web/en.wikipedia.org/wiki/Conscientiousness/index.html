Orderliness and self-discipline are hallmarks of conscientiousness

Conscientiousness is the personality trait of being responsible, organised, hard-working and goal-directed. It is one of the five factors in the Big Five model of personality (the others being openness to experience, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism). People who are conscientious tend to be self-disciplined, dutiful and achievement-oriented; they prefer planned rather than spontaneous behaviour, and they generally exhibit neatness, systematicness and carefulness. Low conscientiousness is associated with flexibility and spontaneity, but can also appear as sloppiness and lack of reliability.

Facets and measurement

In the NEO framework of Costa and McCrae, conscientiousness comprises six facets: competence, order, dutifulness, achievement striving, self-discipline and deliberation. Other models split the trait into two related aspects: industriousness (working hard and striving for achievement) and orderliness (being organised and tidy). Conscientiousness is measured by self-report questionnaires such as the NEO-PI-R, the BFI and IPIP inventories, and by informant ratings, which correlate moderately with self-reports.

Development and correlates

Conscientiousness generally increases through young and middle adulthood — people become more responsible and self-controlled as they take on work and family roles, a pattern known as the maturity principle — and it shows moderate heritability in twin studies. It is the Big Five trait most consistently linked to job performance across occupations, particularly its achievement-striving and dependability elements, and it predicts academic success as well. Meta-analyses also associate high conscientiousness with better health behaviours, reduced risk of obesity, smoking and substance abuse, and greater longevity; conversely, extreme conscientiousness can shade into perfectionism, workaholism and the rigidity seen in obsessive–compulsive personality disorder.
