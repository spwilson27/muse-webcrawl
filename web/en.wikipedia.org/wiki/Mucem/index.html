The Mucem (Musée des Civilisations de l'Europe et de la Méditerranée, Museum of European and Mediterranean Civilisations) is a national museum in Marseille, France, dedicated to the cultures of the Mediterranean. Opened in 2013 when Marseille was European Capital of Culture, it was the first French national museum established outside Paris as part of cultural decentralisation, a policy lineage reaching back to Malraux's Maisons de la culture and continued by projects like Louvre-Lens.

Site and architecture

The museum occupies two sites joined by a slender footbridge: the seventeenth-century Fort Saint-Jean at the entrance to the Old Port, and a new building on the J4 pier by Rudy Ricciotti — a perfect square cube wrapped in a perforated skin of fibre-reinforced concrete that casts shifting shadows like a moucharabieh. Ramps climb around the building's exterior to a roof terrace overlooking the sea.

Collections

The Mucem inherited the collections of Paris's Musée des Arts et Traditions Populaires (closed 2005), founded by Georges Henri Rivière, enriched with comparative Mediterranean material. Its permanent gallery traces Mediterranean history from Neolithic agriculture to contemporary art and society, and large temporary exhibitions address topics from migration and football to perfumes and rural life.
