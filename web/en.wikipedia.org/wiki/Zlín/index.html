Zlín (Czech pronunciation: [zliːn]; German: Zlin) is a city in the Zlín Region of the Czech Republic, in eastern Moravia. It lies in the valley of the Dřevnice river. Zlín is famous as the home of the Bata shoe company, whose founder Tomáš Baťa transformed the small town into a planned industrial city and a celebrated example of functionalist urban design in the early 20th century. From 1949 to 1989 the city was renamed Gottwaldov after communist leader Klement Gottwald.

History

The first written mention of Zlín dates from 1322, when it was a small craft and market settlement. Its modern development began in 1894 when Tomáš Baťa founded a shoe factory there. Bata's company grew into one of the world's largest footwear manufacturers, and the town expanded rapidly with standardized factory buildings, workers' housing estates of brick houses, schools and social facilities designed by architects including František Lydie Gahura and Vladimír Karfík. The Bata Skyscraper ("Building No. 21"), completed in 1938, was one of the tallest buildings in pre-war Czechoslovakia and famously contained Tomáš Baťa's successor's office inside an elevator cabin.

Architecture

Zlín is regarded as a unique open-air museum of interwar functionalism. The city was built on a modular grid based on the 6.15 × 6.15 m Bata module, with red-brick factory buildings, the Grand Cinema (once among the largest cinemas in Europe), the Tomáš Baťa Memorial, and garden-city residential districts. The city's heritage is promoted by the Tomas Bata University, founded in 2001.

Economy and culture

Although shoe production declined after 1989, Zlín remains an industrial and university city. It hosts the Zlín Film Festival, one of the world's oldest festivals of films for children and youth (founded 1961), building on the local Barrandov-affiliated film studios established by the Bata company in the 1930s, where animator Hermína Týrlová worked.
