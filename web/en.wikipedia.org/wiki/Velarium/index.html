Velarium

A velarium ("awning") was a retractable fabric covering used in ancient Rome to shade the audience in an amphitheatre from sun and rain. The most famous example roofed the Colosseum in Rome, where a vast sailcloth awning could be spread over the cavea (seating tiers), leaving only the arena open.

Operation at the Colosseum

The velarium was rigged from 240 masts mounted around the top of the Colosseum's outer wall, using a complex web of ropes. It was operated by a detachment of sailors from the Roman imperial fleets at Misenum and Ravenna, whose barracks (the Castra Misenatium) stood nearby, because the rigging resembled that of a ship. The covering probably shaded most of the roughly 50,000 spectators, with a central opening acting like an oculus.

Etymology and other uses

The word derives from Latin velum, "sail" or "curtain". In zoology, "velarium" also names the strengthened rim of the bell of certain jellyfish, especially box jellyfish, which helps them swim with unusual agility.
