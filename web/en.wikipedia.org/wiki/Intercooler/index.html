Intercooler

An intercooler is a heat exchanger used to cool a gas after compression. Most commonly it cools the intake charge of a turbocharged or supercharged internal-combustion engine: compressing air heats it, and cooling the charge before it reaches the cylinders increases its density, allowing more fuel to be burned for more power and reducing the risk of engine knock. Intercoolers are also used in industrial multi-stage compressors, where cooling between stages improves efficiency.

Types

  • Air-to-air: the charge passes through a finned core cooled by ambient airflow, typically mounted at the front of a vehicle. Simple and light, but dependent on vehicle speed and ambient temperature.
  • Air-to-liquid (charge-air cooler): the charge is cooled by a water circuit with its own radiator. More compact and consistent, common in modern production cars and in marine and stationary engines.

Considerations

An intercooler imposes a pressure drop on the intake air and adds volume that can contribute to turbo lag, so sizing is a compromise between cooling effectiveness and flow restriction. In aircraft piston engines and gas turbines, related devices — aftercoolers and recuperators — play analogous roles. Diesel trucks, performance cars and modern downsized petrol engines almost universally pair turbocharging with intercooling.
