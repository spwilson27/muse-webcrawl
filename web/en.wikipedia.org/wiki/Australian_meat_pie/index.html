Australian meat pie

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An Australian meat pie is a hand-sized meat pie containing diced or minced meat and gravy, sometimes with onion, mushrooms or cheese. It is considered the quintessential Australian dish and is often eaten with tomato sauce (ketchup). Meat pies are a common convenience food at service stations, bakeries, sporting venues and school canteens across Australia and New Zealand.

History

Meat pies arrived with British colonisation, building on a British tradition of portable pastry-encased meats. Pie carts became fixtures of Australian cities in the nineteenth century; Adelaide's pie cart serving the "pie floater" — a meat pie inverted in pea soup — became a South Australian icon. Commercial bakeries industrialised production in the twentieth century, with brands such as Four'N Twenty (founded in Bendigo in 1947) and Mrs Mac's becoming household names.

Description and varieties

A standard pie has a shortcrust base and a puff-pastry lid, filled with minced beef in thickened gravy. Australian food standards require pies to contain a minimum proportion of meat. Varieties include steak and onion, steak and kidney, chicken, curry, and potato-topped pies. Gourmet bakeries have expanded the range to pepper steak, lamb and rosemary, and vegetarian options, and an annual Great Aussie Pie Competition judges entries from bakeries nationwide.

Cultural role

The meat pie is a staple at Australian rules football and rugby league matches and a symbol of working-class fare; consumption is estimated at more than 270 million pies a year in Australia. The pie is closely shared with New Zealand, where similar traditions and competitions exist.
