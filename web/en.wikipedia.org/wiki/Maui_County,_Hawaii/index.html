Maui County, officially the County of Maui, is a county in the U.S. state of Hawaii. It consists of the islands of Maui, Lanai, Molokai (except for the Kalaupapa area, which forms the separate Kalawao County), Kahoolawe, and Molokini. As of the 2020 census, the population was about 165,000. The county seat is Wailuku, and the largest community is Kahului.

History

The county was created in 1905 during the territorial period. Its economy historically centred on sugarcane and pineapple plantations, which declined in the late twentieth century as tourism became dominant.

Geography

The county covers about 1,120 square miles of land. Maui island itself is dominated by the Haleakalā volcano in the east and the West Maui Mountains. The county contains Haleakalā National Park and numerous beaches and resort areas such as Wailea, Kaʻanapali and Hana.

2023 wildfires

In August 2023, devastating wildfires, most catastrophically in Lahaina, killed around 100 people and destroyed much of the historic town, in the deadliest United States wildfire in over a century. Recovery and rebuilding efforts have continued since.

Government

The county is governed by a mayor and a nine-member county council, elected from residency districts.
