The National Ignition Facility (NIF) is a laser-based inertial confinement fusion (ICF) research facility located at the Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory in Livermore, California. The NIF uses 192 powerful laser beams to heat and compress a tiny pellet of hydrogen fuel (deuterium and tritium) to conditions of temperature and pressure found in the cores of stars, with the goal of achieving fusion ignition — a self-sustaining burn releasing more energy than the laser delivered.

Construction and operation

Construction began in 1997 and the facility was completed in 2009 at a cost of about $3.5 billion. The lasers occupy a building roughly the size of three American football fields. A single low-energy pulse is amplified through 192 beamlines of neodymium glass amplifiers, converted to ultraviolet light, and focused into a 10-metre-diameter target chamber onto a centimetre-scale hohlraum containing the fuel capsule. The implosion lasts only billionths of a second and reaches temperatures above 100 million kelvin.

Ignition achievement

On 5 December 2022, NIF achieved scientific breakeven for the first time: a shot delivered 2.05 megajoules of laser energy to the target and produced 3.15 megajoules of fusion energy, a gain of about 1.5. The result was repeated in subsequent experiments in 2023 and 2024 with higher yields. NIF's primary missions also include stockpile stewardship — studying the physics of nuclear weapons without explosive testing — and basic high-energy-density science, including experiments relevant to astrophysics and fusion energy.
