Nanna (Sumerian), in Akkadian Sin (or Suen), is the ancient Mesopotamian god of the moon, one of the oldest and most widely worshipped deities of the region. He is the son of Enlil and Ninlil, born during their journey to the underworld in the myth Enlil and Ninlil; his wife is Ningal ("Great Lady"), and their children are the sun god Utu and the goddess Inanna. His sacred number is 30, for the days of the lunar month, and his symbol — the crescent moon, often interpreted as a boat in which he sails the night sky, or as the horns of his sacred bull — crowned temples from Ur to Harran.

Cult at Ur and Harran

Nanna's principal seat was Ur in southern Sumer, where his temple, the E-gish-shir-gal ("House of the Great Light"), stood beside the great ziggurat built by Ur-Namma (c. 2100 BC). The high priestess of Nanna at Ur — the en-priestess — was among the most powerful religious offices in Mesopotamia, famously held by Enheduanna, daughter of Sargon of Akkad and the earliest named author in world literature, whose hymns to Inanna survive; kings of the Third Dynasty of Ur installed their daughters in the office for centuries. In the north, Sin's temple at Harran (Ehulhul) made that city a byword for moon worship well into the Islamic period, and Neo-Babylonian king Nabonidus — whose mother was a devotee of Sin of Harran — rebuilt the temple and scandalised Babylon by elevating the moon god above Marduk.

Functions and character

As measurer of the month, Nanna governs time, calendars and the night; his phases mark festivals, and lunar eclipses were ominous events read by diviners and countered by rituals appointing a "substitute king" to absorb the threatened fate. Hymns praise him as a shining bull whose horns light the darkness, a father who decrees destinies at the new moon, and the god who makes cattle and the marshes fertile — the moon's crescent linked with the bull's horns and with abundance. In the Lament for Ur, Nanna and Ningal plead in vain with Enlil as their city falls to the Elamites. His boat, the "Crescent of Nanna," carries him nightly across the heavens, and his light was believed to reveal truth and guide travellers.
