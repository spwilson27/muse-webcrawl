KLM Royal Dutch Airlines (Dutch: Koninklijke Luchtvaart Maatschappij, "Royal Aviation Company") is the flag carrier of the Netherlands. KLM's hub is at Amsterdam Airport Schiphol. It is part of the Air France–KLM group, and is a member of the SkyTeam airline alliance. Founded in 1919, KLM is the oldest airline in the world still operating under its original name.

History

KLM was founded on 7 October 1919 by Albert Plesman, making it the world's oldest airline still in operation under its original name. Its first flight took place on 17 May 1920, from London to Amsterdam, and regular service to Batavia (Jakarta) in the Dutch East Indies followed from 1924, becoming one of the pioneering long-haul routes. The airline received the "Royal" (Koninklijke) designation from Queen Wilhelmina at its founding. In 2004 KLM merged with Air France to form Air France–KLM, while retaining its own brand, hub and operating certificate.

Network and fleet

KLM serves more than 160 destinations worldwide together with its KLM Cityhopper regional subsidiary, which operates European feeder services, mainly with Embraer jets. The long-haul fleet centres on Boeing 777 and 787 Dreamliner aircraft and Airbus A330s, with Airbus A321neo aircraft joining the European fleet. KLM Cargo is a major operator of freight services through Schiphol.

Branding

KLM aircraft wear a distinctive blue livery with a crown logo. The airline is known for its miniature Delftware houses filled with Dutch gin, given to business-class passengers since the 1950s — a celebrated collectible representing historic Dutch buildings.
