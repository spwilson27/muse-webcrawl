Acorn squash, named for its acorn-like shape.

Acorn squash (Cucurbita pepo, Turbinata group), also called pepper squash or Des Moines squash, is a winter squash with distinctive longitudinal ridges and sweet, yellow-orange flesh. It is named for its resemblance to an acorn.

Description

The fruit is typically 1–2 pounds (0.5–1 kg), deeply ribbed, and most often dark green, sometimes with an orange patch where it rested on the ground; golden and white varieties also exist. Like other C. pepo squash, it was domesticated in the Americas and was a staple of Indigenous agriculture as part of the Three Sisters planting system with maize and beans.

Culinary use

Acorn squash is most commonly baked, often halved, seeded and filled or brushed with butter and brown sugar, though it can also be roasted, steamed or microwaved. The hard rind is usually not eaten, but the seeds can be roasted like pumpkin seeds. It is a good source of fibre, potassium and vitamin C, and keeps for a month or more in cool storage.
