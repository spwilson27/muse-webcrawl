San Luis Potosí, officially the Free and Sovereign State of San Luis Potosí (Spanish: Estado Libre y Soberano de San Luis Potosí), is one of the 32 states which compose the Federal Entities of Mexico. It is divided into 58 municipalities and its capital city is San Luis Potosí.

Waterfalls of the Huasteca Potosina, in the east of the state

Geography

Located in east-central Mexico, San Luis Potosí is bordered by the states of Nuevo León, Tamaulipas, Veracruz, Hidalgo, Querétaro, Guanajuato, and Zacatecas. The state's terrain varies sharply: the west is a high, arid plateau of the Mexican Altiplano, while the east descends through the Sierra Madre Oriental into the lush Huasteca region, a tropical lowland drained by the Pánuco River basin and famed for waterfalls such as Tamul and the surrealist garden of Las Pozas at Xilitla, created by Edward James.

History

Before Spanish colonization, the territory was inhabited by Chichimeca peoples in the north and Huastec and Pame peoples in the east. Gold and silver deposits were discovered in 1592, and the city of San Luis Potosí was founded that year; its name echoes Potosí in Bolivia, then the richest silver town in the Americas. The state played a role in the Mexican War of Independence and the Reform War; the Plan of San Luis Potosí, proclaimed by Francisco I. Madero in 1910, is regarded as the opening act of the Mexican Revolution.

Economy

Historically a mining state, San Luis Potosí remains a producer of zinc, copper, and other minerals. In recent decades the capital's metropolitan area has become a manufacturing hub, particularly for the automotive industry, with plants operated by General Motors and BMW. Agriculture, including sugar cane in the Huasteca, and tourism round out the economy.
