Pink-headed duck

Pink-headed duck
Scientific nameRhodonessa caryophyllacea
FamilyAnatidae
Former rangeIndia, Bangladesh, Myanmar
StatusCritically endangered, possibly extinct
Last reliable record1949

The pink-headed duck (Rhodonessa caryophyllacea) is a large diving duck that was once found in parts of India, Bangladesh, and Myanmar. The male is unmistakable, with a bright pink head and neck contrasting with a dark chocolate-brown body; the female is duller, with a pale pinkish head.

Description and behaviour

About 60 cm long, the species inhabited secluded wetlands, swamp forest, and flooded grassland, feeding on molluscs, aquatic plants, and small invertebrates. It was shy and largely crepuscular, and its eggs were unusually spherical.

Decline

Always uncommon, the duck declined through hunting and the drainage and conversion of its wetland habitat. The last confirmed wild specimen was collected in 1935 in Bihar, and the last generally accepted sighting came in 1949. A few captive birds survived in aviaries in Europe into the 1920s and 1930s.

Possible survival

Unconfirmed reports from the remote wetlands of northern Myanmar (Kachin State) prompted searches in the 2000s, which failed to find the species. It is classified as Critically Endangered, and is widely considered likely extinct.
