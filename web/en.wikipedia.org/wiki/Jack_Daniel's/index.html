Jack Daniel's is a brand of Tennessee whiskey produced in Lynchburg, Tennessee, by the Jack Daniel Distillery, founded by Jasper Newton "Jack" Daniel in 1875 (registered in 1866). It is among the best-selling American whiskeys in the world, and its black-label flagship product, Old No. 7, is one of the most recognised spirits brands globally. The distillery has been owned by the Brown–Forman Corporation since 1956.

Production

Jack Daniel's is made from a mash of 80% corn, 12% rye, and 8% malted barley, distilled in copper stills and filtered through ten feet of sugar maple charcoal — the "Lincoln County Process" that legally and stylistically distinguishes Tennessee whiskey from bourbon. The water comes from Cave Spring Hollow, an iron-free limestone spring on the distillery grounds. The whiskey is aged in charred American oak barrels made by the company itself, in barrel houses spread across the hills around Lynchburg.

History

Jack Daniel learned distilling as a boy from preacher and distiller Dan Call and from Nathan "Nearest" Green, an enslaved African-American distiller who became the company's first master distiller; the brand's foundation later formally recognised Green's foundational role. Jack Daniel registered his distillery with the U.S. federal government in 1866, making it the oldest registered distillery in the United States. After Daniel's death in 1911 (attributed to blood poisoning following a toe injury from kicking his safe), his nephew Lem Motlow took over the business, guiding it through Prohibition and repeal; notably, Lynchburg sits in Moore County, which remains a dry county even though the whiskey is produced there.

Products

The core range includes Old No. 7 (black label), Gentleman Jack (charcoal-mellowed twice), Single Barrel Select, Tennessee Honey, Tennessee Fire, and Tennessee Apple, along with ready-to-drink products. Sales exceed ten million nine-litre cases annually worldwide.
