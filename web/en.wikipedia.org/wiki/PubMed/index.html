PubMed is a free database of references and abstracts on life sciences and biomedical topics, maintained by the United States National Library of Medicine (NLM) at the National Institutes of Health. It is the principal public interface to the MEDLINE bibliographic database and also includes citations from PubMed Central and other sources.

PubMed contains more than 37 million citations for biomedical literature from MEDLINE, life science journals, and online books. Citations may include links to full-text content from PubMed Central and publisher web sites. Each record is assigned a unique identifier, the PMID.

History

PubMed was developed by the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) and released in January 1996. In June 1997 Vice President Al Gore announced free public access to MEDLINE through PubMed, transforming it from a service used mainly through libraries into one of the most heavily used scientific databases in the world. Searches make use of the MeSH (Medical Subject Headings) controlled vocabulary, and the Entrez retrieval system provides links to related records across NCBI databases.

Features

Users can construct searches with Boolean operators, field tags, and MeSH terms; results can be filtered by article type, date, species, and free-full-text availability. The "similar articles" function ranks related citations algorithmically. A redesigned, mobile-responsive interface became the default in 2020.
