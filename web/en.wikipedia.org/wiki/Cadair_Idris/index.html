Cadair Idris is a mountain in the southern part of the Snowdonia National Park in Gwynedd, Wales, rising to 893 metres (2,930 ft) at its summit, Penygader. It is one of the most popular mountains in Wales for walkers and climbers, and much of it is designated a National Nature Reserve.

Name and mythology

Cadair Idris means "the chair of Idris". Idris is a figure of Welsh legend — variously described as a giant, a warrior, and an astronomer — who was said to sit on the mountain studying the stars. Local folklore holds that anyone who spends a night on the summit will awaken either a poet or a madman, and that the hollow beneath the summit chair was where Idris sat. The mountain also features in tales of the spectral Cŵn Annwn, the hounds of the Welsh otherworld.

Geography and geology

The mountain forms a long east–west ridge of Ordovician volcanic and sedimentary rocks, heavily sculpted by glaciation. Its northern face drops steeply to the Talyllyn valley; to the south lies the glacial lake Llyn Cau beneath towering cliffs in the cwm of Craig Cau. Several species of arctic-alpine plants survive on its high ledges.

Walking routes

Three main paths climb to Penygader: the Pony Path from the north near Dolgellau, generally considered the easiest; the Minffordd Path from the south, climbing steeply past Llyn Cau; and the Fox's Path, a direct but eroded line up the northern face. A stone summit shelter stands just below the top.
