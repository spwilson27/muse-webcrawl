MS Estonia was a cruise ferry built in 1980 by the West German shipyard Meyer Werft in Papenburg. She operated on routes in the Baltic Sea for Viking Line, Effoa, and Wasa Line before being sold in 1993 to Estline, a joint venture between the Estonian state shipping company and Nordström & Thulin, and placed on the Tallinn–Stockholm route.

Sinking

On the night of 27–28 September 1994, while sailing from Tallinn to Stockholm in heavy seas, Estonia sank in the Baltic Sea off the Finnish island of Utö. The official investigation concluded that the locks on the bow visor failed under the force of the waves; the visor tore away, pulling open the loading ramp, and water flooding the car deck destroyed the ship's stability. Estonia capsized and sank in less than an hour. Of the 989 people aboard, 852 died and 137 survived, making it one of the worst maritime disasters of the 20th century and the deadliest peacetime shipwreck in European waters.

The first distress calls were sent shortly after 01:20. The ferry Mariella was the first vessel to reach the scene, and survivors were rescued by ship and helicopter in difficult conditions of storm and darkness.

Aftermath

The wreck, lying in international waters, was declared a maritime grave; Estonia, Sweden, and Finland signed a treaty protecting the site and prohibiting diving. The disaster led to significant changes in safety regulations for ro-ro ferries, including stricter requirements for bow visors and doors under amendments to the SOLAS convention. New expeditions to the wreck in the 2020s, prompted by renewed questions, found damage to the starboard side consistent with a collision with the seabed, but no evidence overturning the official account of the visor failure as the initiating event.
