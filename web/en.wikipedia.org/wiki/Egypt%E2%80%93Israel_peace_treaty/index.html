Egypt–Israel peace treaty

The Egypt–Israel peace treaty was signed in Washington, D.C., on 26 March 1979, following the 1978 Camp David Accords. It was the first peace treaty between Israel and an Arab state, ending the state of war that had existed since 1948.

Terms

Under the treaty, Israel withdrew from the Sinai Peninsula, captured in 1967, returning it to Egypt by 1982; Egypt recognised Israel and established diplomatic relations; and passage of Israeli ships through the Suez Canal and the Straits of Tiran was guaranteed. The treaty has held for decades, though the peace is often described as a "cold peace".

Background

The agreement followed Anwar Sadat's historic visit to Jerusalem in 1977 and the Camp David negotiations mediated by U.S. President Jimmy Carter. Sadat and Menachem Begin shared the 1978 Nobel Peace Prize.
