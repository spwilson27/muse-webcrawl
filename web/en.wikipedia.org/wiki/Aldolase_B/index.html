Aldolase B, also known as fructose-bisphosphate aldolase B or liver-type aldolase, is one of three isoenzymes of fructose-bisphosphate aldolase in humans. It is encoded by the ALDOB gene and is expressed mainly in the liver, kidney and small intestine. Aldolase B plays a key role in both glycolysis and fructose metabolism.

Function

Like the other aldolases, aldolase B catalyses the reversible cleavage of fructose 1,6-bisphosphate into glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate and dihydroxyacetone phosphate, the fourth step of glycolysis. Uniquely among the isoenzymes, it also efficiently cleaves fructose 1-phosphate (produced from dietary fructose by fructokinase) into dihydroxyacetone phosphate and glyceraldehyde, making it essential for fructose catabolism.

Clinical significance

Mutations in ALDOB cause hereditary fructose intolerance, an autosomal recessive disorder in which ingestion of fructose or sucrose leads to accumulation of fructose 1-phosphate, hypoglycaemia, vomiting, and potentially liver and kidney damage. Management consists of strict dietary exclusion of fructose, sucrose and sorbitol.

Isoenzymes

The other human aldolases are aldolase A (muscle type) and aldolase C (brain type), encoded by separate genes with distinct tissue distributions and kinetic properties.
