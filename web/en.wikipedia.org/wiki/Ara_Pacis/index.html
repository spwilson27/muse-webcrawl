Ara Pacis

The Ara Pacis Augustae (Latin, "Altar of Augustan Peace"; commonly shortened to Ara Pacis) is a monument built in Rome between 13 and 9 BC to celebrate the peace established by the Emperor Augustus after his campaigns in Hispania and Gaul. Commissioned by the Roman Senate on 4 July 13 BC to honour the return of Augustus from three years in those provinces, it was consecrated on 30 January 9 BC. Originally located on the northern outskirts of Rome, a Roman mile from the boundary of the pomerium on the west side of the Via Flaminia, the altar stood in the northeastern corner of the Campus Martius.

Structure

The Ara Pacis is made entirely of Luna marble and consists of a traditional open-air altar at its centre, surrounded by a nearly square precinct wall (about 11.65 by 10.62 metres) decorated with reliefs on both its inner and outer faces. Entrances on the east and west sides are approached by steps. The inner altar has two tiers and its own frieze depicting the sacrificial procession of the Vestal Virgins and other ritual scenes.

Iconography

The exterior reliefs carry a rich programme of imagery associated with the Augustan regime. The lower register is filled with scrolling acanthus tendrils, vegetation and small animals, symbolising fertility and abundance. The upper register shows processions of the imperial family and Roman officials on the north and south sides, while the east and west walls carry mythological panels: the Lupercal with Romulus and Remus, Aeneas sacrificing, the goddess Roma seated on arms, and the famous panel of a female figure — variously identified as Tellus, Italia, Pax or Venus — seated amid fertility imagery with two infants.

Rediscovery and reconstruction

Fragments of the altar came to light from the sixteenth century onwards, scattered among collections in Rome, Florence and Paris. In the 1930s the Italian government undertook a major excavation and reconstruction on a new site beside the Mausoleum of Augustus, completing the work in 1938 for the bimillennium of Augustus's birth. The altar is now housed in the Museo dell'Ara Pacis, designed by the American architect Richard Meier and opened in 2006 — the first major new building in Rome's historic centre since the Fascist era.

Significance

The Ara Pacis is regarded as one of the finest surviving examples of Augustan sculpture and a key work of Roman imperial propaganda, presenting peace, fertility and dynastic continuity as the fruits of Augustus's rule. Augustus himself mentions its construction in his Res Gestae Divi Augusti.
