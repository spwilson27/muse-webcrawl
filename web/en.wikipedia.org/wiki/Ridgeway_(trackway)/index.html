The Ridgeway

The Ridgeway is an ancient trackway in southern England, described as Britain's oldest road. It follows the chalk ridge of the Berkshire Downs and Chiltern Hills from Overton Hill near Avebury in Wiltshire to Ivinghoe Beacon in Buckinghamshire, a distance of about 87 miles (140 km) as a modern National Trail.

History

The route has been used for at least 5,000 years by travellers, herdsmen, and soldiers, travelling along dry chalk uplands above marshy valleys. Along it stand Neolithic and Bronze Age monuments including Avebury stone circle, Wayland's Smithy long barrow, and the Uffington White Horse, as well as Iron Age hillforts such as Barbury Castle and Segsbury Camp.

National Trail

Designated a National Trail in 1972 and opened in 1973, the modern Ridgeway follows the ancient route in its western half; east of the Thames at Goring, it follows the related Icknield Way. Motor vehicles are restricted on much of the trail, which is popular with walkers, cyclists, and horse riders.
