Maaloula

Maaloula (Arabic: معلولا, Maʿlūlā) is a town in the Rif Dimashq Governorate of Syria, about 56 km northeast of Damascus, built into the rugged mountainside of the Qalamoun range at an elevation of more than 1,500 metres. The name comes from the Aramaic word maʿʿəlā, meaning "entrance".

Maaloula is famous as one of the last places where a Western Aramaic language — a descendant of the language spoken in the time of Jesus — is still spoken as a living language, alongside the nearby villages of Jubb'adin and al-Sarkha (Bakh'a). Its population has historically included both Christians (mainly Melkite Greek Catholics and Greek Orthodox) and Muslims.

Landmarks

The town contains some of Syria's oldest Christian sites. The Monastery of Saint Sergius and Bacchus (Deir Mar Sarkis) dates to the early centuries of Christianity and stands above the town. The Monastery of Saint Thecla (Deir Mar Taqla) is built around a grotto associated with Saint Thecla, a follower of Saint Paul; according to tradition, the mountain opened a path for her to escape her persecutors, and the gorge remains a pilgrimage site where visitors drink from a spring in the cave.

Syrian civil war

During the Syrian civil war, Maaloula was fought over in 2013 and early 2014, when rebel forces including fighters of the al-Nusra Front briefly seized the town and kidnapped twelve nuns from the St Thecla convent (they were released in March 2014). Government forces retook the town in April 2014. The fighting damaged churches and homes and displaced most of the population, raising fears for the survival of the local Aramaic-speaking community.
