Dhalgren is a 1975 science fiction novel by American writer Samuel R. Delany. It has sold more than a million copies and is among the most popular and controversial of Delany's works. The novel follows a young drifter — bisexual, dyslexic, and suffering from memory loss, known only as "the Kid" — who arrives in Bellona, a Midwestern city isolated by an unexplained catastrophe, where fires burn unchecked, a second sun has appeared in the sky, and ordinary social order has dissolved into gangs ("scorpions"), communes and fluid sexual arrangements.

Structure and themes

The novel has a famously circular structure: its final, fragmented section mirrors and partially repeats its opening, suggesting the narrative loops back on itself. The Kid becomes a poet whose journal may be the text of the novel itself. Dhalgren explores race, sexuality, gender, language, memory and urban decay, employing dense modernist and postmodernist techniques. William Gibson called it a "world of marvelous, hilarious, terrifying confusion" and a riddle never meant to be solved.

Reception

Critics were divided on publication: some hailed it as a major work of literary science fiction, while others found it impenetrable. It has since been widely taught in university courses and is frequently ranked among the most significant experimental novels in the genre.
