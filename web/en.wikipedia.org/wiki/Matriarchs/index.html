Matriarchs

The Matriarchs, also known as the "four mothers" (Hebrew: אימהות), are the wives of the biblical Patriarchs and the ancestral mothers of the Israelites in the Book of Genesis. In Jewish tradition, the matriarchs are Sarah, the wife of Abraham; Rebekah, the wife of Isaac; and Leah and Rachel, the wives of Jacob. (Bilhah and Zilpah, the handmaidens of Leah and Rachel who bore children to Jacob, are sometimes also counted, making six matriarchs; together they are the mothers of the twelve tribes of Israel.)

The four mothers

Sarah (originally Sarai), wife of Abraham, is the mother of Isaac. Rebekah, wife of Isaac, is the mother of Jacob and Esau. Leah, Jacob's first wife, is the mother of six sons including Judah and Levi, and of Dinah. Rachel, Jacob's beloved wife, is the mother of Joseph and Benjamin.

Burial and commemoration

According to Genesis, Sarah, Rebekah, and Leah are buried with their husbands in the Cave of the Patriarchs (Cave of Machpelah) in Hebron. Rachel, who died in childbirth with Benjamin, is buried separately near Bethlehem, at the site of Rachel's Tomb. The matriarchs are invoked in Jewish prayer — for example, the traditional blessing of daughters invokes them alongside the patriarchs ("May God make you like Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel and Leah") — and they are central figures in midrash and feminist readings of the Bible.
