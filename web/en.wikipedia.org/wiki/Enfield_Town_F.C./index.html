Enfield Town F.C.

Full name: Enfield Town Football Club
Nickname: The Towners
Founded: 2001
Ground: Queen Elizabeth II Stadium, Enfield
League: National League South (from 2024–25)

Enfield Town Football Club is a football club based in Enfield, London, England. It is widely credited as the country's first fully supporter-owned football club, formed in 2001 by fans of Enfield F.C. who were disenchanted with the sale of that club's Southbury Road ground and its nomadic existence under chairman Tony Lazarou.

History

The breakaway club began in the Essex Senior League, ground-sharing initially, and won the league in 2002–03 and again in 2004–05 before moving to the Isthmian League. In 2011 the club moved into the renovated Queen Elizabeth II Stadium in Donkey Lane, an Art Deco ground opened for the purpose. Enfield Town won the Isthmian League North Division in 2011–12 and the Alan Turvey Trophy, and spent a decade in the Isthmian Premier Division, regularly reaching play-offs.

In 2023–24 the Towners won the Isthmian Premier Division play-offs, defeating Chatham Town in the final to earn promotion to the National League South — the sixth tier of English football and the highest level in the club's history.

Supporter ownership

The club is owned by the Enfield Supporters Society, with members electing the board on a one-member-one-vote basis. Its founding influenced later fan-owned clubs and is cited in the fan-ownership movement documented by Supporters Direct. The club also runs women's, youth, and disability teams under the banner "A club for the community".
