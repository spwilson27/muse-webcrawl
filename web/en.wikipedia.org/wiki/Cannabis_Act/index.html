Cannabis Act

The Cannabis Act (Bill C-45) is a Canadian federal law that legalised and regulates the production, distribution and recreational use of cannabis in Canada. It received royal assent on 21 June 2018 and came into force on 17 October 2018, making Canada the second country in the world, after Uruguay, to legalise recreational cannabis nationwide.

Provisions

The Act permits adults to possess up to 30 grams of dried cannabis (or equivalent) in public, to buy from licensed retailers, and to grow up to four plants per household for personal use. It sets a minimum age of 18 federally, with provinces and territories able to raise it (most set 19; Quebec later raised it to 21). It creates offences for selling to youth and for unlicensed sale, and amends impaired-driving law.

Implementation

Provinces and territories regulate retail distribution and sale, through government stores, licensed private retailers, or both. Edibles, extracts and topicals were authorised under regulations that took effect in October 2019.

Objectives

The stated goals were to keep cannabis out of the hands of youth, keep profits from organised crime, and protect public health and safety through strict product quality controls.
