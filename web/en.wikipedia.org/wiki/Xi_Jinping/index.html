Xi Jinping

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Xi Jinping (born 15 June 1953) is a Chinese politician who has served as general secretary of the Chinese Communist Party and chairman of the Central Military Commission since 2012, and as president of the People's Republic of China since 2013. He is widely described as China's paramount leader, and his political theory, “Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era”, was written into the party constitution in 2017 and the state constitution in 2018.

Xi is a “princeling”, the son of Xi Zhongxun, a revolutionary veteran and vice-premier who was purged under Mao. During the Cultural Revolution Xi was sent down to Liangjiahe village in Shaanxi (1969–1975), an experience he has made central to his public biography. He joined the party in 1974, studied chemical engineering at Tsinghua University, and rose through provincial posts in Hebei, Fujian and Zhejiang, and briefly Shanghai, gaining a reputation as a disciplined administrator and corruption-fighter.

Leadership

At the 18th Party Congress (November 2012) Xi succeeded Hu Jintao as general secretary. His tenure has been marked by a sweeping anti-corruption campaign that disciplined well over a million officials (and removed potential rivals, critics note); the abolition in 2018 of the two-term limit on the state presidency; a more assertive foreign policy including the Belt and Road Initiative (announced 2013); poverty-alleviation campaigns; tighter party control over the economy, media, education and civil society; and policies in Xinjiang and Hong Kong that drew international criticism and sanctions. In October 2022 the 20th Party Congress gave him a third term as general secretary, breaking the post-Mao norm of two terms, and he began a third presidential term in March 2023.

Assessment

Supporters credit Xi with restoring party discipline, reducing poverty and raising China's global standing; critics describe a concentration of personal power not seen since Mao, with increased repression and economic statism. Assessments of his rule remain sharply divided along political lines.
