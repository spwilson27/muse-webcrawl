Atlantic meridional overturning circulation

AbbreviationAMOC
ComponentGulf Stream, North Atlantic Deep Water

The Atlantic meridional overturning circulation (AMOC) is the Atlantic part of the global thermohaline circulation: warm, salty surface water flows northward (including via the Gulf Stream), cools and sinks in the North Atlantic and Labrador Sea to form North Atlantic Deep Water, and returns southward at depth.

Climate role

The AMOC transports large amounts of heat northward, moderating the climate of northwestern Europe, and influences rainfall patterns including tropical rain belts and monsoons.

Stability and climate change

Freshwater input from Greenland ice melt and increased precipitation could weaken the AMOC. Observations and models suggest a weakening during the 20th–21st centuries, and the IPCC considers further weakening very likely, while an abrupt collapse before 2100 is considered less likely but a major low-likelihood, high-impact risk. A collapse would cool the North Atlantic region, shift tropical rainfall, and raise sea level along the US east coast.
