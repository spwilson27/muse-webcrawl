By-election

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A by-election (also spelled bye-election, and known as a special election in the United States) is an election used to fill an office that has become vacant between general elections. Vacancies arise from the death, resignation, disqualification, expulsion or elevation of the incumbent (or, historically in the UK, appointment to ministerial office, which once required re-election).

Significance

By-elections are watched as barometers of government popularity: governing parties commonly lose ground, protest and tactical voting are magnified, and strong campaigns by opposition or minor parties can produce celebrated upsets in "safe" seats. Their results are discounted by analysts (turnout is lower, local factors loom large) yet they can shift parliamentary arithmetic for minority governments, end careers — ministerial by-election defeats once forced resignations — and test campaign machinery and leaders' authority between general elections. In the US, special elections fill House vacancies (Senate vacancies are often filled by gubernatorial appointment until the next election), and off-cycle special elections are read similarly as signals.
