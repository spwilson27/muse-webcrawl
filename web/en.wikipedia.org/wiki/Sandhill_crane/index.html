Sandhill crane


Scientific name: Antigone canadensis
Family: Gruidae
Conservation status: Least Concern (IUCN)

The sandhill crane (Antigone canadensis, formerly Grus canadensis) is a species of large crane of North America and extreme northeastern Siberia. The common name refers to habitat like that at the Platte River, on the edge of Nebraska's Sandhills on the American Great Plains. It is the most abundant crane species in the world.

Description

Adults are gray overall, with a red forehead, white cheeks, and a long, dark, pointed bill; during breeding the plumage may become stained ochre from preening with iron-rich mud. They stand roughly 80–122 cm (2 ft 7 in – 4 ft) tall with wingspans of 1.65–2.3 m (5 ft 5 in – 7 ft 7 in). Their loud, rattling calls, produced by a coiled trachea, can be heard from great distances.

Distribution and migration

Sandhill cranes breed in wetlands across northern North America and Siberia and winter in the southern United States and northern Mexico. Each spring, hundreds of thousands gather on the Platte River in Nebraska during migration — one of the great wildlife spectacles of North America. Several subspecies are recognized; some populations, such as the Mississippi and Cuban sandhill cranes, are endangered, while the migratory populations are numerous and hunted in some states as game birds.
