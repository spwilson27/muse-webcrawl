Creative Commons

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Creative Commons (CC) is an American non-profit organisation, founded in 2001, that provides standardised free copyright licences enabling creators to grant the public permission to share and use their creative work under conditions of their choice. The licences combine four elements — Attribution (BY), ShareAlike (SA), NonCommercial (NC) and NoDerivatives (ND) — into six licences plus the CC0 public-domain dedication, expressed in three layers: a legal code, a human-readable summary and machine-readable metadata.

Co-founded by Lawrence Lessig and inspired by the free-software movement's copyleft, CC supplied the legal plumbing for the open-content web: Wikipedia itself is published under CC BY-SA (a compatibility the GFDL migration of 2009 enabled), and hundreds of millions of photographs, songs, textbooks and datasets carry CC terms — from Flickr images to open-access scholarship. The licences are irrevocable, operate without registration, and have been ported and then internationalised (version 4.0, 2013); courts in several countries have enforced them, while debates continue over the NC ambiguity and the compatibility of open licensing with platform and AI business models.
