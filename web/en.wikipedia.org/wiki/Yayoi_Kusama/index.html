Yayoi Kusama

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: An Infinity Mirror Room: mirrored walls and ceiling multiplying hanging lights into an endless starfield, visitors reflected within]

Yayoi Kusama (born 22 March 1929) is a Japanese contemporary artist working in sculpture, installation, painting, performance, film and fashion. Since the 1970s her work has been defined by polka dots, nets, pumpkins and mirrored 'Infinity Rooms', all rooted in hallucinations she has experienced since childhood; in the twenty-first century she has become one of the world's best-selling and most visited living artists.

Kusama emerged in New York's avant-garde from 1958 — her Infinity Net paintings, the soft-sculpture Accumulations and provocative happenings preceded Pop and Minimalism — returned to Japan in 1973, and from 1977 has lived voluntarily in a psychiatric hospital in Tokyo, working daily in her studio across the street.

New York years

Arriving in New York in 1958 with, she said, a letter of encouragement from Georgia O'Keeffe, Kusama showed huge white net paintings that critics linked — to her lasting resentment — to the male peers who followed her lead. Her unauthorised mosaic 'happening' at the 1966 Venice Biennale, Narcissus Garden (1,500 mirrored balls offered for sale), got her expelled and made her notorious.

Global late fame

Critical reassessment came with retrospectives in 1989 (New York) and 1993, when she represented Japan at Venice — the country's first solo woman artist there. Blockbuster tours of her Infinity Mirror Rooms (Tate Modern, the Hirshhorn and beyond) drew record queues; collaborations with Louis Vuitton (2012, 2023) carried her dots worldwide, and a museum devoted to her work opened in Tokyo in 2017.
