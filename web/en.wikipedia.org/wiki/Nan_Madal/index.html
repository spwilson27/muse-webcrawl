Nan Madol

Nan Madol is a ruined city built on a series of artificial islets in a lagoon off the eastern shore of Temwen Island, near Pohnpei in the Federated States of Micronesia. Often called the "Venice of the Pacific", it was the ceremonial and dynastic capital of the Saudeleur dynasty until about 1628.

Description

The site comprises some 92 artificial islands of stone and coral fill spread over about 75 hectares, linked by canals. Their walls are built of stacked prismatic basalt "logs" — naturally columnar stones quarried elsewhere on Pohnpei and transported by raft — some weighing several tonnes. The largest structure, the funerary islet of Nandauwas, has walls rising 7.6 metres high. Mortuary, residential, ritual and administrative complexes are separated by function across the canal network.

History

Construction probably began around the twelfth or thirteenth century CE, as the Saudeleur rulers centralised power over Pohnpei. Oral tradition holds that the city was founded by the twin sorcerers Olisihpa and Olosohpa, who levitated the great stones into place. The Saudeleur regime was overthrown by the hero Isokelekel, after which Nan Madol gradually lost importance and was abandoned. The site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2016, simultaneously placed on the list of sites in danger.
