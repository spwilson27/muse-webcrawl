General Certificate of Education

The General Certificate of Education (GCE) is a subject-specific family of academic qualifications introduced in the United Kingdom in 1951. It originally comprised the Ordinary Level (O-Level), taken at about age 16, and the Advanced Level (A-Level), taken at about age 18. An intermediate Advanced Supplementary (AS) level was added later.

History

The GCE replaced the School Certificate and Higher School Certificate in 1951, allowing students to take individual subjects rather than a grouped set of papers. O-Levels were graded from A to E with a pass threshold; A-Levels used a similar scale and became the standard entrance qualification for British universities.

Reform

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, O-Levels were replaced by the General Certificate of Secondary Education (GCSE) from 1988. A-Levels continue as GCE qualifications, administered by examination boards such as AQA, Edexcel, OCR and WJEC. International versions of the GCE, including International A-Levels and O-Levels, are still widely offered by Cambridge Assessment International Education and Edexcel in schools around the world.

Grading

Modern A-Levels are graded A* to E, with the A* grade introduced in 2010 to distinguish the highest achievers. AS-Levels, once worth half an A-Level, were decoupled from the full qualification in England under reforms phased in from 2015.
