Electrical resistance and conductance

The electrical resistance of an object is a measure of its opposition to the flow of electric current. Its reciprocal quantity is electrical conductance, measuring the ease with which an electric current passes. Electrical resistance shares some conceptual parallels with mechanical friction.

SI unit (resistance)Ohm (Ω)
SI unit (conductance)Siemens (S)
SymbolR (resistance), G (conductance)

Ohm's law

For many materials, the current through the object is proportional to the voltage across it: V = IR, where R is the resistance. Materials obeying this relation are called ohmic.

Factors

Resistance depends on the material's resistivity, the object's length (longer means more resistance), and its cross-sectional area (wider means less resistance). Resistance generally increases with temperature in metals.

AC circuits

In alternating-current circuits the opposition to current is generalised as impedance, which includes the effects of capacitance and inductance as well as resistance.
