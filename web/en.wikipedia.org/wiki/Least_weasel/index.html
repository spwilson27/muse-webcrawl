Scientific name: Mustela nivalis
Family: Mustelidae

The least weasel (Mustela nivalis), little weasel, common weasel, or simply the weasel, is the smallest member of the genus Mustela, the family Mustelidae, and the order Carnivora; it is in fact the smallest member of that order in the world. It is native to Eurasia, North America, and North Africa, and has been introduced to New Zealand, Malta, Crete, Bermuda, Madeira, the Azores, and elsewhere.

Description

Least weasels vary greatly in size, with body lengths of 11 to 26 cm and weights from about 25 g in the smallest females to 250 g. The body is long and slender with short legs, adapted to pursuing rodents into their burrows. The fur is brown above and white below; populations in cold regions turn wholly white in winter, a coat once trapped as "ermine" in a broader sense (though true ermine is the stoat). Unlike the stoat, the least weasel's tail lacks a black tip.

Diet and behaviour

The least weasel feeds mainly on small rodents, especially voles and mice, and will also take small birds, eggs, and rabbits much larger than itself. Because of its high surface-area-to-volume ratio and fast metabolism, it must eat roughly a third of its body weight each day. It is active by day and night, does not hibernate, and uses the burrows and nests of its prey as dens. Litters of 4–6 kits are born in spring, and females may breed twice a year when prey is abundant.

Distribution

The species has a circumboreal distribution across Europe, Asia, and northern North America, inhabiting grassland, farmland, woodland edge, and tundra. It is classified as Least Concern by the IUCN, though in New Zealand it is an introduced pest threatening native birds.
