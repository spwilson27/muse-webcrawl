Nintendo 64

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Nintendo 64 console and controller

The Nintendo 64 (abbreviated N64) is a home video game console developed by Nintendo. It was first released in Japan on 23 June 1996, in North America on 29 September 1996, and in Europe on 1 March 1997. Named for its 64-bit central processing unit, it was the last major home console to use ROM cartridges as its primary storage medium, at a time when competitors such as Sony's PlayStation had moved to optical discs.

Hardware

The system paired a 93.75 MHz NEC VR4300 CPU with a Reality Coprocessor graphics chip designed with Silicon Graphics, and shipped with 4 MB of RDRAM, expandable to 8 MB with the Expansion Pak. Its distinctive three-pronged controller introduced a central analog control stick and the trigger-style Z button, and could accept accessories such as the Rumble Pak and Controller Pak memory cards. The console supported up to four controllers out of the box, encouraging local multiplayer.

Software

Launch titles Super Mario 64 and Pilotwings 64 demonstrated the system's 3D capabilities; Super Mario 64 in particular set the standard for 3D platforming and character control. Other landmark releases include The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (1998), widely cited among the greatest video games ever made, GoldenEye 007 (1997), Mario Kart 64, Super Smash Bros., and Star Fox 64. In total, 393 games were released for the platform.

Reception and legacy

The Nintendo 64 sold approximately 32.93 million units worldwide, trailing the PlayStation but praised for its hardware power, its analog-stick controller design, and a library of critically acclaimed first-party games. Nintendo's decision to retain cartridges increased manufacturing costs and limited storage, contributing to the loss of key third-party support, most notably from Square. The console was succeeded by the GameCube in 2001.
