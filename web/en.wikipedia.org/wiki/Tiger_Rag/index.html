"Tiger Rag" is a jazz standard first recorded by the Original Dixieland Jass Band in 1917 and published in 1918. Famously featuring brass breaks that imitate a tiger's roar and the shouted phrase "Hold that tiger!", the tune became one of the most recorded songs of the early jazz era and a staple of Dixieland and traditional jazz bands worldwide.

History

The Original Dixieland Jass Band (later "Jazz Band") recorded "Tiger Rag" for Aeolian-Vocalion in 1917 and again for Victor in March 1918. The Victor recording, coupled with "Skeleton Jangle", became a hit. Authorship was contentious: the band members registered the tune, but New Orleans musicians claimed elements of it circulated earlier, and Jelly Roll Morton claimed credit for turning an old quadrille into the piece. Like many early jazz standards, it likely crystallised from shared musical materials in New Orleans.

Legacy

"Tiger Rag" was recorded hundreds of times, including a celebrated 1930s version by Louis Armstrong and big-band arrangements by Duke Ellington and Art Tatum's virtuoso piano performance. It became associated with Clemson University and Louisiana State University ("LSU Tigers") as a fight song, and the phrase "Hold that tiger" entered popular culture. The 1918 ODJB recording was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and selected for the US National Recording Registry.
