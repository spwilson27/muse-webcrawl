South Aegean volcanic arc

The South Aegean volcanic arc is a volcanic arc in the South Aegean Sea formed by plate tectonics. It is the result of subduction of the African Plate beneath the Aegean Sea Plate along the Hellenic Trench south of Crete. The arc stretches about 450 km from the Gulf of Corinth in the west to the Turkish coast near Bodrum in the east.

Volcanoes

The arc's volcanic centres, west to east, include Sousaki, Methana, Milos, Santorini, the submarine Kolumbo volcano, Nisyros, Yali, and Kos. Of these, Santorini and Nisyros are considered the most active, and Kolumbo erupted in 1650. The volcanic field has produced andesitic to rhyolitic magmas for roughly the last 2–3 million years, with some centres dating back further.

Minoan eruption

The most famous event of the arc is the Minoan eruption of Santorini (Thera), around 1600 BC, one of the largest volcanic eruptions in recorded history. It devastated the Minoan settlement of Akrotiri, buried under ash, and generated tsunamis that struck Crete. Santorini's present caldera was largely formed by this eruption. The most recent eruption of Santorini occurred in 1950 at Nea Kameni, the islet in the centre of the caldera.

Monitoring

The volcanoes of the arc are monitored by Greek scientific institutes because eruption and tsunami hazards affect densely populated tourist islands.
