Stuart Ernest Piggott (28 May 1910 – 23 September 1996) was a British archaeologist, best known for his work on prehistoric Wessex. He held the Abercromby Chair of Archaeology at the University of Edinburgh from 1946 to 1977 and was a leading figure of the culture-historical school in British archaeology.

Career

Piggott began his career at the Royal Commission on the Ancient and Historical Monuments of Wales and excavated at sites including Avebury under Alexander Keiller. He coined the term "Wessex culture" for the rich Early Bronze Age burials of southern England. During the Second World War he served in India, where he wrote on Indian prehistory and excavated in the region. His books include The Neolithic Cultures of the British Isles (1954), Ancient Europe (1965) and The Druids (1968), a sceptical study of Druidic lore. He was sceptical of radiocarbon dating's early impact and of invasion-free explanations of cultural change, positions later overtaken by the discipline's development.
