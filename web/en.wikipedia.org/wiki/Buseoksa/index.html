Buseoksa, a mountain temple founded in the Silla kingdom.

Buseoksa (Korean: 부석사, "Floating Rock Temple") is a Buddhist temple in Yeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea. It was founded in 676 by the monk Uisang, a leading figure of the Hwaeom school, during the reign of King Munmu of Silla. The temple takes its name from a legend that a rock floated or hovered above another rock at the site.

Architecture and treasures

The temple's main hall, Muryangsujeon ("Hall of Infinite Life"), is among the oldest surviving wooden buildings in Korea, dating in its present form to the Goryeo period (rebuilt in 1376). Built with an elegant entasis-profiled column design, it enshrines a clay statue of Amitabha Buddha. Other treasures include the stone lanterns and the Jodang hermitage buildings arranged on long terraces climbing the mountainside below Mount Bonghwang.

Heritage status

In 2018 Buseoksa was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as one of the seven "Sansa, Buddhist Mountain Monasteries in Korea", recognised for its continuous tradition of monastic practice since the seventh century.
