The Sérsic profile (often written Sersic profile in plain ASCII) is a mathematical function that describes how the surface brightness of a galaxy varies with distance from its centre. It was introduced by the Argentine astronomer José Luis Sérsic in the 1960s and generalises earlier empirical laws for galaxy brightness.

Definition

The profile takes the form I(R) = Ie exp{ −bn [ (R/Re)1/n − 1 ] }, where Re is the effective radius enclosing half the total light, Ie the surface brightness at that radius, and n the Sérsic index, which controls the concentration of the profile; bn is a constant chosen so that Re indeed encloses half the light.

Special cases and uses

For n = 4 the profile reduces to the de Vaucouleurs law characteristic of elliptical galaxies, while n = 1 gives the exponential profile of galaxy discs; dwarf galaxies and galaxy bulges typically take intermediate or lower values. Fitting Sérsic profiles — often with separate bulge and disc components — is a standard tool in studies of galaxy structure and evolution.
