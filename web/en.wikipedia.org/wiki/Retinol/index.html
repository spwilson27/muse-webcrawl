Retinol

Retinol, also called vitamin A1, is a fat-soluble vitamin in the vitamin A family found in food and used as a dietary supplement. It is an alcohol form of the retinoids, interconvertible in the body with retinal and retinoic acid. Retinol is essential for vision (as a precursor of the photopigment chromophore 11-cis-retinal), embryonic development, growth, immune function and the maintenance of epithelial tissues.

Sources and metabolism

Preformed vitamin A (retinol and retinyl esters) is obtained from animal foods such as liver, dairy products and eggs, while provitamin A carotenoids such as beta-carotene come from orange and dark green vegetables. Retinol is stored mainly in the liver as retinyl esters and transported in the blood bound to retinol-binding protein. It is oxidised to retinal for the visual cycle and to retinoic acid, a hormone-like molecule that regulates gene expression through nuclear retinoic acid receptors.

Medical and cosmetic uses

Vitamin A deficiency remains a major cause of preventable childhood blindness and increased mortality from infections in low-income countries, and supplementation programmes are a standard public-health intervention. Retinol and other retinoids are widely used in skincare for photoaging and acne. Excess intake is toxic (hypervitaminosis A), and systemic retinoids are teratogenic.
