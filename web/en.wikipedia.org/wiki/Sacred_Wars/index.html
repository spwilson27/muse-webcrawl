Sacred Wars

There were four Sacred Wars in ancient Greek history, all of which were fought over control of the sanctuary of Apollo at Delphi in Phocis, and the Amphictyonic League that administered it. The wars were so named because they were declared by the Amphictyonic council against states accused of sacrilege (hierosylia) against the sanctuary.


The sanctuary at Delphi, focus of the Sacred Wars

First Sacred War (595–585 BC)

The First Sacred War was fought between the Amphictyonic League and the city of Kirrha, the port of Delphi, which was accused of charging tolls on pilgrims and of other impious acts. The war ended with the destruction of Kirrha and the dedication of its territory to Apollo, Pythian Apollo's games — the Pythian Games — being refounded in the aftermath.

Second Sacred War (449–448 BC)

The Second Sacred War was a brief conflict during the so-called First Peloponnesian War period, fought over which state should control Delphi: the Phocians, backed by Athens, or the Delphians, backed by Sparta. Sparta restored Delphi to its own inhabitants; Athens then restored the Phocians, and the settlement was reversed again after the Athenian defeat at Coronea.

Third Sacred War (356–346 BC)

The Third Sacred War, the longest and most consequential, began when the Phocians seized Delphi and plundered the sanctuary's treasures to pay mercenaries. The Amphictyonic League declared war, and the conflict drew in Thebes, Thessaly, Athens and Sparta. It ended with the intervention of Philip II of Macedon, who crushed the Phocians in 346 BC; Phocis was expelled from the League and its two votes were transferred to Macedon, a key step in Philip's rise to dominance over Greece.

Fourth Sacred War (339–338 BC)

The Fourth Sacred War was declared against Amphissa, accused of cultivating sacred land. Philip II was again invited to intervene; his southward advance led instead to confrontation with Athens and Thebes, culminating in the Battle of Chaeronea (338 BC), which established Macedonian hegemony over the Greek city-states.
