Kumite (Japanese: 組手, literally "grappling hands" or "meeting of hands") is one of the three main sections of karate training, along with kata and kihon. Kumite is the part of karate in which a person trains against an adversary, using the techniques learned from the kihon and kata.

Types of kumite

Kumite can be used to develop a particular technique or a skill (for example, effectively judging and adjusting the distance from one's opponent), or it can be done in competition. Training forms range along a spectrum of pre-arrangement and contact: kihon kumite uses basic, pre-arranged attacks and defences; ippon kumite is one-step sparring; jiyu ippon kumite is semi-free one-step sparring; and jiyu kumite is free sparring. Styles differ greatly in contact: traditional Okinawan schools often practise controlled, light contact, while Kyokushin karate is famous for full-contact kumite without gloves, and bogu kumite uses protective armour.

Sport kumite

Under World Karate Federation rules, kumite is a scored, semi-contact contest on an 8 × 8 m mat. Techniques to the body and head earn points (yuko 1, waza-ari 2, ippon 3, with kicks to the head and throws followed by a scoring technique rated highest); excessive contact is penalised. Bouts last three minutes for senior men and two for women in individual competition. Sport kumite featured at the Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games, where karate made its Olympic debut.

Concepts

Effective kumite depends on maai (engagement distance), timing, control of the centre line, and zanshin (lingering awareness after a technique). Practitioners develop combinations of punches (such as kizami-zuki and gyaku-zuki) and kicks, feints, and sweeps, applied only when a real opening exists — the principle often summarised as "no first attack in karate" (karate ni sente nashi).
