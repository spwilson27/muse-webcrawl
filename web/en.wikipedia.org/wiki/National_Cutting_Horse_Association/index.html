National Cutting Horse Association

The National Cutting Horse Association (NCHA) is the governing body for the sport of cutting in the United States. Founded in 1946 by a group of Texas cattlemen and ranchers — its first president was Ray Smyth — the association was established to standardise the rules of cutting competition, promote the cutting horse, and preserve the ranching skills from which the sport developed. It is headquartered in Fort Worth, Texas.

The sport

In cutting, horse and rider enter a herd of cattle, separate ("cut") a single animal from the group, and then prevent it from returning to the herd for a judged two-and-a-half-minute run. Once the cut is made, the rider drops the reins and the horse works on its own, mirroring the animal's movements through quick, low, athletic turns. Judges score the run, typically between 60 and 80 points, on the horse's control of the cow, its degree of difficulty, and its style and courage.

Events and organisation

The NCHA's flagship event is the NCHA Futurity, held annually in Fort Worth since 1962, for three-year-old horses competing in their first season; it is one of the richest events in western horse sports. Other major competitions include the NCHA Super Stakes for four-year-olds, the Summer Spectacular, and a calendar of weekend and regional shows sanctioned through local affiliates. The association maintains the breed and earnings records of cutting horses — overwhelmingly American Quarter Horses — sanctions judges, and publishes the Cutting Horse Chatter. An NCHA Hall of Fame honours outstanding horses, riders, and members.
