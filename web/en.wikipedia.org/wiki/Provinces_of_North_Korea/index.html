Provinces of North Korea

The provinces of North Korea are the first-level administrative divisions of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea. North Korea is divided into nine provinces, together with several directly governed cities and special administrative regions. The provincial system derives from the traditional eight provinces of Korea, reorganised several times since the division of Korea in 1945.

Special cities

In addition to the provinces, Pyongyang is a directly governed city (chikhalsi), while Rason is a special city, and Nampo and Kaesong have held special-city status at various times. Provinces are subdivided into cities (si), counties (gun) and districts.

History

Several provinces were created or reorganised in the early 1950s under Kim Il-sung: Chagang was split from North Pyongan in 1949 and Ryanggang was created in 1954 from parts of South Hamgyong and Chagang. Kaesong and its surrounding region have changed status repeatedly, reflecting the politics of the Korean Demilitarized Zone.
