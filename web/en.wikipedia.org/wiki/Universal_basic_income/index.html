Universal basic income

Universal basic income (UBI) is a social welfare proposal in which all citizens of a given population regularly receive a minimum income in the form of an unconditional transfer payment, without a means test or work requirement.

History and proponents

Precursors include Thomas Paine's Agrarian Justice (1797) and "social dividend" ideas; Milton Friedman proposed a related negative income tax (1962), Martin Luther King Jr. endorsed a guaranteed income, and US President Richard Nixon's Family Assistance Plan nearly passed in 1969–70. Modern advocates span libertarians seeking to replace welfare bureaucracy, left campaigners addressing poverty and precarious work, and technologists fearing automation-driven job loss — notably Andrew Yang's 2020 US presidential "Freedom Dividend".

Evidence and debate

Experiments and partial schemes include Alaska's Permanent Fund Dividend (since 1982), Iran's cash subsidies, Manitoba's Mincome, Finland's 2017–18 trial, and Kenya's GiveDirectly studies; results generally show improved wellbeing and little reduction in work, though trials are short and partial. Critics cite fiscal cost, inflation and work incentives; supporters cite poverty reduction, simplicity and freedom. See social class for the stratification UBI seeks to soften.
