Echidna (mythology)

In Greek mythology, Echidna (Greek: Ἔχιδνα, 'She-Viper') was a monster, half-woman and half-snake, who lived alone in a cave. She was the mate of the fearsome monster Typhon and was the mother of many of the most famous monsters of Greek myth.

Mythology

Hesiod's Theogony describes Echidna as immortal and ageless, dwelling in Arima. With Typhon she bore Orthrus, Cerberus, the Lernaean Hydra, the Chimera, the Sphinx and the Nemean lion; by Orthrus she was grandmother of other monsters.

Death

According to some accounts Echidna was killed by Argus Panoptes, the hundred-eyed giant, who slew her while she slept, as told by Apollodorus. Her name was later given to the egg-laying monotreme mammals of Australia and New Guinea for their mix of traits.
