Kirkus Reviews is an American book review magazine founded in 1933 by Virginia Kirkus, who had run the children's book department at Harper & Bros. It reviews books — traditionally in advance of publication, from galleys — for booksellers, librarians, and publishers, and is known for its concise, unsigned, and sometimes famously severe notices.

Operations

The magazine is published semimonthly and reviews several thousand titles a year across fiction, nonfiction, and children's and young-adult categories. Exceptional books receive a starred review. Since 2005, Kirkus has also run a paid review service for self-published and indie authors (Kirkus Indie), and in 2014 it launched the Kirkus Prize, awarding $50,000 each in fiction, nonfiction, and young readers' literature. The company was sold by Nielsen Business Media to Herb Simon in 2010, after a brief announced closure in 2009 was reversed.
