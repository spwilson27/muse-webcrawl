Piñata

A piñata (Spanish: [piˈɲata]) is a decorated container, often made of papier-mâché, pottery, or cloth, filled with candy and toys, that is broken as part of a celebration. Piñatas are commonly associated with Mexico and with birthday parties and festivals in Latin America and the United States.

History

Similar traditions of breaking filled vessels existed in China, where Marco Polo described figures filled with seeds, and in pre-Columbian Mesoamerica, where the Aztecs honoured the god Huitzilopochtli with clay pots broken with a stick. Spanish missionaries adapted the custom during the colonial period, giving it Christian symbolism during Lent and later Christmas posadas.

Custom

The piñata is suspended from a rope and blindfolded participants, usually children, take turns striking it with a stick while others sing a traditional song (Dale, dale, dale). When it breaks, the contents spill out and are gathered by the participants. Traditional Mexican piñatas for Christmas have seven points representing the seven deadly sins.
