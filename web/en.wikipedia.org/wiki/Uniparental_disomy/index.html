Uniparental disomy
TypesHeterodisomy, isodisomy
MechanismTrisomy rescue, monosomy rescue, gamete complementation

In uniparental disomy both homologues of a chromosome pair come from a single parent.

Uniparental disomy (UPD) occurs when a person receives two copies of a chromosome, or part of a chromosome, from one parent and no copy from the other parent. The chromosome number is normal, but the parental origin is not. UPD is usually the result of trisomy rescue, in which an embryo that began with three copies of a chromosome loses one; if the two remaining copies happen to come from the same parent, UPD results. Rarer mechanisms include monosomy rescue and post-fertilisation errors.

Types

In heterodisomy, the individual inherits the two different homologues of one parent (a meiosis I error). In isodisomy, a single parental chromosome is duplicated (a meiosis II error or monosomy rescue), which can render the offspring homozygous for a recessive mutation carried by that parent.

Clinical significance

UPD is harmless for most chromosomes, but for chromosomes containing imprinted genes — genes expressed only from the maternal or paternal copy — it causes imprinting disorders. Maternal UPD of chromosome 15 causes Prader–Willi syndrome, while paternal UPD 15 causes Angelman syndrome; paternal UPD 11 is associated with Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome, and maternal UPD 7 with a subset of Silver–Russell syndrome. Isodisomy can also unmask autosomal recessive diseases such as cystic fibrosis when only one parent is a carrier. Confirmed cases for most human chromosomes have been reported since the phenomenon was first described by Eric Engel in 1980.
