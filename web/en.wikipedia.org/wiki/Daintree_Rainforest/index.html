Daintree Rainforest

The Daintree Rainforest is a tropical rainforest region in Far North Queensland, Australia, north of Mossman and Cairns. Covering roughly 1,200 square kilometres, it is part of the Wet Tropics of Queensland World Heritage Area and is considered one of the oldest continuously surviving rainforests on Earth, with origins stretching back more than 100 million years.

Ecology

The Daintree contains an extraordinary proportion of Australia's plant and animal species, including ancient flowering plant families, primitive marsupials such as the musky rat-kangaroo, the endangered southern cassowary, and the Bennett's tree-kangaroo. The forest is bisected by the Daintree River, crossed by cable ferry, and extends from the ranges down to the Coral Sea at Cape Tribulation, where the rainforest meets the Great Barrier Reef.

History and conservation

The area is the traditional land of the Eastern Kuku Yalanji people, to whom formal handback of the Daintree National Park occurred in 2021. Logging protests in the 1980s helped secure World Heritage listing in 1988. Tourism is now the region's main industry, with boardwalks, river cruises and guided walks centred on the Daintree village and Cape Tribulation.
