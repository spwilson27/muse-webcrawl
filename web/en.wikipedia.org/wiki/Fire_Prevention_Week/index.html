Fire Prevention Week is observed each year in the United States and Canada during the week in which October 9 falls, in commemoration of the Great Chicago Fire, which began on October 8, 1871, and killed more than 250 people, left 100,000 homeless and destroyed more than 17,400 structures. The week is sponsored by the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) and is the longest-running public health and safety observance in the United States.

History

On the fortieth anniversary of the Chicago fire in 1911, the Fire Marshals Association of North America (now the International Fire Marshals Association) sponsored the first National Fire Prevention Day. In 1920, President Woodrow Wilson issued the first National Fire Prevention Day proclamation, and since 1922 Fire Prevention Week has been observed on the Sunday-through-Saturday period in which October 9 falls. Every U.S. president since Calvin Coolidge has signed a proclamation for the week.

Activities and themes

During the week, firefighters and fire departments visit schools and community groups to teach children and adults about fire safety, the importance of working smoke alarms, home escape planning, and safe cooking and heating practices. Each year the NFPA selects a campaign theme — recent themes have emphasised smoke alarm maintenance, lithium-ion battery safety, and cooking safety — and provides educational materials to fire departments across North America. Sparky the Fire Dog, the NFPA's mascot since 1951, features prominently in children's materials.
