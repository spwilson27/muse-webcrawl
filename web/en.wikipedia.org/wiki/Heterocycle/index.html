Heterocycle

Heterocycle
TypeCyclic organic compound
Defining featureRing containing at least two different elements
Common heteroatomsNitrogen, oxygen, sulfur
ExamplesPyridine, furan, thiophene, imidazole

A heterocyclic compound or ring structure is a cyclic compound that has atoms of at least two different elements as members of its ring(s). Heterocyclic organic chemistry is the branch of organic chemistry dealing with the synthesis, properties, and applications of these rings, which constitute the largest and most varied family of organic compounds.

Classification

Heterocycles are classified by ring size (three- to ten-membered and beyond), by the identity and number of heteroatoms — most commonly nitrogen, oxygen and sulfur — and by saturation. Three-membered rings include epoxides and aziridines; five-membered aromatic rings include pyrrole, furan and thiophene; six-membered aromatic rings include pyridine and pyrimidine.

Importance

Heterocyclic rings form the backbones of many biologically vital molecules: the nucleobases of DNA and RNA, the amino acids histidine, proline and tryptophan, the porphyrin ring of haem and chlorophyll, and most pharmaceuticals. Hantzsch–Widman nomenclature provides the systematic names for heterocyclic systems.
