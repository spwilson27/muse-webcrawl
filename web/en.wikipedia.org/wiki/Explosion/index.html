Explosion

A controlled demolition explosion with an expanding fireball.

An explosion is a rapid expansion in volume associated with an extreme outward release of energy, usually with the generation of high temperatures and release of high-pressure gases. Supersonic explosions created by high explosives are known as detonations and travel through shock waves. Subsonic explosions are created by low explosives through a slower combustion process known as deflagration.

Causes

Explosions can be chemical (the rapid oxidation of a fuel or explosive compound), mechanical (the sudden failure of a pressure vessel, as in a boiling liquid expanding vapour explosion or BLEVE), electrical, or nuclear (the sudden release of energy from fission or fusion reactions). Natural explosions include volcanic eruptions, lightning, and asteroid impacts.

Properties

The destructive effects of an explosion come principally from the blast wave — a shell of compressed air that expands outward faster than the speed of sound in the case of detonation — together with fragmentation, thermal radiation, and ground shock. The amount of damage depends on the quantity of energy released, commonly expressed as a TNT equivalent.

Notable explosions

  • The Halifax Explosion (1917), one of the largest accidental conventional explosions.
  • The Beirut explosion (2020), caused by stored ammonium nitrate.
  • The Tunguska event (1908), an air burst of a cosmic body over Siberia.
  • The largest nuclear explosion, the Tsar Bomba test (1961), at about 50 megatons.
