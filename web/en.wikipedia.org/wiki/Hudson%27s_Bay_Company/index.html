Hudson's Bay Company

Founded: 2 May 1670
Founders: Pierre-Esprit Radisson, Médard Chouart des Groseilliers
Industry: Fur trade, retail

The Hudson's Bay Company (HBC) is a Canadian retail and fur-trading company incorporated by English royal charter in 1670, making it one of the oldest continuously operating commercial corporations in the world. It was granted a monopoly over trade in the Hudson Bay drainage basin, known as Rupert's Land.

History

From its factors and trading posts, HBC effectively governed large parts of what is now Canada. In 1870 it surrendered Rupert's Land to the Dominion of Canada, and over time it transformed into a department-store retailer. Its iconic point blankets and multi-stripe pattern became enduring Canadian symbols.

Modern era

In the 21st century HBC operated Hudson's Bay department stores in Canada and, for periods, Saks Fifth Avenue and Lord & Taylor in the United States. The company's retail operations wound down amid financial difficulties in the mid-2020s.
