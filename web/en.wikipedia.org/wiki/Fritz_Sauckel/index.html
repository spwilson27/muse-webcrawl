Fritz Sauckel

Ernst Friedrich Christoph "Fritz" Sauckel (27 October 1894 – 16 October 1946) was a German Nazi politician and convicted war criminal. As General Plenipotentiary for Labour Deployment from 1942, he directed the forced-labour programme that deported millions of workers from occupied Europe to work in German industry and agriculture. He was sentenced to death at the Nuremberg trials and hanged.

Fritz Sauckel
Born: 27 October 1894, Haßfurt, German Empire
Died: 16 October 1946, Nuremberg (executed)
Office: General Plenipotentiary for Labour Deployment, 1942–1945

Career

Sauckel, a former merchant seaman, joined the Nazi Party in 1923 and rose through the party organization in Thuringia, becoming Gauleiter there in 1927 and head of the Thuringian state government in 1932.

Forced labour programme

Appointed by Hitler in March 1942 to mobilize foreign labour, Sauckel organized the recruitment—and increasingly the coerced deportation—of workers, especially from Eastern Europe and the Soviet Union. Under his programme, roughly five million foreign workers were brought to the Reich, many under brutal conditions; his own directives demanded maximum output with minimum sustenance. At Nuremberg the prosecution used his words against him: he claimed he had not known of the extermination programme and that conditions were beyond his control, but the tribunal found him guilty of war crimes and crimes against humanity.
