Alfred Young

Alfred Young, FRS (16 April 1873 – 15 December 1940) was a British mathematician best known for Young diagrams and Young tableaux, central tools in the representation theory of the symmetric group.

Born16 April 1873, Widnes, Lancashire
Died15 December 1940, Birdbrook, Essex
FieldsMathematics — group theory, invariant theory
InstitutionsSt John's College, Cambridge
Known forYoung diagram, Young tableau, Young symmetriser

Life

Young studied at St John's College, Cambridge, was elected a fellow, and later ordained, spending much of his life as a parish priest in Birdbrook while continuing mathematical research largely outside the academic mainstream.

Work

In a series of papers on quantitative substitutional analysis beginning in 1900, Young introduced the diagrams and tableaux now named for him. A Young diagram (or Ferrers diagram) depicts an integer partition; a Young tableau fills its boxes with numbers. Standard tableaux count irreducible representations of symmetric groups and underpin the hook-length formula and much of modern representation theory. His ideas were taken up and extended by Frobenius, Schur and later physicists applying symmetry to quantum mechanics.

Honours

He was elected Fellow of the Royal Society in 1934 and received honorary recognition late in life as the importance of his combinatorics became clear.
