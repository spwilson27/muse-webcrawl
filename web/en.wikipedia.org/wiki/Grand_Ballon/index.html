Grand Ballon

The Grand Ballon summit and its radar station

Elevation1,424 m (4,672 ft)
LocationHaut-Rhin, Grand Est, France
RangeVosges Mountains

The Grand Ballon (German: Großer Belchen) is the highest summit of the Vosges Mountains in northeastern France, rising to 1,424 metres (4,672 feet) above sea level. It lies in the department of Haut-Rhin, in the Grand Est region, about 25 kilometres northwest of Mulhouse.

Features

The summit carries a distinctive white radar dome—an air-traffic-control radar station—that is visible from across the Alsace plain. Nearby stand a monument to the Diables Bleus (Alpine troops) of the First World War and the ruins of a hotel destroyed in that conflict. The mountain is crossed by the Route des Crêtes, the scenic ridge road built during the First World War.

Sport and recreation

The Grand Ballon is a noted climb in the Tour de France, first included in 1969, and a popular destination for hikers, mountain bikers and paragliders. On a clear day the panorama extends across the Rhine plain to the Black Forest and the Swiss Alps, including the Bernese Oberland.
