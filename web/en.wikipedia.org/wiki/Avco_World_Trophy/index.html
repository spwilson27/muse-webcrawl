The Avco World Trophy, awarded to the WHA's playoff champion.

The Avco World Trophy, originally known as the Avco Cup, was the championship trophy of the World Hockey Association (WHA), a major professional ice hockey league that operated in North America from 1972 to 1979. The trophy was awarded annually to the winner of the WHA playoffs, making it the league's equivalent of the National Hockey League's Stanley Cup.

History

The trophy was donated by the Avco Financial Services Corporation, then the name under which the financial arm of the Avco Corporation operated, as part of a sponsorship arrangement with the new league. The first Avco Cup championship was contested in 1973, when the New England Whalers defeated the Winnipeg Jets in the finals to become the WHA's first champions.

The trophy itself consisted of a large globe mounted on a base, reflecting the "World" name of both the league and the award. Unlike the Stanley Cup, whose winning players' names are engraved on its bands, the Avco World Trophy was a comparatively modest award, and a new presentation accompanied each championship season.

When the WHA folded in 1979 and four of its teams — the Edmonton Oilers, Hartford Whalers (formerly New England), Quebec Nordiques and Winnipeg Jets — joined the NHL, the Avco World Trophy was retired. The original trophy is now in the collection of the Hockey Hall of Fame in Toronto.

Champions

  • 1973 – New England Whalers (defeated Winnipeg Jets)
  • 1974 – Houston Aeros (defeated Chicago Cougars)
  • 1975 – Houston Aeros (defeated Quebec Nordiques)
  • 1976 – Winnipeg Jets (defeated Houston Aeros)
  • 1977 – Quebec Nordiques (defeated Winnipeg Jets)
  • 1978 – Winnipeg Jets (defeated New England Whalers)
  • 1979 – Winnipeg Jets (defeated Edmonton Oilers)

The Winnipeg Jets were the trophy's most successful team, winning three championships including the final one in 1979. The Houston Aeros, led by Gordie Howe, won back-to-back titles in 1974 and 1975. The 1979 final, between Winnipeg and an Edmonton Oilers team featuring a young Wayne Gretzky, was the last championship series in WHA history.
