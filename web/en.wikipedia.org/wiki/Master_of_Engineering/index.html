Master of Engineering

A Master of Engineering (abbreviated MEng, ME, or M.E., depending on the country and institution) is a professional master's degree in engineering. It is offered by universities in many countries, including the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Germany, France (as the diplôme d'ingénieur at master's level), Japan, and Australia.

Structure

MEng programmes are typically practice-oriented and often consist mainly of coursework, sometimes with a capstone project or practicum rather than a research thesis. In the United States, the degree usually requires one to two years of study beyond a bachelor's degree. This distinguishes it from the Master of Science in Engineering (MS), which more often includes a research thesis, although usage varies between institutions.

Integrated degrees

In the United Kingdom, the MEng is commonly an integrated four-year undergraduate master's degree (five years in Scotland) in engineering, accredited for the educational requirements of Chartered Engineer (CEng) status. British engineering bachelor's graduates (BEng) generally need further learning to meet the same standard. Similar integrated models exist in other Bologna Process countries.
