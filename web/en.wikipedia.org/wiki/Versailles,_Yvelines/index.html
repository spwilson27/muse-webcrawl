Versailles, Yvelines

Versailles is a commune in the department of Yvelines, Île-de-France, and the prefecture of the department. Located in the western suburbs of Paris, 17.1 km (10.6 mi) from the centre of Paris, it is worldwide known for the Palace of Versailles and its gardens, a UNESCO World Heritage site and the seat of the French court and government under Louis XIV and his successors.

History

A small village until Louis XIII built a hunting lodge in 1623–24, Versailles was transformed by Louis XIV, who moved the court there in 1682. It remained the capital in all but name until the royal family was forced back to Paris in October 1789 during the Revolution. The Treaty of Versailles ending the First World War was signed in its Hall of Mirrors in 1919.

Geography and city

The planned town, with broad avenues radiating from the palace, retains aristocratic and bourgeois quarters such as Notre-Dame and Saint-Louis, the latter with the cathedral and the Potager du Roi. Today Versailles is an affluent residential and administrative city, home to courts, a university presence, and tourism centred on the palace.

Transport

Several rail lines link Versailles to Paris, notably RER C to Versailles Château Rive Gauche and Transilien services to Rive Droite and Chantiers stations.
