Neprilysin

Neprilysin, also known as membrane metallo-endopeptidase (MME), neutral endopeptidase (NEP), cluster of differentiation 10 (CD10) and common acute lymphoblastic leukemia antigen (CALLA), is an enzyme that in humans is encoded by the MME gene. Neprilysin is a zinc-dependent metalloprotease that cleaves peptides at the amino side of hydrophobic residues and inactivates several peptide hormones.

Function

Neprilysin degrades a variety of signalling peptides, including natriuretic peptides, bradykinin, adrenomedullin, enkephalins and the beta-amyloid peptide involved in Alzheimer's disease. It is widely expressed, notably in the kidney brush border, lung, brain and lymphoid tissue.

Clinical significance

Inhibiting neprilysin raises levels of beneficial natriuretic peptides. The neprilysin inhibitor sacubitril, combined with the angiotensin receptor blocker valsartan, is used in the treatment of heart failure. CD10 expression is also used as a diagnostic marker in several lymphomas and leukemias.
