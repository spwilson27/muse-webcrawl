The Monastery of Saint Anthony of Qozhaya is a Maronite monastery in the Zgharta District of northern Lebanon, built into the cliffs of the Qadisha Valley (the Holy Valley). Dedicated to Saint Anthony the Great, it is one of the oldest and most important monasteries of the valley, historically associated with hermitages cut into the rock by early Christian ascetics, and is part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the valley.

History

Tradition places the monastery's origins in late antiquity, with hermit dwellings around the site dating to the early centuries of Christianity. The present complex grew over the medieval and Ottoman periods and became a major property of the Lebanese Maronite Order, founded in 1695.

Qozhaya is famous for housing the first printing press of the Middle East: in 1610 the monastery printed a Book of Psalms in Syriac using Syriac characters, with a press brought to the valley and type cast by the monks. Portions of the historic press are displayed in the monastery's museum.

Site

The monastery includes the church of Saint Anthony, built into the rock face, extensive cave hermitages, a library, and a museum containing manuscripts, liturgical objects, and agricultural tools. Pilgrims and visitors reach it by a steep road descending into the Qadisha gorge.

During Lebanon's conflicts, the valley's monasteries, including Qozhaya, served as refuges, continuing a role the valley played for persecuted Maronite communities throughout its history.
