Margrethe II

Margrethe II, Queen of Denmark 1972–2024

Margrethe II (born 16 April 1940) is a member of the Danish royal family who reigned as Queen of Denmark from 14 January 1972 until her abdication on 14 January 2024. She is the eldest child of King Frederik IX and Queen Ingrid and succeeded her father on his death. In 2022 she became the longest-reigning current European monarch following the death of Elizabeth II, and in 2023 the longest-serving monarch in Danish history.

Early life and succession

Born at Amalienborg Palace, Copenhagen, one week after the German invasion of Denmark, Margrethe was not originally in the line of succession, as the throne then passed only to men. A 1953 constitutional referendum changed the succession law to male-preference primogeniture, making her heir presumptive. She studied at Cambridge, the Sorbonne, Aarhus University, and the London School of Economics. In 1967 she married French diplomat Henri de Laborde de Monpezat, thereafter Prince Henrik, who died in 2018.

Reign

Margrethe's reign saw Denmark join the European Community in 1973 and develop its modern welfare state; she remained a popular, largely above-politics head of state known for her televised New Year's addresses, her intellectual interests, and her insistence on meeting citizens on regional tours. She celebrated her Golden Jubilee in 2022, the festivities curtailed by the death of Elizabeth II.

Abdication

In her New Year's Eve address on 31 December 2023, Margrethe unexpectedly announced she would abdicate on the 52nd anniversary of her accession, citing age and health. She was succeeded by her elder son, Frederik X — the first Danish monarch to abdicate in nearly 900 years, since Erik III in 1146.

Artistic work

A gifted artist under the pseudonym Ingahild Grathmer, she has illustrated editions of The Lord of the Rings, designed stage costumes and church textiles, and exhibited paintings and decoupage works.
