Otaku (Japanese: おたく, オタク) is a Japanese word describing people with consuming, obsessive interests, particularly in anime, manga, video games and related popular culture. Derived from a formal word for "your house" used as an overly polite form of address, the term entered slang in the 1980s for fans who addressed each other with stiff formality and spent their lives absorbed in hobbies at home. In Japan the word long carried a pejorative, socially withdrawn connotation, while abroad it is often used neutrally or proudly as a synonym for "geek" or "fan".

Etymology and history

The essayist Akio Nakamori is usually credited with popularising the modern usage in his 1983 column "Otaku no Kenkyū" ("An Investigation of Otaku") in the magazine Manga Burikko. Through the 1980s, the growing anime and computer hobby scenes — centred on Tokyo's Akihabara electronics district — embraced a shared culture of conventions, doujinshi (self-published works) and collecting. The massive Comic Market (Comiket), founded in 1975, became the movement's heart.

Public perception darkened in 1989 after the arrest of Tsutomu Miyazaki, a serial murderer whose room was found stacked with anime and horror videos; media coverage fused "otaku" with social menace in the public mind. From the 2000s, however, the image softened: the bestselling romance Densha Otoko ("Train Man", 2004) portrayed an otaku sympathetically, maid cafés and cosplay became tourist attractions, and the Japanese government promoted otaku culture abroad as part of its "Cool Japan" soft-power strategy.

Subculture and economy

Otaku interests span anime and manga, video games, model trains, military hardware, computers, idols, cosplay and much else; female otaku have their own centres, such as Ikebukuro's Otome Road, and their own genres. The Nomura Research Institute famously classified otaku into a dozen market segments and estimated their spending in the hundreds of billions of yen annually. Events such as Comiket draw hundreds of thousands of visitors, and Akihabara — "Akiba" — remains a global pilgrimage site of electronics shops, arcades and themed cafés.

Usage outside Japan

In English and other languages, "otaku" usually denotes a devoted fan of Japanese popular culture in particular, stripped of much of the Japanese stigma, though some long-time fans avoid it for exactly that reason. Comparable terms include "weeaboo" (derogatory) and the broader "geek" and "nerd".
