The Ottoman Cyprus period covers the years from the Ottoman conquest of the island in 1570–71 until 1878, when administration was transferred to Britain under the Cyprus Convention, although Ottoman sovereignty formally continued until 1914. The conquest, a major episode of the Fourth Ottoman–Venetian War, ended over 380 years of Lusignan and Venetian rule.

Conquest

In July 1570 an Ottoman expedition under Lala Mustafa Pasha landed near Larnaca. Nicosia fell on 9 September 1570 after a siege, with heavy loss of life; Famagusta, defended by Marcantonio Bragadin, held out until August 1571 and surrendered on terms that the Ottomans then violated — Bragadin was flayed alive. The Venetian defeat, despite the Holy League's victory at Lepanto in October 1571, left Cyprus permanently in Ottoman hands.

Administration and society

Cyprus was organised first as an eyalet and later attached to the Archipelago province and then administered under various arrangements, governed from Nicosia. The Ottomans restored the autocephalous Orthodox Archbishopric (suppressed under Latin rule), making the Archbishop ethnarch of the Greek Orthodox population within the millet system — a position of lasting political importance. Muslim settlement from Anatolia, including soldiers and craftsmen, formed the nucleus of the Turkish Cypriot community; land was granted as timars, taxes were reorganised, and Latin feudal dues were abolished, initially easing conditions for Orthodox peasants. Over the following centuries the island's population fluctuated with plague, locusts, and economic decline, and dragomans (interpreters) of the Porte gained great local power. In 1878, after the Russo-Turkish War, the Convention of Constantinople assigned Cyprus to British administration in return for a British guarantee of Ottoman possessions in Asia; Britain annexed the island outright in 1914.
