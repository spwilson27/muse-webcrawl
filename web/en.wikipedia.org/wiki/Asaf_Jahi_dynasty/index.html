The Asaf Jahi dynasty was the ruling house of Hyderabad State in south-central India from 1724 to 1948. Its rulers, who bore the title Nizam, governed the largest and wealthiest of the princely states of British India, a territory of some 82,000 square miles with a population of over 16 million at its greatest extent, centred on the city of Hyderabad in the Deccan.

Origins

The dynasty was founded by Mir Qamar-ud-din Khan, a noble of Turco-Mongol descent who served the Mughal emperors and was appointed viceroy (nizam) of the Deccan. Granted the honorific Asaf Jah — comparing his dignity to that of Asaf, the legendary minister of Solomon — he defeated a rival claimant at the Battle of Shakar Kheda in 1724 and thereafter ruled the Deccan as an effectively independent sovereign while nominally acknowledging Mughal suzerainty.

The Nizams

Seven Nizams ruled in succession. The dynasty survived the Anglo-Mysore and Anglo-Maratha wars by aligning with the British East India Company, ceding territory and accepting a subsidiary alliance and a British Resident. Under Mir Mahbub Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VI) and especially Mir Osman Ali Khan (Asaf Jah VII, r. 1911–1948) Hyderabad developed railways, Osmania University, hospitals and industries; Osman Ali Khan was reputed at his accession to be among the richest men in the world.

End of the dynasty

At the Partition of India in 1947, the last Nizam sought independence or accession terms of his own, but in September 1948 the Indian Union annexed Hyderabad in a military action known as Operation Polo. Osman Ali Khan remained as Rajpramukh (head of state) of Hyderabad State until 1956 and died in 1967. The dynasty's palaces — Chowmahalla, Falaknuma, King Kothi and Purani Haveli — survive in Hyderabad, and its jewels have been the subject of celebrated acquisitions and litigation.

List of Nizams

  • Asaf Jah I — 1724–1748
  • Asaf Jah II (Nizam Ali Khan) — 1762–1803
  • Asaf Jah III (Sikandar Jah) — 1803–1829
  • Asaf Jah IV (Nasir-ud-Daula) — 1829–1857
  • Asaf Jah V (Afzal-ud-Daula) — 1857–1869
  • Asaf Jah VI (Mir Mahbub Ali Khan) — 1869–1911
  • Asaf Jah VII (Mir Osman Ali Khan) — 1911–1948
