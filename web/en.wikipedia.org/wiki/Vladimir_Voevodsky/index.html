Vladimir Aleksandrovich Voevodsky (Russian: Владимир Александрович Воеводский; 4 June 1966 – 30 September 2017) was a Russian-American mathematician known for developing motivic homotopy theory, proving the Milnor and Bloch–Kato conjectures, and creating the univalent foundations of mathematics. He was awarded the Fields Medal in 2002.

Life and career

Voevodsky studied at Moscow State University and received his PhD from Harvard University in 1992 under David Kazhdan. After positions at Harvard and Northwestern, he joined the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton as a professor in 2002.

Work

With Fabien Morel, Voevodsky built the motivic homotopy theory of schemes, a framework importing methods of algebraic topology into algebraic geometry, and used motivic cohomology to prove the Milnor conjecture relating Milnor K-theory to étale cohomology — work central to his Fields Medal. He went on to prove the full Bloch–Kato conjecture (the norm residue isomorphism theorem) with Charles Weibel and Markus Rost.

In his later career, shaken in part by errors found in published proofs (including one of his own), Voevodsky turned to the foundations of mathematics and computer-verified proof. His univalent foundations programme, built around homotopy type theory and the univalence axiom, was developed with collaborators at the IAS in 2012–13 and implemented in proof assistants such as Coq (the UniMath library).

Death

Voevodsky died in Princeton in 2017, aged 51, from complications of an aortic aneurysm.
