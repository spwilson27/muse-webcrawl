Mahajanapada

The Mahājanapadas (literally "great realms", from maha, "great", and janapada, "foothold of a people") were sixteen kingdoms and republics that emerged in ancient India during the second urbanisation period, roughly between the 6th and 4th centuries BCE. They are described in ancient Buddhist, Jain and Hindu texts, and they formed the political landscape in which the Buddha and Mahavira lived and taught.

The sixteen realms

The Buddhist text Anguttara Nikaya lists sixteen Mahajanapadas: Kasi, Kosala, Anga, Magadha, Vajji (Vrijji), Malla, Chedi, Vatsa (Vamsa), Kuru, Panchala, Matsya (Machchha), Surasena, Assaka (Asmaka), Avanti, Gandhara and Kamboja. Most were monarchies, but some — notably the Vajjian confederacy centred on Vaishali and the Mallas — were oligarchic republics (gana-sanghas) governed by assemblies of clan chiefs.

Rise of Magadha

Of the sixteen, four — Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa and Avanti — became the dominant powers by the 5th century BCE. Magadha, favoured by fertile land, iron mines, elephant forests and river trade, successively absorbed its rivals under dynasties such as the Haryanka and Shishunaga, and went on to form the core of the Nanda and then the Maurya empires.

Economy and society

The period saw the spread of iron technology, wet-rice agriculture in the Ganges valley, the growth of towns and trade routes, punch-marked coinage, and the rise of merchant and artisan classes. It was also the age of the śramaṇa movements — Buddhism, Jainism and others — which challenged Vedic orthodoxy.
