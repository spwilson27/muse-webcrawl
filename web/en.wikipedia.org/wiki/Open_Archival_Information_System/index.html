Open Archival Information System

The Open Archival Information System (OAIS) is a reference model for an archive — an organization (or part of one) that accepts responsibility for preserving information and making it available to a designated community over the long term, including digital information subject to technological obsolescence. The model was developed by the Consultative Committee for Space Data Systems (CCSDS) and published as ISO standard 14721 (first issued in 2003 and revised in 2012).

The model

OAIS defines a common terminology and conceptual framework rather than a specific implementation. It identifies three roles — Producer, Management and Consumer — and describes information in terms of information packages: the Submission Information Package (SIP) received from a producer, the Archival Information Package (AIP) stored by the archive, and the Dissemination Information Package (DIP) delivered to a consumer. An AIP bundles content information with Preservation Description Information (PDI) covering provenance, context, reference and fixity.

Functional entities

The model specifies six functional entities: Ingest, Archival Storage, Data Management, Administration, Preservation Planning and Access. Preservation Planning, for example, monitors the environment — including changes in technology and the knowledge base of the designated community — and develops migration strategies so that archived information remains independently understandable.

Influence

OAIS has become the foundational vocabulary of digital preservation. It underpins repository audit and certification standards such as ISO 16363 (audit and certification of trustworthy digital repositories) and the CoreTrustSeal, and it shapes the architecture of national libraries, research data repositories and space-agency archives worldwide.
