Three-two pull down

Three-two pull down
TypeFilm-to-video conversion method
PurposeConvert 24 fps film to 29.97 fps interlaced video (NTSC)
Associated processTelecine

Three-two pull down (also written 3:2 pull down) is a term used in filmmaking and television production for the post-production process of transferring film to video. It converts 24 frames per second (fps) film to 29.97 fps video using a telecine, a process by which each film frame is converted into two or three video fields.

Process

The 24 fps of film is slowed to 23.976 fps (a reduction of 0.1%) to match the 29.97 fps of NTSC video. Because 24 film frames must be spread across 60 video fields (30 frames) each second, the fields are distributed unevenly: the first film frame is shown for two fields (one complete video frame), the second for three fields, the third for two fields, and so on. This 2-3-2-3 cadence repeats every four film frames, producing five video frames from four film frames.

Artifacts and inverse telecine

Because some video frames contain fields from two different film frames, three-two pull down can introduce combing artifacts when viewed on progressive-scan displays. To recover the original film frames for progressive playback, a process called inverse telecine (IVTC) detects the cadence and recombines the fields into the original 24 fps frames. Many modern displays and media players perform this automatically.

Variants

Other pull-down patterns exist, including 2:2 pull down used for PAL transfers (with a corresponding 4% speedup of film shot at 24 fps to 25 fps), and advanced pull-down schemes used in some camcorders recording 24p material onto 60i tape.
