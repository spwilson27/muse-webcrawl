Northwest Atlantic Fisheries Organization

The Northwest Atlantic Fisheries Organization (NAFO) is an intergovernmental regional fisheries management organization that manages most fish stocks in the Northwest Atlantic Ocean outside national exclusive economic zones. Its headquarters are in Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada.

History

NAFO was founded in 1979 under the Convention on Future Multilateral Cooperation in the Northwest Atlantic Fisheries, succeeding the International Commission for the Northwest Atlantic Fisheries (ICNAF, established 1949). It was created in response to coastal states, notably Canada, extending their jurisdiction to 200 nautical miles in 1977.

Mandate and operations

NAFO manages fisheries for roughly twenty species — including cod, redfish, Greenland halibut, shrimp and skates — in its Regulatory Area, the high-seas portions of the Convention Area, which covers the Grand Banks and Flemish Cap off Newfoundland. It sets total allowable catches, allocates quotas among contracting parties, and operates a joint inspection scheme, vessel monitoring and a list of vessels engaged in illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing.

Members and disputes

Contracting parties include Canada, the European Union, the United States, Russia, Norway, Japan and others. NAFO was at the centre of the 1995 "Turbot War" between Canada and the European Union (Spain), when Canada seized the Spanish trawler Estai outside its 200-mile zone over Greenland halibut quotas; the dispute was settled by negotiation and strengthened NAFO's compliance measures.
