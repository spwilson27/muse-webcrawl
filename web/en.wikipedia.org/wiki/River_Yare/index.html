The River Yare is a river in the English county of Norfolk. It rises south of Dereham near Shipdham and flows eastwards for about 84 kilometres (52 mi) through Barford, Cringleford and the city of Norwich, then across the Broads to Breydon Water, where it meets the River Bure and flows into the North Sea at Gorleston, Great Yarmouth.

Course

The Yare's upper course winds through a shallow valley past villages including Barnham Broom and Kimberley. Its main tributary, the River Tiffey, joins at Barford; the River Tas joins south of Norwich, and the River Wensum — the larger stream at their confluence — meets it within the city of Norwich. Below Norwich the river becomes tidal and broadens, flowing past the RSPB reserves at Strumpshaw Fen and Buckenham Marshes, Cantley (with its sugar beet factory), Reedham, where it is crossed by a swing bridge and chain ferry, and Berney Arms before reaching Breydon Water.

Navigation

The Yare is navigable from the sea to Norwich, which was historically an inland port served by wherries carrying coal, grain and manufactured goods. Today the lower river is used by pleasure craft of the Broads network and by commercial vessels serving Cantley and Great Yarmouth.

History

The name Yare is thought to be of Celtic origin, possibly meaning "babbling" or "flowing" stream. In Roman times the river mouth near Caister and Burgh Castle (Gariannonum) formed an important harbour; the estuary was much wider in the past before land reclamation created the marshes of Halvergate and Breydon.
