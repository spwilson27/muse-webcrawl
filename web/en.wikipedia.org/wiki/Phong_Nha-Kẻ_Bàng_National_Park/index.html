Phong Nha-Kẻ Bàng National Park is a protected area in Quảng Bình Province in north-central Vietnam, created to protect one of the world's largest karst limestone regions, containing hundreds of caves and grottoes. The park was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003 for its geological values, and the listing was extended in 2015 to recognise its ecosystems and biodiversity.

Caves

The park's cave systems are its fame. Phong Nha Cave, entered by boat along the Son River, has some 7.7 km of surveyed passages. Paradise Cave (Thiên Đường), opened to tourists in 2010, extends over 31 km and contains enormous chambers with spectacular stalactites and stalagmites. Sơn Đoòng Cave, explored by a British–Vietnamese expedition in 2009, is regarded as the largest known cave passage in the world by volume — over 5 km long, with sections more than 200 m high and 150 m wide, containing its own jungle and weather where dolines have collapsed. Én Cave, with its vast entrance, is among the largest cave entrances on Earth.

Nature

The park's tropical limestone forests host high biodiversity, including globally threatened primates such as the Hatinh langur, gibbons, and species like the saola in the wider Annamite range, together with hundreds of bird, fish and invertebrate species, many of them endemic to the karst. During the Vietnam War the area around the park was heavily bombed as part of the Ho Chi Minh Trail network, and its caves sheltered ferries and hospitals.
