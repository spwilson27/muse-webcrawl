Syllabus

A syllabus (plural syllabi or syllabuses) is an academic document that communicates information about a course and defines expectations and responsibilities. It is generally a summary or outline of the topics to be covered, prepared by the instructor and distributed to students at the beginning of a course of study.

Contents

A typical syllabus includes the course title and meeting times, the instructor's contact information and office hours, a course description and learning objectives, required textbooks and materials, a week-by-week schedule of topics, reading assignments and deadlines, and the methods and weights of assessment. It may also state policies on attendance, academic integrity, late work and accommodations for students with disabilities. Because it sets out mutual expectations, a syllabus is sometimes informally described as a "contract" between instructor and students.

Etymology

The word derives, through a misreading in early printed editions of Cicero's letters, from the Greek sittybos, a parchment label or table of contents; the modern sense of an outline of a course of study developed in the nineteenth century.

Other uses

In the Catholic Church, the word is associated with Pius IX's 1864 Syllabus of Errors, a list of propositions the Church condemned. In law, a syllabus is the headnote summary preceding a published judicial opinion, particularly in United States reports. In British and Commonwealth school systems, "syllabus" also refers to the official specification of a subject set by an examination board.
