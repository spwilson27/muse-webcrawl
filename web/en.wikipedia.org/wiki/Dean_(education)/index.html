Dean (education)

A dean is a person with significant authority over a specific academic unit, typically a college, school or faculty within a university. The word derives from the Latin decanus, "chief of ten", via its use for heads of chapters of clergy and of monastic communities, and was adopted by the medieval universities.

Roles

In the modern research university, a dean is the chief academic and administrative officer of a college or professional school (for example, a dean of law or dean of medicine): responsible for budgets, faculty hiring and tenure recommendations, curriculum, fundraising and representing the unit to the provost and president. Associate and assistant deans handle particular portfolios such as research, graduate studies or undergraduate education. A separate role, the dean of students, oversees student life, welfare and discipline rather than an academic unit.

Variations

In British and Commonwealth universities, "dean of faculty" is often a rotating, largely honorary post among senior professors, while executive authority lies with heads of school or a vice-chancellor's team. In North American secondary schools, a "dean of students" or "dean of discipline" is common. In Oxbridge colleges, a dean may be the fellow responsible for discipline, and historically for chapel.
