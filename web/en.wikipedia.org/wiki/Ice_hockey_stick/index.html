Ice hockey stick

An ice hockey stick is a piece of equipment used in ice hockey to shoot, pass, and carry the puck across the ice. Ice hockey sticks are approximately 150–200 cm long, composed of a long, slender shaft with a flat extension at one end called the blade. The blade is the part of the stick used to contact the puck, and is typically 25 to 40 cm long.

Construction

Traditionally, sticks were made of wood, often ash or hickory. Modern sticks are almost universally built from composite materials — carbon fibre and fibreglass over a foam or polymer core — which are lighter, more consistent and allow engineered flex profiles, though they are more expensive and break more abruptly than wood. Sticks are described by their flex rating, lie, blade curve pattern and handedness (left or right shot).

Dimensions and rules

Under NHL rules the shaft may not exceed 63 inches (160 cm) in length and the blade 12.5 inches (32 cm), with limited exceptions for very tall players granted with league approval. Goaltenders use a distinct stick with a wider lower shaft (the paddle) and blade.

History

The earliest hockey sticks were carved from single pieces of wood by Mi'kmaq craftsmen in Nova Scotia in the 19th century. Curved blades were introduced in the 1960s — popularised by Stan Mikita and Bobby Hull — revolutionising shooting, and composite sticks took over professional hockey in the 1990s and 2000s.
