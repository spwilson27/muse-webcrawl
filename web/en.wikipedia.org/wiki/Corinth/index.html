Corinth

Corinth (Κόρινθος)
Country: Greece
Region: Peloponnese
Regional unit: Corinthia
Population (2021): approx. 56,000 (municipality)

Corinth (Greek: Κόρινθος, Kórinthos) is a municipality in Corinthia in Greece, and the successor to the ancient city of Corinth, one of the largest and most important cities of ancient Greece. The modern city lies about 700 metres southwest of the ancient ruins and roughly 78 km west of Athens, near the Isthmus of Corinth where the Corinth Canal joins the Gulf of Corinth with the Saronic Gulf.

Ancient Corinth

Ancient Corinth was a powerful city-state on the Isthmus, controlling land routes between northern Greece and the Peloponnese and trade between its two ports, Lechaeum and Cenchreae. It founded colonies including Syracuse and Corcyra, was famous for its pottery, bronze work and the Corinthian architectural order, and hosted the Isthmian Games. Destroyed by the Roman general Mummius in 146 BC, it was refounded as a Roman colony by Julius Caesar in 44 BC and became a prosperous provincial capital — the Corinth visited by St Paul, to whose church he addressed the epistles First and Second Corinthians.

Archaeology and modern city

The archaeological site includes the Temple of Apollo (c. 540 BC), the agora, the Peirene fountain and the theatre, overlooked by the fortress hill of Acrocorinth. The modern city was rebuilt after earthquakes destroyed Old Corinth (Néa Kórinthos was founded in 1858); it is a regional commercial and agricultural centre.
