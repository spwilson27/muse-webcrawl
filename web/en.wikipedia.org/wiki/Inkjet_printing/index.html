Inkjet printing

Inkjet printing is a type of computer printing that recreates a digital image by propelling droplets of ink onto paper, plastic or other substrates. Inkjet printers range from small inexpensive consumer models to large-format professional machines, and are the most commonly used type of printer for home and office use alongside laser printers.

Methods

Two main technologies are used. In thermal inkjet, used by manufacturers such as HP and Canon, a tiny resistor heats ink to form a vapour bubble that ejects a droplet from the nozzle. In piezoelectric inkjet, used by Epson, a piezoelectric crystal flexes when a voltage is applied, squeezing a droplet out. Industrial printers also use continuous inkjet, in which a constant stream of charged droplets is deflected electrostatically. Drop sizes are measured in picolitres, and resolutions reach several thousand dots per inch.

Applications

Beyond documents and photographs, inkjet technology is used for wide-format posters and signage, textile and packaging printing, product coding and marking, and specialised processes such as printing electronic circuits, pharmaceutical dosages and biological materials. Ink costs, printhead clogging and slower speeds for high-coverage pages are the principal disadvantages compared with laser printing, while low purchase price and high-quality colour output are its main advantages.
