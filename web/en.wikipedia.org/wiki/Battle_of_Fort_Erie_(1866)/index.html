Battle of Fort Erie (1866)

The Battle of Fort Erie was a skirmish fought on 2 June 1866 during the Fenian raids into Canada. Fenian forces of Irish-American Civil War veterans, under Colonel John O'Neill, had crossed the Niagara River and occupied the town of Fort Erie, Canada West (now Ontario). After defeating Canadian militia at Ridgeway earlier that day, the Fenians fought a brief action at Fort Erie before withdrawing back across the river to the United States.

Background

The Fenian Brotherhood, an Irish republican organisation based in the United States, planned to seize Canada and exchange it for Irish independence. On the night of 31 May–1 June 1866, O'Neill led roughly 800–1,000 men across the Niagara River from Buffalo and occupied Fort Erie without initial opposition, cutting telegraph lines and seizing supplies.

The engagement

On 2 June a small Canadian force — including a detachment carried aboard the tug W. T. Robb and troops sent to recapture the town — clashed with Fenian pickets around Fort Erie after the main Fenian body returned from its victory at the Battle of Ridgeway. In the sharp skirmish near the town and the old fort, the outnumbered Canadians were driven off and a number captured, though most were soon released. That night, facing converging Canadian and British regular reinforcements and the arrival of US naval forces to cut off resupply, O'Neill evacuated his command across the Niagara, where American authorities briefly detained the raiders.

Aftermath

Casualties in the Fort Erie skirmish were light on both sides. The 1866 raids, of which Fort Erie and Ridgeway were the principal actions, strengthened Canadian support for Confederation, achieved the following year, and spurred reform of the Canadian militia.
