Palmares (quilombo)

Palmares was a quilombo — a settlement of African people who had escaped slavery in Portuguese Brazil — located in the Serra da Barriga hills of what is now the state of Alagoas. Founded around 1605 and destroyed in 1694, it grew into the largest and longest-lived of Brazil's maroon communities, with a population often estimated in the tens of thousands, organised as a federation of villages under its own kings.

History

Palmares emerged as enslaved Africans fled the sugar plantations of Pernambuco and Alagoas, and it flourished during the confusion of the Dutch invasion of Brazil (1630–1654). Its capital, Macaco, was a fortified town with hundreds of houses, workshops, and a church. The quilombo traded with neighbouring settlers while defending itself against repeated Portuguese and Dutch expeditions.

Zumbi and the fall

In 1678 King Ganga-Zumba accepted a peace treaty with the Portuguese, but his nephew Zumbi rejected its terms and became the quilombo's most famous leader. After years of campaigning, a Portuguese expedition under Domingos Jorge Velho finally destroyed Macaco in 1694; Zumbi was captured and killed on 20 November 1695.

Legacy

Palmares and Zumbi became powerful symbols of Afro-Brazilian resistance. The date of Zumbi's death, 20 November, is commemorated in Brazil as Black Consciousness Day, and the Serra da Barriga is a protected national heritage site.
