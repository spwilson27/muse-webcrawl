Liberation fonts

The Liberation fonts are a family of free and open-source typefaces commissioned by Red Hat and designed by Ascender Corporation (later Monotype) as metric-compatible substitutes for the widely used proprietary fonts of Microsoft Office. Released beginning in 2007, they allow documents created with Times New Roman, Arial, or Courier New to be rendered with identical line breaks and pagination on systems where the Microsoft fonts are unavailable or unlicensed — a key requirement for Linux distributions and open-source office suites.

Licensing and development

The first releases appeared under a GNU GPL-based licence with a font exception; from the 2.00 series (2012), which was re-drawn from Google's metrically identical Croscore fonts (Arimo, Tinos, and Cousine), the family is distributed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Liberation Serif, Sans, and Mono ship by default with many Linux distributions and are the standard fallbacks in LibreOffice when documents request the Microsoft core fonts.
