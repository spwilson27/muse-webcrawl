Subject heading

A subject heading is a term or phrase assigned to a document, book or other item in a library catalogue to describe its content and allow it to be retrieved by topic. Subject headings are drawn from controlled vocabularies so that all works on the same topic are grouped under the same term.

Systems

The best-known system is the Library of Congress Subject Headings (LCSH), maintained since the early twentieth century and used by libraries worldwide. Other schemes include the Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) for biomedical literature, the Sears List of Subject Headings for small libraries, and faceted systems such as FAST (Faceted Application of Subject Terminology).

Structure

Headings may be subdivided by topic, place, time period and form — for example, "Railroads — France — History — 19th century" — in a practice called pre-coordination. Assigning headings requires cataloguers to determine a work's "aboutness" and select the most specific authorised terms.
