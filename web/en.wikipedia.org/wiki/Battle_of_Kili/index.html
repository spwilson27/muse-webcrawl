Battle of Kili

Date1299
LocationKili, near Delhi, India
ResultDelhi Sultanate victory
BelligerentsDelhi Sultanate vs. Chagatai Khanate (Mongols)
CommandersAlauddin Khalji; Zafar Khan (killed) vs. Qutlugh Khwaja

The Battle of Kili was fought in 1299 at Kili, near Delhi, between the forces of the Delhi Sultanate under Sultan Alauddin Khalji and an invading army of the Mongol Chagatai Khanate led by Qutlugh Khwaja, a son of the khan Duwa.

Background

The Mongols of Central Asia repeatedly raided northern India during the late 13th century. Alauddin Khalji, who seized the Delhi throne in 1296, adopted an aggressive defence: strengthening frontier fortresses, maintaining a large standing army, and meeting invasions in the field. In 1299 Qutlugh Khwaja advanced deep into India and reached the outskirts of Delhi itself.

The battle

Alauddin marched out and took position at Kili. The Mongols attempted to envelop the Sultanate army and threatened its baggage and the road to Delhi. Alauddin's general Zafar Khan led a ferocious counterattack that drove back the Mongol wing opposite him; carried away by the pursuit, he advanced far beyond support, was surrounded, and was killed fighting. Despite the loss of his best general, Alauddin held his ground, and during the night the Mongols — their objective of plunder frustrated and their army bloodied — withdrew from the field and retreated toward Central Asia.

Aftermath

The victory confirmed Alauddin's reputation and his military reforms, though Zafar Khan's death was a serious loss; tradition holds that Alauddin was not displeased to be rid of an over-mighty and popular general. The Mongols returned in force in 1303, when they besieged Delhi itself, but after Alauddin's victory at the Battle of Amroha (1305) and further defeats, large-scale Mongol invasions of India effectively ceased.
