Postmarketing surveillance

Postmarketing surveillance (PMS), or post-marketing surveillance, is the practice of monitoring the safety of a pharmaceutical drug or medical device after it has been released on the market. It forms part of pharmacovigilance and is sometimes called Phase IV of clinical research.

Rationale

Pre-approval clinical trials involve limited numbers of patients over limited periods and may exclude groups such as pregnant women, children, and the elderly. Rare, long-term, or population-specific adverse effects may therefore only become apparent once a product is used by millions of people in everyday practice.

Methods

Surveillance relies on spontaneous reporting systems — such as the FDA's MedWatch and FAERS databases, the UK's Yellow Card scheme, and the EU's EudraVigilance — through which health professionals and patients report suspected adverse reactions. Regulators and manufacturers also conduct formal Phase IV studies, registry studies, and analyses of electronic health records and insurance claims to detect safety signals. Confirmed risks may lead to label changes, restricted distribution, additional warnings, or withdrawal of the product, as happened with rofecoxib (Vioxx) in 2004.
