North Downs Way

The North Downs Way is a long-distance footpath and National Trail in southern England, running 153 miles (246 km) from Farnham in Surrey to Dover in Kent along the chalk ridge of the North Downs. It was opened in stages from 1978.

North Downs Way
Length: 153 miles (246 km)
Trailheads: Farnham — Dover
Designation: National Trail (1978)

Route

The trail follows the crest of the Downs through the Surrey Hills and Kent Downs Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty, passing Guildford, Box Hill, Canterbury (via a northern loop), and the white cliffs near Dover. Much of its course parallels the historic Pilgrims' Way, the medieval route from Winchester to the shrine of Thomas Becket at Canterbury.

Walking

Most walkers complete the route in 11–14 days. The path is waymarked with the National Trail acorn symbol and offers frequent rail access, making it one of the most accessible long-distance trails from London.
