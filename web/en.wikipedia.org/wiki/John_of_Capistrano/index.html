John of Capistrano (Italian: Giovanni da Capestrano, 24 June 1386 – 23 October 1456) was an Italian Franciscan friar, preacher, theologian and inquisitor. Celebrated across Europe for his preaching tours and for his role in rallying the defence of Belgrade against the Ottomans in 1456, he was canonised by the Catholic Church and is the patron saint of military chaplains.

Early life

Born at Capestrano in the Abruzzi, the son of a former German baron, John studied law at the University of Perugia, became governor of Perugia in 1412, and married into a wealthy family. Imprisoned during a war between Perugia and the Malatesta, he underwent a conversion, had his marriage annulled, and in 1416 entered the Order of Friars Minor at Monteripido. He was ordained priest in 1420 and became a disciple — and later biographer-adjacent associate — of the great Observant preacher Bernardino of Siena.

Preacher and inquisitor

For forty years Capistrano criss-crossed Italy and then Central and Eastern Europe, preaching in town squares to crowds of tens of thousands, promoting the Franciscan Observance, reforming convents, and preaching devotion to the Holy Name of Jesus. He served as a papal legate and inquisitor; his missions also included severe measures against heretics, usurers and Jewish communities, a record that has drawn modern criticism. He founded libraries and hospitals, and his legal training was used in church governance and canon-law disputes.

Belgrade 1456

After the fall of Constantinople (1453), Pope Callixtus III commissioned Capistrano, then seventy, to preach a crusade against the Ottoman advance. In Hungary he worked alongside the commander John Hunyadi, recruiting a largely peasant crusader army. At the Siege of Belgrade in July 1456 the defenders repelled Sultan Mehmed II's assault — a landmark victory celebrated across Christendom. Both leaders died within months of disease raging in the camp; Capistrano died at Ilok on 23 October 1456.

Veneration

John of Capistrano was canonised in 1690 (the date is also given as 1724 under Benedict XIII). His feast is kept on 23 October. He is namesake of places such as San Juan Capistrano in California, whose mission was founded under his patronage in 1776.
