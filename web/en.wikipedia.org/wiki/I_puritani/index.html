I puritani

I puritani (The Puritans) is an opera by Vincenzo Bellini, first performed at the Théâtre-Italien in Paris on 24 January 1835. The libretto, by Count Carlo Pepoli, is based on the play Têtes Rondes et Cavaliers by Jacques-François Ancelot and Joseph Xavier Saintine, which was itself derived from Walter Scott's 1816 novel Old Mortality. It was Bellini's last opera; he died later the same year, aged 33.

Roles and premiere

The premiere cast was a celebrated quartet of bel canto singers: Giulia Grisi as Elvira, Giovanni Battista Rubini as Arturo, Antonio Tamburini as Riccardo and Luigi Lablache as Giorgio. The opera is famous for the extreme demands it places on the tenor, including the F above high C in the final act duet "Credeasi, misera".

Synopsis

Set in England during the Civil War, the opera tells of Elvira, daughter of the Puritan governor Lord Walton, who is betrothed against her will to the Puritan Riccardo but loves the Royalist Arturo. On their wedding day Arturo helps the imprisoned Queen Henrietta escape in Elvira's bridal veil; believing herself abandoned, Elvira goes mad. In the final act Arturo returns, Elvira's reason is restored, and news of Cromwell's pardon for the Royalists brings the opera to a joyful close.

Music

Notable numbers include Elvira's entrance aria "Son vergin vezzosa", the mad scene "O rendetemi la speme", the tenor aria "A te, o cara", and the rousing bass-baritone duet "Suoni la tromba", which became an anthem of the Italian Risorgimento. The opera is regarded as a pinnacle of the bel canto style.
