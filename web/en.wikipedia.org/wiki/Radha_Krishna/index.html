Radha Krishna

Radha-Krishna (IAST: Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa) are collectively known within Hinduism as the combined forms of the goddess Radha and the god Krishna. Radha and Krishna are the female and male forms, respectively, of God in many Vaishnava traditions, and together they represent the divine couple whose love is the supreme object of devotion.

Theology

In Gaudiya Vaishnavism and several other Krishna-centred traditions, Krishna is regarded as Svayam Bhagavan, the original form of God, and Radha as his original Shakti — his internal potency of bliss (hladini shakti) personified. Their relationship symbolises the soul's longing for God and God's joy in the devotee. In some traditions Radha is considered the supreme deity herself, with Krishna subordinate to her love; devotees aspire to serve the couple as attendants in their eternal pastimes in Vrindavan and Goloka.

Scripture and literature

The love of Radha and Krishna is celebrated in the Bhagavata Purana (though Radha is not named there explicitly), Jayadeva's 12th-century Sanskrit poem Gita Govinda, and the works of poets such as Vidyapati and Chandidas. The Brahma Vaivarta Purana and Garga Samhita give Radha a central role. Their story — Krishna's childhood in Gokul and Vrindavan, the rasa-lila dance with Radha and the gopis, and Krishna's departure to Mathura, leaving Radha in the anguish of separation (viraha) — is one of the most retold narratives in Indian art, music and dance.

Worship

Radha-Krishna are worshipped together in temples across the Braj region, including Vrindavan, Barsana and Mathura, and worldwide by movements such as ISKCON. Major festivals include Janmashtami (Krishna's birth), Radhashtami (Radha's birth), Holi and Jhulan Leela. The mantra "Radhe Radhe" is a common greeting in Braj.
