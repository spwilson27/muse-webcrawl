Vascular anomaly

A vascular anomaly is any disorder of blood vessels or lymphatic vessels. Vascular anomalies are broadly classified by the International Society for the Study of Vascular Anomalies (ISSVA) into two main groups: vascular tumors, such as infantile hemangiomas, which show endothelial proliferation, and vascular malformations, which are errors of vascular development that grow commensurately with the patient.

Malformations are further subdivided by vessel type and flow characteristics into capillary, venous, lymphatic, arteriovenous, and combined malformations. Examples include port-wine stains, venous malformations, and arteriovenous malformations.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies on clinical examination, imaging such as ultrasound and MRI, and sometimes genetics, as many anomalies are associated with somatic mutations in signaling pathways. Treatment may include observation, laser therapy, sclerotherapy, embolization, surgery, and targeted medical therapies such as sirolimus or alpelisib for complex cases.
