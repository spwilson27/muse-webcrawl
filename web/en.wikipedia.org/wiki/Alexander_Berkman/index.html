Alexander Berkman (21 November 1870 – 28 June 1936) was a Russian-American anarchist writer and activist. Born Ovsei Osipovich Berkkan in Vilnius, he emigrated to the United States in 1888 and became a leading figure in the American anarchist movement, working closely with Emma Goldman, his lover and lifelong comrade.

Attentat and imprisonment

During the Homestead Strike of 1892, Berkman attempted to assassinate Henry Clay Frick, the manager of the Carnegie steel works, shooting and stabbing him; Frick survived. Berkman was sentenced to 22 years in prison, of which he served 14. His experiences produced his first book, Prison Memoirs of an Anarchist (1912).

Deportation and later life

In 1917 Berkman and Goldman were imprisoned for opposing conscription, and in 1919 they were deported to Russia during the First Red Scare. Initially sympathetic to the Bolsheviks, Berkman became disillusioned by the suppression of dissent, including the Kronstadt rebellion, and left in 1921. He spent his remaining years in Europe, writing The Bolshevik Myth (1925) and Now and After: The ABC of Communist Anarchism (1929). Suffering from ill health, he took his own life in Nice in 1936.
