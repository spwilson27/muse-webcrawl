Greystones (Irish: Na Clocha Liatha, meaning "the grey stones") is a coastal town in County Wicklow, Ireland, situated on the Irish Sea about 27 km south of Dublin. It developed from a small fishing village into a popular seaside resort and commuter town, and is served by the DART suburban railway, of which it is the southern terminus.

History

The town takes its name from a stretch of grey rocks between two beaches on its seafront. Greystones grew rapidly after the arrival of the Dublin and South Eastern Railway in the 1850s, when it became a fashionable Victorian resort. The harbour, built in the late nineteenth century, suffered long decline through silting and storm damage before a major redevelopment in the twenty-first century produced a new marina and harbour facilities.

Amenities and surroundings

The town is known for its beaches, cliff walk to the neighbouring town of Bray, and views towards Bray Head. The surrounding area includes the village of Delgany and the Glen of the Downs. Greystones has frequently been recognised in surveys for quality of life, and in 2008 it was named among the world's most liveable communities in its population category at the international LivCom awards.

Transport

Greystones railway station, opened in 1855, is the southern terminus of the DART electrified line from Dublin, and also receives InterCity and commuter services on the line to Rosslare. The town is connected to Dublin by the N11 road and by bus services.
