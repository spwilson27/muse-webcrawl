Classes préparatoires aux grandes écoles

The classes préparatoires aux grandes écoles (CPGE), informally called classes prépas or prépas, are two- or three-year intensive courses in France, held mainly in lycées, that prepare students for the competitive entrance examinations (concours) of the grandes écoles — elite institutions for engineering, business, veterinary science and the humanities.

Organisation

Students enter after the baccalauréat on the strength of their school records. The main tracks are scientific (MPSI, PCSI, PTSI leading to MP, PC, PSI, PT), economic and commercial (ECG), and literary (hypokhâgne and khâgne). The workload is famously heavy, with weekly written papers and oral interrogations called colles (or khôlles).

Criticism and reform

The system is praised for its intellectual rigour but criticised for stress and for reproducing social privilege, since relatively few students from modest backgrounds enter the most prestigious prépas in Parisian lycées such as Louis-le-Grand and Henri-IV.
