Cher Ami

Cher Ami (French for "dear friend") was a female homing pigeon who served with the U.S. Army Signal Corps in France during World War I. She is famous for delivering, on 4 October 1918, the message that helped rescue the surrounded American troops known as the Lost Battalion.

The mission

During the Meuse-Argonne Offensive, Major Charles White Whittlesey and more than 500 men were trapped in a hollow on the Charlevaux ravine, cut off, short of food and ammunition, and being shelled by their own artillery. Two pigeons carrying pleas for help were shot down. Cher Ami, the last pigeon, was released carrying a message giving the battalion's position and begging for the friendly barrage to stop. German fire wounded her in the breast, blinded her in one eye, and shot away a leg, but she completed the roughly 25-mile flight in about 25–65 minutes. The barrage was lifted, and 194 survivors were rescued.

Honours and legacy

Cher Ami was awarded the French Croix de Guerre with Oak Leaf Cluster. Army medics saved her life but not her leg. She died of her wounds in June 1919 and was mounted by a taxidermist; her body is displayed at the Smithsonian Institution's National Museum of American History. DNA testing in 2021 confirmed she was female, correcting earlier accounts that described her as male.
