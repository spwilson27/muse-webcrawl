Doune (from Scottish Gaelic An Dùn, "the fort") is a burgh in the Stirling council area of Scotland, on the River Teith near Stirling. It is best known for Doune Castle, a well-preserved medieval stronghold, and historically for the manufacture of pistols.


Doune Castle, built in the late 14th century.

History

The site was occupied by a Roman fort, and the town grew around Doune Castle, built for Robert Stewart, Duke of Albany, in the late 1300s. From the 1640s Doune became renowned for its pistols — metalwork so fine that a Doune pistol is said to have fired the first shot of the 1745 Jacobite rising. The castle later became famous as a filming location for Monty Python and the Holy Grail, Game of Thrones (as Winterfell in the pilot) and Outlander.

Present day

Doune is a small commuter town with a historic market cross, an annual Highland gathering, and David Stirling Memorial nearby commemorating the founder of the SAS, who came from the area.
