Syriac Orthodox Church

Classification: Oriental Orthodox
Primate: Patriarch of Antioch (Ignatius Aphrem II, since 2014)
Liturgy: West Syriac Rite
Headquarters: Damascus, Syria (Bab Touma)
Language: Syriac (liturgical), local vernaculars

The Syriac Orthodox Church, officially the Syriac Orthodox Patriarchate of Antioch and All the East, is an Oriental Orthodox church distinguished by its West Syriac liturgical tradition and its miaphysite Christology. Its head, the Patriarch of Antioch, traces his succession to the apostolic see of Antioch, where followers of Jesus were first called Christians. The church's historical heartland spans northern Mesopotamia — modern southeastern Turkey (especially Tur Abdin), Syria, and Iraq — with a large presence in Kerala, India, and a worldwide diaspora.

History

After the Council of Chalcedon (451), Christians who rejected its dyophysite definition organized separate hierarchies in Syria and Egypt. In the 6th century, Jacob Baradaeus — from whom the church's adherents were long (often pejoratively) called "Jacobites" — rebuilt the persecuted miaphysite church, ordaining bishops and clergy across the region. The church flourished under and after the Arab conquests, preserving Syriac scholarship in monasteries such as Mor Gabriel, founded in 397 and among the oldest functioning monasteries in the world.

The community suffered catastrophic losses in the Sayfo ("Sword") genocide of 1915 in the Ottoman Empire, and more recently from the wars in Iraq and Syria, driving emigration to Europe, the Americas, and Australia. The patriarchal seat moved over the centuries from Antioch to monasteries in Tur Abdin and eventually to Homs and, in 1959, to Damascus, where St George's Cathedral in Bab Touma now serves as headquarters.

India

The Malankara Jacobite Syrian Christian Church in Kerala forms an integral part of the church under the Patriarch, locally led by the Catholicos of the East. Its origins are traditionally ascribed to the mission of St Thomas the Apostle in the 1st century.

Worship and language

The church celebrates the West Syriac Rite, principally the Liturgy of St James, in Classical Syriac — a dialect of Aramaic close to the language of Jesus — supplemented by local languages. It counts several million members worldwide and participates actively in ecumenical dialogue, including agreements with the Roman Catholic Church on Christological declarations (1984) and pastoral cooperation.
