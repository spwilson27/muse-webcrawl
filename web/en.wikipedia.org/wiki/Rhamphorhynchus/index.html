Rhamphorhynchus

Rhamphorhynchus is a genus of long-tailed pterosaurs that lived during the Late Jurassic, roughly 150 million years ago. Its many exquisitely preserved fossils, especially from the Solnhofen limestone of Germany, make it one of the best-known early pterosaurs.

Kingdom Animalia
Group Pterosauria
Period Late Jurassic
Wingspan Up to about 1.8 metres in large specimens

Anatomy

The animal had a long skull ending in a beak-like snout filled with forward-angled teeth. Unlike later pterodactyloid pterosaurs, it retained a long stiff tail, often ending in a diamond- or leaf-shaped soft-tissue vane that may have aided steering. Adults show changes in skull proportions and tooth count as they grew.

Discovery and species

Fossils were first described in the nineteenth century, when some specimens were assigned to Pterodactylus before the distinctive long-tailed form was recognized. Numerous species have been named, but specialists often regard Rhamphorhynchus muensteri as the principal valid species, with many named forms representing growth stages or individual variation.

Paleobiology

The teeth and jaw shape suggest a diet dominated by fish and other small aquatic prey. Some fossils preserve fish remains or associations with the predatory fish Aspidorhynchus, including a famous specimen that appears to capture a fatal encounter. Growth studies indicate that young animals could fly soon after hatching and grew relatively rapidly, though not exactly like birds or mammals.

Preservation

The fine-grained Solnhofen deposits occasionally preserve wing membranes, tail vanes and other soft tissues. Such fossils have been central to reconstructing pterosaur flight and to debates about body covering, including hair-like pycnofibres.
