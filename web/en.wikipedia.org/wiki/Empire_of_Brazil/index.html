The Empire of Brazil was a 19th-century state that broadly comprised the territory of modern Brazil and, until 1828, Uruguay. It was established in 1822 when the Portuguese prince regent Dom Pedro proclaimed Brazil's independence from Portugal and was crowned Emperor Pedro I. It lasted until 1889, when a military coup proclaimed a republic.

History

Pedro I abdicated in 1831 in favour of his five-year-old son, and the empire passed through a turbulent regency before Pedro II was declared of age and crowned in 1841. His nearly fifty-year reign brought political stability unusual in Latin America, economic growth based on coffee exports, victory in the Paraguayan War (1864–1870), and the gradual abolition of slavery — completed with the Golden Law of 13 May 1888, signed by Princess Isabel.

Fall

Abolition alienated slave-owning landowners, while the military and urban republicans grew hostile to the monarchy. On 15 November 1889 Marshal Deodoro da Fonseca led a coup d'état; Pedro II, refusing to resist, went into exile in Europe, and Brazil became a republic. Despite its reliance on slavery for most of its existence, the empire is often remembered for its liberal constitution of 1824, its parliamentary institutions, and the personal moderation of Pedro II.
