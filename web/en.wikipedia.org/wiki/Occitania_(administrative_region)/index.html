Occitania (French: Occitanie; Occitan: Occitània) is an administrative region of France that succeeded the former regions of Languedoc-Roussillon and Midi-Pyrénées on 1 January 2016, as part of the territorial reform of French regions. It is the second-largest region of metropolitan France by area, covering 72,724 km² in the south of the country, with Toulouse as its prefecture and largest city.

Geography

The region stretches from the Pyrenees on the Spanish border to the Mediterranean coast, and from the Massif Central to the Rhône valley. It comprises 13 departments, including Haute-Garonne, Hérault, Gard, Pyrénées-Orientales, Aude, Tarn, Aveyron, Lot, Lozère, Gers, Ariège, Hautes-Pyrénées, and Tarn-et-Garonne. Important cities include Toulouse, Montpellier, Nîmes, and Perpignan.

Name

The region was provisionally called Languedoc-Roussillon-Midi-Pyrénées. The name "Occitanie" was adopted by the regional council in 2016, with the subtitle "Pyrénées–Méditerranée", and was confirmed by decree, despite protests from some Catalan groups in the Roussillon who objected to losing a distinct Catalan identity in the regional name.

Economy and culture

Toulouse is a major centre of the European aerospace industry, home to Airbus headquarters. The region is also noted for agriculture and wine (including the Languedoc vineyards), Mediterranean tourism, and the historic cities of Carcassonne, Albi, and Montpellier. Historically, much of the region spoke varieties of the Occitan language, still reflected in bilingual signage and cultural revival movements.
