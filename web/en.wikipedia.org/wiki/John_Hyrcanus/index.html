John Hyrcanus

John Hyrcanus (born c. 164 BC, died 104 BC) was a Hasmonean Jewish high priest and ruler (ethnarch) of Judea from 134 BC until his death. A son of Simon Thassi, he assumed leadership of Judea after his father and two brothers were murdered by his brother-in-law Ptolemy son of Abubus.

Reign

Hyrcanus survived a siege of Jerusalem by the Seleucid king Antiochus VII Sidetes (134–132 BC), and after Antiochus's death in Parthia, Judea became effectively independent. Hyrcanus then pursued an expansionist policy, conquering Samaria, destroying the Samaritan temple on Mount Gerizim, conquering Idumea and forcibly converting the Idumeans to Judaism, and taking territory east of the Jordan.

Relations with sects

Early in his reign Hyrcanus favoured the Pharisees, but after a dispute at a banquet — recounted by Josephus — he aligned himself with the Sadducees and repealed Pharisaic enactments. The tension between the sects under his rule foreshadowed the later Hasmonean civil conflicts.

Legacy

Under Hyrcanus, the Hasmonean state reached its greatest territorial extent to that point. He was succeeded by his son Aristobulus I, who took the title of king.
