Zainichi Koreans

Regions: Japan (especially Osaka, Kyoto, Tokyo)

Languages: Japanese, Korean

Population: several hundred thousand special permanent residents and naturalized citizens

Zainichi Koreans are ethnic Koreans who reside in Japan, most of them descendants of Koreans who migrated or were brought to Japan during the period of Japanese colonial rule over Korea (1910–1945), and their descendants who retained Korean nationality and special permanent resident status.

History

Large-scale Korean migration to Japan began after the annexation of Korea in 1910; during the Second World War many Koreans were conscripted as labourers. After 1945 most returned to Korea, but several hundred thousand remained. The 1952 San Francisco Peace Treaty stripped them of Japanese nationality, leaving them stateless until arrangements with South Korea (1965) and later special permanent residence legislation (1991).

Organizations

The community is associated with two main organizations: Mindan, aligned with South Korea, and Chongryon, aligned with North Korea, which historically operated schools and businesses serving the community.
