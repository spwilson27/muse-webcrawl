Carnaroli

Carnaroli is an Italian medium-grained rice grown in the Baraggia area of the provinces of Biella and Vercelli in Piedmont. It is traditionally used for making risotto, differing from the more common Arborio rice in its larger grain, higher starch content (which makes for a more creamy risotto), and firmer texture. Because of these qualities, it is often described as the "king of rices" or the "caviar of rice".

Characteristics

Carnaroli grains are among the largest of the Italian superfino rice varieties. The grain has a high content of amylose, a starch that helps the grain keep its shape during the slow cooking of risotto and absorb liquid and flavourings while releasing enough amylopectin to give the dish its characteristic creamy consistency without becoming gluey. Compared with Arborio, Carnaroli is less likely to overcook and is considered more forgiving in risotto preparation — though it is also more expensive and somewhat harder to find outside Italy.

History

Carnaroli was developed in 1945 by the rice grower Ettore De Vecchi, who crossed Vialone Nano with Lencino rice on his farm at Paullo, near Milan. The variety takes its name from Professor Emiliano Carnaroli, president of the Ente Nazionale Risi (National Rice Authority), with whom De Vecchi had discussed the new variety. It is classed as a superfino rice under Italian law and is prized in the regional cuisines of Piedmont and Lombardy.

Culinary use

Carnaroli is the rice of choice for classic risottos such as risotto alla milanese. Its grains are also used in rice salads and other dishes where distinct, firm grains are desired. Chefs often hold Carnaroli longer during the mantecatura (the final beating-in of butter and cheese) to exploit its starch release.
