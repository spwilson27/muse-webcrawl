Hypodermic needle

A hypodermic needle (from Greek hypo-, under, and derma, skin) is a very thin, hollow tube with one sharp tip, commonly used with a syringe to inject substances into the body or extract fluids from it, for example to draw blood.

Design

Needles are usually made of stainless steel with a bevelled point. Their diameter is specified by the Birmingham gauge: higher gauge numbers indicate thinner needles. Lengths and gauges are chosen for the route of administration — intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, or intradermal — and for the viscosity of the fluid.

History and safety

The hollow needle was developed in the mid-19th century by physicians including Alexander Wood and Charles Pravaz, enabling modern injection therapy. Single-use disposable needles are now standard to prevent transmission of blood-borne infections; needlestick injuries and safe disposal in sharps containers are major occupational-health concerns.
