Special constable

A special constable or special police constable (SC or SPC) is generally an auxiliary or part-time member of a police service. In many jurisdictions, notably in the United Kingdom, Canada, and parts of the Commonwealth, special constables are sworn officers who possess the same powers of arrest and enforcement as regular police officers, but typically serve as volunteers or in limited-duty roles.

A special constable patrolling alongside regular officers in England.

United Kingdom

In England and Wales, special constables are volunteer police officers who hold the office of constable and have full police powers throughout England and Wales. They wear the same uniform as regular officers and are organised within each territorial force as the Special Constabulary. The institution grew out of the Special Constables Act 1831, and specials were mobilised in large numbers during both world wars and the 1984–85 miners' strike. Numbers have fluctuated: there were roughly 20,000 specials in England and Wales in the early 2010s, with a long-term decline since. The Metropolitan Police Special Constabulary is the largest single body.

Canada

In Canada, special constables are peace officers appointed under provincial police acts. They include transit enforcement officers (such as those of the TTC in Toronto), university campus police, and officers employed by agencies such as the Niagara Parks Police. Their powers are typically confined to the property or system of their employer.

Scotland and Northern Ireland

Scotland's Special Constabulary operates within Police Scotland on similar volunteer lines. In Northern Ireland the role historically included the part-time reservists of the RUC and the controversial Ulster Special Constabulary (the "B-Specials"), disbanded in 1970.

Other jurisdictions

The term is used in several Australian states for certain appointed officers, in Hong Kong for the Hong Kong Auxiliary Police Force's antecedents, and in the United States in some municipalities, where "special police" may be licensed private officers with arrest powers on specified premises — a different model from the Commonwealth volunteer constable.
