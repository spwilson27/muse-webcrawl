Basler Fasnacht

The Basler Fasnacht is the carnival of Basel, Switzerland, and the country's largest and most famous carnival. Unlike the carnivals of the Rhineland, which precede Lent, the Basel Fasnacht begins on the Monday after Ash Wednesday and lasts exactly 72 hours, ending on Thursday morning. In 2017 it was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Morgestraich

The carnival opens at 4 a.m. on Monday with the Morgestraich ("morning prank"): all lights in the old town are extinguished, and drummers and pipers, wearing elaborate painted masks and carrying illuminated lanterns, march through the darkened streets playing the traditional march. Spectators traditionally eat flour soup (Mählsuppe) and onion or cheese tart.

Cliques and lanterns

Participants are organised into groups called Cliques, which spend the year preparing costumes, masks (Larve) and large painted lanterns satirising a chosen theme (Sujet), often drawn from local or world politics. On Monday and Wednesday afternoons vast cortège processions parade through the city, while on Tuesday evening Gugge brass bands perform in the streets.

Traditions

A distinctive feature is the Schnitzelbank, satirical verses sung in Basel dialect. Throwing confetti (Räppli) is a hallmark; by tradition only single-coloured confetti is thrown, each clique using its own colour.
