Belarus–Russia relations

Countries: Belarus and Russia

Framework: Union State (1999 treaty)

Belarus–Russia relations are among the closest of any post-Soviet pair. The two states are bound by the Union State treaty of 1999, the Commonwealth of Independent States and the Eurasian Economic Union.

History

After the USSR dissolved in 1991, Belarus under Alexander Lukashenko (from 1994) pursued reintegration with Russia in exchange for subsidised energy and market access. Integration repeatedly stalled over sovereignty, currency and energy prices.

Deepening dependence

After the disputed 2020 Belarusian election and ensuing protests, Lukashenko relied heavily on Moscow's support. In 2022 Belarus allowed Russian forces to attack Ukraine from its territory, bringing Western sanctions, and in 2023 Russia announced deployment of tactical nuclear weapons in Belarus.

Trade and energy

Russia is Belarus's dominant trade partner and energy supplier; disputes over oil and gas prices have periodically strained the relationship despite its strategic closeness.
