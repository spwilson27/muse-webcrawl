Central Armed Police Forces

Central Armed Police Forces
Country: India
Administered by: Ministry of Home Affairs
Total strength: about 1 million personnel

The Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF) are the uniformed paramilitary police organisations of India under the authority of the Ministry of Home Affairs. The name replaced the older term "Central Paramilitary Forces" in 2011.

Component forces

The CAPF comprises the Border Security Force (BSF), Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) — the largest, with over 300,000 personnel — Central Industrial Security Force (CISF), Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP), Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB), Assam Rifles and the National Security Guard (NSG), though Assam Rifles is operationally controlled by the Army and NSG is a special federal contingency force.

Roles

The forces guard India's borders, protect industrial installations and airports, conduct counter-insurgency and anti-Naxal operations, assist states in maintaining public order, and provide disaster relief. Officers are recruited largely through examinations conducted by the Union Public Service Commission and the Staff Selection Commission.
