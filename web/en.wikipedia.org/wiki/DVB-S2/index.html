DVB-S2

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

DVB-S2 is the second-generation specification for satellite broadcasting, developed by the DVB Project and standardised by ETSI as EN 302 307 in 2005. It is the successor to DVB-S and was designed to deliver substantially higher throughput for direct-to-home television, high-definition services, professional contribution links and IP-based satellite distribution.

Main features

  • Forward error correction: DVB-S2 replaces the convolutional coding of DVB-S with a powerful concatenation of LDPC (low-density parity check) and BCH codes, operating close to the Shannon limit.
  • Modulation schemes: QPSK, 8PSK, 16APSK and 32APSK, with code rates ranging from 1/4 to 9/10.
  • Adaptive coding and modulation (ACM): For interactive point-to-point links, the modulation and code rate can be adapted frame by frame to changing channel conditions such as rain fade.
  • Generic stream encapsulation: In addition to MPEG transport streams, DVB-S2 can carry generic continuous or packetised streams, simplifying the carriage of IP data.
  • Variable coding and modulation (VCM): Different robustness levels can be applied to different services within the same carrier.

Together these improvements yield a capacity gain of roughly 30 percent over DVB-S under identical link conditions, and much more for professional applications using the higher-order modulations.

Applications

DVB-S2 became the standard transmission format for HDTV satellite platforms worldwide and is widely used for news gathering, programme contribution, VSAT networks and satellite broadband systems. Most modern satellite receivers support both DVB-S and DVB-S2.

DVB-S2X

An optional extension, DVB-S2X (EN 302 307-2), was published in 2014. It adds finer modulation and coding granularity, lower roll-off factors, super-frame formats and additional scrambling sequences, and is aimed mainly at professional, DSNG and high-throughput satellite applications.
