A Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols

King's College Chapel, Cambridge

A Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols is a service of Christian worship traditionally celebrated on or near Christmas Eve. The story of the fall of humanity, the promise of the Messiah, and the birth of Jesus is told in nine short Bible readings (lessons) drawn from Genesis, the prophetic books, and the Gospels, interspersed with the singing of Christmas carols, hymns, and choir anthems.

Origins

The format was devised by Edward White Benson, then Bishop of Truro in Cornwall, for use on Christmas Eve 1880 in the temporary wooden cathedral there. Benson's son A. C. Benson later recalled that his father arranged the service "to keep men out of the pubs on Christmas Eve". The format was taken up widely, and in 1918 Eric Milner-White, Dean of King's College, Cambridge, adapted it for the college chapel, where it has been held every year since — including throughout the Second World War, when the ancient glass had been removed from the chapel windows and the college's name was not broadcast for security reasons.

The King's College service

The service at King's begins with the hymn "Once in Royal David's City", whose first verse is sung unaccompanied by a solo treble, chosen shortly before the service from among the choristers of the Choir of King's College. A bidding prayer, written by Milner-White, invites the congregation to "go in heart and mind to Bethlehem". The nine lessons are read by representatives of the college and the city of Cambridge, rising in seniority to the final lesson (John 1:1–14), read by the Provost. Since 1982 the college has commissioned a new carol each year from a contemporary composer; composers have included Judith Weir, Thomas Adès, and Harrison Birtwistle.

Broadcasting

The BBC began broadcasting the service in 1928, and it has been transmitted on the BBC World Service and radio stations worldwide, becoming one of the longest-running annual broadcasts in the world. A television version, Carols from King's, recorded separately in early December, has been shown since 1954. Because demand for seats far exceeds the chapel's capacity, admission is by ballot and ticket holders traditionally queue from the early morning.

The nine lessons

  1. Genesis 3:8–19 — God tells Adam of the consequences of the Fall.
  2. Genesis 22:15–18 — God promises to bless Abraham's descendants.
  3. Isaiah 9:2, 6–7 — the prophet foretells the coming of a saviour.
  4. Isaiah 11:1–3a, 4a, 6–9 — the peace that the Messiah will bring.
  5. Luke 1:26–35, 38 — the Angel Gabriel visits the Virgin Mary.
  6. Luke 2:1, 3–7 — Luke tells of the birth of Jesus.
  7. Luke 2:8–16 — the shepherds go to the manger.
  8. Matthew 2:1–12 — the Magi are led by the star to Jesus.
  9. John 1:1–14 — John unfolds the mystery of the Incarnation.
