10-second barrier

The 10-second barrier is the physical and psychological milestone in the men's 100 metres sprint of running the distance in under ten seconds. Once thought impossible, it became a defining benchmark of elite sprinting.

History

The first man to run 100 metres in under 10 seconds with hand timing was Armin Hary of West Germany in 1960. The first electronically timed sub-10 run was by Jim Hines of the United States, who clocked 9.95 seconds winning gold at the altitude-assisted 1968 Mexico City Olympics, setting a world record that stood for nearly 15 years.

Spread of sub-10 sprinting

Since then well over a hundred sprinters have broken the barrier under legal conditions. Carl Lewis, Maurice Greene, and Usain Bolt — whose 9.58 world record was set in 2009 — are among the most prominent. The barrier remains a symbol of world-class speed; the women's equivalent mark is often taken as 11 seconds.
