Reserve Bank of India

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is India's central bank and the regulatory body for the Indian banking system. Established on 1 April 1935 under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, it issues the Indian rupee, conducts monetary policy, regulates banks and payment systems, and manages the country's foreign exchange reserves. Its headquarters are in Mumbai.

History

The RBI began as a privately owned institution, modelled partly on recommendations of the Hilton Young Commission (1926). It was nationalised on 1 January 1949. Initially headquartered in Calcutta (Kolkata), it moved permanently to Bombay (Mumbai) in 1937. Early governors included Sir Osborne Smith and Sir C. D. Deshmukh, the first Indian governor.

Functions

The RBI is the sole issuer of currency notes (one-rupee notes and coins are issued by the Ministry of Finance), banker to the government and to banks, and lender of last resort. Since 2016 monetary policy has been set by a six-member Monetary Policy Committee targeting consumer price inflation of 4% within a ±2% band. The Bank regulates commercial and cooperative banks, non-banking financial companies and payment systems (including UPI oversight via the NPCI), manages the foreign exchange market under FEMA, and promotes financial inclusion.
