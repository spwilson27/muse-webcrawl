Charge (heraldry)

In heraldry, a charge is any emblem or device occupying the field of a shield, or borne on another charge such as a cross or an ordinary. Charges include animals, plants, objects, geometric figures and mythical creatures.

Types

Charges are distinguished from ordinaries, the simple geometric bands and shapes that cross the field. Common charges include the lion, eagle, fleur-de-lis, rose, star (mullet), crescent, and cross variants, as well as everyday objects such as keys, swords and ships.

Rules and description

In blazon, charges are described by their type, number, tincture and attitude or posture — for example, a lion rampant or an eagle displayed. Their arrangement follows conventions such as the rule of tincture, which discourages placing metal on metal or colour on colour.
