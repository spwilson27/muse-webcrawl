Cristian Mungiu

Born: April 27, 1968, Iași, Romania
Occupation: Film director, screenwriter, producer

Cristian Mungiu (born 27 April 1968) is a Romanian filmmaker. In 2007 he won the Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival for his film 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days, becoming the first Romanian filmmaker to win the festival's top prize. He is a leading figure of the Romanian New Wave.

Career

Mungiu studied English literature at the University of Iași and worked as a teacher and journalist before studying film directing at the Academy of Theatre and Film in Bucharest. His feature debut, Occident (2002), screened in the Directors' Fortnight at Cannes. 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days (2007), a drama about an illegal abortion in communist Romania, won the Palme d'Or and the FIPRESCI Prize.

His subsequent films include Tales from the Golden Age (2009), an anthology of urban legends from the Ceaușescu era; Beyond the Hills (2012), which won Best Screenplay at Cannes and Best Actress awards for its two leads; Graduation (2016), which earned him the Cannes Best Director Award; and R.M.N. (2022), which premiered in competition at Cannes. Mungiu is also a producer and founder of the production house Mobra Films.
