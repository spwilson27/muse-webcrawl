MUSH

A MUSH (Multi-User Shared Hallucination) is a text-based online social medium and role-playing environment, descended from MUDs and MUCKs. MUSHes are software servers that multiple users can connect to simultaneously to explore a shared virtual world, converse, and role-play — often in settings drawn from fiction or original worlds.

Characteristics

Unlike combat-oriented MUDs, MUSHes typically emphasise cooperative storytelling, character development, and social interaction. They are built on a persistent database of rooms, exits, and objects that players can extend using an internal scripting language ("softcode"). Well-known codebases include TinyMUSH, PennMUSH, TinyMUX, and RhostMUSH. Players traditionally connect via Telnet or specialised MUD clients.

History

The first MUSH, TinyMUSH, appeared in 1989, derived from TinyMUD. MUSHes flourished in the 1990s, hosting communities themed around works such as Anne McCaffrey's Dragonriders of Pern, White Wolf's World of Darkness, and various television franchises. Although their numbers have declined with the rise of graphical games and social media, a number of long-running MUSHes remain active.
