Drilling rig

A drilling rig is an integrated system that drills wells, such as oil or water wells, into the earth's subsurface. Drilling rigs can be massive structures housing equipment used to drill wells that tap natural gas and petroleum reservoirs, or they can be small enough to be moved manually by one person, as in augers.

Components

The central components include the derrick or mast, the drawworks, the rotary table or top drive, the drill string and drill bit, mud pumps that circulate drilling fluid, and a blowout preventer that seals the well under pressure. Power comes from diesel engines or electric drives.

Types

Land rigs are classified by depth capacity (light, medium, heavy, ultra-heavy). Offshore rigs include jackup rigs, semi-submersibles, and drillships, each suited to different water depths. Drilling rigs also bore water wells, geothermal wells, and mineral exploration holes.
