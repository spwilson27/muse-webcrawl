Gate

A gate or gateway is a point of entry to or from a space enclosed by walls, or a hinged barrier used to close and secure an opening in a wall, fence or hedge. Gates may be purely decorative or functional; they can be controlled by a gatekeeper, locked, or automated. Large gates on fortified towns and castles may include murder holes, drawbridges and gatehouses as defensive features.

Types

Gates range from small garden gates to monumental city and temple gates. Common types include field gates and kissing gates for livestock enclosures and footpaths, lychgates at churchyard entrances, turnstiles controlling pedestrian flow, water gates and lock gates on canals, level-crossing gates on railways, and airport boarding gates. City gates in defensive walls were historically the main controlled access points of a town, used for taxation of goods and defence.

Materials and operation

Gates are typically made of wood, wrought or cast metal, or modern materials such as aluminium and steel mesh, hung on hinges or mounted on sliding tracks. Many modern gates are opened automatically by electric motors, activated by remote controls, keypads, loop detectors or access-control systems, sometimes paired with intercoms or cameras.

In culture

Gates figure widely in symbolism and religion — the gates of heaven and hell, the Ishtar Gate of Babylon, and triumphal and memorial gateways such as India's Gateway of India and the Brandenburg Gate. The word is also used metaphorically for scandals (the "-gate" suffix after Watergate) and in computing for logic gates.
