Hypopnea

Specialty: Sleep medicine
Definition: Abnormally shallow breathing or reduced airflow during sleep

Hypopnea is overly shallow breathing or an abnormally low respiratory rate. In sleep medicine, a hypopnea event is a partial reduction of airflow during sleep — by scoring criteria, a decrease of at least 30% in airflow lasting at least 10 seconds, accompanied by either a drop in oxygen saturation of 3–4% or an arousal from sleep. Hypopneas, together with apneas (complete pauses), make up the apnea–hypopnea index (AHI) used to diagnose and grade obstructive sleep apnea.

Mechanism

Obstructive hypopneas occur when the upper airway narrows but does not fully collapse, increasing airflow resistance; snoring often intensifies during these events. Central hypopneas reflect reduced output from the brainstem respiratory centres. Both fragment sleep and can cause intermittent hypoxemia, contributing to daytime sleepiness and cardiovascular strain.

Diagnosis and treatment

Hypopneas are identified during polysomnography or home sleep apnea testing using airflow, effort, and oximetry channels; the precise scoring rule applied (3% versus 4% desaturation) can substantially change the computed AHI. Treatment of obstructive events mirrors that of sleep apnea generally: continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), weight management, oral appliances, positional therapy, and surgery in selected cases.
