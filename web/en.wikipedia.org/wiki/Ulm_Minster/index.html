Ulm Minster

Ulm Minster (German: Ulmer Münster) is a Lutheran church in Ulm, Germany, famous for having the tallest church steeple in the world, at 161.5 metres (530 feet). Construction began in the Gothic style in 1377, financed by the citizens of the free imperial city, and proceeded in phases over five centuries.

Construction

The foundation stone was laid in 1377, and the choir and nave were roofed by the early 15th century under master builders including Ulrich of Ensingen. Work halted in the 16th century at the Reformation, when the church became Protestant, and the steeple was only completed in 1890 following 19th-century restoration, to designs based on the medieval plans.

Features

Visitors can climb 768 steps to a viewing platform near the top of the spire. Inside are notable works of late Gothic art, including the choir stalls carved by Jörg Syrlin the Elder and a tabernacle over 26 metres high. The minster survived Second World War bombing that destroyed much of central Ulm.
