Hagen (legend)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hagen casts the Nibelung hoard into the Rhine.

Hagen (German: Hagen von Tronje; Old Norse: Högni) is a figure of Germanic heroic legend, known chiefly from the Middle High German epic the Nibelungenlied (c. 1200) and from related Norse and continental traditions. He is the grim, one-eyed vassal and counsellor of the Burgundian kings, the slayer of the hero Siegfried, and the principal antagonist of Kriemhild's revenge — a character defined by absolute loyalty (triuwe) to his lords, whatever crimes that loyalty demands.

In the Nibelungenlied

Hagen, brother of the chaplain Dankwart and kinsman of King Gunther, is introduced as the most knowledgeable of the Burgundian court: he alone recognises Siegfried on first sight and recounts his deeds. When the quarrel of the queens (Brunhild and Kriemhild) humiliates the Burgundians, Hagen vows vengeance. Tricking Kriemhild into revealing the one vulnerable spot on Siegfried's body — between the shoulder blades, where a linden leaf fell while the hero bathed in dragon's blood — he murders Siegfried with a spear during a hunt.

Hagen later sinks the Nibelung treasure in the Rhine at Lochheim to keep it from Kriemhild, and counsels the Burgundians to accept her invitation to Etzel's (Attila's) court despite knowing it is a trap. In the final battle in the hall of the Huns, Hagen kills Kriemhild's young son Ortlieb and countless Hunnish warriors, and is captured bound by Dietrich of Bern. Kriemhild beheads him with Siegfried's sword Balmung — the act for which Hildebrand in turn slays her.

Other traditions

In the Norse Poetic Edda and Völsunga saga, Högni is instead Gudrun's brother, a far more sympathetic figure who laughs while his heart is cut out; in the Atlakviða he dies defying Atli. In the Latin Waltharius (10th century), Hagano appears as a former hostage at Attila's court and friend of Walter of Aquitaine. In the Þiðrekssaga Hagen/Högni is a half-brother of the Burgundian kings, of elven descent.

Reception

Hagen is the model of the loyal vassal in German literary history; his name became proverbial for ruthless feudal fidelity. He is a major character (bass-baritone) in Wagner's Götterdämmerung, where he is Alberich's son, and has been played on screen in Fritz Lang's Die Nibelungen (1924) and later adaptations.
