Lizard

Lizards are a widespread group of squamate reptiles, with over 7,000 species, ranging across all continents except Antarctica, as well as most oceanic island chains. The group is paraphyletic in traditional classification, since it excludes the snakes and amphisbaenians, which are nested within the lizard radiation; in cladistic terms all three together form the order Squamata.

Biology

Lizards generally have small heads, long bodies and tails, and four limbs — though limb reduction or loss has evolved repeatedly, as in glass lizards and skinks. Most are ectothermic ("cold-blooded") and regulate body temperature behaviourally. Many can shed their tails voluntarily (autotomy) to escape predators, regrowing a shorter replacement. Chameleons can change colour; geckos have adhesive toe pads that allow them to climb smooth surfaces; and some species, such as the Komodo dragon, are venomous.

Sizes range from tiny chameleons and geckos of a few centimetres to the Komodo dragon, which reaches 3 metres and over 70 kg. Most lizards are carnivorous insectivores, though iguanas and some other groups are herbivorous, and a few species give live birth or reproduce by parthenogenesis.

Distribution and habitat

Lizards occupy habitats from rainforest canopy to desert. They are especially diverse in Australia and Southeast Asia. Because they are ectotherms, few species live in cold climates, though the viviparous lizard ranges inside the Arctic Circle.

Relation to humans

Many species are kept as pets, including bearded dragons, leopard geckos and green iguanas. Lizards feature prominently in the mythology of many cultures, and most species are harmless to humans; only the Gila monster, Mexican beaded lizard and Komodo dragon pose significant venom risks.
