A B-double (also B-train in Canada and South Africa) is a truck-trailer combination in which a prime mover hauls two semi-trailers, the second coupled by a fifth wheel mounted on the rear of the first (lead or "A") trailer. This distinguishes it from an A-double (road train style), where the second trailer rides on a converter dolly. Because both trailers pivot on fifth wheels, the B-double is more stable and manoeuvrable than an equivalent A-double, reversing more easily and tracking better.

Operation

B-doubles were developed in Canada in the 1970s–80s and adopted widely in Australia from the late 1980s, where they became the workhorse of interstate linehaul freight. A standard Australian B-double is up to 26 metres long with a gross combination mass of about 62.5 tonnes (68 tonnes under higher mass limits). They typically run nine axles and carry payloads around 40 tonnes — roughly 60–70% more freight than a single semi-trailer for a modest increase in fuel use and road wear. Related configurations include B-triples and quad-axle "super B" variants in Canada.

Safety and regulation

Studies in Australia and Canada generally find B-doubles have lower crash rates per tonne-kilometre than conventional semi-trailers, attributed to stability, professional fleets and restricted route networks; conversely their length raises overtaking and urban-access concerns, so they are confined to approved routes in most jurisdictions.
