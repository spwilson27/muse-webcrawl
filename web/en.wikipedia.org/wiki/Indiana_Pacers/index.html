The Indiana Pacers are an American professional basketball team based in Indianapolis. They compete in the National Basketball Association (NBA) as a member of the Eastern Conference Central Division. The team was founded in 1967 as a charter member of the American Basketball Association (ABA) and joined the NBA in 1976 as part of the ABA–NBA merger.

History

The Pacers were the most successful team in ABA history, winning three championships (1970, 1972, 1973) behind players such as Roger Brown, Mel Daniels and George McGinnis, coached for much of that era by Bobby "Slick" Leonard. In the NBA, the franchise's best-known era came in the 1990s behind Reggie Miller, whose clutch shooting defined a fierce rivalry with the New York Knicks; the Pacers reached the NBA Finals in 2000, losing to the Los Angeles Lakers. Later teams built around Paul George contended in the Eastern Conference finals in the 2010s, and in 2025 the Pacers, led by Tyrese Haliburton, reached the NBA Finals for the second time.

Name

The name "Pacers" reflects Indiana's history with harness racing pacers and the pace car used in the Indianapolis 500, held in the state.
