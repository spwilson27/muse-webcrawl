Peppermint

Peppermint (Mentha × piperita) is a hybrid mint, a cross between watermint (Mentha aquatica) and spearmint (Mentha spicata), indigenous to Europe and the Middle East and now widely cultivated throughout the world. The plant is a sterile hybrid that reproduces vegetatively by stolons.

Description

Peppermint is a herbaceous perennial growing 30–90 cm tall, with smooth, dark-green leaves often tinged with purple and small pink or lilac flowers in whorls. It was first described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 from specimens collected in England.

Uses

The leaves and the essential oil distilled from them are used as a flavouring in foods, confectionery (especially candy canes and chocolates), chewing gum, toothpaste, and liqueurs such as crème de menthe. The oil's principal component is menthol (roughly 40%), with menthone and other compounds; it is responsible for the characteristic cooling sensation, produced by triggering cold-sensitive receptors in the skin and mucous membranes.

Medicinal use

Peppermint tea is a popular herbal infusion, and peppermint oil has evidence of modest benefit in irritable bowel syndrome when taken in enteric-coated capsules. Peppermint is also a traditional remedy for indigestion and headaches, though it can aggravate gastro-oesophageal reflux in some people.
