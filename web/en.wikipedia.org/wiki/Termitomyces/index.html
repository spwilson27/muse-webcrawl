Termitomyces

Termitomyces is a genus of agaric fungi in the family Lyophyllaceae. Its species live in obligate mutualistic symbiosis with fungus-growing termites of the subfamily Macrotermitinae, which cultivate the fungi on combs of chewed plant material inside their nests in Africa and Asia.

Ecology

The termites provide substrate and a controlled environment; the fungus breaks down lignocellulose and produces nutrient-rich mycelium and fungal nodules (mycotêtes) that the termites eat. Periodically, the fungi produce large edible mushrooms that emerge from termite mounds after rains. Species such as Termitomyces titanicus, among the largest edible mushrooms in the world, and T. clypeatus are prized foods in Africa and Asia.

Taxonomy

The genus was described by Roger Heim in 1942. Around 30–40 species are recognised, and the association between the fungi and their termite hosts is thought to have originated roughly 30 million years ago.
