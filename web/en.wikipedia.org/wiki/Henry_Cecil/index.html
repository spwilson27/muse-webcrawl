Henry Cecil

Sir Henry Richard Amherst Cecil (11 January 1943 – 11 June 2013) was a British flat racing horse trainer. Based at Warren Place stables in Newmarket, he was champion trainer ten times and trained 25 English Classic winners, including four winners of the Epsom Derby. In the later part of his career he trained the undefeated Frankel, widely regarded as one of the greatest racehorses of all time.

Career

Cecil took over the Warren Place yard from his stepfather, Cecil Boyd-Rochfort's successor Noel Murless, his father-in-law, in 1969. Success came quickly, and through the 1970s and 1980s he dominated British racing for owners such as Prince Khalid Abdullah. A mid-career decline in the early 2000s — marked by personal setbacks and the loss of major owners — was followed by a celebrated renaissance, crowned by Frankel's fourteen-race unbeaten career from 2010 to 2012.

Honours

Cecil was knighted in the 2011 Birthday Honours for services to horse racing. He died of cancer in 2013. His total of Classic victories and his handling of horses such as Slip Anchor, Reference Point, Oh So Sharp, and Frankel secure his reputation as one of the outstanding trainers in British racing history.
