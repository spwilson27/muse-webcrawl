The Know Nothings were a nativist political movement in the United States in the 1850s, officially the American Party. Members of the secretive movement, when asked about its activities, were instructed to reply "I know nothing," giving the group its popular name. The party arose in reaction to large-scale immigration, especially by Irish Catholics and Germans, and demanded longer naturalisation periods, restrictions on immigrant office-holding, and limits on the sale of liquor.

Origins

The movement grew out of secret societies such as the Order of the Star Spangled Banner, founded in New York in 1849. In the elections of 1854, Know Nothing candidates swept Massachusetts and won major victories elsewhere, capitalising on the collapse of the Whig Party over the slavery question.

Height and decline

The American Party nominated former president Millard Fillmore for the presidency in 1856; he won 21.5 percent of the popular vote and carried only Maryland. The movement split fatally over slavery, with northern members drifting to the new Republican Party, and by 1860 it had effectively disappeared.

Legacy

The Know Nothings are remembered as the most successful third-party nativist movement in American history, and their name has become a general label for anti-immigrant politics.
