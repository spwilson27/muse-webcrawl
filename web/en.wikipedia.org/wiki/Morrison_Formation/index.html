Morrison Formation

TypeGeologic formation
PeriodLate Jurassic (Kimmeridgian–Tithonian), ~156–147 million years ago
Named forMorrison, Colorado
RegionWestern United States (Colorado Plateau and Rocky Mountains)

The Morrison Formation is a distinctive sequence of Upper Jurassic sedimentary rock found in the western United States, named after the town of Morrison, Colorado, where Arthur Lakes discovered some of its first dinosaur fossils in 1877. It is one of the most fertile sources of dinosaur fossils in North America.

Geology

The formation consists of mudstone, sandstone, siltstone, and limestone deposited in rivers, floodplains, and lakes about 156 to 147 million years ago, during the Kimmeridgian and Tithonian ages. It underlies an area of about 1.5 million square kilometres across several states.

Palaeontology

The Morrison Formation has yielded famous dinosaurs including Allosaurus, Apatosaurus, Brachiosaurus, Diplodocus, Stegosaurus, and Camarasaurus. Its fossil quarries, such as Dinosaur National Monument, Como Bluff, and Bone Cabin Quarry, were central to the late-19th-century "Bone Wars" between Othniel Charles Marsh and Edward Drinker Cope. The formation is also an important source of uranium ore.
