Caste War of Yucatán

The Caste War of Yucatán (1847–1901) was a rebellion of the Maya people of the Yucatán Peninsula against the population of European descent, known locally as Yucatecos, who controlled the region's political and economic power. It was one of the most successful indigenous revolts in the Americas, with the Maya establishing an independent state centred on Chan Santa Cruz that survived for more than fifty years.

Origins

The war began in July 1847, amid political turmoil between rival factions in Mérida and Campeche, when Maya leaders including Manuel Antonio Ay, Jacinto Pat and Cecilio Chi rose against heavy taxes, forced labour and the loss of communal lands to expanding sugar and henequen plantations.

Course of the war

Maya forces overran much of the peninsula and came close to taking Mérida before withdrawing, reportedly to plant their crops. The Yucatecan government, desperate, offered sovereignty to foreign powers in exchange for aid. After 1850 the rebel Maya, organised around the cult of the "Talking Cross" at Chan Santa Cruz and supplied with arms through British Honduras (Belize), maintained a de facto independent state in what is now Quintana Roo.

End

Mexican federal troops under General Ignacio Bravo occupied Chan Santa Cruz in 1901, formally ending the war, though sporadic resistance continued for decades.
