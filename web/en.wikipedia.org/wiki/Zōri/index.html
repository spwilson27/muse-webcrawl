Zōri

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zōri (草履) are flat Japanese sandals made of rice straw or other plant fibers, cloth, lacquered wood, leather, or—most commonly today—synthetic materials. They are thong sandals held on the foot by a strap (hanao) passing between the big toe and the second toe, and are worn with traditional Japanese clothing such as the kimono, usually together with white tabi socks in formal settings.

Types and use

Zōri range from casual styles woven of straw or made of rubber to highly formal types covered in brocade or vinyl with raised soles. Traditionally, the sole was made by weaving rice straw; formal women's zōri may feature gold or silver brocade straps. They are distinguished from geta, which are raised wooden clogs, and from waraji, the simpler straw sandals once worn for everyday labor and travel.

Modern influence

The basic design of zōri inspired the modern flip-flop: after World War II, soldiers returning from Japan brought zōri home, and rubber versions manufactured in postwar Japan spread worldwide as inexpensive beach footwear.
