Inning

An inning in baseball and softball is one of the divisions of a game during which each team has a turn at bat. A regulation professional baseball game consists of nine innings; the visiting team bats in the "top" half of each inning and the home team in the "bottom" half, each half ending after three outs.

Terminology

The term derives from the Old English innung, "a taking in." In cricket, "innings" has a different meaning: a team's turn to bat as a whole (and takes the plural form even in the singular). A portion of an inning in which a pitcher completes three outs without allowing a run is sometimes described as a clean inning; a "1-2-3 inning" is one in which the pitcher retires the side in order.

Extra innings

If the score is tied after the final scheduled inning, play continues into extra innings until one team leads at the end of a complete inning. Some leagues use a tiebreaker rule placing a runner on second base at the start of each extra half-inning; Major League Baseball adopted this rule for regular-season games in 2020.

Shortened games

Games may be shortened by weather; in Major League Baseball a game becomes official ("regulation") after five innings (or four and a half if the home team leads). A complete game is one in which a single pitcher pitches all innings for his team.
