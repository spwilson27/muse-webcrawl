Avocet

The avocets are a genus, Recurvirostra, of wading birds in the family Recurvirostridae, which also includes the stilts. Avocets are distinctive for their long, slender, strongly upcurved bills, which they sweep from side to side through shallow water to detect invertebrate prey.

Species and distribution

There are four species: the pied avocet of temperate Europe and Asia; the American avocet of western North America; the red-necked avocet of Australia; and the Andean avocet of high-altitude lakes in the Andes. All inhabit shallow wetlands, salt pans, estuaries and brackish lagoons.

Behaviour

Avocets feed by sweeping the bill through water or soft mud, detecting crustaceans and aquatic insects by touch; they also peck prey from the surface. They nest on open ground near water, often colonially, laying three to five eggs in a simple scrape. The pied avocet is the emblem of the British Royal Society for the Protection of Birds (RSPB), marking its return as a breeding bird to Britain in the 1940s after an absence of about a century.
