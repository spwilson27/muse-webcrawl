Sofia Carmina Coppola (born May 14, 1971) is an American filmmaker and former actress. The daughter of director Francis Ford Coppola, she made her screen debut as an infant in her father's film The Godfather (1972). After appearing in The Godfather Part III (1990), she moved behind the camera and established herself as a leading auteur known for films about loneliness, privilege, and female interiority, often marked by dreamy visuals and contemporary indie-rock soundtracks.

Career

Following the short film Lick the Star (1998), Coppola's feature directorial debut, The Virgin Suicides (1999), adapted Jeffrey Eugenides' novel to critical acclaim. Her second feature, Lost in Translation (2003), starring Bill Murray and Scarlett Johansson, won her the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay; she became only the third woman nominated for the Academy Award for Best Director. Marie Antoinette (2006) divided critics with its anachronistic pop soundtrack but has been widely reappraised. Somewhere (2010) won the Golden Lion at the Venice Film Festival. Later films include The Bling Ring (2013), The Beguiled (2017) — for which she won Best Director at the Cannes Film Festival — On the Rocks (2020), and Priscilla (2023), a portrait of Priscilla Presley.

Personal life

Coppola was married to director Spike Jonze from 1999 to 2003. In 2011 she married Thomas Mars, lead singer of the French band Phoenix, with whom she has two daughters. Members of the extended Coppola family include her cousin, actor Nicolas Cage.
