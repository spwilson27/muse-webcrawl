Ted Hughes

Ted Hughes (1930–1998)
Born: Mytholmroyd, Yorkshire
Occupation: Poet, children's writer
Office: Poet Laureate (1984–1998)

Edward James Hughes OM OBE FRSL (17 August 1930 – 28 October 1998) was an English poet, translator, and children's writer, widely regarded as one of the greatest poets of his generation. He served as Poet Laureate of the United Kingdom from 1984 until his death.

Life

Raised in Mytholmroyd and Mexborough, Yorkshire, Hughes studied at Pembroke College, Cambridge, switching from English to anthropology and archaeology. In 1956 he married the American poet Sylvia Plath; their marriage and her death by suicide in 1963 shadowed his public life for decades. He later lived in Devon, co-founded the Arvon Foundation for creative writing, and campaigned on environmental issues.

Work

His first collection, The Hawk in the Rain (1957), announced a poetry of violence and vitality in nature. Major works include Lupercal, Crow (1970), Moortown, River, and translations of Ovid in Tales from Ovid. His children's books include The Iron Man (1968). His final collection, Birthday Letters (1998), addressed his relationship with Plath directly and won the Whitbread Prize.

Legacy

Hughes is commemorated by a memorial in Poets' Corner, Westminster Abbey, and by trails and festivals in his native Calder Valley.
