Malayalam calendar

The Malayalam calendar is a sidereal solar calendar used in the Indian state of Kerala. It is based on the Kollam Era (also known as Kollavarsham), whose epoch is 825 CE, traditionally associated with the founding of the port city of Kollam. The Malayalam year equals the Gregorian year plus 824 or 825 depending on the month.

Months

The twelve months of the Malayalam calendar correspond to the sidereal zodiacal signs:

Dhanumid-December – mid-January
Makarammid-January – mid-February

Medammid-April – mid-May
Edavammid-May – mid-June
Midhunammid-June – mid-July
Karkidakammid-July – mid-August

Use

The first day of Chingam (Chingam 1) is celebrated as the Malayalam New Year, while Vishu, the astronomical new year, falls on Medam 1 in mid-April. Onam, Kerala's principal harvest festival, falls in Chingam. The calendar remains widely used for religious festivals, astrology and traditional agriculture, alongside the Gregorian calendar.
