The Great Barrier Reef Marine Park protects a large part of Australia's Great Barrier Reef from damaging activities. It covers approximately 344,400 square kilometres off the coast of Queensland, extending about 2,300 km from near the tip of Cape York Peninsula to south of Biloela, and is one of the largest marine protected areas in the world. The park is managed by the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority (GBRMPA) together with the Queensland Government.

History

The park was established under the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Act 1975, following public campaigns against oil drilling and limestone mining on the reef in the late 1960s and early 1970s. The reef region was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1981. A major rezoning, the Representative Areas Program, took effect in 2004 and raised the proportion of the park in highly protected "no-take" green zones from under 5% to about 33%.

Zoning and management

The park is divided into zones that regulate activities: general use zones permit most fishing and collecting; habitat protection and conservation park zones restrict trawling and other damaging gear; marine national park (green) zones prohibit all extractive activity; and small preservation (pink) zones allow access only for research. Commercial fishing, tourism, shipping, and Traditional Owner use are managed through permits, an Environmental Management Charge on tourism, and joint field management with Queensland.

Threats

Principal threats include climate change — mass coral bleaching events occurred in 1998, 2002, 2016, 2017, 2020, and 2022 — declining water quality from agricultural runoff, outbreaks of the crown-of-thorns starfish, coastal development, and port and shipping activity. The Australian and Queensland governments' Reef 2050 Plan sets the long-term framework for addressing these pressures.
