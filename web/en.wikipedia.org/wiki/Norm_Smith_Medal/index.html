Norm Smith Medal

The Norm Smith Medal is an Australian rules football award presented annually to the player adjudged best on ground in the VFL/AFL Grand Final. It is named after Norm Smith, the Melbourne coach who led the Demons to six premierships between 1955 and 1964 and was named coach of the AFL Team of the Century. The medal was first awarded in 1979, when Carlton's Wayne Harmes won it in a performance famous for a desperate tap-on that set up the winning goal.

Placeholder: the medal awarded to the best player in the AFL Grand Final.

Voting

The winner is decided by a panel of five judges — typically former players, coaches and media representatives chaired by a past great — who award 3-2-1 votes. The votes are tallied on the day and the medal presented on the ground after the match, together with the premiership cup and medals.

Notable winners

Dustin Martin is the only player to have won the medal three times (2017, 2019, 2020), also winning Brownlow Medals in the same era. Other multiple winners include Gary Ablett Sr. (1989, in a losing side), Andrew McLeod, Luke Hodge and Gary Ayres. Maurice Rioli (1982), Gary Ablett (1989), Michael Long (1993), Chris Judd (2005) and Bobby Hill (2023) are among those to have won in either losing or otherwise memorable performances; players from the losing team have won on only a handful of occasions. The first winner from a losing side was Maurice Rioli of Richmond in 1982.
