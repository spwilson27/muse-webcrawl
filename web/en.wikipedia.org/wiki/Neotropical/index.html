Neotropical

The Neotropical realm is one of the eight biogeographic realms used to categorize the Earth's ecological regions. In biogeography it extends from central Mexico southward through Central America and nearly all of South America, including the tropical and temperate parts of the continent, as well as the Caribbean islands and southern Florida.

Flora and fauna

The realm is one of the most species-rich on Earth. The Amazon rainforest alone is home to roughly a tenth of the world's known species. Distinctive fauna include sloths, anteaters, New World monkeys, toucans, hummingbirds, poison dart frogs and cichlid fishes; many families, such as the hummingbirds, are found almost nowhere else. The realm's long isolation as an island continent, followed by the Great American Interchange after the formation of the Isthmus of Panama, shaped its distinctive assemblage.

Ecoregions

Major ecoregions include the Amazon and Atlantic forests, the Cerrado savannas, the Pantanal wetlands, the Andean páramo and the Patagonian steppe.
