The sands of southern Arabia, legendary home of the ʿAad

ʿAad (Arabic: عاد, ʿĀd) was an ancient Arab tribe frequently mentioned in the Quran. According to Islamic tradition, the ʿAad were a prosperous people who inhabited southern Arabia after the time of Noah, and who were destroyed by a violent wind for rejecting the message of their prophet, Hud. They are commonly associated with the legendary city of Iram of the Pillars.

In the Quran

The story of the ʿAad is told in several surahs, including Surah Al-A'raf, Surah Hud, Surah Ash-Shu'ara and Surah Al-Ahqaf — the latter named after the "wind-curved sandhills" said to be the tribe's homeland. The Quran describes the ʿAad as people of great physical stature and power who built monuments and lofty towers, and who boasted that no nation was stronger than they. When they refused to worship God alone and accused Hud of lying, they were destroyed by a furious, bitter wind (rih sarsar) that raged for seven nights and eight days.

Homeland and identity

Islamic geographers generally placed the land of the ʿAad in the deserts of southern Arabia, in or near the region of Hadhramaut and the empty quarter of Rub' al Khali, in present-day Yemen and Oman. The tribe is grouped by Arab genealogists with the other "perished Arabs" (al-ʿArab al-bāʾida), such as Thamud. Some scholars have connected the ʿAad with inscriptions of ancient South Arabia, although no direct historical attestation of the tribe has been established.
