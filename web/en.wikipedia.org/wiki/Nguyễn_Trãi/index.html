Nguyễn Trãi

Nguyễn Trãi (1380–1442), courtesy name Ức Trai, was a Vietnamese scholar, poet, and statesman who served as the chief strategist and adviser to Lê Lợi, founder of the Later Lê dynasty. He is celebrated in Vietnam as a national hero and a towering figure of Vietnamese literature and political thought.

Life and career

Nguyễn Trãi passed the imperial examinations in 1400 under the Hồ dynasty. When Ming China invaded and occupied Đại Việt in 1407, he eventually joined Lê Lợi's Lam Sơn uprising in 1418, drafting proclamations and diplomatic letters that helped secure victory over the Ming in 1427. His Bình Ngô đại cáo ("Great Proclamation upon the Pacification of the Wu", 1428) announced Vietnamese independence and is regarded as a founding declaration of the nation, notable for its emphasis on benevolence and the welfare of the people.

Death and legacy

After falling out of favour at court, Nguyễn Trãi retired to Côn Sơn. In 1442, following the sudden death of the young emperor Lê Thái Tông after visiting him, Nguyễn Trãi was accused of regicide and executed along with members of his family in the notorious Lệ Chi Viên affair. Twenty years later, Emperor Lê Thánh Tông officially exonerated him. His surviving works include the poetry collection Quốc âm thi tập, written in chữ Nôm, and he is honoured by UNESCO as a world cultural figure.
