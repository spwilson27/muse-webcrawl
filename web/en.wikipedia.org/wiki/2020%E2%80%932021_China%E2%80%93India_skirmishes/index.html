2020–2021 China–India skirmishes

Conflict details
Date5 May 2020 – 2021 (disengagements into 2022)
LocationLine of Actual Control, Ladakh and other sectors
ResultPhased disengagement and buffer zones agreed

The 2020–2021 China–India skirmishes were a series of confrontations between Chinese and Indian forces along the disputed Line of Actual Control (LAC), beginning in May 2020 with standoffs at multiple points in Ladakh, including Pangong Tso, the Galwan Valley, Gogra and Hot Springs.

Galwan Valley clash

On 15 June 2020, a violent hand-to-hand clash in the Galwan Valley — fought with clubs and improvised weapons under agreements restricting firearms — killed 20 Indian soldiers and at least four Chinese soldiers, the first combat deaths on the border since 1975. The clash sharply escalated tensions, triggering boycotts of Chinese goods in India, bans on Chinese apps, and a major military build-up on both sides.

Standoff and diplomacy

Tens of thousands of troops faced each other through the Himalayan winter. Talks between corps commanders and foreign ministers produced phased disengagement, beginning at Pangong Tso in February 2021 and later at Gogra and Hot Springs, with buffer zones established in friction areas.

Aftermath

Relations between the two countries remained strained for years, with India insisting that full restoration of ties required complete disengagement and de-escalation along the LAC.
