Ciona intestinalis

Ciona intestinalis (sometimes known as the vase tunicate) is an ascidian, a marine invertebrate in the class Ascidiacea (sea squirts). It is a globally distributed cosmopolitan species and an important model organism in developmental biology.

Description

Adults are solitary, soft-bodied filter feeders with a translucent tunic and two siphons, growing to about 15 cm. They attach to rocks, pilings and ship hulls, and are considered invasive in many harbours.

Model organism

As a chordate with a compact genome (sequenced in 2002), a fixed cell lineage and transparent tadpole-like larvae possessing a notochord, Ciona is used to study chordate evolution, embryogenesis and gene regulatory networks. Research has shown the name covered a species complex, with some populations reassigned to Ciona robusta.

Distribution

Originally Northern Hemisphere temperate waters, it has spread worldwide through shipping.
