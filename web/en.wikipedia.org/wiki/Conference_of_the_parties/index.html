Conference of the parties

A conference of the parties (COP) is the supreme governing body of an international convention or treaty, composed of representatives of the states that are parties to it. Conferences of the parties typically meet periodically to review implementation, adopt decisions and protocols, and set the direction of the treaty regime.

Delegates at a conference of the parties, the decision-making body of many international treaties.

Climate change

The term is most widely known from the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), whose Conference of the Parties has met annually since 1995. Milestone sessions include COP3 at Kyoto (1997), which adopted the Kyoto Protocol; COP15 at Copenhagen (2009); COP21 at Paris (2015), which adopted the Paris Agreement; and COP28 at Dubai (2023), whose outcome text called for transitioning away from fossil fuels. When the parties to the Kyoto Protocol or the Paris Agreement meet in conjunction with the COP, the sessions are known as the CMP and CMA respectively.

Other conventions

Conferences of the parties also govern the Convention on Biological Diversity, the UN Convention to Combat Desertification, the Basel, Rotterdam and Stockholm conventions on hazardous chemicals, CITES (regulating trade in endangered species), and the Ramsar Convention on wetlands, among many others.
