"Diary of a Madman" (Russian: Записки сумасшедшего, Zapiski sumasshedshego) is a short story by Nikolai Gogol, first published in 1835 in his collection Arabesques. Told entirely through the increasingly unhinged journal entries of its narrator, it is one of the earliest literary works to present a narrative wholly through the diary of a character descending into madness, and a classic of Gogol's Petersburg tales.

Plot

Aksenty Ivanovich Poprishchin is a lowly titular counsellor in a St Petersburg government department, employed sharpening quills for his director. Humiliated by his poverty and rank, and hopelessly in love with the director's daughter Sophie, he begins to overhear and read the correspondence of Sophie's little dog Madgie, which "reveals" court gossip. His diary dates grow progressively impossible — "the year 2000, April 43rd", "Martober 86" — as he becomes convinced that he is the missing King of Spain, Ferdinand VIII. Dragged to an asylum he believes is the Spanish court, he is beaten and doused with cold water, and the story closes with his broken plea: "Mother, save your poor son!"

Themes

The story satirises the Table of Ranks and the dehumanising bureaucracy of Nicholas I's Russia: Poprishchin's madness is the only escape the system leaves him, granting him in delusion the rank and dignity reality denies. Its unreliable narration, shifting dates, and mixture of comedy and terror influenced later writers, including Dostoevsky; the tale has been widely adapted for the stage as a one-man play, and Lu Xun borrowed its title for his own famous 1918 story.
