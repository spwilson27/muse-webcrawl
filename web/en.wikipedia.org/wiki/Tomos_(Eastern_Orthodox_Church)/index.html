Tomos (Eastern Orthodox Church)

TypeEcclesiastical decree
TraditionEastern Orthodox Church

A tomos (Greek: τόμος, "a slice, piece, section, volume") in the Eastern Orthodox Church is a formal decree or edict issued by a synod of bishops, most famously a decree of a patriarch granting autocephaly (self-governing status) to a local church, or pronouncing on a doctrinal matter.

Usage

Tomoi have been issued on major theological questions — for example, the fourteenth-century tomoi associated with the hesychast councils affirming the theology of Gregory Palamas — and on questions of church order. In the modern era the term is best known for tomoi of autocephaly granted by the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople to national churches.

Tomos of autocephaly for Ukraine

The most widely reported recent example is the tomos granting autocephaly to the Orthodox Church of Ukraine, signed by Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew I on January 5, 2019, and presented in Istanbul the following day. The act, which followed a unification council in Kyiv in December 2018, was disputed by the Russian Orthodox Church and contributed to a schism between Moscow and Constantinople.
