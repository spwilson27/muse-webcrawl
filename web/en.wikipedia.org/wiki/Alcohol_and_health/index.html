Alcohol and health

Alcohol and health concerns the health effects of consuming alcoholic beverages (ethanol). Alcohol is a widely used recreational drug, but its consumption is associated with a wide range of adverse health outcomes. The World Health Organization has reported that alcohol contributed to more than 2.6 million deaths globally in 2019, and that it is a causal factor in more than 200 disease and injury conditions.

Short-term effects

Short-term effects of alcohol consumption include intoxication, dehydration, and, at high doses, alcohol poisoning, which can be fatal. Acute intoxication impairs judgement, coordination and reaction time, increasing the risk of accidents, injuries and violence.

Long-term effects

Long-term heavy drinking is associated with liver disease (including fatty liver, hepatitis and cirrhosis), cardiovascular disease, pancreatitis, weakened immune function, and an increased risk of several cancers, including cancers of the mouth, throat, oesophagus, liver, colon and breast. Ethanol is classified by the International Agency for Research on Cancer as a Group 1 carcinogen.

Dependence

Regular consumption can lead to tolerance and physical dependence. Alcohol use disorder is characterised by an impaired ability to stop or control drinking despite adverse consequences. Withdrawal from heavy, prolonged use can be dangerous and, in severe cases, fatal without medical supervision.

Debate over moderate consumption

Older observational studies suggested a "J-shaped" curve in which light drinkers had lower cardiovascular mortality than abstainers, but more recent analyses attribute much of this apparent benefit to methodological biases, such as including former drinkers who quit for health reasons in the abstainer group. Major health bodies increasingly conclude that no level of alcohol consumption is entirely safe.
