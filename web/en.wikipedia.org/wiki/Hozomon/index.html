Hozomon

The Hōzōmon (宝蔵門, "Treasure-House Gate") is the inner of two large entrance gates leading to Sensō-ji temple in Asakusa, Tokyo, Japan. Beyond it lies the temple's main hall and five-story pagoda.

Description

The two-story gate (nijūmon) stands about 22.7 metres tall and houses, on its upper floor, many of Sensō-ji's treasures, including a copy of the Lotus Sutra designated an Important Cultural Property of Japan — hence the name "Treasure-House Gate". The ground floor holds two guardian statues (Niō) and three large lanterns, including a great red chōchin. On the rear face hang two enormous waraji straw sandals, each about 4.5 metres long.

History

The original gate, built in the mid-10th century during the Heian period and rebuilt under Tokugawa Ieyasu, burned in an air raid in 1945. The present structure, of fireproof reinforced concrete with traditional styling, was erected in 1964 with a donation from industrialist Yonetarō Motoya.
