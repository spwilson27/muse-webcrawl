Loft

A loft is a building's upper storey or elevated area in a building, often under a roof, or, in modern usage, a large, adaptable open space converted from industrial or warehouse use into a dwelling or workplace. The word derives from the Old Norse lopt, meaning air or sky, and cognates have long denoted an attic or hayloft. Loft apartments are characterised by high ceilings, large windows, exposed structural elements such as brickwork, beams and ducting, and few interior partitions.

History of loft living

Loft living originated in the artists' districts of post-war New York City, above all SoHo in Manhattan, where painters and sculptors from the 1950s and 1960s occupied cheap, spacious floors of cast-iron manufacturing buildings — often illegally, since the spaces were zoned for industry. The large floor plates and north-facing industrial sash windows suited studios, and the practice spread as artists were priced or pushed east and into other cities. New York's 1982 Loft Law (Article 7-C of the Multiple Dwelling Law) recognised and regulated these conversions, granting tenants protections. Thereafter "loft" style became a mainstream real-estate fashion, and developers built new "soft lofts" imitating the aesthetic with finished surfaces, as distinct from "hard lofts" genuinely converted from industrial buildings.

Other uses of the term

  • Hayloft — an upper area of a barn for storing hay
  • Roof loft / attic — space beneath a pitched roof, often converted to bedrooms
  • Organ loft, choir loft, rood loft — elevated galleries in churches
  • Sail loft — a workshop where sails are cut and sewn
  • Pigeon loft — housing for homing pigeons
  • Loft (golf) — the angle of a clubface, unrelated in origin to the architectural sense

Cultural significance

Loft districts became emblematic of gentrification: artist colonisation raised the desirability of industrial neighbourhoods, which were then redeveloped for affluent residents. Sharon Zukin's Loft Living (1982) is the classic sociological study of the process in lower Manhattan.
