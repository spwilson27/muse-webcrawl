Short-time Fourier transform

The short-time Fourier transform (STFT) is a Fourier-related transform used to determine the frequency content of local sections of a signal as it changes over time. It is computed by sliding a window function along the signal and taking the discrete Fourier transform of each windowed segment:

STFT{x(t)}(τ, ω) = ∫ x(t) w(t − τ) e−jωt dt

where w(t) is a window such as the Hann window centred at time τ.

Spectrograms

The squared magnitude of the STFT is the spectrogram, a two-dimensional plot of frequency versus time with intensity showing magnitude — widely used to visualise speech, music, animal vocalisations and other non-stationary signals.

Resolution trade-off

The STFT's fixed window imposes a trade-off governed by the uncertainty principle: a short window gives good time resolution but poor frequency resolution, while a long window resolves frequencies finely but smears events in time. This limitation motivates alternatives such as wavelet transforms and the Wigner–Ville distribution. Dennis Gabor's 1946 formulation, using Gaussian-windowed "logons", is an early special case.

Applications

Applications include speech recognition and synthesis, audio effects such as time-stretching and pitch-shifting (via the phase vocoder), radar and sonar processing, and medical signal analysis.
