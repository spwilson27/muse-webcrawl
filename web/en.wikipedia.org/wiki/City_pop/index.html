City pop

City pop (Japanese: シティ・ポップ, shiti poppu) is a loosely defined subgenre of Japanese pop music (J-pop) that emerged in the late 1970s and peaked in the 1980s. It was originally termed as an offshoot of Japan's Western-influenced "new music" and drew on American soft rock, AOR, funk, disco, boogie and jazz fusion. Associated with Japan's economic boom, city pop evoked an urban, cosmopolitan lifestyle of leisure, driving and romance, with sophisticated arrangements and high production values.

City pop artwork characteristically depicted affluent urban and resort scenes.

History

The genre grew out of the work of bands such as Happy End and Tin Pan Alley in the 1970s, and artists including Tatsuro Yamashita, Mariya Takeuchi, Toshiki Kadomatsu, Anri, Taeko Onuki and Eiichi Ohtaki developed its characteristic sound. Yamashita's Ride on Time (1980) and Takeuchi's "Plastic Love" (1984) became signature songs. City pop declined with the collapse of Japan's asset price bubble at the end of the 1980s.

Revival

In the 2010s, city pop experienced a major international revival through the Internet. Vaporwave and future funk artists sampled it extensively, and "Plastic Love" went viral on YouTube in 2017, introducing the genre to a global audience. Reissues of albums by Takeuchi, Yamashita and others followed, and the genre is credited with influencing contemporary artists in Japan and abroad, including in the K-pop industry.

Characteristics

City pop is defined less by strict musical rules than by an aesthetic: breezy melodies, funk-influenced basslines, lush synthesiser and horn arrangements, and lyrics about city life, summer resorts and driving. Album covers by illustrators such as Hiroshi Nagai, with their pools, palm trees and convertibles, became iconic of the genre.
