Triketone

A triketone is an organic compound containing three ketone functional groups (carbonyl groups, C=O) in its molecule. Triketones are a subclass of the polycarbonyl compounds; when the three carbonyls are adjacent or separated by single methylene groups, the compounds often show distinctive reactivity, including ready enolization and the ability to act as chelating ligands for metal ions.

Examples and chemistry

Well-known examples include ninhydrin (2,2-dihydroxyindane-1,3-dione, the hydrated form of a cyclic vicinal triketone), famous as a reagent for detecting amino acids and used in forensic fingerprint development, and dehydroacetic acid derivatives used as preservatives. Acyclic 1,3,5-triketones can cyclize to form aromatic compounds such as phloroglucinol derivatives.

Triketone herbicides

The term is especially common in agrochemistry: the triketone herbicides, including mesotrione, sulcotrione, and tembotrione, are synthetic analogues of the natural product leptospermone. They kill weeds by inhibiting the enzyme 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate dioxygenase (HPPD), blocking carotenoid synthesis and causing bleaching of susceptible plants. They are widely used for weed control in maize (corn).
