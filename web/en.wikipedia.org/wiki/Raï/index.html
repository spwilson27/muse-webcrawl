Raï

Raï (Arabic: راي), sometimes written rai, is a form of Algerian folk music that dates back to the 1920s. Singers of raï are called cheb ("young") or cheba as opposed to sheikh, for older singers of chaabi. The tradition arose in the city of Oran, in the region of Oranie, from the poor and working-class quarters, and was long performed by women entertainers (the cheikhat) at weddings and in cabarets.

Development

Raï lyrics traditionally addressed social issues such as love, drink, poverty and colonial rule, often with frankness that scandalised respectable society. In the 1970s and 1980s the genre was modernised with synthesisers, drum machines and electric instruments — the so-called "pop raï" — producing international stars such as Khaled, Cheb Mami, Cheb Hasni and Faudel.

International success

From the 1980s, driven by the Algerian diaspora in France, raï achieved worldwide success; Khaled's songs "Didi" (1992) and "Aïcha" (1996) became global hits. In Algeria the genre survived hostility from Islamists during the civil war of the 1990s, in which Cheb Hasni was assassinated. UNESCO inscribed raï on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2022.
