Z Special Unit
Active1942 – 1946
CountryAustralia (Allied)
BranchServices Reconnaissance Department
TypeSpecial forces / reconnaissance
RoleCovert operations behind Japanese lines in the South West Pacific

Z Special Unit was a joint Allied special forces unit formed during the Second World War to operate behind Japanese lines in South East Asia. Predominantly Australian, Z Special Unit was a specialist reconnaissance and sabotage unit that operated as part of the Services Reconnaissance Department (SRD), also known by its cover name, Department C.

Operations

The unit's most famous operation was Operation Jaywick in September 1943, in which operatives paddled folboats from the captured Japanese fishing vessel Krait into Singapore Harbour and sank or damaged some 39,000 tons of Japanese shipping with limpet mines. A follow-up operation, Operation Rimau in 1944, ended in disaster: all 23 participants were killed or captured and executed.

Z Special Unit personnel also conducted reconnaissance and guerrilla operations in Borneo, including operations in support of the 1945 landings (Operation Oboe), and in Portuguese Timor and New Guinea. Many operatives were parachuted into remote areas to gather intelligence and organise local resistance. Training took place at secret facilities in Australia, including at Fraser Island (Z Experimental Station) and Cairns.

Legacy

The unit's traditions are carried on by the Australian Army's special forces, and the 1st Commando Regiment maintains historical links to M and Z Special Units. The vessel Krait is preserved at the Australian National Maritime Museum in Sydney.
