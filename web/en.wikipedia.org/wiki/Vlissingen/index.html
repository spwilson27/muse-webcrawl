Vlissingen

Vlissingen (English exonym: Flushing) is a municipality and a city in the southwestern Netherlands, in the province of Zeeland, on the northern bank of the Western Scheldt estuary at its mouth on the North Sea. With a population of around 44,000, it is a port, shipbuilding centre, and seaside resort, and forms part of the Walcheren peninsula together with the neighbouring provincial capital Middelburg.

History

Vlissingen received city rights in 1315 and grew as a fishing and trading port. It was one of the first Dutch towns to revolt against Spanish rule in 1572, and under the Treaty of Nonsuch (1585) it was held by England as a "cautionary town" until 1616. In the 17th century it was a major base of the Dutch East and West India Companies and of the Admiralty of Zeeland — and the birthplace of Admiral Michiel de Ruyter, whose statue overlooks the sea. The town was bombarded by the British in 1809 during the Walcheren Campaign and was heavily fought over and flooded in 1944 during the Battle of the Scheldt, when Allied forces cleared the estuary to open the port of Antwerp.

Economy and transport

The outer harbour (Vlissingen-Oost) is a deep-water port handling bulk cargo, offshore-energy components, and fruit imports; Damen Schelde Naval Shipbuilding builds warships for the Royal Netherlands Navy. Ferries for foot passengers and cyclists cross to Breskens across the Western Scheldt (the car ferry was replaced by the Western Scheldt Tunnel in 2003). Vlissingen is the terminus of the railway from Roosendaal and a popular domestic beach resort along its long south-facing boulevard.
