Ge Hong (283–343 CE), courtesy name Zhichuan, was a Chinese alchemist, physician and religious thinker of the Eastern Jin dynasty. His Baopuzi ("The Master Who Embraces Simplicity") is a foundational text of religious Daoism, combining Confucian ethics with the pursuit of physical immortality through alchemy, meditation and hygiene.

Life and work

Born into an aristocratic family fallen on hard times, Ge Hong studied with the alchemist Zheng Yin and married Bao Gu, herself a noted physician and acupuncturist. Seeking cinnabar for elixir experiments, he obtained a post in Guangdong and settled near Mount Luofu, where he completed his writings. The Inner Chapters of the Baopuzi catalogue elixirs (often based on cinnabar and gold), magical techniques and the grades of immortals; the Outer Chapters treat Confucian social philosophy.

His medical handbook, Emergency Formulas to Keep Up Your Sleeve, preserved practical remedies drawn from folk practice — including, famously, a description of qinghao preparations against fevers and the first clear account of smallpox. Tu Youyou credited a passage of Ge Hong as the inspiration for extracting artemisinin from sweet wormwood, work recognized by the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
