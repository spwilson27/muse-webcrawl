The Real World is an American reality television series produced by MTV and Bunim/Murray Productions. Premiering in 1992, it is widely credited with launching the modern reality TV genre: each season, a group of young adult strangers are selected to live together in a house in a new city, with cameras recording their lives continuously.

Format

The opening narration states the premise: "This is the true story of seven strangers, picked to live in a house, work together and have their lives taped, to find out what happens when people stop being polite and start getting real." Cast members speak directly to camera in confessional interviews. Seasons were set in cities including New York, Los Angeles, San Francisco, London, Miami, Seattle, Chicago, Las Vegas and New Orleans.

Cultural impact

The third season, The Real World: San Francisco (1994), featuring Pedro Zamora, an HIV-positive AIDS educator whose health declined during filming, was a landmark in American television and was praised by President Bill Clinton. The series tackled issues of race, sexuality, religion and politics, and its format influenced countless later reality programmes, including Big Brother and its own long-running spin-off competition series The Challenge.
