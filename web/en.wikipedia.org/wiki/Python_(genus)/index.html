Python is a genus of constricting snakes in the family Pythonidae, native to the tropics and subtropics of Africa and Asia. The genus was proposed by François Marie Daudin in 1803. Species include the Indian python, the Burmese python and the African rock python, among the largest snakes in the world.

Biology

Pythons are nonvenomous ambush predators that kill prey by constriction. Like all pythons they have heat-sensing pits that detect warm-blooded prey. Unlike most snakes, female pythons incubate their eggs, coiling around the clutch and generating heat by muscular contractions ("shivering" thermogenesis).

Species and conservation

Recognised species include Python molurus (Indian python), Python bivittatus (Burmese python), Python sebae (African rock python) and Python reticulatus is now placed in the genus Malayopython. Burmese pythons released in Florida have become a destructive invasive species in the Everglades.
