Regimental combat team

A regimental combat team (RCT) was a provisional United States Army combined-arms formation used principally during the Second World War and the Korean War. It was built around an infantry regiment, normally of three battalions, reinforced with attached supporting units, typically a field artillery battalion, an engineer company, a medical company and other services as required, making it a self-contained fighting organisation of roughly 4,000–5,000 men.

Use

RCTs allowed divisions to detach semi-independent task forces for separate missions. Famous examples include the 442nd Regimental Combat Team, composed largely of Japanese American volunteers, which became one of the most decorated American units of the Second World War, and the Regimental Combat Team 31 ("Task Force Faith") in the Korean War. The concept was a forerunner of later brigade combat teams.
