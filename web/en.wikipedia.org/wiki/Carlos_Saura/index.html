Carlos Saura Atarés (4 January 1932 – 10 February 2023) was a Spanish film director and photographer, regarded as one of Spain's most important filmmakers. His work spans from allegorical critiques of Francoist Spain to celebrated films about flamenco and tango dance.

Early career

Born in Huesca and raised in Madrid during the Spanish Civil War — experiences that marked his later work — Saura studied at the Instituto de Investigaciones y Experiencias Cinematográficas. His feature debut Los golfos (1960) and La caza (1966), which won the Silver Bear at Berlin, established him as a leading voice of the New Spanish Cinema, using allegory to evade Franco-era censorship.

International acclaim

Working with producer Elías Querejeta and his partner Geraldine Chaplin, Saura made a string of acclaimed films including Peppermint Frappé (1967), Ana and the Wolves (1973) and Cría cuervos (1976), the last a haunting portrait of childhood that won the Grand Prix at Cannes. After Franco's death he turned to dance films: Blood Wedding (1981), Carmen (1983) and El amor brujo (1986) formed a flamenco trilogy with choreographer Antonio Gades. Later works include ¡Ay, Carmela! (1990), Tango (1998) and Flamenco, Flamenco (2010). He died in February 2023, a day before receiving an honorary Goya Award.
