Yuwen Tai (Chinese: 宇文泰; 505–556), known posthumously as Emperor Wen of Northern Zhou, was a general and statesman of the Xianbei-led Western Wei dynasty of China. He became the de facto ruler of Western Wei after 534 and built the military and administrative foundations upon which his son, Yuwen Jue, founded the Northern Zhou dynasty in 557, shortly after his death.

Early career

Yuwen Tai was born into a family of the Yuwen tribe of the Xianbei people, originally from the region of modern Inner Mongolia. He rose through the armies of Northern Wei during the rebellions of the late 520s, eventually serving under the general Heba Yue in the Guanzhong region (modern Shaanxi). When Heba Yue was assassinated in 534, his troops elected Yuwen Tai as their commander.

Paramount leader of Western Wei

Later in 534, when the emperor Xiaowu fled the powerful general Gao Huan and sought refuge with Yuwen Tai, the Northern Wei split into Western Wei under Yuwen Tai's control and Eastern Wei under Gao Huan. Yuwen Tai had Xiaowu poisoned in early 535 and installed Emperor Wen of Western Wei in his place, after whom the state is conventionally named.

Despite being weaker and poorer than Eastern Wei (and its successor, Northern Qi), Western Wei under Yuwen Tai won important victories, notably at the Battle of Shayuan (537), and in 554 conquered Jiangling, the capital of the southern Liang dynasty. Yuwen Tai's reforms included reviving the fubing militia system — soldier-farmers organised under military colonies — which later underpinned the armies of the Sui and Tang dynasties, and the creation of the Twenty-Four Armies under the pillars of state (柱國大將軍).

Death and legacy

Yuwen Tai died in 556 while on a tour of the northern frontier. He designated his nephew Yuwen Hu as guardian of his young sons; the following year Yuwen Hu deposed the last Western Wei emperor and enthroned Yuwen Tai's son Yuwen Jue as the first emperor of Northern Zhou. Yuwen Tai was posthumously honoured as Emperor Wen.
