The M24 Sniper Weapon System (SWS) is a bolt-action sniper rifle developed by Remington Arms for the United States Army, adopted in 1988 as its standard sniper rifle. It is a militarised version of the Remington Model 700, built on the long action to permit later rechambering, and is chambered for the 7.62×51mm NATO cartridge.

Design

The M24 system comprises the rifle, a Leupold 10×40mm fixed-power telescopic sight, detachable bipod, and a deployment kit with tools and spares, all carried in a hard case. The rifle uses a heavy free-floating barrel with Remington's 5R rifling and an H-S Precision stock with an adjustable buttplate. Its internal magazine holds five rounds. The system is credited with an effective range of about 800 metres with match ammunition.

Service

The M24 served as the US Army's principal sniper rifle through the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan. From 2010 the Army began converting its M24s into the XM2010 Enhanced Sniper Rifle, chambered in .300 Winchester Magnum, while the M24 continued in service with other branches, allied forces and police agencies.
