Blackpool's piers are three pleasure piers on the seafront of Blackpool, Lancashire, on England's Fylde Coast: the North Pier, Central Pier and South Pier. They are among the most famous seaside structures in Britain and are closely associated with the growth of Blackpool as a working-class holiday resort in the Victorian era.

North Pier

The North Pier, opened on 21 May 1863, was designed by Eugenius Birch and is the oldest and longest of the three. It was intended as a genteel promenade pier for the more affluent visitors staying at the northern end of the town, with orchestral concerts and open-air dancing. It is 550 yards (503 m) long and is a Grade II listed building. A theatre opened at the pier head in 1939. The pier has suffered repeated storm and fire damage, including serious damage in the storms of December 1997, but has been repaired each time.

Central Pier

The Central Pier opened on 30 May 1868, also to a design by Birch, and is 371 yards (339 m) long. It was aimed at the mass holiday market arriving at Blackpool Central railway station, directly opposite, and became known as the "people's pier", famous for its open-air dancing and, later, roller skating. Its attractions included a big wheel added in 1990 and various fairground rides and amusement arcades.

South Pier

The South Pier, originally the Victoria Pier, opened on Good Friday 1893. The shortest of the three at 149 yards (136 m), it stands at the southern end of the promenade near the Pleasure Beach. It is known for its thrill rides, including a roller coaster and the Adrenalin Zone attractions, and for its Laughing Donkey family bar and live entertainment.

History and significance

The piers were central to Blackpool's development as Britain's archetypal seaside resort, serving the millions of holidaymakers who arrived from the industrial towns of Lancashire and Yorkshire during wakes weeks. All three piers remain in operation and are owned by the Sedgwick family's Blackpool Pier Company and related operators.
