WLW (700 AM) is a commercial news/talk/sports radio station licensed to Cincinnati, Ohio. Owned by iHeartMedia, WLW is a clear-channel station with a nighttime signal extending to much of North America. It is the flagship station of the Cincinnati Bengals Radio Network and the Cincinnati Reds Radio Network, and the official emergency alert system primary entry point station for Southwest Ohio and Northern Kentucky.

For 71 years, WLW was the flagship station of legendary broadcaster Powel Crosley Jr.'s Crosley Broadcasting Corporation. In the 1930s, WLW was authorized to experiment with a power of 500,000 watts — ten times the standard clear-channel limit — making it by far the most powerful radio station in the world at the time. During those years it billed itself as "The Nation's Station".

History

WLW was first licensed on March 2, 1922, founded by industrialist Powel Crosley Jr. Crosley initially entered radio to sell receivers manufactured by his company; the station served as a showcase for Crosley radios. In 1928 the station moved to a new transmitter site in Mason, Ohio, where a 747-foot Blaw-Knox tower was erected — still in use today.

On May 2, 1934, President Franklin D. Roosevelt pressed a ceremonial button in the White House that activated WLW's 500-kilowatt transmitter, the most powerful ever operated by an American broadcast station. Dubbed the "superpower" era, the experiment drew reception reports from around the world but also complaints of interference; the authorization was not renewed, and in 1939 WLW returned to the standard 50,000 watts.

Programming

WLW broadcasts a mix of local and syndicated talk programming, news, and live sports. Its signal and long history of covering regional events — including the 1937 Ohio River flood and decades of Reds baseball — have made it one of the best-known AM stations in the United States.
