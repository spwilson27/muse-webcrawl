Caste

A caste is a fixed social group into which an individual is born within a particular system of social stratification: a caste system. Within such a system, individuals are expected to marry solely within the group and follow culturally defined rules for contact.

India

The best-known caste system is in India, associated with the four varnas — Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras — and thousands of jatis, with Dalits historically placed outside the system and subjected to untouchability. India's constitution abolished untouchability, and caste-based reservations in education and government jobs remain a major political issue. Thinkers such as B. R. Ambedkar campaigned for the annihilation of caste.

Other societies

Caste-like systems have existed in Nepal, Sri Lanka, Bali, Japan (the burakumin), Korea, parts of Africa, and in colonial Latin America under Spanish rule. Sociologists also use caste metaphorically for rigid stratification elsewhere.
