Rail transport in Spain operates on four railway gauges and services are operated by a variety of private and public operators. The total route length in 2012 was 16,026 km (9,922 mi). Spain has the most extensive high-speed network in Europe, and the second most extensive in the world after China.

The Spanish high-speed network is operated under the brand name AVE (Alta Velocidad Española) by the state operator Renfe, with additional services (Avant, Alvia, Euromed and Intercity). Since the liberalisation of the passenger market in 2021, private operators Ouigo España (SNCF) and Iryo (a consortium including Trenitalia and Air Nostrum) compete on the busiest corridors.

History

The first railway line in the Iberian Peninsula was built in 1848 between Barcelona and Mataró. In 1851 a commission recommended that Spanish railways be built to a broad gauge of six Castilian feet, which became the Iberian gauge of 1,668 mm — wider than the standard gauge used in most of Europe, partly for engineering reasons and partly, in later legend, to hinder a French invasion. Early construction was driven by foreign, mainly French and British, capital, and by concessions granted by the state.

Following the Spanish Civil War, in 1941 the network of broad-gauge lines was nationalised and placed under the newly created RENFE (Red Nacional de los Ferrocarriles Españoles). In 2005, in accordance with European Union legislation, RENFE was split into Renfe Operadora, which runs the trains, and Adif, which manages the infrastructure.

High-speed rail

The first Spanish high-speed line, between Madrid and Seville, opened on 14 April 1992 in time for Expo 92. It was built to standard gauge to connect with the European network. Since then, lines have radiated from Madrid to Barcelona and the French border (2008–2013), Valencia, Alicante, Málaga, Valladolid, León, Galicia and Burgos, among others. AVE trains reach commercial speeds of up to 310 km/h. Gauge changers allow trains to run through between the standard-gauge high-speed network and the Iberian-gauge conventional network.

Gauges

Spain is unusual in operating four gauges:

  • Iberian gauge (1,668 mm) — the historic main-line network.
  • Standard gauge (1,435 mm) — all new high-speed lines.
  • Metre gauge (1,000 mm) — most of the former FEVE network in the north, plus some regional lines.
  • 914 mm — the Sóller railway on Mallorca.

Operators and services

Renfe operates long-distance services (AVE, Avlo, Alvia, Euromed, Intercity), medium-distance trains, and the Cercanías commuter networks around major cities. Regional governments in Catalonia (FGC), the Basque Country (Euskotren), Valencia (FGV) and Mallorca (SFM) run their own services. Freight is handled by Renfe Mercancías and private operators. Metro systems operate in Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Bilbao, Seville, Málaga and Palma.
