Vitis

Vitis (grapevines) is a genus of about 60–80 species of vining plants in the family Vitaceae. The genus is native to temperate and subtropical regions of the Northern Hemisphere, especially North America and eastern Asia. Its fruit, the grape, is of enormous economic importance — eaten fresh, dried as raisins, sultanas and currants, and above all fermented into wine. The overwhelming majority of the world's wine comes from one species, the Eurasian Vitis vinifera.

Description

Vitis species are woody climbers (lianas) that climb by tendrils opposite the leaves. Leaves are alternate, often palmately lobed. The small, inconspicuous flowers are borne in panicles; V. vinifera has hermaphrodite flowers while most wild species are dioecious, with separate male and female plants. The fruit is a berry with seeds, varying widely in colour, size, and flavour between species.

Notable species

  • Vitis vinifera — the European wine grape, domesticated in the Caucasus/Near East region thousands of years ago.
  • Vitis labrusca — the North American "fox grape", source of Concord grapes.
  • Vitis riparia, V. rupestris, V. berlandieri — American species whose roots resist the phylloxera aphid; they are used as rootstocks onto which vinifera is grafted, the practice that saved European viticulture after the phylloxera crisis of the 19th century.
  • Vitis amurensis — the frost-hardy Amur grape of northeast Asia.
  • Vitis rotundifolia (often placed in Muscadinia) — the muscadine grape of the southeastern United States.
