Film finance

Film finance is an aspect of film production that occurs during the development stage prior to pre-production, and is concerned with determining the potential value of a proposed film.

Overview

In the United States, films are most often financed through distribution pre-sales, tax incentives, studio or equity investment, gap financing, and debt financing. Producers assemble packages — script, director, cast, budget, and projected returns — to attract financiers.

Risk

Film investment is considered high-risk because box-office performance is unpredictable; financiers mitigate risk through slate financing, completion bonds, and insurance. Revenue streams now include theatrical release, streaming, home entertainment, and television licensing.
