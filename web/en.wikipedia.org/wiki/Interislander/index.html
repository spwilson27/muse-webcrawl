Interislander is a road and rail ferry service across New Zealand's Cook Strait, between Wellington in the North Island and Picton in the South Island. It is a division of KiwiRail and is regarded as an extension of New Zealand's State Highway 1 and the Main North Line across the strait. The crossing takes about three and a half hours and covers roughly 92 kilometres (57 miles), of which about 22 kilometres (14 miles) are across the open strait itself.

History

Ferry services between the two islands date to the 19th century, and the New Zealand Railways Department introduced roll-on/roll-off rail ferries in 1962 with the arrival of the Aramoana. The service carried rail wagons as well as road vehicles and passengers, allowing freight to move between the islands without transshipment. The Interislander brand was adopted in the 1990s as the passenger-focused identity of the operation.

Fleet

Over the decades the fleet has included vessels such as Aramoana, Aranui, Arahanga, Aratika, Arahura and Aratere. In the 2020s the service was operated by Kaiarahi, Aratere and Kaitaki, the latter notable as a passenger/vehicle ferry without rail capability. Plans to replace the ageing fleet with two large new rail-enabled ferries under the iReX project were cancelled in late 2023 after major cost increases, and the government subsequently pursued procurement of smaller replacement vessels.

Route

Sailings depart the Wellington ferry terminal near the central city and travel out through Wellington Harbour, across Cook Strait — known for strong currents and rough seas — and into the sheltered waters of Tory Channel and Queen Charlotte Sound before docking at Picton. The final portion through the Marlborough Sounds is widely regarded as one of the most scenic ferry journeys in the world.

Incidents

The Cook Strait route was the scene of New Zealand's worst maritime disaster of the modern era when the inter-island ferry Wahine foundered in Wellington Harbour during a storm on 10 April 1968, with the loss of 53 lives. More recently, the Aratere ran aground near Picton in June 2024 after a steering failure during an autopilot-related manoeuvre; all passengers and crew were safe.
