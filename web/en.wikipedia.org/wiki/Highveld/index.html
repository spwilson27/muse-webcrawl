Highveld

The Highveld (Afrikaans: Hoëveld, "High Field") is the portion of the South African inland plateau above roughly 1,500 metres elevation, but below 2,100 metres, occupying the northeastern part of the country. It covers Gauteng, the eastern Free State, Mpumalanga's southern districts and parts of Limpopo, North West and the Northern Cape, and includes the country's largest metropolitan area, Johannesburg.

Geography and climate

The Highveld is a gently rolling grassland plateau bounded to the northeast by the Mpumalanga Drakensberg escarpment and to the south by the Vaal River. Its altitude gives it a temperate climate despite its subtropical latitude: summers are warm with dramatic afternoon thunderstorms, winters dry, sunny and cold at night with frequent frost. The vegetation is predominantly open grassland (the "bankeveld" and sourveld), originally with very few trees.

Economy

The Highveld contains the Witwatersrand goldfields, much of South Africa's coal mining and its coal-fired power stations, the maize belt, and the Sasol petrochemical complex at Secunda. These industries make the Mpumalanga Highveld a significant air-pollution region, formally declared a priority area under air-quality law.
