Six degrees of separation

Six degrees of separation

Six degrees of separation is the idea that all people are six or fewer social connections away from each other, so that any two strangers can be linked by a chain of acquaintances of at most about six intermediaries — a "friend of a friend" chain. The concept grew out of the small-world research tradition in social network theory.

History

The idea was anticipated by Hungarian writer Frigyes Karinthy in his 1929 short story "Chains". In the 1960s, psychologist Stanley Milgram's small-world experiment asked volunteers in the United States to forward a letter toward a target person through acquaintances; completed chains averaged around six steps (though most letters never arrived). Later large-scale studies of email and social-media graphs found similar or smaller average separations; a 2016 Facebook study estimated 3.57 intermediaries between its users.

In popular culture

John Guare's 1990 play Six Degrees of Separation (filmed in 1993) popularised the phrase. The parlour game "Six Degrees of Kevin Bacon" applies the idea to film casts, and the Erdős number measures analogous distance in mathematical co-authorship.
