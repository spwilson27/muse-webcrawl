Overview

North Carolina Agricultural and Technical State University (North Carolina A&T) is a public, historically Black, land-grant research university in Greensboro, North Carolina. Founded in 1891 as the state's land-grant institution for Black students under the Second Morrill Act, it has grown into one of the largest historically Black universities in the United States by enrollment.

History

The university began as the A. and M. College for the Colored Race. In 1960, four of its students — the Greensboro Four — staged a sit-in at the segregated Woolworth lunch counter in downtown Greensboro, an event that helped ignite the sit-in movement across the South. Alumnus Ronald McNair, a physicist and astronaut, died in the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster in 1986.

Academics and campus life

North Carolina A&T is known for its engineering programmes and graduates more Black engineers than any other American university. Its athletics teams, the Aggies, compete in NCAA Division I, and its marching band, the Blue and Gold Marching Machine, is a prominent feature of its celebrated homecoming, popularly called "GHOE" — the Greatest Homecoming on Earth.
