International Article Number

The International Article Number (EAN) is a standard describing a barcode symbology and numbering system used in global trade to identify a specific retail product type, in a specific packaging configuration, from a specific manufacturer. It is a superset of the North American Universal Product Code (UPC).

Structure

The most common form, EAN-13, encodes 13 digits: a GS1 country prefix, a company prefix, an item reference assigned by the manufacturer, and a final check digit computed by a modulo-10 algorithm. An 8-digit variant, EAN-8, is used on small packages. The numbers themselves are identifiers only; product data is held in databases keyed by the code, now standardized as the Global Trade Item Number (GTIN).

Administration

EAN was developed in Europe in the 1970s by the European Article Numbering Association. In 2005 the organization merged with the Uniform Code Council to form GS1, which now administers the system worldwide through national member organizations.

Uses

EAN barcodes are printed on virtually all retail consumer goods, scanned at point of sale for pricing, stock control, and logistics. Book and magazine variants underlie the ISBN and ISSN barcode systems.
