Iraqi Kurdistan, officially the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, is an autonomous region in northern Iraq populated predominantly by Kurds. It is governed by the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG), with its capital at Erbil, and comprises the governorates of Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Duhok and Halabja, plus disputed territories claimed against Baghdad.

History

Kurds in Iraq rebelled repeatedly against Baghdad through the twentieth century, notably under Mustafa Barzani. The 1970 autonomy agreement collapsed, and Saddam Hussein's Ba'athist regime carried out the Anfal campaign (1986–1989), including the chemical attack on Halabja in 1988, killing tens of thousands of Kurds. After the 1991 Gulf War uprising, a Western-enforced no-fly zone allowed Kurdish self-government to take root; rival parties — the KDP and PUK — fought a civil war in the mid-1990s before reconciling.

Constitutional status

Iraq's 2005 constitution recognised the Kurdistan Region as a federal region. The KRG has its own parliament, ministries and security forces, the Peshmerga. Disputes persist over Kirkuk and other territories under Article 140, oil exports, and revenue sharing.

2017 referendum

An independence referendum on 25 September 2017 voted overwhelmingly for independence, but Baghdad, with regional and international opposition, retook Kirkuk and most disputed areas in October 2017, and the result was frozen.

Economy and culture

The economy relies on oil, agriculture, trade with Turkey and Iran, and tourism. Kurdish identity is expressed through Newroz celebrations, language (Sorani and Kurmanji dialects) and media. During the war against the Islamic State (2014–2017) the Peshmerga were a key ground force.
