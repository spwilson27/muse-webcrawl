Chromatid

A chromatid is one half of a duplicated chromosome. After DNA replication in S phase, each chromosome consists of two identical DNA molecules, the sister chromatids, joined at the centromere. During mitosis the sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles, ensuring each daughter cell receives an identical genome.

Sister and non-sister chromatids

Sister chromatids are the two copies of the same chromosome, held together by the cohesin protein complex. Non-sister chromatids belong to the two different homologues of a homologous pair; during meiosis, crossing over occurs between non-sister chromatids, exchanging genetic material and producing recombinant chromosomes.

Terminology

Once sister chromatids separate in anaphase, each is considered an independent chromosome in its own right. A dyad is the pair of sister chromatids; a tetrad (bivalent) is the paired structure of four chromatids formed by two homologues in meiosis.
