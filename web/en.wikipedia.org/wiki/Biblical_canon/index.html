Biblical canon

A biblical canon is a set of texts (or "books") considered by a particular Jewish or Christian religious community to be part of the Bible — authoritative scripture. Different traditions include different books.

Canons compared

The Hebrew Bible (Tanakh) has 24 books in three sections: Torah, Prophets, and Writings. Protestant Old Testaments contain the same texts arranged as 39 books. Catholic and Orthodox canons add the deuterocanonical books (e.g. Tobit, Maccabees, Wisdom), with Ethiopian churches accepting still more. All mainstream Christian New Testaments contain 27 books, from the four Gospels through Revelation.

Development

Canons formed gradually over centuries. Athanasius's Festal Letter of 367 CE first lists exactly the 27 New Testament books. The Council of Trent (1546) definitively affirmed the Catholic canon in response to the Protestant Reformation.
