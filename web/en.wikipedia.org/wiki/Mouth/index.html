Mouth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Overview

In animal anatomy, the mouth, also known as the oral cavity or buccal cavity, is the opening through which many animals take in food and issue vocal sounds. In humans it is the first part of the alimentary canal, bounded by the lips in front, the cheeks at the sides, the palate above and the floor of the mouth below, and containing the teeth and tongue. The mouth also plays a major role in communication, facial expression and breathing.

Structure

The oral cavity is divided into the vestibule, between the lips/cheeks and the teeth, and the oral cavity proper within the dental arches. The roof is formed by the hard and soft palate; the soft palate ends in the uvula. The tongue, a muscular organ covered in taste buds, occupies the floor. Three pairs of major salivary glands — parotid, submandibular and sublingual — together with many minor glands secrete saliva, which moistens food, begins starch digestion with the enzyme amylase, and protects the teeth. A full adult dentition has 32 teeth, specialised as incisors, canines, premolars and molars.

Functions

The mouth performs mastication (chewing), mixing food with saliva to form a bolus for swallowing; it begins chemical digestion; it is the principal organ of taste; and it shapes the sounds of speech in articulation with the tongue, lips and palate. It also serves as a secondary airway.

Health

Common conditions affecting the mouth include dental caries, gum (periodontal) disease, cold sores, mouth ulcers, oral candidiasis and, less commonly, oral cancer. Oral hygiene — brushing, flossing and professional dental care — is a major determinant of both oral and general health. In many animals, the mouth is also a weapon and tool, and its form is adapted to diet, from the beaks of birds to the radula of molluscs.
