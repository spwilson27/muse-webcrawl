Adenoma

An adenoma is a benign tumor of epithelial tissue with glandular origin, glandular characteristics, or both. Adenomas can grow from many glandular organs, including the adrenal glands, pituitary gland, thyroid, prostate and others. Some adenomas grow from non-glandular epithelial tissue but express glandular tissue structure. Although adenomas are benign, they may be recognised as having malignant potential: over time adenomas may transform to become malignant, at which point they are called adenocarcinomas.

Common types

Colorectal adenomas (adenomatous polyps) are very common, especially with advancing age, and are the recognised precursors of most colorectal cancers via the adenoma–carcinoma sequence; they are removed at colonoscopy. Pituitary adenomas may secrete hormones such as prolactin or growth hormone, causing prolactinoma or acromegaly. Adrenal adenomas are frequent incidental findings on imaging and are usually non-functioning, though some produce cortisol or aldosterone. Other examples include thyroid follicular adenoma, hepatic adenoma, parathyroid adenoma and fibroadenoma of the breast.

Diagnosis and treatment

Adenomas are often found incidentally during imaging or endoscopy performed for other reasons. Diagnosis is confirmed by biopsy or excision and histopathological examination, which distinguishes adenoma from adenocarcinoma by the absence of invasion. Management depends on site, size and hormone activity: hormone-secreting or large adenomas are usually removed surgically, while small asymptomatic ones may be monitored with surveillance imaging.
