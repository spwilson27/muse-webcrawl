Verificationism

Verificationism, also known as the verification principle or verifiability criterion of meaning, is the philosophical doctrine that a statement is cognitively meaningful only if it can be definitively verified through empirical observation or is analytic.

Origins

Associated with logical positivism and the Vienna Circle in the 1920s–1930s, figures including Moritz Schlick, Rudolf Carnap, and A. J. Ayer popularized it.

Criticism

Critics noted the principle itself is neither analytic nor empirically verifiable, problems with universal laws, and difficulties with historical and ethical statements. Later forms weakened it to confirmability in principle.
