Pierre Gassendi

Pierre Gassendi (22 January 1592 – 24 October 1655) was a French philosopher, Catholic priest, astronomer, and mathematician. He revived Epicurean atomism for the scientific age, reconciling it with Christian theology, and was a leading figure of the early Scientific Revolution.

Philosophy

Gassendi objected influentially to Descartes's Meditations and to scholastic Aristotelianism. His major work, the Syntagma philosophicum (published posthumously in 1658), presented an empiricist epistemology and an atomist physics in which indivisible atoms move in a void — modified so that atoms were created by God. His thought influenced Locke and the later empiricist tradition.

Science

In astronomy, Gassendi made the first telescopic observation of the transit of Mercury in 1631, as predicted by Kepler. He corresponded widely within the Republic of Letters, notably through Marin Mersenne's circle. The lunar crater Gassendi is named in his honour.
