Ministry of Culture (France)

The Ministry of Culture (French: Ministère de la Culture) is the ministry of the Government of France in charge of national museums and monuments, promoting and protecting the arts — visual, plastic, theatrical, musical, dance, architecture and cinema — both in France and throughout the world, as well as managing the National Archives and the regional maisons de la culture. Its headquarters is at the Palais Royal in Paris, opposite the Louvre.

History

The ministry was created in 1959 under President Charles de Gaulle, who appointed the writer André Malraux as its first minister. France had previously administered cultural affairs through a Secretariat of State for Fine Arts, but de Gaulle's decree of 3 February 1959 established a full ministry, reflecting the Fifth Republic's view of culture as a matter of state. Malraux held the post for a decade, launching the network of maisons de la culture (cultural centres) intended to bring theatre, film and exhibitions to provincial cities, and overseeing the famous cleaning of Parisian façades.

Under President François Mitterrand, minister Jack Lang (1981–86, 1988–93) doubled the culture budget toward the symbolic goal of one per cent of state spending, launched the Fête de la Musique, and championed the policy of l'exception culturelle — the argument that cultural goods should be exempt from free-trade rules. The ministry also drove the Grands Projets era of state cultural building, including the Musée d'Orsay conversion and the Bibliothèque nationale de France.

Organisation and responsibilities

The ministry supervises the national museums service (Réunion des musées nationaux), the Centre des monuments nationaux, the National Archives, the Bibliothèque nationale de France, the Centre national du cinéma et de l'image animée (CNC), and regional directorates of cultural affairs (DRAC) in every French region. It supports the French-language policy of the state, heritage protection law, archaeology, and France's network of cultural institutes abroad.

The minister is a member of the Council of Ministers and is often one of the most visible figures in French public life; past holders include Jacques Toubon, Catherine Trautmann, Frédéric Mitterrand, Aurélie Filippetti, Fleur Pellerin, Roselyne Bachelot and Rima Abdul Malak.
