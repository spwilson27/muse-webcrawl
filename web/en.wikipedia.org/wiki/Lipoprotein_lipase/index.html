Lipoprotein lipase

Gene: LPL (chromosome 8p21.3 in humans)

EC number: 3.1.1.34

Type: Enzyme (lipase)

Function: Hydrolyzes triglycerides in lipoproteins

Lipoprotein lipase (LPL) is a member of the lipase gene family, which includes pancreatic lipase, hepatic lipase, and endothelial lipase. It is a water-soluble enzyme that hydrolyzes triglycerides in lipoproteins, such as those found in chylomicrons and very low-density lipoproteins (VLDL), into two free fatty acids and one monoacylglycerol molecule. It is also involved in promoting the cellular uptake of chylomicron remnants, cholesterol-rich lipoproteins, and free fatty acids.

Synthesis and location

LPL is found in many tissues, including adipose tissue, cardiac and skeletal muscle, and mammary glands. It is synthesized in the parenchymal cells of these tissues and then transported to the luminal surface of endothelial cells in capillaries, where it is anchored by the protein GPIHBP1. Its activity is activated by apolipoprotein C-II, a component of the lipoprotein particles it acts upon, and inhibited by apolipoprotein C-III.

Clinical significance

Deficiency of lipoprotein lipase, caused by mutations in the LPL gene, leads to a rare disorder known as familial lipoprotein lipase deficiency (type I hyperlipoproteinemia), characterized by severe hypertriglyceridemia and recurrent pancreatitis. Insulin regulates LPL expression in adipose tissue, and LPL activity is an important determinant of plasma triglyceride levels and thus of cardiovascular risk.
