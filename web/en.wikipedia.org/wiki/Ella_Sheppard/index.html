Ella Sheppard

Born: February 4, 1851, Nashville, Tennessee, U.S.
Died: June 9, 1914 (aged 63), Nashville, Tennessee, U.S.
Occupation: Singer, pianist, arranger, teacher

Ella Sheppard (February 4, 1851 – June 9, 1914) was an American soprano, pianist, composer, and arranger. She was a founding member of the Fisk Jubilee Singers and served as the group's principal accompanist, musical arranger, and trainer.

Early life

Sheppard was born into slavery in Nashville. Her father secured the family's freedom, and the family moved to Cincinnati, where she received musical training. She returned to Nashville to teach and study, enrolling at Fisk University.

Fisk Jubilee Singers

In 1871, Sheppard joined the Jubilee Singers, who toured the United States and Europe to raise funds for Fisk University. She transcribed and arranged many of the slave songs, or spirituals, that the group performed, and helped assemble and rehearse the singers. The group's performances before Queen Victoria and other European royalty brought international fame and saved Fisk from financial collapse.

Legacy

Sheppard is credited with helping to preserve the spirituals tradition by writing down songs that had been passed down orally through generations of enslaved people.
