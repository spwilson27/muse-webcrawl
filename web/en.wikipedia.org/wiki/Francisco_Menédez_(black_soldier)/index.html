Francisco Menéndez (black soldier)

Francisco Menéndez (c. 1704 – after 1748) was a free black militiaman and community leader in Spanish Florida. Born in West Africa, he was enslaved in South Carolina, escaped to Spanish Florida, and became captain of the free black militia at Fort Mose, the first legally sanctioned free black settlement in what is now the United States.

Life

Menéndez was captured in Africa and transported to South Carolina, where he was enslaved. He fought alongside the Yamasee in the Yamasee War (1715) and afterwards escaped south to St. Augustine, where Spanish policy granted freedom to fugitive slaves from the English colonies who converted to Catholicism. In 1726 he petitioned for his freedom, and in 1738 the governor granted unconditional liberty to Menéndez and other formerly enslaved people.

Fort Mose

That same year the Spanish founded Gracia Real de Santa Teresa de Mose (Fort Mose), about two miles north of St. Augustine, and Menéndez was commissioned captain of its free black militia. In 1740, during the War of Jenkins' Ear, British forces under James Oglethorpe briefly captured the fort; Menéndez and the Spanish retook it in the bloody Battle of Fort Mose. Captured by English corsairs in 1741 and sold into slavery in the Bahamas, he escaped and returned to Florida by 1743. After Spain ceded Florida to Britain in 1763, the Fort Mose community evacuated with the Spanish to Cuba.

Legacy

Fort Mose is now a Florida state park and a National Historic Landmark, recognised as a foundational site of African American history.
