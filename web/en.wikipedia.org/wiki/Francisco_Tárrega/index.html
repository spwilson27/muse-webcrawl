Francisco Tárrega

Francisco Tárrega y Eixea (21 November 1852 – 15 December 1909) was a Spanish composer and classical guitarist. Often called "the father of classical guitar", he laid the foundations of modern guitar technique and repertoire, and his works — above all Recuerdos de la Alhambra — remain central to the instrument's literature.

Life

Tárrega was born in Villarreal, in the province of Castellón. After childhood injuries damaged his eyesight, he studied piano and guitar, eventually entering the Madrid Conservatory in 1874 with the support of a wealthy patron. He toured widely in Spain and abroad, settled for a time in Valencia and later Barcelona, and taught pupils — including Miguel Llobet and Emilio Pujol — who carried his school of playing around the world.

Music

Tárrega composed 78 original works and made some 120 transcriptions for guitar of music by Chopin, Beethoven, Mendelssohn, Albéniz and others, demonstrating that the guitar could sustain the mainstream classical repertoire. His tremolo study Recuerdos de la Alhambra and the miniature Lágrima are among the best-known guitar pieces ever written. A short passage from his Gran Vals later became famous worldwide as the Nokia mobile phone ringtone.

Technique

Tárrega advocated playing with the fingertips rather than the nails late in his career, favoured the larger Torres-style guitar, and systematised posture, fingering and rest-stroke technique that remain standard teaching.
