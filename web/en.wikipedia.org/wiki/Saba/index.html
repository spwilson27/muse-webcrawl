Saba is a small island in the Caribbean and a special municipality of the Netherlands. At 13 square kilometres it is the smallest special municipality of the Dutch Caribbean, and its summit, Mount Scenery, is the highest point in the Kingdom of the Netherlands.

Saba rises steeply from the Caribbean Sea; there are no natural beaches.

Geography

Saba is the summit of a dormant volcano in the northern Leeward Islands, northeast of Sint Eustatius. It has four villages — The Bottom (the capital), Windwardside, Zions Hill (Hell's Gate) and St. Johns — connected by a single road famously engineered along the island's cliffs. Juancho E. Yrausquin Airport has one of the world's shortest commercial runways, about 400 metres long.

History

The island was inhabited by Arawak peoples before European contact; Columbus sighted it in 1493. Dutch colonists settled from 1640, though sovereignty was disputed among the Dutch, English, French and Spanish for two centuries. Saba became part of the Netherlands Antilles in 1954, and on 10 October 2010, when the Netherlands Antilles was dissolved, it became a special municipality of the Netherlands proper.

Economy and nature

Tourism, especially diving in the surrounding Saba National Marine Park, is the main industry, complemented by the Saba University School of Medicine, whose students form a large share of the population. The island's cloud-forest summit and coral reefs are protected for their rich biodiversity.
