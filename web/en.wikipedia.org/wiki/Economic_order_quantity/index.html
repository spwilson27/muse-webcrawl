Economic order quantity

Economic order quantity (EOQ)
FieldOperations management, inventory theory
Introduced byFord W. Harris (1913)
PurposeFind the order size minimising total ordering plus holding costs
NotationQ* = √(2DS/H)

Economic order quantity (EOQ) is the order quantity a company should purchase to minimise total inventory costs, balancing the fixed cost of placing orders against the cost of holding stock. The model was developed by Ford W. Harris in 1913 and popularised by R. H. Wilson, and it remains a foundational result of inventory management and operations research.

The model

Assume demand of D units per year at a constant rate, a fixed ordering cost S per order regardless of size, and a holding cost H per unit per year. If Q is the order quantity, annual ordering cost is (D/Q)·S and average inventory is Q/2, so annual holding cost is (Q/2)·H. Total cost is minimised where the two are equal, giving the square-root formula:

Q* = √( 2DS / H )

The cost curve is flat near the optimum, so total cost is relatively insensitive to modest deviations from Q* — a convenient robustness property in practice.

Assumptions and extensions

The classic model assumes constant, known demand; fixed lead time; no stockouts; instantaneous replenishment; and a constant purchase price with no quantity discounts. Numerous extensions relax these assumptions, including the economic production quantity (EPQ) model with finite production rates, quantity-discount models, backordering, probabilistic demand with safety stock, and multi-echelon systems. EOQ thinking underlies modern inventory policies even where just-in-time practices aim to reduce S itself so that smaller orders become optimal.
