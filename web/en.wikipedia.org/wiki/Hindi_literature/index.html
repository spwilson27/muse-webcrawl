Hindi literature

Hindi literature is literature in Hindi and its historical dialect continuum, including Braj, Awadhi, and standard Khari Boli Hindi. It is generally divided into four broad periods: Ādi Kāl (early), Bhakti Kāl (devotional), Rīti Kāl (scholastic), and Ādhunik Kāl (modern).

Bhakti period

The devotional era produced poets such as Kabir, Tulsidas (author of the Ramcharitmanas), Surdas, and Mirabai, whose works remain central to North Indian religious and literary culture.

Modern period

Modern Hindi prose developed in the 19th century with Bharatendu Harishchandra. The 20th century saw the Chhayavad romantic poets — Jaishankar Prasad, Suryakant Tripathi 'Nirala', Sumitranandan Pant, and Mahadevi Varma — and the towering fiction of Munshi Premchand.
