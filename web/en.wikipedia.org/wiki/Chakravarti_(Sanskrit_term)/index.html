Chakravarti (Sanskrit term)

Chakravarti (Sanskrit: चक्रवर्तिन्, cakravartin) is an ancient Indian term for an ideal universal ruler, a monarch who rules over an entire continent or the whole world. The word derives from cakra ("wheel") and vartin ("one who turns"), suggesting a ruler whose chariot wheel rolls unobstructed everywhere.

In Hinduism

In Hindu tradition, a chakravarti is a righteous emperor whose dominion extends over Bharata or the whole earth. Figures such as Bharata and Yudhishthira are described in epic literature with chakravarti attributes, and the concept is associated with the performance of great sacrifices such as the ashvamedha and rajasuya.

In Buddhism and Jainism

Buddhist texts describe the cakkavatti as a wheel-turning monarch who rules by dharma rather than force; the Buddha himself was prophesied to become either a chakravarti or a fully awakened one. In Jainism, chakravartins are among the 63 illustrious persons (shalakapurushas), with Bharata, son of the first Tirthankara Rishabha, as the archetype.

Usage

The title was applied to historical emperors such as Ashoka and was echoed in royal titulature across South and Southeast Asia.
