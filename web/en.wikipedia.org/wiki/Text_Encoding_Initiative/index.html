Text Encoding Initiative

Founded: 1987

Type: Academic consortium

Focus: Standards for encoding machine-readable texts

Key output: TEI Guidelines (P5)

The Text Encoding Initiative (TEI) is a consortium which collectively develops and maintains a standard for the representation of texts in digital form. Founded in 1987, its chief deliverable is a set of Guidelines that specify encoding methods for machine-readable texts, chiefly in the humanities, social sciences, and linguistics.

Guidelines

The TEI Guidelines define several hundred elements and attributes for encoding the structure and features of texts — including prose, poetry, drama, dictionaries, manuscripts, and transcriptions of speech. Originally based on SGML, the Guidelines were revised as TEI P4 for XML and the current version, TEI P5 (first released in 2007), is maintained as XML schemas customized through the "One Document Does it all" (ODD) specification format.

Use

TEI is widely used by libraries, museums, publishers, and scholars to present, publish, and preserve digital texts. Major projects using TEI include the Oxford Text Archive, the Folger Shakespeare Library's digital texts, and numerous digital scholarly editions and text corpora. The TEI Consortium is a membership organization hosted at several universities, and the Guidelines are published openly.
