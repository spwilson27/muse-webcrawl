Federal Insurance Contributions Act

The Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA) is a United States federal payroll tax payable by both employees and employers to fund the Social Security and Medicare programs, which together make up the nation's social insurance programs. The tax also provides benefits for retirees, children, and the disabled. It is sometimes referred to in the United States as the payroll tax (along with other employment taxes).

Rates and structure

For 2024 and 2025, the FICA tax rate for employees is 7.65% of gross compensation: 6.2% for Social Security (OASDI) and 1.45% for Medicare (HI). The Social Security portion applies only up to an annual wage base — $168,600 in 2024 and $176,100 in 2025 — while the Medicare portion has no cap, and an additional 0.9% Medicare tax applies to earnings above thresholds of $200,000 for single filers ($250,000 for married couples filing jointly), paid only by the employee. Employers pay a matching 7.65%; self-employed workers pay both halves, 15.3%, under the Self-Employment Contributions Act (SECA), with an income-tax deduction for the employer-equivalent half.

History

FICA was enacted as part of the Social Security Act of 1935 and its contribution provisions were separated into the FICA chapter of the Internal Revenue Code in 1939. Medicare taxes were added in 1966 following the creation of Medicare in 1965. A temporary payroll tax cut reduced the employee Social Security rate to 4.2% in 2011 and 2012 as an economic stimulus measure.

Criticism

Economists generally hold that the full burden of the payroll tax falls largely on labor. Critics note that FICA is regressive at high incomes because of the Social Security wage cap, and that most low- and middle-income workers pay more in payroll taxes than in federal income tax.
