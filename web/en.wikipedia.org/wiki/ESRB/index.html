Entertainment Software Rating Board

The Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) is a self-regulatory organization that assigns age and content ratings to consumer video games in North America. The ESRB was established in 1994 by the Entertainment Software Association (ESA, formerly the Interactive Digital Software Association) in response to congressional criticism of violent content in video games such as Mortal Kombat and Night Trap.

Ratings appear on game packaging along with content descriptors (e.g., "Blood", "Strong Language"). Publishers submit questionnaires and footage for review; raters assign categories, and the ESRB can fine publishers for nondisclosure, as in the 2005 "Hot Coffee" incident involving Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas. Since 2013, digital-only games have largely been rated through the automated International Age Rating Coalition (IARC) questionnaire process. The system is voluntary, but most retailers refuse to stock unrated or AO-rated games, and console makers require ratings for licensed titles.
