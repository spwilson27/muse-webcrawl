Generation of '98

The Generation of '98 (Spanish: Generación del 98) was a group of novelists, poets, essayists, and philosophers active in Spain at the time of the Spanish–American War (1898), committed to cultural and aesthetic renewal and to confronting Spain's decline after the loss of its last colonies.

Generation of '98
Country: Spain
Period: c. 1898–1914
Key figures: Unamuno, Azorín, Baroja, Machado, Valle-Inclán

Origins

The term was coined by Azorín in essays of 1913. The Disaster of 1898 — the loss of Cuba, Puerto Rico, the Philippines and Guam — prompted a searching examination of Spanish identity, landscape (especially Castile), history and language, in a spirit sometimes called regenerationism.

Members and works

Core figures include Miguel de Unamuno (En torno al casticismo, Del sentimiento trágico de la vida), Pío Baroja (El árbol de la ciencia), Azorín, Ramiro de Maeztu, Antonio Machado (Campos de Castilla), Ramón del Valle-Inclán and Juan Ramón Jiménez (in his early phase). They favoured a plain, precise prose, revived neglected words, and engaged philosophically with Spain's past — Don Quixote, El Cid — as living myths.

Legacy

Scholars debate the group's coherence and its boundary with modernismo, but its interrogation of Spain shaped 20th-century Spanish literature and thought. See also Spanish–American War.
