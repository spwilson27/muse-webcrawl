Military science fiction

Powered armor, popularized by Heinlein's Starship Troopers, is an emblematic technology of the genre.

Military science fiction is a subgenre of science fiction that features the use of science fiction technology, mainly weapons, for military purposes and principal characters who are members of a military organization engaged in military activity, often in space or on other planets. It emphasizes military culture, hierarchy, technology, tactics, and the experience of combat.

Characteristics

Works in the subgenre typically depict futuristic navies and infantry in detail, project present-day or historical military practices into future settings, and adopt the viewpoint of soldiers rather than statesmen or civilians. Recurrent elements include powered armor, starship combat modeled on naval warfare, planetary assaults, and alien adversaries.

Notable works

Foundational texts include Robert A. Heinlein's Starship Troopers (1959), Joe Haldeman's The Forever War (1974) — widely read as a response to Heinlein informed by the Vietnam War — and Orson Scott Card's Ender's Game (1985). Prolific series include David Weber's Honor Harrington novels, David Drake's Hammer's Slammers stories, and Jerry Pournelle's CoDominium books. In other media, the genre encompasses Star Wars, Battlestar Galactica, Warhammer 40,000, the Halo games, and the film Starship Troopers (1997).
