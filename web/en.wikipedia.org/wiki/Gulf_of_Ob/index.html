Gulf of Ob

The Gulf of Ob (Russian: Обская губа, Obskaya guba) is a bay of the Arctic Ocean, located in northern Russia at the mouth of the Ob River. It is the world's longest estuary, extending roughly 1,000 kilometres (620 mi) from the delta of the Ob to the Kara Sea, and lying generally between the Yamal Peninsula to the west and the Gyda Peninsula to the east.

Geography

The gulf is shallow, with depths generally between 10 and 12 metres, which impedes heavy shipping. The Ob delta opens into the southern end of the gulf, and the Taz River enters through the Gulf of Taz, an eastern side branch. The shores are low, marshy tundra, and the gulf is frozen for most of the year, typically from October to June.

Resources

The surrounding Yamal and Gyda peninsulas hold some of the world's largest natural gas reserves, including the Yamburg and Bovanenkovo fields. Liquefied natural gas projects such as Yamal LNG ship through the Northern Sea Route with ice-class tankers, and the port of Sabetta on the gulf's western shore has become a major Arctic energy terminal.
