Number field

In mathematics, an (algebraic) number field is a finite extension of the field of rational numbers. A number field is thus a field that contains the rationals and has finite dimension when considered as a vector space over the rationals. Number fields are the central objects of study in algebraic number theory.

Examples

The simplest number fields are the quadratic fields, obtained by adjoining the square root of a square-free integer to the rationals; examples include the Gaussian rationals, formed by adjoining the imaginary unit i. Cyclotomic fields, obtained by adjoining roots of unity, are another important family, and any number field can be generated by a single algebraic integer by the primitive element theorem.

Ring of integers

Inside a number field one studies its ring of integers, the set of algebraic integers it contains. Unlike the rational integers, a ring of integers need not have unique factorization of elements, a difficulty that led historically to the introduction of ideals, which do factor uniquely into prime ideals. Key invariants of a number field include its discriminant, class number and group of units, the latter described by Dirichlet's unit theorem.
