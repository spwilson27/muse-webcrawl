iOS (formerly iPhone OS) is a mobile operating system developed by Apple Inc. exclusively for its smartphones, principally the iPhone. It was unveiled in January 2007 alongside the first iPhone and released that June. iOS is the world's second-most widely installed mobile operating system, after Android, and forms the basis of Apple's other platforms, including iPadOS, tvOS, and watchOS.

The iOS home screen on an iPhone, showing the app-icon grid and Dock.

History

When Steve Jobs introduced the iPhone in 2007, he said it ran "OS X". The operating system shipped as iPhone OS, with no support for third-party native applications until the release of the iOS SDK and the App Store in 2008. The name changed to iOS in 2010 with the release of the original iPad. iOS 7 (2013) introduced a major flat redesign under Jony Ive; subsequent releases added features such as the Control Center, widgets, Face ID support, and privacy controls. Major versions are announced annually at Apple's Worldwide Developers Conference (WWDC).

Features

The iOS interface centres on a home screen of application icons, with direct-manipulation multi-touch gestures such as tap, swipe, and pinch. Built-in applications include Messages, Mail, Safari, Photos, and the App Store, the sole official channel for most software distribution. Siri, Apple's voice assistant, was integrated in 2011. Security is based on sandboxing, code signing, and hardware-backed encryption with Secure Enclave on devices with Touch ID or Face ID.

Market share

iOS devices account for a substantial share of global smartphone usage and a majority share in some markets such as the United States and Japan. Apple reported more than two billion active devices across its platforms in the mid-2020s.
