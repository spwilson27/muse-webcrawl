The summit of Galdhøpiggen, the highest point in Northern Europe

Galdhøpiggen is the highest mountain in Norway, Scandinavia, and Northern Europe, at 2,469 metres (8,100 ft) above sea level. It lies in the Jotunheimen mountain range within Jotunheimen National Park, in the municipality of Lom in Innlandet county.

History

The mountain's name derives from a mountain pass (gald) and a high peak (hø pigg, "high point"). Its height was disputed with neighbouring Glittertind, whose ice cap once made it marginally higher; as the glacier shrank, Galdhøpiggen's rock summit became unambiguously the highest. The first recorded ascent was made in 1850 by the schoolteacher Steinar Sulheim with local guides.

Climbing

Two main routes reach the summit: from Spiterstulen lodge, a straightforward hike, and from Juvasshytta, which crosses the Styggebreen glacier with guided rope teams. A small cabin stands on the summit, and in summer thousands of hikers make the ascent. In clear weather the view extends over a large part of southern Norway.
