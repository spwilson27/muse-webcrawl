Indian aesthetics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Indian aesthetics is the field of philosophy and art theory developed in the Indian subcontinent over more than two millennia, dealing with literature, drama, music, dance, architecture and the visual arts. Its central concept is rasa, the aesthetic "flavour" or emotion evoked in the spectator or reader by a work of art. The foundational text is the Natya Shastra, attributed to Bharata Muni and composed between roughly 200 BCE and 200 CE.

Rasa theory

The Natya Shastra, a treatise on theatre and performance, identifies eight rasas: shringara (love/erotic), hasya (comic), karuna (compassionate/sorrowful), raudra (furious), vira (heroic), bhayanaka (terrifying), bibhatsa (disgusting) and adbhuta (wondrous). A ninth rasa, shanta (peaceful), was added by later theorists. According to the theory, a performance combines determinants (vibhava), consequents (anubhava) and transitory states (vyabhichari bhava) to awaken a durable emotional state (sthayi bhava) in the audience, which is then savoured as rasa.

Abhinavagupta

The Kashmiri philosopher Abhinavagupta (c. 950–1016 CE), in his commentaries Abhinavabharati and the Locana on Anandavardhana's Dhvanyaloka, gave rasa theory its classical philosophical form. He argued that aesthetic experience involves a form of relishing (asvada) in which emotion is generalized and freed from personal circumstance, akin in some respects to spiritual bliss. Anandavardhana's doctrine of dhvani (suggestion or resonance) held that suggested meaning is the soul of poetry.

Other concepts

Important related concepts include alamkara (figures of speech and ornamentation), riti (style), vakrokti (oblique or striking expression), and aucitya (propriety or fittingness). In the visual arts and architecture, treatises such as the Vastu Shastra and Shilpa Shastra codified proportion, iconography and layout; temple sculpture and classical dance forms such as Bharatanatyam and Kathakali continue to draw explicitly on rasa theory.

Influence

Indian aesthetic theory influenced literary cultures across South and Southeast Asia through the spread of Sanskrit literature. In the modern period, scholars and artists such as Ananda Coomaraswamy and the filmmakers of Indian parallel cinema have reinterpreted rasa for contemporary audiences, and the theory is widely taught in performance studies worldwide.
