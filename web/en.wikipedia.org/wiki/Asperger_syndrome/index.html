Asperger syndrome

Specialty: Psychiatry, developmental medicine

Named after: Hans Asperger

Classification: Subsumed under autism spectrum disorder in DSM-5 (2013) and ICD-11

Asperger syndrome (AS), also known as Asperger's, is a former diagnostic category of developmental disorder characterised by significant difficulties in social interaction and nonverbal communication, along with restricted and repetitive patterns of behaviour and interests. It differed from other autism spectrum disorders by relatively unimpaired spoken language and intelligence.

History

The syndrome is named after the Austrian paediatrician Hans Asperger, who in 1944 described children in his care who lacked nonverbal communication skills, had limited empathy and were physically clumsy. The term was popularised in English by Lorna Wing in 1981. Asperger syndrome was added to the DSM-IV in 1994 as a subtype of pervasive developmental disorder.

Reclassification

In 2013 the DSM-5 removed Asperger syndrome as a separate diagnosis, folding it into autism spectrum disorder (ASD); the ICD-11 followed suit. People previously diagnosed with Asperger syndrome retained the diagnosis in practice, and the term remains widely used in the community, often associated with a distinct identity. Debates continue over Asperger's wartime record in Nazi Vienna.

Characteristics

Features may include intense, focused interests; adherence to routines; atypical prosody and eye contact; sensory sensitivities; and difficulties reading social cues, while verbal ability and cognition are typically average or above.
