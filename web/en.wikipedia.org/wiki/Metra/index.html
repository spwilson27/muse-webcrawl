Metra is the commuter rail system in the Chicago metropolitan area serving the City of Chicago and its surrounding suburbs via Chicago Union Station, the Ogilvie Transportation Center, the LaSalle Street Station, and Millennium Station. It is operated by the Northeast Illinois Regional Commuter Railroad Corporation under the oversight of the Regional Transportation Authority (RTA). With 11 lines and 243 stations over about 487 route miles, it is one of the largest commuter rail systems in the United States.

History

Commuter service around Chicago dates to the 1850s, but by the 1970s the private railroads operating the trains were losing money and threatening abandonment. The RTA began subsidizing the services in 1974, and in 1984 the operations were consolidated under the Metra brand. Metra directly operates most of its lines; the BNSF Line and Union Pacific lines have historically been operated under purchase-of-service agreements with those railroads, and the Metra Electric District and South Shore services involve the Northern Indiana Commuter Transportation District.

Lines

The system's lines radiate from four downtown terminals to suburbs in Cook, DuPage, Kane, Lake, McHenry, and Will counties in Illinois, and to Kenosha, Wisconsin. Major lines include the BNSF Line to Aurora, the Milwaukee District lines, the Union Pacific lines, the Rock Island District, and the electrified Metra Electric District. Two new lines, the SouthWest Service and North Central Service, were added in the 1990s. Before the COVID-19 pandemic, Metra carried roughly 74 million passenger trips per year; ridership has been recovering since.
