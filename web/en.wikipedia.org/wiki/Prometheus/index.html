Prometheus

Prometheus is a Titan god of fire in Greek mythology. He is best known for defying the Olympian gods by stealing fire from them and giving it to humanity in the form of technology, knowledge, and more generally, civilisation. Prometheus is thus a culture hero and trickster figure, credited by some traditions with the creation of humanity from clay.

Mythology

Prometheus, whose name means "forethought", was a son of the Titan Iapetus and brother of Epimetheus ("afterthought"), Atlas, and Menoetius. In Hesiod's Theogony and Works and Days, Prometheus tricks Zeus at Mecone over the division of sacrificial meat, prompting Zeus to withhold fire from mortals. Prometheus stole the fire back, hiding it in a fennel stalk, and gave it to humanity.

Punishment

As punishment, Zeus had Prometheus bound to a rock — in later tradition, in the Caucasus — where an eagle (the offspring of Typhon and Echidna) ate his liver every day, the organ regenerating each night. The torment lasted until Heracles shot the eagle and freed him. Zeus also sent Pandora, the first woman, whose jar released evils into the world, as retribution against mankind.

In literature and culture

Aeschylus' tragedy Prometheus Bound portrays him as a defiant benefactor of humanity. The myth has been retold ever since: Percy Bysshe Shelley's lyrical drama Prometheus Unbound, Mary Shelley's novel Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus, and countless works use Prometheus as a symbol of human striving, rebellion against tyranny, and the dangerous gift of technology. Chemists named the element promethium after him.
