Kolyma Highway

The Kolyma Highway (Russian: Федеральная автомобильная дорога «Колыма», R504) is a road through the Russian Far East connecting Magadan on the Sea of Okhotsk with Nizhny Bestyakh near Yakutsk on the Lena River. It is notorious as the "Road of Bones" (Doroga na kostyakh), because it was built largely by prisoners of the Gulag, many of whom died and are said to have been buried in the roadbed.

History

Construction began in 1932 under the Dalstroy trust, which administered the Kolyma gold-mining region using Sevvostlag camp labour. Prisoners, including many political prisoners, cut the road through permafrost, swamps and mountain passes in some of the coldest inhabited country on Earth, where temperatures fall below −50 °C. The road was substantially complete by 1953, the year of Stalin's death and the beginning of the end of the Kolyma camps. Estimates of the dead along the road vary widely and remain contested, but the route passes numerous camp sites and memorials, including the Mask of Sorrow monument above Magadan.

The road today

Much of the highway remains unpaved gravel, passable year-round but challenging: winter offers frozen river crossings, while summer brings mud and washed-out bridges. Since a bridge over the Lena at Yakutsk has never been completed, vehicles cross by ferry in summer and ice road in winter. The road traverses the gold towns of the Kolyma, the ghost settlement of Kadykchan, and near Oymyakon, one of the coldest villages on Earth. It has become a celebrated route for adventure motorcyclists and overland expeditions.
