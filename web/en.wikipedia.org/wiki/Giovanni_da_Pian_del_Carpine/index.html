Giovanni da Pian del Carpine

Giovanni da Pian del Carpine, OFM (Italian: Giovanni da Pian del Carpine, or variants such as John of Plano Carpini; c. 1185 – 1 August 1252) was a medieval Italian diplomat, Catholic archbishop, explorer and one of the first Europeans to enter the court of a Mongol emperor of the Mongol Empire. He was the author of the earliest important Western account of northern and Central Asia, Rus', and other regions of the Mongol dominion.

Born: c. 1185, Pian del Carpine, Umbria
Died: 1 August 1252, Bar, Montenegro
Known for: Mission to Güyük Khan, 1245–1247

The mission

In 1245, Pope Innocent IV sent Giovanni, then in his sixties, from Lyon with letters to the Mongol khan, protesting Mongol attacks on Christian lands and seeking information about Mongol intentions. Travelling via Kiev and the camp of Batu Khan on the Volga, his party crossed Central Asia and reached the imperial camp near Karakorum in time to witness the enthronement of Güyük Khan in August 1246. Güyük's reply, demanding the submission of the Pope and the kings of Europe, was carried back to the West.

Ystoria Mongalorum

Giovanni's report, the Ystoria Mongalorum ("History of the Mongols"), described Mongol customs, military organization, and tactics in sober detail, and was widely copied. It preceded the more famous journey of William of Rubruck by several years and Marco Polo's travels by a generation.
