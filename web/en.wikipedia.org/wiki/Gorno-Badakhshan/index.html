Gorno-Badakhshan, officially the Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous Region (GBAO), is an autonomous region in eastern Tajikistan. Occupying the Pamir Mountains — the "Roof of the World" — it covers roughly 45 per cent of the country's territory but is home to only about 3 per cent of its population, around 230,000 people. Its capital is Khorog.

Geography

The region borders Afghanistan across the Panj River to the south, China's Xinjiang to the east, and Kyrgyzstan to the north. It contains Tajikistan's highest peaks, including Ismoil Somoni Peak (7,495 m), Lake Karakul, and the 77-km Fedchenko Glacier. The Pamir Highway (M41) crosses the region through Khorog.

People and history

The population consists largely of Pamiri peoples — Shughni, Wakhi, Rushani, and others — who speak Eastern Iranian languages distinct from Tajik and mostly follow Ismaili Islam, led by the Aga Khan, whose development network is a major provider of services in the region. The autonomous oblast was created in 1925 within the Soviet Tajik republic. Gorno-Badakhshan was a centre of resistance during the Tajik civil war of the 1990s, and tensions between Khorog and the central government in Dushanbe have periodically flared since, notably in 2012 and 2021–2022.
