Ice road

Ice road
TypeSeasonal road on frozen water
RegionsCanada, Alaska, Russia, Scandinavia, Antarctica
SeasonTypically January–March in the Northern Hemisphere

An ice road is a winter road built on the frozen surface of a lake, river, or sea, or across frozen ground (where it is sometimes called an ice bridge or winter road). Ice roads are a vital lifeline for remote northern communities and mines that have no year-round road access, allowing heavy freight — fuel, building materials, and equipment — to be hauled in during a short season.

Construction and operation

Routes are prepared by ploughing snow clear so the cold can thicken the ice; crews monitor ice thickness continuously and set strict speed and spacing rules, because a moving vehicle generates a wave under the ice that can crack it if speed limits are exceeded. Speed limits are often as low as 10–25 km/h for loaded trucks on lake crossings.

Notable ice roads

Canada's Tibbitt to Contwoyto Winter Road, serving diamond mines in the Northwest Territories and Nunavut, is among the longest heavy-haul ice roads in the world at about 400 km, most of it over frozen lakes. During the Siege of Leningrad, the "Road of Life" crossed frozen Lake Ladoga to supply the besieged city. Popular culture brought wide attention to the work through the television series Ice Road Truckers.

Climate change

Shortening winters and thinner ice have progressively reduced the operating seasons of ice roads, threatening the economics of northern mines and communities and prompting investment in all-weather roads.
