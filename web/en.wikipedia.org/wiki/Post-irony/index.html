Post-irony

Post-irony (from Latin post, "after") is a term used to denote a state in which earnest and ironic intents become muddled, or in which a return to earnestness is built upon ironic foundations. It may refer to an artwork, a use of language, or a general cultural sensibility in which irony is employed sincerely, or sincerity is expressed through ironic conventions, so that the two become indistinguishable.

Usage

The term is closely related to New Sincerity and metamodernism. In internet culture, post-ironic memes layer irony so deeply that the original sincere sentiment becomes ambiguous — a joke repeated ironically until it is held sincerely, or a sincere statement framed as a joke as protection. Comedians and musicians whose work oscillates between parody and genuine feeling are frequently described as post-ironic.

Distinctions

Post-irony differs from simple irony, where a speaker means the opposite of what is said, and from meta-irony, where it is unclear whether even the ironic layer is itself ironic. In post-irony, the end state is a genuine return to sincerity that retains ironic forms.
