Giuseppe Domenico Scarlatti (26 October 1685 – 23 July 1757) was an Italian composer of the Baroque era. He is best known for his 555 keyboard sonatas, most of them for solo harpsichord, which display a bold harmonic language, brilliant virtuosity, and strong influence from Spanish and Portuguese folk music absorbed during his long service at the courts of Lisbon and Madrid.

Life

Scarlatti was born in Naples, the sixth of ten children of the composer Alessandro Scarlatti. He likely studied with his father and possibly with Bernardo Pasquini and Francesco Gasparini. By 1701 he was organist and composer at the royal chapel in Naples, and operas of his were staged there soon after. In 1709 he entered the service of the exiled Polish queen Maria Casimira in Rome, where he reportedly engaged in a keyboard contest with the young George Frideric Handel; Handel was judged superior on the organ, Scarlatti on the harpsichord, and the two remained friends.

In 1719 Scarlatti moved to Lisbon as music master to Princess Maria Barbara of Braganza. When she married the Spanish crown prince Ferdinand and moved to Spain in 1729, Scarlatti followed, spending the rest of his life in Spain, chiefly in Seville and Madrid. He died in Madrid in 1757.

Music

Only a fraction of Scarlatti's output was published in his lifetime, most famously the Essercizi per gravicembalo (1738), 30 sonatas dedicated to King John V of Portugal. The bulk of the 555 sonatas survive in manuscript collections copied in Spain. They are mostly in binary form, single-movement works of astonishing variety: hand-crossing, rapid repeated notes, acciaccaturas (crushed notes), and modulations to remote keys abound. Ralph Kirkpatrick's 1953 catalogue gives the sonatas their "K." numbers. The sonatas influenced later keyboard composers and remain central to the harpsichord and piano repertoires.
