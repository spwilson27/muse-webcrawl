New York Court of Appeals

The New York Court of Appeals is the highest court in the Unified Court System of the State of New York. Despite the state's naming conventions — in which the "Supreme Court" is the trial court of general jurisdiction — the Court of Appeals is New York's court of last resort, sitting in Albany.

Composition

The court consists of a chief judge and six associate judges, appointed by the governor from nominees of the Commission on Judicial Nomination and confirmed by the State Senate, for 14-year terms. The court sits in Court of Appeals Hall in Albany.

Jurisdiction

The Court of Appeals hears appeals primarily on questions of law, generally from the Appellate Division of the Supreme Court, the intermediate appellate court. Its decisions bind all other New York courts. The court has historically been one of the most influential state courts in the United States; Benjamin N. Cardozo served as its chief judge from 1927 to 1932 before joining the U.S. Supreme Court, and landmark decisions such as MacPherson v. Buick Motor Co. (1916), which reshaped negligence law, originated there.

History

The court was established under the New York Constitution of 1846 and reorganised under the constitution of 1894. Judges were originally elected; the shift to merit-based appointment occurred in 1977.
