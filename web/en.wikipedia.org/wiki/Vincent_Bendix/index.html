Vincent Bendix

Vincent Hugo Bendix (12 December 1881 – 27 March 1945) was an American inventor and industrialist of Swedish descent. He founded the Bendix Corporation, a major manufacturer of automotive, aviation, and household components, and gave his name to the Bendix starter drive and the Bendix automatic washing machine.

Career

Bendix began his career in the bicycle and early automobile industries in Chicago. In 1914 he acquired the rights to a screw-thread starter drive invented for starting automobile engines; the "Bendix drive," which automatically engaged and disengaged the starter pinion from the engine's flywheel, became standard equipment and the basis of his fortune. He organized the Bendix Corporation in 1924 in South Bend, Indiana, expanding into brakes, carburetors, and aviation instruments. Bendix also championed air racing, sponsoring the Bendix Trophy transcontinental air race, first held in 1931.

Later ventures

In the 1930s Bendix lent his name to the Bendix Home Laundry, which introduced one of the first successful automatic washing machines in 1937. His business ventures declined during the Great Depression and he spent his final years in reduced circumstances, dying in New York in 1945. The Bendix name survived through corporate successors in the automotive and aerospace industries.
