Emmerdale

Emmerdale (known as Emmerdale Farm until 1989) is a British television soap opera that has been broadcast on ITV since 16 October 1972. Created by Kevin Laffan, it is set in Emmerdale (known as Beckindale until 1994), a fictional village in the Yorkshire Dales. Produced by ITV Studios, it is filmed at the Leeds Studios with exterior scenes shot at a purpose-built village set in the Harewood estate near Leeds.

History

The programme began as a twice-weekly daytime serial about the Sugden family of Emmerdale Farm, with a gentler, more rural tone than its urban counterparts. In 1989 the "Farm" was dropped from the title as the focus broadened to the whole village and storylines became more dramatic. It moved to peak-time scheduling and increased to several episodes a week, becoming one of Britain's most-watched soaps alongside Coronation Street and EastEnders.

Notable storylines

One of the programme's most famous episodes, broadcast on 30 December 1993, depicted a plane crash over the village, which killed several characters and drew around 18 million viewers. Later high-profile plots have included the storm of 2003, the King's River show-home explosion, and the multi-vehicle Hotten Bypass crash in 2016. Long-running families include the Sugdens, the Dingles, the Tates, and the Kings.

Production and reception

Emmerdale typically airs several 30-minute episodes each week and has won numerous British Soap Awards and National Television Awards, including Serial Drama honours. The outdoor set, built in 1997, is also a visitor attraction during organised tours.
