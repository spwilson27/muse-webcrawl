Babri Masjid

The Babri Masjid (Urdu: بابری مسجد, lit. "Babur's Mosque") was a mosque in Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh, India, built in 1528–29 on the orders of Mir Baqi, a commander of the Mughal emperor Babur. It stood until 6 December 1992, when it was demolished by a large crowd of Hindu nationalist activists (kar sevaks) during a political rally. The demolition, and the Ayodhya dispute over the site — which many Hindus believe to be Ram Janmabhoomi, the birthplace of the deity Rama — became one of the most consequential religious and political controversies in modern India.

History and dispute

The mosque had three domes and was one of the largest in Uttar Pradesh. From the 19th century, Hindus sought access to the site, claiming a temple marking Rama's birthplace had previously stood there; clashes occurred in 1853–55, and in 1949 idols of Rama were placed inside the mosque, after which it was locked by court order. In the 1980s the Vishva Hindu Parishad launched a campaign to build a Ram temple on the site, and BJP leader L. K. Advani's 1990 Rath Yatra mobilised support. On 6 December 1992 a rally at the site turned into a crowd of some 150,000 that overwhelmed security and demolished the mosque within hours. Communal riots followed across India, killing roughly 2,000 people.

Aftermath

After decades of litigation, in November 2019 the Supreme Court of India awarded the disputed 2.77-acre site for a Ram temple, while directing that five acres elsewhere in Ayodhya be allotted for a mosque. The Ram Mandir on the site was consecrated in January 2024; the replacement mosque, named Masjid Muhammad bin Abdullah, is under construction at Dhannipur.
