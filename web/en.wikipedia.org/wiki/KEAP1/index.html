Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1, usually abbreviated Keap1 (the gene is written KEAP1), is a protein that in humans is encoded by the KEAP1 gene on chromosome 19. Keap1 is best known as the principal negative regulator of the transcription factor Nrf2 (encoded by NFE2L2), the master switch of the cellular antioxidant and detoxification response.

Structure

Human Keap1 contains 624 amino acids and several functional domains: an N-terminal region, a BTB/POZ domain that mediates dimerisation and binding to the scaffold protein Cullin 3 (CUL3), a cysteine-rich intervening region (IVR), and six Kelch repeats that fold into a β-propeller binding the Neh2 domain of Nrf2. Numerous reactive cysteine residues — notably Cys151, Cys273, and Cys288 — act as sensors for electrophiles and oxidants.

Function: the Keap1–Nrf2 pathway

Under resting conditions, a Keap1 homodimer binds a single molecule of Nrf2 at two sites ("hinge and latch") and presents it to the CUL3–RBX1 ubiquitin ligase. Nrf2 is polyubiquitinated and rapidly degraded by the proteasome, keeping its levels low. When the cell encounters oxidative or electrophilic stress — or pharmacological inducers such as sulforaphane from broccoli — modification of Keap1's sensor cysteines changes the complex so that newly synthesised Nrf2 escapes degradation, accumulates, and enters the nucleus. There, Nrf2 binds antioxidant response elements (AREs) and switches on genes encoding detoxifying and antioxidant enzymes, including NQO1, heme oxygenase-1 (HMOX1), and the subunits of glutamate–cysteine ligase used to make glutathione.

Clinical significance

Loss-of-function mutations in KEAP1, or activating mutations in NFE2L2, occur in a substantial fraction of non-small-cell lung cancers (roughly a fifth to a quarter of lung adenocarcinomas) and in other tumours. Constitutive Nrf2 activation in these cancers promotes survival, metabolic rewiring, and resistance to chemotherapy and radiotherapy, making the pathway an active target of drug development. Conversely, pharmacological Nrf2 activators that act through Keap1 — notably dimethyl fumarate, approved for multiple sclerosis and psoriasis — exploit the pathway to damp oxidative damage and inflammation.
