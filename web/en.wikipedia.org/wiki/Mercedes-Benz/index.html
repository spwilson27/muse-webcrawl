Mercedes-Benz (German pronunciation: [mɛʁˌtseːdəs ˈbɛnts, -dɛs -]), commonly referred to simply as Mercedes, is a German luxury automotive brand. It is one of the world's leading manufacturers of luxury passenger cars and vans, and was the largest manufacturer of premium vehicles worldwide in many recent years. The brand is a division of the Mercedes-Benz Group AG (formerly Daimler AG).

History

The brand's origins lie with Karl Benz's creation of the first internal combustion engine automobile, the Benz Patent-Motorwagen, patented in January 1886, and with Gottlieb Daimler and engineer Wilhelm Maybach, who converted a stagecoach by adding a petrol engine the same year. The Mercedes automobile was first marketed in 1901 by Daimler Motoren Gesellschaft (DMG). The name "Mercedes" came from Mercedes Jellinek, daughter of dealer Emil Jellinek, who specified the car and whose name the successful racing cars carried.

The Mercedes-Benz brand was created in 1926 when Benz & Cie. and DMG merged into Daimler-Benz AG. The company's trademark, the three-pointed star in a ring, symbolises the dominance of Daimler engines on land, on water and in the air.

Product range

Mercedes-Benz organises its passenger cars into classes, including the A-Class (compact), C-Class (executive), E-Class and the flagship S-Class, alongside SUVs under the GL nomenclature (GLA, GLC, GLE, GLS), the utilitarian G-Class, coupés, roadsters, and the all-electric EQ sub-brand launched with the EQC in 2019 and followed by the EQS and EQE. The high-performance division Mercedes-AMG and the ultra-luxury Mercedes-Maybach sub-brand sit at the top of the range. Mercedes-Benz Vans produces the Sprinter, Vito and V-Class.

Motorsport

Mercedes has a long motorsport history, from the pre-war "Silver Arrows" Grand Prix cars to the modern Mercedes-AMG Petronas Formula One Team, which won eight consecutive Constructors' Championships from 2014 to 2021, with Lewis Hamilton winning six of his seven drivers' titles with the team.
