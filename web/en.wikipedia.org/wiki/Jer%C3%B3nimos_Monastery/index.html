Jerónimos Monastery

LocationBelém, Lisbon, Portugal
Founded1501 (King Manuel I)
StyleManueline (Portuguese Late Gothic)
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (1983)

The Jerónimos Monastery (Mosteiro dos Jerónimos) is a former monastery of the Order of Saint Jerome near the Tagus river in the parish of Belém, Lisbon, Portugal. It is the supreme example of the Manueline style and one of the most important monuments of the Portuguese Age of Discovery.

History

King Manuel I commissioned the monastery in 1501, funding it partly with a tax on the spice trade. It was built on the site of a hermitage where Vasco da Gama and his crew prayed before departing for India in 1497. Architects including Diogo de Boitaca, João de Castilho, Diogo de Torralva and Jérôme de Rouen shaped its church, two-storey cloister and richly carved south portal over the following century.

The monastery houses the tombs of Vasco da Gama and the poet Luís de Camões, and since the nineteenth century the remains of kings of Portugal. Secularised in 1833, the complex later housed institutions and museums; the famous Pastéis de Belém custard tarts originated with its monks. In 1983 UNESCO listed it, together with the nearby Tower of Belém, as a World Heritage Site.
