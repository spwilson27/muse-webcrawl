Saint Ciarán of Clonmacnoise

Ciarán of Clonmacnoise (Ciarán Mac an tSaeir)
Born: c. 516, County Roscommon, Ireland
Died: c. 549, Clonmacnoise
Feast day: 9 September
Known for: Founder of Clonmacnoise; one of the Twelve Apostles of Ireland

Ciarán of Clonmacnoise (c. 516 – c. 549), also called Ciarán the Younger to distinguish him from Ciarán of Saigir, was one of the Twelve Apostles of Ireland and the founder of the great monastic city of Clonmacnoise on the banks of the River Shannon.

Life

Tradition says Ciarán studied under St Finnian at Clonard and St Enda on Inishmore before settling in 544 at Clonmacnoise, at the crossroads of Ireland where the Shannon meets the glacial ridge road of the Esker Riada. He died only months after the foundation, traditionally of plague, at about thirty-three years of age.

Legacy

His foundation grew into a major centre of learning and craftsmanship famous for manuscripts such as the Annals of Tigernach and metalwork and high crosses. His feast day is 9 September.
