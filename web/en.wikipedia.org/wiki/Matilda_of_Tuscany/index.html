Matilda of Tuscany (Italian: Matilde di Canossa; Latin: Matilda; c. 1046 – 24 July 1115), also known as Matilda of Canossa and la Gran Contessa ("the Great Countess"), was a powerful Italian noblewoman, military leader, and one of the few medieval women remembered for her military accomplishments. As the last of the House of Canossa, she ruled an extensive territory in Lombardy and Tuscany — the so-called Matildine lands — and was the principal Italian ally of the Papacy during the Investiture Controversy.

Life

Matilda was the daughter of Boniface III, margrave of Tuscany, and Beatrice of Lorraine. After her father's assassination (1052) and the deaths of her siblings, she inherited the Canossa domains under her mother's regency. Her first marriage, to Godfrey the Hunchback of Lorraine, ended with his assassination in 1076.

The Investiture Controversy and Canossa

A devoted supporter of Pope Gregory VII, Matilda hosted the famous meeting at her castle of Canossa in January 1077, where the excommunicated Emperor Henry IV stood barefoot in the snow for three days seeking absolution — the "Walk to Canossa." When war resumed, Matilda commanded troops in person for the papal cause for decades, suffering defeats but also winning notable victories, such as at Sorbara (1084). Her second marriage (1089), to the teenage Welf V of Bavaria, was a political alliance against Henry IV and soon collapsed. She subsequently resisted Henry V as well, though in 1111 she reached an accommodation with him and was reportedly recognized as imperial vicar in Italy.

Legacy

Matilda endowed churches and monasteries (including San Benedetto Po) and corresponded with leading churchmen such as Anselm of Lucca. She died in 1115, bequeathing her allodial lands to the Papacy — a donation contested by emperors for centuries and central to the territorial claims of the Papal States. Her body was transferred to St Peter's Basilica in Rome in 1634, where Bernini designed her monument; she is one of only a handful of women buried there.
