U Bein Bridge (Burmese: ဦးပိန် တံတား) is a wooden footbridge crossing Taungthaman Lake near Amarapura in Myanmar. At 1.2 kilometres (0.75 mi) long, it is believed to be the oldest and (at the time of its construction) longest teakwood bridge in the world. It was built around 1850 by the mayor U Bein, reportedly using teak salvaged from the dismantled royal palace of Inwa when the capital moved to Amarapura.

Description

The bridge is constructed of more than 1,000 wooden posts driven into the lake bed, with planks laid across them; some posts have been replaced with concrete supports due to rot. It follows a gentle curve across the lake, with nine passageways (now roofed rest pavilions) along its length where sections could originally be lifted to allow boats to pass. The water level of the lake varies seasonally, and in the dry season the bridge stands high above farmland.

Tourism

U Bein Bridge is one of Myanmar's most photographed landmarks, especially at sunset, when monks, cyclists, and fishermen are silhouetted against the sky. Local people continue to use it daily, while visitors hire wooden rowboats on the lake below. Flood damage and deterioration have led to periodic repairs and debate over the conservation of its original teak structure.
