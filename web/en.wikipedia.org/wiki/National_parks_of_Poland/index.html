National parks of Poland

There are 23 national parks in Poland, covering about 3,149 km², roughly 1% of the country's area. They are administered by the Ministry of Climate and Environment.

List

The oldest is Białowieża National Park (1932, refounded 1947), famous for its primeval forest and European bison, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Others include Tatra, Pieniny, Ojców, Karkonosze, Babia Góra, Wielkopolska, Kampinos (near Warsaw), Słowiński with its moving dunes, Biebrza (the largest, protecting wetlands), Wigry, Drawa, Wolin, and the newest, Bieszczady and others in the Carpathians.

Parks protect representative ecosystems, flora and fauna, and provide scientific research and limited tourism.
