2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike

Dates5 November 2007 – 12 February 2008
Duration100 days
PartiesWGA vs AMPTP

The 2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike was a labour conflict between the Writers Guild of America, East and West, and the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers. Lasting 100 days, it halted much of American television and film production.

Issues

The central dispute concerned residuals for "new media": writers demanded a fair share of revenue from digital downloads and internet streaming, fearing that online distribution would grow while their compensation formula, based on an outdated home-video rate, remained negligible.

Impact and settlement

Late-night talk shows went dark, scripted series shortened their seasons, some shows were cancelled, and the Golden Globes ceremony was replaced by a press conference. The strike ended in February 2008 with a deal giving writers a percentage of distributors' gross receipts from streaming. Its legacy shaped later negotiations, and the WGA struck again in 2023, partly over the same streaming issues.
