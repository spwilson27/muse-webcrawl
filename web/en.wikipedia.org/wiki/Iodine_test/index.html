Iodine test

The iodine test is used to test for the presence of starch. A solution of iodine (I2) and potassium iodide (KI) in water — commonly known as Lugol's iodine — has an orange-brown colour. When added to a sample containing starch, the colour changes to a deep blue-black.

Chemistry

The colour change arises because polyiodide chains (such as I5−) become trapped inside the helical coil of amylose, the linear component of starch, forming a charge-transfer complex that absorbs light strongly in the visible range. Amylopectin, the branched component, gives a less intense reddish-brown colour because its shorter chains form fewer stable complexes. On heating, the helix unwinds and the blue colour disappears; it returns on cooling.

Procedure and uses

A few drops of iodine solution are added to the test material. The test is a standard school and laboratory procedure for identifying starchy foods such as potato and rice, and in biology it is used to follow the progress of photosynthesis experiments, in which leaves are tested for starch after exposure to light. Iodine solution is also used to stain specimens for microscopy and as a general indicator for starch in paper and textile manufacture.
