Engine braking

Engine braking occurs when the retarding forces within an engine are used to slow down a motor vehicle, as opposed to using additional external braking mechanisms such as friction brakes or magnetic brakes. It is achieved by releasing the accelerator while in gear; in a petrol engine the closed throttle creates a vacuum that resists the pistons, while pumping and friction losses dissipate the vehicle's kinetic energy.

Engine braking helps control speed on long descents without overheating the service brakes.

Applications

Engine braking is particularly useful on long downhill grades, where continuous use of friction brakes can cause brake fade through overheating. Shifting to a lower gear increases the effect. Diesel engines, lacking a throttle, require dedicated devices: the compression release ("Jake") brake, which releases compressed air at the top of the compression stroke, and exhaust brakes that restrict the exhaust flow.

Regulation

Because compression release brakes are loud, many municipalities prohibit their use in residential areas, with road signs instructing truck drivers accordingly. In hybrid and electric vehicles, regenerative braking provides a similar retarding effect while recovering energy.
