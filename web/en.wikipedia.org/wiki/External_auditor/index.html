External auditor

An external auditor examining client financial records.

An external auditor is an independent accounting professional or firm engaged by an organisation to examine its financial statements and express an opinion on whether they give a true and fair view (or are presented fairly, in all material respects) in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework, such as IFRS or US GAAP. External auditors are distinct from internal auditors, who are employees of the organisation itself.

Role and duties

The external audit is performed under International Standards on Auditing (ISAs) or national equivalents. Auditors obtain reasonable — not absolute — assurance that the financial statements are free of material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error. Work typically involves risk assessment, testing of internal controls, substantive procedures such as confirming balances with third parties, inspecting documents, and analytical procedures. The result is an audit report containing an unmodified ("clean") opinion or, where problems are found, a qualified, adverse, or disclaimed opinion.

Independence and regulation

Independence is the foundation of the role: auditors must be free of financial and other conflicts of interest with the client, and in many jurisdictions are restricted from providing certain non-audit services. Listed companies' auditors are overseen by bodies such as the PCAOB in the United States and the FRC in the United Kingdom. The market for auditing large listed companies is dominated by the "Big Four" firms: Deloitte, PwC, EY, and KPMG.

Liability

External auditors owe duties to their client and, in some circumstances, to third parties, and face professional, regulatory, and legal sanctions for negligent or complicit audits — failures associated with major corporate collapses such as Enron, which destroyed the firm Arthur Andersen in 2002.
