Skateboard

A skateboard is a type of sports equipment used for skateboarding. It is usually made of a specially designed wooden deck — traditionally seven-ply maple — coated with polyurethane and fitted with two metal trucks and four polyurethane wheels. Riders propel themselves by pushing with one foot while the other remains on the board, or by pumping in structures such as bowls and half-pipes.

History

Skateboarding emerged in California in the late 1940s and 1950s, when surfers sought a way to ride on land; early boards were wooden planks with roller-skate wheels attached. The first manufactured skateboards appeared around 1959, and the sport grew in waves of popularity. The invention of polyurethane wheels in the 1970s greatly improved grip and smoothness, enabling the development of vert (ramp) riding and, in the late 1970s, the ollie — the jumping technique that underpins modern street skateboarding. Skateboarding debuted as an Olympic sport at the Tokyo 2020 Games.

Parts

  • Deck — the flat board, usually maple plywood with a concave profile and upturned nose and tail.
  • Grip tape — an abrasive sheet applied to the top of the deck for traction.
  • Trucks — T-shaped metal axles that pivot to allow turning.
  • Wheels — polyurethane, varying in size and hardness by discipline.
  • Bearings — fitted inside the wheels to allow them to spin on the axles.

Types

Standard "popsicle" decks dominate street and park skating. Other forms include longboards for cruising and downhill, cruisers with larger soft wheels, old-school shaped boards, and electric skateboards with motorised drive.
