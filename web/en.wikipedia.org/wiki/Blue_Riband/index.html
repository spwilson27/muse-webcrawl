Blue Riband

[Hero image: RMS Queen Mary, three-time holder of the Blue Riband, at speed in the Atlantic]

The Blue Riband is an unofficial accolade given to the passenger liner crossing the Atlantic Ocean in regular service with the record highest average speed. The term was borrowed from horse racing and was not widely used before 1910. Records were kept for both westbound and eastbound crossings, the westbound being the harder, against the prevailing currents and weather.

History

Great Western (1838) was the first steamship to hold the record; competition intensified among British, German, French and American lines, culminating in the great pre-war liners Mauretania — which held the record for over two decades from 1909 — and later the Normandie, Queen Mary and finally the United States, whose 1952 maiden voyage marks of about 35.6 knots (westbound average 34.5 knots) still stand as the fastest crossings by a liner. In 1935 Harold K. Hales formalised the award with the Hales Trophy for the fastest Atlantic crossing; the rivalry between Normandie and Queen Mary in the 1930s made the Blue Riband famous worldwide.

Because the "record" is for average speed on a revenue voyage between defined points (traditionally Ambrose Light and Bishop Rock, or US and European ports), specialist craft such as catamaran ferries have since crossed faster, claiming the Hales Trophy, but the United States remains the fastest true ocean liner.

Famous holders

  • Great Western (1838)
  • Persia and Scotia (Cunard, 1850s–60s)
  • Adriatic and Germanic (White Star, 1870s)
  • Campania (1890s)
  • Kaiser Wilhelm der Grosse (1898)
  • Mauretania (1909–1929)
  • Bremen (1929)
  • Rex (1933)
  • Normandie (1935)
  • Queen Mary (1936–1952)
  • United States (1952–present, for liners)
