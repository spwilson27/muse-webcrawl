Beluga sturgeon

The beluga sturgeon (Huso huso), also known as the great sturgeon, is a species of anadromous fish in the sturgeon family. Native to the Caspian, Black, Adriatic and Sea of Azov basins, it is one of the largest freshwater fish in the world — reliably recorded at over 5 metres in the past and potentially exceeding a tonne in weight — and among the longest-lived, with specimens estimated at over 100 years old. Its name is unrelated to the beluga whale; both derive from the Russian word for "white."

Biology

Belugas spend most of their lives at sea and migrate up rivers such as the Volga, Danube and Ural to spawn, females maturing only after 15–25 years and spawning once every several years. Unlike most sturgeons, which feed on invertebrates, the beluga is an active predator of fish. Its roe is the celebrated beluga caviar, the largest-grained and most expensive of all caviars, which has driven intense fishing pressure.

Conservation

Overfishing for caviar, poaching, pollution and the damming of spawning rivers have caused catastrophic declines; populations in the Danube and elsewhere survive largely through stocking, and the natural Caspian population is a fraction of its former size. The species is listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List and is protected under CITES Appendix II, with trade in wild beluga caviar suspended at times. Farmed belugas are increasingly raised for caviar as an alternative to wild catch.
