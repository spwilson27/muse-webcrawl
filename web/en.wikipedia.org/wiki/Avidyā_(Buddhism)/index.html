Avidyā (Buddhism)

Avidyā
Sanskritअविद्या avidyā
Paliavijjā
TranslationIgnorance, delusion
OppositeVidyā / prajñā (wisdom)
PositionFirst link of dependent origination; one of the three poisons

Avidyā (Sanskrit: अविद्या; Pali: avijjā) is a Buddhist term usually translated as "ignorance" or " delusion ". It is the fundamental misunderstanding of the nature of reality—particularly of impermanence (anicca), suffering (dukkha) and not-self (anattā)—and functions as the root cause of suffering in the cycle of rebirth (saṃsāra).

Role in doctrine

Avidyā is the first of the twelve links (nidānas) of dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda): conditioned by ignorance, volitional formations arise, and so on through the chain that sustains rebirth. It is also counted among the three poisons (kleśa)—delusion, greed and aversion—shown at the centre of the Bhavacakra (Wheel of Life), where a pig or boar represents ignorance.

Ignorance is not merely lack of information but a deep misperception: taking what is impermanent to be permanent, what is unsatisfactory to be a source of lasting happiness, and what is not-self to be a self. Its antidote is insight (vipassanā) and wisdom (prajñā), cultivated through the Noble Eightfold Path, by which ignorance is dispelled "as the light of dawn dispels darkness".
