ISO 9001 is the internationally recognized standard for quality management systems (QMS). It is the most widely used standard of the ISO 9000 family and the only one in the family to which organizations can be certified. It sets out criteria for a quality management system applicable to any organization, regardless of size or sector.

Principles and structure

The standard is built on quality management principles including customer focus, leadership, engagement of people, a process approach, evidence-based decision making and continual improvement. The 2015 edition follows the Annex SL structure shared by other management standards such as ISO 14001: context of the organization, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation and improvement. It introduced risk-based thinking and reduced mandatory documentation, replacing the required quality manual with "documented information".

Certification

Certification is performed by independent accredited certification bodies, typically on a three-year cycle with annual surveillance audits. More than one million certificates have been issued worldwide. ISO itself does not certify organizations. Evidence on whether certification improves business performance is mixed; supporters cite process discipline and market access, while critics cite bureaucratic compliance.
