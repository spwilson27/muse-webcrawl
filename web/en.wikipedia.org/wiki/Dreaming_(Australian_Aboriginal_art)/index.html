Dreaming (Australian Aboriginal art)

Dreaming
Also: The Dreaming, Tjukurrpa, Jukurrpa
Context: Aboriginal Australian spirituality and art
Media: Ground painting, bark, acrylic on canvas, rock art

In Australian Aboriginal art, a Dreaming is a story owned by an artist or their family group that explains features of the world, ancestral journeys, and moral law. 'The Dreaming' (Tjukurrpa in Arrernte, Jukurrpa in Warlpiri) refers to the timeless creation period when ancestral beings shaped the land.

Representation

Dreaming stories are depicted in sand and body painting, rock art, bark painting, and—since the Papunya movement of 1971–72—acrylic dot paintings on canvas. Concentric circles often mark waterholes or camp sites, tracks show ancestral travel, and restricted sacred details are obscured by dots.

Ownership and protocol

Rights to paint a Dreaming are inherited and governed by kinship law; artists may only depict stories for which they hold custodial rights. This system underpins Indigenous intellectual property debates and authentication of Aboriginal art.
