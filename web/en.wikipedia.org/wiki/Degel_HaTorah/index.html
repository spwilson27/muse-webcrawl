Degel HaTorah

Degel HaTorah (Hebrew: דגל התורה, "Banner of the Torah") is an Ashkenazi Haredi political party in Israel. It was founded in 1988 by Rabbi Elazar Menachem Shach as a breakaway from Agudat Yisrael, representing the Lithuanian (non-Hasidic, or "Litvish") stream of Haredi Judaism. Since 1992 it has contested Knesset elections jointly with Agudat Yisrael as the United Torah Judaism alliance.

History

Tensions between Hasidic and Lithuanian factions within Agudat Yisrael had long simmered. Rabbi Shach, the pre-eminent Lithuanian leader, was dissatisfied with the influence of Hasidic rebbes on the party's council and with its political compromises. In 1988 he founded Degel HaTorah, which won two seats in that year's election, while Agudat Yisrael won five. The two parties reunited for the 1992 elections as United Torah Judaism, an arrangement that has continued, with the parties dividing seats and the rotating leadership between them.

Ideology and leadership

Degel HaTorah's policies are determined by its Council of Torah Sages (Moetzes Gedolei HaTorah), headed successively by Rabbi Shach, Rabbi Yosef Shalom Elyashiv and Rabbi Aharon Yehuda Leib Shteinman, and subsequently by Rabbi Chaim Kanievsky and Rabbi Gershon Edelstein. The party prioritises funding for yeshivas and Haredi education, exemption of yeshiva students from military service, Sabbath observance in the public sphere, and social welfare for large Haredi families. It publishes through the newspaper Yated Ne'eman. Its long-serving Knesset representatives include Moshe Gafni, chairman of the Knesset Finance Committee for many years.
