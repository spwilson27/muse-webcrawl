Art name

Art names are traditionally used in Chinese calligraphy and painting

Used byScholars, artists, writers in East Asian traditions

An art name (in Chinese: hào, Japanese: gō, Korean: ho) is a pseudonym or pen name traditionally adopted by East Asian artists, writers, scholars and statesmen, in addition to their personal and courtesy names. Unlike a courtesy name (zì), which was conferred on reaching adulthood, an art name was typically chosen by the person themselves, often expressing personal philosophy, aspirations, a place of residence, or an admired trait.

Usage

In imperial China, literati might accumulate several art names over a lifetime. Famous examples include Su Shi, known as Dongpo Jushi ("Lay Buddhist of the Eastern Slope"), from which his common name Su Dongpo derives; Tao Yuanming, who styled himself "Master of the Five Willows"; and Li Bai, called the "Banished Immortal." Painters signed works with their art names and seal carvings, making the art name the principal identifier in Chinese art history.

The custom spread to Japan, where Edo-period artists and haiku poets (Bashō took his name from a banana plant) adopted gō, and to Korea, where scholars of the Joseon dynasty used ho extensively. In the modern era, art names remain common among practitioners of calligraphy, ink painting, seal carving and traditional poetry in China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam.
