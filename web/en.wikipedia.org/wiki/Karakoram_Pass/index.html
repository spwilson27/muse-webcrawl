The Karakoram Pass is a high mountain pass between India and China, connecting Ladakh with the Tarim Basin in Xinjiang. At an elevation of about 5,540 metres (18,176 ft), it is the highest point on the ancient caravan route between Leh in Ladakh and Yarkand in the Tarim Basin. The pass lies on the watershed between the Indus River system and the Tarim Basin.

The name "Karakoram" is of Turkic origin, meaning "black gravel", a reference to the dark scree covering much of the region. The pass should not be confused with the Karakoram Highway, which crosses the range much further west at the Khunjerab Pass between Pakistan and China.

History

For centuries the pass was the key link on the southern branch of the Silk Road used by traders carrying silk, spices, tea, ivory and other goods between Central Asia and the Indian subcontinent. The journey over the pass was notoriously dangerous: the extreme altitude, thin air, sudden storms and lack of grazing for pack animals caused heavy losses among caravans, and the route was littered with the bones of pack animals.

In the 19th century the pass featured in the "Great Game" between the British and Russian empires. The British explorer and officer Francis Younghusband crossed it in 1889, and the route was surveyed as a possible invasion path into British India.

Geopolitics

The pass lies close to the trijunction of India, China and the region administered by Pakistan. The boundary in this sector has never been formally delimited between India and China, and the area east of the pass, including the Aksai Chin plateau, is disputed. The pass itself sits on the Line of Actual Control between Indian- and Chinese-controlled territory. The nearby Daulat Beg Oldi, an Indian military outpost and advanced landing ground, is one of the highest airstrips in the world.
