Xerox PARC

Xerox PARC (Palo Alto Research Center) is a research and development company in Palo Alto, California, founded in 1970 by Xerox Corporation. It is famous for inventing many of the technologies that define modern personal computing, including the graphical user interface (GUI), the computer mouse as popularized through its work, laser printing, Ethernet networking, and object-oriented programming.

History

Xerox established PARC in 1970, deliberately locating it far from the company's East Coast headquarters to give researchers freedom. Under managers such as George Pake and Bob Taylor, PARC recruited many of the leading computer scientists of the era, including Alan Kay, Adele Goldberg, and Douglas Engelbart's former colleagues.

The Alto and modern computing

In 1973 PARC completed the Alto, one of the first personal computers. It featured a bitmapped display, a mouse, windows, icons, and Ethernet connectivity — a combination that anticipated the personal computers of the following decades. The Alto was never sold commercially, but it profoundly influenced the Apple Macintosh and Microsoft Windows.

Other innovations

PARC researchers developed the Smalltalk programming language, the Bravo and Gypsy word processors (precursors of modern WYSIWYG editing), InterPress page description language, and early work on very-large-scale integration (VLSI) design. The laser printer, developed from Gary Starkweather's work, became a major commercial success for Xerox.

Legacy

PARC is often cited as an example of a research laboratory whose inventions were commercialized largely by other companies. In 2023, Xerox donated PARC to SRI International, and it continues to operate as a research organization.
