Philip Gröning

Born: April 7, 1959, Düsseldorf, West Germany
Occupation: Film director, documentary filmmaker
Known for: Into Great Silence

Gröning directed the acclaimed documentary Into Great Silence.

Philip Gröning (born April 7, 1959) is a German film director, documentary film maker, and screenwriter. He is best known internationally for Into Great Silence (German: Die große Stille, 2005), a nearly three-hour, almost wordless documentary about the lives of Carthusian monks at the Grande Chartreuse monastery in the French Alps.

Career

Gröning first asked permission to film inside the Grande Chartreuse in 1984; the order's council replied that they would consider it in time, and permission was finally granted some sixteen years later. He lived in the monastery for months, filming alone with a digital video camera and recording all sound himself, and spent years editing the material. The finished film, structured by natural light, chant, and the rhythms of monastic routine, won the Special Jury Prize at the Sundance Film Festival and the European Film Award for Best Documentary.

Other works

His earlier feature films include The Summer Passion (Sommer der Leidenschaft, 1992) and The Terrorists! (Die Terroristen!, 1992), and the drama L'amour, l'argent, l'amour (2000). He later directed the feature The Policeman's Wife (2013), which screened in competition at the Venice Film Festival, and My Brother's Name Is Robert and He Is an Idiot (2018), shown at the Berlin International Film Festival.
