Charge controller

A charge controller, charge regulator or battery regulator limits the rate at which electric current is added to or drawn from electric batteries to protect against electrical overload, overcharging, and may protect against overvoltage. This prevents conditions that reduce battery performance or lifespan and may pose safety risks. A charge controller may also prevent completely draining ("deep discharging") a battery to protect battery life.

Applications

Charge controllers are sold to consumers as separate system components for use in off-grid and hybrid solar power and wind power systems, in recreational vehicles and boats, and in remote installations such as telecommunications relays. In solar applications, the controller sits between the photovoltaic array and the battery bank, disconnecting or modulating the charging source when the batteries reach full charge.

Types

Pulse-width modulation (PWM) controllers effectively hold the battery at a regulated voltage by switching the charging current on and off many times per second; they are simple, robust and inexpensive, but require nominal matching of array and battery voltages. Maximum power point tracking (MPPT) controllers use a DC-to-DC converter to operate the array at its most productive voltage and convert the excess voltage into additional charging current, harvesting significantly more energy — especially in cold weather and with higher-voltage arrays — at greater cost and complexity.

Charging stages

Modern controllers implement multi-stage charging profiles for lead-acid batteries: a bulk stage at maximum safe current, an absorption stage at constant voltage, and a float stage at reduced voltage to maintain full charge, sometimes with periodic equalisation charges. Lithium battery systems instead rely on a battery management system (BMS), which performs cell balancing and protective disconnection.
