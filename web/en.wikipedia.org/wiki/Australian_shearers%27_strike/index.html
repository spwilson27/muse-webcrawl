Australian shearers' strike

1891 Australian shearers' strike
Date: January–May 1891
Location: Central Queensland, Australia
Centre: Barcaldine
Outcome: Strike defeated; spurred labour political movement

The 1891 Australian shearers' strike was one of the earliest and most significant industrial disputes in Australian history. Shearers in central Queensland, organised by the Australian Shearers' Union, struck against pastoralists who sought to cut wages and employ non-union labour under "freedom of contract".

The strike

Strikers established camps at Barcaldine and other towns, electing strike committees and parading under the Eureka flag. The colonial government deployed police and troops, and strike leaders were arrested and tried for conspiracy and sedition at Rockhampton; several were imprisoned on St Helena Island. The strike collapsed in May 1891, but the defeat drove unionists toward parliamentary politics: the Australian Labor Party traces its origins partly to the strike, and the tree of knowledge at Barcaldine became an enduring labour symbol. A second shearers' strike followed in 1894.
