Masocore

Masocore is a video game subgenre with a focus on intense difficulty. The name is a portmanteau of "masochism" and "hardcore". Masocore games are characterised by trial-and-error gameplay, frequent player deaths, and mechanics that demand memorisation and precision, with progress coming through repeated attempts. The term has been used in game journalism and criticism since the late 2000s.

Characteristics

Masocore games foreground difficulty as a design value: hazards are often placed to catch first-time players off guard, checkpoints may be sparse, and death is frequent but restart is fast, encouraging a rhythm of attempt, failure, and refinement. Many are platformers with one-hit deaths. Unlike the difficulty of early arcade games, which was tuned to consume coins, masocore difficulty is a deliberate aesthetic aimed at players who derive satisfaction from mastery.

Examples

Frequently cited examples include the freeware game I Wanna Be the Guy (2007), whose deliberately unfair traps made it a prototype of the form; Edmund McMillen's Super Meat Boy (2010); the Kaizo Mario ROM hacks of Super Mario World, created from 2007 onward; Terry Cavanagh's VVVVVV (2010) and Super Hexagon (2012); and Celeste (2018), which pairs punishing precision platforming with an unusually forgiving assist mode and a story about anxiety and perseverance. The term is sometimes applied more broadly to punishing action games such as FromSoftware's Souls series, though those are more often classed separately.
