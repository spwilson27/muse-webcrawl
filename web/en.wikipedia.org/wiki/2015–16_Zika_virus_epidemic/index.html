The 2015–16 Zika virus epidemic was an epidemic of Zika virus disease that began in Brazil in early 2015 and spread across the Americas and beyond. On 1 February 2016 the World Health Organization declared it a Public Health Emergency of International Concern — only the fourth such declaration — because of the virus's association with birth defects.

Course of the epidemic

Zika, first isolated in Uganda in 1947, had previously caused only mild illness and small outbreaks in Africa, Asia and the Pacific (notably French Polynesia in 2013–14). Confirmed in north-eastern Brazil in May 2015, it spread explosively through an immunologically naive population via Aedes aegypti mosquitoes; by early 2016 local transmission was reported across Latin America and the Caribbean, with travel-associated cases worldwide. Estimated infections in the Americas ran into the millions, though most cases were asymptomatic or mild.

Microcephaly link

In late 2015 Brazilian doctors reported a surge of babies born with microcephaly in the same regions hit first by Zika. In April 2016 the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention confirmed that Zika infection in pregnancy causes microcephaly and other severe fetal brain defects, collectively termed congenital Zika syndrome. Brazil ultimately confirmed several thousand cases. Increased rates of the paralysing Guillain–Barré syndrome were also reported in adults.

Response and decline

Responses included mosquito control, travel advice for pregnant women, and debate over the 2016 Rio Olympics (which went ahead). Transmission declined sharply in 2017 as population immunity rose, and WHO ended the emergency declaration in November 2016; no licensed vaccine emerged.
