Covalent radius


Image placeholder: Covalent radius

The covalent radius of an atom is a measure of the size of an atom that forms part of a single covalent bond. It is usually defined as half the distance between the nuclei of two identical atoms joined by a single bond, although separate values exist for double and triple bonds.

Measurement

Covalent radii are obtained from X-ray diffraction measurements of bond lengths in molecules and crystals. Self-consistent sets of radii have been compiled by researchers such as Linus Pauling, and more recently by Cordero and co-workers (2008) and Pyykkö and Atsumi, who published values for single, double, and triple bonds for most elements.

Trends

Covalent radii increase down a group of the periodic table as new electron shells are added, and generally decrease across a period as the effective nuclear charge rises. They are smaller than metallic or van der Waals radii for the same element because a covalent bond pulls the atoms closer together.
