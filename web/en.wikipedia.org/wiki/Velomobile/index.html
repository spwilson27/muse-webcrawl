Velomobile

A velomobile, velomobiel, velo car, or bicycle car is a human-powered vehicle related to the bicycle, with a body (fairing) that provides aerodynamic benefit and/or weather protection. Velomobiles are usually recumbent tricycles (or occasionally quadracycles) enclosed in a streamlined shell of fibreglass, carbon fibre, or aluminium.

Characteristics

Thanks to their very low aerodynamic drag, velomobiles are the fastest human-powered vehicles in ordinary use: a fit rider can cruise at 40–50 km/h on the flat with an effort comparable to riding a conventional bicycle at 25 km/h. The enclosure protects the rider from rain and cold, and the three wheels make the machine stable when stopped.

Disadvantages include greater weight (typically 25–40 kg), higher cost, poorer ventilation in hot weather, reduced visibility and manoeuvrability, and difficulty on very steep hills, where the weight penalty outweighs the aerodynamic advantage.

History

Enclosed pedal cars appeared in France in the 1930s, notably Charles Mochet's Vélocar, which set speed records before the UCI banned recumbents from competition in 1934. Interest revived in the 1970s and 1980s with the International Human Powered Vehicle Association and designs such as the Danish Leitra. Modern production velomobiles — including the Dutch Quest, Mango, and Strada, the German Milan, and the Romanian Frikar — are built in the Netherlands, Germany, and other European countries, many now offering electric assist.

Use

Velomobiles are used mainly for commuting, touring, and record attempts. Hour records and long-distance events such as Race Across America have featured fully faired machines derived from the velomobile concept.
