Narragansett people

Narragansett
Regions: Rhode Island, United States
Language: English; historically Narragansett (Algonquian)
Religion: Traditional beliefs, Christianity
Status: Federally recognized tribe (since 1983)

The Narragansett are a Native American people and the federally recognized Narragansett Indian Tribe of Rhode Island. At the time of European contact in the early 17th century, the Narragansett were one of the most powerful Indigenous peoples of southern New England, inhabiting territory that included much of present-day Rhode Island, particularly the western shore of Narragansett Bay.

History

The Narragansett name is often interpreted as "people of the small point." When the English founded colonies in New England, the Narragansett were led by sachems such as Canonicus and his nephew Miantonomi. Roger Williams, founder of Rhode Island, learned their language and wrote A Key into the Language of America (1643), an important early record of the Narragansett language, and the Narragansett sold land to Williams for the settlement of Providence.

The Narragansett largely avoided the Pequot War of 1636–1638 and allied with the English against the Pequot. Relations deteriorated, however, and during King Philip's War (1675–1676) colonial forces attacked the Narragansett fortified village in the Great Swamp Fight of December 1675, killing hundreds, including many noncombatants. The surviving Narragansett were dispersed, and many were killed, enslaved, or absorbed into other communities, including the Eastern Niantic, with whom the modern tribe shares ancestry.

Later history and recognition

In the 19th century the state of Rhode Island "detribalized" the Narragansett and sold most of their remaining reservation lands, acts the tribe contested for generations. A land-claims settlement in 1978 returned about 1,800 acres in Charlestown, Rhode Island, and the tribe gained federal recognition in 1983. The tribe holds an annual powwow in August, a tradition documented since the colonial period.

Language and culture

The Narragansett language, an Eastern Algonquian language closely related to Massachusett, ceased to be spoken fluently in the 19th century but is the subject of revitalization efforts drawing on historical documents such as Williams's Key. Traditional subsistence combined maize agriculture with fishing, shellfishing, and hunting in the coastal woodlands.
