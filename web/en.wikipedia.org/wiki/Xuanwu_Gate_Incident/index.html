The Xuanwu Gate Incident (Chinese: 玄武門之變) was a palace coup that took place on 2 July 626 at the Xuanwu Gate, the northern gate of the Taiji Palace in Chang'an, early in China's Tang dynasty. Li Shimin, Prince of Qin, ambushed and killed his elder brother Li Jiancheng, the Crown Prince, and his younger brother Li Yuanji, clearing his path to the throne.

Background

Li Shimin was the second son of Li Yuan (Emperor Gaozu), founder of the Tang. His brilliant generalship had been decisive in unifying China under the dynasty, but Gaozu had named the eldest son, Li Jiancheng, as heir. A bitter rivalry developed between the brothers' factions, marked by assassination attempts — including an alleged poisoning of Li Shimin — and mutual denunciations at court.

The coup

Learning that his brothers planned to move against him, Li Shimin memorialised the emperor accusing them of misconduct and was summoned to a confrontation the next morning. He instead laid an ambush at the Xuanwu Gate, whose guards he had suborned. As Jiancheng and Yuanji rode through the gate on 2 July, Li Shimin personally shot Jiancheng dead; his officer Yuchi Gong killed Yuanji. Li Shimin's men then, in armour, informed the emperor, who was boating on a palace lake, of what had happened.

Aftermath

Three days later Gaozu named Li Shimin Crown Prince, and on 4 September 626 he abdicated in his favour. Li Shimin reigned as Emperor Taizong, one of the most celebrated rulers in Chinese history. The sons of his slain brothers were executed, though Taizong later adopted one of them into his own line.
