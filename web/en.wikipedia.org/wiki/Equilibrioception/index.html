Equilibrioception

Equilibrioception, commonly referred to as the sense of balance, is one of the physiological senses related to balance. In animals, it allows a perception of balance and spatial orientation, and helps prevent the animal from falling over when standing or moving. It is related to the vestibular system located inside the inner ear.

Mechanism

In humans, equilibrioception is mainly detected by the vestibular system, a set of organs in the inner ear. The semicircular canals detect rotational movements of the head, while the otolith organs—the utricle and saccule—detect linear accelerations and the direction of gravity. Signals from these organs travel via the vestibulocochlear nerve to the brainstem, cerebellum and cerebral cortex, where they are integrated with visual and proprioceptive information.

Dysfunction

Dysfunction of the sense of balance can produce symptoms such as dizziness, vertigo, disorientation and nausea. Causes include Ménière's disease, benign paroxysmal positional vertigo, labyrinthitis, and certain medications or injuries that affect the inner ear.

In other animals

Most animals possess some form of balance sense. Invertebrates often use statocysts—small sacs containing a dense particle (statolith) that shifts with gravity—to orient themselves. Fish and amphibians also possess lateral-line and otolith structures that serve similar functions.
