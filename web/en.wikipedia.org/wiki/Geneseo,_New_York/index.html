Geneseo is a town in Livingston County, New York, United States, and the county seat. It lies in the Genesee River valley south of Rochester. The town includes the village of Geneseo, one of the best-preserved historic villages in the region; the entire village was designated a National Historic Landmark district in 1991, a rare honour for a whole community. The name comes from the Seneca word Gen-nis-he-yo, meaning "beautiful valley".

History

The area was Seneca territory; a major Seneca town stood nearby before the Sullivan Expedition of 1779 destroyed the settlements during the American Revolutionary War. European-American settlement began in the 1790s, and the Wadsworth family—brothers James and William Wadsworth, who arrived in 1790—became the dominant landowners, building the Homestead estate and shaping the town through land sales, conservation, and philanthropy. The Geneseo Historic District preserves many 19th-century homes and the famous bronze bear fountain at the head of Main Street, erected in 1888.

Education

Geneseo is home to the State University of New York at Geneseo (SUNY Geneseo), founded in 1871 as a normal school and now a highly ranked public liberal arts college with roughly 4,000–5,000 students, a large share of the town's population. The college's presence dominates the local economy and culture.

The National Warplane Museum, located at the Geneseo airport, hosts the annual "Greatest Show on Turf" airshow featuring vintage warbirds.
