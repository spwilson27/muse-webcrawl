The A-135 (NATO reporting name: ABM-4 Gorgon) is a Russian anti-ballistic missile system deployed around Moscow to defend the capital against a limited ballistic missile attack. It became operational in 1995 as the successor to the earlier A-35 system, and it is the only operational ABM system permitted under the terms originally set by the 1972 Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty, which allowed each side one defended site.

Components

The heart of the system is the Don-2N battle management radar at Pushkino, a large truncated-pyramid phased-array radar that tracks incoming warheads and guides interceptors. The system originally employed two types of nuclear-armed interceptor missiles: the long-range 51T6 (SH-11 Gorgon) exoatmospheric interceptor, retired in the mid-2000s, and the silo-launched, high-acceleration 53T6 (SH-08 Gazelle) endoatmospheric interceptor. The 53T6 was designed to destroy reentry vehicles with a 10-kiloton nuclear warhead, though modernized variants (the PRS-1M) have been tested with conventional warheads.

Status

The A-135 remains in service with the Russian Aerospace Forces' 9th Division of Missile Defense. Russia is developing the A-235 system as its eventual replacement.
