Japanese imperial succession debate

The Japanese imperial succession debate is a public and political debate in Japan over the rules governing succession to the Chrysanthemum Throne. Under the Imperial House Law of 1947, only male-line males of the imperial house may succeed, a restriction that has raised concern as the number of eligible male heirs has dwindled.

Background

Historically, Japan has had eight reigning empresses, all of whom were female-line descendants of earlier emperors and generally served as interim sovereigns. The 1947 law, enacted during the Allied occupation, abolished the collateral imperial branch families and restricted succession to male offspring in the male line.

Issues

With the birth of Prince Hisahito in 2006, the only male heir of his generation, debate eased temporarily, but long-term questions remain about whether to allow female emperors, restore former branch families, or permit adoption of male-line descendants from abolished houses.

Government panels

An expert panel in 2005 recommended allowing female succession, but the report was shelved after Princess Kiko's pregnancy was announced. Later panels under subsequent governments have continued to examine options while avoiding a definitive recommendation.
