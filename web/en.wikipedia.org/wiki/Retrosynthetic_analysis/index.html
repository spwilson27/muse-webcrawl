Retrosynthetic analysis

Retrosynthetic analysis is a technique for planning the synthesis of complex organic molecules by working backwards from the target molecule to progressively simpler precursor structures. The approach was formalized by Elias James Corey, who received the 1990 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for its development.

Concepts

In retrosynthetic analysis, the target molecule is transformed into precursors through imagined bond disconnections, producing fragments called synthons — idealized reagents that correspond to real synthetic equivalents. Applying a disconnection repeatedly generates a tree of possible routes, which is evaluated for availability of starting materials, step count, yield, and practicality. Functional group interconversions adjust the oxidation state or functionality of a molecule to enable a desired disconnection.

Strategies

Common strategies include disconnection at heteroatoms, use of latent functionality, reconnection steps that temporarily simplify a target, and protecting groups that allow chemists to direct reactions to one site of a multifunctional molecule.
