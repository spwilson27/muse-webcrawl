The Progress in International Reading Literacy Study (PIRLS), pronounced "pearls", is an international assessment of reading comprehension that has been conducted every five years since 2001 by the International Association for the Evaluation of Educational Achievement (IEA). PIRLS tests the reading achievement of fourth-grade students — children in their fourth year of formal schooling, typically around ten years old — because this is regarded as an important transition point in children's development as readers: having learned to read, they now read to learn.

History

PIRLS grew out of the IEA's earlier Reading Literacy Study of 1991. The first full PIRLS cycle was administered in 2001 in 35 countries. Subsequent cycles followed in 2006, 2011, 2016 and 2021, with participation growing to more than 50 countries and additional benchmarking regions. The 2016 cycle introduced ePIRLS, an online assessment of reading in a simulated internet environment. The 2021 cycle, disrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic, moved toward a fully digital assessment in most participating systems.

Framework and assessment design

The PIRLS assessment framework distinguishes two major purposes for reading that account for most of the reading done by young students in and out of school:

  • Reading for literary experience — students read short stories or excerpts and respond to questions about plot, characters, setting and language.
  • Reading to acquire and use information — students read non-fiction texts such as articles, diagrams, instructions and web-like pages, and use the information to answer questions.

Within each purpose, students are assessed on four comprehension processes: focusing on and retrieving explicitly stated information; making straightforward inferences; interpreting and integrating ideas and information; and evaluating and critiquing content and textual elements. Roughly half of the assessment items are multiple choice and half require constructed written responses. Alongside the test, PIRLS collects extensive background questionnaire data from students, parents, teachers and school principals about the contexts for learning to read.

Results

Across cycles, a group of high-performing education systems — including Singapore, Hong Kong, Russia, Ireland, Finland and Northern Ireland — have consistently appeared at or near the top of the international rankings. In PIRLS 2016, Singapore achieved the highest average score (576), followed by Russia and Northern Ireland. PIRLS 2021 results, released in May 2023, again placed Singapore first. The results receive wide media attention and are used by governments to inform literacy policy, curriculum reform and teacher education.

Related studies

PIRLS is one of a family of international large-scale assessments. Its sister study, TIMSS (Trends in International Mathematics and Science Study), assesses mathematics and science in grades four and eight. The OECD's PISA study assesses 15-year-olds, while prePIRLS (later PIRLS Literacy) was designed as a less difficult assessment for countries where fourth-graders are still consolidating basic reading skills.
