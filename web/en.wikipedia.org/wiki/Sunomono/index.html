Sunomono

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sunomono (Japanese: 酢の物, "vinegared things") is a category of Japanese dishes consisting of ingredients marinated or dressed in a rice vinegar-based dressing. Light, sour and refreshing, sunomono are served in small portions as a side dish or appetiser, often in a set meal, and are valued as a palate cleanser.

Preparation

The standard dressing combines rice vinegar, sugar and salt, sometimes with soy sauce, dashi or mirin. The most common version, kyuri no sunomono, is made with thinly sliced Japanese cucumber that is salted and squeezed to remove excess water before being dressed and garnished with sesame seeds. Other common ingredients include wakame seaweed, octopus (tako no sunomono), crab or imitation crab, shrimp, and mozuku seaweed, a speciality of Okinawa. Sunomono are closely related to namasu, a similar preparation of raw vegetables (and sometimes seafood) in sweet vinegar, traditionally served at New Year.

In Japanese dining

Because vinegar is believed to stimulate the appetite and aid digestion, sunomono frequently appear at the start of a meal or alongside rich, fatty dishes. Their bright acidity and small scale exemplify the balance of flavours and colours sought in Japanese home cooking and kaiseki cuisine alike.
