Data preservation

Data preservation is the act of conserving and maintaining both the safety and integrity of data. Preservation is carried out by formal activities governed by policies, regulations, rules, and standards intended to keep digital information accessible and usable over the long term, beyond the lifespan of the systems that created it.

[ Hero image: A digital archive storage facility with rows of media shelves ]

Challenges

Digital records face threats physical records do not: storage media decay ("bit rot"), file formats and software become obsolete, and the hardware needed to read older media disappears. Unlike paper, a digital object that is simply left alone will typically become unreadable within decades. Preservation therefore requires active management rather than passive storage.

Strategies

  • Migration — periodically converting files into current, preferably open, formats.
  • Emulation — preserving the original software environment so files can be rendered as originally intended.
  • Fixity checking — using checksums to detect silent corruption and repair it from redundant copies.
  • Replication — keeping multiple geographically distributed copies, as in the LOCKSS principle ("lots of copies keep stuff safe").

Frameworks

The Open Archival Information System (OAIS) reference model, standardised as ISO 14721, defines the functions and information packages a long-term archive must manage. Repositories may be certified against criteria such as the CoreTrustSeal. Research funders increasingly require data management plans ensuring that publicly funded research data are preserved and shared, following the FAIR principles of findability, accessibility, interoperability, and reusability.
