Apéritif

An apéritif is an alcoholic drink served before a meal to stimulate the appetite. The word derives from the Latin aperire, “to open”. Apéritifs are typically dry rather than sweet and are often based on wine, fortified wine, vermouth, bitters or spirits flavoured with herbs and botanicals.

Types

Common apéritifs include dry sherry, champagne and other sparkling wines, pastis, Campari, Aperol, Lillet and vermouth. In France the pre-dinner drink is also a social ritual, sometimes accompanied by olives, nuts or small savoury snacks. In Italy the related custom of aperitivo may include a buffet of appetizers.

History

Flavoured wines and herbal tonics were used medicinally in antiquity and the Middle Ages. The modern apéritif culture grew in nineteenth-century Europe, especially in Turin, Paris and other café cities, as commercial vermouths and bitters became widely available.

The counterpart after a meal is the digestif, which is generally richer or sweeter and is intended to aid digestion.
