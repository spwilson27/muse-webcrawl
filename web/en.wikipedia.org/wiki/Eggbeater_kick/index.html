Eggbeater kick

Also called: Rotary kick

Used in: Water polo, synchronised swimming, lifesaving, treading water

The eggbeater kick is a form of treading water in which the swimmer's legs move in alternating circular motions, resembling the action of an eggbeater. Each leg traces a circle in opposite phase to the other — one pushing back while the other recovers forward — producing near-continuous lift that keeps the swimmer's head, and if desired shoulders and arms, above the surface.

Technique

The swimmer sits upright in the water with the knees bent and apart. Each foot sweeps in a circle: the leg rotates outward and pushes water backwards and downwards with the sole and inner shin, then feathers back to the start. Because the two legs alternate, thrust never pauses, unlike the breaststroke-style kick used in slower treading. Skilled performers can raise themselves high enough to keep the waist or even hips clear of the water briefly, and can move in any direction or hold position against a current.

Uses

The kick is fundamental in water polo, where players must stay high in the water to pass, shoot and contest the ball with their hands free, and in synchronised (artistic) swimming, where it underpins lifts and sustained vertical figures. It is also taught in lifesaving, since it allows a rescuer to keep both hands free to support a casualty or hold equipment.
