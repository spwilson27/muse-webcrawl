Farm Hall is a country house in Godmanchester, near Huntingdon in England, famous as the place where ten German nuclear scientists were interned by the British and American authorities from July 1945 to January 1946 under Operation Epsilon. Unknown to the detainees, their conversations were recorded with hidden microphones; the resulting transcripts are a key source on the German wartime nuclear programme.

The detainees

The ten scientists — Erich Bagge, Kurt Diebner, Walther Gerlach, Otto Hahn, Paul Harteck, Werner Heisenberg, Horst Korsching, Max von Laue, Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker and Karl Wirtz — had been captured in Germany in spring 1945 as part of the Allied Alsos Mission's effort to assess the German uranium project.

The transcripts

The recordings captured the scientists' reaction to the news of the atomic bombing of Hiroshima on 6 August 1945: initial disbelief, then Heisenberg's improvised calculations of the critical mass, and discussions about how far the German programme had really progressed and why it had failed to produce a bomb. The transcripts — released by the British government in 1992 — fuelled lasting debate, particularly over whether Heisenberg and others had deliberately slowed the work or simply pursued an unworkable path and miscalculated key parameters.
