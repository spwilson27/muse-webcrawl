Magnificat (Bach)

Johann Sebastian Bach's Magnificat, BWV 243, is a musical setting of the biblical canticle Magnificat. It is scored for five vocal parts (SSATB), choir, and a Baroque orchestra including trumpets and timpani. It is one of Bach's most popular vocal works, and one of the few Latin-texted compositions in his output of sacred music for Leipzig.

Versions

Bach first composed a version in E-flat major, BWV 243a, for Christmas 1723 at the Thomaskirche in Leipzig, interpolating four Christmas hymns (laudes) into the structure. Around 1733 he revised the work into the better-known D major version, BWV 243, transposing it down, removing the Christmas interpolations (making it suitable for performance year-round), and refining the instrumentation — for example adding recorders/flutes to the aria "Esurientes".

Structure

The D major Magnificat divides the Latin text into twelve movements: five choruses framing solos and duets. It opens with a brilliant orchestral ritornello and chorus "Magnificat anima mea Dominum", includes the soprano aria "Quia respexit", the alto aria "Et misericordia", the bass aria "Quia fecit mihi magna", the duet "Et misericordia" — among eleven text movements — and closes with the doxology "Gloria Patri" and a reprise of the opening music for "Sicut erat in principio". A performance lasts roughly 30 minutes.

Context

The Magnificat is the canticle of Mary from the Gospel of Luke (1:46–55), sung traditionally at Vespers. In Leipzig, Latin Magnificats were performed on high feast days; Bach's setting, written in his first year as Thomaskantor, demonstrates his mastery of the festive style with a five-part choir and full orchestra.
