Battle of Ia Drang

The Battle of Ia Drang was fought from 14 to 18 November 1965 in the Ia Drang Valley of South Vietnam's Central Highlands. It was the first major battle between the United States Army and the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN), and a landmark test of the US Army's new airmobile doctrine, in which infantry were delivered to battle by helicopter.

Background

In the autumn of 1965 the PAVN planned to cut South Vietnam in two through the Central Highlands. The 1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile), newly arrived, launched Operation Silver Bayonet to find and fight the infiltrating regiments. On 14 November, the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry under Lieutenant Colonel Harold G. Moore landed at Landing Zone X-Ray at the base of the Chu Pong massif — directly beside a large PAVN force.

The battle

At X-Ray, Moore's roughly 450 men were attacked by battalions of the PAVN 66th Regiment over three days. Supported by artillery, helicopter gunships and close air support, the cavalrymen held a shrinking perimeter; a platoon cut off on the first day suffered heavily before fighting its way back. US forces were withdrawn on 16 November after inflicting severe casualties, estimated at more than 1,000 PAVN killed. On 17 November, the 2nd Battalion, 7th Cavalry, marching overland to Landing Zone Albany, was ambushed in one of the war's bloodiest single actions, losing 155 killed before air and artillery support broke the attack.

Aftermath

American deaths totalled 234 during the Ia Drang fighting, with some 240 wounded. Both sides claimed success: the US validated helicopter mobility and overwhelming firepower, while the PAVN learned to fight "close to the belt" of American units to negate that firepower. The battle was chronicled in the book We Were Soldiers Once… and Young by Moore and journalist Joseph Galloway, later adapted into the film We Were Soldiers.
