Happy Days

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Happy Days is an American television sitcom that aired on ABC from January 15, 1974, to July 19, 1984, with a total of 255 half-hour episodes spanning 11 seasons. Created by Garry Marshall, the series presents an idealized view of life in the 1950s and early 1960s in the Midwestern United States, and stars Ron Howard as Richie Cunningham, Henry Winkler as Arthur "Fonzie" Fonzarelli, and Tom Bosley and Marion Ross as Howard and Marion Cunningham.

Premise

The series revolves around teenager Richie Cunningham and his family: his father Howard, owner of a hardware store; his mother Marion; his younger sister Joanie; and his older brother Chuck, who largely disappears after the early seasons. Richie's friends Potsie Weber and Ralph Malph and the local greaser Arthur "Fonzie" Fonzarelli round out the ensemble, with much of the action set at Arnold's Drive-In.

Cultural impact

Happy Days became one of the most successful sitcoms of the 1970s and launched several spin-offs, including Laverne & Shirley and Mork & Mindy. Fonzie became a pop-culture icon, and his leather jacket is displayed at the Smithsonian Institution. The phrase "jumping the shark," describing the moment a show begins its decline, derives from a fifth-season episode in which Fonzie water-skis over a shark.
