Sleepy Hollow is a village in the town of Mount Pleasant, in Westchester County, New York, United States. The village is located on the east bank of the Hudson River, approximately 25 miles (40 km) north of New York City, and is known worldwide as the setting of Washington Irving's short story "The Legend of Sleepy Hollow" (1820), featuring the Headless Horseman.

History

The area was part of the Dutch patroonship of Adriaen van der Donck and later the manor of Frederick Philipse, whose Old Dutch Church of Sleepy Hollow was built about 1685 and remains in use. The village was incorporated in 1874 as North Tarrytown; in a 1996 referendum, residents voted to rename it Sleepy Hollow to honour Irving's story and the village's heritage. Washington Irving is buried in Sleepy Hollow Cemetery, which adjoins the churchyard where the legendary Horseman is said to ride.

Landmarks and economy

Landmarks include Philipsburg Manor, the Sleepy Hollow Lighthouse, and the Rockefeller estate Kykuit, located in the village's Pocantico Hills area. A General Motors assembly plant in the village closed in 1996. Halloween tourism draws large crowds each autumn to events tied to the Irving legend.
