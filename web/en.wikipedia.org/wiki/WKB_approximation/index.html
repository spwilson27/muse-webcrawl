WKB approximation
Named after: Wentzel, Kramers, Brillouin (and Jeffreys)
Field: Quantum mechanics; asymptotic analysis

In physics, the WKB approximation (Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin, also known as the JWKB or semiclassical approximation) is a method for finding approximate solutions to linear differential equations with spatially varying coefficients, most famously the Schrödinger equation. It was developed in 1926 by Gregor Wentzel, Hendrik Kramers and Léon Brillouin, building on work by Harold Jeffreys.

Method

The wavefunction is written as an exponential of a series in ℏ, and the approximation is valid where the potential varies slowly compared with the de Broglie wavelength. In classically allowed regions the solution oscillates with a slowly varying amplitude proportional to 1/√p(x); in forbidden regions it grows or decays exponentially.

Connection formulas

At turning points, where the energy equals the potential, the approximation breaks down and connection formulas link the oscillatory and exponential solutions through Airy functions. Applications include tunnelling probabilities, the Bohr–Sommerfeld quantisation rule and alpha decay lifetimes. See also semiclassical physics.
