The Godavari is the second-longest river in India, after the Ganges, and the largest river of peninsular India. Sometimes called the Dakshina Ganga ("Ganges of the South"), it rises at Trimbakeshwar near Nashik in Maharashtra, flows eastward across the Deccan Plateau for about 1,465 kilometres, and empties into the Bay of Bengal in Andhra Pradesh, forming a broad delta.

Course

From its source in the Western Ghats, the Godavari flows through Maharashtra, receiving tributaries such as the Pravara and Purna, before entering Telangana, where it is joined by the Manjira and the Pranhita — itself formed by the Wainganga, Penganga and Wardha rivers. Flowing past Bhadrachalam, it cuts through the Papikonda hills of the Eastern Ghats and emerges onto the coastal plain at Rajahmundry, where the Dowleswaram Barrage, built under Sir Arthur Cotton in the 19th century, diverts its waters into an extensive canal irrigation network. Below Rajahmundry the river splits into distributaries before reaching the sea near Antarvedi and Yanam.

Tributaries and projects

Major tributaries include the Pranhita, Indravati, Sabari, Manjira and Penganga. Large projects on the river include the Jayakwadi dam, the Sriram Sagar Project, the Polavaram Project and the Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Project in Telangana, among the largest lift-irrigation schemes in the world.

Cultural significance

The Godavari is sacred in Hinduism; the Godavari Pushkaram festival, held every twelve years at Rajahmundry and other ghats, draws millions of pilgrims. Nashik, on its upper course, is one of the four sites of the Kumbh Mela.
