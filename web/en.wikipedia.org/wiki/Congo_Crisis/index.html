The Congo Crisis (French: Crise congolaise) was a period of political upheaval and conflict between 1960 and 1965 that began almost immediately after the Republic of the Congo achieved independence from Belgium. It involved an army mutiny, the secession of the mineral-rich provinces of Katanga and South Kasai with Belgian support, the intervention of United Nations peacekeepers, and the manoeuvring of the United States and the Soviet Union during the Cold War.

Background and outbreak

The Congo became independent on 30 June 1960, with Joseph Kasa-Vubu as president and Patrice Lumumba as prime minister. On 5 July, soldiers of the Force Publique mutinied against their Belgian officers, and violence against Europeans spread. Belgium intervened militarily without the Congolese government's consent. On 11 July, Moïse Tshombe declared the secession of Katanga, backed by Belgian mining interests; South Kasai followed in August.

UN intervention and Lumumba's fall

The UN Security Council authorised the peacekeeping force ONUC, which grew to some 20,000 troops. Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld refused, however, to use it against Katanga, insisting on neutrality. Lumumba, frustrated, accepted Soviet logistical aid, alarming the West. In September 1960 Kasa-Vubu dismissed Lumumba, and army chief Joseph-Désiré Mobutu staged a coup. Lumumba was arrested, transferred to Katanga, and murdered there in January 1961 — a killing in which Belgian officials were complicit, as a Belgian parliamentary inquiry concluded in 2001. Hammarskjöld died in a plane crash in Northern Rhodesia in September 1961 while travelling to negotiate a ceasefire.

End of the Katanga secession and aftermath

After fighting between ONUC and Katangese forces, the secession ended in January 1963 and Katanga was reintegrated. A leftist rebellion in the east in 1964 (the Simba rebellion) was defeated with the help of mercenaries and US-Belgian intervention, including the Stanleyville hostage rescue. Amid continuing instability, Mobutu seized power in a second coup on 25 November 1965, ending the crisis; he ruled the country (renamed Zaire) for over three decades.
