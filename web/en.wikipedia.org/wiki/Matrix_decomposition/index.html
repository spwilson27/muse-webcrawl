Matrix decomposition

In the mathematical discipline of linear algebra, a matrix decomposition or matrix factorization is a factorization of a matrix into a product of matrices. There are many different matrix decompositions; each finds use among a particular class of problems. Decompositions are central to numerical linear algebra, where they provide stable and efficient algorithms for solving systems of linear equations, computing eigenvalues, and many other tasks.

A matrix decomposition expresses a matrix as a product of simpler, structured matrices.

Examples

LU decomposition

In numerical analysis, different decompositions are used to implement efficient matrix algorithms. The LU decomposition factors a matrix as the product of a lower triangular matrix L and an upper triangular matrix U, sometimes with a permutation matrix:

A = PLU

It is the matrix form of Gaussian elimination and is used to solve linear systems, compute determinants, and find inverses. A related variant, the Cholesky decomposition, applies to symmetric (or Hermitian) positive-definite matrices and factors A = LL*.

Eigendecomposition

The eigendecomposition applies to diagonalizable square matrices and writes

A = PDP−1

where D is a diagonal matrix of eigenvalues and the columns of P are the corresponding eigenvectors. For symmetric matrices, the spectral theorem guarantees an orthogonal eigendecomposition.

Singular value decomposition

The singular value decomposition (SVD) exists for every real or complex matrix and factors it as

A = UΣV*

where U and V are unitary matrices and Σ is a diagonal matrix of non-negative singular values. The SVD underlies principal component analysis, low-rank approximation, and the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse.

QR decomposition

The QR decomposition factors a matrix into an orthogonal matrix Q and an upper triangular matrix R. It is commonly computed by Householder reflections or Givens rotations and is the basis of the QR algorithm for eigenvalues and of least-squares solvers.

Other decompositions

  • Polar decomposition: A = UP, a unitary matrix times a positive-semidefinite Hermitian matrix.
  • Schur decomposition: A = UTU*, with T upper triangular.
  • Jordan decomposition: expresses a matrix in Jordan normal form.
  • Takagi factorization, LDU decomposition, and rank factorizations for structured or rank-deficient matrices.

Applications

Matrix decompositions are used in solving linear systems, least-squares fitting, signal processing, statistics, computer graphics, quantum mechanics, and control theory. Choosing the right decomposition depends on the structure of the matrix (symmetric, sparse, positive-definite) and on numerical stability requirements.
