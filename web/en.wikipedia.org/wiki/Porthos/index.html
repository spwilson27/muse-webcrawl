Porthos, Baron du Vallon de Bracieux de Pierrefonds is a fictional character in the novels The Three Musketeers (1844), Twenty Years After (1845) and The Vicomte de Bragelonne (1847–1850) by Alexandre Dumas. He and the other two musketeers, Athos and Aramis, are friends of the novel's protagonist, d'Artagnan.

Porthos is a huge, vain and flamboyant man, immensely strong, fond of fine clothes, good food and wine, and somewhat dim-witted, yet brave, loyal and good-hearted. His vanity is a running source of comedy: in The Three Musketeers he is mortified when a baldric he has boasted of turns out to be only partly covered in gold lace. His great strength is legendary; in the later novels he is described as a giant of a man able to perform feats of strength well into old age.

Character in the novels

In The Three Musketeers Porthos is a valued member of the King's Musketeers. Through the influence of his friends he marries the elderly, wealthy widow of a procurator named Coquenard, whose fortune later funds his ambitions. By Twenty Years After he has become a rich landowner, dissatisfied because, alone among his friends, he has no title; he plays a key role in the attempt to rescue Charles I of England and is made a baron as a reward. In The Vicomte de Bragelonne he returns, still larger than life, and dies heroically at Belle-Île while covering the escape of Aramis, crushed beneath fallen stones after helping to collapse a grotto on their pursuers.

Historical basis

The fictional Porthos is loosely based on Isaac de Porthau (1617–1712), a Gascon musketeer in the company of the King's Musketeers under Captain de Tréville, who later served as secretary of the Parliament of Béarn. Little is known of his life, and Dumas borrowed little more than the name.

In film and television

Porthos has been played by many actors, including Alan Hale Sr. and Alan Hale Jr. (in different adaptations of The Three Musketeers), Frank Finlay in Richard Lester's films of the 1970s, Oliver Platt in the 1993 Disney film, Gérard Depardieu in The Man in the Iron Mask (1998), Ray Stevenson in the 2011 film, and Howard Charles in the BBC series The Musketeers (2014–2016).
