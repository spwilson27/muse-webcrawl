Bořivoj I (c. 852 – c. 889) was the first historically documented Duke of Bohemia and the forefather of the Přemyslid dynasty. A member of the Přemyslid family, he originally governed territory around Levý Hradec in central Bohemia. His reign is traditionally dated to about 867–889, though many details rest on later legends, especially the chronicle of Cosmas of Prague and the legends of Saint Ludmila.

Christianisation

According to legend, Bořivoj and his wife Ludmila were baptised by Saint Methodius, the apostle of the Slavs, around 883 during a visit to the court of Svatopluk I of Great Moravia, to whose sphere Bohemia then belonged. Bořivoj founded the first Christian churches in Bohemia: the Church of Saint Clement at Levý Hradec and the Church of the Virgin Mary near Prague Castle, whose establishment — traditionally around 885 — is associated with the founding of Prague itself.

Rule and succession

Bořivoj's baptism provoked a pagan revolt led by a noble named Strojmír, forcing the duke briefly into exile in Moravia before he was restored with Svatopluk's help. After his death around 889, Bohemia passed briefly under direct Moravian rule before his sons Spytihněv I and Vratislaus I succeeded in turn. His widow Ludmila, later canonised, became the patron saint of Bohemia and grandmother of Saint Wenceslaus.
