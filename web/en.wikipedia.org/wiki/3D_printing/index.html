3D printing, or additive manufacturing, is the construction of a three-dimensional object from a digital model by depositing, joining or solidifying material layer by layer. It contrasts with subtractive manufacturing, in which material is cut away from a solid block.

History

The first working 3D printer is generally credited to Chuck Hull, who patented stereolithography in 1986 and founded 3D Systems. Fused deposition modelling (FDM) was developed by Scott Crump, founder of Stratasys, in the late 1980s. For two decades the technology was used mainly for rapid prototyping in industry. After key FDM patents expired in 2009, open-source projects such as RepRap drove the cost of desktop printers down dramatically, sparking a consumer and maker boom.

Processes

Major process families include material extrusion (FDM/FFF), vat photopolymerisation (stereolithography and DLP), powder bed fusion (SLS and laser metal melting), material jetting, binder jetting, and directed energy deposition. Materials range from thermoplastics such as PLA and ABS to resins, nylon, metals, ceramics and even living cells in bioprinting.

Applications

3D printing is used for prototyping, tooling, dental and medical implants, custom prosthetics, aerospace and automotive components, architectural models, jewellery, and construction (printed concrete structures). It also raises legal and policy questions around intellectual property and the printing of weapons.
