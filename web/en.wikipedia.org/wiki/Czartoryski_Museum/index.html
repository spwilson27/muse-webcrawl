The Czartoryski Museum (Polish: Muzeum Czartoryskich) in Kraków, Poland, is one of the country's oldest museums and home to one of Europe's most important private art collections, assembled by the Czartoryski family. Its most celebrated treasures include Leonardo da Vinci's Lady with an Ermine and Rembrandt's Landscape with the Good Samaritan.

History

The collection was founded in 1796 by Princess Izabela Czartoryska (née Fleming) at the family estate in Puławy, where she created one of the first museums in Poland, housing Polish historical memorabilia and works of art in the Temple of the Sibyl and the Gothic House. Her motto for the collection was "The past to the future."

After the failed November Uprising of 1830–31, the collection was partly confiscated and partly evacuated; much of it eventually followed the family into exile in Paris, at the Hôtel Lambert. In the 1870s Prince Władysław Czartoryski brought the collection to Kraków, where the museum opened in its present home in 1878, in a complex formed from three historic buildings in the Old Town.

Second World War and losses

During the Second World War the museum was looted by the German occupation authorities. Many works were seized; while most were recovered after the war, some major pieces remain missing, most famously Raphael's Portrait of a Young Man, which has never been found. The Lady with an Ermine, taken to Germany, was returned to Kraków after the war.

Recent history

The museum was long administered by the Princes Czartoryski Foundation. After an extensive renovation, during which the collection was largely housed at Wawel Castle, the museum reopened in December 2019. In 2016 the Polish state purchased the entire collection — some 86,000 museum objects and 250,000 library items — from Prince Adam Karol Czartoryski for a sum far below its estimated market value, making it state property administered through the National Museum in Kraków.

Collection highlights

Alongside the Leonardo and Rembrandt, the museum holds Polish historical relics, militaria, decorative arts, ancient art, and the Czartoryski Library, one of Poland's most valuable collections of manuscripts and early printed books.
