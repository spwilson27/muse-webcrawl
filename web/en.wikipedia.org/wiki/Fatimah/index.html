Fatimah

Fatimah bint Muhammad (Arabic: Fāṭima, c. 605 or 612 – 632 CE), commonly known as Fatimah al-Zahra, was the daughter of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and his wife Khadija, wife of Ali ibn Abi Talib, and mother of Hasan and Husayn. She is revered in Islam, especially in Shia tradition, as a member of the Ahl al-Bayt.

Life

Fatimah was born in Mecca and endured the boycott and persecution of the early Muslims. After the Hijra to Medina in 622 she married Ali. Their household was known for poverty and piety; the Qur'anic verses of purification (33:33) and events such as the Mubahala are associated with her family in tradition.

Death and succession dispute

Fatimah died a few months after Muhammad in 632. Disputes over her inheritance — notably the oasis of Fadak — and over Ali's claim to leadership became central to the Sunni–Shia divide. Her burial place in Medina is uncertain, a point of devotion and controversy.

Veneration

Titles include al-Zahra (the Radiant), al-Batul and Sayyidat Nisa al-Alamin (Mistress of the Women of the Worlds). In Shia Islam she is one of the Fourteen Infallibles; in Sunni Islam she is honoured as one of the four perfect women. Her sermon at the Prophet's Mosque (the Fadak sermon) is preserved in early sources.
