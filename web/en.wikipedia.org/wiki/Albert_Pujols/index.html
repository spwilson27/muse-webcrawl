José Alberto Pujols Alcántara (born 16 January 1980) is a Dominican-American former professional baseball first baseman and designated hitter, widely regarded as one of the greatest right-handed hitters in the history of Major League Baseball. Nicknamed "the Machine," he played 22 seasons, chiefly for the St. Louis Cardinals and the Los Angeles Angels.

Cardinals years

Pujols was drafted by the Cardinals in the 13th round in 1999 and won the National League Rookie of the Year Award in 2001. Over eleven seasons in St. Louis he won three National League Most Valuable Player Awards (2005, 2008, 2009) and two World Series championships (2006 and 2011), batting above .300 with 30 or more home runs in each of his first ten seasons — an unprecedented start to a career.

Angels, Dodgers and milestones

In December 2011 Pujols signed a ten-year, $254 million contract with the Angels. His production declined through injuries, but he reached major milestones: his 600th home run in 2017, his 3,000th hit in 2018, and the all-time record for most double plays grounded into was offset by passing Willie Mays on the home run list in 2020. Released by the Angels in 2021, he finished that season with the Dodgers, then returned to St. Louis for a farewell 2022 season in which a remarkable second half carried him to 703 career home runs — fourth in MLB history at his retirement.

Legacy

Pujols retired with 3,384 hits, 703 home runs and 2,218 runs batted in, second on the all-time RBI list. Through the Pujols Family Foundation he has supported people with Down syndrome and impoverished communities in the Dominican Republic.
