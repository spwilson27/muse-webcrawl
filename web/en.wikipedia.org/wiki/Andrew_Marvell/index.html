Andrew Marvell (31 March 1621 – 16 August 1678) was an English metaphysical poet, satirist, and politician who served as Member of Parliament for Hull through the Commonwealth and Restoration periods. In his lifetime he was known chiefly as a public servant — assistant to John Milton in the Latin secretaryship, and a diligent correspondent for his Hull constituents — while his lyric poems circulated in manuscript and were published only after his death, in 1681. He is now ranked among the finest poets of the seventeenth century.

Life and career

Marvell was educated at Hull Grammar School and Trinity College, Cambridge, travelled in Europe during the Civil War, and in 1650 became tutor to Mary Fairfax, daughter of the Parliamentary commander Lord Fairfax, at Nun Appleton House in Yorkshire — the setting of his great poem "Upon Appleton House." He later tutored Cromwell's ward William Dutton, wrote poems in praise of the Lord Protector, and in 1657 joined Milton in the foreign office. Elected MP for Hull in 1659, he held the seat until his death, surviving the Restoration by his usefulness and discretion; legend credits him with helping shield the blind Milton from reprisals. In his last years he published influential prose satires, notably The Rehearsal Transpros'd and An Account of the Growth of Popery and Arbitrary Government (1677).

Poetry

Marvell's lyrics fuse metaphysical wit with classical poise: "To His Coy Mistress" presses a carpe diem argument ("Had we but world enough and time…") from cosmic premises to comic urgency; "The Garden" praises contemplative retirement ("Annihilating all that's made / To a green thought in a green shade"); and the "Horatian Ode" (1650) balances admiration for Cromwell's energy with sympathy for the executed Charles I, a poised ambivalence unmatched in political poetry of the age.

Reputation

Long valued mainly by specialists, Marvell was championed by T. S. Eliot in a famous 1921 essay, and his reputation has risen steadily since; he is now grouped with Donne, Herbert, and Vaughan among the major metaphysical poets.
