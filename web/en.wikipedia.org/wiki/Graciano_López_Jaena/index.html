Graciano López Jaena (December 18, 1856 – January 20, 1896) was a Filipino journalist, orator, reformist, and national hero who is best known for founding La Solidaridad, the newspaper of the Propaganda Movement in Spain. He is regarded as one of the leading figures of the Filipino reform movement alongside José Rizal and Marcelo H. del Pilar.

Early life and education

Born in Jaro, Iloilo, López Jaena entered the seminary at Jaro intending to become a priest, but left to study medicine. His opposition to the abuses of the Spanish friars in the Philippines drove him first into writing and then into exile in Spain in 1880, after the publication of his satirical work "Fray Botod", which lampooned a greedy friar.

Propaganda work

In Spain, López Jaena became a celebrated orator and journalist. In 1889 he founded La Solidaridad in Barcelona, the organ of the Propaganda Movement, which advocated for Philippine representation in the Spanish Cortes, secularization of parishes, and equality before the law. He wrote the novel Discursos y artículos varios among many essays and speeches.

Death and legacy

López Jaena died of tuberculosis in Barcelona in 1896, impoverished and largely forgotten, just months before the Philippine Revolution began. He is honoured in the Philippines with schools, streets and monuments bearing his name.
