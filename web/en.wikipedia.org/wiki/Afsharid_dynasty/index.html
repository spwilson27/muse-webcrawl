The Afsharid dynasty was an Iranian dynasty founded by Nader Shah (r. 1736–1747) of the Afshar tribe, a Turkic tribe settled in Khorasan. Nader rose as a military commander in the service of the Safavid claimant Tahmasp II, expelled the Afghan Hotak invaders who had toppled the Safavids, campaigned successfully against the Ottomans and Russians, and in 1736 had himself crowned Shah, ending Safavid rule. At its greatest extent his empire stretched from the Caucasus and Mesopotamia to the Indus, briefly making Iran the strongest power between the Ottoman Empire and Mughal India.

Nader Shah's reign

Nader reformed the army around musketeers and mobile artillery, and attempted a religious settlement — recasting Iran's Twelver Shi'ism as a fifth Sunni-recognised school (the "Ja'fari" madhhab) — to reduce sectarian conflict with his Sunni neighbours and soldiers. His most famous campaign, the invasion of Mughal India in 1738–39, culminated in the Battle of Karnal and the sack of Delhi, from which he carried off immense treasure including the Peacock Throne and the Koh-i-Noor and Daria-i-Noor diamonds; the plunder let him remit Iranian taxes for three years. In later years his rule turned harsh and suspicious: heavy taxation, revolts, and the blinding of his own son Reza Qoli Mirza (after an assassination attempt) marked a reign ended by his murder by his own officers in 1747.

Decline

The empire disintegrated almost at once. Nader's nephew Adel Shah and then his grandson Shahrokh Shah succeeded in Khorasan, while the west fell to Karim Khan Zand and Central Asian and Afghan successor states emerged — Ahmad Shah Durrani, once of Nader's army, founded the Durrani Empire in Afghanistan. Shahrokh, blinded and reduced to a puppet, held Mashhad until 1796, when Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar took the city and extinguished the Afsharid line, torturing the aged Shahrokh to reveal the whereabouts of Nader's remaining jewels.
