Peter Sellers

Born: Richard Henry Sellers, 8 September 1925, Southsea, Portsmouth, England

Died: 24 July 1980 (aged 54), London, England

Occupation: Actor, comedian

Known for: Inspector Clouseau; Dr. Strangelove; Being There

Peter Sellers (born Richard Henry Sellers; 8 September 1925 – 24 July 1980) was an English actor and comedian, renowned for his mastery of accents and his gift for inhabiting multiple characters within a single film.

Career

Sellers first gained fame in the BBC radio comedy series The Goon Show (1951–1960) alongside Spike Milligan and Harry Secombe. Film success followed with The Ladykillers (1955) and I'm All Right Jack (1959). As the bumbling Inspector Clouseau in Blake Edwards's The Pink Panther (1963) and its sequels, he created one of cinema's most enduring comic characters.

In Stanley Kubrick's Dr. Strangelove (1964) Sellers played three roles — Group Captain Lionel Mandrake, President Merkin Muffley and Dr. Strangelove himself — earning an Academy Award nomination. Other notable films include Lolita (1962), What's New Pussycat? (1965), Casino Royale (1967) and The Party (1968). His final celebrated performance was the simple-minded gardener Chance in Being There (1979), which brought him a second Oscar nomination.

Personal life and death

Sellers was married four times, including to Britt Ekland. Plagued by ill health after a series of heart attacks beginning in 1964, he died of a further heart attack in London in 1980, aged 54.
