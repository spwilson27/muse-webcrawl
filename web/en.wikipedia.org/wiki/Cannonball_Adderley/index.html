Cannonball Adderley

Julian Edwin "Cannonball" Adderley (September 15, 1928 – August 8, 1975) was an American jazz alto saxophonist of the hard bop era of the 1950s and 1960s. He is remembered for his work with trumpeter Miles Davis, including the landmark album Kind of Blue (1959), and for leading successful bands with his brother, cornetist Nat Adderley.

Biography

Adderley was born in Tampa, Florida, and studied music education at Florida A&M University. His nickname "Cannonball" derived from "cannibal," an accolade for his hearty appetite acquired in high school. After moving to New York in 1955, his powerful, blues-drenched alto playing — influenced by Charlie Parker and Benny Carter — quickly made his reputation.

Career

Adderley joined Miles Davis's sextet in 1957, recording Milestones and Kind of Blue alongside John Coltrane. In 1959 he formed a quintet with his brother Nat, which became one of the defining groups of soul jazz. Their recordings include "This Here" by pianist Bobby Timmons, "Work Song," and the 1966 hit single "Mercy, Mercy, Mercy!" written by keyboardist Joe Zawinul, which crossed over to the pop charts. Adderley also helped introduce Zawinul and other young musicians to wider audiences.

Death and legacy

Adderley died of a stroke in Gary, Indiana, in 1975, aged 46. His accessible blend of bebop virtuosity, blues and gospel feeling made him one of the most popular jazz musicians of his era, and he was inducted into the DownBeat Jazz Hall of Fame.
