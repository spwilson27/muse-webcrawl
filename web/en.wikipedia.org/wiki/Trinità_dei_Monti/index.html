Trinità dei Monti

The Trinità dei Monti (French: La Trinité-des-Monts) is a late Renaissance church in Rome, Italy, famous for its commanding position above the Spanish Steps, which descend to the Piazza di Spagna below. Its twin bell towers and the Sallustiano obelisk in front make it one of the city's most recognisable landmarks.

History

The church and adjoining convent were founded with French royal patronage: in 1494 Saint Francis of Paola bought land on the Pincio for the Minim friars, and construction, funded by Kings Charles VIII and Louis XII of France, began in 1502 in a French Gothic-influenced style before being completed in Renaissance forms; the church was consecrated in 1585 by Pope Sixtus V. It remains a French national church in Rome, administered with the other French foundations known as the Pieux Établissements. The convent is noted for the anamorphic frescoes by Emmanuel Maignan and Jean-François Niceron in its corridors.

Art and architecture

Inside are works by Daniele da Volterra, including a celebrated Deposition, and by other Mannerist artists. The monumental staircase before the church — the Spanish Steps, built 1723–1725 by Francesco de Sanctis — links the church to the piazza, and the obelisk, a Roman imitation Egyptian obelisk from the Gardens of Sallust, was erected in 1789.
