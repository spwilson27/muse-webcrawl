Psychometrics

The normal curve underlying many standard scores

Psychometrics is the field of study within psychology concerned with the theory and technique of measurement. Psychometrics generally covers specialized fields within psychology and education devoted to testing, measurement, assessment, and related activities. Psychometricians develop instruments such as questionnaires and tests, and theorise about validity and reliability.

History

The field traces its roots to Francis Galton's measurements of individual differences, James McKeen Cattell's "mental tests", and Alfred Binet and Théodore Simon's 1905 intelligence scale. Charles Spearman's factor analysis and his 1904 work on general intelligence (g) founded modern test theory, later extended by Louis Thurstone, J. P. Guilford, and others.

Theory

Two main frameworks dominate. Classical test theory models an observed score as a true score plus error, and yields reliability coefficients such as Cronbach's alpha. Item response theory models the probability of a particular response as a function of the person's latent trait level and item parameters such as difficulty and discrimination, enabling adaptive testing and item banking.

Applications

Psychometric methods underpin educational testing, clinical assessment, personnel selection, and the measurement of personality, including instruments such as the Big Five inventories and standardised examinations used worldwide.
