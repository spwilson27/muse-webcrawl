Multitrait-multimethod matrix

The multitrait-multimethod (MTMM) matrix is an approach to examining construct validity developed by Donald T. Campbell and Donald W. Fiske in their influential 1959 paper "Convergent and discriminant validation by the multitrait-multimethod matrix". Several traits are each measured by several methods, and all the resulting measures are intercorrelated in a single matrix.

Introduced byCampbell & Fiske (1959)
PurposeAssessing construct validity
Key conceptsConvergent validity, discriminant validity, method variance

Structure

The matrix is organized into blocks: monotrait-heteromethod correlations (the same trait measured by different methods — these should be high, evidencing convergent validity), heterotrait-monomethod correlations (different traits by the same method — these should be lower, and not inflated by shared method variance), and heterotrait-heteromethod correlations (which should be lowest). Reliability coefficients lie on the main diagonal.

Interpretation criteria

Campbell and Fiske proposed that convergent correlations be substantially nonzero, that they exceed correlations between different traits measured by the same method, and that the pattern of trait intercorrelations be similar across methods. Violations point to method effects contaminating the measures.

Modern developments

Today the MTMM design is usually analysed with confirmatory factor analysis and structural equation models that separate trait, method, and error components, but the original matrix remains a classic descriptive tool in psychometrics.
