Construct validity

A construct embedded in a nomological network

Construct validity concerns how well a set of indicators reflects a concept that is not directly measurable — a construct in the social sciences, such as intelligence, depression, or personality traits. It was formally proposed by Lee Cronbach and Paul Meehl in their 1955 paper "Construct validity in psychological tests", and evaluates whether a test or measure actually measures the theory-laden construct it claims to measure.

Nomological network

Cronbach and Meehl argued that a construct is embedded in a nomological network: a set of theoretical relationships connecting the construct to other constructs and to observable outcomes. Evidence for construct validity accumulates as predictions derived from this network are confirmed — for example, that scores correlate with measures of related constructs (convergent validity), fail to correlate with unrelated ones (discriminant validity), and predict relevant real-world criteria.

Unified view

Samuel Messick later argued that validity is a unitary concept: all validity is construct validity, and evaluation of a test should also weigh the social consequences of its use. Threats to construct validity include confounding constructs, mono-method bias, and construct under-representation, in which a test captures too narrow a slice of the concept.
