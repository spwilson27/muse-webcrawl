Terry Deary

Born: 3 January 1946, Sunderland, England

Occupation: Author

Notable works: Horrible Histories

Terry Deary (born 3 January 1946) is a British author of more than 200 books, which have sold over 25 million copies in 40 languages. His most famous series, Horrible Histories, was first published in 1993 and presents history with an emphasis on the gruesome, humorous and overlooked details that traditional textbooks omit. The series has been adapted into an award-winning television programme, stage shows and other media.

Early life and career

Deary was born in Sunderland and worked in a variety of jobs — including as a professional actor, theatre director and drama teacher — before becoming a full-time writer. His early books included children's fiction such as The Custard Kid. His time working with Theatre Powys, a touring schools theatre company in Wales, influenced his approach of making education entertaining.

Horrible Histories

Commissioned by Scholastic, the Horrible Histories series began with Terrible Tudors and Awful Egyptians in 1993, illustrated by Martin Brown. Books in the series adopt an irreverent tone, describing "history with the nasty bits left in". The books have been credited with encouraging reluctant readers and changing the teaching of history in British schools, though some critics have questioned their irreverence and factual liberties.

Deary has also written other non-fiction series (including Horrible Science-adjacent titles and the "Terry Deary's Terribly True" books) and dozens of works of fiction for children and young adults. He is an outspoken critic of schools and libraries in some respects, having argued that schools can inhibit children's love of reading, and he has campaigned against plans relating to public lending and libraries' funding.
