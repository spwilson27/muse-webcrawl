Cherokee Purple is a popular heirloom tomato cultivar prized for its rich, sweet, smoky flavour and dusky purple-rose fruit. It is one of the best-known "black" tomatoes and a staple of home gardens and farmers' markets in the United States.

History

The variety was popularized by North Carolina tomato collector Craig LeHoullier, who received seed in 1990 from John D. Green of Tennessee. Green reported that the tomato had been grown by the Cherokee people and passed down for roughly a century. LeHoullier named it "Cherokee Purple" and distributed it through the Seed Savers Exchange, after which it spread widely.

Description

Plants are indeterminate, producing medium-to-large flattened globes of about 280–340 grams. Ripe fruit is a deep dusky rose to purple-brown with green shoulders. It is considered a colour mutation of the older Cherokee heirloom tomatoes and carries the "gf" (green flesh) trait that deepens its colour.

Cultivation

Cherokee Purple performs well in hot, humid climates of the American South and matures in about 80 days from transplant. It is susceptible to cracking after heavy rain and to common tomato diseases, but is grown for flavour over durability.
