Hotspot (Wi-Fi)

A hotspot is a physical location where people can access the Internet, typically using Wi-Fi technology, via a wireless local area network (WLAN) created by a router connected to an Internet service provider. Hotspots are commonly found in airports, hotels, coffee shops, libraries, bookstores and other public venues, and may be offered free of charge, as a perk for customers, or on a paid basis.

History

The concept of public wireless access was proposed in the early 1990s, and the first commercial hotspot networks appeared in the late 1990s as the IEEE 802.11 standards spread. Companies such as T-Mobile HotSpot, Boingo Wireless and Wayport built networks of hotspots in airports, hotels and coffee chains. The spread of smartphones and mobile broadband later led to the "personal hotspot" feature, allowing a phone to share its cellular connection over Wi-Fi.

Types

Free hotspots are open to the public at no charge, often supported by advertising or as an amenity. Commercial hotspots require payment or a subscription, typically through a captive portal page where users log in or purchase access. Personal hotspots are created by an individual's own device, such as a smartphone or portable router.

Security

Public hotspots pose security risks because traffic on open networks can be intercepted, and rogue hotspots with legitimate-sounding names can be set up to harvest data. Users are advised to use encrypted connections, virtual private networks (VPNs), and to avoid sensitive transactions on untrusted networks. Modern protocols such as WPA3 and opportunistic wireless encryption (OWE) improve the security of public access points.
