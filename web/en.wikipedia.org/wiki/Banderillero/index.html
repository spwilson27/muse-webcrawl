Banderillero

A banderillero placing a pair of banderillas.

A banderillero (Spanish for "flagman") is a torero in a bullfight who performs the tercio de banderillas, the second third of the corrida, in which pairs of barbed darts decorated with coloured paper — the banderillas — are thrust into the bull's shoulders. Banderilleros form part of the matador's cuadrilla (team); a matador normally employs three banderilleros, and they also assist with the cape during the earlier stages of the fight.

Role in the fight

Three pairs of banderillas are usually placed. The banderillero runs toward the bull holding a banderilla in each hand, arcs around its horns at the last moment, rises on his toes, and plants both sticks in the top of the bull's shoulders. The act requires speed, agility and precision; performed well, it is one of the most applauded moments of a corrida. The banderillas are intended to correct the bull's charge and to further weaken and subdue it before the final third.

Matador as banderillero

Some matadors are celebrated for performing their own banderillas, combining both roles in their cuadrilla. Famous matador-banderilleros have included Antonio Ordóñez's contemporaries and, in the modern era, figures such as Julián López "El Juli", David Fandila "El Fandi" (particularly noted for the violín placement), and Juan José Padilla. In Portugal, where the corrida is performed on horseback in its first phase, banderillas are placed by the cavaleiro from horseback.
