Munchausen syndrome

Munchausen syndrome is a psychiatric disorder in which affected people feign disease, illness, or psychological trauma in order to draw attention, sympathy, or reassurance to themselves. In current diagnostic terminology it is classified as factitious disorder imposed on self (DSM-5). People with the disorder may fabricate symptoms, falsify medical records, contaminate laboratory samples, or deliberately harm themselves so as to require treatment, and they often move between hospitals ("hospital hopping" or peregrination) when suspicions arise.

Terminology and history

The term was coined by the British physician Richard Asher in 1951, naming the condition after Baron Munchausen, the 18th-century German officer famous for telling fantastical, exaggerated stories about himself, popularised in Rudolf Erich Raspe's book. Asher described patients who travelled widely and invented dramatic but false medical histories.

Signs and course

Clues include dramatic but inconsistent histories, symptoms that do not fit known patterns, eagerness for invasive procedures, extensive medical knowledge, hostility or departure when confronted, and improvement patterns that track attention rather than treatment. The related condition Munchausen syndrome by proxy, now called factitious disorder imposed on another, involves a caregiver (often a parent) fabricating or inducing illness in a dependent, and is regarded as a form of abuse; it must be distinguished from malingering, where symptoms are feigned for external gain such as money or avoidance of duty, and from somatic symptom disorders, where symptoms are not consciously produced.

Management

Management is difficult: patients frequently reject psychiatric help and leave care when confronted. Recommended approaches include non-punitive, face-saving confrontation, coordinated care with a single primary physician to reduce unnecessary procedures, and psychotherapy addressing underlying needs for care and attention; prognosis is generally guarded, particularly for chronic peregrinating cases.
