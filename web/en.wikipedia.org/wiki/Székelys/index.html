The Székelys (Hungarian: székelyek; Romanian: secui) are a Hungarian-speaking ethnic group living mainly in the Székely Land (Székelyföld) of eastern Transylvania, in present-day Romania. Historically they formed a distinct, privileged warrior estate of the Kingdom of Hungary, settled along its eastern frontier in exchange for military service.

History

The origins of the Székelys are debated: medieval chronicles linked them to the Huns of Attila, while modern scholarship variously connects them with Turkic or Hungarian groups settled as border guards. In medieval Transylvania they were one of the three privileged "nations" of the Union of the Three Nations (1438), alongside the Hungarian nobility and the Transylvanian Saxons, and were organised into seats (székek) with their own military obligations and freedoms from taxation. The 1562 Székely uprising and its suppression curtailed these liberties. After the Treaty of Trianon (1920) Transylvania—including Székely Land—passed to Romania.

Culture and politics

Székely traditions include carved wooden gates (székelykapu), the use of the old Hungarian (rovás) script, and distinctive folk dress and cuisine. Since 1989, Székely political movements in Romania have sought territorial autonomy for Székely Land, an aim reflected in the activities of the Democratic Alliance of Hungarians in Romania (UDMR) and civic organisations; the Székely flag—a blue field with a golden sun and crescent—was adopted by the Székely National Council.
