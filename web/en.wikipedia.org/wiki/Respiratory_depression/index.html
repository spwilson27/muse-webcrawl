Respiratory depression

Respiratory depression, also known as hypoventilation in the relevant central sense, is a condition in which breathing becomes too slow or too shallow to maintain adequate gas exchange, leading to increased carbon dioxide (hypercapnia) and respiratory acidosis, and if severe, hypoxaemia. It most often results from depression of the respiratory centres in the brainstem.

Causes

The most common causes are central nervous system depressant drugs, including opioids, benzodiazepines, barbiturates, alcohol and general anaesthetics. Opioid-induced respiratory depression is a major cause of death in opioid overdose; it results from reduced responsiveness of brainstem chemoreceptors to carbon dioxide. Other causes include brainstem injury or stroke, raised intracranial pressure, severe hypothyroidism, and neuromuscular disease.

Signs and management

Signs include a reduced respiratory rate, shallow tidal volumes, drowsiness or coma, pinpoint pupils in opioid toxicity, and cyanosis in severe cases. Management begins with supporting the airway and ventilation, including bag-valve-mask ventilation or mechanical ventilation if needed. Specific reversal agents include naloxone for opioids and flumazenil (used cautiously) for benzodiazepines.
