Treaty of London (1913)

Signed30 May 1913
LocationLondon, United Kingdom
PartiesBalkan League, Ottoman Empire

The Treaty of London (1913) was signed on 30 May following the London Conference of 1912–1913. It dealt with the territorial adjustments arising out of the conclusion of the First Balkan War.

Background

In the First Balkan War, the combined armies of the Balkan League (Serbia, Greece, Bulgaria and Montenegro) had effectively defeated the Ottoman Empire, occupying almost all of its remaining European territories. The Great Powers convened an ambassadors' conference in London in December 1912 to impose a settlement, pressure being applied on both sides.

Terms

  • The Ottoman Empire ceded all territory west of the Enos–Midia line (from Enos on the Aegean Sea to Midia on the Black Sea) to the Balkan League, except Albania;
  • An independent Albanian state was recognized, its boundaries to be fixed by the Great Powers;
  • The island of Crete was ceded to Greece;
  • The status of the Ottoman Aegean islands was left to the Great Powers to decide (they later awarded most to Greece).

Aftermath

The treaty did not settle how the conquered territory was to be divided among the Balkan allies. Disputes between Bulgaria on one side and Serbia and Greece on the other over Macedonia led within weeks to the outbreak of the Second Balkan War (June–July 1913), after which the final Balkan settlement was imposed by the Treaty of Bucharest.
