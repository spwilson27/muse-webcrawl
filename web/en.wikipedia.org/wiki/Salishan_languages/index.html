Salishan languages

The Salishan (or Salish) languages are a family of Indigenous languages of the Pacific Northwest of North America, traditionally spoken in what is now British Columbia, Washington, Oregon, Idaho, and Montana. The family comprises about 23 languages, divided into branches including Coast Salish, Interior Salish, Tsamosan, and the divergent Bella Coola (Nuxalk). Most Salishan languages are now severely endangered, though revitalisation efforts are underway.

Traditional distribution of the Salishan language family.

Branches

Major branches include:

Coast Salish — including Halkomelem, Lushootseed, Northern Straits Salish, and Squamish, spoken along the coast and in the Fraser and Puget Sound lowlands.
Interior Salish — including Shuswap (Secwepemctsín), Lillooet, Thompson, Okanagan, and Montana Salish (Flathead), spoken on the interior plateau.
Tsamosan — languages of the Olympic Peninsula and southwest Washington coast, including Quinault and Chehalis.
Nuxalk (Bella Coola) — an isolated northern outlier, historically surrounded by Wakashan and Athabaskan languages.

Characteristics

Salishan languages are noted for complex consonant inventories — including ejectives, uvulars, and in some languages no true nasal consonants — and for polysynthetic word structure, in which roots combine with many prefixes and suffixes. They are also well known in linguistics for debates over whether all words can serve as predicates, challenging traditional noun–verb distinctions.
