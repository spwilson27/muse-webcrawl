Summits on the Air

Founded2002 (United Kingdom)
TypeAmateur radio awards programme
ParticipantsActivators, chasers, summit-to-summit contacts
ScopeWorldwide; over 100 associations

Summits on the Air (SOTA) is an amateur radio awards programme that encourages portable radio operation from hill and mountain summits around the world. Launched in the United Kingdom in 2002 by John Linford (G3WGV) and Richard Newstead (G3CWI), SOTA combines hillwalking with amateur radio: radio amateurs carry lightweight equipment to recognised summits, operate from the summit, and exchange contacts with stations at home and on other summits. The programme has grown to include associations in well over a hundred countries and territories and a database of more than 100,000 qualifying summits.

How it works

Each qualifying summit is assigned a unique reference (for example G/LD-001 for Scafell Pike in England's Lake District) and a points value based on its height above sea level, with higher summits worth more points. An activator climbs the summit and, operating within the "activation zone" near the top using power carried to the summit (not from a vehicle), must make at least four contacts to claim the points. Chasers — stations anywhere, typically at home — score points for contacting activators, and contacts between two portable summit stations earn both sides "summit-to-summit" credit. Activators announce their plans ("alerts") and report their presence ("spots") through the SOTAwatch website, and all contacts are logged in an online database that administers the awards.

Awards

Awards are issued for cumulative points and achievements: activator milestones at 100, 250, 500 and 1,000 points culminate in the "Mountain Goat" award at 1,000 activator points, while chasers reaching 1,000 points earn the title "Shack Sloth". Separate awards recognise summit-to-summit contacts, completing regions, and activating on different bands and modes.

Equipment and operation

SOTA operation is dictated by what can be carried up a mountain: compact HF or VHF transceivers, lithium batteries, lightweight wire antennas such as end-fed half-wave dipoles supported by fishing poles, and modes from SSB and FM voice to CW (Morse code, popular for its efficiency with low power) and digital modes. The programme has become one of the most popular outdoor activities in amateur radio and has inspired similar schemes for parks (POTA), islands, and lighthouses.
