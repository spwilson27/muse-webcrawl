Ahimsa

Ahimsa
TraditionsHinduism, Jainism, Buddhism
MeaningNon-violence; reverence for all life
Key exponentsMahavira; Mahatma Gandhi
[Image: A Jain monk gently sweeping the ground before his steps so as not to harm small creatures]

Ahimsa (Sanskrit: 'non-harm') is the ethical principle of not causing harm to any living being, central to Jainism — where it is the supreme vow, observed even toward insects — and foundational in Hinduism and Buddhism. The Chandogya Upanishad lists it among the virtues; Patanjali's Yoga makes it the first of the restraints (yamas).

Gandhi and after

Mahatma Gandhi — who learned ahimsa deeply from Jain influence in Gujarat, notably Raychandbhai — made it the active force of satyagraha, nonviolent resistance that helped end British rule in India and inspired Martin Luther King Jr. and later movements. Jain practice remains its strictest laboratory: filtered water, no eating after dark, and vocations screened for harm.
