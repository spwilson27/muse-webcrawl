Microplate reader

Microplate reader
Also called: Plate reader
Used for: High-throughput assays
Formats: 96, 384, 1536 wells

A microplate reader (also known as a plate reader) is a laboratory instrument used to detect biological, chemical or physical events in samples contained in a microplate. It is a cornerstone of high-throughput screening in drug discovery and of routine assays in research and clinical laboratories.

Detection modes

Common detection modes include absorbance (used for ELISA and cell-viability assays such as MTT), fluorescence intensity, luminescence, time-resolved fluorescence, and fluorescence polarization. Multimode readers combine several of these in one instrument. A light source illuminates each well, and a detector measures the signal well by well across plates of 96, 384 or 1536 wells.

Applications

Applications range from enzyme kinetics and immunoassays to reporter-gene studies, nucleic-acid quantification and compound screening, where thousands of candidate molecules are tested against a target each day.
