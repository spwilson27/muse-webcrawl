Compact of Free Association

The Freely Associated States are the Marshall Islands, Micronesia and Palau.

The Compacts of Free Association (COFA) are international agreements establishing and governing the relationships of free association between the United States and the three Pacific Island sovereign states of the Federated States of Micronesia (FSM), the Republic of the Marshall Islands (RMI), and the Republic of Palau. As a result, these countries are sometimes known as the Freely Associated States.

Provisions

Under the compacts, the United States provides financial assistance, federal services (such as postal, weather and aviation services), and access for citizens of the Freely Associated States to live, study and work in the United States without a visa. In return, the United States assumes full responsibility and authority for defence of the islands, including the right to deny military access by third countries — a strategic arrangement covering a vast area of the Pacific.

History

The compacts grew out of the US-administered Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands, created after World War II. The FSM and Marshall Islands compacts took effect in 1986 and Palau's in 1994. The economic provisions were amended and extended in 2003–2004 for twenty years. After prolonged negotiations, renewed agreements providing funding for a further twenty years were signed in 2023 and enacted by the US Congress in March 2024.

Issues

Debates around the compacts include the legacy of US nuclear testing in the Marshall Islands, the treatment of COFA migrants in the United States — whose access to federal benefits was restricted in 1996 and progressively restored, most fully in 2020 — and the strategic competition between the United States and China in the Pacific.
