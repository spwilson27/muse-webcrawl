A Sportsman's Sketches (Russian: Записки охотника, literally "A Hunter's Notes"), also known as Sketches from a Hunter's Album, is an 1852 collection of short stories by Ivan Turgenev. It was the first major writing by Turgenev to gain him recognition.

Content

The stories are narrated by a landowner wandering his estate on hunting trips, encountering peasants, serfs, and fellow landowners of the Oryol region. Through episodes such as "Khor and Kalinych", "Bezhin Meadow", and "The Singers", Turgenev portrayed serfs as fully realised human beings with rich inner lives — a quiet but powerful indictment of serfdom.

Impact

The collection is credited with influencing Tsar Alexander II's decision to emancipate the serfs in 1861, and Turgenev himself regarded it as his most important contribution. The stories were first published in the journal The Contemporary beginning in 1847 and issued as a book in 1852; their author was briefly arrested and exiled to his estate for an obituary of Gogol published at the same time.
