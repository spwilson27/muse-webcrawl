Task force (naval)

A task force (TF) is a unit or formation established to work on a single defined task or activity. Originally introduced by the United States Navy, the term has been adopted for many purposes in place of the earlier "task fleet". A naval task force comprises groups of ships and support units temporarily combined under one commander to carry out a specific operation or mission.

United States Navy usage

The task force concept was developed in the U.S. Navy during the interwar period and used extensively in the Pacific War of the Second World War. Task forces are designated by numbers reflecting their parent fleet: for example, Task Force 16 and Task Force 17 fought at the Battle of Midway, and the fast carrier force alternated between Task Force 38 (Third Fleet) and Task Force 58 (Fifth Fleet) depending on whether Admiral Halsey or Admiral Spruance was in command. Subordinate elements are called task groups (TG), task units (TU), and task elements (TE), each with decimal suffixes (e.g., TG 58.1).

Modern practice

Today, numbered task forces remain in use across the U.S. Navy's fleets, such as Task Force 70 (carrier strike forces in the Seventh Fleet area). The concept has been adopted by other navies and for joint and combined operations: examples include the British Task Force sent to the South Atlantic during the Falklands War of 1982 and multinational Combined Task Forces such as CTF 150 and CTF 151, which conduct counter-terrorism and counter-piracy patrols in the Arabian Sea and Gulf of Aden.
