Medical school

[Image: Students at a university medical school]
Medical students during pre-clinical teaching

A medical school is a tertiary educational institution — or part of one — that teaches medicine and awards professional degrees for physicians. Degrees differ by country: they include the Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery (MBBS/MBChB) common in the United Kingdom and Commonwealth, the Doctor of Medicine (MD) used in the United States and elsewhere, and the Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine (DO) in the US.

Admissions and curriculum

Entry is highly competitive and typically requires strong results in science subjects and admission tests such as the MCAT (North America), UCAT or GAMSAT (UK and Australia). Curricula generally combine basic medical sciences (anatomy, biochemistry, physiology, pharmacology, pathology and microbiology) with progressively greater clinical training in hospitals and community settings, through lectures, problem-based learning, clinical skills teaching and clerkships or placements in specialties such as medicine, surgery, paediatrics, obstetrics and psychiatry.

Models around the world

In many countries (UK, India, China and much of Europe) medicine is an undergraduate-entry programme of five or six years; in the United States and Canada it is a four-year graduate-entry programme following a bachelor's degree, followed by residency training of three to seven years. The 1910 Flexner Report reshaped North American medical education around university-based scientific training and full-time clinical faculty. After graduation, doctors complete supervised postgraduate training (internship, foundation years or residency) and licensing examinations before independent practice.

History

Organised medical teaching is ancient — associated with the Hippocratic schools of Greece, the bimaristans of the medieval Islamic world, and the universities of Salerno, Bologna, Montpellier and Padua. Modern schools number in the thousands worldwide and are listed and quality-assured through bodies such as the World Directory of Medical Schools.
