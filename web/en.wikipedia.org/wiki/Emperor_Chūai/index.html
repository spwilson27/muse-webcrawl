Emperor Chūai

Emperor Chūai (仲哀天皇, Chūai-tennō)
Reign: 192–200 (traditional)
Predecessor: Seimu
Successor: Jingū (regent), then Ōjin
Born: 149 (traditional)
Died: 200 (traditional)

Emperor Chūai (仲哀天皇, Chūai-tennō), also known as Tarashinakatsuhiko no Sumeramikoto, was the 14th legendary emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. Historians consider Chūai a legendary figure, and the name Chūai-tennō was assigned to him posthumously by later generations.

Legendary narrative

No firm dates can be assigned to this emperor's life or reign, but he is conventionally considered to have reigned from 192 to 200. According to the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, Chūai was a son of the legendary prince Yamato Takeru and a nephew of Emperor Seimu. The chronicles relate that the emperor died suddenly while planning a campaign against the Kumaso in Kyushu; some accounts attribute his death to his dismissal of a divine message delivered through his wife, the Empress Jingū, who then undertook the legendary conquest of the Korean peninsula in his stead.

Consorts and children

Chūai's consort was Okinagatarashihime, better known as Empress Jingū, who served as regent after his death. Their son, Homutawake, became Emperor Ōjin. According to tradition, Ōjin was born after Jingū's return from Korea, an unusually long gestation that later tradition explained by the empress's strategic delay.
