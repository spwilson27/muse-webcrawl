Treaty of the Prut

The Treaty of the Prut, also called the Treaty of Huși, was signed on the banks of the Prut River on 23 July 1711 between the Ottoman Empire and the Tsardom of Russia. It concluded the Pruth River Campaign of the Great Northern War, in which Peter the Great's army had been encircled by a much larger Ottoman force.

Background

After his defeat at Poltava in 1709, Charles XII of Sweden took refuge in Ottoman territory and persuaded the Porte to declare war on Russia. Peter invaded Moldavia in 1711 expecting support from the local prince Dimitrie Cantemir, but Ottoman and Tatar forces surrounded the Russians near Stănilești, forcing Peter to negotiate.

Terms

Russia agreed to return the fortress of Azov to the Ottomans, demolish Taganrog and several southern fortresses, withdraw from Polish affairs, and grant Charles XII safe passage back to Sweden. In return, Peter and his army were allowed to withdraw intact — terms widely regarded as remarkably lenient given the Ottoman military advantage, and often attributed to the diplomacy (and alleged bribery) surrounding Grand Vizier Baltacı Mehmed Pasha.

Aftermath

The settlement freed Russia to concentrate on the war against Sweden in the north, while the Ottomans, dissatisfied with Russian delays in fulfilling the terms, twice came close to renewing the war before the Treaty of Adrianople (1713) confirmed the peace.
