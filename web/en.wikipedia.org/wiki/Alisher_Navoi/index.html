Alisher Navoi

Born9 February 1441, Herat, Timurid Empire
Died3 January 1501 (aged 59), Herat
OccupationPoet, statesman, linguist, painter, patron of the arts
LanguageChagatai Turkic, Persian
Notable worksKhamsa; Muhakamat al-Lughatayn

Alisher Navoi (Uzbek: Alisher Navoiy; 9 February 1441 – 3 January 1501), also rendered Ali-Shir Nava'i, was a Timurid poet, writer, statesman, linguist and mystic who is regarded as the greatest representative of Chagatai literature and the founder of Uzbek classical literature. Writing under the pen name Navoi ("the melodious") in Turkic and Fani ("the mortal") in Persian, he championed the literary use of his native Turkic tongue at a time when Persian dominated court letters.

Life

Navoi was born in Herat into a family close to the Timurid court and was a childhood friend and later vizier of Sultan Husayn Bayqara. He used his wealth and position to patronise scholars, artists and architects, endowing schools, hospitals, caravanserais and Sufi lodges, and presiding over a brilliant cultural circle in Herat.

Works

Navoi's output includes some thirty works. His masterpiece is the Khamsa ("Quintet", 1483–85), five epic poems totalling over 50,000 couplets, modelled on the quintets of Nizami and Amir Khusrau. In Muhakamat al-Lughatayn ("Judgment Between the Two Languages", 1499) he argued that Turkic was in no way inferior to Persian as a literary language. He also compiled four divans of lyric poetry in Chagatai and one in Persian, wrote biographical dictionaries of poets (Majalis al-Nafais), and works on prosody and Sufi thought.

Legacy

Navoi is a national hero of Uzbekistan: the city of Navoi, the Alisher Navoi National Park and Tashkent's opera and ballet theatre bear his name, and his birthday is widely celebrated. His works influenced Turkic literature across Central Asia for centuries.
