Ching Ling Foo

Ching Ling Foo (Chinese: 程連蘇; 1854–1922), born Zhu Lianmou (or Chee Ling Qua in some accounts), was a Chinese magician who was the first East Asian performer to achieve international fame in Western vaudeville. Born in Beijing, he brought an authentic Chinese conjuring act to the United States, first appearing at the Trans-Mississippi Exposition in Omaha in 1898 and subsequently touring to great acclaim.

Act

Ching performed in traditional costume with a troupe of Chinese acrobats, jugglers and musicians. His signature wonders included producing a huge bowl of water — sometimes containing ducks or a child — from an empty cloth, breathing fire, and producing yards of ribbon and paper from his mouth. Unlike later Western imitators, his act presented genuine Chinese performance traditions alongside stage magic.

Rivalry with Chung Ling Soo

In 1905 the American imitator William Robinson, who performed in yellowface under the name Chung Ling Soo, clashed with Ching when both were booked in London. Ching publicly challenged Robinson to duplicate his tricks and denounced him as a fraud and impostor; the confrontation fizzled when Ching was deported over a visa dispute before the contest could occur, but the feud became one of the most famous in magic history. Ching continued to tour internationally until shortly before his death in Shanghai in 1922.
