Devín Castle (Slovak: Devínsky hrad) is a ruined castle in Devín, a borough of Bratislava, Slovakia. It stands on a high cliff above the confluence of the Danube and Morava rivers, at the western edge of the Little Carpathians, and is one of the most important archaeological and historical sites in Slovakia.

History

The rocky outcrop was fortified from the Bronze Age and later used by Celts and Romans — the site lay near the frontier of the Roman province of Pannonia. In the 9th century it was a major stronghold of Great Moravia, associated with Prince Rastislav, with a church and fortifications on the summit. A medieval stone castle developed from the 13th century and passed through the hands of several noble families. In 1809 Napoleon's troops, retreating from the region, blew up the castle, leaving the ruin that became a powerful symbol for Slovak national revival in the 19th century; the poet Ľudovít Štúr and his followers made celebrated excursions to Devín.

Today

The castle is administered by the Bratislava City Museum. Exhibitions in the surviving structures present finds from the Great Moravian period onward, and the site offers views across the border into Austria. The slender Maiden Tower, on a rock in the river, is the castle's best-known landmark.
