William Cecil, 1st Baron Burghley (13 September 1520 – 4 August 1598) was an English statesman and the chief advisor of Queen Elizabeth I for most of her reign. As Secretary of State and later Lord High Treasurer, he shaped the religious settlement, foreign policy and administration of Elizabethan England over four decades.

Early career

Cecil studied at St John's College, Cambridge, and at Gray's Inn. He first entered royal service under Edward VI as secretary to the Duke of Somerset, surviving the duke's fall, and sat in Parliament. Under Mary I he kept a low profile while quietly conforming outwardly to Catholicism.

Chief minister to Elizabeth I

On Elizabeth's accession in 1558 Cecil became her Secretary of State and closest counsellor. He engineered the Elizabethan Religious Settlement of 1559, managed relations with Scotland that culminated in the Treaty of Edinburgh, and supervised the recoinage that stabilised English currency. He built a formidable intelligence and patronage network, worked closely with Francis Walsingham against plots surrounding Mary, Queen of Scots, and oversaw England's cautious drift toward war with Spain, which came in 1585.

Later years and legacy

Created Baron Burghley in 1571 and appointed Lord High Treasurer in 1572, Cecil built the great houses of Burghley House and Theobalds. He died in 1598 and was succeeded in influence by his younger son, Robert Cecil. Historians regard him as the principal architect of the Elizabethan state.
