Tumah and taharah

Tumah and taharah
Hebrew: טומאה וטהרה
Field: Jewish law (Halakha)

Tumah (Hebrew: טומאה) and taharah (טהרה) are the Hebrew terms for the states of ritual impurity and purity in Jewish law. A person or object that is tamei has contracted ritual impurity from one of the sources specified in the Torah, while one that is tahor is in a state of ritual purity.

Sources of impurity

The principal sources of tumah described in the Torah include contact with a human corpse, certain skin conditions (tzaraat), seminal and menstrual discharges, and the carcasses of certain animals. Impurity could be transmitted by touch, carrying, or being under the same roof, and it restricted access to the Temple in Jerusalem and to sanctified foods.

Purification

Purification typically involved waiting until sunset, immersion in living water or a ritual bath (mikveh), and in some cases additional rites such as the ashes of the red heifer for corpse impurity. Since the destruction of the Second Temple, most practical consequences of the laws have lapsed, though the laws of niddah (menstrual impurity within marriage) and the priestly avoidance of corpse impurity remain in observance.
