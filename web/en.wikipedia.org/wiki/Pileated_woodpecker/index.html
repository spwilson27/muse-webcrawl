Pileated woodpecker
Scientific nameDryocopus pileatus
Length40–49 cm (16–19 in)
RangeNorth America
ConservationLeast Concern

The pileated woodpecker (Dryocopus pileatus) is a large, mostly black woodpecker native to North America. It is one of the largest woodpeckers on the continent, nearly crow-sized, with a striking flaming-red crest and a white stripe running down the neck.

Description and behaviour

Adults are 40–49 cm long with a wingspan of about 66–75 cm. Males have a red stripe on the cheek; females do not. Their drumming is loud and slow, and their call is a wild, cackling laugh. They excavate large, rectangular holes in dead trees in search of carpenter ants and wood-boring beetle larvae, and the cavities they abandon shelter many other species.

Habitat and status

Pileated woodpeckers inhabit mature and second-growth forests across Canada, the eastern United States, and parts of the Pacific coast. After declining with forest clearing, populations recovered with regrowth and the species is rated Least Concern. The cartoon character Woody Woodpecker was modelled loosely on this species.
