S-400 missile system

Russian designationS-400 Triumf
NATO reporting nameSA-21 Growler
DesignerAlmaz-Antey
In service2007–present (Russia)
RangeUp to ~400 km (40N6 missile)

The S-400 Triumf (Russian: С-400 Триумф, "Triumph"; NATO reporting name SA-21 Growler) is a long-range surface-to-air missile system developed by Russia's Almaz-Antey and fielded by the Russian Aerospace Forces from 2007. Developed from the S-300 family, it is designed to engage aircraft, cruise missiles and ballistic missiles at ranges of up to about 400 km and altitudes of up to 185 km.

Components

A typical S-400 regiment combines a command post, the 91N6E panoramic surveillance radar and 92N6E engagement radars, and several battalions of transporter-erector-launchers each carrying four tubes. The system can fire several missile types — the very-long-range 40N6, the long-range 48N6 family, and the medium-range 9M96 series — allowing layered engagement of targets in heavy electronic-warfare conditions.

Exports and controversies

China was the first foreign customer, followed by Turkey — whose purchase, despite its NATO membership, led the United States to remove Turkey from the F-35 programme and impose CAATSA sanctions in 2020 — and India, which signed a major contract in 2018. Belarus and other states have also received systems. S-400s have been deployed to Syria's Khmeimim air base and were widely used in Russia's war against Ukraine from 2022, where Ukrainian strikes destroyed several launchers and radars.
