Stanford–Binet Intelligence Scales

The Stanford–Binet Intelligence Scales is an individually administered intelligence test that was revised from the original Binet–Simon Scale by Stanford psychologist Lewis Terman in 1916. It was one of the first tests to produce an "intelligence quotient" (IQ) score and remains in use, now in its fifth edition (SB5, 2003).

First published1916
Revised byLewis Terman (Stanford University)
Current editionFifth edition (2003)
MeasuresFive factors: fluid reasoning, knowledge, quantitative reasoning, visual-spatial processing, working memory

History

Alfred Binet and Théodore Simon developed their scale in France in 1905 to identify children needing educational support. Terman's American revision standardized the test on a large sample, introduced age-normed scores and popularized the IQ metric. Later editions (1937, 1960, 1986, 2003) progressively modernized content, scoring and factor structure.
