Alfred Tarski

Alfred Tarski (14 January 1901 – 26 October 1983), born Alfred Teitelbaum, was a Polish-American logician and mathematician. A founder of model theory, he is famous for his semantic definition of truth and, with Stefan Banach, the Banach–Tarski paradox.

Life

Tarski studied at the University of Warsaw under Łukasiewicz and Leśniewski, becoming the youngest person to receive a doctorate there. Caught in the United States by the outbreak of war in 1939 while lecturing, he settled at Berkeley from 1942, building a renowned school of logic.

Work

Truth and definability

In The Concept of Truth in Formalized Languages (1933/35) Tarski defined truth for formal languages using Convention T — "'snow is white' is true if and only if snow is white" — and proved truth for a sufficiently rich language cannot be defined within that language itself.

Other contributions

He co-proved the Banach–Tarski paradox (1924), gave a decision procedure for real closed fields, developed cylindric and relation algebras, and shaped modern model theory and semantics alongside Kurt Gödel.
