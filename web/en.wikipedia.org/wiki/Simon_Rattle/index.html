Sir Simon Denis Rattle (born 19 January 1955) is an English conductor. He rose to international prominence during the 1980s and 1990s as music director of the City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra (1980–1998), and he served as chief conductor of the Berlin Philharmonic from 2002 to 2018. He was music director of the London Symphony Orchestra from 2017 to 2023, and in 2023 he became chief conductor of the Bavarian Radio Symphony Orchestra in Munich.

Early life and education

Rattle was born in Liverpool, the son of Pauline Lila Violet and Denis Guttridge Rattle, a lieutenant in the Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve during the Second World War. He received his early education at Liverpool College and St Anne's College, Oxford, where he studied English language and literature. His musical talent was recognised early: he appeared as a percussionist and pianist, and at the age of nineteen won the John Player International Conducting Competition in 1974, which led to engagements with the Bournemouth Symphony Orchestra and the Royal Liverpool Philharmonic.

Career

From 1980 to 1998 Rattle was music director of the City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra, transforming it into an ensemble of international standing and presiding over the opening of Symphony Hall, Birmingham, in 1991. In 1999 he was elected chief conductor and artistic director of the Berlin Philharmonic, succeeding Claudio Abbado; he took up the post in 2002 and remained until 2018, when he was succeeded by Kirill Petrenko. His Berlin tenure was marked by innovative programming, education work through the orchestra's Digital Concert Hall, and acclaimed performances of Mahler, Sibelius and the French repertoire.

Rattle became music director of the London Symphony Orchestra in September 2017. In 2021 it was announced that he would become chief conductor of the Bavarian Radio Symphony Orchestra (Symphonieorchester des Bayerischen Rundfunks) from the 2023–24 season, while taking the title of conductor emeritus with the LSO.

Recordings and honours

Rattle has recorded extensively, notably for EMI and Warner Classics, including cycles of the symphonies of Mahler, Sibelius, Brahms and Beethoven. He was appointed CBE in 1987, knighted in 1994, and appointed to the Order of Merit in 2014. He is married to the Czech mezzo-soprano Magdalena Kožená.
