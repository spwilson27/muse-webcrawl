Lancelot "Capability" Brown (1716–1783) was an English landscape architect widely regarded as Britain's most famous gardener. He designed over 170 parks surrounding the finest country houses and estates in Britain, many of which survive today. His nickname came from his habit of telling clients that their estates had great "capability" for improvement.

Style

Brown swept away the formal parterres and geometry of earlier baroque gardens, replacing them with an idealised natural landscape: smooth, undulating grass rolling down to serpentine lakes (often formed by damming streams), with belts and clumps of trees framing views of classical buildings. Sunken fences (ha-has) kept livestock out while preserving unbroken vistas. His style came to define the English landscape garden recognised worldwide.

Career

Born in Kirkharle, Northumberland, Brown trained as a gardener and became head gardener at Stowe in Buckinghamshire, where he worked under William Kent. He went independent in the 1750s and worked for many of the wealthiest aristocrats of the day — at Blenheim Palace, Chatsworth, Petworth, Croome Court, Highclere Castle, and Burghley, among others. At Croome he also designed the house and estate buildings, acting as architect as well as landscaper; he drained and remodelled entire landscapes, sometimes moving hills and creating lakes miles in circumference.

He was appointed Master Gardener at Hampton Court by George III in 1764. Despite earning a fortune, he died comparatively modestly and is buried at Fenstanton in Cambridgeshire.
