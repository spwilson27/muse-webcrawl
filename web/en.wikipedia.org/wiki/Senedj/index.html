Senedj (also spelled Sened) was an ancient Egyptian king (pharaoh) who may have ruled during the 2nd Dynasty, in the early dynastic period around the 28th century BC. His existence is attested only in later king lists and a small number of inscriptions, and his position in the sequence of 2nd-Dynasty rulers — and even the correct reading of his Horus name — remain subjects of Egyptological debate.

Attestation

Senedj's name appears in the Saqqara king list and the Turin Royal Canon, which credit him with a reign of more than 40 years — figures many scholars consider inflated. The writing board from Giza (a New Kingdom scribal exercise) also lists him among revered royal ancestors. No contemporary monuments, tomb, or serekh can be assigned to him with certainty.

Identity debate

Some Egyptologists have proposed that Senedj is identical with the Horus king Seth-Peribsen, or that "Senedj" was the birth name used alongside a Horus name recorded separately, since 2nd-Dynasty king lists sometimes split single rulers into two entries. Others link him with Weneg or Raneb. Because the end of the 2nd Dynasty is marked by obscure rulers and possibly divided kingship between Upper and Lower Egypt, Senedj's precise identity and reign remain unresolved.
