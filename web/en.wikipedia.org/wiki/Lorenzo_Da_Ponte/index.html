Lorenzo Da Ponte (born Emanuele Conegliano; 10 March 1749 – 17 August 1838) was an Italian librettist, poet and Catholic priest who later lived in London and the United States. He is best remembered as the author of the libretti for three of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart's greatest operas: The Marriage of Figaro (1786), Don Giovanni (1787) and Così fan tutte (1790).

Life

Born to a Jewish family in Ceneda, Conegliano converted to Catholicism with his family and took the name of the bishop who baptised them. Ordained a priest, he led a scandal-prone life in Venice and was banished in 1779 for adultery and public immorality. In Vienna he became poet to the imperial theatres under Joseph II, writing libretti for Salieri, Martín y Soler and Mozart. After the emperor's death he fell from favour, worked in London as a librettist and bookseller, fled his creditors to the United States in 1805, and reinvented himself as a grocer, distiller and teacher of Italian. In 1825 he became the first professor of Italian literature at Columbia College in New York, and he was instrumental in bringing Italian opera to America, culminating in the opening of the Italian Opera House in 1833. His memoirs (1823–1827) are a lively, if unreliable, account of his extraordinary career.
