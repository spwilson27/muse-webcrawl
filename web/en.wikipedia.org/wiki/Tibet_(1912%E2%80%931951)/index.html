Tibet (1912–1951)

CapitalLhasa
GovernmentTheocratic absolute monarchy
Head of stateDalai Lama
Period1912–1951

Tibet was a de facto independent state from 1912, following the collapse of the Qing dynasty, until 1951, when it was annexed by the People's Republic of China following the signing of the Seventeen Point Agreement. During this period Tibet was ruled from Lhasa by the Ganden Phodrang government headed by the Dalai Lama.

History

After the Xinhai Revolution of 1911, Qing forces in Tibet were expelled, and the 13th Dalai Lama returned from exile and issued a declaration asserting Tibetan independence in 1913. Tibet thereafter conducted its own internal and much of its external affairs, maintained its own army, currency, and postal service, and concluded treaties, including the 1914 Simla Convention with British India, which China did not ratify.

End of the period

In 1949 the Chinese Communist Party won the Chinese Civil War, and in October 1950 the People's Liberation Army defeated Tibetan forces at the Battle of Chamdo. In May 1951 a Tibetan delegation signed the Seventeen Point Agreement in Beijing, and Tibet was incorporated into the People's Republic of China.
