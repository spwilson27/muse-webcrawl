Sachertorte (German pronunciation: [ˈzaxɐˌtɔʁtə]) is a chocolate cake, or torte, of Austrian origin, invented by Franz Sacher in 1832 for Prince Clemens von Metternich in Vienna. It consists of a dense chocolate sponge layered and coated with apricot jam and covered in a glossy dark chocolate icing, and is traditionally served with unsweetened whipped cream.

History

According to the famous account, the 16-year-old apprentice Franz Sacher, standing in for an ill head chef, created the cake for a dinner hosted by Chancellor Metternich, whose instruction was: "Let there be no disgrace tonight." Sacher's son Eduard perfected the recipe while training at the Demel bakery and later at the Hotel Sacher, founded by Eduard in 1876, where the cake became a fixture of Viennese coffee-house culture.

The "cake war"

In the twentieth century the Hotel Sacher and the Demel confectionery fought a long legal battle — the Sachertorte dispute — over the right to the name "Original Sacher-Torte". A 1963 settlement gave the Hotel Sacher the name and the round chocolate seal, while Demel's version, which places the jam directly under the icing rather than in the middle, uses a triangular seal. Austria celebrates National Sachertorte Day on 5 December.
