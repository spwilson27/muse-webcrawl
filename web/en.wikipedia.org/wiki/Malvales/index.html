The Malvales are an order of flowering plants. As circumscribed by the APG IV system (2016), the order comprises about 6,000 species in ten families, including many economically important plants.

Families

  • Malvaceae — the mallow family: cotton, cacao, hibiscus, okra, baobab, durian, kapok, balsa
  • Thymelaeaceae — daphne and relatives
  • Cistaceae — rock-roses
  • Dipterocarpaceae — major tropical timber trees of Asia
  • Bixaceae — annatto
  • Sarcolaenaceae — endemic to Madagascar
  • Sphaerosepalaceae
  • Cytinaceae — parasitic plants
  • Muntingiaceae
  • Neuradaceae

Description

Many members have fibrous stems (bast fibres, as in jute relatives), stellate hairs, palmate leaves and mucilage canals. Molecular phylogenetic work greatly expanded the traditional Malvaceae, absorbing the former families Bombacaceae, Sterculiaceae and Tiliaceae.

Economic importance

The order supplies cotton — the world's principal natural textile fibre — as well as cacao (chocolate), cola nuts, okra, jute-like fibres, balsa wood and numerous ornamentals such as hibiscus, hollyhock and linden/lime trees.
