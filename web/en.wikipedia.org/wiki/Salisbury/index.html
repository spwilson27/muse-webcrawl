Salisbury is a cathedral city and civil parish in Wiltshire, England, at the confluence of the rivers Avon, Nadder and Bourne. It is best known for Salisbury Cathedral, whose 123-metre (404 ft) spire is the tallest in Britain, and for its proximity to Stonehenge and the abandoned hillfort settlement of Old Sarum.

History

The original settlement stood at Old Sarum, an Iron Age hillfort 2 miles north, where a cathedral and castle were built after the Norman Conquest. In 1220 Bishop Richard Poore founded the present city — "New Sarum" — on a grid plan in the valley below, and the cathedral was built in a single campaign (1220–1258) in the Early English Gothic style. The cathedral holds the best-preserved of the four surviving original copies of Magna Carta (1215) and the world's oldest working mechanical clock (1386).

Salisbury received its city charter in 1227 and grew rich on the wool trade. It was long a centre of clockmaking, bell-founding and cutlery, and today functions as a market and service city for south Wiltshire, with tourism focused on the cathedral close, Stonehenge and the surrounding chalk country.

2018 poisoning

In March 2018 the former Russian intelligence officer Sergei Skripal and his daughter Yulia were poisoned in Salisbury with a Novichok nerve agent, an attack the British government attributed to Russian military intelligence; a local woman, Dawn Sturgess, died after exposure in nearby Amesbury. The incident caused a major diplomatic crisis and prolonged decontamination of the city.
