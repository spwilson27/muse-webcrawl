Thomas Campbell (1763–1854) was an Irish-born Presbyterian minister who emigrated to the United States in 1807 and became, with his son Alexander Campbell, a founder of the American Restoration Movement (the Stone-Campbell Movement), from which the Churches of Christ and the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ) descend.

Declaration and Address

Frustrated by sectarian division among Presbyterians on the Pennsylvania frontier, Campbell organised the Christian Association of Washington in 1809 and issued its Declaration and Address, one of the foundational documents of the restoration plea. It called for Christian unity based on the Bible alone, famously urging: "Where the Scriptures speak, we speak; where the Scriptures are silent, we are silent."

Later life

Campbell's movement merged in 1832 with the "Christians" led by Barton W. Stone. He spent his later years in Bethany, Virginia (now West Virginia), where his son founded Bethany College, and continued preaching and writing until his death in 1854.
