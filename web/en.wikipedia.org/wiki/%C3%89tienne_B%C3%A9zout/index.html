Étienne Bézout

Étienne Bézout (31 March 1730 – 27 September 1783) was a French mathematician best known for Bézout's theorem in algebraic geometry and Bézout's identity in elementary number theory.

Work

Bézout worked on the theory of equations, publishing Théorie générale des équations algébriques in 1779. His theorem states that two plane algebraic curves of degrees m and n intersect in exactly mn points, counting multiplicity and points at infinity, provided the curves have no component in common. Bézout's identity states that for integers a and b, there exist integers x and y such that ax + by = gcd(a, b).

Career

Born in Nemours, Bézout became a member of the French Academy of Sciences and examiner of naval and artillery candidates, for whom he wrote widely used textbooks.
