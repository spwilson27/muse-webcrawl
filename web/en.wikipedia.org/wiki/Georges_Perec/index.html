Georges Perec

Born 7 March 1936, Paris, France
Died 3 March 1982 (aged 45), Ivry-sur-Seine, France
Occupation Novelist, filmmaker, essayist
Notable works Life: A User's Manual, A Void, W, or the Memory of Childhood

Georges Perec (7 March 1936 – 3 March 1982) was a French novelist, filmmaker, documentalist, and essayist. He was a member of the Oulipo group of writers and is one of the most significant French authors of the post-war period. His works use formal constraints — most famously the lipogram, a text written without a particular letter — alongside games, puzzles, and inventories to explore memory, loss, and everyday life.

Life

Perec was born in Paris to Polish Jewish immigrant parents. His father died in 1940 fighting in the French army; his mother perished in the Holocaust after being deported, probably to Auschwitz. Perec was raised by his paternal aunt and uncle in Paris. He studied history and sociology at the Sorbonne but left without a degree, worked during his military service in a parachute regiment, and from 1962 to 1978 earned his living as a neurophysiological research documentalist (archiviste) at a CNRS laboratory — a job that gave him time to write and influenced his passion for classification.

His first novel, Les Choses (Things: A Story of the Sixties, 1965), won the Prix Renaudot. In 1967 he joined the Oulipo (Ouvroir de littérature potentielle), the group founded by Raymond Queneau and François Le Lionnais devoted to writing under mathematical constraints, and remained devoted to it for the rest of his life.

Works

Perec's most famous formal tour de force is La Disparition (1969), translated into English by Gilbert Adair as A Void — a 300-page novel written entirely without the letter "e", the most common letter in French. Its mirror image is Les Revenentes (1972), in which "e" is the only vowel used. W ou le souvenir d'enfance (W, or the Memory of Childhood, 1975) alternates autobiographical fragments with the story of a totalitarian island society organized around sport, obliquely confronting the Holocaust.

His masterpiece is generally considered to be La Vie mode d'emploi (Life: A User's Manual, 1978), which won the Prix Médicis. Structured like a knight's tour across a chessboard and describing, room by room, a Paris apartment building at a single moment, it interweaves dozens of stories, lists, and puzzles around the figure of the painter Valène and the millionaire Bartlebooth's fifty-year project of painting and destroying 500 jigsaw puzzles.

Perec also wrote radio plays, crossword puzzles for Le Point, the essay collection Penser/Classer (Think/Classify), the infra-ordinary urban observations of Espèces d'espaces (Species of Spaces, 1974) and An Attempt at Exhausting a Place in Paris (1975), and co-directed the film Un homme qui dort (1974). A heavy smoker, he died of lung cancer in 1982, aged 45.
