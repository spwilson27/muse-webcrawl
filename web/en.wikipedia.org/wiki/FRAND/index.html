FRAND

FRAND (fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory), also known as RAND in the United States, describes the licensing terms that owners of standard-essential patents (SEPs) typically commit to when contributing their technology to a technical standard set by a standard-setting organization (SSO).

Background

SSO policies, such as those of ETSI and IEEE, require participants to disclose essential patents and agree to license them on FRAND terms, so that a patent holder cannot block implementers of a standard or extract monopoly rents attributable to standardization itself. The specific meaning of "fair and reasonable" royalty rates has been the subject of extensive global litigation, particularly in the mobile telecommunications industry over 3G, 4G, and 5G standards.

Key disputes and rulings

Major cases have addressed how to calculate FRAND royalties (for example, on the end device versus the smallest saleable component), whether injunctions are available against willing licensees, and which courts may set global rates. Landmark decisions include Microsoft v. Motorola (US), Unwired Planet v. Huawei (UK Supreme Court, 2020, confirming UK courts can set global FRAND terms), and the European Court of Justice framework in Huawei v. ZTE (2015) governing negotiation conduct before seeking injunctions.

Policy debates

Regulators and industry groups continue to debate transparency of SEP declarations, over-declaration, royalty stacking, and the role of patent pools. The European Commission proposed an EU SEP regulation in 2023 to increase transparency and FRAND determination procedures.
