Robert Herrick

Born24 August 1591, Cheapside, London, England
Died15 October 1674 (aged 83), Dean Prior, Devon, England
OccupationPoet, Anglican cleric
Notable worksHesperides (1648); "To the Virgins, to Make Much of Time"

Robert Herrick (baptised 24 August 1591 – buried 15 October 1674) was a 17th-century English lyric poet and Anglican cleric. He is best known for his collection Hesperides (1648), which contains the famous carpe diem poem "To the Virgins, to Make Much of Time", opening "Gather ye rosebuds while ye may".

Life

Herrick was born in Cheapside, London, the son of a goldsmith who died in a fall when Robert was an infant. He was apprenticed to his uncle, a goldsmith, before going up to St John's College, Cambridge. In London he became one of the "Sons of Ben" — the circle of poets gathered around Ben Jonson — and a friend of the playwright and poet circle around the court of Charles I. Ordained in 1623, he became vicar of Dean Prior in Devon in 1629. Ejected under the Commonwealth in 1647, he returned to London, published Hesperides (with the religious poems of Noble Numbers) in 1648, and was restored to his Devon living at the Restoration.

Poetry

Hesperides contains over 1,200 short poems: lyrics of love and transience, country festivals and customs, epigrams, epitaphs and fairy poems such as "Oberon's Feast". His polished couplets and song-like measures influenced later lyric poets, and his work was rediscovered and celebrated in the 19th century.
