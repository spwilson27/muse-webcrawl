Jack Layton

John Gilbert Layton

Born: July 18, 1950, Montreal, Quebec

Died: August 22, 2011, Toronto, Ontario

Offices: Leader of the New Democratic Party (2003–2011); Leader of the Opposition (2011)

Spouse: Olivia Chow

John Gilbert "Jack" Layton (July 18, 1950 – August 22, 2011) was a Canadian politician who led the New Democratic Party (NDP) from 2003 until his death in 2011. In the 2011 federal election he led the NDP to its best result in history — 103 seats and the status of Official Opposition — in a breakthrough widely called the "Orange Wave," particularly in Quebec.

Early life and municipal politics

Layton was born in Montreal and raised in Hudson, Quebec, the son of Doris and Robert Layton, a Progressive Conservative cabinet minister. He studied political science at McGill University and earned a PhD from the University of Toronto in 1983, then taught at Ryerson Polytechnical Institute. Elected to Toronto City Council in 1982, Layton became a prominent municipal voice on housing, homelessness, public health, and the environment, and served as president of the Federation of Canadian Municipalities.

NDP leadership

Layton won the NDP leadership in January 2003 on the first ballot, despite not holding a federal seat; he entered Parliament in 2004 representing Toronto—Danforth. Under his leadership the party steadily increased its seat count — 19 in 2004, 29 in 2006, and 37 in 2008 — and in 2005 Layton negotiated amendments to the federal budget with the Liberal minority government in exchange for NDP support.

The 2011 election

In the 2011 campaign, Layton's optimistic style and strong debate performances, especially in French, propelled the NDP past the Liberals and the Bloc Québécois. The party won 103 seats, 59 of them in Quebec, and Layton became Leader of the Official Opposition — the first NDP leader to hold the post.

Illness, death, and legacy

Layton had been treated for prostate cancer and hip surgery before the election. In July 2011 he announced a leave of absence to fight a new cancer. He died on August 22, 2011, at age 61, and was given a state funeral in Toronto. His final public letter, published posthumously, closed with the words: "My friends, love is better than anger. Hope is better than fear. Optimism is better than despair. So let us be loving, hopeful and optimistic. And we'll change the world." Layton is remembered as one of the most popular Canadian political figures of his era.
