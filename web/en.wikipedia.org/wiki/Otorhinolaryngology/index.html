Otorhinolaryngology

Otorhinolaryngology (from Greek: οὖς ous, ear; ῥίς rhis, nose; λάρυγξ larynx; and -λογία, study) is a surgical subspecialty within medicine that deals with the surgical and medical management of conditions of the head and neck. Doctors who specialize in this area are called otorhinolaryngologists, otolaryngologists, head and neck surgeons, or ENT surgeons or physicians (for ear, nose and throat). Patients seek treatment from an otorhinolaryngologist for diseases of the ear, nose, throat, and related structures of the head and neck, including the sinuses, larynx, oral cavity and upper pharynx.

Subspecialties

The field comprises otology and neurotology (diseases of the ear and balance system, including hearing loss and cochlear implants), rhinology (nose and paranasal sinuses, including endoscopic sinus surgery), laryngology (voice, airway and swallowing disorders), head and neck oncologic surgery (tumours of the head and neck, including thyroid and salivary gland surgery), facial plastic and reconstructive surgery, pediatric otorhinolaryngology, and sleep medicine and surgery for obstructive sleep apnea and snoring. Common conditions treated include otitis media, tonsillitis, sinusitis, hearing loss, vertigo, nasal obstruction, and head and neck cancers.

Training

In the United States, otolaryngology–head and neck surgery is a five-year residency following medical school, entered through a competitive match; fellowships of one to two years provide subspecialty training. In the United Kingdom, training follows core surgical training with a national training number in ENT, leading to a Certificate of Completion of Training. Otorhinolaryngology was one of the earliest surgical specialties to organize, with the specialty's roots in the 19th-century development of the laryngoscope and otoscope.
