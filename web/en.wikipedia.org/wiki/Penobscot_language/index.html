Penobscot language

FamilyAlgic → Algonquian → Eastern Algonquian
RegionMaine
StatusDormant; under revitalisation

The Penobscot language is an Eastern Algonquian language historically spoken by the Penobscot people of Maine. It is closely related to, and often treated as a dialect of, Abenaki — specifically Eastern Abenaki — though the Penobscot Nation regards it as its own language.

History and documentation

Penobscot was the primary language of the Penobscot community until the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, when pressure from English schooling and settlement caused rapid decline. The linguist Frank Siebert devoted decades from the 1930s onward to documenting the language with fluent elders, producing a dictionary and texts that remain its principal scholarly record. The last fully fluent first-language speakers died in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries.

Revitalisation

The Penobscot Nation has undertaken revitalisation efforts using Siebert's materials and community teachers, including language classes, immersion activities for children, and the publication of teaching resources, aiming to bring the language back into everyday community use.
