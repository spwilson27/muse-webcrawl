São Paulo is one of the 26 states of Brazil, located in the Southeast Region. It is the most populous and economically powerful state in the country, home to roughly a fifth of Brazil's population and about a third of its GDP. Its capital, also called São Paulo, is the largest city in the Southern Hemisphere.

History

The region was settled by Portuguese colonists from the early 16th century; the town of São Paulo was founded by Jesuit missionaries in 1554. From São Paulo the bandeirantes launched expeditions into the interior in search of gold, enslaved Indigenous people and new territory, greatly expanding Brazil's borders. In the 19th century, coffee became the state's dominant export and drove the construction of railways and waves of European and Japanese immigration. On 9 July 1932 the state rose in the Constitutionalist Revolution against Getúlio Vargas's provisional government; the uprising was defeated but is still commemorated as a state holiday.

Economy

São Paulo is Brazil's industrial heartland, with major automotive, aerospace (Embraer is headquartered in São José dos Campos), pharmaceutical, and financial sectors. The state is also a leading agricultural producer of sugarcane, coffee, oranges and ethanol. The port of Santos is the busiest container port in Latin America.

Geography and cities

Beyond the capital, major cities include Campinas, Santos, São José dos Campos, Ribeirão Preto, Sorocaba and São José do Rio Preto. The state contains stretches of Atlantic Forest, including reserves along the Serra do Mar escarpment.
