The Bonn Agreement was the initial series of agreements intended to re-create the State of Afghanistan following the US-led invasion and the fall of the Taliban in late 2001. Formally the Agreement on Provisional Arrangements in Afghanistan Pending the Re-establishment of Permanent Government Institutions, it was signed on 5 December 2001 at the Petersberg near Bonn, Germany, by representatives of Afghan factions under United Nations auspices. The Taliban were not represented.

Provisions

The agreement established an Interim Authority chaired by Hamid Karzai, which took power in Kabul on 22 December 2001, and set a roadmap: an Emergency Loya Jirga in 2002 to create a Transitional Administration, a constitutional commission and Constitutional Loya Jirga (which adopted the 2004 constitution), and national elections, held for the presidency in 2004 and parliament in 2005. It also requested a UN-mandated international security force (ISAF) for Kabul.

Legacy

The Bonn framework underpinned the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan's institutions for two decades. Critics have argued that the exclusion of the Taliban, the empowerment of warlords, and the highly centralised presidential system it produced contributed to the state's later fragility; the republic collapsed with the Taliban's return to power in August 2021.
