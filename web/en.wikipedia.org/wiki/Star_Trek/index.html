Star Trek is an American science fiction media franchise created by Gene Roddenberry, which began with the eponymous 1960s television series and became a worldwide pop-culture phenomenon. The franchise has expanded into various films, television series, video games, novels, and comic books, and it has a cult following, fostering a distinctive fan subculture.

The original series follows the voyages of the crew of the starship Enterprise, a space-exploration vessel built by the United Federation of Planets in the 23rd century, on a mission "to explore strange new worlds, to seek out new life and new civilizations, to boldly go where no man has gone before."

History

Roddenberry, a former Los Angeles police officer and television writer, pitched Star Trek as a "Wagon Train to the stars." The series was produced by Desilu Productions and aired on NBC from September 1966 to June 1969. Though cancelled after three seasons, it became a hit in syndication and spawned an animated series (1973–74), then a film series beginning with Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979).

Star Trek: The Next Generation (1987–1994), set a century later, became one of the most successful syndicated dramas of its era and was followed by Deep Space Nine (1993–1999), Voyager (1995–2001) and Enterprise (2001–2005). A reboot film series began in 2009 with Star Trek, directed by J. J. Abrams. The streaming era added Discovery (2017–2024), Picard (2020–2023), the animated Lower Decks and Prodigy, and Strange New Worlds (2022–present).

Themes and culture

The franchise is noted for its optimistic vision of the future, its progressive treatment of race, gender and diplomacy for its era, and concepts such as the Prime Directive, warp drive, transporters and communicators that anticipated real technologies. Its fans, known as Trekkies (or Trekkers), maintain one of the largest fan communities in the world, and the franchise influenced the naming of the Space Shuttle Enterprise.
