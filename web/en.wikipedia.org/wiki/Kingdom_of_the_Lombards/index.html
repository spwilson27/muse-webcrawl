Kingdom of the Lombards

The Kingdom of the Lombards (Regnum Langobardorum) was an early medieval kingdom established by the Germanic Lombards in Italy, lasting from their invasion in 568 until the Frankish conquest by Charlemagne in 774. Its capital was Pavia, and its kings ruled much of the Italian peninsula, though Byzantine enclaves and the southern Lombard duchies of Spoleto and Benevento limited its control.

History

Alboin led the Lombards into Italy in 568, quickly taking the Po valley. King Rothari issued the Edict of Rothari (643), the first Lombard law code. Liutprand (712–744) brought the kingdom to its greatest power and unity, but in 774 Charlemagne defeated Desiderius and took the Lombard crown, ending the kingdom in the north; Benevento survived as an independent principality for centuries.

Society

The Lombards gradually adopted Latin language and Catholic Christianity, merging with the Roman population; their name survives in the modern region of Lombardy.
