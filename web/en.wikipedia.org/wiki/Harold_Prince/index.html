Harold Smith Prince (born Harold Smith; 30 January 1928 – 31 July 2019), commonly known as Hal Prince, was an American theatrical producer and director associated with many of the best-known Broadway musical productions of the 20th century. Over the course of his career, he won 21 Tony Awards, more than any other individual, including eight for directing, eight for producing the year's Best Musical, and three special awards.

Career

After serving in the U.S. Army in postwar Germany, Prince began in theatre as an assistant stage manager to producer George Abbott. With Robert E. Griffith he produced The Pajama Game (1954), Damn Yankees (1955), West Side Story (1957), and Fiorello! (1959). As producer-director he mounted A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to the Forum (1962), Fiddler on the Roof (1964), and Cabaret (1966).

Collaboration with Sondheim and later work

From 1970 Prince's partnership with composer-lyricist Stephen Sondheim defined the "concept musical": Company (1970), Follies (1971), A Little Night Music (1973), Pacific Overtures (1976), Sweeney Todd (1979), and Merrily We Roll Along (1981). He later directed two of the most commercially successful musicals ever written for Andrew Lloyd Webber: Evita (1978) and The Phantom of the Opera (1986), the latter the longest-running show in Broadway history at its closing in 2023. Other major productions include Kiss of the Spider Woman (1993) and the acclaimed 1994 revival of Show Boat.

Legacy

Prince is widely credited with pioneering the concept musical and with championing serious, formally daring subject matter on Broadway. He received the Kennedy Center Honors in 1996 and the National Medal of Arts in 2000.
