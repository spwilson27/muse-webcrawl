Prospect Park

Prospect Park is a 526-acre (213 ha) public park in Brooklyn, New York City. Designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux after their work on Central Park, it opened in 1867 and is considered one of the designers' masterpieces. Olmsted reportedly preferred it to Central Park.

Features

The park contains the Long Meadow, one of the largest urban meadows in the United States; Prospect Park Lake; wooded areas known as the Ravine; the Prospect Park Zoo; the Brooklyn Botanic Garden adjacent to it; the bandshell; and the historic Litchfield Villa. Entrances are marked by Grand Army Plaza with its Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch at the northern corner.

Management

The park is operated by the Prospect Park Alliance in partnership with the New York City Department of Parks and Recreation. It hosts concerts, sports leagues, and the Brooklyn Marathon, and serves the surrounding neighborhoods of Park Slope, Prospect Heights, Windsor Terrace, and Flatbush.
