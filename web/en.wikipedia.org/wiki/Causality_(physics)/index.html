Causality (physics)

Causality in physics is the principle that an effect cannot occur before its cause, and that influences cannot propagate faster than the speed of light in vacuum. It structures theories from classical mechanics to relativity and quantum field theory.

Relativity

In special relativity, events fall into timelike, lightlike, and spacelike separations; only timelike or lightlike separated events can be causally connected, as organised by the light cone. The invariant causal ordering of timelike events is the same for all observers, protecting cause and effect from reversal.

Quantum theory

In quantum field theory, microcausality requires that observables at spacelike separation commute. Apparent faster-than-light effects, such as entanglement correlations, cannot transmit information (no-signalling). Retrocausality and closed timelike curves in general relativity remain speculative topics constrained by chronology protection arguments.
