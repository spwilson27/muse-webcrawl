Urine-diverting dry toilet

The pedestal separates urine at the front from faeces at the back

A urine-diverting dry toilet (UDDT) is a type of dry toilet with urine diversion that can be used to provide safe, affordable sanitation in both developing and developed countries. The distinguishing feature is a pedestal or squatting pan with two outlets, so that urine is collected separately from faeces, keeping the faecal fraction dry.

Operation

Dry faeces are stored in a vault or container and, after a dehydration period (aided by adding ash, lime, or dry soil after each use), can be used as a soil conditioner. Urine, collected in a separate tank, can be diluted and used as a nitrogen-rich fertiliser. Because no water is used for flushing, UDDTs save water and avoid contaminating water bodies, and they are a core technology of ecological sanitation (ecosan).

Applications

Large-scale UDDT programmes have been implemented in countries such as China, India (including the eThekwini municipality in South Africa with tens of thousands of units), and in eco-housing projects in Sweden and Germany, where urine separation is also studied for recovering phosphorus and reducing wastewater treatment loads.
