South Yorkshire is a ceremonial county and metropolitan county in England, in the Yorkshire and the Humber region. It was created on 1 April 1974 by the Local Government Act 1972, from the southern part of the West Riding of Yorkshire and the former county boroughs of Barnsley, Doncaster, Rotherham and Sheffield.

Geography

The county spans the eastern foothills of the Pennines in the west — including part of the Peak District National Park near Sheffield — to the low-lying Humberhead Levels in the east around Doncaster. The principal rivers are the Don, the Dearne and the Rother.

History and economy

South Yorkshire was historically a heartland of coal mining and steel production. Sheffield became world-renowned for steel and cutlery, including the invention of stainless steel in 1913, while the South Yorkshire Coalfield supported dozens of collieries. The miners' strike of 1984–85, including the confrontation at the Orgreave coking plant, and subsequent pit closures transformed the local economy, which has since shifted toward services, education, logistics and advanced manufacturing; Doncaster is a major rail and distribution centre, and the Meadowhall shopping centre near Sheffield opened in 1990 on a former steelworks site.

Governance

The county comprises four metropolitan boroughs: Sheffield, Rotherham, Barnsley and Doncaster. The South Yorkshire Mayoral Combined Authority, led by a directly elected mayor (first elected in 2018, initially as the Sheffield City Region mayor), coordinates transport, skills and economic development.
