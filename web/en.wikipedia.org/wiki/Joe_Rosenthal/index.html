Joseph John Rosenthal (October 9, 1911 – August 20, 2006) was an American photographer who received the Pulitzer Prize for his iconic World War II photograph Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima, taken on February 23, 1945. The image — six United States Marines raising a flag atop Mount Suribachi during the Battle of Iwo Jima — became one of the most reproduced photographs of the war and the model for the Marine Corps War Memorial near Washington, D.C.

Early life and career

Rosenthal was born in Washington, D.C., to Russian Jewish immigrant parents. Rejected by the US Army as a photographer because of poor eyesight, he joined the Associated Press and accompanied American forces in the Pacific as a war correspondent. On Iwo Jima he climbed a pile of rubble atop Suribachi and, almost without looking, snapped the second flag-raising of the day, using a Speed Graphic camera. When later asked whether the picture was posed, he misunderstood the question — he thought the inquirer meant a different, deliberately arranged group photograph he had also taken — and his offhand "sure" fueled a decades-long myth that the famous image had been staged. It had not been.

Later life

After the war Rosenthal worked for more than three decades as a staff photographer at the San Francisco Chronicle, retiring in 1981. He received numerous honors later in life, including being made an honorary Marine. He died in 2006 in Novato, California, at age 94.
