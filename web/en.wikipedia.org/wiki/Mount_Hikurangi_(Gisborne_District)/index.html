Mount Hikurangi (Gisborne District)

Mount Hikurangi (Māori: Hikurangi) is a 1,752-metre (5,748 ft) peak in the Raukūmara Range of the Gisborne District, in the northeast of New Zealand's North Island. It is the highest non-volcanic peak on the North Island and, because of its eastern location, is famously the first place in the world to see the sunrise on the mainland each day — a distinction that drew visitors for the dawn of 1 January 2000.

The mountain is sacred (tapu) to the iwi Ngāti Porou, who regard Hikurangi as the first part of Maui's waka, Nukutaimemeha, to emerge from the ocean when Māui fished up the North Island. Carved figures of Māui and his family stand on the mountain, erected for the millennium celebrations.

Hikurangi should not be confused with the lower Mount Hikurangi near Whangārei in Northland. The Gisborne peak lies within remote, steep country inland from Ruatoria; a hut and track maintained with local iwi support provide access for climbers, and guided sunrise ascents are a notable feature of tourism in Tairāwhiti.
