Kazakh Khanate

The Kazakh Khanate around 1700, spanning much of the Eurasian steppe

The Kazakh Khanate (Kazakh: Қазақ Хандығы, Qazaq Handyğy) was a Kazakh state that existed on the territory of modern Kazakhstan and neighbouring regions from 1465 to 1847. It emerged from the disintegration of the Golden Horde and is regarded as the founding polity of the Kazakh nation. At its height under Kasym Khan in the early 16th century, it controlled a vast territory from the Caspian Sea to the Altai Mountains.

Foundation

The khanate was founded by Kerei Khan and Janibek Khan, descendants of the White Horde khans, who led their followers away from the Uzbek khan Abul-Khayr around 1465 and settled in the Chu valley of Moghulistan. After the death of Abul-Khayr in 1468, many of his former subjects joined them, and the seceders became known as qazaqs — "free ones" or "adventurers".

Golden age

Under Kasym Khan (r. 1511–1521), the khanate reached its greatest extent and power, reportedly fielding up to 200,000 horsemen and codifying steppe customary law in the code known as Kasym Khannyn Kaska Zholy ("The Bright Path of Kasym Khan"). His successors included Haqnazar Khan, who reunified the khanate after a period of civil war in the later 16th century, and Esim Khan, whose legal code supplemented Kasym's.

Division into zhuzes

From the late 17th century the khanate fragmented into three tribal confederations known as zhuzes: the Senior (Uly) Zhuz in the southeast, the Middle (Orta) Zhuz in the centre and north, and the Junior (Kishi) Zhuz in the west. Each zhuz was led by its own khans, though a khan of the whole — notably Tauke Khan (r. 1680–1718), author of the Zheti Zhargy ("Seven Charters") law code — was sometimes recognised.

Dzungar wars and Russian absorption

In the 1720s invasions by the Dzungar Khanate devastated the Kazakh steppe in the catastrophe remembered as the "Great Disaster" (Aktaban Shubyryndy). Seeking protection, khans such as Abul Khair of the Junior Zhuz swore allegiance to the Russian Empire from 1731 onward. Over the following century Russia progressively absorbed the khanate; the office of khan was abolished in the Middle Zhuz in 1822 and in the Junior Zhuz in 1824, and the last khan, Kenesary Kasymov, was killed in 1847 while resisting Russian rule.
