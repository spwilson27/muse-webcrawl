Peter Williston Shor (born 14 August 1959) is an American professor of applied mathematics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT). He is best known for his work in quantum computing, in particular for devising Shor's algorithm, a quantum algorithm for factoring integers exponentially faster than the best known classical algorithms.

Early life and education

Shor grew up in Washington, D.C., and as a high school student placed third in the USA Mathematical Olympiad. He graduated from Caltech in 1981 and received his PhD in applied mathematics from MIT in 1985, supervised by Tom Leighton, with a thesis on probabilistic analysis of bin-packing algorithms.

Career

After a postdoctoral year at the Mathematical Sciences Research Institute in Berkeley, Shor joined AT&T Bell Laboratories. There, in 1994, he published his factoring algorithm, showing that a quantum computer could factor large integers and compute discrete logarithms in polynomial time — problems on which the security of widely used public-key cryptography, such as RSA, depends. The result was a landmark for quantum computation and spurred worldwide research on building quantum computers and on post-quantum cryptography.

Shor also made fundamental contributions to quantum error correction, showing in 1995 that quantum information can be protected against decoherence using encoded states (the Shor code) and later fault-tolerant quantum computation. In 2003 he moved to MIT as a professor of applied mathematics. He is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and has received numerous honours, including the Rolf Nevanlinna Prize, the Gödel Prize, and a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship.
