Tarquinius Superbus

Lucius Tarquinius Superbus ("Tarquin the Proud"), who reigned traditionally from 535 to 509 BC, was the legendary seventh and final king of Rome. He was the son (or grandson) of Lucius Tarquinius Priscus, the fifth king, and the son-in-law of Servius Tullius, the sixth, whom he murdered to seize the throne. Tarquin is remembered in Roman tradition as a tyrant, whose overthrow after the rape of Lucretia by his son Sextus led to the founding of the Roman Republic.

Reign

According to Livy and Dionysius of Halicarnassus, Tarquin ruled by fear: he abolished the privileges his predecessors had granted, executed leading senators, neglected the Senate, and judged capital cases alone. Abroad he was a successful commander, subduing Latin and Volscian towns and founding colonies at Signia and Circeii. He completed the great Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus on the Capitoline Hill and constructed the Cloaca Maxima sewer system, using forced plebeian labour.

Legend credits him with acquiring the Sibylline Books: a prophetess offered nine books of prophecy at a high price; when Tarquin refused, she burned three and offered the remaining six at the same price, then three more at the same price — which he finally paid.

Overthrow and exile

The revolt of 509 BC, led by Lucius Junius Brutus and Lucius Tarquinius Collatinus after the death of Lucretia, expelled the Tarquin family and established the Republic with two annually elected consuls. Tarquin made repeated attempts to regain the throne, enlisting Veii and Tarquinii, then Lars Porsena of Clusium, and finally his son-in-law Octavius Mamilius of Tusculum, whose Latin army was defeated at the Battle of Lake Regillus. He died in exile at Cumae in 495 BC.
