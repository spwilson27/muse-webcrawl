Lipase

A lipase is a family of enzymes that catalyse the hydrolysis of fats (lipids). Lipases break triglycerides into free fatty acids and glycerol (or mono- and diglycerides). They are found in most living organisms and are essential for digestion, transport and processing of dietary lipids.

Types and roles

Pancreatic lipase, secreted into the small intestine, is the principal enzyme for digesting dietary fat in humans and works with bile salts and the cofactor colipase. Other lipases include lingual and gastric lipases, lipoprotein lipase (which clears triglycerides from circulating lipoproteins), hormone-sensitive lipase (which mobilises stored fat in adipocytes), and lysosomal acid lipase. Blood levels of pancreatic lipase are measured clinically as a marker for acute pancreatitis.

Industrial uses

Lipases are used industrially in laundry detergents, food processing (cheese ripening, flavour development), biodiesel production, and the manufacture of enantiopure pharmaceutical intermediates, thanks to their activity in organic solvents and their stereoselectivity.
