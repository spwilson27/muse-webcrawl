Hundred Days' Reform

Date11 June – 21 September 1898
LocationQing China
Key figuresGuangxu Emperor, Kang Youwei, Liang Qichao, Empress Dowager Cixi
OutcomeSuppressed by coup; reforms rescinded

The Hundred Days' Reform was a failed programme of national, cultural, political and educational reform in Qing dynasty China, enacted by the Guangxu Emperor between 11 June and 21 September 1898. Inspired by reformers such as Kang Youwei and Liang Qichao, the emperor issued over a hundred edicts modernising the examination system, administration, military, education and economy.

Suppression

The reforms alarmed conservative officials and the Empress Dowager Cixi, who, with the support of General Ronglu and Yuan Shikai's betrayal of a reformist plot, staged a coup on 21 September 1898. The emperor was placed under house arrest on Yingtai island, most edicts were rescinded, and six reformers — the "Six Gentlemen", including Tan Sitong — were executed, while Kang and Liang fled into exile.

Significance

The failure of reform from above discredited gradual modernisation for many Chinese intellectuals and is often seen as a step toward the revolutionary upheavals that ended the Qing in 1911.
