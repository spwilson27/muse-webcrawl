British Museum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The British Museum's columned Greek Revival front entrance in London]

The British Museum in the Bloomsbury area of London is a public museum dedicated to human history, art and culture. Its permanent collection of some eight million works is among the largest and most comprehensive in the world, documenting human culture from its beginnings to the present.

It was established in 1753, largely based on the collections of the physician and scientist Sir Hans Sloane, and first opened to the public in 1759 at Montagu House. Admission is free.

Collections and controversies

Iconic objects include the Rosetta Stone, the Parthenon (Elgin) Marbles, the Sutton Hoo treasure, the Lewis chessmen and Egyptian mummies. The museum's encyclopaedic, colonial-era collecting is increasingly debated: Greece seeks the Parthenon sculptures' return, and other source countries press claims on objects such as the Benin Bronzes.

The building

The museum's Greek Revival facade and the Great Court — redeveloped in 2000 around the old British Library Reading Room, with a tessellated glass roof by Foster and Partners — are landmarks.
