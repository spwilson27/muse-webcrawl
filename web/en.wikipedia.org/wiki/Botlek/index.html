Botlek

The Botlek is a harbour and industrial area of the Port of Rotterdam in the Netherlands. It lies south-west of Rotterdam on the south bank of the Nieuwe Maas, between the city and the later Europoort and Maasvlakte extensions. It is named after a former creek in the area.

Botlek
Port: Rotterdam, Netherlands
Developed: 1947–1956
Industry: Petrochemicals, storage

Development

After the Second World War, Rotterdam needed deep-water port capacity for the growing oil trade. Work on the Botlek began in 1947, and the area opened in the 1950s as the second of the port's post-war expansion zones after the Eemhaven. Refineries and chemical plants lined its new basins, making it a centre of the petrochemical industry.

Infrastructure

The Botlek is connected to the road network by the Botlek Bridge (1955) and the Botlek Tunnel on the A15 motorway, which pass over and under the Oude Maas. Rail and pipeline corridors link its tank terminals to the German hinterland. Industry in the area includes chemical producers such as LyondellBasell, ExxonMobil, and Air Products.

Expansion westward

As the Botlek filled, port growth moved west to Europoort in the 1960s and the Maasvlakte on reclaimed North Sea land, but the Botlek remains a major cluster of refining, chemical production, and bulk liquid storage within Europe's largest port.
