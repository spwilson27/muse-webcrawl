Biblical manuscript

A biblical manuscript is a handwritten copy of a portion of the text of the Bible. Biblical manuscripts vary in size from tiny scrolls containing individual verses to huge polyglot codices. The study of these manuscripts, known as textual criticism, seeks to reconstruct the original wording of the biblical texts.

Hebrew Bible manuscripts

The oldest surviving manuscripts of the Hebrew Bible include the Dead Sea Scrolls, dating from the 3rd century BC to the 1st century AD, discovered near Qumran from 1947 onward. The authoritative medieval text is the Masoretic Text, preserved in codices such as the Aleppo Codex (c. 920 AD) and the Leningrad Codex (1008 AD).

New Testament manuscripts

New Testament manuscripts are classified as papyri, uncials (written in capital letters on parchment), minuscules, and lectionaries. Famous examples include Codex Vaticanus and Codex Sinaiticus, both 4th-century uncial codices containing much of the Bible in Greek. More than 5,800 Greek New Testament manuscripts are catalogued.

Versions and translations

Early translations — the Septuagint (Greek), the Vulgate (Latin), the Syriac Peshitta, and Coptic, Armenian, and Ethiopic versions — are also important witnesses used by textual critics to trace the history of the text.
