North American Mesoscale Model

The North American Mesoscale Model (NAM) is a regional numerical weather prediction model run by the United States National Centers for Environmental Prediction (NCEP). It provides short-range forecasts for North America at mesoscale resolution.

NAM
Operator: NCEP / NOAA
Domain: North America
Resolution: 12 km (3 km nest)
Forecast length: 84 hours

Configuration

The NAM is built on the Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) Nonhydrostatic Mesoscale Model core. The parent domain runs at 12 km grid spacing four times daily out to 84 hours; a 3 km nest covers the continental United States, with additional fire-weather and Alaska nests. It replaced the earlier Eta model in 2006.

Use

NAM output is widely used by National Weather Service forecast offices, broadcast meteorologists, and private forecasting for precipitation type, convective storms, winds, and temperature. Its convection-allowing nest informs severe-weather outlooks alongside the HRRR model.
