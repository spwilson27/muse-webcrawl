Sayagyi U Ba Khin (19 March 1899 – 19 January 1971) was a prominent Burmese Vipassana meditation teacher and the first Accountant General of independent Burma. He is best known as the teacher of S. N. Goenka, through whom his method of Vipassana meditation spread worldwide. "U" is a Burmese honorific meaning "Mr." or "uncle", and "Sayagyi" means "great teacher".

Life

Born in Yangon (Rangoon) in 1899, Ba Khin worked as a clerk in the Accountant General's office while studying meditation. He learned Vipassana from Saya Thetgyi, a lay teacher who had studied under the revered scholar-monk Ledi Sayadaw. In 1937 he had a breakthrough experience during a course, and eventually became an authorised teacher. After Burma's independence in 1948, he became the country's first Accountant General, combining a demanding civil service career with intensive meditation teaching.

Teaching

In 1952, Prime Minister U Nu supported his founding of the International Meditation Centre (IMC) in Rangoon, where Ba Khin taught civil servants—including many foreign students—in intensive ten-day courses. His method focuses on developing awareness of bodily sensations through the scanning of attention across the body. He emphasised that Vipassana is a universal, non-sectarian technique, open to people of all faiths. In 1955 he met the young businessman S. N. Goenka, who cured his migraines through the practice and, after fourteen years of training, was authorised by Ba Khin in 1969 to teach in India—beginning the movement that spread Vipassana courses to hundreds of centres worldwide.
