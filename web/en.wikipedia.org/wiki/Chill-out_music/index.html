Chill-out music

Stylistic origins: Ambient, downtempo, trip hop, acid jazz, lounge

Cultural origins: Late 1980s–early 1990s, Ibiza, Spain; United Kingdom

Typical instruments: Synthesiser, sampler, drum machine, electric piano

Chill-out (also written chillout or chill out) is a loosely defined form of popular music characterised by slow tempos and relaxed moods. The term "chill out" was popularised by British electronic duo The KLF with their 1990 ambient house album Chill Out, and by the "chill-out rooms" at raves, where DJs played slower, atmospheric music to give dancers a place to rest from the faster music of the main dancefloor.

History

The genre is closely associated with the Balearic island of Ibiza, where DJs such as José Padilla compiled sunset soundtracks at venues like the Café del Mar in San Antonio. Padilla's compilations, released from 1994 onwards as the Café del Mar series, helped define the sound for a mass audience. Chill-out draws on ambient music pioneered by Brian Eno, downtempo, trip hop acts such as Massive Attack and Portishead, and the softer side of acid jazz and lounge music.

Characteristics

Chill-out tracks typically feature slow beats (often below 110 BPM), mellow synthesiser pads, sampled vocals, gentle melodies and influences from world music, bossa nova, dub and jazz. Rather than a strict genre, the label is often used as an umbrella for any relaxed electronic or acoustic music intended to create a calm atmosphere.
