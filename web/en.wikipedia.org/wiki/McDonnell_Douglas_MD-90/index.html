The McDonnell Douglas MD-90 is an American five-abreast, single-aisle airliner developed by McDonnell Douglas from the earlier MD-80 series, itself a derivative of the DC-9. The MD-90 features more powerful, quieter, and more fuel-efficient International Aero Engines V2500 turbofans, a stretched fuselage, and an updated glass cockpit similar to that of the MD-11.

The MD-90 was launched in 1989, first flew on 22 February 1993, and entered service with Delta Air Lines in 1995. Production ended in 2000 after 116 aircraft had been built, making it the smallest-selling member of the DC-9 family. Two aircraft were assembled in China under the Trunkliner program before that effort was cancelled.

Design

Compared with the MD-80, the MD-90 is about 1.4 metres (4.6 ft) longer, seating up to 172 passengers in a high-density layout. Its V2500 engines are noticeably larger in diameter than the MD-80's JT8D-200 series engines, giving the aircraft a distinctive appearance and a particularly quiet cabin, since the engines are mounted at the rear of the fuselage. The flight deck introduced electronic flight instrumentation, reducing the flight crew to two pilots.

Variants

The standard MD-90-30 was joined by a small number of MD-90-30ER extended-range aircraft. A proposed shortened MD-90-40 and other developments were never built. After Boeing merged with McDonnell Douglas in 1997, the MD-90 competed awkwardly with Boeing's own 737 Next Generation family, and orders dried up.

Operators

Delta Air Lines became the largest operator, acquiring second-hand MD-90s from around the world and operating a fleet of more than 60 at its peak; it retired the type in 2020. Other operators included Saudi Arabian Airlines, Japan Airlines (through JAS), China Southern Airlines, EVA Air, and Uni Air.
