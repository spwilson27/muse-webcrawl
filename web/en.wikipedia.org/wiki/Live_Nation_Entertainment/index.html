Live Nation Entertainment

A large outdoor concert promoted by Live Nation

Live Nation Entertainment, Inc. is an American multinational entertainment company and the world's largest live entertainment company and concert promoter. Formed in 2010 by the merger of Live Nation and ticket seller Ticketmaster, it promotes tours and festivals, operates hundreds of venues, sells tickets, and manages artists, generating annual revenue of more than US$20 billion in recent years.

History

Live Nation originated in 1996 as SFX Entertainment, a roll-up of regional concert promoters, which Clear Channel Communications acquired in 2000. In 2005 Clear Channel spun off its entertainment division as the independent company Live Nation. Under CEO Michael Rapino, the company expanded into ticketing, artist management, and long-term "360-degree" deals with artists such as Madonna and Jay-Z.

In 2009 Live Nation announced a merger with Ticketmaster; after a review, the United States Department of Justice approved it in January 2010 subject to a consent decree and divestitures, creating Live Nation Entertainment. The consent decree governed Ticketmaster's licensing and retaliatory conduct for a decade and was extended and strengthened in 2019 after findings of violations.

Business

The company operates three main segments: concerts (promotion and venue operation), ticketing (Ticketmaster), and sponsorship and advertising. It owns or operates well over 300 venues, including amphitheatres, clubs, and the House of Blues chain, and promotes major festivals such as Bonnaroo, Lollapalooza (via C3 Presents), and Electric Daisy Carnival partnerships. Liberty Media is its largest shareholder.

Criticism and legal issues

Live Nation has faced sustained criticism over ticket fees, market dominance, and the Ticketmaster platform's handling of high-demand on-sales — most visibly the November 2022 presale for Taylor Swift's Eras Tour, which crashed and prompted congressional hearings. In May 2024, the U.S. Department of Justice and numerous states sued to break up the company, alleging monopolisation of live events; the case remains contested. The company was also scrutinised after the crowd crush at the Astroworld Festival in Houston in 2021, which it co-promoted and which resulted in ten deaths and extensive litigation.
