Bohr–van Leeuwen theorem

Bohr–van Leeuwen theorem
FieldStatistical mechanics
Named afterNiels Bohr, Hendrika Johanna van Leeuwen
StatementClassical equilibrium magnetisation is identically zero
First statedBohr (1911); van Leeuwen (1919/1921)

The Bohr–van Leeuwen theorem states that when statistical mechanics and classical mechanics are applied consistently, the thermal average magnetisation of a system in equilibrium is always zero. In other words, magnetism cannot be explained by classical physics alone.

Content and proof idea

In a classical system, the partition function of charged particles in a magnetic field can be shown — by shifting the momentum variables to absorb the vector potential — to be independent of the field. Since magnetisation derives from the field-dependence of the free energy, it vanishes exactly. The theorem explains why diamagnetism, paramagnetism and ferromagnetism require quantum mechanics.

History

Niels Bohr arrived at the result in his 1911 doctoral dissertation; Hendrika Johanna van Leeuwen independently proved it in her 1919 dissertation (published 1921), and the theorem now bears both names.
