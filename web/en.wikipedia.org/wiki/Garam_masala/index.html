Garam masala

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Garam masala (from Hindi गरम मसाला, garam masālā, "warm spice mix") is a blend of ground spices originating in the Indian subcontinent. The word garam refers not to chilli heat but to the intensity of the spices and, in Ayurvedic tradition, to their warming effect on the body.

Composition

Recipes vary by region and household. A typical North Indian blend includes black and green cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, cumin, coriander seed, black peppercorns, bay leaves and mace or nutmeg. Punjabi versions tend to emphasize black cardamom and black pepper; South Indian blends may add star anise, fennel and stone flower. The whole spices are usually dry-roasted and ground; the blend is often added near the end of cooking, or sprinkled over a finished dish, to preserve its aroma.

Garam masala is a defining seasoning of Mughlai and North Indian dishes such as chole, rajma, biryanis and many meat curries, and has spread through the South Asian diaspora into British and global cooking. It differs from generic curry powder, a British colonial-era invention that typically includes turmeric and chilli.
