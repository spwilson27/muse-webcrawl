Jagiellonian Globe

Datec. 1510
LocationCollegium Maius Museum, Kraków, Poland
SignificanceAmong the oldest surviving terrestrial globes; the earliest to depict the Americas

The Jagiellonian Globe (Polish: Globus Jagielloński), dated to about 1510, is one of the oldest preserved terrestrial globes and is generally regarded as the earliest surviving globe to show the Americas. It is held by the museum of the Collegium Maius of the Jagiellonian University in Kraków.

Description

The globe is made of gilded copper and forms part of a mechanical armillary sphere fitted with a clockwork mechanism; it is about 18 centimetres in diameter. On its surface, in the position of the newly reported lands across the Atlantic, appears the inscription America noviter reperta ("America, newly discovered"), reflecting knowledge of the voyages of Columbus and Vespucci reaching Kraków within two decades of 1492.

History

The instrument probably came to the university in the early sixteenth century, when Kraków was a notable centre of astronomy and cartography. It takes its name from the Jagiellonian dynasty and the university itself.
