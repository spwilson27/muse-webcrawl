Council of Epaon

Held in 517 at Epaon (present-day Albon, France).

The Council of Epaon was a church synod held in 517 at Epaon (or Epaone), usually identified with present-day Albon in south-eastern France, in the kingdom of Burgundy. Convened under the Burgundian king Sigismund, it was attended by some two dozen bishops from his realm and enacted forty canons that became an important source of early medieval canon law in Gaul.

Canons

The council's legislation addressed clerical discipline, church property, and relations between Christians and Jews. Among its notable provisions, it prohibited clergy from attending Jewish banquets, forbade the building of altars of wood (requiring stone instead), regulated the ordination of widows and deaconesses, and laid down rules on penance and the reconciliation of heretics. It also addressed the incest prohibitions in marriage, extending the degrees within which marriage was forbidden.

Context and legacy

Held shortly after Sigismund's conversion from Arianism to Catholic Christianity and his founding of the abbey of Saint-Maurice d'Agaune, the council formed part of the integration of the Burgundian kingdom into the Catholic world of post-Roman Gaul. Its canons were widely copied into later canonical collections and influenced the subsequent Council of Orléans and other Merovingian synods.
