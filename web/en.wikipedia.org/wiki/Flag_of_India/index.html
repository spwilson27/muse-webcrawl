Flag of India

Use: National flag
Proportion: 2:3
Adopted: 15 August 1947
Design: A horizontal triband of saffron, white and green with a navy-blue Ashoka Chakra in the centre

The national flag of India, colloquially called the Tiranga (the tricolour), is a horizontal rectangular tricolour of India saffron, white and India green, with the Ashoka Chakra, a 24-spoke wheel, in navy blue at its centre. It was adopted in its present form during a meeting of the Constituent Assembly held on 22 July 1947, and it became the official flag of the Dominion of India on 15 August 1947.

The flag was designed by Pingali Venkayya, an agriculturist and freedom fighter from Andhra Pradesh. Earlier versions used by the Indian independence movement bore a spinning wheel (charkha) in the centre, symbolising self-reliance; the Constituent Assembly replaced it with the Ashoka Chakra, the eternal wheel of law (dharma) from the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath.

Symbolism

India's first prime minister, Jawaharlal Nehru, explained that the saffron band indicates strength and courage, the white band truth and peace, and the green band fertility, growth and auspiciousness. The Chakra shows that there is life in movement and death in stagnation. The flag must by law be made of khadi, hand-spun cloth, a rule administered by the Bureau of Indian Standards; since a 2021 amendment, machine-made polyester flags are also permitted.

Flag code

The use of the flag is governed by the Flag Code of India, 2002. The flag may be flown by private citizens on all days (a right affirmed by the Supreme Court in 2004), must never touch the ground or water, and when damaged must be destroyed privately, preferably by burning. The official ratio of length to width is 3:2.
