Dosimeter

A dosimeter is a device that measures exposure to ionizing radiation, such as alpha and beta particles, neutrons and gamma rays, over a period of time. Dosimeters are worn by personnel who work with radioactive materials or radiation-generating equipment — in nuclear power, medicine, industry and research — so that their cumulative radiation dose can be monitored and kept within regulatory limits.

Types

  • Film badge dosimeter — photographic film in a holder with filters of different materials; the developed film's darkening indicates dose and radiation type.
  • Thermoluminescent dosimeter (TLD) — a crystal such as lithium fluoride stores energy from radiation and releases it as light when heated, allowing the dose to be read out.
  • Optically stimulated luminescence (OSL) dosimeter — similar in principle to the TLD but read out with light.
  • Electronic personal dosimeter (EPD) — uses a semiconductor detector (typically a PIN diode) to provide a direct digital readout, often with dose-rate alarms.
  • Quartz fibre and direct-reading pocket dosimeters — a charged electroscope whose fibre deflection shows accumulated dose.

Use

Personal dosimeters are usually worn on the torso between the waist and neck. Results are recorded over a wear period of weeks or months and form part of a worker's official dose record. Dosimeter readings are expressed in terms of dose equivalent, in sieverts or millisieverts, and are compared against occupational exposure limits set by bodies such as the International Commission on Radiological Protection.

Related instruments

A dosimeter differs from a survey meter or Geiger counter, which measures instantaneous radiation levels rather than cumulative personal dose, and from an area dosimeter, which monitors a fixed location.
