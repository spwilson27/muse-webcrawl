Nutella

Product type: Hazelnut cocoa spread
Owner: Ferrero
Country: Italy
Introduced: 1964

Nutella is a brand of sweetened hazelnut cocoa spread manufactured by the Italian company Ferrero. First introduced in 1964, it is now sold in approximately 160 countries and is one of the world's best-known food brands.

History

Nutella grew out of earlier products developed in Piedmont, Italy, where hazelnuts were plentiful but cocoa was expensive after the Second World War. In 1946 Pietro Ferrero created a solid block of hazelnut paste and cocoa called Giandujot, followed by a spreadable version, Supercrema, in 1951. His son Michele Ferrero reformulated and relaunched the product in 1964 under the name Nutella, combining the English word "nut" with the Italian diminutive suffix "-ella".

Ingredients and use

The main ingredients are sugar, palm oil, hazelnuts (about 13%), cocoa solids, and skimmed milk powder. Nutella is commonly spread on bread, toast, and crêpes, and used as a filling or topping in baking and desserts. Its use of palm oil and high sugar content has drawn criticism from health and environmental campaigners, which the company has addressed through sustainability certification programmes.
