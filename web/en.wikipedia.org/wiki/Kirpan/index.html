Kirpan

The kirpan is one of the five articles of faith worn by Khalsa Sikhs.

The kirpan is a curved, single-edged dagger or short sword carried by Sikhs as an article of faith. It is one of the Five Ks (panj kakkār) mandated by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699 for initiated Khalsa Sikhs. The word combines kirpa (mercy, compassion) and aan (honour, dignity): the kirpan symbolises a Sikh's duty to protect the weak and uphold justice, rather than serving primarily as a weapon.

Form and wearing

Kirpans range from a few centimetres to full sword length; in everyday wear in Western countries they are usually small blades worn in a sheath (mian) suspended from a cloth strap (gatra) across the torso, often under clothing.

Legal disputes

The carrying of kirpans has prompted legal cases in several countries over weapons rules in schools, courthouses, and aircraft. Courts in Canada (notably the Supreme Court's 2006 Multani decision) and India, whose constitution expressly permits Sikhs to carry kirpans, have upheld the practice as religious freedom; in the United Kingdom, the Offensive Weapons Act 2019 includes a defence for kirpans carried for religious reasons.
