Household regiment

A household regiment is a military unit forming part of a sovereign's household troops — the guard forces charged with protecting the monarch and royal residences, and usually holding the highest precedence in their army. The term is especially associated with the British Household Division, but comparable formations exist in many monarchies.

British Household Division

In the United Kingdom, the Household Division consists of the Household Cavalry (the Life Guards and the Blues and Royals) and the five regiments of Foot Guards: the Grenadier Guards, Coldstream Guards, Scots Guards, Irish Guards, and Welsh Guards. These regiments mount the King's Guard at Buckingham Palace and other royal residences and play the leading ceremonial role at events such as Trooping the Colour, while also serving as operational combat units in wartime.

Other countries

Historic and current equivalents include France's former Maison militaire du roi and the modern Republican Guard, Spain's Royal Guard, Denmark's Royal Life Guards, Sweden's Life Guards, Thailand's King's Guard units, and the Imperial Guard traditions of Russia and Japan. In republics, presidential guards perform a similar function without being "household" units in the strict sense.
