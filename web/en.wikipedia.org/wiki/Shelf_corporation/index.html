Shelf corporation

A shelf corporation, shelf company, or aged corporation is a company or corporation that has had no activity and has been left dormant — metaphorically, put on the "shelf" to "age". Such a company can later be sold to a person or group who wish to start a company without going through the process of creating a new one.

Reasons for buying

Common reasons for purchasing a shelf corporation include:

  • Saving time — avoiding the administrative steps of incorporating a new company.
  • Corporate history — an older company may appear more established, which can help when bidding for contracts, since some tenders require a company to have existed for a minimum period.
  • Access to credit — a company with several years of existence may find it easier to obtain bank accounts, credit lines, and investment capital, although reputable lenders typically investigate whether the company actually traded during that period.
  • Appearance of longevity — an aged company may inspire more confidence among customers and suppliers.

Risks and regulation

The use of shelf companies is legal in many jurisdictions, and a legitimate market exists for them, with prices rising according to the age of the company. However, shelf companies have also been misused for money laundering, tax evasion, and fraud, because the buyer can acquire a ready-made corporate vehicle whose origins are difficult to trace. Regulators and financial authorities in a number of countries have therefore tightened disclosure and beneficial-ownership requirements, and due-diligence obligations on banks make it harder to exploit an aged company to fabricate a trading history it never had.

A buyer should verify that the shelf company has no hidden debts, tax liabilities, or prior obligations, since these pass with the company.
