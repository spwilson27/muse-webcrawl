Spring Festival

The Spring Festival (traditional Chinese: 春節; simplified Chinese: 春节; pinyin: Chūnjié) is the Chinese New Year festival, celebrating the beginning of a new year on the traditional Chinese calendar. It is one of the most important holidays in Chinese culture, marked by family reunions, feasting, red decorations, fireworks, and the giving of red envelopes containing money. The festival begins on Chinese New Year's Eve and traditionally lasts 15 days, ending with the Lantern Festival.

History and mythology

The festival has roots in ancient year-end sacrifices and agricultural rites honouring ancestors and household gods. Legend tells of Nian, a mythical beast that terrorised villages at the turn of the year until an old beggar (or the noise of firecrackers and the colour red) drove it away—explaining the enduring customs of setting off fireworks and displaying red. The modern name "Spring Festival" was adopted in the Republic of China era, and the holiday was standardised under the People's Republic, which grants a week-long public holiday.

Customs

Preparations include thorough house-cleaning to sweep away ill fortune, decorating with red paper cuttings, couplets bearing auspicious phrases, and the character fu (福, "fortune"), often hung upside down to symbolise fortune's arrival. On New Year's Eve families gather for a reunion dinner, often featuring fish (symbolising surplus), dumplings in northern China, and niangao rice cakes in the south. Elders give children red envelopes (hongbao) with money. The holiday period triggers chunyun, the world's largest annual human migration, as hundreds of millions travel home. Festivities close with the Lantern Festival on the 15th day, featuring lantern displays and sweet glutinous rice balls (tangyuan).

Celebration worldwide

Spring Festival is celebrated across the Chinese diaspora and in countries with significant Chinese heritage, including Singapore, Malaysia, and Vietnam (where the related Tết is observed). Major cities worldwide hold parades with lion and dragon dances. In 2023, the United Nations adopted Lunar New Year as a floating holiday, and in 2024 UNESCO inscribed the Spring Festival on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
