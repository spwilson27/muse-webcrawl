Tendai

Tendai-shū
Founder: Saichō (Dengyō Daishi)
Founded: 805 CE, Mount Hiei, Japan
Head temple: Enryaku-ji
Root text: Lotus Sutra

Tendai (天台宗, Tendai-shū) is a Japanese school of Mahayana Buddhism, derived from the Chinese Tiantai school and founded by Saichō in 805. Centred on Mount Hiei's Enryaku-ji, it became the dominant, state-sponsored Buddhism of early Heian Japan.

Doctrine

Based on the Lotus Sutra, Tendai teaches ekayāna — all teachings are one vehicle — and synthesises meditation, esoteric ritual, Pure Land and precepts. Zhiyi's classification of sutras and ichinen sanzen (three thousand realms in a single thought-moment) underpin it.

Influence

Pure Land, Zen, Nichiren and Shingon founders — Hōnen, Eisai, Dōgen, Nichiren — all trained in Tendai on Mount Hiei.
