Saṃsāra


Concept: Cycle of birth, death and rebirth
Traditions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism
Driven by: Karma

Saṃsāra is a Sanskrit and Pali term meaning the cycle of death and rebirth to which life in the material world is bound. In Indian religions, beings wander through successive existences — as humans, animals, gods or other forms — conditioned by karma, until liberation is attained.

In Hinduism

In Hindu thought, the individual self (ātman) transmigrates through saṃsāra according to its karma. Liberation, called moksha, releases the self from the cycle and unites it with ultimate reality.

In Buddhism

Buddhism teaches that saṃsāra is characterised by suffering (dukkha) and driven by ignorance, craving and aversion. The cycle is often depicted in the Bhavacakra, the wheel of life, divided into six realms. Escape comes through nirvana, reached by following the Noble Eightfold Path.

In Jainism

Jainism describes the soul as weighed down by karmic particles through successive rebirths across four states of existence; liberation (moksha) frees the soul to rise to the top of the universe.
