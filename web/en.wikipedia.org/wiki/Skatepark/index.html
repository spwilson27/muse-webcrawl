Skatepark

A skatepark, or skate park, is a purpose-built recreation environment designed for skateboarding, BMX riding, inline skating, and scooters. Skateparks typically feature obstacles such as bowls, pools, half-pipes, quarter pipes, ramps, rails, ledges, stairs, and banks, constructed from concrete, wood, or metal.

History

The first skateparks were built in the 1970s during the early growth of skateboarding; early parks such as Skatopia in Florida and Carlsbad Skatepark in California opened in 1976, inspired in part by the empty swimming pools ridden by skaters during California droughts. Many early parks closed in the early 1980s, but the 1990s and 2000s saw a revival in which municipalities widely funded public concrete parks. DIY and community-built parks, such as Burnside Skatepark in Portland, Oregon (begun in 1990 under a bridge), also became influential.

Design

Modern parks are often divided between street-style plazas replicating urban obstacles and transition (vert) areas of bowls and ramps. Shotcrete and poured concrete are the preferred materials for durability and smooth riding surfaces. Well-designed parks allow continuous "flow", letting riders move between obstacles without pushing.

Notable skateparks

Well-known skateparks include Venice Beach Skatepark and the Vans Skate Park in California, Southbank in London (a found street spot under the Southbank Centre), FDR Skatepark in Philadelphia, and Burnside in Portland.
