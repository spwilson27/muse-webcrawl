Kanō Jigorō

Kanō Jigorō (嘉納 治五郎, 28 October 1860 – 4 May 1938) was a Japanese educator and martial artist, the founder of judo. He established the Kodokan in 1882, transforming the older jujutsu schools into a modern discipline combining physical education, contest and moral cultivation.

Early life and jujutsu training

Born in Mikage, near Kobe, Kanō was a slight youth who took up jujutsu to strengthen his body, studying under masters of the Tenjin Shin'yō-ryū and Kitō-ryū schools. While a student at Tokyo Imperial University he analysed the techniques of many schools, retaining effective throws and grappling methods and discarding dangerous ones, and organised them into a new system he named judo — "the gentle way".

The Kodokan and philosophy

In 1882 Kanō opened the Kodokan dojo in a temple in Tokyo with just nine students. His principles — seiryoku zen'yō (maximum efficient use of energy) and jita kyōei (mutual welfare and benefit) — made judo an educational philosophy as much as a fighting art. Kodokan judo famously prevailed in matches against rival jujutsu schools sponsored by the Tokyo police in the 1880s, establishing its reputation.

International role

Kanō was the first Asian member of the International Olympic Committee (from 1909) and worked to spread judo worldwide and to bring the Olympic Games to Japan. He died of pneumonia aboard the liner Hikawa Maru in 1938 while returning from an IOC meeting in Cairo. Judo became an Olympic sport at the 1964 Tokyo Games.
