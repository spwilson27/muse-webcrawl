Clan Farquharson

Clan Farquharson (Scottish Gaelic: Clann Fearchair) is a Highland Scottish clan based in Aberdeenshire, in the area of Deeside around Braemar and Ballater, and a member of the Chattan Confederation (Clan Chattan). The clan takes its name from Farquhar, son of Alexander "Ciar" Mackintosh of Rothiemurchus, who settled in the Braes of Mar in the fourteenth century. The chief's seat was Invercauld House near Braemar.

The Farquharson country lies in upper Deeside, around Braemar and Invercauld.

History

The Farquharsons rose as a Deeside clan allied through marriage and bonds of manrent with their Mackintosh kin and the Clan Chattan confederation. Finlay Mòr Farquharson (c. 1480s–1547), a noted soldier, was the clan's effective progenitor of the main line; he carried the royal standard at the Battle of Pinkie in 1547, where he was killed. The clan supported the Stewart cause: Farquharsons fought under Montrose in the civil wars of the 1640s, and John Farquharson of Inverey ("the Black Colonel") was a prominent Jacobite in the rising of 1689.

In the Jacobite rising of 1715 the clan's chief was captured at Preston. In the rising of 1745 the Farquharsons joined Prince Charles Edward Stuart; Francis Farquharson of Monaltrie led the clan's men, who fought at Culloden in 1746 as part of the Chattan regiment. "Colonel Anne" — Anne Farquharson, Lady Mackintosh — famously raised the Mackintoshes for the Prince while her husband held a government commission.

Invercauld and the modern clan

The Invercauld estate remained the clan centre, and the Farquharson chiefs were closely connected with the royal family's presence on Deeside from Queen Victoria's era onward. The clan's gathering at the Braemar Gathering highland games continues today. The present chiefly line descends through the Farquharsons of Invercauld; the clan's motto is Fide et fortitudine ("By fidelity and fortitude"), and its plant badge is Scots fir.
