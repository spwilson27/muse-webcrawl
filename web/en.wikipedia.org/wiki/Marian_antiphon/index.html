Marian antiphon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Marian antiphons are a group of Latin chants in honour of the Virgin Mary sung in the Roman Catholic Liturgy of the Hours, traditionally at the conclusion of Compline (Night Prayer). There are four seasonal antiphons, each assigned to a portion of the liturgical year.

The four antiphons

  • Alma Redemptoris Mater — from Advent until the Feast of the Presentation (2 February).
  • Ave Regina Caelorum — from 3 February until Holy Week.
  • Regina Caeli — during Eastertide, from Compline of Holy Saturday until Pentecost.
  • Salve Regina — from after Pentecost until Advent.

History and music

The antiphons date from the eleventh and twelfth centuries; the Salve Regina is traditionally attributed to Hermann of Reichenau. They have inspired countless polyphonic settings by composers from Dufay and Palestrina to Poulenc, and remain part of the chant repertoire preserved in the Liber Usualis and the post-conciliar Liturgia Horarum.
