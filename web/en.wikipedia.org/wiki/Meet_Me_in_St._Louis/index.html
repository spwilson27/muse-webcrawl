Meet Me in St. Louis is a 1944 American Technicolor musical film made by Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer and directed by Vincente Minnelli. Divided into seasonal vignettes, it tells the story of the Smith family in St. Louis, Missouri, in the year leading up to the Louisiana Purchase Exposition (the 1904 World's Fair).

Plot

The film follows the four Smith daughters, especially Esther (Judy Garland) and young Tootie (Margaret O'Brien), through four seasons of 1903–1904: summer parties, a Halloween adventure, and Christmas. When their father announces the family will move to New York, the household is thrown into distress — until he relents, and the family attends the opening of the World's Fair together.

Music

The score introduced songs that became standards: "The Trolley Song", "The Boy Next Door", and "Have Yourself a Merry Little Christmas", sung by Garland in a celebrated Christmas Eve scene. The title song dates from 1904. "The Trolley Song" was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major commercial and critical success, MGM's most profitable musical of the 1940s after Easter Parade. It is frequently ranked among the greatest film musicals and was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 1994. It was adapted into a Broadway stage musical in 1989.
