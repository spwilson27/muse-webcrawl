Random forest

A random forest is an ensemble machine-learning method for classification and regression, developed by Leo Breiman (building on work by Adele Cutler, Tin Kam Ho, and others). It operates by constructing many decision trees during training and outputting the mode of the classes (classification) or the mean prediction (regression) of the individual trees.

Algorithm

Each tree is grown on a bootstrap sample of the training data (bagging), and at each split only a random subset of features is considered. This randomness decorrelates the trees, so averaging them reduces variance and overfitting relative to a single deep decision tree. Out-of-bag samples provide an internal estimate of generalisation error, and the method yields measures of variable importance.

Applications

Random forests are widely used for tabular data across bioinformatics, remote sensing, finance, and ecology, and are valued for strong out-of-the-box performance with modest tuning. Related methods include extremely randomised trees (ExtraTrees) and gradient-boosted trees.
