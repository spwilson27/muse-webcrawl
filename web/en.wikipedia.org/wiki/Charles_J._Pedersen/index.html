Charles John Pedersen (October 3, 1904 – October 26, 1989) was an American organic chemist best known for discovering crown ethers, a class of cyclic polyethers that can selectively bind metal cations. He shared the 1987 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Donald J. Cram and Jean-Marie Lehn for the development and use of molecules with structure-specific interactions of high selectivity.

Early life

Pedersen was born in Busan, Korea, to a Norwegian father and a Japanese mother. He was educated in Japan and the United States, earning a degree in chemical engineering from the University of Dayton and a master's degree from MIT.

Career

Pedersen spent his career at DuPont. In 1967 he published his discovery of a method of synthesizing crown ethers, work that founded the field of supramolecular chemistry and changed how chemists think about molecular recognition.
