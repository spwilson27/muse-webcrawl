Distribution (marketing)

Distribution is one of the four elements of the marketing mix. Distribution is the process of making a product or service available for the consumer or business user who needs it, and it can be done directly by the producer or service provider, or by using indirect channels with distributors or intermediaries. The three other elements of the marketing mix are product, pricing, and promotion.

Distribution channels

A distribution channel is the path through which products and services travel from the vendor to the consumer. Channels may include wholesalers, retailers, agents, brokers, and the Internet. Direct distribution occurs when the producer sells directly to the end consumer (for example, through its own stores or website), while indirect distribution inserts one or more intermediaries between the producer and the consumer. The number of intermediaries is described as the length of the channel: a zero-level channel is direct, a one-level channel contains a single intermediary such as a retailer, and longer channels add wholesalers and agents.

Distribution strategies

Producers choose a distribution intensity according to the product and market:

  • Intensive distribution — stocking the product in as many outlets as possible, typical of convenience goods;
  • Selective distribution — using a limited number of intermediaries, common for shopping goods and branded products;
  • Exclusive distribution — granting a single distributor sole rights within a territory, common for luxury and specialty goods.

Channel management

Firms must select, motivate, and evaluate channel members and manage channel conflict, which can arise between intermediaries at the same level (horizontal conflict) or between different levels of the same channel (vertical conflict). Physical distribution and logistics — transportation, warehousing, inventory management, and order processing — support the marketing channel by ensuring products are available at the right place and time.
