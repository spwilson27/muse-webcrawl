Sagittaria latifolia

Sagittaria latifolia Willd.
Family: Alismataceae
Genus: Sagittaria
Common names: broadleaf arrowhead, duck-potato, wapato

Sagittaria latifolia is a plant found in shallow wetlands in much of North America, generally in the shallow waters of ponds, marshes and slow-moving streams. It is known variously as the broadleaf arrowhead, duck-potato, wapato, or (as Sagittaria esculenta) Chinese arrowhead.

Description

The plant produces distinctive arrow-shaped emergent leaves and white three-petalled flowers in summer. Below the water it forms starchy tubers (corms) that were an important traditional food for Indigenous peoples of the Americas, who harvested them by loosening them from the mud with their toes so the tubers floated to the surface. The tubers are also eaten by waterfowl, giving rise to the name "duck-potato".

Uses

The tubers can be eaten raw or cooked, and the plant is also grown as an ornamental in water gardens and as a marginal plant in ponds.
