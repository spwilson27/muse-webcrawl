Hakuin Ekaku

Hakuin Ekaku (白隠 慧鶴, January 19, 1686 – January 18, 1769) was one of the most influential figures in Japanese Rinzai Zen Buddhism. He is regarded as the reviver of the Rinzai school from a period of stagnation, refocusing it on rigorous koan practice, and all modern Rinzai lineages trace their transmission through him. He was also a prolific calligrapher and painter.

Life

Born in the village of Hara at the foot of Mount Fuji, Hakuin was ordained as a young teenager after a terrifying sermon on the hells drove him to seek liberation. He studied under several masters, experiencing his first great awakening while practising with the hermit Baō, and finally received transmission in the line of Shōju Rōnin. Settling at the small, dilapidated temple Shōin-ji in his home village in 1716, he spent five decades teaching monks and laypeople.

Teachings

Hakuin systematized koan training into a graded curriculum still used in Rinzai monasteries, insisting on post-enlightenment practice and on "Zen sickness" remedies drawn from Taoist breathing methods, which he described in Yasenkanna ("Idle Talk on a Night Boat"). He formulated the famous koan "You know the sound of two hands clapping; what is the sound of one hand?" He also emphasized extending Zen practice to laypeople and wrote in accessible Japanese, including the didactic Orategama.

Art

Hakuin's brushwork — bold portraits of Daruma, self-portraits, and calligraphy — was intended as a teaching device ("the Zen of the brush") and is now among the most celebrated of Zen art.
