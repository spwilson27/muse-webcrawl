Plasma

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Blood plasma is the liquid component of Blood, making up about 55% of its volume, in which red cells, white cells and platelets are suspended. It is a pale yellow fluid that is about 90–92% water, carrying dissolved proteins, salts, nutrients, hormones and waste products around the body.

Composition and functions

Plasma proteins include albumin (which maintains osmotic pressure and carries molecules), globulins (including antibodies of the immune system) and fibrinogen (essential for clotting). Plasma transports glucose, amino acids, lipids, electrolytes, dissolved gases and metabolic wastes such as urea to the organs that process them. When clotting factors are removed, the remaining fluid is called serum.

Medical use

Donated plasma is transfused to treat shock, burns and clotting disorders, and is fractionated into products such as immunoglobulins and clotting-factor concentrates. During the COVID-19 pandemic, convalescent plasma from recovered patients was also used experimentally as a treatment.
