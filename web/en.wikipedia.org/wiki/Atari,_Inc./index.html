Atari, Inc.

Atari, Inc. was an American video game developer and home computer company founded in 1972 by Nolan Bushnell and Ted Dabney in Sunnyvale, California. It is widely credited with founding the arcade video game industry with its first product, Pong (1972), one of the earliest arcade video games. Atari's home console, the Atari Video Computer System (later renamed the Atari 2600), popularised the use of microprocessor-based hardware and interchangeable game cartridges, and helped create the home video game market.

History

Founding and Pong

Bushnell and Dabney formed Atari in June 1972, taking the name from a term in the board game Go. Their first arcade game, Pong, designed by Allan Alcorn, was installed at a bar in Sunnyvale and proved so popular that the coin box overflowed. Pong became the first commercially successful video game and launched the arcade industry.

Home consoles and computers

After selling the company to Warner Communications in 1976, Atari launched the Video Computer System in 1977. Sales exploded after the release of a home version of Space Invaders in 1980. Atari also entered the home computer market with the Atari 400 and 800 in 1979, and later the ST line in 1985.

Crash and split

Atari was hit hard by the video game crash of 1983, posting massive losses tied to unsold inventory and high-profile failures. In July 1984 Warner sold Atari's consumer electronics and home computer divisions to Jack Tramiel, who formed Atari Corporation, while the arcade division continued as Atari Games. The original Atari, Inc. effectively ceased to exist as a single company.

Legacy

Atari is remembered as a pioneer of the video game industry and of Silicon Valley start-up culture — its informal workplace and creative engineering environment influenced later technology companies. The Atari brand has passed through several owners since and continues to be used for games and hardware.
