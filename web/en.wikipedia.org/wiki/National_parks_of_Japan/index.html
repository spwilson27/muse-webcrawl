National parks of Japan

The national parks of Japan (国立公園, Kokuritsu Kōen) are places of scenic beauty designated by the Ministry of the Environment under the Natural Parks Law. There are more than 30 national parks, covering about 5.8% of the country's land area. The system also includes quasi-national parks, designated by the ministry on the nomination of prefectural governments, and prefectural natural parks.

History

Japan's first national parks law was enacted in 1931, and the first parks — Setonaikai, Unzen, and Kirishima — were designated in 1934, followed shortly by Aso, Nikko, and others. The Natural Parks Law of 1957 replaced the earlier statute and established the framework still in use. New parks continue to be added, most recently in the 21st century, including parks recognizing the volcanic landscapes of Hokkaido and the subtropical islands of the southwest.

Notable parks

Among the best known are Fuji-Hakone-Izu, encompassing Mount Fuji and the Izu Peninsula; Shiretoko in Hokkaido, a UNESCO World Heritage Site noted for drift ice and brown bears; Yakushima, protecting ancient cedar forests; Kerama Shotō, designated in 2014 for its coral seas off Okinawa; and Akan Mashu and Daisetsuzan in Hokkaido. Ise-Shima National Park protects the area around the Ise Grand Shrine.

Management

Unlike the United States model, much land within Japanese national parks is privately owned, and protection is achieved through zoning that regulates development by degrees, from special protection zones to ordinary zones. Parks receive tens of millions of visitors annually and are central to domestic tourism and nature conservation.
