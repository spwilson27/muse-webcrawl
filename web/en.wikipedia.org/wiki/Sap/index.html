Sap

Sap is a fluid transported in the xylem and phloem tissues of a plant. Xylem sap consists mainly of water, mineral elements, and hormones moving from the roots to the leaves, while phloem sap is rich in sugars produced by photosynthesis and carries nutrients to growing and storage tissues.

Composition and transport

Phloem sap contains sucrose as its principal sugar, along with amino acids, hormones, and minerals. Xylem sap is much more dilute. In spring, before leaves emerge, the positive pressure that develops in the stems of some trees — notably maples — drives a conspicuous flow of xylem sap.

Uses

Humans harvest sap from several species. Maple sap is boiled down to make maple syrup; birch sap is drunk as a beverage in northern Europe and Asia; palm sap is fermented into palm wine or boiled into jaggery; and the milky latex sap of the rubber tree is the source of natural rubber. Pine resin, another plant exudate, is tapped for turpentine and rosin.
