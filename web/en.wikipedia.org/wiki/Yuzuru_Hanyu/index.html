Yuzuru Hanyu

Yuzuru Hanyu (born 7 December 1994) is a Japanese former competitive figure skater. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest figure skaters of all time, being a two-time Olympic champion (2014, 2018), a two-time World champion (2014, 2017), a four-time Grand Prix Final champion (2013–2016), a Four Continents champion (2020), the 2010 World Junior champion and the 2011 Japanese national champion six times over.

Career highlights

Born in Sendai, Hanyu was skating at the rink when the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake struck; his home and rink were damaged, and he trained in Yokohama and later Toronto under coach Brian Orser. He won his first Olympic title at Sochi 2014, becoming the first Asian men's singles skater to win Olympic gold, and defended it at Pyeongchang 2018 despite an ankle injury, becoming the first man since Dick Button (1952) to win consecutive Olympic titles. Hanyu was the first skater to land a quadruple loop in competition and set nineteen world records under the modern judging system, including the first score above 300 points in total. In 2020 he completed a "Super Slam" by winning all major senior and junior titles.

Professional career and honours

At the 2022 Beijing Olympics Hanyu attempted the first quadruple Axel in competition, finishing fourth, and in July 2022 announced his move to professional skating, headlining solo ice shows such as Prologue, Gift at Tokyo Dome, and the Re_Pray tour. He was awarded the People's Honour Award by the Japanese government in 2018, the first individual athlete and youngest recipient at the time. Hanyu is also known for his charity work for earthquake recovery in Tōhoku and for carrying a Winnie-the-Pooh tissue box that became a celebrated trademark — fans shower the ice with Pooh plush toys after his performances.
