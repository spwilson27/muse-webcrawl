Llyn Tegid

LocationGwynedd, Wales
Length3.7 miles (6 km)
Max depthc. 42 m
OutflowRiver Dee

Llyn Tegid, also known in English as Bala Lake, is the largest natural lake in Wales, situated at the town of Bala in Gwynedd, within Snowdonia (Eryri). It is about 3.7 miles long and up to a mile wide, formed in a glacial valley along the Bala fault.

Ecology

The lake is a Ramsar wetland and Site of Special Scientific Interest. It is the only home of the gwyniad, a freshwater whitefish (Coregonus lavaretus) unique to the lake, and also supports perch, pike, roach, and the rare glutinous snail in its catchment. Its level is managed to help control flooding on the River Dee and to supply water downstream.

Folklore and recreation

Welsh legend tells of a drowned palace beneath the waters and of Tegid Foel, husband of the witch Ceridwen in the tale of Taliesin. The lake is popular for sailing, windsurfing, kayaking, fishing and wild swimming, and the narrow-gauge Bala Lake Railway runs along its southern shore.
