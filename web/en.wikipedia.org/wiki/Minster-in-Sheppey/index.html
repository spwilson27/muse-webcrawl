Minster-in-Sheppey is a village and civil parish on the Isle of Sheppey in the Swale district of Kent, England. It lies on the north coast of the island, east of Sheerness, and is one of the oldest settlements on Sheppey. The parish includes the adjoining settlement of Minster-on-Sea.

History

The village takes its name from the minster church founded here in the seventh century. Around 664–670, Sexburga, the widowed queen of King Eorcenberht of Kent, founded a nunnery at Minster, one of the earliest religious houses in Anglo-Saxon England. The community was destroyed by Viking raids in the ninth century, but the church was later refounded as a Benedictine priory in the twelfth century by Archbishop William of Corbeil.

The surviving church of St Mary and St Sexburga is unusual in combining a parish church and a monastic church under one roof, giving it two naves. The prominent gatehouse of the abbey, built in the late medieval period, remains a landmark visible from the sea.

Geography

Minster sits on raised ground near the island's northern cliffs, which are famous among fossil collectors for Eocene fossils — including fossilised shark teeth and shells — eroded from the London Clay. The cliffs and foreshore form part of a Site of Special Scientific Interest. The village has grown in modern times with housing estates, caravan parks and holiday facilities serving the Sheppey coast.
