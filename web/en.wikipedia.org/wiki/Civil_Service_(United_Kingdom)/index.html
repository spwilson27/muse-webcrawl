Civil Service (United Kingdom)

His Majesty's Civil Service is the permanent bureaucracy of employees that supports the Government of the United Kingdom. Civil servants are politically impartial and appointed on merit, serving governments of any party.

HM Civil Service
Head: Cabinet Secretary
Employees: c. 500,000
Core values: Integrity, honesty, objectivity, impartiality
Key reform: Northcote–Trevelyan Report (1854)

History

The modern service stems from the Northcote–Trevelyan Report (1854), which recommended recruitment by examination and a unified service; the Civil Service Commission was created in 1855. The Fulton Report (1968) criticised the "generalist" tradition.

Structure

Civil servants work in ministerial departments, executive agencies (created under the 1988 Next Steps programme) and non-departmental public bodies. The most senior official is the Cabinet Secretary, also Head of the Civil Service. Devolved administrations in Scotland and Wales have their own arrangements within the Home Civil Service; Northern Ireland has a separate service.

Code and neutrality

The Civil Service Code, given statutory footing by the Constitutional Reform and Governance Act 2010, enshrines impartiality; special advisers are a limited exception as temporary political appointees.
