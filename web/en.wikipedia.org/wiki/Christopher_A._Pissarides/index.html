Sir Christopher Antoniou Pissarides (born 20 February 1948) is a Cypriot economist and Regius Professor of Economics at the London School of Economics. He specialises in the economics of labour markets, macroeconomic policy, economic growth and structural change. In 2010 he shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Peter Diamond and Dale Mortensen "for their analysis of markets with search frictions".

Early life and education

Pissarides was born in Nicosia, Cyprus. He studied economics at the University of Essex, receiving his BA in 1970 and MA in 1971, and completed his PhD in 1973 at the London School of Economics under Michio Morishima.

Career and research

Pissarides has spent most of his career at the LSE. With Mortensen he developed the matching function approach to unemployment, in which the number of hires depends on both vacancies and job seekers; the resulting equilibrium model of unemployment with frictions is known as the Diamond–Mortensen–Pissarides (DMP) model. His monograph Equilibrium Unemployment Theory (1990; 2nd ed. 2000) became the standard reference for the theory. He has also worked on job creation and destruction, labour market policy, and the effects of technology — including artificial intelligence and robotics — on work, with later research at the LSE's Centre for Economic Performance and the Institute for the Future of Work.

Honours

Pissarides is a Fellow of the British Academy and of the Econometric Society. He was knighted in 2013 for services to economics. In 2011 he became the first Cypriot to receive a Nobel Prize.

Selected publications
