Club Atlético River Plate, commonly known as River Plate, is an Argentine professional sports club based in the Belgrano neighbourhood of Buenos Aires. Founded on 25 May 1901, the club is best known for its professional football team, which competes in the Argentine Primera División and plays its home matches at the Estadio Monumental, the largest stadium in Argentina.

River Plate is one of the most successful clubs in Argentine football, having won a record 38 Primera División championships. The club has also won the Copa Libertadores four times, most recently in 2018, when it defeated its arch-rivals Boca Juniors in the famous final played at the Santiago Bernabéu Stadium in Madrid.

History

The club was founded in the La Boca district of Buenos Aires through the merger of two neighbourhood teams, Santa Rosa and La Rosales. The name "River Plate" is the English translation of Río de la Plata, reportedly taken from crates marked "River Plate" seen at the port. In the 1930s the club earned the nickname "Los Millonarios" (The Millionaires) after making expensive signings, including Bernabé Ferreyra, one of the most costly transfers in world football at the time.

River's legendary forward line of the 1940s, known as La Máquina (The Machine), is considered one of the greatest attacking units in football history. In 2011 the club suffered the only relegation in its history, returning to the top division a year later. Under coach Marcelo Gallardo from 2014 to 2022, River enjoyed one of the most successful periods in its history, winning two Copa Libertadores titles.

Rivalry

River's rivalry with Boca Juniors, the Superclásico, is one of the most famous derbies in world football. Both clubs originated in La Boca before River moved to the more affluent Núñez/Belgrano area, giving the rivalry a class dimension: River associated with the middle and upper classes, Boca with the working class.
