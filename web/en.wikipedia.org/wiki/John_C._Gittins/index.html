John C. Gittins

John Charles Gittins (born 1938) is a British statistician and emeritus professor at the University of Oxford, best known for devising the Gittins index, a celebrated solution to the multi-armed bandit problem in sequential decision-making.

Career

Gittins worked at the Admiralty Research Laboratory and later held academic posts at the University of Cambridge and the London School of Economics before moving to Oxford, where he became Professor of Statistics. His research spanned operations research, clinical trial design, and stochastic scheduling.

Gittins index

In work begun in the 1970s and published in his 1989 book Multi-armed Bandit Allocation Indices, Gittins showed that optimal allocation among competing stochastic "bandit" processes can be achieved by computing a dynamic allocation index for each arm separately and always playing the arm with the highest index. The result has applications in clinical trials, job scheduling, telecommunications, and adaptive experimentation. He was awarded the Rollo Davidson Prize in 1982 and the Guy Medal in Silver by the Royal Statistical Society.
