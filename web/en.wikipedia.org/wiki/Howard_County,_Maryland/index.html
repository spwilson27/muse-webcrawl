Howard County, Maryland

Howard County is a county in the U.S. state of Maryland, located between Baltimore and Washington, D.C. Its county seat is Ellicott City, and its most populous community is the planned city of Columbia. The population was about 334,000 at the 2020 census.

History

The county was created in 1851 from parts of Anne Arundel and Baltimore counties and named for John Eager Howard, a Revolutionary War officer and former governor of Maryland. Ellicott City grew around mills on the Patapsco River and an early station of the Baltimore and Ohio Railroad.

Columbia

In the 1960s developer James Rouse bought some 14,000 acres of farmland and built Columbia, one of the most successful planned communities in the United States, designed around village centres and a commitment to racial and economic integration.

Demographics and economy

Howard County is consistently ranked among the wealthiest and most highly educated counties in the United States, with a strong public school system, the Howard County Public School System. Major employers include health care, technology, and government contractors, and the county is home to Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory in Laurel.
