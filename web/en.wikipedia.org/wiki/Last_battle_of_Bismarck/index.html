Last battle of Bismarck

Last battle of the Bismarck

Date: 26–27 May 1941
Location: North Atlantic Ocean
Result: British victory; Bismarck sunk
Strength (British): 2 battleships, 2 heavy cruisers, destroyers and aircraft
Strength (German): Battleship Bismarck

The last battle of the German battleship Bismarck took place in the North Atlantic on 26–27 May 1941, at the climax of Operation Rheinübung. Crippled by a torpedo strike from a British carrier aircraft that jammed her rudders, Bismarck was brought to action and sunk by the battleships HMS King George V and HMS Rodney with supporting cruisers and destroyers. Of her crew of more than 2,200, only about 110–115 survived.

Background

Bismarck, with the heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen, had sailed into the Atlantic to raid Allied convoys. On 24 May, in the Battle of the Denmark Strait, she sank the battlecruiser HMS Hood — one of the Royal Navy's most famous ships — and damaged the battleship HMS Prince of Wales, but was herself hit and began losing fuel. The Admiralty committed overwhelming forces to hunt her down: "Sink the Bismarck". Prinz Eugen detached and escaped, while Bismarck headed for Brest in occupied France.

The torpedo attack

On 26 May a Catalina flying boat sighted Bismarck, and Swordfish torpedo bombers from HMS Ark Royal attacked in the evening. One torpedo struck aft and jammed both rudders 12 degrees to port, leaving the ship able to steam only in circles into the wind and sea. That night British destroyers kept contact and made repeated torpedo attacks.

The final action

On the morning of 27 May, King George V and Rodney opened fire, joined by the cruisers Norfolk and Dorsetshire. Within about half an hour Bismarck's main armament was silenced amid fires and heavy shell damage, though she remained afloat until, out of shells and fearing a U-boat, the British broke off and Dorsetshire delivered torpedoes. The wreck evidence and survivor accounts support that the crew also scuttled the ship; Bismarck sank at about 10:39–10:40. Only around 115 men were rescued (a few more by German vessels). The wreck was located in 1989 by Robert Ballard, about 4,800 metres down, some 650 km west of Brest.
