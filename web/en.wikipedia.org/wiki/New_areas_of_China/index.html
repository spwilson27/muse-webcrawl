New areas (simplified Chinese: 新区; pinyin: xīnqū) are special economic and administrative zones in the People's Republic of China, established by the State Council to pilot reform and drive development. The most famous is the Pudong New Area of Shanghai, created in 1990.

Background

Following the success of the special economic zones of the 1980s, China began designating 'new areas' as large urban development districts with preferential policies. Pudong, across the Huangpu River from old Shanghai, became a global financial centre symbolised by the Lujiazui skyline. Tianjin's Binhai New Area followed in the 1990s–2000s.

Expansion

A second wave of state-level new areas was approved from 2010, including Chongqing's Liangjiang, Zhejiang's Zhoushan Archipelago, Lanzhou, Nansha (Guangzhou), Xixian, and others, spreading the model to central and western China.

Xiong'an

Announced in April 2017, the Xiong'an New Area in Hebei is intended to relieve Beijing of non-capital functions and is among the highest-profile national projects, planned as a green, innovative model city. Critics of the new-area model have noted property speculation and uneven development among some zones.
