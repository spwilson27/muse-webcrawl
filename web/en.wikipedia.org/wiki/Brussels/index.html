Brussels is the capital of Belgium and its largest metropolitan area, and the de facto capital of the European Union: it hosts the European Commission, the Council of the European Union and (jointly with Strasbourg) the European Parliament, as well as NATO headquarters. The Brussels-Capital Region is one of Belgium's three regions, officially bilingual in French and Dutch, though French is the majority language. Historically Dutch-speaking, the city was progressively francised in the 19th and 20th centuries, remaining a Flemish enclave geographically inside Flanders.

History and sights

Brussels grew from a 10th-century fortified settlement on the Senne river into a prosperous cloth and tapestry town of the Duchy of Brabant, ruled in turn by Burgundy, Habsburg Spain and Austria. Its Grand-Place — rebuilt after French bombardment in 1695 — is a UNESCO-listed square of ornate guildhalls around the Gothic town hall. Belgium's independence in 1830 made Brussels a royal capital; King Leopold II embellished it with grand avenues and the Cinquantenaire arches, funded partly by the Congo colony whose brutal history Belgium has increasingly confronted. The Atomium, a 102-metre model of an iron crystal, was built for the Expo 58 world's fair. The Manneken Pis fountain, Belgian waffles, chocolate, beer and the comic-strip tradition (Tintin's creator Hergé was a Brussels native) complete the city's popular image. Brussels was the site of coordinated terrorist attacks in March 2016 that killed 32 people.
