Foam concrete

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Foam concrete, also known as cellular lightweight concrete, foamed concrete or reduced-density concrete, is a lightweight cement-based material produced by introducing a stable foam — generated from a foaming agent — into a cement paste or mortar, so that the hardened material contains a large volume of small, evenly distributed air bubbles. Typical densities range from about 300 to 1,800 kg/m³, compared with around 2,400 kg/m³ for ordinary concrete.

Properties and production

Foam is made either by pre-foaming (blending separately generated foam into the mix) or by mixed foaming (adding foaming agent to the mixer). Because it contains no coarse aggregate, foam concrete is highly flowable and self-levelling, needs no compaction, and offers good thermal insulation and fire resistance; its compressive strength is low to moderate and increases with density, so it is generally non-structural or semi-structural.

Applications

Foam concrete is used for floor screeds and roof insulation fills, lightweight blocks and panels, void filling (for example disused tanks, pipelines and mine workings), trench reinstatement, bridge approach fills and ground stabilisation where its low weight reduces settlement and lateral pressure.
