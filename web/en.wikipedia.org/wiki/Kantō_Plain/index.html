Kantō Plain

The Kantō Plain (関東平野, Kantō heiya) is the largest plain in Japan, covering an area of about 17,000 square kilometres in the Kantō region of Honshū. It spans parts of Tokyo, Saitama, Chiba, Kanagawa, Ibaraki, Tochigi and Gunma prefectures, and contains the Tokyo metropolitan area.

Geography

The plain consists of river terraces and alluvial lowlands built up by the Tone, Arakawa, Tama and Sagami river systems, surrounded on three sides by mountains and hills. Its surface is covered in many areas by Kantō Loam, a thick deposit of volcanic ash originating from Mount Fuji and other volcanoes. Most of the plain lies less than 200 metres above sea level.

Development

Historically the plain's uplands were dry-field farms and its lowlands marshy; large-scale river engineering under the Tokugawa shogunate, including the diversion of the Tone River eastward into the Pacific, converted much of it to productive paddy land. In the 20th century the plain was transformed by urbanisation and industrialisation, and it is now the demographic and economic core of Japan, holding more than a third of the national population.
