Heavenly Stems

The Heavenly Stems or Celestial Stems (Chinese: 天干; pinyin: tiāngān) are a Chinese system of ordinals that first appear during the Shang dynasty, c. 1250 BC, as the characters of the ten days of the week. They are used with the Earthly Branches in the sexagenary cycle, and in Chinese astronomy, calendrics, and divination.

Uses

The Shang kings were given posthumous names containing a stem, such as Da Ding and Wu Ding, apparently denoting the day of the ten-day week on which sacrifices to them were made. In combination with the twelve Earthly Branches, the stems form the 60-unit sexagenary cycle used to record years, months, days and hours; the cycle still names years in the Chinese calendar. In modern Chinese, the stems also serve as a native alphabetical ordering — like "A, B, C" — used in legal contracts (where 甲 and 乙 denote the two parties), school grades, and chemical nomenclature, where stems name organic compound series (e.g., 甲烷, methane).
