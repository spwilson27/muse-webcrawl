Benedict's reagent
Named after: Stanley Rossiter Benedict

Benedict's reagent (often called Benedict's qualitative solution or Benedict's solution) is a chemical reagent and complex mixture of sodium carbonate, sodium citrate, and copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate, used to test for reducing sugars.

Principle

Reducing sugars reduce blue copper(II) ions to copper(I) oxide, which precipitates as a green, yellow, orange or brick-red solid depending on sugar concentration when heated.

Use

The test was historically used to detect glucose in urine in diabetes monitoring, before enzyme-based test strips became standard. It also tests for aldehydes generally.
