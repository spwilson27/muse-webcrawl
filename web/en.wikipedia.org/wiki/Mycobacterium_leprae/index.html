Mycobacterium leprae is an intracellular, acid-fast bacterium that causes leprosy, also known as Hansen's disease. It was identified in 1873 by the Norwegian physician Gerhard Armauer Hansen, making it one of the first bacteria identified as a cause of human disease.

Biology

The bacterium has an exceptionally slow growth rate, dividing roughly every two weeks, and cannot be grown in standard laboratory media. It infects macrophages and Schwann cells, causing the nerve damage characteristic of leprosy. Its genome shows extensive gene decay, with many pseudogenes.

Transmission and treatment

Leprosy spreads mainly through prolonged close contact with untreated cases. It is curable with multidrug therapy combining dapsone, rifampicin, and clofazimine, distributed free by the World Health Organization since 1995. A related species, M. lepromatosis, was identified in 2008.
