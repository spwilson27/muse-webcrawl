History of ancient Israel and Judah

The history of ancient Israel and Judah concerns the Israelite kingdoms of the southern Levant in the Iron Age. According to the Hebrew Bible, a united monarchy under Saul, David, and Solomon split into the northern Kingdom of Israel and the southern Kingdom of Judah after Solomon's death, around the late 10th century BCE.

Kingdom of Israel

The northern kingdom, with capitals at Shechem, Tirzah, and finally Samaria under Omri, was the larger and wealthier of the two. It was conquered by the Neo-Assyrian Empire under Shalmaneser V and Sargon II, with Samaria falling in 722 BCE and much of its population deported.

Kingdom of Judah

Judah, centred on Jerusalem and its Temple, survived as an Assyrian, then Egyptian, then Babylonian vassal. Nebuchadnezzar II destroyed Jerusalem and the First Temple in 586 BCE and exiled much of the population to Babylon, an event of enduring religious significance.

Aftermath

After Cyrus of Persia conquered Babylon in 539 BCE, exiles were permitted to return and rebuild the Temple, inaugurating the Second Temple period. Archaeological evidence and inscriptions such as the Mesha Stele and Tel Dan Stele illuminate both kingdoms. See also Kingdom of Judah.
