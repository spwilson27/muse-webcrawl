Sleeper agent

A sleeper agent or sleeper is a spy or operative who is placed in a target country or organization, not to undertake an immediate mission, but to act as a potential asset if activated in the future. The agent builds an ordinary life under a "legend" (false identity), sometimes for decades, conducting no espionage until receiving a signal — at which point the sleeper may collect information, commit sabotage, or support other operations.

Characteristics

Sleepers may be "illegals" inserted with false documents or recruited locals asked to wait. Because they do nothing suspicious for long periods, they are exceptionally difficult for counterintelligence to detect. Their handlers maintain only sparse contact, sometimes through dead drops or coded broadcasts. A related concept is the sleeper cell, a group of operatives, especially terrorists, who likewise remain dormant until ordered to act.

Examples

The Russian "Illegals Program" uncovered by the FBI in 2010 involved ten long-term undercover agents living as ordinary American suburbanites; they were arrested and exchanged for prisoners in Russia. During the Cold War, both NATO and Warsaw Pact services were believed to maintain stay-behind networks and sleeper assets. The concept is a staple of espionage fiction, from The Manchurian Candidate to the television series The Americans, which dramatized Soviet illegals in the 1980s.
