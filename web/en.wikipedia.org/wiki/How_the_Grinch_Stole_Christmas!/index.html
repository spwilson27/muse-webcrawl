How the Grinch Stole Christmas! is a Christmas children's book written and illustrated by Theodor "Dr. Seuss" Geisel and published by Random House in 1957. Written in rhymed verse, the story criticizes the commercialization of Christmas and satirizes those who profit from exploiting the holiday. It is one of the most popular and enduring Christmas books in the United States.

Plot

The Grinch is a grouchy, solitary creature who lives in a cave on Mount Crumpit above the village of Whoville, home of the cheerful Whos. Annoyed by the Whos' festive celebrations, he decides to ruin Christmas by stealing all their presents, decorations, and food while dressed as Santa Claus, with his dog Max disguised as a reindeer. On Christmas morning he expects the Whos to weep, but instead they gather and sing joyfully, showing that the holiday does not depend on material things. Realizing that Christmas "means a little bit more," the Grinch's heart grows three sizes; he returns the stolen goods and joins the Whos' feast, carving the roast beast himself.

Reception and legacy

The book was praised on publication for its humour and its critique of materialism. The Grinch became one of Dr. Seuss's most famous creations, and the word "grinch" entered common usage for a person who spoils the pleasure of others, especially at Christmas. The story has been adapted many times, most notably the 1966 animated television special narrated by Boris Karloff, the 2000 live-action film directed by Ron Howard starring Jim Carrey (see How the Grinch Stole Christmas (film)), the 2018 animated film The Grinch, and a stage musical.
