Kendal Power Station

Placeholder image: Kendal Power Station, Mpumalanga.

Kendal Power Station is a coal-fired power station in Mpumalanga, South Africa, operated by the national utility Eskom. When fully commissioned it was among the largest coal-fired stations in the world, with an installed capacity of about 4,116 MW from six 686 MW units.

Construction and design

Construction began in 1982 and the six units were commissioned between 1988 and 1993. Kendal was the first power station in the world to employ indirect dry cooling on such a scale: its natural-draught dry cooling towers use air-cooled condensers, drastically reducing water consumption relative to conventional wet-cooled stations — an important consideration in water-stressed Mpumalanga.

Operations

The station burns coal supplied by a dedicated adjacent colliery via conveyor. Like much of the Eskom fleet, Kendal has faced maintenance challenges, boiler tube failures and periods of reduced availability contributing to national load shedding, alongside criticism of its emissions, including particulate pollution in the Highveld priority area.

Significance

Kendal's dry-cooling design served as a template for later South African stations such as Matimba and the newer Medupi and Kusile plants, which also adopted dry cooling.
