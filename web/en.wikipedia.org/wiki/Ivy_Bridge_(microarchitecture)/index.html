Ivy Bridge (microarchitecture)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Intel Core i7 processor based on Ivy Bridge

Ivy Bridge is the codename for Intel's 22 nm die shrink of the Sandy Bridge microarchitecture, released in April 2012 as the third generation of the Intel Core processor family. It was the first mass-market implementation of three-dimensional tri-gate (FinFET) transistors, which Intel claimed delivered improved performance at lower power compared with planar transistors.

Features

Ivy Bridge processors offered modest CPU performance gains (typically 5–15% at the same clock speed) over Sandy Bridge but substantially improved integrated graphics (HD Graphics 2500 and 4000), native USB 3.0 support in the accompanying chipsets, and PCI Express 3.0 support. The chips were socket-compatible with many Sandy Bridge (LGA 1155) motherboards after a BIOS update.

Reception and issues

Enthusiasts criticised Intel's use of thermal paste rather than solder between the die and heat spreader, which led to higher operating temperatures when overclocking despite the more efficient process—a practice that prompted the "delidding" modification trend.
