William Speirs Bruce

William Speirs Bruce
Born: 1 August 1867, London, England
Died: 28 October 1921 (aged 54), Edinburgh, Scotland
Occupation: Polar explorer, oceanographer

William Speirs Bruce FRSE (1 August 1867 – 28 October 1921) was a British naturalist, polar scientist, and oceanographer who organized and led the Scottish National Antarctic Expedition (1902–04) to the South Orkney Islands and the Weddell Sea. Among many other achievements, the expedition established the first permanent weather station in Antarctica, on Laurie Island, which has been operated continuously by Argentina since 1904.

Career

Bruce studied medicine at the University of Edinburgh but abandoned it for natural science, gaining experience on whaling and scientific voyages to the Antarctic and the Arctic. He turned down a place on Scott's Discovery expedition after disagreements, choosing instead to mount a Scottish expedition aboard the Scotia. Beyond Antarctica, Bruce made numerous voyages to Spitsbergen and other Arctic islands, founded the Scottish Oceanographical Laboratory in Edinburgh, and campaigned for the conservation of whales.

Legacy

Despite the scientific success of his expeditions, Bruce received less official recognition than contemporaries such as Scott and Shackleton, partly because of his advocacy of Scottish nationalism and friction with the London geographical establishment. He never received the Polar Medal. His work is commemorated in place names in Antarctica and the Arctic, and he is now regarded as one of the most significant figures in early Antarctic science.
