Nuyorican Movement

The Nuyorican Movement is a cultural, literary and intellectual movement centred on Puerto Ricans in New York City (and the wider Puerto Rican diaspora in the mainland United States). The word "Nuyorican" — a blend of "New York" and "Puerto Rican" — was once used pejoratively but was embraced by writers and artists from the 1960s and 1970s onward, who developed a distinctive literature in English, Spanish and "Spanglish" expressing the experience of migration, poverty, racism and bicultural identity.

Literature and poetry

Foundational figures include poets and playwrights such as Pedro Pietri (author of the celebrated poem "Puerto Rican Obituary", 1973), Miguel Algarín, Miguel Piñero (whose play Short Eyes won major awards in 1974), Tato Laviera, Sandra María Esteves, Nicholasa Mohr and Piri Thomas, whose memoir Down These Mean Streets (1967) was an important precursor. Nuyorican writing often combines oral performance, street language, music and political protest, and emerged alongside the activism of the Young Lords and the civil rights era.

Institutions and influence

The movement's best-known institution is the Nuyorican Poets Cafe, founded in 1973 by Algarín, Piñero and others on the Lower East Side (Loisaida) of Manhattan, which became a legendary venue for poetry readings and later for poetry slams, theatre, music and hip hop. The movement influenced Latino literature generally, performance poetry, and later writers such as Esmeralda Santiago and Junot Díaz. Critics and scholars now treat Nuyorican literature as a major strand of American letters, exploring themes of colonialism, diaspora, language and belonging between the island and the mainland.
