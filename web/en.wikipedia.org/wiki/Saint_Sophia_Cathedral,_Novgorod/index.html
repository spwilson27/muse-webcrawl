The Cathedral of Saint Sophia (the Holy Wisdom of God) in Veliky Novgorod is the oldest cathedral in Russia still in use, built between 1045 and 1050 under Prince Vladimir Yaroslavich. It stands in the Novgorod Kremlin (Detinets) on the Volkhov River and was the spiritual and political centre of the Novgorod Republic: "Where Sophia is, there is Novgorod", ran the saying, and citizens swore oaths and stored the city treasury in it.

Architecture

Built of rough stone and plinthos brick in a restrained Byzantine-derived style, the cathedral has five domes — the central gold (later gilded) one ringed by four lead-covered helmet-shaped domes — three apses and stone galleries. Its frescoes, mostly from the 12th century, include a famous image of Constantine and Helena. The west portal's bronze Magdeburg Gates, made in Germany in the 12th century and long said to be war booty from Sigtuna, are decorated with biblical scenes.

Treasures and history

The cathedral's iconostasis holds the revered icon of Our Lady of the Sign, credited with saving Novgorod from siege in 1170, and the Korsun Gates and relics of saints fill its chapels. The cathedral suffered under Ivan the Terrible's sack of Novgorod (1570), Swedish occupation, and the Second World War, but was restored and returned to the Russian Orthodox Church in 1991. A dove atop the central cross is a beloved symbol of the city.
