Hardware-in-the-loop simulation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) simulation is a technique used in the development and testing of complex real-time embedded systems. HIL simulation provides an effective platform by adding the complexity of the plant (the controlled process) in test and development by including the mathematical representation (model) of all related dynamic systems.

How it works

The real controller — for example an automotive engine control unit — is connected to a real-time simulator that emulates the sensors and actuators of the physical system it will control. The simulator runs a mathematical model of the plant in real time, exchanging electrical signals with the controller so that the controller "believes" it is operating the real plant. Faults and extreme conditions that would be dangerous or expensive to reproduce on real hardware can be tested safely and repeatably.

Applications

HIL testing is widely used in the automotive, aerospace and defense, power electronics, and industrial automation industries. It forms part of the broader model-based design workflow alongside model-in-the-loop and software-in-the-loop testing, and supports automated regression testing of controller software before prototypes are available.
