The Curtiss P-36 Hawk, also known as the Curtiss Hawk Model 75, was an American-designed and built fighter aircraft of the 1930s and 1940s. A contemporary of the Hawker Hurricane and the Messerschmitt Bf 109, it was one of the first of a new generation of combat aircraft — a sleek monoplane design in place of the old biplanes.

Design and development

Designed by Donovan Berlin, the Model 75 was an all-metal low-wing monoplane with retractable landing gear and an enclosed cockpit, powered by a radial engine. The United States Army Air Corps ordered 210 P-36s in 1937 — the largest US military aircraft order since the First World War. Re-engining the airframe with a liquid-cooled Allison V-1710 produced the famous P-40 Warhawk.

Operational history

Export Hawk 75s saw extensive combat. French Hawk 75s fought in the Battle of France in 1940 and were among the most successful French fighters of that campaign. The RAF operated the type as the Mohawk in Burma and India. Finland used its Hawks effectively in the Continuation War against the Soviet Union. P-36s at Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941 managed to get airborne and were credited with shooting down Japanese aircraft.

Operators

Users included the United States, France, the United Kingdom, Finland, the Netherlands East Indies, Norway, Thailand, China, Brazil, Argentina, and Peru.
