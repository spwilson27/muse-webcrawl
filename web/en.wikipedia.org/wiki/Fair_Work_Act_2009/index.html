The Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) is an Act of the Parliament of Australia, passed by the Rudd government, which established the current Australian industrial relations system. It commenced on 1 July 2009 and largely replaced the Howard government's WorkChoices legislation, which had been made under the Workplace Relations Act 1996. The Act is a central piece of Australian labour law and governs the rights and responsibilities of most Australian employers and employees.

Background

WorkChoices, introduced in 2005–06, had deregulated Australian workplace relations, expanding individual statutory agreements (Australian Workplace Agreements) and restricting union activity and unfair-dismissal protections. Opposition to WorkChoices was a major factor in the Howard government's defeat at the 2007 federal election, after which the Labor government of Kevin Rudd, with Julia Gillard as Minister for Employment and Workplace Relations, legislated the Fair Work Act as the centrepiece of its "Fair Work" system.

Key features

National Employment Standards

The Act establishes ten (later eleven) National Employment Standards (NES) that apply to all employees in the national system, covering maximum weekly hours, requests for flexible working arrangements, parental leave, annual leave, personal/carer's leave, compassionate leave, community service leave, long service leave, public holidays, notice of termination and redundancy pay, and provision of a Fair Work information statement. A casual employment information statement and rights concerning casual conversion were added later.

Modern awards and enterprise agreements

The Act provides for industry- and occupation-wide modern awards that set minimum terms alongside the NES, and for collectively bargained enterprise agreements, which must pass the "better off overall test" (BOOT) against the relevant award. Agreements are approved by the Fair Work Commission, established by the Act as Australia's national workplace relations tribunal (from 2013 renamed from Fair Work Australia).

Protections

The Act restored unfair dismissal protections for most employees, established general protections against adverse action for exercising workplace rights or engaging in industrial activity, prohibited sham contracting, and created the Fair Work Ombudsman as regulator and inspectorate. Minimum wages are set annually by an expert panel of the Fair Work Commission following an annual wage review.

Amendments

The Act has been amended many times. Notable reforms include the Albanese government's Fair Work Legislation Amendment (Secure Jobs, Better Pay) Act 2022, which expanded multi-employer bargaining and restricted fixed-term contracts, and the "Closing Loopholes" Acts of 2024, which introduced a right to disconnect, new definitions of employment, and criminal penalties for intentional wage underpayment.
