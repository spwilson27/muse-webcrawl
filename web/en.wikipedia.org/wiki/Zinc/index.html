Zinc is the chemical element with the symbol Zn and atomic number 30. It is a bluish-silver transition metal, moderately reactive, best known for protecting steel from rust by galvanisation and for making brass with copper.

Zinc is an essential trace nutrient — hundreds of enzymes, including those that read DNA, need zinc ions — and zinc oxide is used in sunscreens and ointments. It is the fourth most widely used metal, after iron, aluminium and copper.

[Image: Galvanised steel sheet with a spangled zinc crystal pattern]

History

Brass was made with zinc ores in antiquity, but the metal was first produced in India and China; Andreas Marggraf is credited with isolating pure zinc in 1746 in Berlin. The name may come from the German Zinke, a tooth or spike, describing its crystal shapes.

Uses

Roughly half of all zinc goes to galvanising. Zinc is also the negative electrode in alkaline and zinc–carbon batteries, and die-cast zinc alloys make detailed small parts.
