Eastern Macedonia and Thrace (Greek: Ανατολική Μακεδονία και Θράκη) is one of the thirteen administrative regions (peripheries) of Greece, occupying the north-eastern corner of the country. It comprises the eastern part of Greek Macedonia and the whole of Greek (Western) Thrace, together with the islands of Thasos and Samothrace. Its capital is Komotini, while the largest city and principal port is Kavala.

Geography

The region borders Bulgaria to the north — along the Rhodope Mountains — and Turkey to the east, where the Evros (Maritsa) River forms the frontier. Its landscape combines the Rhodope range, the plains of the Nestos and Evros rivers, and a coastline on the northern Aegean Sea. The Evros Delta and Dadia Forest are protected wetlands and raptor habitats of European importance, and the Nestos gorge and the Falakro mountain are notable natural landmarks.

Regional units and cities

Since the Kallikratis reform of 2011 the region has consisted of six regional units: Drama, Evros, Kavala, Rhodope, Thasos and Xanthi. Principal cities include Kavala, Alexandroupolis (a growing port and energy hub near the Turkish border), Drama, Xanthi, Komotini and Didymoteicho. The Democritus University of Thrace, founded in 1973, has campuses across the region's cities.

History and population

The area passed from Ottoman rule to Bulgaria and then to Greece during and after the Balkan Wars (1912–1913), and its modern population was largely shaped by the 1923 population exchange between Greece and Turkey, which settled Greek refugees from Anatolia and Eastern Thrace here. Uniquely in Greece, a recognised Muslim minority — Turkish-speakers, Pomaks and Roma — remained in Western Thrace under the Treaty of Lausanne, and the region today is one of the most ethnically mixed in the country.

Economy

Agriculture (tobacco around Xanthi and Drama, cotton, cereals and vineyards), marble quarrying in the Drama–Kavala area, food processing and tourism are the traditional mainstays. Alexandroupolis has gained strategic and commercial importance as a port and as a landing point for natural-gas infrastructure, while Kavala has oil and fertiliser installations and ferry links to the north Aegean islands.
