400 Degreez is the third studio album by American rapper Juvenile, released on November 3, 1998, by Cash Money Records and Universal Records. Produced entirely by Mannie Fresh, it is Juvenile's best-selling album and the album that brought New Orleans bounce-influenced Southern hip hop to a national mainstream audience.

Background and release

Juvenile (born Terius Gray) had built a regional following in New Orleans before signing with Cash Money Records. Following the regional success of his second album Solja Rags (1997), Cash Money secured a distribution deal with Universal, and 400 Degreez became the label's commercial breakthrough. The album's title refers to being "hot" — 400 degrees — a recurring theme in Juvenile's lyrics.

Singles

The album produced some of the defining singles of late-1990s hip hop. "Ha" was released as the lead single, followed by "Back That Azz Up" (also known in its edited form as "Back That Thang Up"), which became Juvenile's signature song and a top-20 hit on the Billboard Hot 100, featuring Mannie Fresh and Lil Wayne. A remix of "Ha" featuring Jay-Z also received significant airplay.

Reception and legacy

The album peaked at number 9 on the Billboard 200 and was certified 4× platinum by the RIAA, making it the best-selling album of Juvenile's career and one of Cash Money's landmark releases. Critics praised Mannie Fresh's production, which blended New Orleans bounce rhythms with polished mainstream hip hop, and the album is frequently cited as a cornerstone of Southern hip hop's takeover of the mainstream in the 2000s.
