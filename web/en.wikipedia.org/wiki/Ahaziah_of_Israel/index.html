Ahaziah (Hebrew: אֲחַזְיָה, ʾĂḥazyā, "Yah has grasped") was the eighth king of the northern Kingdom of Israel, the son of Ahab and Jezebel and a member of the House of Omri. According to the Hebrew Bible, he reigned for about two years, around 853–852 BC. He should not be confused with his nephew and contemporary Ahaziah of Judah.

Biblical account

The Books of Kings describe Ahaziah as continuing in the sins of his parents, serving Baal and provoking the God of Israel. During his reign Moab rebelled against Israel following Ahab's death (2 Kings 3:5).

Ahaziah fell through the lattice of his upper chamber in Samaria and was badly injured. He sent messengers to consult Beelzebub (Baal-zebub), the god of Ekron, about his recovery. The prophet Elijah intercepted the messengers with a rebuke — "Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are sending to inquire of Baal-zebub?" — and prophesied that the king would not leave his bed. When Ahaziah twice sent captains with fifty men to arrest Elijah, fire from heaven consumed them; a third captain pleaded for mercy and was spared. Elijah delivered the prophecy in person, and Ahaziah died, succeeded by his brother Jehoram, having no son (2 Kings 1).

Chronology

William F. Albright dated Ahaziah's reign to 850–849 BC, while Edwin R. Thiele proposed 853–852 BC; the brief reign is consistently given in the biblical text as two years.
