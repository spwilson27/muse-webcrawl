Fiat 127

ManufacturerFiat
Production1971–1983
ClassSupermini (B-segment)
LayoutFront-engine, front-wheel drive

The Fiat 127 is a supermini car produced by the Italian manufacturer Fiat from 1971 to 1983. It was introduced as a replacement for the Fiat 850 and is credited with popularizing the modern supermini format: a transverse front engine driving the front wheels, combined with a hatchback body.

Design

Launched initially as a two-door saloon, the 127 adopted the powertrain layout pioneered in the Autobianchi Primula and Fiat 128. In 1972 a three-door hatchback version followed, whose large rear opening and folding seat set the template for small hatchbacks across Europe. The car's 903 cc engine and light, spacious body made it economical and practical.

Reception and legacy

The 127 was named European Car of the Year in 1972 and became one of Fiat's best sellers, with millions built in Italy and under licence abroad, including as the SEAT 127 in Spain and derivations in Brazil and Yugoslavia. Its basic concept influenced a generation of small cars, and it was replaced in 1983 by the Fiat Uno.
