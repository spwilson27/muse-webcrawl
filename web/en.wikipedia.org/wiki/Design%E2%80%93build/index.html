Design–build

Design–build (or design/build, D–B) is a project delivery system used in the construction industry in which a single entity — the design–builder — contracts with the project owner to provide both design and construction services under one contract. It contrasts with the traditional design–bid–build method, in which the owner contracts separately with a designer and a contractor.

Characteristics

Because one organisation is responsible for both design and construction, design–build offers a single point of accountability, allows construction to overlap with design (fast-tracking), and can reduce disputes between the designer and the builder. Owners may select a design–builder on qualifications, on price, or on a combination ("best value"), sometimes through a competitive proposal process based on a preliminary design or performance specification.

Variations and criticism

Variants include design–build–operate, design–build–finance, and bridging, in which the owner's own designer develops the design to a partial stage before the design–builder takes over. Critics note that owners may lose the independent professional advice of an architect acting solely in their interest, and that design quality can be subordinated to construction cost. Even so, the method has grown to account for a large share of non-residential construction spending in the United States and is widely used for infrastructure and industrial facilities.
