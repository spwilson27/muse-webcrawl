The First Punic War (264–241 BC) was the first of three wars fought between the Roman Republic and Carthage for control of the western Mediterranean. For 23 years, in the longest continuous conflict and greatest naval war of the ancient world, the two powers struggled for supremacy. The war was fought primarily on the Mediterranean island of Sicily and its surrounding waters, and also in North Africa, Corsica and Sardinia. It ended with a Roman victory and the annexation of Sicily by Rome.

Background

In 264 BC Rome and Carthage were the two great powers of the western Mediterranean. Rome, having recently unified the Italian peninsula, intervened in Sicily when the Mamertines, mercenaries holding Messina, appealed for help against Syracuse and Carthage. The Roman decision to cross to Sicily brought the two empires into direct conflict over the island.

Course of the war

Rome rapidly captured much of Sicily, including Agrigentum in 262 BC, but Carthage held the strongly fortified western ports. Lacking a navy, Rome built a large fleet modelled on a captured Carthaginian warship and equipped it with the corvus, a boarding device that turned sea battles into infantry engagements. Rome won major naval victories at Mylae (260 BC) and Ecnomus (256 BC), then invaded North Africa under Marcus Atilius Regulus; after initial success Regulus was defeated and captured at Tunis in 255 BC.

The war settled into a grinding stalemate in western Sicily, where the Carthaginian general Hamilcar Barca waged an effective guerrilla campaign from Mount Eryx. Rome rebuilt its fleet with private funds and won a decisive victory at the Battle of the Aegates Islands on 10 March 241 BC, cutting off the Carthaginian garrisons in Sicily and forcing Carthage to sue for peace.

Aftermath

Under the Treaty of Lutatius, Carthage evacuated Sicily, which became Rome's first overseas province, and paid a large indemnity of 3,200 talents. Carthage's weakness during the subsequent Mercenary War allowed Rome to seize Sardinia and Corsica as well in 238 BC. The unresolved rivalry and Carthage's expansion in Spain under the Barcid family led to the Second Punic War in 218 BC.
