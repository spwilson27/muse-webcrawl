Advanced Power Management

Image placeholder: a laptop of the APM era

Advanced Power Management (APM) is an obsolete application programming interface developed by Intel and Microsoft and released in 1992 for managing power consumption in personal computers. APM placed control of hardware power states largely in the system BIOS, with the operating system requesting changes through a standard interface.

Overview

The APM specification defined device power states (such as on, standby, suspend and off) and allowed the BIOS to monitor inactivity — for example keyboard, mouse and disk idle time — and to power down devices or the whole system accordingly. The last revision, APM 1.2, was published in 1996.

Replacement by ACPI

APM's BIOS-centred design had significant drawbacks: the BIOS acted without the operating system's knowledge, which could cause lost data or inconsistent device states, and implementations varied widely between manufacturers. The Advanced Configuration and Power Interface (ACPI), introduced in 1996 by Intel, Microsoft and Toshiba, shifted control to the operating system and superseded APM. Microsoft dropped support for APM after Windows XP, and the Linux kernel removed its remaining APM support in later releases.
