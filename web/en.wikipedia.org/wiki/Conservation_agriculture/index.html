Conservation agriculture

Conservation agriculture (CA) is a farming system that promotes maintenance of a permanent soil cover, minimum soil disturbance, and diversification of plant species. It enhances biodiversity and natural biological processes above and below the ground surface, contributing to increased water and nutrient use efficiency and sustained crop production.

Three principles (FAO)

  1. Minimum mechanical soil disturbance — no-till or reduced tillage.
  2. Permanent soil organic cover — crop residues or cover crops protect the soil.
  3. Species diversification — rotations and associations including legumes.

Benefits and adoption

Promoted by the FAO, CA can reduce erosion, improve soil structure and water infiltration, lower fuel and labour costs, and sequester carbon. It is practised on well over 100 million hectares worldwide, notably in North and South America and Australia. Critics note dependence on herbicides for weed control in some no-till systems and challenges for smallholders in retaining residues also needed as livestock feed.
