Greater Basque Country

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Greater Basque Country (Basque: Euskal Herria; Spanish: País Vasco) is the name given to the home of the Basque people in the western Pyrenees, straddling the border between France and Spain on the Atlantic coast. It comprises the autonomous communities of the Basque Country and Navarre in Spain and the Northern Basque Country in France.

The concept of a single Basque homeland is expressed in the Basque-language term Euskal Herria, which literally means "the land of the Basque language" or "the Basque-speaking land". The term is formed from euskara (the Basque language) and herria (land, country, people).

Territories

The Greater Basque Country is traditionally divided into seven territories, known in Basque as the zazpiak bat ("the seven are one"):

  • In Spain (Hegoalde, the South): Álava (Araba), Biscay (Bizkaia), Gipuzkoa, and Navarre (Nafarroa).
  • In France (Iparralde, the North): Labourd (Lapurdi), Lower Navarre (Nafarroa Beherea), and Soule (Zuberoa).

Geography and population

The region covers roughly 20,000 square kilometres and has a population of around three million. The largest city is Bilbao, followed by Vitoria-Gasteiz, Pamplona, San Sebastián, Barakaldo, and Bayonne. The landscape ranges from the rugged western Pyrenees and the green Cantabrian coast to the drier Ebro valley in southern Navarre.

Political status

Since the Spanish transition to democracy, the territories south of the Pyrenees have been organised into two autonomous communities: the Basque Autonomous Community (Álava, Biscay and Gipuzkoa) and the Chartered Community of Navarre. The northern territories form part of the French department of Pyrénées-Atlantiques; in 2017 a single intercommunal authority, the Communauté d'agglomération du Pays Basque, was created for the French Basque Country.
