René-Théophile-Hyacinthe Laennec (17 February 1781 – 13 August 1826) was a French physician and musician who invented the stethoscope in 1816 while working at the Hôpital Necker in Paris. He pioneered its use in diagnosing various chest conditions, correlating auscultatory findings with post-mortem pathology.

Invention of the stethoscope

According to his own account, Laennec, embarrassed to place his ear directly on a young female patient's chest, rolled a sheet of paper into a tube and found that heart sounds were transmitted and amplified. He developed the instrument as a turned wooden monaural tube and coined the name from the Greek stethos (chest) and skopein (to examine).

Contributions

His 1819 work De l'auscultation médiate described the sounds of the chest and named conditions such as bronchiectasis, emphysema, and pneumothorax; he also described Laennec's cirrhosis of the liver. He died of tuberculosis at the age of 45.
