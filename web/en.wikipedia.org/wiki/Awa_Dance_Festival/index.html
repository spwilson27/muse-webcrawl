Awa Dance Festival

Awa Dance Festival (Awa Odori)
LocationTokushima, Tokushima Prefecture, Japan
Dates12–15 August (Obon season)
ParticipantsOver 100,000 dancers (ren); ~1.3 million visitors
Origin1587 (tradition), Bon Odori folk dance

The Awa Dance Festival (阿波踊り, Awa Odori) is part of the Obon festival in Tokushima Prefecture in Japan and is the largest dance festival in Japan, attracting over 1.3 million tourists every year. Groups of choreographed dancers and musicians called ren dance through the streets, typically accompanied by the shamisen lute, taiko drums, shinobue flute and the kane bell.

History

The dance is traditionally said to date to 1587, when Hachisuka Iemasa, lord of Tokushima, offered sake to townspeople celebrating the completion of Tokushima Castle, and the merrymakers' drunken dancing became the festival's seed; its roots lie in the older Obon dances honouring the spirits of the dead. The name "Awa Odori" itself was popularized in the early 20th century as tourism grew.

The dance

Spectators are often quoted the festival's famous phrase: "The dancers are fools, the watchers are fools; both are fools, so why not dance?" (Odoru ahō ni miru ahō, onaji ahō nara odoranya son son). Female dancers wear yukata and distinctive half-moon straw amigasa hats, dancing on the toes of geta sandals with arms raised; male dancers often wear shorter happi and dance with more vigorous, crouching movements.
