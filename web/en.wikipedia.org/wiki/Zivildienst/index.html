Zivildienst

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zivildienst (German for "civilian service") is a form of alternative civilian service available to conscientious objectors in countries such as Austria and Switzerland, in place of compulsory military service. In Austria, men who declare themselves unable to serve in the armed forces for reasons of conscience instead perform service in social institutions such as hospitals, nursing homes, emergency medical services, schools, and organizations assisting people with disabilities or refugees.

Austria

Austria's civilian service was established in 1975. The regular service lasts nine months, longer than the six months of basic military service. Civilian servicemen, known as Zivildiener, are assigned through recognized organizations; the Austrian Red Cross is the largest single sponsor. Because the service supports large parts of the social and emergency-care infrastructure, proposals to shorten or abolish conscription have repeatedly raised questions about replacing this workforce.

Switzerland

Switzerland introduced civilian service in 1996 for recognized conscientious objectors. It lasts 1.5 times as long as the refused military service and is performed in public-interest institutions in the social, health, environmental, and cultural sectors.
