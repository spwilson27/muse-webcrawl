Tannin (monster)

Tannin (Hebrew: תַּנִּין, tannīn; plural tanninim) is a term in the Hebrew Bible denoting a great sea creature or monster, sometimes rendered "dragon", "serpent", "whale", or "jackal" in translation depending on context. The tanninim are associated with the primordial waters of chaos.

Biblical usage

In Genesis 1:21, God creates "the great tanninim" on the fifth day — notably, they are ordinary creatures of God's making, not rival powers. Elsewhere tannin appears as an enemy subdued by God (Psalm 74, Isaiah 51, where it is linked with Rahab), as a creature praising God (Psalm 148), and in Ezekiel as a symbol of Pharaoh, the "great tannin" of the Nile.

Background

Scholars connect the imagery with the ancient Near Eastern chaos-combat myth, in which a storm god defeats a sea dragon — compare Ugaritic tnn and the dragon Lotan/Leviathan. In later rabbinic and modern Hebrew usage, tanin came to mean simply "crocodile".
