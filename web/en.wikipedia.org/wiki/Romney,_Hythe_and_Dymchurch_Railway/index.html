Romney, Hythe and Dymchurch Railway

Romney, Hythe and Dymchurch Railway
Opened: 16 July 1927
Length: 13.5 miles (21.7 km)
Gauge: 15 in (381 mm)
Locale: Kent, England

The Romney, Hythe and Dymchurch Railway (RH&DR) is a 15 in (381 mm) gauge light railway in Kent, England, operating steam and internal combustion locomotives. The line runs for about 13.5 miles (21.7 km) from Hythe via Dymchurch, St Mary's Bay, New Romney and Romney Sands to Dungeness, close to Dungeness nuclear power station and the Dungeness lighthouse.

History

The railway was the dream of millionaire racing driver Count Louis Zborowski and was built after his death by his friend Captain J. E. P. Howey. It opened in 1927 between Hythe and New Romney and was extended to Dungeness in 1928. During the Second World War the line was taken over by the military and operated an armoured train; it was also involved in the construction of the PLUTO pipeline. The railway claims to be the "world's smallest public railway".

Operation

The fleet consists largely of one-third-scale steam locomotives designed by Henry Greenly, with names such as Green Goddess, Typhoon, and Hurricane. The line runs a regular timetabled public service, carries schoolchildren under contract, and is a popular tourist attraction across Romney Marsh.
