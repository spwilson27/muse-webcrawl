Molecular physics

Molecular physics is the study of the physical properties of molecules, the chemical bonds between atoms and the molecular dynamics. It overlaps with atomic physics and theoretical chemistry, and it is concerned with the structure of molecules, their energy levels, and their interactions with radiation and with each other.

Scope

Molecular physics investigates the electronic structure of molecules and the motion of their nuclei. Molecular energy is conventionally separated into electronic, vibrational and rotational contributions, each with characteristic energy scales and associated spectroscopy: electronic transitions occur in the visible and ultraviolet, vibrations in the infrared, and rotations in the microwave region. The Born–Oppenheimer approximation, which treats nuclear motion as slow compared with electronic motion, underlies most calculations.

Methods

Key experimental techniques include rotational, vibrational and electronic spectroscopy, mass spectrometry, X-ray and electron diffraction for determining molecular structure, laser spectroscopy, and molecular beam experiments. Theoretically, molecular physics applies quantum mechanics — through molecular orbital theory, valence bond theory and quantum chemistry computation — to explain bonding, spectra and reaction dynamics.

Applications

The field underpins atmospheric and astrochemistry (identifying molecules in interstellar space by their spectra), the design of lasers and molecular electronics, plasma and combustion chemistry, and the study of cold and ultracold molecules for quantum simulation and precision measurement.
