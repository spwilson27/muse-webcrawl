Imereti

Imereti (Georgian: იმერეთი) is a region (mkhare) in western Georgia, situated along the middle and upper reaches of the Rioni River. Its capital and largest city is Kutaisi, historically one of the principal cities of Georgia. The region covers an area of about 6,680 square kilometres and had a population of roughly 480,000 in recent census figures.

History

In antiquity, eastern Imereti formed part of the kingdom of Colchis, while its later history was tied to the medieval Kingdom of Georgia. Following the fragmentation of the Georgian kingdom in the fifteenth century, the Kingdom of Imereti emerged as an independent state, frequently contending with Ottoman influence, until it was annexed by the Russian Empire in 1810.

Geography

The region ranges from the lowlands of the Rioni valley to foothills of the Greater and Lesser Caucasus. It is known for its humid subtropical climate, lush vegetation, and karst caves such as Prometheus Cave near Tskaltubo.

Culture

Imeretians speak a distinct dialect of Georgian and are known for traditions including polyphonic singing, and regional cuisine such as Imeretian khachapuri and pkhali.
