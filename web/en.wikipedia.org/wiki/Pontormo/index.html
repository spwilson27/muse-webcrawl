Pontormo

Jacopo Carucci (24 May 1494 – 2 January 1557), usually known as Jacopo da Pontormo or simply Pontormo, was an Italian Mannerist painter and portraitist from the Florentine School. His work departed from the calm perspectival regularity of High Renaissance art, favouring elongated figures, ambiguous space, and vivid, acidic colour.

Life and career

Born in Pontorme, near Empoli, Pontormo was apprenticed in Florence and came to the attention of the Medici. Vasari's Lives portrays him as a gifted but eccentric, solitary figure. His masterworks include the frescoes of the Visitation at Carmignano (1528–1530), the Vertumno e Pomona lunette at the Medici villa of Poggio a Caiano (1520–1521), and above all the altarpiece known as The Deposition (1525–1528) in the Capponi Chapel of Santa Felicita, Florence — a swirling composition with no cross or tomb visible, suffused with pastel pinks, blues, and greens.

Style and legacy

Pontormo's diary of his final years (1554–1556), recording his obsessive work on the (now destroyed) frescoes of San Lorenzo, survives as a remarkable document of artistic anxiety. His pupil Bronzino carried his elegant, cool manner into the Medici court portrait. Long overshadowed, Pontormo was revalued in the twentieth century as one of the most original painters of the Italian Renaissance.
