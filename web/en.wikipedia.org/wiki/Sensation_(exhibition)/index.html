Sensation (exhibition)

Sensation was an exhibition of the collection of contemporary art owned by Charles Saatchi, including many works by the Young British Artists (YBAs). It was mounted at the Royal Academy of Arts in London in 1997, then travelled to the Hamburger Bahnhof in Berlin (1998–99) and the Brooklyn Museum in New York (1999–2000). The show became notorious for the controversy aroused by works such as Marcus Harvey's portrait of Myra Hindley made from children's handprints, and Chris Ofili's The Holy Virgin Mary.

Contents

Among the 110 works by 42 artists were Damien Hirst's shark in formaldehyde, The Physical Impossibility of Death in the Mind of Someone Living; Tracey Emin's tent Everyone I Have Ever Slept With 1963–1995; Marc Quinn's frozen-blood self-portrait head Self; and Ron Mueck's hyperreal Dead Dad. In London the exhibition drew around 300,000 visitors, despite protests; three Royal Academicians resigned over it.

New York controversy

At the Brooklyn Museum the show provoked a celebrated confrontation: New York mayor Rudolph Giuliani denounced Ofili's The Holy Virgin Mary — a Black Madonna decorated with elephant dung and collaged images — as "sick" and anti-Catholic, and threatened to withdraw city funding from the museum. The museum sued on First Amendment grounds and won; a federal judge barred the city from cutting its subsidy. A visitor later smeared white paint over the Ofili painting, which was restored.

Legacy

Sensation cemented the YBAs' international fame and remains a landmark case in debates over public funding, freedom of expression and the marketing of "shock art".
