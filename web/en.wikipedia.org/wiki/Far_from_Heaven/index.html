Far from Heaven

Far from Heaven is a 2002 American drama film written and directed by Todd Haynes, starring Julianne Moore, Dennis Quaid, Dennis Haysbert and Patricia Clarkson. The film is a homage to the 1950s melodramas of Douglas Sirk, particularly All That Heaven Allows (1955), recreating their lush Technicolor style to tell a story about a suburban housewife whose seemingly perfect life unravels as she confronts her husband's homosexuality and her own attraction to her African American gardener.

Plot

Hartford, Connecticut, 1957. Cathy Whitaker (Moore) is the idealised wife of a successful television advertising executive, Frank (Quaid). After discovering Frank in a sexual encounter with a man, the couple attempt to save the marriage through conversion therapy, but Frank's struggle with his sexuality and drinking deepens. Meanwhile Cathy forms a bond with her gardener, Raymond Deagan (Haysbert), a widowed African American man. Their friendship provokes racist gossip and violence from the white community, and Raymond is ultimately forced to leave town for the safety of his daughter. Frank leaves Cathy for a man, and in the final scene Cathy bids Raymond a silent farewell at the train station.

Reception

The film was widely acclaimed for Edward Lachman's cinematography, Elmer Bernstein's score and Moore's performance, and it received four Academy Award nominations, including Best Actress, Best Original Screenplay, Best Cinematography and Best Original Score. Critics praised how Haynes used the language of 1950s melodrama to expose the era's repressions around race, gender and sexuality. It is frequently listed among the best films of the 2000s.
