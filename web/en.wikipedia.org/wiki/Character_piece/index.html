Character piece

A character piece is a short piece of music, usually for solo piano, which expresses a specific mood, scene or idea, often indicated by an evocative title. It was one of the characteristic genres of Romantic music in the 19th century, when thousands of such pieces were written for performance in the home and the salon as well as the concert hall. The German term is Charakterstück.

Characteristics

Unlike earlier keyboard pieces identified only by tempo or dance type, the character piece carries a descriptive or poetic title — a place, a person, a natural scene, an emotional state — which the music is meant to illustrate. Typical forms include the nocturne, the ballade, the impromptu, the song without words, the romance, and titled pieces such as "Träumerei" ("Dreaming") from Robert Schumann's Kinderszenen ("Scenes from Childhood"). Most are in simple ternary form and last only a few minutes.

History

Descriptive keyboard pieces had precedents in the programme pieces of François Couperin and in the sonatas of the early Romantics, but the character piece flourished from about 1830. Felix Mendelssohn's Songs Without Words (eight books, 1829–1845) established the lyrical piano miniature as a serious genre. Robert Schumann composed cycles of character pieces such as Papillons, Carnaval and Kinderszenen, linked by literary programmes. Frédéric Chopin raised forms such as the nocturne, mazurka, ballade and scherzo to large-scale character pieces.

Later in the century Franz Liszt, Johannes Brahms (in his late piano pieces, Opp. 116–119), Edvard Grieg (the ten books of Lyric Pieces), Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (The Seasons), and the Czech and Russian schools all contributed prolifically to the genre. The character piece also expanded beyond the piano: examples exist for orchestra and chamber ensembles, though the intimate piano original remained the norm.

Decline and legacy

By the early 20th century the genre receded as musical styles changed, but short titled pieces continued in the work of composers such as Debussy (Children's Corner, the Préludes) and Prokofiev (Visions fugitives). The character piece is often seen as an ancestor of later programmatic miniatures and of the mood-based cue in film music.
