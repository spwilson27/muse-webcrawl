Greenwashing

Greenwashing
Coined1986, by Jay Westerveld
TypeMisleading marketing / PR
Blend of"green" + "whitewashing"

Greenwashing (a compound of "green" and "whitewashing"), also called green sheen, is a form of marketing spin in which environmentally friendly imagery, language or claims are used deceptively to persuade the public that an organisation's products, aims or policies are more environmentally sound than they really are. The term was coined in 1986 by environmentalist Jay Westerveld, criticising hotels that promoted towel reuse as conservation while making little other environmental effort.

Techniques

Common greenwashing tactics include vague labels ("natural," "eco-friendly," "green") with no verifiable meaning; highlighting one small green attribute of an otherwise damaging product; nature imagery on packaging; irrelevant or long-expired certifications; hidden trade-offs; and publicising voluntary measures while lobbying against environmental regulation. The marketing consultancy TerraChoice catalogued these as the "seven sins of greenwashing." Carbon-neutral and net-zero claims based on dubious offsets have become a major focus of criticism.

Response

Regulators have increasingly acted against greenwashing: advertising standards bodies have banned misleading airline, oil-company and consumer-goods advertisements; the EU has adopted rules restricting generic environmental claims and offset-based "climate neutral" product labels; and securities and competition authorities in several countries have fined financial firms for overstating the sustainability of investment products. Surveys consistently show high consumer scepticism of corporate green claims.
