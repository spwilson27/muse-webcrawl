A goose (plural geese) is a bird of any of several waterfowl species in the family Anatidae. The true geese are the species of the genera Anser (the grey geese and white geese) and Branta (the black geese); the Cape Barren goose (Cereopsis novaehollandiae) is a related Australian species. Geese are larger than ducks and smaller than swans, with relatively long necks, and are known for their loud honking calls and strong migratory flights in V-shaped formations.

Species and distribution

Well-known species include the greylag goose and domestic goose (Anser anser and its domestic form), the Canada goose (Branta canadensis), the snow goose, the barnacle goose, the brant, and the Hawaiian goose or nene. Geese are found across the Northern Hemisphere and in Australia; many northern species migrate long distances between Arctic breeding grounds and temperate wintering areas.

Behaviour and diet

Geese are primarily herbivorous, grazing on grasses, sedges, and aquatic plants, and also eat grain and crops. They are strongly territorial during breeding, form long-term monogamous pair bonds, and both parents defend and tend the goslings. Their intelligence and loud alarm calls have made them traditional farmyard watch animals.

Domestication and human use

Geese were domesticated independently in Europe from the greylag goose and in Asia from the swan goose more than 3,000 years ago — they were among the earliest domesticated animals, depicted in ancient Egyptian art. They are raised for meat, eggs, feathers and down, and for foie gras. Geese feature prominently in folklore and idiom, from Aesop's goose that laid golden eggs to the "wild goose chase".
