Hallmark

Hallmark
Type: Assay mark on precious metal
Purpose: Certify purity and origin
UK authority: Assay offices (London, Birmingham, Sheffield, Edinburgh)

A hallmark is an official mark or series of marks struck on items made of precious metals — platinum, gold, palladium and silver — to certify their content and purity. The term derives from the marks applied at Goldsmiths' Hall in London.

History

Hallmarking is one of the oldest forms of consumer protection, dating in England to the 14th century. In the United Kingdom, items above a minimum weight must be assayed and hallmarked by an independent assay office before sale; a traditional UK hallmark includes a sponsor's mark, a standard (fineness) mark, and an assay office mark, with optional date and traditional symbols.

Modern practice

Many countries operate hallmarking regimes, and the 1972 Vienna Convention established a Common Control Mark allowing mutual recognition among member states. The word is also used figuratively — a distinguishing characteristic — and is associated in North America with the greeting-card company Hallmark Cards.
