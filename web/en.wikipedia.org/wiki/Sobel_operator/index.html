Sobel operator

The Sobel operator highlights regions of high spatial gradient, corresponding to edges.

The Sobel operator, sometimes called the Sobel–Feldman operator or Sobel filter, is used in image processing and computer vision, particularly in edge detection algorithms, where it creates an image emphasising edges. It was presented by Irwin Sobel and Gary Feldman at the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory in 1968.

Formulation

The operator consists of a pair of 3×3 convolution kernels. One kernel estimates the gradient in the horizontal (x) direction and the other in the vertical (y) direction:

Gx = [-1 0 +1; -2 0 +2; -1 0 +1] * A
Gy = [+1 +2 +1; 0 0 0; -1 -2 -1] * A

Here A is the source image. The kernels can be understood as combining a smoothing (averaging) operation in one direction with a derivative operation in the perpendicular direction, which gives the Sobel operator some resistance to noise. The gradient magnitude at each point is commonly computed as the square root of the sum of the squares of the two components, and the gradient direction is given by the arctangent of their ratio.

Properties and extensions

Because the kernels are separable, the computation can be performed efficiently. The operator is simple and fast, making it popular for real-time applications, though more modern methods such as the Canny edge detector offer better localisation and noise performance. The idea extends to higher dimensions; 3D Sobel operators are used in volumetric data. The Scharr operator is a related filter with improved rotational symmetry.
