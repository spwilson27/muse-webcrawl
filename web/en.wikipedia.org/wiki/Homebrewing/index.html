Homebrewing

Homebrewing is the brewing of beer, mead, cider and other fermented drinks on a small scale for personal, non-commercial purposes. Practised for millennia in the home before brewing industrialised, it revived as a hobby in the late twentieth century and has strongly influenced the modern craft beer movement; many professional brewers began as homebrewers.

Process

Beer homebrewing generally follows the commercial process at small scale. Beginners often start with malt-extract kits; more advanced brewers use all-grain methods: mashing crushed malted barley (and other grains) in hot water to produce wort, boiling it with hops, cooling, then fermenting with yeast in a fermenter for one to several weeks. The beer is then bottled with a small addition of sugar for carbonation, or kegged. Typical homebrew batches are around 19–23 litres (5 US gallons). Careful sanitation is emphasised, since contamination by wild yeast or bacteria is the most common cause of failure.

Legality and history

Home production of wine and beer for personal use is legal in many countries, usually with quantity limits and a ban on sale. In the United States, federal law prohibited homebrewing even after the repeal of Prohibition until 1978, when President Jimmy Carter signed legislation legalising it (effective 1979); state-level bans persisted in places, with Mississippi and Alabama the last to legalise in 2013. In the United Kingdom, a licence requirement dating to the nineteenth century was abolished in 1963, sparking a homebrew boom. Organisations such as the American Homebrewers Association (founded 1978 by Charlie Papazian) and magazines and competitions helped standardise techniques; Papazian's slogan "Relax, don't worry, have a homebrew" became emblematic of the hobby.

Related hobbies

Home winemaking, mead-making, cider-making and home distillation (often more tightly regulated) are closely related activities, as are home production of kombucha and other fermented foods.
