Dao

The Dao (Chinese: 道; pinyin: dào; Wade–Giles: tao), literally "way", "path" or "road", is a central concept in Chinese philosophy, above all in Daoism, denoting the fundamental, ineffable principle that is the source and pattern of the universe.

Meaning

The Daodejing attributed to Laozi opens with the famous paradox: "The dao that can be spoken is not the eternal Dao; the name that can be named is not the eternal name." The Dao is simultaneously the way things are and the way humans should align themselves with the natural order. The graph 道 combines the radicals for "movement" and "head", suggesting both a path and a guiding principle; in ordinary usage it also means to speak.

In Daoism

For Laozi and Zhuangzi, the ideal is wu wei — effortless action in accordance with the Dao — rather than striving against the current of things. The Dao gives birth to the One, the One to the Two (yin and yang), the Two to the Three, and the Three to the "ten thousand things". Religious Daoism later elaborated the Dao into cosmologies, pantheons and practices aimed at longevity and immortality.

Wider usage

The word spread through East Asia: Japanese dō appears in budō arts such as judō and aikidō, and Korean do in taekwondo — each "the way" of a discipline. Confucianism also speaks of the Dao as the proper moral way of humans and rulers, though with a more social emphasis. In modern Chinese, dao compounds denote roads, doctrines and methods.
