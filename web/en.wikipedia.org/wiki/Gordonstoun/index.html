Gordonstoun School is a co-educational independent school for boarding and day pupils in Moray, Scotland. It is named after the 150-acre estate owned by Sir Robert Gordon in the 17th century; the school now uses this estate as its campus. It is one of the last remaining full boarding schools in the United Kingdom and a member of Round Square, a network inspired by its founder, Kurt Hahn.

History

Gordonstoun was founded in 1934 by Kurt Hahn, a German Jewish educator who fled Nazi Germany. Hahn's educational philosophy emphasised character development, physical challenge, service to others, and international understanding. The school's motto is Plus est en vous ("There is more in you"). Hahn also founded the Outward Bound movement and influenced the creation of the Duke of Edinburgh's Award.

Royal connections

Several members of the British royal family have been educated at Gordonstoun, including Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, and Charles III, who attended in the 1960s. The school's seamanship programme, in which pupils serve on the school's sail training vessel, remains a distinctive feature of its curriculum.

Curriculum

Alongside academic study leading to GCSEs and A-levels (and the Scottish curriculum in earlier years), pupils participate in outdoor education in the Cairngorms, seamanship on the Moray Firth, and community service, including the school's own fire, coastguard, and mountain rescue services.
