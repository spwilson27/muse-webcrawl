The history of Greece stretches from the Bronze Age Aegean civilisations through classical antiquity, the Byzantine Empire, Ottoman rule, and the modern Greek state established after the War of Independence.

Bronze Age

The Cycladic, Minoan (Crete) and Mycenaean civilisations flourished in the 3rd and 2nd millennia BCE. The Mycenaean collapse around 1100 BCE ushered in a Greek Dark Age.

Classical Greece

The city-states (poleis) such as Athens and Sparta rose in the Archaic period. Athens developed democracy in the 5th century BCE and, with Sparta, repelled the Persian invasions at Marathon (490 BCE) and Salamis (480 BCE). The Peloponnesian War (431–404 BCE) weakened the poleis, enabling Macedonian domination under Philip II and Alexander the Great, whose conquests spread Hellenistic culture across the Near East.

Roman and Byzantine periods

Rome annexed Greece by 146 BCE. After the empire's division, Greece formed part of the Byzantine Empire, centred on Constantinople, until the Ottoman conquest culminating in 1453.

Ottoman rule and independence

Ottoman rule lasted nearly four centuries. The Greek War of Independence began in 1821 and, with British, French and Russian support, led to recognition of the Kingdom of Greece in 1830.

Modern Greece

The 20th century saw the Balkan Wars, the Greco-Turkish War (1919–1922) and population exchange, occupation and civil war in the 1940s, a military junta (1967–1974), restoration of democracy, and accession to the European Community in 1981.
