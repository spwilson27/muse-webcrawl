Arterial blood gas test

Arterial blood gas test
SynonymsABG, arterial blood gas analysis
PurposeMeasure blood gas tensions, pH, bicarbonate and oxygen saturation
SampleArterial blood (radial, brachial or femoral artery)

An arterial blood gas test (ABG) is a blood test performed using blood from an artery, most commonly the radial artery. It measures the partial pressures of oxygen (PaO2) and carbon dioxide (PaCO2), blood pH, and the concentrations of bicarbonate (HCO3−) and base excess, together with oxygen saturation (SaO2). The test is a cornerstone of assessment in respiratory failure, acid–base disorders, critical care and anaesthesia.

Procedure

Blood is drawn anaerobically into a heparinised syringe, usually from the radial artery after an Allen test to confirm collateral circulation; the brachial and femoral arteries are alternatives, and an indwelling arterial line may be used in critically ill patients. Air bubbles are expelled, the sample is mixed and analysed promptly, traditionally on ice, in a blood gas analyser that measures pH and gas tensions directly with electrodes and calculates bicarbonate and base excess. Many analysers also report electrolytes, glucose, lactate and haemoglobin derivatives.

Interpretation

Interpretation proceeds stepwise: oxygenation is assessed from PaO2 and the alveolar–arterial gradient; pH determines whether the patient is acidaemic or alkalaemic; PaCO2 identifies a respiratory component and bicarbonate a metabolic one. Disorders are classified as respiratory or metabolic acidosis or alkalosis, and the adequacy of compensation is checked against expected values. The anion gap and lactate help identify causes of metabolic acidosis such as diabetic ketoacidosis, renal failure and sepsis.

Alternatives

Venous blood gases correlate reasonably with arterial values for pH, bicarbonate and lactate and are less painful, though they cannot reliably assess oxygenation. Capillary ("arterialised") samples are used in neonates and children. Pulse oximetry provides non-invasive oxygen saturation but no information about ventilation or acid–base status.
