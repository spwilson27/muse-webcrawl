Environmentalism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Environmentalism (or environmental rights) is a broad philosophy, ideology and social movement about supporting life, habitats and surroundings, centred on concern for the natural environment and its protection from human-caused harm. It ranges from conservation and pollution control to the political programme of green politics — ecological wisdom, social justice, grassroots democracy and nonviolence, as in the Global Greens Charter.

History

Roots lie in 19th-century conservation and preservation movements (national parks, Sierra Club 1892) and public-health reform. Modern environmentalism is dated from Rachel Carson's Silent Spring (1962) on pesticide harms, the first Earth Day (1970), landmark legislation of the 1970s (clean air and water acts, endangered-species protection), and the institutionalisation of environmental ministries and agencies. From the late 1980s, climate change became the dominant frame: IPCC assessments, the UN Framework Convention (1992), Kyoto Protocol (1997) and Paris Agreement (2015), alongside biodiversity (Convention on Biological Diversity 1992) and chemicals regimes.

Currents and debates

Currents include mainstream reformist NGOs, deep ecology, eco-socialism, ecomodernism (technology-led decoupling), environmental justice (highlighting race and class in exposure to pollution), and degrowth. Green parties entered many parliaments from the 1980s (Germany's Die Grünen the prototype) and some governments. Contentious issues include nuclear power, GMOs, carbon pricing vs. regulation, and the pace and distributive fairness ("just transition") of decarbonisation.
