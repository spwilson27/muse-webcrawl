La Tour d'Auvergne

The House of La Tour d'Auvergne was an important French noble family originating in the Auvergne. Descended from the counts of Auvergne in the Middle Ages, the family rose through the viscounty of Turenne, which it acquired by marriage in the 15th century, and became one of the principal princely houses of early modern France.

History

Bertrand VI de La Tour married Louise de La Trémoille and their son Bertrand VII acquired the viscounty of Turenne in 1444 by marrying the heiress. In 1551 Henri de La Tour d'Auvergne married Charlotte de La Marck, heiress to the principality of Sedan and the duchy of Bouillon, which became the family's prized sovereign enclaves on France's eastern frontier.

Notable members

The family's most famous members include Henri de La Tour d'Auvergne, Viscount of Turenne (1611–1675), Marshal of France and one of the greatest commanders of the 17th century; his brother Frédéric Maurice, duc de Bouillon; Cardinal Emmanuel Théodose de La Tour d'Auvergne; and the soldier-antiquarian Théophile Malo Corret de La Tour d'Auvergne (1743–1800), named "First Grenadier of France" by Napoleon and killed at Oberhausen. The family's possessions at Sedan and Bouillon passed to the French crown in the 17th century by exchange, and its Turenne branch ended in the 18th century.
