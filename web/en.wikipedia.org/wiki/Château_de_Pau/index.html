The Château de Pau, birthplace of Henry IV.

The Château de Pau is a castle in the centre of the city of Pau, capital of Béarn in the Pyrénées-Atlantiques department of south-western France. It dominates the old town from a rocky outcrop above the Gave de Pau, facing the Pyrenees.

History

The castle began as a fortified post of the viscounts of Béarn in the 11th–12th centuries, named for the palisade (pau in Béarnais) around it — also the origin of the city's name. Gaston Fébus, Count of Foix and Viscount of Béarn, greatly enlarged it in the 14th century, building the great brick keep. Under the Albret kings of Navarre it became a Renaissance residence; Marguerite d'Angoulême held court there, and on December 13, 1553 Henry IV of France was born in the castle — legend holds that his lips were rubbed with garlic and Jurançon wine, and that he was cradled in a tortoise shell preserved there to this day.

Museum

Restored under Louis-Philippe and Napoleon III, the château now houses the Musée national du Château de Pau, devoted to Henry IV, with notable collections of tapestries, furniture and Béarnais history. The Swedish royal house of Bernadotte also traces its origins to Pau, the birthplace of Jean Bernadotte, later Charles XIV John of Sweden.
