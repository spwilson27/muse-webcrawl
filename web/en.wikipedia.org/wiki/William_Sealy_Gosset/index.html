William Sealy Gosset (13 June 1876 – 16 October 1937) was an English statistician, chemist and brewer famous for his work under the pseudonym "Student". He developed Student's t-distribution and the associated Student's t-test, foundational tools of modern statistical inference.

Early life and Guinness

Gosset was born in Canterbury, Kent, and educated at Winchester College and New College, Oxford, where he took degrees in chemistry and mathematics. In 1899 he joined the Guinness brewery at St. James's Gate, Dublin, where he worked for the rest of his life, rising to Head Brewer.

Statistical work

At Guinness, Gosset needed methods for quality control based on very small samples — of barley, hops and stout. Because the company forbade employees from publishing (to protect trade secrets), he published his results anonymously as "Student". His 1908 paper "The Probable Error of a Mean" in Biometrika introduced the t-distribution. He studied statistics under Karl Pearson in London in 1906–07 and later collaborated with Ronald A. Fisher, who gave the t-test its modern form and the name "Student's distribution".

Later life

Gosset applied statistics to agricultural trials and brewing science throughout his career, and in 1935 moved to London as Head Brewer of the new Guinness Park Royal brewery. He died in Beaconsfield, Buckinghamshire, on 16 October 1937.
