Yule log

The Yule log, Yule clog, or Christmas block is a specially selected log burnt on a hearth as a Christmas tradition in regions of Europe, particularly England, France and parts of Germany and the Balkans. The custom derives from the earlier midwinter festival of Yule and was widely recorded by folklorists of the 19th century; by extension, "Yule log" also names a chocolate cake (the French bûche de Noël) made to resemble the log.

Origins

The Yule log is commonly held to originate in Germanic pagan midwinter celebrations, though firm medieval documentation is scarce and the custom is first clearly recorded in early modern England and Germany. The English antiquarian John Aubrey and later writers describe the log being hauled in with ceremony on Christmas Eve, lit with a fragment of the previous year's charred remnant, and kept burning through the Twelve Days of Christmas.

Customs

Traditions varied by region. The log was often the largest the hearth could hold, sometimes a whole tree root, and was not to be bought but taken from one's own land or received as a gift. Keeping the fire alight was considered lucky; allowing it to go out was an ill omen. The ashes and the saved charred piece were believed to protect the house against fire and lightning and were sometimes placed under the bed or in the fields. In France the chalendal or carignon could burn from Christmas to New Year, and in Serbia and other Balkan countries the badnjak — an oak branch or log — is still ceremonially burned on Christmas Eve.

The cake

As domestic hearths shrank in the 19th century, French pâtissiers popularised the bûche de Noël, a sponge roll filled with buttercream and decorated to look like a bark-covered log. It remains a staple Christmas dessert in France, Belgium, Switzerland, Quebec and other francophone regions.

Modern media

In 1966 the New York television station WPIX broadcast a film of a crackling fireplace — the original televised "Yule Log" — a tradition later imitated widely and adapted into streaming loops and ambient videos.
