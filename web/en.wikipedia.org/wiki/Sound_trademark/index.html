Sound trademark

A sound trademark, also called a sound mark, is a trademark consisting of a distinctive sound or short musical jingle that identifies the source of goods or services. Familiar examples include the NBC chimes, the Intel five-note bong, the roar of the MGM lion, and Netflix's "ta-dum". Sound marks form part of the broader category of non-traditional trademarks alongside colours, scents, and shapes.

Registration requirements

To be registrable, a sound must be distinctive and capable of distinguishing one undertaking's goods or services from another's. Functional sounds, such as the ordinary noise a product makes, generally cannot be monopolised. Historically, applicants had to represent sounds graphically, usually with musical notation; many jurisdictions now accept audio files with the application.

By jurisdiction

In the United States, sound marks have been registered since the NBC chimes in 1950. The European Union reformed its rules in 2017 to permit filing in audio format. Other countries, including India, Japan, and Australia, also recognise sound trademarks, though standards of distinctiveness vary.
