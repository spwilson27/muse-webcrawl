Television station

A television station is a set of equipment managed by a business, organisation, or other entity, such as an amateur television (ATV) operator, that transmits video and audio content via radio waves directly from a transmitter on the Earth's surface to any number of tuned receivers within a signal footprint. Television stations are licensed by national regulators — such as the Federal Communications Commission in the United States, Ofcom in the United Kingdom, or the CRTC in Canada — to broadcast on a specific frequency channel within a defined area.

Organisation

Stations may be owned and operated by a television network or may be independently owned affiliates carrying network programming under contract. A network's flagship and owned-and-operated stations ("O&Os") produce or distribute the network feed; affiliates supplement it with local news, weather, sports, and syndicated programmes. In many countries broadcasting is dominated by national networks whose output is distributed to regional stations and transmitters; public service broadcasters such as the BBC, NHK, and PBS member stations follow variations of this model.

Facilities and transmission

A typical station comprises studios and a master control room, editing and newsroom facilities, and a transmitter site, often on a tower or mountaintop some distance away, connected by a studio–transmitter link. In the digital era, one channel allocation can carry several programme streams ("multicasting" or subchannels), and stations increasingly share transmission facilities. Stations identify themselves with call signs (in the Americas and parts of Asia) or network branding and channel numbers, and generate revenue mainly from advertising, carriage fees, or public funding.

History

Experimental stations operated in the late 1920s and 1930s; regular commercial broadcasting began around 1939–1941 in the United States and after the Second World War elsewhere. The number of stations grew rapidly in the 1950s with the assignment of VHF and later UHF channels. Cable and satellite distribution from the 1970s onward multiplied the number of channels, and digital television transitions in the 2000s and 2010s reshaped station operations worldwide.
