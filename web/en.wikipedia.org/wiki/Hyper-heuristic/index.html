Hyper-heuristic

A hyper-heuristic is a search method or learning mechanism for selecting or generating heuristics to solve computational search problems. Whereas a metaheuristic searches the space of solutions directly for a particular problem, a hyper-heuristic operates one level higher: it searches the space of heuristics, choosing or combining simple low-level heuristics appropriate to the problem instance at hand.

Motivation and framework

The goal is to raise the level of generality of automated problem solving — to build methods that work well across many instances or even problem domains, rather than one heavily tuned algorithm. In the standard framework, the hyper-heuristic interacts with the problem domain only through a set of low-level heuristics and simple feedback (such as the quality of the current solution), separated by a "domain barrier" that hides problem-specific information. Hyper-heuristics are classified as selection hyper-heuristics (choosing from existing heuristics, using reinforcement learning or choice functions) or generation hyper-heuristics (creating new heuristics from components, often by genetic programming).

Applications

Hyper-heuristics have been applied to timetabling, vehicle routing, packing, scheduling, and satisfiability. The Cross-domain Heuristic Search Challenge (CHeSC 2011) benchmarked hyper-heuristics across multiple domains, and the HyFlex software framework was developed to support such research.
