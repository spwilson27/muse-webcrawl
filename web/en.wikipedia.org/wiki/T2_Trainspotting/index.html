T2 Trainspotting is a 2017 British black comedy-drama film directed by Danny Boyle and written by John Hodge. It is the sequel to Trainspotting (1996), and is loosely based on Irvine Welsh's novel Porno (2002), with material from the original novel. The film reunites the original cast — Ewan McGregor as Renton, Ewen Bremner as Spud, Jonny Lee Miller as Sick Boy, and Robert Carlyle as Begbie — twenty years after the events of the first film.

Plot

Mark Renton, who stole £16,000 from his friends at the end of the first film and built a new life in Amsterdam, returns to Edinburgh after his mother's death and a health scare. He finds Simon "Sick Boy" Williamson running his aunt's pub and a blackmail scam with his Bulgarian partner Veronika; Daniel "Spud" Murphy still struggling with heroin addiction; and Francis Begbie in prison, from which he escapes after feigning injury. Renton's attempts at reconciliation give way to a scheme to convert the pub into a brothel financed by a development grant, while the escaped Begbie pursues revenge, leading to a violent confrontation at the pub and a bitter, unresolved reunion among the survivors.

Production

Boyle had long discussed a sequel, and production was announced in 2015 once McGregor and Boyle reconciled after a long estrangement. Filming took place in Edinburgh and Glasgow in 2016. The film makes extensive use of flashbacks, archival footage from the original film, and updated versions of its famous motifs, including a new "Choose Life" monologue and music by Underworld, Iggy Pop, Wolf Alice and Young Fathers.

Reception

The film premiered in Edinburgh on 22 January 2017 and was released in the United Kingdom on 27 January. It received generally positive reviews, with praise for the cast and Boyle's direction, though some critics considered it inferior to the original; it grossed over $42 million worldwide against an $18 million budget.
