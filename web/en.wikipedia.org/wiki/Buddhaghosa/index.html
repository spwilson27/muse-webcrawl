Buddhaghosa was a 5th-century Indian Theravada Buddhist commentator, translator, and philosopher. He is best known for his work the Visuddhimagga ("The Path of Purification"), a comprehensive summary of the core of Theravada Buddhist doctrine and meditation practice. Buddhaghosa translated and systematised the Sinhala commentaries on the Pali Canon preserved at the Mahavihara monastery in Anuradhapura, Sri Lanka, producing the commentaries (atthakatha) that became authoritative for the Theravada tradition.

Life

According to traditional accounts preserved in the later chronicle known as the Buddhaghosuppatti, Buddhaghosa was born near Bodh Gaya in India into a Brahmin family and was well educated in the Vedas and Indian philosophy. After debating with a Buddhist monk and being converted to Buddhism, he travelled to Sri Lanka to study the Sinhala commentaries on the Pali Canon held at the Mahavihara in Anuradhapura.

To test his competence, the elder monks of the Mahavihara asked him to compose a work summarising the doctrine; the result was the Visuddhimagga. Satisfied with his abilities, the community granted him access to the Sinhala commentarial tradition, which he translated into Pali. The historicity of many details of this biography is uncertain, and scholars regard the later biographical account as largely hagiographic.

Works

Buddhaghosa is traditionally credited with commentaries on nearly the whole of the Pali Canon, including commentaries on the Vinaya Pitaka (the Samantapasadika), the four principal Nikayas of the Sutta Pitaka, and several books of the Abhidhamma Pitaka. His commentaries draw extensively on older Sinhala sources, especially the lost Maha-atthakatha, and on the work of earlier commentators.

The Visuddhimagga

The Visuddhimagga is organised around the threefold training of virtue (sila), concentration (samadhi), and wisdom (panna), corresponding to the structure of the Noble Eightfold Path. Its twenty-three chapters cover monastic discipline, meditation subjects (kammatthana), the development of concentration and insight, and the analysis of phenomena according to the Abhidhamma. The work remains one of the most influential manuals of Theravada meditation and doctrine.

Influence

Buddhaghosa's systematisation did much to define orthodox Theravada interpretation in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia. His commentaries became the standard lens through which the Pali Canon was read, and his synthesis of doctrine shaped later scholastic and meditative traditions. Modern scholars debate the extent to which his interpretations reflect the earliest Buddhist teachings as opposed to later Abhidhamma developments.
