The American Triple Crown, formally the Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing, is a series of three horse races for three-year-old Thoroughbreds held each spring in the United States: the Kentucky Derby at Churchill Downs in Louisville, the Preakness Stakes at Pimlico in Baltimore, and the Belmont Stakes at Belmont Park in New York. Winning all three in a single season is widely considered the greatest achievement in American horse racing.

History

The three races were founded in the 19th century (Belmont 1867, Preakness 1873, Derby 1875) but were not run as a linked series until the 20th. Sir Barton in 1919 was the first horse to win all three, before the phrase was even coined; sportswriter Charles Hatton popularised the term "Triple Crown" in the 1930s. Gallant Fox (1930) was the first winner celebrated under the name in his own time.

Winners

  1. Sir Barton (1919)
  2. Gallant Fox (1930)
  3. Omaha (1935)
  4. War Admiral (1937)
  5. Whirlaway (1941)
  6. Count Fleet (1943)
  7. Assault (1946)
  8. Citation (1948)
  9. Secretariat (1973) — set still-standing records in all three races
  10. Seattle Slew (1977) — only winner to be undefeated at the time
  11. Affirmed (1978) — defeating Alydar in all three legs
  12. American Pharoah (2015) — ended a 37-year drought
  13. Justify (2018)

Format and debate

The races are run over five weeks: the Derby on the first Saturday in May, the Preakness two weeks later, and the Belmont three weeks after that. The compressed schedule and the Belmont's demanding 1½-mile distance ("the Test of the Champion") have fuelled long-running debate about whether the spacing should be lengthened to attract more horses and protect their welfare.
