John Peter Zenger

John Peter Zenger (October 26, 1697 – July 28, 1746) was a German printer and journalist in New York City. His acquittal on charges of seditious libel in 1735 became a landmark precedent for freedom of the press in the American colonies and an early stepping stone toward the First Amendment.

The New-York Weekly Journal

Zenger emigrated from the Palatinate to New York as a boy and trained as a printer. In 1733 he began publishing the New-York Weekly Journal, backed by opponents of the unpopular colonial governor William Cosby. Articles in the paper sharply criticised Cosby's administration.

Trial

In November 1734 Zenger was arrested for seditious libel and jailed for more than eight months while awaiting trial. Defended by the Philadelphia lawyer Andrew Hamilton, his case was argued on the ground that truthful statements should not be libellous. Despite instructions from the bench that truth was no defence under the law of the time, the jury acquitted Zenger in August 1735.

Legacy

The verdict did not formally change the law of libel, but it made prosecutions of critical printers politically untenable and became a celebrated symbol of press freedom in America.
