Alan Guth

Alan Harvey Guth (born 27 February 1947) is an American theoretical physicist and cosmologist who first proposed the theory of cosmic inflation — the idea that the early universe passed through a brief phase of exponential expansion — in 1980. He is the Victor F. Weisskopf Professor of Physics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology.

Early life and education

Guth was born in New Brunswick, New Jersey, and grew up in Highland Park. He attended MIT, earning bachelor's and master's degrees in 1969 and a PhD in elementary particle physics in 1972, and held postdoctoral positions at Princeton, Columbia, Cornell, and SLAC, where the work leading to inflation began in 1979.

Cosmic inflation

While studying grand unified theories and the problem of why magnetic monopoles predicted by them are not observed, Guth realised that a "false vacuum" state in the very early universe could drive an exponential expansion — increasing the universe's size by an enormous factor (at least 1026) in a fraction of a second before "decaying" into the hot Big Bang. His 1981 paper, "Inflationary universe: A possible solution to the horizon and flatness problems", showed that inflation also explains why the universe appears spatially flat and why distant regions have nearly identical temperatures. Guth's original "old inflation" model had a flaw in ending the expansion smoothly (the "graceful exit" problem), resolved shortly afterwards by Andrei Linde and by Andreas Albrecht and Paul Steinhardt in the "new inflation" models. Guth later developed ideas on eternal inflation and, with others, the Borde–Guth–Vilenkin theorem on the past incompleteness of inflationary spacetimes.

Later work and writings

Guth has continued to work on inflationary cosmology, the multiverse implications of eternal inflation, and the nature of time. His 1997 book The Inflationary Universe: The Quest for a New Theory of Cosmic Origins recounts the theory's development for general readers. He is among the cosmologists most often mentioned in connection with future Nobel honours for inflation, work for which he shared the 2014 Kavli Prize in Astrophysics with Andrei Linde and Alexei Starobinsky.
