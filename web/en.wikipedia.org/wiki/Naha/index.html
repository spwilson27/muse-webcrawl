Naha (Japanese: 那覇市, romanized: Naha-shi; Okinawan: Naafa) is the capital city of Okinawa Prefecture, the southernmost prefecture of Japan. As of 2020, the city has an estimated population of about 318,000 and a population density of roughly 8,000 people per km². The total area is about 39.98 km².

Naha is located on the southern part of Okinawa Island, the largest of the Ryukyu Islands. It has been the capital of Okinawa since the time of the Ryukyu Kingdom, when it served as the kingdom's principal port and political centre adjacent to Shuri, the royal capital.

History

Though Naha was already a significant port by medieval times, it grew in importance after King Shō Hashi unified Okinawa in the 15th century and established his court at nearby Shuri Castle. The city prospered as a centre of maritime trade between Japan, China, Korea, and Southeast Asia. Commodore Matthew Perry's squadron called at Naha in 1853 during his expedition to open Japan to trade.

Following the abolition of the Ryukyu Kingdom in 1879, Naha became the capital of Okinawa Prefecture. The city was almost completely destroyed during the Battle of Okinawa in 1945 and was rebuilt under the United States Civil Administration of the Ryukyu Islands until Okinawa's reversion to Japan in 1972.

Geography and economy

Naha's economy relies heavily on tourism, retail, and services; Naha Airport serves as the main gateway to Okinawa. Kokusai-dōri ("International Street"), a 1.6-kilometre shopping and entertainment boulevard built on former battlefields, is the city's main street. The Okinawa Urban Monorail (Yui Rail) connects the airport to Shuri via the city centre.

Landmarks

Shuri Castle, a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Gusuku Sites and Related Properties of the Kingdom of Ryukyu, overlooks the city; its main hall was destroyed by fire in October 2019, and reconstruction is under way. Other attractions include the Shikina-en royal gardens, the Tamaudun royal mausoleum, the former Japanese Navy underground headquarters, and the Naminoue Shrine.
