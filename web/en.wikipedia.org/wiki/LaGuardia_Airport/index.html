LaGuardia Airport

LaGuardia Airport (IATA: LGA) is a civil airport in East Elmhurst, Queens, New York City. It is named after Fiorello La Guardia, a former mayor of New York City. Operated by the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey, the airport primarily serves domestic destinations and is the third-busiest airport in the New York metropolitan area, after John F. Kennedy International and Newark Liberty International.

History

The airport opened in 1939 on the site of the former Glenn H. Curtiss Airport. Mayor La Guardia had famously refused to disembark from a flight that landed in New Jersey, demanding to be flown to New York, which helped spur construction of an airport within the city. For decades LaGuardia was notorious for cramped, aging terminals.

Redevelopment

An $8 billion rebuilding programme, begun in 2016, replaced Terminals B, C and D with two new terminal complexes connected to parking garages and a central hall. The new Terminal B opened in stages from 2018 and won the UNESCO Prix Versailles for best new airport in 2021. The redeveloped airport features expanded taxiways, larger gate areas and extensive public art.

Perimeter rule

Since 1984, a Port Authority "perimeter rule" has generally limited nonstop flights to destinations within 1,500 statute miles, with exceptions including Denver and flights on Saturdays. The rule shapes LaGuardia's role as a short-haul, business-oriented airport.
