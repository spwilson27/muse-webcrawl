Pregabalin

Pregabalin
Trade name: Lyrica, others
Class: Gabapentinoid
Formula: C8H17NO2
Legal status: Schedule V (US); Class C (UK)

Pregabalin, sold under the brand name Lyrica among others, is an anticonvulsant and anxiolytic medication used to treat epilepsy, neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia, restless leg syndrome, opioid withdrawal, and generalized anxiety disorder.

Medical uses

Pregabalin is used as an adjunctive treatment for partial seizures, for neuropathic pain associated with diabetic peripheral neuropathy, postherpetic neuralgia, and spinal cord injury, and for fibromyalgia. In the European Union it is also approved for generalized anxiety disorder. Its use for other chronic pain conditions is common but less well supported by evidence.

Mechanism of action

Despite being a structural analogue of the neurotransmitter GABA, pregabalin does not act directly on GABA receptors. It binds to the α2δ subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels in the central nervous system, reducing the release of excitatory neurotransmitters such as glutamate, noradrenaline, and substance P.

Adverse effects and misuse

Common side effects include dizziness, drowsiness, weight gain, blurred vision, and peripheral edema. Pregabalin has a potential for misuse and dependence, particularly among people with a history of substance use, and is a controlled substance in many jurisdictions. Withdrawal symptoms can occur after abrupt discontinuation.

History

Pregabalin was invented by chemist Richard Bruce Silverman at Northwestern University and developed by Pfizer as a successor to gabapentin. It was approved in the European Union and the United States in 2004. It became available as a generic in the US in 2019.
