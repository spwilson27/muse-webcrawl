Subarachnoid space

In anatomy, the subarachnoid space (or subarachnoid cavity) is the interval between the arachnoid mater and the pia mater, two of the membranes (meninges) surrounding the brain and spinal cord. It is filled with cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and contains the delicate connective tissue trabeculae, as well as the major blood vessels of the brain, which run within it before penetrating the brain tissue.

Structure

The space varies in width: where the arachnoid separates from the pia over the brain's sulci, it is narrow, while at the base of the brain and around the spinal cord it expands into larger pools of CSF called subarachnoid cisterns, such as the cisterna magna and the lumbar cistern. CSF circulates through the subarachnoid space after leaving the ventricular system through the foramina of Luschka and Magendie, and is absorbed into the venous system via arachnoid granulations.

Clinical significance

Bleeding into the subarachnoid space from a ruptured cerebral aneurysm causes a subarachnoid haemorrhage, a medical emergency. Lumbar puncture accesses the subarachnoid space in the lumbar region to sample CSF or to administer spinal anaesthesia, taking advantage of the enlargement of the space below the termination of the spinal cord.
