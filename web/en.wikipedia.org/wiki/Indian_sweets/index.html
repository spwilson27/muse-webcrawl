Indian sweets

Indian sweets — collectively known as mithai — are the confectionery and dessert traditions of the Indian subcontinent. They are typically made from sugar combined with milk products, flours, nuts, and flavourings such as cardamom, saffron, and rose water, and play a central role in festivals, religious offerings, and celebrations.

Categories

Milk-based sweets include rasgulla and rasmalai (from chenna in Bengal), gulab jamun (fried khoya dumplings in syrup), barfi, peda, and sandesh. Syrup-soaked and fried sweets include jalebi and imarti. Flour- and lentil-based sweets include laddu, mysore pak, and various halwas; nut-based sweets include kaju katli made from cashews.

Regional traditions

Bengal is famed for its chenna-based sweets, Punjab and North India for khoya sweets, Tamil Nadu for mysore pak and palkova, and Maharashtra for shrikhand and modak. Sweets are exchanged in large quantities during Diwali and given as prasad at temples.
