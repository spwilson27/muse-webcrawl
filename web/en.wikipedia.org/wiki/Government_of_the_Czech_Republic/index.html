The Government of the Czech Republic (Czech: Vláda České republiky) is the supreme body of executive power in the Czech Republic. It consists of the Prime Minister, deputy prime ministers and ministers, and it directs the state administration through the ministries. The government is accountable to the Chamber of Deputies, the lower house of Parliament, and must win its confidence after appointment.

Formation and role

Under the 1993 Constitution, the President appoints the Prime Minister — normally the leader able to command a majority — who proposes the other members of the government. Within thirty days the government must present itself to the Chamber of Deputies and request a vote of confidence. The government issues regulations, prepares the state budget and most legislation, and conducts foreign and security policy together with the President. Its seat is the Straka Academy in Prague's Malá Strana district.

Ministries

The ministries include Finance; Foreign Affairs; Defence; the Interior; Industry and Trade; Justice; Labour and Social Affairs; Health; Education, Youth and Sports; Transport; Agriculture; the Environment; Culture; and Regional Development, together with ministers without portfolio for areas such as European affairs, science and legislation. The armed forces answer to the Ministry of Defence; see Armed Forces of the Czech Republic.
