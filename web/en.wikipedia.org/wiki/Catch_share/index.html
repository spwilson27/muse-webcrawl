Catch share

A catch share is a system of fisheries management in which individual fishermen, cooperatives or communities are allocated a specific portion of the total allowable catch of a fishery. Such systems are also known as individual fishing quotas (IFQs), individual transferable quotas (ITQs), and dedicated access privileges.

Background

Under open-access or limited-entry fisheries managed only by an overall quota, fishermen race to catch as much as possible before the total quota is filled — the so-called "race for fish" or "derby fishery". This tends to shorten seasons, depress prices, encourage overcapitalisation and increase risks to safety. By securing each participant a defined share of the catch, catch share programs aim to end the race and align the economic incentives of fishermen with the long-term health of the stock.

Program design

Catch share programs differ in who receives shares, whether shares may be transferred or leased, what caps on consolidation apply, and how monitoring and enforcement are funded. Programs exist in countries including the United States, Canada, Iceland, New Zealand and Australia, covering fisheries from Alaskan halibut and sablefish to New England groundfish sectors.

Effects and criticism

Studies have associated catch shares with reduced overfishing, longer and safer seasons, and improved product quality. Critics raise concerns about consolidation of quota in fewer hands, effects on small-scale fishermen and fishing communities, and the allocation of a public resource to private holders without charge.
