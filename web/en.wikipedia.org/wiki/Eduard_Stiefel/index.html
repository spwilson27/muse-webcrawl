Eduard Stiefel

Eduard Stiefel
Born: 21 April 1909, Zürich, Switzerland
Died: 25 November 1978, Zürich, Switzerland
Known for: Stiefel–Whitney classes, conjugate gradient method, Z4 computer

Eduard Stiefel (21 April 1909 – 25 November 1978) was a Swiss mathematician. Together with Cornelius Lanczos and Magnus Hestenes, he invented the conjugate gradient method, and gave what is now understood to be a partial construction of the Stiefel–Whitney classes of a real vector bundle, thus co-founding the study of characteristic classes.

Biography

Stiefel studied at ETH Zurich, where he received his doctorate in 1935 under the supervision of Heinz Hopf. He became a full professor at ETH in 1943 and founded the Institute for Applied Mathematics there in 1948. During the Second World War he served in the Swiss Army, rising to the rank of colonel in the general staff.

Stiefel achieved his most important results in topology in the mid-1930s. His doctoral work introduced the classes now named after him and Hassler Whitney. In later life he turned to numerical analysis and the practical use of computers: at his institute the Z4, designed by Konrad Zuse, was installed in 1950 — making ETH Zurich one of the first institutions in Europe with a working program-controlled computer — and Stiefel's group developed early programming tools and numerical software, including work connected with the ALGOL effort.

Legacy

The Stiefel manifold, Stiefel–Whitney classes, and the conjugate gradient method all bear his influence, and he is remembered as both a pure mathematician and a pioneer of scientific computing in Switzerland.
