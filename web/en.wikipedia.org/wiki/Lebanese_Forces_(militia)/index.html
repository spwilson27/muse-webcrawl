The Lebanese Forces (LF) was the main coalition of Christian militias during the Lebanese Civil War. Formed in 1976 as the military wing of the Lebanese Front, it brought together the Kataeb Regulatory Forces, the Tigers Militia of the National Liberal Party, the Guardians of the Cedars, al-Tanzim and the Marada movement (which later withdrew) under a unified command dominated by Bachir Gemayel after 1977.

Formation and campaigns

The LF was created in the summer of 1976 in response to the polarisation of the civil war, fighting in the battles for East Beirut, the siege of Tel al-Zaatar (1976) and the defence of Zahle against the Syrian army (1980–81). It received arms, funding and training principally from Israel and also from Iraq. Gemayel forcibly unified the Christian militias, destroying the Tigers Militia in the Safra massacre of July 1980.

After the 1982 Israeli invasion and Gemayel's election as president, Israeli forces withdrew and the LF fought the Mountain War (1983–84) against Druze and Syrian forces. Internal Christian conflicts culminated in the 1990 "War of Elimination" between the LF under Samir Geagea and General Michel Aoun's army units.

Disbandment

Under the Taif Agreement (1989) and the general amnesty law of 1991, the LF handed over its heavy weapons and dissolved as an armed force; many fighters were integrated into the Lebanese Armed Forces. Its successor political party, also named the Lebanese Forces, was banned in 1994 while Geagea was prosecuted, and was legalised again after 2005.
