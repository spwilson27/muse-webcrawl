Talking point

Talking point
DomainPolitics, public relations, media
PurposePre-established message repeated to support an argument or position

A talking point in discourse is a succinct, pre-established message or formula designed to support a predetermined position in public debate, especially in politics and media. Talking points are crafted by campaigns, parties, governments, corporations, and advocacy groups and distributed to spokespeople, officials, and surrogates so that many voices repeat the same phrasing across interviews, debates, press conferences, and social media. The repetition is the strategy: a phrase heard from many sources appears to be a consensus conclusion rather than a coordinated message.

Practices

Political organizations circulate daily talking points memos — a practice particularly associated with American presidential politics since the late 20th century, where morning memos from party headquarters or a campaign set the day's message. Television pundits and officials who repeat identical phrases verbatim are often identified by journalists and critics as "using talking points," a charge carrying the implication of insincerity or message discipline over substance. Related concepts include the "message of the day," the sound bite, and Frank Luntz-style tested language, in which wording is selected through polling and focus groups for maximum persuasive effect.

Neutral usage

Outside politics, "talking points" can be used neutrally for any prepared list of topics or key facts for a presentation, negotiation, interview, or meeting — brief notes meant to keep a speaker on track. In diplomacy and business, officials prepare talking points before meetings to ensure sensitive issues are raised consistently.

Criticism

Critics argue that heavy reliance on talking points degrades public discourse by substituting rehearsed slogans for responsive argument, and interviewers frequently press subjects to answer "beyond the talking points." Defenders of message discipline note that concise repetition is how complex policies are communicated at all in a crowded media environment, and that coordinated messaging is a normal feature of organized advocacy.
