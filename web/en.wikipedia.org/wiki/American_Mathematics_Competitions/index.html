American Mathematics Competitions
Organizer: Mathematical Association of America
First held: 1950 (AHSME)
Levels: AMC 8, AMC 10, AMC 12
Next round: AIME, USA(J)MO, IMO team selection

The American Mathematics Competitions (AMC) are a series of examinations in secondary school mathematics administered by the Mathematical Association of America (MAA). The competitions are the first in a series of contests that determine the United States team for the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO).

Structure

The program consists of several levels. The AMC 8 is a 25-question, 40-minute multiple-choice examination for students in grade 8 and below. The AMC 10 and AMC 12 are 25-question, 75-minute examinations for students in grades 10 and 12 and below respectively. High scorers on the AMC 10 and AMC 12 are invited to take the American Invitational Mathematics Examination (AIME), a 15-question, three-hour examination. Top performers on the AIME combined with AMC scores qualify for the United States of America Mathematical Olympiad (USAMO) or the junior olympiad (USAJMO).

History

The competitions trace their origin to 1950, when the first American High School Mathematics Examination (AHSME) was held in New York. The AMC 10 was introduced in 2000, and the examinations were restructured into the modern AMC 10/12 A and B format, giving students two dates on which to compete.

Rules

Calculators are not permitted on any AMC examination. Problems range in difficulty from routine exercises to questions demanding deep insight, and are designed so that no problem requires knowledge beyond the corresponding grade-level curriculum, although the hardest problems draw on creative problem-solving.
