Skardu
Region: Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan
Elevation: ~2,228 m (7,310 ft)
District: Skardu District

Skardu (Urdu: سکردو) is a city in Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan, and the capital of Skardu District, one of the districts composing the region. Located in the Skardu Valley at the confluence of the Indus and Shigar Rivers, at an elevation of about 2,228 metres, the city is an important gateway for trekking and mountaineering expeditions to the high peaks of the Karakoram, including K2, Broad Peak, and the Gasherbrums.

Geography

Skardu lies in a broad, arid valley surrounded by mountains. The Indus River flows past the city, and the nearby Satpara and Shangrila (Lower Kachura) lakes are notable attractions. The cold desert around Katpana, with its high sand dunes, is among the highest cold deserts in the world.

History

The area was historically part of the Balti kingdom of Maqpon, whose rulers built forts in the region, including the Kharpocho Fort overlooking the city, dating to around the 16th century. Skardu later came under Dogra rule from Kashmir in the 19th century and, following the events of 1947–48, became part of Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan. The population is predominantly Balti, speaking the Balti language and following Shia Islam.

Tourism and transport

Skardu Airport receives flights from Islamabad, weather permitting, and the city is connected to the Karakoram Highway by the Skardu road along the Indus gorge. The Deosai National Park, a high-altitude plateau known for its wildlife and flowers, is accessible from Skardu.
