Pole star

[Image: Polaris at the centre of circumpolar star trails]

Overview

A pole star (or polar star) is a star closely aligned to Earth's axis of rotation, i.e. lying near one of the celestial poles, and thus appearing almost stationary while the rest of the sky wheels around it. The current north pole star is Polaris (Alpha Ursae Minoris); the southern pole star, Sigma Octantis, is barely visible to the naked eye.

History and navigation

Pole stars have guided navigators and travellers for millennia — in the age of sail, the altitude of Polaris gave latitude directly. Axial precession changes the pole star over millennia: Egyptian pyramid builders used Thuban; Greek sailors used Kochab; after Polaris, the role passes to stars in Cepheus (Gamma Cephei, Alderamin) and, around 13,700 AD, to brilliant Vega. In Shakespeare and other early-modern literature the "still point" of the pole star symbolised constancy ("I am constant as the Northern Star").
