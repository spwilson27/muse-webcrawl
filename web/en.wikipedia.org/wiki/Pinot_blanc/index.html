Pinot blanc

Pinot blanc is a white wine grape variety that arose as a genetic mutation of Pinot noir (via the unstable Pinot gris). It produces fresh, dry, light-to-medium-bodied white wines and is most importantly grown in Alsace (France), Germany (where it is called Weißburgunder), Austria (Weißer Burgunder) and northern Italy (Pinot bianco).

History and identity

Pinot blanc belongs to the Pinot family of varieties, in which the same vine can mutate between black (noir), grey (gris) and white (blanc) berry colours. Because of this instability, Pinot blanc was long confused with Chardonnay, which it resembles in the vineyard, and in Alsace wines labelled Pinot blanc may legally include the related Auxerrois grape. DNA profiling in the 1990s confirmed Pinot blanc as a colour mutation within the Pinot lineage.

Viticulture and wines

The variety buds early and ripens mid-season, yielding generously. Wines are typically fermented dry, with flavours of apple, pear, citrus and white flowers, and are generally best drunk young, though richer Alsatian and German examples can age. Pinot blanc is also used for sparkling wines, including Crémant d'Alsace, and in Italy as a base for spumante.

Regions

Alsace is its classic home, where it makes straightforward, refreshing dry whites. In Germany, Weißburgunder plantings have expanded markedly in Baden, the Pfalz and Rheinhessen. It is also grown in Austria, north-eastern Italy (Alto Adige, Friuli), Slovenia, Croatia, Hungary, and in smaller quantities in Oregon and British Columbia.
