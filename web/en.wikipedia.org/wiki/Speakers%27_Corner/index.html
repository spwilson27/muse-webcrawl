Speakers' Corner

Speakers' Corner is an area located in Hyde Park, London, in the north-east corner near Marble Arch, where open-air public speaking, debate and discussion are allowed. It is traditionally held that speakers there may talk on any subject, as long as the police consider their speeches lawful. There is no immunity from the law, nor are any subjects taboo, but in practice the police tend to be tolerant and therefore intervene only when they receive a complaint or if they hear profanity.

History

The area close to the site had long been a place of public gatherings. The gallows at Tyburn, near the modern corner, were a site where the condemned could make a final speech. In 1855, riots broke out in Hyde Park over the Sunday Trading Bill, which forbade buying and selling on a Sunday, the only day working people had off. After the Reform Act 1867, which widened the franchise, the corner became a regular venue for demonstrations and political discussion.

Famous speakers

Historical figures including Karl Marx, Vladimir Lenin, George Orwell and William Morris are thought to have spoken at Speakers' Corner. More recently it has been associated with evangelists, activists of many causes, and hecklers, whose interventions are considered part of the tradition.

Other Speakers' Corners

Similar areas have been established in other countries and in other London parks, as well as in cities such as Singapore, Sydney and Amsterdam, although some are regulated and require permits.
