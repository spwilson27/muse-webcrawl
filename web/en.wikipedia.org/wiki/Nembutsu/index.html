Nembutsu

The nembutsu (Japanese: 念仏) is the central practice of Pure Land Buddhism: the recitation of the name of the Buddha Amitābha (Amida). In Japanese it takes the form Namu Amida Butsu — "I take refuge in Amida Buddha" — derived from the Sanskrit Namo'mitābhāya Buddhāya. In Chinese the practice is called niànfó and in Korean yeombul. Pure Land teachers teach that sincere recitation, relying on Amida's Primal Vow rather than one's own effort, ensures rebirth in Amida's Pure Land (Sukhāvatī), where enlightenment is readily attained.

Origins

The practice is grounded in the Larger Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, in which the bodhisattva Dharmākara — the future Amida Buddha — vowed that all beings who sincerely entrusted themselves to him and called his name even ten times would be born in his land. In China, masters such as Tanluan, Daochuo, and Shandao systematised nianfo practice; Shandao taught that oral recitation of the name was the decisive karmic act for rebirth.

In Japan

In Kamakura-period Japan, Hōnen (1133–1212), founder of the Jōdo-shū school, taught the exclusive practice of the nembutsu (senju nembutsu), accessible to everyone regardless of learning or moral capacity, in his treatise Senchaku Hongan Nembutsushū. His disciple Shinran (1173–1263), founder of Jōdo Shinshū, radicalised the doctrine: faith (shinjin) is itself Amida's gift, and the nembutsu is spoken in gratitude rather than as a means of earning rebirth — the teaching of "other power" (tariki) as opposed to "self power" (jiriki). Another tradition, the Ji-shū of Ippen, spread the practice through ecstatic dancing nembutsu (odori nembutsu).

Practice

The nembutsu may be recited aloud or silently, individually or in groups, counted on prayer beads (juzu), and repeated from a few times to tens of thousands of times a day. Related practices include meditative visualisation of Amida and his land (kansō nembutsu) in earlier Chinese and Tendai traditions. The six written characters of the formula are also a major subject of East Asian calligraphy and devotional art.
