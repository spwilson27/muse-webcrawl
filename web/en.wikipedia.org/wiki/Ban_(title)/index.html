Ban (title)

Ban was a South Slavic noble and governmental title used from the 7th century to 1941, principally in Croatia and Bosnia, for the governor of a province (a banate or banovina) ruling in the name of a king. The title is generally held to derive from the Avar word bajan (a rich lord or khan).

The Ban combined viceregal and military authority

History

Bans governed border provinces of the medieval Kingdom of Hungary–Croatia, combining civil, military and judicial powers and ranking immediately below the king. The most powerful office was the Ban of Croatia (later Croatia, Slavonia and Dalmatia), held by such figures as Petar Berislavić, Nikola Šubić Zrinski, and Josip Jelačić, the 19th-century ban celebrated as a Croatian national hero. Bosnia, too, was ruled by bans such as Kulin (1180–1204) before its rulers took the royal title.

In the Kingdom of Yugoslavia, the reorganisation of 1929 created nine provinces called banovinas, each headed by a ban appointed by the king, including the Banovina of Croatia established in 1939. The office disappeared with the monarchy in 1941–45.
