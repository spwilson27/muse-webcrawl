Shippingport Atomic Power Station

Shippingport Atomic Power Station
Location: Shippingport, Pennsylvania, USA
Status: Decommissioned
Commission date: 1957
Decommission date: 1982
Capacity: 68 MW (final core)

The Shippingport Atomic Power Station, on the Ohio River near Shippingport, Pennsylvania, was the world's first full-scale atomic electric power plant devoted exclusively to peacetime uses. It was a project of the U.S. Atomic Energy Commission and the Duquesne Light Company, championed by Admiral Hyman G. Rickover.

History

Ground was broken in 1954, and President Dwight D. Eisenhower — who had launched the "Atoms for Peace" programme — waved a symbolic neutron wand to start construction. The reactor achieved criticality on 2 December 1957 and began feeding electricity to the Pittsburgh-area grid on 18 December 1957.

Design and cores

Shippingport used a pressurized water reactor derived from naval propulsion designs. It operated with three successive cores: the first used enriched uranium oxide in a seed-and-blanket arrangement; the second increased output; the third, a light-water breeder core using thorium and uranium-233, operated from 1977 to 1982 and demonstrated breeding in a light-water system, producing slightly more fissile material than it consumed.

Decommissioning

The plant shut down in 1982. Its decommissioning, completed in 1989, was a landmark project: the 956-ton reactor pressure vessel was removed intact and shipped by barge to the Hanford Site in Washington for burial, demonstrating that a commercial-scale reactor could be dismantled safely.
