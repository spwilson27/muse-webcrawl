Radiator (heating)

Radiators and convectors are heat exchangers designed to transfer thermal energy from one medium to another for the purpose of space heating. Despite the name, most radiators transfer the bulk of their heat by convection rather than by thermal radiation: hot water or steam circulates inside the radiator, heating the surrounding air, which rises and circulates around the room.

History

The heating radiator was invented in the mid-19th century. Russian businessman Franz San Galli, in St. Petersburg, is credited with inventing the radiator around 1855, and Joseph Nason and Robert Briggs developed early American designs; their patents of the 1860s–1870s established the familiar vertical column radiator. Radiators spread rapidly through steam-heated apartment buildings, offices and schools.

Types

Steam radiators operate with steam from a boiler in one-pipe or two-pipe systems, reaching high surface temperatures. Hot-water radiators use circulated water at lower temperatures and are controlled by thermostatic radiator valves. Construction materials include traditional cast iron (heavy, slow to heat, slow to cool), pressed-steel panel radiators common in modern European homes, aluminium sectional radiators, and low-profile convectors that use finned tubes and sometimes fans. Electric radiators, including oil-filled types, convert electricity directly to heat.

Operation and control

Radiators are usually placed on external walls and under windows to counteract cold downdraughts. Air trapped at the top of a hot-water radiator is released with a bleed key. Modern condensing boilers and heat pumps work most efficiently with larger radiators running at lower water temperatures.
