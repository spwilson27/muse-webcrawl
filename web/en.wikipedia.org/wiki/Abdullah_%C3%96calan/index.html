Abdullah Öcalan

Abdullah Öcalan (born 4 April 1948 or 1949), also known as Apo (short for Abdullah, and Kurdish for "uncle"), is a Kurdish political activist and founding leader of the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK), a Kurdish nationalist organisation that waged an armed insurgency against Turkey from 1984. He has been imprisoned on the island of İmralı in Turkey since his capture in 1999, and from prison has developed the ideology of "democratic confederalism".

Early life

Öcalan was born in the village of Ömerli (Amara) in Şanlıurfa Province in southeastern Turkey, to a poor Kurdish-Turkish family. He studied political science at Ankara University, where he became involved in leftist and Kurdish activism during the politically turbulent 1970s.

Founding of the PKK

In 1978, at a meeting in the village of Fis, Öcalan and his associates formally founded the PKK, a Marxist-Leninist organisation seeking an independent Kurdish state. After the 1980 Turkish military coup he fled to Syria, from where he directed the party; on 15 August 1984 the PKK launched its armed campaign in Turkey. The conflict has since claimed tens of thousands of lives. The PKK is designated a terrorist organisation by Turkey, the United States, the European Union and others.

Capture and imprisonment

Expelled from Syria in 1998 under Turkish pressure, Öcalan travelled between Russia, Italy and Greece before being captured by Turkish intelligence in Nairobi, Kenya, in February 1999. He was tried for treason and sentenced to death; the sentence was commuted to life imprisonment when Turkey abolished capital punishment in 2002. He has since been held as the sole prisoner for much of the time on İmralı island in the Sea of Marmara.

Ideology

From prison Öcalan has issued influential theoretical writings, notably developing "democratic confederalism", a framework favouring decentralised, communal self-government and women's liberation over a separate Kurdish nation-state, influenced in part by the American anarchist Murray Bookchin. His calls from prison have at times shaped peace processes between the PKK and the Turkish state.
