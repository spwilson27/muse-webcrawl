Ātman (Hinduism)

Ātman Sanskrit: आत्मन्
Meaning: Self, soul, breath
Key texts: Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita
Related concept: Brahman
Liberation: Moksha
The ātman, the innermost self or soul in Hindu thought.

Ātman (Sanskrit: आत्मन्) is a Sanskrit word that means inner self, spirit or soul. In Hindu philosophy, especially in the Vedanta school, ātman is the first principle: the true self of an individual, beyond identification with phenomena. It is the essence that is eternal, unchanging and unborn, distinct from the material body, mind and ego.

The concept is developed in the early Upanishads, where the great sayings (mahāvākya) identify ātman with Brahman, the ultimate reality: tat tvam asi — "thou art that". According to the Bhagavad Gita, the ātman cannot be cut by weapons, burned by fire, wetted by water or withered by the wind; at death it discards the body as a person discards worn-out garments.

Views in the schools

The schools of Hindu philosophy differ on the relation between ātman and Brahman. Advaita Vedanta teaches their complete identity and regards liberation (moksha) as the realisation of that identity. Dvaita Vedanta holds the individual self to be eternally distinct from God, while Vishishtadvaita regards selves as modes or attributes of Brahman. Buddhism, by contrast, denies any permanent self in its doctrine of anattā (non-self), a position debated for centuries with the Hindu schools. Release from the cycle of rebirth (saṁsāra) is achieved, in the Hindu view, through self-knowledge, devotion or disciplined action, depending on the school.
