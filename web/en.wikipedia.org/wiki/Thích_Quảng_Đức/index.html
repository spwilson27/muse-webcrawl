Thích Quảng Đức

Thích Quảng Đức (1897 – 11 June 1963) was a Vietnamese Mahayana Buddhist monk who died by self-immolation at a busy Saigon road intersection on 11 June 1963. He was protesting the persecution of Buddhists by the Catholic-dominated government of President Ngô Đình Diệm during the Buddhist crisis.

Self-immolation

Photographs by Malcolm Browne of the monk seated calmly in the lotus position as flames engulfed him circulated worldwide and won the Pulitzer Prize and World Press Photo of the Year. The act intensified international pressure on Diệm's regime, which fell in a coup later that year. John F. Kennedy remarked that no news picture in history had generated so much emotion.

Legacy

Quảng Đức is venerated as a bodhisattva in Vietnamese Buddhism; his heart, which reportedly remained intact after cremation, is kept as a relic. A monument stands at the intersection in Ho Chi Minh City, and the car that carried him to the site is preserved at Thiên Mụ Pagoda in Huế. The image famously appeared on the cover of Rage Against the Machine's 1992 debut album.
