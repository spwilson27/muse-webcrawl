Eritrean Defence Forces

Eritrean Defence Forces
Founded: 1991 (as EDF)
Headquarters: Asmara
Commander-in-Chief: President Isaias Afwerki
Service branches: Army; Navy; Air Force
Conscription: National service, officially 18 months, in practice often indefinite

The Eritrean Defence Forces (EDF) are the military forces of the State of Eritrea, comprising the Eritrean Army, the Eritrean Navy, and the Eritrean Air Force. They descend from the armed wing of the Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF), which won independence from Ethiopia in 1991 after a thirty-year liberation war.

History

After independence the EPLF's army became the national military, and demobilisation of many fighters followed. Eritrea fought a border war with Yemen over the Hanish Islands (1995) and, most destructively, the Eritrean–Ethiopian War of 1998–2000, a full-scale conventional war in which tens of thousands died before the Algiers Agreement ended hostilities. Border tensions persisted for nearly two decades, with a large standing army maintained along the frontier until the 2018 peace declaration between Eritrea and Ethiopia. Eritrean forces intervened in the Tigray War in Ethiopia from late 2020, drawing international criticism over reported atrocities, and withdrew only partially as the conflict wound down under the 2022 Pretoria agreement.

National service

All Eritreans are nominally required to perform 18 months of national service (military and civilian) after finishing secondary school at the Sawa Defence Training Center. In practice, since the 1998–2000 war, service has routinely been extended for years or indefinitely, with low pay and harsh conditions. A 2016 UN Commission of Inquiry found the system to constitute enslavement and cited it as a major driver of Eritreans fleeing the country as refugees; the government maintains the policy is necessary for national defence.

Organisation and equipment

The army is by far the largest branch and is organised into infantry divisions and mechanised units equipped largely with Soviet-era and second-hand equipment, including T-54/55 tanks and towed artillery. The small navy operates patrol craft from the Red Sea ports of Massawa and Assab, and the air force flies a modest fleet including MiG-29 fighters and Mi-17 helicopters. Because of official secrecy, manpower figures are estimates, commonly in the range of 150,000–200,000 active personnel, with large reserve obligations tied to national service.
