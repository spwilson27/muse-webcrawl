Dallas (1978 TV series)

Dallas is an American prime-time television soap opera that aired on CBS from April 2, 1978, to May 3, 1991. Created by David Jacobs and produced by Lorimar Television, the series revolves around a wealthy and feuding Texas family, the Ewings, who own the independent oil company Ewing Oil and the cattle-ranching land of Southfork. It ran for 14 seasons and 357 episodes, and was one of the most successful American television dramas of all time.

Premise and cast

The original ensemble centred on patriarch Jock Ewing (Jim Davis) and matriarch Miss Ellie (Barbara Bel Geddes), their scheming elder son J.R. Ewing (Larry Hagman) and his wife Sue Ellen (Linda Gray), and younger son Bobby (Patrick Duffy), whose marriage to Pamela Barnes (Victoria Principal) reopened the old Ewing–Barnes feud. Hagman’s portrayal of the ruthless, charismatic J.R. made him the show’s breakout star and one of television’s most famous villains.

"Who shot J.R.?" and the dream season

The 1980 cliffhanger in which J.R. was shot by an unseen assailant sparked the global phenomenon "Who shot J.R.?" The concluding episode, "Who Done It", aired on November 21, 1980, drew an estimated 83 million American viewers and a record Nielsen rating, and was eventually revealed to implicate Kristin Shepard (Mary Crosby). The series is also notorious for its 1985–86 "dream season": after Patrick Duffy left and Bobby was killed off, Duffy’s return was explained by having the entire season revealed as Pamela’s dream, a twist that angered many viewers and hastened the show’s decline in its final years.

Legacy

Dallas pioneered the prime-time soap format, paving the way for spin-off Knots Landing (created by Jacobs before Dallas) and rivals such as Dynasty and Falcon Crest. Southfork Ranch in Parker, Texas, remains a tourist attraction. A continuation series of the same name aired on TNT from 2012 to 2014, reuniting Hagman, Duffy, and Gray with a new generation of Ewings.
