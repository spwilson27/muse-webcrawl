Utility cooperative


A rural electric cooperative crew

A utility cooperative is a type of cooperative that delivers a public utility service — electricity, water, natural gas, sewerage or telecommunications — and is owned by the customers it serves. Members elect the board of directors democratically (one member, one vote), and margins beyond operating costs are typically returned to members as patronage capital credits rather than paid to shareholders.

Electric cooperatives in the United States

The best-known examples are America's rural electric cooperatives, created after the Rural Electrification Act of 1936, when only about 10% of farms had electricity because investor-owned utilities found rural lines unprofitable. Federal loans through the Rural Electrification Administration let farmers form cooperatives and string their own lines; within a generation farm electrification was nearly universal. Today some 900 distribution and generation-and-transmission cooperatives serve more than 40 million people across most of the land area of the United States, coordinated by the National Rural Electric Cooperative Association (NRECA).

Other utilities and countries

Telephone and broadband cooperatives serve rural areas in the US and elsewhere, and water cooperatives are common in Finland and parts of Latin America. Cooperatives are distinguished from municipal utilities (owned by local government) and investor-owned utilities (owned by shareholders) by their member ownership and not-for-profit operation.
