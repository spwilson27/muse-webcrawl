History of operating systems

FieldComputer science
MilestonesGM-NAA I/O 1956; OS/360 1964; Unix 1969; MS-DOS 1981; Linux 1991

The history of operating systems follows computing from hand-loaded programs through batch systems, time-sharing and Unix to today's mobile and cloud platforms.

Early systems

Early computers ran one manually loaded job at a time. In the mid-1950s GM-NAA I/O (1956) introduced batch processing; monitors and resident supervisors automated job sequencing. IBM's OS/360 (1964) unified a product line under one operating system family.

Time-sharing and Unix

CTSS (1961) and Multics pioneered interactive time-sharing. Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie at Bell Labs created Unix (1969), written in C from 1973, whose portability and tool philosophy spread widely through universities and BSD. CP/M (1974) brought operating systems to microcomputers.

Personal and modern era

MS-DOS (1981) and the Macintosh GUI (1984) defined the PC era; Windows rose to dominance in the 1990s. Linus Torvalds's Linux kernel (1991) powered servers and, via Android (2008), most smartphones, while Apple's iOS derived from Unix-based macOS. Virtualisation and containerisation now let many operating systems share cloud hardware.
