Orvieto Cathedral

The west façade of Orvieto Cathedral

Location: Orvieto, Umbria, Italy

Denomination: Roman Catholic

Architectural style: Italian Gothic

Groundbreaking: 1290

Completed: 1591

Orvieto Cathedral (Italian: Duomo di Orvieto; Cattedrale di Santa Maria Assunta) is a large 14th-century Roman Catholic cathedral situated on a volcanic plug in the town of Orvieto in Umbria, central Italy. The building was constructed under the orders of Pope Urban IV to commemorate and provide a suitable home for the Corporal of Bolsena, the relic of a miracle which is said to have occurred in 1263 in the nearby town of Bolsena when a travelling priest who had doubts about the truth of transubstantiation found that his Host was bleeding so much that it stained the altar cloth.

The cathedral is known for its elaborately decorated façade, which includes large bas-reliefs and sculptures by the architect Lorenzo Maitani, and for the Chapel of San Brizio inside, which contains a cycle of frescoes by Fra Angelico and Luca Signorelli, most famously Signorelli's Last Judgment cycle (1499–1502), considered one of the masterpieces of the Italian Renaissance.

History

Construction began in 1290 on a site where an older church, Santa Maria del Vescovado, had stood. The first master builder was possibly Fra Bevignate da Cingoli, though the design is sometimes attributed to Arnolfo di Cambio. The richly sculpted façade, begun by Lorenzo Maitani around 1310, is divided into three gables with four bas-relief panels depicting scenes from Genesis, the Old Testament, the New Testament, and the Last Judgment.

Inside, the Chapel of the Corporal (1350–1356) houses the relic in a silver-gilt reliquary made by Ugolino di Vieri in 1337, modelled on the façade itself. The Chapel of San Brizio (the New Chapel) was begun by Fra Angelico in 1447, who painted part of the ceiling, and completed by Luca Signorelli from 1499, whose dramatic frescoes of the Apocalypse and the Last Judgment are thought to have influenced Michelangelo's work in the Sistine Chapel.

Architecture

The nave is round-arched and built of alternating bands of white travertine and green-black basalt, a pattern repeated throughout the interior. The rose window, designed by Andrea Orcagna in the mid-14th century, is set within a square frame above the three portals. The cathedral's great bronze doors were added in the 20th century.
