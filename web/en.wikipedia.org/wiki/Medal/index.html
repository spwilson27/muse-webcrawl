Medal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A medal or medallion is a small portable artistic object, a thin disc, normally of metal, carrying a design, usually on both sides. They typically have a commemorative purpose of some kind, and many are presented as awards. They may be intended to be worn, suspended from clothing or jewellery with a ribbon or chain, or mounted for display.

History

Portrait medals emerged in Renaissance Italy, where Pisanello's medal of John VIII Palaiologos (1438) is considered a founding work of the art form. Medals had precedents in Roman coinage and in the large presentation pieces of antiquity. From the 17th century onward, states increasingly issued medals to commemorate battles, coronations, and treaties, and to reward military and civilian service.

Types

Award medals are given for achievement, bravery, or long service; military decorations such as the Medal of Honor and the Victoria Cross are among the most prestigious. Commemorative medals mark events or anniversaries. Sporting medals — notably the gold, silver, and bronze medals of the Olympic Games — are awarded to competition winners. Religious medals, such as the Miraculous Medal, are worn as devotional objects. Table medals and art medals are produced for collectors.

Manufacture

Medals are generally struck with dies, like coins, though large art medals may be cast. Common metals include gold, silver, bronze, and copper alloys; finishes range from proof-like polishing to antique patinas. The obverse usually carries the principal design and the reverse a secondary design or inscription.
