Khotanese, also called Khotan Saka, is an extinct Eastern Iranian language once spoken in the Kingdom of Khotan, an oasis state on the southern rim of the Taklamakan Desert in the Tarim Basin (present-day Xinjiang, China). Together with the closely related Tumshuqese, it forms the Saka branch of the Iranian languages.

History

Khotanese is attested in manuscripts dated from roughly the 5th to the 10th century CE, recovered from Khotan, Dunhuang, and other Silk Road sites by expeditions of Aurel Stein and others. The language died out after the Karakhanid conquest of Khotan around the year 1000, as the population shifted to Turkic languages.

Literature

The surviving corpus is dominated by Buddhist texts, including translations of Mahayana sutras, the original Khotanese Book of Zambasta, and the apocryphal Śūraṅgama Sūtra version, alongside secular documents such as letters, contracts, medical texts, and travel accounts. The pioneering modern scholar of the language was H. W. Bailey, whose dictionary and editions remain foundational.
