Toy Story

[Image: Woody the cowboy doll and Buzz Lightyear the space ranger standing together on a child's bed, in bright computer animation.]

Toy Story is a 1995 American animated comedy film produced by Pixar Animation Studios and released by Walt Disney Pictures. It was the first entirely computer-animated feature film, as well as the first feature film from Pixar. Directed by John Lasseter, it stars the voices of Tom Hanks as Woody, a pull-string cowboy doll, and Tim Allen as Buzz Lightyear, a deluded space-ranger action figure.

Plot

Woody is the favourite toy of the boy Andy and the leader of his toys, who come alive when humans are absent. Woody's position is threatened by Andy's birthday gift, Buzz Lightyear, who believes he is a real space ranger. Their rivalry strands them outside the house, in the clutches of the toy-torturing neighbour Sid; escaping together to reach Andy's moving van, the two become friends, and Buzz accepts his identity as a toy. The score by Randy Newman includes the song "You've Got a Friend in Me".

Impact

Made on a budget of about $30 million, the film grossed over $373 million worldwide and was praised for its wit, emotion and technical breakthrough. It received a Special Achievement Academy Award, and Lasseter and his team were credited with launching the modern era of computer animation. Sequels followed in 1999, 2010 and 2019, each a major critical and commercial success, and the franchise has become a cornerstone of Disney-Pixar.
