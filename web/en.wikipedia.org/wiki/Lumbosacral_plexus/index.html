Lumbosacral plexus

The lumbosacral plexus is the network of nerves formed by the ventral rami of the lumbar and sacral spinal nerves (L1–S4) that supplies the lower limb, the pelvic girdle and parts of the pelvis and perineum. It is conventionally divided into an upper lumbar plexus (L1–L4) and a lower sacral plexus (L4–S4), connected by the lumbosacral trunk.

Lumbar plexus

Formed within and behind the psoas major muscle, the lumbar plexus gives off the iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal, genitofemoral and lateral femoral cutaneous nerves, and two large terminal branches: the obturator nerve (L2–L4), supplying the medial thigh, and the femoral nerve (L2–L4), supplying the anterior thigh and, via the saphenous nerve, the medial leg.

Sacral plexus

The sacral plexus lies on the piriformis muscle in the pelvis. Its branches include the superior and inferior gluteal nerves, the pudendal nerve, and its great terminal branch, the sciatic nerve (L4–S3) — the largest nerve in the body — which supplies the posterior thigh and divides into the tibial and common fibular (peroneal) nerves for the leg and foot.

Clinical significance

The plexus can be injured by pelvic fractures, tumours, childbirth, retroperitoneal haematoma or diabetes (lumbosacral radiculoplexus neuropathy). Damage causes patterns of weakness and sensory loss spanning several nerve-root territories, distinguishing plexopathy from a single radiculopathy or peripheral nerve lesion. Lumbosacral plexus blocks are also used in regional anaesthesia.
