Queen

This article is about the female monarch. For the British rock band, see Queen (band).

A queen is a female monarch, equivalent in rank and title to a king. Historically, queens have ruled as heads of state in their own right (queens regnant) or as the wives of kings (queens consort). In most modern monarchies a queen regnant occupies the throne by hereditary succession in the same way as a king.

A queen regnant holds sovereign authority in her own right, whereas a queen consort is the wife of a reigning king.

Types

A queen regnant is a reigning monarch, such as Elizabeth II of the United Kingdom or Margrethe II of Denmark. A queen consort is the wife of a reigning king and usually shares his rank and status but not his sovereign powers. A queen dowager is the widow of a king, and a queen regent exercises the monarch's authority during a minority, absence, or incapacity. A queen mother is a dowager who is also the mother of the current sovereign.

Etymology

The English word derives from Old English cwēn, "woman, wife, queen", from Proto-Germanic *kwēnō, cognate with Greek gynē ("woman"). Historically "queen" and "quean" were the same word until spelling diverged in Middle English.

Notable queens regnant

Well-known queens regnant include Hatshepsut of ancient Egypt, Jadwiga of Poland, Isabella I of Castile, Elizabeth I of England, Christina of Sweden, Maria Theresa of Austria, Victoria of the United Kingdom, and Liliʻuokalani, the last monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaii.
