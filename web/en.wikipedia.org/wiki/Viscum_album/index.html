Viscum album

Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Santalaceae
Genus: Viscum
Species: V. album
Common name: European mistletoe

Viscum album is a species of mistletoe in the family Santalaceae, commonly known as European mistletoe, common mistletoe or simply mistletoe. It is native to Europe and western and southern Asia. It is a hemiparasitic plant that grows on the branches of trees, drawing water and nutrients from its host while photosynynthesising for itself.

Description

Viscum album is an evergreen shrub forming dense, globular bushes up to about a metre across on host branches. Its stems fork repeatedly, bearing leathery, yellowish-green, strap-shaped leaves in opposite pairs. The plant is dioecious, with inconspicuous yellowish flowers appearing in spring. The fruit is a waxy white or yellowish berry containing a sticky seed embedded in glutinous pulp.

Life cycle and hosts

Seeds are spread chiefly by birds — notably the mistle thrush and blackcap — which wipe the sticky seeds onto bark or pass them through the gut. On germination, the seedling sends a haustorium into the host's wood, tapping the xylem. The plant grows on a wide range of trees, including apple, hawthorn, poplar, lime and, more rarely, oak. Recognised subspecies specialise on broadleaved trees, pines and firs respectively.

Toxicity and chemistry

The plant contains toxic compounds including the lectins viscumin and mistletoe lectins, and viscotoxins. Berries and leaves are poisonous to humans in quantity, though birds eat the berries unharmed. Extracts of mistletoe have a long history in European folk medicine, and mistletoe preparations (such as Iscador) are widely used as a complementary therapy in cancer care in German-speaking countries, although evidence of efficacy remains contested.

Cultural significance

Mistletoe features prominently in folklore: it was sacred to the druids according to Pliny the Elder, figures in Norse myth in the death of Baldr, and is associated with the Roman Saturnalia and later Christmas customs, including kissing beneath a sprig of mistletoe. The plant is the county flower of Herefordshire in England, and its decline in some traditional orchards has prompted conservation efforts.
