Approximations of π

Approximations of π have been pursued since antiquity, reflecting the importance of the mathematical constant π (pi) — the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, approximately 3.14159 — in geometry, engineering, and astronomy.

Value of π: ≈ 3.1415926535…
Common approximations: 22/7, 355/113, 3.14, 3.1416

History

Babylonian and Egyptian sources used values near 3.125 and 3.16. Archimedes of Syracuse obtained bounds between 223/71 and 22/7 by inscribing and circumscribing polygons. In China, Zu Chongzhi computed π to seven digits and gave the remarkably accurate fraction 355/113. Indian mathematician Madhava developed infinite series for π, later generalized by Leibniz and Gregory.

Modern computation

With algorithms such as the Chudnovsky algorithm and powerful computers, trillions of digits of π have been calculated. For most practical purposes, a handful of digits suffice — NASA has noted that 15 digits are enough for interplanetary navigation.
