Thutmose I

Reignc. 1506–1493 BC
PredecessorAmenhotep I
SuccessorThutmose II
Dynasty18th Dynasty
BurialKV38, later KV20

Thutmose I (sometimes read as Thothmes) was the third pharaoh of the 18th Dynasty of ancient Egypt. He ascended to the throne after the death of Amenhotep I, to whom he may not have been directly related, and reigned for roughly thirteen years.

Military campaigns

Thutmose I is remembered as a great warrior king who greatly expanded Egypt's borders. In the south he campaigned deep into Nubia, reaching the Fourth Cataract of the Nile and establishing Egyptian control there. In the north he led an expedition across Syria to the Euphrates River, setting up a boundary stela — the furthest reach of any Egyptian army to that date — in confrontation with the kingdom of Mitanni.

Building works

At Karnak, Thutmose I added pylons, courts, and obelisks to the temple of Amun. He is also traditionally credited as the first pharaoh to be buried in the Valley of the Kings, his tomb having been prepared by his architect Ineni.

Family

His daughter by his great royal wife Ahmose was Hatshepsut, who later ruled as pharaoh. He was succeeded by his son Thutmose II.
