Nuclear Suppliers Group

Nuclear Suppliers Group (NSG)
Formation: 1974
Type: Multilateral export control regime
Membership: 48 participating governments
Purpose: Non-proliferation of nuclear weapons through export controls

The Nuclear Suppliers Group (NSG) is a multilateral export control regime and a group of nuclear supplier countries that seeks to prevent nuclear proliferation by controlling the export of materials, equipment and technology that can be used to manufacture nuclear weapons.

History

The NSG was founded in response to India's nuclear test of May 1974, which demonstrated that nuclear technology transferred for peaceful purposes could be diverted to weapons. The founding members — Canada, France, Japan, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, the United States and West Germany — first met in 1975 and published guidelines in 1978. The group is sometimes called the "London Club" or London Suppliers Group.

Guidelines

The NSG maintains two sets of guidelines. Part 1 covers exports of items especially designed or prepared for nuclear use, such as reactors, enrichment and reprocessing equipment, and nuclear materials; since 1992 these transfers require the recipient to accept comprehensive IAEA safeguards. Part 2, adopted after the revelations about Iraq's clandestine programme, covers dual-use equipment, materials and technologies that could contribute to an unsafeguarded nuclear fuel cycle or weapons programme. Members apply the guidelines voluntarily under their national export licensing systems, and decisions in the group are taken by consensus.

Membership and controversies

The group has 48 participating governments. In 2008 the NSG granted a waiver allowing nuclear trade with India despite India not having signed the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty, a decision that remains controversial. India has since sought full membership, as has Pakistan; both applications have been debated without consensus. China is a member, while countries such as Israel and North Korea are not.
