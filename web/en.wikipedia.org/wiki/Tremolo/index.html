Tremolo

Tremolo (Italian for "trembling") is a musical effect consisting of a rapid repetition or fluctuation of a note. It takes several forms: the rapid reiteration of a single note (as on mandolin or in string playing), rapid alternation between two notes, or a wavering of volume, as produced by the tremolo stop on an organ or the electronic tremolo effect on guitar amplifiers.

Notation and technique

In notation, tremolo is indicated by slashes through the stem of a note, the number of slashes indicating the speed of repetition, or by a measured repetition written out in full. String players produce it with rapid short bow strokes; classical guitarists and mandolinists use fast repeated plucking — famously in Tárrega's Recuerdos de la Alhambra — to sustain a melody on instruments whose notes naturally decay.

Distinctions

Tremolo is often confused with vibrato: tremolo is a fluctuation of amplitude (volume), vibrato a fluctuation of pitch. On electric guitars, the whammy bar is (mis)named a "tremolo arm" although it produces vibrato, while the vibrato unit on some amplifiers is labelled "tremolo" — a long-standing terminological tangle popularized by Fender.
