Legolas is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, appearing as one of the nine members of the Fellowship of the Ring in The Lord of the Rings. He is a Sindarin Elf, the son of Thranduil, King of the Woodland Realm of Northern Mirkwood, and serves as the Fellowship's archer. His name means "green leaf" in Sindarin — leg (green) and golas (a collection of leaves).

Role in The Lord of the Rings

Legolas comes to Rivendell as a messenger bearing news of Gollum's escape from his father's custody, and is chosen at the Council of Elrond to represent the Elves in the Fellowship. Throughout the quest his keen eyesight, unmatched archery, and ability to walk lightly over snow prove invaluable; he shoots down a Ringwraith's flying steed on the Anduin and kills a Mumak-linked war host at the Pelennor. In Lothlórien, Galadriel gifts him a longbow of the Galadhrim.

His growing friendship with the Dwarf Gimli — beginning in rivalry, deepened by Gimli's courtesy in Lothlórien and their friendly kill-count contest at Helm's Deep — is one of the trilogy's central emotional arcs, helping to heal the ancient feud between Elves and Dwarves. After the War of the Ring, Legolas leads a company of Elves to Ithilien, and after Aragorn's death he sails over the Sea to the Undying Lands, taking Gimli with him — the only Dwarf so honoured.

Portrayal

Orlando Bloom portrayed Legolas in Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings film trilogy (2001–2003) and reprised the role in The Hobbit films, where the character was given an expanded part not present in the book. Tolkien commentators have noted Legolas's relatively sparse characterisation in the novel — his age, for instance, is never stated — compared with the films' emphasis on acrobatic combat.
