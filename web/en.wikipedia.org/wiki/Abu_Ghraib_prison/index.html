Abu Ghraib prison (Arabic: سجن أبو غريب) was a prison complex in Abu Ghraib, Iraq, located 32 kilometres (20 mi) west of Baghdad. Under Saddam Hussein it held political prisoners and was notorious for torture and executions. After the 2003 US-led invasion of Iraq, it was operated by Coalition forces and became infamous in 2004 when photographs of prisoner abuse by US military personnel became public.

History

The prison was built by British contractors in the 1950s and 1960s. Under Saddam Hussein's Ba'athist regime, it was run by the Directorate of General Security and held thousands of political prisoners, many of whom were tortured or executed. In 2002 Saddam granted a general amnesty that emptied the prison.

Torture and prisoner abuse scandal

In April 2004, CBS News and The New Yorker published photographs showing Iraqi detainees being abused and humiliated by American guards. The scandal sparked worldwide outrage; several soldiers were court-martialled and imprisoned, and official investigations, including the Taguba Report, found systematic abuse. The US transferred control of the facility to the Iraqi government in 2006.

Later use

Renamed Baghdad Central Prison, it reopened in 2009. It was closed in 2014 amid security concerns as the Islamic State advanced in Iraq.
