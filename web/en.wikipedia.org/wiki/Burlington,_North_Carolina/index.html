Burlington, North Carolina

Burlington is a city in Alamance and Guilford counties in the U.S. state of North Carolina. It is the principal city of the Burlington metropolitan statistical area, which encompasses all of Alamance County, and had a population of 57,303 at the 2020 census. The city lies in the Piedmont, roughly midway between Greensboro and Durham, and is served by Interstates 40 and 85.

History

The town originated in the 1850s as "Company Shops", the repair and maintenance centre built by the North Carolina Railroad roughly equidistant between Raleigh and Charlotte. The name, a rare example of a town literally named for a place of work, lasted until 1887 when residents adopted Burlington, reportedly after the Burlington railroad shops' reputation; the city was incorporated in 1893. Textile manufacturing came to dominate the economy — E. M. Holt's coloured-cotton mills dated from before the Civil War nearby — and in 1923 local hosiery businesses merged to form Burlington Mills, which grew under Spencer Love into Burlington Industries, for decades the world's largest textile company.

Economy and culture

After the decline of textiles, the economy diversified into healthcare (Alamance Regional Medical Center), laboratory services (Labcorp has major operations in the city), retail at Alamance Crossing and the Tanger Outlets in nearby Mebane, and light manufacturing. Elon University is located in the neighbouring town of Elon. The Burlington Royals (later Sock Puppets) played Appalachian League baseball at Burlington Athletic Stadium. Annual events include the Carousel Festival, named for the Dentzel menagerie carousel in City Park.
