KFC Corporation, doing business as KFC (also commonly referred to by its historical name Kentucky Fried Chicken), is an American fast food restaurant chain headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky, that specialises in fried chicken. It is the world's second-largest restaurant chain (as measured by sales) after McDonald's, with tens of thousands of locations across roughly 145 countries.

History

Harland Sanders, an entrepreneur who operated a service station in Corbin, Kentucky, began selling fried chicken from his roadside restaurant during the Great Depression. Sanders developed his "Original Recipe" of chicken seasoned with eleven herbs and spices and cooked in a pressure fryer, which he patented in 1940. The first Kentucky Fried Chicken franchise opened in Salt Lake City, Utah, in 1952, operated by Pete Harman. Sanders adopted the persona of a Kentucky colonel — an honorary title granted by the state — and became the brand's enduring mascot.

Sanders sold the company in 1964 to a group led by John Y. Brown Jr. and Jack Massey. The chain passed through Heublein and PepsiCo before being spun off in 1997 into what became Yum! Brands, KFC's present parent. KFC was an early entrant into international markets and, in 1987, became one of the first Western fast-food chains in China, where it grew to become the country's largest restaurant chain.

Products and marketing

The core product is Original Recipe chicken on the bone, sold in buckets for family meals, alongside chicken sandwiches (such as the Zinger), chicken strips, popcorn chicken, pot pies and sides including fries, coleslaw and mashed potatoes with gravy; menus are heavily localised abroad. Long-running slogans include "Finger Lickin' Good". Since 1991 the company has primarily branded itself by the initials KFC, and advertising campaigns have featured a rotating cast of actors portraying Colonel Sanders.
