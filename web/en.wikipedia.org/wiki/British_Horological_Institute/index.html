The British Horological Institute (BHI) is the national organisation representing the horological profession in the United Kingdom — the makers, repairers and restorers of clocks and watches. Founded in 1858, it is one of the world's oldest horological institutes and is responsible for setting professional standards, providing qualifications and training, and promoting the craft and science of timekeeping.

History

The BHI was founded at a time when the British watch and clock industry, centred on Clerkenwell in London, Coventry and Liverpool (Prescot), faced growing competition from Swiss and American factory production. Its founders sought to raise standards of workmanship and theoretical knowledge among British horologists. Its monthly magazine, the Horological Journal, first published in September 1858, is regarded as the world's oldest technical trade journal in continuous publication. In 1973 the Institute moved its headquarters to Upton Hall, a Georgian country house near Newark-on-Trent in Nottinghamshire, which also houses its museum and library.

Activities

The Institute runs examinations and distance-learning courses in clock and watch servicing and repair, leading to professional grades of membership (indicated by post-nominals such as MBHI and FBHI). It maintains a register of accredited repairers, publishes the Horological Journal, and operates the Museum of Timekeeping at Upton Hall, whose collections include clocks, watches, tools and the speaking clock apparatus, together with an extensive specialist library. The BHI also maintains connections with the Worshipful Company of Clockmakers, founded by royal charter in 1631.
