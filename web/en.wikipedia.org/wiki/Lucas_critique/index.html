Lucas critique

Lucas critique
AuthorRobert Lucas Jr.
Publication"Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique" (1976)
FieldMacroeconomics, econometrics

The Lucas critique, named for American economist Robert Lucas Jr., argues that it is naive to predict the effects of a change in economic policy entirely on the basis of relationships observed in historical data. It was set out in Lucas's influential 1976 paper "Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique".

Statement

The parameters of conventional macroeconometric models are not structural — that is, invariant to policy — because the behaviour of economic agents depends on their expectations of future policy. If a policy changes, rational agents adjust their behaviour, altering the very relationships the model uses. For example, exploiting the Phillips curve trade-off between inflation and unemployment fails once workers and firms come to expect inflation and build it into wage and price decisions.

Impact

The critique, together with the rational expectations revolution, transformed macroeconomics. It discredited large-scale Keynesian econometric models for policy analysis and motivated "microfounded" modelling, in which aggregate outcomes are derived from optimising behaviour described by deep parameters — preferences, technology and constraints — that are presumed policy-invariant. Modern DSGE models are its descendants.

Criticism

Critics argue that the practical magnitude of expectation effects varies, that empirical macroeconometric models remain useful for short-run forecasting, and that the microfoundations adopted in response impose implausible assumptions of their own.
