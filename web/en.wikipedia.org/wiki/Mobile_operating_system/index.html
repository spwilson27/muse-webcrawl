Mobile operating system

A mobile operating system is an operating system designed for smartphones, tablets, and other mobile devices. It combines features of a personal computer operating system with additional features useful for mobile or handheld use, such as touchscreens, cellular radios, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, GPS, cameras, and sensors.

Market

The modern market is dominated by two platforms: Google's Android, based on the Linux kernel and used by many manufacturers, and Apple's iOS, used exclusively on Apple devices. Together they account for virtually all smartphones sold worldwide.

History

Early mobile operating systems included Palm OS, Symbian, BlackBerry OS, and Windows Mobile, which powered PDAs and early smartphones in the 1990s and 2000s. The launch of the iPhone in 2007 and of Android in 2008 reshaped the market around multitouch interfaces and app stores, and most rival platforms — including Windows Phone and BlackBerry 10 — were discontinued during the 2010s.

Characteristics

Mobile operating systems emphasize power management, touch-based interaction, sandboxed applications distributed through curated app stores, and integration with cloud services and mobile networks.
