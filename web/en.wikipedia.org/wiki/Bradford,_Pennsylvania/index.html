Bradford is a city in McKean County, Pennsylvania, United States, situated in the Tuna Creek valley in the Allegheny foothills near the New York state line, about 78 miles south of Buffalo. It is known as the birthplace of the American oil industry boom in northern Pennsylvania and as the home of the Zippo Manufacturing Company and W. R. Case & Sons Cutlery Co.

History

Settled in 1823 and originally called Littleton, Bradford grew slowly as a lumber village until oil was struck nearby in the 1870s. The Bradford oil field proved extraordinarily productive — by 1881 the region was producing an estimated 83 percent of the world's crude oil supply — and the town boomed, incorporating as a city in 1879. Bradford's crude, rich in high-grade Pennsylvania-grade oil, remained prized for lubricants long after production declined. The Bradford era also produced the first billion-dollar oil company and a legacy of petroleum engineering.

Economy and landmarks

Zippo, founded in 1932 by George G. Blaisdell, has made its windproof lighters in Bradford ever since; the Zippo/Case Museum downtown displays both Zippo lighters and Case knives. The University of Pittsburgh at Bradford, founded in 1963, is a regional campus of the University of Pittsburgh. The Penn-Brad Oil Museum, just south of the city, preserves the area's oil heritage, and the nearby Allegheny National Forest and Kinzua Bridge State Park draw visitors to the region.

Geography and climate

Bradford lies in a deep valley at an elevation of about 1,450 feet and is noted for its cold winters; the surrounding hills record some of the lowest temperatures in Pennsylvania. The city covers about 3.5 square miles. U.S. Route 219 passes through Bradford, and Bradford Regional Airport serves the area.
