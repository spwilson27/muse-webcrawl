General Instruction of the Roman Missal

[Image placeholder: Roman Missal]

The General Instruction of the Roman Missal (GIRM; Latin: Institutio Generalis Missalis Romani) is the detailed document governing the celebration of the Mass of the Roman Rite in the Catholic Church. It appears at the beginning of the Roman Missal and sets out the structure of the Mass, the roles of the priest, deacon, and lay faithful, the arrangement and furnishing of churches, and the norms for the choice and preparation of texts and liturgical objects.

History

The first GIRM accompanied the revised Missal promulgated by Pope Paul VI in 1969 as part of the liturgical reforms following the Second Vatican Council. It was revised for the 1975 second edition of the Missal and again, more substantially, for the third typical edition promulgated by Pope John Paul II in 2000 (published in 2002). Episcopal conferences produce official translations and may issue adaptations — for example, the version for dioceses in the United States includes adaptations approved by the Holy See, such as norms on posture and the purification of vessels.

Contents

The document's nine chapters cover the importance and dignity of the Eucharistic celebration; the structure, elements, and parts of the Mass (Introductory Rites, Liturgy of the Word, Liturgy of the Eucharist, Concluding Rites); the duties and ministries in the Mass; the different forms of celebration; the arrangement of churches, altar, ambo, and tabernacle; requirements for sacred vessels, vestments, and the bread and wine; the choice of Mass texts; and Masses for various needs and occasions, including Masses for the dead.
