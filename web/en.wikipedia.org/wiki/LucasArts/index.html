LucasArts

LucasArts, formerly Lucasfilm Games, was an American video game developer and publisher founded in 1982 by George Lucas as part of Lucasfilm. It became famous for its graphic adventure games of the late 1980s and 1990s and for numerous Star Wars and Indiana Jones titles. After Disney acquired Lucasfilm in 2012, LucasArts was shut down as an internal developer in April 2013 and survived as a licensor; the Lucasfilm Games brand was revived in 2021.

History

The division began inside Lucasfilm's Computer Division, producing games such as Rescue on Fractalus! and Ballblazer. Under the name Lucasfilm Games it released the adventure games Maniac Mansion (1987), built on the SCUMM engine, followed by The Secret of Monkey Island (1990), Indiana Jones and the Fate of Atlantis (1992), Day of the Tentacle (1993), Sam & Max Hit the Road (1993), Full Throttle (1995) and Grim Fandango (1998).

Renamed LucasArts in 1990, the company also found success with flight simulators such as Secret Weapons of the Luftwaffe and the X-Wing series, and later with action games including Star Wars: Dark Forces and shooters and strategy titles set in the Star Wars universe.

Legacy

LucasArts' adventure games are regarded as classics of the genre, noted for humour, strong writing and a design philosophy that avoided player death and dead ends. Many of its designers, including Ron Gilbert and Tim Schafer, went on to influential careers elsewhere in the industry.
