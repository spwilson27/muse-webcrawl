Dangerous goods (DG), often called hazardous materials or hazmat in North America, are substances or articles that pose a risk to health, safety, property or the environment during transport. Their carriage is regulated internationally through the UN Recommendations on the Transport of Dangerous Goods (the "Orange Book") and modal rules such as the IMDG Code for sea and IATA DGR for air.

Classification

Dangerous goods are divided into nine hazard classes: 1 explosives; 2 gases; 3 flammable liquids; 4 flammable solids; 5 oxidizers and organic peroxides; 6 toxic and infectious substances; 7 radioactive material; 8 corrosive substances; and 9 miscellaneous dangerous substances and articles, including lithium batteries. Each entry has a four-digit UN number (for example UN 1203 for petrol) used on labels, placards and transport documents.

Regulation and handling

Rules govern classification, packaging, labelling, documentation, training and emergency response. In Europe, road transport follows the ADR agreement; in the United States, Title 49 of the Code of Federal Regulations. GHS pictograms serve a related classification system for supply and workplace use.
