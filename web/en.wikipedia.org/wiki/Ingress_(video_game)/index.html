Ingress (video game)

Ingress (or Ingress Prime since 2018) is an augmented reality (AR) mobile game developed and published by Niantic for Android and iOS. First released in closed beta in November 2012, it is a location-based game in which players physically move through the real world to interact with virtual "portals" located at places of public interest such as statues, murals, libraries, and historical markers. Ingress is widely regarded as a pioneer of location-based gaming and laid the groundwork for Niantic's later hit Pokémon Go, which reused much of the portal database as PokéStops and Gyms.

Gameplay

Players join one of two factions: the Enlightened, who believe the exotic matter (XM) leaking through portals can uplift humanity, and the Resistance, who seek to protect humanity from it. Using the in-game scanner map, players visit portals in the real world and "hack" them for items, deploy resonators to claim them for their faction, and link three portals to create triangular control fields that score "mind units" for their team. Higher-level actions, cooperative "operations," and global scoring cycles encourage organized team play.

Story

The game's backstory involves the discovery of exotic matter at CERN and the machinations of the fictional Niantic Project. The narrative has been extended through novels, comics, live events, and an anime series, Ingress: The Animation, released in 2018.

Events and community

Niantic organizes large real-world events called Anomalies, in which thousands of players compete at specific cities, along with Mission Days and First Saturday events. The game built a devoted global community and encouraged exploration, exercise, and local organizing, themes Niantic carried into its later titles.

Ingress Prime

In November 2018 Niantic relaunched the game as Ingress Prime, rebuilt on the Unity engine with a redesigned interface. The original scanner application was retired in September 2019.
