Grenoble (French pronunciation: [ɡʁənɔbl]) is a city in southeastern France, at the foot of the French Alps where the river Drac joins the Isère. It is the prefecture of the Isère department and a major centre of scientific research, higher education and high technology, often described as the "capital of the Alps."

History

Founded as the Gallic settlement of Cularo and renamed Gratianopolis under Rome, Grenoble grew as a fortified town on the route into Italy. In 1788 its "Day of the Tiles" — a popular uprising against royal troops — became one of the harbingers of the French Revolution. The city expanded in the 19th and 20th centuries on glove-making, hydroelectric power ("white coal") pioneered in the surrounding valleys, and later on physics and electronics research. It hosted the 1968 Winter Olympics.

Science and modern city

Grenoble is one of Europe's leading research hubs, hosting the European Synchrotron Radiation Facility, the Institut Laue–Langevin neutron source, the Minatec micro- and nanotechnology campus and the Université Grenoble Alpes, with tens of thousands of students. The Bastille fortress above the old town, reached by the famous spherical-cabin cable car ("Les Bulles"), overlooks a city that has also become a showcase for green urban policy, including extensive tramways and cycling infrastructure.
