Military Assistance Command, Vietnam

Active: 8 February 1962 – 29 March 1973
Country: United States
Headquarters: Tan Son Nhut Air Base, Saigon, South Vietnam
Commanders: Paul D. Harkins (1962–64); William Westmoreland (1964–68); Creighton Abrams (1968–72); Frederick C. Weyand (1972–73)
Engagements: Vietnam War

The United States Military Assistance Command, Vietnam (MACV) was a joint-service command of the United States Department of Defense, established on 8 February 1962, in response to the increase in United States military assistance to the Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam). MACV directed all U.S. military advisory and assistance efforts in South Vietnam and, as American involvement escalated, came to command the great majority of U.S. combat forces in the country.

History

MACV succeeded and absorbed the functions of the Military Assistance Advisory Group, Vietnam (MAAG Vietnam). Its first commander was General Paul D. Harkins. In June 1964 he was succeeded by General William Westmoreland, under whom American ground combat forces grew to more than half a million personnel at their 1969 peak. Major subordinate commands included U.S. Army Vietnam (USARV), the Naval Forces Vietnam, the Seventh Air Force, III Marine Amphibious Force, and the I and II Field Forces.

MACV headquarters were located at Tan Son Nhut Air Base near Saigon; the headquarters complex was nicknamed "Pentagon East". Creighton Abrams took command in 1968 and oversaw the policy of Vietnamization and the progressive withdrawal of U.S. forces. Following the Paris Peace Accords of January 1973, MACV was disestablished on 29 March 1973, its remaining functions passing to a Defense Attaché Office.

Studies and Observations Group

MACV included a covert component, the Studies and Observations Group (MACV-SOG), formed in 1964 to conduct clandestine cross-border reconnaissance and unconventional warfare operations in Laos, Cambodia, and North Vietnam. Its existence remained classified for many years after the war.
