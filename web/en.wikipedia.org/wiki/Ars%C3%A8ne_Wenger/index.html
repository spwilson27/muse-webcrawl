Arsène Charles Ernest Wenger (born 22 October 1949) is a French football manager and former player, best known for managing Arsenal from 1996 to 2018 — the longest-serving and most successful manager in the club's history at the time of his departure.

Arsenal years

Wenger arrived in England in 1996 as a relatively unknown figure and revolutionised English football with new methods in diet, training, scouting and sports science. He won three Premier League titles and seven FA Cups, including league-and-cup doubles in 1998 and 2002. His 2003–04 "Invincibles" side went an entire Premier League season unbeaten, a run that stretched to a record 49 league matches.

Earlier career

Before Arsenal, Wenger won the French league title with Monaco in 1988 and spent a successful spell in Japan with Nagoya Grampus Eight. His modest playing career included a league title with Strasbourg in 1979.

After management

In 2019 Wenger became FIFA's Chief of Global Football Development. He has been a prominent voice on the future of the game, including proposals concerning the offside law and the international match calendar.
