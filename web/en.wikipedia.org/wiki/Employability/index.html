Employability

Employability

Field: Human resource management, labour economics, education policy

Components: Skills, knowledge, personal attributes, career self-management

Related: Human capital, lifelong learning, graduate employability

Employability refers to the attributes, skills, and knowledge that enable a person to gain employment, maintain it, and obtain new employment if required. It encompasses not only technical and academic qualifications but also transferable ("soft") skills, personal qualities, and the ability to present oneself effectively to employers. The concept became central to labour-market and higher-education policy from the 1990s onward, as job security gave way to careers spanning multiple employers.

Definitions

A widely used definition is that of Mantz Yorke: employability is "a set of achievements — skills, understandings and personal attributes — that makes graduates more likely to gain employment and be successful in their chosen occupations, which benefits themselves, the workforce, the community and the economy." Scholars such as Ronald McQuaid and Colin Lindsay emphasise that employability also depends on external factors — local labour demand, transport, childcare, and employer attitudes — and not solely on the individual.

Models

Several frameworks have been proposed, including the "USEM" model (understanding, skills, efficacy beliefs, metacognition) and Dacre Pool and Sewell's CareerEDGE model, which places self-efficacy, self-confidence, and self-esteem at the base of five components: career development learning, experience, degree subject knowledge, generic skills, and emotional intelligence.

Policy significance

Governments and universities use employability to justify investment in education and training, and employers' surveys commonly report that graduates lack workplace skills such as communication, teamwork, and problem-solving. Critics argue that the discourse places responsibility for unemployment on individuals while downplaying structural conditions in the labour market.
