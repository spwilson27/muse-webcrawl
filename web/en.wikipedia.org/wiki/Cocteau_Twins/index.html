Cocteau Twins

Cocteau Twins were a Scottish rock band active from 1979 to 1997. They were formed in Grangemouth by Robin Guthrie (guitars, drum machine) and Will Heggie (bass), with Elizabeth Fraser (vocals) joining in 1981. The band's signature sound—Guthrie's densely processed guitars and Fraser's distinctive, often wordless vocals—was foundational to the dream pop and ethereal wave genres.

History

The band took its name from an early song by fellow Scots Simple Minds, itself referencing Jean Cocteau. Signed to the independent label 4AD, they released their debut album Garlands (1982), a stark post-punk record. After Heggie's departure, multi-instrumentalist Simon Raymonde joined in 1984, consolidating the classic trio line-up.

Through the 1980s the band refined a lush, layered sound on albums including Treasure (1984), Victorialand (1986), and Blue Bell Knoll (1988). Heaven or Las Vegas (1990) is widely regarded as their masterpiece, balancing accessibility with their trademark abstraction; Fraser's lyrics, a mixture of English, invented words, and glossolalia, became somewhat more intelligible. The band left 4AD for Fontana in the early 1990s, releasing Four-Calendar Café (1993) and Milk & Kisses (1996) before splitting in 1997 amid the breakdown of Fraser and Guthrie's relationship.

Legacy

Cocteau Twins profoundly influenced shoegaze, dream pop, and ambient pop; Fraser later sang on Massive Attack's hit "Teardrop" (1998). A planned reunion for the 2005 Coachella festival was cancelled, and the members have pursued separate projects, with Raymonde running the Bella Union label.
