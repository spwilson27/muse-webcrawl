Tisha B'Av

Observed byJews
TypeDay of mourning and fasting
Date9 Av (Hebrew calendar)

Tisha B'Av (Hebrew: תִּשְׁעָה בְּאָב, "the ninth of Av") is an annual fast day in Judaism, on which a number of disasters in Jewish history are commemorated, primarily the destruction of both Solomon's Temple by the Neo-Babylonian Empire and the Second Temple by the Roman Empire in Jerusalem.

Observance

Tisha B'Av falls in July or August in the Gregorian calendar. It is a twenty-five-hour fast accompanied by prohibitions similar to those of Yom Kippur, including abstaining from washing, wearing leather shoes, and marital relations. The Book of Lamentations, believed to mourn the destruction of Jerusalem, is read aloud in synagogue with a melancholy chant, together with kinnot (dirges). The period leading up to the fast, the Three Weeks and the Nine Days, is marked by customs of mourning.

Events commemorated

Tradition holds that both Temples were destroyed on the ninth of Av — the First Temple in 586 BCE and the Second in 70 CE. Later calamities, including the fall of Betar in the Bar Kokhba revolt (135 CE), the expulsion of the Jews from England (1290) and from Spain (1492), were also associated with the date.
