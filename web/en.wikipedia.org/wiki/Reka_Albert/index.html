Réka Albert

Réka Albert (born 1972) is a Romanian-Hungarian scientist and professor of physics and biology at Pennsylvania State University. She is known for her work in network science, in particular the Barabási–Albert model of scale-free networks, which she developed with Albert-László Barabási while a PhD student at the University of Notre Dame.

Work

Albert's 1999 paper with Barabási, "Emergence of Scaling in Random Networks" (Science), and their 2002 review in Reviews of Modern Physics, are among the most cited works in network science. The Barabási–Albert model showed that growth and preferential attachment produce degree distributions following a power law, explaining the prevalence of hubs in networks such as the World Wide Web, metabolic networks, and citation networks. Her later research has applied network methods to systems biology, including Boolean network models of cellular signalling and the identification of control targets in biological networks.
