Failure to thrive

Failure to thrive (FTT), also known as faltering growth or weight faltering, indicates insufficient weight gain or absence of appropriate physical growth in children. It is diagnosed when a child's weight or rate of weight gain is significantly below that of similar children, commonly defined by growth falling across two or more percentile lines on standard growth charts, or weight below the 3rd–5th percentile.

Causes

Causes are traditionally divided into organic (medical) and non-organic (psychosocial/environmental), though the two often coexist. Inadequate caloric intake is the most common final pathway: feeding difficulties, poverty and food insecurity, neglect, or errors in formula preparation. Other mechanisms include inadequate absorption (coeliac disease, cystic fibrosis), increased metabolic demands (congenital heart disease, chronic infection), and defective utilisation of nutrients (genetic or metabolic disorders).

Management

Evaluation focuses on a detailed dietary and social history, physical examination, and growth parameters, with targeted laboratory testing rather than broad screening. Treatment addresses the underlying cause and provides nutritional rehabilitation, often with a multidisciplinary team including a paediatrician, dietitian, and social worker. Most children are managed as outpatients; hospitalisation is reserved for severe malnutrition, suspected abuse or neglect, or failed outpatient management. The term is also used in geriatrics for a syndrome of weight loss, decreased appetite and functional decline in older adults.
