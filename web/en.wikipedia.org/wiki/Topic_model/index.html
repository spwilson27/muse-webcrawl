Topic model

FieldMachine learning, natural language processing
Key modelsLSA, pLSA, LDA, HDP
Key peopleDavid Blei, Andrew Ng, Michael I. Jordan

In machine learning and natural language processing, a topic model is a type of statistical model for discovering the abstract "topics" that occur in a collection of documents. A topic model is a frequently used text-mining tool for discovering hidden semantic structures in a text body. Intuitively, given that a document is about a particular topic, one would expect particular words to appear more or less frequently: "dog" and "bone" will appear more often in documents about dogs, "cat" and "meow" in documents about cats, while words like "the" and "is" appear roughly equally in both.

History and models

Early approaches include latent semantic analysis (LSA), which factorises a term–document matrix by singular value decomposition, and probabilistic LSA (pLSA), introduced by Thomas Hofmann in 1999. The most widely used topic model, latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA), was introduced by David Blei, Andrew Ng and Michael I. Jordan in 2003. LDA treats each document as a mixture of topics and each topic as a distribution over words, both drawn from Dirichlet priors, and learns them by variational inference or Gibbs sampling. Extensions include the hierarchical Dirichlet process, which infers the number of topics, correlated and dynamic topic models, and neural topic models.

Applications

Topic models are used to organise digital libraries, trace the evolution of scientific fields (as in large-scale analyses of journal archives and of the journal Science), analyse historical newspaper corpora in the digital humanities, and explore legal and social-media text. Evaluation uses held-out likelihood and coherence measures, though human judgement of topic quality remains essential.
