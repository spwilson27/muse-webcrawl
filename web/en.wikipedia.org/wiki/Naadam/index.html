Naadam

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Naadam (Mongolian: Наадам, "the three games of men") is a traditional festival celebrated in Mongolia, Inner Mongolia and Tuva. The festival is also locally termed "eriin gurvan naadam" (эрийн гурван наадам), "the three games of men". The games are Mongolian wrestling, horse racing, and archery, and are held throughout the country during midsummer. Women have started participating in the archery and horseracing games, but not in Mongolian wrestling.

In 2010, Naadam was inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Overview

The biggest festival, the National Naadam, is held in the Mongolian capital Ulaanbaatar during the national holiday from 11 to 13 July, in the National Sports Stadium. It begins with an elaborate introduction ceremony featuring dancers, athletes, horse riders, and musicians. After the ceremony, the competitions begin.

Naadam is the most widely watched festival among Mongols and is believed to have existed for centuries in one fashion or another. It has its origins in the activities, such as military parades and sporting competitions, conducted following the celebration of various occasions, including weddings or spiritual gatherings. It later served as a way to train soldiers for battle and was also connected to Mongols' nomadic lifestyle. Mongols follow a written legacy of Naadam, and folk songs, dance, and oral history preserve its rituals.

The three games

Wrestling

512 or 1024 wrestlers meet in a single-elimination tournament that lasts nine or ten rounds. Mongolian traditional wrestling is an untimed competition in which wrestlers lose if they touch the ground with any part of the body other than the feet or the hands. Winners earn honorary titles based on the number of rounds won, ranging from Falcon and Elephant up to Lion and the highest title, Champion (avarga).

Horse racing

Unlike Western horse racing, which consists of short sprints generally not much longer than 2 km, Mongolian horse racing as featured in Naadam is a cross-country event, with races 15–30 km long. The length of each race is determined by age class; horses are ridden by children between 5 and 13 years old. The winning horse is honoured with songs, and the top five finishers are given the traditional beverage airag (fermented mare's milk).

Archery

Competitors shoot arrows at cylindrical targets ("suren") arranged along a low wall. Men shoot from 75 metres and women from 65 metres. Archers wear traditional dress and use recurve bows made of horn, bark, and wood. Teams are greeted with the cry of "uukhai" when an arrow hits the target.

History

The earliest evidence of Naadam-like games appears on Bronze Age rock drawings in the Mongolian steppe. The festival became closely associated with the commemoration of the 1921 Mongolian revolution and the founding of the Mongolian People's Republic, which is why the national games are held on 11–13 July, overlapping with Revolution Day celebrations.
