Housing Benefit

Housing Benefit is a means-tested social security benefit in the United Kingdom, administered by local authorities, that helps people on low incomes pay their rent. Introduced nationally in 1982–83, it is available to tenants in both the social rented sector (council and housing association homes) and the private rented sector, and is one of the largest items of UK welfare spending, claimed by several million households.

Eligibility and calculation

Entitlement depends on the claimant's income, savings (usually disqualifying above £16,000), rent level and household circumstances. For private tenants, the amount is capped by the Local Housing Allowance (LHA), based on percentile rents in the local market area and the number of bedrooms the household is deemed to need. Since 2013, working-age social tenants have had their benefit reduced for spare bedrooms under the policy formally called the removal of the spare room subsidy, widely known as the "bedroom tax".

Reform and Universal Credit

Under the welfare reforms of the 2010s, Housing Benefit is being replaced for most working-age claimants by the housing costs element of Universal Credit, administered by the Department for Work and Pensions. Housing Benefit remains in place for pension-age claimants and for people in supported, sheltered and temporary accommodation.
