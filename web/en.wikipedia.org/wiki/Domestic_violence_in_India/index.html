Domestic violence in India

Domestic violence in India includes any form of violence suffered by a person from a biological relative, but typically is the violence suffered by a woman by male members of her family or relatives. According to a National Family and Health Survey in 2005, total lifetime prevalence of domestic violence was 33.5% and 8.5% for sexual violence among women aged 15–49; later surveys (NFHS-5, 2019–21) found that around 30% of ever-married women in that age group had experienced spousal violence.

Forms

Forms include physical, sexual, verbal and emotional abuse, economic abuse, and dowry-related violence, including dowry deaths and acid attacks. Dowry harassment, though prohibited by the Dowry Prohibition Act 1961, remains a major category of reported offences.

Law

Section 498A of the Indian Penal Code (now carried into the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita) criminalises cruelty by a husband or his relatives. The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005 provides civil remedies — protection orders, residence orders, monetary relief and custody — to women in domestic relationships, including live-in relationships. India also operates helplines (notably 181) and One Stop Centres (Sakhi) for survivors.
