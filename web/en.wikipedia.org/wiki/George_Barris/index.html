George Barris

George Barris
Born: November 20, 1925, Chicago, Illinois, U.S.
Died: November 5, 2015 (aged 89), Encino, California, U.S.
Occupation: Automobile customizer, builder

George Barris (born George Salapatas; November 20, 1925 – November 5, 2015) was an American designer and builder of many famous Hollywood custom cars, most notably the Batmobile used in the 1960s Batman television series, the Munster Koach for The Munsters, and the vehicles used in The Beverly Hillbillies. He was widely known as the self-proclaimed "King of the Kustomizers".

Early life and career

Barris was born in Chicago and moved with his family to California as a child. Together with his older brother Sam, he developed an early passion for modifying cars, restoring a 1925 Buick and then customising a 1929 Model A Ford. The brothers opened Barris Brothers' Custom Shop in Bell, California, in the mid-1940s, later moving to Lynwood and then North Hollywood. Their work — chopped tops, frenched headlights, elaborate candy paint — helped define the Southern California custom-car scene of the late 1940s and 1950s. The influential "Hirohata Merc", a 1951 Mercury built for Bob Hirohata, is often cited as one of the defining custom cars of the era.

Hollywood cars

Barris's shop, Barris Kustom Industries, became the go-to builder for film and television producers. In 1965, producer William Dozier gave Barris just three weeks to create a Batmobile for the upcoming Batman series; Barris converted his 1955 Lincoln Futura concept car into the now-iconic vehicle. Other famous creations include the Munster Koach and Drag-U-La for The Munsters, the truck for The Beverly Hillbillies, the futuristic car driven in Knight Rider-era productions involving his designs, and custom cars for celebrities such as Elvis Presley, Frank Sinatra, and Zsa Zsa Gabor.

Legacy

Barris promoted custom-car culture for decades through car shows, model-kit licensing, and publishing. Some of his claims to have created particular cars — including aspects of the Batmobile's history and the Knight Rider KITT cars — have been debated by historians and other builders. He died at his home in Encino, California, in 2015, shortly before his 90th birthday.
