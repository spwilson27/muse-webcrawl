Roslin, Midlothian

Roslin (formerly Rosslyn) is a village in Midlothian, Scotland, about seven miles south of Edinburgh. It is famous for Rosslyn Chapel, founded in 1446, and for the Roslin Institute, where Dolly the sheep, the first mammal cloned from an adult cell, was created in 1996.

History

The name may derive from the Celtic ros (moor) and linn (waterfall). Roslin was the site of the Battle of Roslin in 1303, during the First War of Scottish Independence, in which a smaller Scottish force defeated an English army. The village later grew around coal mining, carpet manufacture and a gunpowder mill established in 1803.

Rosslyn Chapel

Founded by William Sinclair, 1st Earl of Caithness, Rosslyn Chapel (the Collegiate Chapel of St Matthew) is renowned for its elaborate stone carvings, including the Apprentice Pillar. It gained worldwide fame through Dan Brown's novel The Da Vinci Code (2003) and its film adaptation.

Roslin Institute

The Roslin Institute, an animal sciences research institute now part of the University of Edinburgh, moved to the Easter Bush campus near the village. In 1996 Ian Wilmut, Keith Campbell and colleagues there produced Dolly the sheep.
