Cyprus problem


Map of divided Cyprus and the UN buffer zone (placeholder image)

The Cyprus problem, also known as the Cyprus conflict or Cyprus issue, is an ongoing dispute between Greek Cypriots and Turkish Cypriots over the governance and division of the island of Cyprus. It has persisted since the 1960s and led to the island's de facto division in 1974.

Background

Cyprus gained independence from Britain in 1960 under a power-sharing constitution guaranteed by Greece, Turkey and the United Kingdom. Intercommunal violence in 1963–64 collapsed the arrangement and brought a UN peacekeeping force (UNFICYP). In July 1974 a coup by Greek Cypriot nationalists seeking union with Greece prompted Turkey's military intervention; the island was partitioned along the Green Line, with mass displacement on both sides. In 1983 the Turkish Cypriot side unilaterally declared the "Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus," recognized only by Turkey.

Settlement efforts

The internationally recognized Republic of Cyprus joined the European Union in 2004, the same year Greek Cypriot voters rejected the UN's Annan Plan in a referendum while Turkish Cypriots approved it. Successive rounds of reunification talks based on a bizonal, bicommunal federation — most recently at Crans-Montana in 2017 — have failed, leaving the division in place.
