Sápmi is the cultural region traditionally inhabited by the Sámi people. It stretches across northern Norway, Sweden, and Finland and Russia's Kola Peninsula — an area formerly often called "Lapland", a term now considered outdated or offensive by many Sámi.

Geography and culture

The region covers roughly 389,000 square kilometres of subarctic and boreal terrain. The Sámi speak a family of Uralic languages and maintain a distinct culture centred historically on reindeer herding, coastal fishing, and hunting, with the traditional costume (gákti) and joik singing as emblematic expressions.

Political representation

Sámi parliaments in Norway, Sweden, and Finland represent Sámi interests, and the Sámi flag and national day (6 February) are shared across the four states. Land and resource rights, including disputes over mining, wind power, and reindeer pasture, remain central political questions.
