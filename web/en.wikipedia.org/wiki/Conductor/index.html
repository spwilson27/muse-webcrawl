Conductor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Conductors lead orchestras, choirs, bands, and opera performances

A conductor is a person who directs a musical ensemble — such as an orchestra, choir, concert band, or opera company — during rehearsal and performance. The conductor uses gestures, typically with a baton, to set tempo, cue entrances, and shape interpretation, a practice described in the article on conducting.

The term "conductor" is also used in other fields:

Other meanings

Electrical conductor — a material, such as copper or aluminum, that allows the flow of electric current with low resistance; contrasted with an insulator. Electrical conductors are used in wires, cables, and components, and their conductivity depends on the material and temperature.

Train conductor — a railway employee responsible for the operation and safety of a train, including managing the crew, checking tickets, and making announcements; in North America the conductor, rather than the engineer (driver), is in overall charge of the train.

Bus conductor — historically, a person who sells and checks tickets on a bus or tram, largely replaced by automated fare collection in many systems.

Lightning conductor — a lightning rod: a metal rod mounted on a structure and connected to earth to protect the building from lightning strikes.

In physics and engineering, conductors are also classified as thermal conductors, and superconductors carry current with zero resistance below a critical temperature.
