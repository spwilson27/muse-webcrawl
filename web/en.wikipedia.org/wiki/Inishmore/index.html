Inishmore

Native name: Árainn / Inis Mór
Location: Galway Bay, Atlantic Ocean
Archipelago: Aran Islands
Area: 31 km²
Population: ~840

Inishmore (Irish: Árainn, or Inis Mór, meaning "the great island") is the largest of the Aran Islands in Galway Bay, County Galway, on the west coast of Ireland. With a population of about 840, it is the largest island in the group and a popular tourist destination, reachable by ferry from Rossaveal and Doolin, and by air from Inverin.

Geology and landscape

Like the other Aran Islands, Inishmore is an extension of the Burren in County Clare, composed of karst limestone formed under a tropical sea some 350 million years ago. The island is famous for its maze of dry stone walls dividing small fields, and for landscapes of bare limestone pavement scored by deep fissures known as grykes.

Historic sites

The island's best-known monument is Dún Aonghasa, a semicircular stone fort perched on a 100-metre cliff, dating in part to the Bronze Age and often described as one of the finest prehistoric forts in Europe. Other notable sites include Dún Eoghanachta, Dún Eochla, the Black Fort (Dún Dúcathair), the early Christian church Teampall Bheanáin — among the smallest churches in the world — and the Worm Hole (Poll na bPéist), a natural rectangular tidal pool at the base of the cliffs.

Culture

Inishmore is part of the Gaeltacht, and Irish is the everyday language of most islanders. The island is known for traditional Aran knitwear, particularly the Aran sweater with its distinctive cable patterns. The main settlement is Kilronan (Cill Rónáin), where ferries arrive. Tourism, fishing and farming are the principal economic activities.
