AbodeThrace; the heavens
ParentsAstraeus and Eos
Principal membersBoreas (north), Notus (south), Eurus (east), Zephyrus (west)

The Anemoi (Greek: Ἄνεμοι, "Winds") are the gods of the winds in Greek mythology, each assigned to a cardinal direction from which his wind blows. The four principal Anemoi are Boreas, the cold north wind of winter; Notus, the south wind bringing the storms of late summer and autumn; Eurus, the unlucky east wind; and Zephyrus, the gentle west wind of spring. They were the sons of the dawn goddess Eos and the Titan Astraeus.

The four winds

Boreas, the strongest and most violent, was said to live in Thrace; he abducted the Athenian princess Orithyia and was honoured in Athens after his winds destroyed Persian ships. Zephyrus, the mildest, was the husband of Chloris (Flora) and, in one myth, the rival of Apollo for the love of Hyacinthus. Notus brought fog and rain-laden storms feared by sailors, while Eurus, associated with bad luck, was omitted from some lists of the winds.

Other winds and cult

Minor Anemoi governed the intermediate directions — Kaikias (north-east), Apeliotes (south-east), Skiron (north-west) and Lips (south-west) — and all eight are carved on the Tower of the Winds in Athens, a 1st-century BC horologion topped by a weather-vane Triton. The winds were kept in a cave by Aeolus, their appointed ruler in the Odyssey, who gave Odysseus a bag containing all but the west wind. In Roman mythology the winds were the Venti, including Aquilo (Boreas) and Favonius (Zephyrus).

In art

The Anemoi are depicted as winged men, sometimes with billowing cheeks blowing gusts; Boreas carried off Orithyia on the chest of the Delian League treasures, and Botticelli's Primavera shows Zephyrus seizing Chloris at the painting's right edge.
