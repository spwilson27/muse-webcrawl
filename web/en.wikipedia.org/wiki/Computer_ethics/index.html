Computer ethics

Computer ethics is a part of practical philosophy concerned with how computing professionals should make decisions regarding professional and social conduct. It addresses moral issues arising from the use of computers and information technology, including privacy, intellectual property, accountability for software errors, and the social effects of computing.

History

The field's foundations were laid in the 1940s by Norbert Wiener, whose work on cybernetics considered the social consequences of computing. In the 1970s Donn Parker examined computer-related crime, and Walter Maner coined the term "computer ethics". James Moor's influential 1985 paper "What Is Computer Ethics?" argued that computers generate "policy vacuums" and "conceptual muddles" requiring new ethical analysis. Deborah Johnson's textbook of the same era helped establish the subject academically.

Key issues

Major topics include data privacy and surveillance; copyright, software piracy, and open licensing; computer crime and security responsibilities; accuracy and reliability of information; automation and employment; the digital divide; and, more recently, algorithmic bias and the ethics of artificial intelligence. Professional bodies such as the ACM and IEEE publish codes of ethics for computing practitioners.
