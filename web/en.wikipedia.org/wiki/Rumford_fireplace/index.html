Rumford fireplace

The Rumford fireplace is a tall, shallow fireplace designed by Sir Benjamin Thompson, Count Rumford, an Anglo-American physicist best known for his work on heat. Its design, published in the 1790s, greatly increased the efficiency of fireplaces and reduced smoke in rooms.

Design

The key features of the Rumford design are a shallow firebox, angled ("splayed") sides that reflect radiant heat into the room, a rounded throat above the fire, and a narrow, streamlined flue opening that encourages smoke to rise while reducing turbulence that would push smoke into the room. Rumford's principles were derived from his experiments on the nature of heat.

Influence

Rumford fireplaces became extremely popular in Britain and America in the early 19th century; Thomas Jefferson had them installed at Monticello. They remain valued in restoration work for their efficiency and clean-burning character.
