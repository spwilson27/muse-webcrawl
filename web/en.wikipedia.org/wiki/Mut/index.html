Mut

Mut, also known as Maut and Mout, was an ancient Egyptian mother goddess with many aspects and attributes that changed over the thousands of years of the culture's history. She was considered a primal deity, associated with the waters of Nu from which everything was born. Her name in Egyptian means "mother", and she was initially the female counterpart of the solar creator; at Thebes she became the wife of Amun and mother of Khonsu, forming the Theban Triad.

Depictions

Mut was usually depicted as a woman wearing the double crown (the combined crowns of Upper and Lower Egypt) and a dress, holding the was sceptre and an ankh. Earlier she had been associated with a vulture headdress, vultures being symbols of maternity in Egyptian writing — the hieroglyph for mut ("mother") was a vulture. She could also appear as a lioness, in her fierce aspect associated with the Eye of Ra and with the goddess Sekhmet.

Worship

Mut's principal temple was the Precinct of Mut at Karnak in Thebes, south of the main Amun precinct, with its crescent-shaped sacred lake, the Isheru. She was honoured during the New Kingdom and later periods with festivals in which her statue was carried in a barque across the lake. As Amun rose to national prominence, Mut correspondingly gained importance as his consort, sometimes absorbing or overlapping with other goddesses such as Bastet and Sekhmet. Hundreds of statues of Mut in her lioness form were erected at her Karnak temple under Amenhotep III.
