The Battle of Uman was a major encirclement battle fought on the Eastern Front of World War II in the summer of 1941, during the opening weeks of Operation Barbarossa, Nazi Germany's invasion of the Soviet Union. Around the Ukrainian town of Uman, forces of the German Army Group South closed a pocket around two Soviet armies.

Background

Army Group South, under Field Marshal Gerd von Rundstedt, advanced into Ukraine with the objective of destroying Soviet forces west of the Dnieper River. The Soviet Southwestern Front, commanded by Mikhail Kirponos, and the Southern Front under Ivan Tyulenev attempted to hold a line along the Bug River and then fall back in an orderly fashion.

The encirclement

The 1st Panzer Group under Ewald von Kleist broke through Soviet defences and drove south-east, while the German 17th Army pressed from the west and the 6th Army applied pressure from the north. The pincers met near Uman in early August, trapping the Soviet 6th and 12th Armies, commanded by Ivan Muzychenko and Pavel Ponedelin. Repeated Soviet breakout attempts failed under heavy air attack by the Luftwaffe.

Outcome

By 8 August 1941 the pocket had been eliminated. German reports claimed roughly 103,000 prisoners, including both army commanders, along with large quantities of tanks and artillery. The destruction of the two armies opened the way for the German advance to the Dnieper crossings and set the stage for the still larger encirclement at Kiev the following month.

Significance

Uman was one of the first of the great "cauldron" battles (Kesselschlachten) of 1941, in which German armoured spearheads encircled entire Soviet armies. Despite these catastrophic losses, the Red Army continued to raise new formations, and the delays imposed on Army Group South contributed to disputes within the German high command over whether to prioritise Moscow or Ukraine.
