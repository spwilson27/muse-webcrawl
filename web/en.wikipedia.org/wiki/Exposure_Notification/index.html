The Exposure Notification system (originally the Privacy-Preserving Contact Tracing Project) is a framework developed jointly by Apple and Google to support digital contact tracing during the COVID-19 pandemic. Announced on 10 April 2020 and released on 20 May 2020, it provided an application programming interface that public-health apps could use on iOS and Android.

How it works

The system uses Bluetooth Low Energy to exchange rotating anonymous proximity identifiers between nearby phones. If a user tests positive and consents, their recent identifiers are uploaded to a server; other devices periodically download these and check for matches locally, notifying users of possible exposures without revealing identities or locations.

Adoption

Dozens of countries and US states built official apps on the framework, including Germany's Corona-Warn-App and the NHS COVID-19 app in England and Wales. Google and Apple restricted use to one app per country (later loosened) from recognised public-health authorities.

Privacy and criticism

The decentralised design was praised by cryptographers and privacy advocates, though critics noted limited epidemiological data collection, variable adoption, and the dependence of a public-health tool on two private companies. The system was wound down as the pandemic emergency ended in 2023.
