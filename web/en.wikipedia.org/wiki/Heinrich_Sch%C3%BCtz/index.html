Heinrich Schütz

Born18 October 1585 (baptised), Köstritz
Died6 November 1672, Dresden
EraEarly Baroque
OccupationComposer, organist, Kapellmeister

Heinrich Schütz (18 October 1585 – 6 November 1672) was a German composer and organist, generally regarded as the most important German composer before J. S. Bach and a key figure of early Baroque music.

Life

Schütz studied in Venice with Giovanni Gabrieli and later encountered the new monodic style of Monteverdi. From 1617 he served as Kapellmeister at the electoral court of Dresden, a post he held for most of his life, though the Thirty Years' War severely disrupted the court's musical establishment; he spent periods working in Copenhagen.

Works

His output is dominated by sacred vocal music, including the Psalmen Davids (1619), the austere Musikalische Exequien (1636), the Geistliche Chormusik (1648), and his three late Passions. His opera Dafne (1627), the first German opera, is lost. Schütz fused Italian polychoral and concertato techniques with German Lutheran tradition.
