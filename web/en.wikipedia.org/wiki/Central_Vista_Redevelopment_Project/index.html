Central Vista Redevelopment Project

The Central Vista Redevelopment Project is a major redevelopment of the central administrative area of New Delhi, India, along the ceremonial axis running from Rashtrapati Bhavan to India Gate. Announced in 2019, the project includes a new Parliament House, a revamped Kartavya Path, a new residence and office for the prime minister, a Vice President's Enclave, and a Common Central Secretariat consolidating government ministries.

Components

The new Parliament House, inaugurated on 28 May 2023, is the project's centrepiece, built because the 1927 building could not accommodate post-delimitation expansion of the Lok Sabha. The central avenue, formerly Rajpath, was redeveloped and renamed Kartavya Path in September 2022, with new lawns, canals, food courts and a statue of Subhas Chandra Bose installed under the canopy near India Gate. The old Parliament House was redesignated Samvidhan Sadan.

Other components include the Vice President's Enclave (completed 2024), the Kartavya Bhavan buildings of the Common Central Secretariat housing ministries such as Home Affairs and External Affairs, conversion of the North and South Blocks into the Yuge Yugeen Bharat national museum, and a new Prime Minister's residence and office complex.

Costs and criticism

Official estimates for the project have been reported at around ₹20,000 crore in total, with the new Parliament building initially tendered at about ₹862 crore. The project was controversial: critics questioned the expense during the COVID-19 pandemic, the environmental clearances, tree felling, and changes to heritage precincts. Petitions reached the Supreme Court, which in January 2021 upheld the project by majority while imposing conditions on heritage and environmental compliance. Supporters argue the Lutyens-era buildings are inadequate, seismically vulnerable and expensive to maintain, and that consolidating ministries saves rent.
