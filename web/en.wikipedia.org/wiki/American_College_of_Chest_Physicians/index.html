The American College of Chest Physicians (CHEST) is a professional medical society for physicians and other health professionals specialising in pulmonology, critical care medicine, sleep medicine and related fields. Founded in 1935, it is headquartered in Glenview, Illinois, and has members in more than 100 countries.

History

The college grew out of the Federation of American Sanatoria, an organisation concerned with the treatment of tuberculosis. As chest medicine expanded beyond tuberculosis, the organisation evolved into a broad-based society for specialists in diseases of the chest, adopting its present identity as CHEST.

Activities

CHEST publishes the monthly peer-reviewed journal CHEST, one of the leading journals in respiratory and critical care medicine. It is well known for its evidence-based clinical practice guidelines, particularly its antithrombotic therapy guidelines for venous thromboembolism, which are widely cited in the management of blood clots. The college holds an annual scientific meeting and provides board-review and simulation-based education through its CHEST Education and simulation centre programs.

Membership

Fellows of the college may use the post-nominal letters FCCP (Fellow of the American College of Chest Physicians). Membership includes physicians, advanced practice providers, respiratory therapists and other clinicians involved in chest medicine.
