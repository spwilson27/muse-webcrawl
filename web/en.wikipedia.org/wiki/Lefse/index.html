Lefse

Lefse (Norwegian pronunciation: [ˈlɛ̂fsə]) is a traditional soft Norwegian flatbread. It is made with potatoes, flour, butter, milk, and sugar, and is cooked on a large, flat griddle. Special tools — grooved rolling pins and long turning sticks — are used to roll the dough paper-thin and lift it onto the hot surface.

Preparation

Cooked potatoes are riced and mixed with butter, milk or cream, sugar and flour, then chilled before rolling. The dough is rolled into very thin rounds, baked briefly on each side, and stored folded in cloths. To serve, the lefse is softened, spread with butter and sugar — often with cinnamon — and rolled up and sliced.

Variations and culture

Regional varieties include the thin, crisp hardangerlefse and thicker potato-free types. In the Upper Midwest of the United States, where many Norwegian immigrants settled, lefse remains a Christmas tradition and is sold in Scandinavian bakeries; some communities hold lefse festivals, and in 1983 the town of Rushford, Minnesota, made a record-sized lefse.
