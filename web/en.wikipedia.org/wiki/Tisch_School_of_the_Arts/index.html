New York University Tisch School of the Arts

The New York University Tisch School of the Arts (commonly called Tisch) is the performing, cinematic, and media arts school of New York University, headquartered at 721 Broadway in Manhattan. Founded in 1965, it is one of the most selective arts schools in the United States and offers undergraduate, graduate, and doctoral programmes across some seventeen departments and programmes, enrolling more than 4,000 students.

History

NYU established the School of the Arts in 1965 to provide conservatory training within a research university. Its founding departments included theatre, film, and dance. In 1982, after a major gift from Laurence A. Tisch and Preston Robert Tisch, the school was renamed in their honour. It subsequently expanded into recorded music (the Clive Davis Institute), game design, interactive media arts, performance studies, and dramatic writing, and operates programmes in Los Angeles, Singapore (Tisch Asia, 2007–2015), and at NYU's global sites.

Programmes

The school's best-known units include the Maurice Kanbar Institute of Film and Television, whose alumni include Martin Scorsese, Spike Lee, Joel Coen, Ang Lee, and Chris Columbus; the New Studio on Broadway and other acting studios for drama training; the Clive Davis Institute of Recorded Music; the ITP/IMA interactive media programmes; and departments of dance, design for stage and film, photography and imaging, cinema studies, and performance studies. Admission to artistic programmes requires a creative portfolio or audition in addition to academic review.

Notable alumni

Graduates have included filmmakers Oliver Stone and M. Night Shyamalan, actors Philip Seymour Hoffman, Adam Sandler, Kristen Bell, and Rachel Brosnahan, and figures across Broadway, television, and the music industry. Tisch alumni have won Academy Awards, Tonys, Emmys, and Grammys in large numbers, and the school regularly ranks among the top film schools in the world.
