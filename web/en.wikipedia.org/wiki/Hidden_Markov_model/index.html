Hidden Markov model

A hidden Markov model (HMM) is a statistical model of a system that is assumed to be a Markov process with unobservable ("hidden") states. The model is characterised by transition probabilities between hidden states and emission probabilities governing the observable outputs produced in each state. HMMs were described by Leonard Baum and co-workers in the 1960s.

Problems and algorithms

Three canonical problems are associated with HMMs: evaluation (computing the probability of an observation sequence, solved by the forward algorithm), decoding (finding the most likely hidden state sequence, solved by the Viterbi algorithm), and learning (estimating the model parameters from observations, solved by the Baum–Welch algorithm, an instance of expectation–maximisation).

Applications

Hidden Markov models became famous for their application to speech recognition in the 1970s and 1980s, and are widely used in bioinformatics — for gene prediction and sequence alignment via profile HMMs — as well as in part-of-speech tagging, handwriting recognition, gesture recognition, bio-sequence analysis and cryptanalysis.
