Motion vector

In video compression, a motion vector is a two-dimensional vector used for inter-frame prediction that gives the offset from the coordinates of a block of pixels in the current frame to the coordinates of a matching block in a reference frame. Motion vectors are a key element of motion-compensated prediction, allowing an encoder to describe a moving region by reference to previously decoded content plus a displacement, rather than encoding the region from scratch.

Motion estimation

The process of finding motion vectors is called motion estimation. The encoder searches the reference frame for the block that best matches the current block, typically minimizing a cost measure such as the sum of absolute differences. Full-search and faster heuristic search patterns are used, and sub-pixel accuracy is achieved by interpolating the reference frame to half-pixel or quarter-pixel positions.

Applications

Motion vectors are used in standards including MPEG-2, H.264/AVC, HEVC, and AV1. Because motion vectors themselves consume bits, they are usually predicted from neighboring vectors and the difference is entropy-coded. Beyond compression, motion-vector fields are used in video processing tasks such as frame-rate conversion, deinterlacing, and object tracking.
