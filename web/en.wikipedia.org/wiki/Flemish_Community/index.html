The Flemish Community (Dutch: Vlaamse Gemeenschap) is one of the three communities of the Belgian federal state, alongside the French Community and the German-speaking Community. Belgium's constitution divides competences between the federal level, three territorial regions, and three communities responsible for "person-related" matters — education, culture, language use, health policy and welfare — for their language group.

Territory and institutions

The Community exercises its competences in the Flemish Region (the provinces of West Flanders, East Flanders, Antwerp, Limburg and Flemish Brabant) and, for Dutch-language institutions, in the bilingual Brussels-Capital Region. Uniquely among Belgium's entities, the Flemish Community and the Flemish Region merged their institutions: there is a single Flemish Parliament and a single Flemish Government exercising both community and regional powers.

History

The communities were created by the Belgian state reforms beginning in 1970, which responded to the Flemish Movement's long campaign for Dutch-language cultural autonomy and education. Successive reforms (1980, 1988–89, 1993, 2001, 2011) expanded community powers; from 1980 the Flemish councils were directly elected, and the first direct elections to the Flemish Parliament took place in 1995.

Competences

The Flemish Community runs Dutch-language education from nursery school to universities such as KU Leuven and Ghent University, funds public broadcasting (VRT), libraries, museums and sport, and oversees much health and family policy, including the child-benefit system (Groeipakket). It also conducts its own foreign policy within its fields of competence, maintaining delegations abroad.

Identity

The Community's feast day, 11 July, commemorates the Battle of the Golden Spurs (1302). Its flag is the Flemish lion, a black lion on a yellow field derived from the arms of the Counts of Flanders.
