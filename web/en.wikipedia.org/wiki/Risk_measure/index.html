Risk measure

In mathematical finance, a risk measure is a mapping from a set of random variables (representing portfolio returns or losses) to the real numbers, intended to quantify the amount of capital that must be held in reserve to make a risky position acceptable to a regulator or risk manager.

Properties

A risk measure is monotone if a portfolio that always loses more is assigned at least as much risk; translation invariant if adding cash reduces required capital by the same amount. A coherent risk measure (Artzner et al., 1999) additionally satisfies subadditivity — diversification never increases measured risk — and positive homogeneity. Convex risk measures relax homogeneity to a convexity condition, penalizing concentration.

Examples

Common measures include value at risk (VaR), which is not generally subadditive, and expected shortfall (conditional VaR), which is coherent and is favoured in modern banking regulation (the Basel framework's FRTB standard uses expected shortfall at the 97.5% level). Distortion risk measures and expectiles are also widely studied.
