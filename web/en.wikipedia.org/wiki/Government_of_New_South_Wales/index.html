Government of New South Wales

The Government of New South Wales, also referred to as the NSW Government, is the Australian state democratic administrative authority of New South Wales. It is modelled on the Westminster system of responsible government and comprises the executive (the Governor, the Premier and ministers of the Crown, and departments and agencies), the legislature (the Parliament of New South Wales), and the judiciary (headed by the Supreme Court of New South Wales). The government was first formed under the Constitution Act 1856, which established responsible self-government in the colony, and continued after Federation in 1901 as a state of the Commonwealth of Australia under the Constitution Act 1902 (NSW). The seat of government is Sydney.

Constitutional framework

New South Wales was founded as a British penal colony in 1788 and was originally governed by an appointed Governor with near-autocratic powers. An advisory Legislative Council was established in 1823 and became partly elective in 1843. The Constitution Act 1855 (Imp) and the colony's own Constitution Act established a bicameral parliament and responsible government in 1856, with the first ministry under Stuart Donaldson. Upon Federation in 1901, New South Wales became a state of the Commonwealth; its constitution continued in force, consolidated in the Constitution Act 1902 (NSW), which remains the state's principal constitutional document, subject to amendment by the state parliament (with certain entrenched provisions requiring a referendum).

Executive

Executive power is formally vested in the Governor, the representative of the sovereign (Charles III, King of Australia), who acts on the advice of the Executive Council — in practice, of the Premier and Cabinet. The Premier is the head of government, conventionally the leader of the party or coalition commanding a majority in the Legislative Assembly. Ministers administer portfolios through departments and agencies; the NSW public sector, including NSW Health, the Department of Education and Transport for NSW, is one of the largest employers in Australia. Major integrity bodies include the Independent Commission Against Corruption (ICAC, est. 1988), the Ombudsman and the Auditor-General.

Legislature

The Parliament of New South Wales consists of the sovereign (represented by the Governor), the Legislative Assembly (93 members elected from single-member districts for fixed four-year terms) and the Legislative Council (42 members elected by proportional representation for eight-year terms, with half facing election every four years). The Legislative Assembly is the house of government formation and primary financial initiative; the Council acts as a house of review and is rarely controlled by the government of the day. Elections use optional preferential voting.

Judiciary

The court system is headed by the Supreme Court, the oldest superior court in Australia (established 1824), headed by the Chief Justice. Below it sit the District Court and the Local Court, together with specialist bodies such as the Land and Environment Court. Appeals ultimately lie to the High Court of Australia. Judicial officers are appointed by the Governor on ministerial advice and enjoy tenure to retirement age.

Local government

Below the state level, New South Wales is divided into local government areas administered by elected councils responsible for local planning, roads, waste and community services. Amalgamations in 2016 reduced the number of councils to 128, a reform that remained politically contentious.
