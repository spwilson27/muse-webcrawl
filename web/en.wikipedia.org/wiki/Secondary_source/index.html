Secondary source

Secondary sources analyse, interpret and synthesise primary sources

In scholarship, a secondary source is a document or recording that relates or discusses information originally presented elsewhere. A secondary source contrasts with a primary source — an original object or document created at the time under study — and is itself one or more steps removed from the event or material it describes.

Classification

Secondary sources involve generalisation, analysis, interpretation, or evaluation of primary sources. Examples include scholarly journal articles reviewing original research, history textbooks, biographies, encyclopedias and critical commentaries. Whether a given work counts as primary or secondary depends on the field and on the use being made of it: a historian's biography, for instance, is a secondary source about its subject but a primary source for the history of the historian's own era.

In historical method

Historians rely heavily on secondary sources — the work of other historians — while grounding new claims in primary evidence. Citing established secondary literature is a core scholarly practice, both for situating one's argument and for giving readers a path into a subject. Tertiary sources, such as encyclopedias and bibliographies, compile and summarise secondary sources in turn.

In other fields

In law, secondary sources include treatises, restatements and law review articles that explain and comment on primary legal authorities such as statutes and cases. In science, review articles that synthesise many original studies are secondary sources, and systematic reviews and meta-analyses built on them are cornerstones of evidence-based practice.
