Borre mound cemetery

The Borre mound cemetery (Norwegian: Borrehaugene) is a burial site in Borre National Park in the municipality of Horten, Vestfold county, Norway. It contains seven large burial mounds and a cairn, dating from around 600 AD into the Viking Age. The site is one of the largest collections of burial mounds in Northern Europe.

Borre mound cemetery
Location: Horten, Vestfold, Norway
Period: c. 600–1000 AD
Part of: Borre National Park

Description

The mounds are between 5 and 7 metres high and up to 45 metres in diameter. One of them was excavated following its collapse, revealing the remains of a ship burial: the Borre ship, dated to around 900 AD. Finds from the excavations gave their name to the Borre style, a phase of Viking ornament art characterised by gripping beasts and interlaced ribbon animals.

History

The cemetery has been connected with the Yngling dynasty of kings mentioned in the sagas. According to Snorri Sturluson's Ynglinga saga, kings of Vestfold were buried at Borre. The first excavations took place in 1852 under the antiquarian Nicolay Nicolaysen, when the Borre ship grave was investigated.

National park

Borre National Park was established in 2006 as Norway's first national park centred on a cultural heritage site. A visitor centre, Midgard Viking Centre, opened near the mounds in 2000 and presents exhibitions about the Viking Age and the burials.
