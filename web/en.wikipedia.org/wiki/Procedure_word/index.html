Procedure word

Procedure words (often called prowords) are words or short phrases used in radio voice procedure to convey standard messages quickly and unambiguously, especially on noisy or low-quality circuits. They are used by military forces, maritime services, aviation, amateur radio operators and emergency services.

Examples

Common prowords include Roger ("I have received your last transmission"), Wilco ("received and will comply"), Over ("my transmission is ended; I expect a response"), Out ("my transmission is ended; no response expected"), Break (separating parts of a message), Say again (requesting repetition — "repeat" is avoided because in artillery use it means fire again), Mayday and Pan-pan for distress and urgency, and How copy? asking how well a transmission was received.

Standardisation

Allied military procedures are set out in publications such as NATO's ACP 125, and maritime usage in the IMO Standard Marine Communication Phrases. Procedure words complement the NATO phonetic alphabet, brevity codes and signal flags, and aim to minimise misunderstanding between operators of different languages and services.
