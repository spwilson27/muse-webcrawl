Food intolerance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Food intolerance is a detrimental reaction, often delayed, to a food, beverage, food additive, or compound found in foods that produces symptoms in one or more body organs and systems. Unlike a food allergy, it does not involve the immune system in the same immediate, IgE-mediated way.

Difference from food allergy

Food allergies trigger an immune response — often rapid and potentially severe (anaphylaxis) — while food intolerance reactions are generally metabolic or pharmacological in origin, slower in onset, and dose-dependent.

Causes and types

  • Lactose intolerance — deficiency of the enzyme lactase, preventing digestion of milk sugar
  • Non-celiac gluten sensitivity — symptoms after gluten ingestion without coeliac disease markers
  • Fructose malabsorption
  • Histamine intolerance — impaired breakdown of dietary histamine
  • Reactions to food additives such as sulfites, tartrazine, or MSG
  • Pharmacological reactions to naturally occurring compounds such as caffeine or salicylates

Symptoms

Symptoms include bloating, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, flatulence, nausea, headaches and fatigue, typically appearing hours after eating the offending food.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis relies on dietary history, exclusion diets, and specific tests such as the hydrogen breath test for lactose and fructose malabsorption. Management is avoidance of the trigger food. Commercial food-intolerance IgG tests are not considered reliable by major allergy societies.
