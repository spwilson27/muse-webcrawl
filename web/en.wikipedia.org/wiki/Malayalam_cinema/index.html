Malayalam cinema

Malayalam cinema, nicknamed Mollywood, is the segment of Indian cinema dedicated to motion pictures in the Malayalam language, spoken mainly in the state of Kerala. Based largely in Kochi, it is renowned across India for realistic storytelling, strong screenwriting and acclaimed performances, often on modest budgets. The first Malayalam film was the silent feature Vigathakumaran (1928, released 1930), produced and directed by J. C. Daniel; the first talkie was Balan (1938).

History and character

The industry developed a distinctive literary realism: Chemmeen (1965) won the President's Gold Medal for best feature film, and directors such as Adoor Gopalakrishnan, G. Aravindan and John Abraham led a parallel "new cinema" movement in the 1970s. The 1980s and early 1990s are often called a golden age of popular cinema, built on the screenplays of writers like M. T. Vasudevan Nair and the superstar duo Mohanlal and Mammootty. A "new generation" movement after about 2011 brought younger filmmakers, urban themes and technical polish, and in the 2020s Malayalam films gained pan-Indian streaming audiences for thrillers, dramas such as Manjummel Boys and Premalu (2024), and craft-led storytelling.

Recognition

Malayalam films and artists regularly win National Film Awards out of proportion to the industry's size, including multiple best actor awards for Mammootty and Mohanlal, and international festival recognition for Adoor Gopalakrishnan and others. The industry also supplies celebrated technicians — cinematographers, editors and composers — to the other Indian film industries.
