Lopamudra

Lopamudra
Known forVedic hymn (Rigveda 1.179), wife of Agastya
Also known asKaushitaki, Varaprada

Lopamudra (Sanskrit: लोपामुद्रा), also known as Kaushitaki and Varaprada, is a female sage (rishika) of the Vedic period and the wife of the sage Agastya. She is credited as the seer of a hymn of the Rigveda (1.179), a dialogue between herself and Agastya concerning the tension between asceticism and married life, and is regarded as one of the most celebrated women of Vedic literature.

Legend

According to the Mahabharata, Agastya created Lopamudra from the most beautiful parts of various animals and creatures, because he wished for a wife who would combine all excellences, and placed her secretly in the palace of the king of Vidarbha, who raised her as his daughter. When she came of age, Agastya asked for her hand. Despite her reluctance to exchange palace luxury for forest asceticism, she married him — on condition that he first acquire wealth so that she would not have to abandon all comforts. Agastya obtained riches from several kings and from the demon Ilvala, and the couple lived together; their son was the poet Dridhasyu (Idhmavaha).

The Rigvedic hymn

Rigveda 1.179, attributed to Lopamudra and Agastya, is a dialogue hymn in which Lopamudra urges her husband, grown old in ascetic practice, to attend to his duties as a householder, and reflects on the waning of youthful strength. It is notable in Vedic literature for its frank treatment of desire and for being one of the hymns composed by a woman.

Legacy

Lopamudra is honoured as one of the great women of the Vedic tradition, and traditions associated with her are preserved in the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, and later texts such as the Giridhara Ramayana. A river named Lopamudra is mentioned in South Indian tradition, sometimes identified with a tributary of the Kaveri.
