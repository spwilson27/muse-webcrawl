Vegetarian Society

The Vegetarian Society of the United Kingdom is a British registered charity and campaigning organisation promoting vegetarianism. Founded on 30 September 1847 at Ramsgate, Kent, it is one of the oldest vegetarian organisations in the world, and the group credited with coining the word "vegetarian" in its modern usage.

History

The society grew out of the Bible Christian Church and Alcott House circles that abstained from meat. Early members adopted the name vegetarian — from Latin vegetus, lively — rather than "Pythagorean". Mahatma Gandhi was a member during his time in London and wrote for its journal. A London branch became independent, and rival bodies later merged and split at various points, with the Vegan Society founded in 1944 by former members.

Work

The society runs campaigns, provides recipes and nutrition information, operates a cookery school, and licenses the Vegetarian Society Approved trademarks for vegetarian and vegan products. It publishes a magazine and supports National Vegetarian Week in the UK.
