Rankine–Hugoniot conditions

Rankine–Hugoniot conditions
FieldFluid dynamics, gas dynamics

The Rankine–Hugoniot conditions, also known as the Rankine–Hugoniot relations, describe the relationship between the states on either side of a shock wave or combustion wave in a one-dimensional flow in a gas. Named after William John Macquorn Rankine and Pierre Henri Hugoniot, they express the conservation of mass, momentum, and energy across the discontinuity.

Equations

In the rest frame of the shock, with subscripts 1 (upstream) and 2 (downstream), mass flux m, density ρ, velocity u, pressure p, and internal energy e:

  • Mass: ρ1u1 = ρ2u2
  • Momentum: p1 + ρ1u12 = p2 + ρ2u22
  • Energy: e1 + p1/ρ1 + ½u12 = e2 + p2/ρ2 + ½u22

The Hugoniot

Eliminating the velocities gives the Hugoniot relation linking pressure and specific volume across the shock, defining the Hugoniot curve of thermodynamically admissible downstream states. Combined with the equation of state of the gas, it determines the shock adiabat used in detonation theory.

Applications

The conditions are fundamental in shock-tube analysis, supersonic aerodynamics (normal and oblique shocks), detonation physics, and astrophysical flows such as supernova remnant shocks.
