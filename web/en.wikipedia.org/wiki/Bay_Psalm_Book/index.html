Bay Psalm Book

The Bay Psalm Book, in full The Whole Booke of Psalmes Faithfully Translated into English Metre, was printed in Cambridge, Massachusetts, in 1640. It was the first book printed in British North America and is one of the most famous and valuable early American books; eleven copies of the first edition are known to survive.

Contents and production

The ministers of the Massachusetts Bay Colony — principally Richard Mather, Thomas Weld and John Eliot — prepared new metrical translations of the Psalms from the Hebrew, deliberately preferring literal fidelity over elegance, as the preface explains: "God's altar needs not our polishings". The book was printed on the press of Stephen Daye, the first printer in the colony, brought from England at the expense of the Reverend Jose Glover. Some 1,700 copies of the first edition were produced.

Use and later editions

The Bay Psalm Book was used in the churches of New England for over a century, going through numerous editions; a revised version by Henry Dunster and Richard Lyon (1651) long served as the standard. It remained in use in some congregations until the eighteenth century.

Rarity and value

Of the eleven surviving first-edition copies, most are held by institutions such as the Library of Congress, Yale, Harvard and the Boston Public Library. On 26 November 2013 a copy belonging to Boston's Old South Church was sold at Sotheby's in New York for US$14.2 million, then the world record price for a printed book at auction.
