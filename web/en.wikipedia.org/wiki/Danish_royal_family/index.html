The Danish royal family is the dynastic family of the monarch of Denmark. The current monarch is King Frederik X, who acceded to the throne on 14 January 2024 following the abdication of his mother, Queen Margrethe II — the first voluntary abdication of a Danish sovereign in nearly 900 years. Members of the family belong to the House of Glücksburg, a branch of the House of Oldenburg, which has provided Denmark's monarchs since 1863.

Principal members

  • King Frederik X (born 1968) and Queen Mary (born Mary Donaldson in Tasmania, Australia, 1972), married in 2004
  • Crown Prince Christian (born 2005), heir apparent, followed in the succession by Princess Isabella (born 2007) and twins Prince Vincent and Princess Josephine (born 2011)
  • Queen Margrethe II (born 1940), the king's mother, who retains her title after abdication
  • Prince Joachim (born 1969), the king's younger brother, and his wife Princess Marie

Succession and titles

Succession follows absolute primogeniture, adopted by constitutional amendment and referendum in 2009. In 2022 Queen Margrethe II announced that Prince Joachim's children would cease to hold prince/princess titles from 2023, retaining instead the titles Count/Countess of Monpezat — a move intended to slim down the monarchy. The surname Monpezat derives from the family of the late Prince Henrik, the French-born consort of Margrethe II.

Role and popularity

Denmark is a constitutional monarchy: the monarch formally appoints the government, presides over the Council of State and performs official state duties, while executive power rests with the government and legislature. The Danish monarchy is among the oldest continuing monarchies in the world, tracing an unbroken line of kings for over a thousand years, and consistently enjoys high public support.
