Haumea

136108 Haumea Type: Dwarf planet (plutoid)
Discovery: 2004 (announced 2005)
Moons: Hiʻiaka, Namaka
Rotation period: ~3.9 hours
Mean distance: ~43 AU

Haumea (minor-planet designation 136108 Haumea) is a dwarf planet located beyond Neptune's orbit in the Kuiper belt. It is one of the most elongated large objects in the Solar System, stretched into a triaxial ellipsoid by its extremely rapid rotation — a day on Haumea lasts only about 3.9 hours, the fastest known for any large object in the Solar System.

Artist's impression of Haumea with its moons Hiʻiaka and Namaka.

Discovery and naming

Haumea was discovered independently in 2004–2005 by teams led by Mike Brown at Palomar Observatory and José Luis Ortiz at the Sierra Nevada Observatory, a dispute that became one of astronomy's notable priority controversies. The International Astronomical Union recognised it as a dwarf planet in 2008 and named it for Haumea, the Hawaiian goddess of fertility and childbirth; its moons were named for her daughters, Hiʻiaka and Namaka.

Physical characteristics

Haumea's mass is about one-third that of Pluto, and its remarkably high density implies it is composed mostly of rock with only a thin mantle of water ice. Its surface shows crystalline water ice and a dark reddish spot. Haumea heads a collisional family of icy bodies thought to be fragments knocked off by an ancient impact. In 2017 observations of a stellar occultation revealed that Haumea also has a narrow ring, about 70 km wide, orbiting at roughly 2,285 km from its centre.
