Fethullah Gülen (27 April 1941 – 20 October 2024) was a Turkish Islamic scholar, preacher, and poet who founded the Gülen movement, also known as Hizmet ("Service"), a global network of schools, businesses, media outlets, and dialogue organizations inspired by his teachings.

Early life and education

Gülen was born in the village of Korucuk near Pasinler in Erzurum Province, eastern Turkey, the son of an imam. He received his early education in religious schools and memorized the Quran. After military service he became a state-licensed imam, preaching in Erzurum, Edirne, and İzmir, where in the 1960s he began attracting a circle of followers.

The Hizmet movement

From his base in İzmir, Gülen encouraged his followers to build dormitories, tutoring centers, and eventually a worldwide network of schools emphasizing science and interfaith dialogue. His sermons and writings, including works such as Toward a Global Civilization of Love and Tolerance, promoted a modernist reading of Islam compatible with democracy, education, and market enterprise. The movement grew to include media groups and a bank, and spread to more than a hundred countries.

Exile and conflict with the Turkish government

Gülen left Turkey for the United States in 1999, citing medical treatment, and lived in self-imposed exile in the Poconos region of Pennsylvania. After the 2013 corruption investigations in Turkey, relations between Gülen's movement and the government of Recep Tayyip Erdoğan collapsed. Following the failed coup attempt of 15 July 2016, the Turkish government accused Gülen of masterminding the plot, designated his movement a terrorist organization, and repeatedly sought his extradition — accusations Gülen denied. He died in Pennsylvania in October 2024.
