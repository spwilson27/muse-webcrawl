Baron Samedi

Baron Samedi (French: Baron Samedi, "Baron Saturday") is one of the loa of Haitian Vodou. He is a loa of the dead, head of the Guede family of spirits, and is usually depicted as a tall figure in a top hat and tailcoat, with a skull-like face, dark glasses (often with one lens missing, so he can watch both worlds), cotton plugs in his nostrils, and a nasal, sardonic voice.

Role in Vodou

Baron Samedi stands at the crossroads between the living and the dead and presides over cemeteries; the first grave dug in a new cemetery is consecrated to him, and no one can die until he permits the grave to be dug. Despite his macabre portfolio, he is known for obscenity, laughter, debauchery, and a love of rum (traditionally infused with hot peppers), cigars, and roasted peanuts. He is also a powerful healer: it is said that if the Baron refuses to dig a person's grave, that person will recover, and he is invoked against curses and death magic. His wife is the loa Maman Brigitte, and his aspects or associates include Baron Cimetière, Baron Kriminel, and Baron La Croix; the rowdy Guede spirits who serve the dead are under his authority, and the festival of Fèt Gede (November 1–2) honors him and the ancestors.

Cultural influence

The Baron's iconography—top hat, frock coat, skull face—has spread widely in popular culture, influencing characters in film and fiction; he is the model for the villain in the James Bond film Live and Let Die and appears in numerous novels, games, and comics. Haitian dictator François "Papa Doc" Duvalier deliberately cultivated a resemblance to Baron Samedi in dress and manner to intimidate the population.
