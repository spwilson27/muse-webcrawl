BBC Light Programme

The BBC Light Programme was a national radio station broadcast chiefly on long wave (1500 metres) by the BBC from 29 July 1945 until 29 September 1967, when it was succeeded by BBC Radio 2 and BBC Radio 1. It offered light entertainment and popular music, together with news, sport, and drama.

Programming

The Light Programme inherited much of the wartime Forces Programme's style. Its schedule included enduring comedy and entertainment series such as ITMA (until 1949), Take It from Here, The Goon Show (on the Home Service but emblematic of the era), Hancock's Half Hour, and Round the Horne, as well as the long-running soap Mrs Dale's Diary, the request show Housewives' Choice, Music While You Work, and Saturday Club, an early vehicle for British pop music. Sport, including Sports Report, was a staple.

Replacement

Faced with competition from offshore pirate radio stations, the BBC reorganised its radio output in 1967. On 30 September 1967 the Light Programme's audience was split between the new BBC Radio 1 (pop) and BBC Radio 2, which inherited the Light Programme's long-wave and FM frequencies and much of its mainstream entertainment output.
