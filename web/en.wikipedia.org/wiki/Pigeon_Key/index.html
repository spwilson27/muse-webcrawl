Pigeon Key is a small island containing the historic district of the same name, in Monroe County, Florida, United States. It is located off the old Seven Mile Bridge, at approximately mile marker 45 of the Overseas Highway, about 2.2 miles (3.5 km) west of Marathon. The island is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

History

Pigeon Key served as a work camp for Henry Flagler's Florida East Coast Railway during the construction of the Seven Mile Bridge, built between 1908 and 1912 as part of the Key West Extension of the Overseas Railroad. At its height, around 400 workers lived on the five-acre island, housed in dormitories and served by a mess hall, schoolhouse, and hospital building, many of which survive. The camp's location under the bridge midway across the open water made it a vital staging point for construction of the bridge's 546 concrete piers.

After the railroad was destroyed by the 1935 Labor Day hurricane, the bridge was converted for automobile traffic, and Pigeon Key later served as a maintenance base for the Overseas Highway. When the new Seven Mile Bridge opened in 1982, the old bridge section to Pigeon Key remained in use as a fishing pier and pedestrian route, though it was closed for several years for restoration before reopening in 2022.

Pigeon Key today

The island is operated by the Pigeon Key Foundation as a historic site and marine science education center, hosting school groups for overnight field trips. Eight historic buildings remain, painted the railway's characteristic yellow. Access is via the restored old bridge on foot or by a small tram, or by ferry from Marathon. The island is said to have been named for flocks of white-crowned pigeons once seen roosting there.
