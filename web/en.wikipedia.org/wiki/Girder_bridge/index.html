Girder bridge

A girder bridge is a bridge that uses girders as the means of supporting its deck. The two most common types of modern steel girder bridges are plate and box girder bridges. The term "girder" is often used interchangeably with "beam" to describe the load-bearing elements, with the distinction that girders are the main horizontal supports of a structure, supporting smaller beams.

Design

A girder bridge is the most common and simplest form of bridge: logs across a stream are its primitive ancestor. The deck is carried on parallel girders spanning between piers or abutments, and loads are transferred to the foundations mainly by vertical compression. Plate girders are built up from steel plates welded or riveted into an I-section; box girders have a hollow rectangular or trapezoidal cross-section with better torsional stiffness, suiting them to curved alignments and longer spans. Prestressed concrete girders are widely used for highway viaducts and overpasses.

Spans

Simple-span girder bridges typically serve spans up to about 80 metres, while continuous box girder designs with haunched sections can exceed 250 metres. The world's longest girder bridge spans are found among the high-speed rail viaducts of China, and the girder type remains the default choice for the vast majority of road bridges worldwide.
