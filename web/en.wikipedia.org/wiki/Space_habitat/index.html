Space habitat

A space habitat, also called a space settlement, space colony or orbital colony, is a proposed type of space station intended as a permanent settlement rather than as a simple exploratory outpost. No space habitat has yet been built, but many designs have been proposed, from relatively small stations using near-term technology to vast self-sustaining structures housing millions of people.

Design concepts

Influential designs emerged from studies in the 1970s associated with Gerard K. O'Neill and NASA Ames summer studies. Recurring configurations include:

  • Bernal sphere — a rotating sphere whose inner surface provides living space under centrifugal "gravity".
  • Stanford torus — a doughnut-shaped habitat, about 1.8 km in diameter, rotating once per minute to simulate Earth gravity on its inner rim, proposed for about 10,000 inhabitants.
  • O'Neill cylinder ("Island Three") — paired counter-rotating cylinders kilometres long, with alternating land and window panels and mirrors to reflect sunlight inside.

Common requirements include radiation shielding (often metres of regolith or water), a closed or nearly closed life-support system recycling air and water, agricultural capacity, and a source of power, usually solar.

Motivations and criticism

Advocates argue that space habitats could relieve population and industrial pressure on Earth, exploit solar energy and extraterrestrial materials without gravity wells, and provide insurance for humanity against planetary catastrophe. Critics counter that the costs and technical risks are enormous, that no economic return has been demonstrated, and that hostile environments on Earth (oceans, Antarctica) remain largely uncolonised at far lower expense.

Related ideas

Space habitats differ from surface bases on the Moon or Mars, and from existing stations such as the International Space Station, which is a research facility rather than a settlement. The concept is a staple of hard science fiction, from Arthur C. Clarke's works to the television series Babylon 5 and the novel and film Elysium.
