Fisk Jubilee Singers

The Fisk Jubilee Singers are an African-American a cappella ensemble from Fisk University in Nashville, Tennessee. Formed in 1871, the group performed an arrangement of spirituals — the sacred songs created by enslaved African Americans — and toured to raise money for their struggling university. They introduced the spiritual to concert audiences around the world and are credited with preserving the genre and helping to create the musical traditions of gospel and other American music.

History

Fisk University, a school for newly emancipated slaves founded in 1866, was in dire financial straits by 1871. Treasurer and music professor George Leonard White assembled a choir of students — most of them formerly enslaved — and took them on tour to earn money for the school. Naming the group for the biblical Year of Jubilee, White's singers at first sang conventional popular songs, but soon added the spirituals, then often called "slave songs", which became the sensation of their concerts.

The Singers toured the United States and Europe, performing for figures including President Ulysses S. Grant, Queen Victoria, and Mark Twain. The proceeds of their tours funded the construction of Jubilee Hall, completed in 1876 and now a National Historic Landmark.

Legacy

The original group gave way to successive generations of Fisk student singers, who continue to perform today. In 2008 the Fisk Jubilee Singers received the National Medal of Arts, the highest honor for artists in the United States. The ensemble is widely credited with establishing the concert spiritual tradition and popularizing songs such as "Steal Away" in arranged choral form.
