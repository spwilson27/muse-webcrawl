Elastane

Elastane, also known as spandex and by the brand name Lycra, is a synthetic fibre known for its exceptional elasticity. It is stronger and more durable than natural rubber and is a polyester-polyurethane copolymer. Elastane was invented in 1958 by chemist Joseph Shivers at DuPont's laboratory in Waynesboro, Virginia.

Properties

Elastane fibres can be stretched to five times their length and recover to their original shape. The polymer is a long-chain synthetic material containing at least 85% segmented polyurethane. Segmented polyurethanes consist of soft, flexible segments joined by rigid segments, which gives the material its combination of stretch and strength.

Elastane is usually blended in small percentages (typically 2–5%) with other fibres such as cotton, wool, or nylon to give fabrics elasticity and comfort. Even a small amount greatly improves the fit and shape retention of a garment.

Uses

Elastane is used in swimwear, sportswear, activewear, underwear, hosiery, denim, and other clothing where stretch is desired. It is also used in some medical garments and wetsuit components.

Production

Elastane fibres are produced mainly by dry spinning: the polymer solution is extruded through spinnerets into a column of heated gas, which evaporates the solvent and solidifies the filaments. World production is dominated by China, and the largest manufacturer is the Inviya/Invista family of brands; Lycra is a trademark of The Lycra Company.
