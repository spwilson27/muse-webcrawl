Right of abode

The right of abode is an immigration status in certain jurisdictions entitling a person to live and work in a territory without immigration control, including freedom from deportation and from time limits on stay. Holders are typically citizens or a privileged class of residents.

United Kingdom

In British law, the right of abode (under the Immigration Act 1971) is held by all British citizens and by certain Commonwealth citizens who held it before 1983 — broadly those with a parent born in the UK or who were settled there. It differs from indefinite leave to remain in that it cannot lapse through absence and is not a grant of leave but a status evidenced by a certificate of entitlement in the holder's passport.

Hong Kong

Under the Basic Law and Immigration Ordinance, permanent residents — including Chinese citizens born in Hong Kong and foreign nationals ordinarily resident for seven continuous years — hold the right of abode in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.
