The Galleria dell'Accademia di Firenze, or "Gallery of the Academy of Florence", is an art museum in Florence, Italy. It is best known as the home of Michelangelo's sculpture David. It also has other sculptures by Michelangelo and a large collection of paintings by Florentine artists, mostly from the period 1300–1600 (the Trecento to the Late Renaissance). It is smaller and more specialised than the Uffizi, the main art museum in Florence.

History

The gallery was founded in 1784 by Pietro Leopoldo, Grand Duke of Tuscany, alongside the Accademia di Belle Arti (Academy of Fine Arts), to which it remains adjacent. It gathered didactic works for the academy students from suppressed churches and convents. Michelangelo's David was moved there in 1873 from the Piazza della Signoria to protect it from the weather, and a purpose-built tribune, designed by Emilio De Fabris, was completed in 1882.

Collections

Beyond the original David (a replica stands in the Piazza della Signoria), highlights include Michelangelo's unfinished Prisoners (Slaves) and Saint Matthew, Giambologna's plaster model for the Rape of the Sabine Women, works by Sandro Botticelli, Filippino Lippi, Perugino, Orcagna and Lorenzo Monaco, Byzantine-style icons, and the Museum of Musical Instruments with pieces from the Medici and Lorraine collections, including instruments by Stradivari and Bartolomeo Cristofori.
