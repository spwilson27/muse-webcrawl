Philip the Tetrarch


Philip (c. 26 BC – AD 34), known as Philip the Tetrarch or Herod Philip II, was ruler of the northeastern part of his father Herod the Great's kingdom from 4 BC until his death.

Philip the Tetrarch (c. 26 BC – 34 AD) was a son of Herod the Great and his fifth wife, Cleopatra of Jerusalem. On Herod's death in 4 BC, the kingdom was divided among his sons: Philip received the territories north and east of the Jordan — Batanaea, Trachonitis, Auranitis and parts of Gaulanitis — with the title of tetrarch ("ruler of a quarter").

Rule

The Jewish historian Josephus portrays Philip as the most moderate and just of Herod's sons, a ruler who travelled through his territory to hear cases and administer justice without excessive display. He rebuilt the city of Paneas, renaming it Caesarea Philippi in honour of the emperor Tiberius and himself, and raised the fishing village of Bethsaida on the Sea of Galilee to the status of a city, renaming it Julias after Livia (Julia), the wife of Augustus. He minted coins bearing the image of the emperor — the first Herodian ruler to place portraits on his coins.

Family and death

Philip married his niece Salome, daughter of Herodias and Herod II. He died childless in AD 34 after a reign of thirty-seven years, and his territory was briefly incorporated into the province of Syria before being granted by Caligula to his nephew Agrippa I in AD 37.

Philip the Tetrarch should not be confused with his half-brother Herod II (sometimes called Herod Philip I), the first husband of Herodias.
