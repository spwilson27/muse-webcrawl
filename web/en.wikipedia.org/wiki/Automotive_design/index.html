Automotive design

Automotive design is the process of developing the appearance (and to some extent the ergonomics) of motor vehicles, including automobiles, motorcycles, trucks, buses, coaches, and vans. The functional design and development of a modern motor vehicle is typically done by a large team from many different disciplines, of which "automotive designers" focus on the visual and tactile character of the vehicle.

Elements

Design work is divided broadly into exterior design, interior design, and colour and trim design. Designers work from initial sketches through digital renderings and full-scale clay models, balancing aesthetics with aerodynamic performance, pedestrian and occupant safety regulations, manufacturing feasibility and brand identity. Proportions — wheelbase, overhangs, cabin position and wheel size — are fundamental to a vehicle's visual stance.

History

Early automobiles imitated horse-drawn carriages, but by the 1930s streamlining influences produced integrated fenders and enclosed cabins. Harley Earl at General Motors established the first major manufacturer design department and introduced the annual model change and the concept car. Postwar design landmarks include the tailfin era of the 1950s, the wedge shapes of 1970s Italian studios such as Italdesign and Bertone, and the aerodynamic, flush-surfaced cars of the 1980s. Contemporary design is shaped by electric vehicle packaging, lighting signatures, and aerodynamic efficiency.
