Diazonium compound

Image: Benzenediazonium cation

Diazonium compounds or diazonium salts are a group of organic compounds sharing a common functional group R–N2+ X−, where R can be any organic group, such as an alkyl or an aryl, and X is an inorganic or organic anion, such as a halide. Aryl diazonium salts are especially important intermediates in organic synthesis.

Preparation

The most common method of preparing diazonium salts is diazotization: the treatment of an aromatic amine, such as aniline, with nitrous acid (usually generated in situ from sodium nitrite and a mineral acid) at low temperature (0–5 °C). The reaction was first described by Peter Griess in 1858.

Reactions

Diazonium salts are versatile intermediates. The diazo group can be replaced by many other groups: heating in water gives phenols; treatment with hypophosphorous acid replaces it with hydrogen; the Sandmeyer reaction uses copper(I) salts to install chlorine, bromine, or a cyano group; and treatment with potassium iodide gives aryl iodides. In azo coupling, diazonium salts act as electrophiles toward electron-rich aromatics such as phenols and anilines to form azo compounds, the basis of the azo dye industry.

Properties and safety

Diazonium salts are often unstable and can be explosive when dry, so they are usually prepared and used in solution without isolation. Aryl diazonium chlorides decompose on warming with loss of nitrogen gas.
