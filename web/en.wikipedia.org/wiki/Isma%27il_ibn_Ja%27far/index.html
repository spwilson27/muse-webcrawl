Ismāʿīl ibn Jaʿfar (Arabic: إسماعيل بن جعفر; c. 719 – c. 762) was the eldest son of Ja'far al-Sadiq, the sixth Imam of Shia Islam, and the eponymous ancestor of the Isma'ili branch of Shia Islam. According to most accounts he predeceased his father, and the dispute over whether the imamate passed to him or to his younger brother Musa al-Kazim produced one of the major early divisions within Shia Islam.

Life and the succession question

Isma'il was born in Medina to Ja'far al-Sadiq and Fatima bint al-Husayn al-Athram. Many of Ja'far's followers expected Isma'il, as the designated heir (nass), to succeed him. Isma'il's early death — reported by most sources to have occurred around 762, before Ja'far's own death in 765 — threw the succession into question. Those who held that the imamate had passed to Isma'il or to his son Muhammad ibn Isma'il became known as Isma'ilis; some early followers even denied Isma'il's death and awaited his return as the Mahdi. Others accepted Ja'far's later designation of Musa al-Kazim, whose line continued as the Twelver Imams.

Legacy

The Isma'ili tradition, which traces its imamate through Isma'il and Muhammad ibn Isma'il, later produced the Fatimid Caliphate (909–1171) and survives today in Nizari and Musta'li communities worldwide, including the followers of the Aga Khan. Historians note the scarcity and partisan nature of the earliest sources on Isma'il's life, which make details of the events surrounding his death difficult to establish.
