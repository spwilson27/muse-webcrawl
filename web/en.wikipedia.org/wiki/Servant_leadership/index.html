The servant-leader is "servant first", in Greenleaf's formulation.

Servant leadership is a leadership philosophy in which the leader's primary goal is to serve others—employees, customers, and the community—rather than to accumulate power or direct from the top of a hierarchy. The term was coined by Robert K. Greenleaf in his 1970 essay The Servant as Leader, inspired in part by Hermann Hesse's novel Journey to the East. Greenleaf proposed a simple test: do those served grow as persons—becoming healthier, wiser, freer, more autonomous, and more likely themselves to become servants?

Characteristics

Larry Spears, a longtime head of the Greenleaf Center for Servant Leadership, distilled Greenleaf's writings into ten characteristics of the servant-leader: listening, empathy, healing, awareness, persuasion, conceptualisation, foresight, stewardship, commitment to the growth of people, and building community. Later scholarship has modelled servant leadership through dimensions such as empowerment, humility, authenticity, and standing back.

Applications and research

The philosophy has been adopted by companies often cited for employee-centred cultures, including Southwest Airlines, Starbucks, and The Container Store, and has influenced fields from nursing and education to agile software development (the "servant leader" role of a Scrum Master). Academic research generally finds servant leadership positively associated with employee engagement, trust, job performance, and organisational citizenship behaviour, though critics note risks of leader burnout, fuzzy boundaries of authority, and difficulty reconciling the model with crisis decision-making.
