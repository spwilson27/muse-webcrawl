Seven Sisters Waterfall, Norway

The Seven Sisters (Norwegian: Dei sju systrene or De syv søstre) is the 39th tallest waterfall in Norway. The 410-metre (1,350 ft) tall waterfall consists of seven separate streams cascading down the cliffside into the Geirangerfjorden in Stranda Municipality in Møre og Romsdal county. The tallest single drop is about 250 metres (820 ft).

Location and name

The falls lie on the northern side of the Geirangerfjord, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, just south of the village of Geiranger. They are best viewed from the water, and are a highlight of ferry and cruise journeys along the fjord. The name comes from the seven separate streams, which legend compares to seven sisters dancing down the mountainside. Directly across the fjord is a single-stream waterfall called the Suitor (Friaren, also known as Skageflåfossen), said in folklore to be courting the sisters, and nearby lies the Bridal Veil falls (Brudesløret).

Flow

The flow depends on snowmelt and season; the falls are at their fullest in late spring and early summer, and in dry or drought years some of the seven streams may shrink markedly. The water comes from the Geit Mountain plateau above.
