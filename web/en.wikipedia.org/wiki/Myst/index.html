Myst

Developer: Cyan, Inc.

Designers: Rand and Robyn Miller

Released: 1993 (Macintosh)

Genre: Graphic adventure / puzzle

The island of Myst, the game's starting point.

Myst is a graphic adventure puzzle video game developed by Cyan and designed by brothers Rand and Robyn Miller. Released in 1993 for the Macintosh, it places the player — the unnamed "Stranger" — on a deserted island reached through a magical linking book, where exploration and environmental puzzles reveal a story of the explorer Atrus, his wife Catherine, and their two sons, Sirrus and Achenar, imprisoned in books for their crimes. With minimal dialogue, no dying and no time pressure, the game relied on atmosphere, pre-rendered 3D imagery and an ambient score by Robyn Miller.

Gameplay and story

Players navigate by clicking between still frames (with short video transitions) and interact with machines, books and mechanisms. From Myst island, linking books lead to satellite "Ages" — Selenitic, Stoneship, Mechanical and Channelwood — each holding red and blue pages that free one of the brothers while they plead their innocence and accuse the other. Returning all pages reveals the truth and allows the player to reach Atrus on the Age of D'ni, with several endings depending on the player's final choice.

Reception and legacy

Myst was a phenomenon: it became the best-selling PC game of the 1990s, with over six million copies sold, and is often credited with popularising the CD-ROM as a medium for games. Critics praised its graphics, sound and world-building, and it spawned the sequels Riven (1997), Myst III: Exile, Myst IV: Revelation, Myst V: End of Ages and the MMO spin-off Uru, as well as novels and remakes including realMyst and a 2021 VR version.
