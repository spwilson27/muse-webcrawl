The Ethiopian Air Force (ETAF; Amharic: የኢትዮጵያ አየር ኃይል) is the air service branch of the Ethiopian National Defense Force. Founded in 1929, it is one of the oldest independent air arms in Africa. The ETAF has operated Swedish, American, Soviet/Russian and other aircraft across its history, and has seen combat in the Second Italo-Ethiopian War, the Ogaden War (1977–78), the Eritrean–Ethiopian War (1998–2000) and more recent internal conflicts.

History

Ethiopia's first aircraft, a Potez 25, arrived in August 1929, and a small aviation arm was built up under Emperor Haile Selassie with foreign instructors. The force was destroyed during the Italian invasion of 1935–36. Rebuilt after liberation with Swedish help — flying Saab 17 and Saab B 17 aircraft and later Saab Safirs and T-28s — the air force was modernised under American assistance in the 1950s and 1960s with F-86 Sabres and F-5s. After the 1974 revolution the Derg regime turned to the Soviet Union, receiving MiG-21s, MiG-23s and Mi-24 helicopters, which bore the brunt of the Ogaden War against Somalia.

Post-1991

After the fall of the Derg in 1991 the air force was rebuilt around a smaller fleet. In the 1998–2000 war with Eritrea its newly acquired Su-27 fighters, flown initially with foreign technical support, won air superiority and its Su-25s and Mi-24s provided close support. In the 2020s the ETAF has added unmanned aerial vehicles, including Turkish Bayraktar TB2 and Iranian Mohajer-6 drones, which have been used in the conflicts in Tigray and with Fano militias.

Organisation and equipment

The air force is headquartered at Bishoftu (Debre Zeit), home to its main base and the Ethiopian Air Force Academy. Combat types have included the Su-27, Su-25, MiG-23, L-39 trainers, Mi-17 and Mi-24 helicopters, and C-130 and An-12 transports, supplemented by modern UAVs. Exact strengths are not officially published.
