Balochistan is a province of Pakistan. Located in the southwestern region of the country, it is Pakistan's largest province by land area — about 44 percent of the national territory — but the least populated and least developed. Its capital and largest city is Quetta.

Geography

The province borders Iran to the west, Afghanistan to the north, and the Arabian Sea to the south, with a coastline of about 770 kilometres. The terrain is mostly arid mountains and desert. The deep-water port of Gwadar on the Makran coast is the centrepiece of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC).

History and politics

At independence in 1947, the princely state of Kalat and other Baloch territories acceded to Pakistan, a process whose legitimacy remains disputed by Baloch nationalists. The province has experienced a long-running separatist insurgency punctuated by military operations, driven by grievances over resource extraction — notably the Sui gas field, supplying Pakistan since the 1950s — political marginalisation, and enforced disappearances.

Economy

Balochistan is rich in natural gas, coal, copper, and gold (including the Reko Diq deposit), and produces much of Pakistan's fruit harvest around Quetta and the northern valleys, despite chronic water scarcity.
