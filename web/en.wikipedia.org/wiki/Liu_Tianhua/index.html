Liu Tianhua (Chinese: 刘天华; 1895–1932) was a Chinese composer, musician and music educator, best known for transforming the erhu from a lowly folk instrument into a respected solo concert instrument. He was the younger brother of the writer Liu Bannong.

Life

Born in Jiangyin, Jiangsu, Liu learned Western brass instruments in a school band and later studied both Chinese and Western music in Shanghai, where he survived the 1913 Second Revolution working in theatre and opera troupes. From 1922 he taught at the music institute of Peking University, where he devoted himself to the reform of Chinese music: collecting folk melodies, improving instruments, and founding with colleagues the Society for the Improvement of National Music (1927) and its journal.

Work

Drawing on violin technique — he studied the violin and Western theory — Liu expanded the erhu's expressive range, standardised its tuning, and specified a fixed position system. His ten erhu solos, including Beautiful Night (Liang Xiao, 良宵), Song of a Sick Man (病中吟), Song on a Deserted Mountain (空山鸟语, "Birdsong in a Deserted Mountain"), Bright Walk (光明行) and Candles Flickering Red (烛影摇红), remain core repertoire. He also composed three pieces for the pipa, wrote 47 erhu études, and made some of the earliest recordings of Chinese instrumental music.

Liu died suddenly of scarlet fever in 1932, aged only 37, while collecting music in Beijing. His students, notably Jiang Fengzhi, carried his school forward, and his compositions are compulsory study for erhu players in China today.

Selected compositions
