Stakeholder theory

Stakeholder theory is a theory of organisational management and business ethics that accounts for multiple constituencies impacted by business entities, such as employees, suppliers, customers, local communities, governmental bodies and the environment, rather than only shareholders. It holds that a firm's managers have obligations to balance the interests of all its stakeholders.

The classic stakeholder model: the firm surrounded by its stakeholder groups

Development

The modern theory is associated above all with R. Edward Freeman's Strategic Management: A Stakeholder Approach (1984), building on earlier work at the Stanford Research Institute in the 1960s. Freeman defined a stakeholder as "any group or individual who can affect or is affected by the achievement of the organisation's objectives".

Debates

Stakeholder theory contrasts with the shareholder-primacy doctrine articulated by Milton Friedman, who argued a firm's only social responsibility is profit within the rules. Critics of stakeholder theory question how managers can be accountable to many masters at once; defenders argue that long-run shareholder value itself depends on satisfying other stakeholders, a stance reflected in the Business Roundtable's 2019 statement on corporate purpose.
