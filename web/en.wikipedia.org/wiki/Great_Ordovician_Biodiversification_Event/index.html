Great Ordovician Biodiversification Event

The Great Ordovician Biodiversification Event (GOBE) was a major radiation of marine life during the Ordovician period, roughly 485–460 million years ago, in which the number of marine families and genera increased several-fold, transforming Cambrian-type faunas into Palaeozoic-type ecosystems.

PeriodOrdovician, c. 485–460 Ma
Groups radiatedBrachiopods, bryozoans, molluscs, echinoderms, plankton
Preceded byCambrian explosion

What happened

Unlike the rapid Cambrian explosion of body plans, the GOBE was a diversification within phyla: orders and families multiplied. Plankton diversified, enabling new food webs; filter-feeders, reefs of stromatoporoids and corals, and deep burrowers expanded; predatory nautiloid cephalopods grew large.

Causes

Proposed drivers include cooling climate, rising oxygen, high sea levels flooding continents with epicontinental seas, increased nutrient supply, and possibly an asteroid breakup event increasing meteorite flux. The event ended with the Late Ordovician mass extinction. See Ordovician and Cambrian explosion.
