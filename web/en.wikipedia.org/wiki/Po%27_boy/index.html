Po' boy


A shrimp po' boy

A po' boy (also po-boy, po boy or poor boy) is a sandwich originally from Louisiana. It traditionally consists of meat, which is usually roast beef, ham, or fried seafood (shrimp, crawfish, fish, oysters, or crab), served in New Orleans French bread, known for its crisp crust and fluffy centre.

History

The sandwich is generally credited to Clovis and Benjamin Martin, former streetcar conductors who opened a restaurant in New Orleans in 1921. During the 1929 streetcar workers' strike, the Martins served free sandwiches to the striking men — the "poor boys" — and the name stuck. The bread was developed with baker John Gendusa to be a consistent, sandwich-friendly loaf.

Varieties and preparation

A po' boy ordered "dressed" comes with lettuce, tomato, pickles, and mayonnaise; fried shrimp and oyster po' boys are often dressed and served with hot sauce or rémoulade-like dressings. Other fillings include roast beef with gravy ("debris" po' boys use the beef that falls into the gravy), ham and cheese, and fried catfish. The sandwich is a staple of Louisiana casual dining and festivals, including the annual Oak Street Po' Boy Festival in New Orleans.
