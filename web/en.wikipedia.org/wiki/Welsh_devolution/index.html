Welsh devolution

Welsh devolution is the process by which the Parliament of the United Kingdom has transferred legislative and executive powers to Wales, creating a Welsh parliament and government in Cardiff. It is part of the wider devolution settlement in the United Kingdom.

History

Early 20th-century campaigns for Welsh home rule gained little traction, and a 1979 referendum on a Welsh Assembly was rejected by nearly four-fifths of voters. Following Labour's 1997 election victory, a second referendum in September 1997 approved a Welsh Assembly by a narrow margin (50.3% to 49.7%). The Government of Wales Act 1998 created the National Assembly for Wales, which first met in May 1999, originally with secondary legislative powers only.

Expansion of powers

The Government of Wales Act 2006 formalised a separate Welsh Government, allowed the Assembly to seek primary legislative powers, and — following a 2011 referendum in which 63.5% voted in favour — gave the Assembly direct primary law-making powers in devolved areas. The Wales Act 2014 granted tax powers, the Wales Act 2017 moved Wales to a reserved-powers model (devolved except where expressly reserved), and in 2020 the Assembly renamed itself the Senedd (Welsh Parliament). The Senedd, based in the Senedd building in Cardiff Bay, legislates on health, education, transport, environment, agriculture, housing and other devolved matters, while foreign affairs, defence and macroeconomic policy remain reserved to Westminster.
