Far-right politics in France

The far-right in France (French: extrême droite) exhibits an extensive history of party politics, organizations, and movements. It has encompassed monarchist, counter-revolutionary, nationalist, and populist currents, and in the modern era is most prominently represented by the National Rally (Rassemblement national, formerly the National Front).

History

The French far right originated in opposition to the French Revolution of 1789, in monarchist and Catholic counter-revolutionary traditions. In the late nineteenth century, the Dreyfus affair divided the country, and movements such as Charles Maurras's Action Française promoted integral nationalism and monarchism. In the 1930s, far-right leagues such as the Croix-de-Feu rioted in Paris in February 1934.

During the Second World War, the collaborationist Vichy regime under Marshal Philippe Pétain implemented authoritarian and antisemitic policies. After the war, far-right currents re-emerged around Poujadism in the 1950s and opposition to Algerian independence, including the terrorist campaign of the OAS.

The National Front / National Rally

The National Front was founded in 1972 by Jean-Marie Le Pen. It achieved its breakthrough in the 1980s and 1990s on an anti-immigration platform. In 2002 Le Pen reached the second round of the presidential election, losing to Jacques Chirac. His daughter Marine Le Pen took over in 2011, pursued a strategy of "de-demonization," renamed the party National Rally in 2018, and reached the presidential run-off in 2017 and 2022, losing to Emmanuel Macron both times with an increased share of the vote.

Other movements

Other far-right formations have included Éric Zemmour's Reconquête party, founded for the 2022 presidential election, and various identitarian, monarchist, and Catholic traditionalist groups.
