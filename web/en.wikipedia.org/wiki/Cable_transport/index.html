Cable transport

Cable transport is a broad class of transport modes based on vehicles pulled by cables instead of an internal power source. Cable transport is often closely associated with high-altitude mountain locations, but systems are also used in urban public transport and for moving freight and materials.

Types

Aerial lifts, in which vehicles are suspended from overhead cables, include:

  • Aerial tramways, with large cabins shuttling back and forth on support cables
  • Gondola lifts, with many smaller cabins circulating continuously on a loop of cable
  • Chairlifts, common at ski areas, carrying seated passengers with their feet dangling
  • Surface lifts, such as T-bars and rope tows, which pull skiers along the ground

Surface cable systems include funiculars and cable railways, in which rail vehicles are hauled up steep inclines by a cable, and cable cars such as the San Francisco cable car system, where the vehicle grips a continuously moving cable in a conduit below the street. Freight systems include aerial ropeways (material ropeways) historically used in mining and agriculture.

History

Rope and cable systems have ancient origins, including ropeways in China and Japan. The modern cable car was pioneered in the 19th century: the Hallidie cable car system opened in San Francisco in 1873, and aerial ropeways developed in the late 1800s for mines in Europe and the Americas. Modern detachable-grip gondolas have made cable transport a practical urban transit mode, as seen in La Paz, Bolivia (Mi Teleférico) and Medellín, Colombia (Metrocable).
