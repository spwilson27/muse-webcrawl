Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd

The Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in north-west Wales (Gwynedd), designated in 1986. It comprises the castles of Beaumaris and Harlech and the castles and town walls of Caernarfon and Conwy, built by King Edward I of England during his conquest of Wales in the late 13th century.

History

Following his campaigns of 1277 and 1282–83 against the Welsh princes, Edward launched a massive building programme to consolidate English rule, constructing a ring of great fortresses around Snowdonia. Master mason James of St George directed much of the work, which imported English settlers into walled new towns alongside the castles. The castles withstood the revolt of Madog ap Llywelyn in 1294–95 (though Conwy and Caernarfon were tested hard) and figured in the rising of Owain Glyndŵr in the early 15th century.

The sites

  • Caernarfon Castle — imperial in conception, with polygonal towers and banded masonry recalling Constantinople; birthplace of Edward II, the first English Prince of Wales.
  • Conwy Castle and its town walls — among the best-preserved medieval fortifications in Britain.
  • Harlech Castle — perched on a rocky crag above the sea; famous for its sieges in the Wars of the Roses.
  • Beaumaris Castle — the last and most technically perfect of Edward's castles, a model of concentric design, left unfinished.

Significance

UNESCO describes the four sites as the finest examples of late 13th- and early 14th-century military architecture in Europe. They are cared for by Cadw, the Welsh historic environment service.
