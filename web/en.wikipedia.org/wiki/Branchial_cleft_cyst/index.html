Branchial cleft cyst

Branchial cleft cyst
Origin: Congenital, from branchial apparatus remnants
Usual site: Lateral neck, anterior to sternocleidomastoid
Treatment: Surgical excision
A branchial cleft cyst presenting as a lateral neck swelling

A branchial cleft cyst is a congenital cyst that develops in the neck from remnants of the branchial (pharyngeal) apparatus of the early embryo. It typically presents as a painless, smooth, fluctuant swelling on the side of the neck, most often along the anterior border of the sternocleidomastoid muscle, and usually becomes apparent in late childhood or early adulthood, sometimes enlarging during an upper respiratory infection.

Classification

Branchial anomalies are classified by the cleft or pouch from which they arise. Second branchial cleft cysts are by far the most common, accounting for the great majority of cases; they may be associated with a sinus tract or fistula passing between the internal and external carotid arteries to the tonsillar fossa. First cleft anomalies occur around the ear and parotid gland, while third and fourth cleft (pouch) anomalies are rare and lie lower in the neck.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is made from the clinical picture supported by ultrasound, CT or MRI, and fine-needle aspiration yielding turbid fluid containing cholesterol crystals and squamous cells. In adults over about 40, a cystic neck mass must be investigated to exclude a cystic metastasis, for example of HPV-related oropharyngeal cancer. Definitive treatment is complete surgical excision, ideally when the cyst is not infected, to minimise recurrence.
