Élysée Palace

The Élysée Palace (French: Palais de l'Élysée) is the official residence and office of the President of the French Republic. Located on the Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré in the 8th arrondissement of Paris, near the Champs-Élysées, it has housed the presidency since the Second Republic. By extension, "the Élysée" is used as a metonym for the French presidency itself.

History

The palace was built between 1718 and 1722 for the Count of Évreux, Louis Henri de La Tour d'Auvergne, to designs by Armand-Claude Mollet, on what was then the edge of Paris. It passed through several owners, including Madame de Pompadour, and was purchased by Louis XV in 1753. Napoleon Bonaparte used it as a residence, and his nephew Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte, later Napoleon III, made it the presidential residence in 1848.

The palace today

The palace contains the president's office and residence, meeting rooms where the Council of Ministers convenes on Wednesdays, and reception rooms such as the Salon doré and the Salle des fêtes. Its gardens host the president's annual garden party traditionally held around Bastille Day, 14 July. The palace is guarded by the Republican Guard and is closed to the public except on European Heritage Days.
