Triple deity

Triple deity
ExamplesTrimurti (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva), the Morrígan, Hecate, the Norns, the three Fates
Common themesCreation, preservation, destruction; birth, life, death; past, present, future

A triple deity is a deity represented in three forms or aspects, or a grouping of three deities worshipped together. Triple deities occur in many mythologies and religions around the world, and the threefold pattern often maps onto cycles of life, time or the cosmos.

Examples

In Hinduism, the Trimurti unites Brahma the creator, Vishnu the preserver and Shiva the destroyer. Greek religion venerated Hecate in three forms, and the Fates—Clotho, Lachesis and Atropos—spun, measured and cut the thread of life; their Norse counterparts were the Norns. Celtic religion offers triple goddesses such as the Morrígan, while Roman state religion honoured the Capitoline Triad of Jupiter, Juno and Minerva.

Interpretation

Scholars have linked triple groupings to Indo-European ideology, notably Georges Dumézil's theory of a tripartite social order of priests, warriors and producers reflected in divine triads. In modern Neopaganism, particularly Wicca, the Triple Goddess of Maiden, Mother and Crone—associated with the phases of the Moon—is a central figure, popularised by Robert Graves's The White Goddess.
