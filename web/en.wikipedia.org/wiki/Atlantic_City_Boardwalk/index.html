The Atlantic City Boardwalk is a promenade in Atlantic City, New Jersey, in the United States. Opened in 1870 as a solution to the problem of beach sand being tracked into hotels and railroad cars, it is often credited as the first boardwalk in the United States and became the model for countless seaside promenades that followed.

History

The first boardwalk, built in 1870 by the city in partnership with local businessmen, was a simple eight-foot-wide wooden walkway that could be taken up and stored during the winter. Successive versions grew wider, longer, and more permanent; by the early twentieth century the Boardwalk stretched for miles and was lined with grand hotels, amusement piers, theaters, and shops. Rolling chairs — wicker pushchairs moved by attendants — became an iconic sight, as did attractions such as the Steel Pier.

The Boardwalk's fortunes rose and fell with Atlantic City itself. After decades of decline, the legalization of casino gambling in New Jersey in 1976 brought a wave of new casino-hotels along the Boardwalk beginning with Resorts International in 1978. Major storms, including the 1944 Great Atlantic hurricane and Hurricane Sandy in 2012, damaged sections of the walkway, which were rebuilt each time.

Description

Today the Boardwalk runs roughly four miles within Atlantic City and continues into neighboring Ventnor City, making the combined walkway about 5.5 miles long — among the longest boardwalks in the world. It is lined with casinos, hotels, restaurants, arcades, and shops selling salt water taffy, a confection long associated with the city. Landmarks along the route include Boardwalk Hall (the former Convention Hall, home to a famous pipe organ), the Absecon Lighthouse nearby, and several historic piers.

In popular culture

The Boardwalk gave its name to the HBO television series Boardwalk Empire (2010–2014), set in Atlantic City during Prohibition. The street names of the original American edition of the board game Monopoly were taken from Atlantic City, with Boardwalk as the most expensive property.
