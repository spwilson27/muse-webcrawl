Tskhinvali (Georgian: ცხინვალი; Ossetian: Цхинвал) is a city in the disputed region of South Ossetia in the South Caucasus. It is recognised by most of the international community as part of Georgia, but is controlled by the self-proclaimed Republic of South Ossetia, recognised by Russia and a handful of other states, of which Tskhinvali is the capital. The city lies on the Greater Liakhvi River, about 90 km north-west of Tbilisi.

History

The area has been inhabited since the Bronze Age; the town itself is documented from 1398 and grew as a trading settlement where Georgians, Ossetians, Armenians and Jews lived side by side. Under the Soviet Union it was capital of the South Ossetian Autonomous Oblast within the Georgian SSR, and from 1934 to 1961 it was named Staliniri in honour of Joseph Stalin. The Georgian–Ossetian conflict of 1991–1992 left the city under separatist control, and it was heavily fought over again during the Russo-Georgian War of August 2008, when Georgian forces shelled and briefly entered the city before being driven out by Russian and Ossetian forces. Russia subsequently recognised South Ossetia's independence, a step condemned by Georgia and most other states.

Geography and economy

Tskhinvali sits in a valley at about 860 metres above sea level. Before the conflicts it had timber, food-processing and electrical industries; the wars and isolation severely damaged its economy, and reconstruction has depended largely on Russian subsidies. The Roki Tunnel through the Caucasus to North Ossetia in Russia is the region's main link to the outside world.
