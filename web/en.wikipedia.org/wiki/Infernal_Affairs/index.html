Infernal Affairs

Directed by: Andrew Lau, Alan Mak
Starring: Andy Lau, Tony Leung, Anthony Wong, Eric Tsang
Release date: 2002
Country: Hong Kong
Language: Cantonese

Infernal Affairs (Chinese: 無間道) is a 2002 Hong Kong crime thriller film directed by Andrew Lau and Alan Mak. It tells the story of a police officer who infiltrates a triad gang and a triad member who has infiltrated the police force; each is tasked with uncovering the identity of the other mole.

Plot

Chan Wing-yan, an undercover police officer, has spent a decade inside the gang of crime boss Hon Sam. Meanwhile Lau Kin-ming, a triad member, has risen through the ranks of the Hong Kong Police Force as Sam's mole. When a drug deal nearly exposes both men, each side races to identify the other's infiltrator, and the two moles become locked in a tense game of cat and mouse.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major critical and commercial success, winning numerous awards including Best Film at the Hong Kong Film Awards. It spawned two sequels, Infernal Affairs II (2003), a prequel, and Infernal Affairs III (2003). Martin Scorsese's 2006 film The Departed is a remake of the story and won the Academy Award for Best Picture.

Title

The Chinese title, "Mou gaan dou", refers to Avīci, the lowest level of hell in Buddhism, a place of ceaseless suffering — a metaphor for the moles' endless torment.
