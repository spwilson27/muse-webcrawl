Einasto profile

TypeDensity profile (mathematical function)
Introduced byJaan Einasto (1965)
Used forGalaxy light distributions, dark matter haloes, star clusters

The Einasto profile is a mathematical function that describes how the density ρ of a spherical stellar system or dark matter halo varies with distance r from its centre. It was introduced by the Estonian astrophysicist Jaan Einasto in 1965 to model the Milky Way and other galaxies.

Definition

The profile has the form ρ(r) ∝ exp{ −dn[ (r/r−2)1/n − 1 ] }, where r−2 is the radius at which the logarithmic slope of the density equals −2, n is the "Einasto index" controlling the curvature of the profile, and dn is a constant chosen so that r−2 encloses half the total mass. Its logarithmic slope is a power law in radius, so the profile steepens smoothly outward rather than following a single power law.

Applications

High-resolution cosmological N-body simulations have shown that the Einasto profile fits simulated dark matter haloes better than the earlier Navarro–Frenk–White profile, especially in resolution of the inner and outer slopes. It is also used to model elliptical galaxies, globular clusters, and the dark matter haloes inferred for dwarf galaxies.
