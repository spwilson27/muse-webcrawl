The Archies

The Archies was an American fictional rock band featured in the animated television series The Archie Show (1968) and based on the Archie Comics characters Archie Andrews, Betty Cooper, Veronica Lodge, Jughead Jones and Reggie Mantle. Remarkably, the cartoon group's records were real: recorded by session musicians under producer Don Kirshner, the Archies scored one of the biggest hits of the rock era with the 1969 single "Sugar, Sugar".

History

After the success of the Monkees, Don Kirshner sought a band he could control completely and found it in an animated group. Lead vocals were sung by Ron Dante, with Toni Wine as the female voice; songwriters included Jeff Barry, Andy Kim, Bobby Bloom and Ellie Greenwich. The Archies' first album appeared in 1968, and their singles sold briskly to the show's young audience.

"Sugar, Sugar"

Written by Jeff Barry and Andy Kim, "Sugar, Sugar" topped the Billboard Hot 100 for four weeks in the autumn of 1969, was the year's best-selling single in the United States, and reached number one in the United Kingdom for eight weeks. It remains one of the defining records of bubblegum pop. Other Archies singles included "Bang-Shang-A-Lang", "Jingle Jangle" (another US top-ten hit) and "Who's Your Baby?".

The Archies are frequently cited as the most successful purely fictional band in chart history — a cartoon creation whose records outsold most flesh-and-blood groups — and "Sugar, Sugar" has been covered and sampled repeatedly, including a prominent version by Wilson Pickett.
