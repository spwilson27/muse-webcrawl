Judson Dance Theater

Judson Dance Theater was a collective of dancers, composers, and visual artists who performed at the Judson Memorial Church in Greenwich Village, Manhattan, New York City, between 1962 and 1964. The artists involved were Yvonne Rainer, Steve Paxton, David Gordon, Trisha Brown, Lucinda Childs, Deborah Hay, Elaine Summers, Robert Morris, Robert Rauschenberg, and John Cage, among others. The group is credited with founding postmodern dance, rejecting the virtuosity and narrative conventions of modern dance in favour of ordinary movement, improvisation, and interdisciplinary collaboration.

Origins

The collective grew out of a composition class taught by musician Robert Dunn at Merce Cunningham's studio in 1960–62, itself influenced by John Cage's experimental composition classes. Dunn encouraged students to use chance procedures, scores, and non-dance movement. On 6 July 1962 the group presented "A Concert of Dance" at Judson Memorial Church, whose arts ministry under Reverend Howard Moody and associate minister Al Carmines offered performers an unusually open venue.

Work and methods

Judson artists treated pedestrian actions — walking, sitting, dressing, carrying objects — as dance material, and frequently used tasks, games, verbal instructions, and chance operations as choreographic structures. Yvonne Rainer's Trio A (1966), developed in this milieu, and her "No Manifesto" (1965), rejecting spectacle, virtuosity, and seduction of the viewer, became emblematic statements of the postmodern turn. Steve Paxton's experiments led to contact improvisation; Trisha Brown developed her "accumulation" pieces and equipment dances.

Legacy

Although Judson Dance Theater dissolved as a named collective after 1964, its members continued working through the Grand Union improvisation group in the 1970s and profoundly influenced contemporary dance, performance art, and minimalism. A major retrospective, Judson Dance Theater: The Work Is Never Done, was held at the Museum of Modern Art in 2018.
