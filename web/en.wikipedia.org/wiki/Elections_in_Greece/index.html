Elections in Greece

Elections in Greece are held to elect the 300 members of the Hellenic Parliament, from which the government is formed, as well as the President of the Republic (chosen by Parliament), members of the European Parliament, and regional and municipal authorities. Voting is compulsory in principle for citizens aged 17 and over, though the obligation is not enforced in practice.

Legislative elections

Greece uses a form of proportional representation. A party must pass a 3% national threshold to enter Parliament. For much of the modern era, a "reinforced proportionality" system awarded a bonus of extra seats to the largest party to encourage stable single-party governments. Under the system used from 2023, the winning party can receive a sliding-scale bonus of up to 50 seats depending on its vote share. Parliamentary terms last four years, though early elections are common.

Main parties

Since the restoration of democracy in 1974, Greek politics was dominated by the centre-right New Democracy and the centre-left PASOK. The Greek government-debt crisis reshaped the party system: the left-wing Syriza won the elections of 2015, governing under Alexis Tsipras, while New Democracy returned to power in 2019 under Kyriakos Mitsotakis and won again in 2023.

Presidential and European elections

The President of the Hellenic Republic is elected by Parliament for a five-year term. Greece also elects 21 members of the European Parliament, and holds local elections for regions and municipalities, typically every five years.
