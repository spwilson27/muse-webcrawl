Thomas Struth (born 11 October 1954) is a German photographer associated with the Düsseldorf School, having studied under Bernd and Hilla Becher at the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf alongside Andreas Gursky, Candida Höfer, and Thomas Ruff.

Work

Struth first gained attention with black-and-white photographs of empty city streets in Düsseldorf, New York, and elsewhere in Europe, Asia, and America. He is best known for his Museum Photographs (begun 1989), monumental colour images of visitors contemplating famous paintings in institutions such as the Louvre, the Prado, and the Hermitage, which reflect on spectatorship and the social life of art. Other long-running series include family portraits made with a psychoanalyst collaborator, the lush jungle images of Paradise, photographs of audiences, and large-format views of industrial and technological sites such as shipyards, fusion reactors, and space facilities.
