Public bank

A public bank is a bank or banking institution owned by a state, municipality or other public body, rather than by private investors. Public banks may pursue policy goals — such as infrastructure finance, regional development or lending to underserved sectors — alongside or instead of profit maximisation.

Examples

Well-known examples include Germany's KfW development bank and its regional Landesbanken and Sparkassen, the Bank of North Dakota in the United States (the only general bank owned by a US state), and large state-owned commercial banks in countries such as China and India. Postal savings banks have historically also been public institutions.

Debate

Supporters argue public banks can lend counter-cyclically in crises and fund long-term public investment cheaply; critics point to risks of political interference, inefficient lending and contingent liabilities for taxpayers.
