Catalogues of classical compositions

Catalogues of classical compositions are systematic listings of a composer's works, usually compiled by musicologists, which assign each composition a catalogue number used for identification in scholarship, publishing and recording. They complement or replace the sometimes inconsistent opus numbers assigned by composers and publishers.

ExamplesKöchel (Mozart), BWV (Bach), Hob. (Haydn), D (Schubert)
PurposeIdentification, dating, scholarship

Major catalogues

Among the best known are Ludwig von Köchel's catalogue of Mozart (K. numbers, 1862), the Bach-Werke-Verzeichnis (BWV, 1950), Anthony van Hoboken's Haydn catalogue (Hob.), Otto Erich Deutsch's Schubert catalogue (D numbers), and the catalogues for Beethoven (including the WoO list of works without opus). For earlier music, RISM and thematic catalogues serve similar functions.

Use

Catalogue numbers are typically grouped by genre rather than strictly chronological (as in the BWV) or arranged chronologically (as Deutsch attempted for Schubert). They appear in concert programmes, recordings and editions — for example Mozart's Jupiter Symphony is universally cited as K. 551 — and are essential where opus numbers are missing, duplicated or misleading.
