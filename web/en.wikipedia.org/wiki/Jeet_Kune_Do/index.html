Jeet Kune Do

Jeet Kune Do (Chinese: 截拳道; "the way of the intercepting fist") is a martial arts philosophy and system developed by Bruce Lee in the 1960s. Rejecting fixed forms and classical styles, it emphasises practicality, efficiency, directness and simplicity, absorbing what is useful from any source — principally Wing Chun, Western boxing and fencing.

Jeet Kune Do
Founder: Bruce Lee (1940–1973)
Created: c. 1967, Los Angeles / Seattle
Influences: Wing Chun, boxing, fencing, wrestling
Focus: Interception, economy of motion

Origins

Lee, trained in Hong Kong in Wing Chun under Ip Man, began teaching in Seattle and Oakland as Jun Fan Gung Fu. His 1964 fight with Wong Jack Man and his study of boxing, fencing footwork and philosophy led him in 1967 to name his evolving approach Jeet Kune Do — the art of intercepting an opponent's attack, or intention, with the lead hand or foot.

Principles

Core ideas include the strong-side-forward stance, the straight lead punch as chief weapon, non-telegraphed movement, the five ways of attack (single direct, by combination, by drawing, hand immobilisation and progressive indirect), centreline theory, and the maxim "be like water." Lee taught four ranges — kicking, punching, trapping and grappling — and stressed physical conditioning and honest self-expression over classical ritual: "Absorb what is useful, discard what is useless."

After Lee

Lee closed his schools before his death in 1973, leaving notes published posthumously as the Tao of Jeet Kune Do (1975). His students Dan Inosanto and others developed divergent "Original" and "Concepts" lineages, and Jeet Kune Do influenced modern mixed martial arts. See martial arts.
