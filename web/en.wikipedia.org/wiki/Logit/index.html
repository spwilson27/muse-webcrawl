Logit

In statistics, the logit (/ˈloʊdʒɪt/ LOH-jit) function is the quantile function associated with the standard logistic distribution. It has many uses in data analysis and machine learning, in particular in data transformations, and in logistic regression.

Mathematically, the logit is the inverse of the standard logistic function:

σ(x) = 1 / (1 + e−x)

so the logit is defined as

logit p = log(p / (1 − p)) = log p − log(1 − p) = −log((1/p) − 1)

where p is a probability. In this way, the logit function maps a probability value in the range (0, 1) to the real number line. Equivalently, if p is a probability, then p/(1 − p) is the corresponding odds; the logit of the probability is the logarithm of the odds, i.e. the log-odds.

Definition

If p is a probability, then p/(1 − p) is the corresponding odds of the event; the logit of the probability is the logarithm of the odds. Given two probabilities p and q, their log odds ratio is the difference of their logits.

History

The logit model was introduced by Joseph Berkson in 1944, who coined the term. The term was borrowed by analogy from the similar probit model introduced by Chester Ittner Bliss in 1934. G. A. Barnard in 1949 coined the commonly used term log-odds; the log-odds of an event is the logit of the probability of the event.

Uses and properties

The logit in logistic regression is a special case of a link function in a generalized linear model: it is the canonical link function for the Bernoulli distribution. The logit function is the negative of the derivative of the binary entropy function. The logit is also central to the probabilistic Rasch model for measurement, which has applications in psychological and educational assessment, among other areas.
