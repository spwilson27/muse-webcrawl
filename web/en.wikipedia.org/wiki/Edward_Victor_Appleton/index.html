Sir Edward Victor Appleton (6 September 1892 – 21 April 1965) was an English physicist, Nobel Prize winner (1947) and pioneer in radiophysics. He won the Nobel Prize for his investigations of the physics of the upper atmosphere (the ionosphere) using radio waves.

Research

In experiments in 1924 with Miles Barnett, Appleton demonstrated the existence of a reflecting layer in the upper atmosphere—later called the Appleton layer (the F layer of the ionosphere)—which reflects radio signals and enables long-distance shortwave communication. His work underpinned the development of radar.

Career

Appleton held chairs at King's College London and Cambridge, served as secretary of the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research, and became principal of the University of Edinburgh in 1949.
