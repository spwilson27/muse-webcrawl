Andrea Gabrieli (c. 1532/33 – 30 August 1585) was an Italian composer and organist of the late Renaissance. A leading figure of the Venetian School, he was the first internationally famous composer to work at St Mark's Basilica in Venice, and was highly influential in spreading the Venetian style in Italy and southern Germany. He was the uncle of the composer Giovanni Gabrieli.

Life

Born in Venice, Gabrieli may have studied with Adrian Willaert, the founder of the Venetian School. He worked for a time at the court of Duke Albrecht V in Munich, where he associated with Orlande de Lassus, and in 1566 became second organist at St Mark's, rising to first organist in 1584, a year before his death.

Music

Gabrieli composed in nearly every contemporary genre: masses, motets, psalms and ceremonial music for voices and instruments for the great state occasions of the Venetian Republic, madrigals, and keyboard music including ricercars, canzonas and intonazioni. His music for divided choirs and instruments exploited the galleries of St Mark's, laying the foundation for the polychoral style perfected by his nephew Giovanni.

His pupils included Hans Leo Hassler and Jan Pieterszoon Sweelinck's generation of north German organists, through whom his methods shaped the early Baroque.
