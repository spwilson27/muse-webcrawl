Coelenterazine
FormulaC26H21N3O3
Molar mass423.47 g/mol
TypeLuciferin

Coelenterazine is a luciferin, a light-emitting compound found in many marine organisms. It is the substrate of the enzyme luciferase and of photoproteins such as aequorin. When oxidised by luciferase in the presence of oxygen, coelenterazine produces coelenteramide, carbon dioxide and blue light (emission maximum around 475–480 nm).

Distribution

Coelenterazine occurs across at least eight animal phyla, including radiolarians, ctenophores, cnidarians (notably the jellyfish Aequorea victoria), squid, copepods and some fish. Many animals cannot synthesise it and obtain it through their diet.

Applications

Coelenterazine is widely used as a reporter substrate in molecular biology: with aequorin it measures intracellular calcium, and with Renilla luciferase it serves as a reporter of gene expression in bioluminescence assays.
