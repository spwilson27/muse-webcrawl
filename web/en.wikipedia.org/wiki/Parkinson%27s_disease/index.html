Parkinson's disease

SpecialtyNeurology
SymptomsTremor, rigidity, slow movement, difficulty walking
Usual onsetAge over 60
CausesUnknown
TreatmentMedications (levodopa), deep brain stimulation

Parkinson's disease (PD), or simply Parkinson's, is a long-term neurodegenerative disease of the central nervous system affecting both the motor system and non-motor systems. The symptoms usually emerge slowly, and as the disease progresses, non-motor symptoms become more common. Early symptoms include tremor, rigidity, slowness of movement, and difficulty with walking. Problems may also arise in cognition, behaviour, sleep, and sensory systems.

The cause of Parkinson's disease is unknown, but it is believed to involve both genetic and environmental factors. The motor symptoms result from the death of cells in the substantia nigra, a region of the midbrain, which leads to a deficit of dopamine.

Signs and symptoms

The most obvious early symptoms are tremor, rigidity, slowness of movement, and difficulty walking. A mask-like facial expression, small handwriting, and a shuffling gait are also characteristic. Non-motor symptoms include sleep disturbances, depression, and cognitive problems.

Diagnosis

There is no lab test for the condition; diagnosis is based on medical history and a neurological examination. Imaging such as a DaT scan can support the diagnosis.

Treatment

There is no cure. Treatment initially consists of the medication levodopa (L-DOPA), sometimes together with dopamine agonists. As the disease progresses, deep brain stimulation may be used.
