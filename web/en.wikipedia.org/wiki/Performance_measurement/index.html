Performance measurement

Performance measurement is the process of collecting, analysing, and reporting information about the performance of an individual, group, organisation, system, or component. It involves defining what is to be measured, selecting indicators, gathering data, and comparing results against targets or benchmarks in order to support management decisions and accountability.

Approaches

Traditional performance measurement relied heavily on financial indicators such as profit, return on investment, and cost per unit. From the late twentieth century, critics argued that financial measures alone are backward-looking and incomplete, prompting multidimensional frameworks:

  • Balanced scorecard — Kaplan and Norton's framework measures performance across four perspectives: financial, customer, internal processes, and learning and growth.
  • Key performance indicators (KPIs) — a small set of metrics chosen to track progress against critical objectives.
  • Benchmarking — comparing measures against peer organisations or best practice.
  • Performance prism and EFQM Excellence Model — stakeholder-oriented frameworks used especially in Europe.

In the public sector

Governments use performance measurement to demonstrate value for money and improve services, through published targets, league tables, and inspection regimes. Such systems are subject to Goodhart's law: when a measure becomes a target, it may cease to be a good measure, because those measured adapt their behaviour to the indicator rather than to the underlying goal.

Related concepts

At the individual level, organisational performance measurement connects to performance appraisal and performance-related pay. In software and engineering contexts, performance measurement refers instead to quantifying the speed, throughput, and resource use of systems.
