Gelatin dessert

CourseDessert
Main ingredientsGelatin, sweetener, flavourings, colourings
VariationsJell-O (US), jelly crystals (UK/Australia), aspic (savoury)

A gelatin dessert is a sweetened, flavoured dessert made with gelatin (or a vegetarian gelling agent such as agar) dissolved in hot liquid and set by chilling into a translucent, quivering gel. In the United States it is strongly associated with the brand Jell-O; in British and Commonwealth usage the dessert itself is called "jelly".

History

Jellied dishes descend from medieval European aspics and calf's-foot jellies, which required laborious extraction of gelatin from animal bones. Industrial powdered gelatin was patented in 1845 by Peter Cooper. In 1897, in Le Roy, New York, Pearle Bixby Wait and his wife May trademarked a flavoured gelatin dessert under the name "Jell-O"; sold to the Genesee Pure Food Company and later to General Foods, it became a mass-market staple, promoted as "America's most famous dessert" and embedded in 20th-century American food culture, from moulded salads with fruit, vegetables and whipped toppings to the official state snack of Utah (2001).

Preparation and variations

Powdered mixes are dissolved in boiling water, diluted with cold water and refrigerated until set. Fresh pineapple, kiwi, papaya and figs contain proteolytic enzymes that prevent setting unless cooked. Savoury counterparts include aspic; layered, moulded and "jello shot" versions are common, and agar- or carrageenan-based alternatives cater to vegetarian and halal or kosher requirements.
