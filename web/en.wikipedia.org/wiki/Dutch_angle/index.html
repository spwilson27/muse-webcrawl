Dutch angle

Dutch angle
Also called: Dutch tilt, canted angle, oblique angle
Field: Cinematography

The Dutch angle, also known as the Dutch tilt or canted angle, is a type of camera shot that involves setting the camera at an angle on its roll axis so that the shot is composed with vertical lines at an angle to the side of the frame. The effect conveys unease, tension, disorientation, or psychological disturbance.

History

The technique is strongly associated with German Expressionist cinema of the 1920s — hence "Dutch" as a corruption of Deutsch — in films such as The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari. Carol Reed used extreme canted angles in The Third Man (1949), and the shot became a staple of film noir, horror, and comic-book adaptations; the 1960s Batman television series used Dutch angles for every villain's lair. Roger Ebert famously criticised the overuse of the technique in Battlefield Earth (2000).

Use

By violating the viewer's expectation of a level horizon, the Dutch angle signals that something in the scene is wrong. It is also used dynamically in action sequences and video games to suggest movement and instability.
