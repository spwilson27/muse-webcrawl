Degustation

Degustation
DefinitionCareful, appreciative tasting of food or drink; also a multi-course tasting menu
FromFrench dégustation, from Latin degustare, "to taste"
Typical courses5–12 small portions (tasting menu)

Degustation is the careful, appreciative tasting of various food, focussing on the gustatory system, the senses, high culinary art and good company. In restaurant usage, a degustation menu — usually called a tasting menu in English — is a meal of many small courses chosen by the chef to showcase technique, ingredients, and progression of flavour, often offered with paired wines selected by a sommelier.

The tasting menu

The format was popularised by French nouvelle cuisine and the grand restaurants of France, and spread worldwide with chefs such as Joël Robuchon, Ferran Adrià, and the fine-dining avant-garde. A typical degustation runs from five to a dozen or more courses, beginning with snacks and raw or cured preparations, building through fish and meat to palate cleansers and desserts. Portions are small so that diners can experience the whole sequence without satiety blunting appreciation.

Wine degustation

In wine, dégustation is the analytical tasting of samples — assessing colour, aroma, flavour, and structure — practised by winemakers, merchants, and critics, in forms from cellar tastings directly from the barrel to blind comparative tastings.

Criticism

Tasting menus have been criticised for their cost, length (often three hours or more), and the loss of diner choice, and some prominent chefs have abandoned the format; others defend it as the purest expression of a kitchen's identity and seasonality.
