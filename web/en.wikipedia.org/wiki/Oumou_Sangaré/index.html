Oumou Sangaré

Oumou Sangaré (born 25 February 1968) is a Malian singer-songwriter, widely regarded as the leading exponent of Wassoulou music and nicknamed the "songbird of Wassoulou." Her songs, sung in Bambara, draw on the traditional music of the Wassoulou region south of Mali and frequently address women's lives and social issues, including low-caste marriage, polygamy, and women's rights.

Born in Bamako to a family of singers, Sangaré began performing as a child to support her family. Her debut album Moussolou ("Women"), released in 1989 and produced by Ibrahima Sylla, sold hundreds of thousands of copies in West Africa and launched her international career through subsequent albums such as Ko Sira, Worotan, Seya and the Grammy-winning collaboration Imagine with Herbie Hancock. Her 2017 album Mogoya and its remix Mogoya Remixed brought renewed acclaim.

Beyond music, Sangaré is a successful businesswoman — in hotels, agriculture and vehicle importation — and an advocate for women's empowerment, serving as a goodwill ambassador for the UN Food and Agriculture Organization. In 2022 she released Timbuktu, recorded partly during Mali's lockdowns.
