Reference librarian

A reference librarian is a librarian who specialises in reference work — assisting library users with finding information, answering questions, and teaching research skills. Reference librarians staff reference desks in public, academic, school and special libraries, and increasingly provide help through digital channels such as chat, email and video consultation.

Duties

The core task is the reference interview: clarifying what a patron actually needs, which is frequently different from the question first asked. Reference librarians search catalogues, subscription databases and the open web; locate and evaluate sources; manage interlibrary loan requests; and create research guides and tutorials. In academic libraries they often hold liaison roles for specific departments, contributing to collection development and information-literacy instruction. Many also develop and maintain the library's reference collection of encyclopedias, directories, bibliographies and databases.

History

Formal reference service developed in the late 19th century; Samuel Swett Green's 1876 paper "Personal Relations Between Librarians and Readers" is widely regarded as the founding statement of reference work, calling on librarians to actively help readers rather than merely guard collections. Green put his ideas into practice at the Worcester Public Library in Massachusetts.

Digital reference

From the late 20th century, reference services moved online: email and web-form queries, consortial chat services (such as the former 24/7 collaborative networks and QuestionPoint), and embedded librarianship in course-management systems. These services raised new questions about staffing models, quality assessment and user privacy, but extended reference help well beyond library buildings and hours. Contemporary debates concern the impact of generative artificial intelligence on question-answering and the librarian's continuing role in evaluating information quality.

Qualifications

Professional reference positions typically require a master's degree in library and information science (such as the ALA-accredited MLIS) and subject knowledge relevant to the library's community.
