The Oklahoma City bombing was a domestic terrorist truck bombing of the Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, United States, on April 19, 1995. Prior to the September 11 attacks, it was the deadliest terrorist attack in the history of the United States, and it remains the deadliest act of domestic terrorism in U.S. history. Perpetrated by anti-government extremist Timothy McVeigh, the bombing killed 168 people, injured 680 others, and destroyed or damaged 324 buildings and 86 cars within a sixteen-block radius.

Attack and investigation

McVeigh parked a rented Ryder truck packed with about 2,300 kg of ammonium nitrate fertiliser, nitromethane and diesel fuel in front of the nine-storey building and ignited time fuses. The blast sheared off the building's north face; more than a third of the structure collapsed, crushing the America's Kids daycare centre on the second floor — 19 of the dead were children. Within 90 minutes, Oklahoma Highway Patrol trooper Charlie Hanger stopped McVeigh for driving without a licence plate and arrested him for illegal weapons possession; forensic evidence quickly tied him to the bombing. Terry Nichols, his army friend and accomplice, was charged as co-conspirator.

McVeigh said he chose the date — the second anniversary of the fiery end of the Waco siege — and targeted the federal government in retaliation for Waco and Ruby Ridge. He was convicted on all counts in 1997, sentenced to death, and executed by lethal injection at the federal prison in Terre Haute, Indiana, on June 11, 2001. Nichols was sentenced to life imprisonment.

Aftermath

The bombing led to the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, hardened security at federal buildings (setback barriers and blast-resistant design), and remains a reference point in debates over domestic extremism. The site is now the Oklahoma City National Memorial & Museum; its Field of Empty Chairs holds 168 chairs, one for each victim, and the surviving elm — the "Survivor Tree" — stands across from the reflecting pool.
