Vaspurakan (Armenian: Վասպուրական; also spelled Vasbouragan) was a historic province and later a kingdom of Armenia, centred on Lake Van in the Armenian Highlands, in what is now eastern Turkey. The name derives from Middle Persian vaspūr ("prince"), meaning roughly "land of princes". It was the largest province of Greater Armenia and for centuries the hereditary domain of the Artsruni dynasty.

History

Province of Greater Armenia

Under the Arsacid kings, Vaspurakan was one of the great provinces of the Kingdom of Armenia, governed by the Artsruni family, one of the leading princely (nakharar) houses. After the partition of Armenia between Rome and Persia in 387 and the fall of the Arsacid monarchy in 428, the region passed under Sasanian, then Arab, rule, while the Artsruni retained considerable local power.

Artsruni kingdom

In 908, Gagik I Artsruni was crowned king of Vaspurakan, with the support of the Arab emir of Azerbaijan, as a counterweight to the Bagratuni king Smbat I. The kingdom's golden age under Gagik (908–943) saw extensive building, notably the palatine Church of the Holy Cross on the island of Akhtamar (Aghtamar) in Lake Van, famed for its extraordinary exterior relief sculpture depicting biblical and royal scenes. The royal court at Akhtamar and Van was a centre of Armenian culture, produced illustrated manuscripts, and maintained its own catholicosate for a time.

Byzantine annexation

Pressured by Muslim neighbours and court intrigue, King Senekerim-Hovhannes Artsruni ceded Vaspurakan to the Byzantine emperor Basil II in 1021–1022, receiving lands around Sebasteia (Sivas) in Cappadocia in exchange. Tens of thousands of Armenians migrated with him. Vaspurakan became a Byzantine theme (catepanate). After the Battle of Manzikert in 1071 the region fell to the Seljuk Turks, and later passed through Mongol, Timurid, Safavid, and Ottoman hands, remaining heavily Armenian-populated until 1915.

Geography

The province stretched around Lake Van — the largest lake of the region — encompassing fertile valleys and mountain districts south of the lake toward the Hakkari ranges and east toward the Persian frontier near Khoy.
