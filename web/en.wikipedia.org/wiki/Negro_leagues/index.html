Negro leagues

The Negro leagues were professional baseball leagues in the United States composed primarily of African American players (and, for a period, Latin American players) during the era when organised baseball enforced racial segregation. Seven leagues operated between 1920 and about 1960 are collectively regarded as the major Negro leagues.

History

Black professional teams existed from the 1880s, but the first successful league, the Negro National League, was founded in 1920 by Rube Foster, owner of the Chicago American Giants. Other major leagues included the Eastern Colored League, the Negro American League, and the second Negro National League. The leagues staged their own World Series and East–West All-Star Games, the latter often outdrawing the major-league equivalent.

Players

The Negro leagues showcased some of the finest players of the century, among them Satchel Paige, Josh Gibson, Cool Papa Bell, Oscar Charleston, Buck Leonard and Monte Irvin — many of whom never had the opportunity to play in the white major leagues during their primes.

Decline and legacy

After Jackie Robinson broke the colour line with the Brooklyn Dodgers in 1947, integration drew the Negro leagues' best players and much of their audience to organised baseball, and the leagues declined, folding by the early 1960s. In 2020, Major League Baseball officially recognised the seven Negro major leagues as major leagues, and in 2024 it incorporated Negro league statistics into its official records. The Negro Leagues Baseball Museum in Kansas City preserves their history.
