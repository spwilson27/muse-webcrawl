High Court of Admiralty

The High Court of Admiralty was historically a court of England and Wales (and later of the United Kingdom) exercising jurisdiction over maritime matters. Established in the 14th century under the authority of the Lord High Admiral, it dealt initially with piracy and other offences committed at sea, and later with civil disputes over shipping, salvage, collision, prize and maritime contracts.

History and procedure

Unlike the common-law courts, the Admiralty court proceeded according to civil law principles, without a jury, and its practitioners were the civilians of Doctors' Commons. Its criminal jurisdiction over offences on the high seas was effectively transferred to the common-law courts by the Offences at Sea Act 1535. The court heard famous prize cases arising from Britain's wars, and from the 19th century its judge sat in the Doctors' Commons court and later the Royal Courts of Justice. The Judicature Acts of 1873–75 folded the court into the new High Court of Justice as part of the Probate, Divorce and Admiralty Division.

Modern admiralty jurisdiction

Today the admiralty jurisdiction in England and Wales is exercised by the Admiralty Court within the King's Bench Division of the High Court, governed by the Senior Courts Act 1981. Scotland has its own admiralty jurisdiction in the Court of Session, the separate Scottish High Court of Admiralty having been abolished in the 19th century.
