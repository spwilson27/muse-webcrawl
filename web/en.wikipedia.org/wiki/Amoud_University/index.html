Amoud University (Somali: Jaamacadda Camuud) is a comprehensive public university located in the Amoud Valley, about five kilometres east of Borama in the Awdal region of Somaliland. Established in 1998, it was the first post-secondary institution to be founded in Somaliland after the Somali Civil War, and it is frequently ranked among the leading universities in the Somali territories.

History

The idea for the university emerged from community fundraising among Awdal elders and the Somali diaspora following the destruction of educational infrastructure in the civil war. Amoud opened in November 1998 with 66 students and three faculties — education, business administration and agriculture — staffed largely by returning diaspora academics. It has since grown into a multi-faculty institution with additional campuses in Borama and other towns.

Faculties

The university comprises faculties including medicine and surgery, education, business and public administration, economics, agriculture and environment, engineering, computing and ICT, law, dentistry, nursing and midwifery, and health sciences. Instruction is in English. Its medical school, supported by international partners, supplies doctors to hospitals across the region.

Significance

Amoud University is regarded as a symbol of community-led reconstruction in Somaliland: it is owned by the community rather than the state, governed by a board drawn from local stakeholders, and financed through tuition, diaspora support and endowments. Its graduates include many of the region's civil servants, teachers and health professionals.
