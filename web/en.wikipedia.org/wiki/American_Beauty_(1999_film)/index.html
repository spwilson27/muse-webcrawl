American Beauty (1999 film)

American Beauty is a 1999 American black comedy-drama film written by Alan Ball and directed by Sam Mendes in his feature directorial debut. Kevin Spacey stars as Lester Burnham, an advertising executive in the suburbs who suffers a midlife crisis, quits his job, and becomes infatuated with his teenage daughter's friend (Mena Suvari), while his wife Carolyn (Annette Bening) pursues an affair and obsesses over real-estate success. The film is narrated posthumously by Lester.

Plot

Lester Burnham, dead by the film's end, recalls the last year of his life: blackmailing his employer for a severance package, working at a fast-food drive-through, buying the Pontiac Firebird of his youth, and defying his wife. Interwoven threads follow his daughter Jane and her boyfriend Ricky Fitts — a drug-dealing teenage videographer fascinated by a plastic bag dancing in the wind — and Ricky's repressed father, Colonel Frank Fitts, whose misunderstanding leads to the fatal conclusion.

Themes

Critics have read the film as a critique of suburban American values, beauty and conformity, and the repression of sexuality; Ball described it as a meditation on how people imprison themselves and on the beauty found in ordinary things. The rose — Carolyn's cultivated American Beauty roses — is its central symbol.

Reception

The film was a major critical and commercial success, winning five Academy Awards including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor, Best Original Screenplay, and Best Cinematography. It grossed over $356 million worldwide on a $15 million budget.
