Acrylate
Parent compoundAcrylic acid
Functional groupCH2=CH–COO–
Common examplesMethyl acrylate, ethyl acrylate, butyl acrylate, methyl methacrylate

Acrylates are the salts, esters, and conjugate bases of acrylic acid. The acrylate ion is CH2=CH–COO−. More broadly, the term covers a large family of vinyl monomers containing the acrylate or methacrylate group, which polymerise readily by free-radical polymerisation to form acrylic plastics, resins, paints, adhesives, and superabsorbent materials.

Production

Acrylic acid is produced industrially by the two-stage oxidation of propylene via acrolein. Its esters — methyl, ethyl, n-butyl, and 2-ethylhexyl acrylate — are manufactured on a scale of millions of tonnes per year. Methyl methacrylate, the monomer for poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA, "acrylic glass"), is made by the acetone cyanohydrin process and newer routes based on ethylene or isobutylene.

Polymers and uses

  • Acrylic paints and coatings — water-borne emulsions of acrylate copolymers.
  • Adhesives and sealants — including pressure-sensitive adhesives and anaerobic thread-lockers.
  • Superabsorbent polymers — cross-linked sodium polyacrylate, used in disposable nappies (diapers).
  • Acrylic fibres and textiles.
  • PMMA — transparent sheet, lenses, and bone cement (the latter polymerised in situ during surgery).
  • Cyanoacrylates — the "superglues", a distinct but related family.

Safety

Acrylate monomers are irritants and potent contact sensitisers; occupational exposure in dentistry, nail salons, and manufacturing is regulated. The polymers themselves are generally inert and biocompatible.
