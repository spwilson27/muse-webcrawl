Grape juice

Grape juice is a beverage obtained from crushing and blending grapes into a liquid. It is consumed as a drink in its own right, used as a base for non-alcoholic "wine" alternatives, and — in the case of unpasteurised must — is the raw material of wine before fermentation.

Production

Commercial grape juice is typically made from Concord grapes in the United States, giving it a deep purple colour and strong flavour, while white grape juice is made from green varieties such as Niagara. The juice is pasteurised to prevent fermentation and spoilage. Thomas Bramwell Welch pioneered the commercial pasteurised grape-juice industry from 1869, having developed the product as a non-alcoholic substitute for communion wine.

Religious use

Grape juice is used in place of wine in communion by many churches and temperance-minded denominations, and unfermented grape juice is permitted where alcohol is prohibited.

Nutrition

Grape juice contains natural sugars comparable to many soft drinks, modest amounts of vitamin C, and polyphenol antioxidants such as resveratrol found in grape skins.
