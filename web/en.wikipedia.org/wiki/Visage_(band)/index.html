Visage (band)

Visage were a British synth-pop band, formed in London in 1978. The band was a studio project led by singer Steve Strange and drummer Rusty Egan, closely associated with the New Romantic movement and the Blitz club scene. They are best known for the 1980 single "Fade to Grey", an international hit that became one of the defining songs of the early 1980s electronic pop era.

History

Formation and "Fade to Grey" (1978–1981)

Strange and Egan had previously played in the punk band the Moors Murderers and the group the Photons before forming Visage with Midge Ure and Billy Currie of Ultravox, along with members borrowed from Magazine: Barry Adamson, John McGeoch and Dave Formula. The band's name referenced the French word for "face" and the Roxy Music song "Visage". Their debut single, a cover of "In the Year 2525", appeared in 1979.

Their self-titled debut album Visage (1980) contained "Fade to Grey", co-written by Currie and Chris Payne, with French spoken passages by Brigitte Arens. The single reached number 8 in the UK and number one in several European countries, selling over a million copies.

Later work and revivals

The second album, The Anvil (1982), named after a New York gay club, produced the singles "The Damned Don't Cry" and "Night Train". After line-up instability, the third album Beat Boy (1984) was less successful and the band dissolved. Strange re-formed Visage in the 2000s; a new incarnation, fronted by Strange with former members Steve Barnacle and others, released the album Hearts and Knives in 2013, followed by Demons to Diamonds (2015), completed after Strange's death in February 2015.

Legacy

Visage are regarded as a key link between disco, glam rock and the electronic pop of the 1980s. The Blitz club, where Egan DJ'd and Strange presided over the door, launched the careers of Spandau Ballet, Culture Club and others, and Visage's image-driven approach anticipated the music-video era.
