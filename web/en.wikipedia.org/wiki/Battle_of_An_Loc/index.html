Battle of An Lộc
Date13 April – 20 July 1972
LocationAn Lộc, Bình Long Province, South Vietnam
ResultSouth Vietnamese victory

The Battle of An Lộc was a major battle of the Vietnam War that lasted 66 days, fought between 13 April and 20 July 1972 in Bình Long Province, South Vietnam. It was part of North Vietnam's Easter Offensive (Nguyễn Huệ Offensive), a three-pronged invasion launched on 30 March 1972.

Background

An Lộc, a provincial capital about 100 km north of Saigon near the Cambodian border, sat astride Highway 13, the main road from Cambodia toward the South Vietnamese capital. Capturing it would give the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) a staging point and a prospective capital for the Provisional Revolutionary Government. After overrunning Lộc Ninh to the north, PAVN forces — the 5th, 7th and 9th Divisions, supported by tanks and artillery — advanced on An Lộc in early April 1972.

The siege

The town was defended by the Army of the Republic of Vietnam (ARVN) 5th Division under Brigadier General Lê Văn Hưng, reinforced by Ranger and provincial forces. The first assault on 13 April was beaten back, but the PAVN surrounded the town and subjected it to one of the heaviest artillery bombardments of the war — by some estimates tens of thousands of shells. Defenders were compressed into a perimeter roughly a kilometre square.

United States airpower proved decisive. B-52 Stratofortress strikes, tactical aircraft, and AC-130 gunships broke up massed PAVN attacks, while supplies were delivered by parachute after helicopters proved too vulnerable; high-altitude drops using the GRADS system improved accuracy late in the siege. PAVN tank attacks on 11 and 14 May were repulsed with heavy losses.

Aftermath

By late May the siege was effectively broken, and in June ARVN forces reopened Highway 13. Fighting sputtered into July before the PAVN withdrew. North Vietnamese casualties were estimated in the thousands, with over 40 tanks destroyed; An Lộc itself was almost completely destroyed. The stand at An Lộc, along with the defence of Kontum and the recapture of Quảng Trị, blunted the Easter Offensive and influenced the Paris peace negotiations.
