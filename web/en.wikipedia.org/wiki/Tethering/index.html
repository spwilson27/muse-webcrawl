Tethering

In computing, tethering is the sharing of a mobile device's Internet connection with other connected computers. Connection of the phone with other devices can be done over wireless LAN (Wi-Fi), over Bluetooth, or by physical connection using a cable, for example through USB. When tethering over Wi-Fi, the phone creates a mobile hotspot that other devices can join; tethering is built into modern mobile operating systems such as Android and iOS as a "personal hotspot" feature.

Methods

USB tethering is typically the fastest and also charges the phone, but works with one device at a time. Wi-Fi tethering supports several devices at once at the cost of higher battery drain. Bluetooth tethering is slower but more energy-efficient. Some devices also support wired Ethernet sharing.

Carrier policies

Mobile carriers have historically restricted or charged extra for tethering, detecting it through traffic inspection and requiring tethering-specific data plans. Many modern plans include a hotspot data allowance, after which speeds may be reduced.

Other meanings

More generally, tethering means tying an object — such as an animal, a balloon, or an astronaut on a spacewalk — with a rope or tether so that it cannot drift or stray beyond a set range.
