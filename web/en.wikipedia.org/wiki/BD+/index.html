BD+

BD+ is an optional extra layer of copy protection available on Blu-ray Discs.

BD+ is a component of the Blu-ray Disc digital rights management (DRM) system, developed by Cryptography Research Inc. and licensed through the Blu-ray Disc Association. It supplements the mandatory AACS content-scrambling system with a small virtual machine embedded in every licensed Blu-ray player, allowing content providers to execute disc-specific protection code on the player itself.

How it works

Each BD+ disc may carry native code programs that run inside the player's BD+ virtual machine. These programs can inspect the player's environment — checking its make and model, its stored keys and whether it shows signs of tampering — and can transform portions of the audio and video stream at playback time, so that the data physically stored on the disc is incomplete or subtly scrambled until the BD+ program corrects it. Because the protection code ships on the disc, studios could in principle update their defences on new releases when earlier schemes were broken, unlike the fixed CSS scheme used on DVDs.

History and circumvention

BD+ was notably adopted by 20th Century Fox from 2007. The scheme was first defeated publicly by the commercial ripping program AnyDVD HD from SlySoft in 2008, and open-source implementations of the BD+ virtual machine followed, so BD+ discs can now be played and copied by common tools. Like most DRM, BD+ occasionally caused playback failures on legitimate players, particularly early models and PC software, and has largely faded from prominence along with the physical-disc market itself.
