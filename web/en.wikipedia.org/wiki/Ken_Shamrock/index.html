Ken Shamrock

Shamrock during his professional wrestling career

Kenneth Wayne Shamrock (born Kenneth Wayne Kilpatrick; February 11, 1964) is an American retired mixed martial artist and professional wrestler. A pioneer of mixed martial arts (MMA) and one of the UFC's first stars, he headlined numerous events and was the first UFC Superfight Champion. He is best known for his time in the Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC), Pancrase in Japan, and the World Wrestling Federation (WWF, now WWE), where he was an Intercontinental Champion. His nickname, "The World's Most Dangerous Man", was coined by ABC News.

Early life and Pancrase

Born at MacDill Air Force Base in Florida, Shamrock had a troubled youth in California before being taken in at the Shamrock Ranch boys' home run by Bob Shamrock, whose surname he adopted. After a start in professional wrestling and shootfighting, he helped found the Pancrase organisation in Japan in 1993, becoming its first King of Pancrase and popularising submission grappling.

UFC career

Shamrock fought at the inaugural UFC 1 in 1993, losing to Royce Gracie. Their rematch at UFC 5 in 1995 — for the new Superfight Championship — ended in a draw after 36 minutes, and Shamrock went on to defend the Superfight title against Dan Severn and Kimo Leopoldo. His 2002–2006 trilogy against Tito Ortiz was among the biggest box-office rivalries of the early Zuffa era, and he coached opposite Ortiz on The Ultimate Fighter 3. Shamrock was inducted into the UFC Hall of Fame in 2003, in its inaugural class alongside Gracie.

Professional wrestling

In the WWF (1997–1999), Shamrock portrayed an intense shootfighter character, winning the Intercontinental Championship and the 1998 King of the Ring tournament, and feuding with The Rock and Steve Austin. He later wrestled for TNA, winning the NWA World Heavyweight Championship in 2002, and returned to Impact Wrestling in 2019, entering its Hall of Fame in 2020.
