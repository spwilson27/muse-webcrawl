Los Alamos National Laboratory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Los Alamos National Laboratory
Founded: 1943, as Project Y of the Manhattan Project
Location: Los Alamos, New Mexico, USA
First director: J. Robert Oppenheimer

Los Alamos National Laboratory is a United States Department of Energy laboratory in northern New Mexico, founded in 1943 as the secret weapons-design site (Project Y) of the Manhattan Project.

The laboratory was built on the Pajarito Plateau, on the site of a boys' ranch school, and directed by J. Robert Oppenheimer. There, scientists and engineers — including many European émigrés — designed and built the first atomic bombs: the uranium gun-type device used at Hiroshima and the plutonium implosion device tested at Trinity and used at Nagasaki.

After the war Los Alamos remained the centre of American nuclear weapons design, developing thermonuclear weapons in the 1950s (see thermonuclear weapon) alongside rival Livermore Laboratory, founded in 1952. Today its missions span stockpile stewardship without explosive testing, supercomputing, materials science, bioscience and nonproliferation, employing roughly ten thousand people in the town that grew up around it.
