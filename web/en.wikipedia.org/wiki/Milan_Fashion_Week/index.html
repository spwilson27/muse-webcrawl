Milan Fashion Week (Italian: Settimana della moda di Milano) is a clothing trade show held semi-annually in Milan, Italy. The autumn/winter event is held in February/March of each year and the spring/summer event in September/October. It is one of the "Big Four" global fashion weeks, together with New York, London and Paris.

History

Milan Fashion Week was established in 1958 and is organised by the Camera Nazionale della Moda Italiana (National Chamber of Italian Fashion), founded the same year. In the 1970s Milan rose to challenge Florence and Paris as the centre of Italian fashion, as ready-to-wear houses such as Armani, Versace, Dolce & Gabbana, Prada, Gucci, Fendi, Valentino, Moschino and Bottega Veneta made the city synonymous with Italian luxury and craftsmanship.

Events

The week's calendar comprises runway shows, presentations and trade events for womenswear (Milano Fashion Week Donna) and menswear (Milano Fashion Week Uomo, held in January and June), staged at venues around the city including the Quadrilatero della moda fashion district and large show spaces such as the Palazzo Reale area and former industrial sites. Recent editions have combined physical shows with digital presentations, a format accelerated during the COVID-19 pandemic, when the July 2020 edition was held entirely online.

Significance

Milan's fashion industry is a major pillar of the Italian economy and of the city's identity; fashion weeks draw tens of thousands of buyers, journalists and visitors and generate substantial revenue for the city. Milan is particularly associated with leather goods, tailoring and luxury prêt-à-porter.
