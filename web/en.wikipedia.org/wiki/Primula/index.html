Primula

Scientific classification

Kingdom: Plantae

Order: Ericales

Family: Primulaceae

Genus: Primula L.

Primula is a genus of herbaceous flowering plants in the family Primulaceae. They include the primrose (P. vulgaris), a familiar wildflower across Europe, as well as cowslips (P. veris) and auriculas. The genus includes roughly 500 species, native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere and to mountainous regions in the south.

The genus name Primula is a diminutive of the Latin primus, meaning "first", referring to the early flowering of many species in spring. Primulas are characterised by basal rosettes of leaves and clusters of tubular or bell-shaped flowers, often with a contrasting eye.

Cultivation

Primulas are popular garden and bedding plants. Many hybrids and cultivars, especially of Primula × polyantha (polyanthus), are grown for their bright spring flowers in a wide range of colours. They prefer moist, humus-rich soil and partial shade. Some species, such as the Himalayan candelabra primulas, are grown beside streams and ponds.

Ecology

Many Primula species exhibit heterostyly, producing two flower forms ("pin" and "thrum") with reciprocal placement of anthers and stigmas, promoting cross-pollination. Charles Darwin studied this phenomenon and described it in his 1862 paper on dimorphism in Primula. The plants are an important early source of nectar for insects.
