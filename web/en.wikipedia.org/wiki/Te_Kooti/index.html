Te Kooti

Bornc. 1832, near Gisborne, New Zealand
Died1893, Ohiwa, Bay of Plenty, New Zealand
OccupationMāori leader, prophet, guerrilla fighter
Known forTe Kooti's War; founding the Ringatū faith

Te Kooti Arikirangi Te Turuki (c. 1832 – 1893) was a Māori leader and prophet in 19th-century New Zealand. He founded the Ringatū faith and led armed resistance against the New Zealand government during the New Zealand Wars, in a campaign known as Te Kooti's War.

Early life

Te Kooti was born near Gisborne, on the East Coast of the North Island. As a young man he was associated with mission Christianity and became known for his knowledge of the Bible. He worked as a trader and seaman and developed a local reputation as a troublemaker among settlers.

Exile and visions

In 1866, after fighting alongside government forces against the Pai Mārire (Hauhau) movement at Waerenga-a-Hika, Te Kooti was accused of aiding the enemy and was exiled without trial to the Chatham Islands. While imprisoned there he experienced visions and developed the faith that became Ringatū ("the upraised hand"). In July 1868 he led a mass escape of prisoners, seizing the schooner Rifleman and sailing back to the mainland.

Te Kooti's War

After landing near Poverty Bay, Te Kooti sought to live peacefully but was pursued by colonial forces. His followers carried out attacks including the raid on Matawhero in November 1868. For several years he evaded capture through the rugged Urewera and King Country regions, pursued by government troops and Māori allies of the Crown. He was eventually pardoned in 1883.

Later life and legacy

After his pardon Te Kooti travelled among his followers, consolidating the Ringatū Church, which continues today as one of the major Māori Christian movements. He died in 1893 at Ohiwa. His life has been the subject of novels, plays and scholarship, and he remains one of the most debated figures of the New Zealand Wars.
