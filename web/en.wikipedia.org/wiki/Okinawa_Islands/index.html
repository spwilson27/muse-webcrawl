Okinawa Islands

The Okinawa Islands (Japanese: 沖縄諸島, Okinawa Shotō) are an island group in Okinawa Prefecture, Japan, in the southwestern part of the Japanese archipelago. They form part of the larger Ryukyu Islands group. The largest island is Okinawa Island, home to the prefectural capital, Naha.

Geography

The group includes Okinawa Island and numerous smaller islands, including Kume Island, Iheya Island, Izena Island, Ie Island, Sesoko Island, Minna Island, Tsuken Island, Kudaka Island, Aguni Island and the Kerama Islands. The islands are surrounded by coral reefs and have a subtropical climate.

History

The islands were part of the Ryukyu Kingdom, which maintained tributary relations with both China and Japan before being annexed by Japan in the 1870s. During the Second World War, Okinawa Island was the site of the Battle of Okinawa (1945), one of the largest and bloodiest battles of the Pacific War. After the war, the islands were administered by the United States until their reversion to Japan in 1972. A substantial United States military presence remains, centred on bases on Okinawa Island.

Culture

The islands have a distinct culture and Indigenous languages, the Ryukyuan languages, including Okinawan. Traditional arts such as karate, which originated in Okinawa, and the musical instrument sanshin are associated with the islands.
