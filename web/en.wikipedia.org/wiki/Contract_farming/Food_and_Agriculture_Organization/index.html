Food and Agriculture Organization

The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) is a specialised agency of the United Nations that leads international efforts to defeat hunger and improve nutrition and food security. Its Latin motto, fiat panis, translates to "let there be bread". It was founded on 16 October 1945 in Quebec City, Canada, and its headquarters have been in Rome, Italy, since 1951.

Work

The FAO works in over 130 countries, helping governments and communities with agriculture, forestry, fisheries and rural development. Its activities include collecting and publishing statistics (such as the FAOSTAT database), setting standards through bodies like the Codex Alimentarius Commission (jointly with the WHO), monitoring food security through reports such as The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World, and responding to emergencies such as locust outbreaks and animal diseases.

Programmes and guidance

The FAO promotes sustainable agriculture and provides technical guidance on topics including contract farming, value chains, land tenure (through the Voluntary Guidelines on Land Tenure), and smallholder support. It publishes flagship reports including The State of Food and Agriculture and The State of World Fisheries and Aquaculture.

Governance

The FAO Conference, composed of all member nations, meets every two years to set policy and approve the budget. The Director-General serves a four-year term, renewable once. Qu Dongyu of China became Director-General in August 2019 and was re-elected in 2023.
