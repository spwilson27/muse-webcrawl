Agribusiness

Agribusiness is the industry engaged in farming, food production and the associated supply chains. The term was coined in 1957 by John Davis and Ray Goldberg of Harvard Business School, who defined it as "the sum total of all operations involved in the manufacture and distribution of farm supplies; production operations on the farm; and the storage, processing, and distribution of farm commodities and items made from them."

Sectors

Agribusiness spans input suppliers (seed, fertiliser, pesticides, machinery and animal feed), farm production, food processing and manufacturing, distribution, retail and food service. Large firms in the sector include seed and agrochemical companies such as Bayer and Corteva; grain traders such as Cargill, Archer Daniels Midland and Bunge; meat processors such as Tyson Foods and JBS; and machinery makers such as John Deere.

Organisation of production

Much production is coordinated through contracts and vertical integration rather than open markets. In sharecropping-like arrangements of earlier eras and in modern contract farming, processors and retailers supply inputs and specifications to growers and purchase the output, shifting some risks while asserting control over methods and quality. Poultry and hog production in the United States are heavily organised in this way.

Debates

Agribusiness is debated in relation to market concentration, environmental effects of industrial agriculture, animal welfare, food safety and the livelihoods of small farmers. Critics argue that consolidation gives a few corporations disproportionate power over farmers and food prices; supporters emphasise productivity gains and low consumer food costs.
