HMS Warrior is a 40-gun steam-powered armoured frigate built for the Royal Navy in 1859–1861. She was the first iron-hulled armoured warship, built in response to the French ironclad Gloire, and with her sister Black Prince formed the Warrior-class. On completion she was the largest, fastest and most powerful warship in the world, and she rendered all existing wooden fleets obsolete, though she never fought in battle — her very existence acted as a deterrent.

Design

Warrior's iron hull carried an armoured citadel of 4.5-inch (114 mm) wrought-iron plate backed by teak. She combined a single Penn horizontal trunk steam engine of 5,267 indicated horsepower with a full sailing rig, reaching about 14 knots under steam. Her armament mixed smoothbore 68-pounders with Armstrong 110-pounder breech-loaders.

Career and preservation

Advances in naval technology overtook her within a decade; she was withdrawn from seagoing service in 1875 and relegated to subsidiary roles, eventually serving as a depot hulk and oil-fuel pontoon (Warrior as C77) at Pembroke Dock. Saved in 1979, she was restored over eight years at Hartlepool to her 1862 appearance and towed to Portsmouth in 1987, where she is preserved as a museum ship at Portsmouth Historic Dockyard, administered by the National Museum of the Royal Navy.
