International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement

Founded1863 (ICRC); 1919 (IFRC)
HeadquartersGeneva, Switzerland
ComponentsICRC · IFRC · 191 National Societies
MottoPer humanitatem ad pacem

The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement is an international humanitarian movement, founded to protect human life and health, to ensure respect for all human beings, and to prevent and alleviate human suffering. It comprises three distinct components: the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC), and the National Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies of individual countries — 191 of them as of the mid-2020s, together enlisting millions of staff and volunteers worldwide.

History

The movement grew out of the work of Swiss businessman Henry Dunant, who witnessed the aftermath of the Battle of Solferino in 1859 and proposed both volunteer relief societies in each country and an international treaty protecting the wounded. His ideas led to the founding of the ICRC in Geneva in 1863 and the first Geneva Convention in 1864. The League of Red Cross Societies (now the IFRC) was founded in 1919 to coordinate peacetime relief work. Dunant shared the first Nobel Peace Prize in 1901, and the ICRC has received the prize three times.

Fundamental Principles

The whole movement is guided by seven Fundamental Principles, proclaimed in 1965: humanity, impartiality, neutrality, independence, voluntary service, unity and universality. National societies act as auxiliaries to their public authorities in the humanitarian field while maintaining autonomy, and there may be only one Red Cross or Red Crescent society in each country.

Emblems

The protective emblems recognised by the Geneva Conventions are the red cross on white (the reverse of the Swiss flag), the red crescent (adopted by the Ottoman Empire in the 1870s and formally recognised in 1929), and, since 2005, the red crystal, which enabled the admission of Israel's Magen David Adom — the red lion and sun of Iran remains recognised though unused. Misuse of the emblems is prohibited because their protective function in armed conflict depends on universal respect for them.

Roles of the components

The ICRC, a neutral and independent organisation, leads the movement's work in armed conflict: visiting detainees, restoring family links, promoting international humanitarian law and assisting victims. The IFRC coordinates international assistance by national societies in natural disasters and health emergencies and supports their development. National societies provide services ranging from disaster response and blood services to first aid training and community health.
