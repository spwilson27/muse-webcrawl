Electronic component

Electronic component
Classes: passive, active, electromechanical
Examples: resistor, capacitor, transistor, IC
Mounting: through-hole, surface-mount

An electronic component is any basic discrete device or physical entity in an electronic system used to affect electrons or their associated fields. Components are the building blocks of circuits and are usually soldered onto a printed circuit board.

Classification

Passive components — resistors, capacitors, and inductors — cannot introduce net energy into a circuit and do not rely on a separate power source. Active components, such as transistors, diodes, and integrated circuits, can amplify or switch signals using power from a supply. Electromechanical components include switches, relays, connectors, and motors.

Packaging

Early components were mounted with leads through holes in a board; modern electronics overwhelmingly uses surface-mount technology, in which tiny components are soldered directly onto pads, enabling dense, automated assembly.

Integrated circuits

An integrated circuit packages many components — sometimes billions of transistors — on a single chip of semiconductor, forming devices from simple logic gates to complete processors and memories.
