The original World Trade Center was a large complex of seven buildings in the Financial District of Lower Manhattan in New York City. It opened on 4 April 1973 and was destroyed during the September 11 attacks in 2001. At the time of their completion, the Twin Towers — the original 1 World Trade Center (the North Tower) at 1,368 feet (417 m) and 2 World Trade Center (the South Tower) at 1,362 feet (415.1 m) — were the tallest buildings in the world. The complex was designed by architect Minoru Yamasaki and developed by the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey.

Complex

Besides the twin 110-storey towers, the site included the Marriott World Trade Center hotel (3 WTC), 4 and 5 WTC (low-rise plaza buildings), 6 WTC (the U.S. Custom House) and 7 WTC, a 47-storey office building completed in 1987. The complex contained 13.4 million square feet of office space and had its own ZIP code. The North Tower housed the Windows on the World restaurant and an observation deck, Top of the World, stood atop the South Tower.

History

The project was controversial for displacing the Radio Row electronics district and for its austere modernist design, but the towers became icons of the New York skyline and symbols of American economic power. In 1974 Philippe Petit performed a famous high-wire walk between the towers. The complex survived a major fire in 1975 and a truck bombing in the North Tower's basement garage on 26 February 1993, which killed six people.

Destruction

On the morning of 11 September 2001, al-Qaeda terrorists hijacked two Boeing 767 airliners and flew them into the North and South Towers. Both towers collapsed within two hours as a result of impact damage and fire; 7 WTC also collapsed later that day, and the remaining buildings were damaged beyond repair and demolished. The site was redeveloped with a new complex including One World Trade Center and the National September 11 Memorial & Museum.
