Cincture

A cincture used with liturgical vestments

The cincture is a rope-like or ribbon-like article worn with certain Christian liturgical vestments, encircling the body around or above the waist. Two main types exist: the more common is a long rope-like cord, often with tasselled ends, tied around the waist over the alb; the other is a flat broad ribbon of cloth worn with the cassock, sometimes called a fascia in the Roman Catholic Church.

Use

In the Roman Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, and Methodist traditions, the cincture is worn by clergy and servers over the alb or surplice. For priests it is traditionally white, though coloured cinctures matching the liturgical colour of the season are also used. Members of religious orders may wear a cincture as part of their habit, sometimes with three knots symbolising the vows of poverty, chastity, and obedience — notably in the Franciscan tradition.

Symbolism

The cincture is traditionally interpreted as a symbol of chastity and purity. A traditional prayer said while tying it asks God to "gird me with the cincture of purity and quench in my heart the fire of concupiscence." The term derives from the Latin cinctura, a girdle.
