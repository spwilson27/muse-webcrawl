Paddy Buckley Round

The Paddy Buckley Round is a long-distance fell running challenge in Snowdonia, North Wales. The route is a circuit of about 61 miles (98 km) taking in 47 summits, with roughly 28,000 feet (8,500 m) of ascent, and is conventionally attempted within 24 hours. It is regarded as the Welsh counterpart of the Bob Graham Round in the Lake District and the Ramsay Round in Scotland, together forming the "Big Three" British 24-hour rounds.

Route

The round starts and finishes at Llanberis and traverses the principal mountain ranges of Snowdonia, including the Glyderau, the Carneddau, the Moelwynion, and the Snowdon massif itself. The 47 peaks include Snowdon (Yr Wyddfa), Carnedd Llewelyn, Glyder Fawr, and Tryfan. The route is usually divided into five legs, each beginning at a road crossing where support crews can assist the runner.

History

The round was devised by Paddy Buckley, a climber and fell runner associated with the area, and was first completed in 1982 by Martin Stone. Unlike the Bob Graham Round, the challenge has no formal governing club or registration; completions are recorded informally. The fastest times have steadily fallen, with leading fell runners completing the circuit in well under 20 hours.
