Tomb of the Leopards

LocationTarquinia, Italy
TypeEtruscan tomb
Datec. 480–450 BC
Part ofNecropolis of Monterozzi

The Tomb of the Leopards (Italian: Tomba dei Leopardi) is an Etruscan burial chamber in the Necropolis of Monterozzi near Tarquinia, Italy. Dating to around 480–450 BC, it is celebrated for its vivid wall paintings depicting a banqueting scene.

Description

The tomb consists of a single rectangular chamber with a double-sloped ceiling. It takes its name from the two leopards painted facing each other in the pediment of the back wall, flanking a sacred tree. Below them, the main fresco shows banqueters reclining on couches (klinai), attended by servants and musicians playing the lyre and the double flute. The figures wear brightly coloured garments and garlands, and the scene is framed by painted trees and foliage.

Significance

The paintings are among the best-preserved examples of Etruscan art and provide evidence for Etruscan banqueting customs, dress, music and funerary ritual. The Monterozzi necropolis, including this tomb, is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site covering the Etruscan necropolises of Cerveteri and Tarquinia.
