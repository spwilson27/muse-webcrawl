African Democratic Rally
French: Rassemblement Démocratique Africain (RDA)
Founded: 1946, Bamako
Key leader: Félix Houphouët-Boigny
Region: French West and Equatorial Africa

The African Democratic Rally (French: Rassemblement Démocratique Africain, RDA) was a political party and interterritorial movement in French West Africa and French Equatorial Africa, founded in 1946 to press for African political rights and, eventually, independence.

History

The RDA was launched at a congress in Bamako in October 1946, uniting parties across France's African colonies under the leadership of Félix Houphouët-Boigny of Côte d'Ivoire. Initially allied with the French Communist Party in the National Assembly, the RDA broke with the communists in 1950 under colonial pressure and moved toward cooperation with French governments. Its territorial affiliates went on to lead several colonies to independence around 1960.

Legacy

Many post-independence ruling parties in francophone West Africa descended from RDA affiliates, and its name survives in successor parties in several countries.
