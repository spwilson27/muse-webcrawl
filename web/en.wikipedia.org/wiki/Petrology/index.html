Petrology

Petrology (from Ancient Greek pétros 'rock' and lógos 'study') is the branch of geology that studies rocks and the conditions under which they form. Petrology has three subdivisions: igneous, metamorphic, and sedimentary petrology. Igneous and metamorphic petrology are commonly taught together because both make heavy use of chemistry, chemical methods, and phase diagrams. Sedimentary petrology is commonly taught together with stratigraphy because it deals with processes that form sedimentary rock.

Background

Lithology is the basis for subdividing rock sequences into individual lithostratigraphic units for the purpose of mapping and correlation between areas. In certain applications, such as site investigations, lithology is described using a standard terminology such as in the European geotechnical standard Eurocode 7.

Petroleum geology is a specific field of petrology that studies hydrocarbons (oil and natural gas), addressing questions about their origin, migration, and accumulation in the Earth's subsurface.

Petrography

Petrology relies heavily on petrography, the detailed description of rocks in hand specimen and in thin section under a petrographic (polarizing) microscope. The mineral assemblage, texture, and fabric of a rock record its history of formation, deformation, and alteration. Classical petrographic work was pioneered in the 19th century by Henry Clifton Sorby, who demonstrated that thin slices of rock could be examined by transmitted light.

Branches

Igneous petrology

Igneous petrology studies rocks formed from molten material (magma and lava), including volcanic and plutonic rocks. It is concerned with magma generation by partial melting, fractional crystallization, magma mixing, and the classification of igneous rocks by composition and texture. See also igneous rock and magma.

Metamorphic petrology

Metamorphic petrology examines the mineralogical and textural changes that occur when rocks are subjected to elevated temperature, pressure, and chemically active fluids. Concepts such as metamorphic grade, facies, and index minerals (developed by Pentti Eskola and others) allow petrologists to reconstruct pressure–temperature paths. See metamorphic rock.

Sedimentary petrology

Sedimentary petrology deals with clastic and chemical sedimentary rocks—sandstones, shales, limestones, and evaporites—and with the processes of weathering, transport, deposition, and diagenesis that produce them. Provenance analysis uses detrital mineralogy to identify source terrains.

Methods

Modern petrology combines field observation with laboratory techniques: optical microscopy, X-ray diffraction, electron microprobe analysis, mass spectrometry for isotopic dating and tracing, and experimental petrology, in which rock compositions are subjected to controlled high pressures and temperatures to determine phase equilibria.
