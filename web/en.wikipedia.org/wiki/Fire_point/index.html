Fire point

The fire point of a fuel is the lowest temperature at which the vapour of that fuel will continue to burn for at least five seconds after ignition by an open flame. It is closely related to, but somewhat higher than, the flash point, at which vapours ignite momentarily but do not sustain combustion.

Measurement

Fire point is typically determined together with flash point using standard apparatus such as the Cleveland open cup tester (ASTM D92) for petroleum products. The sample is heated at a controlled rate and a test flame is passed over its surface; the flash point is recorded when a flash appears, and the fire point when burning is sustained for five seconds.

Relation to other temperatures

For most flammable liquids, the fire point is roughly 10 °C above the flash point. Both are lower than the autoignition temperature, at which the material ignites spontaneously without an external flame. Fire point data are used in classifying fuels and assessing fire hazards in storage and transport.
