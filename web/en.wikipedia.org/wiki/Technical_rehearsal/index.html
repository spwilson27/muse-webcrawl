Technical rehearsal

A technical rehearsal, often shortened to tech rehearsal or simply tech, is a rehearsal in theatre production that focuses on the technological and production elements of a performance — lighting, sound, scenery, props, costumes, and special effects — rather than on acting or performance interpretation. It is typically one of the final rehearsals before a production opens, and the first in which all technical elements are combined with the performers on stage.

Purpose

The technical rehearsal allows the production team to integrate and troubleshoot every cue in the show. Lighting and sound cues are set and timed, scenery changes are practised, quick costume changes are rehearsed backstage, and any potentially hazardous staging — such as fight choreography, flying effects, or pyrotechnics — is walked through slowly and safely. The goal is to ensure that all departments can execute their parts of the performance reliably and in sequence.

Conduct

Technical rehearsals are usually led by the stage manager, who calls the cues, in coordination with the director and the heads of each technical department. Because the rehearsal proceeds cue to cue — stopping and starting whenever an element needs adjustment — it can be lengthy and tedious for performers; a first tech for a complex show may run for many hours. A common format is the "cue-to-cue", in which only the portions of the show surrounding technical cues are performed, skipping long stretches of dialogue with no cues.

Related rehearsals

The technical rehearsal is often followed by one or more dress rehearsals, in which the show is run in full with costumes and makeup. A "dry tech" is a technical rehearsal held without actors, in which the crew alone sets and runs the cues; a "wet tech" includes the performers. The period during which a production moves into the theatre and completes its technical rehearsals is sometimes called "tech week" or, informally and affectionately, "hell week".
