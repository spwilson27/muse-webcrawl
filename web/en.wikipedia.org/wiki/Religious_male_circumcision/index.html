Religious male circumcision

Religious male circumcision is the practice of circumcision performed for religious reasons, most prominently in Judaism and Islam, and also in some Christian traditions, including Coptic, Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox churches, and in various traditional African and Austronesian cultures.

Judaism and Islam

In Judaism, circumcision (brit milah) is performed on the eighth day of a boy's life in accordance with the covenant described in Genesis 17, by a trained mohel. In Islam, circumcision (khitan) is widely practised, generally regarded as a sunnah of the Prophet Muhammad rather than a Quranic commandment; the age at which it is performed varies by region, from infancy to puberty. Religious circumcision of minors has been the subject of legal and ethical debate in several countries, concerning the balance between religious freedom and children's rights.
