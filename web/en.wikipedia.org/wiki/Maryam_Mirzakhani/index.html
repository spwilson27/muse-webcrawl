Maryam Mirzakhani (Persian: مریم میرزاخانی; 12 May 1977 – 14 July 2017) was an Iranian mathematician and a professor of mathematics at Stanford University. Her research topics included Teichmüller theory, hyperbolic geometry, ergodic theory, and symplectic geometry. In 2014, Mirzakhani was awarded the Fields Medal — the most prestigious award in mathematics, often equated with the Nobel Prize — becoming the first woman and the first Iranian to be honoured with the award. The award recognised her contributions to the dynamics and geometry of Riemann surfaces and their moduli spaces.

Early life and education

Mirzakhani was born in Tehran and attended the National Organization for Development of Exceptional Talents (NODET) Farzanegan School. She won gold medals at the International Mathematical Olympiads of 1994 and 1995, achieving a perfect score in the latter. She earned her bachelor's degree from Sharif University of Technology in 1999 and her PhD from Harvard University in 2004 under Curtis McMullen. Her dissertation gave a new proof of Edward Witten's conjecture and an asymptotic formula for the growth of the number of simple closed geodesics on hyperbolic surfaces.

Career and research

After a Clay Research Fellowship and a professorship at Princeton, Mirzakhani joined Stanford in 2008. Her later work, including a monumental collaboration with Alex Eskin on the dynamics of moduli spaces of abelian differentials, has been described as "the theorem of the decade" in her field. She also worked on counting problems for geodesics and on the volume of moduli spaces, giving a new proof of the Kontsevich–Witten theorem.

Death and legacy

Mirzakhani was diagnosed with breast cancer in 2013 and died on 14 July 2017, aged 40, after the disease spread. Her birthday, 12 May, is now celebrated internationally as International Women in Mathematics Day. The Maryam Mirzakhani New Frontiers Prize, awarded to early-career women mathematicians, was established in her honour.
