MaterialScots pine log

The Pesse canoe is believed to be the world's oldest known boat. It is a dugout canoe made from the hollowed trunk of a Scots pine tree, dating to the early Mesolithic period. Carbon dating indicates it was constructed somewhere between approximately 8040 and 7510 BC.

Discovery

The canoe was discovered in 1955 during the construction of the A28 motorway near the village of Pesse, in the Dutch province of Drenthe. It was found in a peat bog, which had preserved the wood for some ten thousand years. A local farmer initially took it for a trough or beam, but it was recognised as an ancient dugout and transferred to the Drents Museum in Assen, where it is preserved in a freeze-dried state and displayed.

Description

The boat measures about 298 centimetres (9 feet 9 inches) long and 44 centimetres (17 inches) wide — small by later standards, leading some sceptics to question whether such a small log boat could have functioned as a vessel. The doubts were addressed by experimental archaeology: a replica built in 2001 was successfully paddled, demonstrating that the craft was seaworthy for a single occupant. Tool marks suggest it was hollowed out with flint tools, and possibly shaped with the aid of fire.
