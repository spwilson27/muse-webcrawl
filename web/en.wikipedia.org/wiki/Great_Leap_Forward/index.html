Great Leap Forward

The Great Leap Forward was an economic and social campaign by the Chinese Communist Party from 1958 to 1962, led by Mao Zedong, that aimed to rapidly transform China from an agrarian economy into a socialist, industrial society through rapid industrialization and collectivization. Agriculture was reorganized into huge people's communes, and peasants were mobilized to produce steel in small-scale "backyard furnaces".

Famine and aftermath

Instead of growth, the campaign contributed to the Great Chinese Famine (1959–1961), one of the deadliest famines in human history, with excess death estimates ranging from about 15 million to more than 40 million. Causes included unrealistic production quotas, inflated harvest reports and resulting excessive state grain procurement, diversion of labor from farming, collectivized canteens, disastrous agricultural techniques promoted by the Four Pests campaign, and the continued export of grain. The failure discredited Mao temporarily, and reformers Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping took over economic management in the early 1960s.
