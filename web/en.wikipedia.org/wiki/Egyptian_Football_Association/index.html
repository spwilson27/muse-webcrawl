The Egyptian Football Association (EFA; Arabic: الاتحاد المصري لكرة القدم) is the governing body of association football in Egypt. Founded on 3 December 1921, it organises the Egypt national football team, sanctions the country's league and cup competitions — including the Egypt Cup, first played in 1921–22 — and oversees refereeing, coaching, and youth development.

History

The EFA was founded in Cairo in 1921, taking over a football scene that had grown up around British military teams, schools, and the early clubs Al Ahly (1907) and Zamalek (1911). Egypt affiliated with FIFA in 1923 and became the first African nation to appear at both the Olympic football tournament (1920, actually preceding affiliation) and the FIFA World Cup (1934, in Italy, where it was the sole African and Asian representative). In 1957 Egypt was one of the founding members of the Confederation of African Football (CAF), whose first Africa Cup of Nations Egypt won that year in Khartoum.

National teams and competitions

Under the EFA, the Egypt national team — "the Pharaohs" — became the most successful side in Africa Cup of Nations history with seven titles (1957, 1959, 1986, 1998, 2006, 2008, and 2010), including an unprecedented three consecutive championships. Domestically the association supervises the Egyptian Premier League (founded 1948), the Egypt Cup, the Egyptian Super Cup, and the lower divisions, and it administers women's football and the national futsal and beach soccer teams. The association is headquartered in the Gezira district of Cairo.

Crises

The EFA's history includes episodes of political interference and crisis, most gravely the Port Said Stadium riot of February 2012, in which 74 Al Ahly supporters were killed at a match against Al Masry; league football was suspended and then played behind closed doors for extended periods. FIFA has repeatedly required the association's normalisation committees to respect its autonomy from government.
