Vercors

The Vercors is a massif of limestone mountains and plateaus in the French Prealps, in the departments of Isère and Drôme, south-west of Grenoble. Its highest point is the Grand Veymont at 2,341 metres. The name also refers to the author Vercors (Jean Bruller), who took his pen name from the region.

Geography

The massif is a dissected limestone plateau ringed by cliffs, cut by gorges such as the Bourne, and riddled with caves, including the Gouffre Berger, once the deepest known cave in the world. Much of it is protected as the Vercors Regional Natural Park, noted for its forests, alpine meadows, griffon vultures and reintroduced ibex.

History

During the Second World War the plateau became a stronghold of the French Resistance — the Maquis du Vercors. In June–July 1944 the maquisards proclaimed the Free Republic of the Vercors and were crushed by German forces, including glider-borne troops, with heavy losses among fighters and civilians; villages such as Vassieux-en-Vercors were destroyed. The battle remains one of the most remembered episodes of the Resistance.
