The Pirate Bay

Type: BitTorrent index and magnet link site
Founded: 15 September 2003
Founders: Gottfrid Svartholm, Fredrik Neij, Peter Sunde

The Pirate Bay (often abbreviated TPB) is a Swedish website that indexes torrent files and magnet links, facilitating peer-to-peer file sharing via the BitTorrent protocol. Founded in 2003 by members of the Swedish anti-copyright organization Piratbyrån, it became the world's most visited torrent directory and a focal point of legal and political debates over copyright and file sharing.

History

The site's founders — Gottfrid Svartholm, Fredrik Neij, and spokesman Peter Sunde — separated The Pirate Bay from Piratbyrån in 2004. In May 2006 Swedish police raided the site's hosting facility, seizing servers; the site returned within days. In April 2009 the founders and financier Carl Lundström were convicted in the Pirate Bay trial in Stockholm of assisting copyright infringement, receiving prison sentences and large fines, upheld in part on appeal.

Blocking and domains

Courts in numerous countries have ordered ISPs to block the site, and its domains have been seized or changed repeatedly (including a move to a .se domain and various others). The site survived a December 2014 raid and returned in early 2015. In 2012 it shifted to magnet links as its default to reduce hosting requirements.

Cultural impact

The Pirate Bay helped inspire the Pirate Party movement and films such as TPB AFK (2013). Despite sustained legal pressure, it remains one of the most-visited torrent sites.
