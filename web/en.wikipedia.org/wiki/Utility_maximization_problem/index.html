Utility maximization problem

The utility maximization problem is an optimization problem in microeconomics representing the idea that individuals and organizations strive to attain the highest level of utility given their resources and constraints. In consumer theory, the consumer chooses a bundle of goods that maximizes utility subject to a budget constraint: maximize u(x) subject to p·x ≤ m, where p is the price vector, x the consumption bundle, and m income.

Solution

The solution to the problem is the Marshallian (ordinary) demand function, which gives the utility-maximizing quantities as functions of prices and income, and the indirect utility function, which gives the maximum attainable utility at those prices and income. Under standard assumptions of continuous, locally non-satiated preferences, a solution exists, characterized at an interior optimum by the condition that the marginal rate of substitution equals the price ratio.

Duality

The utility maximization problem has a dual, the expenditure minimization problem, whose solution is the Hicksian (compensated) demand function. Relationships between the two problems underpin results such as Roy's identity and the Slutsky equation.
