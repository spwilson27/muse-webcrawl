Delaware and Raritan Canal

The canal and towpath near Stockton, New Jersey

The Delaware and Raritan Canal (D&R Canal) is a canal in central New Jersey, built in the 1830s to provide an efficient route for the transportation of freight between Philadelphia and New York City. It stretches approximately 77 miles (124 km), including its feeder canal, and connects the Delaware River at Bordentown with the Raritan River at New Brunswick.

History

Construction began in 1830 and the canal opened in 1834. The main canal runs about 44 miles (71 km), while a 22-mile (35 km) feeder canal draws water from the Delaware River at Raven Rock southward to Trenton. In its busiest years during and after the American Civil War, the canal carried more tonnage than the more famous Erie Canal, with coal from Pennsylvania constituting the bulk of cargo. Competition from railroads gradually reduced its importance, and the canal closed to commercial navigation in 1932.

State park

In 1974 most of the canal was designated a New Jersey state park. The Delaware and Raritan Canal State Park is one of central New Jersey's most popular recreational corridors, used for hiking, bicycling, jogging, horseback riding, canoeing and fishing along its crushed-stone towpath and the parallel multi-use trail on the feeder canal.
