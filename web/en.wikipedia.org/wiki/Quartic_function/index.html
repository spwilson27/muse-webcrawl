Quartic function

In algebra, a quartic function is a function of the form

f(x) = ax⁴ + bx³ + cx² + dx + e,

with a ≠ 0, defined by a polynomial of degree four, called a quartic polynomial. A quartic equation sets such a polynomial equal to zero. Quartics are the highest-degree polynomials solvable in general by radicals — a consequence of the Abel–Ruffini theorem, which rules out a general formula for degree five and above.

History

The solution of the quartic was discovered by Lodovico Ferrari around 1540 and published in Gerolamo Cardano's Ars Magna (1545), along with the cubic solution of Tartaglia and Cardano. Ferrari's method reduces the quartic to a cubic "resolvent" equation whose solution allows the quartic to be factored into two quadratics.

Graph and properties

The graph of a quartic is a smooth curve with up to three turning points and up to two points of inflection; it has between zero and four real roots. If the leading coefficient is positive the curve rises to +∞ at both ends. Special cases include the biquadratic (only even powers), which reduces to a quadratic in x², and the depressed quartic, in which a substitution removes the cubic term.

Applications

Quartic equations arise in geometry (intersections of conic sections and torus problems), in optics, in the computation of distances between skew lines and ellipsoids, and in computer graphics, for example in ray tracing of quartic surfaces and in some interpolation schemes.
