Papilledema

Papilledema
SpecialtyOphthalmology, neurology
DefinitionOptic disc swelling due to raised intracranial pressure
Usual lateralityBilateral

Papilledema is optic disc swelling that is caused by increased intracranial pressure; by definition it is (almost always) bilateral and occurs without initial loss of visual acuity, distinguishing it from other causes of disc swelling such as optic neuritis, which is typically unilateral and painful with early visual loss. The swollen disc shows blurred margins, venous engorgement, peripapillary haemorrhages, and loss of spontaneous venous pulsation.

Causes

Any cause of raised intracranial pressure can produce papilledema: intracranial mass lesions and haemorrhage, meningitis, hydrocephalus, cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, malignant hypertension, and idiopathic intracranial hypertension (pseudotumor cerebri), the last most often in young women with obesity.

Symptoms and management

Patients may report headache, pulsatile tinnitus, nausea, and transient visual obscurations — brief grey-outs of vision lasting seconds. Untreated, chronic papilledema causes progressive peripheral visual field loss and can end in optic atrophy and blindness. Management is directed at the underlying cause and lowering intracranial pressure; papilledema is a medical urgency requiring prompt neuroimaging.
