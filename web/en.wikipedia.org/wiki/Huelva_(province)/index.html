The province of Huelva (Spanish: Provincia de Huelva) is a province of southern Spain, in the western part of the autonomous community of Andalusia. It borders Portugal to the west (across the Guadiana River), the provinces of Badajoz, Seville and Cádiz, and the Atlantic Ocean (Gulf of Cádiz).

Its capital is the port city of Huelva. Other significant towns include Isla Cristina, Lepe, Moguer, Palos de la Frontera, Punta Umbría, Almonte, Ayamonte and Aracena.

Geography

The province has an area of 10,148 square kilometres. Its northern third lies in the Sierra Morena — including the Sierra de Aracena y Picos de Aroche Natural Park, noted for dehesa oak landscapes and the production of jamón ibérico around Jabugo — while the south is a flat coastal plain with extensive marshes (marismas) along the Tinto, Odiel and Guadiana rivers. The Doñana National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Europe's most important wetlands, lies largely within the province near Almonte, home also to the famous El Rocío pilgrimage.

History

From the port of Palos de la Frontera, Christopher Columbus sailed on his first voyage to the Americas on 3 August 1492, with crews drawn heavily from Palos, Moguer and Huelva; the nearby La Rábida Monastery, where Columbus planned the voyage, and the Muelle de las Carabelas replicas of the Niña, Pinta and Santa María commemorate this heritage. The province has one of the world's oldest mining districts: the Riotinto copper mines in the Iberian Pyrite Belt, worked since Tartessian and Roman times and developed on an industrial scale in the 19th century by the British Rio Tinto Company.

Economy

Modern Huelva is Spain's leading producer of strawberries and other red fruits, centred on Moguer, Lepe and Palos, alongside fishing (Isla Cristina and Punta Umbría, noted for white prawns and tuna), chemical and petrochemical industry at the Port of Huelva, copper mining, and beach and nature tourism along the Costa de la Luz.
