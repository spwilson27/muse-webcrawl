Farofa

Farofa is a traditional Brazilian dish made from toasted cassava flour (farinha de mandioca). It is mainly consumed in Brazil, where typical recipes include raw cassava flour fried in butter or oil with salt and various seasonings, and it is commonly served as an accompaniment to meat dishes, feijoada, and barbecue (churrasco).

Preparation

Farofa is prepared by toasting cassava flour in a pan with fat — commonly butter, vegetable oil, or bacon drippings — until golden. Ingredients frequently added include finely chopped onion, garlic, salt, bacon, sausage, eggs, olives, bananas, carrots, and fresh herbs such as parsley. The texture of the finished dish is similar to coarse breadcrumbs or couscous, and it can range from dry and crisp to moist, depending on the amount of fat used.

Varieties

Many regional and festive varieties exist. Farofa made with banana (farofa de banana) is popular in parts of Brazil, while richer versions containing dried fruits and nuts are prepared at Christmas. In retail, pre-mixed seasoned farofa is widely sold in Brazilian supermarkets. The dish has roots in the foodways of Indigenous peoples of Brazil, for whom cassava flour was a staple, and it developed further during the colonial period.
