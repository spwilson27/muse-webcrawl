Wilhelm von Humboldt

Born: June 22, 1767, Potsdam

Died: April 8, 1835, Tegel

Occupation: Linguist, philosopher, diplomat

Friedrich Wilhelm Christian Karl Ferdinand von Humboldt (June 22, 1767 – April 8, 1835) was a Prussian philosopher, linguist, government functionary, diplomat, and founder of the Humboldt University of Berlin. He was the elder brother of the naturalist Alexander von Humboldt.

Education reform

As Prussian minister responsible for education in 1809–1810, Humboldt designed a comprehensive reform of the school and university system based on the ideal of Bildung — the all-round self-cultivation of the individual. He founded the University of Berlin in 1810, embodying principles of academic freedom, the unity of teaching and research, and education through science that became the model for the modern research university worldwide.

Linguistics

Humboldt's linguistic work, crowned by his posthumous On Language (1836), argued that language is not a finished product (ergon) but an activity (energeia) that shapes thought and reflects the worldview of a people. His studies ranged from Basque and ancient Greek to the Kawi language of Java, and he pioneered the typological classification of languages as isolating, agglutinative, and inflectional.

Political thought

His early essay The Limits of State Action (written 1792, published 1851) defended individual liberty against state interference and influenced John Stuart Mill's On Liberty. As a diplomat he represented Prussia at the Congress of Vienna.
