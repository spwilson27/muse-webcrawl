The Waldorf Astoria New York is a luxury hotel and condominium residence in Midtown Manhattan in New York City. The structure, in the Art Deco style, was designed by architects Schultze and Weaver and completed in 1931. The hotel sits at 301 Park Avenue between 49th and 50th Streets, occupying a full city block.

The Waldorf Astoria New York on Park Avenue

It was the first hotel to offer room service, and was the tallest and largest hotel in the world when it opened. The name derives from the German town of Walldorf and the Astor family, whose fortunes founded the original hotels. The current building is the successor of two earlier hotels, the Waldorf and the Astoria, which stood where the Empire State Building now rises.

History

The original Waldorf Hotel opened in 1893 at Fifth Avenue and 33rd Street, built by William Waldorf Astor. His cousin John Jacob Astor IV opened the adjoining Astoria Hotel in 1897, and the two were soon joined by a corridor and operated as the Waldorf-Astoria. The combined hotel became famous for its lavish public rooms and for hosting the city's elite social events under maître d'hôtel Oscar Tschirky, known as "Oscar of the Waldorf".

When the site was sold for the Empire State Building, the hotel closed in 1929. The new Waldorf Astoria rose on Park Avenue and opened on October 1, 1931, in the depths of the Great Depression. President Herbert Hoover welcomed it in a radio address from the White House. Conrad Hilton acquired management rights in 1949, and the hotel became known for hosting every U.S. president from Hoover to Barack Obama, as well as world leaders, royalty, and celebrities. Composer Cole Porter lived in the hotel's towers for decades.

The Waldorf Towers and notable residents

The upper floors, known as the Waldorf Towers, contained long-term residences occupied by figures including former president Herbert Hoover, General Douglas MacArthur, and the Duke and Duchess of Windsor. The hotel also maintained a private railway siding, Track 61, beneath the building connected to Grand Central Terminal, used by President Franklin D. Roosevelt.

Renovation and conversion

Hilton sold the building in 2014 to the Chinese insurer Anbang (later Dajia Insurance Group) for $1.95 billion, then the highest price ever paid for a U.S. hotel. The hotel closed in March 2017 for a major renovation converting much of the building into luxury condominiums, called The Towers of the Waldorf Astoria, while retaining a smaller hotel component of about 375 rooms. The reopening was delayed several times; the hotel portion was scheduled to welcome guests again in the mid-2020s.

Cultural significance

The hotel gave its name to the Waldorf salad, created by Oscar Tschirky in the 1890s. Its name became a generic symbol of luxury, and the double hyphen in "Waldorf-Astoria" was dropped in 2009 as part of a global rebranding, though a hyphen had connected the twin original hotels.
