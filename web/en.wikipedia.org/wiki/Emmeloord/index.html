Emmeloord is a town in the municipality of Noordoostpolder in the province of Flevoland, Netherlands. It is the administrative capital of the municipality and the central settlement of the Noordoostpolder, land reclaimed from the Zuiderzee as part of the Zuiderzee Works.

History

Emmeloord was founded in 1943 on newly drained polder land, near the site of a former island settlement of the same name on Schokland. The town was laid out as the service centre for ten planned villages arranged in a ring around it, according to modernist planning principles of the period. After the war it grew into a regional centre for agriculture, trade and services.

Geography

The town lies about 3 metres below sea level at the heart of the Noordoostpolder, crossed by the Lemstervaart and Urkervaart canals. Its landmark is the Poldertoren, a 65-metre water tower built in 1957–1959 at the town's central point, which also marks the geographical centre of the polder.

Transport

Emmeloord has no railway station; it is served by regional buses and lies on the A6 motorway between Lelystad and Joure, with the N50 connecting it to Kampen and Zwolle.
