Regina Riot

The Regina Riot was an incident that occurred on Dominion Day, 1 July 1935, in Regina, Saskatchewan, Canada. It was the violent climax of the On-to-Ottawa Trek, in which unemployed men from federal relief camps in British Columbia travelled east by freight train to protest against camp conditions and demand work and wages from the government of Prime Minister R. B. Bennett.

Background

To deal with mass unemployment during the Great Depression, the federal government ran relief camps for single unemployed men, who worked for twenty cents a day in isolated locations. In April 1935 camp workers in British Columbia went on strike, and in June about a thousand of them set out for Ottawa by train. The trek was halted at Regina on Bennett's orders; only the trek leaders were allowed to proceed to a meeting in Ottawa, which broke down in acrimony on 22 June.

The riot

On the evening of 1 July, at a public meeting in Market Square, Royal Canadian Mounted Police and Regina city police moved in — with clubs, tear gas and firearms — to arrest the trek leaders. Hours of street fighting followed across downtown Regina. One city police detective, Charles Millar, was killed, and dozens of police and trekkers were injured; over a hundred men were arrested. A trekker, Nick Schaack, later died of his injuries.

Aftermath

The trekkers were dispersed and returned west with assistance from the Saskatchewan government, while public opinion reacted strongly against the federal government's handling of the affair; the incident contributed to Bennett's defeat in the 1935 election. The relief camps were abolished by the incoming Mackenzie King government. The Regina Riot is remembered as one of the defining confrontations of Canada's Depression years, commemorated in Regina with plaques and in the play and exhibitions devoted to the Trek.
