Riverfront Stadium

Location: Cincinnati, Ohio
Opened: June 30, 1970
Closed: September 22, 2002
Demolished: December 29, 2002
Tenants: Cincinnati Reds (MLB), Cincinnati Bengals (NFL)

Riverfront Stadium was a multi-purpose stadium in Cincinnati, Ohio, on the banks of the Ohio River. Opened in 1970, it was the home of the Cincinnati Reds of Major League Baseball and the Cincinnati Bengals of the National Football League. From 1996 until its demolition it was sponsored as Cinergy Field. The stadium was demolished by implosion on December 29, 2002.

History

Riverfront Stadium replaced Crosley Field, the Reds' home since 1912. Construction began in 1968 as part of a riverfront redevelopment, and the stadium opened on June 30, 1970, with a Reds game against the Atlanta Braves. Weeks later it hosted the 1970 Major League Baseball All-Star Game, famous for Pete Rose's collision with catcher Ray Fosse at home plate in the twelfth inning.

The stadium was a circular "cookie-cutter" design typical of its era, convertible between baseball and football configurations with movable seating sections. Its playing surface was AstroTurf for most of its life, replaced by natural grass in 2001, and it was one of the first stadiums with synthetic turf in the outfield warning track areas.

The Big Red Machine

Riverfront was the home park of the "Big Red Machine," the Reds teams of the 1970s that won consecutive World Series in 1975 and 1976. The park hosted World Series games in 1970, 1972, 1975, 1976, and 1990, when the Reds swept the Oakland Athletics. It also saw Hank Aaron tie Babe Ruth's career home run record on Opening Day 1974, and Pete Rose pass Ty Cobb's all-time hits record on September 11, 1985.

The Bengals played at Riverfront from their 1970 season until 1999, reaching Super Bowls XVI and XXIII following the 1981 and 1988 seasons.

Replacement and demolition

By the 1990s both teams sought separate, single-purpose stadiums. The Bengals moved to Paul Brown Stadium in 2000 and the Reds to the adjacent Great American Ball Park in 2003. Riverfront Stadium's seating bowl was partially demolished in 2001 to make room for the new ballpark, reducing its capacity for its final seasons. The remainder was imploded in December 2002.
