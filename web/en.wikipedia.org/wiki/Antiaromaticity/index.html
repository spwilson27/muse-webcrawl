Antiaromaticity

Antiaromaticity
π electrons4n (n = 0, 1, 2, …)
EffectDestabilisation of cyclic conjugated systems

Antiaromaticity is a property of cyclic, planar, fully conjugated molecules containing 4n π electrons, which renders them especially unstable — the opposite effect to aromaticity, described by Hückel's rule.

Examples

Cyclobutadiene is the classic antiaromatic compound: it is so reactive that it can only be observed at very low temperatures, and it distorts from a square geometry to relieve its instability. The cyclopentadienyl cation is another example.

Characteristics

Antiaromatic compounds show paratropic ring currents in NMR spectroscopy, bond localisation, and high reactivity, typically distorting out of planarity or adopting localised double bonds to escape antiaromatic destabilisation.
