Dogon people

The Dogon are an ethnic group indigenous to the central plateau region of Mali, in West Africa, south of the bend of the Niger River. They are best known for their villages along the Bandiagara Escarpment, their distinctive architecture of mud-brick houses and granaries, and their rich cosmology and masked dances.

Culture

Dogon society is organised around lineages and the hogon, a spiritual leader. Their traditional religion centres on the creator god Amma and ancestor spirits, expressed in elaborate wooden masks used in dama funerary dances. Dogon cosmology attracted wide attention in the 20th century through the work of anthropologist Marcel Griaule, whose accounts of Dogon astronomical knowledge — including controversial claims about the star Sirius — remain disputed.

Language

The Dogon languages form a diverse group of around twenty related languages and dialects spoken by several hundred thousand people.
