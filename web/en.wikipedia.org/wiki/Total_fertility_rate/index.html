Total fertility rate

The total fertility rate (TFR) of a population is the average number of children that would be born to a woman over her lifetime if she experienced the age-specific fertility rates of a given period and survived through her reproductive years. It is one of the most widely used measures in demography.

Replacement level

A TFR of about 2.1 children per woman is considered the replacement level in developed countries — slightly above two to allow for child mortality and the sex ratio at birth; the figure is higher where mortality is greater. Populations with sustained TFR below replacement eventually decline in the absence of immigration, while population momentum can keep numbers growing for decades after fertility falls.

Trends

The global TFR fell from about 5 children per woman in the 1960s to roughly 2.3 in the early 2020s. The highest rates are found in sub-Saharan Africa (Niger has been estimated above 6), while many countries in East Asia and Europe record rates below 1.5, with South Korea falling below 0.8 — the lowest national figure recorded. Fertility decline is a central element of the demographic transition and drives concerns about population ageing and shrinking workforces.
