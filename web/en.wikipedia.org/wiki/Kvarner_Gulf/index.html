The Kvarner Gulf (Croatian: Kvarnerski zaljev; Italian: Golfo del Quarnero) is a bay in the northeastern Adriatic Sea, part of Croatia's maritime territory between the Istrian peninsula to the west and the Croatian mainland littoral to the east and south. It is sometimes divided into the main Kvarner Gulf and the smaller Kvarnerić ("Little Kvarner") between the islands.

Geography

The gulf is studded with some of the largest Adriatic islands: Krk, Cres, Lošinj, Rab, and the northern part of Pag. The mainland shore is dominated by the Velebit mountain range, which channels the fierce northeasterly bora wind down onto the gulf, occasionally disrupting the Krk Bridge — the 1,430-metre tied-arch road bridge (opened 1980) that links Krk to the mainland and was among the longest concrete arch spans in the world. The deep, sheltered waters of the Bakar and Rijeka bays host Croatia's largest seaport.

History and economy

The port city of Rijeka at the gulf's head grew as the main maritime outlet of the Hungarian half of the Habsburg Monarchy, and the riviera town of Opatija pioneered Adriatic tourism in the 19th century as a climatic health resort of the Viennese aristocracy. Today the gulf's economy rests on the Port of Rijeka, shipbuilding, the INA oil refinery at Urinj, an LNG terminal off Krk (operational since 2021), and tourism across the Kvarner islands, marketed as one of Croatia's principal holiday regions.

Nature

The Cres–Lošinj archipelago is known for a resident population of bottlenose dolphins protected in a marine reserve proposal, and the cliffs of Cres hold one of the last colonies of Eurasian griffon vultures in the region. The gulf's mix of karst islands, submarine springs (vrulje), and seagrass meadows supports notable biodiversity.
