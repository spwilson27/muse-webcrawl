The Great Hanshin earthquake (阪神・淡路大震災, Hanshin-Awaji Daishinsai) occurred on 17 January 1995 at 05:46 JST in the southern part of Hyōgo Prefecture, Japan, including the Hanshin metropolitan area between Kobe and Osaka. It measured magnitude 6.9 on the moment magnitude scale and 7.2 on the JMA magnitude scale; the maximum intensity on the JMA seismic intensity scale was 7, the highest category. The quake killed 6,434 people and caused approximately ¥10 trillion in damage.

A toppled elevated section of the Hanshin Expressway, one of the quake's most iconic images.

Seismology

The earthquake's hypocentre was about 17.6 km beneath the northern tip of Awaji Island, roughly 20 km from central Kobe. It resulted from movement on the Nojima Fault, part of a network of active faults running beneath the region. The rupture propagated northeastward toward Kobe and southwestward along Awaji Island, producing a surface fault scarp on Awaji. Because the fault system ran directly beneath a densely populated urban corridor, shaking was exceptionally destructive.

Damage

More than 200,000 buildings were damaged or destroyed, and some 300,000 people were left homeless. Fires broke out across Kobe, particularly in the Nagata ward, where wooden housing burned for days. The elevated Hanshin Expressway toppled sideways in several places, and the Port of Kobe—then among the world's busiest container ports—was severely damaged, with cranes and quay walls ruined. Much of the damage was attributed to buildings constructed before Japan's stricter 1981 seismic code.

Aftermath

The disaster exposed shortcomings in Japan's disaster-response system: the government's initial response was widely criticised as slow, and offers of foreign assistance were at first declined. At the same time, an enormous volunteer effort—about 1.4 million people participated in relief activities in 1995—led that year to be remembered as the "first year of volunteerism" in Japan. Reconstruction took a decade; the port eventually recovered but never fully regained its former ranking. The quake spurred nationwide reforms in building inspection, seismic retrofitting and emergency management. A memorial ceremony is held in Kobe every 17 January.
