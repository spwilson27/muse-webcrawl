Admiral Flota Sovetskogo Soyuza Kuznetsov (Russian: Адмирал Флота Советского Союза Кузнецов, "Admiral of the Fleet of the Soviet Union Kuznetsov") is an aircraft carrier (designated a "heavy aircraft-carrying cruiser" in Russian service) serving as the flagship of the Russian Navy. She is the only aircraft carrier in Russian service and the lead ship of her class; her sister ship became China's first carrier, Liaoning, after being sold incomplete.

Design

Built at the Black Sea Shipyard in Nikolayev, Ukrainian SSR, the ship uses a STOBAR configuration: aircraft take off under their own power via a 14.3° ski-jump bow and land using arrestor wires. Unusually for a carrier, she carries a heavy offensive armament of twelve P-700 Granit anti-ship cruise missiles in flush deck launchers, reflecting the Soviet concept of the ship as a cruiser that also operates aircraft. Her air wing centred on the Su-33 fighter, later supplemented by the MiG-29K.

Service history

Commissioned in the final days of the USSR, the ship — successively named Riga, Leonid Brezhnev, and Tbilisi during construction — joined the Northern Fleet. Chronic problems with her steam plant, piping, and arresting gear, and Russia's straitened post-Soviet budgets, kept her deployments rare and short; she is notorious for sailing with an ocean-going tug in attendance. Her only combat deployment came in late 2016, when her aircraft flew strikes in Syria from the eastern Mediterranean; two jets were lost in landing accidents.

From 2017 the carrier entered a major refit at Murmansk that suffered a series of disasters: in October 2018 the PD-50 floating drydock sank, dropping a crane onto the flight deck and killing workers; in December 2019 a major fire killed two and injured others; and another fire occurred in December 2022. Her return to service has been repeatedly postponed.
