Semi-automatic transmission

A semi-automatic transmission is a multiple-speed transmission where part of its operation is automated (typically the actuation of the clutch) but the driver's input is still required to launch the vehicle from a standstill and to shift gears manually. Semi-automatics differ from fully automatic transmissions, which can change gears without driver input.

Types

Historical implementations include pre-selector gearboxes of the 1930s, the Volkswagen Autostick, and the Citroën and Renault systems that automated clutch engagement while retaining a gear lever. Modern forms include automated manual transmissions (AMTs), which automate both clutch and gear selection of a conventional manual gearbox but allow manual override, and the electrohydraulic paddle-shift systems long used in Formula One and in road cars such as Ferrari's F1 transmission. Dual-clutch transmissions are usually classed as semi-automatic when they offer a manual mode.

Motorcycles

On motorcycles, semi-automatic systems such as Honda's Dual Clutch Transmission (DCT) and centrifugal-clutch designs allow gear changes without a hand-operated clutch lever while preserving rider control over gear selection.
