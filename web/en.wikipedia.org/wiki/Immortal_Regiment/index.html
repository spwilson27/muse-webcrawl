Immortal Regiment

The Immortal Regiment (Russian: Бессмертный полк, Bessmertny polk) is a civic initiative and mass commemorative march held in Russia and in other countries on Victory Day, 9 May. Participants march carrying photographs of relatives who fought in the Second World War (the Great Patriotic War), preserving their memory and honouring their service.

History

The initiative originated in Tomsk, Siberia, in 2012, when local journalists Sergei Lapenkov, Sergei Kolotovkin, and Igor Dmitriev organised a march in which some 6,000 people carried portraits of their veteran relatives. The idea spread rapidly: by 2013 it was held in over a hundred Russian cities, and in subsequent years it became a nationwide event drawing millions of participants, with President Vladimir Putin joining the Moscow march carrying a portrait of his father.

Portraits are often mounted on placards or printed on shirts, and marchers may also record their relatives' stories on the movement's website, creating a crowdsourced archive of wartime biographies.

International spread

The Immortal Regiment has been organised in dozens of countries, including former Soviet republics, Israel, the United States, Germany, and China, often coordinated through Russian diaspora communities. After the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, some marches abroad were cancelled or contested, and the format of official Victory Day commemorations in Russia changed, while the portrait march itself continues as a central element of the holiday.

Criticism

Critics have argued that a grassroots initiative of family remembrance has been increasingly appropriated by the Russian state as patriotic propaganda, and there have been reported incidents of participants being given portraits of unknown veterans to carry, or of portraits being discarded after official marches. Supporters maintain the movement's core meaning—personal family memory—remains genuine for most participants.
