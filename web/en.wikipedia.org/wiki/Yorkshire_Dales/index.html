The Yorkshire Dales are a collection of river valleys (dales) and limestone hills in the Pennines of northern England, spanning North Yorkshire, Cumbria, and a small part of Lancashire. Much of the area is protected as the Yorkshire Dales National Park, established in 1954 and extended in 2016. The name "dale" derives from the Old Norse dalr, reflecting the region's Scandinavian settlement history.

Geography

Each dale is named for its river: Wensleydale (whose river is the Ure), Swaledale, Wharfedale, Nidderdale, Airedale, and Ribblesdale among them. The underlying Carboniferous limestone produces characteristic karst scenery — limestone pavements such as those above Malham Cove, scars and gorges, and cave systems including White Scar Cave and Gaping Gill. Between the limestone plateaus rise the gritstone-topped Yorkshire Three Peaks: Whernside, Ingleborough, and Pen-y-ghent, a popular walking challenge.

Landscape and culture

The pastoral landscape is defined by drystone walls and field barns enclosing hay meadows and sheep pastures farmed for centuries; Swaledale and Wensleydale sheep breeds bear the valleys' names, and Wensleydale is equally famous for its crumbly white cheese. Waterfalls such as Aysgarth Falls and Hardraw Force, market towns including Hawes, Grassington, and Settle, and the Settle–Carlisle railway are among the Dales' best-known attractions. The area's villages and farming life were portrayed in the books of James Herriot and their television adaptations.
