Luigi Cadorna (1850–1928) was an Italian general who served as Chief of Staff of the Royal Italian Army during the first two and a half years of the First World War. He directed the repeated Battles of the Isonzo but was dismissed after the disastrous defeat at Caporetto in 1917.

Career

Son of General Raffaele Cadorna, who had commanded the troops that took Rome in 1870, Luigi Cadorna rose through staff appointments and succeeded Alberto Pollio as chief of staff in July 1914. When Italy entered the war in May 1915, Cadorna launched a series of frontal offensives against Austro-Hungarian positions along the Isonzo River — eleven battles in two years — gaining little ground at enormous cost.

Discipline and Caporetto

Cadorna was notorious for harsh discipline, including the revival of decimation and the execution of hundreds of Italian soldiers. In October 1917 a combined German and Austro-Hungarian offensive shattered the Italian Second Army at Caporetto, inflicting hundreds of thousands of casualties and prisoners. Cadorna was removed as chief of staff and replaced by Armando Diaz, though he was later made a Marshal of Italy under Mussolini in 1924.

Assessment

Historians generally judge Cadorna rigid, aloof, and unimaginative, blaming his attritional strategy and refusal to delegate; his postwar memoirs attempted to shift blame for Caporetto onto his subordinates and the troops themselves.
