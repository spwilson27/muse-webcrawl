Bounty hunter

[Image: A bail enforcement agent in the United States]

A bounty hunter is a private agent working for a bail bondsman (or bail agent) who captures fugitives or criminals for a commission or bounty. The occupation, officially known as bail enforcement agent, bail agent, recovery agent, bail recovery agent, or fugitive recovery agent, has traditionally operated outside the legal constraints that govern police officers and other agents of the state. This is because a bail bond agreement between a defendant and a bail bondsman is essentially a contract: the defendant agrees, as a condition of release, that the bail bondsman may hire a bounty hunter to locate, arrest, and surrender the defendant if they fail to appear in court.

In the United States, bounty hunters are generally permitted to engage in such activity because it arises from the common-law right of a bail bondsman to recapture a client. The practice is largely unique to the United States; most other countries prohibit bounty hunting or restrict it heavily, regarding paid fugitive recovery as the exclusive province of law enforcement.

History

The practice has roots in English common law, where sureties in criminal cases had the right to seize a principal who failed to appear. In the United States, the Supreme Court case Taylor v. Taintor (1872) affirmed broad powers for bail agents, stating that the bondsman's custody of the defendant is a continuation of the original imprisonment and that the bondsman may pursue the defendant into another state and arrest them without process.

Legal status

Regulation varies widely by state. Some states, such as Illinois, Kentucky, Oregon, and Wisconsin, have outlawed commercial bail bonding altogether, effectively eliminating bounty hunting within their borders. Others require licensing, training, background checks, and notification of local police before an arrest. Federal law generally does not regulate the profession.

Practice

Bounty hunters typically receive a percentage of the bail bond — often around ten percent — for returning a fugitive. They use investigative techniques including surveillance, interviews, background checks, and database searches. Because most fugitives return within a short distance of where they were arrested, recovery work is usually local or regional.

In popular culture

Bounty hunters are a recurring figure in Westerns and crime fiction, from historical figures such as Steve McQueen's character in the television series Wanted: Dead or Alive to modern reality programming such as Dog the Bounty Hunter.
