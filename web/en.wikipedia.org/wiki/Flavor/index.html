Flavor

Flavor (American English) or flavour (British English) is the overall impression of a food or other substance, determined primarily by the chemical senses of taste and smell. The "trigeminal senses", which detect chemical irritants in the mouth and throat, as well as temperature and texture, are also important to the overall Gestalt of flavor perception. The flavor of a food can be altered with natural flavoring or artificial flavoring, which affects these senses.

Taste and smell

Of the three chemical senses, smell is the main determinant of a food item's flavor. While the taste system detects five basic tastes — sweetness, sourness, saltiness, bitterness, and umami — the number of food smells is unbounded. A food's flavor, therefore, can be easily altered by changing its smell while keeping its taste similar; this is exemplified in artificially flavored jellies, soft drinks and candies, which, while made of bases with a similar taste, have dramatically different flavors due to the use of different scents or flavorings. Most of the taste of food is actually smell: people with a blocked sense of smell, as during a cold, find food bland.

Flavorings

A flavoring is a substance added to impart or modify flavor. Natural flavorings are obtained from plant or animal materials, while artificial (synthetic) flavorings are created by flavor chemists ("flavorists") from chemical compounds. Vanilla, citrus oils, and spices are common natural flavor sources; esters such as isoamyl acetate (banana) are typical artificial flavorings. Flavorings are regulated in most jurisdictions, and labelling distinguishes "natural" from "artificial" products.

Other factors and metaphorical uses

Texture, temperature, pungency (from capsaicin in chillies or piperine in pepper), astringency, and even the colour and presentation of food influence perceived flavor. The word is also used metaphorically — a "flavor of the month", or in particle physics, where "flavour" denotes the generation types of elementary particles (see Flavour (particle physics)).
