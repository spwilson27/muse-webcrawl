Wajid Ali Shah
Born: 30 July 1822, Lucknow
Died: 1 September 1887, Calcutta
Reign: Nawab of Awadh, 1847–1856

Wajid Ali Shah (1822–1887) was the eleventh and last Nawab (King) of Awadh (Oudh), reigning from 1847 until the British East India Company annexed his kingdom in 1856 under the doctrine of lapse, on grounds of alleged misgovernance.

Patron of the arts

Despite his reputation as a pleasure-loving ruler, Wajid Ali Shah was a major patron of music, dance and Urdu poetry. He composed under the pen name "Akhtarpiya", is credited with popularizing the thumri vocal form and Kathak dance at his Lucknow court, and staged elaborate rahas dance-dramas, including his own Indar Sabha tradition pageants.

Exile

Deposed in 1856, he travelled to petition Queen Victoria but settled at Garden Reach (Matiaburj) in Calcutta, where he recreated a miniature Lucknow with a famed menagerie and court, living there until his death in 1887. The annexation of Awadh was a major grievance behind the Indian Rebellion of 1857, in which his wife Begum Hazrat Mahal played a leading role.
