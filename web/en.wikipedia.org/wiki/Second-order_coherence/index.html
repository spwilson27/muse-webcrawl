Second-order coherence

In quantum optics, second-order coherence describes the correlation between intensity measurements at two points in space and time, quantified by the normalised second-order correlation function g(2). Whereas first-order coherence concerns interference of field amplitudes, second-order coherence concerns the statistics of light itself and distinguishes classical from non-classical sources.

Values and interpretation

For a perfectly coherent laser-like field, g(2)(0) = 1; for thermal (chaotic) light, g(2)(0) = 2, a phenomenon called photon bunching observed in the Hanbury Brown and Twiss effect. Values below 1 cannot be explained classically: g(2)(0) < 1 indicates photon antibunching, and for an ideal single-photon source g(2)(0) = 0. Measuring g(2) is thus a standard test in experiments with quantum dots, colour centres and other single-photon emitters.
