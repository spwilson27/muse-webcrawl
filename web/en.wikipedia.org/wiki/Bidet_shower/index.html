Bidet shower

A bidet shower — also known as a handheld bidet sprayer, health faucet, or (in some regions) a "bum gun" — is a hand-held, trigger-operated nozzle installed beside a toilet to spray water for perineal washing after toilet use. Similar in appearance to a kitchen sink sprayer, it offers an alternative or supplement to toilet paper and to the separate ceramic bidet fixture.

A bidet shower installs on the toilet's water supply.

Use and prevalence

Bidet showers are ubiquitous in Finland, where they appear in nearly every bathroom, and throughout much of Asia and the Middle East, where water washing is customary. They are also common in parts of South America. The device connects to the toilet's water supply with a flexible hose and a T-valve; squeezing the trigger releases the spray.

Advantages and considerations

Advocates cite improved hygiene, reduced toilet-paper consumption, and usefulness for washing cloth nappies, cleaning the toilet bowl, and for people with limited mobility. Building codes in some countries restrict installation because of backflow-prevention requirements, and water pressure must be controlled to avoid leaks from the hose, which remains under pressure.
