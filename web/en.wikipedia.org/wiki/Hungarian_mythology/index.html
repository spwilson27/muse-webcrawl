Hungarian mythology

Hungarian mythology (Hungarian: magyar mitológia) comprises the myths, legends, and religious beliefs of the ancient Hungarians (Magyars) before their Christianization at the end of the 10th century. Knowledge of it is fragmentary, reconstructed from chronicles, folk tales, and linguistic and archaeological evidence.

Cosmology and deities

The ancient Hungarian worldview is often described as a three-layered cosmos — Upper World (Felső világ), Middle World (Középső világ), and Underworld (Alsó világ) — connected by the World Tree (Életfa or Tetejetlen fa). The chief god is often identified as Isten, with figures such as the sky-mother Boldogasszony later merging with the Virgin Mary in folk religion.

Legendary figures

Key legends include the brothers Hunor and Magor, who pursued the miraculous White Stag (Csodaszarvas) and became ancestors of the Huns and Magyars, and the Turul, a great bird of prey said to have led the Hungarians to the Carpathian Basin and to have appeared in a dream to Emese, mother of Álmos.

The táltos

Religious practice is thought to have centred on the táltos, a shaman-like figure believed to communicate with the spirit world, heal the sick, and control the weather.
