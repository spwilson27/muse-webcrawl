Moons of Pluto

The dwarf planet Pluto has five known moons, in order of distance: Charon, Styx, Nix, Kerberos, and Hydra. All are named for figures associated with the Greek underworld.

Charon

Charon, discovered by James Christy in 1978, is by far the largest, with a diameter of about 1,212 km — just over half that of Pluto itself. The pair form a true binary system: both bodies are tidally locked, always showing the same face to each other, and they orbit a barycentre lying outside Pluto's surface. The New Horizons flyby of July 2015 revealed Charon's reddish north polar region, nicknamed Mordor Macula.

The small moons

Nix and Hydra were discovered with the Hubble Space Telescope in 2005, Kerberos in 2011, and Styx in 2012. They are small, irregular bodies only tens of kilometres across, orbiting in near-resonant periods (approximately a 1:3:4:5:6 chain with Charon). New Horizons found them rotating rapidly and chaotically — their poles wobble as they tumble — an effect of the shifting gravitational field of the Pluto–Charon binary. The small moons are thought to be debris from the giant impact that formed Charon.
