Horses of Saint Mark

The Horses of Saint Mark (Italian: Cavalli di San Marco), also known as the Bronze Horses, the Horses of the Hippodrome, or the Quadriga, are a set of four ancient bronze statues of horses that form part of a quadriga monument for a four-horse chariot. They are among the most famous surviving sculptures of classical antiquity and the only surviving example of an ancient four-horse chariot group.

Origins

The horses are cast in an alloy that is about 96% copper, with small amounts of tin and lead, and were originally gilded. Their date and origin are debated — proposals range from 4th-century BC Greece, sometimes attributing them to the school of Lysippos, to imperial Rome. They stood for centuries above the Hippodrome of Constantinople.

History

In 1204, during the Fourth Crusade, Venetian forces under Doge Enrico Dandolo looted the horses and installed them on the terrace of St Mark's Basilica in Venice, where they became a symbol of the republic. Napoleon had them removed to Paris in 1797 and mounted on the Arc de Triomphe du Carrousel; they were returned to Venice in 1815 after his fall. During the First World War they were hidden for safety. In the 1980s, because of air pollution, the originals were moved inside the basilica's museum and replaced on the loggia by replicas.
