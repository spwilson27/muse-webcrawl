Cù Sìth

Cù Sìth
Grouping: Mythological creature
Region: Scottish Highlands, Ireland
Habitat: Fairy mounds, moorland

The cù sìth (Scottish Gaelic, literally "fairy dog") is a mythical hound found in the folklore of Scotland and, in related form, Ireland. It is said to be an enormous, otherworldly dog associated with the fairies.

Description

Folklore describes the cù sìth as the size of a young bull, with shaggy dark green fur — green being the colour traditionally linked to the fairies in Celtic tradition — and paws as broad as a man's hand. It was believed to dwell in the clefts of rocks and fairy mounds (sìthean).

In folklore

The hound was feared as a harbinger of death. It was said to hunt silently but occasionally utter three terrifying barks that could be heard far out to sea; those who heard the third bark before reaching safety would be overcome with terror. Like its Welsh counterpart the Cŵn Annwn and the English black dog traditions, it belongs to a wider family of spectral hound legends in the British Isles.
