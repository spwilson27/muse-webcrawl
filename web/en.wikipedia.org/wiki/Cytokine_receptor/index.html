Cytokine receptor

Cytokine receptors are cell surface receptors that bind cytokines — the signalling proteins of the immune and haematopoietic systems, including interleukins, interferons, colony-stimulating factors, erythropoietin and tumour necrosis factors. They transduce signals controlling cell proliferation, differentiation, survival and immune responses.

Families

Cytokine receptors are grouped by structure into several families: the type I cytokine receptors (haematopoietin family), which share a WSXWS motif and signal through associated Janus kinases (JAKs) and STAT transcription factors; the type II receptors for interferons and IL-10 family cytokines; the TNF receptor family, characterised by cysteine-rich domains and, in some members, intracellular death domains; the IL-1 receptor family with immunoglobulin-like domains; the IL-17 receptor family; and the chemokine receptors, which are G protein-coupled receptors. Many type I receptors are built from shared subunits — for example the common gamma chain (γc) used by the receptors for IL-2, IL-4, IL-7, IL-9, IL-15 and IL-21; its mutation causes X-linked severe combined immunodeficiency.

Signalling

Ligand binding brings receptor subunits together, activating associated JAK tyrosine kinases, which phosphorylate the receptor and STAT proteins; phosphorylated STATs dimerise, enter the nucleus and switch on target genes. Signalling is restrained by SOCS proteins and phosphatases. Drugs that block cytokine receptors or their signalling — such as the IL-6 receptor antibody tocilizumab, the IL-2 receptor antibody basiliximab, and small-molecule JAK inhibitors — are widely used in autoimmune disease, transplantation and oncology.
