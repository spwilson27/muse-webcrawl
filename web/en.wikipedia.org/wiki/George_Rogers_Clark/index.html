George Rogers Clark

George Rogers Clark (November 19, 1752 – February 13, 1818) was an American military officer and surveyor of the Revolutionary War frontier.

Born: Charlottesville, Virginia
Died: Louisville, Kentucky
Known for: Illinois campaign of 1778–79; capture of Fort Sackville

George Rogers Clark was an American soldier from Virginia who became the highest-ranking Patriot military officer on the northwestern frontier during the American Revolutionary War. He is famed for capturing the Illinois country and Fort Sackville at Vincennes from the British, earning the title "Conqueror of the Old Northwest". He was the elder brother of William Clark of the Lewis and Clark Expedition.

Early life

Clark was born near Charlottesville, Virginia, and had little formal education but trained as a surveyor. He explored the Ohio River valley and fought in Lord Dunmore's War (1774), then settled in Kentucky, where he organised the Kentucky militia's defence against British-allied Native American raids.

Illinois campaign

In 1778 Clark persuaded Virginia's governor Patrick Henry to authorise a secret expedition against British posts north of the Ohio. With about 175 men he floated down the Ohio, seized Kaskaskia on the Mississippi without a shot on 4 July 1778, and won over Cahokia and Vincennes. When the British under Henry Hamilton retook Vincennes in December, Clark made a legendary mid-winter march through flooded country in February 1779 and forced Hamilton's surrender at Fort Sackville — the "Hair-Buyer General" giving up on 25 February 1779.

Later years

Clark's campaigns helped Virginia claim the territory ceded by Britain in the Treaty of Paris (1783), becoming the Old Northwest. He never received the reimbursement he expected for financing his expeditions and fell into debt; campaigns in 1780, 1782, and the failed Wabash expedition of 1786 were his last. Embittered and drinking heavily, he spent his final years at Locust Grove near Louisville, Kentucky, dying in 1818. The George Rogers Clark Memorial at Vincennes and the George Rogers Clark National Historical Park commemorate him.
