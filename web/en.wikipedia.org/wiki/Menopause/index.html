Menopause

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Menopause is the point in a woman's life when menstrual periods stop permanently because the ovaries cease releasing eggs and greatly reduce oestrogen production. It is confirmed after twelve months without a period and occurs naturally at an average age of about 51 in Western countries (normal range roughly 45–55). The transition leading up to it, perimenopause, lasts several years.

Symptoms and health effects

Falling oestrogen causes hot flushes and night sweats in most women, plus sleep disturbance, vaginal dryness, mood changes and joint aches in many. Long term, oestrogen loss accelerates bone thinning (raising osteoporosis and fracture risk) and changes cardiovascular risk. Early menopause (before 45) or surgical removal of the ovaries brings these changes sooner and stronger.

Management

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) is the most effective treatment for flushes and protects bone; modern assessment holds that for most women under 60 the benefits outweigh the small risks, which include breast cancer and clots depending on regimen. Non-hormonal drugs, vaginal oestrogen, exercise, and bone-protective treatments are alternatives or additions. Menopause care and workplace awareness have become prominent public-health topics.
