Hexi Corridor

Hexi Corridor
Chinese: 河西走廊
Location: Gansu, China
Length: ~900 km

The Hexi Corridor (Chinese: 河西走廊; pinyin: Héxī Zǒuláng, "Corridor West of the Yellow River") is a historical region and transport route in modern Gansu province, China, referring to a depression running for about 900 km to the northwest of Lanzhou, between the Qilian Mountains to the south and the Beishan and Longshou mountains to the north.

Geography

The corridor is a chain of oases fed by rivers descending from the snow-capped Qilian range, threaded between mountains and the Gobi and Tengger deserts. Its principal oasis cities, from east to west, are Wuwei, Zhangye, Jiuquan, and Dunhuang. Elevations lie mostly between 1,000 and 2,000 m above sea level.

History

The corridor became part of Chinese territory under Emperor Wu of Han, who in the 120s BCE established the commanderies of Wuwei, Zhangye, Jiuquan and Dunhuang after defeating the Xiongnu under Huo Qubing. Because it was the only practicable passage between the Yellow River valley and the Western Regions, the corridor formed the main east–west trunk of the Silk Road, carrying silk, jade, glassware, Buddhist scripture and art between Chang'an and Central Asia.

Modern transport

Today the corridor carries the Lanxin railway, expressways and the Lanzhou–Xinjiang high-speed line, and remains the overland gateway from China proper to Xinjiang and Central Asia.
