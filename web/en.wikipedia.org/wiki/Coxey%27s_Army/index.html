Coxey's Army

DateMarch–May 1894
LeaderJacob S. Coxey
OriginMassillon, Ohio
DestinationWashington, D.C.

Coxey's Army was a protest march by unemployed workers from the United States, led by Ohio businessman Jacob Coxey. They marched on Washington, D.C. in 1894, the second year of a four-year economic depression that was the worst in United States history to that time.

The march

Coxey and about 100 men began the march from Massillon, Ohio, on Easter Sunday, 25 March 1894, intending to present a "petition in boots" to Congress demanding a federal public-works programme funded by the issue of paper money. Sympathetic "industrial armies" set out from other parts of the country to join them.

Arrival and aftermath

When the marchers — about 500 strong — reached the Capitol on 1 May 1894, Coxey attempted to read a speech and was arrested for walking on the grass. The protest dispersed without achieving its aims, but it is remembered as the first significant popular protest march on Washington and an early expression of populist demands for government action on unemployment.
