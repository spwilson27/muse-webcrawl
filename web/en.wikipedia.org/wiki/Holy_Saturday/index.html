Holy Saturday

Holy Saturday (Latin: Sabbatum Sanctum), also known as the Great Sabbath, Black Saturday, or Easter Eve, is the final day of Holy Week, the day after Good Friday and before Easter Sunday. It commemorates the day that Jesus' body lay in the tomb following his crucifixion, and, according to Christian tradition, his descent into hell (the Harrowing of Hell) to free the righteous dead. It is the last day of Lent in Western Christianity and of the Paschal Triduum.

Observances

Holy Saturday is traditionally a day of quiet, fasting, and waiting. In the Roman Catholic Church no Mass is celebrated during the day, altars remain stripped from Holy Thursday and Good Friday, and the sacraments are restricted. After nightfall begins the Easter Vigil, described by the Church as the "mother of all vigils": a service of light in which the Paschal candle is lit from a new fire, the Exsultet is sung, a series of Old Testament readings recounts salvation history, catechumens are baptized and confirmed, and the first Mass of Easter is celebrated.

In Eastern Orthodoxy, Holy and Great Saturday is marked by the Vesperal Divine Liturgy of St. Basil, at which the altar cloths and vestments change from dark to bright white, and at midnight the Paschal services begin. In Jerusalem, the ceremony of the Holy Fire at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre draws vast crowds of pilgrims. In some countries, such as Poland, families bring baskets of Easter foods (święconka) to church to be blessed on Holy Saturday.
