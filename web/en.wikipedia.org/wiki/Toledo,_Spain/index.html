Toledo (Spanish: [toˈleðo]) is a city and municipality of Spain, the capital of the province of Toledo and the de jure seat of the government and parliament of the autonomous community of Castilla–La Mancha. It sits on a hill above the Tagus River, about 70 km south of Madrid. Toledo was declared a World Heritage Site by UNESCO in 1986 for its extensive monumental and cultural heritage.

History

Known in Roman times as Toletum, the city became the capital of the Visigothic Kingdom after the fall of Rome and hosted the famous Councils of Toledo. Under Muslim rule (as Ṭulayṭulah) it was a major centre of learning; after its capture by Alfonso VI of Castile in 1085, the Toledo School of Translators rendered Arabic and classical works into Latin and Castilian, transmitting Greek and Islamic learning to Europe.

Toledo is known as the "City of the Three Cultures" for the historical coexistence of Christian, Muslim and Jewish communities, reflected in monuments such as Toledo Cathedral, the Alcázar, the Synagogue of Santa María la Blanca and the Mosque of Cristo de la Luz. The painter El Greco made the city his home, and his View of Toledo is among the most famous landscapes in Western art. Toledo steel and its damascene craftwork have been renowned for centuries.
