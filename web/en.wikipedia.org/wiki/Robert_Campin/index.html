Robert Campin

Robert Campin
Born: c. 1375, Valenciennes
Died: 26 April 1444, Tournai
Known for: Early Netherlandish painting; the Mérode Altarpiece

Robert Campin (c. 1375 – 26 April 1444), now usually identified with the Master of Flémalle, was the first great master of Flemish and Early Netherlandish painting. He operated a large workshop in Tournai, where Rogier van der Weyden was among his apprentices.

Work

Campin's paintings ground sacred scenes in meticulously observed contemporary domestic interiors filled with symbolic objects. Works attributed to him and his workshop include the Mérode Altarpiece (c. 1425–1428, now at The Cloisters, New York), the Werl Triptych (1438, Prado), and portraits such as A Woman (c. 1435, National Gallery, London) among the earliest realistic Northern portraits.

Identification

No signed works survive; the identification of Campin with the Master of Flémalle, proposed in the early 20th century from documentary evidence about his workshop, is widely but not universally accepted. His naturalism, oil technique and disguised symbolism influenced Jan van Eyck and the whole Northern Renaissance.
