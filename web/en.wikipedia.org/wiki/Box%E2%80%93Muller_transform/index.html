Box–Muller transform

The Box–Muller transform, by George Edward Pelham Box and Mervin Edgar Muller, is a random number sampling method for generating pairs of independent, standard, normally distributed random numbers. It transforms two independent uniform random variables into two independent standard normal random variables, and was published in 1958.

Basic form

Given two independent samples from the uniform distribution on (0, 1], one computes a radius from the exponential distribution (via the square root of −2 ln u1) and an angle uniform on [0, 2π) (from 2π u2); the Cartesian coordinates of that polar pair are two independent standard normal deviates. The construction rests on the fact that the joint density of two independent standard normals is radially symmetric, with a chi distribution for the radius.

Variants and use

The polar (Marsaglia polar) method avoids trigonometric functions by rejection sampling points in a unit disk. The ziggurat algorithm is generally faster in modern implementations, but the Box–Muller transform remains a textbook-standard, exact method and is widely used in simulation, statistics software, and Monte Carlo work.
