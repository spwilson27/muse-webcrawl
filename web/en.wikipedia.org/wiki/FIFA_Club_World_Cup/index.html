FIFA Club World Cup

The FIFA Club World Cup is an international men's association football competition organised by FIFA, the sport's global governing body. The competition was first contested in 2000 as the FIFA Club World Championship, then held annually from 2005 to 2023 between the winners of each of the six continental confederation championships, along with the host nation's league champion. From 2025, it was expanded to a 32-team tournament held every four years, modelled on the World Cup format, while the former annual competition was rebranded as the FIFA Intercontinental Cup.

History

The first tournament was held in Brazil in 2000 and won by Corinthians. After a hiatus caused by the collapse of FIFA's marketing partner ISL, the competition relaunched in Japan in 2005, absorbing the Intercontinental Cup (the European–South American club play-off contested since 1960). The annual format crowned champions including São Paulo, Internacional, AC Milan, Manchester United, Barcelona, Inter Milan, Bayern Munich, Real Madrid, Liverpool, Chelsea and Manchester City. Real Madrid is the most successful club in the old format with five titles.

The 2025 expansion

The expanded 32-team tournament, hosted by the United States in June–July 2025, featured 12 European clubs, six South American clubs, and representatives of the AFC, CAF, CONCACAF and OFC plus the host. Chelsea won the 2025 edition, defeating Paris Saint-Germain 3–0 in the final at MetLife Stadium on 13 July 2025. The expansion drew criticism over player workload and fixture congestion, alongside praise for a large prize pool of about US$1 billion.

Format of the expanded tournament

Teams are drawn into eight groups of four, with the top two advancing to a single-elimination knockout stage culminating in a final; there is no third-place match. Qualification is based on winning the continental Champions League or on a four-year confederation ranking of clubs.
