Harrow football

Harrow football is a code of football played exclusively at Harrow School in London, England. It is one of the surviving public school football games from which both association football (soccer) and rugby football partly descend.

Gameplay

The game is played between two teams of eleven on a muddy pitch. The ball, called a "yacker", is larger and more spherical than an association football. Players may kick the ball and use their shoulders, but may not pass it forward by hand; goals, called "bases", are scored by kicking the ball between two poles. Handling is restricted, tackles and physical contests are central, and the game is famously rugged in wet conditions.

History

Harrow's rules were among those considered during the formation of the Football Association in 1863, though Harrow did not join, preferring its own game. An annual match between former pupils and the school remains a fixture.
