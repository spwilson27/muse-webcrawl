Tree of life

The tree of life is a widespread motif in mythology, religion and philosophy. For the concept in biology, see Tree of life (biology). For the Kabbalistic diagram, see Tree of life (Kabbalah).

The tree of life is a fundamental archetype in many of the world's mythologies, religious traditions and philosophical systems. It generally represents the interconnectedness of all life, the link between heaven, earth and the underworld, and themes of creation, fertility, immortality and rebirth. Related motifs include the world tree (such as Yggdrasil in Norse mythology) and the sacred tree guarded or planted at the centre of the world.

In the Abrahamic traditions

In the Book of Genesis, the tree of life stands in the Garden of Eden alongside the tree of the knowledge of good and evil; eating of its fruit confers immortality, and after the Fall, cherubim guard the way to it so that fallen humanity cannot eat and live forever. The tree reappears in the Book of Revelation as bearing twelve kinds of fruit, with leaves "for the healing of the nations". In Jewish mysticism, the tree of life is the diagram of the ten sefirot through which the divine manifests in creation.

Other traditions

Ancient Egyptian religion knew the sacred sycamore of the goddess Nut; Mesopotamian art shows stylised sacred trees tended by kings and genies; the Maya world tree (Wakah-Chan) stood at the centre of the cosmos; Chinese mythology has the peach tree of immortality of the Queen Mother of the West; and in Mesoamerican and shamanic traditions more broadly, a central tree or axis connects the planes of existence. The Bodhi tree beneath which the Buddha attained enlightenment is closely related in function.

In biology

Charles Darwin used the image of a branching tree — the only illustration in On the Origin of Species — to depict the descent of all species from common ancestors, and the "tree of life" remains the standard metaphor for evolutionary relationships, now drawn from molecular phylogenetics and complicated by horizontal gene transfer among microbes.
