Forbidden fruit

Forbidden fruit is a name given to the fruit growing in the Garden of Eden which God commands mankind not to eat. In the biblical narrative, Adam and Eve eat the fruit from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil and are exiled from Eden. As a metaphor outside the Abrahamic religions, the phrase typically refers to any indulgence or pleasure that is considered illegal or immoral.

Biblical narrative

In the Book of Genesis, God places Adam in the Garden of Eden and permits him to eat of all the trees except the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. Eve is later created, and the serpent persuades her that eating the fruit will make them like God, knowing good and evil. She eats and gives some to Adam, who also eats. Their eyes are opened, they realise they are naked, and God expels them from the garden — an event known in Christian theology as the Fall of Man and connected with the doctrine of original sin.

Identity of the fruit

Genesis does not name the species of fruit. In Western art and tradition it is most often depicted as an apple, partly because of the Latin pun between mālum (apple) and malum (evil). Other candidates proposed in Jewish and other traditions include the fig (Adam and Eve cover themselves with fig leaves), grapes, pomegranate, wheat, and citron.

In Islam

In the Quran, Adam and his wife dwell in Paradise and are forbidden from approaching a particular tree; Satan whispers to them and they eat, after which they are sent down to Earth. Unlike in Christian theology, their repentance is accepted by God and the sin is not inherited.

Metaphorical use

The phrase "forbidden fruit" has passed into common usage for anything that is desired precisely because it is prohibited.
