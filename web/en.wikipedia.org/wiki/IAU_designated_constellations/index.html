IAU designated constellations

In contemporary astronomy, the sky is divided into 88 official constellations recognised by the International Astronomical Union (IAU). Each constellation is a precisely defined region of the celestial sphere, so that every point on the sky belongs to exactly one constellation. The list was formally adopted by the IAU in 1922, and the official boundaries were drawn by the Belgian astronomer Eugène Delporte and published in 1930.

History

Forty-eight of the constellations descend from the catalogue of Ptolemy's Almagest (2nd century CE). The southern sky, invisible from the Mediterranean, was filled in by Dutch navigators Pieter Dirkszoon Keyser and Frederick de Houtman in the 1590s, by Johann Bayer's Uranometria (1603), by Johannes Hevelius, and by Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille, who charted the southern heavens from the Cape of Good Hope in the 1750s.
