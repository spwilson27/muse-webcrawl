Nuclear power in South Africa

Nuclear power in South Africa is provided by the Koeberg Nuclear Power Station, the only commercial nuclear power plant on the African continent. South Africa also operates the SAFARI-1 research reactor at Pelindaba. Nuclear energy supplies around 4–5% of the country's electricity, the bulk of the remainder coming from coal.

Koeberg

Koeberg, located about 30 kilometres north of Cape Town on the Atlantic coast, has two French-designed pressurized water reactors of roughly 930 MWe each, commissioned in 1984 and 1985. The plant is operated by the state utility Eskom. A steam generator replacement and refurbishment programme in the 2020s aimed to extend Koeberg's operating life by 20 years, to around 2044–2045, though the outages involved contributed to national electricity shortages.

History and expansion plans

South Africa developed nuclear weapons under apartheid and dismantled them before joining the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty in 1991 — the only country to have developed and then voluntarily given up nuclear weapons. It also pioneered the pebble bed modular reactor (PBMR) project, which was abandoned in 2010 after large expenditure without a customer. Government proposals in the 2010s for a large new nuclear build (the so-called "nuclear deal", notably with Russia) were controversial and were set aside in 2017 after a court ruled the procurement process unlawful. More recent plans have contemplated smaller additions of around 2,500 MWe.

Uranium

South Africa has significant uranium resources, produced largely as a by-product of gold mining in the Witwatersrand basin.
