Chervil (Anthriscus cerefolium), sometimes called French parsley or garden chervil, is a delicate annual herb related to parsley. It is used to season mild-flavoured dishes and is a constituent of the French herb mixture fines herbes.

Description

Chervil grows to 40–70 cm (16–28 in) tall, with tripinnate leaves that may be curly. The small white flowers form small umbels 2.5–5 cm (1–2 in) across. The fruit is an oblong-ovoid beaked schizocarp around 1 cm long.

Cultivation and uses

Native to the Caucasus, chervil was spread by the Romans through most of Europe, where it is now naturalised. Its flavour is delicate, with hints of parsley, tarragon, and anise. Because its flavour is lost quickly with heat and drying, fresh chervil is added at the end of cooking. It is used in French cooking in omelettes, salads, soups, and sauces, and is one of the four classic fines herbes, together with tarragon, chives, and parsley.
