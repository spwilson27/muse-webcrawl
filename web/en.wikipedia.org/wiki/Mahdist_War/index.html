Mahdist War

The Mahdist War (1881–1899), also known as the Mahdist Revolt or the Sudan Campaign, was a war between the Mahdist Sudanese state and the forces of the Khedivate of Egypt and, later, the United Kingdom. It began when the religious leader Muhammad Ahmad proclaimed himself the Mahdi and raised a revolt against Turco-Egyptian misrule in Sudan. The war saw the destruction of Egyptian armies, the fall of Khartoum and death of General Gordon in 1885, and the eventual Anglo-Egyptian reconquest culminating at the Battle of Omdurman in 1898.

Course of the war

Muhammad Ahmad's Ansar ("helpers") annihilated Egyptian expeditions, including the 1883 army of Hicks Pasha at Shaykan, and captured El Obeid and most of Sudan. Khartoum fell in January 1885 after a ten-month siege; Major-General Charles George Gordon, sent to oversee evacuation, was killed, provoking a political storm in Britain. The Mahdi died months later and was succeeded by the Khalifa Abdallahi, who ruled the Mahdist state from Omdurman and repelled Ethiopian and other invasions, though famine devastated the country.

Reconquest

In 1896 an Anglo-Egyptian army under Herbert Kitchener advanced methodically up the Nile, building a railway across the Nubian Desert. After victory at Atbara in April 1898, Kitchener destroyed the Khalifa's army at Omdurman on 2 September 1898, where Maxim guns and artillery inflicted some 11,000 Mahdist deaths for fewer than 50 of his own men. The Khalifa was hunted down and killed at Umm Diwaykarat in November 1899. Sudan was then administered as an Anglo-Egyptian condominium until independence in 1956.
