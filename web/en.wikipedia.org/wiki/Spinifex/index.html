Spinifex (grass)

[Image: Spinifex hummocks covering red sandplain country]

Overview

Spinifex is the common name for Australian grasses of the genus Triodia, which form spiky, resinous hummocks across the arid interior — spinifex country covers roughly a quarter of the continent. (The name is also used for unrelated coastal grasses of the genus Spinifex.) Triodia species are deeply adapted to fire: hummocks burn fiercely and resprout, and Aboriginal burning mosaics ("fire-stick farming") shaped spinifex landscapes for millennia.

Uses and ecology

Aboriginal peoples used spinifex resin (from species such as T. pungens) as an adhesive for hafting tools, and the seeds as food. Spinifex hummocks shelter a rich desert fauna, including the spinifex hopping mouse and many lizard species. Its sharp, silica-laden leaves make cross-country travel on foot an education in patience.
