Samhain

Samhain (Irish: Samhain, pronounced "sow-in") is a Gaelic festival marking the end of the harvest season and beginning of winter, traditionally celebrated from sunset on 31 October to sunset on 1 November — halfway between the autumn equinox and winter solstice. It is one of the four Gaelic seasonal festivals, along with Imbolc, Beltane and Lughnasa.

TypeGaelic seasonal festival

Traditions

In Irish mythology Samhain was a liminal time when the boundary with the Otherworld thinned and the aos sí (spirits/fairies) could cross; offerings were left, bonfires lit, and divination practised. Livestock were brought down from summer pastures and slaughtered for winter. Mumming and guising in costume are early antecedents of trick-or-treating.

Modern observance

Samhain influenced Halloween; today it is also celebrated by Wiccans and other neopagans as a major sabbat honouring the dead. See also Saint Patrick for later Irish tradition.
