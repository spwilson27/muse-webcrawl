AC/DC

AC/DC
Origin: Sydney, New South Wales, Australia
Formed: 1973
Genres: Hard rock, blues rock, rock and roll
Known for: Back in Black, "Highway to Hell", "Thunderstruck"

AC/DC are an Australian rock band formed in Sydney in 1973 by Scottish-born brothers Malcolm and Angus Young. Their music has been variously described as hard rock, blues rock and heavy metal, but the band themselves call it simply "rock and roll".

History

The band released their debut album High Voltage in 1975 in Australia. With vocalist Bon Scott, they achieved international breakthrough with Highway to Hell (1979). Scott died in 1980 and was replaced by Brian Johnson, with whom the band recorded Back in Black (1980), one of the best-selling albums of all time, with estimated sales of more than 50 million copies worldwide.

Style and legacy

AC/DC's sound is built on simple, powerful guitar riffs, a driving rhythm section, and high-voltage stage energy, with Angus Young performing in a schoolboy uniform. The band has sold more than 200 million records worldwide and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2003.

Selected discography

Studio albums include T.N.T. (1975), Dirty Deeds Done Dirt Cheap (1976), Let There Be Rock (1977), Highway to Hell (1979), Back in Black (1980), For Those About to Rock We Salute You (1981), The Razors Edge (1990) and Power Up (2020).
