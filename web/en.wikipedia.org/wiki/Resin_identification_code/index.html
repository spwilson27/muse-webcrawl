Resin identification code

The resin identification code (RIC) is a set of symbols appearing on plastic products that identify the plastic resin from which the item is made. Introduced by the Society of the Plastics Industry in 1988, the codes appear inside a "chasing arrows" triangle.

Codes

  • 1 – PET (polyethylene terephthalate): bottles; commonly recycled.
  • 2 – HDPE (high-density polyethylene): jugs, pipes; commonly recycled.
  • 3 – PVC (polyvinyl chloride): pipes, blister packs; rarely recycled curbside.
  • 4 – LDPE (low-density polyethylene): films and bags.
  • 5 – PP (polypropylene): tubs, caps; increasingly recycled.
  • 6 – PS (polystyrene): foam and rigid packaging.
  • 7 – Other: all other resins, including polycarbonate and multilayers.

Misuse and reform

The chasing-arrows design is frequently mistaken for a guarantee that an item is recyclable, leading many jurisdictions to restrict its use. ASTM now administers the standard (D7611); California and other states have moved to reserve the symbol for genuinely recyclable plastics.
