United States Department of Energy national laboratories


The national laboratories form a network of large multiprogram research facilities across the United States.

The United States Department of Energy national laboratories are a system of seventeen federally funded research and development centers administered by the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE). They conduct long-term, multiprogram scientific research in areas including energy, nuclear security, environmental science, and basic physical science, and host major user facilities such as synchrotrons, neutron sources, and supercomputers. Most are operated for the government by private contractors under "government-owned, contractor-operated" (GOCO) arrangements.

History

The system grew out of the Manhattan Project of World War II, which created large laboratories at Los Alamos, Oak Ridge, and Hanford, and university-based sites that became Argonne and Lawrence Berkeley. After the war the Atomic Energy Commission, and later the DOE (created in 1977), expanded the network and broadened its missions from weapons and nuclear energy into fundamental science, energy technologies, and environmental cleanup.

The laboratories

  • Ames National Laboratory — Iowa
  • Argonne National Laboratory — Illinois
  • Brookhaven National Laboratory — New York
  • Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab) — Illinois
  • Idaho National Laboratory — Idaho
  • Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory — California
  • Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory — California
  • Los Alamos National Laboratory — New Mexico
  • National Energy Technology Laboratory — Pennsylvania / West Virginia / Oregon
  • National Renewable Energy Laboratory — Colorado
  • Oak Ridge National Laboratory — Tennessee
  • Pacific Northwest National Laboratory — Washington
  • Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory — New Jersey
  • Sandia National Laboratories — New Mexico / California
  • Savannah River National Laboratory — South Carolina
  • SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory — California
  • Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility — Virginia

Workforce and funding

Together the laboratories employ roughly 60,000–70,000 staff and spend on the order of $25 billion annually. Three laboratories — Los Alamos, Lawrence Livermore, and Sandia — are principally devoted to nuclear weapons work under the National Nuclear Security Administration; the Office of Science is the largest supporter of basic research in the physical sciences in the United States and manages ten of the labs.
