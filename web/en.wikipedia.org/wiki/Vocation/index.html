Vocation

A vocation (from Latin vocatio, "a call" or "summons") is an occupation or way of life to which a person feels specially drawn or called, often with a spiritual dimension. Originally a religious term for a divine calling, it has broadened to mean one's profession or life's work.

Religious sense

In Roman Catholicism, vocation refers to a calling from God — to priesthood, religious life, marriage or single life — discerned through prayer. In the Protestant Reformation, Martin Luther broadened the concept: every lawful occupation, including ordinary secular work done in service of one's neighbour, could be a calling (Beruf), an idea sociologist Max Weber later explored in The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism.

Modern usage

Today "vocation" commonly denotes work experienced as a meaningful calling rather than merely a job, and gives its name to vocational education and training oriented toward particular trades and professions.
