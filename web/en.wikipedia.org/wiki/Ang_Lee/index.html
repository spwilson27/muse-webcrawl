Ang Lee (Chinese: 李安; pinyin: Lǐ Ān; born 23 October 1954) is a Taiwanese filmmaker. His films are known for their emotional charge and exploration of repressed, hidden emotions. Lee has been honoured with numerous accolades, including three Academy Awards, four BAFTA Awards, and three Golden Globe Awards, and he was the first non-white filmmaker to win the Academy Award for Best Director.

Career

Lee studied theatre at the University of Illinois and film at New York University. His early Chinese-language "Father Knows Best" trilogy — Pushing Hands (1991), The Wedding Banquet (1993) and Eat Drink Man Woman (1994) — won international acclaim.

Moving to English-language cinema, he directed Sense and Sensibility (1995), The Ice Storm (1997) and Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (2000), which became the highest-grossing foreign-language film in American history and won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. After Hulk (2003), he directed Brokeback Mountain (2005), winning his first Best Director Oscar, and Lust, Caution (2007). Life of Pi (2012), adapted from Yann Martel's novel, won him a second Best Director Oscar. Later films include Billy Lynn's Long Halftime Walk (2016) and Gemini Man (2019), both noted for experimentation with high frame rates.
