Breaking Bad is an American crime drama television series created by Vince Gilligan for AMC. Set and filmed in Albuquerque, New Mexico, it follows Walter White (Bryan Cranston), a mild-mannered high-school chemistry teacher diagnosed with terminal lung cancer who turns to cooking crystal methamphetamine to secure his family's future, descending into the criminal underworld as the drug kingpin "Heisenberg".

Plot

Partnering with former student Jesse Pinkman (Aaron Paul), White produces exceptionally pure blue methamphetamine while evading his brother-in-law, DEA agent Hank Schrader. Across five seasons, White's moral transformation from protagonist to antagonist drives the series.

Reception

The series is widely considered one of the greatest television shows of all time. It won 16 Primetime Emmy Awards, and its final season achieved near-universal acclaim. It spawned the prequel series Better Call Saul and the film El Camino.
