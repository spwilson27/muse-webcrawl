The Fitzwilliam Museum is the art and antiquities museum of the University of Cambridge. It is located on Trumpington Street opposite Fitzwilliam Street in central Cambridge. It was founded in 1816 following the bequest of the library and art collection of Richard Fitzwilliam, 7th Viscount Fitzwilliam, who left his collections and £100,000 to the university.

Collections

The museum holds a collection of over half a million objects, including antiquities from Egypt, Greece and Rome; applied arts; coins and medals; manuscripts and printed books; and paintings, drawings and prints. Its picture collection includes works by Titian, Rubens, Rembrandt, Monet and Picasso. The museum's founder's building, a grand Neoclassical structure designed by George Basevi and completed by C. R. Cockerell, opened to the public in 1848.

Admission and role

Admission is free. The Fitzwilliam is the lead partner of the University of Cambridge Museums consortium, one of the Arts Council England-funded Major Partner Museum programmes. In 2012 the museum was the scene of a notable theft of Chinese jade artefacts.
