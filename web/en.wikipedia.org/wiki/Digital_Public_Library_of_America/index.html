Digital Public Library of America

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Digital Public Library of America (DPLA) is an American non-profit organisation and discovery portal that provides free public access to digitised cultural heritage materials from libraries, archives, museums, and other institutions across the United States. Rather than hosting most content itself, the DPLA aggregates metadata — tens of millions of records — and links users to the items at partner institutions. It launched on 18 April 2013 at the Boston Public Library.

History

The idea of a national digital library for the United States was championed by the historian Robert Darnton and others, and took concrete form at a meeting at Harvard's Berkman Center for Internet & Society in October 2010. A planning process funded by the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation and others involved hundreds of librarians and members of the public. Dan Cohen became the founding executive director in 2013. The launch event was planned for the Boston Public Library but was overshadowed by the Boston Marathon bombing three days earlier; the site nonetheless went live on schedule.

Network model

The DPLA works through a network of service hubs (statewide or regional aggregators) and content hubs (large institutions such as the Smithsonian Institution, the New York Public Library, and the Internet Archive's partner collections). Hubs share metadata in a common application profile based on the Europeana Data Model, and the DPLA exposes an open API so that anyone can build applications on the aggregated data. The model was consciously influenced by Europeana, the European digital library.

Programmes

Beyond the portal, the DPLA runs exhibitions and primary-source sets for educators, supports the aggregation of records through its Cultural Heritage Hubs, and launched The Palace Project, an e-book platform for libraries, in cooperation with LYRASIS. It has also been involved in rights standardisation work (rightsstatements.org) and in community archiving initiatives. The organisation is supported by foundations including the Knight Foundation, the Mellon Foundation, and the Institute of Museum and Library Services.
