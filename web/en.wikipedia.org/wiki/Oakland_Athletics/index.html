Oakland Athletics

The Oakland Athletics (often called the A's) are an American professional baseball team in Major League Baseball's American League West division. Founded in Philadelphia in 1901, the franchise moved to Kansas City in 1955 and then to Oakland, California, in 1968. The Athletics have won nine World Series championships, including three consecutive titles from 1972 to 1974 under owner Charles O. Finley.

History

Under manager Connie Mack, the Philadelphia Athletics won five World Series between 1910 and 1930. After the Kansas City years (1955–1967), the club moved west and, behind players such as Reggie Jackson, Catfish Hunter, and Rollie Fingers, won three straight championships in the 1970s. The late-1980s "Bash Brothers" era produced another title in 1989. In the early 2000s, general manager Billy Beane's analytical, low-budget approach — chronicled in Michael Lewis's book Moneyball — made the A's a model of statistical team-building.

Relocation

After decades at the Oakland Coliseum and years of failed stadium negotiations, the club announced plans to relocate to Las Vegas, Nevada. The team left Oakland after the 2024 season, temporarily playing in West Sacramento under the name "Athletics" while a new Las Vegas ballpark is developed.
