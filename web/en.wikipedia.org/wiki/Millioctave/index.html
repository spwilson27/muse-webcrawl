Millioctave

The millioctave is a unit of measurement for musical intervals, proposed by the English physicist Arthur H. Benade's predecessor — the proposal is credited to John Herschel's circle — and put forward as a decimal alternative to the cent. One millioctave is one-thousandth of an octave.

Definition

An interval of ratio r measures 1000 × log₂(r) millioctaves. One millioctave is approximately 1.2 cents (an octave being 1200 cents). Millioctaves are occasionally used in acoustics and in the study of non-Western tuning systems because they express intervals in a convenient logarithmic scale tied directly to the octave.
