The Riddle of the Sands

The Riddle of the Sands: A Record of Secret Service is a 1903 novel by Erskine Childers, an early and influential example of the spy novel and the invasion literature genre. It is often credited with helping to shape British naval thinking before the First World War.

Plot

The narrator, Carruthers, a minor Foreign Office official bored with London society, accepts an invitation from his friend Arthur Davies to join a yachting holiday among the Frisian Islands off the German coast of the North Sea. Davies, an experienced small-boat sailor, has become suspicious of a German naturalised Englishman, Dollmann, who attempted to lure him onto the sands. Sailing the small yacht Dulcibella through the shifting channels behind the East Frisian Islands, the two men uncover evidence of a secret German plan to assemble a flotilla of barges and invade England across the shallow waters.

Significance

Childers drew on his own sailing expeditions in the region. The novel warned of Britain's vulnerability to invasion from across the North Sea and is often said to have influenced the establishment of naval bases such as Rosyth. It remained influential for decades and paved the way for the spy fiction of writers such as John Buchan and Ian Fleming.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into a 1979 film starring Michael York as Carruthers and Simon MacCorkindale as Davies.
