Full name: Robert Stephenson Smyth Baden-Powell
Born: 22 February 1857, Paddington, London
Died: 8 January 1941, Nyeri, Kenya

Robert Stephenson Smyth Baden-Powell, 1st Baron Baden-Powell, OM, GCMG, GCVO, KCB (22 February 1857 – 8 January 1941) was a British Army officer, writer, and founder of the worldwide Scout Movement. This article concerns his life and career under his full peerage title, by which he is formally styled in reference works.

Early life and army career

Baden-Powell was born in Paddington, London, the son of the Reverend Professor Baden Powell, a Savilian Professor of Geometry at Oxford, and Henrietta Grace Smyth. Educated at Charterhouse School, he joined the 13th Hussars in India in 1876 and served in southern Africa, Malta, and elsewhere, distinguishing himself in reconnaissance and frontier campaigning, including the Ashanti and Matabele campaigns and service in Zululand. His defence of Mafeking during the Second Boer War (1899–1900) made him a household name in Britain. He subsequently organised the South African Constabulary and became Inspector General of Cavalry in 1903, retiring in 1910 with the rank of lieutenant-general.

Founder of Scouting

Following the Brownsea Island experimental camp of 1907 and the publication of Scouting for Boys in 1908, Baden-Powell devoted the rest of his life to the Boy Scout and Girl Guide movements. He was made a baronet in 1922 and raised to the peerage as Baron Baden-Powell of Gilwell in 1929 — Gilwell Park being the movement's training centre near London. He travelled extensively, attended successive World Jamborees, and with his wife Olave, Lady Baden-Powell, promoted the movements' international growth.

Death and legacy

In 1939 Baden-Powell moved to Nyeri in Kenya, where he died on 8 January 1941 and was buried in St Peter's Cemetery, Nyeri. His headstone carries a circle with a dot in the centre, the trail sign meaning "I have gone home". His writings include Scouting for Boys, The Wolf Cub's Handbook, and his farewell message to Scouts, still read at Scout gatherings worldwide.
