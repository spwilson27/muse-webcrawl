Hashomer Hatzair (Hebrew: השומר הצעיר, "The Young Guard") is a Labor Zionist, secular Jewish youth movement founded in 1913 in Galicia, then part of the Austrian Empire. It is the oldest Zionist youth movement still in operation and was historically associated with the Kibbutz Artzi federation and the Mapam political party in Israel.

History

The movement originated in 1913 with a scouting organisation called Hashomer ("The Guard") in Galicia, which merged with the cultural circle Ze'irei Zion to form Hashomer Hatzair around 1916 among Jewish refugee youth in Vienna during the First World War. Influenced by the German Wandervogel youth movement, scouting, Marxism and the psychoanalysis of its early mentors, it promoted a synthesis of Zionism and socialism: the building of a Jewish homeland in Palestine through collective agricultural settlement — the kibbutz — and a classless society.

Members began emigrating to Mandate Palestine in 1919, and in 1927 the movement's kibbutzim federated as Kibbutz Artzi. During the Second World War, Hashomer Hatzair members were prominent in ghetto resistance, most famously Mordechai Anielewicz, leader of the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising in 1943. In Israel the movement co-founded the left-wing Mapam party in 1948 and for decades advocated a bi-national or two-state accommodation with the Arabs of Palestine.

Ideology and activities

Hashomer Hatzair educates youth aged roughly 10–18 in local branches called kenim ("nests"), emphasising peer-led education, social justice, equality and personal fulfilment through communal life (hagshama). Its traditional platform combined Zionism, socialism and "brotherhood of peoples." The movement operates today in Israel and in diaspora communities across Europe, the Americas and Australia.
