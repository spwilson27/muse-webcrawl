Abdul Salam Arif

Abdul Salam Mohammed Arif (21 March 1921 – 13 April 1966) was an Iraqi military officer and statesman who served as the second President of Iraq from 1963 until his death in a helicopter crash in 1966. Along with Abd al-Karim Qasim, he was a principal leader of the 14 July Revolution of 1958 that overthrew the Hashemite monarchy.

Early life and career

Arif was born in Baghdad into a Sunni Arab family with tribal connections to the Jumaila tribe of the Ramadi region. He joined the Iraqi Army, graduated from the military academy, and became a member of the clandestine Free Officers movement that plotted the overthrow of the monarchy. During the 1948 Arab–Israeli War he served in Palestine, an experience shared by many of the future Free Officers.

The 1958 revolution and fall

On 14 July 1958, brigades under Qasim and Arif seized Baghdad; King Faisal II and Crown Prince 'Abd al-Ilah were killed, and a republic was proclaimed. Arif became Deputy Prime Minister, Minister of the Interior and deputy commander-in-chief. A pan-Arab nationalist aligned with Gamal Abdel Nasser of Egypt, Arif favoured immediate union with the United Arab Republic, while Qasim resisted. Within months Qasim stripped Arif of his posts; Arif was tried and sentenced to death for plotting, though the sentence was commuted, and he spent the following years under house arrest.

Presidency

Arif returned to power in the Ramadan Revolution of 8 February 1963, in which the Ba'ath Party and nationalist officers overthrew and executed Qasim. Arif became President, with the Ba'athist Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr as prime minister. In November 1963 Arif, backed by the army, purged the Ba'ath from government and ruled with fellow officers. His government pursued Arab nationalist policies, nationalised banks and major industries in 1964, and negotiated a stillborn unity plan with Egypt. His younger brother, Abdul Rahman Arif, was a senior officer and minister in his government.

Death

Arif died on 13 April 1966 when the helicopter carrying him between Basra and Baghdad crashed in southern Iraq; sabotage was widely suspected but never proven. He was succeeded by his brother Abdul Rahman Arif.
