Øre

Øre (Norwegian and Danish, pronounced [ˈøːrə]) and öre (Swedish) is the centesimal subdivision of the krone currencies of Norway, Denmark, and Sweden. One krone equals 100 øre.

Etymology and history

The name derives from the Latin aureus, the Roman gold coin, and cognates have been used in Scandinavia since the Middle Ages. Øre coins circulated in Denmark and Norway from the era of the Scandinavian Monetary Union in the 1870s.

Modern use

Inflation has eroded the unit's value: Norway withdrew its last øre coin (the 50 øre) in 2012, and Denmark retains only the 50 øre coin. In Sweden, all öre coins were withdrawn by 2010. The øre nonetheless survives as a unit of account — prices are still quoted in kroner and øre, with cash totals rounded to the nearest krone.
