In mathematics, an exact couple is a system of groups and homomorphisms arranged in a triangle, exact at each vertex, that serves as a bookkeeping device for computations in homological algebra and algebraic topology. Exact couples were introduced by William S. Massey in 1952. By forming a "derived couple" from an exact couple, one obtains a sequence of approximations whose associated graded object is a spectral sequence.

Definition

An exact couple consists of two groups (or modules) D and E and three homomorphisms, conventionally written i: D → D, j: D → E, and k: E → D, such that the triangle is exact at each corner: im(i) = ker(j), im(j) = ker(k), and im(k) = ker(i). In the bigraded version, the groups are indexed by pairs of integers and the maps have specified degrees; this is the form used in practice.

Derived couple and spectral sequences

Given an exact couple, one defines a differential d = jk: E → E, which satisfies d² = 0, and replaces E by the homology of this differential and D by the image of i. The result is again an exact couple — the derived couple — and iterating the construction produces a spectral sequence whose successive pages are the E terms. Exact couples arising from filtered complexes or from towers of fibrations underlie many standard spectral sequences, including the Serre spectral sequence of a fibration and the spectral sequences of a filtered chain complex.
