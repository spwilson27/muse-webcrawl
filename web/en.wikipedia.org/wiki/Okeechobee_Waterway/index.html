Okeechobee Waterway

The Okeechobee Waterway is a navigable waterway system in Florida, United States, that crosses the state from Fort Myers on the Gulf of Mexico to Stuart on the Atlantic Ocean, a distance of about 154 miles (248 km) including the open-water crossing of Lake Okeechobee. Maintained by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, it allows vessels to cross the Florida peninsula rather than sailing around its southern tip through the Florida Keys.

Route

From the Gulf side, the waterway follows the Caloosahatchee River eastward from Fort Myers through locks at Ortona and Moore Haven to Lake Okeechobee. Boats may cross the lake directly by the cross-lake route or follow the shallower rim route around its southern shore. From the lake's eastern shore, the St. Lucie Canal runs east through the Port Mayaca Lock and the St. Lucie Lock to the St. Lucie River and the Atlantic Intracoastal Waterway near Stuart. Five locks lift and lower vessels between sea level and the lake's regulated water level.

History

The route developed from nineteenth-century canal digging — notably Hamilton Disston's dredging of the Caloosahatchee in the 1880s — and from federal flood-control and navigation works undertaken after the catastrophic hurricanes of 1926 and 1928. The modern waterway, with its locks and the Herbert Hoover Dike encircling the lake, was substantially completed by the 1940s. Today it is used mainly by recreational boaters touring the "Great Loop" and by commercial traffic moving across the peninsula.
