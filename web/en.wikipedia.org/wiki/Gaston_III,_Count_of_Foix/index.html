Gaston III, Count of Foix

Born30 April 1331, Orthez, Béarn
Died1 August 1391 (aged 60), L'Hôpital-d'Orion, Béarn
TitleCount of Foix, Viscount of Béarn
Reign1343–1391

Gaston III (30 April 1331 – 1 August 1391), known as Gaston Fébus (or Phoebus), was Count of Foix and Viscount of Béarn in southwestern France. One of the great feudal lords of the Hundred Years' War era, he kept his Pyrenean territories prosperous and effectively independent between warring France and England.

Life

Succeeding his father Gaston II, killed at the Siege of Algeciras in 1343, Gaston took the surname Fébus — after Apollo Phoebus, god of the sun — alluding to his golden hair. He fought on the Baltic crusade with the Teutonic Knights at Königsberg and, returning in 1358, rescued the Dauphine and other noblewomen from the peasant Jacquerie at Meaux, a celebrated exploit. He defeated the Count of Armagnac at Launac in 1362, consolidating his dominance of the region, and amassed a famous fortune through ransoms and tolls while building castles across Béarn.

Works and court

Gaston's court at Orthez was renowned for its magnificence, praised by the chronicler Froissart, who visited in 1388. Gaston wrote the Livre de chasse ("Book of the Hunt"), the most famous medieval treatise on hunting, dedicated to Philip the Bold of Burgundy; lavishly illuminated manuscripts of it survive. He also composed prayers in the Livre des oraisons and was a skilled musician. His later years were darkened by the death of his only legitimate son, Gaston, allegedly by accidental poisoning.
