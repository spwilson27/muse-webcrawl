Capture of Portobelo (1739)

The Capture of Portobelo was a British naval victory of 21 November 1739, when a squadron under Vice-Admiral Edward Vernon captured the Spanish colonial port of Portobelo (in modern Panama) during the opening weeks of the War of Jenkins' Ear. Vernon had famously boasted in Parliament that he could take the well-fortified port with only six ships, and his success made him a national hero in Britain.

Background

Portobelo, on the Caribbean coast of the Isthmus of Panama, was one of the principal ports of the Spanish treasure-fleet system. Silver from Peru was shipped to Panama City, carried across the isthmus by mule train, and loaded onto galleons at Portobelo during its famous trade fairs. Its defences had been rebuilt after earlier raids, notably the sack by Henry Morgan in 1668.

Relations between Britain and Spain deteriorated through the 1730s over smuggling, the asiento trade, and Spanish coastguard depredations — symbolised by the alleged severing of Captain Robert Jenkins's ear in 1731. War was declared in October 1739, and Vernon was sent to the West Indies with orders to destroy Spanish shipping and strongholds.

The attack

On 20 November 1739 Vernon's six ships — Burford, Norwich, Humber, Sheerness, Statford and Worcester — approached the port. The next morning they engaged the harbour defences, centred on the Iron Castle (Castillo de San Felipe) at the harbour mouth and the Gloria and San Jerónimo castles. A shift of wind allowed the British ships to anchor close under the Iron Castle's guns and silence it after a brief bombardment. Landing parties then stormed the remaining forts; the governor surrendered the town the following day.

Aftermath

British casualties were remarkably light, and Vernon ordered the fortifications demolished so the port could not again shelter Spain's treasure trade. News of the victory reached London in March 1740 and provoked extravagant celebrations: medals were struck, taverns and streets were named after Vernon and Portobelo (Portobello Road in London among them), and the song "Rule, Britannia!" was composed in the patriotic fever of the moment. The enthusiasm fed unrealistic expectations that contributed to the disastrous British expedition against Cartagena de Indias in 1741.

Legacy

Portobelo's defences were later rebuilt in the escuela de fortificación abaluartada style and are now part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with the nearby Fort San Lorenzo.
