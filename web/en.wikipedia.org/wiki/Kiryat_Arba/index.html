Kiryat Arba

Kiryat Arba Hebrew: קִרְיַת־אַרְבַּע ("City of Four")
Location: Adjacent to Hebron, West Bank
Founded: 1968–1971
Status: Israeli settlement and local council
Population: about 7,000

Kiryat Arba is an Israeli settlement and local council on the outskirts of the Palestinian city of Hebron in the southern West Bank. Its name ("City of Four") is the biblical name of Hebron itself in the Hebrew Bible, and the settlement was founded by religious Zionist settlers closely associated with the Gush Emunim movement.

History

Following the Six-Day War of 1967, a group led by Rabbi Moshe Levinger settled in Hebron, first living in the city's Park Hotel and then in the military government compound. In 1970–71 the Israeli government approved the construction of Kiryat Arba on a hill east of the city, and families moved into the new neighbourhood. It became a separate local council in 1979.

The settlement is closely tied to the Cave of the Patriarchs in Hebron, a site holy to Jews, Muslims and Christians. In 1994 a resident of Kiryat Arba, Baruch Goldstein, killed 29 Palestinian worshippers at the Cave of the Patriarchs in the massacre that bears the site's name, an atrocity widely condemned in Israel and abroad.

Status

The international community considers Israeli settlements in the West Bank illegal under international law, a position Israel disputes. Kiryat Arba's residents feature prominently in debates over the settlement movement, and the settlement remains a frequent flashpoint in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.
