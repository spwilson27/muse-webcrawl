Cerrejón Formation

TypeGeological formation
PeriodMid-to-late Paleocene (Selandian–Thanetian)
LocationCesar-Ranchería Basin, La Guajira, Colombia
Named forCerrejón coal mine

The Cerrejón open-pit coal mine, where the formation is exposed.

The Cerrejón Formation is a geological formation in Colombia dating back to the Middle–Late Paleocene, approximately 60 to 58 million years ago. It is exposed in the Cesar-Ranchería Basin in the department of La Guajira, in and around the Cerrejón coal mine, one of the largest open-pit coal mines in the world.

Paleoenvironment and fossils

The formation was deposited in a river-dominated delta plain adjacent to a shallow sea, in one of the earliest known Neotropical rainforests, growing just a few million years after the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event. Its fossil assemblage includes abundant plant macrofossils and pollen, giant turtles such as Carbonemys cofrinii and Puentemys, crocodyliforms including Cerrejonisuchus and Acherontisuchus, and most famously Titanoboa cerrejonensis, the largest known snake, estimated at about 13 metres (42 feet) long and over a tonne in weight.

Significance

Because the size of ectotherms such as snakes is constrained by temperature, Titanoboa has been used to infer that equatorial temperatures in the Paleocene were several degrees warmer than today. The flora indicates that Paleocene Neotropical forests, though less diverse than modern rainforests, already contained many modern plant families.
