Start codon

The start codon is the first codon of a messenger RNA (mRNA) transcript translated by a ribosome. The start codon always codes for methionine in eukaryotes and Archaea and for a modified Met (fMet) in bacteria, mitochondria, and plastids. The most common start codon is AUG (i.e., ATG in the corresponding DNA sequence).

Mechanism

The start codon is often preceded by a 5' untranslated region (5' UTR). In prokaryotes this includes the ribosome binding site (the Shine–Dalgarno sequence); in eukaryotes the ribosome typically scans from the 5' cap to the first AUG, whose efficiency is influenced by the surrounding Kozak consensus sequence. Translation terminates at a stop codon.

Alternative start codons

Alternative start codons are codons other than AUG that can initiate translation, though usually less efficiently. Bacteria commonly use GUG and UUG; in such cases the codon still recruits the initiator fMet-tRNA, so the first amino acid incorporated is methionine regardless of the codon. In rare cases, eukaryotes also initiate at non-AUG codons such as CUG.
