Tonal language

A tonal language is a language in which pitch is used to distinguish the meaning of words, in the same way that consonants and vowels do. In such languages, the same sequence of sounds pronounced with different tones can mean entirely different things.

The best-known tonal language is Mandarin Chinese, which has four main tones (plus a neutral tone): for example, mā (high level) means "mother", má (rising) means "hemp", mǎ (falling-rising) means "horse", and mà (falling) means "scold". Other tonal languages include Thai, Vietnamese, Yoruba, and many languages of sub-Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia.

Tone systems

Tone may be level (high, mid, low) or contour (rising, falling, dipping). Some languages have only two tones, while others, such as Cantonese, have six or more. Tone can also interact with grammar, marking tense or plurality, and can change through sandhi, where a tone changes in the context of neighbouring tones. A majority of the world's languages are estimated to be tonal to some degree.
