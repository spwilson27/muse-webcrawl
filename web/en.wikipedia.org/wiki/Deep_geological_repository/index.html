Deep geological repository

A deep geological repository is an underground facility excavated deep within a stable geological formation to isolate radioactive waste from the human environment for tens of thousands to millions of years. It is the internationally favoured end-point for high-level waste and spent nuclear fuel.

Deep geological repository
Depth: Typically 300–1,000 m
Host rock: Granite, clay, salt
Examples: Onkalo (Finland), WIPP (USA, salt, defence waste)
Design: Multi-barrier system

Design

The multi-barrier concept combines waste form (e.g. vitrified glass or spent fuel), corrosion-resistant canisters (copper/steel in Sweden and Finland's KBS-3 design), bentonite clay buffer, backfilled tunnels and the host rock itself. Heat from decay, groundwater movement, glaciation and future human intrusion must be modelled in safety cases.

Facilities worldwide

Finland's Onkalo repository at Olkiluoto, licensed in 2015 and constructed by Posiva, is the world's first for spent fuel, with disposal operations beginning in the mid-2020s. Sweden's Forsmark repository was approved in 2022. The U.S. Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP) in New Mexico salt has operated since 1999 for transuranic defence waste, while the Yucca Mountain civilian project was politically abandoned. Programmes in France (Cigéo), Switzerland, Canada and the UK proceed slowly, largely due to siting consent.

Ethics and marking

Repositories raise intergenerational ethics questions and how to warn far-future societies—studies have proposed markers, archives and "nuclear semiotics".
