Antigonus I Monophthalmus

Antigonus I
Born: 382 BC
Died: 301 BC, Ipsus
Reign: 306–301 BC
Dynasty: Antigonid
Son: Demetrius I Poliorcetes

Antigonus I Monophthalmus (the One-Eyed, 382–301 BC) was a Macedonian general under Alexander the Great and founder of the Antigonid dynasty. After Alexander's death he became one of the most powerful Diadochi.

Career

Appointed satrap of Phrygia in 333 BC, he expanded control over Asia Minor and Syria after 323 BC. He fought Eumenes, and in the Wars of the Diadochi opposed Cassander, Ptolemy, Lysimachus and Seleucus. In 306 BC after Demetrius's victory at Salamis in Cyprus, Antigonus assumed the royal title.

Death

He was killed at the Battle of Ipsus in 301 BC, aged about 81, fighting a coalition of Lysimachus and Seleucus. His Asian empire was divided, but his son Demetrius retained ambitions in Macedon.
