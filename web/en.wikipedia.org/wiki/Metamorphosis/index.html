Metamorphosis

[Image: Complete metamorphosis: from caterpillar through chrysalis to butterfly]

Overview

Metamorphosis is a biological process by which an animal physically develops after birth or hatching, involving a conspicuous and relatively abrupt change in body structure through cell growth and differentiation. Insects, amphibians, molluscs, crustaceans and many other groups undergo metamorphosis, usually accompanied by a change of habitat and behaviour.

Types

Insects show ametabolous (no change), hemimetabolous (incomplete: egg–nymph–adult, as in grasshoppers and dragonflies) or holometabolous (complete: egg–larva–pupa–adult, as in butterflies, beetles, flies and bees) development; about 80–90% of insect species are holometabolous. Amphibian metamorphosis — tadpole to frog — is driven by thyroid hormone, as insect moulting is by ecdysone and juvenile hormone. The process was described in wonder by Maria Sibylla Merian's Surinam studies (1705) and is a symbol of transformation across cultures.
