Turkish Angora

The Turkish Angora (Turkish: Ankara kedisi) is a breed of domestic cat originating in the Ankara region of Turkey. It is one of the ancient, naturally occurring breeds, known for its long, silky coat, plumed tail, and elegant, fine-boned build.

Characteristics

Turkish Angoras have a long, silky single coat with no undercoat, most famously white, though many colours and patterns are now accepted. The eyes may be blue, green, amber, or odd-eyed (heterochromia), a trait especially prized in white cats. The body is long and muscular, with a wedge-shaped head and large, upright ears. The breed is intelligent, playful, and athletic.

History

Long-haired cats from the Ankara (historically Angora) region were among the first long-haired cats brought to Europe, in the 17th century, and contributed to the development of the Persian breed. In Turkey the cats are considered a national treasure; Ankara Zoo maintains a breeding programme for pure white Angoras. The breed was formally recognised by major registries in the 1970s, initially only in white.

Health

White Turkish Angoras with blue or odd eyes may carry the gene associated with congenital deafness. The breed is also predisposed to hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and to ataxia in kittens from certain lines.
