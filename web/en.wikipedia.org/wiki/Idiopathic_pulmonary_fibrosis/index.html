Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

Specialty: Pulmonology

Symptoms: Progressive breathlessness, dry cough, finger clubbing

Usual onset: 50–70 years

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, progressive lung disease characterised by the scarring (fibrosis) of lung tissue of unknown cause. It is the most common of the idiopathic interstitial pneumonias and corresponds to the histological and radiological pattern of usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP).

Signs and symptoms

Patients typically develop gradually worsening breathlessness on exertion and a persistent dry cough over several years. Examination often reveals fine end-inspiratory "Velcro" crackles at the lung bases and, in about half of patients, clubbing of the fingers. The disease mainly affects people between 50 and 70 years of age and is more common in men and in current or former smokers.

Cause and diagnosis

By definition the cause is unknown, though genetic factors (such as variants in the MUC5B promoter and telomere-maintenance genes), ageing, cigarette smoke and microaspiration are implicated. Diagnosis rests on high-resolution CT showing a UIP pattern — subpleural, basal-predominant reticulation with honeycombing — and exclusion of known causes such as connective tissue disease, hypersensitivity pneumonitis and drug toxicity; lung biopsy is reserved for uncertain cases.

Treatment and prognosis

There is no cure. The antifibrotic drugs pirfenidone and nintedanib slow the decline in lung function. Management includes pulmonary rehabilitation, supplemental oxygen, vaccination and treatment of comorbidities; lung transplantation is an option for selected patients. Median survival from diagnosis is roughly 3–5 years, though the course varies, and some patients suffer acute exacerbations with high mortality.
