High-speed rail in the United States

High-speed rail in the United States is limited compared with Europe and Asia. The only service meeting international high-speed definitions is Amtrak's Acela on the Northeast Corridor, reaching 150 mph (240 km/h) on short sections. Several dedicated high-speed projects are under construction, notably California High-Speed Rail and Brightline West.

US High-Speed Rail
Operator: Amtrak, Brightline
Fastest: Acela, 150 mph
Under construction: California HSR, Brightline West
First HSR proposal: 1960s

Definitions and history

The High-Speed Ground Transportation Act of 1965 led to the Metroliner and Turbotrain. The Northeast Corridor between Boston and Washington, D.C. became the focus of investment. Acela Express launched in 2000, and its successor Avelia Liberty trainsets (Acela II) were introduced in the 2020s with tilting and higher capacity.

Current services

Besides Acela, Amtrak Northeast Regional trains reach 125 mph, Brightline in Florida reaches 125 mph between West Palm Beach and Orlando (opened 2023) and is classified as higher-speed rail, and Cascades, Keystone and Empire services reach 110 mph on upgraded conventional track.

Projects under construction

California High-Speed Rail

Approved by voters in 2008 (Proposition 1A), the system aims to link San Francisco to Los Angeles at 220 mph. The initial operating segment in the Central Valley (Merced to Bakersfield) has been under construction since 2015 amid cost rises to over $100 billion for the full Phase 1.

Brightline West

A privately developed 218-mile line from Las Vegas to Rancho Cucamonga, California, broke ground in 2024, targeting 200 mph electric trains and opening around the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics.

Texas Central

A proposed Shinkansen-based Dallas–Houston line has faced land and financing delays.

Obstacles

Federalism, freight railroad ownership of most track, automobile and airline competition, high land costs, and lack of sustained federal funding have slowed development compared with other countries.
