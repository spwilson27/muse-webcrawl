Malleus

The malleus, or hammer, is a small hammer-shaped bone in the middle ear. Along with the incus and the stapes, it is one of the three ossicles that conduct sound vibrations from the eardrum to the inner ear.

Structure

The malleus is the largest of the three ossicles, measuring about 8 mm in length. It consists of a head, which articulates with the incus; a neck; and a handle (manubrium), which is embedded in the tympanic membrane (eardrum). Two small processes project from the bone: the lateral process, which is attached to the upper part of the eardrum, and the anterior process, a slender spine of bone.

Function

Sound waves striking the eardrum cause it to vibrate. Because the handle of the malleus is attached to the eardrum, the bone moves with it and transmits the vibration to the incus, which in turn moves the stapes. The ossicular chain acts as a lever system and matches the impedance between air and the fluid of the inner ear, allowing efficient transfer of sound energy to the cochlea.

The tensor tympani muscle attaches to the handle of the malleus; when it contracts it pulls the handle inward, increasing the tension of the eardrum and dampening the transmission of very loud, low-frequency sounds.

Development and evolution

The malleus develops embryologically from the first pharyngeal arch. In non-mammalian vertebrates its homologue is the articular bone, which forms part of the lower jaw joint; during mammalian evolution, bones of the jaw joint were repurposed into the sound-conducting apparatus of the middle ear.
