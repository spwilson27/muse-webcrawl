Battle of Itsukushima

The Battle of Itsukushima (Itsukushima no Tatakai), also called the Battle of Miyajima, was fought on 16 October 1555 on and around the island of Itsukushima (Miyajima) in Aki Province, Japan. It was the only battle ever fought on the sacred island, home of the Itsukushima Shrine. The forces of Mōri Motonari defeated the army of Sue Harukata, who had seized control of the Ōuchi clan.

Background and battle

After the coup of 1551, in which Sue Harukata overthrew and caused the death of Ōuchi Yoshitaka, Motonari — once an Ōuchi retainer — resolved to destroy him. Harukata landed on Itsukushima in 1555 and occupied the island, building Miyao Castle. Motonari, feigning construction of his own fort on the island to draw Sue in, landed his smaller force by night in a storm, using the local pirate fleets of the Murakami to control the sea. At dawn the Mōri attacked Miyao Castle from behind while a detachment stormed the castle; trapped on the island with no escape, the Sue army collapsed. Harukata fled and committed suicide.

Aftermath

The victory made Motonari master of Aki and began the Mōri rise to dominance in western Honshu; within two years he had taken the Ōuchi territories of Suō and Nagato. Because the island was sacred, Motonari ordered an extensive ritual purification after the battle.
