Osborne 1

The Osborne 1, the first commercially successful portable computer.

The Osborne 1 is the first commercially successful portable computer, released on April 3, 1981, by the Osborne Computer Corporation. It weighed about 24.5 pounds (11.1 kg), cost US$1,795, and ran the CP/M operating system.

Specifications

The machine used a Zilog Z80 processor at 4 MHz with 64 KB of memory, dual 5¼-inch floppy disk drives, and a tiny built-in 5-inch monochrome CRT display showing 52 columns of text. Designed by Lee Felsenstein, it came bundled with a valuable software package including the WordStar word processor, SuperCalc spreadsheet and the dBASE II database — a bundle worth roughly as much as the hardware. The case closed into a self-contained "luggable" unit that could fit under an airline seat, though it required mains power.

History and the "Osborne effect"

The Osborne 1 sold strongly at first, and the company grew rapidly. However, Osborne pre-announced improved successor machines (the Executive and Vixen) long before they shipped, causing dealers to cancel orders for the existing model — a cautionary tale popularly known as the "Osborne effect". The company filed for bankruptcy in September 1983.
