Seattle Metropolitans

The Seattle Metropolitans were a professional ice hockey team based in Seattle, Washington, which played in the Pacific Coast Hockey Association (PCHA) from 1915 to 1924. In 1917, they became the first American team to win the Stanley Cup.

History

The Metropolitans were founded for the 1915–16 PCHA season, coached by Pete Muldoon, and played at the Seattle Ice Arena. Their colours were green, red and white, with striped jerseys. The team featured players such as goaltender Hap Holmes, Frank Foyston, Jack Walker and Bernie Morris.

1917 Stanley Cup

In March 1917 the Metropolitans defeated the National Hockey Association champion Montreal Canadiens, three games to one, to win the Stanley Cup—the first time the trophy was won by a team based in the United States. Seattle returned to the Stanley Cup Final in 1919 against Montreal, a series cancelled during the influenza pandemic with the teams tied, and in 1920, losing to the Ottawa Senators.

Demise

The team folded in 1924 when its arena was converted and the PCHA merged into the Western Canada Hockey League. Seattle had no top-level team again until the Seattle Kraken joined the NHL in 2021; the Kraken have honoured the Metropolitans' colours and history.
