Treaty of Wehlau

The Treaty of Wehlau was signed on 19 September 1657 at Wehlau (now Znamensk, Kaliningrad Oblast) between the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and Frederick William, Elector of Brandenburg and Duke of Prussia, during the Second Northern War.

Terms

Poland released the Elector from his feudal allegiance for the Duchy of Prussia, recognizing full sovereignty of the Hohenzollerns in Prussia—the status confirmed in the subsequent Treaty of Bromberg (November 1657) and ratified internationally in the Peace of Oliva (1660). In return, Frederick William renounced his alliance with Sweden, undertook to aid Poland against the Swedes with troops, and agreed the duchy would revert to Poland should his male line die out; the towns of Lauenburg and Bütow passed to him as a fief, and he received Draheim in pawn and Elbing's revenues pledged.

Significance

Sovereignty over Prussia made possible the Elector's successor's coronation as "King in Prussia" in 1701 and laid a foundation stone of the later Kingdom of Prussia and German unification.
