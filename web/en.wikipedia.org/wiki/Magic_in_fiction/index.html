Magic in fiction

Magic in fiction is the endowment of characters or objects in works of fiction with powers that operate beyond the laws of nature known in the real world. Magic serves as a frequent plot device in fantasy literature, film, television and games, and its treatment ranges from mysterious and unknowable forces to rule-bound "magic systems".

Systems of magic

Authors vary widely in how structured fictional magic is. In some works it is a wild, folkloric force tied to spirits, gods or prophecy; in others it is learned like a science, with spells, ingredients and costs. Fantasy author Brandon Sanderson popularised the distinction between "hard" magic systems, whose rules readers can understand, and "soft" ones that remain wondrous and mysterious, as in the works of J. R. R. Tolkien.

Common conventions

Recurring devices include the wizard or sorcerer figure, spellbooks and wands, magical artefacts, innate talent versus learned skill, and prices or limitations that keep magic from solving every problem. Folkloric motifs such as geasa, curses and true names recur across traditions.
