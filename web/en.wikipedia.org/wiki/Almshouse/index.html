Almshouse

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

PurposeCharitable housing for poor or elderly people
OriginMedieval England; tradition from the 10th century
ResidentsSometimes called almsmen / almswomen or Bedesmen

An almshouse is charitable housing provided to people of a particular community or background — traditionally the elderly poor — allowing them to live independently with dignity. Almshouses are usually endowed by a benefactor or charity, and the residents (historically called almsmen and almswomen) typically pay little or no rent, though they may be expected to observe rules and attend chapel.

History

The tradition descends from medieval hospitals and chantries attached to monasteries and guilds; the oldest almshouse still in operation in England is often said to be the Hospital of St Cross in Winchester, founded in the 1130s. After the Reformation, merchants, livery companies and private philanthropists founded thousands of almshouses, often built as handsome rows or quadrangles around a courtyard with a chapel and the founder's arms displayed. Examples include the almshouses of the Charterhouse in London and the Royal Chelsea Hospital for soldier pensioners.

Almshouses today

There are still some 1,600 almshouse charities in the United Kingdom providing around 36,000 dwellings, coordinated by the Almshouse Association. New almshouses continue to be built, typically for older people of limited means with a local or occupational connection. In the United States the word "almshouse" also historically denoted the municipal poorhouse or poor farm, an institution with a much harsher reputation.
