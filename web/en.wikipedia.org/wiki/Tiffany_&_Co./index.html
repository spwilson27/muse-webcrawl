Tiffany & Co. (colloquially known as Tiffany's) is an American luxury jewellery and specialty design house headquartered in New York City. Founded in 1837 by Charles Lewis Tiffany and John B. Young as a "stationery and fancy goods" store, the company became renowned for its diamonds, silverware and fine jewellery, and for its distinctive robin's-egg blue packaging color, known as Tiffany Blue. Its flagship store on Fifth Avenue in Manhattan, opened in 1940, is a New York landmark, immortalized by the 1961 film Breakfast at Tiffany's.

History

Tiffany introduced the English standard of sterling silver purity to the United States and won international acclaim at the 1867 Paris Exposition. The company supplied swords and flags during the American Civil War, revamped the Great Seal of the United States in 1885, and in 1886 introduced the "Tiffany setting" six-prong solitaire diamond ring, which became an icon of engagement jewellery. Gemologist George Frederick Kunz and designer Louis Comfort Tiffany, the founder's son, further built the firm's reputation for colored gemstones and art glass.

Products and ownership

Tiffany sells jewellery, sterling silver goods, china, crystal, watches, fragrances and personal accessories through hundreds of stores worldwide. Famous stones associated with the house include the 128.54-carat yellow Tiffany Diamond. In January 2021, after a contentious negotiation, Tiffany was acquired by the French luxury conglomerate LVMH for about $15.8 billion, then the largest luxury acquisition ever completed.
