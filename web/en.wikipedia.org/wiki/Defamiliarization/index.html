Defamiliarization

Defamiliarization or ostranenie (Russian: остранение) is the artistic technique of presenting to audiences common things in an unfamiliar or strange way so they gain a new perspective on the familiar. The concept was introduced by Russian literary critic Viktor Shklovsky in his 1917 essay "Art as Device" (also translated as "Art as Technique"), a foundational text of Russian Formalism.

Theory

Shklovsky argued that habitual perception makes us see things only by recognition, "algebraically"; the purpose of art is to restore the sensation of things as perceived, not merely recognised. Art achieves this by making objects strange — increasing the difficulty and duration of perception. His classic example was Tolstoy, who estranges flogging, property and opera by describing them as if for the first time, or narrating Kholstomer from a horse's point of view.

Related concepts

The idea influenced the Prague linguistic circle and was adapted by Bertolt Brecht as the Verfremdungseffekt ("alienation" or "distancing effect") in epic theatre, intended to prevent passive identification and provoke critical thought. Related notions include the Romantic "making strange" praised by Coleridge and the surrealist dislocation of everyday objects.
