Miloš Forman

Miloš Forman (1932–2018), director of One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest and Amadeus.

Jan Tomáš "Miloš" Forman (18 February 1932 – 13 April 2018) was a Czech-American film director, screenwriter, and professor, regarded as one of the most important filmmakers of the Czechoslovak New Wave and of postwar American cinema. He won two Academy Awards for Best Director.

Early life and Czech career

Born in Čáslav, Czechoslovakia, Forman was orphaned when his parents died in Nazi concentration camps. After studying at the Film and TV School of the Academy of Performing Arts in Prague (FAMU), he gained international attention with Black Peter (1964), Loves of a Blonde (1965), and The Firemen's Ball (1967), gentle satires of provincial Czech life that were nominated for Academy Awards and, in the last case, banned by the communist authorities.

Hollywood success

Forman emigrated after the Warsaw Pact invasion of 1968 ended the Prague Spring. His American breakthrough was One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest (1975), which won all five major Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Director. He followed it with the film of the musical Hair (1979), Ragtime (1981), and Amadeus (1984), a lavish drama about Mozart and Salieri that won eight Academy Awards, including his second Best Director Oscar. Later films include Valmont (1989), The People vs. Larry Flynt (1996), Man on the Moon (1999), and Goya's Ghosts (2006).
