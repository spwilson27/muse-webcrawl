Adelaide Hoodless

Adelaide Sophia Hoodless
Born: 27 February 1858, Strabane, Canada West
Died: 26 February 1910, Toronto, Canada
Known for: Founder of the Women's Institutes

Adelaide Sophia Hoodless (née Hunter; 27 February 1858 – 26 February 1910) was a Canadian educational reformer who founded the international Women's Institutes movement and campaigned for domestic science (home economics) education in public schools.

Life

Hoodless was moved to action after the death of her infant son, which she attributed to unsafe milk. She campaigned for pasteurization, pure food, and better household sanitation, and promoted teaching domestic science to girls as a school subject in Ontario.

Women's Institutes

In 1897, working with Erland Lee of Stoney Creek, Ontario, she helped found the first Women's Institute, an organization for rural women focused on education in homemaking, child care, and community improvement. The movement spread across Canada and internationally, and Hoodless also helped inspire organizations linked to the founding of the Victorian Order of Nurses, Macdonald Institute, and YWCA work in Canada.

Legacy

Her childhood home in St. Joseph / Strabane area is preserved, and schools and a rose are named in her honour. She is regarded as one of Canada's notable women reformers of the turn of the 20th century.
