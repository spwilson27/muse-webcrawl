Semi-active radar homing

Semi-active radar homing (SARH) is a common type of missile guidance system, perhaps the most common type for longer-range air-to-air and surface-to-air missile systems. The missile itself carries only a radar receiver: the target must be illuminated ("painted") by a radar on the launch platform, and the missile homes on the reflected signal.

Principle

The launching aircraft or ground station illuminates the target with its radar, often using continuous-wave illumination. The missile's seeker detects the reflected energy and computes the intercept course, comparing the signal with a reference sampled from the rear-facing antenna to derive Doppler and angle data. Because the transmitter remains on the launcher, the missile can be smaller and cheaper than an active radar homing design, but the launcher must keep the target illuminated for the entire engagement, limiting manoeuvre and exposing it to attack.

Examples

Well-known SARH missiles include the AIM-7 Sparrow and R-27 (AA-10 Alamo) air-to-air missiles and the naval RIM-66 Standard and land-based MIM-23 Hawk surface-to-air missiles. Many such systems have been supplemented or replaced by active-radar "fire-and-forget" weapons such as the AIM-120 AMRAAM.
