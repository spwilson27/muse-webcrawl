Point Pelee National Park

ProvinceOntario
ClaimSouthernmost mainland point of Canada
FameBird and monarch migration
[Image: The tip of Point Pelee at dawn]
The tip of Point Pelee at dawn

Point Pelee National Park is a national park in Ontario, Canada, at the southern tip of mainland Canada, a forested sand spit jutting into Lake Erie — famous as a landing point for migrating birds and monarch butterflies crossing the lake.

Overview

The point lies further south than Rome and about a third of the US states' northern borders; its Carolinian forest of hackberry, tulip-tree and prickly pear cactus is a flora found nowhere else in Canada. Each May, birders crowd its boardwalks for warblers exhausted from the lake crossing; each autumn monarchs stage for the flight south. The park, created in 1918 partly at ornithologists' urging, was also Caldwell First Nation land — the nation was finally compensated and has been rebuilding its presence since a 2020 settlement. A shuttle runs visitors to the tip, where currents make swimming dangerous and gulls wheel over the sand.
