Dorothy Edith Smith CM (6 July 1926 – 3 June 2022) was a British-born Canadian sociologist known for her development of feminist standpoint theory and the method of institutional ethnography. Her work examined how people's everyday lives are coordinated by text-mediated social relations — what she called the "relations of ruling".

Career

Smith earned her PhD at the University of California, Berkeley in 1963, taught at the University of British Columbia and then the Ontario Institute for Studies in Education at the University of Toronto, and later at the University of Victoria. Her books include The Everyday World as Problematic (1987), The Conceptual Practices of Power (1990), and Institutional Ethnography: A Sociology for People (2005).

Ideas

Drawing on Marx, phenomenology and ethnomethodology, Smith argued that sociology should begin from women's standpoint in everyday life, and she analysed how documents, forms and professional discourses organise experience from afar. She was appointed a Member of the Order of Canada in 1999.
