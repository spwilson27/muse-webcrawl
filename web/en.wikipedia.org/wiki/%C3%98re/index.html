Øre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The øre is a unit of currency used in Denmark, Norway, and historically Sweden, equal to one hundredth of a krone or krona. The name derives from the Latin aureus, a gold coin, and has been used for small-denominational coinage in Scandinavia since the Middle Ages.

Usage

In Denmark, the 50-øre coin is the smallest in circulation; smaller øre denominations were withdrawn, and cash prices are rounded to the nearest 50 øre. Norway phased out its 50-øre coin in 2012, so the øre survives there only as a unit of account in electronic payments. Sweden's öre coins were withdrawn by 2010, though the unit remains in pricing.

History

Scandinavian monarchs struck øre-related denominations from medieval times, and the modern decimal system — 100 øre to the krone — dates from the Scandinavian Monetary Union of the 1870s, when Denmark, Sweden, and Norway adopted a shared gold-based currency. After the union dissolved, each country retained the krone and its øre subdivision independently.
