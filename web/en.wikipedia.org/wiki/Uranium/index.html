Uranium

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Yellowcake uranium oxide powder in a steel drum with radiation trefoil markings]

Uranium is a chemical element with the symbol U and atomic number 92. It is a silvery-grey metal in the actinide series, weakly radioactive, and occurs in soil, rock and seawater at low concentrations. Its isotopes uranium-235 (fissile, ~0.7% of natural uranium) and uranium-238 (~99.3%) drive its uses in nuclear power and weapons.

Discovered by Martin Klaproth in 1789 (and named after Uranus), uranium's radioactivity was revealed by Henri Becquerel in 1896 — the discovery that launched atomic physics. Enrichment raises the U-235 share for reactors (a few percent) or weapons (much higher).

Mining and issues

Major producers include Kazakhstan, Canada and Australia. Uranium mining and waste raise environmental and health issues, and spent fuel's radioactivity lasts millennia.
