Albert of Riga (c. 1165 – 17 January 1229), also known as Albert of Buxhoeveden, was a German bishop and crusader who founded the city of Riga in 1201 and led the Christian conquest and colonisation of Livonia (in present-day Latvia and Estonia) during the Livonian Crusade.

Bishop and founder of Riga

A canon of Bremen Cathedral, Albert was consecrated bishop in 1199 and sailed to the Daugava River in 1200 with a fleet of ships and crusading soldiers. In 1201 he founded Riga at a trading site near the mouth of the river, transferred his see there, and promoted settlement by German merchants and colonists. Pope Innocent III supported his mission, granting indulgences to those who took part.

Conquest of Livonia

To secure a permanent military force, Albert founded the Livonian Brothers of the Sword in 1202. Over the following decades he campaigned, negotiated and divided conquered lands between his bishopric, the Sword Brothers and, later, Danish allies in Estonia. In 1207 Emperor Philip of Swabia enfeoffed him with Livonia as an imperial prince. His expansion brought him into chronic disputes with the Sword Brothers and the Danes over shares of the conquest.

Death and legacy

Albert died in Riga in 1229 and was buried in Riga Cathedral. Latin chronicler Henry of Livonia recorded his deeds in the Chronicle of Henry of Livonia. Albert is remembered as the effective founder of Riga and of German rule in the eastern Baltic, a legacy central to Latvian and Estonian national histories.
