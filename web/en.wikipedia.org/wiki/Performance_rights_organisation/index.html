Performance rights organisation

A performance rights organisation (PRO), also known as a performing rights society, is an association, corporation or agency that collects licence fees on behalf of songwriters, composers and music publishers when their musical works are publicly performed, and distributes the money as royalties to its members.

Function

Public performance includes broadcasts on radio and television, streaming, live concerts, and music played in businesses such as shops, restaurants and bars. PROs issue blanket licences covering their entire repertoire, monitor usage, and distribute royalties according to surveys, census data and reporting rules.

Major organisations

Examples include ASCAP and BMI in the United States (with SESAC and GMR as smaller, invite-only or boutique competitors), PRS for Music in the United Kingdom, SOCAN in Canada, APRA AMCOS in Australia and Australasia, GEMA in Germany, and SACEM in France. International coordination is provided in part through CISAC.

Criticism

PROs have been criticised over distribution formulas that favour major publishers, opaque sampling methods, and licence demands on small venues; in the United States ASCAP and BMI operate under long-standing antitrust consent decrees.
