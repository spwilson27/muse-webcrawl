Richard Stallman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Richard Stallman, portrait photograph with long hair and beard]

Richard Matthew Stallman (born 1953), often known by his initials rms, is an American free-software movement activist and programmer. He launched the GNU Project in 1983 to create a completely free Unix-like operating system, founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF) in 1985, and wrote the GNU General Public License (GPL).

Born in New York City, Stallman was a hacker at MIT's AI Lab in the 1970s; the collapse of that community and the rise of proprietary software motivated his campaign for software freedom, defined by four freedoms: to run, study, share, and modify software.

Work

Stallman wrote major GNU components himself, including Emacs (begun 1976 in earlier form; GNU Emacs 1985), the GNU Compiler Collection (GCC, 1987), and the GNU Debugger. GNU tools combined with Linus Torvalds's Linux kernel (1991) formed the complete free system he had aimed for; he insists on the name GNU/Linux for such systems.

He resigned as FSF president in 2019 following controversy over public remarks, and returned to the FSF board in 2021, drawing further criticism. His uncompromising positions — against DRM, proprietary software, and network services that replace local computing — and personal conduct have long made him a polarising figure, even among allies.

Ideas

Stallman's 'copyleft' — using copyright law to guarantee freedoms downstream — underpins the GPL family of licences and influenced Creative Commons and open-content movements. He distinguishes 'free software' from 'open source' as an ethical versus pragmatic framing.
