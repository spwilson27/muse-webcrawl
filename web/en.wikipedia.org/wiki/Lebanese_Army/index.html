The Lebanese Army is the land warfare branch of the Lebanese Armed Forces, the military of the Republic of Lebanon. Founded on 1 August 1945, it is by far the largest of Lebanon's three services, which also include a small navy and air force.

History

The army's roots lie in units raised under the French Mandate. During the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990) the army fractured along sectarian lines, but it was rebuilt after the war into one of the country's few widely respected cross-sectarian institutions. In the decades since, it has been deployed to maintain internal security, patrol the southern border, and fight militant groups, including campaigns against jihadist factions along the Syrian frontier.

Structure and support

The armed forces rely heavily on foreign military assistance, particularly from the United States, France, and other partners, for equipment and training. Conscription was abolished in the 2000s, and the service is now all-volunteer.
