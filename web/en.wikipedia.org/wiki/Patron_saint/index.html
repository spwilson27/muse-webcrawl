Patron saint

A patron saint, patroness saint, patron hallow or heavenly protector is a saint regarded in Catholicism, Anglicanism, Eastern Orthodoxy or Oriental Orthodoxy as the heavenly advocate of a nation, place, craft, activity, class, clan, family, or person.

Concept

Believers ask for the patron saint's intercession and guidance in matters associated with the saint's life or sphere. Patronage may arise from a saint's historical connection to a place, from martyrdom there, from a profession the saint practised, or from popular devotion over centuries.

Examples

Well-known examples include Saint George, patron of England; Saint Andrew, patron of Scotland; Saint Patrick, patron of Ireland; Saint Nicholas, patron of children, sailors and merchants; Saint Barbara, patron of miners and artillerymen; and Saint Joseph, patron of workers and of the universal Church.

History

The practice developed in late antiquity and the early Middle Ages, as churches were built over martyrs' tombs and cities adopted local martyrs as protectors. By the medieval period nearly every trade guild, town and occupation had an acknowledged patron.

In other traditions

Analogous figures appear in other religions, such as patron deities in Hinduism and folk traditions, and protective kami in Shinto, though the theology differs.
