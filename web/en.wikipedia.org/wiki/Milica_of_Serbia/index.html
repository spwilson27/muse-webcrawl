Milica of Serbia

Milica Hrebeljanović (c. 1335 – 11 November 1405), known in tradition as Tsaritsa Milica, was the wife of Prince Lazar of Serbia and, after his death at the Battle of Kosovo in 1389, regent of Serbia during the minority of her son Stefan Lazarević. She is venerated as a saint in the Serbian Orthodox Church.

Life

Milica was a daughter of Prince Vratko Nemanjić, a descendant of the Nemanjić dynasty. She married Lazar around 1353 and bore him several children, including Stefan and daughters who were married into Balkan ruling houses — most famously Olivera, given in marriage to Sultan Bayezid I after Kosovo, a match Milica herself negotiated to secure her son's position.

Regency

After Lazar's death, Milica ruled as regent, balancing Ottoman pressure against ties with Hungary. In 1390 she arranged the translation of Prince Lazar's relics to his foundation, the Ravanica monastery. Once Stefan came of age she withdrew to the Ljubostinja monastery, her own endowment, taking the monastic name Evgenija (later Jevgenija/Jefgenija as a nun), but remained an influential counsellor; in 1398 she interceded with Bayezid, together with the nun Jefimija, to clear Stefan of accusations of treason.

Literary work and cult

Milica is one of the earliest Serbian women writers: her Prayer of a Mother, a poetic supplication, survives. She died at Ljubostinja in 1405 and was buried there; her feast day is celebrated on 19 July (Old Style) in the Serbian Orthodox Church.
