Whitney embedding theorem

In differential topology, the Whitney embedding theorem, proved by Hassler Whitney in 1936, states that a smooth m-dimensional manifold can be smoothly embedded in Euclidean space of dimension 2m. That is, every smooth manifold admits an embedding into R2m that is a homeomorphism onto its image with a smooth structure agreeing with the original.

A manifold embedded in Euclidean space

Statement and refinements

The theorem means abstract manifolds can always be realised as concrete submanifolds of Euclidean space, justifying the study of manifolds intrinsically. Whitney later showed that a smooth m-manifold (m > 1) can be embedded in R2m−1 — the "strong" Whitney embedding theorem — and immersed in R2m−1 as well (the Whitney immersion theorem). The dimension 2m−1 is generally best possible: real projective spaces show that, for example, the real projective plane cannot be embedded in R3.

Proof idea

Whitney's proof begins with an immersion obtained by generic projection and then removes pairs of self-intersection points by a local modification now known as the Whitney trick, which pairs intersection points of opposite sign and cancels them using an embedded disc (the Whitney disc). The trick works smoothly in codimension three and above, and its failure in dimension four is a central source of the exotic phenomena of four-dimensional topology.
