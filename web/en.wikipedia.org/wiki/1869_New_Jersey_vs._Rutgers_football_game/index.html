1869 New Jersey vs. Rutgers football game

The 1869 New Jersey vs. Rutgers football game, played on November 6, 1869, in New Brunswick, New Jersey, is regarded as the first intercollegiate American football game ever played. Rutgers defeated the visiting College of New Jersey (now Princeton University) by a score of 6–4. A rematch a week later in Princeton was won by New Jersey, 8–0.

The game

The match grew out of a rivalry between the neighbouring colleges, sharpened by Princeton's 40-game winning streak over Rutgers in baseball and a disputed cannon incident years earlier. Each side fielded 25 men. The rules, drawn up by the Rutgers captain William J. Leggett, resembled association football (soccer): players could not run with or throw the ball, and goals were scored by kicking it between the opponents' posts, each goal counting one "run". The first team to six goals would win.

Rutgers, led by Leggett and with the large and energetic Stephen Gano Michael in its ranks, won by scoring the final goals after the sides traded scores to 4–4. Contemporary reports praised the spectacle, and the game is commonly called the "birthplace of college football".

Legacy

The site of the game, now occupied by the Rutgers gymnasium in New Brunswick, is commemorated as the birthplace of American college football. The 1969 centennial of college football was celebrated with national retrospectives, and Rutgers and Princeton's 1869 meetings remain the recognised starting point of the sport's intercollegiate history, even though the game bore little resemblance to modern American football.
