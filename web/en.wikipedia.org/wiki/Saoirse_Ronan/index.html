Saoirse Una Ronan (/ˈsɜːrʃə ˈuːnə ˈroʊnən/; born 12 April 1994) is an Irish actress. Primarily known for her work in period dramas since adolescence, she has received numerous accolades, including a Golden Globe Award, and nominations for four Academy Awards and five British Academy Film Awards.

Early life

Ronan was born in the Bronx, New York City, to Irish parents from County Dublin, and was raised in Ireland, in County Carlow. Her father, Paul Ronan, is also an actor. Her first name, Saoirse, is Irish for "freedom".

Career

Ronan's breakthrough came at age 13 as Briony Tallis in Atonement (2007), which earned her an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actress — one of the youngest nominees in that category. She starred in The Lovely Bones (2009), the action film Hanna (2011) and Wes Anderson's The Grand Budapest Hotel (2014). She earned further Oscar nominations for Brooklyn (2015), in which she played an Irish immigrant in 1950s New York; for Greta Gerwig's Lady Bird (2017), which won her the Golden Globe for Best Actress in a Musical or Comedy; and for Gerwig's Little Women (2019), as Jo March. She also played Mary Stuart in Mary Queen of Scots (2018) and appeared in Ammonite (2020) and Bad Apples. On stage she played Lady Macbeth in the Almeida Theatre's The Tragedy of Macbeth (2021) and made her Broadway debut in a revival of The Crucible-era material only in readings; her Broadway debut came in 2025.

Recognition

By the age of 25 Ronan had received four Academy Award nominations, placing her among the youngest performers to achieve that total, and she is frequently cited among the finest actresses of her generation.
