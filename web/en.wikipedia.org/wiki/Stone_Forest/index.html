Stone Forest

The Stone Forest or Shilin (Chinese: 石林) is a notable set of limestone formations about 500 km² in area, located in Shilin Yi Autonomous County, Yunnan Province, China, approximately 90 km (56 mi) from the provincial capital Kunming. The tall rocks appear to grow from the ground like stalagmites, many resembling petrified trees or animals, creating the impression of a forest made of stone.

Geology

The formations are karst landforms developed in Permian limestone and dolomite. Some 270 million years ago the region lay beneath a shallow sea; after the sea retreated, rainwater and groundwater dissolved the rock along fractures, while seismic uplift exposed the sculpted pinnacles, some rising 30 metres or more. Differential erosion of harder and softer layers produced the labyrinth of pillars, arches, pavilions, and ponds found today.

Areas and culture

The scenic area includes the Greater Shilin, Lesser Shilin, Naigu Stone Forest, Zhiyun Cave, and Long Lake. A famous pillar, Ashima, is tied to the legend of a beautiful Sani (Yi) girl of the same name, whose tragic love story is retold in poetry and song. The Sani people celebrate the Torch Festival around the site each year on the 24th day of the sixth lunar month, with wrestling, bullfighting, dancing, and music.

Recognition

In 2007 the Stone Forest was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the South China Karst, recognised for its outstanding karst landscapes and biological diversity. It is classified as a national 5A tourist attraction in China.
