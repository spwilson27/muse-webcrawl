Deutsche Lufthansa AG is the flag carrier of Germany and, with its subsidiaries, one of the largest airlines in Europe. The name derives from Luft ("air") and Hansa (after the Hanseatic League). Its core hubs are Frankfurt and Munich. The wider Lufthansa Group includes Swiss International Air Lines, Austrian Airlines, Brussels Airlines, Eurowings, and a stake in cargo carrier Lufthansa Cargo.

History

The original Deutsche Luft Hansa was founded in 1926 and became the national airline of Nazi Germany before being dissolved in 1945. The present company was founded in Cologne on 6 January 1953 as Aktiengesellschaft für Luftverkehrsbedarf (Luftag) and acquired the Lufthansa name in 1954; scheduled services began on 1 April 1955. East Germany operated its own short-lived Lufthansa before creating Interflug. Lufthansa was state-owned until its full privatisation in the 1990s. In 1997 it co-founded the Star Alliance, the world's first major global airline alliance.

Operations

Lufthansa operates a mixed Airbus and Boeing fleet of wide- and narrow-body aircraft, including the Airbus A380, Boeing 747-8, and Airbus A350. Its loyalty programme, Miles & More, is among Europe's largest. The airline is organised under the Lufthansa Group holding company based in Cologne, with operational headquarters at the Lufthansa Aviation Center at Frankfurt Airport.
