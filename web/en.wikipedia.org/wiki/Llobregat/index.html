Llobregat

The Llobregat is one of the most important rivers of Catalonia, in north-eastern Spain. It rises in the Pyrenees at Castellar de n'Hug in the comarca of Berguedà, at an elevation of about 1,259 metres, and flows roughly 170 kilometres (105 miles) south-eastward before emptying into the Mediterranean Sea just south of Barcelona, near the Barcelona–El Prat Airport.

Course

Flowing through the Pre-Pyrenees and the Catalan pre-coastal depression, the Llobregat passes towns such as Berga, Manresa, Martorell, and Sant Boi de Llobregat. Its principal tributaries include the Cardener and the Anoia, which join it on its lower course. Near the sea the river forms a broad delta plain, much of it occupied by farmland, wetlands protected as a natural park, and airport infrastructure.

Historical significance

To the Romans the river was known as the Rubricatum. In the late Middle Ages and early modern period its valley formed part of the frontier zone often called the Marca Hispanica, and later historians used the Llobregat as a symbolic boundary of "Old Catalonia". In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the river's water power was harnessed for textile mills, and colonies of factory villages (colònies tèxtils) grew up along its banks.

Use and ecology

The Llobregat is a vital source of drinking water for the Barcelona metropolitan area, abstracted at treatment plants in its lower basin. Reservoirs such as La Baells regulate its flow. Parts of the delta remain important for migratory birds and agriculture despite heavy urban and industrial pressure.
