The Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA; English: International Automobile Federation) is an international organisation with two primary functions: it is the governing body of world motor sport, and it is a federation of the world's motoring and mobility organisations. Founded in Paris on 20 June 1904 as the Association Internationale des Automobile Clubs Reconnus, it assumed its present name in 1946.

Motor sport

The FIA sanctions the world's foremost racing championships, including the Formula One World Championship, the World Rally Championship, the World Endurance Championship, Formula E and the World Rallycross Championship. Through its World Motor Sport Council it writes the technical and sporting regulations for these series, homologates circuits and vehicles, licenses drivers (including the Formula One super licence) and promotes safety — a role transformed after the deaths of Ayrton Senna and Roland Ratzenberger in 1994, leading to major advances in crash structures, circuit design and devices such as the halo.

Mobility and road safety

Through its member automobile clubs, the FIA represents motorists in areas such as touring, consumer protection and road safety. Its FIA Foundation supports global road-safety campaigns, and the federation works with the United Nations on reducing road deaths and on sustainable mobility policy.

Governance

The FIA is governed by a president elected by its General Assembly; notable past presidents include Jean-Marie Balestre and Max Mosley, who served from 1993 to 2009, followed by Jean Todt (2009–2021) and Mohammed Ben Sulayem. Its headquarters are at 8 Place de la Concorde in Paris, with offices in Geneva and Valleiry.
