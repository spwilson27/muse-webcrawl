Second Epistle to the Corinthians

The Second Epistle to the Corinthians, commonly referred to as Second Corinthians (or 2 Corinthians), is a book of the New Testament attributed to Paul the Apostle, written to the Christian community in Corinth. It is one of the most personal of Paul's letters, revealing his emotions, his defence of his apostolic ministry, and his strained relationship with the Corinthian church.

Composition

Paul wrote the letter from Macedonia around AD 55–57, after an earlier painful visit and a severe letter. Many scholars argue that the canonical text combines two or more letters: chapters 1–9 are generally reconciliatory in tone, while chapters 10–13 are sharply polemical against rival teachers Paul calls "super-apostles".

Contents

The epistle discusses comfort in affliction, the ministry of reconciliation, generous giving for the collection for the Jerusalem church, and a defence of Paul's apostleship, including the catalogue of his hardships and the account of his "thorn in the flesh". The letter closes with exhortations and a Trinitarian benediction: "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Spirit be with all of you."
