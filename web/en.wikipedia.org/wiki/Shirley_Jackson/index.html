Shirley Jackson

Shirley Hardie Jackson (December 14, 1916 – August 8, 1965) was an American writer known primarily for her works of horror and mystery. Over her career she wrote six novels, two memoirs, and more than 200 short stories. She first gained wide notice for the short story "The Lottery," published in The New Yorker in 1948, which describes a small town that observes a ritualistic annual stoning.

Life

Born in San Francisco and raised in Burlingame, California, Jackson attended Syracuse University, where she met her husband, the literary critic Stanley Edgar Hyman. The couple settled in North Bennington, Vermont, where Hyman taught at Bennington College. Jackson wrote prolifically while raising four children, producing domestic humour columns alongside her darker fiction. Plagued by ill health, she died of heart failure in 1965 at the age of 48.

Works

Her novels include The Road Through the Wall (1948), Hangsaman (1951), The Bird's Nest (1954), The Sundial (1958), The Haunting of Hill House (1959) — widely considered one of the finest haunted-house novels ever written — and We Have Always Lived in the Castle (1962). Her memoirs Life Among the Savages and Raising Demons collect humorous sketches of family life.

Legacy

Jackson's reputation has grown steadily since her death; Stephen King and many other writers cite her as a major influence. The Shirley Jackson Awards, established in 2007, honour outstanding achievement in psychological suspense, horror, and the dark fantastic. Ruth Franklin's biography Shirley Jackson: A Rather Haunted Life (2016) won the National Book Critics Circle Award.
