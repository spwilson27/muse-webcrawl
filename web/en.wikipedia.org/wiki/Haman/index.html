Haman (Hebrew: הָמָן, Hāmān) is the main antagonist in the Book of Esther in the Hebrew Bible. A high official of the Persian king Ahasuerus and described as an Agagite — a descendant of Agag, king of the Amalekites — Haman plots to exterminate all the Jews of the Persian Empire, but his scheme is thwarted by Queen Esther and Mordecai.

In the Book of Esther

Promoted above all other officials, Haman demands that courtiers bow to him. Mordecai, a Jew, refuses, and in his fury Haman casts lots (purim) to choose a day for the destruction of Mordecai's entire people. The king seals the genocidal decree without knowing its target. Haman also builds a gallows for Mordecai. At a banquet, Esther reveals her Jewish identity and exposes the plot; the enraged king orders Haman hanged on the very gallows he had prepared. The festival of Purim commemorates the deliverance.

Traditions

In Jewish tradition Haman became the archetypal persecutor. During Purim readings of the Book of Esther, congregants drown out his name with noisemakers (graggers), and triangular pastries called hamantaschen ("Haman's pockets") are eaten. In the Quran, a figure named Haman appears as a minister of Pharaoh, a separate character in a different setting.
