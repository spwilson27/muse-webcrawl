University of Oslo

The University of Oslo (Norwegian: Universitetet i Oslo, UiO) is a public research university in Oslo, Norway. Founded in 1811 as the Royal Frederick University, it is the oldest university in Norway and, with about 28,000 students, the country's largest. Until 1946 it was Norway's only university.

History

The university was established in 1811, modelled on the University of Copenhagen and the new University of Berlin, and named for King Frederick VI of Denmark. Its neoclassical central buildings on Karl Johans gate, completed in the 1850s, hosted the Nobel Peace Prize ceremony in the Aula from 1947 to 1989. The main campus is now at Blindern, northwest of the city centre.

Faculties and research

UiO has eight faculties, including medicine, law, humanities, mathematics and natural sciences, dentistry, social sciences, education, and theology. Five Nobel laureates have been affiliated with the university, among them Fridtjof Nansen, Ragnar Frisch, and Trygve Haavelmo. The university also runs notable museums, including the Museum of Cultural History with its Viking ship collection (now being rehoused at the Museum of the Viking Age) and the Natural History Museum.
