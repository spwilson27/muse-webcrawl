Empire Service

The BBC Empire Service was a shortwave radio service launched by the British Broadcasting Corporation on 19 December 1932, broadcasting from Daventry in England to listeners throughout the British Empire. It was opened with a Christmas message from King George V in 1932, written by Rudyard Kipling — the first royal Christmas broadcast. The Empire Service is the forerunner of today's BBC World Service.

History

The service was championed by John Reith, the BBC's first Director-General, who saw shortwave broadcasting as a means of binding the empire together. Early programmes, transmitted in English, were aimed largely at expatriate Britons in the Dominions and colonies, and reception was often difficult because of the limitations of 1930s shortwave technology.

Foreign-language broadcasts began with Arabic in 1938, followed by European languages as war approached. In 1939 the service was reorganised, and in 1965 the General Overseas Service was renamed the BBC World Service.
