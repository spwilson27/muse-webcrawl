International Civil Rights Center and Museum

The International Civil Rights Center and Museum (ICRCM) is a museum and educational center in Greensboro, North Carolina, United States. It is located in the former F. W. Woolworth building where, on 1 February 1960, four African American students — the Greensboro Four — staged a sit-in at the whites-only lunch counter, igniting a nationwide movement.

Exhibits

The centerpiece of the museum is the original lunch counter and stools, preserved in place. Galleries trace the history of segregation, the sit-in movement, and the broader civil rights struggle, including exhibits on Jim Crow laws, voter registration drives, and nonviolent protest training.

History

The Woolworth store closed in 1993. Sit-In Movement, Inc., founded by Melvin "Skip" Alston and Earl Jones, purchased the building and, after years of fundraising, opened the museum on 1 February 2010, the 50th anniversary of the sit-ins.

Legacy

The museum serves as a teaching institution on civil and human rights, hosting school groups, lectures, and commemorative events each February.
