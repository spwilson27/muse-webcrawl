The Nelson Mandela Rules are the United Nations Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners, adopted by the UN General Assembly on 17 December 2015. The revised rules — 122 in total — update the original Standard Minimum Rules of 1955 and are named in honour of the late South African President Nelson Mandela, who spent 27 years in prison under the apartheid regime, to recognise the legacy of his struggle for democracy, human rights and the humane treatment of prisoners.

History

The original Standard Minimum Rules were adopted by the First United Nations Congress on the Prevention of Crime and the Treatment of Offenders in Geneva in 1955 and approved by the Economic and Social Council in 1957. After six decades, an intergovernmental expert group process, hosted in part by South Africa in Cape Town in March 2015, produced a comprehensive revision modernising the rules in nine thematic areas, including healthcare, investigations of deaths in custody, discipline and the use of solitary confinement. The General Assembly adopted the revision on 17 December 2015, recommending that they be known as the "Nelson Mandela Rules".

Key provisions

Rule 1 states that all prisoners shall be treated with respect due to their inherent dignity and value as human beings, and prohibits torture and other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment. The Rules set standards for prisoners' accommodation, hygiene, food, exercise and contact with the outside world; require that prisoners receive healthcare equivalent to that available in the community, free of charge; and require independent investigation of deaths and serious injuries in custody.

A landmark innovation concerns solitary confinement: the Rules define it as confinement for 22 hours or more a day without meaningful human contact, and "prolonged" solitary confinement as exceeding 15 consecutive days. Prolonged solitary confinement is prohibited, as is solitary confinement for pregnant women, mothers with infants and prisoners with mental or physical disabilities when their conditions would be exacerbated. The Rules also restrict disciplinary sanctions, searches and the use of instruments of restraint.

Status

The Nelson Mandela Rules are a non-binding "soft law" instrument, but they are the universally acknowledged minimum standard for prison management and are widely used by courts, inspectorates and prison administrations as a benchmark. UNODC and penal-reform organisations promote their implementation worldwide; Nelson Mandela International Day (18 July) is used to raise awareness of the Rules.
