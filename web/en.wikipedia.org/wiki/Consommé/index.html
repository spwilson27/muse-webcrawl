Consommé

A consommé is a type of clear soup made from richly flavoured stock that has been clarified, a process that uses egg whites to remove fat and sediment. The result is a transparent liquid of amber or golden colour with an intense, concentrated flavour. Consommé is a hallmark of classical French cuisine and is traditionally served as a starter, plain or with garnishes.

Preparation

To clarify stock, beaten egg whites are mixed with ground meat, mirepoix (finely chopped onion, carrot and celery), tomatoes or other acid, and cold stock. As the mixture is slowly heated and gently stirred, the egg proteins coagulate into a floating "raft" that traps impurities. The liquid is then carefully ladled through the raft or through cloth, leaving a crystal-clear soup. The process is slow and demands care; disturbing the raft clouds the consommé and forces the process to start again.

Variations

Consommés are named for their garnishes and flavourings. Consommé julienne is served with finely shredded vegetables; consommé madrilène is flavoured with tomato and served chilled; a double consommé is made with twice the usual proportion of meat for an especially strong flavour, while a petite marmite is served in a small earthenware pot with the meat and vegetables retained. When well chilled, a rich consommé sets into a savoury jelly and may be served cold in summer.

History

Clarified broths were refined in the great kitchens of French haute cuisine during the 19th century, notably in the era of Antonin Carême and Auguste Escoffier, who codified consommé as one of the fundamental soups (potages clairs) of the classical repertoire.
