Ocean Parkway (Brooklyn)

Ocean Parkway is a broad boulevard in the borough of Brooklyn in New York City. Running roughly north–south for about five and a half miles from Prospect Park to the Atlantic Ocean shore at Brighton Beach, it was designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux as part of their vision of landscaped parkways linking parks to residential districts.

Design

The parkway consists of a central roadway flanked by service roads, broad tree-lined malls, and pedestrian paths. Its bicycle path, completed in 1894, is often cited as the first bicycle path in the United States. The central mall planted with rows of trees follows the model Olmsted and Vaux developed for Eastern Parkway, built shortly before.

History

Construction began in 1874 and the road opened in stages through 1876, spurring development of the surrounding neighbourhoods of Kensington, Midwood, and Gravesend as it passed through what was then largely farmland. The southern end connects to the Coney Island area and the Shore Parkway system. Ocean Parkway was designated a New York City scenic landmark in 1975.

Route

The parkway begins at the southern entrance of Prospect Park (Park Circle), passes Church Avenue, Foster Avenue, Avenue J, Kings Highway, and Avenue U, and terminates near Brighton Beach at Sea Breeze Avenue.
