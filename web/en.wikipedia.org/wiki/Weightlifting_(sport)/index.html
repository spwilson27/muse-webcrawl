Weightlifting, officially Olympic weightlifting, is a strength sport in which athletes compete to lift the heaviest possible barbell loaded with weight plates in two standardised lifts: the snatch and the clean and jerk. Each lifter receives three attempts in each lift, and the combined total of the best successful attempt in each determines the overall result.

The lifts

The snatch is a single-motion lift in which the barbell is pulled from the floor to a locked-arms position overhead in one continuous movement, caught in a deep squat. The clean and jerk is a two-part lift: the bar is first pulled to the shoulders (the clean), and then driven overhead with a split or squat under the bar (the jerk). A third lift, the clean and press, was removed from competition after the 1972 Olympics.

Competition

Athletes compete in bodyweight categories. The sport is governed internationally by the International Weightlifting Federation (IWF), founded in 1905. Weightlifting featured at the first modern Olympic Games in 1896 and has been a permanent fixture since 1920; women's events were added at Sydney 2000.

Technique and training

Success in the sport depends as much on technique, speed and mobility as on raw strength: lifters must pull the bar fast enough to dive beneath it and receive it in a stable squat. Training typically combines the competition lifts and variations with squats, pulls and presses.
