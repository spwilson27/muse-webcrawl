Paxos is a family of protocols for solving consensus in a network of unreliable or fallible processors. Consensus is the process of agreeing on one result among a group of participants — a fundamental problem in distributed computing, since the participants or the network connecting them may fail. Paxos algorithms provide safety (never returning a wrong result) under a wide range of conditions, while progress is guaranteed only when a majority of processors are functioning and the network is sufficiently stable.

History

The protocol derives from work by Leslie Lamport, who described it in a 1989 technical report framed as a parable about a fictional parliament on the Greek island of Paxos. Because reviewers found the story confusing, the paper was not published until 1998, as "The Part-Time Parliament." A plainer restatement, "Paxos Made Simple" (2001), followed. Paxos became the reference standard for fault-tolerant replication, underpinning systems such as Google's Chubby lock service and Spanner, and influencing later protocols such as Raft.

Protocol overview

In basic Paxos, processes act in three roles: proposers, which advocate values; acceptors, which vote on them; and learners, which learn the chosen value. Consensus proceeds in two phases. In the prepare phase, a proposer selects a proposal number n and asks acceptors to promise not to accept proposals numbered less than n; each acceptor replies with its promise and any value it has already accepted at the highest number below n. In the accept phase, if a majority of acceptors promised, the proposer asks them to accept a value — its own, unless an acceptor reported a previously accepted value, in which case it must adopt the most recently reported one. A value is chosen when a majority of acceptors accept it. This rule guarantees that all correct learners eventually agree on a single value.

Variants and implementations

Multi-Paxos amortises the prepare phase across many consensus instances under a stable leader, forming the basis of practical state machine replication. Other variants include Cheap Paxos, Fast Paxos, and Flexible Paxos. Because Paxos is notoriously difficult to implement correctly, simpler-to-understand alternatives such as Raft have also achieved wide adoption; comparative surveys have found many published Paxos implementations to be subtly incorrect.
