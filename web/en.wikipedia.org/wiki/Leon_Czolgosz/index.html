Leon Frank Czolgosz (May 5, 1873 – October 29, 1901) was an American wireworker and anarchist who assassinated United States president William McKinley in Buffalo, New York, in September 1901.

Background

Born in Michigan to Polish immigrant parents, Czolgosz worked in factories in Ohio and became disillusioned with industrial capitalism after witnessing labour conflict and losing his job in the depression of the 1890s. He drifted toward anarchism, attending a lecture by Emma Goldman in 1901, though anarchist circles, distrusting him as a possible police spy, largely shunned him. He was inspired in part by Gaetano Bresci's assassination of King Umberto I of Italy in 1900.

Assassination and execution

On September 6, 1901, at the Pan-American Exposition in Buffalo, Czolgosz approached McKinley in a receiving line at the Temple of Music with a .32-calibre revolver concealed in a handkerchief and fired twice. McKinley died of gangrene from his wounds on September 14. Czolgosz was tried, convicted in a proceeding lasting barely more than a day of testimony, and electrocuted at Auburn Prison on October 29, 1901. His last words are reported as "I killed the President because he was the enemy of the good people — the good working people."
