2018 Hawaii earthquake

On 4 May 2018, a magnitude 6.9 earthquake struck the island of Hawaii (the "Big Island"), the largest quake to hit the state since 1975. The shock occurred on the south flank of Kīlauea volcano during its major 2018 lower Puna eruption, one day after new volcanic fissures had begun opening in the Leilani Estates subdivision.

Setting and sequence

The earthquake was preceded by a magnitude 5.4 foreshock about an hour earlier and followed weeks of elevated seismicity as magma migrated from Kīlauea's summit into the volcano's Lower East Rift Zone. The M6.9 event, generated by slip on the décollement beneath the volcano's mobile south flank, was felt throughout the Hawaiian Islands and caused power outages, road cracking and minor structural damage on the Big Island. No tsunami was generated.

Relationship to the eruption

The quake coincided with the early phase of the 2018 eruption, which destroyed more than 700 homes in Puna, displaced thousands of residents, drained the summit lava lake and triggered a series of summit collapse explosions through the summer. Scientists interpreted the flank earthquake as part of the volcano's adjustment to the withdrawal of magma feeding the rift eruption. The eruption was declared over in late 2018 after roughly three months of activity.
