Chemical element

A chemical element is a chemical substance that cannot be broken down into other substances by chemical reactions: it consists of atoms with the same number of protons in their nuclei (atomic number). As of now, 118 elements are known, from hydrogen (1) to oganesson (118), organised in the periodic table by Dmitri Mendeleev (1869) and others; 94 occur naturally on Earth, the rest being synthetic.

Elements are the building blocks of all ordinary matter: their atoms combine into compounds and mixtures; their isotopes vary in neutron count. The modern definition replaced ancient notions (the classical four elements, alchemical principles) through the work of Boyle, Lavoisier (first modern list, 1789, 33 'elements'), Dalton's atomic theory and Mendeleev's predictive table. Naming follows IUPAC, drawing on places, people, properties and mythology.
