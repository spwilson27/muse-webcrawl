Makiko Tanaka

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Makiko Tanaka (田中 眞紀子, Tanaka Makiko, born 14 January 1944) is a Japanese politician, the daughter of former Prime Minister Kakuei Tanaka. Elected to the House of Representatives in 1993, she became one of Japan's most popular politicians for her blunt speaking style, and played a key role in Junichiro Koizumi's rise to the premiership.

Foreign minister

Appointed Minister for Foreign Affairs in April 2001, Tanaka was Japan's first female foreign minister. Her tenure was turbulent: she clashed repeatedly with foreign ministry bureaucrats, whom she accused of mismanagement and corruption, and her public feuds undermined the cabinet. Koizumi dismissed her in January 2002 after a dispute over the attendance of NGOs at an Afghanistan reconstruction conference.

Later career

Tanaka left the Liberal Democratic Party in 2009 and joined the Democratic Party of Japan, serving briefly as Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology in 2012 under Yoshihiko Noda. She lost her seat in the general election of December 2012. Her memoir-style writings about her father and Japanese politics have been bestsellers in Japan.
