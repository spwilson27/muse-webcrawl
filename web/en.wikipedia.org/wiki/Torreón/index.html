Torreón (Spanish pronunciation: [toreˈon]) is a city in the Mexican state of Coahuila, the centre of the Comarca Lagunera metropolitan area, which also includes Gómez Palacio and Lerdo in neighbouring Durango. It is one of Mexico's youngest major cities, having grown up around a railway junction in the 1890s, and takes its name — "big tower" — from a watchtower built to oversee flooding of the Nazas River.

History

The city grew explosively after the Mexican Central Railway arrived in 1883 and made the site a major crossing; irrigated cotton farming in the Laguna region turned Torreón into a boomtown of banks, ginning plants, and industry. During the Mexican Revolution the city was a strategic prize, changing hands several times; Pancho Villa's forces captured it in the Battle of Torreón of 1913–14. The city is also remembered for the Torreón massacre of May 1911, in which revolutionary troops killed over 300 members of the city's Chinese community, an atrocity for which Mexico formally apologised in 2021.

Landmarks and economy

Above the city stands the Cristo de las Noas, one of the largest statues of Christ in Latin America (about 22 metres tall), reached by cable car. Torreón's economy includes metallurgy and smelting (the Met-Mex Peñoles complex is among the world's major silver refiners), dairy and food processing for the Lala group, textiles, and automotive parts. The city is home to the football club Santos Laguna, based at the Estadio Corona (TSM), and to the Canal de la Perla, a rediscovered nineteenth-century irrigation channel now used as an underground walkway and exhibition space.
