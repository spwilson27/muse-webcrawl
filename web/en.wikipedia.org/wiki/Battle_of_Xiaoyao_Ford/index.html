Battle of Xiaoyao Ford

The Battle of Xiaoyao Ford, also known as the Battle of Hefei, was fought in AD 215 between the warlords Cao Cao and Sun Quan in the late Eastern Han dynasty of China, during the campaigns for the fortress city of Hefei. It is famous for the Wei general Zhang Liao's defence of the city with a small garrison against Sun Quan's much larger army.

Background

While Cao Cao campaigned against Zhang Lu in Hanzhong, Sun Quan personally led a large army — traditionally given as 100,000 men — against Hefei, which was held by only about 7,000 troops under Zhang Liao, Li Dian and Yue Jin. Before leaving, Cao Cao had left a sealed letter instructing the generals on how to respond to such an attack.

The battle

Following Cao Cao's instructions, Zhang Liao and Li Dian — setting aside a personal feud — led 800 picked men in a dawn raid on Sun Quan's camp at Xiaoyao Ford. Zhang Liao cut his way through the Wu lines almost to Sun Quan's command tent before Wu troops rallied; he then fought his way out, turning back to rescue his remaining men when he heard their cries. The raid shattered Wu morale. Disease then spread through Sun Quan's army, and after a siege of more than ten days he withdrew. During the retreat Zhang Liao attacked again at the ford, and Sun Quan barely escaped by leaping his horse over a broken bridge.

Legacy

The victory made Zhang Liao's name a byword for valour; a later Wu saying held that crying children in Jiangdong would stop when told "Zhang Liao is coming". The battle is a celebrated episode of the Records of the Three Kingdoms and the novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, and remains a standard example of a small force defeating a larger one through audacity.
