RMS Queen Mary 2

RMS Queen Mary 2 (QM2) is a British transatlantic ocean liner operated by Cunard Line. She was built in 2003 by Chantiers de l'Atlantique in Saint-Nazaire, France, and named by Queen Elizabeth II in January 2004. At the time of her construction she was the longest, widest, and tallest passenger ship ever built, and remains the largest dedicated ocean liner — as opposed to cruise ship — in service.

Design

Queen Mary 2 is 345 metres (1,132 ft) long, with a gross tonnage of about 149,000 GT and capacity for roughly 2,700 passengers and 1,250 crew. Unlike most modern cruise ships she was designed for regular scheduled Atlantic crossings between Southampton and New York, with a reinforced hull and a service speed of up to 30 knots. Propulsion is by diesel-electric pods (mermaid azimuthing pods) supplemented by gas turbines. Her design deliberately echoes the golden age of ocean liners, with a long bow, raked funnel in Cunard red and black, and traditional class-influenced dining arrangements.

Service

QM2 took over the transatlantic flagship role from Queen Elizabeth 2 in 2004 and also performs cruises and annual world voyages. She carries the Royal Mail Ship (RMS) prefix by arrangement with Royal Mail. In 2016 she underwent a major "remastering" refit that added staterooms and refreshed public spaces. She remains the only ship offering regularly scheduled transatlantic liner crossings.
