Henryk Sienkiewicz

Henryk Adam Aleksander Pius Sienkiewicz (5 May 1846 – 15 November 1916) was a Polish novelist and journalist, one of the most popular Polish writers of the late 19th and early 20th centuries. He received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1905 "because of his outstanding merits as an epic writer".

Works

Sienkiewicz is best known internationally for Quo Vadis (1895–96), a novel of Christians in Nero's Rome, translated into dozens of languages and repeatedly filmed. In Poland he is revered above all for the Trilogy — With Fire and Sword (1884), The Deluge (1886), and Fire in the Steppe (1888) — sweeping novels of 17th-century Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth wars, written, in his words, "to lift up hearts" during the partitions of Poland. His novel The Teutonic Knights (1900) recounts the struggle against the German Order culminating in the Battle of Grunwald.

Life

He travelled widely, reporting from the United States in the 1870s, and later campaigned for Polish relief during the First World War, co-founding a relief committee in Switzerland with Ignacy Jan Paderewski. He died in Vevey in 1916; his remains were returned to Warsaw in 1924.
