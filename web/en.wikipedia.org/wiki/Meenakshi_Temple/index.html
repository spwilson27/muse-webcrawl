Meenakshi Temple (also called Meenakshi Sundareswarar Temple or Meenakshi Amman Temple) is a historic Hindu temple located on the southern bank of the Vaigai River in the temple city of Madurai, Tamil Nadu, India. It is dedicated to the goddess Meenakshi, a form of Parvati, and her consort Sundareswarar, a form of Shiva.

History

Tradition attributes the original temple to the Pandya kings of Madurai, with the shrine mentioned in Tamil literature of the early centuries CE. The earlier structure was largely destroyed in the 14th century during the invasions of Malik Kafur's forces, and the present complex was rebuilt mainly in the 16th and 17th centuries under the Nayak rulers of Madurai, especially Tirumala Nayaka.

Architecture

The complex is a masterpiece of Dravidian architecture, enclosed by high walls with fourteen gopurams — monumental gateway towers covered in thousands of brightly painted stucco figures of gods, demons, and animals. The tallest, the southern tower, rises about 52 metres. Inside are the twin sanctums, the Hall of a Thousand Pillars with its sculpted columns, and the sacred temple tank, the Potramarai Kulam (Golden Lotus Tank). The temple draws an estimated 15,000 visitors a day, and its annual Meenakshi Tirukalyanam (celestial wedding) festival attracts around a million devotees.
