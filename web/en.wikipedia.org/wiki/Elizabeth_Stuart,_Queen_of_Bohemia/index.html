Elizabeth Stuart (19 August 1596 – 13 February 1662) was Electress of the Palatinate and briefly Queen of Bohemia as the wife of Frederick V. Because her husband's reign in Bohemia lasted only one winter (1619–1620), she is remembered in English and Czech histories as the Winter Queen. She was the second child and eldest daughter of James VI and I, King of Scotland, England and Ireland, and his wife Anne of Denmark.

Through her daughter Sophia, Electress of Hanover, Elizabeth became an ancestress of the British monarchs from George I onward: the Act of Settlement 1701 settled the English crown on Sophia and her Protestant descendants.

Early life

Elizabeth was born at Dunfermline Palace in Fife and named after Elizabeth I of England. After her father succeeded to the English throne in 1603, she was brought up in England, partly at Coombe Abbey in Warwickshire under the care of Lord and Lady Harington. During the Gunpowder Plot of 1605, the conspirators planned to seize the nine-year-old princess and proclaim her queen as a Catholic puppet monarch; the plot failed before she could be taken.

Marriage

On 14 February 1613 Elizabeth married Frederick V, Elector Palatine, at the Chapel Royal in Whitehall. The marriage, part of James's policy of balancing Protestant and Catholic alliances, was celebrated with elaborate festivities. The couple travelled to Heidelberg, where Elizabeth's lively court became a centre of Protestant culture in the Empire.

Queen of Bohemia

In 1619 the Bohemian estates, in revolt against the Habsburg emperor, offered their crown to Frederick. Against the advice of James I, Frederick accepted, and he and Elizabeth were crowned in Prague in November 1619. Their rule collapsed after the Battle of White Mountain on 8 November 1620, and the family fled into exile in the Dutch Republic — giving Elizabeth her lasting nickname, the "Winter Queen".

Exile and later life

The couple settled in The Hague, supported by Dutch and English subsidies. Frederick died in 1632, leaving Elizabeth a widow with a large family; she worked tirelessly through correspondence to advance the restoration of her son Charles Louis to the Palatinate, achieved in part by the Peace of Westphalia in 1648. She returned to England in 1661 after the Restoration of her nephew Charles II and died in London on 13 February 1662. She was buried in Westminster Abbey.
