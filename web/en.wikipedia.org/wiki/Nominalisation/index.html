Nominalisation

In linguistics, nominalisation (or nominalization) is the use of a word that is not a noun (such as a verb or adjective) as a noun, or the creation of a noun from such a word. Examples include "decision" from decide, "refusal" from refuse, and "goodness" from good.

Morphology

English forms nominalisations with suffixes such as -tion, -sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -al and -ing, and by conversion (zero derivation), as in "a run" from to run. Other languages use productive affixes or classifiers for the same purpose.

Functions and style

Nominalisation packs processes into things, enabling abstraction and compact academic or bureaucratic prose ("the implementation of the policy"). Style guides often warn that heavy nominalisation can obscure agency and make writing harder to read, recommending verbs where clarity matters.
