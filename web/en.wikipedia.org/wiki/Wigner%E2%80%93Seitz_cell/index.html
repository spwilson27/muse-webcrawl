Wigner–Seitz cell

Wigner–Seitz cell
Named after: Eugene Wigner and Frederick Seitz
Field: Solid-state physics; crystallography

The Wigner–Seitz cell is a type of primitive cell used in the study of crystals, defined as the region of space closer to a given lattice point than to any other. It is constructed by drawing lines from a lattice point to its neighbours and taking the smallest volume enclosed by the perpendicular bisectors of those lines.

Properties

The cell contains exactly one lattice point and tiles all space by lattice translations. It is the Voronoi cell of the lattice. In reciprocal space, the Wigner–Seitz cell of the reciprocal lattice is the first Brillouin zone, central to band theory. Wigner and Seitz introduced the construction in 1933 in work on the electronic structure of sodium metal, where the cell is approximated by a sphere (the Wigner–Seitz radius, rs). See also Brillouin zone.
