Red River Rivalry

The Red River Rivalry (historically the Red River Shootout) is an American college football rivalry game played annually between the Oklahoma Sooners and the Texas Longhorns. Since 1932 the game has been played at the Cotton Bowl stadium in Dallas, Texas, during the State Fair of Texas, usually in mid-October.

History

The teams first met in 1900, and the rivalry takes its name from the Red River, which forms much of the border between Oklahoma and Texas. The Cotton Bowl is famously split along the 50-yard line, with Texas fans on one side and Oklahoma fans on the other. The winner receives the Golden Hat trophy, a gold ten-gallon hat mounted on a wooden block, awarded since 1941, along with other trophies.

Significance

The game has frequently carried conference and national championship implications, and many meetings have featured both teams ranked in the top 25. Both programs moved from the Big 12 Conference to the Southeastern Conference in 2024, preserving the annual meeting. The rivalry is considered one of the most famous in American college football.
