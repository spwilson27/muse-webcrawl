Nakshatra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nakshatra (Sanskrit: नक्षत्रम्, romanized: Nakṣatram) is the term for lunar mansion in Hindu astrology and Indian astronomy. A nakshatra is one of 27 (sometimes also 28) sectors along the ecliptic. Their names, related to prominent star clusters in the respective sectors, are prominent in Hindu astrology and in the Indian calendar.

The starting point for the nakshatras is traditionally the point on the ecliptic directly opposite to the star Spica called Chitrā. The nakshatras are used to define the Moon's position in a native's birth chart; each nakshatra is subdivided into four quarters (pada) of 3°20′ each, and the position of the Moon determines the sequence of planetary periods (dashas) in the Vimshottari system of Vedic astrology.

List of nakshatras

The classical list of 27 nakshatras, beginning with Ashvini, is:

  1. Ashvini
  2. Bharani
  3. Krittika
  4. Rohini
  5. Mrigashira
  6. Ardra
  7. Punarvasu
  8. Pushya
  9. Ashlesha
  10. Magha
  11. Purva Phalguni
  12. Uttara Phalguni
  13. Hasta
  14. Chitra
  15. Swati
  16. Vishakha
  17. Anuradha
  18. Jyeshtha
  19. Mula
  20. Purva Ashadha
  21. Uttara Ashadha
  22. Shravana
  23. Dhanishta
  24. Shatabhisha
  25. Purva Bhadrapada
  26. Uttara Bhadrapada
  27. Revati

An occasional 28th nakshatra, Abhijit (the star Vega), is inserted between Uttara Ashadha and Shravana in some traditions and used for particular astrological purposes.

In the Vedas and mythology

A complete list of nakshatras appears already in the Atharvaveda and the Taittiriya Brahmana. In mythology, the nakshatras are personified as the 27 daughters of the prajapati Daksha, all of whom were married to Chandra, the Moon god; his favouritism toward Rohini led Daksha to curse him with wasting (kshaya), a myth explaining the waning of the Moon, mitigated by Shiva.

Usage

Nakshatras play a central role in muhurta (electional astrology), in matching horoscopes for marriage (with the Ashtakoota guna milan system based on the Moon's nakshatra), and in naming infants, whose names traditionally begin with the syllable corresponding to the pada of the birth nakshatra.
