Sydney Brenner

Sydney Brenner (1927–2019)
Born: Germiston, South Africa
Known for: C. elegans model organism, mRNA
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (2002)

Sydney Brenner (13 January 1927 – 5 April 2019) was a South African biologist who shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with H. Robert Horvitz and John Sulston for discoveries on genetic regulation of organ development and programmed cell death.

Career

Educated at the University of the Witwatersrand and Oxford, Brenner worked at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge with Francis Crick; together they helped establish the triplet genetic code and, with François Jacob and Matthew Meselson, the existence of messenger RNA.

C. elegans

In the 1960s Brenner established the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans as a model organism, enabling tracing of every cell lineage and founding a field that led to the Nobel-winning work on apoptosis. He later helped found institutes in Singapore and elsewhere and promoted genomics, including work on the pufferfish genome.
