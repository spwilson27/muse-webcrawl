Habitat for Humanity International, generally referred to as Habitat for Humanity or simply Habitat, is a global non-profit housing organisation. Founded in 1976 by Millard and Linda Fuller in Americus, Georgia, it works in local communities across all 50 U.S. states and in approximately 70 countries, building and improving homes in partnership with people in need of decent, affordable housing.

Model

Habitat's model is often summarised as "a hand up, not a handout". Partner families are selected on the basis of need, willingness to partner, and ability to repay; they contribute "sweat equity" by helping to build their own and others' homes, and then purchase the house with an affordable, often no-interest, mortgage. Payments are recycled into a local "Fund for Humanity" that finances further construction. Homes are built largely by volunteers using donated money and, frequently, donated materials.

Habitat operates through independent local affiliates, and also runs ReStore shops selling donated furniture, appliances and building materials to fund its work. It undertakes disaster-response building and neighbourhood revitalisation as well as new construction.

History and reach

The Fullers developed the concept of "partnership housing" at Koinonia Farm, a Christian intentional community near Americus founded by Clarence Jordan, and tested it in Zaire (now the Democratic Republic of the Congo) in the early 1970s before incorporating Habitat in 1976. Former U.S. President Jimmy Carter and Rosalynn Carter became its best-known volunteers from 1984, leading annual Carter Work Projects that drew worldwide attention. By the 2020s Habitat reported having helped more than 59 million people improve their housing since its founding.
