Blogger (service)

Blogger is an American online content management system founded in 1999 that enables its users to write blogs with time-stamped entries. Developed by Pyra Labs, it was bought by Google in 2003 and was one of the earliest dedicated blog-publishing tools, helping to popularise the format. Blogs are hosted by Google at a subdomain of blogspot.com, or on a user's own domain.

History

Pyra Labs launched Blogger in August 1999. It grew through the dot-com boom largely by word of mouth, offering free blog publishing at a time when most tools required users to hand-code pages and upload them by FTP. After Pyra's funds ran low, Google acquired the company in February 2003. A major redesign, "Blogger in beta", followed in 2006 with integration into Google accounts, and the service migrated fully to Google servers by 2007.

Features

Blogger provides a WYSIWYG editor, customisable templates editable in HTML and CSS, gadgets/widgets, multi-author blogs, comment moderation, and integration with Google services including AdSense for monetisation. Blogs can be made public or restricted to invited readers. Country-specific Blogspot domains were introduced in 2012 to allow content removal under local law without global takedowns.

Blocking and censorship

Blogger has been periodically blocked in several countries, including Pakistan, China, Turkey, India and Iran, because of content hosted on individual blogs. It remains one of the world's most visited blog platforms, though newer services and social media have reduced its relative prominence since the 2000s.
