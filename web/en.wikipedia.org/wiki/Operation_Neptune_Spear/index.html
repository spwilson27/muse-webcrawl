Operation Neptune Spear was the code name for the United States special operations mission that killed Osama bin Laden, founder of al-Qaeda, on 2 May 2011. A team of United States Navy SEALs from the Naval Special Warfare Development Group (DEVGRU, popularly "SEAL Team Six") raided bin Laden's compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan, in an operation directed by the Central Intelligence Agency. President Barack Obama announced the death of bin Laden in a televised address on the night of 1 May 2011 (Washington time).

Background

Intelligence work over several years identified a courier, Abu Ahmed al-Kuwaiti, whose trail led analysts to a large, unusually secure compound in Abbottabad, a garrison town north of Islamabad. Surveillance increased the CIA's confidence that a high-value target — dubbed "the Pacer" — was resident there, though there was no photographic confirmation of bin Laden himself. After months of deliberation, Obama authorised the raid on 29 April 2011.

The raid

Two dozen SEALs flew from Afghanistan in stealth-modified helicopters. One helicopter clipped a compound wall during landing and was destroyed by the assault team, with no serious injuries. The raiders cleared the buildings; bin Laden was shot and killed on the upper floor. His identity was confirmed by DNA testing. Four other people in the compound were killed, including bin Laden's son Khalid and the courier. The raid lasted about forty minutes. Bin Laden's body was flown to Afghanistan and later buried at sea aboard USS Carl Vinson in the North Arabian Sea, following Islamic rites.

Aftermath

The operation strained US–Pakistani relations, as Pakistan had not been informed in advance; Pakistan later imprisoned Shakil Afridi, a doctor who had assisted the CIA's surveillance effort. Materials seized from the compound provided a large intelligence haul on al-Qaeda. Details of the raid remain partly disputed between official accounts and reporting by journalists and participants.

Not to be confused with Operation Neptune, the 1944 Normandy naval operation.
