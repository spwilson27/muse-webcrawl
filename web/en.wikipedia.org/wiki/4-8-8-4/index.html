4-8-8-4

A 4-8-8-4, in the Whyte notation for classifying steam locomotives by wheel arrangement, is a locomotive with four leading wheels, two sets of eight driving wheels, and four trailing wheels. Only one class was ever built to this arrangement: the Union Pacific Big Boy locomotives of the United States.

Union Pacific Big Boy

Twenty-five Big Boys were built by the American Locomotive Company (ALCO) for the Union Pacific Railroad between 1941 and 1944. They were designed to haul heavy freight trains over the steep grades of the Wasatch Mountains in Utah without helper locomotives. At over 40 metres long and weighing some 600 tonnes with tender, they are among the largest and most powerful steam locomotives ever operated.

Preservation

Eight Big Boys survive. Union Pacific restored No. 4014 to working order in 2019, making it the world's largest operating steam locomotive, used on excursion and publicity tours.
