Dwarf galaxy

Dwarf galaxy
Type: Small galaxy
Stars: 107–109 typical
Examples: Sagittarius Dwarf, Fornax Dwarf

A dwarf galaxy is a small galaxy composed of about 1,000 to several billion stars, compared with the Milky Way's 200–400 billion stars. The Large Magellanic Cloud, with about 30 billion stars, is sometimes classified as a dwarf galaxy.

Types

Dwarf elliptical (dE), dwarf spheroidal (dSph) and dwarf irregular (dI) galaxies are recognised. Dwarf spheroidals are faint, diffuse, dominated by old stars and dark matter, with little gas. Ultra-faint dwarfs, discovered in the 2000s, are among the most dark-matter-dominated and metal-poor systems known.

Formation and role

In hierarchical cosmology, dwarf galaxies are building blocks that merge to form larger galaxies. Many orbit the Milky Way and Andromeda Galaxy as satellites; tidal interaction can strip them, as with the Sagittarius Dwarf Spheroidal. Their abundance and dark matter content test the Lambda-CDM model — historically the 'missing satellites problem'.

Examples

Notable dwarfs include the Magellanic Clouds, Fornax, Sculptor, Carina, and Leo I. See also Galaxy and Dark matter.
