Building automation

A building management system integrates control of a building's services.

Building automation is the automatic, centralized control of a building's systems — heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC), lighting, power, fire and security — through a building management system (BMS) or building automation system (BAS). The goals are improved occupant comfort, efficient operation of building systems, reduced energy consumption and operating costs, and better monitoring and alarm handling.

Architecture

A typical system is layered: field devices (sensors, actuators, valves and dampers) connect to direct digital controllers, which communicate over networks using protocols such as BACnet, KNX, LonWorks or Modbus to a supervisory workstation where operators view graphics, trends and alarms. Modern systems increasingly integrate with lighting control, access control, elevators and energy metering, and with cloud analytics for fault detection and optimization.

History

Automated building controls began with pneumatic thermostats and control panels in the early 20th century, moved to electronic and then digital direct control in the 1970s–1990s, and have since converged with IT networks and the Internet of things. "Smart building" developments add occupancy sensing, demand response and predictive control.
