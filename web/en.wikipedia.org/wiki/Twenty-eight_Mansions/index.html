Twenty-eight Mansions

The Twenty-eight Mansions (Chinese: 二十八宿, èrshíbā xiù) are a system of constellations used in traditional Chinese astronomy to track the motion of the Moon along the ecliptic, analogous to the lunar mansions (nakshatra) of Indian astronomy and the manzil of Arabic astronomy. Each "mansion" is a segment of the ecliptic marked by a determinative star or asterism, through which the Moon passes in the course of one sidereal month.

Structure

The mansions are grouped in fours of seven among the Four Palaces (Four Symbols) of the Chinese sky:

  • Azure Dragon of the East: Horn (角), Neck (亢), Root (氐), Room (房), Heart (心), Tail (尾), Winnowing Basket (箕)
  • Black Tortoise of the North: Dipper (斗), Ox (牛), Girl (女), Emptiness (虚), Rooftop (危), Encampment (室), Wall (壁)
  • White Tiger of the West: Legs (奎), Bond (婁), Stomach (胃), Hairy Head (昴), Net (畢), Turtle Beak (觜), Three Stars (參)
  • Vermilion Bird of the South: Well (井), Ghosts (鬼), Willow (柳), Star (星), Extended Net (張), Wings (翼), Chariot (軫)

The mansions are of unequal width, and each is measured from its determinative star. The full list is preserved in texts from the Warring States period onward; a complete set of the 28 names appears in the Shiji's "Treatise on the Celestial Offices" (c. 100 BC) and in the tomb of Marquis Yi of Zeng (433 BC), whose lacquer chest lid carries the earliest complete list as part of a star map.

Uses

Beyond lunar tracking, the mansions served calendrical, astrological and feng shui purposes: they were correlated with days of the week, animals, and directions in popular almanacs, and in Daoist ritual the 28 mansions were personified as deities. The system was transmitted to Korea, Japan (as the nisshuku), and Vietnam, where it remains part of traditional astrology.
