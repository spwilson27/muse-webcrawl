John William McCormack (December 21, 1891 – November 22, 1980) was an American politician from Boston, Massachusetts, who served in the United States House of Representatives from 1928 to 1971 and as Speaker of the House from 1962 to 1971.

Career

Raised in South Boston, McCormack left school to support his family and was admitted to the bar after private study. He served in the Massachusetts legislature before winning a special election to Congress in 1928. He rose through the Democratic leadership, serving as majority leader under Speaker Sam Rayburn and succeeding Rayburn on his death.

Speakership

As Speaker, McCormack presided over the passage of much of President Lyndon B. Johnson's Great Society legislation, including the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the laws establishing Medicare and Medicaid. He retired in 1971 and died in 1980.
