Therapeutic index

Definition: TI = TD50 / ED50

Field: Pharmacology

Related concepts: Therapeutic window; margin of safety; certain safety factor

The therapeutic index (TI), also called the therapeutic ratio, is a quantitative measurement of the relative safety of a drug. It is the ratio of the dose that produces toxicity in half the population (the median toxic dose, TD50) to the dose that produces the desired therapeutic effect in half the population (the median effective dose, ED50):

TI = TD50 / ED50

In animal studies the lethal dose LD50 is often used in place of the toxic dose. A larger therapeutic index indicates a safer drug; a small index indicates a narrow margin between effective and toxic doses.

Clinical significance

Drugs with a narrow therapeutic index (NTI) — such as warfarin, digoxin, lithium, phenytoin and ciclosporin — require careful dosing and often therapeutic drug monitoring, because small changes in dose or blood concentration can lead to treatment failure or serious toxicity. The related concept of the "therapeutic window" describes the range of doses between the minimum effective and minimum toxic concentrations.

Limitations

The therapeutic index derived from population median values does not capture individual variability, idiosyncratic reactions, or the overlap between effective and toxic dose–response curves; more conservative measures such as the margin of safety (TD1/ED99) are sometimes preferred. Regulatory agencies such as the U.S. FDA apply heightened scrutiny to narrow-therapeutic-index products, for example in assessing generic equivalence.
