Philippe Van Parijs

Born: May 23, 1951, Brussels, Belgium
Occupation: Philosopher, political economist
Known for: Defence of basic income; linguistic justice

Van Parijs is a leading academic advocate of universal basic income.

Philippe Van Parijs (born 23 May 1951) is a Belgian political philosopher and political economist, best known as a proponent of the concept of an unconditional basic income and for his theory of linguistic justice. He held the Hoover Chair of Economic and Social Ethics at the Université catholique de Louvain and has been a visiting professor at Harvard University and the University of Oxford.

Work

His book Real Freedom for All: What (if Anything) Can Justify Capitalism? (1995) gives a liberal-egalitarian defence of basic income: guaranteeing every person the greatest possible real freedom to do whatever they might want to do, funded in part by taxing scarce assets such as jobs. In 1986 he co-founded the Basic Income Earth Network (BIEN), which links academics and activists promoting basic income worldwide. His later book Linguistic Justice for Europe and for the World (2011) addresses the fairness problems raised by the global spread of English as a lingua franca.
