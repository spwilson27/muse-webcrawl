Magic system

Magic system
GenreFantasy fiction, role-playing games
Related conceptsWorldbuilding, hard and soft magic

A magic system is a set of rules, principles and limitations governing the use of magic within a fictional work. A staple of fantasy literature, film and role-playing games, a magic system defines what magic can and cannot do, who can use it, what it costs, and how it shapes the societies of the imagined world.

Hard and soft magic

Author Brandon Sanderson popularised a distinction between "hard" magic systems, whose rules are clearly explained to the reader and can be used by characters to solve problems in a quasi-scientific way, and "soft" magic systems, which remain mysterious and are used chiefly to create wonder or dread. Sanderson's "laws" of magic argue that an author's ability to use magic satisfyingly to resolve conflict is proportional to how well the reader understands it, that limitations are more interesting than powers, and that existing elements should be developed before new ones are added.

Common elements

Magic systems commonly specify a source or fuel (such as mana, memorised spells, or pacts with supernatural beings), a method of access (innate talent, study, ritual, or artefacts), and costs or risks (exhaustion, corruption, or backlash). In role-playing games, systems such as the Vancian spell-slot model of Dungeons & Dragons quantify these constraints numerically.

Examples

Well-known systems include the elemental bending of Avatar: The Last Airbender, the investiture-based arts of Brandon Sanderson's Cosmere, the true-name magic of Ursula K. Le Guin's Earthsea, and the spellcasting of the tabletop and video game traditions derived from Jack Vance's Dying Earth.
