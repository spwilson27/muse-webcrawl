Gurkha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Gurkha soldiers in uniform with their distinctive kukri knives]
Gurkhas of the British Army.

The Gurkhas are soldiers native to the Greater Himalayan region — principally Nepal — who serve in the Nepalese, British and Indian armies. Renowned for their courage and loyalty, Gurkha regiments have fought in many conflicts since the early 19th century and are famous for their distinctive curved kukri knife.

History

Gurkha service with Britain began after the Anglo-Nepalese War (1814–1816): impressed by their fighting qualities, the British East India Company began recruiting Gurkhas, who won renown in the Indian Rebellion of 1857 and both world wars. After Indian independence in 1947, Gurkha regiments were divided between the British and Indian armies under a tripartite agreement with Nepal.

Regiments and honours

The Brigade of Gurkhas in the British Army includes infantry, engineers, signals, logistics and band units. Gurkha soldiers have won numerous gallantry awards, including Victoria Crosses. The motto associated with them includes 'Kaphar hunnu bhanda marnu ramro' — 'better to die than be a coward'.
