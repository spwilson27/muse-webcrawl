Google Doodle

A Google Doodle is a special, temporary alteration of the logo on Google's homepages intended to commemorate holidays, events, achievements, and historical figures. The first Google Doodle honoured the 1998 edition of the long-running annual Burning Man event in Black Rock City, Nevada, and was designed by Google co-founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin to notify users of their absence.

Development

In 2000, intern Dennis Hwang was asked to design a Doodle for Bastille Day; its popularity led to Hwang becoming chief doodler, and Google formed a dedicated team of illustrators and engineers, the "Doodlers". More than 5,000 Doodles have been created for homepages around the world. Doodles have evolved from simple static images to animations, videos and interactive games, such as the playable Pac-Man Doodle of 2010 and celebrations of the FIFA World Cup, the Olympics and the 44th anniversary of hip hop.

Doodle 4 Google

Google holds an annual competition, "Doodle 4 Google", in which school students in various countries submit designs on a theme; the winning entry appears on the homepage and its creator receives scholarships and prizes.
