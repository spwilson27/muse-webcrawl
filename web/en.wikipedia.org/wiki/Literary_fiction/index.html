Literary fiction

Literary fiction is often characterised by stylistic prose and psychological depth.

Literary fiction is a publishing category for fiction that is considered to have literary merit — characterised by a focus on style, psychological depth, and complex, layered characterisation — as distinct from genre fiction, which is marketed primarily on plot and adherence to genre conventions such as mystery, romance, or science fiction.

Characteristics

Literary fiction tends to be character-driven rather than plot-driven, following the protagonist's introspective transformation; it often employs stylistic experimentation, symbolism, and social commentary, and may end ambiguously. Works frequently associated with the category include those of Virginia Woolf, James Joyce, Toni Morrison, and Haruki Murakami.

Debate

The boundary is contested and largely a marketing distinction: many celebrated works blend literary and genre elements, and critics note that "literary fiction" functions as a genre in its own right, with its own conventions and readership. Prizes such as the Booker Prize and the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction reinforce the category.
