Hibernia oil field

The Hibernia oil field is an oil field in the North Atlantic Ocean, approximately 315 kilometres (196 mi) east-southeast of St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador, Canada, in shallow water of 80 m depth. It was discovered in 1979 and is produced from the Hibernia platform, the world's largest (in terms of weight) offshore oil and gas platform.

Geology

The field lies in the Jeanne d'Arc Basin on the Grand Banks. Its principal reservoirs are Early Cretaceous fluvial sandstones of the Hibernia Member and the deeper Avalon reservoirs, with recoverable reserves originally estimated at over 1.2 billion barrels of oil.

Development

Production began on 17 November 1997. Because of the danger from icebergs, the platform uses a massive concrete gravity base structure (GBS) designed to withstand the impact of a one-million-tonne iceberg, surrounded by a serrated ice wall. Crude oil is stored in the GBS and offloaded to shuttle tankers that carry it to a transshipment terminal at Whiffen Head. The field is operated by the Hibernia Management and Development Company, with ExxonMobil Canada as the largest partner.
