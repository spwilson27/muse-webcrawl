Dresden

Country Germany
State Saxony
Population ~555,000 (city)
River Elbe

Dresden is the capital city of the German state of Saxony and its second most populous city after Leipzig. It lies in a valley of the river Elbe, near the Czech border, and is renowned for its baroque and rococo architecture, art collections and musical tradition.

History

Dresden became the residence of the Saxon electors and kings, who rebuilt it in splendour in the 18th century — earning it the nickname "Florence on the Elbe" — with landmarks such as the Zwinger palace, the Semperoper and the Frauenkirche. In February 1945, Allied bombing raids devastated the city centre and killed an estimated 25,000 people; the bombing remains one of the most controversial episodes of the Second World War.

Reconstruction

Under East Germany some monuments were restored while others were left in ruins as memorials. After reunification the Frauenkirche was rebuilt (1994–2005) from its own fire-blackened stones and reconsecrated as a symbol of reconciliation. The reconstructed old town, the Dresden State Art Collections and a major microelectronics cluster ("Silicon Saxony") define the modern city.
