Cogset

A cogset is the set of sprockets (cogs) mounted on the hub of a bicycle's rear wheel, over which the drive chain runs. Together with the chainrings at the cranks, the cogset provides the gear ratios available to the rider. On modern bicycles the cogset usually takes the form of a cassette, a pre-assembled cluster of sprockets that slides onto a splined freehub body; older designs used a screw-on freewheel in which the sprockets and ratchet mechanism formed a single unit.

Design

Cassettes for road bicycles commonly have 8 to 13 sprockets, while mountain bikes use wider-ranging cassettes, sometimes with a largest sprocket of 50 teeth or more paired with a single chainring. Sprocket teeth are shaped and ramped (for example Shimano's Hyperglide) to allow the chain to shift smoothly under load. Cogsets are wear items: as the chain stretches with use, it wears the sprocket teeth into a hooked profile, and a worn cassette will cause a new chain to skip.

The gear range of a cogset is expressed as the ratio between its largest and smallest sprockets; wide-range cassettes permit low climbing gears while retaining high gears for descents.
