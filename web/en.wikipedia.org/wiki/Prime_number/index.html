Prime number

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The number spiral with prime numbers marked, showing diagonal patterns (the Ulam spiral)]

A prime number (or a prime) is a natural number greater than 1 that is not a product of two smaller natural numbers — its only divisors are 1 and itself. The first primes are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, ...; 2 is the only even prime. Euclid proved around 300 BC that there are infinitely many primes.

Primes are the 'atoms' of arithmetic: the fundamental theorem of arithmetic states that every integer greater than 1 factors uniquely into primes. Their distribution is studied through the prime number theorem and the unsolved Riemann hypothesis.

Testing and records

Algorithms such as the sieve of Eratosthenes find all primes up to a bound; modern primality tests handle enormous numbers. The largest known prime is almost always a Mersenne prime (of the form 2^p − 1), found by distributed computing projects such as GIMPS.

Applications

Large primes are fundamental to public-key cryptography, where the difficulty of factoring products of two large primes secures RSA encryption.
