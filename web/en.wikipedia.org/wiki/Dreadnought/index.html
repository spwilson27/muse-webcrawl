Dreadnought

Namesake HMS Dreadnought (1906)
Key features "All-big-gun" armament, steam turbine propulsion
Superseded Pre-dreadnought battleships

The dreadnought was the predominant type of battleship in the early 20th century. The type takes its name from the Royal Navy's HMS Dreadnought, launched in 1906, whose revolutionary design — an "all-big-gun" main armament of uniform calibre and steam-turbine propulsion — rendered every existing battleship obsolete overnight.

Origins

Naval thinkers including Vittorio Cuniberti had argued for a battleship armed solely with the largest guns; long-range gunnery at Tsushima (1905) confirmed that mixed batteries were impractical to spot and control. Dreadnought carried ten 12-inch guns and turbines giving her 21 knots, against the 18 knots of pre-dreadnoughts.

Arms race and service

Her launch triggered a naval arms race, above all between Britain and Imperial Germany, that became one of the contributing tensions before the First World War. Successive generations — super-dreadnoughts with larger guns and superfiring turrets — fought at Jutland (1916), the only full-scale clash of dreadnought fleets. The Washington Naval Treaty of 1922 limited numbers, and by the Second World War aircraft carriers had displaced the battleship as the decisive capital ship.
