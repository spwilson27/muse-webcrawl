The AgustaWestland AW159 Wildcat (previously Future Lynx and Lynx Wildcat) is a British military helicopter developed from the Westland Lynx by AgustaWestland, now Leonardo Helicopters. It serves in two principal variants: the Army's battlefield reconnaissance Wildcat AH1 and the Royal Navy's maritime Wildcat HMA2.

Development

The programme began as the Future Lynx to replace the Lynx fleets of the British Army and Royal Navy. Although it shares the Lynx's general layout, the AW159 is extensively redesigned, with new engines (two LHTEC CTS800 turboshafts), a new tail, composite structures, and modern avionics. The first aircraft flew on 12 November 2009, and deliveries began in 2012; 62 aircraft were ordered for the UK (34 AH1 and 28 HMA2).

Roles and armament

The AH1 performs battlefield reconnaissance, command and control, and light transport. The naval HMA2 conducts anti-surface and anti-submarine warfare, search and rescue, and ship protection, deploying from frigates and destroyers. Armament options include the Martlet (Lightweight Multirole Missile), Sea Venom anti-ship missile, Sting Ray torpedoes, and depth charges; a door-mounted machine gun can also be fitted.

Exports

South Korea selected the AW159 in 2013 for the Republic of Korea Navy, taking eight aircraft, and the Philippine Navy acquired two for anti-submarine duties, delivered in 2019.
