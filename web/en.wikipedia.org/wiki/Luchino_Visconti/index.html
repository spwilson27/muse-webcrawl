Luchino Visconti

Luchino Visconti di Modrone, Count of Lonate Pozzolo (2 November 1906 – 17 March 1976), was an Italian film and theatre director, screenwriter and writer. One of the fathers of cinematic neorealism, he was also celebrated for opulent historical dramas about the decline of the aristocracy, including Senso (1954), The Leopard (1963), The Damned (1969) and Death in Venice (1971). His 1943 debut Ossessione, an unauthorised adaptation of The Postman Always Rings Twice, is often cited as the first neorealist film.

Life and career

Visconti was born into one of Milan's wealthiest aristocratic families, which had ruled Milan in the Middle Ages. He worked in Paris as an assistant to Jean Renoir before returning to Italy. Ossessione (1943) shocked fascist censors with its sordid realism; Visconti himself was active in the anti-fascist resistance. La Terra Trema (1948), filmed in Sicily with non-professional fishermen speaking dialect, applied neorealism in its purest form, while Bellissima (1951) and Rocco and His Brothers (1960) explored postwar Italian society.

Historical epics

With Senso and above all The Leopard (1963), adapted from Giuseppe Tomasi di Lampedusa's novel of Sicily during the Risorgimento and starring Burt Lancaster, Visconti turned to lavish period reconstruction animated by a Marxist-aristocrat's meditation on historical change; The Leopard won the Palme d'Or at Cannes. His "German trilogy" — The Damned (1969), Death in Venice (1971) and Ludwig (1972) — examined decadence and fascism. His final film, The Innocent (1976), was released after his death in Rome in March 1976. Visconti was also a renowned director of opera and theatre, staging famous productions with Maria Callas at La Scala.
