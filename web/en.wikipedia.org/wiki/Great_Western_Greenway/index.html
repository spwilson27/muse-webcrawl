Great Western Greenway

The Great Western Greenway (Irish: Bealach Mór an Iarthair) is a greenway rail trail in County Mayo, Ireland. Running 42 kilometres (26 miles) from Westport to Achill Island, it follows the route of the former Midland Great Western Railway branch line to Achill, which closed in 1937. Opened in stages between 2010 and 2011, it was Ireland's longest off-road walking and cycling trail at the time of its completion and helped popularize the "greenway" concept in Ireland.

Route

The trail runs from Westport through Newport and Mulranny to Achill Sound, along the north shore of Clew Bay beneath Croagh Patrick and the Nephin Beg mountains. It passes over the viaduct at Newport and through tunnels and cuttings of the old railway. The disused line had carried trains from 1894–95 until its closure in 1937; the route is associated with the Irish folklore prophecy of Brian Rua Ua Cearbhain, said to have foretold carriages on iron wheels carrying emigrants away—and, in the return journey, bringing people back.

Development and impact

The greenway was developed by Mayo County Council with Fáilte Ireland and local landowners who permitted the trail across their lands. The final section opened in July 2011. The greenway won the European Greenways Association's European Greenways Award and became an economic catalyst for Westport, Newport, and Achill, drawing hundreds of thousands of users annually and spurring bike-hire and hospitality businesses. It served as a model for later Irish greenways, including the Waterford Greenway and the Royal Canal Greenway.
