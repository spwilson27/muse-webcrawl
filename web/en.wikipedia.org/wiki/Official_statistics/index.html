Official statistics

Official statistics are statistics produced by government agencies and other official bodies — such as national statistical offices, central banks and international organisations — to inform public policy, democratic debate and research. They include censuses, national accounts, inflation and unemployment figures, health and education statistics, and trade data.

Principles

The United Nations Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics, adopted by the UN General Assembly in 2014, set out norms including professional independence, impartiality, scientific methodology, confidentiality of individual data, and equal access for all users. Many countries protect these principles in statistics legislation and through independent statistical authorities, such as the UK Statistics Authority or Statistics Canada.

Production

Official statistics are compiled from censuses, sample surveys, and administrative records collected for other purposes, such as tax and health registers. Increasingly, statistical offices supplement these with new data sources. Results are released to pre-announced schedules, often under strict embargo rules, because figures such as GDP growth and consumer price inflation move financial markets.

Trust and misuse

Because official statistics underpin government decisions, their independence is politically sensitive. Disputes have arisen where governments were accused of manipulating figures — for example over Greek deficit statistics during the eurozone crisis, or inflation measurement in Argentina in the late 2000s and early 2010s, which drew IMF censure.
