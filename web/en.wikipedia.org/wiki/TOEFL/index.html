The Test of English as a Foreign Language (TOEFL) is a standardized test of English-language proficiency for non-native speakers, administered by the Educational Testing Service (ETS). It is one of the world's most widely accepted English tests, used for university admissions in the United States, Canada and many other countries, as well as for scholarships, immigration and professional licensing. Since 1964, tens of millions of people have taken the test.

Formats

The original paper-based test (PBT) scored candidates from 310 to 677. A computer-based test (CBT) ran from 1998 to 2006. The TOEFL iBT (Internet-based test), launched in 2005, is now the dominant format. It tests four skills — Reading, Listening, Speaking and Writing — each scored from 0 to 30, for a total of 0 to 120. From July 2023 the iBT was shortened to under two hours, removing unscored trial questions and streamlining the writing section. A paper edition and the TOEFL Essentials test are offered in some markets, and the TOEFL ITP serves institutional testing within countries.

Scoring and acceptance

Most universities require scores between about 61 and 100 on the iBT, with highly selective institutions commonly expecting 100 or more. Scores are generally considered valid for two years. TOEFL's principal competitor is IELTS; both tests map scores to the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR).

Criticism

The test has been criticized for its cost (roughly US$180–325 depending on country), for encouraging formulaic preparation, and for high-stakes consequences attached to modest score differences; ETS has responded with fee-reduction programmes, free practice materials and the shorter format introduced in 2023.
