Green's theorem

Green's theorem relates a line integral around a simple closed curve C to a double integral over the plane region D bounded by C. For vector field components L and M with continuous partial derivatives, ∮C (L dx + M dy) = ∬D (∂M/∂x − ∂L/∂y) dA.

Interpretation

Green's theorem is a special case of the Kelvin–Stokes theorem in two dimensions. It expresses how the total circulation around the boundary of a region equals the sum of the infinitesimal circulations (curl) throughout its interior. An alternative form relates the outward flux across the boundary to the divergence within the region.

Applications

The theorem is used to compute areas from boundary integrals (a principle exploited by the planimeter), in fluid dynamics, and in complex analysis, where it helps establish Cauchy's integral theorem. It is named after the British mathematician George Green, who published the result in 1828.
