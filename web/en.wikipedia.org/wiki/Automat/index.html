Automat


Image placeholder: automat food vending wall

An automat is a fast-food restaurant in which simple foods and drinks are served by coin-operated (and later bill-operated) vending machines. Customers insert money into a machine, a small glass door unlocks, and the customer retrieves the item — such as a sandwich, a slice of pie or a cup of coffee — from a compartment that is restocked from a kitchen behind the vending wall.

History

The first automats appeared in Berlin in 1895, developed by Max Sielaff and the Quisisana company, and spread through northern Europe. In the United States the format was popularised by Horn & Hardart, which opened its first automat in Philadelphia in 1902 and in New York in 1912. The New York automats became cultural landmarks, noted for their Art Deco interiors, uniform food quality and egalitarian appeal to office workers, artists and the unemployed during the Great Depression.

Decline and survival

American automats declined after the Second World War as suburbanisation and fast-food chains with drive-through service grew; the last Horn & Hardart automat in New York closed in 1991. In the Netherlands, however, the automat (automatiek) remains widespread: chains such as FEBO serve croquettes, frikandellen and other snacks from vending walls across the country.
