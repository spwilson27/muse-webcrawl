Defence industry of Russia

The defence industry of Russia is a strategically important sector of the Russian economy, employing several million people in the design and manufacture of military equipment. Much of the industry is organised within large state-owned holding companies, most notably Rostec, and Russia has historically been one of the world's largest exporters of arms, second only to the United States for much of the post-Cold War period.

History

The modern Russian defence industry descends from the vast military-industrial complex of the Soviet Union, which at its height employed millions of workers in often closed cities across the USSR. The collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991 brought a drastic fall in state orders, widespread arrears and the partial conversion of defence plants to civilian production. From the 2000s, rising state procurement and export sales through the state intermediary Rosoboronexport revived the sector, and a series of state armament programmes funded modernisation of Russia's nuclear forces, air defences, combat aircraft and naval forces.

Structure

Major groupings include Rostec, which controls hundreds of enterprises including Russian Helicopters and the Kalashnikov Concern; the United Aircraft Corporation; the United Shipbuilding Corporation; Almaz-Antey, producer of the S-300 and S-400 air defence systems; and Tactical Missiles Corporation. The industry produces the full range of conventional and strategic weaponry, from small arms and the AK family of rifles to intercontinental ballistic missiles and nuclear submarines.

Exports

Russian arms exports were traditionally concentrated on India, China, Algeria and Vietnam, as well as a range of Middle Eastern and African customers. Sales included Su-30 and Su-35 fighters, T-90 tanks, S-400 air defence systems and Kilo-class submarines. Western sanctions imposed after 2014 and greatly expanded following the Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022 restricted the industry's access to components and finance, while the demands of the war itself sharply increased domestic production of munitions, armoured vehicles, drones and missiles.
