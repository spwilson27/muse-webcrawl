Outside broadcasting

Outside broadcasting (OB) is the electronic field production (EFP) of television or radio programmes from a mobile remote broadcast television studio. Professional video cameras and microphones are used to record and transmit signals to the production truck and back to the broadcast centre.

Equipment

The centrepiece of an outside broadcast is the OB truck (or scanner), a mobile control room typically equipped with a vision mixer, audio mixing console, video and audio monitoring, replay servers, and communications systems. Signals from cameras may travel by cable, fibre optics, microwave links, or satellite uplinks. Small events increasingly use flypacks and remote (REMI) production, in which signals are sent to a centralised studio for mixing.

Uses

Outside broadcasts are used for sports coverage, concerts, news events, state occasions, and entertainment shows. Major sporting events such as the Olympic Games and the FIFA World Cup involve some of the largest OB operations in television, with dozens of cameras and hundreds of crew.

History

The BBC pioneered outside broadcasting in the 1930s, notably with the televised coronation of King George VI in 1937. OB technology evolved from heavy multi-core camera cables through triax and fibre to modern IP-based workflows and bonded-cellular contribution.
