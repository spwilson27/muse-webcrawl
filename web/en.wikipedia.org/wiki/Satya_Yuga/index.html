Satya Yuga (Sanskrit: Satya Yuga, "the age of truth"), also called Krita Yuga, is the first and best of the four ages (yugas) in the cyclical Hindu cosmology described in texts such as the Mahabharata and the Puranas. It is a golden age in which humanity lives in complete accordance with dharma, free from suffering, disease and vice.

In Satya Yuga dharma stands firm on all four legs, like the bull of righteousness.

Description

In the traditional scheme, a cycle of four yugas (a mahayuga) lasts 4,320,000 human years, of which Satya Yuga accounts for 1,728,000. During this age, human beings are said to be virtuous, long-lived and gigantic in stature; there is no agriculture or labour because the earth yields abundantly, no buying or selling, and meditation (dhyana) is the principal spiritual practice. Each succeeding age — Treta, Dvapara and Kali — sees virtue decline by one quarter, symbolised by dharma as a bull standing on progressively fewer legs.

Aftermath

According to current traditional chronology, humanity now lives in the Kali Yuga, the final and most degenerate age, which began in 3102 BCE. When it ends, the cycle is expected to begin anew with a restored Satya Yuga, the transition in some accounts associated with Kalki, the prophesied tenth avatar of Vishnu.
