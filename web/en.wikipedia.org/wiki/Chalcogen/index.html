Chalcogen

Samples of the chalcogen elements

The chalcogens are the chemical elements in group 16 of the periodic table. This group is also known as the oxygen family. Group 16 consists of the elements oxygen (O), sulfur (S), selenium (Se), tellurium (Te), polonium (Po), and livermorium (Lv). Often, oxygen is treated separately from the other chalcogens, and sometimes excluded from the scope of the term "chalcogen" altogether, because of its very different chemical behaviour from sulfur, selenium, tellurium, and polonium.

The word "chalcogen" is derived from the Greek khalkós (χαλκός), meaning copper or bronze, combined with the Latinised Greek suffix -genēs, meaning "born" or "producer". Since most copper ores are oxides or sulfides, the name roughly means "ore-former".

Properties

All chalcogens have six electrons in their outermost shell, giving them an electron configuration ending in s²p⁴. They most commonly exhibit oxidation states of −2, +2, +4, and +6 (except oxygen, which is usually −2 or −1 and strongly electronegative). Down the group, metallic character increases: oxygen, sulfur, and selenium are nonmetals; tellurium is a metalloid; and polonium is a metal.

Compounds

Compounds of the chalcogens include the oxides, sulfides, selenides, and tellurides; metal sulfides are among the most important ores. Hydrogen compounds include water (H₂O), hydrogen sulfide (H₂S), and their heavier analogues, which are increasingly acidic and toxic down the group. Sulfur forms the industrially vital sulfuric acid, and sulfur oxides are significant air pollutants associated with acid rain. Compounds containing chalcogen–chalcogen bonds, such as disulfide bonds in proteins, are important in biochemistry.

Occurrence and uses

Oxygen is the most abundant element in the Earth's crust and is essential to respiration. Sulfur has been known since antiquity and is used in fertilisers, sulfuric acid manufacture, and vulcanisation of rubber. Selenium is used in photocells, solar cells, and xerography, and is an essential trace nutrient. Tellurium is used in thermoelectric devices and thin-film solar panels. Polonium, discovered by Marie and Pierre Curie in 1898, is highly radioactive and has niche uses as a heat source and anti-static device. Livermorium is a synthetic element first produced in 2000; only a few atoms have ever been made.
