Children's television series

Children's television series (or children's television shows) are television programs designed specifically for children. They are typically characterised by easy-going content that is humorous, educational, or both, and are commonly broadcast in the mornings and afternoons, when children are awake, or during the early evening.

History

Children's television emerged soon after the medium itself; early programs in the late 1940s included Howdy Doody in the United States and Watch with Mother in the United Kingdom. The launch of Sesame Street in 1969 was a landmark in educational programming, combining entertainment with research-based curriculum goals for preschool viewers. Later decades brought influential live-action and animated series across public broadcasters and dedicated children's channels such as Nickelodeon, Cartoon Network, and CBeebies.

Genres

Common formats include educational preschool shows, animated cartoons, puppet and costume-character programs, game shows, and live-action drama and comedy for older children and tweens. Many countries regulate advertising during children's programming and require educational content quotas.
