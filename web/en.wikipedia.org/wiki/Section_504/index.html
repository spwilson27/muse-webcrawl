Section 504

The 1977 sit-in in San Francisco pressed for Section 504 regulations to be issued

Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 is a United States federal civil rights law that prohibits discrimination on the basis of disability in programmes and activities receiving federal financial assistance, and in programmes conducted by federal agencies. Its central sentence provides that no otherwise qualified individual with a disability shall, solely by reason of disability, be excluded from participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under any such programme or activity.

History

Although enacted in 1973, Section 504 had little immediate effect because implementing regulations were not issued for four years. Disability rights activists, frustrated by the delay, staged protests across the country in April 1977, including a 25-day occupation of the federal building in San Francisco — the longest sit-in at a federal building in United States history. The regulations were signed later that month. Section 504 became a model for the later Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990, which extended similar protections to most private employers and public accommodations.

In education

In schools, Section 504 requires that students with disabilities that substantially limit a major life activity receive a free appropriate public education, often implemented through a "504 plan" of accommodations — such as extended test time or preferential seating — rather than the specialised instruction provided under an Individualized Education Program (IEP) under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act. Its definition of disability is broader than IDEA's, so some students qualify under Section 504 but not for an IEP.

Scope

Beyond education, Section 504 applies to federal agencies themselves and to recipients of federal funds, including hospitals, universities, state and local governments and many contractors, covering employment, accessibility and programme participation.
