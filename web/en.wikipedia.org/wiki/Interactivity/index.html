Interactivity

Interactivity is the ability of a system, medium, or work to respond to the actions of its users, who in turn respond to the system's output, creating a two-way exchange. The concept is central to computing, new media studies, communication theory, and design.

Definitions

Definitions of interactivity vary: some emphasize technical features such as user control and responsiveness, while others emphasize the quality of communication between participants. Communication scholar Sally McMillan distinguished interactivity in terms of direction of communication and level of user control, while other theorists treat genuine conversation as the paradigm case.

Interactivity in media

Traditional broadcast media are largely one-way, but digital media — video games, websites, social media, and interactive television — allow users to select, produce, and modify content. Interactive art and literature extend these possibilities, making the audience a participant in the work.

Human–computer interaction

In human–computer interaction, interactivity is shaped by interface design: feedback, affordances, and response time determine whether a system feels responsive. Good interactive design creates a sense of dialogue between the user and the machine.
