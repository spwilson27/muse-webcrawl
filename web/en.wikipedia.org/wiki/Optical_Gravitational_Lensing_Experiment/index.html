Optical Gravitational Lensing Experiment

The Optical Gravitational Lensing Experiment (OGLE) is a Polish astronomical project based at the University of Warsaw, founded in 1992 and led by Andrzej Udalski. Using the 1.3-metre Warsaw telescope at Las Campanas Observatory in Chile, OGLE monitors hundreds of millions of stars toward the Galactic bulge, the Magellanic Clouds and the Galactic disc, searching for gravitational microlensing events and variable stars.

Discoveries

OGLE has detected thousands of microlensing events, including some of the first exoplanets found by the microlensing method. Its long, dense photometric surveys have produced enormous catalogues of variable stars — Cepheids, RR Lyrae stars and eclipsing binaries — used to map the three-dimensional structure of the Milky Way and the Magellanic System.

Survey phases

The project has run in phases (OGLE-I through OGLE-IV and beyond), each with larger cameras and wider fields of view. OGLE-IV's mosaic camera covers several square degrees per pointing, enabling near-real-time alerts of ongoing microlensing events to the worldwide follow-up community.
