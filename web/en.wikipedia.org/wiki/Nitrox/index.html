Nitrox

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A cylinder labelled for enriched air nitrox

Nitrox refers to any nitrogen–oxygen gas mixture; in recreational and technical diving the term usually means enriched air nitrox (EAN or EANx), a breathing gas with more than the 21% oxygen of normal air — typically 32% or 36% oxygen, designated EAN32 and EAN36. Reducing the nitrogen fraction reduces the amount of nitrogen absorbed during a dive.

Uses and benefits

The principal benefit of nitrox is extended no-decompression limits at moderate depths, or equivalently a greater safety margin when air tables or air-equivalent computer settings are used. Nitrox can also shorten surface intervals and may reduce post-dive fatigue for some divers, though that claim is debated. It is widely used in recreational diving, in scientific and aquarium diving, and as a decompression gas in technical diving, where high-oxygen mixtures accelerate off-gassing; pure oxygen and EAN50 are common decompression gases.

Risks and limits

More oxygen means a shallower maximum operating depth: the higher oxygen partial pressure at depth risks central nervous system oxygen toxicity, which can cause convulsions without warning and is usually fatal underwater if the regulator is lost. For EAN32 the maximum operating depth at a partial pressure limit of 1.4 bar is about 33 m; for EAN36 about 28 m. Divers must analyse the gas in every cylinder, label it, set their dive computer accordingly, and plan depths within the oxygen limit. Equipment used with high-pressure oxygen fractions must be oxygen-clean and oxygen-compatible to reduce fire risk.

History

Oxygen-enriched mixtures were used by navies and commercial divers through the 20th century, and NOAA published nitrox procedures for scientific diving in the late 1970s. Recreational adoption followed in the late 1980s and early 1990s — initially controversial, with critics warning of danger — until training agencies standardized courses; enriched air is now the most popular specialty certification in recreational diving.
