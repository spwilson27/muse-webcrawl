Toledo Cathedral

The Primatial Cathedral of Saint Mary of Toledo (Spanish: Catedral Primada Santa María de Toledo), otherwise known as Toledo Cathedral, is a Roman Catholic church in Toledo, Spain. It is the seat of the Metropolitan Archdiocese of Toledo and one of the masterpieces of Spanish High Gothic architecture.

History

Construction began in 1226 under Ferdinand III of Castile and Archbishop Rodrigo Jiménez de Rada, on the site of the city's former main mosque, which itself stood where a Visigothic church had been. The five-naved cathedral, modelled in part on Bourges Cathedral in France, was essentially completed by the end of the fifteenth century, though chapels and decoration continued to be added for centuries.

Features

Notable elements include the great main altarpiece, a towering gilded and polychromed work; the choir with its carved stalls; the sacristy, which houses paintings by El Greco, Goya, and Van Dyck; and the Transparente, an extraordinary Baroque altarpiece by Narciso Tomé (1729–1732) illuminated by a hidden skylight. The cathedral's treasury holds the great monstrance of Enrique de Arfe. El Greco, Toledo's most famous resident artist, painted several works for the cathedral, including The Disrobing of Christ.
