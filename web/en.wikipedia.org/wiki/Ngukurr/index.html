Ngukurr is an Aboriginal community in south-east Arnhem Land in the Northern Territory of Australia, situated on the banks of the Roper River. Predominantly Aboriginal, the town is home to people from several language groups of the region, including Ngandi, Marra, Warndarrang, Nunggubuyu, Ritharrŋu, Wandarang, and Alawa speakers; Kriol is widely spoken as a lingua franca.

History

The community began in 1908 as the Roper River Mission, founded by the Church Missionary Society. Missionaries gathered Aboriginal people from surrounding country, and children of mixed descent were among those removed to institutions under the policies now remembered as the Stolen Generations. The mission transferred to community control in the late 1960s and 1970s, and the settlement adopted the name Ngukurr. The land is held under the Aboriginal Land Rights (Northern Territory) Act, and the community is governed through the Roper Gulf Regional Council.

Community and culture

Ngukurr is known for its art centre, Yugul Mangi Arts, and for distinctive painting styles that combine traditional iconography with mission-era influences. The writer and artist Waanyi author Alexis Wright has connections to the region, and Ngukurr is the setting and subject of the feature documentary work of local filmmakers. Services include a school, health clinic, store, and an airstrip; access by road via the Roper Highway can be cut for weeks in the wet season when the Roper River floods. Fishing for barramundi and hunting remain important, and outstations on surrounding clan estates are occupied seasonally.
