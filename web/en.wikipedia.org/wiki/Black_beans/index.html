Black beans

Scientific namePhaseolus vulgaris (black-seeded varieties)
FamilyFabaceae
UsesSoups, stews, side dishes

Black beans are small, shiny black varieties of the common bean (Phaseolus vulgaris). They are a staple of Latin American and Caribbean cuisines and are used in dishes such as Cuban black bean soup, Brazilian feijoada, and Mexican frijoles negros.

Culinary use

Black beans have a dense, meaty texture and a mild, slightly sweet flavour. They hold their shape well in long cooking, which makes them suited to soups and stews; their cooking liquid darkens and thickens the dish. They are commonly seasoned with onion, garlic, cumin, peppers, and bay leaf.

Nutrition

Like other beans, black beans are rich in protein, dietary fibre, folate, iron, and magnesium, and their dark skins contain anthocyanin pigments with antioxidant properties. They form a large part of vegetarian and vegan diets in the Americas.
