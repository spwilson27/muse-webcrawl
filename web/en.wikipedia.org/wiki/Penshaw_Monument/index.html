Penshaw Monument

Penshaw Monument (Earl of Durham's Monument)
Location: Penshaw Hill, Sunderland, Tyne and Wear, England
Built: 1844–1845
Architects: John and Benjamin Green
Height: 30 metres (98 ft) columns

Penshaw Monument, a landmark of the Sunderland skyline

The Penshaw Monument, officially the Earl of Durham's Monument, is a memorial on Penshaw Hill near Sunderland in north-east England, erected in 1844 in memory of John George Lambton, 1st Earl of Durham (1792–1840). Modelled on the Temple of Hephaestus in Athens, it consists of a rectangular arrangement of eighteen Doric columns supporting an entablature — but no roof — standing on a high, visible hilltop.

Construction

The monument was designed by Newcastle architects John and Benjamin Green and built of local gritstone. The foundation stone was laid on 28 August 1844 by the Earl of Zetland. A spiral staircase inside one of the corner columns once allowed visitors to walk along the top of the entablature; after a teenage boy fell to his death in 1926 the upper walkway was closed. The columns are each about 30 metres of structure on a stone base, and the monument is a Grade I listed building. It is now in the care of the National Trust and remains a beloved symbol of Sunderland, featuring on the badge of Sunderland A.F.C. — local folklore even connected the hill with the legend of the Lambton Worm.
