Unreality of time

"The Unreality of Time" is the best-known philosophical work of the Cambridge idealist philosopher J. M. E. McTaggart (1866–1925), published as an article in the journal Mind in 1908. In it, McTaggart argues that time is unreal — that our perception of time is an illusion and that the distinctions between past, present, and future have no basis in reality.

The A-series and B-series

McTaggart distinguished two ways of ordering events in time. The A-series orders events as past, present, or future; these properties continually change, as a future event becomes present and then past. The B-series orders events by the permanent relations earlier than and later than, which never change. McTaggart argued that change — and therefore time — requires the A-series, but that the A-series is contradictory: every event must be past, present, and future, yet these are incompatible properties. His attempt to resolve the contradiction (each event is successively future, present, and past) generates a vicious regress, since those "successive" moments themselves require A-determinations.

Influence

Though few philosophers accept McTaggart's conclusion, his terminology became foundational to the philosophy of time: presentists and growing-block theorists defend versions of the A-theory, while eternalists defend the B-theory, holding that the B-series suffices for time without temporal becoming.
