Brian Kernighan

Brian Wilson Kernighan
Born: 1942, Toronto, Canada
Fields: Computer science

Brian Wilson Kernighan (born 1942) is a Canadian computer scientist. He worked at Bell Labs and contributed to the development of Unix alongside its creators Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie. Kernighan's name became widely known through co-authorship of the first book on the C programming language, The C Programming Language (1978), with Dennis Ritchie; the book is known to programmers simply as "K&R".

Career

Kernighan earned his doctorate in electrical engineering from Princeton University in 1969 and spent most of his career at Bell Labs' Computing Science Research Center, where he worked on programming languages and software tools. He co-created the AWK and AMPL programming languages and contributed to the Unix document-processing tools, including the pic and eqn preprocessors for troff. Since 2000 he has been a professor in the Department of Computer Science at Princeton University.

Books

Besides The C Programming Language, Kernighan co-authored The Elements of Programming Style (1974), Software Tools (1976), The Unix Programming Environment (1984) with Rob Pike, The Practice of Programming (1999) with Pike, and Understanding the Digital World (2017). He also coined the term "Unix" (originally a pun, "Unics", on Multics).

"Hello, World!"

Kernighan popularised the "Hello, World!" program as the canonical first example: he used it in an internal Bell Labs memorandum on B programming in 1974, and it became famous through its prominent place in The C Programming Language.
