Dunkel

Dunkel (German: [ˈdʊŋkl̩]), or Dunkles, is a word used for several types of dark German lager. Dunkel is the German word meaning "dark", and dunkel beers typically range in colour from amber to dark reddish brown. They are characterised by their smooth, malty flavour and are the traditional lager style of Munich.

Characteristics

Munich dunkel typically has an alcohol content of 4.5% to 5.6% by volume, with flavours of bread crust, toast and light caramel derived from Munich malt, balanced by mild noble-hop bitterness. Before modern malting made pale lagers possible, dunkel was the standard Bavarian beer; it remains associated with breweries such as Augustiner, Hofbräu and Paulaner.

Related styles

Closely related dark lagers include Schwarzbier ("black beer"), which is drier and darker, and the dark wheat beer Dunkelweizen. Dunkel also survives as a descriptive term in the Franconian unfiltered lager tradition.
