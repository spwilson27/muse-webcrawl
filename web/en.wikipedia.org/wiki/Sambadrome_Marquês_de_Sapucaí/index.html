The Sambadrome Marquês de Sapucaí (Portuguese: Sambódromo da Marquês de Sapucaí) is a purpose-built carnival parade venue in downtown Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Designed by architect Oscar Niemeyer and built in 1984 under governor Leonel Brizola, it provides a permanent home for the competitive parades of the city's samba schools during the Rio Carnival.

Design

The Sambadrome consists of a 700-metre parade avenue, 13 metres wide, flanked by grandstands and boxes with a capacity of roughly 72,500 people. At its southern end the avenue passes beneath a distinctive parabolic concrete arch — 25 metres high — into the Praça da Apoteose (Apotheosis Square), where parades disperse. Niemeyer's complex doubles outside carnival season as a public school, with classrooms under the grandstands, reflecting Brizola's educational programme. The avenue follows the older Marquês de Sapucaí street, named after Cândido José de Araújo Viana, Marquis of Sapucaí.

Use

During carnival, the Sambadrome hosts the top-tier parades: the Special Group schools perform on Carnival Sunday and Monday nights, the Série A (second division) on Friday and Saturday, and the champions return for a non-competitive parade the following Saturday. Each school has about an hour to traverse the avenue. The venue was expanded and renovated for the 2012 carnival, and during the 2016 Summer Olympics it hosted archery competitions and the marathon finish. It has also served as a mass vaccination site and a venue for concerts and religious gatherings.
