Blessing

A priestly blessing

A blessing is a short invocation for divine help, blessing, and guidance, usually at the end of worship, and often a specific invocation that God may help and protect a particular person or group. In religion, a blessing is the imparting of God's favour, protection, or approval, whether conferred by a deity, by clergy, or by laypeople. The English word derives from Old English blēdsian, related to blōd (blood), originally referring to consecration with sacrificial blood.

In the Hebrew Bible and Judaism

In Genesis, God blesses creation, the patriarchs, and their descendants; blessing (berakhah) is a central theme of Israelite religion. The Priestly Blessing of Numbers 6:24–26 — "The LORD bless you and keep you..." — is still used in Jewish and Christian liturgy. Jewish tradition prescribes blessings before eating, over wine on the Sabbath (Kiddush), and on countless occasions of wonder and gratitude.

In Christianity

Christians receive blessings at baptism, marriage, confirmation, and the dedication of churches, homes, and objects. In Roman Catholic, Orthodox, and Anglican practice, priests bless people and sacramentals, often sprinkling holy water with an aspergillum and making the sign of the cross; the Urbi et Orbi blessing is given by the Pope from St Peter's Basilica. A benediction is the closing blessing of a service. In many traditions, the blessing of animals on the feast of St Francis of Assisi (October 4) is popular.

Other religions

In Islam, blessings on the Prophet (salawat) are a prescribed devotion, and the greeting as-salāmu ʿalaykum is itself a blessing of peace. In Hinduism, blessings are given by elders, gurus, and priests, often with a tilak mark, flowers, or prasad; devotees touch the feet of elders to receive ashirvad. Buddhist monks give blessings with sprinkled water and chanting of protective verses (paritta) in Theravada countries.

Everyday usage

The word is also used secularly: to "count one's blessings," to give a project one's blessing (approval), and in the response "bless you" to a sneeze, a custom of debated origin often attributed to the plague era and Pope Gregory I.
