Hor-Aha

Hor-Aha
Pharaoh of Egypt, First Dynasty
Reign: c. 3080–3035 BC (approx.)
Predecessor: Narmer (or Menes)
Successor: Djer
Burial: Chamber B10/B15/B19, Umm el-Qa'ab, Abydos

Hor-Aha (or Aha) was the second pharaoh of the First Dynasty of Egypt, reigning in the early 31st century BC. His Horus name means "Horus the Fighter". He is widely regarded as the son and successor of Narmer, the king credited with the unification of Upper and Lower Egypt, and some scholars identify Hor-Aha himself with the semi-legendary Menes.

Reign

According to the Egyptian historian Manetho, Aha reigned for some 62 years, though modern estimates are shorter. Inscriptions and labels from his reign record campaigns against Nubians, the founding of a temple of the goddess Neith at Sais, and royal rituals including a "Following of Horus". The famous ebony and ivory labels from Abydos, among the earliest examples of hieroglyphic writing, include a label from the tomb of Queen Neithhotep — often thought to be his mother — depicting events of his reign.

Tomb

Hor-Aha's tomb at Umm el-Qa'ab near Abydos consists of three large chambers (B10, B15 and B19), the largest burial complex of its time there. Nearby subsidiary graves contained retainers, and a pit held the bodies of young lions. The scale of the complex marks a significant development from earlier royal tombs.

Identity debate

Whether Hor-Aha, Narmer, or another figure corresponds to Menes, the traditional founder of the First Dynasty, remains debated. Seal impressions naming the early kings in order support the sequence Narmer → Hor-Aha → Djer.
