The governor of Maryland is the head of government of the U.S. state of Maryland and the commander-in-chief of the state's military forces. The governor is the highest-ranking official in the state and has a broad range of appointment powers, in many ways making the office one of the strongest governorships in the United States. The current governor is Wes Moore, a Democrat who took office on January 18, 2023.

History

Maryland's governorship traces its roots to the colonial period, when proprietary governors ruled on behalf of the Lords Baltimore. Following independence, the Constitution of 1776 provided for a governor elected annually by both houses of the Maryland General Assembly. The Constitution of 1851 changed the office to a popularly elected four-year term, and the Constitution of 1867, still in effect today, refined the governor's powers further.

Powers and duties

The governor serves as the state's chief executive, charged with faithfully executing Maryland law. Key powers include preparing and presenting the state budget — Maryland's governor has unusually strong budgetary authority, as the legislature may reduce but generally not increase items in the executive budget — vetoing legislation (subject to override by a three-fifths vote of the General Assembly), granting pardons and commutations, convening special sessions of the legislature, and commanding the Maryland National Guard.

Election and term

Governors are elected in statewide elections held in midterm years and serve four-year terms. Under Maryland's Constitution, a governor may serve two consecutive terms but may run again after sitting out a term. Candidates must be at least 30 years old and have been residents and registered voters of the state for five years.

Residence and office

The official residence of the governor is Government House in Annapolis, adjacent to the Maryland State House. The governor's executive offices are located in the State House, the oldest state capitol in continuous legislative use in the United States.
