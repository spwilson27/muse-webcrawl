The Justice and Development Party (Turkish: Adalet ve Kalkınma Partisi, AKP or AK Parti) is a political party in Turkey self-describing as embodying conservative democracy. It has governed Turkey continuously since 2002 under Recep Tayyip Erdoğan.

History

The AKP was founded in 2001 by reformist members of the banned Islamist Virtue Party, including Erdoğan and Abdullah Gül, who presented the party as a moderate, pro-European "conservative democratic" movement rather than an Islamist one. In the November 2002 general election it won a large parliamentary majority, and Erdoğan became prime minister in 2003.

In government

Early AKP governments pursued economic liberalisation, EU accession reforms and infrastructure megaprojects, and repeatedly won elections (2007, 2011). The party survived a closure case in 2008 and a military e-memorandum in 2007. Over time critics accused it of growing authoritarianism, especially after the 2013 Gezi Park protests and corruption investigations, and after the failed coup attempt of 15 July 2016, which was followed by mass purges. A 2017 referendum replaced Turkey's parliamentary system with an executive presidency; Erdoğan won the presidency in 2014 and again under the new system in 2018 and 2023. The party leads the People's Alliance with the Nationalist Movement Party (MHP).

Ideology

The AKP combines social conservatism, religious-nationalist themes, welfare-oriented populism and developmentalist economics. Its supporters credit it with raising living standards and giving a political voice to Turkey's conservative Anatolian heartland; opponents charge it with eroding secularism, judicial independence and media freedom.
