Lorentz gas

The Lorentz gas, also known as the Ehrenfest wind-tree model in a related form, is a model in statistical mechanics introduced by Hendrik Lorentz in 1905 to study the motion of electrons in a metal. It consists of a point particle moving freely among a fixed array of immobile, non-overlapping spherical (or circular, in two dimensions) scatterers, with which it collides elastically.

Description

Between collisions the particle travels in a straight line; on striking a scatterer it is specularly reflected. When the scatterers are placed on a periodic lattice, the model is a dynamical billiard closely related to the Sinai billiard. Depending on the density of scatterers and the particle's energy, the motion may be normal diffusion or display anomalous, superdiffusive transport.

Significance

The Lorentz gas is one of the simplest deterministic models exhibiting chaotic behaviour and diffusion, and it has been used to test ideas in ergodic theory, transport theory and the foundations of statistical mechanics. Numerical studies of the model famously revealed long-time tails in velocity autocorrelation functions and fractal structures in its transport coefficients.
