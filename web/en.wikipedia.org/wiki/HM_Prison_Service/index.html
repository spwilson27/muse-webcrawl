His Majesty's Prison Service is the service responsible for operating most prisons in England and Wales. It forms part of His Majesty's Prison and Probation Service (HMPPS), an executive agency of the Ministry of Justice. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own separate prison services.

Role and organisation

The Prison Service holds people remanded in custody by the courts and those serving custodial sentences, as well as some immigration detainees. Its stated aims are to hold prisoners securely, treat them with humanity, and help them lead law-abiding lives in custody and after release. Public sector prisons are run directly by the service; a number of prisons in England and Wales are operated under contract by private companies. Prisons are classified by function and security category, from Category A (maximum security) to Category D (open prisons), with separate estates for women and young offenders.

History

Prisons in England and Wales were nationalised under the Prison Act 1877, ending the patchwork of locally run gaols. The Prison Commission administered the system until 1963, when responsibility passed to the Home Office; the Prison Service became an executive agency in 1993 and moved to the newly created Ministry of Justice in 2007. In 2017 it was combined with the National Probation Service to form HMPPS. The service has frequently been the subject of debate over overcrowding, staffing levels, violence and reoffending rates, with the prison population of England and Wales standing at well over 80,000 in recent years.
