Al-Samh ibn Malik al-Khawlani (Arabic: السمح بن مالك الخولاني; died 721) was an Umayyad governor of al-Andalus who led Muslim expansion beyond the Pyrenees into southern Gaul. Appointed wali around 718–719, he reorganised the administration and taxation of the conquered Iberian territories and established Narbonne as the base for operations in Septimania.

Campaigns in Gaul

In 719–720 al-Samh's forces took Narbonne, massacring or enslaving much of its population according to the chronicles, and subdued the remaining Visigothic resistance in Septimania. In 721 he advanced on Toulouse, capital of Duke Odo of Aquitaine, and besieged the city.

Battle of Toulouse and death

Odo returned with an army and attacked the besiegers on 9 June 721. The ensuing Battle of Toulouse was a major defeat for the Umayyads; al-Samh was mortally wounded and died shortly afterwards, traditionally at Narbonne, as his army retreated. The defeat checked Muslim expansion in Aquitaine for a decade, until raids resumed under his successors.
