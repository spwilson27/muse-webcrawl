Golden Melody Awards

The Golden Melody Awards (Chinese: 金曲獎) are an annual Taiwanese music awards honouring outstanding achievement in the Mandarin- and Chinese-language popular and traditional music industry. First presented in 1990 by Taiwan's Government Information Office and now administered by the Ministry of Culture's Bureau of Audiovisual and Music Industry Development, they are widely regarded as the equivalent of the Grammy Awards for the Chinese-speaking world.

History

The ceremony began in January 1990 at the Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall in Taipei. Over the years the awards expanded to recognise works in Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hakka, and Formosan languages, and in 2017 a separate set of Golden Melody Awards for traditional and art music was established alongside the popular music ceremony.

Categories

Major categories include Song of the Year, Album of the Year, Best Mandarin Album, Best Male and Female Mandarin Singers, Best New Artist, and Best Musical Arranger. Artists such as Jay Chou, Jolin Tsai, A-Mei, and Eason Chan are among the most decorated performers in the awards' history. The ceremony is broadcast across Asia and is a major event in Mandopop.
