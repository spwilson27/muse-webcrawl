Samuel Houston (March 2, 1793 – July 26, 1863) was an American general and statesman who played a central role in the history of Texas. He was the only American to serve as governor of two different states (Tennessee and Texas) and the only governor elected from a foreign country's independence movement, having led the Texian Army to victory over Mexico at the Battle of San Jacinto and served twice as president of the Republic of Texas.

Early life

Born in Virginia, Houston moved with his widowed mother to Tennessee, where as a teenager he ran away to live with the Cherokee of Chief Oolooteka (John Jolly) on Hiwassee Island; he was adopted into the tribe and given the name Colonneh, "the Raven." He served under Andrew Jackson in the War of 1812, being gravely wounded at the Battle of Horseshoe Bend. With Jackson's patronage he became a lawyer, congressman (1823–1827), and governor of Tennessee (1827–1829). His career collapsed in 1829 after his brief marriage to Eliza Allen ended; he resigned the governorship and returned to the Cherokee, then in Indian Territory.

Texas Revolution and the Republic

Houston moved to Mexican Texas in 1832. As tensions with Mexico erupted, he became commander-in-chief of the Texian Army. On April 21, 1836, at the Battle of San Jacinto, his forces destroyed Santa Anna's army in eighteen minutes, securing Texan independence; Houston was wounded in the ankle and captured Santa Anna the next day. He served as president of the Republic of Texas from 1836 to 1838 and again from 1841 to 1844, pursuing annexation to the United States while trying to avoid war with Mexico and keep peace with Native American nations.

Statesman and final years

After annexation in 1845, Houston represented Texas in the U.S. Senate for fourteen years, a rare Southern unionist who opposed the Kansas–Nebraska Act and the drift toward secession. Elected governor of Texas in 1859, he refused in 1861 to swear allegiance to the Confederacy and was deposed. He retired to Huntsville, Texas, where he died in 1863. The city of Houston, founded in 1836, is named for him.
