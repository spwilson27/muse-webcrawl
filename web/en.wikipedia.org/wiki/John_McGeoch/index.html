John Alexander McGeoch (25 August 1955 – 4 March 2004) was a Scottish guitarist who played with several influential post-punk and new wave bands, including Magazine, Siouxsie and the Banshees, Visage, and Public Image Ltd. He is regarded as one of the most innovative and influential guitarists of the post-punk era, with Spin magazine ranking him among the greatest guitarists of all time.

Early life

McGeoch was born in Greenock, Scotland, and grew up in Fort William. He studied fine art at Manchester Polytechnic, where he met Howard Devoto and joined Devoto's band Magazine after Devoto left Buzzcocks.

Career

With Magazine (1977–1980), McGeoch developed a dramatic, angular guitar style on albums including Real Life, Secondhand Daylight, and The Correct Use of Soap. In 1980 he joined Siouxsie and the Banshees, contributing to the albums Kaleidoscope, Juju, and A Kiss in the Dreamhouse; his flanger-drenched playing on tracks such as "Spellbound" and "Happy House" became a signature sound of the era. Recurrent ill health and exhaustion led him to leave the Banshees in 1982. He later formed the Armoury Show with former Skids members, and from 1986 to 1992 he was a member of John Lydon's Public Image Ltd, playing on Happy?, 9, and That What Is Not — making him PiL's longest-serving guitarist. His playing was widely admired by other musicians; Johnny Marr and Radiohead's Jonny Greenwood have cited him as an influence, and John Frusciante has praised his Banshees work.

Later years and death

After leaving music professionally in the 1990s, McGeoch trained as a nurse. He died in his sleep on 4 March 2004 in Launceston, Cornwall, at the age of 48. In 2022 a biography, The Light Pours Out of Me: The Authorised Biography of John McGeoch by Rory Sullivan-Burke, was published.
