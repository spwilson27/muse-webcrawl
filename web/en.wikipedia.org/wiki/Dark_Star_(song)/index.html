"Dark Star" is a song by the American rock band the Grateful Dead, with music by Jerry Garcia and lyrics by Robert Hunter. First released as a single in 1968, it became the band's signature vehicle for extended improvisation: performed live hundreds of times between 1968 and 1994, it frequently stretched beyond twenty or thirty minutes, dissolving its simple two-verse structure into open-ended modal exploration.

Composition and early performances

The song emerged from the band's collaboration with Hunter, who wrote the lyrics — beginning "Dark star crashes, pouring its light into ashes" — after hearing the music. The studio single of 1968 sank without trace, but in concert "Dark Star" became the centrepiece of the Dead's psychedelic sets, famously captured on the 1969 album Live/Dead in a performance of over 23 minutes.

Improvisational role

Built on a sparse bass figure from Phil Lesh and a simple chord vamp, "Dark Star" served as a launching point for collective improvisation, often segueing into or out of other songs such as "St. Stephen" and "The Eleven". Certain performances are held in legendary regard by fans — notably those of 27 August 1972 at Veneta, Oregon, and 9 May 1977 — and the song's name is used by Dead archivists as shorthand for the band's exploratory peak years.

Legacy

Rolling Stone included "Dark Star" in its list of the 500 greatest songs of all time, and the song gave its name to the Dark Star Orchestra, a tribute band that recreates entire historical Dead concerts.
