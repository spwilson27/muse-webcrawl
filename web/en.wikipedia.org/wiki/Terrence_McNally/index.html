Terrence McNally

Born: November 3, 1938, St. Petersburg, Florida, U.S.
Died: March 24, 2020, Sarasota, Florida, U.S.
Occupation: Playwright, librettist, screenwriter

Terrence McNally (November 3, 1938 – March 24, 2020) was an American playwright, librettist, and screenwriter. Described as "the bard of American theater" and "one of the greatest contemporary playwrights the theater world has yet produced," McNally was the recipient of five Tony Awards, including the Tony Award for Lifetime Achievement in the Theatre in 2019. He won the Tony Award for Best Play for Love! Valour! Compassion! and Master Class and the Tony Award for Best Book of a Musical for Kiss of the Spider Woman and Ragtime.

Career

McNally's career in the theatre spanned six decades. His early play And Things That Go Bump in the Night (1965) was followed by works including Next (1969), The Ritz (1975), Frankie and Johnny in the Clair de Lune (1987), Lips Together, Teeth Apart (1991), Corpus Christi (1998), and Mothers and Sons (2014). He wrote the books for the musicals The Rink (1984), Kiss of the Spider Woman (1993), Ragtime (1998), The Full Monty (2000), Catch Me If You Can (2011), and Anastasia (2017), and the libretto for the opera Dead Man Walking (2000). His plays frequently addressed gay life, the AIDS epidemic, family, and faith with warmth and humor.

Personal life and death

McNally was a survivor of lung cancer. He married civil rights lawyer Thomas Kirdahy in 2003; Kirdahy produced many of his later works. McNally died on March 24, 2020, in Sarasota, Florida, from complications of COVID-19.
