The Kiev Pechersk Lavra (Ukrainian: Києво-Печерська лавра, Kyievo-Pecherska lavra), also known as the Kyiv Monastery of the Caves, is a historic Eastern Orthodox Christian monastery complex in Kyiv, Ukraine. Founded in 1051, it became the foremost centre of Orthodoxy in the lands of Kievan Rus' and remains one of the holiest pilgrimage sites of Eastern Christianity. Its name derives from the Ukrainian word pechera, "cave", for the cave systems in which its first monks lived; "lavra" is an honorific title granted to senior monasteries.

History

The monastery was founded by the monk Anthony of Kiev, who settled in a cave on Berestov Mount above the Dnieper, and his disciple Theodosius, who organised the community on the model of the Studion monastery of Constantinople. Under their successors it grew into a great centre of learning and art: the monk Nestor, associated with the Lavra, is credited with compiling the Primary Chronicle, the fundamental source for early Rus' history. The monastery survived the Mongol destruction of Kiev in 1240, became a stronghold of Orthodox culture under Polish-Lithuanian rule, and ran a famous printing press from the 17th century. Closed and turned into a museum reserve under Soviet rule, much of the complex was returned to the Church from 1988 onwards.

Site

The complex covers about 28 hectares on the wooded right bank of the Dnieper, divided between the state museum reserve and the active monastery. Landmarks include the Dormition Cathedral (rebuilt and reconsecrated in 2000 after its wartime destruction), the Refectory Church, the Church of the Saviour at Berestove, and the 96.5-metre Great Lavra Bell Tower (1731–1745), long the tallest free-standing bell tower in the world. Below ground, the Near and Far Caves form labyrinthine chapels holding the mummified remains of saints — including, in tradition, Ilya Muromets — which pilgrims venerate to this day.

Modern status

The Lavra and Saint Sophia Cathedral are together inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the Ukrainian state has moved to end the lease of the monastery's churches to the Ukrainian Orthodox Church (Moscow Patriarchate), transferring areas to the Orthodox Church of Ukraine — a dispute reflecting the religious realignment of wartime Ukraine.
