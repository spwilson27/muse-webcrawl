Frederick I (German: Friedrich I.; 11 July 1657 – 25 February 1713) was Elector of Brandenburg (as Frederick III) from 1688 and Duke of Prussia in personal union with Brandenburg. He crowned himself the first King in Prussia at Königsberg on 18 January 1701, founding the Prussian monarchy that his descendants would expand into a great power.

Rise to the crown

The second son of Frederick William, the "Great Elector", Frederick succeeded his father in 1688. Ambitious for royal status, he negotiated with Emperor Leopold I, who, needing Brandenburg's support in the impending War of the Spanish Succession, agreed by the Crown Treaty of 16 November 1700 to recognise Frederick as king — but only "in" Prussia, since the duchy lay outside the Holy Roman Empire, while his imperial lands remained an electorate.

Reign

Frederick modelled his Berlin court on Versailles, spending lavishly on palaces, ceremony and the arts. He founded the University of Halle (1694), the Prussian Academy of Sciences (1700) under Leibniz's influence, and the Academy of Arts. Building projects included the expansion of the Berlin Palace and Charlottenburg Palace, named for his beloved second wife, Sophie Charlotte of Hanover.

His courtly extravagance strained state finances, a burden his frugal son and successor, Frederick William I, would reverse. Frederick died in Berlin in February 1713.
