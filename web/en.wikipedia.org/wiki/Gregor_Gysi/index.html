Gregor Gysi

Gregor Florian Gysi (German pronunciation: [ˈɡʁeːɡoːɐ̯ ˈɡiːzi]; born 16 January 1948) is a German attorney and politician of The Left (Die Linke) party. He played a prominent role in the end of communist rule in East Germany in 1989, becoming the last leader of the ruling Socialist Unity Party of Germany (SED) and then of its successor, the Party of Democratic Socialism (PDS). He served as leader of the Left's group in the Bundestag, and for many years was the most prominent face of the democratic socialist left in reunified Germany.

Early life and career

Gysi was born in Berlin, the son of Klaus Gysi, a high-ranking SED cultural official and later East German minister of culture, and Irene Gysi. He studied law at the Humboldt University of Berlin, joined the SED in 1967, and became one of the few independent lawyers in East Germany, defending dissidents such as Rudolf Bahro and Robert Havemann — work that later led to persistent, contested allegations about his contacts with the Stasi, which he has always denied.

Political career

In December 1989, as the SED regime collapsed amid mass protests, Gysi was elected party chairman and steered its transformation into the PDS, committing it to democratic socialism and preserving it from dissolution. He led the party in the first free East German elections of 1990 and into the Bundestag of reunified Germany. In 2002 he briefly served as deputy mayor and senator for economics in Berlin before resigning over an air-miles affair. In 2005 he returned to the Bundestag as a leading figure of the new Left party formed from the PDS and the WASG, serving as co-chair of its parliamentary group with Oskar Lafontaine and later as group leader and, from 2013 to 2015, leader of the opposition. He has remained a member of the Bundestag, known for his wit and rhetorical skill, and has served as the Left party's foreign-policy spokesman and, from 2020, its spokesman on foreign affairs as well as president of the European Left (2016–2019).
