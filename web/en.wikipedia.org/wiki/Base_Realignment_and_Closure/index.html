Base Realignment and Closure

Base Realignment and Closure (BRAC) is a process used by the United States Department of Defense and Congress to reorganise the military's base structure, closing installations considered surplus and realigning others to improve efficiency. Because individual base closures are politically sensitive, the process relies on an independent commission whose recommendations take effect unless Congress rejects them as a whole.

History

Earlier rounds of closures were managed under the Military Bases Closure legislation of the 1970s and 1980s. The modern BRAC framework was created by the Defense Base Closure and Realignment Act of 1990. Five major BRAC rounds were conducted in 1988, 1991, 1993, 1995 and 2005.

Process

The Secretary of Defense proposes a list of closures and realignments based on military criteria. The BRAC Commission reviews the list, holds public hearings, and may add or remove installations before sending its recommendations to the President, who forwards them to Congress. Congress then has 45 days to pass a joint resolution of disapproval; otherwise the recommendations become binding.

Impact

The rounds closed hundreds of installations and realigned many more, with the Department of Defense estimating recurring annual savings of several billion dollars. Closed bases have been redeveloped for civilian uses including airports, universities and business parks, though redevelopment has sometimes been slowed by environmental contamination.
