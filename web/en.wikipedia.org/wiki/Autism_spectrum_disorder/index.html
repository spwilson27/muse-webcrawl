Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterised by persistent differences in social communication and interaction, alongside restricted, repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests or activities, and differences in sensory processing.

Diagnosis

The term derives from the work of Leo Kanner (1943) and Hans Asperger (1944). The DSM-5 (2013) merged the earlier separate diagnoses of autistic disorder, Asperger syndrome, childhood disintegrative disorder and pervasive developmental disorder not otherwise specified into the single diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder, with severity levels based on the support required. Diagnosis is based on behavioural observation and developmental history; no biomarker test exists.

Causes

Twin studies show high heritability, and hundreds of genes associated with neurodevelopment have been implicated. Advanced parental age and some prenatal factors are associated with modestly increased likelihood. Extensive research has found no link between autism and vaccines.

Support and intervention

There is no cure; supports focus on communication, education and quality of life. Early developmental and behavioural interventions can improve outcomes for some children. Speech and language therapy, occupational therapy and augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) are commonly used. Co-occurring conditions such as anxiety, ADHD, epilepsy and intellectual disability are frequent and are treated in their own right.

Society and culture

The autism rights movement and the neurodiversity paradigm frame autism as a difference rather than a deficit, advocating acceptance and accommodation.
