Asiatic wild ass

Scientific name: Equus hemionus

Family: Equidae

Status: Near Threatened (IUCN)

The Asiatic wild ass (Equus hemionus), also called the hemione or Asian wild ass, is a wild member of the horse family native to the deserts and steppes of Asia. It is larger than the African wild ass and resembles a mule in build, with a short mane, pale sandy coat and a dark dorsal stripe.

Subspecies

Five subspecies are generally recognised: the Mongolian wild ass or khulan (E. h. hemionus), the Turkmenian kulan (E. h. kulan), the Persian onager (E. h. onager), the Indian wild ass or khur (E. h. khur) of the Little Rann of Kutch, and the extinct Syrian wild ass (E. h. hemippus), which died out in 1927.

Behaviour and habitat

Asiatic wild asses live in small herds led by a dominant mare, with territorial stallions near water sources. They feed on grasses, herbs and shrubs, can survive on low-quality forage and little water, and can run at speeds up to 64–70 km/h. Once ranging from the Mediterranean to Mongolia, they have lost most of their range to hunting and competition with livestock; surviving populations are fragmented across Iran, Turkmenistan, Kazakhstan, Mongolia, India and China, with reintroduced herds in Israel's Negev desert.

Conservation

The species is listed as Near Threatened, though individual subspecies vary: the Indian wild ass has recovered under protection, while the Persian onager remains endangered.
