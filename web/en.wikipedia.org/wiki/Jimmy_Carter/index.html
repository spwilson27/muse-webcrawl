Jimmy Carter

James Earl Carter Jr. (October 1, 1924 – December 29, 2024) was an American politician and humanitarian who served as the 39th president of the United States from 1977 to 1981. A Democrat, he was previously governor of Georgia (1971–1975) and a Georgia state senator. In his long post-presidency he founded the Carter Center, won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2002, and became the longest-lived U.S. president, reaching 100 years of age.

Early life and naval career

Born and raised in Plains, Georgia, Carter graduated from the U.S. Naval Academy in 1946, married Rosalynn Smith, and served as a submariner under Admiral Hyman Rickover in the early nuclear submarine programme. On his father's death in 1953 he resigned to run the family peanut farm, building it into a successful business and entering politics in the civil-rights era.

Presidency

Carter defeated Gerald Ford in 1976, promising honesty after Watergate. His achievements included the Camp David Accords (1978) between Egypt and Israel, the Panama Canal Treaties, the SALT II treaty, normalisation with China, the creation of the Departments of Energy and Education, and a strong human-rights and environmental record. His term was overwhelmed by stagflation, the 1979 energy crisis, the Iranian Revolution and the Iran hostage crisis (1979–1981), and the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan; he lost decisively to Ronald Reagan in 1980.

Post-presidency

The Carter Center (founded 1982) monitored elections, mediated conflicts and nearly eradicated Guinea worm disease. Carter mediated in North Korea and Haiti, wrote more than thirty books, volunteered for Habitat for Humanity, and received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2002. After entering hospice care in February 2023, he died at home in Plains in December 2024 and received a state funeral in Washington.
