Federalism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Federalism is a mode of government that combines a general government (the central or "federal" government) with regional governments (provincial, state, cantonal, territorial or other sub-unit governments) in a single political system, dividing the powers between the two so that neither is subordinate to the other. Federalism thus differs from a Unitary state, where regional bodies exist only at the centre's discretion, and from a confederation, where the general government is subordinate to the units.

Features

Classic federal constitutions — those of the United States (1789), Switzerland (1848), Canada (1867), Australia (1901) and Germany (1949) — assign exclusive competences to each level, guarantee the existence of the constituent units, provide a constitutional court to police the boundary, and give the units representation in a federal second chamber. Roughly two dozen countries are formally federal, including very large or diverse states such as India, Brazil, Mexico, Russia and Nigeria.

Rationale and tensions

Federalism is defended as a means of accommodating regional diversity, bringing government closer to citizens, creating competing "laboratories of democracy", and dividing power as a check on tyranny. Its costs include complexity, overlapping responsibilities, unequal services between regions, and disputes over finance and supremacy — fought out historically in contests over states' rights in the United States and in continuing fiscal negotiations in Canada, Germany and Australia. The principle of subsidiarity, influential in the European Union, holds that decisions should be taken at the lowest level capable of dealing with them effectively.

Asymmetrical and cooperative federalism

Some federations are asymmetrical: Spain's autonomous communities and Canada's treatment of Quebec hold different powers. "Cooperative federalism" describes arrangements where levels of government share programmes and funding, contrasted with "dual federalism", in which each level operates in separate spheres.
