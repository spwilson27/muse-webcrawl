Trofim Lysenko

Trofim Denisovich Lysenko (Russian: Трофим Денисович Лысенко; 29 September 1898 – 20 November 1976) was a Soviet agronomist and biologist. Backed by Joseph Stalin, he rejected Mendelian genetics in favour of a doctrine of environmentally acquired inheritance, and his dominance over Soviet biology — known as Lysenkoism — devastated Soviet agriculture and genetics for decades.

Rise

Lysenko rose to prominence in the late 1920s through his promotion of vernalization — chilling seeds to alter flowering time — which was hailed in the Soviet press as a peasant-scientist's breakthrough promising larger harvests. He became president of the Lenin All-Union Academy of Agricultural Sciences and director of the Institute of Genetics.

Lysenkoism

Lysenko denied the existence of genes and DNA-based heredity, holding that organisms could be "trained" by environment and pass acquired traits to offspring. At the August 1948 session of the agricultural academy, his views were declared the only correct line; Mendelian genetics was banned as "bourgeois pseudoscience." Scientists who opposed him were dismissed, imprisoned, or died — most famously the geneticist Nikolai Vavilov, who starved in prison in 1943. Lysenkoist methods contributed to disastrous crop failures in the USSR and influenced policy in Maoist China.

Fall

Lysenko lost institutional power after Khrushchev's fall in 1964, and Soviet genetics gradually recovered. "Lysenkoism" has become a general term for the political suppression of science and the elevation of ideology over evidence.
