Taa, also known as ǃXóõ (also spelled ǃKhong or ǃXoon), is a language spoken in Botswana and Namibia, in and around the Kalahari Desert. It is famous among linguists for having one of the largest sound inventories of any language in the world, including an exceptionally large number of click consonants.

Classification

Taa was traditionally classified as a member of the Khoisan language family, within the Southern Khoisan (Tuu) group. As the genetic unity of "Khoisan" has been questioned, Taa is now often treated as part of the small Tuu family or as essentially an isolate with a few closely related varieties. The Eastern dialect, ǃXóõ, is the best documented.

Phonology

Taa has one of the most complex phonologies known. Depending on the analysis and dialect, it has well over 100 consonants, including around 80 or more click consonants formed with several influxes (such as dental ǀ, alveolar ǃ, lateral ǁ, and palatal ǂ) combined with numerous effluxes or accompaniments involving voicing, aspiration, nasalization, glottalization, and uvular friction. It also has a large vowel system with distinctions of length, nasalization, pharyngealization, and breathy or creaky phonation, as well as contrastive tone.

Status

The number of fluent speakers is small — only a few thousand — and the language is considered endangered, as younger speakers increasingly shift to Setswana, Afrikaans, or other regional languages. Nevertheless, Taa has been the subject of intensive documentation, including a dictionary and grammar projects by linguists such as Anthony Traill and later researchers working with the DoBeS programme.
