TARGET2 (Trans-European Automated Real-time Gross Settlement Express Transfer System) was the real-time gross settlement (RTGS) system owned and operated by the Eurosystem for settling payments in euro in central bank money. It replaced the original TARGET system beginning in November 2007.

Operation

TARGET2 used a single shared technical platform, operated on behalf of the Eurosystem by the central banks of Germany, France, and Italy, through which credit institutions settled interbank payments, monetary policy operations, and ancillary systems' transactions. It processed average daily volumes of roughly 350,000 payments worth around €1.7 trillion.

Balances controversy

During the euro-area sovereign debt crisis, large cross-border imbalances accumulated in TARGET2 accounts as capital flowed from peripheral to core countries, sparking public debate — notably by economist Hans-Werner Sinn — about the system's risk implications. In March 2023, TARGET2 was replaced by the consolidated T2 platform.
