Central business district

A central business district (CBD) is the commercial and business centre of a city. In North America it is frequently called the downtown, while in other English-speaking regions terms such as "city centre" are common. The CBD typically contains the highest concentration of offices, retail, hotels, and civic institutions in the city, along with its tallest buildings and highest land values.

Characteristics

Because land in the CBD is scarce and expensive, development tends to be vertical, producing the characteristic skyline of skyscrapers in large cities. The district is usually the focal point of a city's transport network, with converging rail, subway, and bus routes. Historically the CBD grew at the original core of the city — often a port, market, or administrative centre — and in urban geography models such as the concentric zone model of Ernest Burgess it forms the innermost zone, characterized by peak land value at the "peak land value intersection".

Development and decline

In the mid-20th century many central business districts, especially in the United States, lost residents and retail trade to suburban shopping centres and edge cities, though office functions often remained. From the late 20th century, many cities pursued downtown revitalization, encouraging residential conversion, cultural facilities, and mixed-use development. The spread of remote work in the 2020s prompted further debate about the future of office-centred CBDs.

Examples

Well-known CBDs include Midtown and Lower Manhattan in New York City, the City of London and Canary Wharf in London, La Défense in Paris, and the central districts of Tokyo, Hong Kong, Sydney, and Singapore.
