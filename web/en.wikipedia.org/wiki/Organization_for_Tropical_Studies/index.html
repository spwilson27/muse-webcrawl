The Organization for Tropical Studies (OTS; Spanish: Organización para Estudios Tropicales) is a non-profit consortium of more than sixty universities and research institutions from the United States, Costa Rica, and other countries, founded in 1963 to promote education, research and the responsible use of natural resources in the tropics. Its administrative headquarters are at Duke University in Durham, North Carolina, with a Costa Rican office in San José.

Biological stations

OTS owns and operates biological field stations in Costa Rica that are among the most important sites in tropical ecology:

  • La Selva Biological Station, lowland rainforest in the Caribbean lowlands, acquired in 1968 — one of the world's most intensively studied tropical forest sites.
  • Palo Verde Biological Station, in the seasonal dry forest and wetlands of Guanacaste province.
  • Las Cruces Biological Station, a mid-elevation forest site in the south, home to the Wilson Botanical Garden.

OTS also runs programmes in South Africa based near Kruger National Park, focusing on savanna ecology and global change.

Education and research

The organisation is famous for its intensive field courses — notably its graduate course in tropical ecology, running since the 1960s, in which cohorts of students work across Costa Rica's ecosystems designing and executing short research projects. Tens of thousands of researchers and students have passed through OTS stations, which host long-term ecological datasets and thousands of published studies. OTS also conducts environmental policy education for decision-makers and supports conservation in the regions surrounding its stations.
