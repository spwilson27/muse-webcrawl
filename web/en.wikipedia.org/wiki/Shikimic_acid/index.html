Shikimic acid

Shikimic acid, more commonly known as its anionic form shikimate, is a cyclitol carboxylic acid (C7H10O5) that is an important biochemical intermediate in plants and microorganisms. Its name comes from the Japanese flower shikimi (シキミ, Illicium anisatum), from which it was first isolated in 1885.

Role in biosynthesis

Shikimic acid is the central intermediate of the shikimate pathway, which converts simple carbohydrate precursors into the aromatic amino acids phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan, and onward into lignin, alkaloids, flavonoids and other compounds. Animals lack the pathway and must obtain the aromatic amino acids from their diet; this is why the pathway's enzymes, absent in humans, are attractive targets for herbicides such as glyphosate and for antimicrobial drugs.

Industrial use

Shikimic acid is the starting material for the industrial synthesis of the antiviral oseltamivir (Tamiflu). It has traditionally been extracted from star anise, and is also produced by engineered strains of Escherichia coli.
