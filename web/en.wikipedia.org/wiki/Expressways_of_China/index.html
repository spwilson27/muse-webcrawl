Expressways of China

A modern expressway interchange in eastern China.

The expressways of China form the national trunk road network (Chinese: 中国国家高速公路网), the largest controlled-access highway system in the world. By the end of 2022 the network exceeded 177,000 kilometres in total length, having surpassed the United States Interstate Highway System in 2011. The system is administered under a unified numbering plan, the China National Expressway Network Plan.

History

China's first expressway, the Shenyang–Dalian (Shenda) Expressway in Liaoning, began construction in 1984 and fully opened in 1990. In 1988 only a handful of kilometres existed; the network then grew at unprecedented speed, passing 100,000 km in 2013 and 160,000 km by 2019. Successive plans — the "7-9-18" network (7 radial routes from Beijing, 9 north–south routes, 18 east–west routes) — were later expanded in the 2022 national highway plan, which projects further growth by 2035.

Numbering

National expressways are designated with the letter G followed by one to four digits: single-digit routes radiate from Beijing (e.g., G1 Beijing–Harbin), two-digit routes form the north–south and east–west grid, and regional ring lines and city bypasses carry longer numbers. Provincial expressways use the letter S. Lane markings, signage, and tolling use the national ETC electronic toll collection system, one of the largest in the world.

Tolls and speed limits

Most Chinese expressways are toll roads, with tolls generally charged by distance. The maximum speed limit is 120 km/h for cars. Notable structures include some of the world's longest bridges and high-pier bridges in the mountainous southwest, such as the Duge Bridge, the highest in the world.
