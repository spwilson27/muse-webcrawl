Der Ring des Nibelungen (The Ring of the Nibelung), WWV 86, is a cycle of four German-language epic music dramas (often called "operas") composed by Richard Wagner. The works are based loosely on characters from Germanic heroic legend, namely Norse legendary sagas and the Nibelungenlied. The composer and librettist himself designated the cycle a Bühnenfestspiel ("stage festival play"), structured in three days with a prologue (Vorabend).

The four operas

The four parts that constitute the Ring cycle are, in sequence:

  1. Das Rheingold (The Rhinegold) — the prologue or Vorabend
  2. Die Walküre (The Valkyrie) — the first day
  3. Siegfried — the second day
  4. Götterdämmerung (Twilight of the Gods) — the third day

Story

The plot revolves around a magic ring that grants the power to rule the world, forged by the Nibelung dwarf Alberich from gold he stole from the Rhine. Several mythic figures struggle for possession of the ring, including Wotan, chief of the gods; the hero Siegfried ultimately wins it, but is betrayed and murdered. His lover Brünnhilde returns the ring to the Rhinemaidens as she immolates herself on Siegfried's funeral pyre, and Valhalla burns as the gods perish.

Composition and performance

Wagner wrote the libretti in reverse order, beginning with Siegfrieds Tod (which became Götterdämmerung) in 1848 and completing the poem of the whole cycle in 1853; the music was composed between 1853 and 1874. The first complete performance took place at the Bayreuth Festspielhaus in August 1876, in a festival mounted under the patronage of Ludwig II of Bavaria. A full performance of the cycle takes place over four nights and runs roughly 15 hours. The cycle is famous for its dense web of leitmotifs associated with characters, places, ideas and plot elements.
