Kazanlak

Country: Bulgaria
Province: Stara Zagora
Population: about 44,000
Known for: Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak; Rose Valley; Festival of Roses

Kazanlak (Bulgarian: Казанлък) is a town in Stara Zagora Province, Bulgaria, situated in the middle of the plain of the same name, at the foot of the Balkan mountain range, at the eastern end of the Rose Valley. It is the administrative centre of Kazanlak Municipality and is best known for the nearby Thracian Tomb of Kazanlak, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and for the production of rose oil.

History

The region has been inhabited since the Neolithic era. In the 4th–3rd centuries BC the area formed part of the Thracian Odrysian kingdom, and the nearby city of Seuthopolis, capital of King Seuthes III, now lies submerged beneath the Koprinka reservoir. Numerous Thracian burial mounds in the surrounding "Valley of the Thracian Rulers" have yielded rich finds, including the Kazanlak Tomb, discovered in 1944, with its celebrated Hellenistic frescoes.

The modern town developed during the Ottoman period and became a centre of rose-oil distillation and crafts. During the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78 the area saw fighting around the Shipka Pass nearby.

Rose Valley

Kazanlak lies in the Rose Valley, where the oil-bearing Damask rose (Rosa × damascena) has been cultivated since the 17th century. Bulgaria is one of the world's largest producers of rose oil, a key ingredient in perfumery, and the harvest is celebrated each June with the Festival of Roses, which attracts visitors from around the world. The town's Rose Museum documents the industry.

Economy

Besides rose products, Kazanlak is known for the Arsenal weapons factory, founded in 1924, one of Bulgaria's largest employers, and for the manufacture of musical instruments and hydraulic machinery.
