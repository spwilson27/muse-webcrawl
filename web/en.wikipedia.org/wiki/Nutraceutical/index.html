Nutraceutical

A nutraceutical is a food or food component that is claimed to provide health and medical benefits, including the prevention and treatment of disease. The term, a portmanteau of "nutrition" and "pharmaceutical," was coined in 1989 by Stephen DeFelice, founder of the Foundation for Innovation in Medicine. Products marketed as nutraceuticals range from isolated nutrients and dietary supplements to herbal products, functional foods, and processed foods such as fortified cereals and drinks.


Nutraceutical products span supplements, herbal preparations, and fortified foods.

Regulation

"Nutraceutical" has no single legal definition. In the United States, such products are generally regulated as dietary supplements under the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994, which does not require proof of efficacy before marketing; structure-and-function claims are permitted provided they carry a disclaimer that they have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. The European Union requires authorisation of health claims by the European Food Safety Authority, while Canada licenses "natural health products" and Japan has a longstanding category of Foods for Specified Health Uses (FOSHU).

Criticism

Critics note that many nutraceutical claims rest on weak or preliminary evidence, that products vary in potency and purity, and that aggressive marketing can blur the line between food and medicine. Supporters point to examples with substantial evidence, such as plant sterols for cholesterol reduction and omega-3 fatty acids, and to consumer demand for dietary approaches to health.
