Interactive Connectivity Establishment

AbbreviationICE
StandardRFC 8445 (2018); originally RFC 5245
RelatedSTUN, TURN, WebRTC, SIP

Interactive Connectivity Establishment (ICE) is a technique used in computer networking to find ways for two computers to talk to each other as directly as possible in peer-to-peer networking, even when one or both sit behind network address translators (NATs) or firewalls. It is standardized by the IETF in RFC 8445 and is a core component of WebRTC.

How it works

Each endpoint gathers a list of candidate addresses: its own local (host) addresses, server-reflexive addresses learned via STUN, and relayed addresses allocated on TURN servers as a last resort. Candidates are exchanged through a signalling channel (for example, a SIP exchange or a WebRTC offer/answer), prioritized, and then tested in pairs with STUN connectivity checks. The first pair that passes checks in both directions is nominated for the media or data stream; if no direct path works, traffic falls back to the TURN relay.

Use

ICE underpins browser-to-browser audio and video calls (WebRTC), many VoIP systems, online games and remote-access tools. Its strategy of trying every plausible path simultaneously makes connections robust across the wide variety of NAT behaviours found on the public internet, at the cost of some setup complexity and chatter.
