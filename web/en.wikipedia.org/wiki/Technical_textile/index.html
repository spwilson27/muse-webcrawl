Technical textile
DefinitionTextile materials manufactured primarily for functional performance rather than aesthetics
Common fibresPolyester, nylon, aramid, carbon, glass, UHMWPE
SectorsAgrotech, Buildtech, Geotech, Indutech, Medtech, Mobiltech, Protech, Sportech and others

Technical textiles are textile materials and products manufactured primarily for their technical performance and functional properties rather than for aesthetic or decorative characteristics. They range from woven geotextiles that stabilise roads and embankments to woven and knitted medical implants, ballistic protective clothing, automotive airbags and tyre cord, filtration media, and fibre-reinforced composites for aircraft.

Classification

The industry trade-fair organiser Messe Frankfurt popularised a twelve-fold classification: Agrotech (agriculture), Buildtech (construction), Clothtech (clothing components), Geotech (geotextiles), Hometech (furniture and flooring components), Indutech (industrial applications), Medtech (medicine and hygiene), Mobiltech (transportation), Oekotech (environmental protection), Packtech (packaging), Protech (protection) and Sportech (sports).

Materials and manufacture

Technical textiles employ high-performance fibres such as aramids (e.g. Kevlar), carbon fibre, glass fibre, ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene and high-tenacity polyester, formed into woven, knitted, braided and nonwoven structures. Nonwovens dominate volume in hygiene, filtration and geotextile markets. Finishing treatments add flame retardancy, antimicrobial action, water repellency or conductivity; "smart textiles" integrate sensors and electronics.

Market

Technical textiles account for a substantial and growing share of global fibre consumption — roughly a quarter to a third by volume — with the fastest growth in medical, transport and construction applications in Asia.
