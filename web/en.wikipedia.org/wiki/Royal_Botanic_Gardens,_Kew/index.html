The Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew — generally known as Kew Gardens — is a 132-hectare (330-acre) botanic garden in south-west London, between Richmond and Kew, that houses the largest and most diverse botanical and mycological collections in the world. It is one of London's top visitor attractions and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003. The gardens at Kew are managed by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, a non-departmental public body sponsored by the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs, which also runs Wakehurst in Sussex, home of the Millennium Seed Bank.

History

The gardens trace their origin to the exotic garden laid out at Kew Park by William Chambers and others for Augusta, Princess of Wales, from 1759; the botanist Sir Joseph Banks effectively directed Kew under George III, sending out plant collectors worldwide and making it a centre of economic botany. In 1840 the gardens passed to the state, and under the first official director, Sir William Hooker, and his son Joseph Dalton Hooker the grounds were enlarged, the iconic glasshouses built, and Kew's role in the botany of empire — distributing rubber, tea, quinine (cinchona), and sisal around the world — established.

The gardens and collections

Kew contains some 50,000 living plant taxa, together with one of the world's largest herbaria (over 7 million preserved specimens) and a vast library and archive. Landmark structures include the Palm House (1844–48, by Decimus Burton and Richard Turner), the Temperate House (1860–99, the world's largest surviving Victorian glasshouse), the Princess of Wales Conservatory (1987), the Alpine House, the 18-metre-high Xstrata Treetop Walkway (2008), and the Pagoda (1762) by William Chambers. The site also includes Kew Palace, the smallest of the British royal palaces.

Science

Kew's scientists conduct research in plant and fungal taxonomy, conservation, and sustainable use; its Millennium Seed Bank at Wakehurst stores billions of seeds from tens of thousands of wild species as insurance against extinction. Kew's Plants of the World Online database and periodic State of the World's Plants and Fungi reports are key global references.
