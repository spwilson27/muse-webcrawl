Generalisability

FieldResearch methodology, statistics
Related conceptsExternal validity, sampling, transferability

Generalisability (also spelled generalizability) is the extent to which the findings of a research study can be applied to other populations, settings, times, or contexts beyond those directly investigated. It is closely related to the concept of external validity in experimental design.

Bases of generalisation

In quantitative research, generalisation from a sample to a population rests on sampling methodology: probability samples drawn representatively from a defined population support statistical generalisation, provided sample sizes are adequate and response biases are controlled. In experimental research, generalisability also depends on whether the treatment, measures, and setting of the study resemble those to which results are to be applied.

Qualitative research

In qualitative research, statistical generalisation is usually not the goal; instead, concepts such as transferability (the reader's judgement, aided by thick description, of whether findings apply elsewhere), analytic generalisation to theory, and naturalistic generalisation are used. Critics note that over-generalising from unrepresentative samples — for example, drawing universal conclusions from studies of Western university students — is a common source of error in published research.
