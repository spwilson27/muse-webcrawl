European Court of Justice

European Court of Justice
Established:1952
Location:Kirchberg, Luxembourg City, Luxembourg
Composition:One judge from each member state, assisted by advocates-general
Part of:Court of Justice of the European Union

The European Court of Justice (ECJ), formally just the Court of Justice, is the supreme court of the European Union in matters of European Union law. Together with the General Court, it forms the Court of Justice of the European Union. Its task is to ensure that "the law is observed" in the interpretation and application of the EU treaties, and it is seated in the Kirchberg quarter of Luxembourg City.

Jurisdiction and procedure

The Court rules on actions brought by member states, EU institutions, and, in limited circumstances, individuals; gives preliminary rulings on the interpretation of EU law at the request of national courts; and hears infringement proceedings brought by the European Commission against member states that fail to comply with EU law. Its judgments on preliminary references are binding on the referring court and have shaped foundational doctrines such as direct effect and the primacy of EU law, established in cases including Van Gend en Loos (1963) and Costa v ENEL (1964).

Composition

The Court consists of one judge from each member state, appointed for renewable six-year terms, assisted by advocates-general who deliver independent opinions on cases. Proceedings are conducted in the language of the case, with French as the Court's internal working language, and its decisions are issued as single collegiate judgments without dissenting opinions.
