David (Donatello)

David is the title of two statues of the biblical hero by the Italian Renaissance sculptor Donatello: an early marble version (1408–1409) and a far more famous bronze version of the 1440s. The bronze David, now in the Bargello in Florence, was revolutionary as the first unsupported standing work in bronze cast since classical antiquity and the first free-standing nude male statue since antiquity.

The bronze David

Commissioned by the Medici, the bronze depicts a youthful, almost adolescent David standing in contrapposto over the head of Goliath, wearing only boots and a laurel-decked hat, a sword in his right hand and a stone in his left. A feather from Goliath's helmet brushes up David's inner leg, a detail often read as sensuous. The statue stood in the courtyard of the Palazzo Medici in Florence and carried an inscription celebrating David as a defender of the city against tyrants.

The marble David

The earlier marble David, carved for Florence Cathedral and later moved to the Palazzo della Signoria, is a clothed, Gothic-influenced figure that already shows Donatello's naturalism developing beyond medieval models.

Legacy

The bronze David is considered a masterpiece of Renaissance sculpture and a foundational work in the revival of classical forms; later Davids by Verrocchio and Michelangelo followed its Florentine civic symbolism.
