Duncan Grant

Duncan James Corrowr Grant
Born: 21 January 1885, Rothiemurchus, Scotland
Died: 8 May 1978, Aldermaston, England
Known for: Painting, decorative arts
Movement: Post-Impressionism

Duncan Grant (1885–1978) was a Scottish painter and designer, a central figure of the Bloomsbury Group and one of the leading British Post-Impressionists of his generation. He was a cousin of Lytton Strachey and studied at the Westminster School of Art and the Slade, and in Paris under Jacques-Émile Blanche, absorbing the influence of Matisse, Derain and Picasso.

Career

Grant exhibited in Roger Fry's landmark Post-Impressionist exhibitions of 1910 and 1912 and co-founded the Omega Workshops (1913–1919) with Fry and Vanessa Bell, applying modernist design to furniture, textiles and interiors. In 1916 he moved with Vanessa Bell to Charleston farmhouse in Sussex, whose painted interiors became the Bloomsbury circle's country home; he lived and worked there for the rest of his life with Bell, with whom he had a daughter, Angelica.

Work and legacy

A conscientious objector in the First World War, Grant later received major commissions including murals for the liner RMS Queen Mary (rejected as too modern), decorations at St Blaise Chapel, and designs for textiles and ceramics. His portraits, landscapes and still lifes — marked by lyrical colour and sensual line — brought him wide recognition, and he continued painting vigorously into his nineties.
