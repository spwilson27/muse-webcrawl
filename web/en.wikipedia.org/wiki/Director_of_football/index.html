Director of football

A director of football (DoF) is a senior management role in association football clubs, responsible for overseeing aspects of the club's football operations outside of the day-to-day coaching of the first team. The role is common in European football and has become increasingly prevalent in English football since the 2000s.

Responsibilities

The director of football typically acts as an intermediary between the head coach (or manager) and the club's board of directors. Their duties often include:

  • Player recruitment and scouting strategy
  • Contract negotiations with players and agents
  • Overseeing the club's academy and youth development
  • Long-term squad planning and transfer policy
  • Sports science, medical and performance departments

Variations of the title

Similar roles are known by other titles, including sporting director, technical director, and in North American sports, general manager. The exact scope varies by club; in some cases the DoF wields considerable power over transfers, while in others the head coach retains final say.

Notable directors of football

Well-known figures who have held the role include Monchi (at Sevilla and Roma), Michael Edwards (Liverpool), Txiki Begiristain (Manchester City), and Ralf Rangnick. The position has sometimes been a source of friction when responsibilities overlap with those of a traditional manager, as in British football where managers historically controlled transfers.
