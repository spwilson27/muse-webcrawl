Active protection system

An active protection system (APS) is a system designed to prevent anti-tank missiles and other projectiles from acquiring and/or destroying a target, most commonly an armoured fighting vehicle. APS measures are divided into "soft-kill" measures, which confuse or decoy the incoming weapon's guidance (such as jammers, smoke and dazzlers), and "hard-kill" measures, which physically intercept and destroy the incoming projectile before it reaches the vehicle.

[Image: Tank fitted with an active protection system]

History

The Soviet Union developed the first operational hard-kill system, Drozd, fitted to some tanks in the late 1970s and used in Afghanistan. Later systems include the Russian Arena, the Israeli Trophy — which has intercepted live fire in combat since 2011 and has been adopted by several Western armies — the German AMAP-ADS/StrikeShield, and the American Iron Curtain and Quick Kill programmes.

Operation

A typical hard-kill APS uses radar or other sensors to detect an incoming threat, track its trajectory, and fire a countermeasure — a blast, projectile or explosively formed penetrator — timed to destroy or deflect the warhead metres from the vehicle. Soft-kill systems such as the Soviet Shtora use electro-optical jammers to disrupt the guidance of anti-tank guided missiles.

Limitations

APS cannot easily defeat kinetic-energy penetrator rounds travelling at very high speed, and hard-kill countermeasures pose some risk to nearby infantry. Cost, weight and power demands also limit deployment.
