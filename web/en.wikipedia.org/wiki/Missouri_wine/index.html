Missouri wine

Missouri wine refers to wine made from grapes grown in Missouri. German immigrants established vineyards along the Missouri River in the 1830s and 1840s, in an area called the Rhineland around Hermann. Before Prohibition, Missouri was the second-largest wine-producing state in the United States.

History

Missouri rootstock saved European vineyards in the 1870s: nurseryman Hermann Jaeger and entomologist Charles Valentine Riley helped supply phylloxera-resistant American rootstock to France. Prohibition destroyed the industry; revival began in the 1960s–1980s. Augusta became the first federally recognised American Viticultural Area (AVA) in 1980.

Grapes

The state grape is the Norton (Cynthiana), a native American variety producing dry red wine. Other common varieties include Chambourcin, Vidal blanc, Vignoles, Chardonel, and Seyval blanc.
