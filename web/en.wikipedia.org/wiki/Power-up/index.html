Power-up

Power-ups are a staple game mechanic, granting temporary or permanent advantages.

In video games, a power-up is an object that adds temporary benefits or extra abilities to the player's character or units, as a reward for exploration, skill or luck. Power-ups are usually obtained by touching an in-world item, and their effects may be instant or lasting, and may accumulate or expire after a set time. The term is also used more loosely for permanent upgrades and collectible items.

History

The concept dates to early arcade games: the power pellets in Pac-Man (1980) that briefly let the player eat the ghosts are a canonical example. The Super Mario series popularised the term and the design pattern: the Super Mushroom enlarges Mario, the Fire Flower grants a projectile attack, and the Starman confers brief invincibility. Shooter games use power-ups for health, armour, ammunition and special weapons, as in the items scattered through the arenas of Doom and Quake; racing games give boosts and weapons in titles such as Mario Kart.

Design

Power-ups serve to vary gameplay, reward risk-taking, and create moment-to-moment decisions about when to grab and use an advantage. Designers distinguish between immediate-effect items (restored health), timed effects (invincibility), permanent upgrades, and collectibles that must be accumulated. Placement matters: valuable power-ups are often guarded or located in exposed positions to balance their benefit. Excessively strong power-ups may be counterbalanced by short duration, by loss on taking damage, or by "rubber-banding" mechanics that direct advantages to trailing players.
