Joseph ben Ephraim Karo

Born1488, Toledo or Portugal
DiedMarch 24, 1575 (13 Nisan 5335), Safed, Ottoman Palestine
OccupationRabbi, legal codifier, kabbalist

Joseph ben Ephraim Karo (1488 – March 24, 1575), also spelled Yosef Caro, was a prominent rabbi, Talmudist, and kabbalist, best known as the author of the Shulchan Aruch, the most widely accepted codification of Jewish law (halakha) to this day. He is often referred to simply as HaMechaber ("the Author") or Maran ("our master").

Life

Karo was born in 1488, probably in Toledo, Spain, or in Portugal. Following the expulsion of the Jews from Spain in 1492, his family moved through Portugal to the Ottoman Empire, settling first in Constantinople and then Adrianople, where he studied under his father and uncle. After travels through Nikopol and Salonica, he eventually settled in Safed in the Galilee around the mid-1530s. Safed was then a center of Jewish scholarship and Kabbalah. In 1538 he received ordination from Jacob Berab as part of an attempt to renew classical semicha. Karo headed a large yeshiva in Safed and died there in 1575.

Works

Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch

Karo's magnum opus, Beit Yosef, is a vast commentary on the Arba'ah Turim, tracing every law to its Talmudic sources and analyzing the rulings of earlier authorities. He labored on it for some twenty years (1522–1542). Aiming to unify Jewish practice, Karo generally decided each question by majority among three "pillars" of halakha: Isaac Alfasi, Maimonides, and Asher ben Jehiel. The Shulchan Aruch ("Set Table"), first published in Venice in 1565, is a distilled code drawn from Beit Yosef, stating practical rulings without their reasoning. Together with the glosses of Moses Isserles (the Mappah), which added Ashkenazi practice, it became the standard code of Jewish law.

Other works

His Kesef Mishneh is a major commentary on Maimonides' Mishneh Torah, identifying the sources of Maimonides' rulings. Karo also wrote responsa, commentaries, and the mystical diary Maggid Mesharim, recording discourses he attributed to a heavenly mentor (maggid) — the personified Mishnah — over some fifty years.

Legacy

The Shulchan Aruch remains authoritative for Orthodox Judaism, and Karo is regarded as the leading halakhic authority of the last five centuries. His synagogue and tomb in Safed are sites of pilgrimage.
