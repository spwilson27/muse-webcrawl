Body image

Body image is a person's thoughts, feelings and perception of the aesthetics or sexual attractiveness of their own body. The concept of body image is used in a number of disciplines, including neuroscience, psychology, medicine, psychiatry, psychoanalysis, philosophy, and cultural and feminist studies; the term is often used in the media.

Body image can be negative ("body negativity"), positive ("body positivity") or neutral. A person with a negative body image may feel self-conscious or ashamed, and may feel that others are more attractive than them. In a time where social media use is high, people are often exposed to unrealistic body ideals, which can lead to feelings of inadequacy.

History of the term

The term "body image" was first coined by the Austrian neurologist and psychoanalyst Paul Schilder in his book The Image and Appearance of the Human Body (1935). Schilder described body image as the picture of our own body which we form in our mind — the way in which the body appears to ourselves.

Body image disturbance

Disturbances of body image are central to several psychological conditions, including body dysmorphic disorder, anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa and other eating disorders. Negative body image is associated with low self-esteem, depression and anxiety. Research suggests that exposure to thin-ideal media imagery can increase body dissatisfaction, particularly among adolescents and young women, though men are also affected, often through a muscular ideal.

Body positivity

The body positivity movement, which gained prominence in the 2010s, advocates acceptance of all bodies regardless of size, shape, skin tone, gender and physical abilities, while challenging unrealistic beauty standards. Critics note tensions between the movement's health messages and the medical risks of obesity, and some argue the term has been diluted by commercial use.
