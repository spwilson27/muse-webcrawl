Student financial aid

Student financial aid (or student aid) is financial support given to individuals enrolled in post-secondary education, to help meet the costs of tuition, fees, books, housing and living expenses. Aid may come from governments, educational institutions, employers, charities and private lenders.

Types

Gift aid — grants and scholarships, such as the United States federal Pell Grant — does not need to be repaid and may be awarded on the basis of financial need, academic merit, or other criteria. Self-help aid includes student loans, which must be repaid with interest, and work-study programmes, which provide part-time employment. In the United States, eligibility for federal aid is determined through the FAFSA form, which computes an index of a family's ability to pay.

Systems by country

Systems vary widely: many European countries charge low or no tuition with state maintenance support, while in the United States, United Kingdom (since tuition fee reforms) and Australia, government-backed income-contingent or mortgage-style loans are a major source of funding, giving rise to large national student debt totals.
