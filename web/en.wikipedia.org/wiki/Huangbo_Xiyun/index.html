Huangbo Xiyun, Tang-dynasty Chan master.

Huangbo Xiyun (Chinese: 黃檗希運; died 850) was an influential Chinese Chan Buddhist master of the Tang dynasty. A student of Baizhang Huaihai, he taught at Mount Huangbo in present-day Jiangxi, from which he took his name. He is best known as the teacher of Linji Yixuan, founder of the Linji school of Chan (known in Japanese as Rinzai Zen), and as the author of teachings recorded in the Ch'uan-hsin fa-yao ("Essentials of Mind Transmission") and the Wanling record.

Teaching

Huangbo taught the doctrine of "One Mind" (or "Mind-only"): that there is nothing outside the one Buddha-mind, and that seeking the Buddha apart from one's own mind is futile. He dismissed reliance on scriptures, rituals, merit-making and gradual cultivation, insisting that enlightenment is immediate recognition of one's original mind. His sermons, recorded by his lay disciple Pei Xiu — a high official and later chancellor who also wrote his biographical funerary inscription — are marked by an uncompromising, iconoclastic style, including blows and shouts as teaching devices.

Legacy

Through Linji, Huangbo became an ancestral figure of one of the two surviving houses of Chan/Zen. His sayings, translated into English notably by John Blofeld as The Zen Teaching of Huang Po, became among the most widely read Chan texts in the West. A famous anecdote describes the tall master ("a giant of a man") slapping the future emperor Xuanzong, then a novice monk, for bowing to an image of the Buddha — remarking that one should not "seek the Buddha" through images.
