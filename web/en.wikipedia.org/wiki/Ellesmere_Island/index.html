Ellesmere Island (Inuktitut: Umingmak Nuna, "land of muskoxen") is Canada's northernmost island and the third largest island in the Arctic Archipelago, lying in the Qikiqtaaluk Region of Nunavut. It is separated from Greenland by the Nares Strait; its northern cape, Cape Columbia, is the northernmost point of land in Canada.

Geography

Large parts of the island are covered by ice caps and glaciers, including some of the largest ice shelves in the Arctic, which have been breaking up as the region warms. The northern coast is indented by deep fjords, and Quttinirpaaq National Park protects much of the north. Barbeau Peak (2,616 m) is the highest mountain in Nunavut.

History and settlement

Paleo-Eskimo peoples occupied the island from about 2000 BCE, and Norse voyagers likely visited it; its modern name honours Francis Egerton, 1st Earl of Ellesmere. The only permanent settlement is Grise Fiord, Canada's northernmost civilian community, founded in 1953 by relocated Inuit families. The Canadian Forces Station Alert, the world's northernmost permanently inhabited place, sits at the island's northeastern tip, and the research station at Eureka serves scientific work.
