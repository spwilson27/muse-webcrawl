Vehicle regulations

ScopeMotor vehicle design, construction and equipment
Key bodiesUNECE WP.29, NHTSA, national type-approval authorities

Vehicle regulations are the legal requirements governing the design, construction, equipment and performance of motor vehicles. They cover matters such as lighting, braking, crashworthiness, occupant protection, emissions and noise, and they determine whether a vehicle may be sold or registered for use on public roads in a given market.

International harmonisation

Much international harmonisation work is carried out by the World Forum for Harmonization of Vehicle Regulations (WP.29), a body of the United Nations Economic Commission for Europe. Regulations developed under the 1958 and 1998 UN agreements are adopted by many countries, allowing a vehicle approved in one contracting party to be accepted, wholly or in part, in others. The European Union operates a whole-vehicle type-approval framework built largely on UN regulations.

United States

In the United States, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) issues the Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards (FMVSS), covering crash avoidance, crashworthiness and post-crash survivability. Unlike the type-approval systems used elsewhere, the US system is based on manufacturer self-certification. Emissions are regulated separately by the Environmental Protection Agency and, in California, by the California Air Resources Board.

Areas covered

Typical regulatory areas include seat belts and airbags, lighting and signalling devices, mirrors and visibility, tyres, glazing, anti-theft devices, pedestrian protection, and limits on exhaust emissions and external noise. Periodic roadworthiness inspections, where required, check that vehicles in service continue to meet minimum standards.
