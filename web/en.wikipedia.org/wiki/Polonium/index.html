Polonium

Polonium is a chemical element with symbol Po and atomic number 84 — a highly radioactive metal discovered by Marie and Pierre Curie in July 1898 in pitchblende, and named by Marie Curie after her native country of Poland (then partitioned and not independent). It was the first element discovered by the Curies in their radioactivity research.

Polonium-210, its most common isotope (half-life ~138 days), is an intense alpha emitter used in tiny amounts in anti-static devices and (as Po–Be sources) neutron initiators and space heaters; it is also extraordinarily toxic — the poison used in the 2006 assassination of Alexander Litvinenko in London, identified by its alpha signature at HPA laboratories. Polonium occurs naturally in minute quantities in uranium ores and accumulates in tobacco plants from phosphate fertilisers, contributing to smoke's radioactivity.
