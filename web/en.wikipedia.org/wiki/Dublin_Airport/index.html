Dublin Airport

Dublin Airport (Irish: Aerfort Bhaile Átha Cliath) (IATA: DUB, ICAO: EIDW) is an international airport serving Dublin, Ireland. It is operated by DAA (formerly Dublin Airport Authority). The airport is located in Collinstown, 7 km (4.3 mi) north of Dublin city centre, in County Fingal. It is by far the busiest airport in Ireland, handling the great majority of the country's air passengers, and is the headquarters of Ireland's flag carrier Aer Lingus and of Ryanair, Europe's largest airline group.

History

Construction of the airport began in 1937 at Collinstown, a former First World War airfield site, and the first flight — an Aer Lingus service to Liverpool — departed on 19 January 1940. The original terminal building, designed by Desmond FitzGerald, is an early example of modernist airport architecture. The airport grew rapidly with the jet age; a new runway (10/28) opened in 1989. Terminal 2 opened in November 2010, and a second parallel runway (10L/28R, the "North Runway") opened in August 2022.

Operations

Dublin Airport has two terminals. Terminal 1 is used principally by Ryanair and other short-haul carriers, while Terminal 2 is used by Aer Lingus, long-haul carriers, and U.S. airlines. Dublin is one of few airports outside North America with United States border preclearance facilities, allowing passengers to complete U.S. immigration and customs before departure. The airport is a major transatlantic gateway, with services to numerous destinations in the United States and Canada, as well as Europe and the Middle East. A planning-imposed annual passenger cap of 32 million, dating from the Terminal 2 permission, has been the subject of ongoing legal and political debate as traffic recovered beyond that level after the COVID-19 pandemic.

Ground transport

The airport is connected to Dublin city centre by bus and coach services and by motorway; a proposed MetroLink rail line would link the airport to the city centre.
