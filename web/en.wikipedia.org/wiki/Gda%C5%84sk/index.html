Gdańsk (German: Danzig) is a city on the Baltic coast of northern Poland, situated at the mouth of the Motława River. With a population of about 486,000, it is Poland's principal seaport and the capital of the Pomeranian Voivodeship; together with Gdynia and Sopot it forms the Tricity metropolitan area.

History

Founded in the 10th century, Gdańsk became a flourishing Hanseatic port trading grain and timber. It passed between Polish, Teutonic Order, Prussian and German control over the centuries, and after the First World War became the Free City of Danzig under the League of Nations. The first shots of the Second World War were fired at the Polish post office and the Westerplatte peninsula in Gdańsk on 1 September 1939.

Solidarity

In August 1980, strikes at the Gdańsk Shipyard led by Lech Wałęsa gave birth to Solidarity, the first independent trade union in the Soviet bloc, a key event in the eventual fall of communism in Eastern Europe.

Landmarks

The Main Town's Long Market (Długi Targ) features the Neptune Fountain, Artus Court and the great Gothic St Mary's Church; the medieval Gdańsk Crane stands over the Motława waterfront.
