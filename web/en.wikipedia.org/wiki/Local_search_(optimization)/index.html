Local search (optimization)

In computer science, local search is a metaheuristic method for solving computationally hard optimization problems. Starting from a candidate solution, it iteratively moves to a neighbouring solution — one obtained by a small, local change — chosen according to a criterion that considers only information about the immediate neighbourhood. The search stops at a solution that is optimal with respect to its neighbourhood, a local optimum.

Methods

The simplest local search is hill climbing, which always moves to the best improving neighbour. Variations designed to escape local optima include:

  • Simulated annealing — sometimes accepts worse moves with a probability that decreases over time.
  • Tabu search — forbids recently visited solutions to force exploration.
  • Variable neighbourhood search — systematically changes the neighbourhood structure.
  • Large neighbourhood search — destroys and repairs large parts of the solution.

Algorithms such as the Lin–Kernighan heuristic for the travelling salesman problem, and the 2-opt and k-opt moves, are classic examples of local search.

Applications

Local search is applied to problems including satisfiability (SAT), scheduling, vehicle routing, protein structure prediction and operations research problems where exact methods do not scale.
