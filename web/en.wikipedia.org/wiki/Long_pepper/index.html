Long pepper

Scientific namePiper longum
FamilyPiperaceae
OriginIndia (also Indonesia for Piper retrofractum)
HeatPungent, from piperine and piperlongumine

Long pepper (Piper longum), sometimes called Indian long pepper or pippali, is a flowering vine in the family Piperaceae, cultivated for its fruit, which is usually dried and used as a spice and seasoning. The fruit is a cluster of tiny berries embedded in a catkin-like spike about the size of a hazelnut catkin.

History

Long pepper reached Europe before black pepper and was highly prized in ancient Greece and Rome — it was the "pepper" of classical antiquity and one of the earliest spices traded from Asia, fetching very high prices. Its popularity declined after the introduction of black pepper and, later, chillies from the Americas, though it remains important in Indian cooking (in pickles, curries and the Ayurvedic trikatu mixture), North African spice blends and Indonesian cuisine, where the related Piper retrofractum is used.

Flavour

The taste resembles black pepper but hotter, with sweet, earthy and slightly resinous undertones. The heat comes largely from the alkaloid piperine, shared with black pepper, and the compound piperlongumine, which has attracted research interest.
