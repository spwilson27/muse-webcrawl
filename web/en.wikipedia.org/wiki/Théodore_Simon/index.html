Théodore Simon (10 July 1873 – 4 September 1961) was a French psychiatrist and psychologist. Together with Alfred Binet, he developed the first practical intelligence test, the Binet–Simon scale.

Career

Simon trained as a physician and worked in asylums for people with intellectual disabilities, including at Perray-Vaucluse and Saint-Yon. There he met Alfred Binet, who was studying the measurement of intelligence. In 1905 Binet and Simon published their first scale of intelligence — thirty tasks of increasing difficulty designed to identify schoolchildren needing special education — followed by revisions in 1908 and 1911 that introduced the concept of mental age. The scale was later adapted in the United States as the Stanford–Binet Intelligence Scales.

After Binet's early death in 1911, Simon continued work in psychiatry and psychometrics, directed hospitals, and founded the journal L'Année psychologique's successor activities; he remained a leading figure in French psychology for decades.
