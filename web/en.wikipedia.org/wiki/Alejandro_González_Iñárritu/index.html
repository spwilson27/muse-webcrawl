Alejandro González Iñárritu

Alejandro González Iñárritu (born 15 August 1963) is a Mexican filmmaker. He is primarily known for making modern psychological drama films about the human condition. He is the first Mexican director to win the Academy Award for Best Director, and one of only three directors — with John Ford and Joseph L. Mankiewicz — to win it in consecutive years.

Career

After a career in radio and advertising in Mexico, González Iñárritu broke through with his feature debut Amores perros (2000), a triptych of intertwined Mexico City stories. He continued the "death trilogy" with 21 Grams (2003) and Babel (2006), the latter winning him the Best Director prize at Cannes. Biutiful (2010) was followed by Birdman (2014), shot to appear as a single continuous take, which won the Academy Award for Best Picture and Best Director. The Revenant (2015) brought him a second consecutive Best Director Oscar. His virtual-reality installation Carne y Arena (2017) received a Special Achievement Academy Award, and his semi-autobiographical Bardo premiered in 2022. Together with Alfonso Cuarón and Guillermo del Toro he is one of the "Three Amigos" of Mexican cinema.
