The Arthur Ravenel Jr. Bridge is a cable-stayed bridge over the Cooper River in South Carolina, connecting downtown Charleston with Mount Pleasant. Opened in 2005, it carries U.S. Route 17 across the harbor and replaced two aging cantilever truss bridges, the John P. Grace Memorial Bridge (1929) and the Silas N. Pearman Bridge (1966).

Design and construction

The bridge's main span of 1,546 feet (471 m) was, at its completion, among the longest cable-stayed spans in North America. Two diamond-shaped towers rise 575 feet (175 m) above the water and support the deck with 128 stay cables. The deck accommodates eight traffic lanes—four more than the two bridges it replaced—plus a 12-foot-wide shared bicycle and pedestrian path, named Wonders' Way in memory of Garrett Wonders, a cyclist killed in a traffic collision, whose advocacy helped secure the path.

The structure was designed to withstand severe hurricanes of the magnitude of Hurricane Hugo (1989) and major earthquakes, and the towers rest on drilled-shaft rock-socketed foundations protected by man-made rock islands. Construction began in 2001 and the bridge opened a year ahead of schedule, on July 16, 2005, following a week of public celebration that included a walk across the span and a concert.

Financing and naming

The project cost roughly $632 million. Funding combined federal loans, a State Infrastructure Bank agreement, sales-tax revenue from Charleston County, and other state sources. Former state senator Arthur Ravenel Jr. played a central role in securing funding for the bridge, which was named in his honor despite initial controversy over financing a bridge through such a large state commitment.

Events

The bridge is the centerpiece of the annual Cooper River Bridge Run, a 10-kilometre race and walk that crosses the span each spring and draws tens of thousands of participants. Its pedestrian path is used year-round by walkers, runners, and cyclists and offers views over Charleston Harbor and the Atlantic approaches.
