Erechtheus

Erechtheus (Ancient Greek: Ἐρεχθεύς) was a legendary king of Athens in Greek mythology. He was closely associated with the goddess Athena and with Poseidon, under the cult title Poseidon Erechtheus, and was venerated in the Erechtheion on the Acropolis of Athens.

Mythology

In some traditions Erechtheus was born of the earth (Gaia) after Hephaestus's seed fell upon her, and was raised by Athena, who placed the infant in a chest given to the daughters of Cecrops. He is often conflated or confused with Erichthonius, another earth-born early Athenian king; later sources sometimes distinguish two kings named Erechtheus. According to myth, Erechtheus went to war with Eleusis and sacrificed his daughters to secure victory, before being struck down by Poseidon (or by Zeus at Poseidon's request).

Cult

Erechtheus was one of the eponymous heroes of the Athenian tribes, and his tomb and cult were located in the Erechtheion, alongside the sacred olive tree of Athena and the salt spring of Poseidon.
