Radio direction finder

A radio direction finder (RDF) is a device for finding the direction (bearing) to a radio source. Before satellite navigation, RDF was a principal method of air and marine navigation, using fixed ground stations that took bearings on an aircraft or ship's transmissions, or airborne and shipborne sets that took bearings on ground non-directional beacons.

Principle

A loop antenna receives a signal with a figure-of-eight pattern, giving two maxima and two sharp minima ("nulls") 180 degrees apart; operators rotated the loop to find the null, which gave a more precise bearing. A sense antenna resolved the 180-degree ambiguity. Later systems, such as the automatic direction finder (ADF) fitted to aircraft, combined these elements automatically.

History and uses

RDF was used extensively in both world wars for navigation and for locating enemy transmitters ("huff-duff" high-frequency direction finding helped locate U-boats in the Second World War). It remains in use for search and rescue homing on emergency beacons, for wildlife telemetry tracking, and in amateur radio "fox hunting" competitions.
