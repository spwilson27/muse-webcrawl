East Timor

East Timor, officially the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste, is a country in Southeast Asia comprising the eastern half of the island of Timor, the exclave of Oecusse on the island's north-western coast, and the nearby islands of Atauro and Jaco. Its capital and largest city is Dili. The country has a population of about 1.3 million.

History

Timor was colonised by Portugal in the 16th century and known as Portuguese Timor. After Portugal's Carnation Revolution, East Timor unilaterally declared independence in November 1975, but was invaded and occupied by Indonesia days later and annexed as its 27th province. The occupation was marked by a violent conflict and widespread human rights abuses. Following a UN-sponsored referendum in 1999, in which voters overwhelmingly rejected autonomy within Indonesia, East Timor became the first new sovereign state of the 21st century on 20 May 2002, after a period of UN transitional administration.

Geography and economy

The terrain is mountainous, with Mount Ramelau (Tatamailau) the highest point at 2,963 m. Portuguese and Tetum are the official languages. The economy depends heavily on petroleum and gas revenues from the Timor Sea, supplemented by coffee exports.
