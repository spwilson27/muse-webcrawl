Ancient Roman religion — glossary
ScopeTerms of Roman cult, priesthoods and ritual
Key sourcesVarro, Ovid's Fasti, Festus, inscriptions

This glossary of ancient Roman religion defines key terms used in the study of the religious practices of ancient Rome.

Selected terms

  • Augur — a priest who interpreted the will of the gods from the flight and behaviour of birds (auspicia).
  • Haruspex — a diviner, of Etruscan origin, who read omens in the entrails of sacrificed animals, especially the liver.
  • Lares — guardian deities of households, crossroads and the state, honoured at small shrines (lararia).
  • Penates — household gods of the store cupboard, kept with the Lares and Vesta.
  • Pontifex maximus — the chief priest of the Roman state religion, head of the college of pontiffs.
  • Pietas — dutiful respect toward the gods, one's family and the state; a cardinal Roman virtue.
  • Pax deorum — the "peace of the gods", the state of divine favour maintained by correct ritual.
  • Sacrum — that which is consecrated to the gods; opposed to profanum, the ordinary.
  • Vestal Virgins — priestesses of Vesta who tended Rome's sacred fire and vowed chastity for thirty years.
  • Numen — divine power or presence, the active will of a deity.
  • Genius — the attendant divine spirit of a person, place or institution.
  • Suovetaurilia — a major sacrifice of a pig (sus), sheep (ovis) and bull (taurus).
