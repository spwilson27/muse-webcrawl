The Battle of Santa Cruz de Tenerife was a naval action fought on 20 April 1657 during the Anglo-Spanish War (1654–1660), in which an English fleet under Admiral Robert Blake attacked a Spanish treasure fleet anchored in the harbour of Santa Cruz de Tenerife in the Canary Islands. Despite fighting under the guns of powerful shore defences, Blake destroyed the entire Spanish fleet without losing a single ship — one of the most celebrated feats of his career, accomplished only weeks before his death.

Background

Blake had been cruising to intercept the Spanish treasure fleets returning from the Americas. The Plate Fleet under Don Diego de Egües had taken refuge at Santa Cruz, where its silver was unloaded and stored ashore while the empty ships lay protected by forts and a boom, in a position the Spanish considered impregnable.

The battle

On 20 April Blake sailed directly into the bay with 23 ships, engaging the forts and the anchored fleet at close range. After hours of bombardment, English boarding parties and gunfire sank, burned or captured all sixteen Spanish ships. A favourable shift of wind then allowed the English fleet to withdraw with remarkably few casualties.

Aftermath

Although the treasure itself had already been landed and so escaped capture, the destruction of the fleet was a severe blow to Spanish finance and prestige. Blake died on the voyage home in August 1657. The action was later often compared with Nelson's attack on the same port in 1797, which failed.
