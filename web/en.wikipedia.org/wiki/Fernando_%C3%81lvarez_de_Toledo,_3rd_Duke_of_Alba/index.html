Fernando Álvarez de Toledo y Pimentel, 3rd Duke of Alba (29 October 1507 – 11 December 1582), known as the Grand Duke of Alba and in the Netherlands as the "Iron Duke", was a Spanish nobleman, general and statesman. He served under emperors Charles V and Philip II and was one of the most feared military commanders of the sixteenth century.

Military career

Alba fought in the Italian Wars, the Schmalkaldic War — where he played a leading role in the Imperial victory at Mühlberg in 1547 — and the campaign that captured Tunis in 1535. He was made a knight of the Order of the Golden Fleece and became one of Philip II's closest advisers.

Governor of the Netherlands

In 1567 Philip sent Alba to the Spanish Netherlands to suppress the Dutch Revolt. He established the Council of Troubles, nicknamed the "Council of Blood", which condemned thousands, including the counts of Egmont and Horn, who were executed in Brussels in 1568. His harsh taxes, notably the "Tenth Penny", deepened resistance, and his failure to end the revolt led to his recall in 1573. In Dutch memory he remains a byword for tyranny.

Conquest of Portugal

In 1580, in his seventies, Alba commanded the Spanish invasion of Portugal during the Portuguese succession crisis, defeating the forces of António, Prior of Crato, at the Battle of Alcântara and securing the Iberian Union for Philip II. He died in Lisbon in 1582.
