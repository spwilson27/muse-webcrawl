Vacation Bible school

Vacation Bible school (commonly abbreviated VBS) is a specialized form of religious education for children, usually run by local churches during school holidays — most often for one week in the summer. It combines Bible study with songs, crafts, games, skits and recreation, usually organized around a unifying theme.

History

The VBS movement traces its roots to the 1890s in New York City, where Eliza Hawes ran a summer "Everyday Bible School" for children of working families at Epiphany Baptist Church. The concept spread through American Protestant churches in the early 20th century, and publishers began producing standardized curricula, a practice continued today by denominations and Christian publishing houses.

Format

A typical program runs for half-days over five days and is staffed largely by volunteers. Children rotate through stations for Bible lessons, music, crafts, snacks and outdoor games, often grouped by age. Many churches make VBS free and open to the community as an outreach ministry; some hold it in the evenings to accommodate working parents.
