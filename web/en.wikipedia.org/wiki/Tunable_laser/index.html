Tunable laser

A tunable laser is a laser whose output wavelength can be adjusted in a controlled manner over a range of wavelengths. Tuning is achieved by selecting the emission wavelength within the gain bandwidth of the laser medium — for example by rotating a diffraction grating or prism in the cavity, adjusting an intracavity filter or etalon, or, in semiconductor lasers, varying the injection current or temperature. Gain media with broad emission bands, such as organic dyes and titanium-doped sapphire, permit especially wide tuning ranges.

TypeLaser
ExamplesDye laser, Ti:sapphire laser, external-cavity diode laser
Tuning methodsGrating, prism, etalon, current/temperature control
ApplicationsSpectroscopy, telecommunications, metrology

Applications

Tunable lasers are essential tools in high-resolution spectroscopy, where a narrow-linewidth source must be scanned across atomic or molecular absorption features. In optical communications, tunable transmitters can be set to any channel of a dense wavelength-division multiplexing grid, simplifying inventory. Swept-source tunable lasers are also the light source of choice for optical coherence tomography.
