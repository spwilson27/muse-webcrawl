North Ossetia, officially the Republic of North Ossetia–Alania (Ossetian: Цӕгат Ирыстон — Алани), is a republic of Russia situated in the North Caucasus on the northern slopes of the Greater Caucasus. Its capital and largest city is Vladikavkaz. Most of the population is ethnic Ossetian, an Iranian-speaking people descended in part from the Alans; the majority adhere to Orthodox Christianity, with a Muslim minority.

Geography

The republic spans the foothills and mountains along the upper Terek River and includes the Darial Gorge on the Georgian Military Road, the main pass through the central Caucasus. To the south lies South Ossetia, a disputed territory recognised by Russia as independent from Georgia; to the east is Ingushetia, with which North Ossetia has a territorial dispute over the Prigorodny District, the focus of a brief ethnic conflict in 1992.

History

The Ossetians' ancestors, the Alans — Iranian nomads related to the Sarmatians — settled in the central Caucasus in late antiquity. North Ossetia entered the Russian Empire in the late 18th century. In the Soviet era it became the North Ossetian ASSR within the Russian SFSR. The republic declared state sovereignty in 1990 and in 1995 adopted the name "Alania" alongside North Ossetia.

In September 2004, the republic was the scene of the Beslan school siege, in which Chechen-led militants seized School Number One in Beslan; the crisis ended with the deaths of more than 330 hostages, most of them children — the deadliest terrorist attack in modern Russian history.
