Spanish Socialist Workers' Party

The Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (Spanish: Partido Socialista Obrero Español, PSOE) is a social-democratic political party in Spain and, with the People's Party, one of the two major parties of the democratic era.

History

Founded in 1879 by Pablo Iglesias, the PSOE is one of the world's oldest socialist parties. It governed during parts of the Second Republic, was suppressed under Franco, and in exile and clandestinity maintained opposition. After legalisation in 1977, Felipe González led it to a landslide in 1982; it governed 1982–1996, modernising Spain and joining the European Community.

Recent decades

José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero governed 2004–2011. After defeats and internal crisis, Pedro Sánchez returned as leader, became prime minister in 2018 via a no-confidence motion, and led coalition governments with Unidas Podemos and later Sumar from 2020.

Organisation

The PSOE is federally structured with regional federations such as Catalonia's PSC. Its youth wing is Juventudes Socialistas.
