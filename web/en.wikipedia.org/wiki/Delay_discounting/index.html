Delay discounting

Delay discounting is the process by which a reward loses value the further into the future it is received; it describes the decreasing subjective value of an outcome as the delay to its receipt increases. Delay discounting is a central concept in behavioural economics, psychology and the study of impulsivity and addiction.

Measurement

Discounting is usually measured with choice tasks in which participants choose between a smaller, sooner reward and a larger, later one. The point at which a person switches preference — the indifference point — is found for several delays, and a discount function is fitted to the results.

Hyperbolic discounting

Human and animal choices are often better described by a hyperbolic function than by the exponential function assumed in classical economics. Hyperbolic discounting predicts preference reversals: a person may prefer £110 in 31 days over £100 in 30 days, yet prefer £100 now over £110 tomorrow, even though the interval is the same. The steepness of discounting is commonly summarised by a rate parameter k.

Clinical significance

Steeper delay discounting is associated with substance use disorders, gambling, obesity, ADHD and other conditions involving impulsive choice, and is studied as a transdiagnostic behavioural marker.
