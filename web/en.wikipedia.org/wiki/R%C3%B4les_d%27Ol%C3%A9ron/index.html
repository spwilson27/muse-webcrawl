Roles of Oléron

The Roles of Oléron (French: Rôles d'Oléron) were the first formal statement of "maritime law" in north-western Europe. They were promulgated by Eleanor of Aquitaine in about 1160, reportedly after her experience of the laws of the Kingdom of Jerusalem during the Second Crusade, which were based on the Rhodian Sea Law and the Lex mercatoria.

The Roles are a series of judgments and customary rules governing the relations between shipmasters, merchants, and mariners engaged in the wine trade between Gascony, Brittany, Normandy, and England. The name derives from the island of Oléron off the Atlantic coast of France, then a major centre of the wine and salt trades.

Content and influence

The articles address such matters as the duties of the master, the wages of the crew, pilotage, collision, jettison of cargo, and the treatment of wrecked goods. The Roles were incorporated into the Black Book of the Admiralty in England and influenced later maritime codes, including the Laws of Wisby and the Hanseatic ordinances. They are regarded as a landmark in the development of admiralty law.
