The World Lacrosse Championship is the international championship for men's field lacrosse, governed by World Lacrosse. It was first held in 1967 in Toronto, Canada, as part of Canada's centennial celebrations, and has been contested every four years since 1974.

History

The tournament has historically been dominated by the United States and Canada, the only two nations to have won it. The United States won the first edition and has accumulated the most titles, while Canada won in 1978, 2006 and 2014. A notable participant is the Haudenosaunee Nationals (formerly the Iroquois Nationals), representing the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, whose peoples originated the sport; they have finished third on several occasions. The 2023 championship was held in San Diego, California, with 30 teams participating, and was won by the United States, defeating Canada in the final.

Format

Teams are seeded into pools, with the strongest nations placed in the top ("Blue") division. Pool results determine placement into knockout rounds leading to medal games and final rankings for all participating teams.
