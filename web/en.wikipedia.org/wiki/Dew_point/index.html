Dew point

The dew point is the temperature to which air must be cooled to become saturated with water vapor, assuming constant air pressure and constant water content. When cooled below the dew point, moisture capacity is reduced and airborne water vapor will condense to form liquid water known as dew. When this occurs through contact with a colder surface, dew will form on that surface. If the temperature falls below freezing before saturation, frost forms instead (the frost point).

Relationship to humidity and comfort

Unlike relative humidity, the dew point does not vary directly with air temperature, making it a more direct measure of how humid the air feels. At a given pressure, a higher dew point means more moisture in the air. Dew points below about 10 °C feel dry; values around 16–18 °C feel muggy; above about 21 °C the air feels oppressive. Dew point is widely used in weather forecasting, aviation (where it predicts fog and carburettor icing), and building science (condensation risk).

Measurement and calculation

Dew point can be measured directly with a cooled-mirror hygrometer, or approximated from temperature and relative humidity using formulas such as the Magnus approximation. The spread between temperature and dew point indicates how close the air is to saturation: the smaller the spread, the higher the humidity and the greater the likelihood of fog.
