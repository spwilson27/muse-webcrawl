Mark Daniel Ronson (born 4 September 1975) is a British-American DJ, record producer, songwriter and musician. He is best known for his collaborations with artists such as Amy Winehouse, Lady Gaga, Adele, and Bruno Mars, and for hit productions including "Rehab", "Valerie", "Uptown Funk" and "Shallow". He has won nine Grammy Awards, an Academy Award and a Golden Globe Award.

Early life

Ronson was born in Notting Hill, London, into a prominent musical family — his stepfather is Foreigner guitarist Mick Jones — and moved to New York City as a child. He became a fixture of the New York club scene as a DJ in the 1990s before moving into production.

Career

His debut album Here Comes the Fuzz (2003) was followed by Version (2007), an album of cover versions featuring artists such as Lily Allen and Winehouse, which reached number 2 in the UK. He produced Winehouse's breakthrough album Back to Black (2006), winning the Grammy for Producer of the Year.

"Uptown Funk" (2014), featuring Bruno Mars, topped charts worldwide and spent 14 weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100. Ronson co-wrote and produced "Shallow" from A Star Is Born (2018) with Lady Gaga, winning the Academy Award for Best Original Song, and was executive producer of the soundtrack to Barbie (2023), including the hit "I'm Just Ken". His memoir Night People appeared in 2025.

Personal life

Ronson married actress Grace Gummer in 2021; they have two daughters. His sisters include DJ Samantha Ronson and designer Charlotte Ronson.
