Yi mein (Chinese: 伊麵; pinyin: yī miàn; Cantonese Yale: yī mihn), also called e-fu noodles, are a variety of Cantonese egg noodle made from wheat flour. They are characterised by a spongy, chewy texture and a golden colour, achieved by making the dough with soda water and deep-frying the formed noodle cakes before drying. Yi mein are especially associated with celebratory banquets, where they symbolise longevity.

History

Legend attributes the noodles to the household of Yi Bingshou (1754–1815), a Qing-dynasty official and calligrapher in Guangdong; his cook, so the story goes, accidentally dropped egg noodles into hot oil, then rescued the dish by re-cooking them in broth, impressing the guests. The noodles took the family name Yi and became known as Yi fu mian ("Yi household noodles"), shortened to yi mein.

Preparation

After deep-frying, the noodles are sold as flat dried cakes. To serve, they are briefly blanched or soaked in hot water or broth, which restores a springy, absorbent texture. Because they soak up sauce readily, yi mein are commonly braised in superior stock and served with lobster, crab, mushrooms, or garlic chives. A well-known banquet dish pairs them with cheese and lobster in Hong Kong restaurants.
