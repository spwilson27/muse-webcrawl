Land cover

Global land cover classification from satellite data

Land cover is the physical material at the surface of the Earth. Land covers include grass, asphalt, trees, bare ground, water, and other materials. Land use, by contrast, describes the purposes humans apply to that cover — such as agriculture, settlement or recreation — and the two terms are frequently used together in mapping and environmental monitoring.

Identifying, delineating and mapping land cover is important for global monitoring studies, resource management, and planning activities. Two major methods of land cover data collection are field survey and analysis of remotely sensed imagery, especially from satellites such as Landsat and the Copernicus Sentinels.

Classification systems

Widely used schemes include the FAO Land Cover Classification System, the European CORINE Land Cover programme, and the IGBP classes used in MODIS global land-cover products. Typical classes include forests (evergreen/deciduous, needleleaf/broadleaf), shrublands, savannas, grasslands, wetlands, croplands, urban and built-up areas, snow and ice, barren land, and water bodies.

Land cover change

Detecting land cover change over time documents processes such as deforestation, urbanisation, desertification and wetland loss. Change data feed into carbon-cycle and climate models, biodiversity assessments and land-degradation monitoring.
