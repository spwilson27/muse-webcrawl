Heat transfer

Heat transfer is a discipline of thermal engineering that concerns the generation, use, conversion, and exchange of thermal energy (heat) between physical systems. Heat transfer is classified into various mechanisms, such as thermal conduction, thermal convection, thermal radiation, and transfer of energy by phase changes. Engineers also consider the transfer of mass of differing chemical species (mass transfer in the form of advection), either cold or hot, to achieve heat transfer.

Heat moves from hotter to colder regions by conduction, convection and radiation.

Overview

Heat is defined in physics as the transfer of thermal energy across the boundary of a system due to a temperature difference with another system or its surroundings. Thermal energy is the kinetic energy of atoms and molecules, so heat is the transfer of energy arising from a difference of temperature. The quantification of heat transfer requires the analysis of various mechanisms and laws, beginning with the second law of thermodynamics, which states that heat spontaneously flows from hotter to colder bodies.

Mechanisms

Conduction

Conduction is the transfer of energy from the more energetic particles of a substance to adjacent less energetic ones as a result of interactions between the particles. In solids, conduction is mediated by the combination of molecular vibrations and energy transport by free electrons. Fourier's law states that the rate of heat conduction through a material is proportional to the negative gradient in the temperature and to the cross-sectional area through which heat flows.

Convection

Convection is the transfer of heat between a surface and a moving fluid with different temperatures. It combines the mechanisms of conduction and fluid motion (advection). Convection may be natural, driven by buoyancy forces arising from density differences caused by temperature variations, or forced, where the fluid is moved by a pump, fan, or other external means. Newton's law of cooling models the convective heat flux as proportional to the temperature difference.

Radiation

Thermal radiation is energy emitted by matter as electromagnetic waves (or photons) resulting from the thermal motions of charged particles. Unlike conduction and convection, radiation requires no intervening medium and can propagate through a vacuum; it is the means by which the Sun's energy reaches the Earth. The Stefan–Boltzmann law gives the total radiation emitted by a black body as proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature.

Phase change

Energy transfer also occurs through phase transitions such as boiling, condensation, melting and sublimation, which involve latent heat: the energy absorbed or released during a change of state without a change in temperature.

Applications

Heat transfer analysis underpins the design of heat exchangers, boilers, condensers, radiators, heating and refrigeration systems, electronic cooling, building insulation, and thermal management of spacecraft. Engineers use the concepts of thermal resistance networks, fins, and overall heat transfer coefficients to predict temperatures and heat rates.
