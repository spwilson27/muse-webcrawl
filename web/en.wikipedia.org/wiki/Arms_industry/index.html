Arms industry

The arms industry, also known as the defence industry or the military industry, is a global business that manufactures and sells weapons and military technology. It encompasses the commercial production of arms, military vehicles, aircraft, ships, missiles, and electronics for armed forces and, in part, for civilian markets. Global arms sales by the largest companies exceed US$500 billion annually, and world military expenditure passed US$2 trillion per year in the early 2020s.

Assembly of military aircraft — high fixed costs and state customers characterise the industry.

Structure

The industry is dominated by a small number of large prime contractors, most of them American: Lockheed Martin, RTX (formerly Raytheon Technologies), Northrop Grumman, Boeing, and General Dynamics have headed the SIPRI Top 100 arms-producing companies list, followed by firms such as BAE Systems (UK), Airbus (trans-European), Leonardo (Italy), and large Chinese state-owned conglomerates such as Norinco and AVIC. Russia's Rostec and its subsidiaries are major exporters. Because weapons development is expensive and governments are the main customers, the industry is closely tied to state policy, export licensing, and offset agreements.

Arms trade

The United States is the world's largest arms exporter, accounting for roughly two-fifths of global transfers, followed by France, Russia, China, and Germany. Major importers include India, Saudi Arabia, and Ukraine (after 2022). International transfers are regulated imperfectly by instruments such as the Arms Trade Treaty (2013), Wassenaar Arrangement, and national export-control laws.

Criticism and debate

Critics — from President Eisenhower's warning about the "military–industrial complex" in 1961 to modern campaign groups — argue the industry profits from conflict, encourages arms races, and lobbies for higher military spending. Defenders argue it provides deterrence, national security, and high-skill employment, and that demand originates with governments' security policies rather than the manufacturers.
