Arnoldo Mondadori Editore S.p.A., known as Mondadori Group, is Italy's largest publishing company and one of the largest consumer book and magazine publishers in Europe. Founded in 1907 in Ostiglia by the 18-year-old Arnoldo Mondadori, the company grew from a small printing shop into the dominant force in Italian publishing. Since 1991 it has been controlled by Fininvest, the holding company of the Berlusconi family, and its headquarters, a celebrated building designed by the Brazilian architect Oscar Niemeyer, is located in Segrate near Milan.

The Mondadori headquarters in Segrate, designed by Oscar Niemeyer (inaugurated 1975).

History

Arnoldo Mondadori began with a small print shop publishing the newspaper Luce! and school books. In the 1920s and 1930s the company launched popular magazines, children's books (including the Italian editions of Walt Disney's Mickey Mouse comics, Topolino, from 1932), and in 1935 the famous yellow-covered detective series I libri gialli, which gave Italian its word for crime fiction, giallo. After the Second World War Mondadori launched the news magazine Epoca (1950) and later Panorama (1967), and published Italian and translated literature through imprints that today include Einaudi, Piemme, Sperling & Kupfer, Rizzoli (acquired in 2016) and Mondadori Electa.

The struggle for control of the company in the late 1980s — the so-called guerra di Segrate between Silvio Berlusconi and Carlo De Benedetti — ended in 1991 with an arbitration award in Berlusconi's favour, a judgment later found to have been influenced by the corruption of a judge, which led to a large civil damages award against Fininvest in 2009–2011.

Operations

The Mondadori Group is Italy's market leader in trade books and publishes magazines such as Chi, Donna Moderna, Grazia, TV Sorrisi e Canzoni and Focus. It also operates Italy's largest network of bookstores under the Mondadori Store brand, with several hundred franchised and directly operated shops.
