Cortinarius

Cortinarius is a globally distributed genus of mushrooms in the family Cortinariaceae. It is suspected to be the largest genus of agarics, containing over 2,000 species. A common feature among all species is that young specimens have a cortina (veil) between the cap and the stem, hence the name.

Scientific classification

Kingdom: Fungi

Division: Basidiomycota

Order: Agaricales

Family: Cortinariaceae

Genus: Cortinarius (Pers.) Gray

Description and ecology

Cortinarius species are ectomycorrhizal, forming associations with trees and shrubs worldwide. They typically produce a rusty-brown spore print, and many are vividly coloured. Recent phylogenetic work has proposed splitting the genus into several smaller genera, though the broad concept remains widely used.

Toxicity

Several species, notably Cortinarius orellanus and C. rubellus, contain the toxin orellanine, which can cause potentially fatal kidney failure days after ingestion. Because identification is difficult, consumption of Cortinarius species is generally discouraged.
