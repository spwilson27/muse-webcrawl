Golden Horse Awards

The Taipei Golden Horse Film Festival and Awards (Chinese: 台北金馬影展) is a film festival and awards ceremony held annually in Taiwan. It was founded in 1962 by the Government Information Office of the Republic of China (Taiwan). The awards ceremony is usually held in November or December in Taipei, and is one of the most prestigious and honoured film awards in the world of Chinese-language cinema, often compared to the Academy Awards.

History and eligibility

The name "Golden Horse" derives from the Chinese phrase jīnmǎ, combining characters that evoke cinematic splendour. Films in any Chinese dialect from any country are eligible, which has made the awards the pre-eminent honour for Chinese-language film across Taiwan, Hong Kong, mainland China and the diaspora. Major categories include Best Feature Film, Best Director, Best Actor and Best Actress.

Notable moments

Directors such as Ang Lee, Hou Hsiao-hsien, Edward Yang and Wong Kar-wai have been recognised by the awards. In 2019 mainland Chinese films and filmmakers largely boycotted the ceremony following political controversy at the previous year's event, though participation gradually resumed in later years.

Together with the Golden Bell Awards for television and the Golden Melody Awards for music, the Golden Horse Awards form Taiwan's "three golds" of entertainment honours.
