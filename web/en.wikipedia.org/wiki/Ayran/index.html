Ayran is a cold, salted yogurt-based beverage made by mixing yogurt with water and salt, popular across Turkey, Central Asia, the Balkans, the Caucasus and parts of the Middle East. It is often described as Turkey's national drink and is traditionally served frothy, sometimes prepared in a suspended churn or, in southeastern Turkey, in the copper bowls of the yayık tradition. A mint-flavoured variant is common in some regions.

Preparation and consumption

Traditional ayran is made from sheep's or cow's milk yogurt thinned with water and seasoned with salt; vigorous whisking or churning produces a characteristic foam. Industrially produced ayran is sold in cups and bottles throughout Turkey and the diaspora. It is the classic accompaniment to kebabs, pide and other rich or spicy dishes, and is valued as a refreshing, rehydrating summer drink. In 2013, Turkish prime minister Recep Tayyip Erdoğan publicly promoted ayran as the national drink in preference to alcohol, a statement that attracted wide comment.

Related drinks

Closely related drinks include Iranian doogh (often carbonated and flavoured with mint), Armenian tan, Albanian dhallë, Bulgarian ayryan, and the fermented mare's-milk-adjacent Central Asian chal/shubat family. Indian lassi and Caucasian kefir-based drinks occupy the same culinary space but differ in preparation and flavouring.
