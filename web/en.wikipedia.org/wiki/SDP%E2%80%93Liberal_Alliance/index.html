The SDP–Liberal Alliance was a centrist political and electoral alliance in the United Kingdom between the newly formed Social Democratic Party (SDP) and the Liberal Party, lasting from 1981 until the two parties merged in 1988 to form the Social and Liberal Democrats, later the Liberal Democrats.

History

The SDP was founded in 1981 by the "Gang of Four" — Roy Jenkins, David Owen, Bill Rodgers and Shirley Williams — who broke from Labour over Europe and unilateral nuclear disarmament. The Alliance with the Liberals under David Steel pooled candidates and campaigned as a "third force" challenging the two-party system.

Elections

In the 1983 general election the Alliance won 25.4% of the vote — close behind Labour — but only 23 seats under first-past-the-post, an outcome often cited as illustrating the system's disproportionality. After a weaker 1987 result, most members merged the parties in 1988; Owen led a continuing SDP rump for a time.
