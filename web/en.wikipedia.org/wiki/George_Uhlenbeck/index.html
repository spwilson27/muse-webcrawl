George Eugene Uhlenbeck (6 December 1900 – 31 October 1988) was a Dutch-American theoretical physicist. With Samuel Goudsmit he proposed the concept of electron spin in 1925, one of the key ideas of quantum mechanics.

Electron spin

Uhlenbeck was born in Batavia (now Jakarta) and studied at Leiden under Paul Ehrenfest. As a young graduate student, with Goudsmit, he suggested that the electron behaves as if it spins on its own axis, carrying an intrinsic angular momentum and magnetic moment. The hypothesis explained the anomalous Zeeman effect and fine structure of spectra; though the "spinning ball" picture is not literal, spin became a fundamental quantum number, later incorporated by Dirac's relativistic theory.

Career

After a period as tutor to the Dutch ambassador's son in Rome, Uhlenbeck emigrated to the United States in 1927, joining the University of Michigan, where he built a noted school of theoretical physics and summer symposia. During the Second World War he worked on radar at the MIT Radiation Laboratory. He later moved to the Rockefeller Institute (later Rockefeller University) in New York.

Other work

With Leonard Ornstein, Uhlenbeck formulated the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, a stochastic process describing the velocity of a Brownian particle with friction, foundational in the mathematics of diffusion and noise. He also made contributions to statistical mechanics and the theory of phase transitions.
