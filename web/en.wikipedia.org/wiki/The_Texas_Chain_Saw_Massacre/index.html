Overview

The Texas Chain Saw Massacre is a 1974 American independent horror film produced, co-composed, and directed by Tobe Hooper, who co-wrote it with Kim Henkel. It stars Marilyn Burns as Sally Hardesty and Gunnar Hansen as Leatherface, a chainsaw-wielding killer in a mask of human skin. The film follows a group of friends who, while traveling through rural Texas, fall victim to a family of cannibals.

Made on a shoestring budget with a cast of mostly unknown actors, the film was marketed as being based on a true story to attract audiences, though its plot is largely fictional, partly inspired by the crimes of Ed Gein. It was initially controversial for its violence — much of it implied rather than shown — and was banned in several countries, yet it grossed over $30 million worldwide.

Legacy

Regarded as one of the most influential horror films ever made, The Texas Chain Saw Massacre helped establish the slasher genre and conventions such as the 'final girl' and the masked, seemingly unstoppable killer. It spawned a franchise of sequels, remakes, and spin-offs. In 2024 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
