National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency

AbbreviationNGA
Formed1996 (as NIMA); renamed NGA in 2003

The National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA) is a combat support agency of the United States Department of Defense and a member of the U.S. Intelligence Community. It produces geospatial intelligence (GEOINT) — the analysis of imagery, satellite data, maps and geographic information — for national security policymakers, military commanders and civil users.

History

The agency traces its lineage to the Defense Mapping Agency and the wartime map-making services of the Second World War. In 1996 these functions were consolidated as the National Imagery and Mapping Agency (NIMA), and the National Defense Authorization Act for 2004 renamed it the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency to reflect the shift from static maps to multi-source geospatial analysis. In 2011 its eastern headquarters moved to a vast new campus at Fort Belvoir North Area in Springfield, Virginia; a major western campus sits in St. Louis, Missouri, heir to the old Aeronautical Chart and Information Center.

Mission

NGA analysts exploit reconnaissance satellite imagery and commercial remote sensing to map terrain, identify targets, assess damage, support navigation safety and respond to disasters. The agency provides the mapping foundation for GPS-era precision operations, supplies nautical and aeronautical charts, and has released unclassified mapping in crises such as the Haitian earthquake and Ebola outbreak. Its motto reflects the ambition to "know the Earth, show the way, understand the world."
