Brownie (folklore)

A brownie, the helpful household spirit of Scottish and northern English folklore.

A brownie or broonie (Scots) is a household spirit or hobgoblin from Scottish and northern English folklore. Brownies are said to come out at night while the household sleeps and perform helpful tasks — milking cows, threshing grain, cleaning and tidying — in exchange for small offerings of food, traditionally a bowl of milk or cream and a cake.

Characteristics

Brownies are described as small, wizened, brown-clad or naked hairy figures. They are easily offended: criticizing their work, spying on them, or offering them payment — especially clothing — may cause a brownie to abandon the household forever. This motif appears in the tale of the brownie of Bodsbeck and, in adapted form, in the house-elves of modern fantasy literature. Unlike the more ambivalent boggart, into which an offended brownie might degenerate, the brownie is ordinarily benevolent.

Regional variants

Similar beings include the English hob, the Scandinavian tomte or nisse, the Slavic domovoi and the German Heinzelmännchen. Folklorists have compared the brownie's labour to that of Roman lares and other household deities.

In popular culture

Palmer Cox's illustrated "Brownie" books of the 1880s and 1890s made the creatures famous in North America and gave their name to the Kodak Brownie camera and, indirectly, to the junior branch of the Girl Guides and Girl Scouts.
