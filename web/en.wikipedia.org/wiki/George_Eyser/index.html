George Eyser

George Louis Eyser
Born: August 31, 1870, Dänisch-Nienhof, German Empire
Died: March 6, 1919, Denver, Colorado, U.S.
Sport: Artistic gymnastics

George Louis Eyser (August 31, 1870 – March 6, 1919) was a German-American gymnast who competed at the 1904 Summer Olympics in St. Louis, where he won six medals in a single day despite having a wooden prosthesis for a left leg. He is one of the most successful disabled athletes in early Olympic history.

Life and career

Eyser emigrated from Germany to the United States as a teenager and settled in St. Louis, Missouri, becoming a U.S. citizen in 1894. He worked as a bookkeeper and competed for the Concordia Turnverein gymnastics club. His left leg had been amputated after he was run over by a train, and he performed on a wooden prosthesis. At the 1904 Games — held in his home city as part of the Louisiana Purchase Exposition — events were dominated by American competitors.

1904 Olympic results

On October 29, 1904 Eyser won gold medals in the parallel bars, vault and rope climbing, silver medals in the pommel horse and the individual all-around four-event competition, and a bronze medal in the horizontal bar. His team, Concordia, placed fourth. His six medals in one day stood as an extraordinary achievement, particularly given the rope-climbing and vaulting events performed with a prosthetic leg. Eyser's story was largely forgotten for decades and was revived only through later Olympic scholarship.
