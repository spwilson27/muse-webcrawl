Nagara architecture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nagara (Sanskrit: नागर, romanized: nāgara, lit. 'of the city') is a style of Hindu temple architecture that originated in northern India. It is one of the three major styles of Hindu temple architecture described in ancient Indian texts, the others being Dravida (of southern India) and Vesara (a hybrid style of the Deccan).

Nagara temples are characterized by a tall, curvilinear tower called a shikhara rising above the sanctum (garbhagriha), crowned by an amalaka (a ribbed stone disc) and a kalasha finial. Unlike Dravida temples of the south, Nagara temples typically lack elaborate boundary walls and gateway towers (gopurams), and the entire structure is often raised on a high stone platform (jagati).

Features

The plan of a Nagara temple follows the vastu-purusha-mandala, a sacred geometric grid. The ground plan is typically cruciform (sarvatobhadra) with projections (rathas) on each face of the shikhara. The elevation is divided into horizontal registers by bands of molding, and the shikhara curve is marked by vertical lines of chaitya-arch motifs.

The temple usually comprises a sanctum, an antechamber (antarala), an assembly hall (mandapa), and sometimes an entrance porch. In the developed form, multiple subsidiary shikharas of decreasing height may cluster around the main tower.

Regional schools

Several regional schools of the Nagara style developed between the 7th and 13th centuries:

  • Odisha (Kalinga) school — exemplified by the Lingaraja Temple at Bhubaneswar and the Sun Temple at Konark, with its four-part plan and curvilinear deul.
  • Khajuraho (Chandela) school — noted for soaring shikharas resembling mountain ranges and rich sculptural programs.
  • Solanki (Maru-Gurjara) school — of Gujarat and Rajasthan, refined in temples such as Modhera Sun Temple and the Jain temples of Mount Abu, distinguished by intricate carving and stepped tanks.
  • Hill schools — of the western Himalayas, blending stone and timber construction.

Notable examples

Famous Nagara temples include the Kandariya Mahadeva Temple at Khajuraho, the Lingaraja Temple in Bhubaneswar, the Jagannath Temple at Puri, the Konark Sun Temple, the Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh (one of the earliest stone examples, c. 500 CE), and in modern times the Akshardham temples and the Ram Mandir at Ayodhya, consecrated in 2024.
