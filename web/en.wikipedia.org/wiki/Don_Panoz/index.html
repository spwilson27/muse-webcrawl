Donald Panoz (13 February 1935 – 11 September 2018) was an American entrepreneur who built fortunes in pharmaceuticals and motor racing. He co-founded the drug-delivery company that became the Elan Corporation in Ireland, then founded the Panoz automobile company with his son Dan, created the American Le Mans Series, and bought circuits including Road Atlanta and Sebring International Raceway.

Pharmaceuticals

After working for drug companies in the US, Panoz moved to Ireland in 1969 and co-founded Elan, which pioneered transdermal drug delivery — most famously the nicotine patch — and grew into a major pharmaceutical company. He later developed resorts and vineyards, including Château Élan in Georgia.

Motorsport

In 1989 he bankrolled his son Danny's Panoz Auto Development; its front-engined Esperante and GTR-1 race cars challenged convention at Le Mans. In 1999 he launched the American Le Mans Series in partnership with the ACO, and his organisation revitalised Sebring and Petit Le Mans at Road Atlanta. The DeltaWing experimental car project was another notable venture. Panoz was inducted into the Motorsports Hall of Fame of America.
