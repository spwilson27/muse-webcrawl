The Lamborghini Miura is a sports car produced by Italian automaker Lamborghini between 1966 and 1973. The car was the first supercar with a rear mid-engined two-seat layout, although the concept was first seen in the Renault 5 Turbo and earlier road cars such as the Ford GT40. The Miura is widely considered to have instigated the trend of high-performance, mid-engined two-seaters that became the standard formula for supercars.

Development

The Miura began as a skunkworks project by Lamborghini's young engineering team — Gian Paolo Dallara, Paolo Stanzani, and test driver Bob Wallace — who worked after hours to create a car with a transversely mounted mid-engine V12, contrary to founder Ferruccio Lamborghini's preference for comfortable grand tourers. The rolling chassis was shown at the 1965 Turin Motor Show and attracted many orders despite having no bodywork. The styling was then assigned to Marcello Gandini at Bertone, who produced the car's celebrated low, curvaceous shape with distinctive "eyelashes" around the headlights.

Variants

The original P400 (1966–1969) produced 350 PS (345 hp) from its 3.9-litre V12. The P400 S (1968–1971) added power and refinements, while the final P400 SV (1971–1973) gained wider rear bodywork, revised suspension, and 385 PS. A single open-top roadster was built, later converted into the Zn75 show car, and the one-off Jota test car inspired a handful of SV/J conversions. About 764 Miuras were built in total.

Legacy

When launched, the Miura was the fastest production car in the world. It remains one of the most celebrated classic sports cars; in 2006 Lamborghini built a Miura concept car to mark the model's 40th anniversary, though it was not intended for production.
