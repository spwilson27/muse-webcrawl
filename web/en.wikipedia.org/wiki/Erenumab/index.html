Erenumab

Trade name: Aimovig
Type: Monoclonal antibody
Target: CGRP receptor
Use: Prevention of migraine
Route: Subcutaneous injection, monthly

Erenumab, sold under the brand name Aimovig, is a human monoclonal antibody medication used for the prevention of migraine. It blocks the calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor, and was the first drug of its class approved, receiving US Food and Drug Administration approval in May 2018. It is developed and marketed by Amgen (with Novartis in some markets) and is given as a monthly self-administered subcutaneous injection of 70 mg or 140 mg.

Medical use

Erenumab is indicated for preventive treatment of migraine in adults, both episodic and chronic. In phase III trials (including STRIVE and ARISE), it reduced monthly migraine days by roughly 1–3 days more than placebo, with about half of patients achieving at least a 50% reduction in some studies. Long-term open-label data suggested sustained effect for many patients, though real-world discontinuation is common.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include injection-site reactions and constipation; post-marketing experience led to label warnings about serious constipation and new or worsening hypertension. As a large-molecule antibody it is not metabolized by cytochrome P450, so drug interactions are few. Unlike the later gepants and lamina-targeting antibodies (galcanezumab, fremanezumab, eptinezumab), which bind the CGRP ligand, erenumab binds the receptor itself.

History

The drug emerged from Amgen's neuroscience programme based on the CGRP hypothesis of migraine, strengthened by earlier small-molecule CGRP antagonist studies. Its approval inaugurated the CGRP class of migraine preventives.
