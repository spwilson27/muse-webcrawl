Direct-administered municipality

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A direct-administered municipality (Chinese: 直辖市; pinyin: zhíxiáshì; lit. "direct-controlled city") is a province-level administrative division in the People's Republic of China. Such municipalities are directly administered by the central government rather than forming part of a province, and their governments hold the same rank as provincial governments.

The four municipalities

China has four direct-administered municipalities:

  • Beijing — the national capital, in northern China.
  • Tianjin — a major port city near Beijing on the Bohai Sea.
  • Shanghai — the country's largest city by urban population and a global financial centre, at the mouth of the Yangtze River.
  • Chongqing — in southwestern China; created in 1997 from part of Sichuan province, it is by far the largest in area (about 82,000 km², comparable to Austria) because it administers a wide rural hinterland of counties and districts.

Administration

Each municipality is headed by a mayor, with a municipal people's congress as its legislative body; the Communist Party secretary of the municipality outranks the mayor and, for these four cities, is typically a member of the Party's Politburo. Below the municipal level are districts (and, in Chongqing's case, also counties and autonomous counties), followed by towns and townships.

Historically, the Republic of China administered a larger number of special municipalities, and on Taiwan the term survives for the special municipalities (such as Taipei and Kaohsiung) administered directly by the central government there.
