Subcontractor

A subcontractor is a person or business that undertakes part of a larger contract, performing work for a general (or prime) contractor rather than directly for the ultimate client. Subcontracting is especially common in construction, engineering, defence and information technology, where specialised trades and services are brought in for portions of a project.

Role in construction

In building projects, a general contractor typically self-performs little of the physical work and instead engages subcontractors for trades such as excavation, concrete, structural steel, plumbing, electrical work, HVAC and finishing. The general contractor remains responsible to the client for the whole works, while each subcontractor is responsible to the general contractor under a subcontract.

Legal and commercial aspects

Subcontracts usually mirror ("flow down") relevant obligations of the main contract, covering scope, programme, quality, insurance and payment terms. Disputes commonly concern late payment, variations and delays. In some jurisdictions, statutes such as security-of-payment legislation give subcontractors rights to prompt payment and, in certain cases, liens against the project property.
