The Bibliotheca Hagiographica Latina (BHL) is a catalogue of Latin hagiographic materials, including ancient and medieval saints' lives (vitae), accounts of miracles, and martyrdom narratives (passiones), compiled by the Société des Bollandistes in Brussels. It organises the texts alphabetically by the name of the saint.

Contents and use

Each work is assigned a BHL number, by which scholars conventionally cite individual hagiographic texts — for example, distinguishing among the many different lives of a popular saint such as Martin of Tours. Entries list the incipit and explicit of each text and indicate printed editions. The BHL covers saints of the Latin West up to about 1500 and lists well over 9,000 items.

Editions

The catalogue was published in two volumes in 1898–1901 as part of the Bollandists' Subsidia Hagiographica series. A supplement by Hippolyte Delehaye appeared in 1911, and a Novum Supplementum, edited by Henri Fros, in 1986. The BHL is one of a family of Bollandist reference works, alongside the Bibliotheca Hagiographica Graeca (BHG) for Greek texts and the Bibliotheca Hagiographica Orientalis (BHO) for Syriac, Arabic, Armenian and other oriental traditions.
