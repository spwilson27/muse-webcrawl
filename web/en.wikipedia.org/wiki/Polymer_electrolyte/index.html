Polymer electrolyte

A polymer electrolyte is a polymer matrix that conducts ions, typically consisting of a salt dissolved in a polymer host such as polyethylene oxide (PEO). Ion transport occurs mainly in the amorphous phase, coupled to segmental motion of the polymer chains.

Types

Classes include dry solid polymer electrolytes, gel polymer electrolytes (in which a liquid plasticiser is incorporated), and composite electrolytes containing ceramic fillers. Proton-exchange membranes such as Nafion conduct protons and form a related class used in fuel cells.

Applications

Polymer electrolytes are used in solid-state lithium batteries, where they promise improved safety over liquid electrolytes, as well as in fuel cells, electrochromic devices, and supercapacitors. Their main drawback is low ionic conductivity at room temperature.
