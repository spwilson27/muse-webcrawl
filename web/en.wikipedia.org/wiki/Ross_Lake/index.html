Ross Lake

Ross Lake is a reservoir in the North Cascades of Washington state, United States, formed by Ross Dam on the Skagit River. The lake extends about 37 kilometres (23 miles) north, crossing the border into British Columbia, Canada, where its northern tip lies in Skagit Valley Provincial Park. It lies within the Ross Lake National Recreation Area, administered by the National Park Service as part of the North Cascades National Park Service Complex.

History

Ross Dam was built by Seattle City Light in stages beginning in the 1930s, with construction interrupted by the Second World War and completed to its present height of about 160 metres (540 feet) in 1949. It is the tallest of three dams in the Skagit River Hydroelectric Project, upstream of Diablo Dam and Gorge Dam, which together supply a substantial share of the city of Seattle's electricity. The reservoir is named for James D. Ross, the superintendent of Seattle City Light who championed the project.

A long controversy attended proposals to raise the dam further under a treaty arrangement with British Columbia; a 1984 agreement between Seattle and the province settled the matter by having British Columbia supply equivalent power instead, leaving the lake at its present level.

Geography and recreation

Ross Lake sits at an elevation of about 490 metres (1,602 feet) amid the rugged peaks of the North Cascades, including Colonial Peak and Pyramid Peak. It is known for cold, clear water, forested shores, and mountain scenery. The lake has no direct road access from the United States side; visitors reach it by hiking trails, by boat from Diablo Lake via a portage around Ross Dam, by floatplane, or by a gravel road from Hope, British Columbia, to the lake's north end at Hozomeen.

Facilities include the floating Ross Lake Resort, boat-in campgrounds, and the Hozomeen campground at the north end. Fishing for rainbow trout, cutthroat trout, brook trout, and native bull trout (char) is popular under special regulations. Seasonal drawdowns for power generation lower the lake level in winter, exposing broad shorelines before refill in late spring.

Ecology

The lake and surrounding forests support wildlife including black bears, mountain goats, deer, and numerous bird species. The North Cascades ecosystem around Ross Lake is one of the most biologically diverse in the United States, and the lake's fisheries are jointly managed with attention to the threatened bull trout.
