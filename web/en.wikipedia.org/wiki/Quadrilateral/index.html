Quadrilateral

In Euclidean geometry, a quadrilateral is a four-sided polygon with four vertices and four edges. The interior angles of a simple quadrilateral sum to 360 degrees. The word comes from the Latin quadri- (four) and latus (side).

Types

Familiar quadrilaterals include the square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, trapezoid (trapezium) and kite. A quadrilateral whose vertices lie on a single circle is called cyclic; one with an incircle tangent to all sides is tangential. Quadrilaterals can also be concave (with one interior angle greater than 180 degrees) or complex (self-intersecting, such as the bow-tie shape).

Classification

The special types form a hierarchy: every square is a rectangle and a rhombus; every rectangle and rhombus is a parallelogram; and every parallelogram is a trapezoid under the inclusive definition. Diagonals, midline segments and area formulas — including Bretschneider's formula for a general quadrilateral — relate the sides and angles.
