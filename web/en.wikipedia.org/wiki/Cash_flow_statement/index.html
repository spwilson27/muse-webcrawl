Cash flow statement

TypeFinancial statement
SectionsOperating, investing, financing activities
StandardsIAS 7; ASC 230 (US GAAP)

In financial accounting, a cash flow statement, also known as a statement of cash flows, reports the cash generated and used by a company during a specified period. Together with the balance sheet and income statement, it is one of the three core financial statements.

Structure

Cash flows are classified into three categories: operating activities (cash from the core business, such as receipts from customers and payments to suppliers and employees), investing activities (purchase and sale of long-term assets and investments), and financing activities (borrowings, share issues, dividends and repayments). The statement reconciles the opening and closing cash and cash equivalents balances.

Methods

Operating cash flow may be presented by the direct method, listing gross cash receipts and payments, or the indirect method, which starts from net income and adjusts for non-cash items (such as depreciation) and changes in working capital — including movements captured by the cash conversion cycle. Accounting standard-setters encourage the direct method, but the indirect method is far more common in practice.

Purpose

Because accrual accounting can show profits without corresponding cash, the cash flow statement is essential for assessing liquidity, solvency and the quality of earnings.
