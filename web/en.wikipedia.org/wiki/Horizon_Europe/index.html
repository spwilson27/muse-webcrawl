Horizon Europe

Horizon Europe is the European Union's key funding programme for research and innovation, running from 2021 to 2027 with a budget of approximately €95.5 billion. It succeeds Horizon 2020 and is the largest transnational research funding programme in the world, supporting everything from curiosity-driven basic science to market-creating innovation.

Structure

The programme is organised into three pillars. Pillar I, "Excellent Science", funds the European Research Council, Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions and research infrastructures. Pillar II, "Global Challenges and European Industrial Competitiveness", is organised into clusters covering health, culture, civil security, digital and industry, climate and energy, and food and natural resources, and includes large-scale "EU missions" targeting goals such as climate-neutral cities and beating cancer. Pillar III, "Innovative Europe", centres on the European Innovation Council, which supports breakthrough and disruptive innovation, including through equity investments in start-ups.

Participation

EU member states participate automatically, and a number of non-EU countries are associated to the programme, paying contributions in return for near-equal access. The United Kingdom's association was agreed in 2023 and took effect from January 2024, following post-Brexit negotiations. Switzerland's association was long delayed by broader political disputes between Bern and Brussels.
