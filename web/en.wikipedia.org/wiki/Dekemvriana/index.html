Dekemvriana

[Image: Athens during the December 1944 fighting]
British and government troops held central Athens during the Dekemvriana

The Dekemvriana (Greek: Δεκεμβριανά, "the December events") was a series of clashes fought in Athens from 3 December 1944 to 11 January 1945 between the Greek left-wing resistance movement EAM–ELAS and the Greek government, backed by British forces. The fighting, occurring just weeks after Greece's liberation from Axis occupation, was the opening act of the conflict that culminated in the Greek Civil War of 1946–1949.

Course of events

Tensions between EAM, the communist-led resistance front that controlled most of the countryside, and the returning government of national unity under Georgios Papandreou came to a head over the demobilization of the guerrilla armies. When EAM ministers resigned and a mass demonstration was called for 3 December 1944, police fired on the crowd in Syntagma Square, killing more than two dozen protesters. General fighting followed: ELAS attacked police stations and government positions throughout Athens and Piraeus, while British troops under General Ronald Scobie intervened on the government's side with armour and air power. Winston Churchill flew to Athens on Christmas Day to broker a conference, which failed; by mid-January, reinforced British forces had driven ELAS from the capital. The Varkiza Agreement of February 1945 formally ended the crisis, providing for ELAS's disarmament and a plebiscite on the monarchy.

Significance

The Dekemvriana was one of the first open clashes of the emerging Cold War: Stalin, bound by the Percentages Agreement of October 1944 that assigned Greece to the British sphere, withheld support from the Greek communists. The defeat of EAM was followed by a period of right-wing persecution (the "White Terror"), which in turn drove many former resistance members back to the mountains and into full civil war.
