Hargeisa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hargeisa (Somali: Hargeysa) is the capital and largest city of Somaliland, a self-declared republic internationally recognized as part of Somalia. It lies in the Maroodi Jeex region in the northwestern part of the Horn of Africa, in a valley at an elevation of about 1,334 metres. Its population is estimated at well over one million.

History

Hargeisa grew from a watering place and trading settlement on the route between the coast at Berbera and the Ogaden interior, and was an important centre of the Qadiriyya Sufi order. It became the capital of the British Somaliland protectorate in 1941, replacing Berbera. In 1988, during the Somali civil war, the city was largely destroyed by aerial bombardment by the Somali government — the ruined neighbourhoods and a downed fighter jet mounted as a war memorial remain reminders of the destruction. After Somaliland declared independence in 1991, Hargeisa was rebuilt, largely with remittances from the diaspora.

Economy and landmarks

The city is a regional hub for trade, livestock exports, money transfer companies and construction. Landmarks include the central market, Hargeisa International Airport, the Somaliland National Museum, and the MiG war memorial in the city centre.
