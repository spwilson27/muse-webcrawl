Malcolm Knowles.

Malcolm Shepherd Knowles (1913–1997) was an American adult educator, famous for the adoption of the theory of andragogy — the art and science of helping adults learn — and for his work on self-directed learning. He is frequently described as a central figure in the development of adult education in the United States in the second half of the 20th century.

Career

Knowles was educated at Harvard and the University of Chicago, where he studied under Eduard Lindeman. He directed adult education at the Boston YMCA and the Adult Education Association of the USA before becoming a professor at Boston University and later North Carolina State University.

Andragogy

Knowles's framework rests on assumptions about adult learners: their self-concept moves toward self-direction; their experience is a resource for learning; their readiness to learn is tied to the developmental tasks of their social roles; their orientation is problem-centred rather than subject-centred; and their motivation is increasingly internal. He advocated learning contracts, experiential techniques and the teacher as facilitator rather than lecturer.
