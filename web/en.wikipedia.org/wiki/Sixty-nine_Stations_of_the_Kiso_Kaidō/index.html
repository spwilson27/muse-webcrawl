Sixty-nine Stations of the Kiso Kaidō

The Sixty-nine Stations of the Kiso Kaidō (Japanese: 木曾街道六十九次, Kisokaidō Rokujūkyū-tsugi) is a series of Japanese ukiyo-e woodblock prints depicting the sixty-nine post stations of the Kiso Kaidō (also called the Nakasendō), the inland highway that connected Edo (modern Tokyo) with Kyoto through the mountains of central Honshu during the Edo period. The series was produced between about 1834 and 1842.

The series

The series was begun by Keisai Eisen, who designed the first twenty-odd prints, and completed by Utagawa Hiroshige, who took over and produced the majority of the designs. Counting the starting point at Nihonbashi in Edo and the final station at Ōtsu, the full set comprises seventy-one prints. It is sometimes called the "other" station series in comparison with Hiroshige's earlier and more famous Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō, which depicted the coastal road; the Kiso Kaidō series is noted for its mountain landscapes, river crossings, rain and snow scenes, and vignettes of travellers and station life.

The road

The Kiso Kaidō ran through the Kiso Valley of present-day Nagano and Gifu prefectures. Its post towns (shukuba) provided lodging, food, porters, and horses for travellers, including the processions of daimyō travelling to and from Edo under the sankin-kōtai system. Several stations, such as Narai-juku and Tsumago-juku, survive today as preserved historic districts.
