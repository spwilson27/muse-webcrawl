Combustibility

Combustibility
Property ofMaterials
Related measuresFlash point, flammability limit

Combustibility is a measure of how readily a substance burns in air, that is, undergoes combustion. A material described as combustible will ignite and burn under specified conditions; materials that do not burn are termed non-combustible.

Definitions

Building and safety codes distinguish combustible from flammable materials, often using flash point: liquids with a flash point above a threshold (commonly around 37.8 °C / 100 °F) are classed as combustible liquids, while those below are flammable. Construction codes classify building materials by standard furnace tests of sustained burning.

Testing

Combustibility of building products is assessed with reaction-to-fire tests such as the non-combustibility furnace test (ISO 1182, ASTM E136) and classifications under EN 13501 (Euroclasses A1–F).
