Compañía Mexicana de Aviación, S.A. de C.V., usually shortened to Mexicana de Aviación, was Mexico's oldest airline and one of the oldest continuously operating airlines in the world before it suspended operations in 2010. It was the fourth airline founded in the world, after KLM, Avianca, and Qantas. A new state-owned airline using the Mexicana name began flying in December 2023.

History

Mexicana was founded in Mexico City on July 12, 1921, as Compañía Mexicana de Transportación Aérea, initially carrying payroll to oil fields near Tampico. It began international services in the 1930s and for decades was Mexico's flagship carrier alongside Aeroméxico, with which it shared government ownership for much of the twentieth century.

Privatised in 2005, the airline struggled with high costs, labour disputes, and the effects of the 2008 financial crisis and the 2009 swine flu outbreak. After filing for bankruptcy protection (concurso mercantil), Mexicana suspended all operations on August 28, 2010, and was declared bankrupt in 2014.

Relaunch

In 2023 the Mexican government purchased the Mexicana brand and assets for roughly 815 million pesos and relaunched the airline under military-owned operator OLAMSA. The new Mexicana de Aviación began operations on December 26, 2023, with Boeing 737 aircraft flying domestic routes from Felipe Ángeles International Airport.

Destinations and fleet

At its peak the original Mexicana served destinations across Mexico, the United States, Canada, Central and South America, and Europe, operating a fleet centred on Airbus A320-family and Boeing 767 aircraft. Its regional subsidiary MexicanaClick operated Fokker 100s.
