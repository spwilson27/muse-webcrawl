Morphological freedom

Morphological freedom refers to a proposed civil right of a person to either maintain or modify their own body, on their own terms, through informed, consensual use of available technologies and the assistance of others. The term is central to transhumanist thought.

Origins

The concept was articulated by the philosopher Max More in the 1990s and elaborated by Anders Sandberg, who defined it as "the ability to change one's body according to one's desires" and an extension of bodily autonomy and self-ownership. Transhumanists regard morphological freedom as both a freedom from coercion over one's body and a positive freedom to access means of self-transformation, such as prosthetics, genetic modification, and cosmetic surgery.

Debates

Discussions of morphological freedom intersect with debates over body modification, gender transition, human enhancement, reproductive technology, and disability rights. Critics raise concerns about social pressure to conform to enhancement norms, unequal access, and the commodification of the body.
