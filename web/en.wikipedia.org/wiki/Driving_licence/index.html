Driving licence

Type: Official permit document

Purpose: Authorisation to drive motor vehicles on public roads

A driving licence (British English) or driver's license (American English) is an official document authorising its holder to operate motor vehicles on public roads. Licences are usually issued by category — cars, motorcycles, trucks, buses — after written, practical and medical tests.

History

The earliest licences appeared in the early 1900s as motor vehicles spread; the United Kingdom introduced a licence in 1903, and testing followed decades later. Karl Benz received a written permit to drive his patented motor car in Mannheim in 1888, sometimes cited as the first licence.

Categories and graduated licensing

Many systems use graduated licensing for new drivers: a learner permit with supervision, a provisional stage with restrictions, then a full licence. Points systems record offences such as drunk driving, and accumulating points can lead to suspension.

International use

The 1949 Geneva and 1968 Vienna conventions standardised licence formats. An International Driving Permit translates a national licence for use abroad. The European Union uses a common card format and categories.

As identification

In countries without national identity cards, notably the United States, the driving licence serves as the de facto photo identification document.
