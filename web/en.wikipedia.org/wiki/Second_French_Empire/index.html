The Second French Empire was the regime in France from 1852 to 1870, ruled by Napoleon III, nephew of Napoleon I. It followed the French Second Republic, which Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte had led as president before his coup d'état of December 1851.

Authoritarian and liberal phases

The early "authoritarian empire" censored the press and ruled through plebiscites, while pursuing rapid industrialization, railway expansion, and Baron Haussmann's rebuilding of Paris. From the 1860s the "liberal empire" relaxed controls, legalized strikes (1864), and granted parliament greater powers under Émile Ollivier.

Foreign policy

France fought in the Crimean War, intervened in Italy (1859) aiding unification, expanded colonial holdings in Algeria, Senegal, Indochina, and New Caledonia, and undertook the disastrous Mexican intervention (1861–1867) installing Maximilian I. The Ems Dispatch crisis led to the Franco-Prussian War; defeat and Napoleon III's capture at Sedan (1 September 1870) collapsed the empire, and the Third Republic was proclaimed on 4 September 1870.

Legacy

The era modernized French infrastructure, banking (Crédit Mobilier), and cities, and Karl Marx's The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte remains a classic analysis of its origins. See Napoleon III.
