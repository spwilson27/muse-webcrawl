CBS Broadcasting Inc., commonly known simply as CBS, is an American commercial broadcast television and radio network. It is the flagship property of the CBS Entertainment Group division of Paramount Global and is one of the "Big Three" American television networks, alongside ABC and NBC. The network's name comes from the initialism of its former legal name, the Columbia Broadcasting System.

History

CBS began as a radio network in 1927, built up under president William S. Paley into a dominant force in American broadcasting, famous for news under Edward R. Murrow and for entertainment that lured stars from rival NBC. It began television broadcasts in 1941 and became the top-rated American network for much of the 1950s through the 1970s, with programmes such as I Love Lucy, The Ed Sullivan Show and 60 Minutes (launched 1968).

Programming

CBS is known for procedural dramas such as the NCIS and CSI franchises, sitcoms including The Big Bang Theory, reality series Survivor and The Amazing Race, the late-night Late Show, and extensive sports coverage including the NFL, NCAA basketball and golf's Masters Tournament. Its news division produces CBS Evening News, Face the Nation and CBS Sunday Morning.

Identity

The famous CBS "eye" logo, designed by William Golden, debuted in 1951 and remains one of the most recognisable marks in media. The network is headquartered at the CBS Building ("Black Rock") in Manhattan, with production centred in New York and Los Angeles.
