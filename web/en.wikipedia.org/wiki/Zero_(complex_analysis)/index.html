Zero (complex analysis)

In mathematics, and more specifically in complex analysis, a zero (sometimes also called a root) of a holomorphic function f is a complex number a such that f(a) = 0.

Multiplicity of a zero

A complex number a is a simple zero of f, or a zero of multiplicity 1, if f can be written near a in the form f(z) = (z − a)g(z) where g is holomorphic and g(a) is not zero. In general, the multiplicity of a zero a of f is the smallest positive integer n such that f can be written in the form f(z) = (z − a)ng(z) with g holomorphic and g(a) ≠ 0. Equivalently, the multiplicity is the order of the first non-vanishing derivative of f at a.

Existence of zeros

The fundamental theorem of algebra states that every non-constant polynomial with complex coefficients has a zero in the complex plane; counting multiplicity, a polynomial of degree n has exactly n zeros. More generally, Picard's theorem and results such as Rouché's theorem and the argument principle give powerful tools for locating and counting zeros of analytic functions.

Properties

The zeros of a holomorphic function on a connected open set are isolated unless the function is identically zero: if a sequence of distinct zeros has a limit point inside the domain, the identity theorem implies the function vanishes everywhere. This is a key difference from functions of a real variable such as |x| or sine-like constructions, which may have accumulating zeros. Zeros also play a central role in factorisation theorems such as the Weierstrass factorisation theorem for entire functions.
