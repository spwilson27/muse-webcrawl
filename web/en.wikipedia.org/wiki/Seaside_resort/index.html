Seaside resort

A seaside resort is a town, village or hotel dependent on tourism from visitors seeking leisure by the sea. Such resorts typically offer beaches, promenades, piers, amusements and accommodation, and developed in large numbers from the eighteenth century onward as sea bathing came to be regarded as healthful.

History

Early British resorts such as Scarborough, Brighton and Margate grew with spa culture and then boomed with the arrival of the railways in the nineteenth century, which brought mass working-class excursion traffic; Blackpool became the archetype of the popular resort. Continental Europe developed resorts along the French Riviera and the Baltic, while in the United States Atlantic City, Coney Island and later Florida and California resorts flourished. After the mid-twentieth century, cheap air travel diverted many holidaymakers to warmer destinations, prompting long decline and later regeneration efforts in traditional resorts.
