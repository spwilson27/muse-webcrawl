Policy network

Policy networks are a concept in political science and public administration describing the patterns of relationships between government actors and other stakeholders — interest groups, businesses, experts and civil society organisations — that participate in making public policy in a particular sector. The literature emerged from studies of sub-governments and "iron triangles" in the United States and of corporatism and inter-organisational networks in Europe.

Typologies

A widely used typology, developed by R. A. W. Rhodes and David Marsh, places networks on a continuum. At one end are policy communities: small, stable, tightly integrated groups with restricted membership and shared values. At the other end are issue networks: large, open, fluid constellations of actors with fluctuating access and conflicting interests. Between them lie professional, intergovernmental and producer networks.

Use in research

Policy network analysis is used to explain why policies differ between sectors and countries, how agendas are set, and how governance operates beyond formal hierarchies. Critics argue that the concept can be merely descriptive, and debate continues over whether networks themselves explain outcomes or simply reflect the distribution of resources and power among their members.
