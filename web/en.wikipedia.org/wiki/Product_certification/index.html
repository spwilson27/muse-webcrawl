Product certification

Product certification or product qualification is the process of certifying that a product has passed performance and quality assurance tests, and that it meets qualification criteria stipulated in regulations, contracts or specifications. Certified products typically bear a certification mark that can be checked in a register maintained by the certification body.

Process

Certification is usually carried out by an independent third-party body accredited to standards such as ISO/IEC 17065. The process involves type testing of representative samples, assessment of the manufacturer's production and quality management system, and ongoing surveillance, including periodic retesting and factory inspections, for as long as the certification remains valid.

Examples

Well-known certification schemes include the European CE marking, the North American marks of UL, CSA and ETL, energy labels such as Energy Star, and organic and fair-trade certification for food and textiles. Many jurisdictions legally require certification for products such as electrical equipment, medical devices, toys and construction materials before they may be sold.

Purpose

Certification protects consumers and users, facilitates market access and international trade through mutual recognition agreements, and gives manufacturers a way to demonstrate compliance with safety, health and environmental requirements.
