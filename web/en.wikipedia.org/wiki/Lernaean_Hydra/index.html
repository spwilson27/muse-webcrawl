The Lernaean Hydra or Hydra of Lerna (Greek: Λερναῖα Ὕδρα) is a serpentine water monster in Greek mythology. It dwelt in Lake Lerna in the Argolid, one of the entrances to the Underworld, and was the offspring of Typhon and Echidna. For every head that was cut off, two more grew in its place, and one of its heads was immortal. Its blood and breath were poisonous.

The second labour of Heracles

Killing the Hydra was the second of the Twelve Labours of Heracles. Sent by King Eurystheus, Heracles covered his mouth and nose against the Hydra's venomous breath and attacked it with a sickle or sword. Realising that two heads sprouted for each one destroyed, he called on his nephew Iolaus, who cauterised each neck stump with a burning brand so that no new heads could grow. Heracles then cut off the immortal head with a golden sword given by Athena and buried it, still hissing, under a heavy rock. He dipped his arrows in the Hydra's gall, making them lethally poisonous — a weapon that would later cause his own death.

Eurystheus refused to count the labour, arguing that Heracles had received help from Iolaus. A giant crab, sent by the goddess Hera, bit Heracles during the fight; he crushed it, and Hera placed it in the sky as the constellation Cancer.

Legacy

The Hydra's name survives in the freshwater polyp genus Hydra, known for its regenerative ability, and in the constellation Hydra, the largest in the sky. "Hydra-headed" remains a metaphor for a problem that multiplies when attacked.
