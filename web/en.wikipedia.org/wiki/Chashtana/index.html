Chashtana (also spelled Castana) was a ruler of the Saka Western Satraps in northwestern India during the 1st and 2nd centuries CE. He is regarded as the founder of the Kardamaka dynasty of Western Kshatrapas and is one of the earliest known Saka kings to rule over the region of Malwa and parts of Gujarat and Rajasthan.

Reign

Chashtana's reign is generally dated from around 78 CE to about 130 CE. Many scholars associate the beginning of his rule with the start of the Saka era in 78 CE, an era that remained in use in the Indian calendar for centuries, although this identification is debated. His coins, issued in silver in the tradition of Indo-Greek and earlier Satrap coinage, bear legends in Greek and Brahmi scripts.

Chashtana is mentioned in the geographical work of Ptolemy, who refers to him as "Tiastenes", ruler of Ozene (Ujjain). The Junagadh inscription of his grandson Rudradaman I traces the lineage from Chashtana through his son Jayadaman.

Legacy

Chashtana established a dynasty that would rule western India for nearly three centuries. His grandson Rudradaman I became the most celebrated ruler of the line, famous for his military exploits and for the first long inscription in classical Sanskrit, at Junagadh. The Kardamaka satraps continued to issue coinage and administer the region until the dynasty was extinguished by the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II in the late 4th or early 5th century CE.
