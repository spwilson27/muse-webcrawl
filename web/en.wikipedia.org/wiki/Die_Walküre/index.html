Die Walküre ("The Valkyrie", WWV 86B) is the second of the four music dramas that constitute Richard Wagner's cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen. It was first performed on 26 June 1870 at the National Theatre in Munich — against Wagner's wishes and before the complete cycle was ready — and received its premiere as part of the full Ring at the first Bayreuth Festival in 1876. It is the most popular of the four Ring operas in the modern repertoire.

Synopsis

Act 1: the fugitive Siegmund takes refuge in the house of his enemy Hunding, whose wife Sieglinde — Siegmund's long-lost twin sister — helps him draw the sword Nothung from an ash tree, where the god Wotan had planted it. Act 2: Wotan orders his favourite daughter, the Valkyrie Brünnhilde, to defend Siegmund in his coming duel with Hunding, but under pressure from his wife Fricka, guardian of marriage, he countermands the order. Brünnhilde defies him and shields Siegmund; Wotan intervenes, shattering Nothung, and Siegmund is killed. Brünnhilde flees with the pregnant Sieglinde. Act 3 opens with the Ride of the Valkyries; Wotan condemns Brünnhilde to be stripped of her divinity and left asleep on a rock for the first man who finds her. At her plea, he surrounds her with a ring of magic fire that only a fearless hero — the unborn Siegfried — will cross, and bids her a long farewell.

Music

The score is built from the Ring's web of leitmotifs, including the Sword, the Valkyries' battle cry ("Hojotoho!"), Wotan's Farewell, and the Magic Fire music that closes the opera.
