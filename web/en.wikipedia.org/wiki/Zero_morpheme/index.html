Zero morpheme

Notation: Ø

Field: Morphology (linguistics)

A zero morpheme is a morpheme that carries meaning but has no overt phonetic form, conventionally written with the symbol Ø. The concept is used in morphological analysis where a grammatical contrast is marked by the absence of an affix.

Examples

In English, the plural of sheep is analysed as sheep + Ø, in contrast to regular plurals marked with -s. Likewise, the past tense of verbs such as cut and hit is treated as carrying a zero past-tense morpheme, and zero derivation (conversion) allows a noun such as hammer to function as a verb with no change in form.

Discussion

Zero morphemes are controversial in some theoretical frameworks, where alternative analyses avoid positing invisible elements, but they remain a standard descriptive device in much morphological writing.
