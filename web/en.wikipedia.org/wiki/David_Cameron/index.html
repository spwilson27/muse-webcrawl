David William Donald Cameron, Baron Cameron of Chipping Norton (born 9 October 1966), is a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 2010 to 2016. He was Leader of the Conservative Party from 2005 to 2016 and Member of Parliament for Witney from 2001 to 2016. After seven years out of frontline politics, he returned to government as Foreign Secretary under Rishi Sunak from November 2023 to July 2024, taking a life peerage to sit in the House of Lords.

Early life and career

Cameron was born in London into a wealthy family with stockbroking and banking roots, and educated at Eton and Brasenose College, Oxford, where he read Philosophy, Politics and Economics and graduated with a first. He worked at the Conservative Research Department, as a special adviser to Chancellor Norman Lamont and Home Secretary Michael Howard, and then spent seven years as a director at the media company Carlton Communications before entering Parliament in 2001.

Party leader and prime minister

Elected party leader in 2005 on a modernising platform — rebranding the Conservatives on the environment, social liberalism and public services — Cameron led the party into the 2010 election, which produced a hung parliament. He formed a coalition government with the Liberal Democrats, the UK's first coalition since 1945. His government pursued deficit reduction ("austerity"), raised the personal tax allowance, reformed welfare and education, held the 2011 AV referendum, and legalised same-sex marriage in England and Wales in 2013. In foreign policy he intervened in Libya (2011) and won the 2014 Scottish independence referendum with Scotland voting to remain in the UK.

Cameron unexpectedly won an outright majority in the 2015 general election, partly at the expense of his former coalition partners. He then honoured his manifesto pledge to hold an in–out referendum on membership of the European Union. Campaigning to remain, he lost the referendum of 23 June 2016 (52% Leave, 48% Remain) and resigned the next morning, succeeded by Theresa May on 13 July 2016; he soon also resigned his Commons seat.

After Downing Street

Cameron's memoir For the Record appeared in 2019. His lobbying work for the failed finance firm Greensill Capital, revealed in 2021, prompted inquiries and criticism about lobbying rules. In November 2023 Rishi Sunak brought him back as Foreign Secretary, in which role he dealt with the Ukraine war and the Israel–Hamas war, leaving office when the Conservatives lost the July 2024 general election.
