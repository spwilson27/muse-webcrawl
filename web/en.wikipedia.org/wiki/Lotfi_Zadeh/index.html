Lotfi Zadeh

Lotfi Aliasker Zadeh (4 February 1921 – 6 September 2017) was a mathematician, computer scientist, electrical engineer and professor of computer science at the University of California, Berkeley. He is best known for proposing fuzzy mathematics, consisting of fuzzy sets, fuzzy logic and related concepts, which introduced the idea that set membership can be a matter of degree rather than an all-or-nothing proposition.

Life and career

Zadeh was born in Baku, Azerbaijan, to an Azerbaijani mother and an Iranian journalist father, and grew up in Tehran. He studied electrical engineering at the University of Tehran before emigrating to the United States in 1944, taking degrees at MIT and Columbia University, where he completed his doctorate in 1949. He joined Berkeley in 1959 and chaired its electrical engineering department in the 1960s. With Charles Desoer he co-authored an influential textbook on linear system theory.

Fuzzy sets and soft computing

In his seminal 1965 paper "Fuzzy Sets", Zadeh proposed sets whose elements have degrees of membership between 0 and 1, generalising classical set theory. The idea was initially controversial in the West but was embraced in Japan and elsewhere for control systems, consumer appliances and artificial intelligence. He later developed broader frameworks including fuzzy algorithms, computing with words, and soft computing. Zadeh received many honours, including the IEEE Medal of Honor in 1995.
