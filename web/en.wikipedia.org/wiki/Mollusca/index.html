Mollusca

Mollusca is a large phylum of invertebrate animals, whose members are known as molluscs or mollusks. Around 85,000 extant species are recognised, making it the second-largest animal phylum after Arthropoda. Molluscs include gastropods (snails and slugs), bivalves (clams, oysters, scallops and mussels), cephalopods (squid, octopuses and cuttlefish), chitons and several smaller classes. They inhabit marine, freshwater and terrestrial environments, and range in size from microscopic snails to the colossal squid.

Anatomy

Despite their diversity, molluscs share a basic body plan: a soft body with a muscular foot for locomotion, a visceral mass containing the internal organs, and a mantle — a fold of tissue that secretes the shell (when present) and encloses a mantle cavity housing the gills (ctenidia). Most molluscs possess a radula, a toothed, tongue-like feeding organ, absent in bivalves. The circulatory system is usually open, and respiration and excretion are centred on the mantle cavity.

Diversity and evolution

Molluscs first appeared in the Cambrian period; early fossils such as Kimberella may represent stem-group forms. Gastropods are the most species-rich class, comprising around 80% of molluscs. Cephalopods evolved large brains, image-forming eyes and jet propulsion. Many molluscs pass through a trochophore larval stage, linking them to annelids within the Lophotrochozoa.

Importance to humans

Molluscs are a major food source (oysters, mussels, squid), and shells have been used as currency, ornament and tools. Some are agricultural pests or intermediate hosts of parasites such as schistosomes; cone snails produce potent venoms studied pharmacologically. Pearls and mother-of-pearl come from bivalve shells.
