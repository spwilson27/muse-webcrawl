Puppetry

Puppetry is a form of theatre or performance that involves the manipulation of puppets — inanimate objects, often resembling a human or animal figure, that are animated or manipulated by a human called a puppeteer. The puppeteer uses movements of their hands, arms, or control devices such as rods or strings to move the body, head, limbs, and in some cases the mouth and eyes of the puppet. Puppetry is one of the oldest forms of theatre, found in most human societies, where puppets have served in ritual, storytelling, satire, education, and entertainment.

Types of puppets

  • Hand or glove puppets — worn on the hand, as in Punch and Judy.
  • Rod puppets — controlled by rods from below or behind.
  • Marionettes — suspended and manipulated by strings from above.
  • Shadow puppets — flat figures held between a light source and a screen, as in Indonesian wayang kulit.
  • Ventriloquist figures — operated while the performer appears to throw their voice.
  • Bunraku puppets — large Japanese puppets each operated by three visible puppeteers.

History

Articulated figures survive from ancient Egypt, Greece, and China, and puppet traditions flourished across Asia — Indian kathputli, Chinese shadow theatre, Japanese bunraku — and in Europe through commedia dell'arte figures such as Pulcinella, ancestor of Mr. Punch. In the 20th century, puppetry reached mass audiences through television: Jim Henson's Muppets on Sesame Street and The Muppet Show, Gerry Anderson's Supermarionation series, and productions for adults such as Avenue Q and the giant street-theatre puppets of Royal de Luxe. Puppetry techniques also underpin animatronics and much digital character animation.
