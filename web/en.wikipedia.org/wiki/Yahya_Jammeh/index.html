Overview

Yahya Abdul-Aziz Jemus Jungkung Jammeh (born 1965) is a Gambian politician and former military officer who ruled the Gambia from the 1994 coup until January 2017, when he was forced into exile after losing the December 2016 presidential election to Adama Barrow and initially refusing to step down.

Rule and exile

Jammeh's 22 years combined infrastructure building and flamboyant self-promotion ("Sheikh Professor Alhaji Dr.") with systematic repression documented by the later Truth, Reconciliation and Reparations Commission (TRRC, 2018–2021): the paramilitary "Jungulers" death squad, the 2005 killing of over 50 West African migrants, persecution of alleged "witches" (2009), threats against LGBT Gambians, claims to cure HIV/AIDS with herbs, and the 2015 declaration of an "Islamic Republic" (reversed by his successor). ECOWAS's threatened military intervention (Operation Restore Democracy) in January 2017 persuaded him to leave for Equatorial Guinea. The TRRC recommended prosecutions; the Gambian government accepted most recommendations in principle, and asset-recovery proceedings (including US forfeiture of his Maryland property) have proceeded.
