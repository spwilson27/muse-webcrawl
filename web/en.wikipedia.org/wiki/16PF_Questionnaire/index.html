16PF Questionnaire

A 16PF profile plots sten scores across Cattell's sixteen primary factors

The Sixteen Personality Factor Questionnaire (16PF) is a self-report personality test developed by psychologist Raymond Cattell and his colleagues, first published in 1949. Derived from factor-analytic studies of the trait vocabulary of everyday language, it measures sixteen primary personality dimensions and five global (second-order) factors, and has been widely used in occupational assessment, counselling and research.

The sixteen primary factors

The primary scales are: Warmth, Reasoning, Emotional Stability, Dominance, Liveliness, Rule-Consciousness, Social Boldness, Sensitivity, Vigilance, Abstractedness, Privateness, Apprehension, Openness to Change, Self-Reliance, Perfectionism and Tension. Scores are reported as stens (standard tens), ranging from 1 to 10 against norm groups.

Global factors

Second-order analysis of the primary scales yields five global factors — Extraversion, Anxiety, Tough-Mindedness, Independence and Self-Control — which correspond closely to the later Big Five model of personality, whose development Cattell's work influenced. The fifth edition of the questionnaire (1993) contains 185 multiple-choice items and typically takes 35–50 minutes to complete.

Applications and criticism

The 16PF is used for employee selection and development, career guidance and clinical screening. Like other self-report inventories, it has been criticised for susceptibility to response styles and faking, and its sixteen-factor structure has not always been recovered in independent replications, though it remains one of the most extensively researched personality instruments.
