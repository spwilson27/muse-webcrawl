X-Men is a 2000 American superhero film directed by Bryan Singer from a screenplay by David Hayter, based on the Marvel Comics superhero team of the same name. The film depicts a world in which mutants — humans born with superhuman abilities — are distrusted by society. Two factions emerge: Professor Charles Xavier's X-Men, who seek peaceful coexistence, and Magneto's Brotherhood of Mutants, who believe war between mutants and humans is inevitable.

Cast

The film stars Patrick Stewart as Professor X, Ian McKellen as Magneto, Hugh Jackman as Wolverine, Halle Berry as Storm, Famke Janssen as Jean Grey, James Marsden as Cyclops, Anna Paquin as Rogue, Rebecca Romijn as Mystique and Tyler Mane as Sabretooth. Jackman's performance launched him to international stardom.

Reception and legacy

The film was a commercial and critical success, grossing $296 million worldwide. Together with Blade (1998) and Spider-Man (2002), it is credited with reviving the superhero genre and launching the long-running X-Men film series, beginning with the sequel X2 (2003).
