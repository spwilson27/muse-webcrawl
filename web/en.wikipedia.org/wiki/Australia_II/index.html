Australia II (KA–6) is an Australian 12 Metre class America's Cup challenge racing yacht that won the 1983 America's Cup, ending a 132-year winning streak by the New York Yacht Club. Launched in 1982, Australia II was owned by a syndicate directed by businessman Alan Bond, representing the Royal Perth Yacht Club. Skippered by John Bertrand, she defeated the American defender Liberty, skippered by Dennis Conner, in the best-of-seven final series by four races to three.

Design

Australia II was designed by Ben Lexcen (formerly Bob Miller), with tank testing carried out at the Netherlands Ship Model Basin. Her most famous feature was a radical winged keel, with horizontal wings mounted on the bottom of a shortened keel, which reduced drag and improved performance, particularly when sailing to windward. The design was kept secret before the regatta—skirts were hung around the hull when the yacht was out of the water—and became the subject of controversy, with the New York Yacht Club challenging the legality of the keel; it was ruled permissible under the 12 Metre rule.

The 1983 America's Cup

After winning the challenger elimination series (the first Louis Vuitton Cup), Australia II met Liberty in September 1983 off Newport, Rhode Island. The American yacht won the first two races, but the Australians recovered to level the series 3–3, setting up a winner-take-all seventh race on 26 September 1983. In the final race, Australia II came from behind after a broken sail change to pass Liberty on the final downwind leg and win by 41 seconds.

Significance

The victory ended the longest winning streak in sporting history—the United States had defended the Cup since 1851—and was celebrated across Australia; Prime Minister Bob Hawke famously declared that "any boss who sacks anyone for not turning up today is a bum". Australia II's sail number, KA–6, and the boxing kangaroo flag flown by the syndicate became national symbols. The yacht is now preserved at the Western Australian Maritime Museum in Fremantle. In 1987, Kookaburra III defended, but lost, the Cup at Fremantle in the first defence held outside the United States.
