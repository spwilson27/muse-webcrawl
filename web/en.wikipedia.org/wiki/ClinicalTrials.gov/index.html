ClinicalTrials.gov

ClinicalTrials.gov
Type: Clinical trials registry and results database
Operator: US National Library of Medicine (NIH)
Launched: 2000
Scale: Hundreds of thousands of studies from over 200 countries

ClinicalTrials.gov is a registry and results database of publicly and privately supported clinical studies conducted around the world. It is maintained by the National Library of Medicine at the US National Institutes of Health, and is the largest clinical trials registry in the world.

History

The database was established following the Food and Drug Administration Modernization Act of 1997, which mandated a registry of trials of experimental treatments for serious or life-threatening diseases, and it became publicly available in February 2000. Registration requirements expanded with the ICMJE policy of 2005, under which major medical journals require prospective trial registration as a condition of publication, and the FDA Amendments Act of 2007, which extended registration and added a results database.

Contents and use

Each record summarises a study's purpose, eligibility criteria, locations, interventions and outcome measures, identified by a unique NCT number. Sponsors and investigators are responsible for submitting and updating their information. The registry is used by patients seeking trials to join, researchers monitoring the evidence base, and regulators and journals checking that results are reported as promised.

Significance

ClinicalTrials.gov is a central instrument against publication bias and selective outcome reporting: prospective registration makes it possible to detect trials that go unpublished or whose reported outcomes differ from those originally planned. Compliance with results reporting has been the subject of continuing enforcement and public scrutiny.
