Tirumala Venkateswara Temple

The Tirumala Venkateswara Temple is a Hindu temple in Tirumala, Andhra Pradesh, dedicated to Venkateswara, an incarnation of Vishnu, who is believed to have appeared there to save mankind in the Kali Yuga. It is often described as one of the richest and most visited temples in the world.

History

The temple's origins are ancient; it was patronised by Pallava, Chola, Pandya and Vijayanagara rulers, the latter — notably Krishnadevaraya — contributing gold and jewels. The shrine follows the Vaikhanasa Agama tradition; the saint Ramanuja is credited with organising its rituals in the 11th–12th centuries.

Worship and administration

The temple is administered by the Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams. Daily rituals begin with Suprabhatam; major festivals include the annual Brahmotsavams. Pilgrims traditionally offer their hair in tonsure, and the prasadam laddu of Tirupati holds a geographical indication tag. Donations in the hundi make the temple among the wealthiest religious institutions globally.
