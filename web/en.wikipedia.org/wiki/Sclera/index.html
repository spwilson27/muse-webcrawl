Sclera

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The sclera, also known as the white of the eye or, in older literature, as the albuginea oculi, is the opaque, fibrous, protective outer layer of the eye containing mainly collagen and some crucial elastic fiber. In humans, and some other vertebrates, the whole sclera is white or pale, contrasting with the coloured iris, but in many mammals the visible part of the sclera matches the colour of the iris, so the white part does not normally show while other vertebrates have distinct colors for both of them. In the development of the embryo, the sclera is derived from the neural crest.

Structure

The sclera forms the posterior five-sixths of the connective tissue coat of the globe of the eye. It is continuous with the dura mater and the cornea, and maintains the shape of the globe, offering resistance to internal and external forces, and provides an attachment for the extraocular muscle insertions. The sclera is perforated by many nerves and vessels passing through the posterior scleral foramen, the hole formed by the optic nerve. At the optic disc the outer two-thirds of the sclera continues with the dura mater of the optic nerve; the inner third presents as a plate, the lamina cribrosa, across which the optic nerve fibres pass.

Function

The sclera, along with the intraocular pressure of the eye, maintains the shape of the eyeball and protects its delicate inner structures from trauma. Its whiteness in humans is thought to aid social signalling: because the sclera is visibly white against a pigmented iris, the direction of another person's gaze is easy to detect — a trait sometimes called the cooperative eye hypothesis.

Clinical significance

Inflammation of the sclera is called scleritis, a painful condition that can threaten vision; inflammation of its outer layer is episcleritis, usually milder. In jaundice, the sclera takes on a yellow colour because of bilirubin accumulation, a sign often visible in the eyes before the skin. Blue sclerae can be a feature of osteogenesis imperfecta, in which thin sclera allows the underlying pigmented tissue to show through. With age, the sclera may develop fatty deposits and a slightly yellowish tinge.
