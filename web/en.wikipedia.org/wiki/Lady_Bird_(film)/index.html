Lady Bird is a 2017 American coming-of-age comedy-drama film written and directed by Greta Gerwig in her solo directorial debut. Set in Sacramento, California, in 2002–2003, it stars Saoirse Ronan as Christine "Lady Bird" McPherson, a high-school senior who longs to escape her hometown for an East Coast college, and Laurie Metcalf as her strong-willed mother, Marion. The film is semi-autobiographical, drawing on Gerwig's own Sacramento upbringing.

Plot

Lady Bird attends a Catholic girls' school, dreams of attending a prestigious out-of-state university "where culture is", and clashes with her mother over money, ambition, and affection. Over her senior year she navigates first love, a falling-out with her best friend Julie, a school musical, and her father's quiet depression after losing his job. Accepted at a New York college with financial aid, she leaves — and, in a closing phone call home, finally introduces herself by the name her mother gave her.

Reception

Lady Bird premiered at Telluride in September 2017 and became one of the best-reviewed films of its decade; it briefly held a perfect score on Rotten Tomatoes across a record number of reviews. It was named one of the year's ten best films by the American Film Institute and the National Board of Review, won the Golden Globe for Best Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy and Best Actress (Ronan), and received five Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Original Screenplay. Gerwig became only the fifth woman nominated for the Best Director Oscar.

Themes

Critics highlighted the film's unsentimental portrait of a mother–daughter relationship, its treatment of class and hometown ambivalence — "I wanted to leave, but I also wanted to be from somewhere" — and Gerwig's remark that "attention is a form of love", which underlies the film's final scenes.
