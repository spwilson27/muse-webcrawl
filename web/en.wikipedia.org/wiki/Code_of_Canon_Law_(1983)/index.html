The 1983 Code of Canon Law (abbreviated 1983 CIC from its Latin title Codex Iuris Canonici) is the current code of canon law for the Latin Church of the Catholic Church. It was promulgated by Pope John Paul II on 25 January 1983 with the apostolic constitution Sacrae disciplinae leges, and took effect on the first Sunday of Advent that year.

Structure

The Code contains 1,752 canons arranged in seven books: general norms; the People of God; the teaching function of the Church; the sanctifying function of the Church; the temporal goods of the Church; sanctions in the Church; and processes.

Revision

The revision was announced by Pope John XXIII alongside the Second Vatican Council, whose ecclesiology shaped the new Code. Pope Francis later amended parts of it, notably Book VI on penal sanctions (revised by Pascite gregem Dei, 2021) and provisions on institutes of consecrated life and the Roman Curia.
