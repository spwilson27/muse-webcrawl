Acoustical consultant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An acoustical consultant is a professional who advises on the control of sound and vibration in buildings, the environment and products. Acoustical consultants work within the discipline of acoustics, applying physics, engineering and measurement to problems such as room acoustics for performance spaces, sound insulation between dwellings, environmental noise assessment for new development, and the acoustic design of recording studios, schools, hospitals and open-plan offices.

Work

Typical work includes predicting noise from roads, railways, airports and industrial sites; measuring existing sound levels with sound level meters and analysers; computer modelling of sound propagation and room response; specifying constructions that meet building-regulation requirements for insulation; and designing the internal geometry and finishes of auditoria so that music and speech are clear in every seat. Consultants also act as expert witnesses in planning inquiries and noise-nuisance cases.

Training and professional bodies

Most acoustical consultants hold degrees in acoustics, physics, engineering or related subjects, often followed by a specialist postgraduate qualification such as the diploma of the UK Institute of Acoustics. Professional bodies include the Institute of Acoustics (United Kingdom), the Acoustical Society of America, and the National Council of Acoustical Consultants. Pioneers of the profession include Wallace Clement Sabine, founder of architectural acoustics, and firms founded in the mid twentieth century that specialised in concert-hall design.
