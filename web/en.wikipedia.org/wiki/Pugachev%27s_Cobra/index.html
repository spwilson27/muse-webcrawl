Pugachev's Cobra

Pugachev's Cobra is an aerial maneuver in which an aircraft rapidly raises its nose to a vertical or beyond-vertical attitude — typically to an angle of attack of 90° to 120° — momentarily stalls its forward motion while maintaining altitude, and then returns to level flight. It is a post-stall maneuver that dramatically decelerates the aircraft.

History

The maneuver is named for Viktor Pugachev, a Sukhoi test pilot who first performed it publicly in a Sukhoi Su-27 at the Paris Air Show at Le Bourget in June 1989, stunning Western observers and demonstrating the Flanker's exceptional low-speed control authority. A similar pitch-up had been performed earlier by Syrian pilots and was reportedly flown in the Saab 35 Draken (the Swedish "kort parad"), but Pugachev's demonstration gave the maneuver its name and fame.

Technique and requirements

To perform the Cobra, the pilot must override the angle-of-attack limiter and pitch the aircraft up sharply; powerful engines and robust pitch authority keep the aircraft from losing altitude or departing controlled flight, and thrust vectoring makes the maneuver easier in later designs such as the Su-35 and Su-57. Its combat utility is debated: in theory it could cause a pursuing aircraft to overshoot or break a Doppler radar lock, but it leaves the aircraft slow and energy-depleted.
