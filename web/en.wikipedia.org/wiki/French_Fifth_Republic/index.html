The French Fifth Republic (Cinquième République) is France's current republican system of government, established by the Constitution of 4 October 1958. It emerged from the collapse of the Fourth Republic during the Algerian War and the crisis of May 1958, which brought Charles de Gaulle back to power. The new constitution greatly strengthened the executive, in particular the President, in reaction against the parliamentary instability of the preceding regime.

Origins

The Fourth Republic was paralysed by ministerial instability and unable to resolve the Algerian conflict. Following the Algiers putsch of 13 May 1958, President René Coty threatened to resign unless de Gaulle was appointed head of government with power to draft a new constitution. A draft prepared under Michel Debré, drawing on de Gaulle's Bayeux speech of 1946, was approved by referendum on 28 September 1958 with about 79 percent of the vote in metropolitan France, and promulgated on 4 October. De Gaulle became the Republic's first President in January 1959.

Institutions

The Constitution creates a semi-presidential system: a President elected by universal suffrage (directly since a 1962 referendum), a Prime Minister and government responsible to the National Assembly, a bicameral Parliament (National Assembly and Senate), and a Constitutional Council that reviews legislation. The presidential term was shortened from seven years to five (the quinquennat) by referendum in 2000, so that presidential and legislative elections normally coincide, reducing the risk of "cohabitation".

Development

Periods of cohabitation — a President of one camp and a Prime Minister of another — occurred in 1986–1988, 1993–1995 and 1997–2002. The emergency powers of Article 16 have been invoked only once, during the Generals' Putsch of April 1961. A constitutional revision in 2008 limited the President to two consecutive terms, strengthened parliamentary oversight, and allowed the Constitutional Council to review existing laws through the question prioritaire de constitutionnalité. The Fifth Republic is the third-longest-lived French regime since 1789, after the Third Republic and the constitutional monarchy of the July Monarchy era.
