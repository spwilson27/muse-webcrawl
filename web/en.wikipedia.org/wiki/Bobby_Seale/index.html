Robert George Seale (born October 22, 1936) is an American political activist and author who co-founded the Black Panther Party with Huey P. Newton in October 1966 in Oakland, California, serving as the party's chairman. Seale was one of the defendants in the Chicago Eight trial of 1969, charged in connection with protests at the 1968 Democratic National Convention.

Early life and activism

Seale was born in Texas and raised in Oakland after his family moved there during the Second World War. He served in the U.S. Air Force before being discharged, then worked as a sheet-metal mechanic and attended Merritt College, where he met Newton. Influenced by Malcolm X and by accounts of police violence against Black residents, the two founded the Black Panther Party for Self-Defense, drafting its Ten-Point Program and organising armed patrols to observe police — lawful under California open-carry rules at the time.

Trials

At the 1969 Chicago conspiracy trial, Seale repeatedly demanded to represent himself and protested the proceedings; Judge Julius Hoffman ordered him bound and gagged in the courtroom before severing his case, and sentenced him to four years for contempt (later reversed on appeal). In 1970–71 he stood trial in New Haven, Connecticut, on charges connected to the death of Panther member Alex Rackley; the case ended in a hung jury and the charges were dismissed.

Later life

Seale ran for mayor of Oakland in 1973, forcing a runoff. He later left the Panthers, wrote the memoir A Lonely Rage (1978) and a cookbook, Barbeque'n with Bobby Seale (1988), and has lectured widely on the history of the Black Panther Party and community organising.
