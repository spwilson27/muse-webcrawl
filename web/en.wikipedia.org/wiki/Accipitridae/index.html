Accipitridae

The Accipitridae is one of the four families within the order Accipitriformes, and is a family of small to large birds of prey with strongly hooked bills and variable morphology based on diet. They feed on a range of prey items from insects to medium-sized mammals, with a number feeding on carrion and a few feeding on fruit. The Accipitridae have a cosmopolitan distribution, being found on all the world's continents (except Antarctica) and a number of oceanic island groups.

Characteristics

The family includes the eagles, hawks, kites, harriers, Old World vultures and buzzards. Members range in size from the tiny pearl kite, at about 23 cm in length, to the cinereous vulture, which can exceed 115 cm and weigh up to 14 kg. They have powerful talons, keen eyesight — typically several times more acute than a human's — and broad wings adapted either for soaring or, in the forest hawks of the genus Accipiter, for rapid pursuit through woodland.

Taxonomy and evolution

The family contains around 250 species in some 70 genera, divided into a number of subfamilies. Molecular studies have substantially revised its internal arrangement, separating the osprey (Pandionidae) and secretarybird (Sagittariidae) into their own families and grouping the New World vultures in Cathartidae. The earliest fossils of accipitrid-like birds date from the Eocene.

Diet and behaviour

Most accipitrids are carnivores hunting live prey, but the Old World vultures specialise in scavenging carcasses, and the honey buzzard feeds largely on wasp larvae. Many species are migratory, concentrating at narrow sea crossings and mountain passes during migration.
