Dermoid cyst

A dermoid cyst is a teratoma of a cystic nature that contains an array of developmentally mature, solid tissues. It frequently consists of skin, hair follicles, and sweat glands, while other commonly found components include clumps of long hair, pockets of sebum, blood, fat, bone, nails, teeth, eyes, cartilage, and thyroid tissue. Because dermoid cysts grow slowly and contain mature tissue, they are almost always benign.

Sites

Dermoid cysts can occur wherever embryonic ectoderm may be trapped, most often in the ovaries (where they are the most common ovarian germ cell tumor in young women), the testes, the sacrococcygeal region in infants, and along embryonic fusion lines of the head and neck, such as the lateral brow. Intracranial and spinal dermoids are rarer.

Diagnosis and treatment

They are frequently incidental findings on ultrasound, CT, or MRI, where fat, calcification, and hair produce characteristic appearances (such as the "Rokitansky nodule"). The standard treatment is complete surgical excision, taking care not to spill the cyst contents, which can cause chemical peritonitis or meningitis depending on site. Ovarian dermoids can cause ovarian torsion and require prompt surgery. Malignant transformation is rare, occurring in roughly 1–2% of cases, usually to squamous cell carcinoma.
