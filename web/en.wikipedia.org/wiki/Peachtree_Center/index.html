Peachtree Center

Peachtree Center is a district of high-rise office buildings, hotels, and retail space in downtown Atlanta, Georgia, United States. Developed and designed largely by architect John C. Portman Jr. beginning in the 1960s, the complex is credited with revitalizing downtown Atlanta and with pioneering the inward-facing, multi-block "city within a city" model of urban development.

History

The first component, the Atlanta Merchandise Mart (now AmericasMart Atlanta), opened in 1961. Portman went on to add a succession of office towers, the Hyatt Regency Atlanta (1967) with its landmark 22-storey atrium, the Marriott Marquis, and the Westin Peachtree Plaza. The buildings are connected by a network of pedestrian sky bridges and an internal shopping level, allowing visitors to move between blocks without reaching the street.

Components

Major elements of the district include the office towers along Peachtree Street, the AmericasMart trade mart complex, several large convention hotels, and the Peachtree Center station on the MARTA rail system, one of the deepest underground stations in the United States. The complex hosts or adjoins annual events such as Dragon Con, whose host hotels cluster around the district.

Criticism

Urbanists have criticized Peachtree Center for turning its back on the street: its enclosed, privately managed interior spaces and sky bridges draw pedestrian life away from public sidewalks, a pattern repeated in many of Portman's later projects in other cities.
