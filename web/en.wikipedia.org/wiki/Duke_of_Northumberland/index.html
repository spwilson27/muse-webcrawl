Duke of Northumberland is a title in the Peerage of Great Britain, created in 1766 for Hugh Percy, 2nd Earl of Northumberland, of the Percy family. The dukes' principal seat is Alnwick Castle, with a London residence at Syon House. The title had earlier creations in 1551 for John Dudley and in 1683 for George FitzRoy, both of which became extinct.

History

The Percy family were great medieval magnates in the north of England; Henry Percy, "Hotspur", was killed at the Battle of Shrewsbury in 1403. The earldom passed by marriage in 1749 to Sir Hugh Smithson, who took the name Percy and was created Duke in 1766. The 1st Duke commissioned Robert Adam to remodel Alnwick and Syon.

The current holder is Ralph Percy, 12th Duke of Northumberland. The heir apparent is George Percy, Earl Percy.
