2020 United States census

The 2020 United States census was the 24th decennial census of the United States. Its reference day, Census Day, was 1 April 2020. The count determined the resident population of the United States to be 331,449,281, an increase of 7.4 per cent over the 308,745,538 enumerated in the 2010 census. It was the first US census in which most households were invited to respond online.

The 2020 census was the first to offer an online response option to most households.

Conduct

As required by the US Constitution, the census is used to apportion seats in the House of Representatives among the states and to redraw congressional and state legislative districts. Households could respond by internet, telephone or paper questionnaire. The field operation was severely disrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic: in-person follow-up with non-responding households was suspended in March 2020 and repeatedly rescheduled, and the counting deadline was contested in court before data collection ended on 15 October 2020.

The 2020 census was also the first in which the Census Bureau applied differential privacy to its published data, injecting controlled noise to protect respondents' confidentiality.

Controversies

The Trump administration sought to add a question on citizenship status to the questionnaire. In Department of Commerce v. New York (2019), the Supreme Court blocked the question on procedural grounds, finding the administration's stated rationale pretextual. A subsequent presidential memorandum attempting to exclude undocumented immigrants from the apportionment base was challenged in court and was rescinded by President Biden in January 2021 before taking effect.

Results and apportionment

Apportionment results were released on 26 April 2021. Texas gained two House seats; Colorado, Florida, Montana, North Carolina and Oregon each gained one. California, Illinois, Michigan, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania and West Virginia each lost one. New York's loss famously turned on a margin of just 89 people. The population growth rate of 7.4 per cent was the second-lowest in US history, ahead of only the 1930s.

State rankings

California remained the most populous state at 39,538,223, followed by Texas (29,145,505) and Florida (21,538,187), which overtook New York (20,201,230). The fastest-growing state by percentage was Utah, at 18.4 per cent. Wyoming was the least populous state, with 576,851 residents — fewer than the District of Columbia (689,545).
