The Falaise pocket or battle of the Falaise pocket (German: Kessel von Falaise) was the decisive engagement of the Battle of Normandy in the Second World War. Allied forces formed a pocket around the German Seventh Army and Fifth Panzer Army in northern France, south of the town of Falaise, and largely destroyed them between 12 and 21 August 1944.

Background

Following Operation Cobra, the American breakout from Normandy at the end of July 1944, Allied forces advanced rapidly. Hitler ordered a counterattack toward Avranches (Operation Lüttich), which failed and left German armies exposed. General Omar Bradley and General Bernard Montgomery then planned to encircle them: American and French forces swung north from Argentan while British and Canadian forces (Operation Totalize and Operation Tractable) pressed south toward Falaise.

The battle

Canadian forces captured Falaise on 16 August. The gap between the two Allied spearheads narrowed to a corridor around Trun–Chambois. The Polish 1st Armoured Division seized the Mont Ormel ridge (Hill 262) and held it against repeated breakout attempts on 19–21 August, while American and French forces closed the pocket at Chambois and Saint-Lambert-sur-Dives. German units escaping eastward were subjected to devastating Allied artillery and air attack along roads crammed with wrecked vehicles — a corridor aircrew dubbed the "Corridor of Death".

Aftermath

Estimates suggest 10,000–15,000 German soldiers were killed and 40,000–50,000 captured within the pocket, though substantial numbers escaped across the Seine before the gap was fully sealed. The destruction of the German armies in Normandy cleared the way for the liberation of Paris on 25 August and the Allied advance to the German border.
