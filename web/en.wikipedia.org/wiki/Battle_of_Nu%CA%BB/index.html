Battle of Nuʻu

The Battle of Nuʻu was fought in 1791 on the island of Maui in the Hawaiian Islands, between the forces of Kamehameha I and the Maui chief Kalanikūpule. It formed part of Kamehameha's wars of conquest that eventually unified the Hawaiian Islands under his rule.

Background

Following his victory at the Battle of Kepaniwai in 1790 — in which Kamehameha's army, aided by cannon served by John Young and Isaac Davis, overwhelmed Maui forces in the ʻĪao Valley — Kamehameha returned to Hawaiʻi island, leaving Maui under its chiefs. In 1791 the Maui ruler Kalanikūpule raided the Waipiʻo district of Hawaiʻi, prompting Kamehameha to invade Maui again.

Battle

Kamehameha landed at Hāna and advanced along the Kaupō coast. Near Nuʻu, in the Kaupō district of southeastern Maui, his forces fought the Maui army in a hard engagement in which the use of firearms and cannon again proved decisive, and Kalanikūpule's forces were driven from the field.

Aftermath

The victory secured Maui for Kamehameha and set the stage for his invasion fleet's expedition to Oʻahu in 1795, culminating at the Battle of Nuʻuanu and the unification of most of the islands.
