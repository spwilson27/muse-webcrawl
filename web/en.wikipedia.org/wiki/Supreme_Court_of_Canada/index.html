Supreme Court of Canada

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Supreme Court of Canada is the highest court in the Canadian judicial system, the final arbiter of the Constitution Acts and of federal and provincial law alike (Canada's single, unified court system makes it a general court of appeal for civil and criminal matters, unlike the United States model). It consists of nine judges appointed by the federal government, of whom three must come from Quebec by statute — a guarantee of the civil-law tradition's and the francophone minority's presence — with regional conventions allocating the other seats.

Role

The Court hears appeals by leave (typically a few dozen a year), issues references (advisory opinions on questions the federal or provincial governments put to it — a function that has produced leading constitutional doctrine, from the Patriation Reference (1981) to the Quebec Secession Reference (1998)), and since 1982 reviews legislation under the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, applying the Charter's internal limits (section 1's "reasonable limits" and the Oakes proportionality test) and subject to the section 33 "notwithstanding clause" letting legislatures override certain rights temporarily — a built-in legislative reply unique among liberal constitutions. The appointment process (executive choice softened by advisory boards and parliamentary hearings) is a live debate in Canadian institutional politics.
