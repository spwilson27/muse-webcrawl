BAE Systems Hawk

The BAE Systems Hawk is a British single-engine, jet-powered advanced trainer aircraft. It was first known as the Hawker Siddeley Hawk, and subsequently produced by its successor companies, British Aerospace and BAE Systems. It has been used in a training capacity and as a low-cost combat aircraft by many air forces worldwide.

Development and design

The Hawk was designed in the late 1960s and early 1970s to replace the Folland Gnat and Hawker Hunter in the advanced training role. It is a tandem-seat, low-wing monoplane powered by a Rolls-Royce Turbomeca Adour turbofan. Entering RAF service in 1976, it became famous as the aircraft of the Red Arrows aerobatic display team, which has flown the Hawk since 1979.

Variants

The original Hawk T1 was followed by export single-seat and dual-role versions (the 60, 100, and 200 series). The extensively redesigned Hawk Mk 120/127 and the RAF's Hawk T2 (Mk 128) feature a modern glass cockpit, radar or laser rangefinder options, and the Adour Mk 951 engine. The type was developed into the carrier-capable T-45 Goshawk for the United States Navy. India licence-builds the Hawk under an agreement with Hindustan Aeronautics Limited.
