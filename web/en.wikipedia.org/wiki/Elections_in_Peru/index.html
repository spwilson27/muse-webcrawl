Elections in Peru

Elections in Peru are held at the national, regional and local levels. At the national level, Peruvians elect a President of the Republic and a unicameral Congress. Peru uses a system of compulsory voting for citizens between the ages of 18 and 70, and elections are administered by the National Jury of Elections (Jurado Nacional de Elecciones, JNE) and the National Office of Electoral Processes (ONPE).

Presidential elections

The President of Peru is elected for a five-year term and may not be re-elected for an immediately succeeding term. Presidential elections use a two-round system: if no candidate receives more than half of the valid votes in the first round, a run-off is held between the two leading candidates. The president is elected together with two vice-presidents on the same ticket.

Congressional elections

The Congress of the Republic of Peru has 130 members, elected for five-year terms by proportional representation in multi-member electoral districts corresponding largely to the country's regions. A 5 percent national threshold applies for parties to obtain seats. Peru's Congress was bicameral until the 1993 constitution made it unicameral; a 2024 constitutional reform approved the return to a bicameral legislature from 2026.

Regional and local elections

Regional governors and municipal mayors and councils are elected every four years. Regional and municipal elections are held simultaneously nationwide, most recently in 2022.

Electoral system and voting

Voting is compulsory, and failure to vote is punishable by a fine scaled to the district's poverty level. Peruvians living abroad vote in presidential elections and are represented by two congressional seats reserved for the overseas constituency since 2021.
