Sistrunk procedure

FieldOtolaryngology / surgery
Described byWalter Sistrunk, 1920
TreatsThyroglossal duct cyst

The Sistrunk procedure is a surgical operation for removing a thyroglossal duct cyst, a congenital neck mass arising from remnants of the thyroglossal duct. Described by Walter Sistrunk in 1920, it excises the cyst together with the central part of the hyoid bone and a core of tissue following the duct tract toward the base of the tongue.

Background

The thyroid gland descends during development from the foramen cecum at the tongue base to its position in the neck, leaving a tract that normally disappears. Persistence of the tract can form a midline cyst, typically presenting in childhood, which may become infected.

Technique

Through a transverse neck incision, the cyst is dissected free and the middle portion of the hyoid bone is resected, since the tract passes through or behind it. A core of tongue muscle up to the foramen cecum is also removed to take the entire tract.

Outcomes

Sistrunk's operation reduced recurrence rates from around 50 percent with simple excision to roughly 2-5 percent, and remains the standard treatment for thyroglossal duct cysts.
