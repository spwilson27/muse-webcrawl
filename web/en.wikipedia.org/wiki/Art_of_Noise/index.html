Art of Noise (also styled The Art of Noise) were a British avant-garde synth-pop group formed in London in 1983 by producer Trevor Horn, journalist Paul Morley, composer Anne Dudley and engineers Gary Langan and J. J. Jeczalik. Pioneers of sampling, they built collages of sound with the Fairlight CMI and scored international hits while concealing their identities behind masks and graphics.


Art of Noise artwork

Career

Signed to Horn and Morley's ZTT label, the group released the EP Into Battle with the Art of Noise (1983) and the album Who's Afraid of the Art of Noise? (1984), including "Close (to the Edit)" and "Moments in Love". After leaving ZTT for China Records, they had further hits with "Paranoimia" (with Max Headroom), a Grammy-winning cover of "Peter Gunn" with Duane Eddy (1986), and "Kiss" with Tom Jones (1988), which reached the UK top five.

Influence

The group's cut-and-paste production — sampled orchestral stabs, car engines, spoken fragments and Fairlight-sequenced beats — helped define the sound of 1980s pop and hip hop production. Art of Noise disbanded in 1990, reuniting briefly for the concept album The Seduction of Claude Debussy (1999).
