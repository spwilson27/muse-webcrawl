The University of California, Berkeley (UC Berkeley, Berkeley, or Cal) is a public land-grant research university in Berkeley, California. Founded in 1868 as the University of California, it is the flagship and oldest campus of the University of California system, and is widely regarded as one of the world's top public universities.

History

Berkeley was created in 1868 by the merger of the private College of California with the state Agricultural, Mining, and Mechanical Arts College. The campus grew under presidents such as Benjamin Ide Wheeler and Robert Gordon Sproul. Its physics department became famous in the 1930s under Ernest Lawrence, inventor of the cyclotron; Berkeley scientists played central roles in the Manhattan Project, and the campus's Radiation Laboratory gave rise to Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. Sixteen chemical elements have been discovered at Berkeley — more than at any other university — including berkelium and californium, named for the school. In 1964 the campus was the birthplace of the Free Speech Movement.

Academics

The university is organized into 15 colleges and schools, including the College of Letters and Science, College of Engineering, Haas School of Business, and Berkeley Law. It operates on a semester system and enrolls roughly 32,000 undergraduates and 13,000 graduate students. Berkeley faculty, alumni, and researchers have included more than 100 Nobel laureates, and the university is classified among "R1: Very high research activity" institutions.

Campus and student life

The 1,232-acre main campus sits on the eastern shore of San Francisco Bay, centered on landmarks such as Sather Tower (the Campanile), the third-tallest bell and clock tower in the world, and Sather Gate. Its athletic teams, the California Golden Bears, compete in NCAA Division I, principally in the Atlantic Coast Conference since 2024, with a historic rivalry with Stanford.
