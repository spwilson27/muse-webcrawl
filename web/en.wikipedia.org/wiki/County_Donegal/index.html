County Donegal

Contae Dhún na nGall; county town Lifford; largest town Letterkenny.

County Donegal (Irish: Contae Dhún na nGall, "county of the fort of the foreigners") is a county in the Republic of Ireland, in the province of Ulster. It is the largest county in Ulster and the fourth-largest in Ireland, known for its rugged Atlantic coastline, mountains, and strong Irish-language (Gaeltacht) culture. The county town is Lifford, while Letterkenny is the largest town and commercial centre.

Geography

Donegal has the longest coastline of any Irish county and a largely mountainous interior dominated by the Derryveagh and Blue Stack ranges; Mount Errigal, at 751 metres, is its highest peak. The Slieve League cliffs on the south-west coast are among the highest sea cliffs in Europe. The county borders counties Londonderry, Tyrone, Fermanagh, Leitrim, and Sligo—nearly all in Northern Ireland or along a short southern edge—giving it a distinct, somewhat isolated position within the Republic.

History

The county corresponds largely to the medieval kingdom of Tír Chonaill, ruled for centuries by the O'Donnell dynasty, whose defeat and the Flight of the Earls in 1607 opened the way to the Plantation of Ulster. The county was formally established in its present form in the 1580s by the English administration. Donegal suffered heavily in the Great Famine of the 1840s–50s, and emigration shaped its modern history.

Culture and economy

Donegal contains one of Ireland's largest Gaeltacht regions, centred on the Rosses, Gweedore, and the Fanad and Inishowen peninsulas, and is noted for fiddle music, tweed weaving, and a distinctive dialect of Irish. Fishing (from Killybegs, one of Ireland's principal fishing ports), tourism along the Wild Atlantic Way, agriculture, and textiles underpin the economy. The county's Gaelic football team won the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship in 1992 and 2012.
