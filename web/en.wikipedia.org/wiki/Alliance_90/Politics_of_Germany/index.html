Alliance 90/The Greens in the politics of Germany

Politics of Germany
System: Federal parliamentary republic
Legislature: Bundestag and Bundesrat
Major parties: CDU/CSU, SPD, Greens, FDP, AfD, Left

Within the politics of Germany — a federal parliamentary republic in which the Chancellor heads the government and the Bundestag is elected by mixed-member proportional representation — Alliance 90/The Greens has grown from a protest movement of the 1980s into a regular party of government at state and federal level.

Electoral history

The Greens entered the Bundestag in 1983 with 5.6% of the vote, failed to clear the 5% threshold in the West in 1990 (while Alliance 90 won seats in the East), merged in 1993, and thereafter consistently polled between roughly 7% and, at their 2021 peak, 14.8%, becoming the third-largest Bundestag group.

Coalitions

The party has governed in numerous "red-green" state coalitions with the SPD, in Germany's first Green-led state government in Baden-Württemberg (Winfried Kretschmann, from 2011), in black-green and Jamaica coalitions, and federally in 1998–2005 under Gerhard Schröder and 2021–2025 in Olaf Scholz's traffic-light coalition with the SPD and FDP, holding the vice-chancellorship.

Impact

Green participation shifted German policy on nuclear power (phase-out completed 2023), renewable energy, immigration and citizenship, and foreign policy, and normalised coalition options across the party system.
