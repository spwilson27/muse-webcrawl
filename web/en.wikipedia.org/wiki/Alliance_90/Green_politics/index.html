Alliance 90/The Greens within Green politics

Green politics
Core tenets (Four Pillars): Ecology, social justice, grassroots democracy, nonviolence
Leading example: Alliance 90/The Greens (Germany)

Alliance 90/The Greens is the most prominent national example of green politics, a political ideology that aims to foster an ecologically sustainable society often, but not always, rooted in environmentalism, nonviolence, social justice and grassroots democracy.

Origins

The German Greens emerged in the late 1970s from anti-nuclear, peace, feminist and environmental movements, founding Die Grünen in Karlsruhe in 1980 around the Four Pillars. Early internal debate pitted "Fundi" fundamentalists against "Realo" pragmatists; the Realo wing, associated with Joschka Fischer, prevailed as the party entered state and then federal government.

Green politics in government

In the federal SPD–Green government of 1998–2005 the party achieved the nuclear phase-out agreement, renewable energy legislation (EEG), citizenship reform and ecological tax reform. Merging in 1993 with Alliance 90, the East German civil-rights movement, it returned to federal government in 2021 in the traffic-light coalition, leading ministries for economy and climate, foreign affairs, environment and agriculture.

Criticism and evolution

Critics debate the party's compromises in office — including support for military interventions in Kosovo and Afghanistan and for coal phase-out timelines — while supporters see it as proof green politics can govern a major industrial economy.
