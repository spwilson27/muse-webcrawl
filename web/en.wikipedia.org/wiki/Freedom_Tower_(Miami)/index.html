Freedom Tower (Miami)

Freedom Tower
Location: Downtown Miami, Florida, United States
Completed: 1925
Height: 78 m (255 ft), 17 floors
Architectural style: Mediterranean Revival

The Freedom Tower (Spanish: Torre de la Libertad) is a historic tower in Downtown Miami, Florida, designed by the New York architectural firm Schultze and Weaver and built in 1924–1925. It originally served as the headquarters and printing facility of the Miami News newspaper.

History

The tower is modelled after the Giralda in Seville, Spain, with a 78-metre cupola. After the newspaper moved out in 1957, the building was used from 1962 to 1974 by the United States government as a processing, detention and assistance centre for Cuban refugees fleeing Fidel Castro's communist regime — the role that gave it its name. For the Cuban exile community it became a powerful symbol of liberty, sometimes called the "Ellis Island of the South."

Later use

After passing through private hands, the tower was donated to Miami Dade College in 2005. It now houses the Museum of Art and Design (MOAD) and serves as a venue for exhibitions and cultural events. The Freedom Tower was designated a National Historic Landmark in 2008.
