Mirror stage

The mirror stage (French: stade du miroir) is a concept in the psychoanalytic theory of Jacques Lacan, introduced in his 1949 essay "The Mirror Stage as Formative of the I Function". It describes a developmental phase, occurring between roughly six and eighteen months of age, in which an infant recognizes its own image in a mirror.

The concept

For Lacan, the infant, whose motor coordination is still fragmented and unmastered, jubilantly identifies with the unified, coherent image it sees in the mirror. This identification forms the ego — the "Ideal-I" — but it is founded on a misrecognition (méconnaissance), because the coherent image contrasts with the child's actual experience of bodily fragmentation. The ego is thus, for Lacan, an alienating construct, and the mirror stage is the origin of the Imaginary order, one of his three registers of psychic life alongside the Symbolic and the Real.

Influence

The mirror stage became one of the most influential ideas in post-structuralism, literary theory and film studies, where it underpins theories of spectatorship and identification.
