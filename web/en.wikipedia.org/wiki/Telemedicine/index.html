Telemedicine

Telemedicine is the delivery of health care services at a distance using telecommunications and information technology. It allows clinicians to evaluate, diagnose and treat patients without an in-person visit, and includes video consultations, remote monitoring, store-and-forward imaging and mobile health (mHealth) applications. The broader term telehealth also covers non-clinical services such as training and administration.

Types

  • Synchronous — real-time interaction, e.g. video or phone consultations.
  • Asynchronous — store-and-forward exchange of images, records or messages, common in radiology, dermatology and pathology.
  • Remote patient monitoring — continuous collection of physiological data from wearables and home devices.

History

Early telemedicine used telephone and radio to serve remote communities and ships at sea. NASA developed telemetry for astronauts in the 1960s, and hospital-based video links followed. Adoption accelerated dramatically during the COVID-19 pandemic, when regulators relaxed licensing and reimbursement rules and virtual visits became a standard part of primary care.

Benefits and limitations

Telemedicine improves access for rural and mobility-limited patients, reduces travel and can lower costs. Limitations include the absence of physical examination, technology and connectivity barriers (the digital divide), variable reimbursement, and concerns about privacy, licensure across jurisdictions and diagnostic accuracy for some conditions.
