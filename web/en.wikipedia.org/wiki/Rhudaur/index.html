Rhudaur is a fictional kingdom in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. It was one of the three successor kingdoms created when the northern Dúnedain kingdom of Arnor was divided in the year 861 of the Third Age among the three sons of King Eärendur; the others were Arthedain and Cardolan.

Geography and history

Rhudaur comprised the northeastern portion of Eriador, the lands between the Ettenmoors, the Weather Hills, and the river Hoarwell (Mitheithel), including the Angle south of the Trollshaws where a remnant of the Stoor hobbits long survived. Its population included many Hill-men who were not of Númenórean descent, and the line of Isildur failed early in Rhudaur.

Rhudaur soon fell under the influence of Angmar, the realm of the Witch-king, and became its secret ally — described in Tolkien's appendices as an evil people ruled by a Hill-chieftain in the Witch-king's service. It contested control of the Weather Hills and the tower of Amon Sûl (Weathertop) with Arthedain and Cardolan. In T.A. 1409 Rhudaur was overrun and occupied by Angmar, and the few remaining Dúnedain were slain or fled westward; thereafter the land was empty and best avoided by travellers.
