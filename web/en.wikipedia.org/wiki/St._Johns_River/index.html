The St. Johns River (Spanish: Río San Juan) is the longest river in the U.S. state of Florida and its most significant for commercial and recreational use. At 310 miles (500 km) long, it flows north from marshes near Vero Beach through a chain of lakes to empty into the Atlantic Ocean near Jacksonville. It is one of the few major rivers in the United States that flow northward.

Geography

With a drop of only about 30 feet (9 m) over its entire length, the St. Johns is one of the "laziest" rivers in the world, which makes it highly susceptible to tidal effects — ocean tides are felt as far south as Lake Monroe, some 160 miles upstream. The river widens into broad lakes, including Lake Harney, Lake Jesup, Lake Monroe, and Lake George, the second-largest lake in Florida. Its principal tributaries include the Ocklawaha River, Econlockhatchee River, and Wekiva River.

History

Humans have lived along the river for at least 12,000 years; the Timucua peoples inhabited its banks at European contact. The French established Fort Caroline near its mouth in 1564, and the Spanish named the river Río de San Juan after the mission of San Juan del Puerto. In the 19th century the river was Florida's main commercial artery, with steamboats carrying tourists and citrus; Harriet Beecher Stowe, who wintered at Mandarin, promoted it in Palmetto Leaves (1873).

Ecology

The basin supports manatees, largemouth bass fisheries, and extensive wetlands. The river has suffered from pollution, algal blooms, and proposed water withdrawals for growing coastal cities, which remain the subject of environmental litigation and restoration efforts.
