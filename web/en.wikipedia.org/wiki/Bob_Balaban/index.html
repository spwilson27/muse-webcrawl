Bob Balaban

Robert Elmer Balaban (born August 16, 1945) is an American actor, author, producer and director. He was one of the producers nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture for Gosford Park (2001), in which he also appeared. Balaban is particularly known for his collaborations with directors Christopher Guest and Wes Anderson.

Early life

Balaban was born in Chicago, Illinois, into a family prominent in show business: his father founded the Balaban and Katz theatre chain, and his uncle Barney Balaban was president of Paramount Pictures for nearly thirty years. He attended Colgate University and studied acting at HB Studio in New York.

Career

Balaban appeared in films including Midnight Cowboy (1969), Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977), Waiting for Guffman (1996), Best in Show (2000), A Mighty Wind (2003), Moonrise Kingdom (2012) and The Grand Budapest Hotel (2014). On television he played NBC president Russell Dalrymple in Seinfeld and narrated episodes of various series. He directed the film Parents (1989) and episodes of numerous television shows.

Writing

Balaban is the author of the children's book series McGrowl and the actor's diary Playing Around, about the radio drama company he co-founded.
