Jefferson–Hemings controversy

The Jefferson–Hemings controversy concerns the historical debate over whether U.S. President Thomas Jefferson fathered children with Sally Hemings, an enslaved woman at Monticello. The allegation was first published in 1802 by journalist James T. Callender during Jefferson's presidency.

Evidence

Oral histories of Hemings descendants long asserted Jefferson's paternity. A 1998 DNA study showed a match between the Jefferson male line and a descendant of Eston Hemings, Sally Hemings's youngest son, while ruling out the Carr nephews whom Jefferson's defenders had proposed.

Consensus

Following the DNA results and a 2000 Thomas Jefferson Foundation report, most historians now accept that Jefferson fathered at least some, and probably all six, of Hemings's children. Monticello's exhibits and interpretation now present the relationship as fact.
