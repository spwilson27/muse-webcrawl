Discharge planning

Discharge planning is the process of preparing a patient to leave a hospital or other care setting and ensuring that the services, equipment, medication and follow-up care they need are arranged in the community. Effective discharge planning aims to reduce length of stay, prevent unplanned readmissions and ensure continuity of care between hospital and primary or community services.

Process

Planning ideally begins on or shortly after admission. It involves assessing the patient's needs and home circumstances; a multidisciplinary team — doctors, nurses, social workers, pharmacists, therapists and discharge coordinators or case managers — together with the patient and family, arranges follow-up appointments, medication reconciliation, transport, home care, rehabilitation or placement in a skilled nursing or residential facility. A written discharge summary communicates diagnoses, treatment and follow-up plans to the patient's primary care physician.

Policy

In the United States, hospitals participating in Medicare are required to have a discharge planning process under the Conditions of Participation, strengthened by the IMPACT Act of 2014 and the 2019 discharge planning rule, which emphasise patient choice and information sharing with post-acute providers. Poorly coordinated discharges are associated with medication errors and avoidable readmissions, which payment programmes such as the Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program penalise.
