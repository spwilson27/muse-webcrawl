Gordon Relief Expedition

The Gordon Relief Expedition (1884–85), also known as the Nile Expedition, was a British military expedition sent to relieve Major-General Charles George Gordon, who was besieged by the forces of the Mahdi at Khartoum during the Mahdist War in Sudan. Commanded by General Garnet Wolseley, the expedition arrived two days after Khartoum fell and Gordon was killed.

Background

In early 1884 Gordon was sent to Khartoum to evacuate Egyptian garrisons threatened by the Mahdist uprising. Instead of withdrawing, he organised the city's defence and became besieged from March 1884. Public pressure in Britain forced the government of William Gladstone to organise a relief force, though it did not depart until the autumn.

The expedition

Wolseley ascended the Nile with some 10,000 men, using specially built whaler boats rowed by Canadian voyageurs among others. A Camel Corps crossed the Bayuda Desert to cut off the great bend of the Nile, fighting the battles of Abu Klea (17 January 1885) and Abu Kru. The advance steamers reached Khartoum on 28 January 1885, two days after the Mahdists had stormed the city on 26 January and killed Gordon.

Aftermath

The failure provoked public outrage in Britain and severely damaged Gladstone's government; Queen Victoria sent a famous telegram of reproach. British and Egyptian forces withdrew from most of Sudan, which remained under Mahdist rule until the reconquest of 1896–98 culminating at Omdurman. Gordon became a martyr figure of the Victorian era.
