Mount Ōmine (大峯山, Ōmine-san), also known as Mount Sanjō (山上ヶ岳, Sanjō-ga-take), is a mountain in Tenkawa, Nara Prefecture, Japan, with an elevation of 1,719 metres. It is the highest peak of the Ōmine mountain range and the most sacred mountain of Shugendō, the Japanese mountain ascetic tradition founded by En no Gyōja in the 7th century.

The mountain forms part of the "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range," inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004, and lies within Yoshino-Kumano National Park. It is also listed among the 100 Famous Japanese Mountains.

Shugendō and the nyonin kinsei

According to tradition, En no Gyōja opened Mount Ōmine as a place of ascetic practice after receiving visions of the deities Zaō Gongen and others. For more than 1,300 years the mountain has been a training ground for yamabushi, practitioners of Shugendō who undertake severe austerities — hanging over cliffs at the Nishi no Nozoki ("Western Lookout"), climbing chains, and meditating under waterfalls — as a path to enlightenment.

Mount Ōmine is the most prominent surviving example of nyonin kinsei, the historical exclusion of women from sacred mountains, a practice maintained by the temple on the basis of religious tradition. The ban, which dates back centuries across many Japanese sacred peaks, remains in force at Ōmine and has periodically drawn public debate, particularly around the time of the World Heritage inscription; UNESCO accepted the site with the tradition noted as a matter for the religious community.

Ōminesan-ji

Near the summit stands Ōminesan-ji (大峯山寺), the head temple of the practice on the mountain, dedicated to Zaō Gongen. Its main hall houses large images of Zaō Gongen, En no Gyōja, and the deity Zao's attendants. The temple is open during the climbing season, roughly from early May to late September (the togaki or mountain-opening period), when yamabushi and pilgrims ascend in large numbers.

Pilgrimage route

The historic pilgrimage route, the Ōmine Okugakemichi, runs some 170 km from Yoshino to the Kumano shrines, crossing dozens of sacred sites (nabiki) along the ridge. Ascending Mount Ōmine from Dorogawa Onsen on the north side is the most common approach for present-day climbers; the trail passes the "gate of training" and trial sites before reaching the summit shrine.
