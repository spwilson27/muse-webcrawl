European Car of the Year
First awarded: 1964 (Rover 2000)
Jury: Motoring journalists from across Europe
Organised by: Seven European motoring magazines
Announcement: Traditionally at the Geneva Motor Show

The European Car of the Year (ECOTY) is an annual award given to the best new car released in the European market over the preceding year. Established in 1964, it is judged by a jury of senior motoring journalists from European publications and is generally regarded as the most prestigious European automotive award.

History and judging

The award was founded by Fred van der Vlugt of the Dutch magazine Autovisie, with the first winner being the Rover 2000 in 1964. Eligible cars must be new models available in at least five European countries with projected sales of at least 5,000 units. Jurors—around 60 from more than 20 countries—award points on criteria including design, comfort, safety, economy, handling, performance, functionality, environmental requirements and value for money.

Notable winners

Early winners included the Austin 1800, Renault 16 and Peugeot 504. Fiat has been the most successful brand in the award's history, with nine victories. More recent decades saw back-to-back wins for models such as the Volkswagen Golf (2013) and electric cars: the Nissan Leaf (2011) was the first electric winner, followed by others including the Jaguar I-Pace (2019), Kia EV6 (2022) and Jeep Avenger (2023).
