European Synchrotron Radiation Facility

Location: Grenoble, France

Founded: 1988 (first beam 1994)

Storage ring circumference: 844 m

Electron energy: 6 GeV

The European Synchrotron Radiation Facility (ESRF) is an international research institute in Grenoble, France, operating one of the world's most intense sources of X-rays. It is supported by 22 countries, including France, Germany, Italy, the United Kingdom, Spain and the Nordic states, and shares its site on the "Presqu'île" with the Institut Laue–Langevin neutron source and a laboratory of the European Molecular Biology Laboratory.

Facility

Electrons are accelerated to 6 GeV and stored in an 844-metre-circumference ring. As they pass through bending magnets and insertion devices such as undulators, they emit synchrotron radiation — extremely bright beams of X-rays directed into around forty experimental stations, or beamlines, arranged tangentially around the ring. Thousands of visiting researchers use the facility each year for experiments in physics, chemistry, materials science, biology, medicine, earth science and cultural heritage; notable work includes studies of proteins, batteries, fossils and the analysis of paintings.

Extremely Brilliant Source

Between 2018 and 2020 the facility shut down to install a completely new storage ring, the Extremely Brilliant Source (EBS), based on a multi-bend achromat lattice pioneered by the ESRF. The upgrade increased the brilliance and coherence of the X-ray beams by a factor of about 100, making the ESRF the first fourth-generation high-energy synchrotron in operation when user service resumed in 2020.
