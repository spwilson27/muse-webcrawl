Stockholm University (Swedish: Stockholms universitet) is a public research university in Stockholm, Sweden, founded as a college in 1878, with university status since 1960. It is one of Scandinavia's largest universities, with more than 33,000 students, around 1,700 doctoral students, and roughly 5,500 staff.

History

The institution began as Stockholms högskola (Stockholm University College), founded in 1878 as a radical alternative to the established universities at Uppsala and Lund, offering lectures open to the public and emphasising natural sciences. Sofia Kovalevskaya, the first woman to hold a full professorship in mathematics in northern Europe, taught there from 1884. The college gained the right to award degrees in the early 20th century and became a state university in 1960. The main campus moved to Frescati, north of the city centre, in the 1970s.

Organisation

The university has four faculties: Humanities, Law, Social Sciences, and Science. Notable research environments include the Stockholm Resilience Centre, the Institute for International Economic Studies (IIES), the Oskar Klein Centre for cosmoparticle physics, and SciLifeLab, a national molecular biosciences centre shared with other institutions. The university is closely associated with the Nobel Prizes: the Arrhenius Laboratory is named for Svante Arrhenius, and several laureates have been affiliated with the university.

Rankings and notable people

Stockholm University regularly ranks among the top 200 universities in the world. Alumni and faculty include prime minister Ingvar Carlsson, economist and Nobel laureate, and authors such as August Strindberg, who studied at Uppsala but whose Stockholm milieu shaped his work; mathematician Sofia Kovalevskaya and geochemist Hans Pettersson are among its historical figures.
