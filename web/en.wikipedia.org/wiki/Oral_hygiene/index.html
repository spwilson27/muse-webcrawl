Oral hygiene

Oral hygiene
FieldDentistry / preventive medicine
Core practicesTooth brushing with fluoride toothpaste, interdental cleaning (floss, brushes)
PreventsDental caries, gingivitis, periodontitis, halitosis

Oral hygiene is the practice of keeping one's oral cavity clean and free of disease and other problems (such as bad breath) by regular brushing of the teeth (dental hygiene) and adopting good hygiene habits. It is important that oral hygiene be carried out on a regular basis to enable prevention of dental disease and halitosis. The most common types of dental disease are tooth decay (cavities, dental caries) and gum diseases, including gingivitis and periodontitis.

Practices

Professional guidelines generally recommend brushing twice a day with a fluoride toothpaste, cleaning between the teeth daily with floss or interdental brushes, and regular dental check-ups and professional cleaning. Fluoride strengthens enamel and helps reverse early demineralisation. Limiting the frequency of sugar intake, avoiding tobacco and moderating alcohol reduce oral disease risk. Chewing sticks such as the miswak have been used for centuries and remain common in parts of Africa and Asia.

Health significance

Plaque — a biofilm of bacteria on the teeth — drives both caries and gum disease; if not removed it mineralises into tartar (calculus), which can only be removed professionally. Periodontitis, the advanced form of gum disease, destroys the tissues supporting the teeth and is a leading cause of tooth loss in adults. Poor oral health is also associated with systemic conditions including cardiovascular disease and diabetes, though the causal pathways are still studied.
