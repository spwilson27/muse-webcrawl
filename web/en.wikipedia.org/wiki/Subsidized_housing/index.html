Subsidized housing

Subsidized housing
FormsPublic housing, housing vouchers, rent supplements, tax credits
PurposeAffordable shelter for low-income households

Subsidized housing is government-supported accommodation provided at below-market cost to low- and moderate-income households. Subsidies may be paid to public or non-profit housing providers, to private landlords, or directly to tenants, and may take the form of publicly owned housing, rental vouchers, rent supplements, interest-rate subsidies, or tax incentives for developers.

Types

Public housing

In public housing, the state or a municipal housing authority owns and manages the dwellings directly. Large-scale examples include the council housing of the United Kingdom, the HLM of France, and the public housing estates of Singapore (HDB) and Hong Kong, where a majority of the population lives in subsidised homes.

Tenant-based assistance

Voucher programmes, such as Section 8 (the Housing Choice Voucher Program) in the United States, subsidise rent in privately owned housing, typically capping the tenant's contribution at about 30 percent of household income.

Supply-side incentives

Programmes such as the US Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) encourage private construction of affordable units through tax benefits.

Debates

Subsidised housing is debated over issues of cost, concentration of poverty, maintenance of ageing estates, waiting-list fairness, and whether assistance should be tied to places or to people. Critics of large estates cite social segregation; supporters argue that only direct provision guarantees housing as a social right.
