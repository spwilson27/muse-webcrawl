Willow

Willows, also called sallows and osiers, form the genus Salix, comprising around 350 species of deciduous trees and shrubs in the family Salicaceae, found primarily on moist soils in cold and temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere.

Description

Most species have elongated, often lanceolate leaves and bear flowers in catkins in early spring, before or with the leaves. Willows are dioecious, with male and female catkins on separate trees. The weeping willow (Salix babylonica and hybrids) is widely planted as an ornamental near water.

Uses

Willow bark contains salicin, the precursor of aspirin, and has been used medicinally since antiquity. Flexible willow stems (withies) are woven into baskets and hurdles; willow wood is used for cricket bats. Willows are also planted for erosion control and phytoremediation.
