Saddle bronc and bareback riding

Saddle bronc riding and bareback riding are two of the classic roughstock events of rodeo, in which a rider attempts to stay on a bucking horse for eight seconds while demonstrating control and style. Saddle bronc riding, in which the rider uses a specialised saddle and a single rein, is known as rodeo's "classic event"; bareback riding is performed without a saddle, using a leather rigging fitted with a handle.

Saddle bronc riding

The saddle bronc rider sits in a modified stock saddle with no horn, gripping a thick rein attached to the horse's halter. The rider must "mark out" the horse — spurs touching the horse's shoulders on the first jump out of the chute — and then spur in rhythm with the bucking, lifting the rein hand and maintaining a full sweeping stroke. Judges score the rider and the horse equally, each up to 25 points from two judges, for a maximum of 100 points.

Bareback riding

The bareback rider grips a handle on a leather rigging cinched around the horse just behind the withers, wedging a gloved hand into the grip. The rider leans back against the bucking motion and spurs from the horse's neck to near the rigging in time with its jumps. The event is considered especially punishing physically because of the forces transmitted through the rider's arm and back.

Equipment and animals

Bucking horses are bred for the sport and flanked with a soft strap that encourages kicking. Riders wear chaps, a protective vest, and a glove; spurs must have loose, dulled rowels. Competitions are sanctioned by bodies such as the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA), whose season culminates in the National Finals Rodeo.
