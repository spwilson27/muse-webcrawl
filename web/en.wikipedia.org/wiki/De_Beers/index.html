De Beers

De Beers Group is a South African–British corporation specialising in diamond mining, exploration, retail and trading. Founded in 1888 by Cecil Rhodes, it long dominated the world diamond market through its Central Selling Organisation, at its peak controlling around 85–90% of global rough diamond distribution.

Industry: Mining, luxury goods

History

After diamonds were discovered at Kimberley, Rhodes, financed by Rothschild backing, merged rival claims into De Beers Consolidated Mines in 1888. Under Ernest Oppenheimer (from the 1920s) the company built a single-channel marketing cartel, stockpiling diamonds to stabilise prices and creating demand through advertising by N.W. Ayer, including the 1948 slogan "A Diamond Is Forever" and the two-months'-salary guideline for engagement rings.

Modern era

Antitrust pressure, new mines outside its control, and the rise of lab-grown diamonds reduced De Beers' market share to roughly a third by the 2010s. It pleaded guilty in the U.S. in 2004 to price-fixing (industrial diamonds). Anglo American acquired the Oppenheimer family's stake in 2011–2012, taking its holding to 85%, with Botswana holding 15%. Operations include Debswana, Namdeb, and De Beers Canada; retail brands include De Beers Jewellers and Forevermark. The company has faced criticism over colonial-era practices, treatment of San people in Botswana, and "blood diamond" issues addressed by the Kimberley Process.
