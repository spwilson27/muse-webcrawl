Rampant

In heraldry, rampant is an attitude (position) of a beast, typically a lion, depicted rearing up with its forelegs raised and in profile, standing on one hind leg with the head turned to face the viewer. It is one of the most common attitudes for heraldic animals.

Description

A beast rampant is shown standing erect on its left hind leg, with the head in profile, both forelegs raised and the right foreleg above the left, and the tail erect. The term derives from the Old French rampant, present participle of ramper, "to climb" or "creep". When applied to animals other than lions, qualifications are used; a horse in this position is usually called forcene.

Variants

  • Rampant guardant — the head is turned to look at the viewer.
  • Rampant reguardant — the head is turned backward over the shoulder.
  • Rampant sejant — the beast sits on its haunches with forelegs raised.
  • Rampant addorsed — two beasts rampant back to back.

Notable examples

The lion rampant appears in the royal arms of Scotland, in the arms of Flanders and numerous other coats of arms, and on flags such as the Royal Standard of Scotland, commonly called the "Lion Rampant".

Other uses

In architecture, a "rampant arch" is an arch whose supports are at different heights. In ordinary usage, "rampant" also means unrestrained or unchecked, as in "rampant inflation".
