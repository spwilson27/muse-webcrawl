Declaration of Paris

The Declaration of Paris of 1856 was an international agreement regulating maritime warfare, negotiated after the Crimean War. It abolished privateering, protected neutral goods and established rules for blockades.

History

Signed on 16 April 1856 by major European powers, it was the first multilateral treaty open to all nations.

Principles

Its four principles were: privateering is abolished; the neutral flag covers enemy goods except contraband; neutral goods except contraband are not liable to capture; blockades must be effective to be binding.

Legacy

Most major maritime nations eventually acceded, and its principles became part of customary international law.
