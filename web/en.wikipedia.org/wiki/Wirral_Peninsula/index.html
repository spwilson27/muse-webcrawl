The Wirral Peninsula, commonly called the Wirral, is a roughly rectangular peninsula in North West England, about 15 miles (24 km) long and 7 miles (11 km) wide, lying between the River Dee to the west and the River Mersey to the east, with the Irish Sea to the north. Most of the peninsula forms the Metropolitan Borough of Wirral in Merseyside, with a population of about 320,000; its main town is Birkenhead, facing Liverpool across the Mersey.

History

The name "Wirral" derives from the Old English Wirhealhale, "nook of land where bog myrtle grows." In the 10th century the peninsula saw significant Norse settlement, evidenced by place names such as Thingwall ("assembly field") and by the Battle of Brunanburh (937), fought by some accounts near Bromborough. Birkenhead Priory was founded around 1150. The peninsula's fortunes were transformed in the 19th century when Birkenhead became a shipbuilding centre — the Cammell Laird yard launched vessels including CSS Alabama — and when Port Sunlight, a model village for soap workers, was built by Lord Leverhulme in 1888.

Geography and transport

The Wirral's landscape is low sandstone ridges and agricultural land, with coastal resorts at West Kirby, New Brighton, and Hoylake — home of the Royal Liverpool Golf Club, a frequent venue of The Open Championship. The Mersey Railway Tunnel (1886) and two road tunnels connect the peninsula to Liverpool, and the M53 motorway runs its length. The tidal island of Hilbre, off West Kirby, is a nature reserve noted for birdwatching and grey seals.
