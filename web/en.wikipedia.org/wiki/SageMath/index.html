SageMath

Original author: William A. Stein
Initial release: 24 February 2005
Written in: Python, Cython
Operating system: Linux, macOS, Windows
Type: Computer algebra system
License: GNU GPL

SageMath (previously Sage or SAGE, "System for Algebra and Geometry Experimentation") is a computer algebra system (CAS) with features covering many aspects of mathematics, including algebra, combinatorics, graph theory, group theory, numerical analysis, number theory, calculus and statistics. It is free and open-source software released under the terms of the GNU General Public License version 2 or later.

Design

SageMath was designed by William A. Stein, a mathematician at the University of Washington, who released the first version in 2005. Rather than building a system from scratch, SageMath was designed to unify many existing specialized open-source mathematics packages — including NumPy, SciPy, matplotlib, Symmetrica, GAP, PARI/GP, Singular, Maxima and others — into a common interface based on the Python programming language. Users interact with SageMath through a command-line interface, a Jupyter notebook, or the online CoCalc platform.

Features

The system supports symbolic computation, arbitrary-precision arithmetic, algebraic number theory, elliptic curves and modular forms, linear algebra over many fields, plotting of functions and data in two and three dimensions, and interactive data analysis. Its development has been driven by a large community of volunteer contributors, and it is widely used in mathematics research and teaching.
