The London and North Eastern Railway (LNER) was the second largest of the "Big Four" railway companies created by the Railways Act 1921 in Britain. It operated from 1 January 1923 until nationalisation on 1 January 1948, when it was divided among the Eastern, North Eastern and Scottish regions of British Railways.

Territory and constituents

The LNER covered the area north and east of London. Its main routes were the East Coast Main Line from London King's Cross via York and Newcastle to Edinburgh, and the Great Central Main Line from London Marylebone via Leicester and Sheffield to Manchester. Constituent companies included the Great Northern Railway, Great Eastern Railway, Great Central Railway, North Eastern Railway, Hull and Barnsley Railway, North British Railway and Great North of Scotland Railway.

Achievements

Under chief mechanical engineer Nigel Gresley the LNER produced some of Britain's most famous locomotives, including the A3 Pacific Flying Scotsman and the streamlined A4 Pacifics. In 1938 A4 No. 4468 Mallard set the world speed record for steam traction, 126 mph (203 km/h), which still stands. The company also introduced prestige expresses such as the Silver Jubilee (1935) and the Coronation (1937).

Although the second largest of the Big Four, the LNER was financially the weakest, dependent on declining heavy freight from coal and steel; it paid no dividend on its ordinary shares during its existence.
