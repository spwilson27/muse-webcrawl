Fifth column

A fifth column is any group of people who undermine a larger group or nation from within, usually in favour of an enemy group or nation. The term originated during the Spanish Civil War: in 1936 the Nationalist general Emilio Mola was said to have boasted that, as his four military columns advanced on Madrid, a "fifth column" of supporters inside the city would rise up to help. The phrase was popularised by Ernest Hemingway's 1938 play The Fifth Column.

Usage

The term was widely used during the Second World War for suspected enemy sympathisers, and fears of a fifth column contributed to policies such as the internment of Japanese Americans. It has since been applied to spies, saboteurs, sleeper agents and any internal faction accused of secretly aiding an external enemy. A "fifth columnist" engages in espionage or sabotage from within a society at war or under siege.
