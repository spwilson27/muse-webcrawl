In radio astronomy, a fast radio burst (FRB) is a transient radio pulse of length ranging from a fraction of a millisecond to a few milliseconds, caused by some high-energy astrophysical process not yet completely understood. Although extremely energetic at their source, the strength of the signal reaching Earth has been described as 1,000 times less than from a mobile phone on the Moon. The first FRB was discovered by Duncan Lorimer and his student David Narkevic in 2007 when they were looking through archival pulsar survey data, and it is therefore commonly referred to as the Lorimer Burst.

Discovery and observations

Many FRBs have since been recorded, including several that have been detected to repeat in seemingly irregular ways, and one (FRB 180916) that repeats periodically. Most FRBs are extragalactic, but the first Milky Way FRB was detected by the CHIME radiotelescope in April 2020 and was linked to a magnetar, SGR 1935+2154. The extreme dispersion measures of most bursts indicate sources far beyond the Milky Way.

Physical origin

The leading hypothesis is that FRBs are produced by young, highly magnetised neutron stars called magnetars, possibly through giant flares or starquake-driven reconnection. Other proposed models include mergers of neutron stars, black hole interactions, and decaying axion stars. The repeating and apparently periodic behaviour of some sources constrains models in which the source is destroyed in a single cataclysmic event.
