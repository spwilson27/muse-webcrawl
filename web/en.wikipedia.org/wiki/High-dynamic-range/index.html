High dynamic range

High dynamic range (HDR) is a technology that extends the range of luminance and colour that can be represented in images and video, allowing brighter highlights, deeper shadows and more vivid colours than standard dynamic range (SDR). The term is used in photography, computer graphics, and video display technology.

Imaging

In photography, HDR techniques combine multiple exposures of the same scene — or use sensors with greater bit depth — to capture a wider range of brightness than a single exposure permits, followed by tone mapping to fit the result to a display. HDR image formats such as Radiance HDR (.hdr) and OpenEXR long predate consumer HDR displays and are widely used in film visual effects and 3D rendering, where scene-referred linear data preserves realistic lighting.

Video and displays

HDR video standards include HDR10 (static metadata, 10-bit colour, PQ transfer function), Dolby Vision and HDR10+ (dynamic, scene-by-scene metadata), and HLG (hybrid log-gamma, designed for broadcast). HDR displays typically reach peak brightness of 600–4,000 nits and support wide colour gamuts such as DCI-P3 and Rec. 2020. HDR content is delivered by streaming services, Ultra HD Blu-ray and modern game consoles.

Standards

The perceptual quantizer (PQ, SMPTE ST 2084) and hybrid log-gamma (HLG) electro-optical transfer functions are the two foundations of consumer HDR, standardised in ITU-R Recommendation BT.2100.
