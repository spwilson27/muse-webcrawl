Dipsacales

KingdomPlantae
CladeAsterids (campanulids)
OrderDipsacales
FamiliesAdoxaceae, Caprifoliaceae

The Dipsacales are an order of flowering plants in the asterid clade of the eudicots. Under the APG IV system the order contains two families: the Adoxaceae, including elders and viburnums, and the Caprifoliaceae, including honeysuckles, teasels, valerians and scabiouses.

Characteristics

Members of the order typically have opposite leaves, flowers arranged in cymes, and an inferior ovary. Many are shrubs or woody climbers; the Caprifoliaceae also include herbaceous plants such as teasel (Dipsacus), from which the order takes its name.

Familiar plants

The order includes many garden and economically significant plants: elderberry (Sambucus), valued for its flowers and fruit; Viburnum shrubs; honeysuckles (Lonicera); valerian, formerly used medicinally; and the snowberry. The moschatel (Adoxa moschatellina), a small woodland herb, gives the Adoxaceae their name.

Classification

Older systems, such as Cronquist's, recognised more families within the order, separating the Valerianaceae and Dipsacaceae; molecular phylogenetic work merged these into an enlarged Caprifoliaceae.
