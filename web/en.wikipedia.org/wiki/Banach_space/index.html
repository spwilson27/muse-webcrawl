Banach space

In mathematics, a Banach space is a complete normed vector space: a vector space equipped with a norm that induces a metric in which every Cauchy sequence converges to an element of the space. Banach spaces are among the central objects of functional analysis.

Stefan Banach (1892–1945)

Definition and history

A normed space is Banach if it is complete in the metric d(x, y) = ‖x − y‖. The concept was introduced by Stefan Banach in his 1920 doctoral dissertation and developed in his monograph Théorie des opérations linéaires (1932), with parallel work by Norbert Wiener and Hans Hahn. Every finite-dimensional normed space is complete (hence Banach), and every normed space has a completion that is a Banach space.

Examples

  • Euclidean space Rn and Cn under any norm;
  • The sequence spaces ℓp for 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞;
  • The function spaces Lp of p-integrable functions, and C(K), the continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space with the supremum norm;
  • Hilbert spaces — Banach spaces whose norm comes from an inner product — are a special subclass.

Key theorems

The foundations of the theory include the Hahn–Banach theorem (extending linear functionals), the uniform boundedness principle, the open mapping theorem and the closed graph theorem. Banach spaces support a rich theory of linear operators, duality and approximation, with applications to differential equations, probability and quantum mechanics.
