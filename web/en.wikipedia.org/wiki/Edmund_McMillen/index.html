Edmund McMillen

BornMarch 2, 1980, Santa Cruz, California, U.S.
OccupationVideo game designer, artist
Known forSuper Meat Boy, The Binding of Isaac

Edmund Charles McMillen (born March 2, 1980) is an American video game designer and artist known for his Flash games and independent titles with a distinctive, often grotesque visual style. He is the co-creator of Super Meat Boy (2010) and the creator of The Binding of Isaac (2011) and its remake The Binding of Isaac: Rebirth (2014), among the most influential roguelike games of their era.

Career

Early Flash work

McMillen grew up in Santa Cruz, California, and began publishing games and animations on the website Newgrounds in the early 2000s. His Flash titles included Gish (2004), a physics-based platformer that won the Independent Games Festival's grand prize, Aether (2008, with Tyler Glaiel), and the original Meat Boy (2008, with Jonathan McEntee). A compilation, The Basement Collection, later gathered many of these works.

Super Meat Boy

With programmer Tommy Refenes, as Team Meat, McMillen developed Super Meat Boy, a precision platformer released on Xbox Live Arcade in 2010 to critical acclaim and strong sales. The game's development was chronicled in the documentary film Indie Game: The Movie (2012), in which McMillen featured prominently.

The Binding of Isaac

Created with Florian Himsl during a game jam, The Binding of Isaac (2011) is a roguelike dungeon crawler inspired by The Legend of Zelda and by McMillen's own religious upbringing; its procedurally generated levels, item combinations and dark themes made it a cult success. The ground-up remake Rebirth (2014), developed with Nicalis, and its expansions (Afterbirth, Afterbirth+, Repentance) expanded the game enormously, and the series has sold millions of copies. McMillen later released The End Is Nigh (2017, with Tyler Glaiel) and The Legend of Bum-bo (2019), a prequel to Isaac, and the card game Tapeworm; he also designed the physical card game The Binding of Isaac: Four Souls (2018), funded through a highly successful Kickstarter campaign.

Style and themes

McMillen's work frequently explores religion, childhood, bodily grotesquerie and personal anxiety, mixing cute character designs with dark subject matter. He has been credited with helping to popularise the modern roguelike ("roguelite") design pattern of permanent death combined with persistent unlocks and synergistic items.
