Cervical cerclage

[Image: Diagram of a cervical cerclage stitch in place during pregnancy]

Cervical cerclage, also known as a cervical stitch, is a treatment for cervical weakness (cervical insufficiency), in which the cervix starts to shorten and open too early during a pregnancy, risking miscarriage or preterm birth. In the procedure, a band of strong thread is stitched around the cervix to keep it closed, and is removed near the end of pregnancy or at the onset of labour.

Indications and timing

Cerclage may be offered to women with a history of mid-trimester losses or preterm births attributed to cervical weakness (history-indicated), to those whose ultrasound shows a short cervix in the current pregnancy (ultrasound-indicated), or, less commonly, as an emergency ("rescue") procedure when the cervix has already begun to dilate. It is typically placed between 12 and 24 weeks of gestation and removed around 36–37 weeks, or earlier if labour begins or membranes rupture.

Techniques

The best-known transvaginal techniques are the McDonald cerclage, a purse-string suture placed high on the cervix, and the Shirodkar cerclage, in which the bladder is reflected and a tape is placed at the level of the internal os. Transabdominal cerclage, placed at the cervico-isthmic junction via laparotomy or laparoscopy, is reserved for women with a very short or damaged cervix or after failed vaginal cerclage; delivery is then by caesarean section. Risks include infection, bleeding, membrane rupture and cervical injury.
