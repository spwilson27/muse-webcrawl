Stopover

A stopover is a break in a journey at an intermediate point. In air travel, a stopover is an intermediate stop of more than a specified duration between a passenger's origin and destination — traditionally more than 24 hours on an international itinerary, or more than 4 hours on a domestic itinerary in the United States — during which the passenger leaves the airport and may spend time in the stopover city. A shorter halt at an intermediate airport is instead called a layover or connection.

Air travel

Many airlines offer formal stopover programmes that let passengers spend one or more nights in the carrier's hub city at no extra airfare, sometimes bundling discounted hotel stays and tours. Well-known examples include Icelandair's stopover in Reykjavík, Turkish Airlines' programme in Istanbul, Finnair's in Helsinki, Singapore Airlines' in Singapore, and TAP Air Portugal's in Lisbon and Porto. Such programmes are used by airlines and tourism authorities to promote the hub city as a destination.

Fare rules

Whether a stopover is permitted, and whether it incurs extra charges or taxes, depends on the fare rules of the ticket. Stopovers may be limited in number, may be restricted to particular transfer points (usually the airline's hubs), and on round-the-world or award tickets are often a key means of visiting extra destinations on one fare.

Other transport

The term is also used generally for any planned pause in a road, rail, or sea journey, and in logistics for intermediate points where goods are transferred or stored.
