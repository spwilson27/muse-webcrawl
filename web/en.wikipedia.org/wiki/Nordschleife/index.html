Nordschleife

The Nordschleife ("North Loop") is the longer of the two circuits of the Nürburgring in the Eifel mountains of Germany. Opened in 1927, the modern configuration is about 20.8 km (12.9 mi) long with more than 70 corners, sweeping through forested hills around the village of Nürburg and the medieval Nürburg Castle.

History

The original 1927 Nordschleife lap was 22.8 km. It hosted the German Grand Prix and became notorious for its danger; Jackie Stewart nicknamed it the "Green Hell" (Grüne Hölle). After Niki Lauda's near-fatal crash in the 1976 German Grand Prix, Formula One moved to other venues, and a new Grand Prix circuit was built in 1984.

Modern use

The Nordschleife remains in use for the Nürburgring 24 Hours and VLN/NLS endurance series, manufacturer testing and industry record attempts, and public "tourist rides" (Touristenfahrten) during which it operates as a one-way toll road. It is widely regarded as the most demanding purpose-built race track in the world.
