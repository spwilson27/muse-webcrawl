Sabena Flight 548 was a Boeing 707-320 of the Belgian airline Sabena that crashed on approach to Brussels Airport on 15 February 1961, killing all 72 people on board and one person on the ground. Among the dead were the entire United States figure skating team, who were travelling to the World Figure Skating Championships in Prague. It remains one of the most devastating disasters in the history of American sport.

Accident

The flight departed New York Idlewild (now JFK) on the evening of 14 February 1961. After an uneventful transatlantic crossing, the aircraft was cleared to approach Brussels. While manoeuvring near the airport — it had reportedly been required to circle while another aircraft cleared the runway — the 707 entered a steep spiral dive and crashed into farmland near the village of Berg, Kampenhout, at about 10:05 local time. A farmer working in the field was killed by debris. The exact cause was never definitively established; investigators suspected a malfunction in the tailplane (stabiliser) trim or spoiler mechanism that caused a loss of pitch control.

The US figure skating team

On board were all 18 members of the US figure skating team — including ladies' contender Laurence Owen, her sister Maribel Owen, their mother, the champion skater-turned-coach Maribel Vinson Owen, men's skater Bradley Lord, ice dancers, pairs skaters — along with 16 officials, judges, coaches and family members. The team had been on its way to the 1961 World Championships, which the International Skating Union cancelled in mourning.

Aftermath

The crash wiped out a generation of American skating talent: the United States had dominated the sport in the 1950s. In response, the US Figure Skating Memorial Fund was established to support the training of promising skaters; it has since distributed millions of dollars. The disaster prompted major rebuilding of the American programme, which recovered to win Olympic gold with Peggy Fleming in 1968. A memorial to the victims stands near the crash site at Berg, and a monument exists at the Wereldskampioenschappen site in Belgium; the team was inducted into the US Figure Skating Hall of Fame in 2011.
