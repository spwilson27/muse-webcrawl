Loch Linnhe

Loch Linnhe
Location: Highland, Scotland
Type: Sea loch
Length: about 31 miles (50 km)
Connects: Firth of Lorn to Loch Eil

Loch Linnhe is a sea loch on the west coast of Scotland, in the Highland council area. It runs north-east for about 31 miles (50 km) from the Firth of Lorn, following the line of the Great Glen Fault, to Fort William at its head, where it continues inland as Loch Eil. The loch forms part of the route of the Caledonian Canal.

Geography

The loch varies in width from a few hundred metres to about two miles. Its shores include the settlements of Onich, Ballachulish, Corran and Kinlocheil; the Corran Ferry crosses the narrows at Corran. The island of Lismore lies at its mouth, and the mountains of Ardgour and Moidart rise on its western shore. Ben Nevis, the highest mountain in Britain, stands close to its head near Fort William.

Loch Linnhe is noted for its scenery and wildlife, including seals, porpoises and golden eagles on the surrounding hills, and for the way its dark waters reflect the hills — Linnhe is sometimes linked in folk etymology to the Gaelic for "the dark pool".
