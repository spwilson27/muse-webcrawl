Apollo–Soyuz

Apollo–Soyuz Test Project
Mission typeJoint U.S.–Soviet orbital docking
Dates15–24 July 1975
CrewsStafford, Brand, Slayton (Apollo); Leonov, Kubasov (Soyuz 19)

The Apollo–Soyuz Test Project (ASTP), conducted in July 1975, was the first joint space mission of the United States and the Soviet Union and the last flight of Apollo hardware. An American Apollo command module docked in orbit with a Soviet Soyuz spacecraft, and the crews conducted joint experiments, exchanged gifts and shared meals — a symbol of the détente era and effectively the end of the Space Race.

The mission

Soyuz 19, crewed by Alexei Leonov and Valery Kubasov, launched from Baikonur on 15 July 1975; the Apollo craft, crewed by Thomas Stafford, Vance Brand and Deke Slayton, followed from Kennedy Space Center hours later carrying a special docking module that served as an airlock between the incompatible cabin atmospheres. The two craft docked on 17 July and stayed joined for about 44 hours before separating and re-docking once more.

Significance

The mission tested a universal docking system later used on Shuttle–Mir and the International Space Station. It was the final Apollo flight and the last crewed U.S. launch until the Space Shuttle in 1981; no further U.S.–Soviet joint flight occurred until 1995.
