The Right Opposition (Russian: Правая оппозиция) was a faction within the Communist Party of the Soviet Union associated with Nikolai Bukharin, Alexei Rykov, and Mikhail Tomsky. It opposed the rapid industrialisation and forced collectivisation launched by Joseph Stalin from 1928, instead defending the gradualist, market-tolerant policies of the New Economic Policy (NEP).

History

Bukharin and his allies had previously supported Stalin against the Left Opposition of Leon Trotsky and the United Opposition. The alliance broke down over the grain procurement crisis of 1927–1928: Stalin adopted emergency requisitioning and then a crash programme of collectivisation and Five-Year industrialisation, which Bukharin denounced (in his "Notes of an Economist", 1928) as "military-feudal exploitation" of the peasantry. The Rightists were removed from leadership posts during 1929–1930 — Bukharin lost the editorship of Pravda and the leadership of the Comintern; Tomsky was removed from the trade unions; Rykov was replaced as premier by Molotov in 1930.

Fate

In the Great Terror, all three leaders perished: Tomsky died by suicide in 1936 after being implicated at the first Moscow Trial; Bukharin and Rykov were defendants at the Trial of the Twenty-One (1938) and were executed. Bukharin was posthumously rehabilitated in the USSR in 1988.
