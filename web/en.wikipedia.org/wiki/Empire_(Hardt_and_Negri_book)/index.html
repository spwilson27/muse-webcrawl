Empire (Hardt and Negri book)

Empire is a book by post-Marxist philosophers Michael Hardt and Antonio Negri, published in 2000 by Harvard University Press. Written in the mid-1990s and published just before the September 11 attacks, the book became an influential and widely discussed work of political theory, sometimes described as a manifesto for the alter-globalization movement.

Summary

Hardt and Negri argue that a new global form of sovereignty, which they call "Empire", has emerged. Unlike the imperialism of the modern era, Empire has no territorial centre of power and relies on a decentered, deterritorializing apparatus of rule that incorporates the entire global realm. The authors contrast the multitude — the productive, creative subjects of globalization — with Empire, arguing that the multitude holds the potential to create a democratic global society.

Reception and sequels

The book drew comparisons to The Communist Manifesto and sparked extensive debate. Hardt and Negri followed it with Multitude: War and Democracy in the Age of Empire (2004), Commonwealth (2009), and Assembly (2017).
