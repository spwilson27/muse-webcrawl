Marian antiphons

The Marian antiphons are a group of four Latin hymns to the Virgin Mary sung in the Roman Catholic Liturgy of the Hours, traditionally at the end of Compline (Night Prayer), each assigned to a season of the liturgical year.

The four antiphons

Alma Redemptoris Mater is sung from Advent until Candlemas (2 February); Ave Regina Caelorum from then until Holy Week; Regina Caeli during Eastertide; and Salve Regina from Trinity Sunday until Advent. The Salve Regina, attributed to the 11th-century monk Hermann of Reichenau, is the most famous of the four.

Music

The antiphons are sung to Gregorian chant melodies and have inspired countless polyphonic settings by composers from the Renaissance onward, including Palestrina, Victoria and Charpentier. In the Liturgy of the Hours they close Compline and are also sung after other hours on some occasions.

In culture

Geoffrey Chaucer's Prioress's Tale centres on the Alma Redemptoris Mater, and the antiphons are still sung daily in monasteries and at many Catholic schools and shrines.
