KingdomPlantae

A seed plant or spermatophyte (from Greek sperma, "seed", and phyton, "plant"), also known as a phanerogam, is any plant that produces seeds and is therefore an alternative name for such a plant. Seed plants form a clade within the vascular plants (tracheophytes) and include most familiar land plants.

Divisions

The extant seed plants are conventionally divided into five groups: the cycads, Ginkgo, the conifers, the gnetophytes — together comprising the gymnosperms, whose seeds are not enclosed in an ovary — and the flowering plants (angiosperms), whose seeds develop inside a fruit. Several entirely extinct groups, including the seed ferns (pteridosperms) and Bennettitales, are also recognised from the fossil record.

Evolution and life cycle

Seed plants first appear in the fossil record in the Late Devonian, roughly 370 million years ago. The seed habit freed reproduction from dependence on external water: pollen delivers male gametes through the air, and the fertilised ovule matures into a seed containing an embryo and a food supply protected by a seed coat, often adapted for dispersal and dormancy. Like other land plants, spermatophytes show alternation of generations, but the gametophyte generation is greatly reduced and retained within the tissues of the dominant sporophyte.

Importance

Seed plants dominate most terrestrial ecosystems and include nearly all economically important plants — cereal grains, legumes, timber trees, fruits and vegetables — making them the foundation of agriculture and forestry.
