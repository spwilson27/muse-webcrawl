John Barrymore (born John Sidney Blyth; 15 February 1882 – 29 May 1942) was an American actor on stage, screen and radio. A member of the Drew and Barrymore theatrical families, he initially tried to avoid the stage, briefly studying art, before making his way into theatre through family pressure and poverty. He first gained fame as a handsome stage actor in light comedies, then high drama, before transitioning to silent films and "talking pictures."

Early life

Barrymore was born in Philadelphia, the youngest child of actors Maurice Barrymore and Georgiana Drew, and the younger brother of Lionel and Ethel Barrymore. He attended Georgetown Preparatory School and studied art at King's College London and the Art Students League of New York, working briefly as a cartoonist for the New York Evening Journal before joining his father's theatre company in 1903.

Stage career

After years in popular comedies such as The Fortune Hunter (1909) and dramatic successes in Justice (1916) and Peter Ibbetson (1917), Barrymore achieved a towering reputation with Shakespeare. His Richard III (1920) and, above all, his Hamlet (1922) — which ran a record 101 performances on Broadway and was staged in London in 1925 — were widely considered the finest Shakespearean performances of their generation. Critics praised his psychological subtlety and matchless profile, earning him the nickname "The Great Profile."

Film career

Barrymore's screen roles included Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde (1920), Sherlock Holmes (1922), Beau Brummel (1924), Don Juan (1926) — the first feature film with a synchronized soundtrack — and The Sea Beast (1926). With the coming of sound he starred in Dinner at Eight (1933), Twentieth Century (1934), A Bill of Divorcement (1932) opposite Katharine Hepburn, and Grand Hotel (1932). By the late 1930s, heavy drinking and failing memory forced him to use cue cards, and he increasingly played self-parodic roles before his death from pneumonia and complications of alcoholism in 1942.

Personal life and legacy

Barrymore was married four times, including to poet Michael Strange and actress Dolores Costello, with whom he had two children; his granddaughter is actress Drew Barrymore. Though his later years were marked by decline, his Hamlet and his finest screen performances secured his reputation as one of the greatest American actors of the twentieth century.
