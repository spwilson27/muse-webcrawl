Beef bourguignon (French: bœuf bourguignon), also called beef Burgundy, is a French stew of beef braised in red wine — traditionally red Burgundy — and beef stock, flavoured with garlic, onions and a bouquet garni, and garnished with pearl onions, mushrooms and lardons of bacon.

History

The dish is a celebrated example of peasant food refined into haute cuisine: prolonged slow braising tenderises tough cuts of beef. It was codified in the early 20th century, notably by Auguste Escoffier, and popularised internationally by Julia Child, who described it in Mastering the Art of French Cooking (1961) as "certainly one of the most delicious beef dishes concocted by man"; her preparation of it was a signature scene of the film Julie & Julia.

Preparation

Cubes of beef (often chuck) are browned, then simmered for several hours in red wine and stock with carrots, onions and herbs. Mushrooms and small onions are cooked separately and added near the end so they keep their shape. The sauce is reduced and sometimes thickened with a beurre manié. It is typically served with potatoes, noodles or crusty bread. Variants use other red wines when Burgundy is unavailable; see also coq au vin, the analogous chicken dish from the same tradition of French cuisine.
