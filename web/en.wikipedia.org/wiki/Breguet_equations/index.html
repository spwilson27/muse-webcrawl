Breguet range equation
Field: Aeronautics
Named for: Louis Breguet
The Breguet range equation

The Breguet equations, named after the French aviation pioneer Louis Breguet, relate the range and endurance of an aircraft to its aerodynamic efficiency, propulsion efficiency, speed and the logarithm of the ratio of its initial to final weight. They are fundamental first-order tools in aircraft design, showing why range grows only logarithmically with fuel fraction.

The range equation

For a jet aircraft cruising at constant speed and lift-to-drag ratio, the range is R = (V / TSFC) · (L/D) · ln(Winitial / Wfinal), where V is speed, TSFC is thrust-specific fuel consumption, L/D is the lift-to-drag ratio and the weight term is the ratio of take-off weight to landing (empty-of-usable-fuel) weight. A corresponding form exists for propeller aircraft, using power-specific fuel consumption and propeller efficiency. Endurance equations follow by dividing by speed, and for jets maximum endurance occurs at maximum L/D, while maximum range occurs at a slightly higher speed.

Significance

The equations explain the premium placed on aerodynamic cleanliness (high L/D), efficient engines (low specific fuel consumption) and a high fuel fraction, and they underlie classical estimates of ferry range and loiter time for both civil airliners and military aircraft.
