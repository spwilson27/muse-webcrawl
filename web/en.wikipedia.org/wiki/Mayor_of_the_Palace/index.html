Mayor of the Palace

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mayor of the Palace (Latin: maior palatii or maior domus, "chief of the palace") was the title and office of the manager of the household of the late Merovingian kings of the Franks. During the second half of the seventh century, the office evolved into the "power behind the throne", and the mayors became the de facto rulers of the Frankish kingdoms.

Origins

The office originated in the early 6th century as the head of the king's domestic establishment, supervising the palace staff, the royal demesne and the administration of justice in the king's court. There were separate mayors for the Frankish sub-kingdoms of Austrasia, Neustria and Burgundy.

Rise to power

As the Merovingian kings (the later rois fainéants, "do-nothing kings") weakened, the mayors took over military command and government. The Austrasian mayors of the Pippinid family — Pepin of Landen, Grimoald, and Pepin of Herstal — consolidated power; Pepin of Herstal became sole mayor after the Battle of Tertry (687). His son Charles Martel, mayor from 715 to 741, effectively ruled Francia without a king for several years and defeated an Umayyad army near Tours in 732.

End of the office

In 751 Charles's son Pepin the Short, with the sanction of Pope Zachary and the approval of a Frankish assembly, deposed the last Merovingian king, Childeric III, and took the crown himself, founding the Carolingian dynasty. The office of mayor of the palace thereby disappeared, absorbed into the kingship.
