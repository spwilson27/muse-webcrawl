Last Judgment (Giotto)

The Last Judgment
ArtistGiotto di Bondone
Yearc. 1306
MediumFresco
LocationScrovegni Chapel, Padua, Italy

The Last Judgment is a monumental fresco by the Italian artist Giotto di Bondone, painted around 1306 on the entrance (west) wall of the Scrovegni Chapel (also known as the Arena Chapel) in Padua, Italy. It is the culminating scene of Giotto's celebrated fresco cycle in the chapel, which is regarded as one of the masterpieces of Western art and a landmark in the development of European painting.

The Last Judgment covers the entire entrance wall of the Scrovegni Chapel.

Description

The fresco fills the whole wall above the chapel's entrance. At its centre, Christ the Judge sits enthroned within a radiant mandorla, surrounded by angels, his hands raised in a gesture that simultaneously welcomes the saved and dismisses the damned. On either side, arranged in two registers, sit the twelve apostles as co-judges.

Below, the composition divides into two streams. To Christ's right, the blessed proceed in orderly procession toward Paradise, led by the Virgin Mary; among them is shown the chapel's patron, the banker Enrico Scrovegni, presenting a model of the chapel to the Virgin — a famous donor portrait. To Christ's left, the damned are driven by angels toward Hell, depicted in the lower right corner, where Satan, a monstrous blue-grey figure, devours sinners amid flames and tormenting demons. Angels above roll up the fabric of the heavens like a scroll, revealing the gates of the Heavenly Jerusalem behind them.

Context

The Scrovegni Chapel was commissioned by Enrico Scrovegni, whose father Reginaldo had been a notorious usurer — Dante placed him in Hell in the Inferno. The chapel, and its penitential imagery, have often been interpreted as an act of expiation. Giotto's frescoes, completed around 1305–1306, narrate the lives of the Virgin and of Christ, and the Last Judgment forms their theological conclusion: worshippers leaving the chapel passed beneath the scene of their own ultimate judgement.

Significance

Giotto's Last Judgment is noted for its dramatic clarity, the weight and individuality of its figures, and its innovative rendering of the hosts of the saved and damned. Together with the rest of the Scrovegni cycle, it marks a decisive break with Byzantine conventions and a foundational moment of the Italian Renaissance. The chapel's fresco cycle was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2021 as part of "Padova Urbs Picta."
