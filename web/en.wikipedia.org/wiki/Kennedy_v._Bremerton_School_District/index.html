Kennedy v. Bremerton School District

Kennedy v. Bremerton School District
Citation: 597 U.S. 507 (2022)
Decided: 27 June 2022
Question: Free Exercise and Free Speech Clauses

Kennedy v. Bremerton School District, 597 U.S. 507 (2022), was a United States Supreme Court case in which the Court held, 6–3, that a public school district could not prohibit a football coach from praying at midfield after games, finding that doing so violated his rights under the Free Exercise and Free Speech Clauses of the First Amendment.

Background

Joseph Kennedy, an assistant football coach in Bremerton, Washington, prayed at the 50-yard line after games; the district, citing Establishment Clause concerns, directed him to stop and placed him on leave when he continued.

Decision

Justice Neil Gorsuch's majority opinion held that the prayers were private speech, that the district's actions were not neutral toward religion, and abandoned the Lemon test in favour of an analysis by reference to historical practices and understandings. Justice Sotomayor dissented.
