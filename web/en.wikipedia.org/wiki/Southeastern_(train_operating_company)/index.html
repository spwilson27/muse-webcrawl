Southeastern is the trading name of the train operator of the South Eastern franchise in England, running commuter and regional services from London to Kent and parts of East Sussex. It operates one of the busiest commuter networks in Britain and the UK's first domestic high-speed services.

History

The franchise was first operated from 1996 by Connex South Eastern, which lost the contract in 2003 after financial mismanagement; services were run by the state-owned South Eastern Trains until 2006, when Govia's London & South Eastern Railway took over under the Southeastern brand. The franchise integrated the Kent services of the Integrated Kent franchise and, from June 2009, high-speed Class 395 "Javelin" services over High Speed 1 between London St Pancras and Ashford, Dover and Ramsgate.

In September 2021 the Department for Transport terminated Govia's contract after finding that more than £25 million of taxpayer money had not been declared, and the operation passed to the government-owned operator of last resort, SE Trains Limited, retaining the Southeastern name.

Services and fleet

Southeastern runs mainline services on the South Eastern and Chatham main lines, metro services across south-east London, and high-speed services on HS1. Its fleet includes Class 375 and 377 Electrostars, Class 395 Javelins, Class 465/466 Networkers and Class 707 City Beams transferred from South Western Railway.
