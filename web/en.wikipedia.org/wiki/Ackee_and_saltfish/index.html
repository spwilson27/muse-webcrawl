Ackee and saltfish, traditionally served at breakfast.

Ackee and saltfish is the national dish of Jamaica. It combines the ackee fruit — a West African fruit brought to the Caribbean in the 18th century — with salted cod, sautéed with onions, tomatoes, Scotch bonnet peppers and spices.

Preparation

The salt cod is soaked or boiled to remove excess salt, then flaked and sautéed with the vegetables. The ackee arils, which must be fully ripe and naturally opened on the tree, are boiled separately and folded in gently; cooked ackee has a soft, creamy texture often compared to scrambled egg. The dish is typically served with boiled green bananas, yam, breadfruit, festival or fried dumplings, and is eaten mainly at breakfast.

Safety

Unripe or improperly prepared ackee contains the toxin hypoglycin A, which causes Jamaican vomiting sickness; for this reason raw ackee imports are restricted in some countries, including the United States, though canned ackee from approved producers is permitted.
