Vehicle frame

A vehicle frame, also historically known as its chassis, is the main supporting structure of a motor vehicle to which all other components are attached, comparable to the skeleton of an organism. Until the 1930s, virtually every car had a structural frame separate from its body; this is known as body-on-frame construction. Most modern passenger cars instead use unibody (unitary) construction, in which the body and frame are integrated.

Frame designs

The classic ladder frame consists of two longitudinal rails connected by cross members, resembling a ladder. Variants include perimeter frames, X-frames, and backbone (central tube) frames. Trucks, large SUVs, and commercial vehicles continue to use separate ladder frames for strength, towing capacity, and ease of fitting different bodies.

Functions

The frame must support the weight of the powertrain, body, and cargo; resist torsional and bending loads from the road and from acceleration and braking; and provide mounting points for suspension, steering and driveline components. Frame design also influences crash performance, with modern structures incorporating controlled crumple behaviour.

Materials

Frames are usually steel — increasingly high-strength alloys — though aluminium and composite structures are used in performance and electric vehicles; many EV "skateboard" platforms integrate the battery pack into the floor structure.
