Types of cheese

There are many types of cheese, with several hundred distinct varieties produced around the world. Cheeses can be grouped or classified according to criteria such as the source of the milk (cow, goat, sheep, buffalo, or other animals), length of ageing, texture (soft, semi-soft, semi-hard, hard), methods of making, fat content, and characteristic features such as moulds or added flavourings.

Classification by texture and ageing

Fresh cheeses, such as cottage cheese, ricotta, mozzarella, and quark, are unaged and consumed soon after production. Soft-ripened cheeses such as Brie and Camembert develop a bloomy rind of mould. Semi-soft cheeses include Gouda, Havarti, and Port Salut. Semi-hard and hard cheeses, often aged for months or years, include Cheddar, Emmental, Gruyère, Parmigiano-Reggiano (Parmesan), and Pecorino Romano.

Blue and processed cheeses

Blue cheeses such as Roquefort, Gorgonzola, and Stilton are inoculated with cultures of Penicillium moulds that create veins and a sharp, tangy flavour. Processed cheese is made from natural cheese blended with emulsifying salts and other ingredients, giving a smooth texture and long shelf life.

By milk source

Most cheese is made from cow's milk, but many celebrated varieties use other milks: Roquefort and Pecorino from sheep's milk, chèvre and feta-style cheeses from goat's milk, and mozzarella di bufala from water buffalo milk. Regional and protected-designation cheeses are often legally tied to a particular milk, method, and place of origin.
