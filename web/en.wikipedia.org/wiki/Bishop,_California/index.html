Bishop, California

Bishop is a city in California, United States. It is the largest populated place and the only incorporated city in Inyo County. Bishop is located near the northern end of the Owens Valley, at an elevation of 4,150 feet (1,265 m), beneath the eastern escarpment of the Sierra Nevada. The population was 3,819 at the 2020 census.

History

The Bishop Creek post office operated from 1870 to 1889, and a Bishop post office has operated since 1889. The city was named after Samuel Addison Bishop, a cattleman who drove cattle to the Owens Valley in 1861 and established a ranch near the present townsite. Bishop was incorporated in 1903.

Geography and climate

Bishop sits in a high desert valley framed by the Sierra Nevada to the west and the White Mountains to the east. The climate is arid, with hot summers, cold winters, and very little precipitation, most of the valley's water arriving as snowmelt from the Sierra. U.S. Route 395 passes through the city.

Economy and recreation

Bishop serves as a gateway for recreation in the eastern Sierra, including hiking, fishing, rock climbing, and access to Mammoth Lakes and the Ancient Bristlecone Pine Forest. The annual Mule Days celebration, held each Memorial Day weekend since 1969, draws thousands of visitors for mule competitions, a parade, and a rodeo.
