Technology forecasting

Technology forecasting attempts to predict the future characteristics of useful technological machines, procedures or techniques. Researchers create technology forecasts based on past experience and current technological developments, and the discipline is widely used by governments, military planners and firms to guide research and development and long-term strategy.

Methods

Common techniques include:

  • Delphi method — structured, iterative polling of experts, developed at the RAND Corporation.
  • Trend extrapolation — projecting historical growth curves, including S-curves describing performance improvement and substitution.
  • Scenario planning — constructing multiple internally consistent futures.
  • Technology roadmapping — mapping anticipated capabilities against product and market needs.
  • Patent and bibliometric analysis — mining publication and patent trends for emerging fields.

Applications

Technology forecasting informs defence planning, energy policy, corporate R&D portfolios and national foresight programmes. Notable empirical regularities used by forecasters include Moore's law for transistor density and Wright's law (experience curves) for cost declines with cumulative production.

Criticism

Forecasts frequently fail at discontinuities — genuinely new inventions are difficult to anticipate, and expert judgement is subject to optimism bias and hype cycles. Critics note that forecasters often overestimate short-term change and underestimate long-term transformation, a pattern summarised by Amara's law.
