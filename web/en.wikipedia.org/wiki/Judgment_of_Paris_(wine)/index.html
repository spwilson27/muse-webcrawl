The Judgment of Paris, also known as the Paris Wine Tasting of 1976, was a wine competition organised in Paris on 24 May 1976 by British wine merchant Steven Spurrier and American wine consultant Patricia Gallagher. In the blind tasting, French judges compared top-quality Chardonnays and Cabernet Sauvignons from France against wines from California. To the shock of the wine world, Californian wines ranked highest in both categories, an outcome that overturned the assumption of French supremacy in fine wine and transformed the global wine industry.

Background

Spurrier, who ran the wine shop Les Caves de la Madeleine and the Académie du Vin in Paris, stocked only French wines and reportedly did not expect the Californian entries to win. He organised the tasting to mark the United States Bicentennial, inviting nine French judges, including restaurateurs, sommeliers and wine writers such as Odette Khan, Aubert de Villaine and Raymond Oliver.

The tasting

Twenty wines were tasted blind: ten whites (six Californian Chardonnays and four French Burgundies) and ten reds (six Californian Cabernet Sauvignons and four Bordeaux). Each judge scored the wines out of 20. When the scores were tallied, Chateau Montelena's 1973 Chardonnay, made by winemaker Mike Grgich, came first among the whites, and Stag's Leap Wine Cellars' 1973 Cabernet Sauvignon, made by Warren Winiarski, came first among the reds — ahead of celebrated estates such as Château Mouton-Rothschild and Château Haut-Brion.

Reporting and impact

The only journalist present was George M. Taber of Time magazine, whose report brought the result to international attention. French media largely ignored or downplayed the event at first. The tasting is widely credited with legitimising New World wine regions, accelerating investment in Californian wine, and encouraging quality winemaking around the world. Bottles of the two winning wines were later acquired by the Smithsonian Institution's National Museum of American History.

Legacy

The event was dramatised in the 2008 film Bottle Shock. Anniversary re-tastings, including a 30th-anniversary re-enactment in 2006 held simultaneously in Napa and London, again placed Californian wines at the top, reinforcing the original result.
