Caithness
Scottish Gaelic: Gallaibh
Country: Scotland
Main towns: Wick, Thurso
Historic county town: Wick

Caithness (Scottish Gaelic: Gallaibh, "among the strangers" — the Norse) is a historic county and registration county and lieutenancy area of Scotland, forming the far northeast corner of the Scottish mainland.

Geography

Unlike much of the Highlands, Caithness is largely low-lying, flat and treeless, built of Old Red Sandstone laid out in the famous Caithness flagstone pavements. Its northern coast faces the Pentland Firth toward Orkney; at Dunnet Head it holds the most northerly point of mainland Britain, while nearby John o' Groats is the traditional counterpart to Land's End. Much of the interior is the Flow Country, one of the world's largest blanket bogs, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2024.

History

The area is rich in Neolithic chambered cairns and Iron Age brochs. For centuries it was ruled by Norse earls of Orkney, leaving a strong Scandinavian imprint on place-names. Wick grew as a herring port in the nineteenth century, and Thurso serves the Dounreay nuclear site, established in the 1950s.

Language and culture

Caithness has a distinctive Scots dialect with Norse influences. The county flag, adopted in 2016, combines black, gold and blue with a galley and raven recalling its Norse heritage.
