Riemann surface

In mathematics, particularly in complex analysis, a Riemann surface is a one-dimensional complex manifold. These surfaces were first studied by and are named after Bernhard Riemann (1826–1866). Riemann surfaces can be thought of as deformed versions of the complex plane: locally near every point they look like patches of the complex plane, but the global topology can be quite different.

Definitions

A Riemann surface is a connected complex manifold of complex dimension one, equivalently a two-dimensional real manifold equipped with a conformal structure and an orientation. Every Riemann surface is a surface in the ordinary sense; charts map open subsets to open subsets of the complex plane, and transition functions are required to be holomorphic.

Examples

The simplest Riemann surface is the complex plane itself; the Riemann sphere, obtained by adding a point at infinity, is the prototypical compact example. Complex tori are compact Riemann surfaces of genus one, while compact Riemann surfaces of genus g ≥ 2 admit hyperbolic geometry. The uniformization theorem states that every simply connected Riemann surface is conformally equivalent to the Riemann sphere, the complex plane, or the open unit disk. Multivalued functions such as the logarithm and square root become single-valued holomorphic functions on suitable Riemann surfaces.
