Cohesion Fund

The Cohesion Fund supports transport and environment projects in the EU's less prosperous member states.

The Cohesion Fund is a structural fund of the European Union established in 1994 to finance environmental and trans-European transport infrastructure projects in member states whose gross national income per capita is below 90% of the EU average. Together with the European Regional Development Fund and the European Social Fund, it forms part of EU cohesion policy aimed at reducing regional disparities.

Purpose and eligibility

The fund was created under the Maastricht Treaty framework as the Community prepared for monetary union, helping poorer member states invest in infrastructure while meeting convergence criteria. For the 2021–2027 budget period the eligible countries are predominantly in Central, Eastern and Southern Europe, including Poland, Romania, Bulgaria, Croatia, Greece, Portugal, Spain and the Baltic states, among others.

Activities

Supported projects include railways, motorways and ports in the trans-European transport networks (TEN-T), drinking-water and wastewater treatment, waste management, flood protection and renewable energy and energy-efficiency schemes. EU co-financing can cover up to 85% of eligible project costs.

Budget

The Cohesion Fund was allocated roughly €48 billion for 2021–2027 (in current prices, including transfers to the Connecting Europe Facility). Spending is managed jointly by the European Commission and national authorities under shared-management rules, with macroeconomic conditionality provisions allowing suspension of commitments where a member state fails to correct an excessive deficit.
