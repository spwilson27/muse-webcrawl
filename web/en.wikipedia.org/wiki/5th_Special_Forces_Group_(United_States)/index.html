The 5th Special Forces Group (Airborne) is a United States Army Special Forces unit based at Fort Campbell, Kentucky. Activated in 1961, it is one of the most decorated units of its kind and has a long history of operations in Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and Central Asia.

History

The group was activated on 11 April 1961 at Fort Bragg, North Carolina. During the Vietnam War, the 5th Group became heavily involved in advising South Vietnamese forces and running the Civilian Irregular Defense Group (CIDG) program among the Montagnard peoples of the Central Highlands. Its soldiers earned numerous decorations, including a Presidential Unit Citation.

Later operations

After Vietnam, the group's area of responsibility focused on the Middle East and Central Asia. Elements deployed during the Gulf War, and following the September 11 attacks the group was among the first American units into Afghanistan in 2001, working alongside the Northern Alliance during Operation Enduring Freedom. It later served extensively in Iraq.

Organisation

The group consists of a headquarters company and four battalions, each comprising multiple Operational Detachment Alpha (ODA) teams of twelve soldiers trained in unconventional warfare, foreign internal defense, direct action, and special reconnaissance.
