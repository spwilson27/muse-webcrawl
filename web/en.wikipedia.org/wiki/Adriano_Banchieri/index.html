Adriano Banchieri (1568–1634)

Adriano Banchieri (born Tommaso Banchieri; 3 September 1568 – 1634) was an Italian composer, music theorist, organist and poet of the late Renaissance and early Baroque eras. A Benedictine monk, he was one of the founders of the madrigal comedy and, with Orazio Vecchi, a developer of the dramatic forms that led toward opera.

Life

Banchieri was born in Bologna and entered the Benedictine order in 1587, taking the name Adriano and taking his vows in 1589. He served as organist at several churches in Imola, Gubbio, Venice, and Verona before settling in Bologna, where he was associated with the monastery of San Michele in Bosco. In 1609 he published his influential treatise L'organo suonarino, and he founded the Accademia dei Floridi in Bologna in 1615.

Music

Banchieri wrote masses, psalms, motets, organ music, and numerous secular collections. His best-known work is the madrigal comedy La pazzia senile (1598) and its successors, including Il studio dilettevole and Il metamorfosi musicale (1601), in which ensembles of madrigals are strung together into comic dramatic scenes featuring stock characters from the commedia dell'arte. His Festino nella sera del giovedì grasso avanti cena (1608) includes the famous "Contraponto bestiale alla mente", a whimsical piece in which animal sounds are improvised over a bass line.

Theory

As a theorist Banchieri helped popularize the figured bass, contributed to the modern system of naming the notes of the scale, and was an early advocate of the bar line. His writings document the transition from modal counterpoint to the concerted style of the early Baroque.
