The Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic (Azerbaijani: Naxçıvan Muxtar Respublikası) is an autonomous republic and landlocked exclave of Azerbaijan, separated from the rest of the country by Armenia. It borders Armenia to the east and north, Iran to the south and west, and Turkey to the north-west along a short 17 km frontier. Its capital is the city of Nakhchivan.

Geography

The republic covers some 5,500 km² of mountainous terrain in the Lesser Caucasus, drained mainly by tributaries of the Aras River, which forms the border with Iran. The climate is continental and dry. Principal towns include Nakhchivan city, Julfa, Ordubad and Sharur.

History

The region passed between Armenian kingdoms, Persia, and the Ottoman and Russian empires over the centuries. Following the First World War and the Russian Revolution its status was contested between Armenia and Azerbaijan; the 1921 Treaties of Moscow and Kars confirmed it as an autonomous territory under Azerbaijan, with Turkey as a guarantor. In the Soviet period it was the Nakhichevan ASSR within the Azerbaijan SSR. During the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, Armenia's blockade left the exclave dependent on links through Iran and Turkey; the small Sadarak corridor to Turkey gained strategic importance, and a land corridor between Nakhchivan and mainland Azerbaijan has been a recurring element of regional diplomacy.

Administration

Nakhchivan has its own legislature, the Supreme Assembly (Ali Majlis), and its own constitution, while remaining an integral part of Azerbaijan. It is divided into districts (rayons) and the capital city. The region is the ancestral home of Azerbaijan's long-serving president Heydar Aliyev and his family.
