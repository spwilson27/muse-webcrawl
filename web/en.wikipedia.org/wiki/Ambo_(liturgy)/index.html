Ambo (liturgy)

The ambo (from Greek ambon, "pulpit") is a raised stand or lectern in Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and early Roman Catholic churches from which the Scripture lessons are read and the Gospel is proclaimed during the liturgy.

History

The ambo developed in early Christian basilicas, where its elevated position helped the reader's voice carry through the congregation. Early ambos were often substantial stone structures reached by steps, sometimes placed in the middle of the nave. Famous surviving examples include the ambos of the churches of Ravenna and the ornate medieval pulpits of Italian cathedrals, such as those by Nicola Pisano. In the Western Church, the ambo gradually gave way to the pulpit for preaching and the lectern for readings.

Eastern usage

In Byzantine tradition, the ambo came to be a raised platform in front of the iconostasis, often semicircular, from which the deacon reads the Gospel and the litanies. In modern Greek practice the ambo may be a smaller structure, while in the Russian tradition the term can refer to the raised steps (soleas) before the iconostasis.

Modern Roman Catholic usage

Following the Second Vatican Council, the ambo was restored to prominence in Roman Catholic church design: the General Instruction of the Roman Missal prescribes a stationary ambo from which the readings, responsorial psalm, and Exsultet are proclaimed, reserving it for the Liturgy of the Word rather than general announcements.
