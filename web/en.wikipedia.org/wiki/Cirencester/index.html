Cirencester

Cirencester (locally sometimes /ˈsɪsəstər/) is a market town in Gloucestershire, England, 80 miles (130 km) west of London. Cirencester lies on the River Churn, a tributary of the River Thames. It is known as the Capital of the Cotswolds. The town had a recorded population of 20,229 in the 2021 Census.

History

Cirencester was founded in Roman times as Corinium Dobunnorum, which by the early 2nd century AD had become the second-largest town in Roman Britain, after Londinium. It was an important regional capital of the Dobunni tribe, with a forum, basilica, amphitheatre, and some of the finest mosaics in Britain, now displayed in the Corinium Museum. After the Roman withdrawal, the town declined but revived in the Middle Ages as a centre of the wool trade.

Landmarks

The parish church of St John the Baptist is one of the largest parish churches in England, built mainly in the Perpendicular style with a landmark tower. Cirencester Park, the seat of the Bathurst family, is one of the great landscaped parks of England, laid out in the early 18th century. The town is home to the Royal Agricultural University, founded in 1845 as the first agricultural college in the English-speaking world.
