Stanisław Maczek

Born: 31 March 1892, Lwów, Austria-Hungary
Died: 11 December 1994 (aged 102), Edinburgh, Scotland
Rank: Lieutenant general
Commands: 10th Motorized Cavalry Brigade; 1st Polish Armoured Division

Stanisław Władysław Maczek (31 March 1892 – 11 December 1994) was a Polish tank commander and general of the Second World War, commander of the Polish 1st Armoured Division, whose units liberated parts of France, Belgium and the Netherlands in 1944–45. He is notable for never losing a battle as a divisional commander.

Early career

Maczek served in the Austro-Hungarian army on the Italian front in the First World War, then joined the newly independent Polish Army, fighting in the Polish–Ukrainian and Polish–Soviet wars. In 1938 he took command of the 10th Motorized Cavalry Brigade, Poland's first large armoured-motorised formation. In September 1939 his brigade fought a celebrated delaying campaign against the German invasion, then escaped via Hungary to France.

Second World War

In 1940 Maczek's re-formed 10th Armoured Cavalry Brigade fought in the Battle of France; after the French collapse he was evacuated to Britain. In Scotland he built and trained the 1st Polish Armoured Division, which landed in Normandy in late July 1944. In August the division played a decisive role in closing the Falaise Pocket, capturing Mont Ormel (Hill 262, nicknamed the Maczuga — "the Mace") and holding it against desperate German counterattacks. The division then liberated Abbeville, Saint-Omer, Ypres and Ghent, fought the long battle for Breda in the Netherlands (taken in October 1944 without civilian casualties), participated in Operation Pheasant, and in 1945 advanced into Germany, liberating the Oberlangen prisoner-of-war camp and accepting the surrender of the naval base at Wilhelmshaven.

Later life

After the war the communist government of Poland stripped Maczek of his citizenship; he settled in Edinburgh, where — denied a British military pension — he worked for a time as a barman at a hotel. His citizenship was posthumously restored, and in 1990 he received Poland's highest decoration, the Order of the White Eagle. He died in 1994 at the age of 102 and was buried among his soldiers at the Polish military cemetery in Breda.
