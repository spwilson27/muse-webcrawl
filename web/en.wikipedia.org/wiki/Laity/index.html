In religious organizations, the laity consists of all members who are not part of the clergy, usually including any non-ordained members of a religious group. The word derives from the Greek laikos, meaning "of the people." A layperson is an ordinary member of a faith, distinct from a priest, minister or other officeholder.

Christianity

In the Roman Catholic Church, canon law defines the laity as the Christian faithful who, by baptism, share in Christ's priestly, prophetic and kingly office but are not in holy orders or religious life; the Second Vatican Council's Lumen gentium emphasized the laity's role in sanctifying secular life. In the Orthodox Church the laity participate in worship and church governance in ways that blur a strict clergy–laity divide. Protestant traditions since the Reformation have stressed the "priesthood of all believers," giving laypeople central roles as elders, deacons and preachers; in Methodism, lay preachers have been historically important.

Other uses

In Buddhism, the laity (householders, upāsaka and upāsikā) support the monastic sangha with alms and observe basic precepts. By extension, "layperson" in secular usage means someone without specialized knowledge of a subject — as in lay terms or a lay judge.
