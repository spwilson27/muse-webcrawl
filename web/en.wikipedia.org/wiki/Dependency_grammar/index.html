Dependency grammar

Dependency grammar (DG) is a class of modern grammatical theories that are all based on the dependency relation, as opposed to the constituency relation of phrase structure grammars. A dependency grammar represents the structure of a sentence as a set of binary relations between words, where one word (the head or governor) determines the syntactic category of the phrase.

History

The dependency relation was a central notion in the work of the French linguist Lucien Tesnière, whose posthumous work Éléments de syntaxe structurale (1959) is widely regarded as the founding text of dependency grammar. Tesnière's stemma, a diagram of word-to-word dependencies, visually resembles modern dependency trees.

Dependency vs. constituency

Whereas phrase structure grammars divide a sentence into nested constituents (noun phrases, verb phrases, and so on), dependency grammars posit direct links between individual words. A dependency tree contains one node per word plus a root node, and every word except the root depends on exactly one other word.
