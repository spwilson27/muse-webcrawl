Rhône wine

Rhône wine is produced in the Rhône valley of south-eastern France, one of the country's great wine regions. The valley divides into two markedly different sub-regions: the northern Rhône, where Syrah is the sole red grape, and the southern Rhône, where blends dominated by Grenache predominate and the famous appellation Châteauneuf-du-Pape is found.

Northern Rhône

The northern vineyards cling to steep granite slopes along roughly 65 km of the river. Red appellations include Hermitage, Côte-Rôtie, Cornas, Saint-Joseph and Crozes-Hermitage, all made from Syrah. White wines come from Viognier (Condrieu and Château-Grillet), and from Marsanne and Roussanne in Hermitage, Saint-Joseph and Saint-Péray.

Southern Rhône

The south is broader, warmer and flatter, with vineyards spread across the plains around Orange and Avignon. Red blends typically combine Grenache, Syrah and Mourvèdre (the "GSM" blend later exported worldwide). The regional appellation Côtes du Rhône accounts for the great bulk of production; higher classifications include Côtes du Rhône Villages and individual crus such as Gigondas, Vacqueyras, Rasteau and Châteauneuf-du-Pape, where up to thirteen grape varieties are permitted. The south also produces the rosés of Tavel and Lirac and the fortified vins doux naturels of Beaumes-de-Venise and Rasteau.

History

Viticulture in the valley dates to the Greek and Roman eras; Avignon's period as seat of the papacy in the fourteenth century gave Châteauneuf-du-Pape ("the Pope's new castle") its name and early rules governing production. The Rhône's grape varieties have been widely planted abroad, especially in Australia and California, where the "Rhone Rangers" movement promoted them.
