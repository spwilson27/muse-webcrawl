Light crude oil

Light crude oil is liquid petroleum with a low density and high API gravity, which flows freely at room temperature. It generally has a low wax content and a high proportion of light hydrocarbon fractions, making it easier and cheaper to refine into gasoline and diesel fuel.

Characteristics

Crude oil density is commonly measured using the API gravity scale developed by the American Petroleum Institute. Oils with API gravity above roughly 31 degrees are generally classified as light. Light crude typically commands a higher price than heavy crude oil because it yields more high-value refined products with less processing.

Benchmarks

Well-known light crude benchmarks include West Texas Intermediate (WTI), a light, sweet crude used as a pricing benchmark in oil markets, and Brent Crude from the North Sea. The term "sweet" refers to low sulfur content, and light sweet crudes are especially prized by refiners.
