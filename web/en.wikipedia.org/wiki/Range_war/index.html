Range war

A range war, also known as a range conflict or cattle war, is a type of armed conflict that occurred in the American frontier, mostly in the 19th and early 20th centuries. Range wars were fought primarily over control of "open range" land traditionally used for grazing cattle free of charge by ranchers, but increasingly fenced off by homesteaders and sheep farmers.

Causes

Conflicts arose from competition over water, grazing land and fencing, and from disputes between cattle ranchers and sheep herders (sheep were accused of cropping grass too short and fouling water holes), and between large cattle barons and small settlers accused of rustling.

Notable range wars

  • Lincoln County War (1878, New Mexico) — involving Billy the Kid.
  • Johnson County War (1892, Wyoming) — large ranchers hired gunmen to invade Johnson County and kill alleged rustlers.
  • Pleasant Valley War (1882–1892, Arizona) — a feud between cattle and sheep families.
  • Mason County War (1875, Texas).
  • Sutton–Taylor feud (Texas).

In popular culture

Range wars are a staple of Western fiction, appearing in novels such as Shane and The Virginian and in many Hollywood westerns.
