Wastewater

Aeration basins at a municipal wastewater treatment plant.

Wastewater is water generated after the fresh water is used in a variety of applications or processes and which, because of the quality, quantity or timing of its release, can no longer serve a useful purpose in that application. Roughly speaking, wastewater is any water that has been affected by human use. It is "used water from any combination of domestic, industrial, commercial or agricultural activities, surface runoff or stormwater, and any sewer inflow or sewer infiltration".

Types and sources

Sewage is the subset of wastewater that is contaminated with feces or urine, and is conveyed in sewers. Blackwater comes from toilets, while greywater comes from sinks, baths and washing machines and is less contaminated. Industrial wastewater varies widely by industry and can contain heavy metals, solvents and other chemicals. Urban runoff and stormwater carry oil, sediment, nutrients and litter into drains, and in combined sewer systems can overwhelm treatment plants during storms.

Characteristics

Wastewater is characterized physically (temperature, color, odor, turbidity), chemically — including biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), chemical oxygen demand (COD), suspended solids, nitrogen and phosphorus — and biologically, by the presence of pathogens such as bacteria, viruses and parasites. Globally, a large share of wastewater is discharged untreated, contaminating rivers, lakes and coastal waters and spreading disease.

Management

Collection through sewer networks delivers wastewater to treatment plants, where contaminants are removed before discharge or reuse. Treated wastewater is reused for irrigation, groundwater recharge and, in some places, indirect potable reuse. Sludge separated during treatment can be digested for biogas and used as biosolids. Sustainable sanitation also includes decentralized and nature-based systems such as constructed wetlands.
