Stephen Ray Vaughan (October 3, 1954 – August 27, 1990) was an American musician, singer, songwriter, and record producer. He was the guitarist and frontman of the blues rock band Stevie Ray Vaughan and Double Trouble, and is widely regarded as one of the most influential electric guitarists in the history of blues music. Despite a mainstream career that spanned only seven years, Vaughan revived blues rock in the 1980s and paved the way for a renewed interest in the genre.

Early life

Vaughan was born and raised in the Oak Cliff neighborhood of Dallas, Texas. Inspired by his older brother Jimmie Vaughan, he began playing guitar at the age of seven, receiving his first instrument as a gift from his brother. He dropped out of high school in 1971 to pursue music full time, moving to Austin in 1972, where the city's club scene gave him room to develop his style. He played in a succession of bands, including the Nightcrawlers and Paul Ray and the Cobras, before forming Double Trouble in 1978 with bassist Tommy Shannon and drummer Chris Layton, taking the band's name from an Otis Rush song.

Career

Vaughan's breakthrough came in 1982, when Double Trouble performed at the Montreux Jazz Festival. The performance attracted the attention of David Bowie, who invited Vaughan to play on his album Let's Dance (1983), and of producer John Hammond, who helped secure a recording contract with Epic Records. The band's debut album, Texas Flood (1983), recorded in just a few days at Jackson Browne's studio, reached the charts and established Vaughan as a major new voice in blues. It was followed by Couldn't Stand the Weather (1984) and Soul to Soul (1985).

At the height of his success Vaughan struggled with alcohol and cocaine addiction, which came to a head during a European tour in 1986. After a collapse in Germany he entered rehabilitation and returned sober. The live album Live Alive (1986) documented the turbulent period. His fourth studio album, In Step (1989), recorded after his recovery, won the Grammy Award for Best Contemporary Blues Album and included the hit single "Crossfire". In 1990 he recorded Family Style with his brother Jimmie, released shortly after his death.

Death

On August 27, 1990, after a performance at the Alpine Valley Music Theatre in East Troy, Wisconsin, Vaughan boarded a helicopter bound for Chicago. The aircraft crashed into a hill in fog shortly after takeoff, killing all five people aboard. Vaughan was 35 years old. He was buried in Laurel Land Cemetery in Dallas.

Style and legacy

Vaughan's playing combined the influence of blues masters such as Albert King, Freddie King, Otis Rush, and Buddy Guy with the rock energy of Jimi Hendrix, whom he also admired. He was known for his powerful string bending, wide vibrato, and use of heavy-gauge strings on his battered Fender Stratocaster, nicknamed "Number One". He typically played with the guitar tuned down a half-step. Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time, and he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2015, along with Double Trouble. His work inspired a generation of blues and rock guitarists, including Kenny Wayne Shepherd and John Mayer.
