Collie

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Rough Collie, popularised worldwide by the fictional dog Lassie.

Collies are a distinct type of herding dog, native to Scotland and northern England. The word "collie" has long been used for sheepdogs of this area, a group that includes many related landraces and several formally recognised breeds, among them the Rough Collie, the Smooth Collie, the Border Collie, the Shetland Sheepdog, and the Bearded Collie.

History

Collies descend from the working sheepdogs of the Scottish Highlands and the Anglo-Scottish border country, bred for intelligence, stamina, and an instinct to gather and drive livestock. Queen Victoria's fondness for the breed, acquired at Balmoral, helped transform the collie into a fashionable show and companion dog in the later 19th century. Formal breed standards divided the collies into separate breeds, of which the Border Collie retained the strongest working reputation while the Rough Collie became best known as a pet.

Characteristics

Collies are medium-sized, lightly to strongly built dogs with pointed muzzles and semi-erect ears. The Rough Collie has a long, abundant double coat in sable and white, tricolour, or blue merle; the Smooth Collie is the same breed with a short coat in some registries. Border Collies are famed for their intense "eye" and crouching style of herding. Collies are generally intelligent, trainable, and sensitive, though the MDR1 gene mutation common in herding breeds can cause sensitivity to certain drugs such as ivermectin.

In popular culture

The most famous collie is Lassie, the fictional Rough Collie of Eric Knight's 1940 novel Lassie Come-Home, and of films and a long-running television series that made "Lassie" a byword for canine loyalty. Other celebrated collies include Reveille, mascot of Texas A&M University, and the dogs of Albert Payson Terhune's Sunnybank kennels.
