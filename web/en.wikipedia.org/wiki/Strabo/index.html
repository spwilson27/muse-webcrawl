Strabo

Strabo (Greek: Στράβων Strábōn; c. 64 BC – c. AD 24) was a Greek geographer, philosopher and historian who lived in Asia Minor during the transitional period of the Roman Republic into the Roman Empire. He is best known for his Geographica (Geography), a seventeen-book description of the known world that is a principal source for the geography, peoples and history of the ancient Mediterranean.

Life

Strabo was born to a wealthy family in Amaseia, the former capital of the Kingdom of Pontus, and was educated by grammarians and philosophers in Nysa and Rome. He travelled widely — through Egypt (sailing up the Nile as far as Philae in the entourage of the prefect Aelius Gallus), Ethiopia, Italy, Greece and Asia Minor — though much of his geography was compiled from earlier authorities such as Eratosthenes, Hipparchus and Polybius, whose works are otherwise largely lost.

Geographica

The Geographica describes Europe, Asia and Africa in seventeen books, combining physical description with political history, ethnography, myth and local custom. Written for Roman statesmen and commanders, it championed geography as a philosophical and practical pursuit. Unlike Strabo's earlier forty-seven-book Historical Sketches, which is almost entirely lost, the Geography survives nearly complete and remains an essential source for ancient toponyms and for the state of the Roman world under Augustus and Tiberius.
