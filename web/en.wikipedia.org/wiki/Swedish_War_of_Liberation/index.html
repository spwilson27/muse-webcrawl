The Swedish War of Liberation (Swedish: Befrielsekriget), also known as Gustav Vasa's War of Liberation, was a rebellion and civil war fought from 1521 to 1523 in which the Swedish nobleman Gustav Vasa successfully deposed King Christian II of Denmark as regent of Sweden, dissolving the Kalmar Union between Denmark, Norway, and Sweden.

Background

Christian II had reconquered Sweden in 1520 and, following his coronation in Stockholm, ordered the execution of scores of Swedish nobles and churchmen in the Stockholm Bloodbath of November 1520 — among them Gustav Vasa's father, Erik Johansson Vasa. Gustav fled to Dalarna, where, after initial reluctance, the miners and peasants rallied to him; tradition recounts his dramatic ski flight toward Norway and the messengers who overtook him at Sälen, an episode commemorated by the Vasaloppet ski race.

The war

From Mora, Gustav's peasant army grew steadily through 1521, capturing strongholds and gaining support from the Hanseatic city of Lübeck, which supplied ships, soldiers, and credit. Swedish forces took key towns including Uppsala and Västerås, and in 1523 entered Stockholm. On 6 June 1523 the Riksdag at Strängnäs elected Gustav king of an independent Sweden — a date still celebrated as Sweden's National Day. Malmö was retaken in 1524 under the Treaty of Malmö, formally ending Danish claims.

Aftermath

The war ended the Kalmar Union permanently and founded the Swedish Vasa dynasty. Gustav I went on to centralise the monarchy, carry through the Swedish Reformation, and lay the foundations of the modern Swedish state.
