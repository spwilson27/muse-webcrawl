Frederick Carlton "Carl" Lewis (born 1 July 1961) is an American former track and field athlete who is widely regarded as one of the greatest Olympians of all time. He won nine Olympic gold medals, one Olympic silver medal, and ten World Championships medals, including eight gold. Lewis competed in the sprints and the long jump, and was part of world-record-setting United States 4×100 metres relay teams.

Career

Lewis emerged at the University of Houston under coach Tom Tellez. At the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics he matched Jesse Owens' feat of winning four gold medals at a single Games (100 m, 200 m, long jump and 4×100 m relay). At the 1988 Seoul Olympics he retained his 100 m title after Ben Johnson's disqualification for doping, and won the long jump again. He added further golds at Barcelona 1992 and won a remarkable fourth consecutive Olympic long jump title at Atlanta in 1996, aged 35. At the 1991 World Championships in Tokyo, Lewis set a then-world record of 9.86 seconds in the 100 m and took part in the celebrated long jump duel with Mike Powell, in which Powell broke Bob Beamon's 23-year-old world record.

Legacy

The International Olympic Committee named Lewis "Sportsman of the Century" in 1999, and Sports Illustrated called him "Olympian of the Century". After retirement he pursued acting, business and coaching, becoming head track and field coach at the University of Houston.
