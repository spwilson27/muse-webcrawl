Jeffrey Lee Probst (born November 4, 1961) is an American television presenter and producer. He is best known as the host of the American version of the reality television show Survivor, a role he has held since the show's premiere in 2000, and for which he has also served as an executive producer and showrunner.

Early career

Probst was born in Seattle, Washington, and grew up in Bellevue. He attended Seattle Pacific University before leaving to work in the production department of Boeing's Motion Picture/Television studio, writing and producing marketing videos. He later hosted programs for FX, including Backchat, and was a correspondent for Access Hollywood, as well as the host of VH1's Rock & Roll Jeopardy!.

Survivor

In 2000, Probst became the host of Survivor on CBS. His role at Tribal Council—questioning contestants before the elimination vote and snuffing the torch of the voted-out player with the line "The tribe has spoken"—became one of the signatures of the series. Probst has won multiple Primetime Emmy Awards for Outstanding Host for a Reality or Reality-Competition Program and became the show's executive producer and showrunner, taking creative control of the series.

Other work

Probst hosted the syndicated talk show The Jeff Probst Show (2012–2013) and has written adventure novels for middle-grade readers, including the Stranded series co-authored with Chris Tebbetts. He has also directed the feature film Finder's Fee (2001) and the drama Kiss Me (2014).
