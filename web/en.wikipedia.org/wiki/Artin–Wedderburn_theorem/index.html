Artin–Wedderburn theorem

A semisimple ring decomposes as a finite product of matrix rings over division rings

The Artin–Wedderburn theorem is a fundamental classification theorem in abstract algebra. It states that a (unital) ring R is semisimple if and only if it is isomorphic to a finite product of matrix rings Mni(Di) over division rings Di, where the integers ni and the division rings are uniquely determined up to permutation.

History

Joseph Wedderburn first proved the theorem in 1907 for finite-dimensional algebras over a field, in his doctoral thesis at the University of Chicago. Emil Artin later generalised it in 1927 to rings satisfying chain conditions — the semisimple Artinian rings. The result is sometimes called the Wedderburn–Artin theorem.

Consequences

The theorem implies that every semisimple ring is both Artinian and Noetherian, and it underlies the classification of group algebras in representation theory: over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, a finite group algebra decomposes into matrix blocks, one for each irreducible representation — the context of Maschke's theorem. Another famous corollary is Wedderburn's little theorem, that every finite division ring is a field.
