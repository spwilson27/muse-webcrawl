David Haussler Wolpert is an American mathematician, physicist, and computer scientist. He is known for formulating the no free lunch theorems of search and optimization (with William Macready), which establish that, averaged over all possible problems, no optimization or machine-learning algorithm performs better than any other — including blind random search. Wolpert has worked at Los Alamos National Laboratory, NASA Ames Research Center, and the Santa Fe Institute, where he is a professor.

No free lunch theorems

In papers published in the mid-1990s, Wolpert and Macready proved that when performance is averaged uniformly over all possible objective functions, any two optimization algorithms perform identically. Wolpert's companion results for supervised learning show that, averaged over all possible target functions, all learning algorithms have the same off-training-set generalization performance. The theorems are often summarized as "there is no free lunch": improved performance on one class of problems is necessarily offset by worse performance on others, so successful algorithm design requires matching the algorithm's inductive bias to the structure of the problem at hand. The results have been widely discussed, and sometimes misread as implying that algorithm choice does not matter, whereas in practice problems are drawn from structured, non-uniform distributions.

Other research

Wolpert introduced stacked generalization ("stacking"), a method of combining the outputs of multiple learning algorithms through a higher-level learner, which anticipated modern ensemble methods. His work on product distributions extended Bayesian methods to handle distributions over functions and algorithms. He has also made contributions to collective intelligence (the design of multi-agent systems), the thermodynamics of computation, and game theory, and developed results on the physical limits of inference and observation, sometimes discussed in connection with formal limits on prediction devices.

Education and positions

Wolpert earned a bachelor's degree in physics from Princeton University and a PhD in physics from the University of California, Santa Barbara. He held research positions at Los Alamos National Laboratory and NASA Ames Research Center, and a professorship at the Santa Fe Institute, publishing several hundred papers spanning machine learning, statistical physics, and information theory.
