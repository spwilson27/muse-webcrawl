Bundesbank

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Deutsche Bundesbank is the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany, founded in 1957 (succeeding the Bank deutscher Länder of 1948) and headquartered in Frankfurt am Main. Modelled partly on the Federal Reserve and deliberately independent of government instruction, it became Europe's most influential central bank through its successful defence of the Deutschmark — the hard-currency (“stability culture”) tradition rooted in the hyperinflation of 1923 and the currency reform of 1948.

Since 1999 monetary policy for Germany is made within the Eurosystem: the Bundesbank's president sits on the ECB Governing Council (one vote, like every member), the bank implements policy and holds large claims within the TARGET2 settlement system (balances that became a domestic political controversy during the euro crisis), and it retains national tasks — banking supervision (with BaFin), reserves and cash supply. The ECB itself, also in Frankfurt, inherited much of the Bundesbank's institutional DNA (independence, price-stability primacy), and German constitutional litigation over ECB programmes (Gauweiler, the 2020 PSPP judgment of the Federal Constitutional Court) recurs because that inheritance cuts both ways.
