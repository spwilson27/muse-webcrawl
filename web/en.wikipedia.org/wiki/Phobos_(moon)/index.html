Phobos (moon)

Phobos is the innermost and larger of the two natural satellites of Mars, the other being Deimos. Both moons were discovered in 1877 by American astronomer Asaph Hall. Phobos is a small, irregularly shaped body with a mean radius of about 11 kilometres, heavily cratered, with the large Stickney crater dominating one side.

Phobos orbits Mars closer to the planet's surface than any other known planetary moon, completing an orbit in about 7 hours 39 minutes — faster than Mars rotates — and it is gradually spiralling inward; it is expected to break apart into a ring system within tens of millions of years. Its origin is debated, with hypotheses including captured asteroid and impact debris.
