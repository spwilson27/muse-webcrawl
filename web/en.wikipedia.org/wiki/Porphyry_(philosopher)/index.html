Porphyry (philosopher)

Porphyry of Tyre
Born: c. 234 AD, Tyre
Died: c. 305 AD, Rome
Era: Ancient philosophy
School: Neoplatonism

Porphyry of Tyre (Ancient Greek: Πορφύριος, Porphýrios; Arabic: فرفوريوس, Furfūriyūs; c. 234 – c. 305 AD) was a Neoplatonic philosopher born in Tyre, in Roman Phoenicia during late antiquity. He was the student of Plotinus and edited and published the Enneads, the only collection of Plotinus's writings.

His commentary on Euclid's Elements was used as a source by Pappus of Alexandria, and he wrote extensively on philosophy, logic, mathematics, religion, and vegetarianism. His Isagoge, an introduction to logic and philosophy, became the standard textbook on logic throughout the Middle Ages in the Latin, Syriac, and Arabic traditions.

Isagoge and the problem of universals

In the Isagoge, Porphyry posed — but declined to answer — questions about whether genera and species exist in themselves or only in the mind, which went on to frame the medieval problem of universals. The "Porphyrian tree" is a classic diagram of his scheme of classification by genus and differentia.

Other works

Porphyry also wrote Against the Christians, a critique of Christianity that was later burned by imperial order, and On Abstinence from Animal Food, an influential argument for vegetarianism grounded in justice toward animals.
