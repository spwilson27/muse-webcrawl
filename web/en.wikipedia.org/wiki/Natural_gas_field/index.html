A natural gas field is an area containing one or more subsurface reservoirs of natural gas trapped in porous rock formations sealed by impermeable cap rock. Gas fields are found both onshore and offshore, and are frequently associated with oil fields, although some fields produce predominantly gas. They are a major component of the global energy industry, supplying fuel for heating, electricity generation, and industrial processes.

Formation

Natural gas forms when organic-rich source rock is buried and heated over geological time, generating hydrocarbons through the thermal cracking of kerogen. The gas migrates upward through permeable rock until it is trapped in structural or stratigraphic traps. Typical reservoirs are sandstones and carbonates at depths ranging from a few hundred metres to more than 5 kilometres.

Major fields

The largest natural gas field in the world is the South Pars / North Dome field, shared between Iran and Qatar in the Persian Gulf, with recoverable reserves estimated at around 51 trillion cubic metres. Other giant fields include Urengoy and Yamburg in western Siberia, Russia, the Hassi R'mel field in Algeria, and the Groningen field in the Netherlands, once Western Europe's largest.

Exploitation

Gas is extracted through production wells. Because gas expands as pressure drops, many fields produce by natural pressure depletion, sometimes aided by compression. Offshore fields may be developed with fixed platforms, floating production systems, or subsea tiebacks. Produced gas is treated to remove water, carbon dioxide, hydrogen sulfide, and heavier hydrocarbons before being transported by pipeline or converted to liquefied natural gas (LNG) for shipment.
