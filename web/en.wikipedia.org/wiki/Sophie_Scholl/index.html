Sophie Scholl

Sophia Magdalena Scholl (9 May 1921 – 22 February 1943) was a German student and anti-Nazi political activist, active within the White Rose non-violent resistance group in Nazi Germany. She was convicted of high treason after distributing anti-war leaflets at the University of Munich with her brother, Hans. As a result, she and her brother were both executed by guillotine; since the 1970s, she has been extensively commemorated for her anti-Nazi resistance work.

Early life

Scholl was born in Forchtenberg, the fourth of six children of the liberal politician Robert Scholl. Like many of her generation she initially joined Nazi youth organizations, but she became disillusioned, influenced by her father, her studies, and reports of Nazi atrocities on the Eastern Front.

White Rose

At the University of Munich, where she studied biology and philosophy, Sophie joined her brother Hans and friends including Christoph Probst, Alexander Schmorell, Willi Graf, and professor Kurt Huber in the White Rose. The group wrote and distributed leaflets denouncing the Nazi regime and urging resistance. On 18 February 1943, Sophie and Hans were caught scattering leaflets in the university atrium. After a show trial before Roland Freisler's People's Court, both siblings and Probst were executed on 22 February 1943.

Legacy

Sophie Scholl is one of the most famous figures of German resistance to Nazism. Schools, streets, and prizes across Germany bear her name; in 2003 Germans voted her among the ten most important Germans of all time in the television poll Unsere Besten. Her story is told in the film Sophie Scholl — The Final Days (2005).
