Martin-Löf type theory (MLTT) is an intuitionistic type theory developed by the Swedish logician and philosopher Per Martin-Löf beginning in the 1970s. It serves both as a foundation for constructive mathematics and as the basis of proof assistants such as Agda and, indirectly, Coq and Lean.

Judgements and types

The theory is built on judgements such as 'A is a type' and 'a is a term of type A'. Its central features include dependent types, identity types, and a hierarchy of universes. Propositions are identified with types under the Curry–Howard correspondence, so a proof is a program.

Intensional and extensional versions

Martin-Löf published several versions; the 1984 Bibliopolis monograph presents the influential intensional theory, in which definitional equality is decidable but propositional equality requires proof. Homotopy type theory later reinterpreted identity types as spaces of paths.
