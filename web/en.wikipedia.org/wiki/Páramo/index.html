Páramo

LocationNorthern Andes: Colombia, Ecuador, Venezuela, Peru, Costa Rica
Elevationc. 3,100–5,000 m
ClimateCold, humid alpine tundra
Characteristic floraEspeletia (frailejones), tussock grasses, cushion plants

Páramo is a tropical alpine ecosystem found in the high Andes of South and Central America, above the continuous forest line (about 3,100–3,500 m) and below the permanent snow line (about 5,000 m). The largest expanse lies in Colombia and Ecuador, with additional areas in Venezuela, northern Peru and Costa Rica (where it is often grouped with the Talamanca range). Páramos are sometimes described as "the water towers of the Andes" because their soils and vegetation capture and regulate the water supply of millions of people in cities such as Bogotá and Quito.

Ecology

The páramo experiences a harsh climate of intense solar radiation by day and near-freezing temperatures at night, often summarised as "summer every day and winter every night". Vegetation is dominated by tussock grasses, ground rosettes and the emblematic frailejones (Espeletia species) — giant rosette plants with thick, hairy leaves adapted to cold and drought. Despite covering a relatively small area, páramos are exceptionally rich in endemic species, including the spectacled bear, mountain tapir and numerous hummingbirds. The ecosystem stores large amounts of carbon in its deep, wet soils.

Threats

Páramos are threatened by agricultural expansion (especially potato farming), livestock grazing and burning, pine plantations, mining, and climate change, which is driving species upslope. Because of their role in water regulation, several Andean countries have strengthened legal protection of páramo areas.
