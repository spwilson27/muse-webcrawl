Manuel Luis Quezon y Molina (19 August 1878 – 1 August 1944), commonly known as Manuel L. Quezon, was a Filipino statesman, soldier and politician who served as president of the Philippines from 1935 until his death in 1944. He was the first Filipino to head a government of the entire Philippines and is considered the second president of the Philippines after Emilio Aguinaldo.

Early life and career

Quezon was born in Baler, in present-day Aurora province, to Spanish mestizo parents; his father was a schoolteacher. He studied law at the University of Santo Tomas, fought in the Philippine–American War as an aide to Aguinaldo, and after the war entered politics, becoming governor of Tayabas province, Resident Commissioner to the United States House of Representatives (1909–1916), and then president of the Philippine Senate under the Jones Law.

Presidency

As Senate president and leader of the Nacionalista Party, Quezon dominated Philippine politics and campaigned for independence from the United States. The Tydings–McDuffie Act of 1934 established the Commonwealth of the Philippines as a ten-year transition to independence, and Quezon was elected its first president in 1935. His administration promoted a national language based on Tagalog, social justice programs, women's suffrage (approved by plebiscite in 1937), land reform and the development of national defence.

When Japanese forces invaded in December 1941, Quezon's government evacuated to Corregidor and then into exile in the United States, where he headed a government-in-exile in Washington, D.C. His term was extended by the U.S. Congress due to the war. He died of tuberculosis at Saranac Lake, New York, in 1944, and was later reinterred in Manila; in 1979 his remains were moved to the Quezon Memorial Shrine in Quezon City, the city named after him.
