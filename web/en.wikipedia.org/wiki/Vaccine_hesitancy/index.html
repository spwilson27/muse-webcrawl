Vaccine hesitancy

Vaccine hesitancy is a delay in acceptance, or refusal, of vaccines despite the availability of vaccine services. The term covers reluctance or refusal to vaccinate, and is influenced by factors such as complacency, convenience, confidence, and risk calculation. In 2019 the World Health Organization named vaccine hesitancy one of the top ten threats to global health.

Causes

Hesitancy arises from concerns about safety and side effects, distrust of pharmaceutical companies and authorities, religious or philosophical beliefs, and misinformation spread through social media. A major historical episode was the 1998 publication of a fraudulent study by Andrew Wakefield falsely linking the MMR vaccine to autism; the paper was retracted in 2010 and Wakefield was struck off the medical register, but the claim continued to circulate.

Consequences

Falling vaccination coverage has contributed to resurgences of measles and other preventable diseases in several countries. Public health responses include improved communication, reminder systems, presumptive clinician recommendations, and, in some jurisdictions, mandates or restrictions on non-medical exemptions.
