Luther Gulick (social scientist)

Luther Halsey Gulick (January 17, 1892 – January 10, 1993) was an American expert on public administration, a president of the Institute of Public Administration, and a leading figure in New York City government and urban policy.

Career

Born in Osaka, Japan, to missionary parents, Gulick was educated at Oberlin College and Columbia University, where he earned his doctorate in 1920. He spent much of his career directing the Institute of Public Administration in New York and advising governments at every level. During the Second World War he served in Washington, and he later worked on the reorganisation of New York City government, chairing the committee whose recommendations led to the creation of the office of City Administrator, a post he himself held under Mayor Robert F. Wagner Jr. from 1954 to 1956.

POSDCORB

Gulick is best remembered for the acronym POSDCORB, introduced in his 1937 essay "Notes on the Theory of Organization" (published in Papers on the Science of Administration, co-edited with Lyndall Urwick). The letters summarised the chief functions of an executive: Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Coordinating, Reporting, and Budgeting. The framework became a standard reference in public administration teaching, even as later scholars criticised its classical, principle-based approach.
