Industrial engineering

Industrial engineering is an engineering profession that is concerned with the optimization of complex processes, systems, or organizations by developing, improving and implementing integrated systems of people, money, knowledge, information and equipment.

History

The discipline grew out of the scientific management movement of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, associated with Frederick Winslow Taylor and with Frank and Lillian Gilbreth, whose time and motion studies sought to improve efficiency. Henry Gantt, inventor of the Gantt chart, and Harrington Emerson were other early contributors. The field expanded during the Second World War with the development of operations research.

Areas of practice

  • Operations research and management science
  • Production and manufacturing engineering
  • Quality engineering and reliability
  • Ergonomics and human factors
  • Supply chain management and logistics
  • Facilities planning and work design

Methods

Industrial engineers use mathematical modelling, statistical analysis, simulation, queuing theory, and lean and Six Sigma methods to design and improve systems in manufacturing, healthcare, transportation, and services.

Education

Degree programs are accredited in many countries; in the United States the professional society is the Institute of Industrial and Systems Engineers (IISE), founded in 1948.
