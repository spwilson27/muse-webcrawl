Archibald Leman Cochrane CBE (12 January 1909 – 18 June 1988) was a Scottish physician and epidemiologist whose advocacy of randomised controlled trials (RCTs) and systematic evaluation of medical care made him one of the founders of evidence-based medicine. The international organisation Cochrane (formerly the Cochrane Collaboration), which produces systematic reviews of healthcare interventions, is named after him.

Life

Born in Galashiels into a family that had made money in tweed, Cochrane studied at Cambridge and began medical training at University College Hospital, London, interrupting it for psychoanalysis in Vienna and for service as a medical officer with a British ambulance unit in the Spanish Civil War. In the Second World War he served with the Royal Army Medical Corps and spent four years as a prisoner of war in German camps — an experience he later described in a celebrated paper on caring for prisoners with almost no drugs, in which he credited the human body, rather than his treatments, with most recoveries.

Career

After the war Cochrane joined the Medical Research Council's Pneumoconiosis Research Unit in South Wales, conducting classic community epidemiology in the Rhondda Fach valley, including whole-population surveys of tuberculosis and coal miners' lung disease and the long-running Rhondda–Llanelli comparative studies. In 1972 he published Effectiveness and Efficiency: Random Reflections on Health Services, which argued that because NHS resources would always be limited, treatments should be funded only where controlled trials — ideally randomised — had shown them effective. His famous summation: "All effective treatment must be free" under a tax-funded service.

Cochrane also challenged colleagues to compile a critical, periodically updated summary of all relevant RCTs; the resulting perinatal database work of Iain Chalmers and others led to the founding of the Cochrane Collaboration in 1993. Cochrane famously proposed that his own epitaph, and the organisation's logo, reflect the forest-plot results of a meta-analysis — the Cochrane logo indeed depicts the forest plot of trials of corticosteroids for preterm birth.
