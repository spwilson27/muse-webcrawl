Ronald Charles McKernan (September 8, 1945 – March 8, 1973), known as Pigpen, was an American musician best known as a founding member of the Grateful Dead. A blues devotee whose father was an R&B disc jockey, McKernan provided vocals, harmonica, organ and percussion, and fronted the young band's blues and R&B material, singing lead on songs such as "Turn On Your Love Light", "Good Lovin'" and "In the Midnight Hour".

Career

McKernan met Jerry Garcia in the Palo Alto folk scene and played in Mother McCree's Uptown Jug Champions and the Warlocks before the Grateful Dead formed in 1965. In the band's psychedelic years his earthy blues style anchored their sound; he contributed songs including "Operator". Heavy drinking damaged his health, and by 1971–72 liver problems forced him to stop touring; his final concert was on 17 June 1972 at the Hollywood Bowl.

Death and legacy

McKernan died on 8 March 1973 of a gastrointestinal hemorrhage at age 27 — a member of the informal "27 Club". He was posthumously inducted with the Grateful Dead into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1994.
