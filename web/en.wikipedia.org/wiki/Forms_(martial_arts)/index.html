Forms (martial arts)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Forms, in martial arts, are sequences of movements — strikes, blocks, kicks, stances, and turns — practised in a predetermined pattern against imaginary opponents. They serve to preserve and transmit a style's techniques, develop balance, coordination, and muscle memory, and provide a basis for grading and competition.

Names by tradition

Forms are known by many names across cultures: kata in Japanese arts such as karate and judo; taolu in Chinese wushu; poomsae (or hyeong/tul) in Korean taekwondo; kuyen in Filipino martial arts; and jurus in Indonesian pencak silat. Traditional forms often encode applications (Japanese bunkai) that are not obvious from the surface movements.

Purpose and debate

Proponents regard forms training as a repository of technique and a means of developing precision and spirit. Critics, particularly from modern combat sports, argue that forms practice alone does not develop fighting ability without live sparring. Most traditional schools combine forms with partner drills and free practice.
