Akiva Aryeh Weiss (1868–1947) was a Zionist activist and Jewish watchmaker and jeweller who was one of the principal founders of Tel Aviv. Born in Grodno and raised in Łódź, he immigrated to Palestine in 1906 and settled in Jaffa.

Founding of Tel Aviv

Soon after his arrival, Weiss became the driving force behind the Ahuzat Bayit ("homestead") association, a group of Jewish families in Jaffa who sought to build a modern garden suburb outside the crowded, unsanitary alleys of the old city. Weiss chaired the association, negotiated the purchase of sand dunes north of Jaffa with a loan from the Jewish National Fund, and organized the famous lottery of 11 April 1909, in which 66 families drew seashells inscribed with their names and plot numbers to allocate the first parcels of land. That event is conventionally regarded as the founding of Tel Aviv.

Weiss designed his own house at 2 Herzl Street, one of the first built in the new neighbourhood, and promoted a Hebrew urban culture; he was also involved in founding other institutions including a Hebrew publishing venture and the first Hebrew cinema plans. Although his role was later overshadowed by that of Meir Dizengoff, Tel Aviv's first mayor, Weiss is now widely credited as the city's initiating visionary.

Legacy

Streets in Tel Aviv bear his name, and his house has been preserved as a historic landmark. The white and grey seashells used in the founding lottery are displayed in the city's historical exhibitions.
