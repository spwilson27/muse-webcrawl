Kenneth MacAlpin (Gaelic: Cináed mac Ailpín; died 858), sometimes anglicised as Kenneth I, was a king of the Picts and, according to later national tradition, the first king of Scots. He is traditionally regarded as the founder of the kingdom of Alba — the nucleus of medieval Scotland — by uniting the Gaelic kingdom of Dál Riata with the Pictish kingdom, probably in the 840s.

Life and reign

Little contemporary evidence survives. Kenneth succeeded his father Alpín as king of Dál Riata, and by about 843 he had also gained the Pictish throne, though how this came about is disputed: later chronicles describe conquest or treachery — including the legendary "treachery of Scone", in which Pictish nobles were supposedly murdered at a feast — while modern historians tend to favour dynastic claim through Pictish matrilineal succession, a marriage alliance, or gradual Gaelic ascendancy after Viking attacks had devastated Pictland in the 830s and 840s.

Kenneth transferred relics of St Columba from Iona, threatened by Viking raids, to a new church at Dunkeld, and made Scone a royal centre. He is said to have invaded Saxon Northumbria several times. He died, probably of a tumour, at Forteviot in 858 and was buried on Iona. He was succeeded by his brother Donald I, in accordance with the succession system of tanistry.

Legacy

Although the merging of Picts and Scots was a long process completed by his descendants, Kenneth's dynasty, the House of Alpin, ruled the emerging kingdom for generations, and he was remembered in later Scottish tradition as the nation's founding king.
