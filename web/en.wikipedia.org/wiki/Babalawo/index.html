Babalawo
Yoruba: Babaláwo, "father of secrets"
Role: Priest of Ifá / Orunmila
Traditions: Yoruba religion, Santería, Candomblé

A babalawo (Yoruba: babaláwo, literally "father of the mysteries/secrets") is a priest of Ifá, the Yoruba system of divination and wisdom associated with the orisha Orunmila. Female priests are known as iyanifa or iyalawo. Babalawos undergo years of training memorising the vast oral corpus of Ifá verses (odu).

Practice

In consultation, the babalawo casts sacred palm nuts (ikin) or a divining chain (opele) on a carved wooden tray (opon Ifá), generating one of 256 odu patterns. He recites the associated verses — stories, proverbs and prescriptions — and advises the client, often prescribing sacrifices (ebo) or other remedies.

Diaspora

Ifá practice spread through the African diaspora to Cuba (where babalawos are central to Santería/Regla de Ocha-Ifá), Brazil, Trinidad and beyond. UNESCO proclaimed the Ifá divination system a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2005.
