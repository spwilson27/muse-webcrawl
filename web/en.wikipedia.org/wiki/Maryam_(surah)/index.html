Maryam (Arabic: مريم, "Mary") is the nineteenth chapter (surah) of the Quran. It contains 98 verses (ayat) and takes its name from Mary, the mother of Jesus, whose story occupies a central place in the chapter. It is traditionally classified as a Meccan surah, revealed before the Hijrah, though some verses are held to have been revealed in Medina or during the migration to Abyssinia.

Content

The surah opens with the disconnected letters Kāf Hā Yā ʿAin Ṣād. It recounts the story of Zechariah and the miraculous birth of his son John (Yahya), then gives the Quran's fullest account of Mary: her dedication to God, her seclusion, the annunciation by the angel, and the virgin birth of Jesus (Isa) beneath a palm tree. The chapter then mentions Abraham's separation from his father's idolatry, Moses, Aaron, Ishmael, Idris, and other prophets, contrasting their righteous followers with later generations who neglected prayer. It strongly affirms the resurrection and rejects the claim that God has taken a son, while presenting Jesus as a prophet and servant of God.

Context

Tradition holds that Ja'far ibn Abi Talib recited verses from Surah Maryam before the Negus (king) of Abyssinia when the early Muslim emigrants sought refuge there, moving the king to protect them. The surah is noted for its rhyme and rhythm and for its extensive treatment of figures shared with the Christian tradition.
