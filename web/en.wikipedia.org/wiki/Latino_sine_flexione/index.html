Latino sine flexione ("Latin without inflections"), also called Interlingua de Academia pro Interlingua or Peano's Interlingua, is a constructed international auxiliary language based on Latin, invented by the Italian mathematician Giuseppe Peano in 1903. Peano stripped Latin of its complex inflectional grammar — noun cases, verb conjugations, and grammatical gender — retaining Latin vocabulary in simplified, invariant forms, with distinctions of number and tense indicated by separate words or by context. Peano published the proposal in the article "De Latino Sine Flexione, Principio de Maximal Simplicitate" and developed it through the Academia pro Interlingua, which he directed.

Peano promoted Latino sine flexione through the Academia pro Interlingua's journal.

Design

Peano's principles were simplicity and maximal internationality. Vocabulary was drawn from Latin words already widely present in the major European languages, often in the form of the Latin ablative stem stripped of endings. Grammar followed rules Peano published progressively: articles were dispensed with; plurals were marked with -s; the infinitive served as the base verb form, with the suffix -ba for the past and -re style auxiliaries for other tenses evolving in later versions; word order followed that of the modern European languages. Peano himself increasingly wrote his mathematical works in the language — the later editions of his Formulario Mathematico were published in Latino sine flexione, so that mathematics, like the language, might be maximally accessible.

History and influence

The language was discussed at international scientific congresses and was one of the candidates considered by the Delegation for the Adoption of an International Auxiliary Language (1907), which ultimately endorsed Esperanto with reforms (leading indirectly to Ido). Latino sine flexione is historically important as the direct ancestor of Interlingua: the International Auxiliary Language Association (IALA), whose Interlingua was published in 1951 under Alexander Gode, developed its naturalistic approach partly from the tradition of Peano's Academia pro Interlingua, which merged into the IALA's orbit. Because of the shared name "Interlingua," Peano's language is sometimes confused with the IALA language; they are distinct, though related, projects.

Example

The Lord's Prayer in Latino sine flexione begins:

Patre nostro, qui es in celos, que tuo nomine fi sanctificato. Que tuo regno adveni. Que tuo voluntate es facto sicut in celo et in terra.
