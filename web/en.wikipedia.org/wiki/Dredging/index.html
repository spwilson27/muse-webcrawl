Dredging

Dredging is the excavation of material from beneath water, usually the seabed or a riverbed, carried out to deepen or maintain navigable channels, harbours and waterways, to reclaim land, or to extract valuable minerals such as sand, gravel, tin or gold.

Methods and equipment

Dredging vessels include trailing suction hopper dredgers, which drag suction pipes along the bed and store spoil in a hold; cutter-suction dredgers, which dislodge material with a rotating cutter head and pump it through a pipeline; and mechanical dredgers such as grab, bucket-ladder and backhoe dredgers. Maintenance dredging keeps established ports and channels at depth, while capital dredging creates new or deeper facilities — the deepening of the Panama and Suez canals are large examples.

Environmental issues

Dredging stirs up sediment, which can smother marine habitats and release contaminants locked in the bed; disposal of spoil is regulated in many jurisdictions, and projects commonly require environmental assessment and monitoring. Beneficial uses of dredged material include beach nourishment and wetland creation.
