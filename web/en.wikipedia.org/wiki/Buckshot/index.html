Buckshot

Buckshot is a type of shotgun ammunition consisting of a small number of large-diameter pellets (shot). It is one of the most common shotgun loads, used for hunting larger game such as deer — from which the name derives — and widely employed in military and law-enforcement shotguns for close-range use.

Sizes

Buckshot sizes in the United States are designated by numbers and zeros: pellet diameters increase through #4, #3, #2, #1, 0 ("single-ought"), 00 ("double-ought", about 8.4 mm / .33 in), 000 ("triple-ought"), and larger. A typical 12-gauge 00 buckshot shell holds eight or nine pellets. Smaller shot used for birds and small game is called birdshot, and the intermediate category is sometimes termed "goose shot".

Ballistics and use

Because it fires multiple projectiles that spread from the muzzle, buckshot is effective at short ranges but loses pattern density and energy quickly with distance, generally limiting humane hunting use to a few tens of metres. Buffered loads, plated pellets, and specialised choke tubes are used to keep patterns tighter. The same spread characteristics make heavy buckshot a standard defensive and combat load, while reduced-recoil loads are issued for training and patrol use.

Regulation

Many hunting jurisdictions restrict buckshot to certain seasons or game species, or prohibit it in favour of slugs or rifles, because of the risk of wounding without killing at longer range.
