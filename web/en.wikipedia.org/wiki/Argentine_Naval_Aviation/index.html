Argentine Naval Aviation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Dassault Super Étendard of the Argentine Naval Aviation.

The Argentine Naval Aviation (Spanish: Comando de la Aviación Naval Argentina, COAN) is the naval aviation branch of the Argentine Navy and one of its four operational commands. Argentina, along with Brazil, is one of two South American countries to have operated two aircraft carriers. The naval aviation is headquartered at the Comandante Espora Naval Air Base near Bahía Blanca.

History

Argentine naval aviation traces its origins to 1916, when a naval aviation school was established. It grew substantially after World War II with the acquisition of aircraft carriers: HMS Warrior became ARA Independencia in 1958, and the Dutch carrier HNLMS Karel Doorman became ARA Veinticinco de Mayo in 1969. Carrier operations used aircraft such as the F4U Corsair, T-28 Trojan, S-2 Tracker, and A-4Q Skyhawk.

Falklands War

During the Falklands War (Guerra de las Malvinas) of 1982, Argentine naval aviation played a prominent role. Its most famous exploits were the air-launched Exocet attacks flown by Dassault Super Étendards of the 2da Escuadrilla Aeronaval de Caza y Ataque, which sank HMS Sheffield on 4 May 1982 and the container ship Atlantic Conveyor on 25 May. Naval A-4Q Skyhawks also attacked British warships, sinking the frigate HMS Ardent. Lockheed SP-2H Neptune patrol aircraft supported the Exocet strikes by locating targets before being retired mid-war due to airframe fatigue.

Post-war era

The retirement of ARA Veinticinco de Mayo in 1997 ended Argentine fixed-wing carrier operations; her Super Étendards and Trackers subsequently operated from shore bases and occasionally cross-decked with the Brazilian carrier São Paulo and visiting US Navy carriers. The force today operates maritime patrol, anti-submarine, transport, training, and helicopter aircraft, including P-3 Orion patrol planes, S-2T Turbo Trackers, King Air aircraft, and Sea King and Fennec helicopters. In 2017 Argentina purchased five modernized Super Étendard MOD aircraft from France.

Structure

The command is organized into naval air forces (Fuerzas Aeronavales) grouping squadrons by role: fighter-attack, anti-submarine and maritime patrol, helicopters, training, and naval air support, based principally at Comandante Espora, Almirante Zar (Trelew), and Ezeiza.
