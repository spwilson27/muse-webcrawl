Kohlberg's stages of moral development

Kohlberg's stages of moral development is a theory of moral reasoning proposed by psychologist Lawrence Kohlberg, building on the work of Jean Piaget. Kohlberg presented children and adults with moral dilemmas — most famously the Heinz dilemma — and classified the reasoning behind their judgements.

The stages

The theory proposes three levels, each with two stages. Pre-conventional: (1) obedience and punishment, (2) self-interest. Conventional: (3) interpersonal accord and conformity, (4) authority and social order. Post-conventional: (5) social contract, (6) universal ethical principles. People progress through the stages in order, though few reach stage 6.

Criticism

Carol Gilligan argued the theory is biased toward a justice orientation and undervalues an ethic of care; cross-cultural studies question its universality, and the link between moral reasoning and moral behaviour is debated.
