Stomatitis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stomatitis is inflammation of the mouth and lips. It refers to any inflammatory process affecting the mucous membranes of the mouth and lips, with or without oral ulceration. In its widest meaning, stomatitis can have a multitude of different causes and appearances. Common causes include infections, nutritional deficiencies, allergic reactions, radiotherapy, and many others.

Types and causes

  • Aphthous stomatitis (canker sores) — recurrent painful ulcers of unknown cause, the most common form.
  • Herpetic stomatitis — caused by herpes simplex virus, often a primary infection in children.
  • Angular stomatitis (angular cheilitis) — inflammation and cracking at the corners of the mouth.
  • Denture stomatitis — redness of the palate beneath a denture, often associated with Candida.
  • Candidal stomatitis (oral thrush) — fungal infection with white patches.
  • Nutritional — deficiencies of iron, folate, or B vitamins can cause mucosal inflammation.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms include painful sores or ulcers, redness and swelling of the mucosa, difficulty eating and swallowing, and sometimes fever, especially in children with primary herpes infection. The pain can be severe enough to interfere with eating, drinking, and swallowing, leading to dehydration and nutritional problems.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause: antifungals for candidiasis, antivirals in some cases of herpetic infection, correction of nutritional deficiencies, and removal of irritants. Symptomatic measures include bland diet, good oral hygiene, topical anaesthetics, and protective barrier preparations. Most mild cases resolve in one to two weeks.
