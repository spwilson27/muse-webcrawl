The Australian Aboriginal flag is an official flag of Australia that represents Aboriginal Australians. It was designed in 1971 by Harold Thomas, a Luritja man from Central Australia, and was first flown on National Aborigines Day in Adelaide on 12 July 1971.

Design and symbolism

The flag is divided horizontally into a black upper half and a red lower half, with a yellow circle in the centre. The black represents the Aboriginal people of Australia, the red represents the red earth and the spiritual relation to the land, and the yellow circle represents the sun, the giver of life.

History and status

The flag gained popularity during the Aboriginal land rights movement and flew at the Aboriginal Tent Embassy in Canberra from 1972. On 14 July 1995 it was officially recognized as a flag of Australia under the Flags Act 1953. In 2022 the Commonwealth government purchased the copyright from Thomas for $20 million, making the flag freely available for public use.
