Clearing (finance)

FieldFinance
RelatedSettlement, central clearing counterparty

Clearing in finance refers to the process by which financial transactions are prepared for settlement. It is the series of steps between the execution of a trade and its final settlement, during which the obligations of the parties are calculated, matched and confirmed.

Process

Once a trade is executed on an exchange, the clearing process verifies the details of the transaction, computes each party's obligations, and nets offsetting positions. Novation may replace the original contract with contracts against a central counterparty (CCP), which becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer.

Central clearing

Central clearing houses manage counterparty credit risk by requiring margin and maintaining default funds. Following the 2008 financial crisis, reforms such as the Dodd–Frank Act in the United States and EMIR in the European Union mandated central clearing for many standardised over-the-counter derivatives.
