Barium

Barium is a chemical element with the symbol Ba and atomic number 56. It is the fifth element in group 2 of the periodic table, a soft, silvery alkaline earth metal. Because of its high reactivity it is never found in nature as the free element; its most common ores are barite (barium sulfate) and witherite (barium carbonate).

History

Barium minerals attracted attention in the 17th century when stones from Bologna glowed after heating. Carl Wilhelm Scheele recognised baryta as a new earth in 1772, and Humphry Davy first isolated the metal by electrolysis in 1808, naming it from the Greek barys, "heavy".

Applications

Most barium is consumed as barite, ground for use as a weighting agent in drilling fluids for oil and gas wells. Barium sulfate's opacity to X-rays makes it a standard radiocontrast agent for imaging the digestive tract. Barium compounds give a green colour to fireworks, and barium titanate is a piezoelectric ceramic.

Safety

Soluble barium salts are toxic, interfering with potassium channels; insoluble barium sulfate is safe enough to be swallowed for medical imaging.
