Tangkic languages

The Tangkic languages are a small family of Australian Aboriginal languages spoken in the southern Gulf of Carpentaria region of Queensland and the Northern Territory, around the Wellesley Islands and the adjacent mainland. They form a branch of the proposed Pama–Nyungan family, though their internal and external relationships have been debated.

Languages

The family comprises four languages: Kayardild, Yukulta (also called Ganggalida), Lardil (and its ceremonial register Damin), and Yangkaal. Of these, Lardil and Yukulta retain small numbers of speakers, while Kayardild is among the most endangered languages of Australia, with only a handful of fluent speakers in recent decades.

Features

Tangkic languages are noted in linguistics for unusual features. Kayardild is famous for its stacked case system, in which nouns may carry multiple layers of case marking — a phenomenon central to theories of syntax and morphology. Lardil's Damin register, an invented ceremonial language taught to initiated men, is remarkable as the only known language with click consonants outside Africa.

Documentation

Fieldwork by linguists including Nicholas Evans, who documented Kayardild on Mornington Island, has made the family disproportionately influential in typological research despite its tiny speaker base, and community revival programmes on Mornington Island aim to transmit the languages to younger generations.
