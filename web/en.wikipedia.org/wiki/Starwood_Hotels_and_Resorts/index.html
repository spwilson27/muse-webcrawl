Starwood Hotels and Resorts Worldwide, Inc. was an American hotel and leisure company headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut. One of the world's largest hotel companies, it owned, operated, franchised, and managed hotels, resorts, spas, residences, and vacation ownership properties under eleven brands. In 2016 it was acquired by Marriott International, creating the world's largest hotel company.

History

Starwood originated as a real estate investment structure founded in 1930. Barry Sternlicht formed Starwood Capital in 1991 and built the hotel business through acquisitions, notably the purchases of Westin Hotels & Resorts in 1994 (in partnership) and of ITT Corporation's Sheraton chain in 1998 after a celebrated bidding war with Hilton. The company launched the design-led W Hotels brand in 1998 and later the Aloft and Element brands. Its loyalty scheme, Starwood Preferred Guest (SPG), launched in 1999, was widely praised for having no blackout dates on award stays.

Acquisition by Marriott

In November 2015 Marriott announced it would acquire Starwood for about $12.2 billion. A higher rival bid from a consortium led by China's Anbang Insurance Group briefly threatened the deal in 2016, but after Anbang withdrew, shareholders approved the Marriott transaction. The acquisition closed on September 23, 2016; the SPG programme was later merged into Marriott Bonvoy.

Data breach

In 2018 Marriott disclosed that a breach of Starwood's reservation systems, dating to 2014, had exposed data of up to about 500 million guests (later revised to around 383 million records), one of the largest data breaches then known.
