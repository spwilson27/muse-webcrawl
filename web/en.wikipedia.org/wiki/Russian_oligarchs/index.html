Russian oligarchs

Russian oligarchs are business magnates who accumulated great wealth during the era of Russian privatisation following the dissolution of the Soviet Union in the 1990s. Through controversial privatisation schemes, notably the "loans for shares" programme of 1995, a small number of businessmen acquired controlling stakes in formerly state-owned oil, gas, metals and media companies at a fraction of their later value.

The Yeltsin era

Under President Boris Yeltsin, figures such as Boris Berezovsky, Mikhail Khodorkovsky, Vladimir Gusinsky, Roman Abramovich and Mikhail Fridman amassed fortunes and exerted significant political influence, funding election campaigns and controlling national television networks.

The Putin era

After Vladimir Putin became president in 2000, the state reasserted control over the oligarchs: some were prosecuted (Khodorkovsky was imprisoned and his oil company Yukos dismantled), some emigrated, and others retained their wealth by aligning with the Kremlin. Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, many oligarchs were placed under Western sanctions, and their overseas assets, including yachts and property, were frozen or seized.
