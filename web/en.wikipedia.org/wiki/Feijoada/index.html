Feijoada

Feijoada is a stew of beans with beef and pork. The name derives from feijão, Portuguese for "beans". It is widely prepared in the Portuguese-speaking world and is regarded as the national dish of Brazil, where it is traditionally made with black beans and a variety of salted and smoked pork and beef products, including ears, tails, feet, bacon, sausage, and jerked beef.

Brazilian feijoada

In Brazil the stew is served with white rice, sautéed collard greens (couve), orange slices, farofa (toasted cassava flour), and hot sauce. It is traditionally eaten on Wednesdays and Saturdays. A popular — though debated — tradition attributes the dish to enslaved Africans who combined beans with discarded cuts of pork; food historians also point to European bean-and-meat stews such as the Portuguese originals and French cassoulet as antecedents.

Portuguese and other versions

In Portugal, feijoada is made regionally with white, red, or kidney beans; versions also exist in Angola, Mozambique, Macau, and Goa, reflecting Portuguese colonial connections.
