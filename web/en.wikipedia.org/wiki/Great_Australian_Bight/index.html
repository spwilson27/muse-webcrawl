Great Australian Bight

The Great Australian Bight is a large open bight, or bay, off the central and western portions of the southern coastline of mainland Australia. It stretches from Cape Pasley in Western Australia to Cape Carnot on the Eyre Peninsula in South Australia, a distance of about 1,160 km (720 mi). Behind much of the bight lies the arid Nullarbor Plain, and its shoreline includes some of the longest unbroken cliff lines in the world.

Nature and exploration

The bight was first charted by Europeans in 1627 by the Dutch navigator François Thijssen, and named by Matthew Flinders during his 1802 circumnavigation. Its waters are biologically rich, supporting southern right whales that calve near the Head of Bight, sea lion colonies, and commercial fisheries for southern bluefin tuna and abalone. The region has also been a focus of debate over offshore petroleum exploration, with proposals for deep-water drilling opposed by environmental groups and coastal communities.
