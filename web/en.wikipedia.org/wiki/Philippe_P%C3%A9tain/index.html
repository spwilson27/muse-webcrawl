Philippe Pétain

Henri Philippe Benoni Omer Pétain (24 April 1856 – 23 July 1951), commonly known as Philippe Pétain and Marshal Pétain, was a French general who reached the distinction of Marshal of France during the First World War, and later became the head of the collaborationist Vichy France regime from 1940 to 1944.

Born24 April 1856, Cauchy-à-la-Tour
Died23 July 1951, Île d'Yeu
AllegianceFrance
OfficeChief of State of Vichy France (1940–1944)

First World War

Pétain became a national hero for his defence of Verdun in 1916 and for restoring the French Army's morale after the mutinies of 1917, earning the nickname "the Lion of Verdun".

Vichy France

Recalled to government during the Fall of France in 1940, the 84-year-old Pétain signed an armistice with Nazi Germany and headed the authoritarian État français based at Vichy, which collaborated with the occupier.

Trial and death

After the war he was tried for treason, sentenced to death, and the sentence commuted to life imprisonment by Charles de Gaulle in view of his age and First World War service. He died in detention on the Île d'Yeu in 1951.
