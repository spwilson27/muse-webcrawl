Cam Ranh Air Base is a Vietnam People's Air Force (VPAF) base located on the Cam Ranh peninsula near the city of Cam Ranh in Khánh Hòa Province, south-central Vietnam. Built by the United States military during the Vietnam War, it was one of the largest American air bases in South Vietnam and later served as a major Soviet and Russian naval and air facility.

History

American construction and use

In 1965 the United States began building a major airfield and deep-water port on the sheltered Cam Ranh Bay, long considered one of the finest natural harbours in Southeast Asia. The firm RMK-BRJ constructed runways, taxiways, and port facilities at great speed; the base opened for operations in November 1965. The United States Air Force's 12th Tactical Fighter Wing flew F-4 Phantom II missions from the base, and it later hosted tactical airlift, reconnaissance, and special operations units. At its height, Cam Ranh supported thousands of personnel and functioned as a major in-country arrival and logistics hub.

Transfer and the fall of Saigon

Under Vietnamization, the base was turned over to the Republic of Vietnam Air Force in 1972. It was captured by North Vietnamese forces in April 1975, shortly before the fall of Saigon ended the Vietnam War.

Soviet and Russian presence

In 1979 Vietnam granted the Soviet Union access to Cam Ranh Bay, which became the USSR's largest naval base outside its own territory, hosting reconnaissance aircraft, bombers, and a signals intelligence station. Russia retained a reduced presence after 1991 but withdrew its remaining personnel in 2002.

Present day

Today the airfield is operated by the Vietnam People's Air Force; fighter regiments equipped with Su-27 and Su-30 aircraft have been based there. The adjacent civil airport, Cam Ranh International Airport, serves the tourist city of Nha Trang. Vietnam has declared that the base will not be leased to any single foreign power, though foreign warships may make port calls.
