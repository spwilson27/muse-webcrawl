Nonlinear programming

In mathematics, nonlinear programming (NLP) is the process of solving an optimisation problem in which the objective function or some of the constraints are nonlinear. A general formulation is to maximise or minimise a nonlinear function f(x) subject to equality and inequality constraints, also possibly nonlinear.

Methods

If the problem is convex — a convex objective over a convex feasible set — local optima are global, and methods such as gradient-based algorithms are effective. Sequential quadratic programming, interior-point methods and augmented Lagrangian methods are standard general-purpose approaches. Non-convex problems may have many local optima, making global optimisation much harder.

Optimality conditions

First-order necessary conditions for optimality are given by the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions, generalising the method of Lagrange multipliers; for unconstrained problems the gradient must vanish at a local optimum.
