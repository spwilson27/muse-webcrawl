The Elder Wand is a fictional magical object in J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter series. It is one of the three Deathly Hallows, alongside the Resurrection Stone and the Cloak of Invisibility. According to the in-universe legend told in The Tale of the Three Brothers, the wand was fashioned by Death himself for the eldest Peverell brother, Antioch, and is reputed to be the most powerful wand in existence, making its master unbeatable in duels.

Properties and lore

The wand is made of elder wood with a core of Thestral tail hair, and is fifteen inches long. Unlike ordinary wands, its allegiance transfers not only when it is taken by force but whenever its master is defeated or disarmed — even if the wand itself is not touched. Rowling has described this "bloody trail" through history: owners such as Emeric the Evil, Godelot, and the wandmaker Gregorovitch met violent ends, and the wand passed secretly to Gellert Grindelwald, who stole it from Gregorovitch's workshop. Albus Dumbledore won it from Grindelwald in their famous duel of 1945.

In the series

In Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows, Lord Voldemort seeks the wand believing it will overcome Harry Potter's twin-core wand and the protection of Harry's blood. He murders Severus Snape in the mistaken belief that Snape, who killed Dumbledore, is its master; in fact Draco Malfoy had disarmed Dumbledore first, and Harry later disarmed Draco, so the wand's allegiance belonged to Harry. This allows Harry to defeat Voldemort when the wand refuses to kill its true master. Harry uses it to repair his own broken wand, then returns it to Dumbledore's tomb, reasoning that if he dies undefeated its power will die with him. In the film adaptation, Harry snaps the wand and throws the pieces away.
