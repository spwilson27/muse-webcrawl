French house

French house is a style of house music developed by French artists in the mid-1990s. Often described as "French touch", the sound is characterised by heavy use of sampling from funk and disco records, filtered and compressed loops, and a groove-driven, sample-based production aesthetic. Alongside acts such as Daft Punk, the genre's best-known proponents include Stardust, Cassius, Bob Sinclar, Étienne de Crécy, Modjo and Air.

History

French house emerged from the Parisian club scene in the early to mid-1990s. Early releases appeared on labels such as Roulé, founded by Thomas Bangalter of Daft Punk, and Crydamoure, founded by his bandmate Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo. Producers drew heavily on samples from 1970s and 1980s funk and disco, applying filters, phasers and sidechain compression to create the genre's signature sweeping sound.

The genre achieved international commercial success in the late 1990s. Stardust's "Music Sounds Better with You" (1998), built around a looped sample of Chaka Khan's "Fate", became a worldwide hit. Daft Punk's albums Homework (1997) and Discovery (2001) brought the French touch sound to a global audience, the latter spawning hits such as "One More Time" and "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger". Modjo's "Lady (Hear Me Tonight)" (2000) and Bob Sinclar's productions further cemented the style's chart presence.

Characteristics

French house tracks typically run at 120–130 beats per minute and are built around short, looped samples — often a single bar of a funk or disco recording — subjected to filter sweeps that alternately muffle and reveal the sample. Sidechain compression, in which the kick drum momentarily ducks the volume of the bassline or sample, produces the genre's characteristic "pumping" feel. Vocals are frequently processed, pitched or vocoded.

Legacy

The French touch movement is credited with revitalising house music in Europe and influencing the later development of electro house, nu-disco and the "blog house" era of the 2000s. French electronic acts such as Justice carried elements of the style into a harder-edged direction. Daft Punk's continued success, culminating in Random Access Memories (2013), kept the genre in the public eye well into the twenty-first century.
