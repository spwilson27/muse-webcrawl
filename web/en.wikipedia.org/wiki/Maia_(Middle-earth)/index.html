Maia (Middle-earth)

The Maiar (singular Maia) are beings in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, lesser Ainur — angelic spirits created by Eru Ilúvatar — who descended into Arda to help the greater Valar shape the world. They are of the same order as the Valar but of lesser power and stature, and often serve as their attendants and helpers.

Known Maiar

Among the most prominent Maiar are the five Istari (Wizards) sent to Middle-earth in the Third Age: Saruman, Gandalf, Radagast, and the two Blue Wizards. Others include Sauron, originally a servant of Aulë who was corrupted by Morgoth and became the Dark Lord of the Second and Third Ages; the Balrogs, Maiar seduced into Morgoth's service and transformed into demons of fire; Melian, who wed the Elven-king Thingol and protected Doriath with her power; Ossë and Uinen, spirits of the sea; Eönwë, herald of Manwë; Ilmarë, handmaid of Varda; Arien, who guides the vessel of the Sun; and Tilion, who steers the Moon.

Nature

Like the Valar, the Maiar are immortal spirits who can take physical form at will, though embodiment for long periods can bind and limit them. Tolkien's conception draws on the hierarchy of angels in medieval theology, with the Maiar corresponding broadly to lesser angelic orders.
