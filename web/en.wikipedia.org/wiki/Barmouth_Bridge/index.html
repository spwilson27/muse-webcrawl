Barmouth Bridge (Welsh: Pont Abermaw) is a single-track largely wooden railway viaduct crossing the estuary of the River Mawddach near Barmouth, Gwynedd, in north-west Wales. At about 820 metres long, it is the longest timber viaduct in Wales and one of the oldest wooden railway bridges in Britain still in regular use.

History

The bridge was built for the Aberystwith and Welsh Coast Railway and opened in 1867, carrying what is now the Cambrian Coast Line between Barmouth and Morfa Mawddach. The structure originally included a drawbridge section to allow shipping to pass; this was replaced in 1901 by a swing bridge span, which has since been fixed in place.

Structure

The viaduct consists of timber trestle spans resting on piles driven into the estuary bed, with a single steel swing span. The wooden structure has required repeated renewal of its timbers throughout its life.

Marine worm damage and restoration

In the 1980s the bridge was found to be under attack by shipworm, a marine boring mollusc, threatening closure of the line; British Rail repaired the piles. A major multi-million-pound restoration by Network Rail in the 2020s renewed spans and timbers, securing the bridge's future and prompting its listing recognition as a landmark of Welsh transport heritage.

Use

Besides trains, the bridge carries a popular footpath and cycle route across the estuary, forming part of the Wales Coast Path and the Mawddach Trail between Barmouth and Dolgellau.
