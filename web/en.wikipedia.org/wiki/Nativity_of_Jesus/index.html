Nativity of Jesus

The Nativity has been a central subject of Christian art since late antiquity.

The Nativity of Jesus, or the birth of Jesus Christ, is described in the biblical gospels of Matthew and Luke. According to the accounts, Jesus was born to Mary, a virgin betrothed to Joseph, in the town of Bethlehem in Judaea during the reign of Herod the Great. The Nativity is the doctrinal basis of the Christian feast of Christmas and a foundational event of Christian theology, expressing the belief in the Incarnation — that God became human in Jesus.

The gospel accounts

The Gospel of Luke describes the Annunciation to Mary by the angel Gabriel, a Roman census that requires Joseph and Mary to travel from Nazareth to Bethlehem, the birth in humble lodgings where Jesus is laid in a manger, and the proclamation of the birth to shepherds by angels. The Gospel of Matthew instead emphasises the visit of the Magi (wise men) from the East, guided by the Star of Bethlehem, and Herod's massacre of the innocents, from which the family flees into Egypt. The two narratives agree on the virgin birth, the Bethlehem birthplace, and the Davidic lineage of Jesus, but differ in many details, which scholars have long debated and attempted to harmonise.

Date of birth

The Gospels do not specify a calendar date. The celebration of the birth on 25 December is attested in Rome by the fourth century; the date may relate to the Roman festival of Sol Invictus or to a calculation placing the Annunciation on 25 March. Historical estimates of the year of Jesus's birth generally fall between about 6 and 4 BC, based on the death of Herod the Great, creating a paradox in the traditional BC/AD dating devised by Dionysius Exiguus.

In art and culture

The Nativity is one of the most depicted subjects in Western art, from early Christian sarcophagi through the works of Giotto, Botticelli, and Caravaggio. St Francis of Assisi is credited with creating the first nativity scene (crèche) at Greccio in 1223, a tradition carried on in Provençal santons, Neapolitan presepi, and Latin American nacimientos. Nativity plays and carols remain central to Christmas celebrations worldwide, and the story is also recounted in the Quran, which describes the birth of Isa (Jesus) to Maryam (Mary).
