Margaret Benson

Margaret Benson (16 June 1865 – 13 May 1916) was an English author and Egyptologist, the first woman to obtain an official concession to excavate in Egypt. She was the daughter of Edward White Benson, Archbishop of Canterbury, and sister of the novelists E. F. Benson and A. C. Benson and of the priest and writer Robert Hugh Benson.

Egyptology

Suffering from ill health, Benson spent winters in Egypt for the climate. In 1895 she received permission from the Egyptian Antiquities Service to excavate at the temple of the goddess Mut at Karnak, an unprecedented concession for a woman. Working with her companion Janet Gourlay over three seasons (1895–1897), she cleared the precinct and recovered numerous statues, including fine Sekhmet figures and a notable block statue of Amenemone. Her account, The Temple of Mut in Asher (1899), was written with Gourlay.

Writing and later life

Benson also wrote religious and philosophical works, including The Venture of Rational Faith (1908). Her later years were marked by severe mental illness, and she died in 1916 at the Priory, Roehampton.
