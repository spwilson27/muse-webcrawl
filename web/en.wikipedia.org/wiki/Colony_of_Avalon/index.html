Colony of Avalon

The Colony of Avalon was an early English settlement in Newfoundland, founded in 1621 at Ferryland on the Avalon Peninsula by George Calvert, later the first Lord Baltimore. It was among the earliest permanent European settlements in North America and is today an important archaeological site.

Foundation

Calvert, a former Secretary of State, obtained a royal charter in 1623 for the Province of Avalon, named for the legendary island of Avalon associated with King Arthur and Glastonbury, where Calvert hoped Christianity had first taken root in Britain. His first settlers, under Captain Edward Wynne, arrived in August 1621 and began building a settlement that included a mansion house, forge, quarry and defensive works around the sheltered harbour.

Calvert's residence and departure

Calvert himself arrived in 1627 with his family and further settlers, including Catholic priests — the colony was notable for permitting Catholic worship alongside Anglican in its early years. Discouraged by the harsh winters and disagreements with his officials, Calvert left in 1629 and sought a warmer charter, leading to the founding of the Maryland colony. Avalon was subsequently managed by the Kirke family, and Ferryland remained an important fishing station.

Archaeology

Excavations begun in the 1990s have uncovered the waterfront, cobbled street, houses, a smithy and thousands of artefacts, making Ferryland one of the best-preserved early English colonial sites in Canada. The Colony of Avalon Foundation operates a visitor centre and archaeology programme at the site.
