Rosatom, formally the State Atomic Energy Corporation Rosatom, is a Russian state corporation headquartered in Moscow that unites the country's civil and much of its military nuclear complex. Established by law in 2007 from the Federal Agency on Atomic Energy (itself the successor of the Soviet Ministry of Medium Machine Building), it controls more than 350 enterprises and organisations, including the nuclear power plant operator Rosenergoatom, the fuel company TVEL, the engineering and export arm Atomstroyexport, and the world's only fleet of nuclear-powered icebreakers, operated by Atomflot.

Activities

Rosatom manages the entire nuclear fuel cycle in Russia — uranium mining and enrichment (holding a large share of world enrichment capacity), fuel fabrication, reactor construction and operation, and waste management and decommissioning. It operates Russia's nuclear power stations, which supply roughly a fifth of the country's electricity, and is one of the world's largest builders of nuclear power plants abroad, with projects or contracts in countries including Turkey (Akkuyu), Bangladesh (Rooppur), Egypt (El Dabaa), India (Kudankulam), China and Hungary (Paks II). Beyond energy, the corporation pursues nuclear medicine, composite materials, wind power, hydrogen and digital products, and it builds floating nuclear power plants such as the Akademik Lomonosov.

International position and sanctions

Rosatom's integrated offer — build-own-operate financing, fuel supply and take-back of spent fuel — has made it the dominant exporter of nuclear reactors since the 2010s. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Rosatom itself largely avoided comprehensive Western sanctions because of global dependence on its fuel services, although subsidiaries and individuals were designated by some jurisdictions, several European projects were cancelled (such as Finland's Hanhikivi plant), and the corporation's personnel were involved in the occupation of Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant, drawing IAEA criticism.
