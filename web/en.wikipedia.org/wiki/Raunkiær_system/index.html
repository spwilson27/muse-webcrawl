Raunkiær system

Raunkiær life forms classified by the position of perennating buds

The Raunkiær system is a system for categorizing plants using life-form categories, devised by Danish botanist Christen C. Raunkiær (1860–1938). It was first proposed in a talk to the Danish Botanical Society in 1904 and elaborated in works of 1905 and 1907. The system classifies plants according to the position of their perennating (survival) buds during unfavourable seasons, such as winter cold or dry periods.

Categories

Raunkiær's original system recognized five principal life forms. Phanerophytes are large shrubs and trees whose perennating buds are borne on aerial shoots well above the ground (more than about 25 cm), exposed to the full force of the unfavourable season. Chamaephytes are low shrubs and subshrubs whose buds lie close to the ground, no more than about 25 cm above the soil surface, where they may be protected by litter or snow. Hemicryptophytes have their buds at soil level, often in a basal rosette. Cryptophytes or geophytes have buds below the soil surface, on bulbs, corms, rhizomes or tubers. Therophytes are annual plants that survive the unfavourable season only as seeds.

Subdivisions and refinements

The principal categories were subsequently subdivided. Phanerophytes were split by height into mega-, meso-, micro- and nanophanerophytes, and by leaf behaviour into evergreen and deciduous forms. Cryptophytes were divided into geophytes proper, helophytes (marsh plants with buds in mud below water) and hydrophytes (aquatic plants with buds below water). Later workers, including Ellenberg and others, added categories such as stem succulents, epiphytes and aerophytes.

Biological spectra

Raunkiær proposed that the proportion of species in each life-form class in a flora — its biological spectrum — reflects the climate of the region. He established a "normal spectrum" based on a random sample of the world flora, against which regional spectra could be compared. Tropical rain forests show a high percentage of phanerophytes, deserts a high percentage of therophytes, and temperate regions a predominance of hemicryptophytes. The approach remains a standard tool in biogeography and vegetation science.
