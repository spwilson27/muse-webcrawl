Indo-Scythians

The Indo-Scythians were a group of nomadic peoples of Scythian origin — the Sakas — who migrated from Central Asia into South Asia from the middle of the 2nd century BCE to the 4th century CE. Their rule in northern and western India followed that of the Indo-Greeks and overlapped with the later Kushan expansion.

Coinage is a principal source for Indo-Scythian history.

History

Driven southward by movements of the Yuezhi, Saka tribes crossed the Hindu Kush into the Indus valley. The first Indo-Scythian king was Maues (c. 98/85–60/57 BCE), who established power in Gandhara and the Punjab. His successors, including Vonones, Azes I and Azes II, extended Saka power as Indo-Greek rule faded. The era of Azes (beginning 58/57 BCE) is often identified with the Vikrama era still used in Indian calendars.

Western Satraps

In western India, Indo-Scythian dynasties known as the Western Satraps ruled from the 1st to the early 5th century CE. The Kardamaka line of Rudradaman I (c. 130–150 CE) is celebrated in the Junagadh rock inscription, the first long inscription in classical Sanskrit. The Western Satraps were finally extinguished by the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II around 400 CE.

Coinage and culture

Indo-Scythian coins typically bear Greek legends on the obverse and Kharosthi legends on the reverse, depicting the king on horseback and a mixture of Greek, Hindu and Zoroastrian deities — a vivid record of the cultural synthesis of the period.
