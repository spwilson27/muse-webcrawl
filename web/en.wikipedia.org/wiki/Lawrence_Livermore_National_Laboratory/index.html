Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) is a federally funded research and development center in Livermore, California, sponsored by the U.S. Department of Energy. Founded in 1952 at the urging of Ernest Lawrence and Edward Teller as a second nuclear weapons design laboratory alongside Los Alamos, it remains one of the two U.S. laboratories where nuclear weapons are designed, and also pursues work in supercomputing, fusion energy, and national-security science.

History

The laboratory opened in 1952 on a former naval air station, initially as a branch of the Berkeley Radiation Laboratory. Its early thermonuclear work included several failed test devices before successful designs followed; over the Cold War it developed warheads for systems including Polaris and multiple independently targetable reentry vehicles. In 1971 it was renamed for Ernest Lawrence, and since 2007 it has been operated by a private consortium led by Bechtel and the University of California.

Major programs and facilities

LLNL hosts the National Ignition Facility (NIF), the world's largest laser, which in December 2022 achieved fusion ignition — producing more fusion energy than the laser energy delivered to the target — a feat repeated in subsequent experiments. The laboratory is also a leader in high-performance computing: its machines (including Sequoia and the exascale system El Capitan, delivered in the mid-2020s) have repeatedly topped the TOP500 list and support stockpile stewardship — certifying the nuclear arsenal without explosive testing. Other work spans pathogen detection, energy storage, additive manufacturing, and astrophysics.
