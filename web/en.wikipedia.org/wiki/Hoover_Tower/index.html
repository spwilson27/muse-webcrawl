Hoover Tower

Hoover Tower is a 285-foot (87 m) bell tower at Stanford University in California, and one of the campus's most recognizable landmarks. Completed in 1941 for Stanford's 50th anniversary, it houses the offices, library, and archives of the Hoover Institution, the public policy think tank founded by Herbert Hoover—Stanford alumnus and 31st President of the United States—beginning with his 1919 donation to document the causes and consequences of the First World War.

Design and construction

Architect Arthur Brown Jr. based the tower's silhouette on the cathedral tower of Salamanca, Spain, rendered in the stripped, moderne-influenced style of his other Bay Area work. The first nine floors hold book stacks for the Hoover Library; higher floors contain offices and seminar rooms, and the 14th floor has an observation deck with views across the Stanford campus, the San Francisco Bay, and the Santa Cruz Mountains.

Carillon

The tower's carillon of 48 bells, cast by the Marcel Michiels foundry in Tournai, Belgium, was a gift of the Belgian-American Educational Foundation in gratitude for Hoover's famine-relief work in Belgium during and after the First World War. The bells were exhibited at the 1939 New York World's Fair before installation. The largest bell bears an inscription of thanks to Hoover.

Symbolism and events

Strikingly visible above the palm-lined approach to the university, Hoover Tower is a symbol both of Stanford and of the Institution's mission. In 1960 the tower's observation floor figured in a Cold War footnote when Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev, on his American tour, was prevented from going up by student demonstrators. The tower underwent seismic retrofitting and the carillon was restored in the 2000s, and the bells are still played for university occasions.
