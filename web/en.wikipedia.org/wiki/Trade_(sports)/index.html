Trade (sports)

In professional sports, a trade is a transaction in which teams exchange the contractual rights to players, draft picks, or (in some leagues) cash considerations. Trades are a defining feature of North American leagues such as the NBA, NFL, NHL and Major League Baseball, where players are under contract to a single club and cannot simply be bought for a transfer fee as in association football.

Mechanics

Because most North American leagues operate salary caps or luxury-tax systems, trades usually must satisfy matching rules: for example, NBA rules require the salaries exchanged to roughly balance for teams over the cap. Deals may involve three or more teams, and may include future draft picks, pick swaps, or "cash considerations". Most leagues impose a trade deadline partway through the season, after which no trades may be made until the off-season.

Strategy

Contending teams act as "buyers", acquiring established players for a championship push, while struggling teams act as "sellers", exchanging veterans for young players and draft picks to rebuild. Players may hold no-trade clauses allowing them to veto a deal, and some request trades publicly to force a move.

Notable trades

Famous trades include the 1988 deal that sent Wayne Gretzky from the Edmonton Oilers to the Los Angeles Kings, the Boston Red Sox's 1920 sale-like trade of Babe Ruth to the New York Yankees, and the 1989 Herschel Walker trade between the Dallas Cowboys and Minnesota Vikings, whose haul of draft picks helped build a Cowboys dynasty.
