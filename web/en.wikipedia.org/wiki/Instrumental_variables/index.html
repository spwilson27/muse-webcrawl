Instrumental variables estimation

Instrumental variables
A statistical method for estimating causal relations with endogenous regressors.

Instrumental variables (IV) estimation is a method in statistics and econometrics used to estimate causal relationships when controlled experiments are not available and ordinary regression is biased because an explanatory variable is correlated with the error term — a problem called endogeneity, arising from omitted variables, simultaneity, or measurement error. The method uses an instrument: a variable that (i) is correlated with the endogenous regressor (relevance), (ii) affects the outcome only through that regressor (exclusion restriction), and (iii) is not itself correlated with the error term (exogeneity).

Two-stage least squares

The standard estimator is two-stage least squares (2SLS). In the first stage, the endogenous regressor is regressed on the instrument(s) and other exogenous variables; in the second stage, the outcome is regressed on the fitted values from the first stage. With exactly one instrument and one endogenous variable, the IV estimator reduces to the ratio of the reduced-form effect of the instrument on the outcome to its first-stage effect on the regressor (the Wald estimator). Overidentifying restrictions with multiple instruments can be partially tested (Sargan–Hansen J test), while weak instruments — those only slightly correlated with the regressor — produce biased estimates and unreliable inference, a problem highlighted by Bound, Jaeger, and Baker (1995).

Interpretation

Under monotonicity assumptions, IV estimates a local average treatment effect (LATE): the effect for "compliers" whose treatment status is moved by the instrument (Angrist, Imbens & Rubin, 1996). Famous applications include using quarter of birth as an instrument for schooling (Angrist & Krueger, 1991) and draft-lottery numbers as an instrument for military service. The method was foundational to the 2021 Nobel Memorial Prize awarded to David Card, Joshua Angrist, and Guido Imbens. Variants of IV are also used in genetics (Mendelian randomization) and in simultaneous-equations models in macroeconomics.
