Photonics

Photonics is a branch of optics that involves the application of generation, detection, and manipulation of light in the form of photons through emission, transmission, modulation, signal processing, switching, amplification, and sensing. Photonics underlies technologies such as fibre-optic communications, lasers, imaging systems and solar cells, and is closely related to quantum electronics and optoelectronics.

Optical fibres and photonic integrated circuits in a laboratory.

History

The field coalesced in the 1960s with the invention of the laser and the laser diode, and expanded with the development of low-loss optical fibres in the 1970s, which made long-distance optical communication practical. The word "photonics" emphasises the photon as the counterpart of the electron in electronics.

Applications

  • Telecommunications: fibre-optic networks carry most long-distance and internet backbone traffic, using wavelength-division multiplexing to combine many channels in one fibre.
  • Information processing: photonic integrated circuits combine lasers, modulators and detectors on a chip, increasingly used for data-centre interconnects.
  • Medicine: endoscopy, laser surgery, optical coherence tomography and photodynamic therapy.
  • Energy: photovoltaic cells and light-emitting diodes for efficient lighting and displays.
  • Sensing and metrology: lidar, fibre-optic sensors, and spectroscopy.
  • Defence and manufacturing: laser rangefinders, guidance, cutting and welding.

Research areas

Active research includes silicon photonics, plasmonics, photonic crystals, metamaterials, quantum photonics (using single photons for computing and communication), and biophotonics. Photonics is often presented as one of the key enabling technologies of the twenty-first century.
