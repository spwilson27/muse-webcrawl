Safiyya bint Huyayy (Arabic: صفية بنت حيي, c. 610 – c. 670 CE) was a Jewish woman of the Banu Nadir tribe of Medina who became a wife of the Islamic prophet Muhammad, and is therefore honoured by Muslims as one of the "Mothers of the Believers" (Umm al-Mu'minin). Her life is known from the early Islamic biographical tradition (the sīra literature and hadith).

Early life

Safiyya was the daughter of Huyayy ibn Akhtab, chief of the Banu Nadir, one of the principal Jewish tribes of Medina (Yathrib), and Barra bint Samawal of the Banu Qurayza. Her family traced descent from Aaron (Harun). She was first married to the poet Sallam ibn Mishkam, and afterwards to Kenana ibn al-Rabi, a leading figure and treasurer of the Nadir after the tribe's expulsion from Medina to Khaybar.

Khaybar and marriage to Muhammad

In 628 CE (7 AH), Muhammad's forces conquered the oasis of Khaybar. Kenana was killed, and Safiyya was taken captive and allotted to the companion Dihya al-Kalbi, but Muhammad chose her for himself, freed her, and proposed marriage, with her manumission as her dower (mahr). According to the traditional accounts, she accepted Islam and the marriage took place during the return journey at Sadd al-Rawha. A well-known hadith reports that Muhammad placed a cloak over her as a sign of marriage, and the wedding feast (walima) consisted of dates, cheese and barley meal. Muslim sources present the marriage as also serving to reconcile the defeated Jewish tribe.

Later life

Safiyya lived in Medina among Muhammad's widows after his death in 632. Traditions depict her as learned and generous; she maintained ties with her Jewish relatives and left property to a Jewish nephew, which some reports found noteworthy. During the siege of the caliph Uthman's house (656), she is reported to have attempted to aid and defend him. She died in Medina around 50 AH (c. 670 CE), during the reign of Mu'awiya, and was buried in the Baqi cemetery.

In tradition

Safiyya is the narrator of a small number of hadith. A famous report describes a dream she had before Khaybar — that the moon fell into her lap — interpreted as foreshadowing her marriage. Another relates Muhammad's defending her when some of his other wives taunted her Jewish origins, instructing her to reply that her father was Aaron, her uncle Moses, and her husband Muhammad.
