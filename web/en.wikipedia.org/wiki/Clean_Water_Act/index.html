The Clean Water Act (CWA) is the principal law governing pollution of the waters of the United States. Passed in 1972 over President Nixon's veto, it made it unlawful to discharge any pollutant from a point source into navigable waters without a permit.

Structure

  • NPDES permits: the National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System regulates industrial and municipal point sources.
  • Technology-based standards: effluent limitations tightened over time toward the goal of eliminating discharges (the "zero discharge" goal).
  • Section 404: the Corps of Engineers permits dredging and filling of wetlands — a frequent source of litigation.
  • State programs: states set water quality standards for designated uses such as fishing and swimming.

Scope disputes

The phrase "waters of the United States" has generated decades of litigation, culminating in Sackett v. EPA (2023), which narrowed federal jurisdiction to relatively permanent waters and wetlands with a continuous surface connection.
