Earthquake preparedness

Earthquake preparedness is a set of measures taken at the individual, organisational and societal level to minimise the effects of an earthquake. Preparedness measures can range from securing heavy objects and structural modifications in homes and workplaces, to storing supplies and having insurance, to broader planning and infrastructure resilience at the societal level.

Individual measures

Common guidance includes assembling an emergency kit with water, food, first aid supplies, a flashlight and a radio; making a family communication plan; securing tall furniture and water heaters; and knowing how to shut off utilities. During shaking, the widely taught response in many countries is "drop, cover, and hold on": drop to the ground, take cover under sturdy furniture, and hold on until the shaking stops. Annual drills such as the Great ShakeOut practice this response with millions of participants.

Societal measures

Governments reduce risk through seismic building codes, retrofitting of vulnerable structures, land-use planning, and earthquake early warning systems that can provide seconds of notice before strong shaking arrives. Public education campaigns in earthquake-prone regions — including Japan, California, and New Zealand — emphasise preparedness because most casualties result from building collapse and falling objects rather than ground rupture itself.
