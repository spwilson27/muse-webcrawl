The Los Angeles Auto Show, commonly known as the LA Auto Show, is an annual auto show held at the Los Angeles Convention Center in early December in downtown Los Angeles, California. The first Los Angeles auto show was held in 1907 at Morley's Skating Rink, making it one of the oldest exhibitions of its kind in the United States.

The show occupies more than 870,000 square feet (81,000 m2) of exhibit space and features hundreds of vehicles from manufacturers around the world, including concept cars, production debuts, and displays of electric and alternative-fuel vehicles. It is sanctioned by the Organisation Internationale des Constructeurs d'Automobiles (OICA).

History

The inaugural show in 1907 displayed 99 vehicles. Early editions were held at various venues around the city, including the Shrine Auditorium. The show moved to the newly opened Los Angeles Convention Center in 1971 and has been held there since. Through the 2000s the show was traditionally held in early January, but in 2006 it shifted to late November and early December, partly to avoid overlap with the North American International Auto Show in Detroit.

Features

In addition to manufacturer exhibits, the LA Auto Show includes test drives, an aftermarket hall, and the AutoMobility LA conference, a press and trade event focused on automotive technology and mobility held in the days before the public opening. The show also hosts design challenges and awards for concept vehicles.
