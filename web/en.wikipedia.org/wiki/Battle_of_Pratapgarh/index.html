Battle of Pratapgarh

The Battle of Pratapgarh was fought on 10 November 1659 at Pratapgad Fort in the present-day Satara district of Maharashtra, India, between the Maratha forces of Shivaji and the army of the Bijapur Sultanate commanded by Afzal Khan. It is famous for the meeting at which Shivaji killed Afzal Khan, and for the decisive Maratha victory that followed. The battle was a landmark in Shivaji's rise and in the establishment of the Maratha state.

Background

The Bijapur court sent Afzal Khan, one of its most experienced generals, with a large army to crush Shivaji's growing power in the Western Ghats. Shivaji, outmatched in open battle, withdrew to the forested terrain around Pratapgad and drew the Bijapuri army into difficult hill country.

The meeting and battle

A parley was arranged at the foot of the fort. According to Maratha accounts, both men came armed for treachery: Afzal Khan attacked with a concealed dagger, and Shivaji — wearing armour under his clothes — killed him with concealed weapons, traditionally the wagh nakh ("tiger claws") and a bichuwa dagger. On a prearranged signal, Maratha troops ambushed the leaderless Bijapuri army in the forest and routed it, capturing artillery, horses, and treasure.

Aftermath

The victory enormously enhanced Shivaji's prestige and resources; Maratha forces followed it up by overrunning Bijapuri territory toward Kolhapur. The episode became central to Shivaji's legend in Maharashtra.
