Cargill

Cargill, Incorporated is an American multinational food and agriculture corporation headquartered in Minnetonka, Minnesota. Founded in 1865 by William Wallace Cargill as a grain-storage company, it has grown into the largest privately held corporation in the United States by revenue, with operations in some 70 countries spanning grain trading and processing, meat and poultry, animal nutrition, food ingredients, and financial and industrial services. It is one of the "ABCD" quartet of companies that dominate world trade in agricultural commodities.

History

W. W. Cargill bought a grain flat house in Conover, Iowa, at the end of the American Civil War and expanded along the new railroads into Minnesota and the Dakotas, building country elevators and terminal warehouses. The company survived the depression of the 1890s, expanded into flour milling, shipping (on the Great Lakes) and international trading, and after the Second World War grew into a global trader under leaders such as John MacMillan Jr. and Whitney MacMillan. Diversification followed into meatpacking (Excel), poultry, salt, steel, petroleum trading, financial services and animal feed (Nutrena, and later Provimi and Purina's international arm). In 1999 Cargill sold its seed business's international operations to Monsanto while retaining North American seed interests for a time; it remains a giant in cocoa, chocolate, starches, sweeteners, edible oils and malt.

Business

Cargill buys, stores, transports and processes crops worldwide — corn, soybeans, wheat, cocoa, palm oil, sugar and cotton — and slaughters cattle, pigs and poultry on a large scale; it is one of the largest U.S. beef processors and ground-beef suppliers, a major exporter from North and South America, and a significant shipowner/charterer through its ocean transportation business. Its risk-management and trading arms serve food companies and investors.

Criticism

As the largest private company in America, Cargill has attracted scrutiny over deforestation linked to soy and cattle supply chains in Brazil, palm-oil sourcing, labour practices in meat plants, water pollution, and its role in food-price spikes. The company has responded with sustainability commitments, including on deforestation-free supply chains, and publishes annual sustainability reports. Members of the owning families are among the wealthiest people in the United States.
