Gideon Algernon Mantell (1790–1852) was an English obstetrician, geologist and palaeontologist who was among the first to recognise that dinosaurs had existed. His attempts to reconstruct the structure and habits of Iguanodon made him a pioneer of dinosaur science, though his career was marked by a bitter rivalry with Richard Owen.

Iguanodon

A country doctor in Lewes, Sussex, Mantell collected fossils from the Cretaceous rocks of the Weald. In 1822 his wife Mary Ann found large fossil teeth resembling those of an iguana but far larger; Mantell described the animal as Iguanodon in 1825 — the second dinosaur ever named, after Megalosaurus. He went on to describe Hylaeosaurus (1833), one of the three genera on which Owen later based "Dinosauria," and correctly argued that these reptiles had dominated a whole geological era, the "Age of Reptiles."

Rivalry and later life

Mantell's relationship with Owen deteriorated into open hostility; Owen systematically diminished Mantell's contributions, even removing his name from credit for discoveries. A carriage accident in 1841 left Mantell with a crippling spinal injury and chronic pain. Financially strained, he sold his fossil collection to the British Museum. He died in 1852, aged 62, of an opium overdose taken for his pain; his deformed spine was itself displayed as a specimen at the Royal College of Surgeons.
