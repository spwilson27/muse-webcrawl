Nevi'im (Hebrew: נְבִיאִים, 'Prophets') is the second major division of the Hebrew Bible, or Tanakh, situated between the Torah (Instruction) and the Ketuvim (Writings). It contains the narrative of Israel's history under prophetic interpretation and the oracles of the classical prophets, and its books are traditionally divided into the Former Prophets and the Latter Prophets.

Contents

The Former Prophets comprise the books of Joshua, Judges, Samuel (counted as one book in Jewish tradition) and Kings, narrating the conquest of Canaan, the period of the judges, and the rise and fall of the Israelite monarchy down to the Babylonian exile.

The Latter Prophets consist of Isaiah, Jeremiah and Ezekiel, followed by the Book of the Twelve — Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah and Malachi — counted as a single book, giving the traditional total of eight books of Nevi'im.

Liturgical use

Selections from the Nevi'im, called haftarot, are read in synagogue after the Torah reading on Sabbaths, festivals and fast days, usually chosen for thematic links to the Torah portion or the occasion. In Jewish tradition the prophetic books are understood to interpret and apply the covenant of the Torah to Israel's history, rather than to introduce a new law.
