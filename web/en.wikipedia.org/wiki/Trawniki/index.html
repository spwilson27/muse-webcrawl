Trawniki

Trawniki is a village in south-eastern Poland, in Lublin Voivodeship, situated on the Wieprz River about 40 kilometres south-east of Lublin. During the Second World War it was the site of the SS training camp known as SS-Ausbildungs-Lager Trawniki, and of an associated forced-labour camp for Jews.

The SS training camp

The camp was established in 1941 in the grounds of a former sugar factory. Its purpose was to train auxiliary guards, known to historians as "Trawniki men" (Trawnikimänner), recruited largely from among Soviet prisoners of war — ethnic Germans, Ukrainians, Russians, Latvians, Lithuanians, Estonians and others — who volunteered or were coerced into SS service. Under the command of Karl Streibel, some 5,000 men passed through the camp.

Role in the Holocaust

Trawniki-trained guards served at the Operation Reinhard extermination camps of Bełżec, Sobibór and Treblinka, at the Majdanek and Auschwitz concentration camps, and in the liquidation of ghettos including Warsaw, Białystok and Lublin. They also took part in mass shootings, notably the Aktion Erntefest of 3 November 1943. On that same day, the SS murdered the remaining Jewish prisoners of the Trawniki labour camp — about 6,000 people — in pits near the camp. The training camp was dissolved in the summer of 1944 as the Red Army advanced.

Post-war trials

After the war a number of Trawniki men were tried in the Soviet Union and Poland. The most famous case in the West was that of John Demjanjuk, identified by prosecutors as a Trawniki-trained guard; convicted in Germany in 2011 of serving at Sobibór, his case became a landmark in the prosecution of camp personnel without proof of individual murders.
