Portsmouth Historic Dockyard is an area of HM Naval Base Portsmouth in Hampshire, England, open to the public and containing several historic ships, museums, and attractions. It is managed as a heritage destination by the National Museum of the Royal Navy working with charitable trusts for individual ships. The dockyard sits within an operational Royal Navy base, and entry to the historic area is through Victory Gate.

Attractions

HMS Victory

HMS Victory, Lord Nelson's flagship at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805, is preserved in No. 2 dry dock, where she has been since 1922. She remains a commissioned warship — the flagship of the First Sea Lord — and the world's oldest naval vessel still in commission. Visitors tour her gun decks, great cabin, and the spot where Nelson fell, marked by a plaque.

The Mary Rose

The Mary Rose Museum houses the surviving hull of Henry VIII's warship Mary Rose, which sank in the Solent in 1545 and was raised in 1982 in one of the most complex maritime archaeology projects ever undertaken. After decades of spraying with water and polyethylene glycol, the ship was dried and the purpose-built museum — fully reopened in 2016 with views into the hull across nine galleries — displays thousands of recovered artefacts, from longbows to the skeleton of the ship's dog.

HMS Warrior

HMS Warrior, launched in 1860, was the Royal Navy's first iron-hulled, armour-plated warship and, when built, the largest and fastest warship in the world. Restored in Hartlepool and returned to Portsmouth in 1987, she is moored as a museum ship.

Other attractions

  • National Museum of the Royal Navy — galleries on the history of the Royal Navy, including the Trafalgar and Jutland exhibitions, in historic storehouses.
  • HMS M33 — a First World War monitor, veteran of Gallipoli, opened to the public in 2015.
  • Harbour tours — boat trips around Portsmouth Harbour viewing the modern fleet.
  • Boathouse 4 — a working boatbuilding and restoration centre.
  • Portsmouth Naval Base Property Trust historic buildings and the Georgian dockyard architecture, including the ropery and storehouses.
  • Nearby, across the harbour in Gosport, are the Royal Navy Submarine Museum (centred on HMS Alliance) and Explosion! Museum of Naval Firepower, marketed jointly with the dockyard.

History

Portsmouth's royal dockyard dates to the 1490s, when Henry VII built one of the world's first dry docks there. The surviving historic core is largely Georgian, built up during the eighteenth-century wars with France. The heritage area was progressively opened to visitors from the later twentieth century as the ships were preserved and the museums consolidated.
