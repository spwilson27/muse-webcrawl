Sorbs
Upper Sorbian: Serbja · Lower Sorbian: Serby
Region: Lusatia, eastern Germany (Saxony and Brandenburg)
Population: ~60,000 (ethnic); ~20,000–30,000 speakers
Languages: Upper Sorbian, Lower Sorbian, German
Status: Recognised national minority in Germany

The Sorbs are a West Slavic ethnic group native to Lusatia, a region in eastern Germany spanning parts of Saxony and Brandenburg. They are one of Germany's four recognised national minorities and speak the Sorbian languages, the closest living relatives of Polish and Czech.

History

Slavic tribes settled the area between the Elbe and Oder in the 6th century during the Migration Period. Despite centuries of Germanisation pressure — including bans on the language under Nazi rule — Sorbian language and culture survived in Catholic Upper Lusatia and the Spreewald. After 1945, the lignite mining of Lusatia consumed many Sorbian villages, while the GDR formally promoted Sorbian institutions.

Culture and institutions

The umbrella organisation Domowina (founded 1912) represents Sorbian interests. Bilingual signage is standard in the settlement area, and traditions such as Easter riding processions (Osterreiten), painted Easter eggs and the bird wedding (Vogelhochzeit) remain vibrant. Upper Sorbian is centred on Bautzen (Budyšin); the more endangered Lower Sorbian on Cottbus (Chóśebuz).
