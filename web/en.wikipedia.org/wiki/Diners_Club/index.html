Diners Club

TypeCharge card network / subsidiary
Founded1950, New York City
FoundersFrank X. McNamara, Ralph Schneider, Matty Simmons, Alfred Bloomingdale
ParentBMO Financial Group (Diners Club North America); franchises worldwide

Diners Club International Ltd. is a charge card company owned by Discover Financial Services since 2008. Founded in 1950 by Frank X. McNamara and partners, it issued the first independent general-purpose charge card — originally made of cardboard — that could be used at multiple merchants, an innovation generally credited with launching the modern payment-card industry.

History

According to company lore, McNamara had the idea after forgetting his wallet at a New York restaurant in 1949. Diners Club launched in 1950 with 200 cardholders accepted at 27 restaurants, charging merchants a 7% fee and cardholders an annual fee; balances had to be paid in full each month, making it a charge card rather than a revolving credit card. The concept rapidly expanded into travel and entertainment, and inspired competitors such as American Express (1958) and Carte Blanche. Diners Club also pioneered cards in many countries, and its cards were the first to be accepted in the Soviet Union (1969) and China (1980s). Citibank acquired the company in 1981; Discover bought the international network in 2008, and BMO acquired Diners Club North America in 2009. Since 2004, Diners Club cards issued in North America run on the Mastercard network, while international cards are accepted on the Discover network.

Operations

Outside North America, Diners Club operates largely through national franchisees that issue cards in local markets, with strong positions in countries such as Japan and Italy. Cards are aimed at affluent travellers, with airport lounge access and travel benefits.
