Old Hemp

Old Hemp (September 1893 – May 1901) was a stud dog considered to be the progenitor of the Border Collie breed. He was owned by Adam Telfer of Northumberland, England, and was famed for his exceptional herding ability and quiet, commanding working style.

Life and work

Old Hemp was a black, white, and tan tri-colour dog of medium build with a rough coat. Unlike many sheepdogs of his era that barked and bustled at sheep, Hemp worked silently and calmly, controlling stock with an intense gaze — the "eye" that became a hallmark of the breed. Shepherds travelled widely to breed their bitches to him, and he is believed to have sired more than 200 pups.

Legacy

Old Hemp appears in the pedigrees of most modern Border Collies; when the International Sheep Dog Society began its stud book, Hemp was retrospectively entered as number 9. His style and bloodlines shaped the breed on several continents, and he is commemorated as the foundation sire of the Border Collie.
