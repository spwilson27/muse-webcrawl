Double bond

In chemistry, a double bond is a chemical bond between two atoms involving four bonding electrons instead of the usual two in a single bond. The most common double bond, between two carbon atoms, defines the alkenes.

Double bond
Type: Covalent bond
Electrons: 4 (one sigma, one pi)
Example: Ethylene C=C, carbonyl C=O

Structure

A double bond consists of one sigma (σ) bond and one pi (π) bond. It is shorter and stronger than a single bond between the same atoms but, in bond-order terms, less than twice as strong. Rotation about a double bond is restricted, giving rise to cis–trans (E/Z) isomerism in alkenes. Atoms in a C=C unit are sp²-hybridized and planar, with bond angles near 120°.

Types and reactions

Double bonds occur in carbonyls (C=O), imines (C=N), azo compounds (N=N), and in O=O of molecular oxygen. They characteristically undergo addition reactions — hydrogenation, halogenation, hydration — and are central to polymerization and to the chemistry of unsaturated fats.
