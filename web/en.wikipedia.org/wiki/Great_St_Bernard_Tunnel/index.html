Great St Bernard Tunnel

The Great St Bernard Tunnel (French: Tunnel du Grand Saint-Bernard, Italian: Traforo del Gran San Bernardo, German: Grosser-Sankt-Bernhard-Tunnel) is a road tunnel complementing the Great St Bernard Pass, linking Martigny (in the Swiss canton of Valais) with Saint-Rhémy-en-Bosses (in the Italian Aosta Valley), on the route of European route E27.

Overview

The tunnel is 5,798 metres (19,023 ft) long and makes the route usable year-round, whereas the pass road, at 2,469 m above sea level, is closed by snow in winter. It was the first road tunnel through the main Alpine chain to be opened to traffic, inaugurated on 19 March 1964. The tunnel consists of a single gallery carrying traffic in both directions, and a toll is charged for its use.

Location

The northern portal is near Bourg-Saint-Pierre in Switzerland, and the southern portal near Saint-Rhémy-en-Bosses in Italy. The tunnel runs beneath the Great St Bernard Pass, the historic crossing of the Pennine Alps famous for its hospice founded by Saint Bernard of Menthon and for the St. Bernard dogs bred by the canons to assist travellers.

Safety

Following major tunnel fires elsewhere in the Alps, safety in the tunnel has been progressively improved, with works on escape routes, ventilation and signalling. A separate service and rescue gallery has been constructed alongside the main tube to serve as an escape route.
