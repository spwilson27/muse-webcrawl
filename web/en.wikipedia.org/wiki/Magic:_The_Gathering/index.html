Magic: The Gathering (colloquially known as Magic or MTG) is a tabletop and digital collectible card game created by Richard Garfield and released in 1993 by Wizards of the Coast, a subsidiary of Hasbro since 1999. It was the first trading card game and had approximately fifty million players as of 2022.

Gameplay

Each game represents a duel between wizards known as Planeswalkers, who cast spells, summon creatures, and use artifacts depicted on individual cards drawn from their decks. Players generally begin with 20 life points and lose when reduced to zero. Cards draw on five colors of mana — white, blue, black, red, and green — produced primarily by land cards, each color having a characteristic philosophy and strengths.

A player usually wins by reducing the opponent's life total to zero, though alternative win conditions exist. Decks are constructed by players from their own collections, and cards are sold in randomized booster packs of varying rarity, a model that created a large secondary market; some early cards, such as Black Lotus from the game's first printing, have sold for hundreds of thousands of dollars.

Formats and organized play

Games are played in formats that restrict which sets and cards are legal, including Standard, Pioneer, Modern, Legacy, Vintage, and the popular multiplayer casual format Commander. Wizards of the Coast has run organized play ranging from local Friday Night Magic events to large Grand Prix tournaments and a professional circuit. Digital versions include Magic: The Gathering Online (2002) and Magic: The Gathering Arena (2019).

Reception and legacy

Magic has won numerous awards, was inducted into the National Toy Hall of Fame in 2019, and is widely credited with founding the collectible card game genre, influencing later games such as Pokémon and Yu-Gi-Oh! Its revenue has made it one of Hasbro's most important brands.
