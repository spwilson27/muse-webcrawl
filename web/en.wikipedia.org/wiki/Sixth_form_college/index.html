A sixth form college is an educational institution in England, Wales and Northern Ireland where students aged 16 to 19 study typically for advanced school-level qualifications such as A-levels, Business and Technology Education Council (BTEC) qualifications, and at some colleges the International Baccalaureate Diploma. The term "sixth form" derives from the traditional English school year groups: students in their final two years of secondary schooling were grouped as the Lower Sixth and Upper Sixth.

History

Dedicated sixth form colleges emerged from the 1960s as local authorities reorganised secondary education along comprehensive lines, concentrating post-16 provision in separate institutions. The first purpose-built colleges appeared in places such as Luton and Stoke-on-Trent. In 1992 the Further and Higher Education Act removed sixth form colleges (and further education colleges) from local authority control, making them independent corporations funded by national funding councils; since 2010 many have converted to academy status while retaining their sixth-form-only character.

Provision

Unlike school sixth forms, sixth form colleges typically enrol large cohorts — often 1,500 to 3,000 students — allowing a wide curriculum: 40 or more A-level subjects at larger colleges, alongside vocational BTECs and GCSE resits in English and mathematics. Students usually study three A-levels over two years. Colleges are inspected by Ofsted and publish performance tables; high-performing colleges such as Hills Road Sixth Form College (Cambridge) and Greenhead College (Huddersfield) are nationally known.

Admissions

Entry is normally conditional on GCSE results, commonly five passes at grade 4 or above including English and mathematics, with higher grades required for specific subjects. Since 2013 in England, young people have been required to participate in education or training until age 18, making sixth form colleges one of the main routes alongside school sixth forms, further education colleges and apprenticeships.
