Overview

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is a chronic immune-mediated disease in which the myelin sheaths of nerve fibres in the brain and spinal cord, and the fibres themselves, are damaged, disrupting communication within the central nervous system. The name refers to the multiple scars (sclerae/plaques) found in the white matter. Symptoms vary with lesion location: optic neuritis, double vision, limb weakness, sensory disturbance, ataxia, bladder dysfunction and fatigue are common.

Causes and treatment

The cause is unknown; risk is influenced by genetics (HLA-DRB1*15:01), low vitamin D/sunlight, obesity in adolescence, smoking and — strongly, per a landmark 2022 study of US military personnel — Epstein–Barr virus infection. There is no cure, but disease-modifying therapies (interferons, glatiramer, and more effective monoclonals such as ocrelizumab and natalizumab) reduce relapses and slow progression in relapsing forms. Diagnosis rests on MRI and clinical evidence of lesions disseminated in space and time (McDonald criteria).
