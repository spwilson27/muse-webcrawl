District councils of Hong Kong

The district councils of Hong Kong are the municipal councils for the 18 districts of Hong Kong. Known as district boards before 2000, they are Hong Kong's local consultative bodies: they advise the government on district affairs, promote recreational and cultural activities, and oversee aspects of local environmental improvement and community services. They are subordinate to the Home and Youth Affairs Bureau and coordinate with District Officers of the Home Affairs Department.

History

District boards were created in 1982 under Governor Murray MacLehose's District Administration Scheme, initially combining appointed and indirectly elected members; directly elected seats were introduced gradually, and from 1994 (under Governor Chris Patten's reforms) until 2020 the councils were predominantly composed of directly elected members. Following the implementation of the Hong Kong national security law, mass resignations and disqualifications of pro-democracy councillors in 2021 left most councils with only a handful of members.

2023 overhaul

In 2023 the government overhauled the councils' composition, reducing directly elected seats to about 20 percent of each council. Under the new structure, each district council includes appointed members, members elected by district committees, and a smaller number of directly elected members, with terms of four years. The District Officer serves as the council's chair. The first elections under the reformed system were held in December 2023.

Functions

District councils advise on matters affecting residents' well-being, undertake district-level projects such as minor works and community activities within funding allocated by the government, and serve as a channel for consultation on local issues. They have no legislative or revenue-raising powers, and their role is advisory.
