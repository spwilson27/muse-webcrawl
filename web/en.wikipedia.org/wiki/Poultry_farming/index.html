Poultry farming

Poultry farming is the form of animal husbandry which raises domesticated birds such as chickens, ducks, turkeys and geese to produce meat or eggs for food. Poultry — mostly chickens — are farmed in great numbers: more than 60 billion chickens are raised annually for meat, and over 1.6 trillion eggs are produced each year worldwide.

Chickens

Chickens raised for meat are called broilers; they are bred for rapid growth and typically reach slaughter weight in five to seven weeks. Chickens raised for eggs are called laying hens; a modern hybrid hen can lay around 300 eggs per year. Male chicks of laying breeds, which neither lay eggs nor grow fast enough for meat, are culled in most production systems — a practice that has drawn growing ethical scrutiny.

Housing systems

  • Battery cages — small wire enclosures for laying hens, phased out or banned in the European Union and several other jurisdictions in favour of enriched cages or cage-free systems.
  • Barn systems — birds move freely inside a building.
  • Free-range — birds have outdoor access during the day.
  • Organic — free-range plus certified organic feed and restrictions on medication.

Other poultry

Ducks and geese are farmed mainly in Asia, which accounts for the large majority of world duck meat and egg production. Turkeys are raised intensively in North America and Europe, notably for seasonal demand at Thanksgiving and Christmas. Guinea fowl, quail and pigeons (squabs) are produced on smaller scales.

Issues

Intensive poultry farming is associated with concerns over animal welfare, the routine use of antibiotics and antimicrobial resistance, zoonotic diseases such as avian influenza, and environmental impacts from manure and feed production. Conversely, poultry is among the most feed-efficient sources of animal protein, with a lower carbon footprint per kilogram than beef or lamb.

History

The chicken was domesticated from the red junglefowl in Southeast Asia several thousand years ago. Industrial-scale poultry production developed in the twentieth century with selective breeding, formulated feeds, incubation technology and vertical integration of feed mills, hatcheries, farms and processing plants.
