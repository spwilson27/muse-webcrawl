SEB Group (Swedish: Skandinaviska Enskilda Banken AB, "Scandinavian Individual Bank") is a Swedish financial group headquartered in Stockholm. Founded in 1856 by André Oscar Wallenberg as Stockholms Enskilda Bank, it is one of the largest banks in the Nordic region, with particular strengths in corporate and investment banking. The Wallenberg family, through its investment company Investor AB, remains the bank's principal owner.

History

Stockholms Enskilda Bank was Sweden's first private bank. In 1972 it merged with Skandinaviska Banken (founded 1864 in Gothenburg) to form Skandinaviska Enskilda Banken. The bank expanded into the Baltic states in the 1990s, becoming the dominant banking group in Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, and acquired the German bank BfG in 2000 (later sold). During the Swedish banking crisis of the early 1990s SEB came under severe strain but survived without being nationalised, unlike some competitors.

Operations

SEB serves large corporations and financial institutions, small and medium-sized enterprises, and several million private customers. Its divisions cover corporate and investment banking, retail banking in Sweden and the Baltics, life insurance, and asset management. The bank employs roughly 19,000 people and markets itself as the leading Nordic corporate bank, with offices in financial centres including New York, London, Singapore and Shanghai.
