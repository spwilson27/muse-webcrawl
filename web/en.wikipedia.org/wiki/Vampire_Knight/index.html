Vampire Knight (Japanese: ヴァンパイア騎士, Hepburn: Vanpaia Naito) is a Japanese manga series written and illustrated by Matsuri Hino. It was serialized in Hakusensha's shōjo manga magazine LaLa from 2004 to 2013, and its chapters were collected in nineteen tankōbon volumes. The series was licensed for English release in North America by Viz Media under its Shojo Beat imprint.

The story is set at Cross Academy, a prestigious boarding school divided into two classes: the Day Class, attended by ordinary human students, and the Night Class, whose students are secretly vampires. Yuki Cross, the adopted daughter of the academy's headmaster, serves as a guardian of the school alongside Zero Kiryu, tasked with keeping the Night Class's secret and protecting the human students. The series follows Yuki as she uncovers the truth about her own past, her connection to the pureblood vampire Kaname Kuran, and the conflict between humans and vampires.

Plot

Yuki Cross's earliest memory is of being attacked by a vampire and rescued by Kaname Kuran, a pureblood vampire, when she was five years old. She was adopted by Kaien Cross, a former vampire hunter who founded Cross Academy to promote peaceful coexistence between humans and vampires. As a guardian, Yuki patrols the school grounds with Zero Kiryu, whose family was killed by a pureblood vampire and who harbors a deep hatred of vampires while concealing his own transformation into one.

As the story progresses, Yuki learns that she is herself a pureblood vampire, born as Yuki Kuran, and that her memories were sealed away by her mother to protect her. The series explores the love triangle between Yuki, Kaname, and Zero, set against an escalating war involving the Vampire Senate and the villainous pureblood Rido Kuran.

Media

The manga was adapted into a 26-episode anime television series produced by Studio Deen, which aired in 2008 in two seasons: Vampire Knight and Vampire Knight Guilty. A sequel manga, Vampire Knight: Memories, began serialization in 2016. The franchise has also produced drama CDs, light novels, and a fan book.

Reception

Vampire Knight was a commercial success, with the manga selling millions of copies worldwide. It was praised for its gothic atmosphere and artwork, though some critics noted its melodramatic plot elements.
