Neuschwanstein Castle

Neuschwanstein Castle
Location: Hohenschwangau, Bavaria, Germany
Commissioned by: Ludwig II of Bavaria
Construction started: 1869
Architect: Eduard Riedel

Neuschwanstein Castle (German: Schloss Neuschwanstein) is a 19th-century historicist palace on a rugged hill above the village of Hohenschwangau near Füssen in southwest Bavaria, Germany. The palace was commissioned by King Ludwig II of Bavaria as a retreat and in honour of Richard Wagner, whose operas inspired much of its design and decoration.

History

Ludwig II laid the foundation stone in 1869, intending the castle as a private refuge rather than a seat of government. He paid for the palace out of his personal fortune and extensive borrowing rather than Bavarian public funds. When the king died under mysterious circumstances in 1886, only a fraction of the planned rooms had been completed and decorated, though the exterior was largely finished.

The palace was opened to the paying public almost immediately after Ludwig's death and has since become one of the most popular tourist destinations in Europe, receiving more than 1.5 million visitors per year. It served as the inspiration for Sleeping Beauty Castle at Disneyland.

Architecture

The castle was designed by the stage designer Christian Jank and built by architect Eduard Riedel and later Georg von Dollmann. Its towers, turrets and frescoes draw on medieval German legend and Wagnerian themes, particularly Tannhäuser and Lohengrin. Inside, the Singers' Hall and the throne room are decorated with elaborate murals, mosaics and wood carving, even though the throne itself was never completed.
