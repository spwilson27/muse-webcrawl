"Layla" is a song written by Eric Clapton and Jim Gordon, originally recorded by the blues rock band Derek and the Dominos, as the title track of their only studio album, Layla and Other Assorted Love Songs (1970). The song was inspired by Clapton's unrequited love for Pattie Boyd, the wife of his friend George Harrison, and by the Persian tale of Layla and Majnun, a story of doomed love that Clapton had read in Nizami Ganjavi's poem.

Composition

The song is in two distinct movements. The first is a guitar-driven rock section built around a signature riff played by Clapton and Duane Allman on slide guitar, framing Clapton's anguished plea to "Layla". The second movement, composed by drummer Jim Gordon, is a melancholic piano-led coda with layered guitar, often called the "piano exit".

Reception and legacy

On its initial release the song was not a major hit, but it grew steadily in stature. A shortened single version reached the top ten in the United States in 1972, and a live acoustic version from Clapton's Unplugged (1992) won the Grammy Award for Best Rock Song and reached number 12 on the Billboard Hot 100. "Layla" is routinely listed among the greatest rock songs of all time; Rolling Stone placed it at number 27 on its list of the 500 greatest songs.
