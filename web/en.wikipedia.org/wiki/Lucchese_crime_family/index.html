The Lucchese crime family is an Italian-American Mafia crime family and one of the "Five Families" that dominate organized crime in New York City. Founded in the 1920s, it took its modern name from Tommy Lucchese, who led the family from 1951 to 1967, one of the longest and most stable reigns in Mafia history.

History

The family originated in East Harlem and the Bronx. After the Castellammarese War, Gaetano "Tommy" Gagliano became boss, with Lucchese as his underboss; Lucchese succeeded him in 1951 and built substantial influence in the garment district, trucking, and the Teamsters union, as well as at Kennedy Airport. Under Anthony "Tony Ducks" Corallo the family profited from labour racketeering and the concrete industry.

Later years

In the 1980s the entire Lucchese administration was convicted in the Mafia Commission Trial. Vittorio "Vic" Amuso became boss in 1986; his violent regime, run with Anthony "Gaspipe" Casso, provoked defections, and both men were imprisoned in the early 1990s, Amuso for life. The family continued under acting bosses and remains active, though diminished, in the New York metropolitan area and New Jersey, where its faction there has long operated semi-autonomously.
