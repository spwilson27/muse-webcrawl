Salyut 1

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Salyut 1 with a docked Soyuz spacecraft.

Salyut 1 was the world's first space station, launched by the Soviet Union on 19 April 1971. Derived from the military Almaz station programme, the 20-metre, roughly 18-tonne station carried telescopes and cameras for Earth observation and astronomy, and was designed for crews of three arriving by Soyuz ferries.

The first crew, aboard Soyuz 10, reached the station but could not dock properly and returned after two days. The second crew — Georgy Dobrovolsky, Vladislav Volkov and Viktor Patsayev, on Soyuz 11 — lived aboard for 23 days, setting an endurance record. During their return on 30 June 1971 a ventilation valve opened prematurely in the descent module, the cabin air escaped, and all three cosmonauts died of asphyxiation — the only human deaths in space itself. Salyut 1 was deorbited in October 1971 after 175 days.
