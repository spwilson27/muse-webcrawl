Serie C

The Serie C is the third-highest division in the Italian football league system, below Serie B and above Serie D. It is organised by the Lega Italiana Calcio Professionistico (Lega Pro). The league currently consists of 60 teams divided into three regional groups (gironi) of 20 teams each, roughly arranged north, centre and south.

History

Serie C was established in 1935 as part of the reorganisation of Italian football. Over the decades its format changed repeatedly: from numerous regional groups in the post-war period to a single national division, and in 1978 it was split into two levels, Serie C1 and Serie C2, which together formed the fourth tier as well. In 2008 the two levels were renamed Lega Pro Prima Divisione and Lega Pro Seconda Divisione, before the leagues were reunified in 2014 under the name Lega Pro, and the historic name Serie C was restored in 2017.

Format

Each of the three group winners is promoted directly to Serie B, and a fourth promotion place is decided by extensive play-offs involving teams finishing from second to tenth place across the groups. The lowest-placed teams in each group are relegated to Serie D, with play-outs deciding additional relegations. The league is fully professional.
