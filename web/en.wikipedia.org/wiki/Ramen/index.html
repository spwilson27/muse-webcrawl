Ramen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A steaming bowl of ramen with chashu pork, egg and nori]

Ramen is a Japanese noodle soup dish: wheat noodles in a flavoured broth, topped with ingredients such as sliced pork (chashu), nori, menma (bamboo shoots), scallions and a halved seasoned egg. Originating from Chinese noodle dishes brought to Japan in the late 19th century, it became a national obsession after the Second World War, and Momofuku Ando's invention of instant ramen (1958) spread it worldwide.

Regional styles define the map: Sapporo miso ramen, Kitakata shoyu, Tokyo shoyu, Hakata tonkotsu (pork-bone broth) and tsukemen dipping noodles. Broth, tare (seasoning), noodle thickness and toppings combine in endless variation, and ramen shops are judged by devoted rankings.

Contents
1. History

History

Chinese-style noodles were eaten in Yokohama's Chinatown by the early 20th century; after 1945, cheap wheat, black-market stalls and returning soldiers made ramen the food of reconstruction. Ando's Chicken Ramen (1958) and Cup Noodles (1971) industrialised it; in the 2000s–2010s ramen became a global restaurant trend, with chains such as Ippudo and Ichiran expanding abroad.
