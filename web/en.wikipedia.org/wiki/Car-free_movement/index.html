Car-free movement

Image placeholder: A car-free street event.

The car-free movement is a broad, informal network of individuals and organisations advocating reduced reliance on automobiles, particularly in cities. Participants argue that dependence on cars causes pollution, congestion, road deaths, sprawl, noise, and the loss of public space, and promote alternatives including walking, cycling, and public transport, as well as car-free and low-car urban design.

Activities

The movement encompasses groups such as Critical Mass, which holds mass bicycle rides; World Carfree Network, an umbrella organisation; and local campaigns for pedestrianisation, car-free housing, and traffic calming. Recurring events include World Car Free Day (22 September), Park(ing) Day, and open-streets programmes that temporarily close roads to motor traffic. Writer-activists such as Jane Holtz Kay and J. H. Crawford, whose book Carfree Cities proposed car-free urban districts, have influenced the movement's ideas.

Car-free places

Existing car-free or near-car-free places often cited by advocates include Venice, the medina of Fes el Bali, Zermatt, and pedestrianised city centres in Copenhagen, Ghent, and Pontevedra, where restrictions on cars have been credited with safer streets and improved quality of life. Critics counter that car restrictions can disadvantage some residents and businesses, and debate continues over the pace and form of change.
