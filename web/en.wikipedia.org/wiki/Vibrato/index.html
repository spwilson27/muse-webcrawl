Vibrato

TypeMusical performance technique
EffectRegular pulsating pitch variation
Typical rate≈ 5–7 cycles per second

Vibrato (Italian for "vibrating") is a musical effect consisting of a regular, pulsating change of pitch. It is produced by singers and by players of string, wind and other instruments, and is used to add warmth and expressiveness to a sustained tone.

Production

String players produce vibrato by rocking the fingertip on the string with the hand, wrist or arm, alternately sharpening and flattening the pitch. Singers produce it through small oscillations involving the larynx and breath pressure; flautists may pulse the airstream with the diaphragm, and brass players may move the instrument or use the hand or slide. On electric guitar, vibrato can also be produced with a tremolo arm (whammy bar) or by bending the string; electronic vibrato units alter pitch in the signal path.

Characteristics and style

A typical vibrato oscillates about 5 to 7 times per second with a pitch extent of up to a quarter-tone or semitone in singers, though conventions vary widely by era and genre. Historically informed performers of Baroque and early music often use vibrato sparingly, as an ornament, whereas continuous vibrato became the norm in Romantic and modern classical string playing and operatic singing during the late 19th and 20th centuries. Vibrato is distinguished from tremolo, which is a fluctuation of amplitude rather than pitch.
