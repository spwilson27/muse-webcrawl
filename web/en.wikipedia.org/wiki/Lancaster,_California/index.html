Lancaster is a charter city in northern Los Angeles County, California, United States. Located in the Antelope Valley of the western Mojave Desert, it is separated from Los Angeles by the San Gabriel Mountains. Lancaster grew from a small railroad water stop into one of California's larger cities, driven largely by the aerospace industry and affordable housing for commuters to the Los Angeles basin.

History

The Antelope Valley was historically inhabited by Native American peoples. The settlement of Lancaster dates to the 1870s–1880s, when the Southern Pacific Railroad built its line north from Los Angeles; the name is traditionally attributed to a railroad clerk from Lancaster, Pennsylvania. The community was a modest farming and railroad town for decades, its growth tied to alfalfa farming and gold and borax mining in the surrounding desert.

The decisive change came with the aerospace era: nearby Edwards Air Force Base and Air Force Plant 42 in adjacent Palmdale made the Antelope Valley a center of flight testing and military aircraft production. Lancaster incorporated as a city in 1977 and grew rapidly in the 1980s and 1990s as one of California's fastest-growing cities. A notable local feature is the "Musical Road," a stretch of Avenue G grooved in 2008 to play the William Tell Overture as cars drive over it.

Geography and climate

Lancaster covers roughly 94 square miles at an elevation of about 2,350 feet, making it a high-desert city. It has an arid climate with hot summers, cool winters, and occasional snow. The Antelope Valley California Poppy Reserve lies west of the city and draws visitors during spring wildflower blooms.

Economy and transport

Aerospace employers, including operations associated with Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, and Boeing at nearby plants, anchor the economy, along with warehousing, health care, and retail. The city is served by Metrolink's Antelope Valley Line to Los Angeles and by State Route 14 (the Antelope Valley Freeway).
