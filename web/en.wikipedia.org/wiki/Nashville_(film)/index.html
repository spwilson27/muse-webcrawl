Nashville (film)

Released1975

CountryUnited States

Nashville is a 1975 American satirical musical drama film directed by Robert Altman and written by Joan Tewkesbury. Following some two dozen interwoven characters over five days in Nashville, Tennessee, in the run-up to a political rally and gala concert, the film is a mosaic portrait of the country music industry, celebrity culture and American politics in the Bicentennial era.

Plot and structure

The ensemble includes a veteran country star (Henry Gibson), an ailing gospel-country queen (Lily Tomlin), a fragile superstar (Ronee Blakley), an aspiring singer of limited talent (Gwen Welles), a British journalist (Geraldine Chaplin), and the advance man for a populist third-party presidential candidate whose unseen campaign threads through the story. The film's many songs were largely written and performed by the actors themselves; Keith Carradine's "I'm Easy" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song. The narrative converges at a concert at Nashville's Parthenon replica, where a shocking act of violence provides the climax.

Reception and legacy

Nashville was nominated for five Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Director, and is widely regarded as Altman's masterpiece and one of the greatest American films. It was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 1992. Its overlapping dialogue, improvisational ensemble method and multi-strand narrative became hallmarks of Altman's style and influenced generations of filmmakers.
