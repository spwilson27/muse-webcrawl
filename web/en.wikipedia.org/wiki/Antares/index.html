Antares is the brightest star in the constellation of Scorpius and one of the brightest stars in the night sky. Its name means "rival of Mars" (from the Greek anti-Ares), because its reddish colour resembles that of the planet Mars. Antares is a red supergiant star, a slow irregular variable whose apparent magnitude ranges between about 0.6 and 1.6.

Characteristics

Antares lies about 550 light-years from the Sun. With a radius of roughly 680 times that of the Sun, it is among the largest known stars: placed at the centre of the Solar System, its surface would extend beyond the orbit of Mars. It is a member of a binary system with a hot blue-white companion, Antares B, which appears greenish by contrast in telescopes. Antares is a member of the Scorpius–Centaurus association and will end its life in a supernova explosion.

Observation

Antares marks the heart of the scorpion and is best seen in the summer skies of the Northern Hemisphere. Because it lies near the ecliptic, it is regularly occulted by the Moon and, rarely, by planets.
