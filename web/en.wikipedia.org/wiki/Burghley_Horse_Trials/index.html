Burghley Horse Trials

The Burghley Horse Trials is an annual three-day event (now four days) held at Burghley House near Stamford, Lincolnshire, England. It is one of only seven CCI5*-L events in the world — the highest level of eventing competition — and forms part of the Grand Slam of Eventing together with Badminton and Kentucky.

History

The trials were first held in 1961, when the Marquess of Exeter invited the British Horse Society to transfer the national championships to Burghley after hare coursing protests disrupted the event at Harewood. The competition comprises dressage, cross-country and show jumping phases over successive days. Burghley has hosted multiple European and World Championships, and its cross-country course, long designed by Captain Mark Phillips and later Derek di Grazia, is renowned for its size and technical difficulty.
