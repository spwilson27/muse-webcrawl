North Governorate

The North Governorate (Arabic: محافظة الشمال, Muḭāfaẓat ash-Shamāl) is one of the governorates of Lebanon. Its capital is Tripoli, the country's second-largest city. The governorate covers the northernmost part of Lebanon along the Mediterranean coast, extending inland to the slopes of Mount Lebanon.

Geography and administration

The governorate is subdivided into districts (qada'a), including Batroun, Bsharri, Koura, Minieh-Dannieh, and Zgharta, with Tripoli forming its own district. In 2014 the Akkar district was split off to form the separate Akkar Governorate. The region includes the Qadisha Valley, a UNESCO World Heritage Site with early Christian monastic settlements, and the Cedars of God forest near Bsharri.

Demographics and economy

The North Governorate is religiously diverse, with Sunni Muslims predominant in Tripoli, Akkar, and Minieh-Dannieh, and Maronite and Greek Orthodox Christians in Batroun, Zgharta, Koura, and Bsharri. Tripoli, famed for its Mamluk architecture, soap-making, and sweets, is an important port and industrial city, though the governorate as a whole is among Lebanon's poorest regions, with high unemployment and significant refugee populations from Syria.
