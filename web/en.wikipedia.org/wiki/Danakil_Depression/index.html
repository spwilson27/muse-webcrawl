Danakil Depression

Danakil Depression
Location: Afar Region, Ethiopia / Eritrea
Lowest point: −125 m (−410 ft)
Features: Dallol, Erta Ale, Lake Asal nearby

The Danakil Depression is a geological depression in the Afar Triangle of northeastern Ethiopia and Eritrea, formed where three tectonic plates pull apart. Lying up to 125 metres below sea level, it is one of the hottest, lowest and most geologically active places on Earth.

Geology

Rifting between the African, Arabian and Somali plates thins the crust, producing volcanoes such as Erta Ale with its persistent lava lake, the acidic, multicoloured hydrothermal fields of Dallol, and vast salt flats mined by Afar salt caravans. The depression was once flooded by the Red Sea, leaving thick evaporite deposits including potash.

Climate and life

Average temperatures around 34–35 °C make it among the hottest inhabited places; extremophile microbes in Dallol's springs are studied as analogues for early Earth and Mars. Nearby Afar fossil sites yielded Australopithecus afarensis, including "Lucy".
