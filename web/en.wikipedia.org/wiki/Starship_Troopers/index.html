Starship Troopers is a military science fiction novel by American writer Robert A. Heinlein. Written in a few weeks in reaction to the US suspending nuclear tests, the story was first published as a two-part serial in The Magazine of Fantasy & Science Fiction as Starship Soldier, and published in hardcover in 1959. It won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 1960.

Plot and themes

The novel follows young Juan "Johnny" Rico through his military career in the Mobile Infantry of the Terran Federation during an interstellar war against an arachnid species known as "the Bugs". Heinlein explores themes of civic virtue, the franchise restricted to those who complete federal (usually military) service, corporal and capital punishment, and the necessity of war. The book also popularized the concept of powered armor infantry, which influenced later science fiction and even military research.

Reception and adaptations

Starship Troopers has remained controversial for its political philosophy, variously described as militaristic or even fascist — charges Heinlein's defenders dispute. It is a foundational work of the military science-fiction subgenre. It was loosely adapted into the 1997 film Starship Troopers directed by Paul Verhoeven, which satirized the novel's politics, and into an animated series, Roughnecks: Starship Troopers Chronicles.
