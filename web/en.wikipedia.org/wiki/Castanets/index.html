Castanets

Castanets are a percussion instrument used in Spanish, Moorish, Ottoman, Italian, and Latin dances. In Spanish they are known as castañuelas (from castaña, "chestnut") and consist of a pair of concave shells joined by a string, traditionally made of chestnut wood, now often of fiberglass or dense hardwoods.

Technique

The player holds one pair in each hand, striking the shells together with the fingers to produce clicking sounds and rapid rolls (carretillas). In flamenco and Spanish classical dance, the dancer plays them while dancing; traditionally the right hand plays the higher-pitched female pair (hembra) and the left the lower male pair (macho), which marks the basic rhythm.

History

Hand clappers of similar form date to ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome, where they accompanied dance. Castanets became emblematic of Andalusian and Spanish folk dance and appear in orchestral music evoking Spain, including Bizet's Carmen and works by Rimsky-Korsakov and de Falla. Orchestral castanets are mounted on a handle or board for ease of playing.
