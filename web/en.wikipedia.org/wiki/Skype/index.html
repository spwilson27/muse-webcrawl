Skype was a proprietary telecommunications software application operated by Skype Technologies, a division of Microsoft, best known for VoIP-based videotelephony, videoconferencing and voice calls. It also offered instant messaging, file transfer, and calls to landline and mobile phones. First released in 2003, Skype was created by Niklas Zennström and Janus Friis in cooperation with a team of Estonian developers who had previously built the Kazaa file-sharing program.

History

Skype popularised free voice and video calling over the internet, using a peer-to-peer architecture in which some users' computers acted as "supernodes" relaying traffic. The service grew rapidly, registering hundreds of millions of users. eBay acquired Skype in 2005 for $2.6 billion; a group of investors bought a majority stake in 2009, and in May 2011 Microsoft acquired Skype for $8.5 billion, its largest acquisition at the time. Skype subsequently became integrated with Microsoft products and replaced Windows Live Messenger.

Features

  • Free Skype-to-Skype voice and video calls, including group video calls.
  • Instant messaging with file transfer and screen sharing.
  • Skype Credit and subscriptions for cheap calls to landlines and mobiles worldwide.
  • Skype Numbers allowing users to receive calls from ordinary telephones.
  • Live translation features (Skype Translator) in later versions.

Retirement

With the rise of Microsoft Teams and competing services such as Zoom, usage of Skype declined through the late 2010s and early 2020s. In February 2025 Microsoft announced that the consumer Skype service would be retired on 5 May 2025, with users migrated to Microsoft Teams Free.
