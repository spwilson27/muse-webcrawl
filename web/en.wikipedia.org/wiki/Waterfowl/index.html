Waterfowl

Waterfowl are birds of the family Anatidae within the order Anseriformes, comprising the ducks, geese and swans, together in some classifications with the screamers of South America and the magpie goose. There are more than 170 species distributed on every continent except Antarctica, most of them strongly associated with freshwater and coastal wetlands.

Characteristics

Waterfowl generally have long necks, flattened bills with lamellae for filter-feeding or grazing, webbed feet set toward the rear of the body, and dense waterproof plumage over a layer of down. Many species are strong long-distance migrants, moving between northern breeding grounds and warmer wintering areas, and most are capable swimmers and fliers, though a few island forms are flightless.

Relationship with humans

Several species have been domesticated, notably the domestic duck (from the mallard and Muscovy duck) and domestic geese (from the greylag and swan goose), providing meat, eggs, feathers and down. Wild waterfowl are important game birds, and waterfowl hunting is a major driver of wetland conservation funding in countries such as the United States. Many populations are monitored internationally because wetlands are among the most threatened habitats.
