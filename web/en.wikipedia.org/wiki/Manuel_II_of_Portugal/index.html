Manuel II of Portugal

Reign: 1 February 1908 – 5 October 1910
Born: 15 November 1889, Lisbon
Died: 2 July 1932, Twickenham, England
House: Braganza
Predecessor: Carlos I
Successor: Monarchy abolished

Manuel II (15 November 1889 – 2 July 1932), nicknamed "the Patriot" (Portuguese: o Patriota) or "the Bibliophile" (o Bibliófilo), was the last King of Portugal, reigning from 1908 until the fall of the monarchy in 1910.

Early life

Manuel was the second son of King Carlos I and Amélie of Orléans. He was educated as a naval officer and was not expected to inherit the throne, which was destined for his elder brother, Luís Filipe, Prince Royal.

Accession

On 1 February 1908, King Carlos and the Prince Royal were assassinated in Lisbon by republican gunmen in the Terreiro do Paço. Manuel, who was slightly wounded in the attack, ascended the throne at the age of eighteen. His reign was marked by continued political instability, the growth of the republican movement, and the controversial premiership of João Franco's successors.

Revolution and exile

On 5 October 1910, following the Lisbon Regicide's aftermath and a republican uprising supported by the military, the Portuguese First Republic was proclaimed. Manuel fled to Gibraltar aboard the royal yacht and spent the rest of his life in exile in England, residing at Fulwell Park, Twickenham.

Later life

In exile, Manuel devoted himself to bibliography, producing a noted multi-volume catalogue of early Portuguese printed books. He married Princess Augusta Victoria of Hohenzollern in 1913; the marriage was childless. He died suddenly in 1932, aged 42, of acute oedema of the trachea.
