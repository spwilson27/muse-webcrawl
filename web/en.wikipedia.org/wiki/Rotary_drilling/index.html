Rotary drilling

Rotary drilling is a method of drilling boreholes used in oil and gas extraction, water wells, geothermal wells and mineral exploration. A rotating drill bit, suspended from a derrick by a drill string, cuts or crushes rock while drilling fluid — mud — is pumped down the string and returns up the annulus, carrying cuttings to the surface.

History

Rotary drilling was developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries and proved decisive at Spindletop in Texas in 1901, where it outperformed cable-tool drilling in soft formations. It subsequently became the dominant method worldwide.

Equipment

The system includes a drilling rig, drawworks, top drive or rotary table with kelly, drill pipe, drill collars, and a drill bit — commonly a roller-cone bit or polycrystalline diamond compact (PDC) bit. A blowout preventer controls formation pressure.

Process

The bit rotates while weight is applied. Drilling fluid cools the bit, stabilises the hole, controls pressure and removes cuttings. See also directional drilling and oil well.
