Lok Sabha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Lok Sabha (House of the People) is the lower house of India's bicameral Parliament, whose members are directly elected by the people, represent constituencies across the states and union territories, and serve five-year terms unless the House is dissolved sooner. With 543 elected members chosen by first-past-the-post in single-member constituencies, it is one of the largest elected chambers in the world, representing an electorate of roughly a billion people.

Role

The Lok Sabha dominates the parliamentary system: the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to it alone, money bills originate only there (the Rajya Sabha's role reduced to recommendations), and in joint sittings its numbers prevail. Its composition, frozen on 1971 census proportions by constitutional amendment to protect population-control incentives, faces a long-anticipated delimitation after 2026 that promises a major reallocation of seats towards the faster-growing northern states — potentially combined with reserved seats for women under the 2023 Nari Shakti Vandan Act, whose implementation is tied to that delimitation. The Speaker's impartiality, the use of the money-bill route to bypass the upper house (litigated in the Aadhaar case), and declining sitting days are standing controversies of Indian parliamentary practice.
