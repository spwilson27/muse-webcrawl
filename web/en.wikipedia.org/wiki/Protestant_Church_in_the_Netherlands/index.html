The Protestant Church in the Netherlands (Dutch: Protestantse Kerk in Nederland, abbreviated PKN) is the largest Protestant denomination in the Netherlands. It was founded on 1 May 2004 through the merger of the Dutch Reformed Church, the Reformed Churches in the Netherlands, and the Evangelical Lutheran Church in the Kingdom of the Netherlands.

History

The merger crowned decades of ecumenical rapprochement under the motto Samen op Weg ("Together on the Way"). Not all congregations joined: some conservative Reformed congregations formed the Restored Reformed Church and other continuing bodies. The PKN brought together traditions spanning liberal to orthodox Calvinism alongside a small Lutheran minority, which retained its own synod within the church.

Organisation and beliefs

The church is governed by a general synod and presbyterian-synodal polity, with local congregations grouped into classes. Its confessional basis includes the Apostles', Nicene and Athanasian Creeds, the Belgic Confession, the Heidelberg Catechism, the Canons of Dort and the Lutheran Augsburg Confession. The PKN is a member of the World Council of Churches and the World Communion of Reformed Churches. Membership has steadily declined amid broader secularisation in the Netherlands.
