All-time Olympic Games medal table

The all-time medal table for all Olympic Games from 1896 to the present day, including both Summer and Winter Games, ranks National Olympic Committees by the number of medals won by their athletes. The International Olympic Committee does not officially publish an all-time table; the tables compiled by media and reference works generally sort nations first by gold medals, then silver, then bronze.

Combined table (leading nations)

The United States leads the all-time standings in both gold medals and total medals, having surpassed 1,200 golds and 3,000 total medals across Summer and Winter Games. The Soviet Union, despite competing only from 1952 to 1988, remains second in Summer golds; Germany (including West and East Germany separately in some conventions), Great Britain, France, China, Italy, and Japan are among the other nations with several hundred medals.

Conventions and notes

Rankings differ according to whether defunct teams (the Soviet Union, East Germany, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia) are listed separately or merged with successor states, and medals stripped for doping are generally removed from official counts. In the Winter Games, Norway leads the all-time table, ahead of the United States, Germany, Canada, and Austria.
