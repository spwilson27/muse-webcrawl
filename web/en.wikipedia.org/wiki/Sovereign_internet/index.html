Sovereign internet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The sovereign internet is a concept of state-controlled national segments of the internet in which a government seeks the technical and legal capacity to regulate, filter and, if necessary, disconnect its national network from the global internet. The idea is associated particularly with Russia's 'sovereign RuNet' policy, enacted in the Sovereign Internet Law of 2019, and is compared with China's longstanding system of internet controls centred on the Great Firewall.

Features

Measures typically associated with internet sovereignty include domestic domain-name infrastructure, mandatory traffic routing through state-monitored exchange points, deep packet inspection, data-localisation requirements and national alternatives to foreign platforms and services.

Debate

Proponents frame sovereignty as a matter of national security and digital independence. Critics, including advocates of a single global open internet, warn that such policies contribute to the 'splinternet' — the fragmentation of the worldwide network into incompatible national segments.
