Roadside Picnic

Authors: Arkady and Boris Strugatsky
Original title: Пикник на обочине
Language: Russian
Published: 1972 (serial); 1977 (English)
Genre: Science fiction

Roadside Picnic (Russian: Пикник на обочине) is a philosophical science fiction novel by Soviet-Russian authors Arkady and Boris Strugatsky, written in 1971 and published in 1972. It is one of the most influential works of twentieth-century science fiction and the source of the film Stalker (1979) and the S.T.A.L.K.E.R. video game series.

Plot

Thirteen years before the novel opens, extraterrestrials briefly visited Earth, landing in six "Zones" and departing without contact, leaving behind bizarre and dangerous artifacts. The scientist Dr. Valentine Pilman explains the title metaphor: the visit was like a roadside picnic—travelers stopped briefly and left behind litter whose meaning the local animals (humanity) cannot comprehend. The International Institute of Extraterrestrial Cultures studies the Zone, but the artifacts are also harvested illegally by "stalkers," guides who risk their lives among gravitational traps, lethal phenomena, and artifacts such as "empties," batteries, and the mysterious "witch's jelly." Children of stalkers are often mutated.

The novel follows Redrick "Red" Schuhart, a stalker and later laboratory assistant, over eight years of his life in the town of Harmont beside one Zone. Driven by poverty, prison sentences, and obsession, he returns to the Zone repeatedly. In the final section he guides Arthur, the young son of a former colleague, toward the legendary Golden Sphere, said to grant wishes—arriving at a desperate conclusion about what he truly wishes for.

Publication and influence

The novel was serialized in the journal Avrora in 1972 but a Soviet book edition was delayed for years by censorship disputes; the first complete Russian book text appeared only in 1990. The English translation (1977, with a foreword by Theodore Sturgeon) won wide acclaim. Andrei Tarkovsky's film Stalker (1979) loosely adapts the premise, and the Ukrainian S.T.A.L.K.E.R. games transplanted the Zone concept to Chernobyl. A new English translation by Olena Bormashenko appeared in 2012.
