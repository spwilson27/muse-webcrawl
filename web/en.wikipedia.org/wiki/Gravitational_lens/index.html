Gravitational lens

A gravitational lens is a distribution of matter — such as a galaxy or galaxy cluster — that bends light from a distant source as it travels toward an observer, an effect predicted by general relativity and analogous to an optical lens.

PredictedEinstein, 1936; Zwicky, 1937
First discoveredTwin Quasar Q0957+561, 1979
TypesStrong, weak, microlensing

Types

Strong lensing creates multiple images, arcs and Einstein rings. Weak lensing subtly distorts background galaxy shapes, mapping dark matter. Microlensing by stars briefly brightens background stars and is used to find exoplanets.

Applications

Lenses act as natural telescopes magnifying the early universe, weigh galaxies and clusters (confirming dark matter), and — via time delays between images — measure the Hubble constant. The Einstein Cross and the Bullet Cluster are famous examples. See General relativity and Dark matter.
