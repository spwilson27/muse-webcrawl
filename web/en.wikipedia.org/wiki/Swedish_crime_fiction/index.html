Swedish crime fiction
Part of: Scandinavian noir / Nordic noir
Key authors: Sjöwall & Wahlöö, Henning Mankell, Stieg Larsson

Swedish crime fiction is a major, internationally successful genre, central to Scandinavian noir, known for social criticism, bleak settings and morally complex detectives.

History

Early writers included Maria Lang. Maj Sjöwall and Per Wahlöö's ten Martin Beck novels (1965–75) founded the modern Swedish police procedural, using crime to critique the welfare state. Henning Mankell's Kurt Wallander series (from 1991) brought global fame.

Millennium and after

Stieg Larsson's posthumous Millennium trilogy, beginning with The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo (2005), sold tens of millions worldwide and created hacker heroine Lisbeth Salander. Other notable authors include Åsa Larsson, Camilla Läckberg, Håkan Nesser, Arne Dahl, Liza Marklund and Jo Nesbø's Norwegian counterparts in the wider Nordic noir wave. Many works were adapted for film and TV, including BBC's Wallander.
