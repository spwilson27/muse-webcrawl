Grand Mufti

A Grand Mufti is the leading official of religious law in a Sunni Muslim country or jurisdiction. The office ranks above ordinary muftis — Islamic jurists qualified to issue fatwas (non-binding legal opinions on points of Islamic law) — and the Grand Mufti is typically regarded as the highest religious authority in the state.

Role and functions

The Grand Mufti issues fatwas on questions submitted by governments, courts, and members of the public; supervises other muftis; and may oversee religious endowments, mosque appointments, and the determination of the lunar calendar, including the beginning of Ramadan and the dates of religious festivals. In many states the Grand Mufti is appointed by the head of state or government, and the office forms part of the official religious establishment.

History

The institution developed in the Ottoman Empire, where the Sheikh al-Islam of Istanbul stood at the head of the religious hierarchy. As Ottoman territories became independent states, many established their own Grand Mufti. Egypt's office, headed since 2013 by Shawki Allam, is based at Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyyah, founded in 1895. Saudi Arabia created the office of Grand Mufti in 1953; Abdulaziz Al Sheikh held it from 1999 until his death in 2025.

Notable offices

  • Egypt — Grand Mufti of Egypt, resident at Dar al-Ifta in Cairo.
  • Saudi Arabia — Grand Mufti of Saudi Arabia, also head of the Council of Senior Scholars.
  • Jerusalem — The Grand Mufti of Jerusalem was created by the British in 1918; the office was held controversially by Haj Amin al-Husseini from 1921.
  • India — A Grand Mufti of India is recognised by many Sunni Muslims, based in Kerala.
