Hip hop dance

Hip hop dance is a range of street dance styles primarily performed to hip hop music and forming part of hip hop culture. It includes styles created in the 1970s by African American and Puerto Rican youth in New York City — most fundamentally breaking (breakdance), locking, and popping — later grouped together as the "funk styles" and old-school hip hop dances.

History

Breaking emerged at Bronx block parties in the mid-1970s, where DJ Kool Herc extended the percussive "breaks" of funk records and dancers — b-boys and b-girls — performed footwork, power moves, and freezes. On the West Coast, Don Campbell developed locking and the Electric Boogaloos developed popping in California. In the 1980s and 1990s, music videos, films, and crews such as Rock Steady Crew carried the styles worldwide. Breaking debuted as an Olympic sport at the Paris 2024 Games.

Styles and techniques

Breaking is built from toprock, downrock (footwork), power moves, and freezes. Popping relies on quickly contracting and relaxing muscles ("hits") and encompasses sub-styles such as waving, tutting, and gliding; locking features exaggerated freezes and points. Newer commercial "hip hop" choreography taught in studios blends these foundations with jazz and contemporary dance — a distinction purists emphasise between street styles and studio choreography.

Competitions and culture

Battles — improvisational contests judged on skill, originality, and musicality — are central to the culture, from local cyphers to international events such as Red Bull BC One, Juste Debout, and the Battle of the Year.
