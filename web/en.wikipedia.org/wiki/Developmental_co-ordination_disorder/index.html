Developmental co-ordination disorder

Developmental co-ordination disorder (DCD), also known as dyspraxia, is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by impaired motor coordination that significantly interferes with activities of daily living and academic achievement, and is not explained by intellectual disability, visual impairment, or a neurological condition affecting movement.

Signs and presentation

Children with DCD may be delayed in sitting, crawling, or walking, and later struggle with tying shoelaces, handwriting, catching a ball, riding a bicycle, and using cutlery. The condition affects an estimated 5–6% of school-aged children and is more commonly diagnosed in boys. Difficulties often persist into adulthood and are associated with lower self-esteem, anxiety, and reduced participation in physical activity. DCD frequently co-occurs with ADHD, autism spectrum disorder, and specific learning disorders such as dyslexia.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is clinical, using criteria such as those in the DSM-5, standardized motor assessments, and history from parents and teachers. There is no cure; management focuses on occupational and physical therapy, task-specific practice, and educational accommodations that build motor skills and confidence.
