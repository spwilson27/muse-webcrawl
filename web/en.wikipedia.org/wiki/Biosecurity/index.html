Biosecurity

ScopeAgriculture, public health, laboratories, ecology
Related conceptsBiosafety, quarantine, biodefence
Key threatsPests, invasive species, infectious diseases, bioterrorism

Biosecurity refers to the policies, procedures, and measures taken to prevent the introduction and spread of harmful organisms — including pests, invasive species, and infectious diseases — in order to protect agriculture, livestock, ecosystems, and human health. The term is used in several overlapping senses, from farm-level disease control and border quarantine to the security of biological laboratories against theft or misuse of pathogens.

Agricultural and environmental biosecurity

In agriculture, biosecurity includes quarantine inspection at borders, control of animal and plant movements, disinfection and hygiene on farms, and surveillance for diseases such as foot-and-mouth disease and avian influenza. Island nations such as Australia and New Zealand maintain especially strict biosecurity regimes to protect their unique ecosystems and export industries. The field overlaps with the management of invasive alien species, which threaten biodiversity and ecosystem services worldwide.

Laboratory biosecurity

In the laboratory context, biosecurity (sometimes distinguished as "laboratory biosecurity") concerns the protection of dangerous pathogens and toxins from loss, theft, diversion, or deliberate release. It complements biosafety, which concerns preventing accidental exposure and release. International frameworks include the Biological Weapons Convention and national select-agent regulations.

Public health

Human health biosecurity encompasses pandemic preparedness, disease surveillance, and border health measures. Globalisation, climate change, and increasing travel and trade have heightened attention to emerging infectious diseases and the governance of dual-use research of concern in the life sciences.
