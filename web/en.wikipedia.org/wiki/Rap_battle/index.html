Rap battle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A rap battle (also called a battle rap or MC battle) is a competitive performance in which two or more rappers trade verses — improvised ("freestyle") or written in advance — attempting to outdo each other in wordplay, wit, delivery and insults before an audience or panel of judges. The tradition descends from the verbal duelling of "the dozens" and from early hip-hop culture in the Bronx, New York.

History

Battling was central to hip-hop from its beginnings in the 1970s, when MCs competed for reputation at block parties and in parks. Widely cited early moments include Kool Moe Dee's 1981 defeat of Busy Bee Starski, which shifted battling toward lyrical technique. Freestyle competitions such as the Scribble Jam festival and televised formats like BET's Freestyle Friday on 106 & Park brought battling to wider audiences in the 1990s and 2000s; the 2002 film 8 Mile, starring Eminem, dramatised the Detroit battle scene.

Modern battle leagues

From the late 2000s, organised leagues — including the Ultimate Rap League (URL) and King of the Dot in North America, Don't Flop in the UK, and leagues in many other countries — professionalised battle rap around written, a cappella performances uploaded to video platforms. Competitors are judged on material, performance, rebuttals (direct responses to an opponent's lines) and crowd reaction. Diss tracks recorded between rival artists are a related, studio-based form of rap conflict.
