The ZX81 is a home computer produced by Sinclair Research and manufactured in Dundee, Scotland, by Timex Corporation. Launched in March 1981, it was the successor to Sinclair's ZX80 and was sold both as a kit and ready-built. At under £70 in kit form it was the first truly mass-market home computer in the United Kingdom, and it sold over 1.5 million units worldwide. In the United States it was marketed by Timex as the Timex Sinclair 1000.

Design

To minimise cost, the ZX81 used only four chips and relied on the Z80 processor to generate the television display as well as run programs, which caused the screen to flicker or blank during computation. It had a pressure-sensitive membrane keyboard, 1 KB of RAM, an 8 KB BASIC ROM with single-keystroke keyword entry, and used a domestic television as a display and cassette tapes for storage.

Impact

The ZX81 introduced a generation of British programmers to computing and spawned a large ecosystem of software, magazines, and peripherals, including the infamous loosely-fitting 16 KB RAM pack whose wobble could crash the machine. It was succeeded in 1982 by the ZX Spectrum.
