The Lake Shore Limited is a long-distance passenger train operated by Amtrak between Chicago and the northeastern United States, with sections serving New York City and Boston. The train follows much of the historic "Water Level Route" of the New York Central Railroad along the Hudson River and the southern shore of Lake Erie, from which it takes its name.

History

The Lake Shore Limited name dates to the New York Central Railroad, which operated a Lake Shore Limited between New York and Chicago beginning in 1897. Amtrak revived the name on October 31, 1975, for its train over the same route, replacing the earlier Lake Shore service that had been introduced in 1971. For much of its Amtrak history the train has operated with separate New York and Boston sections that combine at Albany–Rensselaer for the journey west to Chicago, and split eastbound at the same station.

Route

Westbound, the New York section departs Pennsylvania Station and travels up the Empire Corridor along the Hudson River through Yonkers, Poughkeepsie and Rhinecliff to Albany. The Boston section crosses Massachusetts via Springfield, following the former Boston and Albany Railroad. From Albany the combined train runs west through Schenectady, Utica, Syracuse, Rochester and Buffalo in New York, then along Lake Erie through Erie, Pennsylvania, and the Ohio cities of Cleveland and Toledo, continuing through northern Indiana to Chicago Union Station. The full New York–Chicago journey covers about 959 miles (1,543 km) and takes roughly 20 hours.

Equipment and services

The train typically uses Amfleet coaches and Viewliner sleeping cars, with a dining car or diner-lounge and a café car providing food service. Sleeping car passengers receive complimentary meals. The Boston section historically included a through sleeping car handled across the Albany split.
