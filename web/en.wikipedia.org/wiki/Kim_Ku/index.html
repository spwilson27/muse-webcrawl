Kim Ku (Korean: 김구; Hanja: 金九; August 29, 1876 – June 26, 1949), also known by his pen name Baekbeom (백범), was a Korean statesman and independence activist. He was a leader of the Korean independence movement against the Empire of Japan and served as the last premier and president of the Provisional Government of the Republic of Korea in exile in China.

Early life

Kim was born in Haeju, Hwanghae Province, to a poor farming family. As a young man he joined the Donghak movement, a religious and social reform movement, and took part in the Donghak Peasant Revolution of 1894. After its suppression he fled briefly to Manchuria before returning to Korea.

In 1896 Kim killed a Japanese man, Tsuchida Josuke, at an inn in Chihapo, believing him to be involved in the assassination of Empress Myeongseong. He was arrested, tortured, and sentenced to death, but his sentence was commuted; he escaped prison in 1898 and later became a Buddhist monk before turning to education and nationalist organizing.

Independence activism

After Japan annexed Korea in 1910, Kim was arrested in connection with the 105-Man Incident and imprisoned. Released in 1915, he went into exile in Shanghai in 1919 following the March 1st Movement, joining the newly proclaimed Provisional Government of the Republic of Korea. He served in several posts, including Minister of Internal Affairs and police chief, and organized armed resistance activities.

In 1931 Kim founded the Korean Patriotic Organization (Hanin Aegukdan), which sponsored celebrated attacks on Japanese officials, including Yun Bong-gil's bombing at Hongkou Park in Shanghai in April 1932 and Lee Bong-chang's attempted assassination of Emperor Hirohito in Tokyo. These acts drew international attention—and Chinese support—to the Korean cause.

Later years and death

During the Second Sino-Japanese War and the Pacific War, Kim led the provisional government from Chongqing and oversaw the Korean Liberation Army. After Japan's surrender in 1945, he returned to Korea, opposing the trusteeship plan and the separate elections that led to the division of the peninsula. In 1948 he traveled to Pyongyang for unification talks with Kim Il-sung, which failed to prevent the establishment of separate governments.

On June 26, 1949, Kim Ku was assassinated in his study at Gyeonggyojang in Seoul by Ahn Doo-hee, a South Korean army lieutenant. His funeral drew enormous crowds, and he remains one of the most revered figures of modern Korean history; his autobiography, the Baekbeom Ilji, is a classic of Korean literature.
