Cardinal Bessarion, Byzantine scholar and Catholic cardinal

Bessarion (Greek: Βησσαρίων; 2 January 1403 – 18 November 1472), born Basilios Bessarion, was a Byzantine Greek scholar, theologian and Catholic cardinal. Originally a monk and later Metropolitan of Nicaea in the Orthodox Church, he became one of the most prominent figures in the movement to reunite the Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic Churches, and a leading transmitter of Greek learning to Renaissance Italy.

Life

Bessarion was born in Trebizond on the Black Sea coast. He studied in Constantinople and then in Mistra in the Peloponnese, where he was a pupil of the Neoplatonic philosopher George Gemistus Plethon. He became a monk, taking the name Bessarion after an early Christian desert saint, and was made Metropolitan of Nicaea in 1437.

In 1438–1439 Bessarion accompanied the Byzantine Emperor John VIII Palaiologos to Italy for the Council of Ferrara–Florence, where he was initially a leading spokesman for the Orthodox side but eventually became convinced of the Latin position on the Filioque and other disputed questions. He read the decree of union in Greek at Florence in 1439. Pope Eugene IV made him a cardinal the same year, and he remained in Italy for the rest of his life.

Career in the Latin Church

Bessarion came close to being elected pope at the conclaves of 1455 and 1464. He served as papal legate in Bologna, on missions to France, and in efforts to organise a crusade after the fall of Constantinople in 1453, though the hoped-for expedition never materialised. He held the title of Latin Patriarch of Constantinople from 1463.

Scholarship and legacy

Bessarion's Roman household became a centre of humanist scholarship. He wrote a major defence of Plato against George of Trebizond, In calumniatorem Platonis (1469), while carefully insisting that Plato and Aristotle could be reconciled. He amassed one of the largest private libraries of Greek manuscripts in Europe and in 1468 donated his collection — hundreds of Latin and Greek codices — to the Republic of Venice, where it formed the nucleus of the Biblioteca Marciana. He died in Ravenna in 1472 and was buried in the church of Santi Apostoli in Rome.
