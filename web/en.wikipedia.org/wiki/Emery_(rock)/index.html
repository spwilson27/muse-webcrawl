Emery (rock)

Emery is a dark granular rock consisting essentially of corundum (aluminium oxide) mixed with magnetite, hematite, or spinel. It is used as an abrasive, ground to powder and applied to paper or cloth (emery paper, emery cloth), formed into wheels, or used loose for grinding and polishing.

Occurrence

Emery is a metamorphic rock formed by the metamorphism of aluminium-rich sediments or bauxite deposits. Historically famous deposits occur on the Greek island of Naxos and in the region around Smyrna (İzmir) in Turkey; other sources include the United States (Peekskill, New York; Chester, Massachusetts) and parts of Asia.

Properties and use

Pure corundum has a Mohs hardness of 9, but natural emery is softer (about 7–8) because of its iron oxide content. Emery was among the earliest industrial abrasives and remains in use for metal finishing, though synthetic abrasives such as aluminium oxide and silicon carbide have largely supplanted it. Emery boards and emery sticks are still common for nail care and fine hand-finishing.
