Federal subjects of Russia

The federal subjects of Russia, also referred to as the subjects of the Russian Federation (Russian: субъекты Российской Федерации), are the constituent entities of Russia, its top-level political divisions according to the Constitution of Russia. The constitution recognises several types of subjects: republics, krais (territories), oblasts (provinces), federal cities, an autonomous oblast, and autonomous okrugs (districts). Russia claims 89 federal subjects, including territories annexed from Ukraine that are not internationally recognised; 83 are widely recognised.

Types

Republics are nominally autonomous and mostly correspond to ethnic groups, with their own constitutions and official languages. Oblasts and krais are more standard administrative units. The federal cities are Moscow, Saint Petersburg, and (claimed) Sevastopol. Autonomous okrugs have varying degrees of self-government, and the single autonomous oblast is the Jewish Autonomous Oblast.

Governance

Each subject elects or appoints a head and a regional legislature, and each is represented by two members in the Federation Council, the upper house of the Federal Assembly. Since 2000 the subjects have also been grouped into federal districts overseen by presidential envoys.
