Prague Spring International Music Festival

The Prague Spring International Music Festival (Czech: Mezinárodní hudební festival Pražské jaro) is a permanent showcase of outstanding performing artists, symphony orchestras and chamber music ensembles held annually in Prague, Czech Republic. It traditionally opens on 12 May, the anniversary of the death of Bedřich Smetana, with a performance of his cycle of symphonic poems Má vlast ("My Country").

History

The festival was founded in 1946 under the patronage of President Edvard Beneš, marking the first anniversary of the end of the Second World War and the 50th anniversary of the Czech Philharmonic, which gave the festival's first concerts under conductor Rafael Kubelík. Since then it has been held every year, even during the decades of communist rule.

Venues and programme

Principal venues include the Rudolfinum's Dvořák Hall and the Smetana Hall of the Municipal House. The festival presents Czech and international orchestras, opera productions, chamber concerts and recitals, and hosts the Prague Spring International Music Competition for young performers, founded in 1947.
