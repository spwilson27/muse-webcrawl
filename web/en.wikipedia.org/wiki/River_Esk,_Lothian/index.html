River Esk, Lothian

The River Esk is a river in Midlothian and East Lothian, Scotland. It is formed by the confluence of the North Esk and South Esk near Dalkeith, and flows about 6.5 km (4 miles) north-east through Musselburgh to enter the Firth of Forth.

Course and history

The North Esk rises in the Pentland Hills and passes Penicuik and Roslin Glen; the South Esk rises in the Moorfoot Hills and passes Temple and Dalkeith. Their combined river at Musselburgh is crossed by the historic Old Bridge (the "Roman Bridge") and was the scene of crossings before the Battle of Pinkie Cleugh (1547), fought on its banks.

The Esk supported mills for paper, grain, and textiles along both branches; today its valley provides riverside paths, and the estuary mudflats at Musselburgh are noted for wintering wading birds.

Several Scottish rivers share the name Esk, from the Brittonic word for water; this one is distinguished as the Lothian Esk.
