Postal history

A nineteenth-century cover, the raw material of postal history.

Postal history is the study of postal systems and how they operate, and of the material they generate, including envelopes ("covers"), postcards, postmarks, routes and rates. Unlike traditional stamp collecting, which focuses on the stamps themselves, postal history treats postal artefacts as evidence of the workings of communications systems, trade, war and society. It is a recognised branch of philately and exhibits in its own right are judged in international competitions.

Scope

Postal historians study such matters as the development of postal routes and rates, the introduction of new services such as registration, airmail and postal orders, markings applied to mail (including instructional, censorship and disinfection marks), military mail, maritime mail, and the postal arrangements of empires, occupations and sieges. Because postage was for centuries normally paid by the recipient, early covers bear manuscript rate markings rather than stamps; adhesive stamps were not introduced until 1840.

Origins of postal systems

Organised relay systems for official messages date back to the Persian Empire's mounted messengers, praised by Herodotus, the Roman cursus publicus, and the Chinese imperial courier service. In medieval Europe, universities, merchant houses and monastic orders ran their own messengers; the Thurn und Taxis family built a pan-European postal network from the late fifteenth century under imperial privilege. State postal monopolies spread in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, and the nineteenth century brought cheap uniform postage, beginning with Rowland Hill's reforms in Britain in 1840, and international coordination through the Universal Postal Union, founded in 1874.

Research

Postal history collections are used by social and economic historians to trace migration, commerce, literacy and the reach of the state. Surviving mail from events such as the Crimean War, the American Civil War and the two world wars is a major focus of collecting and scholarship.
