Desecularization

In the sociology of religion, desecularization is the proliferation of religious organisations and the growth of religious influence in a society that had previously undergone secularization. The concept was prominently developed by sociologist Peter L. Berger, who in The Desecularization of the World (1999) recanted his earlier support for secularization theory, arguing that the world was "as furiously religious as it ever was".

Proponents point to the global growth of Pentecostalism and evangelical Christianity, the resurgence of Islam in public life, the revival of religion in post-communist states such as Russia, and the role of religion in politics worldwide. Critics argue that desecularization is uneven: Western Europe and parts of East Asia have continued to secularize, and rising numbers of religiously unaffiliated people in many countries complicate the thesis.

Related concepts include postsecularism, religious resurgence, and deprivatization of religion, a term coined by José Casanova.
