Royal Dutch Football Association

The Royal Dutch Football Association (Dutch: Koninklijke Nederlandse Voetbalbond, pronounced [ˈkoːnɪŋkləkə ˈneːdərlɑntsə ˈvudbɑlbɔnt]; abbreviated KNVB) is the governing body of football in the Netherlands. Founded on 8 December 1889 by Pim Mulier as the Nederlandsche Voetbal en Athletische Bond, it is one of the founding members of FIFA (1904) and a member of UEFA. Headquartered in Zeist, the KNVB organises the Netherlands national football teams and the principal domestic competitions, including the KNVB Cup, and oversees professional and amateur football for more than 1.2 million registered members, making it the largest sports federation in the country.

History

Football was introduced to the Netherlands by Pim Mulier, who founded the Haarlemsche Football Club in 1879 and helped organise the national association a decade later. The "Royal" predicate was granted in 1929, marking the association's 40th anniversary. The KNVB oversaw the transition from amateurism to professional football in the 1950s, and the professional leagues — the Eredivisie and Eerste Divisie — are now run in cooperation between the KNVB and the league organisations, while the association retains responsibility for the national teams, refereeing, discipline and grassroots football through six district offices.

National teams

The Netherlands men's national team, administered by the KNVB, won the European Championship in 1988 and reached the FIFA World Cup final in 1974, 1978 and 2010. The women's national team won UEFA Women's Euro 2017 on home soil and reached the World Cup final in 2019, achievements that greatly expanded the KNVB's women's football programmes. The association's campus in Zeist includes national training facilities and a sports medical centre.
