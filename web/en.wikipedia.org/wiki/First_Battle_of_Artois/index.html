First Battle of Artois

The First Battle of Artois (17 December 1914 – 13 January 1915) was a battle fought during World War I by the French and German armies on the Western Front. The battle was the first offensive move on the Western Front by any belligerent after the end of the First Battle of Ypres in November 1914. The French assault was designed to capture Vimy Ridge, a commanding position in the Artois region of northern France, and to advance toward the important railway junction of Douai.

The attack by the French Tenth Army under General Louis de Maud'huy made limited gains against the German 6th Army of Crown Prince Rupprecht of Bavaria, in particular around Notre-Dame de Lorette and Carency, but failed to take Vimy Ridge. Winter weather, a shortage of heavy artillery and shells, and strong German defensive positions in trenches and fortified villages limited the results. The French suffered roughly 60,000 casualties, and the battle set the pattern for further costly attempts on the ridge in the Second and Third Battles of Artois in 1915.

Background

After the Race to the Sea in autumn 1914, the front line stabilized from the North Sea to Switzerland. General Joseph Joffre, the French commander-in-chief, planned winter operations to keep pressure on the Germans while the British Expeditionary Force recovered and to support Russia in the east.
