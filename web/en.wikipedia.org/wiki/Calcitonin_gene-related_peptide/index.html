Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) is a member of the calcitonin family of peptides consisting of calcitonin, amylin, adrenomedullin and adrenomedullin 2/intermedin. Its name derives from the fact that in humans the α-CGRP form is produced by alternative splicing of the same gene (CALCA) that encodes calcitonin. CGRP is one of the most potent peptide vasodilators known and plays a central role in the transmission of pain.

Structure and production

CGRP exists in two forms in humans, α-CGRP and β-CGRP, differing by three amino acids. It is produced in both peripheral and central neurons and is released from trigeminal nerve endings, where it acts on receptors composed of the calcitonin receptor-like receptor (CLR) and receptor activity-modifying protein 1 (RAMP1).

Role in migraine

CGRP is strongly implicated in the pathophysiology of migraine: levels rise during attacks, and infusion of CGRP can trigger migraine-like headaches in susceptible people. This has led to a new class of treatments — monoclonal antibodies against CGRP or its receptor (such as erenumab, fremanezumab and galcanezumab) and small-molecule antagonists called gepants (such as rimegepant and ubrogepant) — used for both prevention and acute treatment of migraine.

Other functions

Beyond pain, CGRP participates in cardiovascular regulation, wound healing and immune modulation, acting as a potent dilator of cerebral and coronary arteries.
