Gelderland is a province of the Netherlands, located in the centre-east of the country. It is the largest Dutch province by land area. Its capital is Arnhem, while Nijmegen, the oldest city in the Netherlands, is its largest urban centre; other major towns include Apeldoorn, Ede, Zutphen, Doetinchem and Tiel.

History

The province takes its name from the Duchy of Guelders, which emerged in the 11th–12th centuries around the town of Geldern (now in Germany) and became one of the major principalities of the Low Countries. Its four quarters — Arnhem (the Veluwe), Nijmegen, Zutphen and Roermond — joined the Dutch Revolt, and the southern quarter (Roermond) was partitioned away in the settlements of the Eighty Years' War. Gelderland was a leading province of the Dutch Republic through its role in the States General.

In the Second World War, Gelderland saw heavy fighting during Operation Market Garden (September 1944), centred on the Arnhem road bridge over the Lower Rhine, and during the subsequent front-line months along the great rivers.

Geography

The province is crossed by the Rhine and its distributaries the Waal and IJssel, and by the Maas in the south. Its landscape regions include the forested, sandy Veluwe — home to the Hoge Veluwe National Park and the Kröller-Müller Museum — the fertile river plain of the Betuwe, famed for fruit growing, and the Achterhoek in the east. The provincial government is seated in the Huis der Provincie in Arnhem.
