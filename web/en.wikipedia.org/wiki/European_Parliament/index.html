European Parliament

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The European Parliament (EP) is one of the two legislative bodies of the European Union and one of its seven institutions. Together with the Council of the European Union, it adopts EU legislation (normally on proposals from the European Commission) and the EU budget, and exercises democratic oversight over the EU executive. Its roughly 720 members (MEPs) — the number varies slightly by treaty adjustments — are directly elected every five years by the citizens of the 27 member states, making it the only directly elected supranational parliament with real legislative power.

Seats and organisation

The Parliament's seat is in Strasbourg, France (where twelve plenary sessions a year are held), with committee and additional plenary work in Brussels and a secretariat in Luxembourg — an arrangement long criticised as costly. MEPs sit in transnational political groups (the centre-right EPP, centre-left S&D, liberals of Renew, Greens/EFA, conservatives ECR, the radical-left Left, and populist-right groups among them) rather than national delegations. The Parliament elects the Commission President (following the lead-candidate or Spitzenkandidat practice), approves the Commission as a body, can censure it (the threat forced the Santer Commission's 1999 resignation), and must consent to most international agreements and the long-term budget.

Elections and role

European elections (1979– ) are second-order national contests in most analyses — fought on domestic issues with turnout lower than national elections (about half in 2019 and 2024) — yet the Parliament's powers have grown with every treaty from the Single European Act to Lisbon, fuelling continuing debate about the EU's "democratic deficit" and the Parliament as its remedy.
