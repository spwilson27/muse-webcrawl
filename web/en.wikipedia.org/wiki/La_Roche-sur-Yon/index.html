La Roche-sur-Yon

La Roche-sur-Yon is a commune in the Vendée department in the Pays de la Loire region in western France. It is the prefecture (capital) of the Vendée, with a population of around 55,000 in the commune and more than 120,000 in the urban area.

History

A settlement existed at the site from the Middle Ages, centred on a castle on a rocky outcrop (roche) above the River Yon, held by the princes of La Roche-sur-Yon. The town was largely destroyed during the Wars of the Vendée in the 1790s. In 1804 Napoleon Bonaparte, seeking a new administrative and military centre for the Vendée, decreed the creation of a new planned town on the site—briefly renamed Napoléon-Vendée, a name it bore again under the Second Empire.

Geography and plan

The Napoleonic town was laid out on a regular pentagonal grid around a vast central square, the Place Napoléon, which features a statue of Napoleon and, since 2013, a celebrated installation of mechanical animals by François Delarozière's company Les Machines de l'Île. Key monuments include the Church of Saint-Louis, the prefecture, and the Haras de la Vendée (national stud).

Economy and transport

La Roche-sur-Yon is an administrative, commercial, and service centre for the Vendée, and is known for its university institute (an IUT of the University of Nantes) and cultural institutions such as the Grand R theatre. The town is served by La Roche-sur-Yon station on the Nantes–Bordeaux line and by the A83 and A87 motorways.
