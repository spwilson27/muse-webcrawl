Anna Creek Station

Anna Creek Station is a pastoral lease in the Australian state of South Australia, operating as a cattle station. With an area of roughly 23,700 square kilometres (about 9,140 square miles), it is the world's largest working cattle station—larger than some countries, including Israel and Slovenia. It is located about 680 km north of Adelaide, west of Lake Eyre, with the nearest township being William Creek to its south-west.

History and operation

The station was established in the 19th century following exploration of the region and was long associated with the pastoral empire of Sir Sidney Kidman, whose company S. Kidman & Co held it for much of the 20th and early 21st centuries. In 2016 Anna Creek, together with the neighbouring Peake station, was sold to Williams Cattle Company as part of the break-up of the Kidman portfolio.

Depending on season and feed, Anna Creek runs some 10,000 to 17,000 head of cattle—far fewer than its theoretical capacity because of the arid climate; in severe drought it may be largely destocked. Most of the work is done by a small staff using motorbikes, aircraft for mustering and spotting cattle, and road trains for transport. Much of the property lies within or adjacent to the Woomera Prohibited Area.

Geography

The country is arid gibber plain and breakaway country on the edge of the Simpson–Strzelecki dunefields, crossed by ephemeral watercourses such as Anna Creek itself and Neales River, which flow toward Lake Eyre after heavy rain.
