Library catalog

A library catalog (or library catalogue) is a register of all bibliographic items found in a library or group of libraries, such as a network of libraries at several locations. A catalog for a group of libraries is also called a union catalog. A bibliographic item can be any information entity (e.g., books, computer files, graphics, realia, cartographic materials, etc.) that is considered library material, or a group of library materials, or linked from the catalog as far as it is relevant to the catalog and to the users (patrons) of the library.

History

Early catalogs were lists written on clay tablets, scrolls, and later in book form. The card catalog, with one card per item filed alphabetically in drawers, became standard in the late 19th century, helped by standardised printed cards distributed by the Library of Congress from 1901. Melvil Dewey and others promoted standard card sizes and rules for filing.

The online public access catalog (OPAC) replaced card catalogs beginning in the 1980s, as libraries adopted integrated library systems. OPACs allow searching by author, title, subject, and keyword, and typically show circulation status and location.

Cataloging rules

Libraries describe items using cataloging codes such as the Anglo-American Cataloguing Rules (AACR2) and its successor, Resource Description and Access (RDA). Records are commonly encoded in the MARC (Machine-Readable Cataloging) format for exchange between systems. Classification schemes such as the Dewey Decimal Classification and the Library of Congress Classification assign call numbers that place items on shelves by subject.

Purposes

Charles Ammi Cutter, in his Rules for a Dictionary Catalog (1876), stated the objectives of a bibliographic system: to enable a person to find a book of which the author, title, or subject is known; to show what the library has by a given author, on a given subject, or in a given kind of literature; and to assist in the choice of a book. These aims — finding, collocation, and choice — remain the classic statement of the catalog's functions, later reformulated in the FRBR user tasks: find, identify, select, obtain.

Types

  • Author catalog — entries filed by author or editor
  • Title catalog — entries filed by title
  • Subject catalog — entries filed by subject heading
  • Dictionary catalog — author, title, and subject entries in a single alphabetical sequence
  • Union catalog — combined holdings of many libraries, such as WorldCat
