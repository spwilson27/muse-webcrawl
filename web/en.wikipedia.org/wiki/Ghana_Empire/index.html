Ghana Empire

The Ghana Empire (c. 700–1240), known locally as Wagadou, was a West African empire based in the modern-day southeast of Mauritania and western Mali. It was renowned for its wealth in gold and control of trans-Saharan trade.

History

Founded by Soninke people, Ghana grew rich taxing gold and salt crossing the Sahara. Arab geographers such as al-Bakri (1068) described a large capital with royal and Muslim quarters. Its power waned after Almoravid incursions, drought, and shifting trade routes, and it was eventually absorbed by Sosso and then the Mali Empire under Sundiata Keita.

Economy and society

The king controlled gold nuggets while gold dust circulated. Ironworking, agriculture in the Sahel, and trade in salt, copper, cloth and slaves sustained the state. Despite its name, it is unrelated geographically to the modern Republic of Ghana, which adopted the name in honour of it.
