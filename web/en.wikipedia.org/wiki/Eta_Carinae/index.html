Eta Carinae

Distanceabout 7,500 light-years
Massabout 100–150 M☉ (system)
Luminosityabout 5 million L☉

Eta Carinae (η Carinae, abbreviated to η Car) is a stellar system containing at least two stars with a combined luminosity greater than five million times that of the Sun, located around 7,500 light-years distant in the constellation Carina. The primary is an extremely massive luminous blue variable, and its companion is a hot Wolf–Rayet or O-type star; the two orbit each other with a period of about 5.54 years.

The Great Eruption

Eta Carinae was first recorded by Edmond Halley in 1677 as a fourth-magnitude star. In the 19th century it underwent the "Great Eruption", peaking in 1843 as the second-brightest star in the sky despite its great distance. The eruption expelled more than ten solar masses of material, forming the bipolar Homunculus Nebula that surrounds the system today. A smaller "Little Eruption" occurred around 1890.

Properties and evolution

The primary star is one of the most massive and luminous stars known, radiating close to the Eddington limit and losing mass through a powerful stellar wind. Its extreme mass makes it a candidate to end its life as a hypernova or pair-instability supernova, and it is one of the nearest potential supernova progenitors to Earth—though even then it would pose no serious threat to life.

Observations

Eta Carinae lies within the Carina Nebula (NGC 3372) and is visible only from southern latitudes. Its periastron passages, when the companion plunges close to the primary every 5.54 years, produce dramatic changes in X-ray, radio and optical emission that are monitored intensively by astronomers.
