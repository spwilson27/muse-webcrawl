Faster-than-light

Faster-than-light (FTL) travel and communication are the conjectural propagation of matter or information at speeds greater than the speed of light (c), the speed at which light travels in a vacuum. The special theory of relativity implies that only particles with zero rest mass (i.e., photons) may travel at the speed of light, and that nothing may travel faster. As an object approaches the speed of light, its relativistic mass effectively increases without bound, so accelerating it beyond c would require infinite energy.

Superluminal phenomena

Some phenomena appear to exceed the speed of light without violating relativity: Cherenkov radiation, the phase velocity of waves in some media, the expansion of space itself in cosmology, and quantum entanglement correlations. None of these permits information to be transmitted faster than light. Cosmic expansion can carry distant galaxies apart at recession velocities greater than c, which is permitted because it is space, not an object, that expands.

Speculative concepts

Hypothetical routes to FTL include wormholes, the Alcubierre drive (a speculative warp drive that contracts space in front of a craft and expands it behind), and tachyons — hypothetical particles that always move faster than light. Each faces severe problems, including requirements for exotic matter with negative energy density and the prospect of causality violations such as closed timelike curves.

In fiction

FTL travel is a staple of science fiction, enabling interstellar settings in works such as Star Trek (warp drive) and Star Wars (hyperspace).
