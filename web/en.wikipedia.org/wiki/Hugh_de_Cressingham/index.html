Hugh de Cressingham (died 11 September 1297) was an English cleric and royal administrator who served as treasurer of the English administration in Scotland under King Edward I during the First War of Scottish Independence. His rapacious taxation and notorious parsimony made him one of the most hated symbols of English rule, and he was killed at the Battle of Stirling Bridge.

Treasurer of Scotland

A clerk in royal service, Cressingham was appointed treasurer of Scotland in 1296 after Edward I had deposed King John Balliol and imposed direct English administration, with John de Warenne, Earl of Surrey, as Guardian. Cressingham set about extracting revenue with energy, raising taxes and writing to the king boasting of the sums raised, while reportedly refusing to release funds for necessary military expenditure. Scottish chroniclers portrayed him as corrupt and corpulent, and even English sources concede his unpopularity.

Stirling Bridge and death

In September 1297 Cressingham accompanied Warenne's army north to suppress the rising of William Wallace and Andrew Moray. Against Cressingham's advice — he is said to have urged delay on grounds of cost, then insisted on crossing to save the expense of a longer campaign — the English began crossing the narrow wooden bridge over the River Forth at Stirling on 11 September. Wallace and Moray attacked when only part of the army had crossed, annihilating it. Cressingham was killed in the rout. Scottish tradition held that his body was flayed and his skin cut into trophies; the Lanercost Chronicle records that the Scots made a baldric for Wallace's sword from his skin.
