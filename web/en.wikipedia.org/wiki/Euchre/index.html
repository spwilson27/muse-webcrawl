Euchre

Euchre is a trick-taking card game played with a 24-card deck, most commonly by four players in two partnerships. It was immensely popular in the United States in the 19th century, where it was the country's dominant card game for a time, and it remains widely played in parts of the US, Canada, and elsewhere.

Rules

The deck consists of the 9, 10, jack, queen, king, and ace of each suit. Trump is determined by bidding on an upcard. Unusually, the jack of the trump suit ("right bower") and the jack of the same-colored suit ("left bower") rank as the two highest trumps. The team that calls trump must win at least three of the five tricks; if they fail, they are "euchred" and the opponents score. Lone hands, in which a player plays without their partner, offer extra rewards.

History and influence

Euchre derives from the Alsatian game Juckerspiel, brought to America by German-speaking immigrants. It is historically significant as the game that introduced the joker into the standard deck in the mid-19th century. Euchre influenced the development of other games, and its terminology survives in expressions such as "euchred" for being outwitted.
