Postdoctoral researcher

Postdoctoral researchers carry out much of the day-to-day research in academic laboratories

A postdoctoral researcher, often called a postdoc, is a person who undertakes professional research after completing a doctoral degree, typically a PhD. Postdoctoral positions are usually temporary appointments at universities, research institutes, government laboratories, or in industry, intended to deepen the researcher's expertise, build a publication record, and prepare them for an independent academic or research career. The position is commonly described as a "postdoctoral fellowship" when funded by a competitive fellowship, or a "postdoctoral research associate" appointment when funded through a supervisor's grant.

Role and duties

Postdocs design and conduct experiments or other scholarly investigations, analyse results, publish papers, present at conferences, and often help supervise graduate students. Many also write grant applications, teach occasional courses, and review manuscripts for journals. Although formally trainees or employees depending on the country and institution, postdocs are fully qualified researchers and produce a large share of the published output of academic science.

Duration and career structure

Appointments commonly last one to three years and may be renewed or followed by a second postdoc in another group or country. In the sciences, one or more postdoctoral appointments have become a near-requirement for obtaining a permanent faculty position. Critics of the system note that the expansion of doctoral training has produced far more postdocs than available permanent posts, leading to long periods in temporary employment, relatively low salaries compared with industry, and delayed career stability — a situation sometimes described as the "postdoc bottleneck".

By country

In the United States, postdocs may be classified as employees or as trainees supported by fellowships such as the National Institutes of Health's National Research Service Awards, which set stipend levels that many institutions follow. In the United Kingdom, postdoctoral researchers — often titled research associates or research fellows — are normally salaried employees on fixed-term contracts funded by research councils or charities. Similar structures exist across Europe, Australia, and East Asia, and international mobility between postdoctoral positions is common.

Advocacy

Postdoctoral associations, such as the National Postdoctoral Association in the United States, have campaigned for clearer employment status, better pay and benefits, structured mentoring, and improved career development support for researchers who leave academia. Surveys consistently show that while most postdocs value the research experience, many express concern about job security and work–life balance.
