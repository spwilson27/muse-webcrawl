Thermosetting polymer

A thermosetting polymer, often called a thermoset, is a polymer that is irreversibly hardened by curing from a soft solid or viscous liquid prepolymer or resin. Curing is induced by heat, radiation, or chemical reaction and produces extensive cross-linking between polymer chains, forming a rigid three-dimensional network. Once set, a thermoset cannot be melted and reshaped; on strong heating it degrades or chars instead of melting. This contrasts with thermoplastics, which soften reversibly when heated.

Curing

Thermosetting resins are typically supplied as low-molecular-weight liquids or moulding compounds. During cure, reactive groups combine — for example, an epoxy resin reacting with an amine hardener, or unsaturated polyester reacting with styrene in the presence of a peroxide catalyst — to build the cross-linked network. The process passes through a gel point at which the material becomes an insoluble solid; further curing raises the glass transition temperature and develops full mechanical properties.

Properties

Thermosets are generally harder, stiffer, and more heat- and chemical-resistant than thermoplastics, with good dimensional stability and electrical insulating properties. Their chief disadvantage is that they cannot be remoulded or easily recycled, and they tend to be brittle unless reinforced or toughened.

Examples and uses

Common thermosets include epoxy resins (adhesives, coatings, and composite matrices), phenol-formaldehyde resins such as Bakelite (electrical fittings, brake components), melamine formaldehyde (laminates and tableware), urea formaldehyde (adhesives for plywood and particleboard), unsaturated polyesters (glass-reinforced plastic for boats and panels), polyurethanes in their cross-linked forms (foams, elastomers), and vulcanised rubber. Fibre-reinforced thermoset composites are widely used in aerospace, automotive, marine, and construction applications.

History

The first fully synthetic plastic, Bakelite, patented by Leo Baekeland in 1907, was a thermoset phenolic resin. Thermosets dominated the early plastics industry before the large-scale development of commodity thermoplastics in the mid-20th century.
