Philosophy of mind is the branch of Philosophy that studies the nature of the mind, mental events and functions, consciousness, and their relationship to the body and brain. Its central issue is the mind–body problem: how can subjective experience — the felt quality of seeing red or feeling pain, called qualia — arise from physical processes?

Major positions

Descartes defended substance dualism, holding mind and body to be distinct kinds of substance. 20th-century behaviourism tried to define mental states by behaviour; identity theory identified them with brain states; functionalism, associated with Hilary Putnam, defines them by causal role and underlies much of cognitive science and thinking about artificial intelligence. Eliminative materialists argue folk psychology will be replaced by neuroscience.

Consciousness

Thomas Nagel's 'What is it like to be a bat?' (1974) and David Chalmers' 'hard problem of consciousness' press the difficulty of explaining subjective experience physically. Thought experiments such as the Chinese room (John Searle) and philosophical zombies structure ongoing debate.
