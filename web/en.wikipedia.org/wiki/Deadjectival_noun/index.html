Deadjectival noun

FieldMorphology (linguistics)
MeaningA noun derived from an adjective

A deadjectival noun is a noun that is derived from an adjective. Deadjectival derivation is a common word-formation process: English examples include happiness from happy, kindness from kind, and the rich used collectively for rich people. The term parallels deverbal, describing nouns derived from verbs.

Formation

Languages form deadjectival nouns by affixation (for example English -ness and -ity), by conversion with no change of form, or by adding a definite article to an adjective used substantively, as in German der Alte ("the old man") or Spanish los pobres ("the poor").

Semantics

Such nouns typically denote the quality or state named by the adjective (abstract nouns such as redness), or a person or group characterised by the property (such as the accused or the French). In some languages, including Japanese, the boundary between adjectives and nouns is less sharp, and adjective-derived nominal forms play a larger grammatical role.
