Nijinsky (21 February 1967 – 15 April 1992) was a Canadian-bred, Irish-trained Thoroughbred racehorse and sire. In 1970 he became the first colt in 35 years to win the English Triple Crown, and he remained the last to do so for more than half a century.

Nijinsky (horse)

Nijinsky (1967–1992) was a champion Thoroughbred racehorse, bred in Canada, trained in Ireland by Vincent O'Brien at Ballydoyle, and ridden in his classic victories by Lester Piggott. Named after the ballet dancer Vaslav Nijinsky, he was a son of Northern Dancer out of Flaming Page.

Racing career

Unbeaten in five races as a two-year-old in 1969, including the Dewhurst Stakes, Nijinsky dominated the 1970 season: he won the 2,000 Guineas at Newmarket, the Derby at Epsom by two and a half lengths, the Irish Derby, and the King George VI and Queen Elizabeth Stakes against older horses. In September 1970 he won the St Leger at Doncaster to complete the English Triple Crown — the first since Bahram in 1935. A bout of ringworm interrupted his preparation for the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe, in which he was beaten a head by Sassafrás; he was retired after a disappointing run in the Champion Stakes.

Stud career and legacy

Sold to a syndicate for a then-record sum, Nijinsky stood at Claiborne Farm in Kentucky and became an outstanding sire, fathering 155 stakes winners including Derby winner Golden Fleece, Lammtarra, and the influential Ferdinand and Nureyev's generation of sires; he was North America's champion broodmare sire at his peak. He was the subject of the 1970 film A Horse Called Nijinsky, and The Observer and racing authorities alike rank him among the greatest racehorses of the twentieth century.
