Arthur Oncken Lovejoy (October 10, 1873 – December 30, 1962) was an American philosopher and intellectual historian, regarded as a founder of the discipline of the history of ideas. He taught for most of his career at Johns Hopkins University, where he helped establish the History of Ideas Club and the Journal of the History of Ideas (1940).

Work

Lovejoy's best-known book, The Great Chain of Being: A Study of the History of an Idea (1936), based on his William James Lectures at Harvard, traced the idea of a hierarchical order of all existence — the "great chain of being" — from Plato and Aristotle through medieval and early modern thought. His method of analysing complex doctrines into "unit-ideas" shaped generations of intellectual historians, even as later scholars criticised the approach as overly atomistic.

Other activities

Lovejoy was active in academic freedom causes. As an early member of the American Association of University Professors, he chaired the committee whose work led to the AAUP's 1915 Declaration of Principles on academic freedom and tenure. His other works include Primitivism and Related Ideas in Antiquity (1935, with George Boas) and Essays in the History of Ideas (1948).
