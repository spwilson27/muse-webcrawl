USS Saratoga (CV-3)

USS Saratoga (CV-3) was a Lexington-class aircraft carrier of the United States Navy, converted from a battlecruiser hull under the Washington Naval Treaty and commissioned in 1927. Affectionately called "Sister Sara", she was one of the Navy's first large carriers and a pioneer of carrier aviation.

Interwar service

Together with her sister Lexington, Saratoga demonstrated the potential of carriers in the Fleet Problem exercises of the 1930s, including simulated surprise attacks on the Panama Canal and Pearl Harbor.

World War II

Torp­edoed by a Japanese submarine in January 1942 and again later that year, Saratoga nevertheless supported the Guadalcanal landings, the Eastern Solomons battle, operations in the Solomon Islands, and the invasions of Iwo Jima and Okinawa. In 1945 she was severely damaged by kamikaze attacks off Iwo Jima. By war's end she was among the oldest carriers afloat and had landed aircraft more times than almost any other carrier.

Fate

Surplus after the war, Saratoga was expended in the Operation Crossroads nuclear tests at Bikini Atoll, sinking after the underwater Baker test on 25 July 1946. Her wreck is now a popular scuba diving site.
