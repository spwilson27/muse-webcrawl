Wrigley Building

The Wrigley Building is a skyscraper located at 400–410 North Michigan Avenue in the Near North Side community area of Chicago, Illinois, United States. It was built to house the corporate headquarters of the Wm. Wrigley Jr. Company, the chewing gum manufacturer. The south tower was completed in 1921 and the north tower in 1924, designed by the architectural firm of Graham, Anderson, Probst & White using the shape of the Giralda tower of Seville Cathedral combined with French Renaissance details.

Architecture

The two towers are clad in glazed terra-cotta in six shades of white that brighten toward the top, giving the building its luminous appearance; the clock tower's four dials are each 19 feet 7 inches (5.97 m) in diameter. The towers are connected by walkways at the third and fourteenth floors and by a sky bridge added at the thirty-first floor in 1931. When built, the Wrigley Building was the first major office building north of the Chicago River and helped spur development of the Magnificent Mile. It was also one of the first Chicago office buildings to be floodlit at night.

History

Chewing gum magnate William Wrigley Jr. chose the site, which was the first to be developed under the Michigan Avenue improvement plan. The Wrigley Company sold the building in 2011 to a group including BDT Capital Partners; Zeller Realty Group acquired it in 2018. The building underwent a major renovation completed around 2021, adding amenities while preserving its historic fabric. It remains a Chicago landmark and a designated contributing property within the Michigan–Wacker Historic District.
