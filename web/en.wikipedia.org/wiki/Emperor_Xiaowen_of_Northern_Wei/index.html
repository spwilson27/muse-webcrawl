Emperor Xiaowen of Northern Wei

Emperor Xiaowen of Northern Wei (467 – 26 April 499), personal name Tuoba Hong (later Yuan Hong), was an emperor of the Northern Wei dynasty of China, reigning from 471 to 499. Of Xianbei descent, he is famed for an aggressive programme of sinicization intended to integrate his nomadic-origin dynasty into Chinese civilization.

Sinicization reforms

Guided early in his reign by his grandmother Empress Dowager Feng, and ruling personally from 490, Xiaowen moved the capital from Pingcheng to Luoyang in 494, closer to the Chinese heartland. He ordered the Xianbei to adopt Chinese surnames (Tuoba became Yuan), wear Chinese dress, speak Chinese at court, and intermarry with Chinese aristocratic families. He also implemented the equal-field land system and the three-elders administrative system.

Assessment

The reforms strengthened the state in the short term and shaped northern Chinese institutions later inherited by Sui and Tang, but they alienated the Xianbei military garrisons on the northern frontier, whose resentment eventually erupted in the Rebellion of the Six Garrisons that destroyed the dynasty.
