UK drill

UK drill
Stylistic originsChicago drill, grime, UK garage, road rap
Cultural originsEarly 2010s, Brixton, London, England
Typical tempoAround 140 BPM (with sliding 808 patterns)

UK drill is a regional subgenre of drill music and road rap that originated in the South London district of Brixton in the early 2010s. Drawing influence from the American drill scene in Chicago, most notably from chief proponents such as Chief Keef, the genre is sonically distinct in its use of sliding 808 bass, sparse dark piano or string melodies, and syncopated hi-hats at around 140 beats per minute.

History

Groups such as 150, 67, and Section Boyz pioneered the sound in the mid-2010s, distributing music through YouTube and platforms such as Link Up TV and Pressplay. The genre broke into the mainstream in the late 2010s and early 2020s, with artists including Headie One, Digga D, Unknown T, Central Cee, and Russ Millions achieving UK chart success; Tion Wayne and Russ Millions' "Body" became the first drill song to top the UK Singles Chart in 2021.

Characteristics and controversy

Lyrically, UK drill often features gritty, confrontational depictions of street life delivered in London slang and Multicultural London English. The genre has been controversial: London's Metropolitan Police have associated some drill videos with gang violence and have requested the removal of videos from YouTube, and some artists have been subject to court orders restricting their music. Defenders argue drill is a creative outlet documenting artists' environments and that censorship disproportionately targets young Black musicians.

Influence

The UK drill sound has been adopted internationally, influencing Brooklyn drill in New York and scenes in the Netherlands, France, Australia, and elsewhere, and its rhythmic signature has permeated mainstream pop and hip hop production.
