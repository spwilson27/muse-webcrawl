The American Soccer League (ASL) was the first truly professional soccer league in the United States, operating from 1921 to 1933. Centred on the industrial towns of the Northeast, it attracted strong teams, large crowds and many of the best players from Scotland and Europe, and is regarded as a golden age of early American soccer.

History

The league was formed in 1921 by clubs from the National Association Football League and the Southern New England Soccer League, with backing from textile mills and other industrial sponsors. Teams such as Fall River F.C., Bethlehem Steel, the New York Giants and the Brooklyn Wanderers drew substantial attendances, and star players like Archie Stark, who scored a record 67 goals in the 1924–25 season, became household names in their communities.

The soccer war and decline

In 1928 the league became embroiled in the "Soccer War" with the United States Football Association over participation in the National Challenge Cup and control of the sport. The dispute, combined with the onset of the Great Depression, drove clubs out of business; a diminished second ASL emerged briefly before the original league finally collapsed in 1933. A new, semi-professional American Soccer League took the name the same year and lasted until 1983.

Legacy

Despite its collapse, the ASL demonstrated the commercial potential of professional soccer in the United States decades before the modern era, and many of its players formed the backbone of the United States team that reached the semi-finals of the first World Cup in 1930.
