Caribbean Netherlands

The Caribbean Netherlands (Dutch: Caribisch Nederland) is the collective term for the three special municipalities of the Netherlands located in the Caribbean Sea: Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba — the "BES islands". They became part of the Netherlands proper on 10 October 2010, when the Netherlands Antilles was dissolved; Aruba, Curaçao and Sint Maarten instead became separate constituent countries within the Kingdom of the Netherlands.

Bonaire lies off Venezuela; Saba and Sint Eustatius lie east of Puerto Rico.

Governance

Each island is a openbaar lichaam (public body) governed by an island council and executive council headed by a lieutenant governor, analogous to a Dutch municipality but with exceptions in law. Many Dutch statutes were introduced gradually rather than at once to avoid disruption; the U.S. dollar replaced the Netherlands Antillean guilder in 2011, and Dutch social-security, health and education systems were progressively extended, though benefit levels remain lower than in the European Netherlands. The National Office for the Caribbean Netherlands (RCN) delivers central-government services locally. Residents vote in Dutch national and European Parliament elections.

Geography and economy

Bonaire, in the Leeward Antilles near Venezuela, is arid, ringed by a famous marine park, and its economy rests on tourism (especially diving), the BOPEC oil terminal historically, and solar salt production. Saba and Sint Eustatius, in the northern Lesser Antilles, are volcanic and mountainous — Mount Scenery on Saba (887 m) is the highest point of the entire Kingdom of the Netherlands. Statia hosts an oil transshipment terminal; Saba's economy centres on tourism and the Saba University School of Medicine. In 2017 Hurricane Irma caused severe damage on Saba and Sint Eustatius.

Debates

Incorporation brought improvements in healthcare and infrastructure but also complaints about rising costs, the imposition of Dutch laws on issues such as same-sex marriage and euthanasia that had not applied under the Antilles, and a persistent welfare gap. Referendums and evaluations since 2010 have produced mixed verdicts, and the islands' status remains a live political question in The Hague and on the islands.
