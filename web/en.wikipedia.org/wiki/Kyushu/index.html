Kyushu

Kyushu (九州, Kyūshū, "Nine Provinces") is the third-largest island of Japan's four main islands and the most southerly of them, excepting the Ryukyu Islands. In the past it has been known as Kyūkoku ("Nine Countries"), Chinzei, and Tsukushi-no-shima. The island's name refers to the nine ancient provinces into which it was formerly divided. As of 2018, Kyushu has a population of about 14.3 million.

Geography

Kyushu is mountainous and volcanic: Mount Aso is the largest active volcano in Japan and among the largest in the world. The island is separated from Honshu by the Kanmon Straits (crossed by tunnels and the Kanmon Bridge) and from Shikoku and the Ryukyus by the surrounding seas. Major cities include Fukuoka, Kitakyushu, Kumamoto, Nagasaki, and Kagoshima. The climate is humid subtropical, with warm, rainy summers and heavy precipitation from the tsuyu rainy season and typhoons.

History and economy

Kyushu has long been Japan's gateway to Asia: the Dazaifu government administered foreign relations, Mongol invasions were repelled here in 1274 and 1281, Portuguese traders and missionaries first landed in the 1540s, and Nagasaki's Dejima was Japan's sole window to Europe during the Edo period. In the Second World War, Nagasaki was destroyed by the second atomic bomb on 9 August 1945. Today "Silicon Island" hosts a large share of Japan's semiconductor industry, alongside steel, shipbuilding, agriculture (including shōchū and sweet potatoes), and tourism centred on hot springs such as Beppu and Yufuin.
