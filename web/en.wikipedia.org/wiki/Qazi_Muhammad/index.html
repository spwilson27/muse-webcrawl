Qazi Muhammad (1 May 1893 – 31 March 1947) was an Iranian Kurdish religious leader and politician who served as the president of the short-lived Republic of Mahabad (1946–1947), the first Kurdish attempt at an independent state in the 20th century. A respected qadi (Islamic judge) in Mahabad, he emerged as the political leader of the Kurdish national movement in northwestern Iran.

Republic of Mahabad

Following the Anglo-Soviet occupation of Iran in 1941 and the power vacuum left by the retreating Iranian army, Soviet-backed Kurdish activists founded the Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan (Komala-ye Žiyanewey Kurdistan). On 22 January 1946, the Republic of Mahabad was proclaimed in Mahabad's central square, with Qazi Muhammad elected president. The republic introduced Kurdish-language education and a Kurdish press, and maintained a small army under Mustafa Barzani.

Collapse and execution

The republic depended on Soviet protection; when the USSR withdrew under international pressure in late 1946, Iranian forces re-entered the region. Qazi Muhammad refused to flee, remaining in Mahabad, and was arrested along with his brother Sadr Qazi and cousin Saif Qazi. After a military tribunal, all three were hanged in Mahabad's Chahar Cheragh Square on 31 March 1947. He is widely honoured as a martyr of the Kurdish national cause.
