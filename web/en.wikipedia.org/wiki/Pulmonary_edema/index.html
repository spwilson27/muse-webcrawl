Pulmonary edema

Pulmonary edema (American English), or pulmonary oedema (British English), is abnormal fluid accumulation in the tissue and air spaces of the lungs. It leads to impaired gas exchange and may cause respiratory failure. It is due either to failure of the left ventricle of the heart to remove blood adequately from the pulmonary circulation ("cardiogenic pulmonary edema") or to an injury to the lung tissue or vessels ("noncardiogenic pulmonary edema").

Signs and symptoms

The most common symptom is progressively worsening shortness of breath, which may be sudden. Other features include breathlessness when lying flat (orthopnea), waking at night gasping (paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea), cough producing pink frothy sputum, wheezing, and crackles heard on auscultation. In severe cases there is sweating, anxiety, pallor and, untreated, death by asphyxiation.

Causes

Cardiogenic causes include heart failure, myocardial infarction, severe hypertension and valvular disease. Noncardiogenic causes include acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), kidney failure with fluid overload, inhaled toxins, opioid overdose, and high-altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE).

Treatment

Treatment focuses on reversing the cause and supporting oxygenation: supplemental oxygen, sitting the patient upright, loop diuretics such as furosemide, nitrates for hypertension, and non-invasive or invasive ventilation when needed.
