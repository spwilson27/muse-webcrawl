Duple and quadruple metre

Duple metre and quadruple metre are metres in music characterised by a primary division of the beat into groups of two or four, respectively. Duple metre has two beats per bar, as in 2/2 (cut time) or 2/4, the metre of polkas and marches. Quadruple metre has four beats per bar; its most common form, 4/4, is known as common time and is the most prevalent metre in Western popular music, including rock, pop, and the blues.

Characteristics

In simple duple metre (2/4), each beat divides into two eighth notes; in compound duple metre (6/8), there are two beats, each a dotted quarter note dividing into three eighth notes. Quadruple metres likewise divide into simple (4/4) and compound (12/8) forms. The hierarchy of accents distinguishes them from two bars of duple metre: in 4/4, the first beat is strongest and the third beat carries a secondary accent, giving a strong–weak–medium–weak pattern. March music, hymn tunes, country music, and most dance music rely on these metres because their regular, even groupings suit walking, marching, and dancing steps.
