Traffic (band)

Origin: Birmingham, England
Years active: 1967–1969, 1970–1974, 1994, 2004
Genres: Progressive rock, psychedelic rock, folk rock, jazz rock
Key members: Steve Winwood, Dave Mason, Chris Wood, Jim Capaldi

Traffic were an English rock band formed in Birmingham in April 1967 by Steve Winwood, Jim Capaldi, Chris Wood and Dave Mason. They began as a psychedelic rock group and diversified their sound through instruments such as keyboards like the Mellotron and harpsichord, sitar, and various reed instruments, and by incorporating jazz and improvisational techniques.

History

The band's debut single, "Paper Sun", was a UK hit in 1967, followed by Mason's "Hole in My Shoe". Their debut album Mr. Fantasy (1967) and the follow-up Traffic (1968) established them in both Britain and the United States. Mason's departures and returns, and Winwood's temporary disbanding of the group in 1969 to form Blind Faith, interrupted their run; Winwood, Capaldi and Wood re-formed Traffic in 1970.

The 1970s albums John Barleycorn Must Die (1970), The Low Spark of High Heeled Boys (1971) — whose title track became a US album-rock staple — and Shoot Out at the Fantasy Factory (1973) shifted the band toward progressive rock and jazz-influenced improvisation. Traffic broke up in 1974 following the album When the Eagle Flies; Chris Wood died in 1983.

Winwood and Capaldi revived the name for the album Far from Home (1994) and a tour. Traffic were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2004. Jim Capaldi died in 2005.
