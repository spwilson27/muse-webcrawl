Vicksburg is a historic city in, and the county seat of, Warren County, Mississippi, United States. It is located on a high bluff on the east bank of the Mississippi River across from Louisiana. Vicksburg was built by French colonists in 1719, and the outpost withstood an attack from the native Natchez people. Founded in 1811 by Newitt Vick and incorporated in 1825, the city grew as a river port and commercial center for the surrounding cotton-growing region.

The city is best known as the site of the Siege of Vicksburg during the American Civil War, a 47-day Union siege that ended with the Confederate surrender on July 4, 1863. The fall of Vicksburg, together with the Union victory at Gettysburg the previous day, is widely regarded as a turning point of the war, giving the Union full control of the Mississippi River.

History

The Vicksburg area was first inhabited by Native American peoples, including the Yazoo and Tunica. French explorers founded Fort Saint Pierre nearby in 1719. The settlement that became Vicksburg was established on land owned by Methodist minister Newitt Vick, who died in 1819 before his plan for a town was fully realized; his heirs platted the town in 1824, and it was named in his honor.

During the Civil War, Vicksburg's position atop bluffs commanding a bend in the Mississippi River made it a vital Confederate stronghold — President Jefferson Davis called it "the nailhead that holds the South's two halves together." Union general Ulysses S. Grant conducted a brilliant campaign in the spring of 1863, crossing the river below the city, defeating Confederate forces at Port Gibson, Raymond, Jackson, Champion Hill, and the Big Black River, and then besieging the city. After weeks of bombardment and a failed pair of assaults, Confederate general John C. Pemberton surrendered on July 4, 1863.

Geography and economy

Vicksburg sits where the Mississippi River meets the Yazoo River's historic channel. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers diverted the Yazoo in 1903, creating the Yazoo Diversion Canal, which restored Vicksburg's waterfront. The city is home to the Engineer Research and Development Center (ERDC) of the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, one of the city's largest employers. Riverboat casinos, tourism centered on the Vicksburg National Military Park, and manufacturing also support the local economy.

Landmarks

  • Vicksburg National Military Park, preserving the siege battlefield and the USS Cairo gunboat museum
  • The Old Warren County Courthouse (1858), a National Historic Landmark
  • The McRaven House, one of Mississippi's most famous historic homes
  • The Mississippi River bridges, including the Old Vicksburg Bridge (1930)
