British Library

The British Library is the national library of the United Kingdom and one of the largest libraries in the world. Its collection is estimated at over 170–200 million items, in many languages and formats, including books, journals, newspapers, magazines, sound recordings, patents, maps, prints, drawings and manuscripts. As a legal deposit library it receives copies of all books produced in the United Kingdom and Ireland, and adds some three million items every year, occupying over 9.5 miles (15.3 km) of new shelf space annually.

History

The British Library was created by the British Library Act 1972, which separated it from the British Museum, with which it shared a building in Bloomsbury from 1753. Its core collections were the museum's library departments, built on the collections of Sir Hans Sloane, the Cotton library and the Harley collection, plus the Royal manuscripts presented by George II. In 1997 the library moved to its purpose-built home at St Pancras, designed by Colin St John Wilson — the largest UK public building constructed in the 20th century, and now a Grade I listed building.

Collections

Treasures include two copies of the Gutenberg Bible, the Lindisfarne Gospels, the Codex Sinaiticus, a first folio of Shakespeare, Magna Carta, Leonardo da Vinci's notebook, Beatles manuscripts, and the world's earliest dated printed book, the Diamond Sutra (868 AD). The library also holds the national newspaper collection and an extensive sound archive of over a million discs and 185,000 tapes.

Services

The St Pancras site has eleven reading rooms serving researchers with a Reader Pass. Its Boston Spa site in Yorkshire houses the document supply service and additional storage, including a robotic automated store. In October 2023 the library suffered a major ransomware attack (attributed to the Rhysida group) that took its online catalogue and digital services offline for months.
