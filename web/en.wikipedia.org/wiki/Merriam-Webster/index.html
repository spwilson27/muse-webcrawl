Merriam-Webster, Inc. is an American company that publishes reference books and is known especially for its dictionaries. Founded in Springfield, Massachusetts, in 1831 by brothers George and Charles Merriam, it is the oldest dictionary publisher in the United States.

History

In 1843, after the death of Noah Webster, the Merriam brothers purchased the unsold copies and revision rights to Webster's An American Dictionary of the English Language (1828). Their 1847 revision began the long line of "Webster's" dictionaries published by the company. The name Merriam-Webster was adopted in 1982; before that the company was known as G. & C. Merriam Co.

The company's flagship works include Webster's Third New International Dictionary (1961), whose descriptivist approach provoked public controversy, and Merriam-Webster's Collegiate Dictionary, now in its eleventh edition. Since 1964 Merriam-Webster has been a subsidiary of Encyclopædia Britannica, Inc.

Online presence

Merriam-Webster operates a widely used free online dictionary and thesaurus, and publishes a popular "Word of the Day" feature. Its annual "Word of the Year" announcements receive broad media coverage.
