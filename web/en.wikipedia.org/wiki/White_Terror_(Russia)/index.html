White Terror (Russia)

The White Terror in Russia refers to acts of violence and mass repression carried out by the White Army and allied forces during the Russian Civil War (1917–1923) against supporters and suspected supporters of the Bolsheviks — as well as, in many regions, pogroms against Jewish communities. It was the counterpart to the Bolshevik Red Terror, although unlike the Red Terror it was not a centrally organised state policy; White forces were fragmented among rival governments and warlords.

Course

In Finland's linked 1918 civil war, victorious Whites executed or starved in camps some 12,000–14,000 Reds. Within Russia, Admiral Kolchak's regime in Siberia, General Denikin's forces in the south, and Cossack atamans such as Grigory Semyonov and the "Bloody Baron" Roman von Ungern-Sternberg perpetrated shootings of prisoners, hostage-taking, collective punishments of villages and punitive expeditions. Anti-Bolshevik forces and local militias in Ukraine carried out pogroms in 1919 that killed tens of thousands of Jews — estimates range widely, from about 50,000 upward.

Scale and debate

Historians debate the White Terror's toll: figures from tens of thousands to several hundred thousand victims are proposed for executions and pogroms combined, complicated by the Whites' decentralisation and by Soviet propaganda. What is clear is that White violence, together with antisemitic pogroms, alienated peasants and minorities and contributed to the Whites' failure to build a viable alternative to Bolshevik rule.
