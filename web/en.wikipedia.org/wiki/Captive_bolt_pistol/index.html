Captive bolt pistol

A captive bolt pistol (also called a cattle gun, stunbolt gun, bolt gun, or stunner) is a device used for stunning animals before slaughter. It fires a retractable metal bolt, driven by a blank cartridge or compressed air, into the animal's forehead, causing immediate unconsciousness through concussion and brain damage. Because the bolt never leaves the device — it is "captive" — the tool is safer for operators than a free-bullet firearm.

Types

Penetrating captive bolts enter the skull and disrupt the brain, and are standard for cattle; non-penetrating (mushroom-head) devices deliver a concussive blow without penetration and are used for poultry, pigs, and in settings — such as some halal and shechita-supervised plants and for disease-control culls — where brain destruction or death before bleeding is a concern. Modern pistols are powered by blank cartridges of various strengths or by pneumatic lines in high-throughput abattoirs.

Regulation and welfare

Stunning before slaughter is legally required in the European Union, the United Kingdom, and many other jurisdictions, with limited religious exemptions. After stunning, the animal is bled ("stuck") so that death results from blood loss. Poorly maintained equipment or inaccurate placement can fail to stun, so regulations require backup devices and operator training.

In culture

The device is prominently featured as the weapon of Anton Chigurh in the novel and film No Country for Old Men.
