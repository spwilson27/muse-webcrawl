Tenor horn

The tenor horn (called the alto horn in North America) is a brass instrument in the saxhorn family, pitched in E♭, with a predominantly conical bore, three valves, and an upright bell. It is a standard member of the British brass band, where the tenor horn section supplies the middle harmony voices.

Characteristics

The instrument sounds a major sixth below written pitch and reads treble clef in brass band usage. Its mellow tone sits between the cornets and the baritones and euphoniums. Solos for the tenor horn appear in major brass band test pieces, and composers such as Gustav Holst wrote for it in military band works like the suites for military band.

History

The tenor horn developed in the mid-19th century from Adolphe Sax's saxhorns and became entrenched in the instrumentation of British-style brass bands, which typically field three tenor horns: solo, first, and second.
