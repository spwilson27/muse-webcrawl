The United States dollar (symbol: $; currency code: USD; also abbreviated US$) is the official currency of the United States and several other countries. The Coinage Act of 1792 established the dollar as the standard unit of money, divided into 100 cents, taking the name from the Spanish dollar (pieces of eight) widely used in early America.

Global role

The dollar is the world's primary reserve currency and the most widely used currency in international transactions. A majority of U.S. banknotes in circulation are held outside the United States. Several countries, including Ecuador, El Salvador, and Panama, use the dollar as their official currency, a process known as dollarization.

Issuance

Coins are produced by the United States Mint and banknotes by the Bureau of Engraving and Printing; the Federal Reserve issues Federal Reserve Notes, which constitute most paper currency. Current denominations range from the $1 to the $100 bill.

History

The dollar was initially defined in silver and later gold under bimetallic and gold standards. The Bretton Woods system (1944) made the dollar convertible to gold for foreign governments; President Nixon ended convertibility in 1971, moving the dollar to a fiat system backed by the U.S. economy.
