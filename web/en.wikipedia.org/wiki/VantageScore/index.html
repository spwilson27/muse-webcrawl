VantageScore


The VantageScore scale, 300–850

VantageScore is a consumer credit rating product developed by VantageScore Solutions, LLC, a joint venture of the three major credit bureaus—Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. Announced in March 2006, it was created to compete with the dominant FICO score produced by Fair Isaac Corporation. Unlike FICO, which uses separate bureau-specific models, VantageScore uses a single model that can be applied consistently to data from any of the three bureaus.

Score range and factors

The first two versions of VantageScore used a scale of 501–990, with letter grades resembling academic grades. VantageScore 3.0 (2013) adopted the familiar 300–850 scale used by FICO, making scores easier for consumers to interpret. The model weighs factors including payment history (the most influential), age and type of credit, credit utilization, total balances and debt, recent credit behavior and inquiries, and available credit.

Versions

VantageScore 4.0 (2017) incorporated trended data—information about how balances and payments change over time—and used machine-learning techniques to better score consumers with sparse or "thin" credit files, including those with less than six months of credit history. VantageScore scores are widely distributed to consumers free of charge through personal-finance websites and are used by many lenders, though FICO scores remain more common in mortgage underwriting.

Regulatory developments

In 2022 the Federal Housing Finance Agency announced that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac would validate and approve credit score models including VantageScore 4.0 for use in mortgage lending, with a transition toward requiring two scores (FICO 10T and VantageScore 4.0) from lenders—an important step in VantageScore's adoption in the mortgage market.
