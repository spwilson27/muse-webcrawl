Articles of association

Articles of association of a registered company

The articles of association of a company are its principal internal governing document: the written rules about how the company is to be run, agreed by the shareholders or guarantors, the directors and the company secretary. Together with the memorandum of association (where one exists), they form the company's constitution under the companies legislation of the United Kingdom and many other Commonwealth and common-law jurisdictions.

Contents

Articles typically regulate:

  • the issue, classes and transfer of shares, and variation of class rights;
  • directors' appointment, powers, duties, meetings and removal;
  • general meetings of members, notice, quorum and voting (including proxies);
  • dividends and capitalisation of profits;
  • borrowing powers, accounts and audit;
  • procedures on winding up.

In the United Kingdom, companies that do not register their own articles are governed by statutory model articles prescribed for private and public companies under the Companies Act 2006. Many companies adopt bespoke articles, particularly to entrench shareholder arrangements or special share rights.

Legal effect

Under section 33 of the Companies Act 2006, the provisions of a company's constitution bind the company and its members as if they were covenants signed by each of them. Articles are a public document, filed at Companies House, and may generally be altered by special resolution of the members (requiring a 75% majority), subject to entrenchment provisions and to the requirement that alterations be made in good faith for the benefit of the company as a whole.

Equivalent documents

The closest equivalents elsewhere include the bylaws of a United States corporation (together with its certificate of incorporation), the Satzung of a German company, and the statuts of a French company. Company law in India, Hong Kong, Singapore, Australia (where the document is now simply the "constitution") and many other jurisdictions likewise uses articles of association or a direct descendant of the concept.
