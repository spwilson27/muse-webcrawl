Kushinagar is a town in the Kushinagar district of the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh. It is an important Buddhist pilgrimage site, where Buddhists believe Gautama Buddha attained parinirvana after his death. It is one of the four most sacred sites in Buddhism, together with Lumbini, Bodh Gaya and Sarnath.

History

In ancient times the settlement was known as Kushavati, a capital of the Malla republic. According to Buddhist tradition, the Buddha, aged eighty and ill, travelled to Kushinagar and died between twin sal trees, attaining final nirvana. Emperor Ashoka later patronised the site, and the Chinese pilgrims Faxian and Xuanzang visited and described its monasteries and stupas. The site was largely lost after the decline of Buddhism in India and was re-identified through archaeological excavations in the 19th century by Alexander Cunningham and others.

Sites

The Mahaparinirvana Temple houses a celebrated reclining statue of the Buddha, over six metres long, carved from red sandstone and dating to the Gupta period. Nearby stands the Ramabhar Stupa, marking the traditional place of the Buddha's cremation. Temples and monasteries built by Buddhist communities from many countries — including Myanmar, China, Japan, Thailand, Sri Lanka and Tibet — surround the main shrines, along with a museum displaying artefacts from the excavations.

Transport

Kushinagar is connected by road to Gorakhpur, about 50 km away. An international airport at Kushinagar was inaugurated in 2021 to serve Buddhist pilgrimage tourism on the regional Buddhist circuit.
