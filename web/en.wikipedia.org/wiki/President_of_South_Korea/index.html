President of South Korea

The president of South Korea, officially the president of the Republic of Korea, is the head of state and head of government of South Korea, as well as commander-in-chief of the armed forces. The president is directly elected by popular vote for a single, non-renewable five-year term. The presidency is one of the most powerful executive offices in the democratic world, with authority over appointments, administration and foreign policy conducted under the 1987 constitution of the Sixth Republic.

Powers and role

The president appoints the prime minister (with National Assembly consent), ministers, and judges of the Constitutional Court, issues decrees, may declare emergency measures and martial law (subject to Assembly revocation), grants pardons, and represents the state internationally. Since 2022 the presidential office has been located at the defence ministry compound in Yongsan, Seoul, after Yoon Suk Yeol declined to move into the traditional Blue House (Cheong Wa Dae).

History

The office dates to 1948 under Syngman Rhee. Its powers and mode of election changed repeatedly through periods of authoritarian rule under Park Chung Hee and Chun Doo-hwan, when presidents were selected indirectly. Direct elections were restored in 1987 after mass pro-democracy protests.

Recent history

Several former presidents have faced prosecution after leaving office. Park Geun-hye was impeached and removed in 2017. Yoon Suk Yeol's declaration of martial law on 3 December 2024 led to his impeachment by the National Assembly; the Constitutional Court upheld his removal in April 2025, and Lee Jae-myung won the ensuing election in June 2025.
