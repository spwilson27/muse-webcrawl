Setmelanotide

Setmelanotide, sold under the brand name Imcivree, is a medication used to treat certain rare genetic disorders of obesity. It is a melanocortin-4 receptor (MC4R) agonist. It was developed by Rhythm Pharmaceuticals and approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in November 2020.

Medical uses

Setmelanotide is indicated for chronic weight management in adults and children aged six years and older with obesity due to confirmed genetic variants that impair the melanocortin-4 receptor pathway, specifically:

  • Proopiomelanocortin (POMC) deficiency
  • Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 1 (PCSK1) deficiency
  • Leptin receptor (LEPR) deficiency

Its approval was later extended to Bardet–Biedl syndrome. In these conditions, impaired signalling along the pathway between the leptin receptor and the MC4 receptor produces severe hyperphagia (insatiable hunger) and early-onset obesity; setmelanotide bypasses the defective upstream components by directly activating MC4R.

Mechanism of action

Setmelanotide is a cyclic peptide that acts as an agonist at the melanocortin-4 receptor in the hypothalamus, a key regulator of hunger, satiety and energy expenditure. Activation of MC4R restores signalling that is deficient in patients whose genetic defects interrupt the normal leptin–POMC pathway.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include injection site reactions, skin hyperpigmentation (darkening), nausea, vomiting, headache, diarrhoea and spontaneous penile erections. Setmelanotide also carries a warning regarding serious depression and suicidal ideation, and sexual adverse effects are a recognised class concern because it also interacts with other melanocortin receptors.

History

Setmelanotide received orphan drug and breakthrough therapy designations before its 2020 FDA approval as the first therapy for obesity caused by POMC, PCSK1 and LEPR deficiency. It was approved in the European Union in 2021.
