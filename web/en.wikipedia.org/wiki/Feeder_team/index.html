Feeder team

A feeder team is a sports team, typically in association football, basketball or ice hockey, that is closely affiliated with a larger, usually more senior or more prestigious club and serves primarily to develop young or reserve players for that parent club. Players gain competitive experience at the feeder club before being promoted, recalled on loan, or sold to the parent club.

Concept

Feeder arrangements are formalised through affiliation agreements, shared ownership, or informal partnerships. In some countries, reserve teams play in the same league pyramid as senior clubs; in others, such as Spain, B teams of major clubs compete in the professional divisions under names like Real Madrid Castilla. In England, feeder relationships historically linked lower-league clubs to top-flight sides, though rules were tightened after controversies over conflicts of interest.

Advantages and criticism

Supporters of feeder teams argue that young players benefit from regular first-team football in a controlled environment, while smaller clubs gain access to talent and financial support. Critics argue the system undermines the independence and competitive integrity of smaller clubs, turning them into de facto farm teams and eroding local identity. Multi-club ownership networks, such as those operated by large groups owning clubs in several countries, have drawn particular scrutiny from regulators including UEFA.
