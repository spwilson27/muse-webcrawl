Korenizatsiya

Korenizatsiya
Meaning"Indigenisation" or "nativisation" (from Russian koren, "root")
Period1923 – mid-1930s
CountrySoviet Union
Associated withLenin's nationalities policy; Joseph Stalin (as People's Commissar for Nationalities)

Korenizatsiya ("indigenisation") was the Soviet policy of the 1920s and early 1930s that promoted the languages, cultures, and political advancement of the non-Russian nationalities of the USSR. Adopted formally in 1923, it aimed to root Soviet power in the republics by training indigenous cadres and conducting government, education, and publishing in local languages.

Implementation

Under korenizatsiya, hundreds of national districts and village soviets were created, schooling and newspapers were provided in dozens of languages (some of which received written scripts for the first time), and quotas encouraged the promotion of titular nationals into party and state posts. Its most extensive application was in Ukraine ("Ukrainization") and Belarus, and it extended to Central Asia, the Caucasus, and the Far North. The policy coexisted with the creation of national-territorial units that structured the USSR as a federation of republics.

Reversal

From the mid-1930s the policy was reversed: many national cadres were purged as "bourgeois nationalists", Russian language teaching was made compulsory in 1938, several alphabets were converted to Cyrillic, and whole peoples were deported during the Second World War. Historians such as Terry Martin have described the early Soviet state as an "affirmative action empire", and korenizatsiya remains a key reference point in debates over Soviet nationalities policy and its legacies.
