Football League First Division

Founded: 1888
Folded: 1992
Country: England
Number of teams: 24 (final season)
Most championships: Liverpool (18)

The Football League First Division was a division of the Football League in England from 1888 until 1992, when it was succeeded as the top tier of English football by the Premier League. From 1888 to 1992 it was the top-flight division in the English football league system.

History

The Football League was founded in 1888 by Aston Villa director William McGregor. Originally it consisted of a single division of 12 clubs. The league grew in size and in 1892 a Second Division was added, with the existing division becoming the First Division. The First Division title was for many decades contested largely by clubs from the industrial North and Midlands.

The maximum wage, retained until 1961, limited players' earnings, and the retain-and-transfer system restricted movement between clubs. The abolition of the maximum wage and changes to transfer rules gradually reshaped the competitive balance.

In 1992, the First Division clubs resigned en masse to form the FA Premier League, a competition independent of the Football League, driven largely by the desire to negotiate their own television deals. The First Division thereafter became the second tier of English football until the Football League rebranded its divisions in 2004, when the First Division became the Football League Championship.

Champions

Liverpool won the First Division title 18 times, more than any other club, followed by Arsenal with 10 and Everton with 9. Preston North End won the inaugural title in 1888–89 without losing a match.
