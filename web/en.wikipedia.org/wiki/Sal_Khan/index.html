Salman "Sal" Amin Khan (born 11 October 1976) is an American educator and entrepreneur, best known as the founder and CEO of Khan Academy, a free online education platform used by tens of millions of learners worldwide.

Early life and career

Khan was born in Metairie, Louisiana, to immigrant parents from Bangladesh and India, and grew up in New Orleans. He graduated from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology with degrees in mathematics and in electrical engineering and computer science, and later earned an MBA from Harvard Business School. He worked as a hedge fund analyst before turning to education full-time.

Khan Academy

In 2004 Khan began tutoring his cousin Nadia in mathematics remotely, posting instructional videos to YouTube from 2006 so other relatives could use them. The videos' popularity led him to found Khan Academy as a non-profit in 2008 and to quit his finance job in 2009. The platform grew with support from the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation and Google, offering thousands of short video lessons and practice exercises in mathematics, science, computing, humanities and test preparation, translated into dozens of languages. Khan's approach — short, informal videos narrated over a digital blackboard, combined with mastery-based practice — became a widely discussed model of technology in education.

Other work

Khan authored The One World Schoolhouse: Education Reimagined (2012) and Brave New Words (2024), about artificial intelligence in education. He also founded Khan Lab School in Mountain View, California, and Schoolhouse.world, a free peer-tutoring platform. He has been named to the TIME 100 list of most influential people.
