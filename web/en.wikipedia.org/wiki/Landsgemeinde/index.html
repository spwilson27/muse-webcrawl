Landsgemeinde

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Landsgemeinde is a cantonal assembly in Switzerland in which all eligible citizens gather outdoors, traditionally on a Sunday in spring, to vote by show of hands on laws, budgets and elections. It is one of the oldest forms of direct democracy in the world, attested in the rural cantons since the late Middle Ages, and survives today in two cantons: Appenzell Innerrhoden and Glarus.

Citizens assemble in a town square or meadow; officials present proposals, debate is open to any voter, and votes are estimated by the show of hands, with close results decided by division or (in Glarus) by counting. Historically, men carried swords or sidearms as proof of citizenship. Women's participation came late: Appenzell Innerrhoden admitted women only in 1991, after the Federal Supreme Court ruled its male-only practice unconstitutional — the last Swiss canton to do so.

Decline

Most cantons abolished the Landsgemeinde in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries in favour of ballot-box voting, citing the lack of secrecy, weather, accessibility and the difficulty of counting large assemblies; Obwalden was the last to abandon it (1998). Defenders value it as visible, deliberative self-government and a school of civic responsibility; turnout is often modest, and decisions are subject to the same constitutional rights protections as any Swiss popular vote.
