Nuclear power is a major source of energy in France, with a share of generation comparable to that of any other country. In 2023, nuclear power supplied about 65% of French electricity, the highest percentage in the world. Since June 2020, it has 56 operable reactors totalling roughly 61,000 MWe, one reactor under construction (Flamanville 3, an EPR), and 14 shut down or in decommissioning.

Électricité de France (EDF), the country's main electricity generation and distribution company, manages the country's 56 power reactors. EDF is fully owned by the French government.

History

Following the 1973 oil crisis, Prime Minister Pierre Messmer announced in March 1974 what became known as the Messmer Plan, a huge nuclear power programme aimed at generating all of France's electricity from nuclear power. This decision was taken in the context of France having substantial heavy engineering expertise but few indigenous energy resources. The programme standardised on pressurised water reactors, first from Westinghouse designs and later from domestic Framatome designs.

Fuel cycle and waste

France reprocesses its spent nuclear fuel at the La Hague site in Normandy, recovering plutonium and uranium for reuse and vitrifying the remaining high-level waste. Deep geological disposal is planned at the Cigéo repository near Bure.
