Gustav III (24 January 1746 – 29 March 1792) was King of Sweden from 1771 until his assassination in 1792. A ruler of the Enlightenment, he restored royal power, reformed Swedish government and became a great patron of the arts.

Reign

In August 1772 Gustav carried out a bloodless coup d'état that ended the parliamentary "Age of Liberty" and gave the crown control over government under a new Instrument of Government. His reforms included abolition of torture, freedom of the press (later curtailed), religious tolerance edicts, and economic liberalisation. He founded the Swedish Academy (1786), the Royal Swedish Opera and the Royal Dramatic Theatre, and wrote plays himself.

War and assassination

Seeking to recover Sweden's great-power status, Gustav attacked Russia in 1788; the Russo-Swedish War ended at the Treaty of Värälä (1790) with no territorial change but a Swedish naval victory at Svensksund. On 16 March 1792 he was shot at a masked ball at the Royal Opera House by Jacob Johan Anckarström, part of a noble conspiracy, and died thirteen days later. The assassination inspired Verdi's opera Un ballo in maschera.
