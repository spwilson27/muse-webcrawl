Constance Markievicz

Constance Georgine Markievicz (Polish: Markiewicz; née Gore-Booth; 4 February 1868 – 15 July 1927), known as Countess Markievicz, was an Irish revolutionary, politician, suffragist and socialist. A founding member of Fianna Éireann and the Irish Citizen Army, she took part in the Easter Rising of 1916. In December 1918 she was the first woman elected to the Parliament of the United Kingdom, though, as a Sinn Féin abstentionist, she did not take her seat; instead she became the first female cabinet minister in Ireland — and one of the first in the world — as Minister for Labour in the First Dáil.

Early life

Born Constance Gore-Booth into the Anglo-Irish landowning family of Lissadell House, County Sligo, she studied art at the Slade School in London and the Académie Julian in Paris, where she met the Polish count Casimir Markievicz; they married in 1900 and settled in Dublin. Her younger sister Eva Gore-Booth was a poet and labour activist.

Revolutionary activity

Markievicz joined Sinn Féin and Inghinidhe na hÉireann, helped found the nationalist youth movement Fianna Éireann in 1909, and worked in soup kitchens during the 1913 Dublin lock-out, joining the Irish Citizen Army. In the Easter Rising she served as second-in-command at St Stephen's Green; her unit held out until ordered to surrender. Sentenced to death, her sentence was commuted on account of her sex — a reprieve she reportedly scorned, telling her captors she wished they had "the decency to shoot her."

Political career

Released in the 1917 amnesty, she was elected MP for Dublin St Patrick's in 1918 while imprisoned in Holloway. She served as Minister for Labour in the revolutionary Dáil government (1919–1922), opposed the Anglo-Irish Treaty, and was active in the Civil War on the anti-Treaty side. She joined Éamon de Valera's new Fianna Fáil party in 1926 and was elected to the Dáil for Dublin South in June 1927, but died five weeks later, before taking her seat. She was buried at Glasnevin Cemetery, where de Valera gave the funeral oration.
