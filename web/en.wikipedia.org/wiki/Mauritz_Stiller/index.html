Mauritz Stiller (born Moshe Stiller; 17 July 1883 – 18 November 1928) was a Finnish-Swedish film director, screenwriter, and actor, regarded — with Victor Sjöström — as one of the two great directors of Sweden's silent-film golden age. He is also remembered for discovering and naming Greta Garbo, whom he brought to Hollywood.

Early life

Stiller was born in Helsinki into a Jewish family of Polish origin. Orphaned young and fleeing conscription into the Tsar's army, he settled in Sweden, where he acted in theatre and, from 1912, directed and wrote films for Svenska Biografteatern in Stockholm.

Swedish films

Stiller mastered both sophisticated comedy — Love and Journalism (1916) and the urbane Erotikon (1920), which influenced Ernst Lubitsch — and grand literary drama, including Sir Arne's Treasure (1919) and Gunnar Hede's Saga (1923), adaptations of Selma Lagerlöf. For The Saga of Gösta Berling (1924) he cast a young drama student, Greta Gustafsson, gave her the stage name Garbo, and took her with him when Louis B. Mayer signed him to MGM after seeing the film.

Hollywood and death

In Hollywood, Stiller quarrelled with studio management, was removed from pictures including Garbo's The Temptress (1926), and moved to Paramount, where he directed Pola Negri in Hotel Imperial and The Woman on Trial (both 1927). Ill with pleurisy and disillusioned, he returned to Sweden in 1927 and died in Stockholm in November 1928, aged 45. Garbo's career, launched under his mentorship, outlived him by decades.

Legacy

Stiller's elegant mise-en-scène, psychological comedies, and literary adaptations helped establish Swedish cinema's international reputation in the 1910s and 1920s, and his mentorship of Garbo remains one of cinema's most consequential acts of casting.
