Ewood Park is a football stadium in Blackburn, Lancashire, England, and the home of Blackburn Rovers F.C. since 1890. The stadium, which opened in 1882, has a capacity of 31,367. Rovers initially played at the ground from 1881 to 1882 before returning permanently in 1890 after a period at Leamington Road.

Stands

The ground consists of four main stands: the Bryan Douglas Darwen End, the Ronnie Clayton Blackburn End, the Riverside Stand, and the Jack Walker Stand, named in honour of the local steel magnate whose investment bankrolled the club's 1994–95 Premier League title. A statue of Walker stands outside the ground.

History

Ewood Park was substantially redeveloped between 1993 and 1995 as part of the club's rise under owner Jack Walker and manager Kenny Dalglish, transforming it into a modern all-seater stadium. The record attendance is 62,522, set for an FA Cup tie against Bolton Wanderers in 1929, when the ground was largely standing terraces.

Other uses

The stadium has hosted international fixtures, including matches at UEFA Women's Euro 2005, and concerts and other community events.
