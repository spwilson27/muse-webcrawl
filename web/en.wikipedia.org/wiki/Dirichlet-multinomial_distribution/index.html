Dirichlet-multinomial distribution

In probability theory and statistics, the Dirichlet-multinomial distribution is a family of discrete multivariate probability distributions on a finite support of non-negative integers. It is also called the multivariate Pólya distribution (after George Pólya). It is a compound probability distribution, where a probability vector p is drawn from a Dirichlet distribution and a multinomial observation is then drawn from that vector.

Definition

Let α = (α1, …, αK) be positive concentration parameters and let n be the number of trials. First draw a probability vector p from a Dirichlet distribution with parameter α, then draw counts x = (x1, …, xK) summing to n from a multinomial distribution with probabilities p. Integrating out p gives the Dirichlet-multinomial probability mass function, expressible in terms of gamma functions and rising factorials.

Properties and uses

Compared with the multinomial distribution, the Dirichlet-multinomial allows the category probabilities to vary, producing overdispersion: the variance of each count exceeds the multinomial variance. The beta-binomial distribution is the special case K = 2. The distribution arises naturally in Bayesian statistics as the posterior predictive distribution for categorical data under a Dirichlet prior, and it is used in topic models, document classification, species-abundance modelling, and the analysis of microbiome count data. As all α grow large with fixed proportions, the distribution approaches the multinomial; as they shrink toward zero, draws concentrate on single categories.
