Ahmadiyya

Ahmadiyya, officially the Ahmadiyya Muslim Community or the Ahmadiyya Muslim Jama'at, is an Islamic revival or messianic movement originating in British India in the late 19th century. It was founded by Mirza Ghulam Ahmad (1835–1908), who said he had been divinely appointed as both the Promised Mahdi (the "Guided One") and the Messiah expected by Muslims to appear towards the end times and bring about, by peaceful means, the final triumph of Islam.

Adherents of the Ahmadiyya movement are referred to as Ahmadi Muslims or simply Ahmadis. Estimates of the worldwide population range from 10 to 20 million, with communities in more than 200 countries.

Beliefs

Ahmadi Muslims share the central beliefs of Islam, including the six articles of faith and the five pillars, and accept the Quran as their holy scripture. Distinctive Ahmadi beliefs include the view that Jesus survived crucifixion and died a natural death, that Mirza Ghulam Ahmad was the promised Mahdi and Messiah, and an emphasis on jihad as a peaceful struggle of argument and self-purification rather than violence. The community is led by a caliph, known as the Khalifatul Masih; the fifth and current caliph is Mirza Masroor Ahmad, based in the United Kingdom.

History

The movement was formally established in Qadian, Punjab, in 1889, when Ghulam Ahmad accepted the first pledge of allegiance (bai'at) from his followers. After his death in 1908, the community elected its first caliph, Hakim Nur-ud-Din. A split in 1914 produced two branches, the Ahmadiyya Muslim Community based in Qadian (later Rabwah, Pakistan) and the smaller Lahore Ahmadiyya Movement. Following the partition of India in 1947, the headquarters moved to Rabwah, and after further persecution in Pakistan, the caliphate relocated to London in 1984.

Persecution

Ahmadis face significant persecution in several countries. In Pakistan, a 1974 constitutional amendment declared Ahmadis non-Muslims, and Ordinance XX of 1984 criminalised various Ahmadi religious practices. The community's motto is "Love for All, Hatred for None".
