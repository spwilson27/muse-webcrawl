Oi! is a subgenre of punk rock that originated in the United Kingdom in the late 1970s. The music and its associated subculture aimed to unite punks, skinheads and other working-class youth in a common voice, mixing the raw energy of punk with singalong choruses derived from football terrace chants, pub rock and glam rock. The name derives from the cockney exclamation "Oi!" used to get attention, popularised as a label by journalist Garry Bushell, who compiled the influential 1980 album Oi! The Album.

History

Bands such as Sham 69, the Cockney Rejects and the Angelic Upstarts pioneered the style with songs about unemployment, street life and police harassment. A series of Oi! compilation albums on EMI and independent labels brought the sound to a wider audience in 1980–81. The movement was damaged by the violent Southall riot of July 1981, after which the name Oi! became associated in the press with disorder, and by attempts of far-right groups to attach themselves to parts of the skinhead scene — associations most Oi! bands rejected. A second wave in the 1980s and 1990s, including the Business and international acts, carried the style into street punk scenes worldwide, and the genre continues with festivals and bands across Europe, the Americas and Asia.

Characteristics

Oi! songs are typically short, fast and built around simple chord progressions and shouted, anthemic choruses intended for group singing. Lyrics focus on working-class pride, friendship, football and opposition to authority, generally avoiding elaborate production.
