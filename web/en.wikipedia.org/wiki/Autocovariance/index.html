In probability theory and statistics, given a stochastic process, the autocovariance is a function that gives the covariance of the process with itself at pairs of time points. Autocovariance is closely related to the autocorrelation of the process in question: the autocorrelation is simply the autocovariance normalised by the variance.

Definition

For a weakly stationary process X with mean μ and variance σ², the autocovariance function at lag h is γ(h) = E[(Xt − μ)(Xt+h − μ)]. It depends only on the lag h, not on the absolute time t. At lag 0, γ(0) = σ², the variance of the process; the function is symmetric, γ(−h) = γ(h), and satisfies |γ(h)| ≤ γ(0). More generally, for a non-stationary process the autocovariance K(s, t) = Cov(Xs, Xt) is a function of two time points, and must form a positive semi-definite kernel.

Estimation

From a finite sample x1, …, xn, the sample autocovariance at lag h is computed as the average of products of deviations from the sample mean over all available pairs separated by h. These estimates are used, for example, in fitting ARMA models and in computing long-run variances and heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) standard errors such as the Newey–West estimator.
