The French Alps are the portions of the Alps mountain range that lie in France, along the borders with Italy and Switzerland. They include Mont Blanc (4,808 metres), the highest mountain in the Alps and in Western Europe, and some of the world's most famous ski resorts and mountaineering centres.


The Mont Blanc massif

Geography

The French Alps stretch from Lake Geneva in the north to the Mediterranean hinterland above Nice in the south. They are conventionally divided into the Northern Alps (Savoy and Dauphiné), with high glaciated massifs such as Mont Blanc, the Vanoise and the Écrins, and the Southern Alps (Provence and Maritime Alps), which are lower, drier and more Mediterranean in character. Major rivers rising here include the Rhône, Isère, Durance and Drac.

Tourism and sport

Chamonix, at the foot of Mont Blanc, hosted the first Winter Olympic Games in 1924 and is a cradle of alpinism — Mont Blanc was first climbed in 1786 by Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard. Grenoble (1968) and Albertville (1992) also hosted Winter Games. Purpose-built and village resorts such as Courchevel, Val d'Isère, Tignes and Alpe d'Huez make France one of the world's top ski destinations, while summer tourism centres on hiking, climbing and cycling, including famous Tour de France climbs.

Nature

Three national parks — Vanoise (the first, created 1963, twinned with Italy's Gran Paradiso), Écrins and Mercantour — protect alpine fauna such as ibex, chamois, marmots and golden eagles. The range's glaciers have been retreating rapidly with climate change, a growing concern for water supply and tourism.
