Ant farm

Ant farm

An ant farm is a formicarium designed primarily for the study and observation of ant colonies and ant behaviour. Commercially sold ant farms consist of a thin, transparent enclosure filled with sand, soil or a gel medium, into which live ants are introduced; as the ants tunnel, observers can watch the structure of the colony's nest from the side.

The classic "Ant Farm" was created in 1956 by Milton Levine, who got the idea while watching ants at a picnic, and was marketed by his company Uncle Milton Industries. The kits traditionally shipped with a coupon that could be mailed in to receive live red harvester ants. More than 20 million units are reported to have been sold.

Design

A typical ant farm is a flat box, only a few centimetres deep, so that tunnels and chambers are forced against the transparent front and back panels where they can be seen. Traditional models are filled with sand; the ants excavate visible tunnels and chambers within days. Modern variants use a translucent gel, originally developed from a NASA experiment on growing plants and studying animals in space, which serves simultaneously as nesting medium, food and water source.

Care and keeping

Commercial ant farms usually contain only worker ants and no queen, so the colony cannot reproduce and typically lives for a few months. Keepers supply water and small amounts of food such as seeds, fruit or insects. Because the ants cannot escape easily and cannot establish a breeding colony in most designs, ant farms are marketed as educational toys, although animal-welfare critics note that the short-lived, queenless colonies are a limited representation of a natural ant colony. Serious hobbyists instead keep complete colonies with queens in custom-built formicaria.

Regulation

The shipment of queen ants across state or national borders is restricted in some jurisdictions because escaped ants can become invasive species. For this reason, kits sold in some countries are supplied only with workers, and suppliers may ship ants only within the region where the species is native.
