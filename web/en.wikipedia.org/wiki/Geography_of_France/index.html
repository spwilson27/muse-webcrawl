Geography of France

Location: Western Europe
Area (metropolitan): 551,695 km²
Coastline: 3,427 km (metropolitan)
Highest point: Mont Blanc (4,808 m)

The geography of France is characterised by a varied landscape, from the coastal plains of the north and west to the mountain ranges of the south and east. Metropolitan France, sometimes called "the Hexagon" (l'Hexagone) because of its approximate shape, covers 551,695 km²; together with its overseas regions and territories, the French Republic spans time zones across every ocean.

Relief

France's highest mountains lie in the Alps in the east, where Mont Blanc (4,808 m) is the highest peak in Western Europe, and the Pyrenees along the Spanish border. Older massifs include the Massif Central, the Vosges, the Jura, and the Armorican Massif in Brittany. Lowlands include the Paris Basin, the Aquitaine Basin and the Rhône–Saône corridor. The principal rivers are the Loire (the longest entirely in France), the Seine, the Rhône, the Garonne and the Rhine, which forms part of the eastern border.

Climate

Metropolitan France has several climate types: an oceanic climate in the west, a semi-continental climate in the east, a Mediterranean climate in the south, and mountain climates in the Alps, Pyrenees and Massif Central. Corsica has a largely Mediterranean climate.

Overseas France

France retains numerous overseas departments, regions and territories, including French Guiana in South America, Guadeloupe and Martinique in the Caribbean, Réunion and Mayotte in the Indian Ocean, and French Polynesia and New Caledonia in the Pacific. These give France the world's second-largest exclusive economic zone, of about 11 million km², most of it oceanic.

Environment

About a third of metropolitan France is forested. The country is a major agricultural producer, the largest in the European Union, with farmland covering roughly half the territory. Major environmental issues include air and water pollution, coastal erosion and the effects of heatwaves and drought on agriculture.
