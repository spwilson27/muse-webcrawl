Sir Banastre Tarleton, 1st Baronet (21 August 1754 – 15 January 1833) was a British general and politician, best remembered for his service as a cavalry officer in the American Revolutionary War, where his aggressive tactics earned him the nicknames "Bloody Ban" and "Ban the Butcher" among American Patriots.

American war

Tarleton sailed to America in 1775 and rose rapidly. Commanding the British Legion, a mixed force of Loyalist cavalry and infantry, he won victories in the southern campaigns at Monck's Corner and Lenud's Ferry, and annihilated an American force at the Battle of Waxhaws (1780), after which his men were accused of killing surrendering soldiers — "Tarleton's quarter" became a Patriot rallying cry. He was defeated by Daniel Morgan at the Battle of Cowpens in January 1781 and surrendered with Cornwallis's army at Yorktown later that year.

Later life

Returning to Britain a popular hero, Tarleton sat as MP for Liverpool from 1790 to 1812, defended the slave trade in which his family had interests, and continued his military career, reaching the rank of general. His History of the Campaigns of 1780 and 1781 in the Southern Provinces of North America remains a notable, if partisan, source for the war.
