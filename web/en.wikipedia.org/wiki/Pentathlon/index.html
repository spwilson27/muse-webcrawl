Pentathlon


Origin: Ancient Olympic Games, from 708 BC

A pentathlon is a contest of five events. The name derives from the Greek pente (five) and athlon (competition). The original pentathlon was contested at the ancient Olympic Games from 708 BC and comprised the long jump, javelin throw, discus throw, the stadion foot race, and wrestling; victory in wrestling decided the overall winner, and the pentathlon was admired as the test of the complete athlete.

Modern versions

Several modern sports descend from the concept. The modern pentathlon, created by Pierre de Coubertin for the 1912 Stockholm Olympics, models the skills of a cavalry soldier: fencing, freestyle swimming, equestrian show jumping (replaced by obstacle racing after the Paris 2024 Games), and the combined laser run of pistol shooting and cross-country running. Athletics has staged pentathlons in various forms—most notably the women's indoor pentathlon (60 m hurdles, high jump, shot put, long jump, 800 m) still contested at the World Indoor Championships—before the outdoor women's event was superseded by the heptathlon in the 1980s.

Other pentathlons

Military and naval pentathlon competitions are organised internationally for service personnel, and the "aeronautical pentathlon" is contested at the Military World Games. Masters athletics and youth competitions also use pentathlon formats combining sprints, jumps, and throws.
