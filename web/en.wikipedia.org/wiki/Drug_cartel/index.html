Drug cartel

A drug cartel is a criminal organisation composed of independent drug lords who collude to control the production and distribution of illegal drugs. The term originated with the Colombian organisations — the Medellín Cartel and Cali Cartel — that dominated the cocaine trade in the 1970s and 1980s, and is now applied to major trafficking organisations in Mexico and elsewhere.

History

The Medellín Cartel, led by Pablo Escobar, became notorious for extreme violence against the Colombian state, while the quieter Cali Cartel operated more like a business federation. After the dismantling of the Colombian cartels in the 1990s, Mexican organisations such as the Sinaloa Cartel, the Gulf Cartel, Los Zetas, and the Jalisco New Generation Cartel took over the principal smuggling corridors into the United States, leading to the prolonged Mexican drug war.

Structure

Despite the name, modern cartels rarely function as price-fixing cartels; they are loose, shifting networks of producers, transporters, enforcers, and money launderers.
