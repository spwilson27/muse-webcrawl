Armenia–Turkey relations

Armenia–Turkey relations are the bilateral relations between the Republic of Armenia and the Republic of Turkey. Although Turkey recognised Armenia's independence shortly after the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, the two countries have never established formal diplomatic relations.

History

Relations are strained by Turkey's refusal to recognise the Armenian genocide of 1915, which Armenia and many historians and governments regard as a central issue. In 1993, during the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, Turkey closed its land border with Armenia in solidarity with Azerbaijan, and the border has remained closed since.

Normalisation attempts

In 2009 the two countries signed the Zurich Protocols on establishing diplomatic relations and reopening the border, but ratification stalled and the protocols were later annulled. A new normalisation process began in late 2021 with the appointment of special envoys, and the border was briefly opened in February 2023 to allow humanitarian aid after the Turkey–Syria earthquakes. Talks on fully normalising ties have continued intermittently.
