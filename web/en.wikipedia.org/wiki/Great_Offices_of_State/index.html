Great Offices of State

Whitehall in London, home to the Treasury, the Home Office and the Foreign Office.

The Great Offices of State are the four most senior and prestigious ministerial positions in the government of the United Kingdom, senior to all other Cabinet posts. They are, in order of nominal seniority:

1. Prime Minister — head of the government;
2. Chancellor of the Exchequer — responsible for economic and financial policy, based at HM Treasury;
3. Secretary of State for Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Affairs (Foreign Secretary);
4. Secretary of State for the Home Department (Home Secretary) — responsible for policing, borders and internal affairs.

Significance

Holding one of the Great Offices is generally considered the pinnacle of a British political career, and most prime ministers have previously served in at least one of the other three. The Chancellor traditionally resides at 11 Downing Street, next door to the Prime Minister at 10 Downing Street. Because of their weight, the offices are rarely reshuffled lightly, and their holders dominate the Cabinet along with the Prime Minister. Political commentators frequently assess governments by the "big four" posts, and milestones in representation — the first woman, or the first person of colour, to hold each office — are widely noted. James Callaghan is the only person to have held all four offices.

The term should not be confused with the Great Officers of State, the ancient ceremonial officers of the Crown such as the Lord High Chancellor and the Earl Marshal.
