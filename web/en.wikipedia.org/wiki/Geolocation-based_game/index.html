Geolocation-based game

A location-based game on a smartphone

A geolocation-based game, or location-based game, is a type of video game in which the gameplay evolves and progresses via a player's real-world location, usually obtained through the GPS of a mobile device. Such games are a form of pervasive game, blending play with physical movement through real space.

History

Early examples include the treasure-hunting hobby of geocaching (2000), which used handheld GPS receivers, and BotFighters (2001), one of the first mobile games to use phone location. The spread of smartphones enabled titles such as Niantic's Ingress (2012), in which players capture virtual "portals" at real landmarks, and Shadow Cities.

Pokémon Go

The genre reached mass popularity with Pokémon Go (2016), also by Niantic, which combined location data with augmented reality: players walk to real locations to catch virtual creatures. The game was downloaded over a billion times and became a global cultural phenomenon, while prompting debates about safety, trespassing and the use of public space.

Design and issues

Designers must handle uneven distributions of players and points of interest (rural areas often have fewer), GPS drift and spoofing, battery drain, and privacy concerns arising from continuous location tracking. Data from crowdsourced play has been used to build global databases of landmarks.
