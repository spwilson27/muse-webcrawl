Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan

Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan (13 October 1948 – 16 August 1997) was a Pakistani singer, songwriter, and music director, primarily a vocalist of qawwali, the devotional music of the Sufis. Widely regarded as one of the greatest singers of all time — often called Shahenshah-e-Qawwali, "the Emperor of Qawwali" — he is credited with introducing qawwali to international audiences and held a record for the largest recorded output by a qawwali artist.

Career

Born in Faisalabad into a family of qawwals stretching back some 600 years, Nusrat was trained by his father Fateh Ali Khan and his uncles, and led the family party after his father's death; his first public performance was at his father's funeral in 1964, and he became head of the party in 1971. He developed a virtuoso style — fast, intricate sargam improvisations, performances that could extend for hours, and a powerful voice spanning several octaves.

International recognition

Performances at the WOMAD festival in 1985 and recordings for Peter Gabriel's Real World label brought him worldwide fame. He contributed to Gabriel's Passion (the soundtrack to The Last Temptation of Christ), collaborated with Canadian guitarist Michael Brook on the fusion albums Mustt Mustt (1990) and Night Song (1996), sang on soundtracks for films including Dead Man Walking and Natural Born Killers, and recorded a celebrated remix collaboration with Massive Attack. He also sang extensively for Pakistani and Indian cinema. He died in London in 1997 of complications including kidney and liver problems. His nephew Rahat Fateh Ali Khan succeeded him as a leading qawwali and playback singer.
