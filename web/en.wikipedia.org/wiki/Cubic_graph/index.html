Cubic graph

In the mathematical field of graph theory, a cubic graph is a graph in which all vertices have degree three. In other words, a cubic graph is a 3-regular graph. Cubic graphs are also called trivalent graphs.

A bicubic graph is a cubic bipartite graph. Cubic graphs arise naturally in many branches of mathematics and have been studied extensively because of their rich structure: many difficult problems in graph theory, such as the edge coloring problem and the study of snarks, are no easier when restricted to cubic graphs, yet cubic graphs are small enough to be tractable in other respects.

Symmetry

Several well-known cubic graphs are highly symmetric. The smallest cubic graph is the complete graph K4 with four vertices. Other celebrated examples include the Petersen graph, the utility graph K3,3, the cube graph, the Wagner graph (the 8-vertex Möbius ladder), the dodecahedron graph with 20 vertices, and the Tutte–Coxeter graph with 30 vertices. In 1932, Ronald Foster began collecting examples of cubic symmetric graphs, producing the Foster census.

Coloring and independent sets

By Brooks' theorem, every connected cubic graph other than K4 can be colored with three colors. By Vizing's theorem, every cubic graph has edge chromatic number three or four; a cubic graph whose edges cannot be colored with three colors is called a snark. The Petersen graph is the smallest snark.

Topology and geometry

Cubic graphs arise in topology as the 1-skeletons of polyhedra in some cases; by Steinitz's theorem, the graphs of convex polyhedra are exactly the planar 3-connected graphs, and the cubic ones correspond to polyhedra in which every face-adjacency follows a trivalent vertex figure, such as the cube and dodecahedron.

Hamiltonicity

Much research has concerned whether cubic graphs must have Hamiltonian cycles. Tutte found a 46-vertex cubic planar graph with no Hamiltonian cycle, disproving a conjecture of Tait that every cubic polyhedral graph is Hamiltonian. Barnette's conjecture, still open, proposes that every cubic planar bipartite 3-connected graph is Hamiltonian.

Other properties

By the handshaking lemma, every cubic graph has an even number of vertices. Petersen's theorem states that every cubic bridgeless graph has a perfect matching. The number of cubic graphs on 2n vertices grows rapidly with n and has been tabulated by enumerative combinatorics.
