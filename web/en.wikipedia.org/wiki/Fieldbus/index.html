Fieldbus

Fieldbus is the name of a family of industrial computer network protocols used for real-time distributed control. A fieldbus links instruments, sensors, actuators, and controllers in a plant or factory over a shared digital network, replacing the point-to-point wiring of older analog systems such as 4–20 mA current loops.

History

Development began in the 1980s as process industries sought to reduce cabling costs and add diagnostic data from field devices. A long standards contest — the so-called "fieldbus wars" — produced the IEC 61158 standard, which accommodated multiple competing protocols rather than a single one. Widely used fieldbus systems include Foundation Fieldbus, PROFIBUS, Modbus, DeviceNet, and CAN-based networks.

Characteristics

Fieldbus networks typically operate at the lowest levels of the automation hierarchy, connecting field devices to programmable logic controllers. They offer deterministic message delivery, bus power for instruments on the same cable, and in some variants intrinsic safety for hazardous areas. In modern plants, fieldbus segments are increasingly integrated with industrial Ethernet systems such as PROFINET and EtherNet/IP.
