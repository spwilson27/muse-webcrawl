Alfredo James Pacino (born April 25, 1940) is an American actor. Considered one of the greatest actors of the 20th century, he emerged from the New York stage and the Actors Studio to become a defining figure of the New Hollywood era with performances as Michael Corleone in The Godfather trilogy (1972–1990), Sonny Wortzik in Dog Day Afternoon (1975), and Tony Montana in Scarface (1983). He has received numerous accolades, including an Academy Award, two Tony Awards and three Primetime Emmy Awards, achieving the Triple Crown of Acting.

Career

Born in East Harlem and raised in the South Bronx, Pacino studied at the Actors Studio under Lee Strasberg. He won a Tony Award for Does a Tiger Wear a Necktie? (1969) before his film breakthrough in The Panic in Needle Park (1971), which led Francis Ford Coppola to cast him in The Godfather. Through the 1970s he earned four consecutive Oscar nominations for Serpico, The Godfather Part II, Dog Day Afternoon and ...And Justice for All. After the commercial failure of Revolution (1985), he returned to the stage, then revived his film career with Sea of Love (1989) and The Godfather Part III (1990), winning his Oscar for Scent of a Woman (1992). Later highlights include Heat (1995), The Insider (1999), Angels in America (2003), Once Upon a Time in Hollywood (2019) and The Irishman (2019), which earned him his ninth Academy Award nomination.

Acting style

A method actor, Pacino is known for an intense, volatile screen presence alternating quiet restraint with explosive outbursts, a quality that became a hallmark of his later roles.
