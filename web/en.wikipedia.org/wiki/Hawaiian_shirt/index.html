The aloha shirt, commonly called a Hawaiian shirt, is a style of dress shirt originating in Hawaii. It is typically short-sleeved with a camp collar and button front, printed with bold designs of flowers, palm trees, ocean scenes, and other Hawaiian and Polynesian motifs.

History

The shirt emerged in Honolulu in the 1920s and 1930s, drawing on Japanese kimono fabrics, Filipino and Chinese tailoring, and the colourful prints of the islands. Honolulu shopkeeper Ellery Chun is credited with coining the name "Aloha shirt" and popularising the garment; he trademarked the term in 1937. Tourists and servicemen carried the style to the U.S. mainland, and it was popularised by figures such as Bing Crosby, Elvis Presley (in Blue Hawaii), and President Harry Truman.

In Hawaiian culture

Within Hawaii the aloha shirt is respectable everyday and even business attire. The custom of "Aloha Friday" — wearing aloha attire on Fridays — began in the 1960s and helped inspire casual Friday dress codes elsewhere. Renowned makers include Reyn Spooner, Tori Richard, and the vintage label Kamehameha.
