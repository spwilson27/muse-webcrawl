Détente

Détente (from French, "relaxation") is the easing of strained relations, especially in a political situation. The term is most often used in reference to a period of general easing of Cold War tensions between the Soviet Union and the United States, lasting roughly from the late 1960s through the late 1970s, and characterised by summits, arms-control agreements and increased trade.

Key events and agreements

Détente produced the Strategic Arms Limitation Talks and the SALT I treaty and Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty (1972), the Helsinki Accords (1975), the Basic Principles Agreement, grain sales, and joint projects such as the Apollo–Soyuz Test Project (1975). It was championed by Richard Nixon and his adviser Henry Kissinger, and by Soviet leader Leonid Brezhnev. West Germany pursued a parallel Ostpolitik under Willy Brandt.

Decline

Critics argued that détente let the USSR consolidate gains while continuing expansion in the developing world. The Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in 1979 effectively ended détente, prompting the Carter Doctrine, an Olympic boycott, and, under Ronald Reagan, a renewed arms build-up sometimes called the Second Cold War.
