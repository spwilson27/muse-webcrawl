Tower of St. Olav

The Tower of St. Olav (Swedish: Sankt Olofs torn; Russian: Башня Святого Олафа) is the main keep of Vyborg Castle in Vyborg, Russia, near the Finnish border. It is one of the best-known medieval towers in the region and a symbol of the city.

History

Vyborg Castle was founded by the Swedes in 1293 during the Third Swedish Crusade, and the tower is named for Saint Olaf of Norway. The original late-13th-century tower was rebuilt and raised in height in the 1560s under King Erik XIV of Sweden, giving it its distinctive octagonal upper section. The tower is about 48.6 metres tall, with walls roughly 4–5 metres thick at the base.

Later history

Vyborg changed hands with the Treaty of Nystad (1721), passing from Sweden to Russia. The tower survived fires in the 19th century that destroyed its spire and interiors, and it was restored several times. Today it houses exhibition space of the Vyborg museum and an observation deck with views over the town and Vyborg Bay.
