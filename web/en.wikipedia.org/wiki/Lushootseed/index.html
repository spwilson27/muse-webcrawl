Lushootseed
Native name: dxʷləšúcid
Region: Puget Sound, Washington
Family: Salishan → Coast Salish → Central
ISO 639-3: lut

Lushootseed (dxʷləšúcid) is a member of the Coast Salish language family spoken by several Indigenous peoples of the Puget Sound region of present-day Washington State in the United States. It is closely related to other Central Salish languages and was historically spoken from the area around modern Olympia northward to the Skagit River and east to the Cascade foothills.

Dialects

Lushootseed has two main dialects: Northern Lushootseed, spoken by groups including the Snohomish, Skagit, and Swinomish, and Southern Lushootseed, spoken by groups including the Nisqually, Puyallup, and Duwamish. The dialects differ in phonology and vocabulary but are mutually intelligible.

Phonology

Like other Salishan languages, Lushootseed has a rich consonant inventory including ejective and glottalized consonants, a lateral fricative, and uvular consonants, alongside a small vowel system. Stress and glottalization play important roles in distinguishing words.

Revitalization

By the late twentieth century the number of fluent first-language speakers had fallen sharply. Tribal language programs, notably those of the Tulalip Tribes and the Puyallup Tribe, have worked to document the language and teach it to new generations, aided by the extensive recordings and texts collected by linguists and native speakers such as Vi Hilbert.
