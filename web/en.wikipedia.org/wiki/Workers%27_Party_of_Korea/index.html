Workers' Party of Korea

Founded24 June 1949
HeadquartersPyongyang, North Korea
IdeologyJuche; Kimilsungism–Kimjongilism

The Workers' Party of Korea (WPK) is the founding and ruling party of North Korea (the Democratic People's Republic of Korea). Established in 1949 through the merger of communist parties in the Soviet-occupied north, it has governed the country continuously, led successively by Kim Il Sung, Kim Jong Il and Kim Jong Un, and is guided by the state ideology of Juche.

Organisation

Formally, the party's highest body is its Congress, which elects a Central Committee; in practice power is concentrated in the Politburo and its Presidium and in the party leader, who serves as General Secretary (styled Chairman between 2016 and 2021). Party cells penetrate the military, workplaces and neighbourhoods, and membership—estimated in the millions—is a prerequisite for advancement. Party congresses were held irregularly: the 7th Congress in 2016 was the first since 1980, followed by the 8th in 2021.

History

Kim Il Sung consolidated power during and after the Korean War, purging rival factions in the 1950s and enshrining Juche in place of orthodox Marxism–Leninism. Under Kim Jong Il the military-first policy (Songun) elevated the army alongside the party; Kim Jong Un, succeeding in 2011, has re-emphasised party institutions while continuing the nuclear weapons programme and the hereditary leadership cult.
