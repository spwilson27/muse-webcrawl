Millet

Millets are a group of highly variable small-seeded grasses, widely grown around the world as cereal crops or grains for fodder and human food. Most species generally referred to as millets belong to the tribe Paniceae in the grass family Poaceae. Millets are important crops in the semiarid tropics of Asia and Africa (especially in India, Mali, Nigeria, and Niger), with 97% of millet production in developing countries. The crop is favoured due to its productivity and short growing season under dry, high-temperature conditions.

Types of millet

The most widely grown millets include pearl millet (Pennisetum glaucum), the most important by production, followed by foxtail millet (Setaria italica), proso millet or common millet (Panicum miliaceum), and finger millet (Eleusine coracana). Other species include sorghum (sometimes classed as a "great millet"), teff, fonio, Job's tears, and kodo millet. The different species are not closely related but all function similarly as annual cereal grasses.

History

Millets were among the earliest domesticated crops. Foxtail and proso millet were domesticated in northern China roughly 8,000–10,000 years ago and were staple grains of early Chinese civilization. Pearl millet was domesticated in the Sahel region of West Africa, while finger millet originated in the highlands of East Africa. Millets spread across Eurasia along trade routes and were staple foods in Europe during the Middle Ages before being displaced by wheat, rice, and potatoes in many regions.

Cultivation and uses

Millets are typically annuals 30–130 cm tall, adapted to poor soils and low rainfall; they require far less water than rice or wheat. The grains are small (2–4 mm) and are cooked like rice, ground into flour for flatbreads such as roti and bhakri, brewed into beer, or fed to livestock and poultry. Millet grains are gluten-free and rich in protein, fibre, iron, and B vitamins compared with refined grains. India is the world's largest producer, followed by countries in West Africa and China. The United Nations declared 2023 the International Year of Millets to raise awareness of their nutritional and climate-resilience benefits.
