Glamour photography

Glamour photography is a genre of photography in which the subjects, usually female, are depicted in erotic or alluring poses ranging from fully clothed to nude. The emphasis is on the beauty, charm, and physical appeal of the subject, often enhanced by makeup, lighting, and retouching.

History

The genre developed from early twentieth-century Hollywood studio portraiture, where photographers such as George Hurrell created idealized images of film stars. Pin-up photography of the 1940s and 1950s popularized glamour imagery, and magazines such as Playboy (founded 1953) made glamour photography a mainstream commercial field.

Practice

Glamour photography differs from fashion photography, which emphasizes clothing, and from fine-art nude photography in its commercial, idealized presentation. Modern glamour work includes boudoir photography commissioned privately by clients, as well as editorial and advertising work. Airbrushing and digital retouching have long been used to idealize the subject's appearance.
