Sylvanus Thayer Award

The Sylvanus Thayer Award is an award presented annually by the United States Military Academy at West Point since 1958. It is given to an outstanding citizen of the United States whose service and accomplishments in the national interest exemplify the academy's motto, "Duty, Honor, Country", and it is named for Colonel Sylvanus Thayer, the "Father of the Military Academy", who was superintendent from 1817 to 1833.

Recipients

The first recipient was former Secretary of State and Army Chief of Staff George C. Marshall. Other honorees have included Presidents Dwight D. Eisenhower, Ronald Reagan (the first graduate to receive it), George H. W. Bush, and Jimmy Carter (from the Naval Academy); generals Douglas MacArthur — whose 1962 acceptance speech, "Duty, Honor, Country", is famous — Omar Bradley and Colin Powell; astronauts Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin; and figures such as Henry Kissinger, Barbara Jordan, Bob Dole, Walter Cronkite, Madeleine Albright, Condoleezza Rice, Tom Brokaw and Leon Panetta. The recipient is honoured with a review of the Corps of Cadets and receives a medal bearing Thayer's profile.
