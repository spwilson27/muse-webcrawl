Peter Lindsay Weir AM (born 21 August 1944) is an Australian film director. A leading figure of the Australian New Wave, he is known for films that frequently explore characters confronting unfamiliar or oppressive worlds, and he received an Academy Honorary Award in 2022.

Australian films

Weir gained international acclaim with the dreamlike mystery Picnic at Hanging Rock (1975) and the supernatural thriller The Last Wave (1977). His anti-war film Gallipoli (1981), starring Mel Gibson, became a landmark of Australian cinema, followed by The Year of Living Dangerously (1982), set in Indonesia during the 1965 upheaval.

Hollywood career

Weir's first American film, the thriller Witness (1985), starring Harrison Ford, earned him an Academy Award nomination for Best Director. He went on to direct The Mosquito Coast (1986), the inspirational drama Dead Poets Society (1989) with Robin Williams, the romantic comedy Green Card (1990, which he also wrote), Fearless (1993), and the prescient media satire The Truman Show (1998) with Jim Carrey, which won wide critical acclaim.

His naval epic Master and Commander: The Far Side of the World (2003), adapted from Patrick O'Brian's Aubrey–Maturin novels and starring Russell Crowe, won two Academy Awards. His final film as director was the survival drama The Way Back (2010). Over his career Weir was nominated for six Academy Awards and directed actors in numerous Oscar-nominated performances.
