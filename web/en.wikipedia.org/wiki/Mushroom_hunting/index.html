Mushroom hunting

Mushroom hunting, also known as mushrooming, mushroom picking, mushroom foraging, and similar terms, describes the activity of searching for mushrooms in the wild, typically for culinary use. It is especially popular in temperate regions of Europe — including Poland, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Russia, the Nordic countries and Italy — as well as in Japan, Korea, Canada and the Pacific Northwest of the United States. A person who hunts mushrooms for pleasure is sometimes called a mycophile.

Species sought

Popular edible targets include boletes such as the porcini or king bolete (Boletus edulis), chanterelles (Cantharellus species), morels (Morchella species), hedgehog mushrooms, puffballs, chicken of the woods, oyster mushrooms, and various Agaricus, Lactarius and Russula species. Truffles are located with trained dogs or, historically, pigs. Experienced foragers learn the trees, soils and seasons associated with each species, since many fungi grow in mycorrhizal association with particular tree species.

Identification and safety

Correct identification is critical: several deadly species, such as the death cap (Amanita phalloides) and the destroying angels, resemble edible mushrooms. Foragers are advised to examine all identifying features — cap, gills or pores, stem, ring, volva, flesh colour changes and spore print — and to consult reliable field guides and local experts. A common rule is never to eat a wild mushroom that has not been identified with certainty. Rules of thumb such as judging edibility by colour, peeling, or tarnishing silver are unreliable and dangerous.

Equipment and practice

Typical equipment includes a basket or breathable bag (which allows spores to disperse and keeps mushrooms from spoiling), a small knife for cutting and cleaning stems, and a field guide. Whether mushrooms should be cut or pulled is debated; current evidence suggests neither method significantly harms the underlying mycelium when done carefully. In many countries commercial picking is regulated, and some regions require permits or limit daily quantities.

Cultural traditions

Mushroom hunting is a deeply rooted autumn tradition in much of Central and Eastern Europe, where families pass down knowledge of good spots (grzybobranie in Polish). In Catalonia, mushroom hunters are called boletaires; in Japan, matsutake hunting is a prized seasonal activity. Wild mushrooms gathered by foragers are sold in markets and to restaurants across Europe and North America.
