Minarchism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Minarchism is a libertarian political philosophy holding that the state's sole legitimate functions are the minimal ones of protecting citizens from aggression, theft, breach of contract and fraud — police, military, courts and little else. Such a state is called a night-watchman state. Minarchists differ from anarcho-capitalists, who would abolish the state entirely and rely on markets and voluntary institutions for protection and adjudication.

The term “night-watchman state” (Nachtwächterstaat) was coined by the German socialist Ferdinand Lassalle as a jibe at liberal minimalism and later adopted, half-ironically, by its defenders. Robert Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia (1974) is the best-known philosophical argument that a minimal state can arise justly from anarchy without violating rights, and that any more extensive state cannot. Practical minarchists debate borderline functions — roads, money, environmental harm — and how the minimal state itself should be financed without the taxation most of them regard as illegitimate.
