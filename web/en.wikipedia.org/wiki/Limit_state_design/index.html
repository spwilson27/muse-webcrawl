Limit state design

Limit state design
FieldStructural engineering
Also known asLoad and resistance factor design (LRFD)
Key codesEurocodes, ACI 318, AISC 360

Limit state design (LSD), also known as load and resistance factor design (LRFD), is a method of designing structures so that they do not exceed defined "limit states" during their intended life. A limit state is a condition beyond which the structure no longer satisfies the design requirements.

Types of limit states

Ultimate limit states concern safety and collapse — loss of equilibrium, rupture, buckling or fatigue failure. Serviceability limit states concern normal use — excessive deflection, cracking, vibration or damage that impairs function without collapse. Designs must satisfy both categories.

Method

Limit state design replaces the single global safety factor of older permissible-stress methods with partial factors applied separately to loads (increased) and to material resistances (decreased). Characteristic values of actions and strengths are adjusted by these factors, calibrated statistically so that the probability of reaching a limit state is acceptably small. The approach underlies the Eurocodes, Canadian and Australian codes, and American LRFD specifications for steel and concrete.

History

The method was developed in the Soviet Union in the 1930s–1950s (notably by N. S. Streletsky's school) and adopted across Europe through CEB and ISO work, reaching North America with the AISC LRFD specification of 1986 and subsequent bridge and building codes.
