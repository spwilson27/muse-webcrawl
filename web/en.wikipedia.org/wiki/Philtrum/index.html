Philtrum

Philtrum
Location: Midline of the upper lip, below the nose
Bounded by: Two philtral ridges (columns)
Embryology: Formed where the nasomedial and maxillary processes meet

The philtrum (from Greek phíltron, "love potion") is the vertical groove in the middle of the upper lip, extending from the base of the nose (the columella) to the tubercle of the upper lip. It is flanked on either side by raised ridges known as philtral columns.

Development and function

The philtrum forms during embryonic development where the nasomedial and maxillary processes fuse in the midline of the face. In most mammals the philtrum is a narrow groove that carries dissolved odours from the moist nose to the vomeronasal organ, aiding the sense of smell. In humans and most primates, with their reduced reliance on smell, it is largely vestigial, though it remains a prominent landmark of facial anatomy.

Clinical significance

A flattened or smooth philtrum is one of the characteristic facial features assessed in diagnosing fetal alcohol spectrum disorders. An unusually long or short philtrum can be associated with certain genetic syndromes, and the philtrum is an important reference point in the surgical repair of cleft lip, where recreating the philtral ridges is key to a natural appearance.
