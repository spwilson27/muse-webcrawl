Hérault (French pronunciation: [eʁo]; Occitan: Erau) is a department in the region of Occitanie in southern France. It is named after the Hérault river, which flows through its territory before reaching the Mediterranean Sea at Agde. Its prefecture and largest city is Montpellier.

Geography

The department stretches from the Mediterranean coastline in the south to the foothills of the Cévennes and the Massif Central in the north. Its coastline includes the resort towns of Sète, Palavas-les-Flots and La Grande-Motte, as well as the Étang de Thau, a large lagoon famous for oyster farming. Inland, the terrain rises through the garrigue scrubland of the Montpellier hinterland toward the Haut-Languedoc regional natural park.

History

Hérault was created on 4 March 1790 during the French Revolution, formed from part of the former province of Languedoc. Its boundaries have remained largely unchanged since its creation. During the 19th and 20th centuries the department was one of France's leading wine-producing regions, and viticulture crises — notably the winegrowers' revolt of 1907, centred on Narbonne and Montpellier — left a deep mark on its political culture.

Economy

Viticulture remains important, producing Languedoc AOC wines and the sweet Muscat de Frontignan. Montpellier has grown into a major centre for higher education, research, medicine and technology, while tourism along the coast and around historic towns such as Pézenas and Saint-Guilhem-le-Désert contributes substantially to the local economy.
