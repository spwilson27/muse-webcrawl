Breccia

Breccia composed of angular fragments of older rock in a finer matrix.

Breccia is a rock composed of large angular broken fragments of minerals or rocks cemented together by a fine-grained matrix. The word has its origins in the Italian language, where it means "rubble". A breccia may be a rock of sedimentary, igneous, impact, volcanic or tectonic origin, and is distinguished from a conglomerate by the angularity of its clasts, which indicates they were not transported far from their source.

Types

Sedimentary breccias form from debris flows, talus and collapsed cave deposits. Fault breccias result from the grinding of rock along faults. Volcanic breccias consist of fragments of lava and wall rock in eruptions. Impact breccias, including suevite, are produced by meteorite impacts, and hydrothermal breccias form in some ore deposits, where breccia bodies may host valuable mineralisation such as the copper–gold ores of Olympic Dam in Australia.

Uses

Attractively coloured breccias are cut and polished as decorative stone; brecciated marbles were used in classical architecture and remain popular for cladding and countertops.
