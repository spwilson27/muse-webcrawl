Calendar Round

Length18,980 days
Equivalent52 years of 365 days = 73 cycles of 260 days
ComponentsTzolk'in (260-day) and Haab' (365-day) cycles
RegionMesoamerica (Maya, Aztec, and others)

The Calendar Round is the cycle of 18,980 days (approximately 52 solar years) generated by combining the two principal calendars of ancient Mesoamerica: the 260-day sacred almanac (the Tzolk'in in Maya terminology, the tonalpohualli among the Aztecs) and the 365-day solar year (the Haab' or xiuhpohualli). Because 18,980 is the least common multiple of 260 and 365, any given pairing of a sacred-almanac date with a solar-year date recurs only once in the Round.

How it works

A full date in the system names both components, for example "4 Ahau 8 Cumhu". Day by day, both cycles advance together; since the cycles share a factor of 5, only a subset of combinations ever occurs. After 52 Haab' years (18,980 days), exactly 73 Tzolk'in cycles have also elapsed, and the paired date repeats.

Significance

The 52-year span approximated a full human lifetime, and its completion was a moment of great cosmological anxiety and renewal. Among the Aztecs (Mexica) the end of a Round was marked by the New Fire ceremony: all fires were extinguished, and priests watched the Pleiades from the Hill of the Star; if the stars continued their course, a new fire was kindled in the chest of a sacrificial victim and carried throughout the empire to begin the next 52-year cycle. The last pre-conquest New Fire ceremony was held in 1507.

Because Calendar Round dates repeat, Mesoamerican peoples who tracked events over longer spans — above all the Maya — supplemented them with the Long Count, an absolute count of days from a creation epoch.
