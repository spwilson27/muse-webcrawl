Black Panther is a 2018 American superhero film based on the Marvel Comics character of the same name. Produced by Marvel Studios and distributed by Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures, it is the 18th film in the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU). It was directed by Ryan Coogler, who co-wrote the screenplay with Joe Robert Cole.

Plot

After the death of his father, T'Challa returns to the hidden, technologically advanced African nation of Wakanda to take the throne. His claim is challenged by Erik Killmonger, a Wakandan-American exile who seeks to use the country's vibranium weapons to arm oppressed people of African descent worldwide. T'Challa must defeat Killmonger and decide whether Wakanda should end its isolation and share its technology with the world.

Production

The character Black Panther was created by Stan Lee and Jack Kirby in 1966 and first appeared in the MCU in Captain America: Civil War (2016). Coogler drew on African cultures for Wakanda's design; production designer Hannah Beachler and costume designer Ruth E. Carter researched peoples across the continent, and the score by Ludwig Göransson incorporated traditional African instruments and recordings made in Senegal.

Reception

The film grossed over $1.3 billion worldwide, becoming one of the highest-grossing films of all time and the highest-grossing film by a Black director at the time. Critics praised its direction, screenplay, performances, costumes and soundtrack, and it was widely noted as a cultural milestone. At the 91st Academy Awards it was nominated for seven awards including Best Picture — the first superhero film so honoured — and won for Best Costume Design, Best Original Score and Best Production Design.

Sequel

A sequel, Black Panther: Wakanda Forever, was released in November 2022, following the death of Chadwick Boseman in 2020; the role of T'Challa was not recast.
