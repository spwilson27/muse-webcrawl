Level editor

A level editor (also known as a map editor, campaign editor, or scenario editor) is a software tool used to design levels, maps, scenarios, or campaigns for a video game. An individual involved with the creation of game levels is a level designer or mapper.

In some cases, the creator of a video game releases an official level editor for the game, but this is not always the case. Sometimes players use fan-made level editors. Other times, a game may not even have a usable level editor. Some games have built-in level editing features, particularly certain platform and construction and management simulation games.

Features

Level editors vary enormously in design and features. Typical functionality includes:

  • Placing and transforming geometry — terrain, walls, floors, and props.
  • Placing entities such as enemies, items, triggers, and spawn points.
  • Setting properties and event logic, sometimes through visual scripting.
  • Real-time playtesting of the level from within the editor.
  • Importing and arranging custom assets such as textures and models.

History

One of the earliest games with a built-in level editor was Lode Runner (1983), which let players save their own stages to disk and contributed to the title's longevity. The modding scenes around Doom (1993) and its editor utilities, and later Valve's Hammer Editor for Half-Life, established level editing as a pathway into professional game design. Nintendo's Super Mario Maker (2015) brought touch-based level creation to a mass console audience, and its sequel added online sharing of user-created courses.

Community impact

Games that ship with accessible editors often sustain large modding communities that extend the product's lifespan for decades. User-generated levels may be distributed through built-in browsers, workshops such as Steam Workshop, or fan sites. Some celebrated game genres — the tower defense and MOBA genres among them — began as custom scenarios made with the editors shipped with StarCraft and Warcraft III.
