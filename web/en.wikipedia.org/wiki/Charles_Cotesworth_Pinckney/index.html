Charles Cotesworth Pinckney

Charles Cotesworth Pinckney (February 25, 1746 – August 16, 1825) was an American statesman, military officer, and early leader of the Democratic-Republican era's rival Federalist Party. A signer of the United States Constitution and delegate from South Carolina to the Constitutional Convention, he served as the United States Minister to France and was the Federalist vice-presidential nominee in 1800 and its presidential nominee in 1804 and 1808.

Early life and military service

Pinckney was born in Charleston into a wealthy planter family, educated in England at Westminster School and Christ Church, Oxford, and trained in law at the Middle Temple; he also studied military science in France. During the American Revolutionary War he served as an aide to George Washington, fought at Brandywine and Germantown, and was captured when Charleston fell in 1780, remaining a prisoner until 1782.

Constitutional Convention and diplomacy

At the 1787 Constitutional Convention, Pinckney (together with his cousin Charles Pinckney) advocateded a strong national government and protection of slavery. In 1796 President Washington appointed him minister to France; the French Directory refused to receive him, and he became one of the three envoys in the XYZ Affair of 1797–98, which pushed the United States and France toward the Quasi-War. The phrase "Millions for defense, but not one cent for tribute" became associated with his refusal of a bribe.

Later career

Pinckney was the Federalist candidate for vice president in 1800 and for president in 1804 and 1808, losing to Thomas Jefferson and James Madison respectively. He remained a leading figure in South Carolina, serving as president of the state branch of the Society of the Cincinnati.
