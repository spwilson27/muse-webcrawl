Sound engineering

Sound engineering, also known as audio engineering, is the branch of engineering concerned with the recording, manipulation, mixing, and reproduction of sound. It combines elements of electrical engineering, acoustics, psychoacoustics, and music production. Sound engineers work in recording studios, live sound reinforcement, broadcast, film and television post-production, and the design of audio equipment.

Roles

The field encompasses several specialized roles:

  • Recording engineer – operates recording equipment during studio sessions, placing microphones and setting levels.
  • Mix engineer (mixing engineer) – blends multitrack recordings into a final stereo or surround mix.
  • Mastering engineer – prepares the final mix for distribution, adjusting overall tonal balance and loudness.
  • Live sound engineer – manages front-of-house (FOH) and monitor mixes for concerts and events.
  • Systems engineer – designs and tunes large loudspeaker systems for venues.

Practice

Sound engineering draws on the physics of sound propagation, the behaviour of transducers such as microphones and loudspeakers, signal processing (equalization, dynamic range compression, reverberation), and the characteristics of recording media. Modern practice is centred on the digital audio workstation (DAW), though analogue consoles and tape machines remain in use for their sonic character. See also sound recording and reproduction and acoustics.

Education and professional bodies

Formal training ranges from certificate programs to university degrees in audio engineering, music technology, and acoustical engineering. The Audio Engineering Society (AES), founded in 1948, is the principal professional society for the field and publishes the Journal of the Audio Engineering Society.
