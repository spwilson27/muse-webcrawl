An Olympiad (Greek: Ὀλυμπιάς) is a period of four years, originally the interval between successive celebrations of the ancient Olympic Games. The ancient Greeks used Olympiads as a chronological system, numbering years by the Olympiad and the year within it, reckoned from the first recorded games in 776 BC, won in the stadion race by Coroebus of Elis.

In modern usage the term is associated with the Olympic Games cycle — the Summer Games are held in the first year of an Olympiad — and, by extension, with academic competitions such as the International Mathematical Olympiad, which are called "olympiads" though not held on a four-year cycle.

Ancient reckoning

Historians such as Timaeus and Eratosthenes systematised dating by Olympiads; the system became standard in Hellenistic chronography and was used alongside Roman consular dating and regnal years. The games, and with them the reckoning, endured until the late 4th century AD.

Modern Olympic usage

The modern Olympic movement retains the four-year Olympiad: the Games of the XXXII Olympiad, for example, were the Tokyo 2020 Summer Olympics (held in 2021). An Olympiad begins on 1 January of the year of the Summer Games and ends four years later; if the Games are cancelled, the Olympiad is still counted, as happened in 1916, 1940 and 1944.

Other uses

"Olympiad" also names academic contests (science and mathematics olympiads), chess olympiads held every two years, and various cultural competitions.
