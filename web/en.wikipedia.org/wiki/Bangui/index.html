Bangui (French pronunciation: [bɑ̃ɡi]) is the capital and largest city of the Central African Republic. It stands on the northern bank of the Ubangi River, which forms much of the country's southern border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo. With a population of roughly 890,000 in the city proper, Bangui is the country's political, economic and transport centre and contains most of its population of any single city.

History

The French established a military post at Bangui in 1889, near a village whose name derives from local words for the rapids in the river. It became the capital of the French colony of Ubangi-Shari and, in 1960, of the independent Central African Republic under Barthélemy Boganda's successor David Dacko. The city was the seat of Jean-Bédel Bokassa's regime and his short-lived Central African Empire (1976–1979). Bangui has repeatedly been affected by coups and, in 2013, was seized by the Séléka rebel coalition, triggering a civil conflict and a French and African peacekeeping intervention.

Geography and economy

Bangui has a tropical savanna climate with a long wet season. Its river port handles most of the country's international trade, ferrying goods to and from Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire; the economy centres on administration, trade, diamonds, timber and cotton. Landmarks include the Notre-Dame Cathedral, the Boganda National Museum, the central market and the hilltop site of the former imperial palace at Kolongo.
