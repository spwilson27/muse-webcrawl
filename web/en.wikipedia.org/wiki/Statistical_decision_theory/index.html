Statistical decision theory

Statistical decision theory is a framework for making decisions under uncertainty using statistical models. It unifies estimation, hypothesis testing and prediction as special cases of choosing an action to minimise an expected loss (or maximise an expected utility).

Framework

A decision problem consists of a parameter space Θ, a sample space of observable data, an action space, and a loss function L(θ, a) that measures the cost of taking action a when the true parameter is θ. A decision rule δ maps observed data to an action. The risk function R(θ, δ) is the expected loss of the rule under the distribution indexed by θ.

Approaches

The frequentist approach, developed by Abraham Wald, compares rules by their risk functions. A rule is admissible if no other rule has risk no greater everywhere and smaller somewhere. The minimax rule minimises the maximum risk over θ. The Bayesian approach places a prior distribution on θ and chooses the rule minimising the posterior expected loss, yielding Bayes rules and the Bayes risk.

Applications

Decision theory underlies experimental design, clinical trials, quality control, machine learning and economics.
