Carl Thomas Mozart

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Carl Thomas Mozart (21 September 1784 – 31 October 1858) was the second son, and the elder of the two surviving sons, of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart and Constanze Weber. Unlike his younger brother Franz Xaver Wolfgang Mozart, who became a professional musician, Carl pursued a career as an official in the service of the Austrian Empire in Milan.

Life

Carl was born in Vienna. He was seven years old when his father died in December 1791. Constanze arranged for his education partly in Prague, where he was cared for by Franz Xaver Niemetschek, the first biographer of Mozart. Gifted at the piano, he nonetheless chose — or was steered toward — a secure administrative career: in 1805 he moved to Livorno to enter commerce, then settled in Milan, where he obtained a post in the Austrian administration of Lombardy and rose to become a respected finance official of the imperial government.

Carl remained proud of his father's legacy. He guarded family papers and relics, corresponded about editions and biographies of Mozart's works, and in 1842 travelled to Salzburg for the unveiling of the Mozart monument, where he and his brother were honoured guests. He never married and had no children; he died in Milan in 1858. His brother Franz Xaver, who also died childless, had predeceased him in 1844 — with them, Mozart's direct male line ended.

Legacy

Carl Thomas Mozart's surviving letters and the family documents he preserved in Milan are valuable sources for Mozart scholarship, and his role as custodian of the composer's reputation in Italy helped sustain Mozart's posthumous fame in the nineteenth century.
