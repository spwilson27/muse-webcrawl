George Sand (born Amantine Lucile Aurore Dupin, later Baroness Dudevant; 1 July 1804 – 8 June 1876) was a French novelist, memoirist and journalist, one of the most popular and prolific writers of the European Romantic era. Writing under a masculine pen name, she was among the most widely read French authors of the nineteenth century and a celebrated — and controversial — public figure who defied the conventions expected of women of her time.

Life

Aurore Dupin was born in Paris, the descendant on one side of aristocracy and on the other of a Parisian bird-seller. Raised largely by her grandmother at the estate of Nohant in the Berry countryside, she married Casimir Dudevant in 1822 and had two children, Maurice and Solange. The marriage was unhappy; in 1831 she left for Paris, supported herself by journalism, and began her literary career. Her first solo novel, Indiana (1832), published under the name George Sand, was an immediate success and attacked the institution of marriage.

Sand became famous both for her fiction and for her unconventional life: she wore men's clothing, smoked cigars, and conducted celebrated love affairs, most famously with the poet Alfred de Musset and the composer Frédéric Chopin, with whom she spent the winter of 1838–39 in Mallorca — a journey she chronicled in Un hiver à Majorque. Her estate at Nohant became a gathering place for artists including Franz Liszt, Honoré de Balzac and Gustave Flaubert, with whom she corresponded extensively.

Works

Sand's early "passion novels", such as Indiana (1832), Lélia (1833) and Mauprat (1837), protested against the legal and social subordination of women. In the 1840s she turned to socialist and philosophical themes in Le Compagnon du tour de France and the vast double novel Consuelo (1842) and La Comtesse de Rudolstadt. After the failure of the Revolution of 1848 she withdrew to Nohant and wrote her beloved pastoral novels — La Mare au diable (1846), François le Champi (1847) and La Petite Fadette (1849) — which idealise the peasants of Berry while remaining alert to social injustice. She also wrote an immense autobiography, Histoire de ma vie (1854–55), and dozens of plays.

Legacy

Sand died at Nohant on 8 June 1876. Admired by Flaubert, Dostoevsky and Proust, and championed by later feminist critics, she is remembered as one of the defining voices of French Romanticism and a pioneering figure in women's literary history.
