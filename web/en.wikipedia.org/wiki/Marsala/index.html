Marsala (Sicilian: Maissala; Latin: Lilybaeum) is an Italian town located in the Province of Trapani in the westernmost part of Sicily. Marsala is the most populated town in its province and the fifth largest in Sicily. The town is famous for the docking there of Giuseppe Garibaldi on 11 May 1860 (the Expedition of the Thousand) and for its Marsala wine. A feature of the area is the Stagnone Lagoon — a marine area with salt ponds.

Built on the headland of Cape Boeo, the ancient Carthaginian stronghold of Lilybaeum, Marsala was founded by the Phoenicians after the destruction of their colony on the island of Motya in 397 BC. Lilybaeum withstood sieges by Pyrrhus of Epirus and the Romans during the First Punic War and became a prosperous Roman port.

Marsala wine

Marsala is a fortified wine, dry or sweet, produced in the region surrounding the city, made chiefly from Grillo, Inzolia, and Catarratto grapes and fortified with grape spirit. Its development as an export wine is credited to the English merchant John Woodhouse, who in 1773 shipped wine preserved with added alcohol to England; firms such as Woodhouse, Ingham, and Florio built the trade. Marsala received DOC status in 1969 and is used in cooking (for example in chicken marsala) as well as drunk as an aperitif or dessert wine.

Main sights

Sights include the Archaeological Museum with its Punic ship remains, the cathedral dedicated to Saint Thomas of Canterbury, the Spanish quarter and town gates, the salt pans and windmills of the Stagnone, and excursions to the island of Motya with its Phoenician ruins. The nearby airport of Trapani–Birgi serves the area.
