Cornwallis Island

Location: Northern Canada
Archipelago: Queen Elizabeth Islands, Canadian Arctic Archipelago
Area: 6,995 km² (2,701 sq mi)
Territory: Nunavut
Population: 183 (2021, Resolute)

Cornwallis Island is one of the Queen Elizabeth Islands, part of the Canadian Arctic Archipelago, in the Qikiqtani Region of Nunavut in the Canadian territory of Nunavut. It is in the Wellington Channel, south of Devon Island and north-west of Resolute Bay. With an area of about 6,995 km², it is the 96th largest island in the world and Canada's 21st largest island.

Geography and history

The island is roughly 113 km long and rises to around 359 m; much of it is a low, rocky plateau with polar desert vegetation. It was first sighted by Europeans in 1819 during William Edward Parry's expedition and named for Admiral Sir William Cornwallis. The island figured in the search for the lost Franklin expedition in the 1850s.

Resolute

The hamlet of Resolute (Qausuittuq), on the island's south coast, is one of Canada's northernmost communities. It was established in 1953 when Inuit families were relocated from northern Quebec and Baffin Island during the controversial High Arctic relocation; the Canadian government formally apologized in 2010. Resolute's airport serves as a hub for High Arctic research and resupply, and the area supports the Canadian Armed Forces Arctic training.

Research

The Polar Continental Shelf Program and university research groups have long used Resolute as a logistics base. Cornwallis Island's well-preserved fossil forests and Silurian reef structures are of geological interest.
