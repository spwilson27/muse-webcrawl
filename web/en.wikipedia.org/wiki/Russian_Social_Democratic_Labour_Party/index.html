Russian Social Democratic Labour Party

The Russian Social Democratic Labour Party (RSDLP) was a Marxist socialist political party founded in Minsk in 1898 to unite the revolutionary organisations of the Russian Empire. It became the parent party of both the Bolsheviks and the Mensheviks, whose split shaped the Russian Revolution.

Foundation and split

Nine delegates attended the First Congress in Minsk in 1898, but the Okhrana soon arrested most of the Central Committee. The Second Congress, held in Brussels and London in 1903, adopted a party programme — calling for a democratic republic as a minimum and socialism as a maximum — but split over membership rules and editorial control of the newspaper Iskra. Vladimir Lenin's supporters won key votes and became known as the Bolsheviks ("majority"), while Julius Martov's faction were the Mensheviks ("minority").

Later history

The factions functioned increasingly as separate parties, formally dividing by 1912. After the February Revolution of 1917, Mensheviks participated in the Provisional Government's socialist coalition, while the Bolsheviks under Lenin seized power in the October Revolution and in 1918 renamed themselves the Russian Communist Party (Bolsheviks). The Mensheviks were suppressed in Soviet Russia by the early 1920s, continuing in exile.
