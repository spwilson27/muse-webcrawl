Udai Singh II (4 August 1522 – 28 February 1572) was the Maharana of Mewar in north-western India and the founder of the city of Udaipur. A ruler of the Sisodia Rajput clan, he is chiefly remembered for moving his capital from vulnerable Chittorgarh to the defensible lake city of Udaipur, and as the father of the celebrated Maharana Pratap.

Early life

Udai Singh was a son of Rana Sanga, the great Mewar king defeated by Babur at Khanwa in 1527. As a child he survived a famous assassination attempt: the usurper Banbir sought to kill the young prince, but the nurse Panna Dhai sacrificed her own son in his place, and Udai Singh was raised in safety at Kumbhalgarh. He recovered the throne in 1540.

Founding of Udaipur

Recognising Chittor's exposure, Udai Singh founded a new capital in the Girwa valley by Lake Pichola in 1559, on the advice, legend says, of a hermit he met while hunting. The new city — Udaipur — was shielded by hills, forests and lakes. His foresight proved decisive when Akbar besieged and sacked Chittorgarh in 1568: the royal house survived at Udaipur and Mewar never fully submitted to the Mughals.

Death and succession

Udai Singh died at Gogunda in 1572 and was succeeded by his eldest son, Maharana Pratap, who would become the hero of Mewar's resistance and of the Battle of Haldighati (1576) against the armies of Man Singh I of Amber.
