Railbus

A railbus is a lightweight passenger rail vehicle that shares many aspects of its construction with a bus, typically having a bus (original or modified) body and four wheels on a fixed base, instead of on bogies. Originally designed and developed during the 1930s, railbuses evolved into larger railcars with many similar features.

Railbuses are designed for use on lightly used railway lines where full trains would be uneconomical, and are widely used in countries such as Germany, the Czech Republic and Japan, as well as on rural lines elsewhere. Modern examples include the British Rail Pacer family.
