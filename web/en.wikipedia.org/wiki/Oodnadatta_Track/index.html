Oodnadatta Track

The unsealed Oodnadatta Track in South Australia's far north.

The Oodnadatta Track is a famous unsealed 617-kilometre (383 mi) outback road in the Australian state of South Australia, running between Marla in the north and Marree in the south, via Oodnadatta and William Creek. It follows a traditional Aboriginal trading route and the alignment of the old Central Australian Railway (the "Old Ghan"), and passes alongside the southern shore of Lake Eyre.

History

The route was the pathway of the Overland Telegraph Line and the narrow-gauge railway that reached Oodnadatta in 1891 and was extended to Alice Springs in 1929. When the standard-gauge line was relocated westwards in 1980, the track remained as the road link for remote stations and communities. Explorer John McDouall Stuart used portions of the Aboriginal route on his journeys of 1858–1862.

Points of interest

  • Lake Eyre (Kati Thanda), visible from lookouts near William Creek
  • The Mound Springs — artesian springs of the Great Artesian Basin, including Blanche Cup and the Bubbler
  • Algebuckina Bridge, the longest bridge in South Australia, spanning the Neales River
  • Coward Springs, a former railway siding with a date-palm oasis and natural spa
  • Ruined sidings and fettlers' cottages of the Old Ghan line
  • The Pink Roadhouse at Oodnadatta

Conditions

The track is maintained gravel, usually passable to two-wheel-drive vehicles in dry weather, but it can be closed after rain. Travellers must be self-sufficient with fuel, water and spare tyres; there is no mobile coverage for long stretches.
