Intuitionistic logic

Intuitionistic logic, sometimes called constructive logic, is a system of symbolic logic that differs from classical logic by replacing the traditional concept of truth with the concept of constructive provability. It arose from L. E. J. Brouwer's intuitionism in the foundations of mathematics: a statement is true, for the intuitionist, only if there is a construction (a proof or method) witnessing it. Consequently intuitionistic logic does not accept, as general principles, the law of the excluded middle (A ∨ ¬A) or double-negation elimination (¬¬A → A).

Philosophy

Brouwer held that mathematics is a free creation of the mind and that logical laws are derived from mathematical practice, not the other way round. Because an infinite domain cannot in general be surveyed, one cannot assert that every instance of a property either holds or fails. A proof of an existence statement must provide a way to construct the object; a proof of a disjunction must indicate which disjunct holds. This is codified in the Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov (BHK) interpretation of the logical connectives.

Formal system

Arend Heyting gave the first formal axiomatisation in 1930. Intuitionistic propositional logic can be obtained from classical logic by dropping the axiom of excluded middle (equivalently, double-negation elimination or Peirce's law). Nevertheless, some classical principles remain: ¬¬¬A → ¬A holds, and one direction of De Morgan's laws holds (¬(A ∨ B) ↔ ¬A ∧ ¬B), while the other fails. Gödel showed in 1932 that intuitionistic logic cannot be given a finite-valued truth-table semantics; Kripke's possible-worlds models (with monotonic accessibility) and Heyting algebras provide sound and complete semantics.

Relation to other systems

Through the Gödel–Gentzen negative translation, classical logic embeds into intuitionistic logic: a statement is classically provable if and only if its double-negation translation is intuitionistically provable. Intuitionistic logic is thus strictly weaker in theorems but richer in distinctions. Under the Curry–Howard correspondence, intuitionistic proofs correspond to terminating programs in typed lambda calculi, which is why intuitionistic (constructive) logic underlies proof assistants such as Coq and Agda and Martin-Löf type theory. Constructive mathematics built on this logic yields results with computational content: from a proof that an object exists one can extract an algorithm computing it.
