Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov interpretation

The Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov interpretation, or BHK interpretation, is an explanation of the meaning of the logical connectives in intuitionistic logic in terms of proofs and constructions. Developed from ideas of L. E. J. Brouwer, Arend Heyting, and Andrey Kolmogorov, it gives a proof-conditional semantics: the meaning of a proposition is determined by what counts as a proof of it.

The interpretation

Under the BHK interpretation, a proof of a conjunction A ∧ B is a pair consisting of a proof of A and a proof of B. A proof of a disjunction A ∨ B is a proof of A or a proof of B, together with an indication of which one it is. A proof of an implication A → B is a construction that transforms any proof of A into a proof of B. A proof of ¬A is a construction turning any proof of A into a proof of a contradiction. A proof of an existential statement ∃x P(x) consists of a specific witness together with a proof that it satisfies P, while a proof of ∀x P(x) is an algorithm producing, for any given x, a proof of P(x).

Consequences

Because a proof of A ∨ ¬A would require a general method deciding, for arbitrary A, which disjunct holds, the law of excluded middle is not valid under this interpretation. The BHK interpretation underlies the Curry–Howard correspondence, which identifies intuitionistic proofs with computer programs, and it remains foundational in constructive mathematics and type theory.
