Curry–Howard correspondence

The Curry–Howard correspondence, also called the Curry–Howard isomorphism or the proofs-as-programs interpretation, is the direct relationship discovered between computer programs and mathematical proofs. It rests on two observations: by Haskell Curry, that combinatory logic corresponds to Hilbert-style deduction, and by William Alvin Howard, that intuitionistic natural deduction corresponds to the simply typed lambda calculus.

The correspondence

Under the correspondence, a proposition is a type, and a proof of the proposition is a program of that type. Logical implication corresponds to the function type, conjunction to the product (pair) type, and disjunction to the sum (tagged union) type. Simplification of proofs corresponds to evaluation of programs, so a proposition is provable exactly when its type is inhabited.

History and influence

Howard set out the correspondence in a 1969 manuscript published in 1980. The idea has since been extended to many logics and type systems, including dependent types, and it underlies proof assistants such as Coq and Agda, in which writing a program and proving its correctness are the same activity. It is a cornerstone of constructive logic and the Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov interpretation.
