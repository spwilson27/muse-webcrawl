Boolean algebra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Boolean algebra is a branch of algebra in which the values of the variables are the truth values true and false, usually denoted 1 and 0 respectively. The main operations are conjunction (AND, ∧), disjunction (OR, ∨), and negation (NOT, ¬). It was introduced by George Boole in his 1847 pamphlet The Mathematical Analysis of Logic and developed in his 1854 book An Investigation of the Laws of Thought.

History

Boole's algebra of logic was largely ignored until the twentieth century, when its practical importance became clear. In 1937 Claude Shannon, in his master's thesis at MIT, showed that Boolean algebra could be applied to the analysis and design of switching circuits, founding digital circuit design. Boolean algebra is now fundamental to computer science, electrical engineering, and set theory.

Operations and laws

Boolean expressions obey laws analogous to those of ordinary algebra, including commutativity, associativity, distributivity of ∧ over ∨ (and vice versa in Boolean algebra), identity elements (0 for ∨, 1 for ∧), and complements: x ∧ ¬x = 0 and x ∨ ¬x = 1. De Morgan's laws state that ¬(x ∧ y) = ¬x ∨ ¬y and ¬(x ∨ y) = ¬x ∧ ¬y. Unlike ordinary algebra, Boolean algebra satisfies the absorption laws x ∨ (x ∧ y) = x and x ∧ (x ∨ y) = x, reflecting the idempotence of ∧ and ∨.

Axiomatics and generalizations

A Boolean algebra can be defined abstractly as a complemented distributive lattice. Every Boolean algebra is a special case of a Heyting algebra, the algebraic structure that models intuitionistic logic; in a general Heyting algebra the law x ∨ ¬x = 1 need not hold, so negation is weaker than Boolean complementation. Stone's representation theorem shows that every Boolean algebra is isomorphic to a field of sets.

Applications

Boolean algebra underlies logic gates in digital electronics, Boolean operators in database and web search queries, and conditional expressions in programming languages. In probability and set theory, the algebra of events and the algebra of sets are both instances of Boolean algebra.
