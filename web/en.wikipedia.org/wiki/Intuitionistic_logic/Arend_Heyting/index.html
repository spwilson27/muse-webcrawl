Arend Heyting (Dutch: [ˈaːrənt ˈɦɛitɪŋ]; 9 May 1898 – 9 July 1980) was a Dutch mathematician and logician. He was a student of Luitzen Egbertus Jan Brouwer, the founder of mathematical intuitionism, and is best known for giving the first formal development of intuitionistic logic and intuitionistic arithmetic, making Brouwer's philosophical ideas precise enough for mathematical study.

Life and career

Heyting was born in Amsterdam and studied mathematics at the University of Amsterdam under Brouwer, completing his doctorate in 1925 with work on intuitionistic axiomatics of projective geometry. He spent much of his career teaching, including at a secondary school in Enschede, before becoming a lecturer and then professor at the University of Amsterdam, where he remained until his retirement in 1968. He died in Lugano, Switzerland, in 1980.

Contributions

In 1930 Heyting published a set of formal axioms for intuitionistic propositional and predicate logic, presenting the logic that intuitionists implicitly used in their reasoning about mathematics. The resulting systems are now standard: intuitionistic propositional logic can be characterized algebraically by Heyting algebras, complete lattices in which implication is interpreted relative to pseudo-complementation. Heyting also formalized Heyting arithmetic (HA), the intuitionistic counterpart of Peano arithmetic, which has the same axioms but uses intuitionistic rather than classical logic.

Heyting explained the intended meaning of the logical connectives in terms of constructions and proofs — the account now known as the Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov interpretation (or BHK interpretation), according to which a proof of a disjunction must indicate which disjunct holds, and a proof of an existential statement must provide a witness.

Selected works

  • Intuitionism: An Introduction (1956) — a widely read exposition of intuitionistic mathematics.
  • "Die formalen Regeln der intuitionistischen Logik" (1930) — the formalization of intuitionistic logic.
  • "Die formalen Regeln der intuitionistischen Mathematik" (1930) — formal rules for intuitionistic mathematics.
