Carmarthen

Carmarthen (Welsh: Caerfyrddin) is the county town of Carmarthenshire in southwestern Wales, situated on the River Towy. It claims to be the oldest town in Wales, originating as the Roman settlement of Moridunum, and is traditionally associated with the wizard Merlin of Arthurian legend.

History

The Romans built a fort and later a town at Moridunum, the tribal capital of the Demetae, complete with one of only seven Roman amphitheatres in Britain. In the Middle Ages Carmarthen grew around its Norman castle and Augustinian priory, and became one of Wales's largest boroughs and a busy port. The Welsh name Caerfyrddin was popularly read as "Merlin's fort", giving rise to the town's Merlin legends.

Modern town

Carmarthen is a market and service centre for the surrounding agricultural region and a campus town of the University of Wales Trinity Saint David, whose origins go back to 1848. The town is a stronghold of the Welsh language, and the Carmarthenshire County Museum occupies the former palace of the bishops of St Davids at Abergwili nearby.
