Corn beer

Corn beer is a beer style brewed with corn (maize) as a significant ingredient. The best-known traditional corn beer is chicha de jora, an Andean beverage made from germinated maize (jora) that has been brewed in South America for centuries. Corn is also used as an adjunct grain in modern mass-market lager production.

Overview

Key examples: Chicha de jora, umqombothi, adjunct lagers

Base ingredient: Maize

Traditional corn beers

In the Andes, chicha is prepared by malting maize, mashing and fermenting the wort, sometimes with the starches first broken down by chewing. In southern Africa, maize-and-sorghum beers such as umqombothi are brewed for ceremonies and communal gatherings. Native peoples of North America also produced corn-based fermented drinks.

Modern brewing

Large commercial brewers use corn grits, corn syrup or flaked maize as an adjunct to barley malt. Corn lightens body and flavour, adds fermentable sugars economically, and produces the pale, mild character typical of American-style lagers.
