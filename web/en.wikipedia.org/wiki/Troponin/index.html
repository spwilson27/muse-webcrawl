Troponin

[Image: Troponin complex on the actin filament, regulating muscle contraction with calcium]

Overview

Troponin is a complex of three regulatory proteins (troponin C, I and T) attached to tropomyosin on the actin filaments of striated muscle. When calcium binds troponin C, the complex shifts tropomyosin to expose myosin-binding sites, allowing contraction.

Clinical use

Cardiac troponins I and T are nearly specific to heart muscle, and their measurement in blood is the cornerstone biomarker for diagnosing heart attack (myocardial infarction): a rise and/or fall of cardiac troponin, with at least one value above the 99th percentile, plus clinical evidence of ischaemia, defines infarction in the Universal Definition. High-sensitivity troponin assays detect very low levels, enabling rapid rule-out protocols (0/1-hour algorithms) in emergency departments — and also detect troponin release in other conditions (myocarditis, pulmonary embolism, sepsis, renal failure), requiring clinical interpretation.
