Standard-definition television

Standard-definition television (SDTV, or simply SD) is a television system that uses a resolution not considered high definition. The term is usually applied to the digital television formats that succeeded the analogue broadcast standards of the 20th century: 480i (based on the 525-line NTSC system) and 576i (based on the 625-line PAL and SECAM systems). SDTV pictures are typically broadcast in a 4:3 or 16:9 aspect ratio.

Formats

In digital terms, 480i provides 720 × 480 pixels (or 704 × 480) with interlaced fields at about 60 fields per second, while 576i provides 720 × 576 pixels at 50 fields per second. Digital SDTV is specified in standards such as ITU-R BT.601 and is carried by DVB, ATSC and ISDB broadcasts, as well as on DVDs, which store video at SD resolution.

History and decline

Analogue NTSC, PAL and SECAM broadcasts were all, in effect, standard definition. The transition to digital broadcasting in the 2000s initially kept SD resolutions while adding widescreen, but the availability of high-definition television led broadcasters and streaming services progressively to favour HD and later 4K. Many channels closed their SD simulcasts during the 2010s and 2020s, although SDTV remains in use for some services, archival material and low-bandwidth applications.
