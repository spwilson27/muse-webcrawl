Talavera poblana

TypeMaiolica pottery
OriginPuebla, Mexico
Introduced16th century
RecognitionDenomination of origin; UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2019)

Talavera poblana is a Mexican style of maiolica pottery originating in the city of Puebla and the nearby towns of San Pablo del Monte and others in the states of Puebla and Tlaxcala. It is distinguished by a milky-white glazed base decorated with painted designs, traditionally in blue but now also in other regulated colours.

History

The craft arrived in New Spain in the mid-16th century when potters from Talavera de la Reina and Seville established workshops in Puebla, whose fine local clays proved ideal. Guild ordinances of 1653 regulated quality and design. Talavera tiles (azulejos) came to decorate churches, fountains, kitchens, and building façades throughout colonial Mexico.

Production

Authentic talavera is hand-made from two local clays, thrown or moulded, fired, dipped in a tin-based white glaze, painted with mineral pigments, and fired again. Production is legally restricted to certified workshops in the designated zone under a denomination of origin, and in 2019 the artisanal process was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
