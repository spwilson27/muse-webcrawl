William Brown (admiral)

William Brown (1777–1857), founder of the Argentine Navy.

William Brown (22 June 1777 – 3 March 1857), known in Spanish as Guillermo Brown, was an Irish-born Argentine admiral. He is regarded as the founder of the Argentine Navy and is one of Argentina's national heroes.

Early life

Born in Foxford, County Mayo, Ireland, Brown emigrated as a boy to the United States, served in the American merchant marine, and was press-ganged into the Royal Navy during the Napoleonic Wars. After escaping, he settled in Buenos Aires around 1810 and became a merchant shipowner on the Río de la Plata.

Naval campaigns

In 1814 Brown took command of the fledgling revolutionary squadron of the United Provinces and defeated Spanish royalist forces, capturing the island stronghold of Martín García and blockading Montevideo, a decisive contribution to the independence struggle. He later raided Spanish commerce along the Pacific coast of South America. In the Cisplatine War against Brazil (1825–1828) he won the battles of Los Pozos and Juncal, and he continued to serve Argentina in its later conflicts, including the blockade years of the 1840s.

Legacy

Brown died in Buenos Aires in 1857. Towns, streets, and numerous Argentine warships — including the destroyer ARA Almirante Brown — honour his memory, and he remains a revered figure in both Argentina and Ireland.
