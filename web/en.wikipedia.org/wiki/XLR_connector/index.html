The XLR connector is a type of electrical connector primarily found on professional audio, video, and stage lighting equipment. Its circular design features between three and seven pins, and the connectors are durable, locking, and designed for balanced audio connections that reject interference over long cable runs.

History and naming

The connector was invented by James H. Cannon of the Cannon Electric Company in Los Angeles. The series began with the Cannon "X" connector; a latching version ("XL") followed, and a version with a resilient neoprene insulation ("XLR") gave the series its familiar name. The three-pin XLR became the standard connector for microphones and balanced line-level interconnection in recording studios, live sound, and broadcasting.

Pinout and use

For balanced audio, the standard three-pin wiring (AES) is: pin 1 = chassis ground/cable shield, pin 2 = positive polarity (hot), pin 3 = negative polarity (cold). XLR connectors carry microphone signals and also supply phantom power (typically 48 V) from a mixer to condenser microphones over the same pins. Five-pin XLR is standard for DMX512 stage-lighting data, and other pin counts serve intercoms, power supplies, and loudspeaker or lighting control in specialist contexts. A combined speakON/XLR-style connector exists, but XLR itself is never used for high-power loudspeaker outputs in modern practice for safety reasons.
