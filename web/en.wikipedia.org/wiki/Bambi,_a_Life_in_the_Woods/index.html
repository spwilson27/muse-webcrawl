Bambi, a Life in the Woods (German: Bambi: Eine Lebensgeschichte aus dem Walde) is a 1923 novel by the Austrian author Felix Salten, first serialised in the Viennese newspaper Neue Freie Presse and published in book form by Ullstein. It follows the life of Bambi, a roe deer, from birth through the loss of his mother to a hunter, his lessons from his father the old stag, and his eventual maturity as a solitary prince of the forest.

Themes

Unlike its famous adaptation, the novel is a sombre, unsentimental tale aimed at adults as much as children. The forest animals refer to humankind only as "He" — an almost godlike, terrifying presence — and the book explores mortality, solitude and the indifference of nature. It has often been read as an allegory of the vulnerability of minorities, and the Nazis banned it in 1936 as "political allegory on the treatment of Jews in Europe"; Salten, who was Jewish, fled Austria after the Anschluss.

Adaptations and legacy

Walt Disney's animated film Bambi (1942), based on Whittaker Chambers's 1928 English translation, transformed the story into a gentle children's classic — changing Bambi into a white-tailed deer for American audiences — and made the death of Bambi's mother one of cinema's most famous scenes. Salten also wrote a sequel, Bambi's Children (1939), about Bambi's twin fawns.
