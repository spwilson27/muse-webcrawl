Kobe beef

Kobe beef (神戸ビーフ, Kōbe bīfu) is Wagyu beef from the Tajima strain of Japanese Black cattle, raised in Hyōgo Prefecture according to rules set by the Kobe Beef Marketing and Distribution Promotion Association. It is famed for its flavour, tenderness and extensive fatty marbling.

Standards

To be certified, cattle must be pure Tajima lineage born and raised in Hyōgo, and the meat must meet strict grades for marbling (BMS), yield and weight. Only a few thousand head are certified each year, making genuine Kobe beef rare and expensive. The name is a protected regional collective trademark and a Geographical Indication in Japan.

History

Cattle were introduced to Japan in the 2nd century for rice cultivation labour and were long not eaten. Beef eating spread after the Meiji Restoration, and the port of Kobe, opened to foreign trade in 1868, gave the local beef its name abroad. Much beef marketed as "Kobe" outside Japan is crossbred Wagyu-style beef and not certified Kobe beef, exports of which began only in 2012.
