The yatagan or yataghan (Turkish: yatağan) is a type of Ottoman short sabre or long knife used from the mid-16th to the late 19th century. It takes its name from the town of Yatağan in southwestern Turkey, an early centre of its manufacture. The yatagan was extensively carried in the Balkans and Anatolia and became closely associated with Janissaries and Zeibeks, though it had no standard military issue.

Description

The yatagan characteristically has a single-edged blade with a pronounced forward curve toward the point, and — distinctively — no guard. Its hilt of bone, ivory, horn, or wood ends in two large flaring "ears" at the pommel, which help retain the weapon in the hand when striking. The blade and hilt were often richly decorated with silver or gold filigree, coral inlay, and inscriptions including the maker's name, the owner's name, and verses.

The scabbard was usually of wood covered in leather or metal, sometimes elaborately embossed, and the weapon was worn thrust through the sash or belt at an angle.

Use and legacy

Yatagans were sidearms of militia and irregulars across the Ottoman world, including haiduks and klephts in the Balkans and fighters in the Greek War of Independence, where the type became part of Greek national dress imagery. Bayonets of the mid-19th century — notably the French Chassepot bayonet and the British Pattern 1856 short rifle bayonet — borrowed the yatagan's recurved blade profile, acquiring the name "yatagan bayonets".
