Alamannia (also Alemannia) was the territory inhabited by the Alemanni, a confederation of Germanic peoples who settled in the region between the upper Rhine, the upper Danube and the Alps from the 3rd century AD. The name survives in several modern languages' word for Germany (for example French Allemagne and Spanish Alemania). After periods of independence and of conflict with Rome, Alamannia was subdued by the Franks and became one of the stem duchies of the Frankish and later East Frankish kingdom, evolving into the Duchy of Swabia.

Roman period

The Alemanni first appear in Roman sources in the early 3rd century, raiding the Agri Decumates beyond the Rhine–Danube frontier; by about 260 the Romans abandoned that salient, and the Alemanni settled it. Roman emperors campaigned repeatedly against them — Julian defeated a large Alemannic army at Strasbourg in 357 — while many Alemanni served in the Roman army or settled as federates. In the 5th century Alamannia expanded into Alsace and northern Switzerland as Roman authority collapsed.

Frankish conquest and duchy

The Frankish king Clovis I defeated the Alemanni at the Battle of Tolbiac (traditionally 496), beginning their incorporation into the Frankish realm; further defeats by 506 brought the northern parts under Frankish control, while the south came under Ostrogothic protection until the 530s. Under the Merovingians and Carolingians, Alamannia was ruled by dukes of the Agilolfing and other families, obtained its own law codes (the Pactus and Lex Alamannorum), and was Christianised through missions associated with Columbanus, Gall and Pirmin, and the great monasteries of St Gall and Reichenau. Duke Theudebald's resistance ended with the Frankish mayor Carloman's assembly and massacre of the Alamannic nobility at Cannstatt in 746, after which the duchy was ruled directly or through counts until it re-emerged, recast, as Swabia in the 10th century.

Legacy

The Alemannic dialects of German are still spoken in Baden-Württemberg, Alsace, Switzerland, Liechtenstein and Vorarlberg, and the territory roughly coincides with that dialect area. The name Alamannia also underlies the words for "Germany" in French, Spanish, Portuguese, Turkish and Arabic.
