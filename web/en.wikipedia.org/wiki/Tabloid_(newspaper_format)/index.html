Tabloid (newspaper format)

[Image: Commuters reading tabloid newspapers on a train]

Tabloid is a newspaper format roughly half the size of a broadsheet (about 430 × 280 mm), and by extension a style of popular journalism — big pictures, short stories, celebrity, sport and campaigns. Britain's 'red-top' tabloids (The Sun, the Mirror, the Daily Star) are the archetype, while quality papers adopting the size (The Times, The Guardian) prefer the word 'compact'.

History

The format spread from early-1900s New York (the Daily News) and London; tabloids' ease of handling on trains and their populist voice made them the mass sellers of the 20th century, and their techniques influenced all popular media.
