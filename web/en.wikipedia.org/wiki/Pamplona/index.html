Pamplona (Spanish: [pamˈplona]; Basque: Iruña), historically known as Pampeluna in English, is the capital city of the autonomous community of Navarre in northern Spain. It stands on the Arga River in a basin surrounded by mountains and is an important stop on the Camino de Santiago pilgrimage route.

The old town of Pamplona

History

The city was founded as Pompaelo by the Roman general Pompey in 74 BC on the site of a Vasconic settlement. It later formed part of the Visigothic and Muslim spheres before becoming the capital of the Kingdom of Pamplona, the nucleus of the medieval Kingdom of Navarre. Its citadel and walls, largely preserved, made it a key fortress on the French frontier. Navarre was incorporated into Castile in 1512–1515.

San Fermín

Pamplona is world-famous for the festival of San Fermín, held 6–14 July, whose centrepiece is the encierro, the running of the bulls through the old town streets to the bullring, popularised internationally by Ernest Hemingway's novel The Sun Also Rises (1926). The festival draws hundreds of thousands of visitors, with participants traditionally dressed in white with red scarves.

Economy and culture

The modern city hosts the University of Navarra and the Public University of Navarre, a Volkswagen car plant, and a growing renewable-energy sector. Landmarks include the Gothic cathedral, the Plaza del Castillo, and the Renaissance citadel, now a park and cultural venue.
