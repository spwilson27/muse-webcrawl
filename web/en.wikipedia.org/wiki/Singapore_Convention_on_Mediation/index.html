Singapore Convention on Mediation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The United Nations Convention on International Settlement Agreements Resulting from Mediation, known as the Singapore Convention on Mediation, is a multilateral treaty adopted by the UN General Assembly on 20 December 2018, opened for signature in Singapore on 7 August 2019 (46 states signed on the first day) and in force from 12 September 2020. Prepared by UNCITRAL (whose Model Law on Mediation was revised in parallel), it allows a party to an international commercial settlement agreement reached through mediation to enforce it directly in the courts of a party state, without first suing on the contract — filling the gap that the New York Convention (1958) filled for arbitral awards.

Grounds for refusing enforcement are narrow (incapacity, the agreement's invalidity or non-binding character, mediator misconduct, public policy). Ratification has grown more slowly than for the New York Convention — signatories number in the mid-fifties, with ratifications fewer, and several major economies (including, at writing, the EU member states collectively, the United Kingdom having signed in 2023 and moved toward ratification, and the United States, which signed in 2019 but has not ratified) remaining outside — so the Convention is best described as an emerging pillar of the ADR architecture rather than a settled one. Commercial (not family, consumer or employment) settlements are its scope.
