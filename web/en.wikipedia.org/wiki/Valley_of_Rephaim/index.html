Valley of Rephaim

The Valley of Rephaim (Hebrew: עמק רפאים, Emek Refaim, "Valley of the Giants") is a valley southwest of Jerusalem, running from a point west of the Old City down to the Elah valley region. In the Hebrew Bible it marked part of the boundary between the tribes of Judah and Benjamin and was the scene of battles between David and the Philistines.

Biblical accounts

According to 2 Samuel 5 and 1 Chronicles 14, the Philistines twice camped in the valley after David became king, and David defeated them there, naming one site Baal-perazim. The Rephaim were remembered in tradition as an ancient race of giants. During the First Temple period the valley was fertile agricultural land, with terraces, wine presses, and farmsteads.

Modern Jerusalem

Today the valley floor carries the Jerusalem–Tel Aviv railway line, built in the Ottoman period. The name survives in Emek Refaim Street, the main street of the German Colony neighbourhood of Jerusalem, built in the 19th century by German Templers along the valley's northern edge.
