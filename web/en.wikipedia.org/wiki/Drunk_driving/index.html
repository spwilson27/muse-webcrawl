Drunk driving

Also called: Driving under the influence (DUI), driving while intoxicated (DWI)

Legal threshold: Blood alcohol content limits vary, commonly 0.05% or 0.08%

Drunk driving is the act of operating a motor vehicle while impaired by alcohol. It is illegal in most jurisdictions, usually defined by a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) above a statutory limit, and is a major cause of traffic accidents and fatalities worldwide.

Effects

Alcohol impairs reaction time, judgement, coordination, vision and risk assessment. Crash risk rises sharply with BAC, increasing several-fold at levels common in social drinking and far more at high levels.

Measurement and law

Police use breath tests — breathalysers — and blood tests to measure BAC. Penalties include fines, licence suspension, vehicle impoundment and imprisonment, with harsher penalties for repeat offences and for crashes causing injury or death. Many jurisdictions apply lower or zero limits to novice and commercial drivers. See also Driving licence.

Prevention

Measures include sobriety checkpoints, ignition interlocks for offenders, public education campaigns, designated-driver programmes and organisations such as Mothers Against Drunk Driving (MADD), founded in the United States in 1980.

History

The first drunk-driving laws appeared soon after mass motoring in the early 20th century. Per se BAC limits spread from the 1960s–1980s as evidence of impairment thresholds accumulated.
