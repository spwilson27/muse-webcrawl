The poisoning of Sergei and Yulia Skripal, also known as the Salisbury poisoning, was an attempted murder on 4 March 2018 in Salisbury, England. Sergei Skripal, a former Russian military officer and double agent for British intelligence, and his daughter Yulia were poisoned with the Novichok nerve agent A-234, which investigators found had been applied to the front door handle of Skripal's home. Both were found unconscious on a public bench and were hospitalised in critical condition; both eventually recovered and were discharged.

The bench in Salisbury city centre where Sergei and Yulia Skripal were found unconscious.

Investigation

Detective Sergeant Nick Bailey was also seriously ill after exposure during the investigation. The UK government accused Russia of attempted murder and of breaching the Chemical Weapons Convention; the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons confirmed the agent's identity. In September 2018 British authorities identified two suspects, travelling under the names Alexander Petrov and Ruslan Boshirov, as officers of the Russian GRU military intelligence service; the investigative outlet Bellingcat identified Boshirov as decorated GRU colonel Anatoliy Chepiga and Petrov as Alexander Mishkin.

Aftermath

The attack triggered the largest collective expulsion of Russian diplomats in history, with 28 countries and NATO expelling over 150 Russian officials. On 30 June 2018, a related Novichok exposure in nearby Amesbury poisoned Charlie Rowley and Dawn Sturgess; Sturgess died on 8 July 2018, becoming the only fatality. A public inquiry into Sturgess's death opened in 2024. Russia denied involvement throughout.
