Collage

Collage (from the French coller, "to glue" or "to stick together") is a technique of art creation, primarily used in the visual arts, by which art results from an assemblage of different forms, thus creating a new whole.

A collage may sometimes include magazine and newspaper clippings, ribbons, paint, bits of colored or handmade papers, portions of other artwork or texts, photographs and other found objects, glued to a piece of paper or canvas. The origins of collage can be traced back hundreds of years, but this technique made a dramatic reappearance in the early 20th century as an art form of novelty.

History

The technique of collage appears in medieval times, when gold leaf panels were applied to Gothic cathedrals, and in Japan, where calligraphers applied glued paper in the 10th century. The term collage was coined by both Georges Braque and Pablo Picasso in the beginning of the 20th century when collage became a distinctive part of modern art. Along with the Cubists, the technique was embraced by Dada artists such as Hannah Höch and Kurt Schwitters, and later by the Surrealists, pop artists, and contemporary artists.

Techniques

Related techniques include photomontage, in which the composition is made from photographs or parts of photographs; décollage, the opposite of collage, in which a picture is created by cutting or tearing away parts of an original image; and assemblage, a three-dimensional form often grouped with collage.
