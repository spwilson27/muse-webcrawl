The College of William & Mary is a public research university in Williamsburg, Virginia, United States. Founded in 1693 by letters patent issued by King William III and Queen Mary II, it is the second-oldest institution of higher education in the United States, after Harvard University.

History

The college was established in the Virginia Colony largely through the efforts of Reverend James Blair, its first president. Its Wren Building, begun in 1695, is the oldest academic building in continuous use in the United States. Alumni include three U.S. presidents — Thomas Jefferson, James Monroe and John Tyler — and the college hosted the nation's first honor code and the first law school in the United States. The Phi Beta Kappa honor society was founded there in 1776.

William & Mary became state-supported in 1906 and coeducational in 1918. Its historic campus adjoins Colonial Williamsburg.

Academics

Known for a strong undergraduate liberal-arts focus within a research university, William & Mary comprises the Faculty of Arts and Sciences and schools of business, education, law and marine science (the Virginia Institute of Marine Science). It is classified among "R2" research universities and is one of the original eight "Public Ivies".

Campus and traditions

The campus includes the Sunken Garden, Lake Matoaka and the Crim Dell bridge. Traditions include the Yule Log ceremony and the Charter Day celebration each February.
