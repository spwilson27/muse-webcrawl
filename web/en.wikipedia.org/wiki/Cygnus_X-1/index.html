Constellation: Cygnus
Distance: ≈ 6,000–7,000 light-years
Companion: HDE 226868 (blue supergiant)

Cygnus X-1 (abbreviated Cyg X-1) is a galactic X-ray source in the constellation Cygnus and one of the strongest X-ray sources seen from Earth. Discovered in 1964 during a rocket flight, it was the first X-ray source widely accepted to be a black hole.

Discovery and identification

In 1971 radio observations pinned the source to the blue supergiant star HDE 226868, which it orbits. The unseen companion's mass — now measured at about 21 solar masses — far exceeds the maximum mass of a neutron star, identifying it as a black hole accreting gas shed by the supergiant. Cygnus X-1 was the subject of a famous 1974 wager between Stephen Hawking and Kip Thorne over whether it was truly a black hole; Hawking conceded in 1990. Modern parallax measurements place the system about 6,000 light-years away and show the black hole spinning near the maximum possible rate.
