Cleveland Stadium, commonly called Municipal Stadium or Cleveland Municipal Stadium, was a multi-purpose stadium in Cleveland, Ohio, on the shore of Lake Erie. Opened in 1931, it was home to the Cleveland Indians of Major League Baseball and the Cleveland Browns of the NFL for most of its existence. Its immense size, cold lake winds, and often-cavernous crowds earned it the affectionate-pejorative nickname "The Mistake by the Lake".

History

The stadium was built under Mayor William R. Hopkins as part of a lakefront development plan, partly in hopes of attracting the 1932 Olympics. It hosted its first event in 1931 and the Indians began playing selected games there in 1932, moving in full time in 1947 under owner Bill Veeck. The Browns, founded in 1946, played there through 1995. The stadium hosted the 1954 World Series, four MLB All-Star Games, the first 1948 World Series championship of the Indians, and the Browns' NFL championships of the 1950s and 1964.

Notorious episodes include Ten Cent Beer Night (1974), which ended in a riot and forfeit. By the 1990s the stadium was deteriorating and lacked the suites and amenities of modern venues; the Indians moved to Jacobs Field in 1994 and the Browns' owner moved the team to Baltimore after 1995. The stadium was demolished beginning in November 1996, with debris dumped into Lake Erie to form artificial reefs; the current Browns stadium (opened 1999) stands adjacent to the site.
