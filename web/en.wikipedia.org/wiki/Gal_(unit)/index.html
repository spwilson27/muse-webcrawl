The gal (symbol: Gal), sometimes called galileo after Galileo Galilei, is a unit of acceleration in the centimetre–gram–second system of units (CGS) used extensively in the science of gravimetry. It is defined as 1 centimetre per second squared (1 cm/s²). The milligal (mGal) and microgal (µGal) are correspondingly defined as one thousandth and one millionth of a gal.

The gal is not part of the International System of Units (known by its French-language initials "SI"). In 1978 the CIPM decided that it was permissible to use the gal "with the SI until the CIPM considers that [its] use is no longer necessary". The use of the gal was deprecated by the standard ISO 80000-3:2006, now superseded.

Applications

The gal is most commonly found in geophysics, where variations in the acceleration due to gravity at the Earth's surface — caused by density variations in the crust, elevation and latitude — are measured in milligals by gravimeters. Anomalies of tens to hundreds of milligals are typical in gravity surveys for mineral exploration and geodesy. The Earth's mean surface gravity is roughly 981 Gal.
