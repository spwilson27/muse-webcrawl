Simplex

In geometry, a simplex (plural: simplices or simplexes) is a generalisation of the notion of a triangle or tetrahedron to arbitrary dimensions. Specifically, a k-simplex is a k-dimensional polytope which is the convex hull of its k + 1 vertices. More formally, it is the convex hull of a set of k + 1 affinely independent points in Euclidean space.

Examples

  • A 0-simplex is a point.
  • A 1-simplex is a line segment.
  • A 2-simplex is a triangle.
  • A 3-simplex is a tetrahedron.
  • A 4-simplex is a 5-cell (pentachoron).

A simplex with all edges of equal length is called a regular simplex. The standard n-simplex in coordinate space is the set of points whose coordinates are nonnegative and sum to at most one; it plays a central role in algebraic topology and in the theory of barycentric coordinates.

Elements

The convex hull of any nonempty subset of the vertices of a simplex is itself a simplex, called a face of the original. A k-simplex has exactly C(k+1, m+1) faces of dimension m. Collections of simplices glued along faces form a simplicial complex, the basic combinatorial object of simplicial homology and piecewise-linear topology. Every simplex is also an example of a convex polytope with the minimal possible number of vertices for its dimension.

Volume

The volume of an n-simplex with vertices v0, …, vn can be computed from the Cayley–Menger determinant, or as 1/n! times the absolute value of the determinant of the matrix of edge vectors from one vertex. The regular simplex of side length 1 has volume √(n+1) / (n! √(2n)).

Applications

Simplices appear throughout mathematics and its applications: in finite-element meshes (triangulation), in probability (the probability simplex of distributions over finitely many outcomes), in optimisation (the Nelder–Mead "simplex" method, distinct from Dantzig's simplex algorithm for linear programming), and in algebraic topology, where singular simplices are the building blocks of singular homology.
