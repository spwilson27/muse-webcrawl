Camino Real de Panamá

The Camino Real de Panamá (Royal Road of Panama) was a colonial Spanish mule-train route across the Isthmus of Panama, linking Panama City on the Pacific with the Caribbean ports of Nombre de Dios and later Portobelo. It was one of the most important trade routes of the Spanish Empire.

Trade

From the 16th to the 18th century, silver from Peru was shipped up the Pacific coast to Panama City, carried across the isthmus by mule trains, and loaded onto treasure fleets at Portobelo during its famous trade fairs. The paved portions of the road — up to several metres wide — crossed rivers, swamps, and mountains.

Decline and heritage

Pirate attacks, including Henry Morgan's sack of Panama City in 1671, and changing trade routes reduced the road's importance; the Panama Railway (1855) and later the Panama Canal superseded it. Surviving stone-paved sections in jungle areas are protected as cultural heritage, and Panama has sought UNESCO recognition for the route.
