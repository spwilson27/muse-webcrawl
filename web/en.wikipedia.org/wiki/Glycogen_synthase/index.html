Glycogen synthase

EC number2.4.1.11
ReactionUDP-glucose + glycogen → glycogen (elongated) + UDP
IsozymesGYS1 (muscle), GYS2 (liver)
RegulatorsInsulin, glucose 6-phosphate, glycogen synthase kinase 3

Glycogen synthase (also called UDP-glucose–glycogen glucosyltransferase) is the key enzyme of glycogenesis, the synthesis of glycogen. It catalyses the transfer of glucose units from the activated donor UDP-glucose onto the non-reducing ends of a growing glycogen chain, forming α(1→4) glycosidic bonds. The enzyme cannot start a chain from scratch: it requires a primer provided by the self-glucosylating protein glycogenin.

Isozymes and regulation

Humans have two isozymes: GYS1, expressed in skeletal muscle and most other tissues, and GYS2, expressed in the liver. Glycogen synthase is regulated by covalent modification and allosteric effectors. Phosphorylation by kinases such as glycogen synthase kinase 3 (GSK3) and protein kinase A inactivates it, while the hormone insulin promotes its dephosphorylation by protein phosphatase 1 and hence its activation after meals. Glucose 6-phosphate is a potent allosteric activator that can override the inhibitory effect of phosphorylation. In this way glycogen synthase and glycogen phosphorylase are reciprocally controlled, so that synthesis and breakdown of glycogen do not occur wastefully at the same time.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the liver isozyme gene GYS2 cause glycogen storage disease type 0, characterised by fasting hypoglycaemia and postprandial hyperglycaemia, while defects affecting GYS1 are associated with muscle glycogen storage disease and exercise intolerance. The enzyme's central role in glucose storage also makes its pathway relevant to type 2 diabetes, in which insulin-stimulated glycogen synthesis in muscle is impaired.
