Ooty

Ooty, officially Udagamandalam, is a town and municipality in the Nilgiris district of Tamil Nadu, India. Located at about 2,240 metres (7,350 ft) in the Nilgiri Hills, it became a popular hill station under British rule and served as the summer headquarters of the Madras Presidency. It is nicknamed the "Queen of Hill Stations".

History

The region was originally inhabited by the Toda people. The town was developed in the early 19th century under John Sullivan, collector of Coimbatore, who built its artificial lake and promoted it as a sanatorium. The Nilgiri Mountain Railway, a UNESCO World Heritage metre-gauge line from Mettupalayam, opened in stages by 1908.

Economy and attractions

Tourism, tea estates, horticulture and eucalyptus oil production drive the local economy. Attractions include the Government Botanical Gardens (founded 1848), Ooty Lake, Doddabetta Peak — the highest point in the Nilgiris — and the annual summer flower show.
