E-4 process

E-4 process
Type: Colour photographic process
Introduced: 1966
Discontinued: c. 1996

The E-4 process is a now-obsolete development process for colour reversal (slide) photographic film, most associated with Kodak's Ektachrome films sold between the mid-1960s and the mid-1970s.

History

Kodak introduced the E-4 process in 1966 as the successor to the E-2 and E-3 processes. It was designed for films such as Ektachrome-X and High Speed Ektachrome. Processing required unusually hazardous and temperature-critical chemistry, including a pre-hardener containing formaldehyde and a tertiary butylamine borane reversal bath.

Replacement

The simpler and less toxic E-6 process, introduced in 1976, gradually displaced E-4. The last E-4 films were discontinued in the mid-1980s, and commercial E-4 processing ended in the 1990s as chemicals became unavailable. Remaining E-4 film can sometimes be processed as black-and-white negative film by specialist laboratories.
