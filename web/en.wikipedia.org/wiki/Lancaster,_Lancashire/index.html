Lancaster (/ˈlæŋkəstər, ˈlænkæs-/) is a city and the county town of Lancashire, England, standing on the River Lune. It is the administrative centre of the City of Lancaster district, which also includes Morecambe and Heysham. Lancaster is a historic city, originating as a Roman fort on the Lune, and is dominated by Lancaster Castle, much of it built in the medieval period and long used as a prison and court.

History

The name Lancaster derives from the River Lune and the Old English cæster, referring to the Roman fort on the site. The castle was founded in the 11th century under Roger de Poitou and became a royal stronghold; John of Gaunt, who became Duke of Lancaster, substantially rebuilt it, and the Duchy of Lancaster remains associated with the Crown. In the 18th century Lancaster prospered as a port trading with the West Indies, and its quayside, warehouses and fine Georgian buildings, including the Custom House, reflect that era. The Lancaster witch trials of 1612, in which the Pendle witches were tried at the castle, remain famous.

Today

Lancaster University, founded in 1964 on a campus at Bailrigg south of the city, is one of the city's largest employers and a leading UK university. The city centre is noted for its Georgian architecture, the Ashton Memorial in Williamson Park, and the Lancaster Canal. Lancaster retains city status and its assize courts still sit at the castle, which opened more fully to visitors after the prison closed in 2011.
