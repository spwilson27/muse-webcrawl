Chaharshanbe Suri

Chaharshanbe Suri (Persian: چهارشنبه‌سوری, "Wednesday Feast" or "Festival of Fire") is an Iranian festival celebrated on the eve of the last Wednesday before Nowruz, the Persian New Year. Its best-known rite is jumping over bonfires while chanting a verse asking the fire to take away one's sickness and pallor and give back its warmth and redness. The festival has roots in ancient Iranian, probably Zoroastrian, fire rituals.

Customs

At sunset, people light bonfires in streets and alleys and leap over the flames chanting sorkhi-ye to az man, zardi-ye man az to — "your fiery redness be mine, my sickly yellow be yours." The ritual is understood as a purification that carries away illness and misfortune before the new year. Other customs include:

  • Qāshoq-zani (spoon-banging): children go door to door banging spoons on bowls, in disguise, to receive snacks — reminiscent of trick-or-treating.
  • Fireworks and firecrackers, now a major part of the evening in Iranian cities.
  • Burning rue (esfand) and fortune-telling verses, eating a special nut mixture (ājil-e moshkel-goshā, "problem-solving nuts"), and smashing a clay pot (kuzeh shekani) to break bad luck.

History and significance

The festival's fire rituals are often connected to ancient Zoroastrian observances honouring the spirits of the dead in the last days of the year, linked to the festival of Hamaspathmaidyem and the Farvardigan days. It remains one of the most popular public celebrations in Iran, observed across ethnic and religious lines and in diaspora communities, sometimes in tension with official attempts to discourage it as pre-Islamic or to limit its firecrackers on safety grounds; injuries and deaths from fireworks are reported in most years.
