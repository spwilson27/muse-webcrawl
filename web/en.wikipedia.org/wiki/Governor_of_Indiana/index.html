The governor of Indiana is the chief executive of the U.S. state of Indiana and the commander-in-chief of the state's military forces. The governor is elected by popular vote to a four-year term and may serve at most two consecutive terms under the Constitution of Indiana.

Powers and duties

The governor signs or vetoes legislation passed by the Indiana General Assembly, convenes the legislature, grants pardons and reprieves, appoints the heads of state agencies and members of boards and commissions, and delivers an annual State of the State address. The governor also serves as the state's chief economic development official in practice.

History

The office was created when Indiana became a state in 1816, succeeding the territorial governorship; William Henry Harrison had been the territory's first governor from 1801. Jonathan Jennings was the first state governor. The 1851 constitution lengthened the term from three to four years and barred immediate re-election; a 1972 amendment permitted two consecutive terms. The office gained significant additional appointive and budgetary power during the 20th century.

Residence

The official residence is the Indiana Governor's Residence in Indianapolis, a Georgian-style house acquired by the state in 1973.
