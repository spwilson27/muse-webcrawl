Augrabies Falls

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Augrabies Falls is a waterfall on the Orange River in the Northern Cape province of South Africa, within the Augrabies Falls National Park. The falls are approximately 60 metres (200 ft) high, and the river below plunges into a granite gorge about 18 kilometres long and up to 240 metres deep, one of the most striking river gorges in the country.

Name and setting

The name derives from the Khoi word aukoerebis, "place of great noise", rendered in Afrikaans as Augrabies. The surrounding park protects arid Kalahari-margin landscapes of koppies, quiver trees and riverine thicket; wildlife includes klipspringer, giraffe (reintroduced), Hartmann's mountain zebra and the endemic Augrabies flat lizard, whose males are vividly coloured.

Flow

Flow over the falls varies greatly with the Orange River's regulated releases and summer rains. During exceptional floods — notably in 1988 and 2011 — discharge exceeded several thousand cubic metres per second, well beyond the normal flow of around 50–100 m³/s, and the falls resembled a broad wall of water filling the gorge.
