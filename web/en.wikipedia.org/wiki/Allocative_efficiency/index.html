Allocative efficiency

Allocative efficiency is a state of the economy in which production is aligned with consumer preferences; in particular, every good or service is produced up to the point where the last unit provides a marginal benefit to consumers equal to the marginal cost of producing it. In this state, social welfare is maximized and no reallocation of resources can make someone better off without making someone worse off.

Characteristics

Allocative efficiency occurs when price equals marginal cost (P = MC), as in perfect competition. At any other output level, the value consumers place on additional units either exceeds the cost of producing them (underproduction) or falls short of it (overproduction). It is closely related to Pareto efficiency: a Pareto-optimal allocation is allocatively efficient, but allocative efficiency focuses on matching the mix of goods to consumer demand.

Contrast with productive efficiency

Productive efficiency means producing goods at the lowest possible average cost, on the production possibility frontier. An economy can be productively efficient yet allocatively inefficient if it produces the wrong mix of goods for consumers.

Market failure

Monopolies, externalities, public goods, and asymmetric information can cause allocative inefficiency, generating deadweight loss.
