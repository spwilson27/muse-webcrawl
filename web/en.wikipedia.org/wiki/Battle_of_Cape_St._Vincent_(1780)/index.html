The Battle of Cape St. Vincent of 16 January 1780, sometimes called the Moonlight Battle, was a naval engagement of the American Revolutionary War in which a British fleet under Admiral Sir George Rodney decisively defeated a Spanish squadron under Don Juan de Lángara off the southern coast of Portugal.

Background

Spain had entered the war against Britain in 1779 and laid siege to Gibraltar. Rodney sailed from England in late December 1779 with a fleet escorting a large convoy of supplies for Gibraltar and Minorca. On 8 January 1780 he captured a Spanish convoy and its escort off Cape Finisterre, then pressed on south.

The battle

On 16 January, near Cape St. Vincent, Rodney sighted Lángara's squadron of eleven ships of the line. Though the Spanish vessels were individually strong, many were uncoppered and slow; Rodney ordered a general chase, exploiting his coppered hulls' speed, and signalled his ships to engage on the enemy's lee side to prevent escape into Cádiz. The running fight began in the afternoon and continued by moonlight until about 2 a.m. The Spanish flagship Fénix struck to HMS Bienfaisant, and Lángara was captured. In all, four Spanish ships of the line were taken and the Santo Domingo blew up with nearly all hands; others were wrecked on the shore.

Aftermath

Rodney relieved Gibraltar on 19 January, landing the supplies that allowed the garrison to withstand the Great Siege. The victory was a rare bright spot in a difficult war for Britain and is remembered as the first major action to demonstrate the value of copper sheathing for speed.
