Yom HaZikaron (Hebrew: יוֹם הַזִּכָּרוֹן, "Day of Remembrance"), in full the Memorial Day for the Fallen Soldiers of the Wars of Israel and Victims of Actions of Terrorism, is Israel's official day of remembrance for soldiers killed in the country's wars and for civilians killed in acts of terrorism. It is observed on 4 Iyar, ending at sundown as Independence Day begins — a juxtaposition intended to link the state's existence to the sacrifice of the fallen.

Observance

The day begins at sundown on 3 Iyar with a one-minute memorial siren heard across the country, during which the public stands in silence. The central state ceremony is held at the Western Wall in Jerusalem. The following morning a two-minute siren at 11:00 a.m. opens memorial ceremonies at military cemeteries nationwide, the principal one at Mount Herzl. Places of entertainment close, flags fly at half-mast, television and radio broadcast memorial programming, and the names of the fallen are read aloud in a continuous broadcast.

History

Remembrance of the fallen of the 1948 war began with ad hoc ceremonies in 1949; in 1951 David Ben-Gurion fixed the memorial day on 4 Iyar, adjoining Independence Day, and the Knesset gave it statutory form in the Memorial Day Law of 1963. In 1980 the official scope was extended to include members of the security services and the pre-state undergrounds, and in 1998 victims of terrorism were formally added to the day's commemoration.
