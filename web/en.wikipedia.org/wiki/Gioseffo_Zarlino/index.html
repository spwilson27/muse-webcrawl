Gioseffo Zarlino

Gioseffo Zarlino (31 January or 22 March 1517 – 4 February 1590) was an Italian music theorist and composer of the Renaissance. He was possibly the most famous European music theorist between Aristoxenus and Rameau, and made a large contribution to the theory of counterpoint as well as to musical tuning.

Born: 1517, Chioggia, Republic of Venice
Died: 4 February 1590, Venice
Occupation: Music theorist, composer, maestro di cappella

Life

Zarlino was born in Chioggia, near Venice, and educated by the Franciscans. He studied in Venice with Adrian Willaert and in 1565 succeeded Cipriano de Rore as maestro di cappella of St Mark's Basilica, the most prestigious musical post in northern Italy, which he held until his death.

Works and influence

His masterwork, Le istitutioni harmoniche (1558), codified the rules of counterpoint, defined the modern conception of the major and minor modes through the senario (the numbers 1–6 generating the consonances), and advocated just intonation as the ideal tuning. The Dimostrationi harmoniche (1571) and Sopplimenti musicali (1588) defended his system against critics such as Vincenzo Galilei. Zarlino's ordering of the modes and his rules for dissonance treatment shaped composition teaching for centuries.
