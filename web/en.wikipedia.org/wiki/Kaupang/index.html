Kaupang was a Viking Age trading town located near present-day Larvik in Vestfold, Norway, on the shore of Kaupang Bay (Kaupangkilen) off the Skagerrak. Founded around 780, it is generally considered Norway's first town or urban settlement, predating Tønsberg.

History

The name derives from Old Norse kaupangr, meaning market or trading place. Kaupang flourished under the protection of Danish kings who controlled the Vestfold region, functioning as a port of trade linking the North Sea world with the Norwegian interior. Goods such as soapstone vessels, whetstones, and iron were exported, while silver, silk, glass beads, and Rhenish pottery were imported. The town was abandoned around the middle of the 10th century, possibly as the harbour silted up or political power shifted.

Archaeology

Excavations led by Charlotte Blindheim in the 1950s and a major campaign by Dagfinn Skre and the University of Oslo in 2000–2002 uncovered house plots arranged in rows along the shore, workshops, jetties, and extensive cemeteries with boat graves. Finds include coins from Hedeby and the Frankish world, weights, and evidence of crafts such as amber working, bronze casting, and textile production.
