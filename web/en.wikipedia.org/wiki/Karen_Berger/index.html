Karen Berger

Karen Berger (born February 26, 1958) is an American comic book editor. She is best known as the founding editor and executive editor of Vertigo, DC Comics' mature-readers imprint, which she led from its launch in 1993 until 2013, publishing acclaimed series including The Sandman, Preacher, Hellblazer, Swamp Thing, Y: The Last Man and Fables.

Career at DC Comics

Berger, an English literature and art history graduate of Brooklyn College, joined DC Comics in 1979 as an assistant editor. She soon took over editing titles such as House of Mystery and Wonder Woman, and during the 1980s recruited British writers including Alan Moore, Neil Gaiman, Grant Morrison and Peter Milligan — part of the so-called "British Invasion" of American comics. Her work on Swamp Thing, Hellblazer and The Sandman laid the groundwork for Vertigo.

Vertigo

When DC launched Vertigo in 1993, Berger was its executive editor, shaping a line of creator-driven, literarily ambitious horror, fantasy and crime comics for adult readers. Vertigo titles won numerous Eisner and Harvey Awards and are widely credited with expanding the artistic ambitions and audience of the American comics industry. Berger left DC at the end of March 2013.

Berger Books

In 2017 Dark Horse Comics announced Berger Books, an imprint edited by Berger, which began publishing creator-owned series in 2018, including Mata Hari, The Seeds, She Could Fly and adaptations such as LaGuardia. Berger was inducted into the Will Eisner Award Hall of Fame in 2018.
