Magdeburg

Magdeburg (German: [ˈmakdəbʊʁk]; Low Saxon: Meideborg [ˈmaˑidəbɔɐ̯x]) is the capital of the German state Saxony-Anhalt. The city is situated at the Elbe river and was one of the most important medieval cities in Europe. Its population is about 240,000.

Emperor Otto I, the first Holy Roman Emperor and founder of the archbishopric of Magdeburg, was buried in the city's cathedral after his death. Magdeburg's version of German town law, known as Magdeburg rights, spread throughout Central and Eastern Europe. The city was a member of the Hanseatic League.

History

Founded in the early 9th century and first mentioned in 805, Magdeburg became an archbishopric in 968. During the Thirty Years' War, in May 1631, Imperial troops stormed the city and massacred a large share of its population in the event known as the Sack of Magdeburg — one of the war's worst atrocities, which left the city almost destroyed. The physicist-mayor Otto von Guericke, famous for the Magdeburg hemispheres vacuum experiment (1654), helped rebuild it.

Magdeburg was heavily bombed in the Second World War, including the raid of 16 January 1945. After 1945 it lay in the Soviet occupation zone and became a centre of heavy machinery manufacturing in East Germany. Following reunification in 1990, it became the capital of Saxony-Anhalt. Landmarks include the Gothic Magdeburg Cathedral — the first Gothic cathedral built on German soil — and the modern Green Citadel, the last major building designed by Friedensreich Hundertwasser.
