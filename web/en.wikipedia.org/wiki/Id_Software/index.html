id Software

id Software is an American video game developer based in Richardson, Texas. Founded on February 1, 1991, by John Carmack, John Romero, Tom Hall, and Adrian Carmack, the company pioneered the first-person shooter genre with Wolfenstein 3D (1992), Doom (1993), and Quake (1996), and developed influential game engines that were licensed across the industry. The company's name is pronounced "id" as in the Freudian concept, and it is stylized in lowercase.

History

The founders met while working at Softdisk, a software company in Shreveport, Louisiana, where John Carmack developed a technique for smooth side-scrolling graphics on the IBM PC, demonstrated in a clone of Super Mario Bros. 3. Their shareware game Commander Keen (1990), published by Apogee Software, was a hit, and they left Softdisk to found id Software. Wolfenstein 3D popularized the first-person shooter, and Doom — distributed as shareware and spreading across office networks and the early internet — became a cultural phenomenon, popularizing networked multiplayer deathmatches, user-created modifications ("mods"), and licensed game engines.

Quake (1996) brought true 3D graphics and online multiplayer, but its difficult development split the founders; Romero left to found Ion Storm. Under Carmack's technical leadership, id's engines (id Tech) powered many other studios' games. Activision held a long publishing relationship with the company. In 2009 id was acquired by ZeniMax Media, and in 2021 became part of Microsoft through its acquisition of ZeniMax. Carmack departed in 2013 to work on virtual reality at Oculus.

Recent work

After the troubled Rage (2011), the studio rebooted its flagship series with Doom (2016), a critical and commercial success, followed by Doom Eternal (2020) and Doom: The Dark Ages (2025). id's id Tech 7 engine powers the recent Doom titles, and the studio also supports other ZeniMax developers.
