Orthogonal frequency-division multiple access

In OFDMA, subsets of subcarriers are assigned to individual users.

Orthogonal frequency-division multiple access (OFDMA) is a multi-user version of the orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) digital modulation scheme. Multiple access is achieved by assigning subsets of subcarriers to individual users, allowing simultaneous transmission from several terminals.

Principles

OFDM divides a wideband channel into many narrow, mutually orthogonal subcarriers. In OFDMA each user is allocated a group of subcarriers (and time slots) according to demand and channel conditions, so the base station can exploit frequency-selective fading — assigning each user subcarriers on which their channel is strong ("multi-user diversity"). A variant, single-carrier FDMA (SC-FDMA), is used on the LTE uplink for its lower peak-to-average power ratio.

Applications

OFDMA is used in the downlink of 4G LTE and LTE-Advanced, in 5G NR, in WiMAX (IEEE 802.16), and in the IEEE 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) and 802.11be (Wi-Fi 7) wireless LAN standards, as well as in cable standards such as DOCSIS 3.1. Its advantages include robustness to multipath fading and flexible resource scheduling; its drawbacks include sensitivity to frequency offset and high peak-to-average power ratio on the downlink transmitter.
