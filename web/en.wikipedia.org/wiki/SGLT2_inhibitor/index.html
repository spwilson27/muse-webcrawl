SGLT2 inhibitor

SGLT2 inhibitors
Drug class: Antidiabetic / cardiorenal
Also called: Gliflozins
Target: Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2
Examples: Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, canagliflozin

SGLT2 inhibitors, also called gliflozins, are a class of medications that inhibit the sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) in the proximal tubules of the kidney, reducing reabsorption of glucose and increasing its excretion in the urine (glucosuria). They were developed for the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Clinical use

Landmark outcome trials showed that several gliflozins reduce hospitalisation for heart failure and slow the progression of chronic kidney disease, including in patients without diabetes. Accordingly, agents such as dapagliflozin and empagliflozin are now indicated for heart failure and chronic kidney disease as well as type 2 diabetes, and feature prominently in cardiology and nephrology guidelines.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include genital mycotic infections and urinary tract infections arising from increased urinary glucose. Rarer but serious risks include euglycaemic diabetic ketoacidosis, volume depletion, and (reported with canagliflozin) an increased risk of lower-limb amputation.
