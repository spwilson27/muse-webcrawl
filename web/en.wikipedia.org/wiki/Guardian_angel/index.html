Guardian angel

A guardian angel is a type of angel that is assigned to protect and guide a particular person, group, nation, or place. The belief in tutelary beings appears in ancient Mesopotamian, Greek, and Zoroastrian religion, and developed within Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, where angels are traditionally understood to watch over individuals and record or assist their deeds.

Ancient antecedents

Mesopotamian religion held that each person had a personal god or protective spirit, and Greek thought included the daimon, famously the inner voice of Socrates. Zoroastrianism teaches that each person is accompanied by a fravashi, a guardian spirit. These ideas were later echoed in Roman notions of the genius attending every man and the juno attending every woman.

In Judaism

Jewish scripture and tradition describe angels as messengers and protectors of Israel and of righteous individuals; passages in Daniel present angels watching over nations, and rabbinic literature elaborates a rich angelology. In the Book of Tobit, the angel Raphael travels with and protects Tobias.

In Christianity

Christian belief in guardian angels draws on passages such as Psalm 91 ("For he shall give his angels charge over thee") and Matthew 18:10, where Jesus speaks of children's angels beholding the face of the Father. Theologians including St. Jerome and Thomas Aquinas taught that every person has a guardian angel from birth; Aquinas held that the lowest order of angels serves as guardians. The Catholic Church celebrates the Memorial of the Holy Guardian Angels on October 2, a feast promoted by Pope Clement X in 1670. The well-known prayer "Angel of God, my guardian dear" dates from the medieval period. Eastern Orthodox tradition likewise honours guardian angels, while most Protestant churches, though affirming angels generally, do not emphasise individual guardians.

In Islam

Islam teaches that each person is attended by angels called Mu'aqqibat, mentioned in the Qur'an (13:11), who guard by God's command, and by the noble recording angels, Kiraman Katibin, who record good and bad deeds.
