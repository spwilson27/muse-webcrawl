Western Xia

The Western Xia or Xi Xia (1038–1227), known in Tangut as Mi-nyak and in Chinese as Xī Xià, was a Tangut-led empire in what are now the northwestern Chinese provinces of Ningxia, Gansu, and parts of Shaanxi, Xinjiang, Qinghai, and Inner Mongolia. It covered an area of about 800,000 km² at its height.

Founding

The empire was founded by Li Yuanhao, later Emperor Jingzong, who in 1038 proclaimed himself emperor and demanded recognition from the Song dynasty. He created the Tangut script, an official writing system for the Tangut language, and built a state modelled partly on Chinese institutions.

Relations with Song, Liao, and Jin

Western Xia fought wars with the Song, Liao, and later Jin dynasties, while profiting from trade along the Silk Road. It paid and received tribute at various times and maintained a powerful cavalry-based army.

Destruction by the Mongols

The Mongol Empire under Genghis Khan attacked Western Xia repeatedly from 1205. The final campaign of 1226–1227 destroyed the state; its capital Zhongxing (modern Yinchuan) was devastated and much of its population massacred, an event that coincided with Genghis Khan's own death. Tangut culture and language faded over the following centuries, though Tangut texts survived and were deciphered in the 20th century.
