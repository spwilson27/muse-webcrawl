Toarcian

The Toarcian is the last age (stage) of the Early Jurassic epoch, spanning roughly 182.7 to 174.1 million years ago. It follows the Pliensbachian and precedes the Aalenian of the Middle Jurassic. The stage is named after the town of Thouars (Latin Toarcium) in western France.

Events

The early Toarcian saw a major marine extinction and the Toarcian Oceanic Anoxic Event (T-OAE, about 183 million years ago), associated with Karoo-Ferrar large igneous province volcanism, global warming, and black shale deposition — source rocks such as the Jet Rock of Yorkshire and the Posidonia Shale of Germany.

Fauna

Ammonites (including Dactylioceras and Hildoceras) define its biozones. The Posidonia Shale preserves ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs and crinoid "rafts" of exceptional quality.
