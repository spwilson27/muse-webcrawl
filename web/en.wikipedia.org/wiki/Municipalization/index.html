Municipalization

Municipalization is the transfer of private property or private service provision into public ownership by a municipality or other local authority. Especially associated with the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the movement saw cities take over gas and water supplies, electricity generation, tramways, markets, baths and other services.

History

In Britain, reformers such as Joseph Chamberlain, mayor of Birmingham in the 1870s, championed municipal ownership of utilities; critics derided the policy as "gas and water socialism", while supporters pointed to lower prices, reinvested profits and improved public health. Similar waves of municipal ownership occurred in Germany and the United States, where "municipal ownership" campaigns targeted street railways and electric utilities. In Germany the practice became known as Stadtwerke (municipal works).

Modern usage

Contemporary debates over municipalization most often concern electricity grids, water systems and broadband networks, with advocates citing local accountability and opponents citing efficiency and financing risks. The related term remunicipalization describes cities taking services back into public hands after periods of privatisation or contracting out, a trend seen in European water provision since the 2000s.
