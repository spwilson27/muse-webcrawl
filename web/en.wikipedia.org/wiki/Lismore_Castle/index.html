Lismore Castle

Lismore Castle is a castle in Lismore, County Waterford, Ireland. It is the Irish home of the Duke of Devonshire and stands on the site of a monastery founded in the seventh century. The castle, largely rebuilt in the Gothic style in the mid-nineteenth century, overlooks the Blackwater valley.

History

A monastery was founded at Lismore by Saint Mochuda (Carthage) in about 635 AD, and a castle was later built on the site by Prince John in 1185. The castle passed to Sir Walter Raleigh and then, in 1602, to Richard Boyle, 1st Earl of Cork, whose son Robert Boyle, the father of modern chemistry, was born there in 1627. Through the marriage of Lady Charlotte Boyle to William Cavendish, Marquess of Hartington, later 4th Duke of Devonshire, the castle passed to the Cavendish family in the mid-eighteenth century.

Rebuilding

The 6th Duke of Devonshire extensively rebuilt the castle in the Gothic Revival style between 1850 and 1858, to designs by William Atkinson and with the involvement of Joseph Paxton, the duke's celebrated gardener and architect. The Pugin-influenced interiors include the famous banqueting hall. The medieval King John's Tower and the riding school survive from earlier phases of the building.

Gardens

The castle gardens, among the oldest continuously cultivated in Ireland, comprise about seven acres divided into the Upper Garden, a formal seventeenth-century layout of alleys and yew trees, and the Lower Garden, an informal nineteenth-century pleasure ground with magnolias, camellias and a yew walk said to be where Edmund Spenser composed part of The Faerie Queene. Since 2005 the west wing has housed Lismore Castle Arts, a contemporary art gallery.

Notable guests

Fred Astaire, whose sister Adele married Lord Charles Cavendish, was a frequent visitor. John F. Kennedy, Lucian Freud and Mick Jagger have also been guests at the castle.
