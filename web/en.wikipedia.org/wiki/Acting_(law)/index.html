Acting (law)

In law, an acting official is a person who temporarily performs the duties of an office that is vacant or whose regular holder is absent or unable to act. An acting official generally exercises the full powers of the office but does not hold the office permanently and may not use its full title in some jurisdictions.

United States

In the United States federal government, the Federal Vacancies Reform Act of 1998 governs who may serve in an acting capacity in executive branch offices that require Senate confirmation. It generally designates the first assistant to the office as the automatic acting officer, while allowing the President to direct certain other officials to serve instead, subject to time limits — ordinarily 210 days — while a nomination is pending.

Examples

Acting appointments are routine during presidential transitions and vacancies; prominent cases have included acting attorneys general, acting secretaries of defence and homeland security, and, in constitutional terms, "acting president" provisions under the Twenty-fifth Amendment when a president declares inability to serve.
