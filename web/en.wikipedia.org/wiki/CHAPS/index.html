CHAPS

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

CHAPS (Clearing House Automated Payment System) is a real-time gross settlement payment system operated by the Bank of England for sterling payments in the United Kingdom. It is used for high-value, same-day payments that are settled individually and irrevocably in central bank money.

CHAPS was established in 1984 and is one of the largest real-time gross settlement systems in the world. It is used principally by financial institutions for wholesale financial transactions, but also by businesses and individuals for high-value or time-critical payments such as house purchases and car purchases. Since November 2017 the service has been operated directly by the Bank of England, which took over from the CHAPS Clearing Company.

Operation

Payments are settled in real time across accounts held at the Bank of England. CHAPS operates on business days, with an extended operating window introduced in 2016 running from early morning until early evening. Dozens of direct participants — mainly banks and building societies — access the system, while many more institutions offer CHAPS payments to customers as indirect participants through an agency arrangement.

Volumes

CHAPS processes a relatively small number of payments by volume compared with retail systems such as Faster Payments, but the total value transferred is very large — on the order of hundreds of billions of pounds per day, including settlement of money market and foreign exchange transactions.
