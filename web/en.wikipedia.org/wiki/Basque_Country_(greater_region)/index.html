The Basque Country (Basque: Euskal Herria; Spanish: País Vasco; French: Pays basque) as a greater region is the homeland of the Basque people in the western Pyrenees, straddling the border between France and Spain on the coast of the Bay of Biscay. It comprises the Spanish autonomous communities of the Basque Country and Navarre, and the French Basque Country (the western half of the Pyrénées-Atlantiques department) — seven traditional provinces in total, reflected in the Basque nationalist motto zazpiak bat ("the seven are one").

Territories

The southern Basque Country (Hegoalde), in Spain, consists of Álava, Biscay and Gipuzkoa — which form the Basque Autonomous Community — plus Navarre, which has its own autonomous status. The northern Basque Country (Iparralde), in France, comprises Labourd, Lower Navarre and Soule. Major cities include Bilbao, San Sebastián (Donostia), Vitoria-Gasteiz, Pamplona (Iruñea) and Bayonne (Baiona).

Language and culture

The Basque language (Euskara), a language isolate unrelated to any other known tongue, is co-official in the Spanish Basque territories and spoken by roughly a third of the region's population, with numbers growing through education. Distinctive cultural features include Basque rural sports (herri kirolak), the bertsolari tradition of improvised sung verse, pelota games, and a celebrated gastronomy centred on pintxos and cider houses.

Politics

Basque nationalism, founded as an organised movement by Sabino Arana in the 1890s, has long sought greater self-government or independence. The armed group ETA pursued independence through violence from 1959 until its ceasefire in 2011 and dissolution in 2018. The Basque Autonomous Community enjoys extensive autonomy, including its own police force (Ertzaintza) and tax system under the historic fueros.
