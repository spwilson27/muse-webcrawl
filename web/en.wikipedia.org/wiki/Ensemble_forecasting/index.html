Ensemble forecasting

Ensemble forecasting is a method used in numerical weather prediction in which, instead of a single forecast, a set (ensemble) of forecasts is produced. The ensemble members are generated from slightly different initial conditions and/or model formulations, representing the uncertainty in the observations and in the model itself. The spread among members indicates the predictability of the situation: where members agree, confidence is high; where they diverge, uncertainty is large.

Methods

Initial perturbations are commonly generated by singular vectors (as in the ECMWF Ensemble Prediction System, operational since 1992), bred vectors, or ensemble Kalman filter approaches. Model uncertainty is represented by stochastic physics schemes and by using multiple models (multi-model ensembles, such as the North American Ensemble Forecast System). Typical operational ensembles have 20–50 members run at coarser resolution than the deterministic forecast.

Products

Ensemble output is summarised as probability forecasts ("70% chance of rain"), ensemble means, percentile charts, plume and "spaghetti" diagrams, and strike-probability maps for tropical cyclones. For tropical cyclones, the ensemble mean track is often more skilful than any single deterministic run, and the spread guides warnings.

Applications

Beyond weather prediction, ensemble methods are used in seasonal climate forecasting, hydrological flood forecasting (for example the European Flood Awareness System), air quality prediction and epidemic modelling.
