Las Vegas Strip

The Las Vegas Strip is a stretch of Las Vegas Boulevard in Clark County, Nevada, that is known for its concentration of resort hotels and casinos. The Strip, as it is known, is about 4.2 miles (6.8 km) long, starting just south of the city limits of Las Vegas and stretching through the unincorporated towns of Paradise and Winchester. In fact, most of the Strip is not within the city of Las Vegas at all, a quirk that allows its resorts to operate largely outside the city's jurisdiction and tax structure.

The Strip is one of the most popular tourist destinations in the United States and is famous worldwide for its gambling, fine dining, entertainment, retail, and nightlife. In 2023 it attracted roughly 40 million visitors. Many of the largest hotel, casino, and resort properties in the world are located on the Strip, including the Venetian, MGM Grand, Bellagio, Caesars Palace, and the Luxor.

History

The first casino to be built on what later became the Strip was the El Rancho Vegas, which opened in 1941 on a stretch of U.S. Route 91. It was followed by the Last Frontier in 1942 and Bugsy Siegel's Flamingo in 1946, the resort often credited with setting the template of the luxurious Strip casino-hotel. The name "the Strip" is said to come from Los Angeles police officer Guy McAfee, who named it after the Sunset Strip in Los Angeles.

Through the 1950s and 1960s the Rat Pack-era resorts — the Desert Inn, the Sands, the Dunes, and the Stardust — made the Strip synonymous with glamorous entertainment. In 1966 Howard Hughes arrived and bought up several properties, beginning the era of corporate ownership that displaced the mob interests long associated with Las Vegas gambling. The modern megaresort era began in 1989 with Steve Wynn's Mirage, whose volcano and tropical theme triggered a building boom that produced the Bellagio, the Venetian, Mandalay Bay, and other enormous themed resorts during the 1990s.

Location and boundaries

Historically, casinos that were not in downtown Las Vegas along Fremont Street sat along Las Vegas Boulevard South. The modern Strip is generally considered to run from the "Welcome to Fabulous Las Vegas" sign near Russell Road north to Sahara Avenue, with some definitions extending to the Stratosphere tower. The famous welcome sign, designed by Betty Willis in 1959, stands in the median of Las Vegas Boulevard south of Russell Road and is itself a major tourist attraction.

Resorts and attractions

Notable properties include, from south to north: Mandalay Bay, Luxor, Excalibur, New York-New York, the MGM Grand, Planet Hollywood, Paris Las Vegas (with its half-scale Eiffel Tower), Bellagio (famous for its dancing fountains), Caesars Palace, The Venetian and The Palazzo, the Mirage (which closed in 2024 to be redeveloped as a Hard Rock property), Treasure Island, Wynn and Encore, Resorts World, Circus Circus, and the Sahara. The Strip also hosts the T-Mobile Arena and the Sphere, a spherical entertainment venue opened in 2023.

Transportation

The Las Vegas Monorail runs along the east side of the Strip, and the corridor is served by RTC buses, including The Deuce double-decker route. Pedestrian bridges cross Las Vegas Boulevard at several major intersections. Harry Reid International Airport lies immediately east of the southern Strip.
