Grenville orogeny

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Grenville orogeny
Agec. 1250–980 million years ago (Mesoproterozoic–Neoproterozoic)
LocationEastern North America (Canada, USA), with correlatives worldwide
ResultAssembly of the supercontinent Rodinia
Named forGrenville, Quebec

The Grenville orogeny was a long-lived Mesoproterozoic mountain-building event associated with the assembly of the supercontinent Rodinia. Its rocks are exposed today principally in the Grenville Province of the Canadian Shield, extending from Labrador through Quebec and Ontario into the Adirondack Mountains and Appalachians of the eastern United States, and Grenville-age belts are recognised on other continents, including the Sveconorwegian belt of Scandinavia.

Geology

The orogeny resulted from the collision of the ancient core of North America (Laurentia) with other continental blocks, including Amazonia. Over roughly 300 million years, island arcs and continental fragments were accreted and then large landmasses collided, thickening the crust to produce a mountain range that has been compared in scale to the modern Himalayas. The resulting rocks are typically high-grade metamorphic gneisses and anorthosites, deeply eroded during the following billion years to expose the roots of the range.

Phases

Geologists recognise several major pulses within the orogeny, including the Elzevirian (c. 1250–1190 Ma), Shawinigan (c. 1190–1140 Ma), Ottawan (c. 1090–1020 Ma) — the principal continental collision — and Rigolet (c. 1010–980 Ma) phases.

Significance

The Grenville orogeny marks one of the principal episodes in the growth of Laurentia and is a key constraint on reconstructions of Rodinia. Its front — the Grenville Front — is a major tectonic boundary crossing Ontario and Quebec, separating the older Superior Province craton from the younger Grenville Province.
