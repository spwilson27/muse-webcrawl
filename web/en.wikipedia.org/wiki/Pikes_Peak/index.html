Pikes Peak is the highest summit of the southern Front Range of the Rocky Mountains in North America, at 14,115 feet (4,302 m). Located in Pike National Forest about 12 miles west of Colorado Springs, it is the most visited mountain summit in North America and one of Colorado's famous "fourteeners."

History

The mountain was a landmark for the Ute people, who called it Tava ("sun mountain"). In November 1806 the explorer Zebulon Pike sighted the peak during his expedition into the newly acquired Louisiana Purchase territory; he attempted to climb it but was turned back by deep snow, judging it unclimbable. The first recorded ascent was by Edwin James of the Stephen Long expedition in 1820. Gold seekers of the 1859 rush adopted the slogan "Pikes Peak or Bust."

"America the Beautiful"

In 1893 the writer Katharine Lee Bates visited the summit and, inspired by the view, wrote the poem that became the song "America the Beautiful," with its "purple mountain majesties."

Access and events

The summit can be reached by the Pikes Peak Highway (a toll road opened in its auto form in 1915), by the Manitou and Pikes Peak Cog Railway, and by the Barr Trail on foot. Since 1916 the mountain has hosted the Pikes Peak International Hill Climb, a famous motor race up its winding course. A new Summit Complex visitor centre opened in 2021.
