Transient (oscillation)

A transient is a short-lived burst of energy in a system caused by a sudden change of state. In oscillation and signal processing, the transient response of a system is the temporary behaviour that occurs after an excitation or disturbance, before the system settles into its steady state. Transients commonly take the form of a damped oscillation that decays exponentially with time.

Electrical circuits

In an RLC circuit switched onto a source, currents and voltages swing through a transient oscillation determined by the circuit's resistance, inductance, and capacitance before settling. The amount of damping determines whether the response is underdamped (oscillatory), critically damped, or overdamped. Switching transients, lightning surges, and other voltage spikes are also called transients in power engineering, and surge protectors are designed to clamp them.

Acoustics and music

In acoustics, a transient is the brief, high-amplitude onset portion of a sound — such as the attack of a drum hit or the pluck of a string — which contains much of the information by which listeners identify the instrument. Audio compressors and transient shapers process this portion of a sound separately from its sustained portion.
