Lake Elmenteita
Location: Great Rift Valley, Nakuru County, Kenya
Coordinates: 0°27′S 36°15′E
Type: Soda lake (alkaline, endorheic)
Surface area: approx. 18 km²
Surface elevation: approx. 1,670 m

Lake Elmenteita, also spelled Elementaita, is a shallow alkaline soda lake in the Great Rift Valley of Kenya, about 120 km northwest of Nairobi and just south of Lake Nakuru. Its name derives from the Maasai word muteita, meaning "dusty place", referring to the dry, dusty conditions of the area in the dry season.

Ecology

The lake is a Ramsar site and, since 2011, part of the Kenya Lake System in the Great Rift Valley UNESCO World Heritage Site, together with Lake Nakuru and Lake Bogoria. Over 400 bird species have been recorded around the lake. It is an important feeding ground for lesser and greater flamingos and a breeding site for great white pelicans, which nest on rocky islands in the lake. Introduced tilapia support both birds and a small local fishery.

History and surroundings

The surrounding Soysambu Conservancy, established on the former Delamere estates, protects large areas of yellow fever-tree (Acacia xanthophloea) woodland and Euphorbia forest, with wildlife including Rothschild's giraffes, zebras, eland and colobus monkeys. Nearby sites include the Kariandusi prehistoric site, where Acheulean stone tools were excavated, and hot springs on the lake's southern shore. Lord Delamere, the pioneer settler of British East Africa, farmed in the area in the early 20th century.
