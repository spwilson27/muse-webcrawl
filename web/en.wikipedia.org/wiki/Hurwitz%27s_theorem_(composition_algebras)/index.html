Hurwitz's theorem (composition algebras)

Hurwitz's theorem is a theorem in algebra, proved by Adolf Hurwitz in 1898 (published posthumously), stating that a finite-dimensional normed division algebra over the real numbers in which the norm is multiplicative (a composition algebra) must have dimension 1, 2, 4, or 8. The only such algebras are the real numbers, the complex numbers, the quaternions, and the octonions.

Statement

If A is a finite-dimensional algebra over ℝ with a non-degenerate quadratic form N satisfying N(xy) = N(x)N(y) for all x, y in A, then dim A ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}. The result explains why sums-of-squares identities of the multiplicative type exist only in dimensions 1, 2, 4, and 8.

Related results

The theorem is closely connected to the classification of normed division algebras and to the fact that only the spheres S⁰, S¹, S³, and S⁷ are parallelizable. Generalisations extend the result to composition algebras over other fields, where the possible dimensions remain powers of two up to 8.
