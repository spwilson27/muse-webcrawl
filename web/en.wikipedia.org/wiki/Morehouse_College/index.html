Morehouse College

Morehouse College is a private, historically Black men's liberal arts college in Atlanta, Georgia. Founded in 1867 in Augusta as the Augusta Institute and moved to Atlanta in 1879, it was renamed in 1913 for Henry L. Morehouse of the American Baptist Home Mission Society. It is one of only a few remaining men's colleges in the United States and a member of the Atlanta University Center consortium, alongside Spelman College and Clark Atlanta University.

History and mission

Under its sixth president, Benjamin Elijah Mays (1940–1967), Morehouse became famous for producing leaders of the civil rights movement and for Mays's ideal of the "Morehouse Man" — well-read, well-spoken, well-travelled, well-dressed, and well-balanced. The college's Martin Luther King Jr. International Chapel honours its most famous graduate.

Academics and alumni

Morehouse enrols around 2,200 students and is noted for producing large numbers of Black graduates who go on to earn doctorates and medical degrees, and for its alumni including Martin Luther King Jr., Julian Bond, Spike Lee, Samuel L. Jackson, former Atlanta mayor Maynard Jackson, and former U.S. Senator Raphael Warnock. Its glee club, founded in 1911, is among the oldest collegiate ensembles in the country.
