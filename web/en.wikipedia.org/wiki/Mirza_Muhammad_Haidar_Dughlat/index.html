Mirza Muhammad Haidar Dughlat (c. 1499/1500 – 1551) was a Chagatai Turco-Mongol military commander, ruler of Kashmir, and historian. A member of the Dughlat clan of Moghulistan and first cousin of Babur, the founder of the Mughal Empire, he is best remembered as the author of the Tarikh-i-Rashidi, a fundamental Persian-language history of the Moghuls of Central Asia.

Life

Orphaned after his father was killed by the Shaybanid Uzbeks, Haidar fled across Central Asia, serving at various times his cousins Babur in Kabul and Sa'id Khan, founder of the Yarkand Khanate. As a general of Sa'id Khan he led expeditions into Ladakh, Baltistan and Tibet in the 1530s, attempting (largely unsuccessfully) to spread Islam there. Later, caught in the civil wars of Moghulistan, he took service with the Mughal emperor Humayun and in 1540 seized Kashmir, which he ruled for eleven years — nominally for Humayun, but effectively as an independent ruler. He was killed in 1551 fighting a revolt of the local Kashmiri nobility.

The Tarikh-i-Rashidi

Written in Kashmir in Persian and dedicated to the Moghul khan Abd al-Rashid, the Tarikh-i-Rashidi ("History of Rashid") is the principal source for the history of Moghulistan and the eastern Chagatai Khanate in the 14th–16th centuries, combining dynastic history with the author's own memoirs. It was translated into English in the 19th century by E. Denison Ross and Ney Elias and remains essential reading for historians of Central Asia.
