MATLAB (an abbreviation of "MATrix LABoratory") is a proprietary multi-paradigm programming language and numeric computing environment developed by MathWorks. It allows matrix manipulations, plotting of functions and data, implementation of algorithms, creation of user interfaces, and interfacing with programs written in other languages.

History

MATLAB was invented by mathematician and computer programmer Cleve Moler in the late 1970s at the University of New Mexico to give students access to the LINPACK and EISPACK numerical libraries without writing Fortran. It spread to other universities and was commercialized in 1984 when Moler co-founded MathWorks with Jack Little and Steve Bangert.

Features

The MATLAB language centers on arrays and matrices as the fundamental data type, with built-in support for linear algebra, statistics, signal processing, image processing, control systems and optimization. Add-on "toolboxes" extend the platform for specialized fields, and the companion product Simulink provides graphical block-diagram modeling and simulation for dynamic systems. MATLAB is used extensively in engineering, science, finance and education; MathWorks reports millions of users worldwide.

Alternatives

Free and open-source environments with similar goals include GNU Octave (which aims for syntax compatibility with MATLAB), Scilab, Julia and the Python ecosystem built around NumPy and SciPy.
