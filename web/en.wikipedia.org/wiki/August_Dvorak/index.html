August Dvorak (May 5, 1894 – October 10, 1975) was an American educational psychologist and professor of education at the University of Washington in Seattle. He is best known as the co-creator, with his brother-in-law William Dealey, of the Dvorak keyboard layout, patented in 1936 as an alternative to the QWERTY layout.

Life and work

Dvorak served in the United States Navy during the First World War and later studied learning and human performance. His research into typing errors and hand motion led him to design a layout that placed the most common letters on the home row and favoured alternation between the hands. During the Second World War he conducted typing studies for the Navy, which reported large speed gains for retrained typists. With Dealey and others he authored the 1936 book Typewriting Behavior. Despite his advocacy, Dvorak's layout never displaced QWERTY in the market, though it remains available in modern operating systems.
