CONCACAF

The Confederation of North, Central America and Caribbean Association Football, known by its acronym CONCACAF (stylized as Concacaf), is one of FIFA's six continental confederations governing association football in North America, Central America and the Caribbean. Founded in 1961, it has 41 member associations and is headquartered in Miami, Florida, United States.

History

CONCACAF was formed by the merger of the North American Football Confederation and the Confederación Centroamericana y del Caribe de Fútbol. Long dominated administratively by figures such as Jack Warner and Chuck Blazer, the confederation was engulfed by the 2015 FIFA corruption scandal, after which reforms were enacted under president Victor Montagliani, including the rebranding to "Concacaf" and the launch of the Nations League in 2019.

Competitions

Its flagship national-team tournament is the Gold Cup, held every two years and most often won by Mexico and the United States. Club sides compete in the CONCACAF Champions Cup, and the confederation oversees World Cup qualifying for the region, which will receive expanded places for the 2026 World Cup, co-hosted by the United States, Canada and Mexico.

Members

Members range from the three North American giants to small Caribbean island associations, and include three South American countries — Guyana, Suriname and French Guiana (the last an associate) — for geographical and historical reasons.
