Grasse is a commune in the Alpes-Maritimes department in southeastern France, situated in the hills north of Cannes on the French Riviera. It is regarded as the world's capital of perfume.

Perfume industry

Grasse's perfume industry began in the 16th century, when local tanneries began producing scented gloves to mask the smell of leather. The surrounding countryside produces jasmine, roses, lavender and other flowers used in perfumery, and the town hosts historic houses such as Fragonard, Molinard and Galimard as well as the International Perfume Museum. The skills related to perfume in the Pays de Grasse were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2018.

Setting and culture

The old town's narrow streets and views over the plain toward the Mediterranean have long attracted artists and writers. The painter Jean-Honoré Fragonard was born in Grasse in 1732.
