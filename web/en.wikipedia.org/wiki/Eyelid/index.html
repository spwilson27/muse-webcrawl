Eyelid

Eyelid
SystemIntegumentary / ocular
FunctionProtection and lubrication of the eye

An eyelid is a thin fold of skin that covers and protects the eye. The eyelids help spread tear film over the cornea, shield the eye from foreign bodies and excess light, and close during blinking to keep the ocular surface moist.

Anatomy

Each eye has an upper and a lower eyelid. From outside to inside the eyelid consists of skin, orbicularis oculi muscle, the tarsal plate (dense connective tissue giving the lid its shape), meibomian glands that secrete the oily layer of the tear film, and the conjunctiva lining the inner surface. The upper lid is raised by the levator palpebrae superioris muscle. Eyelashes grow from the lid margins and help trap particles.

Function

A complete blink occurs many times per minute, renewing the tear film and sweeping debris toward the drainage puncta. Reflex blinking protects against approaching objects and bright light, and the lids close fully during sleep.

Clinical significance

Common eyelid conditions include blepharitis (inflammation of the margins), styes, chalazia, ptosis (drooping), entropion and ectropion (inward or outward turning), and tumours such as basal cell carcinoma. Some animals possess a third eyelid, the nictitating membrane.
