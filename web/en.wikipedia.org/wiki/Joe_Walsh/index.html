Joe Walsh

Born: Joseph Fidler Walsh, 20 November 1947, Wichita, Kansas, U.S.

Occupation: Guitarist, singer, songwriter

Associated acts: James Gang, Eagles, Barnstorm

Joseph Fidler Walsh (born November 20, 1947) is an American guitarist, singer and songwriter. He first gained fame with the James Gang, whose hits included "Funk #49", before forming the band Barnstorm and scoring a solo hit with "Rocky Mountain Way" (1973).

Eagles

In 1975 Walsh joined the Eagles, replacing founding guitarist Bernie Leadon. His harder-edged guitar sound reshaped the band on the album Hotel California (1976), for which he co-wrote "Life in the Fast Lane" and contributed the celebrated guitar duet on the title track. His best-known solo song, "Life's Been Good" (1978), satirised rock-star excess.

Later career

Walsh continued recording solo albums after the Eagles' breakup and rejoined them for their 1994 reunion and subsequent tours. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Eagles in 1998. His guitar playing, particularly his slide work and talk-box use, has influenced generations of rock musicians, and he is also known for his work restoring antique radios as a licensed amateur radio operator.
