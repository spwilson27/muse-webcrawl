The ronroco is a Bolivian stringed instrument of the charango family. Larger than the charango, it typically has a scale length of around 50 cm (compared with the charango's roughly 37 cm) and ten strings in five double courses, giving it a deeper, rounder tone. It is a traditional instrument of Andean folk music in Bolivia and neighbouring regions of Argentina, Chile and Peru.

Construction and tuning

The traditional ronroco body was carved from a single block of wood or, like early charangos, fashioned from the shell of an armadillo; modern instruments are built from laminated woods with a separate soundboard. A common tuning is D–G–B–E–B (from the lowest course), though players also use tunings a fourth or fifth below the charango. Strings were historically gut and are now usually nylon or metal.

Prominence

The ronroco gained international recognition through the Argentine composer and multi-instrumentalist Gustavo Santaolalla, who has made it a signature sound in his film scores, including Brokeback Mountain (2005), Babel (2006) — both of which won Academy Awards for Best Original Score — and the video game The Last of Us (2013) and its television adaptation. His playing introduced the instrument's warm, melancholic timbre to global audiences, and its use in the score of The Motorcycle Diaries (2004) likewise brought it to wider attention.
