Puja (Sanskrit: पूजा, pūjā) is a worship ritual performed by Hindus to offer devotional honour, prayer, and offerings to one or more deities, to host and honour a guest, or to celebrate an event. It is the most common form of religious practice in Hinduism, performed daily in homes and temples and elaborately at festivals.

Practice

A puja typically centres on a murti (image or statue) of a deity such as Vishnu, Shiva, Devi, Ganesha, or Krishna, treated as an honoured royal guest. The worshipper rings a bell, invokes the deity's presence, and presents a sequence of offerings — traditionally up to sixteen services (ṣoḍaśopacāra) including water for washing, clothing, sacred thread, sandalwood paste, flowers, incense, a lamp, and food. Verses and mantras accompany each offering, and the ritual often culminates in aarti, the waving of a flame before the deity, after which consecrated food (prasad) is distributed to devotees.

Settings and occasions

Home puja is usually brief and personal, performed at a small shrine morning and evening. Temple puja follows fixed schedules set by tradition and is conducted by priests (pujaris). Special pujas mark life-cycle events — births, weddings, housewarmings (griha pravesh) — and festivals such as Diwali (Lakshmi Puja), Navaratri (Durga Puja), and Ganesh Chaturthi. Large public pujas, notably the Durga Puja pandals of Kolkata, are major civic celebrations.

Meaning and interpretation

Puja expresses bhakti (devotion): the deity is served with love as the supreme guest, and the exchange of offerings and prasad embodies a reciprocal relationship between worshipper and god. The ritual also serves meditation and concentration, focusing the mind through sight (darshan — seeing and being seen by the deity), sound, fragrance, and touch.

Related traditions

Forms of puja are also practised in Jainism and Mahayana Buddhism, and Balinese Hinduism maintains elaborate daily offering rituals. Hindu communities worldwide reproduce temple puja in the diaspora, adapting schedules and scale to local conditions.
