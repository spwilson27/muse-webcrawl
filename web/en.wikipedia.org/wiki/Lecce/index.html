Lecce (Italian: [ˈlettʃe]; Salentino: Lècce) is a city in southern Italy and the capital of the province of the same name in the region of Apulia (Puglia). It is the main city of the Salento peninsula, a sub-peninsula at the heel of the Italian peninsula. Lecce is renowned for its rich Baroque architecture, built from the soft local limestone known as pietra leccese, which has earned it the nickname "the Florence of the South" (la Firenze del Sud).

History

According to legend, the city existed already at the time of the Trojan War and was founded by the Cretan hero Idomeneus. Historically, the Messapian settlement of Sybar stood on the site; it was conquered by the Romans in the 3rd century BC and renamed Lupiae. Under Emperor Hadrian the city was moved a few kilometres to the northeast; Marcus Aurelius later endowed it with a theatre and an amphitheatre, and a harbour was built at nearby San Cataldo.

After the fall of the Western Roman Empire, Lecce was sacked by the Ostrogothic king Totila and passed through Byzantine, Lombard, Saracen and Norman hands. Under the Normans it became the capital of the County of Lecce; the last Norman count, Tancred, became King of Sicily in 1189. In the Spanish period (16th–17th centuries), under Charles V and his successors, the city was rebuilt with powerful fortifications and flourished as a cultural centre — the period in which its famous Baroque churches and palazzi were erected, many designed or decorated by local architects and sculptors such as Giuseppe Zimbalo (nicknamed lo Zingarello).

Main sights

Basilica of Santa Croce

The Church of the Holy Cross (1549–1695) is the supreme expression of Lecce Baroque. Its façade, richly carved with animals, grotesque figures, vegetables and atlantes, is the work of Francesco Antonio Zimbalo, Cesare Penna and Giuseppe Zimbalo.

Piazza del Duomo and the Cathedral

The enclosed cathedral square is one of the finest Baroque set-pieces in Italy, containing the Cathedral of the Assumption of the Virgin (rebuilt 1659–1670 by Giuseppe Zimbalo), the five-storey bell tower (about 70 m high), the Bishop's Palace and the Seminary Palace.

Roman amphitheatre and theatre

The Roman amphitheatre in Piazza Sant'Oronzo, dating to the Augustan age or slightly later, once held more than 25,000 spectators; about a third of it is excavated. The smaller Roman theatre, discovered in 1929, held about 5,000 spectators.

Other sights

  • The Castle of Charles V (1539–1549), built over a medieval fortress.
  • The Column of Saint Oronzo, a Roman column from Brindisi topped by a statue of the patron saint.
  • The churches of San Giovanni Battista, San Nicolò dei Greci, Santa Chiara and San Matteo.
  • The Celestine Palace (Palazzo dei Celestini), today the seat of the provincial administration.

Economy and culture

Lecce's economy rests on agriculture — wine (including the Salice Salentino DOC), olive oil and cereals — food processing, the traditional working of pietra leccese and papier-mâché (cartapesta), and a growing tourism sector. The city is home to the University of Salento, founded in 1955. Its football club, U.S. Lecce, has played many seasons in Serie A.
