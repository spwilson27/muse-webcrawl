Spartacist uprising

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Spartacist uprising (German: Spartakusaufstand), also known as the January uprising, was a general strike and armed revolt in Berlin from 5 to 12 January 1919, during the German Revolution of 1918–19. It took its name from the Spartacus League, the Marxist group led by Karl Liebknecht and Rosa Luxemburg, though the League's leaders were ambivalent about the revolt, which was triggered by the dismissal of Berlin's left-wing police chief Emil Eichhorn.

Course of the uprising

Demonstrators occupied newspaper offices and other key buildings in central Berlin, and a revolutionary committee proclaimed the dismissal of the government of Friedrich Ebert. The government, supported by Defence Minister Gustav Noske, deployed Freikorps units — paramilitary groups of demobilised soldiers — to crush the revolt. After several days of street fighting, the uprising was suppressed.

Aftermath

On 15 January 1919, Liebknecht and Luxemburg were captured and murdered by Freikorps officers. Their deaths made them martyrs of the German left and deepened the lasting split between the Social Democratic Party (SPD) and the newly founded Communist Party of Germany (KPD). The violence of January 1919 foreshadowed the political instability of the Weimar Republic.
