The balanced scorecard (BSC) is a strategic planning and management system used in business and industry, government, and nonprofit organizations to align business activities to the vision and strategy of the organization, improve internal and external communications, and monitor organization performance against strategic goals.

[ Image: Balanced scorecard diagram ]
The four perspectives of the balanced scorecard

Origins

It was originated by Robert S. Kaplan of Harvard Business School and David P. Norton as a performance measurement framework that added strategic non-financial performance measures to traditional financial metrics, first described in their 1992 Harvard Business Review article "The Balanced Scorecard — Measures that Drive Performance."

The four perspectives

The framework views the organization from four perspectives, each with objectives, measures, targets and initiatives:

  • Financial — how the organization appears to shareholders;
  • Customer — how customers see the organization;
  • Internal business processes — what the organization must excel at;
  • Learning and growth — how the organization can continue to improve and create value.

Strategy maps

Later developments linked the perspectives in cause-and-effect "strategy maps," showing how improvements in learning and growth drive process improvements, which drive customer outcomes and ultimately financial results. The approach evolved from a measurement system into a full strategic management system.

Criticism

Critics note the framework's rigidity, the difficulty of establishing the assumed causal links, and the risk of measure overload. Nevertheless, surveys have repeatedly ranked it among the most widely used management tools worldwide.
