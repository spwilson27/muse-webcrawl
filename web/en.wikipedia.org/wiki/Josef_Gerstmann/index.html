Josef Gerstmann


Josef Gerstmann
Born: 17 July 1887, Lemberg, Austria-Hungary
Died: 3 March 1969, New York City, United States
Occupation: Neurologist, psychiatrist
Known for: Gerstmann syndrome, Gerstmann–Sträussler–Scheinker syndrome

Josef Gerstmann (17 July 1887, Lemberg – 3 March 1969, New York City) was an Austrian-born American neurologist and psychiatrist best known for describing the neurological disorder that bears his name, Gerstmann syndrome.

Gerstmann studied medicine in Vienna and served as a military physician in the First World War, where his observations of brain-injured soldiers informed his later research on disorders of body image and spatial orientation. He became director of the Neurological Institute in Vienna.

Gerstmann syndrome

Gerstmann syndrome is a disorder of the dominant parietal lobe characterised by a tetrad of symptoms: finger agnosia, agraphia, acalculia and left–right disorientation. Gerstmann's name is also attached to Gerstmann–Sträussler–Scheinker syndrome, a rare prion disease described with Ernst Sträussler and Ilya Scheinker.

Later life

After the Anschluss in 1938 Gerstmann emigrated to the United States, where he continued clinical and academic work in New York until his death in 1969.
