House edge

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In a casino, the house advantage or house edge is the long-term statistical advantage the casino (the "house") has over players, expressed as a percentage of each bet that the casino expects to keep on average over the long run. It is the inverse of the return to player (RTP). For example, a game with a house edge of 5% has an expected return to players of 95% of the total amount wagered, over a sufficiently large number of trials.

Definition

The house edge is defined as the ratio of the average loss to the initial bet. It arises because the casino pays out winning bets at odds that are lower than the true odds of winning. On an American roulette wheel, for example, a bet on a single number pays 35 to 1 while the true odds against are 37 to 1 (38 pockets), giving the house an edge of about 5.26%. A European single-zero wheel gives an edge of about 2.70%.

Significance

The house edge guarantees the casino a profit over the long term, regardless of short-term fluctuations in results — an application of the law of large numbers. A player's strategy can sometimes reduce the effective edge (for example, basic strategy in blackjack, or card counting, which can in rare cases give a skilled player a small advantage), but in most games the edge is fixed by the rules and payout structure. Related concepts include the hold percentage (the share of chips purchased that the casino actually keeps) and the theoretical win used to rate players for complimentary rewards ("comps").
