James Buchanan Duke

James Buchanan Duke (December 23, 1856 – October 10, 1925) was an American tobacco and electric power industrialist best known for the invention of modern cigarette manufacturing and marketing, and for his involvement with Duke University.

Tobacco business

Duke was the son of Washington Duke, a North Carolina farmer who founded a tobacco business after the American Civil War. James joined the family firm, W. Duke, Sons & Co., and in 1881 the company entered the cigarette business. Duke championed the Bonsack cigarette rolling machine, which mechanized production, and secured a secret reduced royalty rate that gave him a decisive advantage over competitors. In 1890 he brought together the five largest cigarette manufacturers to form the American Tobacco Company, becoming its first president. The trust was ordered dissolved by the US Supreme Court in 1911 under the Sherman Antitrust Act.

Electric power

In the early 1900s Duke invested in hydroelectric power in the Carolinas, co-founding the Southern Power Company (later Duke Power, now Duke Energy), which built a series of generating stations on the Catawba River and supplied electricity to the region's textile mills.

Philanthropy

In 1924 Duke established The Duke Endowment, a trust fund that provided, among other gifts, the funds to transform Trinity College in Durham, North Carolina, into Duke University, named in honour of his father. He died in New York City in 1925 and is buried at Duke Chapel.
