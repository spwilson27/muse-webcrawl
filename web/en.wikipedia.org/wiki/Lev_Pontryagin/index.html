Lev Semyonovich Pontryagin (Russian: Лев Семёнович Понтрягин; 3 September 1908 – 3 May 1988) was a Soviet mathematician. Blinded in an accident at the age of 14, he nevertheless became one of the outstanding mathematicians of the twentieth century, making major contributions to algebraic and differential topology, oscillation theory, and optimal control.

Life

Pontryagin was born in Moscow. He lost his eyesight at fourteen when a primus stove exploded, and his mother became his devoted reader and secretary, describing mathematical symbols to him. He graduated from Moscow State University in 1929, where he had studied under Pavel Alexandrov, and completed his doctorate in 1935. He spent his career at the Steklov Institute of Mathematics and Moscow State University.

Work

While still a student, Pontryagin proved fundamental results on duality in algebraic topology, now known as Pontryagin duality. His work on cobordism and characteristic classes produced the Pontryagin classes, central invariants in the topology of smooth manifolds; the Pontryagin–Thom construction links cobordism theory with homotopy theory. In the 1950s he turned to applied problems and, with his students, developed the theory of optimal control, whose central result is Pontryagin's maximum principle, a necessary condition for an optimal trajectory. His monograph The Mathematical Theory of Optimal Processes (1961, with Boltyansky, Gamkrelidze, and Mishchenko) became the foundational text of the subject and received the Lenin Prize.
