The Yellow Turban Rebellion, alternatively translated as the Yellow Scarves Rebellion (Chinese: 黃巾之亂; pinyin: Huángjīn zhī Luàn), was a peasant revolt during the late Eastern Han dynasty of China. It broke out in 184 CE, the first year of the Zhongping era of Emperor Ling. The rebels were named for the yellow headcloths they wore.

Background

The rebellion was led by Zhang Jue, a faith healer and leader of the Way of Supreme Peace (Taiping Dao), a Taoist religious movement. Floods, famine, plague, and heavy taxation had driven peasants to desperation, and Zhang Jue's promise of a new era — "The Azure Sky has perished; the Yellow Sky will soon rise" — drew hundreds of thousands of followers across the eastern provinces. Zhang styled himself the "General of Heaven", and his brothers Zhang Bao and Zhang Liang also led armies.

Course of the rebellion

The rising was planned for 184 but was betrayed in advance, forcing a premature outbreak. Yellow Turban forces attacked government offices across several provinces. The Han court dispatched generals including Huangfu Song, Zhu Jun, and Lu Zhi, while local officials and gentry raised volunteer forces; the young officer Cao Cao first gained military fame in these campaigns. Zhang Jue died of illness in 184, and the main rebel armies were defeated within about a year, though remnant Yellow Turban bands and related movements such as the Black Mountain bandits remained active for years, with the last pockets suppressed around 205.

Consequences

Although the rebellion was crushed, suppressing it required the dynasty to delegate military power to regional commanders and warlords. The Han court never recovered its authority, and within a few years the empire fragmented into the warlord conflicts that led to the Three Kingdoms period.
