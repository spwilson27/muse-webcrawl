Hounsfield scale

The Hounsfield scale, named after Sir Godfrey Hounsfield, is a quantitative scale for describing radiodensity in computed tomography (CT) imaging. The radiodensity of a substance is expressed in Hounsfield units (HU), calculated relative to the radiodensities of distilled water and air at standard temperature and pressure. By definition, distilled water has a value of 0 HU and air has a value of −1000 HU.

Definition

The Hounsfield unit is defined as HU = 1000 × (μ − μwater) / (μwater − μair), where μ is the linear attenuation coefficient of the material in question. Because the attenuation of air is negligible, the denominator is often approximated by μwater alone.

Applications

Hounsfield values allow radiologists to characterize tissues and lesions objectively: for example, distinguishing fat-containing lesions from soft-tissue masses, identifying calcifications, assessing bone density, and characterizing adrenal adenomas, which typically measure below 10 HU on unenhanced scans. Because the values depend on the X-ray spectrum used, measurements can vary somewhat between scanners and protocols.
