Proportional navigation

Proportional navigation is a guidance law used by most homing missiles. It is based on the observation that two objects are on a collision course when their line of sight does not change direction — that is, the bearing to the target remains constant while the range decreases. The missile commands acceleration proportional to the rate at which the line of sight rotates, steering to null that rotation.

Principle

In its common form, the commanded acceleration is a = N · Vc · λ̇, where N is the navigation constant (typically between 3 and 5), Vc is the closing velocity, and λ̇ is the line-of-sight rate. Proportional navigation is also observed in nature: peregrine falcons and other predators use an equivalent strategy when attacking flying prey.

History

The method was developed during the Second World War, with key contributions from scientists and engineers working on early guided weapons in the United States and Germany, and became the standard guidance law for air-to-air and surface-to-air missiles.
