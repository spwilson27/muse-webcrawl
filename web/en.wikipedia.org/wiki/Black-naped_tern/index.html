Black-naped tern

OrderCharadriiformes
FamilyLaridae
GenusSterna

The black-naped tern (Sterna sumatrana) is a small seabird in the family Laridae, found on tropical and subtropical coasts and islands of the Indian and Pacific Oceans. It takes its name from the distinctive black band that runs from eye to eye across the nape, contrasting with an otherwise clean white head and underparts.

Description

The black-naped tern is about 30–35 cm long with a wingspan of roughly 65–70 cm. The plumage is mostly white, with pale grey upper wings and back, a deeply forked tail, a thin black bill, and black legs. The black nape band and eye stripes give it a banded, hooded appearance. Two subspecies are generally recognised: the nominate S. s. sumatrana of the western part of the range and S. s. mathewsi in the western Indian Ocean.

Distribution and habitat

The species ranges from the Red Sea and East African coast (including Aldabra and the Seychelles) through South and Southeast Asia to Indonesia, northern Australia, New Guinea, and the Pacific islands as far as Samoa and Fiji, and north to southern Japan. It favours low coral islands, sand cays, and rocky islets near clear lagoons.

Behaviour

Black-naped terns feed on small fish and squid, caught by plunge-diving and surface-dipping, usually close to shore. They breed in small colonies on bare sand, shingle, or coral rubble, often alongside other terns, laying one to three eggs in a simple scrape. Like many island terns they are vulnerable to disturbance, introduced predators, and egg collecting, though the global population is considered stable and the species is classified as Least Concern.
