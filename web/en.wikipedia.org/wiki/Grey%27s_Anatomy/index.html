Grey's Anatomy is an American medical drama television series that premiered on March 27, 2005, on ABC as a mid-season replacement. The series focuses on the lives of surgical interns, residents, and attendings as they develop into seasoned doctors while balancing personal and professional relationships. The title is an allusion to Gray's Anatomy, a classic human anatomy textbook.

Premise

The show follows Meredith Grey (played by Ellen Pompeo), daughter of surgeon Ellis Grey, beginning with her first day as a surgical intern at the fictional Seattle Grace Hospital — later renamed Grey Sloan Memorial Hospital. The ensemble format explores the doctors' careers, romances, and crises.

Reception and impact

The series became one of the longest-running scripted primetime shows in the United States and the longest-running American primetime medical drama. It has won Golden Globe and Emmy awards and launched the spin-offs Private Practice and Station 19.

Production

Created by Shonda Rhimes and produced by Shondaland, the series is filmed primarily in Los Angeles, with exterior shots in Seattle.
