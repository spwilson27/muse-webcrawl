Orbital sander

An orbital sander is a hand-held power tool used in woodworking and material finishing that moves its abrasive pad in small circular orbits. Unlike a belt sander, which moves abrasive in a continuous loop, or a disc sander, which spins a pad, the orbital sander vibrates its pad in tiny ellipses, so that no single abrasive particle traces the same path twice. This greatly reduces the swirl marks and cross-grain scratches that other sanders can leave, making it well suited to finish sanding.

Types

The most common variant is the random orbital sander, which combines the orbital motion with a slight, off-centre rotation of the pad, further randomising the scratch pattern. Sheet orbital sanders, often called finishing sanders, use a rectangular pad that takes a quarter or a third of a standard sheet of sandpaper. Palm sanders are smaller versions intended for one-handed use, while larger dual-action sanders are used in automotive refinishing.

Use

Orbital sanders are used to smooth wood, remove old paint or varnish, prepare surfaces between coats of finish, and polish painted or composite surfaces. Pads may carry self-adhesive abrasive discs or hook-and-loop backed discs, and many models include dust extraction ports that connect to a vacuum. Because the action is relatively gentle, orbital sanders remove material more slowly than belt sanders and are rarely used for heavy stock removal.
