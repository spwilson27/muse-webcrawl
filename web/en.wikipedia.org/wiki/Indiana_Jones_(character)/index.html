Indiana Jones (character)

Dr. Henry Walton "Indiana" Jones Jr. is the protagonist of the Indiana Jones franchise, created by George Lucas in homage to the action heroes of 1930s film serials. He is portrayed by Harrison Ford in the films; younger versions were played by River Phoenix and, on television, by Corey Carrier and Sean Patrick Flanery.

Character

Jones is a professor of archaeology who leads a double life as a globe-trotting adventurer. His costume — fedora, leather jacket, satchel and bullwhip — is instantly recognisable. He has a phobia of snakes ("Snakes. Why'd it have to be snakes?"), a dry wit, and a conviction that great artefacts "belong in a museum". His nickname "Indiana" comes from the family dog, a detail revealed in The Last Crusade, where Sean Connery plays his father, Professor Henry Jones Sr.

Creation and reception

Lucas developed the character in the 1970s, and Steven Spielberg cast Ford after Tom Selleck proved unavailable. The American Film Institute ranked Indiana Jones the second-greatest film hero of all time in its 100 Years…100 Heroes and Villains list.
