Scientific nameLimulus polyphemus
FamilyLimulidae
RangeAtlantic coast of North America

The Atlantic horseshoe crab (Limulus polyphemus) is a marine chelicerate arthropod found along the Atlantic coast of North America, from Maine to the Yucatán Peninsula. Despite its name it is more closely related to spiders and scorpions than to true crabs.

Biology

Horseshoe crabs have a distinctive horseshoe-shaped prosoma, a spined opisthosoma and a long tail spine (telson). Females are larger than males and come ashore on sandy beaches in late spring to lay eggs, which are an important food source for migratory shorebirds such as the red knot.

Human uses

Their copper-based blue blood contains Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL), used worldwide to test medical equipment and injectable drugs for bacterial endotoxins. The species is also harvested as bait in eel and conch fisheries and is classified as Vulnerable by the IUCN.
