Deng Xiaoping Theory

Deng Xiaoping Theory (邓小平理论) is a series of political and economic ideologies developed by Chinese leader Deng Xiaoping, constituting the theoretical basis of socialism with Chinese characteristics. It justified China's reform and opening up from 1978, holding that China was in the "primary stage of socialism" and that developing the productive forces — including through markets, private enterprise, and foreign investment — took precedence over ideological purity.

Content

Key elements include the pragmatic principle of "seeking truth from facts"; the idea that it does not matter whether a policy is "capitalist" or "socialist" if it develops the economy ("it doesn't matter whether a cat is black or white, as long as it catches mice"); the one country, two systems formula applied to Hong Kong, Macau, and proposed for Taiwan; and an emphasis on science and technology as a primary productive force, with intellectuals restored to respected status. Deng's 1992 Southern Tour relaunched market reforms after the post-1989 retrenchment.

Status

Deng Xiaoping Theory was written into the constitution of the Chinese Communist Party at its 15th Congress in 1997 and into the state constitution in 1999, alongside Marxism–Leninism and Mao Zedong Thought as a guiding ideology of the People's Republic of China.
