Babergh is a local government district in Suffolk, England, in the south of the county, bordering Essex. Its council is Babergh District Council, which shares offices and a chief executive with neighbouring Mid Suffolk District Council as part of a long-standing partnership between the two authorities.

Geography

The district is largely rural and includes the towns of Sudbury and Hadleigh and many villages. Much of its landscape lies within the Dedham Vale National Landscape (formerly Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty) along the River Stour, famously depicted by the painter John Constable, who was born at East Bergholt in the district. The area is often called "Constable Country".

History

The district was formed on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, from the merger of several earlier authorities including the borough of Sudbury and surrounding rural districts. The name Babergh derives from the Babergh Hundred, an historic division of Suffolk recorded in the Domesday Book.

Governance

Babergh District Council is elected every four years. Since 2011 the council has operated an integrated staffing structure with Mid Suffolk. The district lies within the parliamentary constituencies of South Suffolk and (in part) Suffolk Coastal.
