Sjögren syndrome

Sjögren syndrome (SjS, SS) is a long-term autoimmune disease that primarily affects the body's moisture-producing glands, particularly the salivary and lacrimal glands. Primary symptoms are dry mouth and dry eyes. Other symptoms can include dry skin, vaginal dryness, a chronic cough, numbness in the arms and legs, feeling tired, muscle and joint pains, and thyroid problems.

The condition is named after Swedish ophthalmologist Henrik Sjögren, who described it in 1933. It may occur alone (primary Sjögren syndrome) or together with another autoimmune disease such as rheumatoid arthritis or lupus (secondary Sjögren syndrome). Those affected are at increased risk of lymphoma.

Cause and diagnosis

The exact cause is unclear but involves a combination of genetics and environmental triggers such as viruses. Diagnosis is typically by biopsy of salivary glands and blood tests for specific antibodies, including anti-Ro/SSA and anti-La/SSB.

Treatment

There is no cure; treatment is directed at symptoms, including artificial tears, saliva substitutes, and, in more severe cases, immunosuppressive medication.
