James Rouse

Born: April 26, 1914, Easton, Maryland, U.S.

Died: April 8, 1996 (aged 81), Columbia, Maryland, U.S.

Occupation: Real estate developer, urban planner

James Wilson Rouse (April 26, 1914 – April 8, 1996) was an American businessman who transformed real estate development in the United States. He founded the Rouse Company and is known as the creator of the modern enclosed shopping mall and the planned community of Columbia, Maryland.

Early career

Born in Easton, Maryland, Rouse studied at the University of Virginia and the University of Maryland School of Law. During World War II, he served in the United States Navy in the Pacific.

Projects

In 1956, Rouse built Harundale Mall in Glen Burnie, Maryland, one of America's first enclosed shopping malls. In the 1960s, he acquired farmland in Howard County, Maryland, and developed Columbia, a 13,691-acre planned community designed to be racially, economically, and religiously integrated, which now houses over 100,000 people. In the 1970s and 1980s, his company developed festival marketplaces including Faneuil Hall Marketplace in Boston, Harborplace in Baltimore, and South Street Seaport in New York.

Legacy

Rouse is credited with coining the term "urban renewal" and with founding the Enterprise Foundation (now Enterprise Community Partners) with his wife Patricia Rouse, one of the nation's leading affordable housing nonprofits. In 1995 he was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
