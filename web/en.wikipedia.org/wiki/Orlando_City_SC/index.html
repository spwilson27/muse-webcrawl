Orlando City Soccer Club is an American professional soccer club based in Orlando, Florida, that competes in Major League Soccer (MLS) as a member of the Eastern Conference. The club was awarded MLS's 21st expansion franchise on November 19, 2013, and began play in 2015, succeeding a successful USL Pro team of the same name that had won two regular-season titles and two league championships between 2011 and 2014.

History

Led by English businessman Phil Rawlins and later Brazilian investor Flávio Augusto da Silva, Orlando City drew strong crowds at the Citrus Bowl during its USL years, making a compelling expansion case. Brazilian star Kaká was signed as the club's first Designated Player for its MLS debut in 2015. The club opened its own soccer-specific stadium in downtown Orlando in 2017 (now Inter&Co Stadium, formerly Exploria Stadium). Oscar Pareja became head coach in 2020 and led the Lions to their first major trophy, the 2022 U.S. Open Cup, won 3–0 over Sacramento Republic FC in the final. The Wilf family purchased the club in 2021. The organization also operates the Orlando Pride of the NWSL.

Supporters and rivalries

Supporter groups include the Ruckus and the Iron Lion Firm, known for "The Wall", a standing terrace in the stadium. Orlando's chief rivalry has developed with Inter Miami CF (the Florida derby), and earlier with Atlanta United.
