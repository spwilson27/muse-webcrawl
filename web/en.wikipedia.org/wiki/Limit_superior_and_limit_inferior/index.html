Limit superior and limit inferior

Limit superior and limit inferior
Notationlim sup, lim inf
FieldMathematical analysis

In mathematics, the limit superior (lim sup) and limit inferior (lim inf) of a sequence bound its eventual behaviour. The limit superior is the largest limit point of the sequence (possibly +∞), and the limit inferior the smallest. A sequence converges if and only if its lim sup and lim inf are equal, in which case they equal the limit.

Definition for sequences

For a real sequence (xn), lim sup xn = limn→∞ supk≥n xk — the infimum of the tail suprema — while lim inf is the limit of the tail infima. For the sequence (−1)n(1 + 1/n), the lim sup is 1 and the lim inf is −1, so the sequence diverges.

Properties and uses

Unlike limits, lim sup and lim inf always exist in the extended real numbers for any sequence. They are used in the Cauchy–Hadamard theorem for the radius of convergence of power series, in characterisations of convergence in probability, and in the theory of differential equations. Analogous definitions extend to sequences of sets — as used in probability for "infinitely often" events (Borel–Cantelli lemmas) — and to functions via neighbourhoods.
