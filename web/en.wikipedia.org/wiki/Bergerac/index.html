Bergerac is a commune and subprefecture in the Dordogne department in Nouvelle-Aquitaine, southwestern France, situated on the north bank of the river Dordogne. It is the second-largest town of the department after Périgueux, with around 26,000 inhabitants, and the centre of an important wine-producing region.

History

The town grew around a castle founded in the eleventh century and a bridge over the Dordogne that made it a busy river port for the trade in wine and timber. During the Hundred Years' War Bergerac changed hands repeatedly between French and English forces. It later became a stronghold of Protestantism in the Périgord and suffered during the Wars of Religion; its fortifications were demolished under Louis XIII.

Wine

The Bergerac vineyard, one of the great wine regions of the French southwest, covers some 12,000 hectares around the town under thirteen appellations, including Bergerac rouge and blanc, Côtes de Bergerac, Monbazillac (renowned sweet white wines), Pécharmant and Saussignac. Maison du Vin in the old town presents the region's wines.

The old town and Cyrano

Bergerac's medieval old town preserves half-timbered houses, the church of Notre-Dame, the cloister of the Récollets and two statues of Cyrano de Bergerac — the hero of Edmond Rostand's 1897 play, though the historical Savinien de Cyrano came from Paris and had no real connection with the town. A museum of tobacco recalls the crop once important locally. Tourism, much of it British, is a mainstay of the economy, served by Bergerac Dordogne Périgord Airport.
