Sub rosa

A rose carved over a council chamber table: what is said under the rose stays secret

The Latin phrase sub rosa means "under the rose" and denotes secrecy or confidentiality. It is used when something is told in confidence — a matter discussed sub rosa is not to be repeated outside the room.

Origin

The rose became a symbol of secrecy in the classical world: in Greek myth, Aphrodite gave a rose to her son Eros, who passed it to Harpocrates, the god of silence, so that his mother's indiscretions would not be divulged. In Roman dining rooms, a rose suspended over the table reminded guests that conversation at the banquet was confidential. The practice was revived in medieval and early modern Europe, where roses were carved on the ceilings of council chambers and confessionals; Henry VIII of England is said to have used the device, and the "sub rosa" ceiling ornaments survive in some British government rooms.

Usage

The phrase remains in use in diplomacy, intelligence and parliamentary contexts — meetings held "sub rosa" are off the record. In heraldry and architecture the rose motif continues to signal confidentiality, and the related German expression unter der Rose carries the same sense.
