Lower motor neuron

Lower motor neuron
LocationBrainstem and spinal cord (anterior horn)
ConnectsUpper motor neurons to skeletal muscle
NeurotransmitterAcetylcholine

Lower motor neurons (LMNs) are the motor neurons that connect the brainstem and spinal cord to muscle fibres, forming the final common pathway of the motor system. Their cell bodies lie in the motor nuclei of the cranial nerves and in the anterior (ventral) horn of the spinal cord, and their axons travel through cranial and spinal peripheral nerves to reach the neuromuscular junction, where they release acetylcholine.

Classification

Alpha motor neurons innervate extrafusal muscle fibres and generate force through contraction; gamma motor neurons innervate intrafusal fibres of muscle spindles and regulate their sensitivity; beta motor neurons innervate both. Lower motor neurons receive descending input from upper motor neurons as well as local interneuron circuits.

Lower motor neuron lesions

Damage to lower motor neurons produces a characteristic set of signs: flaccid paralysis, marked muscle atrophy, fasciculations (visible spontaneous twitching of motor units), reduced or absent deep tendon reflexes, and reduced muscle tone. This contrasts with upper motor neuron lesions, which cause spasticity, hyperreflexia, and little atrophy.

Diseases

Conditions affecting lower motor neurons include poliomyelitis, spinal muscular atrophy, peripheral neuropathies, Bell's palsy, and the lower motor neuron component of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). Electrodiagnostic studies such as electromyography help localise and characterise the damage.
