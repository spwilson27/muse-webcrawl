Silicon dioxide

Silicon dioxide, also known as silica, is an oxide of silicon with chemical formula SiO₂, most commonly found in nature as quartz and in various living organisms. It is one of the most abundant compounds in Earth's crust and the main component of sand.

Silica is the raw material of glass, ceramics, cement and silicon metal/semiconductors (after reduction and purification), and is used as a food additive, desiccant (silica gel) and abrasive. Its crystalline polymorphs include quartz, tridymite and cristobalite; amorphous forms include opal and fused silica. Inhaled crystalline silica dust causes silicosis, a serious lung disease of miners, stonecutters and sandblasters — regulated worldwide after disasters such as the Hawks Nest Tunnel.
