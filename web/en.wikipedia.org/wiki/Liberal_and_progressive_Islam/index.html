Liberal and progressive Islam

Liberal Islam and progressive Islam refer to movements within Islam that seek to reinterpret Islamic principles in light of modern values such as democracy, human rights, gender equality and freedom of thought. Adherents argue that the original message of Islam supports these values and that jurisprudence (fiqh) produced in pre-modern societies should be revisited through independent reasoning (ijtihad) and attention to the objectives of the law (maqasid).

History

Reformist currents emerged in the 19th century among thinkers such as Muhammad Abduh and Jamal al-Din al-Afghani, who sought to reconcile Islam with science and modernity. In the late 20th and 21st centuries, scholars including Nasr Hamid Abu Zayd, Amina Wadud, Asma Barlas, Khaled Abou El Fadl and Abdullahi Ahmed An-Na'im developed liberal and feminist readings of the Qur'an, while groups such as Muslims for Progressive Values have promoted inclusive mosques and interfaith work.

Criticism

Conservative and Islamist critics contend that liberal reinterpretations subordinate revelation to Western secular norms, while some liberal Muslims answer that their approach recovers an ethical core obscured by literalism.
