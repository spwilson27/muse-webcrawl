Narciso Martínez

Narciso Martínez (29 October 1911 – 30 June 1992), nicknamed "El Huracán del Valle" ("The Hurricane of the Valley"), was a Mexican-American accordionist widely regarded as the father of conjunto music. His recordings from 1936 onwards defined the Texas-Mexican conjunto sound.

Life and music

Born in Reynosa, Tamaulipas, Martínez grew up in Texas and worked as a farm labourer and zookeeper around Brownsville and San Benito. Largely self-taught on the diatonic button accordion — influenced by Czech and German polka traditions as well as Mexican music — he made his first recordings in 1936 for the Bluebird label, including the hit "La Chicharronera".

Martínez pioneered the defining conjunto format: his fluid, staccato accordion playing carried the melody while Santiago Almeida's bajo sexto provided the bass and rhythm, dispensing with the older duet style. With Almeida he formed one of the most celebrated partnerships in border music. He recorded prolifically for labels such as Falcon and Arhoolie, and later founded his own group, Los Hermanos Martínez. In 1983 he received a National Heritage Fellowship from the U.S. National Endowment for the Arts.
