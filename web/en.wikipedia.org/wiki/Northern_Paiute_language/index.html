Northern Paiute language

Northern Paiute (endonym: Numa or Nimi), also historically called Paviotso, is a Uto-Aztecan language of the Western Numic branch, traditionally spoken by the Northern Paiute people of the Great Basin in what is now eastern California, western Nevada, southeastern Oregon, and southwestern Idaho. In Oregon its variety is sometimes called the Bannock language, closely related to the speech of the Bannock people.

Characteristics

Northern Paiute is closely related to Mono and more distantly to Shoshoni and other Numic languages. Dialects vary considerably from band to band across the Great Basin—speech in the Humboldt River, Pyramid Lake, Walker Lake, and Harney Valley areas differs noticeably—reflecting the dispersed settlement pattern of its speakers. Like other Numic languages it has a system of consonant gradation (alternating "geminating" and "spirantizing" suffixes) and a rich vocabulary adapted to desert subsistence.

Status and revitalisation

The language is seriously endangered: fluent first-language speakers are now few and mostly elderly, with estimates ranging from a few hundred to around 700. Tribal communities including the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe and the Burns Paiute Tribe run language classes, immersion programmes, and documentation projects, and linguists have produced grammars, dictionaries, and recordings to support teaching.
