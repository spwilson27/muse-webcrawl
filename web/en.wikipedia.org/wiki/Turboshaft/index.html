Turboshaft

A turboshaft engine is a form of gas turbine that produces shaft power to drive machinery instead of producing thrust directly. Nearly all of the energy of the hot exhaust gases is extracted by an additional ("free") power turbine stage, leaving the exhaust with little residual velocity. Turboshafts are the standard powerplant for helicopters and are also used in tanks, ships, trains, compressors, pumps and auxiliary power applications.

Operation

Like other gas turbines, a turboshaft comprises an air compressor, combustion chamber and gas-generator turbine that drives the compressor. Downstream, a separate free power turbine — mechanically independent of the gas generator spool — converts the remaining gas energy into shaft rotation. The power shaft typically drives a reduction gearbox, which lowers the turbine's tens of thousands of rpm to the few hundred rpm required by a helicopter rotor. Free-turbine design lets the rotor speed stay nearly constant while the gas generator varies with power demand.

Applications

The first successful helicopter turboshafts entered service in the 1950s, and their high power-to-weight ratio transformed helicopter design, enabling types such as the Bell UH-1 Iroquois (Lycoming T53), the Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk (General Electric T700) and the Mil Mi-8/17 family (Klimov engines). The General Electric T700/CT7 family alone has powered helicopters, turboprops and the M1 Abrams tank's AGT1500 gas turbine. Smaller turboshafts serve as auxiliary power units (APUs) aboard aircraft and in armoured vehicles.
