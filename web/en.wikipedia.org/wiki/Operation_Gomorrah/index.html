Operation Gomorrah

Operation Gomorrah was the codename for a series of air raids on Hamburg, Germany, carried out by the RAF and USAAF in late July and early August 1943 during the Second World War. The raids combined RAF night bombing and American daylight attacks over about a week.

The raid on the night of 27–28 July created one of the first deliberately produced firestorms: exceptionally hot, dry weather, concentrated bombing and disrupted firefighting caused temperatures high enough to generate hurricane-force winds drawing air into the flames. Large districts of the city were destroyed.

Estimates of the dead range from about 34,000 to 43,000, with tens of thousands injured and hundreds of thousands made homeless. The attacks were also notable for the first operational use of "Window" (chaff) to confuse German radar. Hamburg's experience became a byword for the destructive power of area bombing.
