The Battle of Narva was a military campaign fought between the German Army Detachment Narwa and the Soviet Leningrad Front for control of the strategically vital Narva Isthmus in north-eastern Estonia from February to August 1944, during the Second World War.

Background

Following the lifting of the siege of Leningrad in January 1944, Soviet forces pursued the retreating Army Group North toward the Panther Line on the Narva river. Stalin attached great importance to seizing Estonia quickly as a base for operations against Finland and East Prussia. The German position rested on the river line between the Gulf of Finland and Lake Peipus, anchored on the city of Narva.

The fighting

The campaign is commonly divided into the Battle for the Narva Bridgehead (February–April), in which repeated Soviet attempts to break through were repulsed with enormous losses, and the Battle of the Tannenberg Line (July–August), fought after the Germans withdrew to prepared positions on the Sinimäed hills west of the river. The defence was conducted by a multinational Waffen-SS force under Felix Steiner, including Estonian, Dutch, Flemish, Walloon, Danish and Norwegian volunteers alongside German units — which led to the battle being called the "Battle of the European SS".

Outcome

The Soviet offensive failed to achieve its rapid breakthrough: the front held for nearly six months, tying down large Soviet forces and allowing Germany to keep Finland in the war until September 1944. Casualties were very heavy on both sides, and the historic city of Narva was almost entirely destroyed. Only in September 1944, after Finland's exit from the war and a new Soviet offensive further south, did the Germans evacuate Estonia.
