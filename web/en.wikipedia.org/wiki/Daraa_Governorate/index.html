Daraa Governorate

Daraa Governorate (Arabic: مُحافظة درعا, Muḥāfaẓat Darʿā) is one of the fourteen governorates of Syria. Located in the south-west of the country, it borders Jordan to the south, Israel (the Golan Heights) to the west, Rif Dimashq Governorate to the north, and As-Suwayda Governorate to the east. Its capital is the city of Daraa. The governorate covers about 3,730 km² and had a pre-war population of roughly one million.

Geography

The governorate lies within the fertile Hauran plateau, a volcanic plain long known as a granary of Syria, producing wheat, vegetables and grapes. Its districts (mintaqa) are Daraa, Izra and Al-Sanamayn, subdivided into subdistricts.

History

The Hauran region contains ancient sites such as Bosra, famous for its Roman theatre. In March 2011, protests in Daraa city — sparked by the arrest of schoolchildren who had written anti-government graffiti — became a catalyst for the nationwide uprising that developed into the Syrian civil war. The governorate saw prolonged fighting; government forces retook it in 2018 under a reconciliation agreement, though insecurity persisted in subsequent years.
