Oleic acid

Oleic acid is a fatty acid that occurs naturally in various animal and vegetable fats and oils. It is an odorless, colorless oil, although commercial samples may be yellowish. In chemical terms, oleic acid is classified as a monounsaturated omega-9 fatty acid, abbreviated with a lipid number of 18:1 cis-9, and its IUPAC name is (9Z)-octadec-9-enoic acid. Its chemical formula is C18H34O2. Its saturated counterpart is stearic acid, and its trans isomer is elaidic acid.

Occurrence

Oleic acid is the most common monounsaturated fatty acid in nature. It makes up roughly 70% of olive oil, from which its name derives (Latin oleum, oil), and is abundant in pecan, canola, peanut, macadamia and high-oleic sunflower oils. In human adipose tissue it is the second most abundant fatty acid after palmitic acid. Triglycerides containing oleic acid are called olein.

Production and reactions

Oleic acid is obtained commercially by hydrolysis of fats such as tallow and olive oil, followed by separation from other fatty acids. In the body it is biosynthesized by the enzyme stearoyl-CoA desaturase acting on stearic acid. It undergoes the typical reactions of carboxylic acids and alkenes; hydrogenation converts it to stearic acid, and oxidative cleavage at the double bond yields azelaic acid and pelargonic acid.

Uses and health effects

Oleic acid is used in soaps, cosmetics, lubricants and as an emulsifying agent, and as a food additive (E number additives derived from it include calcium oleate). Diets rich in monounsaturated oleic acid, such as the Mediterranean diet, are associated with favourable blood lipid profiles compared with diets high in saturated fat, though it remains debated how much of the observed benefit is due to oleic acid itself.
