Gestation

Gestation is the period of development during which a viviparous organism carries its young inside the body, from conception (fertilisation) until birth. The term is used most often for the pregnancy of mammals, but also applies to some non-mammalian species that bear live young. The developing organism is known successively as a zygote, an embryo, and (in humans after about the eighth week) a fetus.

Gestation in humans

In humans, gestation lasts on average about 280 days (40 weeks) counted from the first day of the last menstrual period, or about 266 days from fertilisation. It is conventionally divided into three trimesters of roughly three months each. Birth before 37 completed weeks is termed preterm, while a pregnancy extending beyond 42 weeks is post-term. Conditions arising during gestation include gestational hypertension and gestational diabetes.

Gestation length correlates broadly with body size and with the maturity of the young at birth: precocial species, whose offspring are born relatively mature, generally have longer gestations than altricial species of similar size. Marsupials are an extreme exception, with very short gestations followed by extended development in the pouch.
