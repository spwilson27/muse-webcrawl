Columbia Pictures Industries, Inc., commonly referred to as Columbia Pictures, is an American film studio and production and distribution company that is the flagship unit of the Sony Pictures Motion Picture Group, a division of Sony Entertainment's Sony Pictures and a subsidiary of the multinational conglomerate Sony. Founded on June 19, 1918, as CBC Film Sales Corporation, it is one of the leading film studios in the world and a member of the "Big Five" major American film studios.

History

The studio began as CBC (Cohn-Brandt-Cohn) Film Sales, a low-budget operation on Hollywood's Poverty Row, and adopted the Columbia name in 1924. Under the autocratic Harry Cohn (president until his death in 1958), Columbia rose to major status through the films of Frank Capra — including It Happened One Night (1934), the first film to sweep the five major Academy Awards — and through stars such as Rita Hayworth and the comedy trio The Three Stooges.

Columbia's Screen Gems television division, launched in 1948, pioneered studio television production. In the 1980s the Coca-Cola Company acquired the studio (1982), and in 1989 Sony purchased it, forming Sony Pictures Entertainment. The Columbia name continued on releases spanning the Spider-Man, Men in Black, Ghostbusters and Jumanji franchises, and acclaimed films from Lawrence of Arabia (1962) to Taxi Driver (1976, with Warner Bros.).

Logo

The studio's emblem, a female figure (representing Columbia, the national personification of the United States) holding a torch, has been in use since 1924 and was restyled several times, most notably in 1992–93 with a painting by Michael Deas modeled by Jenny Joseph.
