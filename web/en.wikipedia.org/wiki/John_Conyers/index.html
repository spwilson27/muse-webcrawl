John James Conyers Jr. (May 16, 1929 – October 27, 2019) was an American politician of the Democratic Party who served as a U.S. representative from Michigan from 1965 to 2017. The longest-serving African American in congressional history and a founding member of the Congressional Black Caucus, Conyers was the sixth-longest serving member of Congress overall.

Early life and military service

Conyers was born and raised in Detroit. He served in the Michigan National Guard and the U.S. Army, including a tour in Korea during the Korean War, before earning bachelor's and law degrees from Wayne State University. He worked as a legislative assistant to Representative John Dingell.

Congressional career

Elected in 1964, Conyers represented districts centred on Detroit for 53 years. He co-founded the Congressional Black Caucus in 1969, chaired the House Judiciary Committee from 2007 to 2011, and was a leading sponsor of the bill establishing the Martin Luther King Jr. Day federal holiday, first introduced in 1968 and enacted in 1983. He repeatedly introduced a bill to study reparations for slavery (H.R. 40) and was an early, prominent critic of the Iraq War. He served as Dean of the House from 2015 to 2017.

Resignation and death

Conyers resigned on December 5, 2017, amid allegations of sexual harassment and a settlement with a former staffer; he denied wrongdoing. He died at his home in Detroit on October 27, 2019, aged 90.
