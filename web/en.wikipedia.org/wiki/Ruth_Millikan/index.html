Ruth Garrett Millikan (born 1933) is an American philosopher of biology, psychology and language, and a leading figure in naturalistic philosophy of mind. She is Board of Trustees Distinguished Professor Emeritus at the University of Connecticut, where she taught for most of her career.

Teleosemantics

Millikan's best-known theory, teleosemantics (or biosemantics), explains the content of mental representations in terms of their biological proper functions — what a representation-producing and representation-consuming mechanism was selected by evolution to do. On this account, a belief represents what it does because systems that produce and use it were designed by natural selection so that the belief is normally true when the organism succeeds. Her book Language, Thought, and Other Biological Categories (1984) founded this research programme.

Other work

Her later books include White Queen Psychology and Other Essays for Alice (1993), On Clear and Confused Ideas (2000), Varieties of Meaning (2004) and Beyond Concepts (2017). She has written on substance concepts, inner and outer representations, and the nature of mental content, engaging critically with Fodor, Dretske and Dennett.

Honours

Millikan received the Jean Nicod Prize in 2002, delivering the lectures published as Varieties of Meaning, the Rolf Schock Prize in Logic and Philosophy in 2017, and the Nicholas Rescher Prize in Systematic Philosophy in 2021.
