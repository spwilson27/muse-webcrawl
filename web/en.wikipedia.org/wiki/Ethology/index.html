Ethology

Ethology is the scientific study of animal behaviour, especially in natural conditions. It treats behaviour as an evolved, adaptive trait — the counterpart to laboratory-based comparative Psychology.

Founders and concepts

Konrad Lorenz, Nikolaas Tinbergen and Karl von Frisch shared the 1973 Nobel Prize for founding the field: Lorenz for imprinting in geese, Tinbergen for his ‘four questions’ (causation, development, function, evolution) and stickleback studies, von Frisch for decoding the honey bee dance. Key concepts include fixed action patterns, innate releasing mechanisms and critical periods.

Modern ethology

Ethology merged with behavioural ecology and cognitive ethology, using field experiments, tracking technology and genetics to study foraging, mating systems, communication and culture in wild populations — from wolves to falcons.
