In computational complexity theory, BQP (bounded-error quantum polynomial time) is the class of decision problems solvable by a quantum computer in polynomial time, with an error probability of at most 1/3 for all instances. It is the quantum analogue of the classical complexity class BPP, and it is the natural class of problems efficiently solvable by quantum computers.

Definition and properties

A language is in BQP if there exists a uniform family of polynomial-size quantum circuits that accepts inputs in the language with probability at least 2/3 and rejects others with probability at least 2/3; the constant 1/3 is arbitrary, since repetition and majority voting drive the error down exponentially. BQP was defined by Bernstein and Vazirani in 1993. It contains P and BPP, and is contained in PP and hence in PSPACE — a quantum computer cannot decide more languages in polynomial time than a classical machine with polynomial space.

Relation to classical classes and known problems

Whether BQP strictly exceeds BPP is unknown, but strong evidence suggests it does: integer factorisation and discrete logarithms lie in BQP thanks to Shor's algorithm (1994) and are not believed to be in BPP, while oracle results show separations between BQP and the polynomial hierarchy. Other prominent problems in BQP include simulation of quantum systems, the Jones polynomial approximation, and certain linear-systems and sampling problems. BQP is not believed to contain NP-complete problems; searching an unstructured database gets only a quadratic speedup via Grover's algorithm, insufficient to place NP in BQP if the class behaves as conjectured.
