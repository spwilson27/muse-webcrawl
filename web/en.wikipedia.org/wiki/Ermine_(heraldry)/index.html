Ermine (heraldry)

Ermine, heraldic fur (image placeholder)

In heraldry, ermine is a "fur", a two-coloured pattern consisting of a white or silver background patterned with black marks, representing the winter coat of the stoat (also called the ermine), whose white fur with a black tail tip was prized for ceremonial garments. The pattern imitates the joining of many stoat pelts, the black spots being the tails of the animals.

The ermine spot

The individual mark, the "ermine spot" (in French moucheture d'hermine), is conventionally drawn as three small dots above an arrow-like or comma-shaped tail. Over the centuries its form has ranged from naturalistic tufts to highly stylised shapes. A field covered with ermine spots is said to be "ermined" or, in blazon, simply ermine.

Variants

Several counter-variants exist: ermines (also called counter-ermine) reverses the colours, with silver spots on a black field; erminois has black spots on gold; and pean has gold spots on black. Ermine is strongly associated with Brittany, whose dukes bore plain ermine and whose flag, the Gwenn-ha-du, bears an ermine canton; it also appears in the robes and coronets of peers and in judicial dress in the United Kingdom.
