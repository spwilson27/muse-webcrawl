Meniscus (liquid)

A concave meniscus of water in glass; the reading is taken at the bottom of the curve.

A meniscus (from Greek mēniskos, "crescent") is the curve in the upper surface of a liquid close to the surface of a container, caused by surface tension and the balance between adhesive and cohesive forces.

Concave and convex menisci

When adhesive forces between the liquid and the container wall are stronger than cohesion within the liquid — as with water in glass — the surface curves upward at the edges, forming a concave meniscus. When cohesion dominates — as with mercury in glass — the surface curves downward, forming a convex meniscus. The contact angle between liquid and wall determines the shape.

Measurement

Volumes in graduated cylinders, burettes, and pipettes are read at the bottom of a concave meniscus (or the top of a convex one), aligned with the observer's eye to avoid parallax error. Meniscus formation also underlies capillary action, the rise or fall of liquid in narrow tubes.
