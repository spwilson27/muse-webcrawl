Bala (Welsh: Y Bala) is a market town and community in Gwynedd, Wales. It lies at the northern end of Bala Lake (Llyn Tegid), the largest natural lake in Wales, in the historic county of Merionethshire. The town is a centre for Welsh language and culture, and a base for watersports and walking in the nearby Snowdonia (Eryri) National Park, whose boundary it lies just outside.

History

Bala was founded as a borough in the early 14th century. In the 18th century it became a centre of the Welsh Methodist revival and of the wool trade, particularly the production of stockings. The theologian Thomas Charles (1755–1814), a leading figure in Welsh Methodism and a founder of the British and Foreign Bible Society, lived in the town; his statue stands on the main street. Mary Jones, a local girl who in 1800 walked 26 miles barefoot to Bala to buy a Welsh Bible from Charles, became an enduring symbol of Bible distribution worldwide.

Bala Lake

Bala Lake is about 3.7 miles (6 km) long and up to a mile wide. It is home to the gwyniad, a rare whitefish found only in the lake, and is used for sailing, kayaking, windsurfing and wild swimming. The narrow-gauge Bala Lake Railway runs along the lake's eastern shore from Llanuwchllyn.

Language

Bala is one of the strongest Welsh-speaking communities in Wales, with a large majority of residents able to speak Welsh. The town hosts Welsh-language festivals and is a focal point for the culture of the Penllyn district.
