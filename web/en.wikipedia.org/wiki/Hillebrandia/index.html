Hillebrandia

Hillebrandia is a monotypic genus of flowering plants in the begonia family (Begoniaceae). Its only species, Hillebrandia sandwicensis, known in Hawaiian as aka ʻaka ʻawa, is endemic to the Hawaiian Islands, where it grows in wet ravines and mesic forests on Kauaʻi, Oʻahu, Molokaʻi, Maui and (rarely) the island of Hawaiʻi.

Description

The plant is a tuberous perennial herb with begonia-like, asymmetrical leaves and pink to white unisexual flowers. Unlike the true begonias (genus Begonia), it has a partially inferior ovary and other floral differences that justify its placement in a separate genus, named for the German physician and botanist William Hillebrand, author of the Flora of the Hawaiian Islands.

Significance

Hillebrandia is remarkable as the only member of the Begoniaceae native to Hawaii. Biogeographic study shows it is an ancient lineage — sister to the enormous genus Begonia — whose arrival in Hawaii by long-distance dispersal predates the formation of the current high islands, implying it once grew on now-submerged islands of the Hawaiian chain.
