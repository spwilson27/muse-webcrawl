Ipswich

Ipswich is the county town of Suffolk, on the estuary of the River Orwell.

Ipswich is a town in Suffolk, England. It is the county town and lies on the estuary of the River Orwell, in the southeast of the county. One of England's oldest towns, it has been continuously inhabited since the early Middle Ages and is notable for its medieval churches and timber-framed buildings.

History

Roman settlement existed nearby, and the Anglo-Saxon town of Gippeswic developed into an important trading port from the 7th century. Ipswich prospered under the Normans and in the wool trade of the Middle Ages; it was the birthplace of Cardinal Thomas Wolsey, Henry VIII's lord chancellor. Its docks were among the largest wet docks in Europe when completed in the 19th century, and the town developed engineering industries such as Ransomes.

Modern Ipswich

The regenerated Waterfront area around the marina now hosts restaurants, the University of Suffolk, and DanceEast. Landmarks include Christchurch Mansion and park, the Ancient House with its decorative pargeting, and the Willis Building, an early work of Foster and Partners. The town's football club, Ipswich Town F.C., plays at Portman Road and won the UEFA Cup in 1981 under Bobby Robson.

Transport

Ipswich railway station provides mainline services to London Liverpool Street via the Great Eastern Main Line, and the town is served by the A12 and A14 roads.
