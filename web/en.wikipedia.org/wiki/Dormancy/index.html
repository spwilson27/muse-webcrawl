Dormancy

Dormancy is a period in an organism's life cycle when growth, development and physical activity are temporarily stopped. This minimizes metabolic activity and conserves energy, allowing the organism to survive unfavourable conditions.

Dormancy
Field: Biology
Purposes: Survive winter, drought
Forms: Hibernation, torpor, diapause, seed dormancy

In animals

Forms include hibernation (winter dormancy of bears, ground squirrels), aestivation during heat and drought, torpor (short-term reductions in body temperature), and diapause in insects, a hormonally controlled arrest of development. Dormancy may be predictive — entered before adverse conditions arrive, cued by photoperiod — or consequential, entered in direct response to conditions.

In plants and seeds

Bud dormancy in temperate trees is induced by shortening days and released by chilling. Seed dormancy prevents germination until conditions suit: imposed by hard seed coats, chemical inhibitors such as abscisic acid, or immature embryos, and broken by cold stratification, scarification, light or fire (as in serotinous species). Bacteria and fungi form dormant spores and cysts that can persist for years.
