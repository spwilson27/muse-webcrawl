Azithromycin

Azithromycin, sold under the brand names Zithromax and Azasite among others, is an antibiotic medication used for the treatment of a number of bacterial infections, including middle ear infections, strep throat, pneumonia, traveler's diarrhea, and certain sexually transmitted infections such as chlamydia. It is a macrolide antibiotic of the azalide subclass.

Medical uses

Azithromycin is used for respiratory tract infections (bronchitis, community-acquired pneumonia), skin and soft-tissue infections, acute otitis media, and sexually transmitted infections including chlamydia and gonorrhea. It is also used for mass drug administration campaigns against trachoma. Its long tissue half-life allows short courses, such as the five-day "Z-Pak".

Mechanism of action

Azithromycin binds to the 50S subunit of the bacterial ribosome, inhibiting protein synthesis and bacterial growth. It concentrates strongly within phagocytes and tissues, which accounts for its prolonged action after dosing stops.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain. More serious concerns include QT prolongation and a small increased risk of cardiovascular death, allergic reactions, and Clostridioides difficile infection.

History

Azithromycin was discovered in 1980 by a team of researchers at the Croatian pharmaceutical company Pliva, led by Slobodan Đokić, and was patented in 1981. Pliva licensed it to Pfizer, which marketed it as Zithromax; it became one of the best-selling antibiotics worldwide. It appears on the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines.
