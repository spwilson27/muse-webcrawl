Ernest Augustus I (German: Ernst August I; 5 June 1771 – 18 November 1851) was King of Hanover from 1837 to 1851 and, in the United Kingdom, the Duke of Cumberland and Teviotdale. The fifth son of George III, he acceded to the Hanoverian throne on the death of his brother William IV because Hanover's semi-Salic succession law excluded William's successor in Britain, Queen Victoria, thus dissolving the personal union between the two crowns dating from 1714.

Reign

Ernest Augustus governed Hanover conservatively. One of his first acts was to annul the relatively liberal constitution of 1833, an act resisted by seven professors of the University of Göttingen — the famous Göttingen Seven — whom he punished by dismissal; three, including the Brothers Grimm, were banished. In 1840 a new, more conservative constitution came into force. During the revolutions of 1848 he made limited concessions but quickly recovered his authority.

The king died at Hanover in November 1851 and was succeeded by his only son, the blind George V, the last king of Hanover. His mausoleum stands in the Berggarten at Herrenhausen.
