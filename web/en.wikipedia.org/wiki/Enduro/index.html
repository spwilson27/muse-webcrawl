Enduro

Enduro is a long-distance form of motorcycle sport run on extended cross-country, off-road courses. Enduro consists of many different obstacles and challenges, and the main type of enduro event, and the format used in the World Enduro Championship, is a time-card enduro, whereby a number of stages are raced in a time trial against the clock.

A competitor riding a special stage in an enduro event.

Format

Riders follow a marked course over rough terrain and must arrive at checkpoints at scheduled times; arriving early or late incurs penalties. Between the timed "liaison" sections are special tests, where riders are timed individually over demanding natural terrain. Events can last one or two days and cover hundreds of kilometres.

Major events

The oldest and most famous event is the International Six Days Enduro (ISDE), first held in 1913 and often described as the "Olympics of motorcycling". Other prominent events include hard enduro races such as the Erzbergrodeo and Romaniacs.

Motorcycles

Enduro motorcycles must generally be road-legal, with lights, silencers and a registration plate, and combine the suspension of a motocross bike with a smoother, more durable engine suitable for long distances.
