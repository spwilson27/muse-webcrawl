Gibson Desert

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Red dunes and spinifex in the Gibson Desert

The **Gibson Desert** is a large, arid region of Western Australia, part of the Great Western Desert. It covers roughly 155,000 km² of red sand plains, dune fields and gravelly rises between the Great Sandy Desert to the north and the Great Victoria Desert to the south. Explorer Ernest Giles, who crossed it in the 1870s, named it for Alfred Gibson, a companion who died searching for water after their horses perished.

The Gibson remains one of Australia's most remote regions, home to Aboriginal communities such as Warburton and to remarkable desert wildlife (bilbies, thorny devils, Princess parrots). Temperatures exceed 40 °C in summer. In 1984 the last previously-uncontacted Aboriginal group, the Pintupi Nine, walked out of the Gibson Desert — a landmark event in Australian history. The 650,000-hectare Gibson Desert Nature Reserve protects a large part of it.

Geography and people

Rainfall averages under 200 mm and is erratic. Spinifex grasslands and mulga woodland cover the stone and sand. Aboriginal occupation spans tens of thousands of years; the Canning Stock Route skirts its eastern edge.
