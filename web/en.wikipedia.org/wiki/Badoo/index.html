Badoo is a dating-focused social networking service founded in 2006 by Russian entrepreneur Andrey Andreev. Operated from London, it was one of the world's most widely used dating platforms, with hundreds of millions of registered users and availability in around 190 countries and 47 languages. In 2019 Badoo became part of the group that, together with the dating app Bumble, formed MagicLab, later renamed Bumble Inc. when it went public in 2021.

Features

Badoo combines social discovery with dating. Its signature feature, "Encounters", presents users with photos of other members to rate as interesting or not, producing matches when interest is mutual. Other features include location-based people search, profile verification via photo checks, video chat, and livestreaming. The service operates on a freemium model: basic use is free, while subscriptions and credits unlock greater visibility and extra features.

History

Launched in November 2006, Badoo grew rapidly through viral adoption on Facebook and became particularly popular in Latin America, Southern and Eastern Europe, and parts of Asia. By the early 2010s it claimed over 150 million users and annual revenues exceeding $200 million. In 2014 Andreev launched Bumble with Whitney Wolfe Herd, and Badoo's infrastructure and team supported the new app's development. Following a 2019 majority investment by Blackstone, the apps were consolidated under MagicLab, which listed on the Nasdaq as Bumble Inc. in February 2021.

Criticism

Badoo has faced criticism over fake profiles, aggressive monetization prompts, and privacy practices; a 2009 University of Cambridge study ranked it among the lowest dating services for privacy protection. The company has responded with photo verification, profile moderation, and safety reporting tools.
