Geography of Mars

Surface area: 144.8 million km²
Highest point: Olympus Mons (21.9 km)
Lowest point: Hellas Planitia (−8.2 km)

The geography of Mars (also termed areography, from Ares, the Greek equivalent of Mars) describes the surface features of the planet Mars. Mars has a surface area of about 144.8 million km², roughly equal to the land area of Earth, and displays the most varied and extreme topography in the Solar System.

Global dichotomy

The most striking large-scale feature is the hemispheric dichotomy: the southern hemisphere consists of ancient, heavily cratered highlands standing 1–3 km above datum, while the northern hemisphere is covered by younger, smooth lowland plains. Hypotheses for the dichotomy include a single giant impact (the proposed Borealis basin) and mantle convection.

Volcanoes and canyons

The Tharsis Rise is a vast volcanic plateau hosting the Tharsis Montes and Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano and mountain known in the Solar System at about 21.9 km above datum and some 600 km across. East of Tharsis lies Valles Marineris, a canyon system stretching more than 4,000 km and up to 7 km deep. Another huge volcanic province, Elysium, lies in the northern hemisphere. The impact basin Hellas Planitia, about 2,300 km wide, is the lowest region of the planet and large enough to be seen from Earth as a bright feature.

Polar caps and water features

Both poles have layered ice caps composed of water ice and frozen carbon dioxide, which grow and shrink with the seasons. Elsewhere, networks of valleys, outflow channels, gullies and putative ancient shorelines record the action of liquid water early in Martian history, and extensive permafrost and buried glaciers persist at mid-latitudes today.

Nomenclature

Martian surface features are named under conventions of the International Astronomical Union: large dark albedo features retain classical names from 19th-century observers such as Giovanni Schiaparelli, craters are named for scientists and towns, and valleys take the word for "Mars" or "star" in various languages.
