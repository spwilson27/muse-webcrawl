Canada Council

Canada Council for the Arts
Type: Crown corporation
Established: 1957
Headquarters: Ottawa, Ontario
Role: national public arts funder

The Canada Council for the Arts (French: Conseil des arts du Canada) is Canada's national public arts funder, a Crown corporation established in 1957 following the recommendations of the Massey Commission on national cultural development. It supports professional artists and arts organisations across the country through peer-assessed grants and operates at arm's length from the federal government.

Activities

The Council funds creation, production, and dissemination in literature, music, theatre, dance, visual and media arts, and Indigenous arts, and it administers major honours including the Governor General's Awards in Visual and Media Arts and the Governor General's Literary Awards. It also runs the Art Bank, a collection of Canadian contemporary art rented to public and private clients, and represents Canada at the Venice Biennale.

Funding

An endowment from the estates of two Canadian industrialists helped launch the Council; today it is funded chiefly by an annual parliamentary appropriation, doubled by the federal government in the years after 2016 to roughly $360 million.
