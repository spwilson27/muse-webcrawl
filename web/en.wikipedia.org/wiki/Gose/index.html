Gose

Gose (pronounced [ˈɡoːzə]) is a warm-fermented beer style that originated in Goslar, Germany. It is usually brewed with at least 50% of the grain bill being malted wheat, and is characterised by a lactic acid tang, additions of coriander and salt, and a modest hop bitterness.

The style takes its name from the river Gose, whose slightly salty water — influenced by mineral springs near Goslar — was originally used in brewing. Gose became most closely associated with the city of Leipzig, where dedicated taverns called Gosenschenken served it, traditionally in long-necked bottles and drunk from cylindrical glasses.

After nearly dying out in the 20th century, gose was revived in Leipzig in the 1980s and 1990s and has since become popular with craft brewers internationally, many of whom produce fruit-flavoured variations. Its sourness comes from inoculation with Lactobacillus bacteria, either before or alongside fermentation with top-fermenting yeast.
