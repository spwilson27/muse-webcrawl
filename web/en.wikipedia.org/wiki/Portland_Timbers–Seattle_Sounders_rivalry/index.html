Portland Timbers–Seattle Sounders rivalry

The Portland Timbers–Seattle Sounders rivalry is a soccer rivalry between the Portland Timbers and Seattle Sounders FC, both based in the Pacific Northwest region of Cascadia. It is considered one of the fiercest rivalries in American and Canadian soccer, and one of the few in Major League Soccer that predates the league itself: the two cities first met on the field in the North American Soccer League in 1975.

Origins

The original Seattle Sounders joined the NASL in 1974 and the original Portland Timbers a year later; their first match, on May 2, 1975, began a derby that continued through the NASL, the Western Soccer Alliance and the USL, with both clubs carrying the same names across leagues. When Seattle joined MLS in 2009 and Portland followed in 2011, the rivalry resumed on a bigger stage, drawing some of the largest crowds in the league.

Cascadia Cup

Since 2004, the Timbers, Sounders and Vancouver Whitecaps have competed for the Cascadia Cup, a supporter-created trophy awarded on the regular-season results between the three clubs. The derby matches are marked by elaborate tifo displays by the Timbers Army and Seattle's Emerald City Supporters.

Notable matches

Highlights of the MLS era include the clubs' 2013 and 2018 MLS Cup Playoff meetings — the 2018 Western Conference semifinal, decided on penalties after a dramatic second leg in Seattle, is widely regarded as one of the greatest playoff series in league history — and Portland's 4–1 home win in 2015 on the way to its MLS Cup title.
