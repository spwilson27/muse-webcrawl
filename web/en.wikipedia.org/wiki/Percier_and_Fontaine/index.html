Charles Percier (1764–1838) and Pierre François Léonard Fontaine (1762–1853) were two French architects and designers who formed the most celebrated professional partnership of the Napoleonic era. Working together from their student days in Rome until Percier's death, they became the official architects of Napoleon Bonaparte and the principal creators of the Empire style in architecture, interior decoration and furniture design.

Career

Both men won the Prix de Rome in the 1780s and studied together at the French Academy in Rome, where they developed a shared fascination with ancient Roman architecture and Renaissance design. Returning to Paris during the Revolution, they made their reputations designing stage sets for the Paris Opéra and interiors for the new class of financiers. In 1799 they were introduced to Joséphine de Beauharnais and remodelled her château of Malmaison, which brought them to Napoleon's attention.

As the Emperor's architects they designed the Arc de Triomphe du Carrousel (1806–1808), the uniform arcaded façades of the Rue de Rivoli, the interiors of the Tuileries, Saint-Cloud and other imperial residences, and countless pieces of furniture, silver and ceremonial ornament. Their Recueil de décorations intérieures (1801–1812) codified the Empire style and spread its influence across Europe and America.

Later years

After the fall of Napoleon, Fontaine continued as architect to Louis XVIII and Louis-Philippe, joining the Louvre to the Tuileries and designing the Palais Royal gallery. Percier retired from practice, taught at the École des Beaux-Arts, and influenced a generation of students including several future architects of Paris. Fontaine's published journal provides one of the richest first-hand accounts of French architecture in the period.

Principal works

  • Château de Malmaison interiors (1799–1803)
  • Arc de Triomphe du Carrousel, Paris (1806–1808)
  • Rue de Rivoli arcades, Paris (begun 1802)
  • Alterations to the Tuileries Palace and Château de Saint-Cloud
  • Château d'Eu and Palais Royal restorations (Fontaine)
