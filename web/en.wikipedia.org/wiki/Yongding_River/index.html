Yongding River

LengthAbout 650 km (400 mi)
SourceGuancen Mountains, Shanxi
MouthHai River, near Tianjin
BasinAbout 47,000 km²

The Yongding River (Chinese: 永定河) is a river in northern China, the largest tributary of the Hai River system. It flows from Shanxi Province through Hebei and the municipalities of Beijing and Tianjin, and is often called Beijing's "mother river," as early settlements of the capital grew along its banks.

Course

The river rises as the Sanggan River in the Guancen Mountains of Ningwu County, Shanxi, flows east through the Yangyuan basin, and is joined by the Yang River near Huailai. Passing through the Guanting Reservoir in the mountains northwest of Beijing, it takes the name Yongding, emerges onto the North China Plain through a gorge at Sanjiadian, and crosses western Beijing — flowing under the Marco Polo Bridge (Lugou Bridge) at Wanping — before continuing southeast to join the Hai River near Tianjin and ultimately reaching the Bohai Sea.

History

Notorious for shifting course and catastrophic flooding, the river was once called the Wuding ("unsettled") River. In 1698 the Kangxi Emperor ordered embankment works and renamed it Yongding — "everlastingly settled." The Marco Polo Bridge over the river, described by Marco Polo and completed in 1192, is famous worldwide as the site of the Marco Polo Bridge Incident of 7 July 1937, which opened the full-scale Second Sino-Japanese War.

Modern issues

Water extraction, damming (notably Guanting Reservoir, completed 1954), and drought left the lower course dry for long stretches from the 1990s onward. In recent decades, ecological water transfers from the Yellow River and from reservoirs in Shanxi and Hebei have been used to restore flows, and in 2021 the Yongding flowed along its full length to the sea for the first time in 26 years. Beijing has developed riverfront parks and a "Yongding River cultural belt" in its western districts.
