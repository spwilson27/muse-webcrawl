The 2002 Commonwealth Games, officially the XVII Commonwealth Games, were held in Manchester, England, from 25 July to 4 August 2002. They were the largest multi-sport event ever held in the United Kingdom up to that time, surpassing even the 1948 London Olympics in the number of participating nations, and formed part of the celebrations of the Golden Jubilee of Queen Elizabeth II.

Highlights

Australia topped the medal table with 207 medals, followed by England with 165. The Games featured the first full programme of events for elite athletes with a disability integrated into a major international multi-sport event. Memorable moments included swimmer Ian Thorpe's seven medals, England's dramatic rugby sevens and hockey performances being eclipsed by Australia, and Debbie Flood and others in a strong home showing; Paula Radcliffe won the 5000 metres before a home crowd. The closing ceremony was famously marred by heavy rain.

Legacy

The Games are widely credited with regenerating east Manchester: the City of Manchester Stadium (now the Etihad Stadium) became the home of Manchester City F.C., the National Cycling Centre velodrome and other venues anchored Sportcity. The event is regarded as a model for Manchester's later sporting and cultural development and helped restore Britain's credibility as a host of major events, paving the way for London's successful 2012 Olympic bid.
