NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt

The NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt, often abbreviated to the AIDS Memorial Quilt or AIDS Quilt, is an enormous memorial and work of folk art commemorating people who have died of AIDS-related causes. It consists of tens of thousands of individual fabric panels, each 3 feet (0.91 m) by 6 feet (1.83 m) — approximately the size of a grave — decorated by friends, lovers and family members in memory of a person lost to the epidemic. The Quilt weighs an estimated 54 tons and is considered the largest community folk-art project in the world.

Origins

The Quilt was conceived in November 1985 by gay rights activist Cleve Jones in San Francisco, after he and others taped placards bearing the names of people lost to AIDS onto the walls of the San Francisco Federal Building during a candlelight march. The sight reminded Jones of a patchwork quilt. In 1987 Jones and Mike Smith co-founded the NAMES Project Foundation, and volunteers began sewing panels in a San Francisco workshop.

Displays

The Quilt was first displayed on the National Mall in Washington, D.C., on 11 October 1987 during the Second National March on Washington for Lesbian and Gay Rights, when it covered an area larger than a football field with 1,920 panels, and names were read aloud throughout the day. It toured North America in 1988, raising funds for local AIDS service organisations. By its last full display on the Mall in 1996, the Quilt covered the entire Mall. Portions continue to be exhibited, and the whole Quilt is displayed digitally through interactive archives.

Cultural significance

The Quilt transformed private grief into public, large-scale political art, helping humanise the epidemic during years when many deaths went unacknowledged. It inspired the Academy Award-winning documentary Common Threads: Stories from the Quilt (1989) and quilting memorials in many countries. In 2019, stewardship of the Quilt transferred to the National AIDS Memorial in San Francisco, with its archival materials held by the Library of Congress. The NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize in 1989.
