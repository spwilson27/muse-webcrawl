Suzhou numerals

The Suzhou numerals, also known as Suzhou mazi or huama, are a Chinese numeral system formerly used in China in commerce and bookkeeping. They are derived from the ancient rod numerals and were used for quickly writing prices and quantities because they could be written much faster than formal Chinese characters.

Suzhou numerals
TypeDecimal positional numeral system
OriginChina, derived from rod numerals
UseCommerce, markets, bookkeeping

Characteristics

The system is decimal and positional. Digits one through nine have cursive forms derived from rod numeral shapes; special compact forms for two, three and five exist to avoid ambiguity in vertical arrangements. A place indicator character could follow the digits to denote units such as tens or hundreds.

History and decline

Suzhou numerals were in wide use in markets, pharmacies, tea houses and on bills well into the twentieth century, and could still be seen in Hong Kong and Macau markets. They have largely been replaced by Arabic numerals in everyday use.
