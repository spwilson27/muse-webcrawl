Battle of Huế

Date31 January – 2 March 1968
LocationHuế, South Vietnam
ResultU.S. and South Vietnamese victory; heavy civilian losses
ContextTet Offensive, Vietnam War

The Battle of Huế was one of the longest and bloodiest battles of the Vietnam War, fought between 31 January and 2 March 1968 during the Tet Offensive. North Vietnamese Army and Viet Cong forces seized much of Huế, the former imperial capital, and were expelled only after weeks of intense urban combat by U.S. Marines, U.S. Army, and South Vietnamese (ARVN) forces.

Battle

Communist forces overran most of the city in the opening hours of Tet, hoisting their flag over the Citadel. Initial U.S. rules of engagement restricted artillery and air support to protect the historic city and civilians, but restrictions were progressively lifted as fighting intensified house-to-house. The Citadel was retaken by ARVN troops by late February, and organized resistance ended in early March.

Massacre at Huế

During the occupation, communist forces carried out mass killings of civilians, officials, and others deemed enemies; mass graves discovered afterwards contained thousands of bodies. Estimates of civilians executed range from about 2,800 to 6,000. The scale and responsibility for the killings remain subjects of historical and political debate.

Significance

The battle destroyed much of Huế, killed thousands of civilians, and — broadcast widely in the United States — deepened American public disillusionment with the war despite the tactical victory.
