The Lockheed F-117 Nighthawk is a retired American single-seat, subsonic twin-engine stealth attack aircraft developed by Lockheed's secretive Skunk Works division. It was the world's first operational aircraft designed around stealth technology, using a faceted airframe that scattered radar waves, radar-absorbent materials and shielded, non-afterburning engines. Despite the "F" designation, the F-117 carried no air-to-air armament and operated as a precision ground-attack aircraft, typically delivering laser-guided bombs from internal bays.

Development

The aircraft grew out of the experimental Have Blue demonstrators of the late 1970s, themselves based on Soviet radar-theory research by Pyotr Ufimtsev. The F-117 first flew at Groom Lake (Area 51) on 18 June 1981, flown by Hal Farley. It reached initial operating capability in October 1983 with the 4450th Tactical Group at Tonopah Test Range Airport, Nevada, flying only at night to preserve secrecy. The programme's existence was not publicly acknowledged until November 1988, when the Air Force released a grainy photograph.

Operational history

The F-117 made its combat debut in Panama in 1989 (Operation Just Cause) and came to public prominence during the 1991 Gulf War, when Nighthawks struck key targets in Baghdad on the opening night of Operation Desert Storm. It later served over Iraq and in the Kosovo War in 1999, when one aircraft (82-0806) was shot down by a Yugoslav S-125 surface-to-air missile — the only F-117 lost in combat; its wreckage reportedly attracted foreign intelligence interest. The type was formally retired on 22 April 2008 as precision-strike duties passed to the F-22, F-35 and other platforms, although demilitarised examples have continued to fly in research and aggressor roles.

Characteristics

The F-117 is powered by two General Electric F404-F1D2 turbofans without afterburners, giving a maximum speed of about Mach 0.92. Its faceted shape made it aerodynamically unstable, requiring quadruplex fly-by-wire controls adapted from the F-16. It carried up to about 2,300 kg (5,100 lb) of ordnance in two internal bays, typically two GBU-series laser-guided bombs, and had a crew of one.
