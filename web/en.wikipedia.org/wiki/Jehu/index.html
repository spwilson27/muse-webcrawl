Jehu (Hebrew: יֵהוּא, romanized: Yēhūʾ, meaning "Jehovah is He") was the tenth king of the northern Kingdom of Israel since Jeroboam I, noted for exterminating the house of Ahab and ending Baal worship in Israel. He was the son of Jehoshaphat, grandson of Nimshi, and possibly a great-grandson of Omri. His reign is conventionally dated from about 841 to 814 BC.

Coup d'état

According to the biblical account in 2 Kings 9, Jehu was a commander in the army of King Jehoram at Ramoth-Gilead. A disciple of the prophet Elisha was sent to anoint Jehu king and charge him with destroying the house of Ahab. Jehu drove his chariot "furiously" to Jezreel, where he killed King Jehoram of Israel and King Ahaziah of Judah, and ordered Queen Jezebel thrown from a palace window to her death.

Purge of the house of Ahab

Jehu went on to massacre the seventy sons of Ahab in Samaria, then slaughtered the worshippers of Baal in Samaria after luring them into the god's temple on the pretext of a great sacrifice. He destroyed the temple of Baal and its sacred pillar. The Deuteronomistic history approves of his extirpation of Baal worship but condemns him for retaining the golden calves at Bethel and Dan.

Foreign relations

The Black Obelisk of the Assyrian king Shalmaneser III depicts Jehu (or his envoy) prostrating himself and paying tribute in about 841 BC — the earliest known depiction of an Israelite king. Later in his reign Jehu lost territory east of the Jordan to Hazael of Aram-Damascus.

Dynasty

Jehu founded the longest-lasting dynasty of the northern kingdom: his descendants Jehoahaz, Jehoash, Jeroboam II and Zechariah ruled Israel for four generations, as the biblical text had promised.
