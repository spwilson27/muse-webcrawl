Afro-Eurasia (also spelled Afrasia or Eurafrasia) is the largest contiguous landmass on Earth, formed by the connected continents of Africa, Europe and Asia. It covers about 85 million square kilometres — roughly 57% of the world's land area — and is home to about 85% of the world's population. In historical and biogeographical usage it corresponds broadly to the "Old World", in contrast to the Americas, the "New World".

Terminology and divisions

Africa and Eurasia are physically joined at the Isthmus of Suez in Egypt (separated artificially by the Suez Canal), while Europe and Asia form the single landmass of Eurasia, conventionally divided along the Ural Mountains, the Ural River, the Caucasus and the straits of the Bosporus and Dardanelles. Geographer Halford Mackinder called Afro-Eurasia the "World Island" in his Heartland theory of geopolitics.

Biogeography and human history

Afro-Eurasia is the ancestral home of humankind: Homo sapiens evolved in Africa and dispersed across the landmass before reaching the Americas and Australasia. Its shared land connections allowed the spread of crops, domesticated animals, technologies and diseases among its regions — the network later linked by routes such as the Silk Road and trans-Saharan trade — giving Afro-Eurasian societies a connected history distinct from the isolated worlds of the Americas and Oceania.
