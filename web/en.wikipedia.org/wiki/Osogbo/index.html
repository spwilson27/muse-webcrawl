Osogbo (also spelled Oṣogbo) is a city in southwestern Nigeria and the capital of Osun State. It lies on the Osun River, roughly 88 kilometres northeast of Ibadan, and is an important centre of Yoruba culture, famous for the Osun-Osogbo Festival and the Sacred Grove dedicated to the river goddess Osun.

History

According to Yoruba tradition, Osogbo was founded several centuries ago by hunters from Ipole, led by figures remembered as Olu-Timehin and Larooye, who settled by the river after making a pact with the goddess Osun. The settlement grew as a market town and became a refuge for people fleeing the Fulani wars of the nineteenth century; its population swelled after the fall of old Oyo, and the town repelled Fulani attacks in the 1840s.

Under British rule Osogbo became a railway and commercial centre; the railway from Lagos reached the town in 1907. It was made capital of Osun State when the state was created in 1991.

Culture

Osogbo is a major centre of Yoruba art. In the 1960s the Austrian artist Susanne Wenger (Adunni Olorisha) settled in the town and, with local artists, created the monumental sculptures and shrines of the New Sacred Art movement inside the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove. The grove, a dense high forest along the river, is one of the last remnants of primary forest in southern Nigeria and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2005.

The annual Osun-Osogbo Festival, held each August, draws devotees and tourists from around the world; its climax is the procession of a young votary, the Arugba, carrying a sacred calabash of offerings to the river. The city is also known for indigo dyeing, the Ataoja's palace, and art schools and galleries that grew out of the Mbari Mbayo movement associated with Ulli Beier and Duro Ladipo.

Economy and transport

Osogbo is a trading centre for yam, cassava, grain, and tobacco from the surrounding farmland, with industries including textiles, food processing, and a steel rolling mill. It sits on the western railway line from Lagos to Kano and on highways linking Ibadan, Ilorin, and Akure.
