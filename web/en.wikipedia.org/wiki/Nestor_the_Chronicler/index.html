Saint Nestor the Chronicler (c. 1056 – c. 1114) was a monk of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra (Monastery of the Caves) in Kievan Rus', traditionally credited as the author of the Primary Chronicle (Tale of Bygone Years), the fundamental source for the early history of the East Slavs. He also wrote a life of St Theodosius of Kyiv and an account of the martyrdom of the princes Boris and Gleb.

Life

Nestor entered the Monastery of the Caves at about seventeen, during the abbacy of St Theodosius. He was ordained a hierodeacon and devoted himself to literary work. He took part in the discovery and translation of the relics of Theodosius in 1091. His relics rest in the Near Caves of the Kyiv Lavra, and he is commemorated on 27 October in the Orthodox calendar.

The Primary Chronicle

The Primary Chronicle, compiled around 1113, traces the history of Rus' from biblical times and the legendary founding of Kyiv through the reigns of the Rurikid princes to the early 12th century. It preserves famous narratives such as the calling of the Varangians, the baptism of Rus' under Vladimir the Great in 988, and Olga of Kyiv's revenge on the Drevlians. Modern scholars debate the extent of Nestor's personal authorship: the chronicle survives in later redactions, principally the Laurentian (1377) and Hypatian (c. 1425) codices, and likely incorporates earlier annalistic compilations.
