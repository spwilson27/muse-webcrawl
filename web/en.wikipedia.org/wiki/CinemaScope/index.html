CinemaScope

The CinemaScope trademark

CinemaScope is an anamorphic lens series used, from 1953 to 1967, and less often later, for shooting widescreen films that could be screened in theatres using existing equipment with only the addition of a special projection lens. Its creation in 1953 by Spyros P. Skouras, president of 20th Century Fox, marked the beginning of the modern anamorphic format in filmmaking, with an aspect ratio of 2.55:1, later 2.39:1 (commonly called 2.40:1).

History

Fox acquired the rights to Henri Chrétien's anamorphic Hypergonar lens system, developed in France in the 1920s. The first film released in CinemaScope was The Robe (1953), a biblical epic whose success prompted other studios to adopt widescreen processes to compete with television. Fox licensed the system widely, and for a time films were advertised with the slogan "in CinemaScope".

Technology

The system squeezed a wide image horizontally onto standard 35mm film during photography and unsqueezed it during projection. Early releases also carried four-track magnetic stereo sound. Competing and successor systems, notably Panavision, gradually displaced CinemaScope lenses, and Fox itself switched to Panavision in the late 1960s, though the name persisted in credits for decades.
