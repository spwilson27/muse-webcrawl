Alois Senefelder, inventor of lithography.

Johann Alois Senefelder (6 November 1771 – 26 February 1834) was a German actor and playwright who invented the printing technique of lithography in the 1790s. His invention, initially called "chemical printing" or "stone printing," became the first fundamentally new printing process since the invention of relief printing and later developed into offset printing, the most widely used printing method of the modern era.

Early life

Senefelder was born in Prague, where his father, an actor at the Royal Theatre, was appearing on stage. He studied law at Ingolstadt but was forced to abandon his studies on his father's death and followed the family profession onto the stage. Writing plays himself, he sought an inexpensive way to publish his own work after the success of one of his plays, and his search for a cheap reproduction method led him to experiment with printing.

Invention of lithography

Around 1796, Senefelder discovered by accident that a design drawn with a greasy crayon on finely polished limestone, when the stone was treated with gum arabic and water and then inked, would transfer the image to paper, since oil-based ink adhered only to the greasy drawing while the wet stone repelled it. Lithography is thus a planographic process, printing from a flat surface, based on the immiscibility of oil and water. He obtained a patent and formed partnerships to exploit the technique, first for printing music and later for art reproduction and commercial work.

Later life and legacy

Senefelder published a full account of his method in his textbook Vollständiges Lehrbuch der Steindruckerey (1818; English translation, A Complete Course of Lithography, 1819). The process spread rapidly through Europe, was embraced by artists such as Goya, Delacroix, and Daumier, and became the dominant method for printing maps, music, posters, and illustrations in the nineteenth century. Senefelder died in Munich in 1834 and is honoured there with a monument; the Senefelderplatz in Berlin is named after him.
