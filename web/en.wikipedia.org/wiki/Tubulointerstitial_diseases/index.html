Tubulointerstitial diseases

Tubulointerstitial diseases (also called tubulointerstitial nephritis or interstitial nephritis) are a group of kidney diseases in which inflammation and injury primarily affect the renal tubules and the interstitium — the tissue between the tubules — rather than the glomeruli or blood vessels. They may be acute or chronic, and causes include drugs, infections, autoimmune diseases, and metabolic disorders.

Acute interstitial nephritis

Acute interstitial nephritis (AIN) most often results from a hypersensitivity reaction to a medication. Classic culprits include β-lactam antibiotics, other antibiotics, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), and proton-pump inhibitors. The classic triad of fever, rash and eosinophilia occurs in only a minority of patients. Presentation is usually an acute rise in serum creatinine with white cells, white cell casts and sometimes eosinophils in the urine; kidney biopsy shows interstitial oedema with an infiltrate of lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils and sometimes granulomas. Treatment centres on stopping the offending drug, with corticosteroids often used when kidney function does not recover promptly.

Infectious causes of acute tubulointerstitial disease include bacterial pyelonephritis, leptospirosis, hantavirus infection, and viral infections such as cytomegalovirus and Epstein–Barr virus.

Chronic tubulointerstitial disease

Chronic forms develop insidiously and progress to interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy, eventually causing chronic kidney disease. Causes include:

  • Prolonged analgesic use (historically, analgesic nephropathy from phenacetin-containing mixtures)
  • Heavy metals such as lead and cadmium
  • Metabolic disorders: chronic hypokalaemia, hypercalcaemia, hyperuricaemia
  • Obstructive uropathy and vesicoureteric reflux
  • Autoimmune diseases such as Sjögren's syndrome and sarcoidosis; IgG4-related disease is a recognised cause
  • Balkan endemic nephropathy and aristolochic acid nephropathy, caused by exposure to aristolochic acid in herbal remedies and contaminated grain
  • Inherited disorders, including autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease (ADTKD) caused by mutations in genes such as UMOD and MUC1

Because the tubules are the primary site of damage, chronic tubulointerstitial disease produces prominent tubular dysfunction: impaired urinary concentrating ability (polyuria, nocturia), renal tubular acidosis, and salt wasting, with relatively bland urine sediment and modest proteinuria (usually under 1 g/day), distinguishing it clinically from glomerular disease.

Diagnosis and prognosis

Diagnosis rests on the clinical history (especially drug exposure), laboratory findings, imaging that often shows normal or enlarged kidneys in acute disease and small shrunken kidneys in chronic disease, and confirmation by kidney biopsy when the diagnosis is uncertain. Acute disease often recovers, at least partially, especially if the cause is removed early; chronic disease tends to progress.
