Phonetic transcription is the visual representation of speech sounds by means of written symbols. Ordinary orthographies represent sounds inconsistently (compare the English spellings ough in through, though, tough and thorough), so linguists, lexicographers, language teachers and speech therapists use dedicated systems in which each symbol corresponds consistently to one sound.

The International Phonetic Alphabet

The most widely used system is the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA), developed from 1888 by the International Phonetic Association. The IPA provides symbols for consonants and vowels found in the world's languages, organised by place and manner of articulation, plus diacritics and suprasegmental marks for features such as aspiration, nasalisation, tone, stress and length. It is the standard notation in dictionaries for showing pronunciation — for example, English phonetic is transcribed /fəˈnɛtɪk/.

Broad and narrow transcription

Transcription may be broad (phonemic), capturing only the sound distinctions that a language uses to differentiate words — written between slashes, /…/ — or narrow (phonetic in the strict sense), recording fine detail of articulation and context-dependent variation, written between square brackets, […]. The English phoneme /p/, for instance, is aspirated [pʰ] at the start of pin but unaspirated [p] in spin. Other systems include the Americanist phonetic notation used for Indigenous American languages and various respelling systems used in general dictionaries.
