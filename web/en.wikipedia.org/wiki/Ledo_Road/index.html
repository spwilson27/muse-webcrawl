Ledo Road

Ledo Road
Length: 1,736 km (1,079 mi)
Built: December 1942 – January 1945
From: Ledo, Assam, India
To: Kunming, China

The Ledo Road (Burmese: လီဒိုလမ်းမ) was a wartime supply road built during World War II connecting Ledo in Assam, India, with Kunming in Yunnan, China, via northern Burma. It was constructed by the Allies after the Japanese occupation of Burma in 1942 closed the Burma Road, severing the overland supply route to the Republic of China. At a ceremony in January 1945, Chiang Kai-shek proposed renaming it the Stilwell Road, in honour of General Joseph Stilwell.

Construction

Construction began in December 1942 under the direction of American engineers, with some 15,000 American troops—about 60% of them African American—and 35,000 local workers. The road crossed the Patkai hills, dense jungle, and steep mountains of the Hukawng and Mogaung valleys, running near the front line of the Burma campaign. It was often called "the man-a-mile road" for the workers who died building it; more than 1,100 Americans died during construction. Bridges, culverts, and an oil pipeline were built alongside the route.

Use and legacy

The first convoy reached Kunming in February 1945. By then, air transport over "the Hump" of the Himalayas was delivering far more tonnage, and the road's strategic value was contested. After the war the road fell into disrepair; long stretches in Myanmar were impassable for decades, though parts in India and China remain in use and sections have been rebuilt amid renewed trade interest.
