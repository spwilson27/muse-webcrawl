Michael VIII Palaiologos (Greek: Μιχαὴλ Δούκας Ἄγγελος Κομνηνὸς Παλαιολόγος; c. 1223 – 11 December 1282) was the founder of the Palaiologan dynasty that would rule the Byzantine Empire until its fall in 1453. He recovered Constantinople from the Latin Empire in 1261 and re-established the empire in its capital.

Rise to power

A successful general of the Empire of Nicaea, Michael became regent and then co-emperor for the young John IV Laskaris, whom he subsequently had blinded and deposed — an act that earned him excommunication by Patriarch Arsenios and a lasting schism. His general Alexios Strategopoulos retook Constantinople on 25 July 1261, largely unopposed, and Michael was crowned in the Hagia Sophia.

Reign

Michael rebuilt the capital and its defences, restored the Byzantine navy, and pursued an energetic diplomacy to hold off Charles of Anjou's planned crusade against him. At the Second Council of Lyon in 1274 he accepted a union of the Greek and Latin churches, a policy deeply unpopular at home and later repudiated. He died on campaign in Thrace in 1282.
