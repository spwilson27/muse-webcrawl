MV Africa Mercy is a 152-metre hospital ship operated by the international charity Mercy Ships. Converted from the Danish rail ferry Dronning Ingrid, she provides free surgical care, medical training, and community health education in developing nations, primarily along the coast of sub-Saharan Africa. When she entered service in 2007 she was the world's largest non-governmental hospital ship.

History

The vessel was built in Denmark in 1980 as the train ferry Dronning Ingrid for Danish State Railways. Mercy Ships purchased her in 1999, and she underwent an extensive conversion in England and elsewhere, during which the former rail decks were rebuilt into a hospital with operating theatres, wards, a dental clinic, laboratory, and radiology facilities. Renamed Africa Mercy, she began her first field service in Liberia in 2007.

Facilities and work

The ship has five operating rooms, recovery and intensive-care wards, and beds for about 80 patients, with volunteer medical and support crew from dozens of nations. Field services, typically lasting about ten months in a host port, focus on surgeries such as maxillofacial repair, cleft lip and palate, cataract removal, orthopaedics, obstetric fistula repair, and general surgery, alongside mentoring and training programmes for local health professionals. In 2022 she was joined in service by the purpose-built Global Mercy, which became the fleet's flagship.
