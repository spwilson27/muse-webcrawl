Puʻu ʻŌʻō (often written Puu Oo) is a volcanic cone in the eastern rift zone of the Kīlauea volcano on the island of Hawaiʻi. Its eruption, which began on 3 January 1983, became one of the longest-lived rift-zone eruptions of the past two centuries, lasting 35 years.

Eruption

The eruption began with fissures opening in the forest east of Kīlauea's summit. Lava fountaining built the Puʻu ʻŌʻō cone, and later flows travelled to the coast, destroying communities including Kalapana and entering the ocean for extended periods. The eruption ended in 2018 following a major collapse at the cone and the much larger lower Puna eruption that year.

Significance

Over its lifetime the eruption added hundreds of acres of new land to the island and produced an enormous volume of lava, monitored continuously by the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory.
