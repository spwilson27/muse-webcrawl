WHIP

This article is about the baseball statistic. In baseball statistics, walks plus hits per inning pitched (WHIP) is a sabermetric measurement of the number of baserunners a pitcher has allowed per inning pitched. It is calculated by adding the number of walks and hits allowed and dividing by the number of innings pitched.

Formula(Walks + Hits) / Innings pitched
Coined byDaniel Okrent (1979)
MeasuresBaserunners allowed per inning

History

The statistic was invented in 1979 by writer Daniel Okrent, one of the founders of rotisserie (fantasy) baseball, who originally called it "innings pitched ratio". The letters WHIP came to stand for walks plus hits per inning pitched. It has since become one of the most commonly used measures of a pitcher's ability to keep runners off the bases.

Interpretation

A WHIP around 1.30 is roughly league average; 1.10 or lower is excellent, and the lowest career marks belong to pitchers such as Addie Joss and Mariano Rivera (about 1.00). Unlike ERA, WHIP does not directly measure runs, and it treats all hits equally regardless of type.
