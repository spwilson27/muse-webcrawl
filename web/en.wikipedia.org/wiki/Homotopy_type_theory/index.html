FieldMathematical logic, foundations of mathematics

BookHomotopy Type Theory: Univalent Foundations of Mathematics (2013)

Homotopy type theory (HoTT) is a branch of mathematics that combines aspects of homotopy theory and type theory. It interprets types as spaces (or ∞-groupoids) and equalities between terms as paths between points, and it forms part of the univalent foundations program initiated by Vladimir Voevodsky.

Key ideas

In HoTT, a proof that two elements are equal is itself data—a path—which may have higher paths between paths, generating a rich higher-dimensional structure. The univalence axiom states that equivalent types are equal, allowing mathematical structures to be transported along equivalences. Function extensionality and propositional resizing follow from or accompany these principles.

Development

The subject crystallized around 2006 from work by Voevodsky on the homotopy interpretation of type theory and the groupoid model of Hofmann and Streicher. A special year at the Institute for Advanced Study in 2012–13 produced the collaborative 600-page textbook, written openly by dozens of participants. HoTT is implemented in proof assistants such as Coq and Agda, and cubical type theories give the axioms computational meaning.
