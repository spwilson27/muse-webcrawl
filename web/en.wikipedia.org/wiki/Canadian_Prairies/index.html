Canadian Prairies

The Canadian Prairies (usually referred to as simply the Prairies in Canada) is a region in Western Canada comprising the provinces of Alberta, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba. The region is largely characterized by a flat plain landscape, fertile soils, and a continental climate with cold winters and warm summers. The Prairies cover an area of roughly 1.9 million square kilometres and form the northern extension of the Great Plains of North America.

Geography and climate

The land rises in three broad steps from east to west: the Manitoba Lowlands, the Saskatchewan Plain, and the Alberta Plateau, culminating in the foothills of the Rocky Mountains. Vegetation ranges from tallgrass prairie in the Red River Valley through mixed-grass prairie to fescue grassland, with aspen parkland forming a transition zone to the boreal forest in the north. The climate is continental and semi-arid in the south-western portion known as Palliser's Triangle, with chinook winds occasionally raising winter temperatures sharply in Alberta.

Economy

Agriculture dominates the region's identity: the Prairies produce most of Canada's wheat, canola, barley, and oats, earning the region the nickname "the world's breadbasket." Saskatchewan is a world-leading producer of potash, Alberta is the centre of Canada's petroleum industry, with the Athabasca oil sands among the largest oil reserves on Earth, and the region holds significant deposits of uranium, natural gas, and coal.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Cree, Assiniboine, Blackfoot, and Sioux, inhabited the plains for millennia, sustained by bison hunting. The fur trade brought European contact, and after Confederation the region was transformed by the transcontinental railway (completed 1885), the Dominion Lands Act, and mass immigration from Britain, the United States, and Eastern Europe. The "Dirty Thirties" drought devastated Prairie agriculture during the Great Depression, prompting large-scale soil conservation and water projects. The major cities are Calgary, Edmonton, Winnipeg, Saskatoon, and Regina.
