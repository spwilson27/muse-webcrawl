Jean Bolland (13 August 1596 – 12 September 1665) was a Flemish Jesuit priest and a prominent hagiographer. He is best known as the founder of the Bollandists, the society of scholars dedicated to the critical study of the lives of the saints, and as the originator of the Acta Sanctorum.

Work

Bolland took up the hagiographical project begun by Heribert Rosweyde, who had planned a comprehensive, critically edited collection of saints' lives. Bolland expanded the scope enormously, organizing the material by the saints' feast days in the calendar year.

With the assistance of Godefridus Henschenius, Bolland published the first two volumes of the Acta Sanctorum in 1643, covering the saints of January. The work introduced rigorous source criticism to hagiography, distinguishing documented fact from legend. The Bollandist society, named after him, continues its work in Brussels to this day.
