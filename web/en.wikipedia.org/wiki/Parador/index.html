Parador

A parador occupying a converted historic building in Spain.

A parador (Spanish: paradores de turismo) is a kind of luxury hotel, usually located in a converted historic building such as a monastery, castle, palace or convent, or in a modern building in a place of particular beauty, in Spain and Portugal. The name derives from the Spanish verb parar, meaning to stop or halt, reflecting their original purpose as stopping places for travellers.

History

The concept was developed in the 1920s under the government of Miguel Primo de Rivera, with the aim of promoting tourism in Spain and preserving historic buildings that might otherwise fall into ruin. The first parador, the Parador de Gredos in the Sierra de Gredos, was inaugurated by King Alfonso XIII in 1928. The network is operated by Paradores de Turismo de España, a state-owned company founded in 1928.

The network

There are nearly one hundred paradors across Spain, from Galicia and the Basque Country in the north to Andalusia in the south, as well as in the Canary Islands and the Spanish territories in North Africa. In Portugal, a similar network, the Pousadas de Portugal, was created in the 1940s and was later privatised. Many paradors are located in buildings of great historical significance, including castles associated with the Reconquista, former monasteries, and Renaissance palaces.

Characteristics

Paradors are generally classified as four- or five-star hotels. They are known for their regional gastronomy, with restaurants serving traditional dishes of the area in which each parador is located. The network is also promoted as a means of distributing tourism income to less-visited regions and encouraging the conservation of Spain's architectural heritage.
