Racial classification of Indian Americans

Indian Americans have been classified under a number of different racial and ethnic categories in the United States, and the classification has changed repeatedly over time in census practice and in law.

History

In the early twentieth century, Indian immigrants' eligibility for naturalized citizenship turned on whether they counted as "free white persons". In United States v. Bhagat Singh Thind (1923), the Supreme Court held that although Indians might be "Caucasian" in the anthropological usage of the time, they were not white "in accordance with the understanding of the common man", and were therefore ineligible for citizenship; many previously naturalized Indians were denaturalized. The Luce–Celler Act of 1946 restored naturalization rights.

Census classification

Censuses variously recorded Indians as "Hindu" (used generically for Indians of all religions from 1920 to 1940), as "other race", and later as white. Following lobbying by Indian American organisations, the 1980 census classified Asian Indians under the new "Asian or Pacific Islander" umbrella, and current standards count them within the Asian category. In some contexts — for example affirmative action and minority business programs — the classification remains debated, and community debates continue over whether "Asian" grouping serves Indian Americans' interests.
