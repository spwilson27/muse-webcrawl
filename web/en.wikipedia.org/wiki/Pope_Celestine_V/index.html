Pope Celestine V

Pope Celestine V (Latin: Caelestinus V; 1209/1215 – 19 May 1296), born Pietro Angelerio (known as Pietro da Morrone), was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States for five months from 5 July to 13 December 1294, when he resigned. He was also a monk and hermit who founded the order of the Celestines as a branch of the Benedictine order.

Papacy

Elected after a deadlock in the papal conclave lasting more than two years, the elderly hermit was persuaded to accept the papacy in the hope of restoring spiritual fervour. Finding himself ill-suited to the office and manipulated by secular rulers, he issued a decree confirming a pope's right to resign and then abdicated — the only pope to do so voluntarily until Benedict XVI in 2013.

Aftermath

His successor, Boniface VIII, had him imprisoned in the castle of Fumone, where he died in 1296. Celestine was canonized in 1313. Dante's Inferno is often read as condemning "him who made, through cowardice, the great refusal", commonly identified with Celestine V.
