JR East

East Japan Railway Company

Native name: 東日本旅客鉄道株式会社

Founded: April 1, 1987

Headquarters: Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan

Area served: Kanto, Tohoku, and parts of Chubu

Daily ridership: about 13 million (pre-pandemic)

The East Japan Railway Company (東日本旅客鉄道株式会社, Higashi-Nihon Ryokaku Tetsudō Kabushiki-gaisha), known as JR East, is a Japanese passenger railway company and the largest of the seven companies in the Japan Railways Group. It operates trains throughout eastern Honshu, including the Greater Tokyo Area and the Tohoku region.

History

JR East was created on April 1, 1987, when the government-owned Japanese National Railways (JNR) was split and privatized into regional companies. Unlike the island companies, JR East was profitable from the start thanks to the dense Tokyo commuter market. It was fully divested by the government in 2002 and is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

Network

JR East's network covers roughly 7,400 km of track. Its high-speed lines — the Tohoku, Joetsu, Hokuriku (to Tsuruga in cooperation with JR West), Yamagata, and Akita Shinkansen — radiate from Tokyo to northern Honshu. The company operates flagship services such as the Hayabusa to Shin-Aomori and Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto (via the Seikan Tunnel). In Tokyo it runs the Yamanote loop line, the Chuo, Sobu, Saikyo, Keihin-Tohoku, and many other commuter lines, and it manages Tokyo Station, Shinjuku — among the world's busiest stations — and other major terminals.

Services and business

Beyond rail, JR East earns substantial revenue from station retail ("eki-naka"), hotels, and real estate around its terminals. It issues the Suica contactless smart card, interoperable with transit across Japan, and has developed advanced trains including the ALFA-X test Shinkansen, which has run at speeds up to 400 km/h in trials. JR East also supplies expertise abroad, consulting on railway projects in Asia and elsewhere.
