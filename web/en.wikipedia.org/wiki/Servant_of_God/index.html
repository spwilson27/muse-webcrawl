Servant of God

Servant of God is a title used in the Roman Catholic Church to indicate that an individual is on the first step toward possible canonization as a saint. The title is granted once a diocesan investigation into the person's life and virtues has been opened and accepted by the Holy See.

Process

The process generally begins at the diocesan level, where a bishop, after consulting the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, opens a cause for beatification and canonization. The candidate is then declared a Servant of God. Evidence of heroic virtue is gathered; if approved by the Pope, the person is declared Venerable. A verified miracle attributed to the candidate's intercession leads to beatification, after which the person is called Blessed, and a second miracle normally leads to canonization as a saint.

Usage in other traditions

The phrase "servant of God" is also a descriptive biblical expression, applied in the Hebrew Bible to figures such as Moses, and in Christian tradition to devoted believers generally. In Islam, the name Abdullah, borne by many men, literally means "servant of God".
