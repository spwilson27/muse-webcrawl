Steve Perry

Stephen Ray Perry (born January 22, 1949) is an American singer and songwriter. He was the lead singer and frontman of the rock band Journey during their most commercially successful periods from 1977 to 1987, and in a partial reunion from 1995 to 1998. During that time, Perry also had a successful solo career, scoring hits with "Oh Sherrie" and "Foolish Heart".

Early life

Perry was born in Hanford, California, to Portuguese parents from the Azores. He grew up in the San Joaquin Valley and developed an interest in music after hearing Sam Cooke's "Cupid" as a child. He played drums in school bands and attended the College of the Sequoias in Visalia before pursuing music professionally, spending years in unsigned bands including the Ice and Pieces.

Journey

In 1977 Perry joined Journey, a San Francisco band that had begun as a jazz-fusion/progressive rock act. His soaring tenor — sometimes described as "The Voice" — reshaped the band toward melodic arena rock. With Perry, Journey released a string of platinum albums including Infinity (1978), Evolution (1979), Departure (1980), Escape (1981), and Frontiers (1983). Signature songs include "Lights", "Any Way You Want It", "Open Arms", "Faithfully", and "Don't Stop Believin'", which became one of the most downloaded songs of the 20th century. Perry co-wrote most of the band's hits with keyboardist Jonathan Cain and guitarist Neal Schon.

Solo career

Perry's debut solo album, Street Talk (1984), produced the top-five single "Oh Sherrie", written about his then-girlfriend Sherrie Swafford. He contributed "Foolish Heart" and appeared on the 1985 charity single "We Are the World". His second solo album, For the Love of Strange Medicine (1994), followed a period away from the industry. After rejoining Journey for Trial by Fire (1996), Perry left the band in 1998 following a hip injury and a decision not to undergo surgery on the band's touring timetable.

Later years

Perry largely withdrew from public performance for two decades, though he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with Journey in 2017. In 2018 he returned with the solo album Traces, his first in 24 years, inspired in part by the death of his partner Kellie Nash. A Christmas album, The Season, followed in 2021.

Vocal style

Perry's voice is a high tenor with an unusually smooth, melismatic delivery influenced by soul singers such as Sam Cooke, Marvin Gaye, and Jackie Wilson. Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest singers of all time, and his phrasing and range have made him one of rock's most imitated vocalists.
