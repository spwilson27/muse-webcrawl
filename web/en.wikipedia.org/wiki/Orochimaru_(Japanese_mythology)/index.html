Orochimaru (Japanese mythology)

Orochimaru (大蛇丸) is a character of Japanese folklore, best known as the arch-enemy of the gallant ninja hero Jiraiya in the tale Jiraiya Gōketsu Monogatari ("The Tale of the Gallant Jiraiya"), which developed through 19th-century serialised fiction, kabuki theatre and kamishibai storytelling. His name combines orochi (大蛇, "great serpent") with the suffix -maru, and he is the master of snake magic.

The tale

In the classic version of the story, Orochimaru was once a follower of Jiraiya — in some versions a young retainer named Yashagorō — who became corrupted by serpent magic. Possessed or transformed by a great snake, he gains the power to command serpents and becomes Jiraiya's nemesis. Their rivalry is structured around a magical "rock–paper–scissors" of animal magic: snake defeats frog, frog defeats slug, and slug defeats snake. Jiraiya commands frog magic and his wife Tsunade commands slug magic; only by combining their powers — and with the help of a special sword — can Orochimaru finally be defeated, after which he is sometimes spared and reconciled.

Origins and related figures

The character draws on older serpent lore, notably Yamata no Orochi, the eight-headed serpent slain by the god Susanoo in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki. The Jiraiya tales were popularised in a 43-installment novel (1839–1868) written by several authors and in kabuki adaptations, fixing the Jiraiya–Tsunade–Orochimaru trio in Japanese popular culture.

In popular culture

Orochimaru appears widely in modern media. He is a principal villain in the manga and anime Naruto, where the three legendary Sannin are named for the folklore trio. He also features in video games and other retellings, including the Samurai Shodown and Ōkami universes, in the latter loosely via the multi-headed Orochi.
