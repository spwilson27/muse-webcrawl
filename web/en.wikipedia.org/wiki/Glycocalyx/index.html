Glycocalyx

The glycocalyx (plural glycocalyces or glycocalyxes), also known as the pericellular matrix and sometime cell coat, is a glycoprotein and glycolipid covering which surrounds the cell membranes of bacteria, epithelial cells, and other cells. It was described in a review article in 1970.

In vascular endothelial tissue, the glycocalyx lines the luminal surface of blood vessels, where it acts as a mechanotransducer of fluid shear forces, a regulator of vascular permeability and a barrier to leucocyte adhesion. Its principal constituents include membrane-bound proteoglycans such as syndecans and glypicans, glycosaminoglycan chains such as heparan sulfate and hyaluronan, and adsorbed plasma proteins.

Damage or shedding of the endothelial glycocalyx is implicated in inflammation, sepsis, diabetes and atherosclerosis. In bacteria, the glycocalyx may form a loosely attached slime layer or a firmly attached capsule that helps evade phagocytosis.
