Wolf Prize in Mathematics

The Wolf Prize in Mathematics is an international award granted in Israel, awarded almost annually by the Wolf Foundation since 1978 to living mathematicians for outstanding achievements in mathematics. Together with the Fields Medal and the Abel Prize, it is regarded as one of the most prestigious honours in the field; unlike the Fields Medal, it has no age limit, and it frequently recognises lifetime achievement.

History

The Wolf Foundation was established in 1976 by Ricardo Wolf, a German-born inventor, diplomat, and philanthropist who settled in Israel. The mathematics prize was first awarded in 1978, with Israel Gelfand and Carl Ludwig Siegel as the inaugural laureates. The prize carries a monetary award of $100,000 (shared when there are two recipients) and is presented by the President of Israel at the Knesset in Jerusalem.

Selected laureates

Recipients include André Weil, Henri Cartan, Andrey Kolmogorov, John Milnor, Mikhail Gromov, Lennart Carleson, John Tate, Peter Lax, Robert Langlands, Andrew Wiles, and Vladimir Drinfeld. Many Wolf laureates have also received the Fields Medal or Abel Prize, and the prize has sometimes presaged the later creation of similar honours for mathematicians excluded from the Fields Medal by its age limit.
