The 6th Regiment Massachusetts Volunteer Militia was an infantry regiment that served in the Union Army during the American Civil War. It was one of the first regiments to respond to President Abraham Lincoln's call for troops following the attack on Fort Sumter, and it gained lasting fame as the first unit to suffer combat casualties in the war during the Baltimore Riot of 1861, an event that earned it the nickname "The Bloody Sixth."

Baltimore Riot

On April 19, 1861—the anniversary of the Battles of Lexington and Concord—the regiment was marching through Baltimore, Maryland, transferring between railroad stations on its way to Washington, D.C., when it was attacked by a mob of Confederate sympathizers. Soldiers fired into the crowd, and the fighting left four soldiers and at least twelve civilians dead. The dead militiamen—Luther C. Ladd, Addison O. Whitney, Charles A. Taylor, and Sumner H. Needham—are sometimes called the first Union combat deaths of the war.

Service

The regiment was organized in 1855 from existing Massachusetts militia companies. After its initial three-month service guarding Washington and its approaches in 1861, the regiment was mustered in again for nine months of service in 1862, serving in Virginia, including at the Siege of Suffolk and the Battle of Deserted House. A third enlistment of one hundred days followed in 1864, during which the regiment performed guard duty at Fort Delaware, a prisoner-of-war camp. The regiment was commanded throughout much of its service by Colonel Edward F. Jones.

Legacy

The regiment's passage through Baltimore inspired the Unionist sentiment behind the poem and song "Maryland, My Maryland," written in response to the riot. Monuments in Lowell and other Massachusetts towns commemorate Ladd and Whitney, two of the fallen soldiers.
