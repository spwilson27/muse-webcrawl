The Commonwealth of the Philippines (Spanish: Commonwealth de Filipinas; Tagalog: Komonwelt ng Pilipinas) was the administrative body that governed the Philippines from 1935 to 1946, aside from a period of exile during the Second World War. It replaced the Insular Government of the Philippine Islands and was established under the Tydings–McDuffie Act as a transitional administration in preparation for the country's full independence after a ten-year period.

History

The Commonwealth was inaugurated on 15 November 1935 in Manila, with Manuel L. Quezon as president and Sergio Osmeña as vice president, under a constitution drafted in 1934–1935 modelled substantially on that of the United States. The Commonwealth era saw the establishment of a national army, the extension of suffrage to women in 1937, the promotion of a national language based on Tagalog, and continued economic dependence on the American market.

World War II and exile

Following the Japanese invasion in December 1941, the Commonwealth government evacuated to Corregidor and then into exile in Washington, D.C., while Japan sponsored a puppet Second Philippine Republic under José P. Laurel. Quezon died in exile in 1944 and was succeeded by Osmeña, who returned with General Douglas MacArthur's forces at Leyte in October 1944. Civil government was progressively restored during the liberation campaign of 1944–1945.

Independence

In the April 1946 elections, Manuel Roxas defeated Osmeña and became the last president of the Commonwealth. In accordance with the Tydings–McDuffie Act and the Treaty of Manila, the United States recognised Philippine independence on 4 July 1946, and Roxas became the first president of the independent Republic of the Philippines.
