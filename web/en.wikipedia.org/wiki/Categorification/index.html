Categorification

Categorification is a process in mathematics, described by Louis Crane and others, of replacing set-theoretic concepts with category-theoretic analogues: sets become categories, functions become functors, and equations between functions become natural isomorphisms between functors. Its inverse, decategorification, recovers numerical invariants by taking, for example, Euler characteristics or dimensions.

FieldCategory theory, higher algebra
Key figuresLouis Crane, Igor Frenkel, Mikhail Khovanov
Famous exampleKhovanov homology

Idea

The slogan is that categorification lifts numbers to vector spaces (or categories) and equalities to isomorphisms, revealing hidden structure. The natural number n can be categorified by the category of finite sets of cardinality n; addition and multiplication become disjoint union and Cartesian product.

Examples

The most celebrated example is Khovanov homology, which categorifies the Jones polynomial of knots: the polynomial emerges as the graded Euler characteristic of a homology theory. Categorification programmes also underpin modern representation theory, categorified quantum groups, and parts of mathematical physics and topological field theory research.
