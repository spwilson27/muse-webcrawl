National Union of Mineworkers (Great Britain)

NUM
Founded: 1945
Headquarters: Barnsley, England
Predecessor: Miners' Federation of Great Britain

The National Union of Mineworkers (NUM) is a trade union for coal miners in Great Britain, formed in 1945 by restructuring the Miners' Federation of Great Britain, founded in 1888. At its height the NUM represented several hundred thousand miners in the nationalized coal industry and was among the most powerful unions in the country.

History

The NUM's national strikes of 1972 and 1974 won substantial pay increases; the 1974 strike contributed to the fall of Edward Heath's Conservative government. Under president Arthur Scargill, the union launched the year-long miners' strike of 1984–85 against the Thatcher government's pit closure programme. The strike — marked by mass picketing, the confrontation at Orgreave, and a split that produced the rival Union of Democratic Mineworkers — ended in defeat in March 1985.

Decline

Following the strike, Britain's deep coal industry contracted rapidly; the last deep mine, Kellingley Colliery, closed in 2015. The NUM continues to exist with a much-reduced membership drawn largely from surface workers and retired miners, and it campaigns on pensions and miners' welfare issues.
