José Antonio Abreu

José Antonio Abreu Anselmi (May 7, 1939 – March 24, 2018) was a Venezuelan orchestra conductor, pianist, economist, educator, activist, and politician best known as the founder of El Sistema (Fundación Musical Simón Bolívar), the publicly funded music education program that has provided free classical music training to hundreds of thousands of Venezuelan children, many from impoverished backgrounds, and has been imitated in dozens of countries.

Early life

Abreu was born in Valera, in the Venezuelan Andes. He studied piano and composition in Caracas while simultaneously earning a degree in economics from the Universidad Católica Andrés Bello, later completing graduate studies at the University of Michigan. He taught economics, served in Venezuela's National Congress, and held government cultural posts, combining an economist's conviction in social development with a musician's vocation.

El Sistema

In 1975 Abreu founded Acción Social por la Música, gathering eleven young musicians in a Caracas parking garage for the first rehearsal of what became El Sistema. The program grew into a national network of neighbourhood orchestras and choirs (núcleos) offering free instruments and intensive ensemble training to children regardless of means. Its flagship Simón Bolívar Youth Orchestra (later Simón Bolívar Symphony Orchestra) toured the world to acclaim, and alumni include Gustavo Dudamel, music director of the Los Angeles Philharmonic, and double bassist Edicson Ruiz of the Berlin Philharmonic.

Abreu framed the orchestra as a model of community and a weapon against poverty: "Music has to be recognized as an agent of social development." El Sistema inspired similar programs in more than 60 countries, including Sistema Scotland and In Harmony in England.

Honors and death

Abreu received the Prince of Asturias Award for the Arts (2008), the Polar Music Prize (2009), the Erasmus Prize (2010), and honorary doctorates from many universities. He died in Caracas on March 24, 2018.
