Auguste and Louis Lumière

Auguste Marie Louis Nicolas Lumière (1862–1954) and Louis Jean Lumière (1864–1948), the Lumière brothers, were French manufacturers of photography equipment and pioneers of cinema, best known for inventing the Cinématographe motion picture system and for the first public film screening.

The Cinématographe

Patented in February 1895, the Cinématographe was a single device that could record, develop and project film, lighter and hand-cranked compared with Edison's Kinetograph. Their first film, Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory (1895), was shot in Lyon.

First screenings

On 28 December 1895 the brothers gave the first commercial public screening at the Salon Indien du Grand Café in Paris, showing ten short films including L'Arroseur arrosé and Arrival of a Train at La Ciotat. Within a few years, Lumière operators such as Alexandre Promio were filming around the world, building a catalogue of over 1,400 "views".

Other work

The brothers' company, founded by their father Antoine, became a major maker of photographic plates; in 1907 they marketed the Autochrome colour photography process. Louis also worked on 3D photography and medical devices, including the tulle gras dressing.
