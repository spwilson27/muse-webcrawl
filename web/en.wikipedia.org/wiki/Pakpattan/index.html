Pakpattan (Punjabi and Urdu: پاکپتن), often referred to as Pākpattan Sharīf, is the capital city of the Pakpattan District in the Punjab province of Pakistan. It is among the oldest cities of the region and is famous throughout South Asia as the home of the shrine of the great Punjabi Sufi saint and poet Baba Farid (Fariduddin Ganjshakar), whose presence made the town a major centre of the Chishti order of Sufism.

History

Pakpattan was originally known as Ajodhan. It grew up at a ferry crossing of the Sutlej River on the route between Multan and Delhi, which gave the settlement commercial and strategic importance. The town owes its fame to Baba Farid, who settled there in the 12th–13th century; tradition holds that the name Pakpattan — "holy ferry" (from pak, pure/holy, and pattan, ferry or crossing) — derives from the crossing sanctified by his presence. After his death in 1266, his tomb became a pilgrimage destination, and the town developed around the shrine complex.

Pakpattan was visited by travellers and rulers over the centuries. The explorer Ibn Battuta visited the shrine, and the town was attacked by Timur during his invasion of India in 1398. Under the Mughals and later the Sikhs it remained a locally significant market and pilgrimage town, and in the British period it became a municipal centre of the Montgomery (later Sahiwal) region before becoming a district headquarters.

Shrine of Baba Farid

The shrine complex includes the saint's tomb, a mosque, and the famous Bahishti Darwaza ("Gate of Paradise"), through which pilgrims pass during the annual urs celebrations held in Muharram. The urs draws hundreds of thousands of devotees from across Pakistan. The shrine is administered under the Auqaf Department of the Government of Punjab.

Geography and economy

Pakpattan lies in the Bari Doab between the Beas and Sutlej rivers, in an intensively farmed canal-irrigated tract. Agriculture — cotton, wheat, maize, sugarcane and citrus — underpins the local economy, together with trade serving the surrounding villages and the pilgrimage traffic to the shrine. The city is connected by road and rail to Sahiwal, Okara and other towns of central Punjab.
