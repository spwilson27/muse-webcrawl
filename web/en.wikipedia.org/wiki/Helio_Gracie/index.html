Hélio Gracie

Born: 1 October 1913, Belém, Pará, Brazil
Died: 29 January 2009, Petrópolis, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
Style: Brazilian jiu-jitsu
Rank: 10th degree red belt (founder)
Notable students: Rolls Gracie, Rickson Gracie, Royce Gracie

Hélio Gracie (October 1, 1913 – January 29, 2009) was a Brazilian martial artist who, together with his brothers Oswaldo, Gastão Jr, George and Carlos Gracie, founded and developed the self-defence martial art system of Gracie jiu-jitsu, also known as Brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ).

Life

Gracie was born in Belém, Brazil. A frail child, he was largely forbidden from training by doctors, but observed his brother Carlos teaching judo-derived techniques learned from Mitsuyo Maeda. According to Gracie family accounts, when Carlos was late for a class, the teenage Hélio taught it himself and, finding some techniques difficult to execute because of his small frame, adapted them to rely on leverage and timing rather than strength. These refinements became the foundation of Gracie jiu-jitsu.

Fighting career

Hélio fought numerous vale tudo challenge matches in Brazil against larger opponents. In 1932 he drew with boxer Antonio Portugal and submitted wrestler Fred Ebert. He lost a famous 1951 bout to the visiting judoka Masahiko Kimura, who broke Gracie's arm with a shoulder lock — thereafter named the "Kimura" in BJJ in honour of the victory — after Gracie refused to submit. In 1955, aged in his forties, he fought his former student Waldemar Santana in a match lasting nearly four hours before losing. His sons, including Rorion, Rickson and Royce Gracie, later spread the art internationally; Royce's victories in the early Ultimate Fighting Championship events in the 1990s brought worldwide fame to the family style.

Death and legacy

Gracie died of natural causes in Petrópolis on 29 January 2009, at the age of 95, having taught and trained into his final years. He is remembered as the patriarch of the Gracie family and a pivotal figure in the development of modern grappling and mixed martial arts.
