Skin whitening

Skin whitening, also known as skin lightening or skin bleaching, is the practice of using chemical substances to lighten skin tone or even out complexion by reducing the concentration or production of melanin in the skin. Products include creams, soaps, and pills; professional treatments include chemical peels and laser therapy.

Active ingredients historically used include hydroquinone, corticosteroids, and mercury compounds; newer formulations use agents such as vitamin C, niacinamide, kojic acid, and arbutin. Several of these substances carry serious health risks: mercury poisoning, exogenous ochronosis from long-term hydroquinone use, and steroid-related skin damage. The World Health Organization has campaigned against mercury-containing skin-lightening products, and many countries have banned them.

Cultural context

The global market for skin lighteners is worth billions of dollars, with high prevalence of use reported in parts of Africa, Asia, and the Caribbean. Critics link demand to colorism — social hierarchies favouring lighter skin with roots in colonialism and caste — and the industry has faced growing backlash. In 2020, major brands renamed "fairness" products (e.g., Unilever's Fair & Lovely became Glow & Lovely) amid the wider reckoning over racial inequality.

Medical uses

Dermatologists legitimately prescribe depigmenting agents for disorders of hyperpigmentation such as melasma and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.
