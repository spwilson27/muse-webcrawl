Julius Erving

Julius Winfield Erving II (born February 22, 1950), commonly known by the nickname Dr. J, is an American former professional basketball player. Erving helped legitimize the American Basketball Association (ABA) and was the best-known player in that league when it merged with the National Basketball Association (NBA) after the 1975–76 season.

Erving won three championships, four Most Valuable Player Awards, and three scoring titles with the ABA's Virginia Squires and New York Nets (now the NBA's Brooklyn Nets) and the NBA's Philadelphia 76ers. During his 16 seasons as a player, none of his teams ever missed the postseason. He is the eighth-highest scorer in ABA/NBA history with 30,026 points (NBA and ABA combined). He was well known for slam dunking from the free-throw line in slam dunk contests and was the only player voted Most Valuable Player in both the ABA and the NBA. The basketball slang of being posterized was first coined to describe his moves.

Early life and college

Erving was born in East Meadow, New York, and raised in Roosevelt, New York. He played basketball at Roosevelt High School. He attended the University of Massachusetts Amherst, where he averaged 26.3 points and 20.2 rebounds per game over two varsity seasons, one of only a handful of players to average 20 points and 20 rebounds per game in NCAA competition. He left college in 1971 to pursue a professional career.

Professional career

Erving signed with the Virginia Squires of the ABA in 1971. After two seasons he joined the New York Nets, leading them to ABA championships in 1974 and 1976 and winning three ABA MVP awards. When the ABA merged into the NBA in 1976, the cash-strapped Nets sold Erving's contract to the Philadelphia 76ers. In Philadelphia he formed high-powered teams that reached the NBA Finals several times and finally won the NBA championship in 1983, sweeping the Los Angeles Lakers. Erving was named NBA Most Valuable Player in 1981.

Erving retired in 1987 after 16 seasons. He was inducted into the Basketball Hall of Fame in 1993 and was named to the NBA's 50th and 75th Anniversary Teams. His graceful, acrobatic style — soaring dunks, finger rolls, and hang-time moves — transformed the game and made him one of basketball's first modern superstars and cultural icons.

Legacy

Erving is widely regarded as one of the greatest basketball players of all time and the most influential player of the 1970s. His number 32 was retired by the Nets and his number 6 by the 76ers. After retirement he became a businessman and television analyst.
