Coining (mint)

Coining is a form of precision stamping in which a workpiece — a blank metal disc or planchet — is subjected to high pressure between two dies so that the metal flows into the dies' design, producing a coin with sharp detail and often a raised rim.

Process

In minting, blanks are fed between an obverse and a reverse die in a coining press. Modern presses can strike hundreds of coins per minute, exerting pressures of many tonnes. The edge may be reeded, lettered or plain, formed by a collar die. Historically, coins were struck by hand with a hammer (hammered coinage) and later by screw presses and steam-powered machinery (milled coinage).
