Pro bono

Pro bono publico ('for the public good'), usually shortened to pro bono, refers to professional work undertaken voluntarily and without payment. Unlike traditional volunteering, it uses the specific skills of professionals to provide services to those who are unable to afford them. The term is most commonly applied to legal services, though it is also used in fields such as marketing, technology, and medicine.

Legal pro bono

Lawyers have traditionally been expected to contribute work for the public good. In the United States, the American Bar Association's Model Rules of Professional Conduct recommend that lawyers provide at least 50 hours of pro bono legal service per year, directed primarily to persons of limited means. Many large law firms maintain dedicated pro bono programmes and count such work in evaluating associates. In some jurisdictions, such as South Korea and parts of Australia, minimum pro bono contributions are mandatory for practising lawyers.

Role in access to justice

Pro bono services are one mechanism for improving access to justice, alongside publicly funded legal aid. Critics note that voluntary pro bono work cannot by itself meet the scale of unmet legal need, while supporters point to its role in high-impact litigation and in serving clients whose cases fall outside legal aid schemes.
