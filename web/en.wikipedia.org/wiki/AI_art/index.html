Artificial intelligence art
MediumImages, video, music and text generated by machine-learning models
Key technologiesGenerative adversarial networks (GANs), diffusion models, transformers
Notable systemsDeepDream, DALL-E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion
RootsAlgorithmic art of the 1960s–70s (e.g. Harold Cohen's AARON)

Artificial intelligence art is visual and other media created with the assistance of generative artificial intelligence. While algorithmic art dates to experiments of the 1960s and 1970s, the field expanded rapidly in the 2010s and 2020s with neural-network systems — first generative adversarial networks, then text-to-image diffusion models such as DALL-E, Midjourney and Stable Diffusion — that produce images from written prompts.

History

Harold Cohen's program AARON produced drawings from the 1970s onward. In 2015 Google's DeepDream popularised hallucinatory neural imagery. GANs, introduced in 2014, enabled photorealistic faces and style transfer. In 2018 the GAN-generated portrait Edmond de Belamy sold at Christie's for $432,500. The release of DALL-E 2, Midjourney and the open-source Stable Diffusion in 2022 brought high-quality text-to-image generation to the general public; Jason Allen's Midjourney work Théâtre D'opéra Spatial won a Colorado state fair art prize the same year, igniting debate.

Criticism and debate

AI art has been criticised over the use of copyrighted works in training data without consent, leading to lawsuits by artists and image libraries; over potential displacement of illustrators and concept artists; and over questions of authorship and copyrightability — the U.S. Copyright Office has held that purely AI-generated material is not copyrightable. Supporters argue the tools democratise image-making and constitute a new medium in which prompting, curation and post-editing are creative acts.
