Hyperreflexia

Specialty: Neurology
Definition: Abnormally brisk deep tendon reflexes, a sign of upper motor neuron lesions

Hyperreflexia is defined as overactive or over-responsive reflexes. Examples of this can include twitching or spastic reactions, which are indicative of diseases of the central nervous system. It is a hallmark sign of an upper motor neuron lesion, arising from loss of descending inhibitory control over the spinal reflex arc. Hyperreflexia is commonly accompanied by other upper motor neuron signs: spasticity, clonus, weakness in a pyramidal pattern, and an extensor plantar response (Babinski sign).

Causes

Common causes include stroke, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury or compression, cerebral palsy, and motor neuron disease (amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, in which hyperreflexia coexists with lower motor neuron signs). Metabolic and toxic causes include hyperthyroidism, serotonin syndrome, tetanus, and strychnine poisoning. In spinal cord injury above approximately T6, hyperreflexia is part of autonomic dysreflexia, a potentially life-threatening syndrome of uncontrolled sympathetic outflow triggered by visceral stimuli below the lesion, producing severe hypertension, headache, and sweating.

Assessment

Deep tendon reflexes are graded on a 0 to 4+ scale; 3+ and 4+ responses (the latter with clonus) are considered brisk. Asymmetry between sides, or a reflex level inconsistent with a patient's other findings, is often more significant than the absolute grade. Brisk reflexes may also be a normal variant in anxious or otherwise healthy individuals, so hyperreflexia is interpreted in clinical context.
