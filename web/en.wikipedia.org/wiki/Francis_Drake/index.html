Francis Drake

Sir Francis Drake (c. 1540 – 28 January 1596) was an English explorer, sea captain, privateer and naval commander of the Elizabethan era. Commissioned by Queen Elizabeth I, he carried out the second circumnavigation of the world (1577–1580) and played a major role in defeating the Spanish Armada in 1588.

Circumnavigation

Sailing with five ships, Drake raided Spanish ports on South America's Pacific coast, captured treasure ships including the Cacafuego, claimed Nova Albion on the California coast, crossed the Pacific and Indian Oceans, and returned to Plymouth in the Golden Hind with immense treasure. He was knighted aboard his ship in 1581.

War with Spain

Drake's 1585–86 West Indies expedition and his 1587 raid on Cádiz — "singeing the King of Spain's beard" — delayed Spanish invasion plans. As a vice admiral in 1588 he helped defeat the Armada, famously, by legend, finishing a game of bowls at Plymouth Hoe. He died of dysentery off Panama during a final expedition in 1596. To the Spanish he was the pirate "El Draque".
