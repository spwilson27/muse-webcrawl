The UK Independence Party (UKIP) is a Eurosceptic, right-wing populist political party in the United Kingdom. Founded in 1993 by the academic Alan Sked from the Anti-Federalist League, its founding aim was British withdrawal from the European Union — a goal achieved following the 2016 referendum and the UK's departure from the EU in 2020.

History

UKIP grew slowly through the 1990s and 2000s, performing best in European Parliament elections, which use proportional representation. Under leader Nigel Farage it surged in the early 2010s: it won the 2014 European Parliament election in the UK with 24 seats — the first time since 1906 that a party other than Labour or the Conservatives had won a UK-wide election — and at the 2015 general election it took 12.6 per cent of the vote, though only one seat under first-past-the-post.

The party's pressure on the Conservative government was widely credited with prompting David Cameron to promise the in–out referendum, and UKIP campaigned prominently for Leave. Farage resigned after the referendum; a rapid turnover of leaders, internal disputes over figures on the far right, and the absorption of its voters by the Conservatives and the Brexit Party (later Reform UK) caused its support to collapse after 2016.

Policies

Beyond Euroscepticism, UKIP has advocated stricter immigration controls, a points-based immigration system, lower taxes, grammar schools and opposition to net-zero climate targets.
