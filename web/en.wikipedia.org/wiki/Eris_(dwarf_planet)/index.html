Eris (dwarf planet)

136199 Eris
Discovered: 5 January 2005 (images from 2003)
Discoverers: M. E. Brown, C. Trujillo, D. Rabinowitz
Diameter: ≈ 2,326 km
Mass: ≈ 1.66 × 1022 kg
Orbital period: 558 years
Moon: Dysnomia

Eris (minor-planet designation 136199 Eris) is the most massive and second-largest known dwarf planet in the Solar System. It is a trans-Neptunian object in the scattered disc, discovered in January 2005 by a Palomar Observatory team led by Mike Brown using images taken in October 2003. Eris is slightly smaller in diameter than Pluto but about 27% more massive.

Discovery and naming

Because Eris appeared larger than Pluto, its discoverers and NASA initially described it as the Solar System's tenth planet. This, together with the prospect of similar objects being found, prompted the International Astronomical Union in 2006 to define the term "planet" formally for the first time, creating the category of dwarf planet, into which both Eris and Pluto were placed. Eris is named after the Greek goddess of strife and discord; its moon is named Dysnomia, after Eris's daughter, the daemon of lawlessness.

Physical characteristics

Eris is extremely distant, currently about three times farther from the Sun than Pluto, and follows a highly inclined, eccentric orbit with a period of 558 years. Its surface is covered in methane ice, giving it a very high albedo. In 2010, observations of a stellar occultation measured its diameter at about 2,326 km, slightly less than Pluto's. Its large moon, Dysnomia, allowed astronomers to determine Eris's mass.
