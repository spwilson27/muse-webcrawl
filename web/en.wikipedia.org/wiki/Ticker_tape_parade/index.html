Ticker tape parade

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A ticker tape parade is a parade held in a city, allowing large amounts of shredded paper (originally actual ticker tape, later confetti) to be thrown from nearby office buildings onto the parade route, creating a celebratory flurry of paper. The concept originated in New York City, where the Financial District's tall buildings lined the route along Broadway from Bowling Green to City Hall Park, a stretch known as the "Canyon of Heroes".

History

The tradition is generally traced to an impromptu celebration on October 28, 1886, during the dedication of the Statue of Liberty, when office workers spontaneously threw ticker tape out of their windows as a parade passed. Over the following century, ticker tape parades became New York's signature civic honor, bestowed on heads of state, military heroes, aviators, astronauts, explorers, and championship sports teams. Charles Lindbergh received one of the largest in 1927 after his solo transatlantic flight, and the Apollo 11 astronauts were honored in 1969.

Modern practice

With the disappearance of paper ticker tape, the paper is now typically confetti cut for the purpose, sometimes made from shredded recycled documents. Since 2003, honorees have also received a granite strip embedded in the sidewalk along the Canyon of Heroes bearing their name and the date of the parade. Ticker tape parades have also been adopted, in modified form, by other cities for championship teams and returning troops, though they remain most strongly associated with New York.
