Dusk

Dusk, the darkest stage of evening twilight.

Dusk occurs at the end of evening twilight, when the Sun has sunk far enough below the horizon that its indirect light fades and darkness follows. The corresponding moment at the start of morning twilight is dawn.

Stages

Three stages of dusk are defined by the Sun's depression below the horizon. Civil dusk ends when the Sun is 6° below the horizon; until then, outdoor activities can usually continue without artificial light and terrestrial objects remain clearly distinguishable. At nautical dusk (12°) the horizon at sea is no longer visible against the sky, ending the period in which sailors could take star sights. At astronomical dusk (18°) the last scattered sunlight disappears and the sky is as dark as it will get, allowing observation of the faintest astronomical objects.

Duration

The length of twilight, and hence the delay between sunset and dusk, depends on latitude and season. Near the equator dusk follows sunset within roughly 20–25 minutes; at high latitudes in summer the Sun may never sink 18° below the horizon, producing nights of continuous twilight, while at the poles twilight can last for weeks.
