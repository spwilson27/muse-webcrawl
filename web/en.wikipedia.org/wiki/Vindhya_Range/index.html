Vindhya Range

The Vindhya Range (also known as the Vindhyachal) is a complex, discontinuous chain of mountain ridges, hill ranges, highlands and plateau escarpments in west-central India. It runs roughly east–west for about 1,000 kilometres across the states of Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh, along the northern side of the Narmada River valley.

Geography

The Vindhyas form the traditional boundary between northern India (Hindustan) and the Deccan plateau to the south, with the Satpura Range running parallel to them on the southern side of the Narmada. The range is relatively low, with most elevations between 300 and 650 metres, and consists largely of ancient sandstones and other sedimentary rocks of Precambrian age. The eastern end of the range approaches the Ganges valley near Mirzapur and Varanasi.

Significance

The Vindhyas hold a central place in Indian mythology and geography: in the epics and Puranas the range is personified, and the sage Agastya is credited with crossing it to carry northern culture southward. Rivers including the Chambal, Betwa and Ken rise in or near the range and flow north to the Yamuna, while the Narmada flows west between the Vindhyas and Satpuras. The region is rich in forests, wildlife sanctuaries, historic forts and the rock shelters of Bhimbetka, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
