Kathrine Switzer

Kathrine Virginia Switzer (born 5 January 1947) is an American marathon runner, author and television commentator. She became famous in 1967 as the first woman to run the Boston Marathon as an officially registered participant, at a time when the race was considered a men's event. Photographs of race official Jock Semple trying to tear off her bib number — 261 — became an iconic image in the history of women's sport.

The 1967 Boston Marathon

Switzer, a 20-year-old journalism student at Syracuse University, registered for Boston using her initials "K. V. Switzer" and was issued bib 261. A few miles into the race, Semple charged at her and tried to rip off her bib, shouting at her to leave "his" race; Switzer's boyfriend, hammer thrower Tom Miller, shouldered Semple aside, and she finished in about 4 hours 20 minutes. Although women were not formally barred by any written rule in the entry form, the AAU subsequently banned women from competing against men, and Switzer was expelled. (The year before, Bobbi Gibb had run Boston unofficially and finished ahead of Switzer's later time.)

Career and advocacy

The incident launched Switzer's lifelong campaign for women's distance running. The Boston Marathon officially admitted women in 1972. Switzer won the 1974 New York City Marathon and ran a personal best of 2:51:37 at Boston in 1975. As a broadcaster she has commentated on marathons for decades, and she organised the Avon International Running Circuit, a global series of women's races that helped build the case for the women's marathon added to the Olympics in 1984. Her memoir is titled Marathon Woman. In 2017, aged 70, she ran Boston again wearing bib 261, which the Boston Athletic Association then retired — the only number ever retired for a woman. She founded the non-profit 261 Fearless to empower women through running.
