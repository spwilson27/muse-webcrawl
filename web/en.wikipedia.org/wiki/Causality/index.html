Causality

Causality
FieldMetaphysics / philosophy of science
Key figuresAristotle (four causes); Hume (regularity); Kant
[Image: A billiard ball striking another, the classic image of cause and effect]

Causality is the relationship between causes and effects: the idea that events bring about other events. Aristotle analysed four kinds of cause (material, formal, efficient, final). David Hume famously argued that we never perceive a necessary connection between cause and effect — only constant conjunction — making causation a projection of habit, a claim Immanuel Kant answered by making causality a category the mind imposes on experience.

Modern debates

Contemporary theories include counterfactual accounts (Lewis: had the cause not occurred, the effect would not), interventionism (causation as what changes under manipulation), and process theories. Physics complicates the picture: relativity constrains causal order, and quantum theory's probabilities strain classical notions, though causal reasoning remains indispensable to science and law.
