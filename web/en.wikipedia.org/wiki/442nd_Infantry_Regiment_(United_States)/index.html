The 442nd Infantry Regiment was a United States Army unit composed almost entirely of second-generation Japanese American (Nisei) soldiers during the Second World War. Fighting in Europe while many of its soldiers' families were held in internment camps at home, the 442nd became, for its size and length of service, the most decorated unit in American military history.

Formation

The regiment was activated in 1943 after the Army reversed its ban on Japanese American enlistment; volunteers came from Hawaii and from the mainland internment camps. It absorbed the 100th Infantry Battalion, a Nisei unit from Hawaii, and adopted the motto "Go for Broke", a Hawaiian gambling slang phrase meaning to risk everything.

Combat record

The 442nd fought in Italy and France. Its most famous action was the rescue of the "Lost Battalion" — a Texas battalion cut off in the Vosges Mountains in October 1944 — which the 442nd reached at the cost of hundreds of casualties. The regiment later participated in the Gothic Line offensive in Italy in 1945. Its members earned more than 18,000 individual decorations, including 21 Medals of Honor and eight Presidential Unit Citations, while suffering very high casualty rates; the Purple Heart total earned it the nickname the "Purple Heart Battalion". In 2010 the unit's veterans were collectively awarded the Congressional Gold Medal.
