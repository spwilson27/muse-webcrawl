Pirate Party (Sweden)

The Swedish Pirate Party, the original Pirate Party

The Pirate Party (Swedish: Piratpartiet) is a political party in Sweden founded on 1 January 2006 by Rick Falkvinge. It was the first party of the international pirate movement, campaigning for reform of copyright and patent law, strengthened privacy, and transparency of public administration.

History

Falkvinge launched the party's website at the start of 2006, and it quickly gathered the signatures needed to register for elections. In the 2006 general election the party won 0.63% of the vote. Its breakthrough came in the 2009 European Parliament election, where, boosted by publicity from the Pirate Bay trial and a proposed telecom surveillance law, it won 7.13% of the Swedish vote and two seats, taken by Christian Engström and (after the Lisbon Treaty took effect) Amelia Andersdotter.

Platform

The party's programme seeks to shorten copyright terms and legalise non-commercial file sharing, phase out the patent system, protect personal privacy against mass surveillance, and make government more open. It has historically declined to position itself on a left–right axis, voting issue by issue. Anna Troberg succeeded Falkvinge as leader in 2011.

Later years

After 2010 the party's national vote share declined, though it kept municipal representation in some areas and its ideas influenced debate across Europe; its Swedish youth wing, Ung Pirat, was for a period one of the country's largest political youth organisations. The party lost its European Parliament seats in 2014 but remains the archetype of pirate parties worldwide.
