Susan B. Anthony

Susan B. Anthony (born Susan Anthony; February 15, 1820 – March 13, 1906) was an American social reformer and women's rights activist who played a pivotal role in the women's suffrage movement. Born into a Quaker family committed to social equality, she collected anti-slavery petitions at age 17, became an agent for the American Anti-Slavery Society, and later campaigned for temperance and women's rights.

Suffrage work

In 1851 Anthony met Elizabeth Cady Stanton, beginning a lifelong partnership: Stanton was the movement's theorist and writer, Anthony its organiser and campaigner. Together they founded the Women's Loyal National League, the American Equal Rights Association, and in 1869 the National Woman Suffrage Association. They also published the newspaper The Revolution and compiled the History of Woman Suffrage.

Arrest and trial

In 1872 Anthony was arrested in Rochester, New York, for voting in the presidential election, and was convicted in a widely publicised trial, refusing to pay the fine imposed. She toured the country lecturing for decades, and in 1878 she arranged for a women's suffrage amendment to be introduced in Congress — the text that, reintroduced repeatedly, finally became the Nineteenth Amendment in 1920, sometimes called the "Susan B. Anthony Amendment".

Legacy

Anthony died in 1906, fourteen years before the amendment's ratification. In 1979 she became the first non-allegorical woman depicted on a circulating U.S. coin, the Susan B. Anthony dollar. Her Rochester home is a National Historic Landmark.
