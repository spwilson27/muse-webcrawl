New Year Honours

The New Year Honours are a part of the British honours system, with the awards granted by the monarch on the advice of the government and announced at the end of December each year (published in The Gazette on or around 30–31 December, traditionally described as the honours list of 1 January). New Year Honours have been awarded since 1890. Together with the Birthday Honours, announced to mark the Sovereign's Official Birthday in June, they form the two main regular opportunities for granting knighthoods, damehoods and appointments to orders of chivalry and gallantry and meritorious-service awards.

The awards

Recipients are drawn from all walks of life — public service, the armed forces, business, the arts, sport, science, charity and local community volunteering, the latter recognised particularly through the British Empire Medal (BEM) and the MBE. Appointments are made to orders including the Order of the Bath, the Order of St Michael and St George, the Royal Victorian Order and the Order of the British Empire, whose grades run from Member (MBE) and Officer (OBE) up to Commander (CBE), Knight or Dame Commander (KBE/DBE) and Knight or Dame Grand Cross (GBE). Life peerages are not part of the list, but knighthoods for services to politics, law (often for senior judges and retiring MPs), and industry feature prominently. Separate lists are issued for other Commonwealth realms, such as Australia (Australia Day Honours and King's Birthday Honours) and New Zealand, which publishes its own New Year Honours.

Process

Anyone may nominate a candidate for an honour through the Cabinet Office honours system. Nominations are assessed by specialist honours committees, whose recommendations pass through the prime minister to the monarch for approval. Recipients are notified in advance and may decline; a small number do, and their names simply do not appear. Awards may later be forfeited, on the advice of the Honours Forfeiture Committee, if a recipient is convicted of a serious offence or brings the system into disrepute.

History

George V held the first New Year investiture ceremonies at Buckingham Palace, and the lists swelled during the First World War with the creation of the Order of the British Empire in 1917 to recognise civilian war work. Reform efforts have repeatedly sought to widen the social and regional spread of awards and to reduce the share going automatically to senior civil servants and military officers. Criticism of the system — over political honours, donors, and honours for officials associated with failures — recurs with each publication, and the lists are closely reported by national and local media, especially for celebrity and sporting recipients.
