Pen y Fan and the central Brecon Beacons.

The Brecon Beacons (Welsh: Bannau Brycheiniog) are a mountain range in South Wales, comprising a chain of Old Red Sandstone peaks south of the town of Brecon. The range's highest summit, Pen y Fan (886 m), is the highest peak in southern Britain south of Snowdonia. In 2023 the national park authority adopted the Welsh name Bannau Brycheiniog as its official name for the park.

Geography and park

The Brecon Beacons National Park, established in 1957, covers about 1,340 km² and extends well beyond the central peaks to include the Black Mountain in the west, Fforest Fawr, and the Black Mountains in the east. The landscape features glacial cwms, waterfalls — notably in "Waterfall Country" around Ystradfellte — limestone caves and reservoirs supplying South Wales.

Activities and military use

The area is popular for hill walking, including the annual Ten Tors-style events and fan dances. Parts of the park are used for military training; the Special Air Service conducts selection exercises over Pen y Fan and the surrounding hills.
