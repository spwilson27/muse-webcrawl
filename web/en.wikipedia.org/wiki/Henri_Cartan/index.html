Henri Cartan

Henri Paul Cartan (8 July 1904 – 13 August 2008) was a French mathematician who made substantial contributions to algebraic topology, complex analysis, and homological algebra. He was the son of the mathematician Élie Cartan and a founding member of the Bourbaki group.

Career

Cartan studied at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris and taught at several French universities, including Lille, Strasbourg, and Paris. His seminar at Paris, the Séminaire Cartan, was influential in shaping post-war mathematics. With Samuel Eilenberg he wrote the foundational text Homological Algebra (1956).

Contributions

His work includes the theory of sheaves, the Cartan–Eilenberg system, contributions to several complex variables (including the theorems known as Cartan's theorems A and B), and the notion of filters in topology. He received the Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 1980 and lived to the age of 104.
