Lekë Dukagjini (1410–1481) was an Albanian nobleman, a member of the Dukagjini family who ruled a principality in the northern Albanian highlands. A contemporary and ally — and at times rival — of the national hero Skanderbeg, he led Albanian resistance to the Ottoman Empire after Skanderbeg's death and is remembered as the supposed codifier of the Kanun, the traditional Albanian customary law that bears his name.

Life

Lekë succeeded his father Pal Dukagjini in 1446 as ruler of the family's mountain domains, centred on the Dukagjin plain and the highlands east of Shkodër. In 1447 he quarrelled with the Venetians over the fortress of Dagnum, a dispute that widened into war; the chroniclers also record his feud with Skanderbeg, with whom he was later reconciled, both men joining their forces against the Ottomans.

After Skanderbeg died in 1468, Lekë became the principal leader of the Albanian resistance, defending the northern fortresses through the Ottoman invasions of the 1470s. As the Ottoman advance became irresistible — Krujë fell in 1478 and Shkodër in 1479 — Lekë withdrew to Venetian territory, where he died in 1481. His family lands were absorbed into the Ottoman sanjak system.

The Kanun of Lekë Dukagjini

Posterity attaches Lekë's name to the Kanuni i Lekë Dukagjinit, the body of customary law that governed life in the northern highlands for centuries: rules on honour (besa), hospitality, marriage, inheritance, boundaries, and blood feud (gjakmarrja). The Kanun was transmitted orally and was systematically collected and published only in the early twentieth century by the Franciscan priest Shtjefën Gjeçovi, whose edition appeared in 1933. Whether Lekë himself compiled the code or merely lent his prestige to it is debated, but his name became inseparably linked with it, and the Kanun survived as the effective law of the highlands well into the modern era, reviving in parts of the north after the fall of communism in Albania.
