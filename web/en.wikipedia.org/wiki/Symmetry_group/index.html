Symmetry group

In group theory, the symmetry group of a geometric object is the group of all transformations under which the object is invariant, equipped with the group operation of composition. Such transformations include rotations, reflections, translations and their combinations. For a structure in space, the symmetry group is a subgroup of the isometry group of the space.

Types

Symmetry groups of finite objects in the plane are either cyclic groups (rotations only) or dihedral groups (rotations and reflections); this classification is sometimes called Leonardo's theorem. In three dimensions, the finite point groups include the symmetry groups of the Platonic solids — for example, the icosahedral group of order 60. Periodic structures such as crystals have space groups: there are 230 space groups in three dimensions, 17 wallpaper groups in two, and 7 frieze groups in one. Continuous symmetry is described by Lie groups, such as the rotation group SO(3) and the circle group of a circle or sphere.

Applications

Symmetry groups underpin crystallography, molecular spectroscopy, particle physics and the study of conservation laws via Noether's theorem. The classification of finite simple groups and the theory of symmetry groups of regular polytopes are major achievements of modern algebra.
