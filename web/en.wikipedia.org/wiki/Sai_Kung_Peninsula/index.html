Sai Kung Peninsula

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

LocationSai Kung District, New Territories, Hong Kong
FeaturesSai Kung Country Park, beaches, volcanic rock formations

The Sai Kung Peninsula (Chinese: 西貢半島) is a peninsula in the eastern New Territories of Hong Kong, forming the bulk of Sai Kung District. Renowned for its unspoiled scenery, rugged coastline, sandy beaches and seafood restaurants, it is often described as the "back garden of Hong Kong".

Geography

The peninsula extends into the South China Sea between Tolo Harbour to the north and Port Shelter to the south. Much of its area is protected within the Sai Kung East and Sai Kung West Country Parks, established in 1978. The terrain is hilly and volcanic in origin; the region is famous for the hexagonal volcanic rock columns of the Hong Kong UNESCO Global Geopark, spectacularly exposed around High Island Reservoir and on nearby islands. Sharp Peak (468 m), one of Hong Kong's most distinctive summits, dominates the eastern part of the peninsula.

Attractions

Sai Kung Town, on the peninsula's western shore, is the main settlement and a hub for seafood restaurants and boat trips to outlying islands. Popular beaches on the Tai Long Wan coast — including Sai Wan, Ham Tin Wan and Tai Long Wan itself — are accessible only on foot or by boat and are considered among Hong Kong's finest. The MacLehose Trail, Hong Kong's longest hiking trail, crosses the peninsula from Pak Tam Chung. High Island Reservoir, completed in 1978, was Hong Kong's first major reservoir built in the sea, closed off by two giant cofferdams.

History

The area was historically a farming and fishing region dotted with Hakka villages. Many remote villages, such as those in the Lai Chi Wo and Tai Long areas, were partly abandoned during the rural exodus of the mid-twentieth century, though some have seen revival and heritage projects in recent years.
