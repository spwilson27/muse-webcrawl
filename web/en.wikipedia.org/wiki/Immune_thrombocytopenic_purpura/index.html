Immune thrombocytopenic purpura

Immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP), also known as immune thrombocytopenia or idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura, is an autoimmune disorder characterised by a low platelet count (thrombocytopenia) leading to bruising (purpura) and an increased risk of bleeding. It occurs when the immune system produces antibodies against the body's own platelets, causing them to be destroyed prematurely, principally in the spleen, and impairing platelet production in the bone marrow.

Signs and symptoms

Many patients have no symptoms. When present, symptoms include petechiae (tiny red or purple spots), larger bruises, nosebleeds, bleeding gums, blood in the urine or stool, and unusually heavy menstrual bleeding. Serious internal bleeding, including intracranial haemorrhage, is rare and occurs mainly at very low platelet counts.

Causes and diagnosis

Primary ITP has no identified cause; secondary ITP may be associated with infections such as HIV, hepatitis C or Helicobacter pylori, other autoimmune diseases such as lupus, or certain drugs. In children it often follows a viral illness and resolves spontaneously; in adults it is more often chronic. Diagnosis is by exclusion, based on an isolated low platelet count on a complete blood count and blood smear, with otherwise normal results.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the platelet count and bleeding risk. Options include observation, corticosteroids, intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), anti-D immunoglobulin, thrombopoietin receptor agonists such as romiplostim and eltrombopag, the spleen tyrosine kinase inhibitor fostamatinib, rituximab, and, in refractory cases, splenectomy.
