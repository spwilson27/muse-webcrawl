Amer Fort

Amer Fort or Amber Fort is a fort located in Amer, Rajasthan, India, about 11 kilometres from Jaipur. The town of Amer and the Amber Fort were originally built by the Kachhwaha Rajputs; the present palace complex was begun by Raja Man Singh I in the late 16th century and enlarged by his successors, notably Mirza Raja Jai Singh I. It served as the seat of the Kachhwaha rulers until the capital moved to Jaipur in 1727.

Architecture

Built of red sandstone and marble on a hill above Maota Lake, the fort is organised around four courtyards. Highlights include the Diwan-i-Aam (public audience hall), the Diwan-i-Khas, the Sukh Niwas with its cooling water cascade, and the celebrated Sheesh Mahal (Mirror Palace), its walls and ceilings inlaid with tiny mirrors and coloured glass that glitter in lamplight. The Ganesh Pol gateway is richly painted with frescoes. A tunnel connects the fort to the nearby Jaigarh Fort as an escape route in wartime.

Recognition

Amer Fort is one of the most visited monuments in India and, in 2013, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the serial "Hill Forts of Rajasthan" listing.
