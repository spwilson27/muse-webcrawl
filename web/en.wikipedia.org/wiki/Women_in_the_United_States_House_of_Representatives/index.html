Women in the United States House of Representatives

Women in the United States House of Representatives have served since 1917, when Jeannette Rankin of Montana became the first woman elected to Congress, four years before the Nineteenth Amendment guaranteed women's suffrage nationally.

Early decades

For much of the 20th century women held only a handful of House seats, often succeeding deceased husbands — the so-called "widow's mandate". Notable early members included Alice Mary Robertson, Rebecca Latimer Felton (Senate), Mary Norton, Frances P. Bolton and Helen Gahagan Douglas.

Growth

Numbers rose steadily from the 1970s. The 1992 elections, dubbed the "Year of the Woman", doubled women's House membership. Further gains followed in 2018, when more than 100 women were elected to the House, including the first Muslim women, first Native American women, and youngest woman ever elected.

Recent representation

In the 118th Congress (2023–25) women held about 125 of 435 voting seats, roughly 29 percent — a record, though still below women's share of the population. Nancy Pelosi, first elected in 1987, became the first female Speaker of the House in 2007 and again in 2019.

Leadership and committees

Women have chaired major committees and served in party leadership in both parties. Milestones include the first woman to chair Appropriations and the bipartisan Congressional Caucus for Women's Issues, founded in 1977.
