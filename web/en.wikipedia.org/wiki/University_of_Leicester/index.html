University of Leicester

The University of Leicester is a public research university based in Leicester, England. It was founded in 1921 as Leicestershire and Rutland University College, a living memorial to those who died in the First World War — reflected in its motto, Ut Vitam Habeant ("that they may have life"). It received its royal charter and university status in 1957.

Research

Leicester is noted for the invention of genetic fingerprinting by Sir Alec Jeffreys in 1984, and for its space research; instruments built by its Space Research Centre have flown on numerous missions, including the Beagle 2 Mars lander. In 2012 the university's archaeologists led the excavation in a Leicester car park that recovered the remains of King Richard III, identified through DNA analysis in 2013 — one of the most famous archaeological discoveries of the century.

Campus

The campus lies just south of the city centre, adjacent to Victoria Park, and includes the Stirling and Gowan Engineering Building (1963), a Grade II* listed landmark of post-war architecture.
