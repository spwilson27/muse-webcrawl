Cornell Notes

The Cornell Notes system (also called the Cornell method) is a note-taking system devised in the 1950s by Walter Pauk, an education professor at Cornell University. It divides a page into three sections — a narrow cue column, a wide note-taking column, and a summary area at the bottom — and is widely taught in schools and universities.

Overview

Developer: Walter Pauk

Institution: Cornell University

Sections: Cues, Notes, Summary

Method

During a lecture or while reading, the student writes notes in the main note-taking column on the right. Afterwards, key words, questions and cues are added to the narrow left-hand column, which can be covered to test recall. Finally, a brief summary of the page is written in the strip at the bottom. Pauk described the process with the "5 Rs": record, reduce, recite, reflect and review.

Use and evidence

The system is promoted as an active-learning strategy that encourages organisation and review rather than passive transcription, and it is a standard feature of study-skills programmes such as AVID.
