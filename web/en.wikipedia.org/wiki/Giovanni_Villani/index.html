Giovanni Villani (c. 1276 or 1280 – 1348) was an Italian banker, official, diplomat and chronicler of Florence. His Nuova Cronica ("New Chronicle") is one of the most important historical sources for fourteenth-century Italy and a landmark of medieval historiography, notable for its wealth of statistical and economic detail.

Life

Villani was a member of the Arte di Calimala, the guild of cloth merchants, and a partner in the Peruzzi banking company, travelling on its business to France and Flanders. He served Florence in several civic offices, including as prior, and was involved in overseeing the mint and the building of the city walls. The collapse of the great Florentine banking houses in the 1340s brought him financial ruin and a spell in prison for debt.

Nuova Cronica

Villani wrote that attending the Roman Jubilee of 1300 and seeing the city's ancient monuments inspired him to write a chronicle of Florence on the model of Rome's historians. The Nuova Cronica runs from the Tower of Babel to 1348, but its value lies in the contemporary books covering Villani's own lifetime, which record prices, wages, population, taxes, building projects and disasters such as the great Arno flood of 1333. Villani died of the Black Death in 1348 while writing; his brother Matteo and nephew Filippo continued the chronicle.
