Bridget of Sweden (Swedish: Birgitta Birgersdotter; c. 1303 – 23 July 1373), also known as Saint Birgitta, was a Swedish mystic and founder of the Bridgettine Order (Ordo Sanctissimi Salvatoris). Widely venerated in Scandinavia and in the Roman Catholic Church, she was canonised in 1391 and in 1999 was named by Pope John Paul II one of the six patron saints of Europe.

Life

Born into one of Sweden's most powerful families, Birgitta married Ulf Gudmarsson and bore eight children, among them Saint Catherine of Sweden. After Ulf's death in 1344 she devoted herself to religious life, recording the visions she had received since childhood in the work known as the Revelations (Revelationes celestes), which circulated widely in medieval Europe. In 1346 King Magnus IV granted her lands at Vadstena, where she founded her order's motherhouse.

Rome and legacy

From 1349 Bridget lived mainly in Rome, urging the popes to return from Avignon, preaching reform, and making pilgrimages including one to the Holy Land shortly before her death in 1373. Her body was translated to Vadstena. The Bridgettine order spread across Europe; Syon Abbey in England was its most famous English house.
