Risk matrix

Risk matrix
Used inRisk assessment, safety engineering, project management, occupational health
AxesLikelihood (probability) × Severity (impact)
Typical form3×3 or 5×5 colour-coded grid

A risk matrix is a tool used during risk assessment to define the level of risk by considering the category of probability or likelihood of an adverse event against the category of its severity or consequence. Plotting hazards on a two-dimensional grid — usually colour-coded green, yellow, orange and red — it provides a simple visual method for prioritizing risks and deciding which require treatment, from workplace safety reviews to military, aviation and project risk management.

Each identified hazard is assigned a likelihood category (e.g. frequent, probable, occasional, remote, improbable) and a severity category (e.g. negligible to catastrophic); the cell where they intersect gives a risk index that is compared against acceptability criteria. Standards such as MIL-STD-882 (system safety) and aviation safety management frameworks use formalized matrices of this kind.

Criticism

Risk matrices are widely used but have well-documented limitations: the categorical scales compress wide quantitative ranges, different assessors score inconsistently, and the multiplication of ordinal categories can produce rankings worse than random for some hazard sets — critiques developed notably by Louis Anthony Cox. Analysts therefore often use matrices for screening and communication rather than as a substitute for quantitative analysis.
