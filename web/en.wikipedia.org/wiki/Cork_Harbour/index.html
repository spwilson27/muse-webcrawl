Cork Harbour

Location: County Cork, Ireland

Coordinates: 51.85°N, 8.27°W

River sources: River Lee, Glashaboy, Owennacurra

Type: Natural harbour / ria

Cork Harbour (Irish: Cuan Chorcaí) is a natural harbour and river estuary at the mouth of the River Lee in County Cork, on the south coast of Ireland. It is often described locally as one of the largest natural harbours in the world by navigable area. The harbour was formed as a ria — a drowned river valley — after the last ice age, and its sheltered, deep waters have made it strategically and commercially important for centuries.

Geography

The harbour is entered from the Celtic Sea between Roches Point and Crosshaven, and opens into the broad Lower Harbour containing Great Island — with the town of Cobh on its southern shore — as well as Haulbowline Island, Spike Island, and Little Island. The Upper Harbour, connected by the channels of Passage West and the narrow straits around Great Island, leads up the Lee to the city of Cork itself. Other harbour towns include Ringaskiddy, Carrigaline, Monkstown, Whitegate, and Aghada.

History

Cork's port grew with the provisions trade of the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, exporting butter, beef, and pork to Britain, Europe, and the Americas. The harbour's defences — Camden Fort Meagher, Fort Carlisle (now Fort Davis), and the star fort on Spike Island — reflect its military significance; Spike Island served in turn as a fortress, prison, and convict depot from which convicts were transported to Australia. Cobh (as Queenstown) was the great Irish emigration port of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, the last port of call of the Titanic in 1912, and close to where the Lusitania was torpedoed in 1915. Under the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 the harbour defences remained British "Treaty ports" until 1938. Haulbowline hosts the headquarters of the Irish Naval Service.

Modern port and industry

The Port of Cork is Ireland's second-largest port by tonnage in many years and has concentrated deep-water berths at Ringaskiddy and Tivoli, handling container, bulk, ferry, and cruise traffic, including large cruise liners calling at Cobh. The lower harbour is also a centre of Ireland's pharmaceutical and chemical industry cluster, while the harbour as a whole is designated a Special Protection Area for its wintering wildfowl and waders.
