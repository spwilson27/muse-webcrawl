Suzdal

Country: Russia
Oblast: Vladimir Oblast
First mentioned: 1024
Population: ~9,300
Status: Golden Ring town; UNESCO sites nearby

Suzdal is a town in Vladimir Oblast, Russia, about 26 km north of Vladimir and 180 km north-east of Moscow. It is one of the oldest Russian towns and a celebrated member of the Golden Ring, preserved almost as a living museum of medieval architecture: despite a population under ten thousand it contains more than 40 historically important churches and several monasteries.

History

Suzdal first appears in chronicles in 1024. In the 12th century, under Yuri Dolgorukiy and his son Andrei Bogolyubsky, it became the capital of the Rostov-Suzdal principality, the political core from which Muscovy later grew. The Mongol invasion of 1238 burned the town, and it gradually lost importance to Moscow, but it flourished as a religious centre with wealthy monasteries patronised by princes and tsars. In the Soviet period Suzdal was spared industrial development, and in 1967 it was designated a protected museum town.

Sights

The Suzdal Kremlin contains the Cathedral of the Nativity (1222–1225), one of the White Monuments of Vladimir and Suzdal UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Other landmarks include the Monastery of Saint Euthymius, whose bells are rung in concert; the wooden architecture museum with churches and peasant houses moved from across the region; and the market square with its trading arcades. The nearby village of Kideksha preserves the Church of Saints Boris and Gleb (1152), among the oldest white-stone buildings in north-eastern Rus'.
