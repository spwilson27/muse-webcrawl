Eau de vie

Eau de vie (French for "water of life", plural eaux de vie) is a clear, colourless fruit brandy produced by fermentation and double distillation of fruit. In French usage the term covers a wide range of spirits distilled from fruits other than grapes; the equivalent German term is Schnaps, and in Alsace and Switzerland local varieties have long traditions.

Production

Ripe fruit is crushed and fermented into a low-alcohol mash, which is then distilled, traditionally twice in a copper pot still. Unlike grape brandies such as Cognac or Armagnac, eaux de vie are normally not aged in wood, so they retain the pure aroma of the fruit; they are rested in glass or stainless steel and bottled at typically 37.5–45% alcohol by volume.

Varieties

Common fruits used include pear (eau de vie de poire, notably Poire Williams), plum (mirabelle, quetsche), cherry (kirsch), raspberry (framboise), apple and apricot. Alsace is a major centre of production, and many eaux de vie carry appellation or geographical-indication protection in the European Union.

Serving

Eau de vie is traditionally served chilled as a digestif after a meal, in small quantities, and is also used in cooking and in some cocktails. A similar Scandinavian term, akvavit, derives from the same Latin phrase aqua vitae.
