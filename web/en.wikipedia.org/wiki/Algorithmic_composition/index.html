Algorithmic composition
FieldComputer music; music technology
Key methodsMarkov chains, generative grammars, cellular automata, neural networks, stochastic processes
PioneersLejaren Hiller, Iannis Xenakis, David Cope

Algorithmic composition is the technique of using algorithms to create music. Composers have employed formal procedures for centuries — from the musical dice games attributed to the eighteenth century, in which pre-written measures were assembled by dice rolls, to Guido of Arezzo's method of mapping vowels to pitches — but the field took off with the computer, which made it practical to execute complex generative rules.

History

In 1957 Lejaren Hiller and Leonard Isaacson produced the Illiac Suite for string quartet, generally considered the first computer-composed score, using Markov chains and rule-based selection. Iannis Xenakis applied probability distributions and stochastic processes to composition in works such as Pithoprakta. Later, David Cope's Experiments in Musical Intelligence (EMI) analysed corpora of existing works and recombined their patterns to compose in the style of composers such as Bach and Chopin.

Methods

Approaches include mathematical models (stochastic processes, Markov chains), knowledge-based systems encoding rules of harmony and counterpoint, generative grammars such as L-systems borrowed from the modelling of plant growth, evolutionary methods using genetic algorithms, cellular automata, and machine-learning models — recurrent neural networks and transformers trained on large music corpora. Algorithmic techniques underpin generative music, live coding and algorave performance, and adaptive video-game soundtracks.
