Witt vector

In mathematics, Witt vectors, introduced by Ernst Witt in the 1930s, are infinite sequences of elements of a commutative ring that provide a way to lift the ring to characteristic zero. The ring of Witt vectors W(R) is central to p-adic number theory and algebraic geometry.

Construction

Addition and multiplication of Witt vectors are given by universal polynomials with integer coefficients, defined via the ghost components. For a perfect field of characteristic p, the p-typical Witt vectors form a complete discrete valuation ring with residue field the original field — for example, the p-adic integers arise as Witt vectors of the finite field with p elements.

Applications

Witt vectors appear in the theory of formal groups, crystalline cohomology, de Rham–Witt cohomology, and the classification of abelian varieties. They also underlie modern prismatic cohomology.
