Blunt instrument
ClassBlunt weapon / tool
Injury typeBlunt trauma
ExamplesClub, baton, hammer, truncheon

A blunt instrument is an object used to cause blunt trauma — injury inflicted by impact without penetration of the skin. The term is used in forensic pathology, criminology and everyday language for weapons such as clubs, batons, hammers and other heavy, hard objects.

Forensic significance

Blunt force injuries include abrasions, contusions, lacerations and fractures. Investigators may be able to match patterned injuries to the shape of the striking object, and in homicide investigations "blunt instrument" is a common description where the exact weapon is unknown or improvised.

Figurative use

By extension, the phrase describes any crude, imprecise means of achieving an end — a policy or argument applied without subtlety or discrimination, as in "a blunt instrument of regulation".
