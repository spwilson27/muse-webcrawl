Sacrosanctum Concilium

Sacrosanctum Concilium, the Constitution on the Sacred Liturgy, is one of the constitutions of the Second Vatican Council. It was approved by the assembled bishops by a vote of 2,147 to 4 and promulgated by Pope Paul VI on 4 December 1963. The title is taken from the opening words of the Latin text, meaning "this sacred Council".

Background

The constitution was the fruit of the twentieth-century Liturgical Movement, which had sought a fuller and more conscious participation of the faithful in the Church's worship. It was the first of the Council's documents to be debated and the first to be promulgated.

Contents

The document sets out general principles for the restoration and promotion of the liturgy. Its most famous affirmation is that "full and active participation by all the people is the aim to be considered before all else" in liturgical reform (SC 14). It called for a revision of the liturgical books, permitted wider use of the vernacular languages in place of Latin, encouraged liturgical adaptation to local cultures (inculturation), and directed reforms of the Mass, the sacraments, the Divine Office, sacred music and sacred art.

"Mother Church earnestly desires that all the faithful should be led to that fully conscious, and active participation in liturgical celebrations which is demanded by the very nature of the liturgy." — Sacrosanctum Concilium, 14

Implementation

To carry out the reform, Paul VI established the Consilium ad exsequendam Constitutionem de Sacra Liturgia in 1964. Its work led to the revised Roman Missal of 1969, commonly associated with the Mass of Paul VI, and to revised rites for all the sacraments and the Liturgy of the Hours.
