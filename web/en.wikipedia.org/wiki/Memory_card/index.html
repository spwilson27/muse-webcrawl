Memory card

A memory card is an electronic data storage device used for storing digital information, typically using flash memory. Memory cards are commonly used in portable electronic devices such as digital cameras, mobile phones, video game consoles, and media players, providing removable, rewritable storage that retains data without power.

History

The development of flash memory at Toshiba in the 1980s, including work by Fujio Masuoka, laid the foundation for memory cards. Early formats included the PCMCIA (PC Card) flash cards of the early 1990s, followed by smaller formats including CompactFlash (1994), SmartMedia (1995), MultiMediaCard (1997), the Sony Memory Stick (1998), Secure Digital (SD, 1999), and xD-Picture Card (2002). A "format war" among manufacturers eventually settled in favour of the SD family, whose microSD variant became the dominant standard for mobile devices.

Formats

  • SD, SDHC, SDXC, SDUC – full-size and micro sizes; capacities from megabytes to terabytes, with speed classes and UHS/V bus ratings.
  • CompactFlash / CFexpress – long favoured in professional cameras; CFexpress uses NVMe over PCIe for very high speeds.
  • Memory Stick – Sony's proprietary format, now largely discontinued.
  • XQD – high-speed card format used in professional photo and video equipment.

Characteristics

Memory cards are rated by capacity and by read/write speed; video speed classes (such as V30, V60, and V90 on SD cards) guarantee minimum sustained write speeds for recording high-resolution video. Modern cards can reach capacities of 1 TB or more in the microSD format. Data is retained through floating-gate or charge-trap flash cells, and cards typically include controllers handling wear levelling and error correction.
