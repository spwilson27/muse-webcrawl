The Battle of Hjörungavágr (modern Hjørungavåg, in Møre og Romsdal, Norway) was a semi-legendary naval battle fought around 986 between the fleet of the Norwegian ruler Earl Hakon Sigurdsson and an invading fleet of the Jomsvikings, the warrior brotherhood of Jómsborg. The Jomsvikings were decisively defeated, and the battle is celebrated in the sagas as one of the great sea fights of the Viking Age.

Background

According to the Jómsvíkinga saga and other sources, the Danish king Harald Bluetooth and Earl Hakon came into conflict over Danish claims to Norway, and the Jomsvikings undertook an expedition to depose Hakon. The saga numbers the fleets very unequally — the Jomsvikings are said to have had 60 large ships against a much larger Norwegian levy fleet — though the figures are generally regarded as saga exaggeration.

The battle

The fleets met in the bay of Hjörungavágr. The Jomsvikings at first gained the advantage, but the tide turned when Earl Eric Hakonarson attacked the wing commanded by Sigvaldi, who eventually fled with part of the fleet. Saga tradition adds that a sudden hailstorm struck the Jomsvikings, attributed to Hakon's sacrifice to the goddess Thorgerd Hölgabrúðr. Bue Digre, wounded, leapt overboard with his treasure chests, while the young Vagn Åkesson and his surviving men were captured; Hakon spared Vagn after his captors proved unable to execute the defiant prisoners in the customary manner without incident.

Legacy

The battle ended the Jomsvikings as a major independent force and secured Hakon's rule over Norway for the remainder of his reign. The story became a favourite subject of saga literature and later Norwegian national romanticism.
