The Ridderzaal (Dutch for "Hall of the Knights") is the main building of the 13th-century Binnenhof complex in The Hague, Netherlands. Its great Gothic hall, framed by two towers, is used for the State Opening of Parliament, official royal receptions and other state occasions.

History

A hall on the site was begun under Floris IV, Count of Holland, and was rebuilt as the present great hall by his son Floris V around 1280–1290, modelled in part on Westminster Hall in London; with its enormous wooden roof spanning about 18 metres, it was among the largest halls of its kind in medieval Europe. After the counts of Holland moved away, the building declined in status: under the Dutch Republic it was used at one point as a book market and lottery hall, and in the 19th century it served as offices, a gymnasium and storage space.

Restoration and present use

The Ridderzaal was restored to a Gothic ideal in a major renovation completed in 1904 under architect Pierre Cuypers and J. van Lokhorst, which removed later accretions and furnished the hall with thrones, stained glass and coats of arms. Since then it has been the ceremonial centre of the Dutch state: each year on Prinsjesdag (the third Tuesday of September) the monarch travels there in the Golden Coach tradition to deliver the Speech from the Throne to a joint session of the States General. The hall is also used for state banquets, investitures in the Order of the Netherlands Lion, and international conferences.
