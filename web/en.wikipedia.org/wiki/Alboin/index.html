Alboin (530s – 28 June 572) was king of the Lombards from about 560 until his assassination in 572. During his reign the Lombards ended their migration by settling in Italy, which they had entered in 568 — the last of the great migrations of the Germanic peoples. Alboin defeated the Gepids in 567, and famously made a drinking cup from the skull of their king Cunimund.

Early reign

Alboin succeeded his father Audoin and consolidated Lombard power in Pannonia. In alliance with the Avars, he destroyed the Gepid kingdom in 567, killing King Cunimund in battle and taking Cunimund's daughter Rosamund as his wife.

Invasion of Italy

In 568 Alboin led the Lombards, together with other peoples, across the Julian Alps into northern Italy. Resistance was scattered: Aquileia, Milan (569), and eventually Pavia fell after a three-year siege in 572. Alboin thus founded the Lombard kingdom in Italy, though he never controlled the whole peninsula.

Death

According to Paul the Deacon, at a banquet in Verona Alboin forced Rosamund to drink from the skull cup made from her father's head. She plotted his murder with the king's foster brother Helmichis and the assassin Peredeo; Alboin was killed in his sleep on 28 June 572. He was succeeded by Cleph.

Legacy

Alboin's conquest reshaped Italy for two centuries, dividing it between Lombard and Byzantine territories. His skull-cup story became one of the most famous tales of the Migration Period.
