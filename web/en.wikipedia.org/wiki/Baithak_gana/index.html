Baithak gana (Sarnami Hindustani: बैठक गाना, "sitting music") is a form of music originating among the Indo-Surinamese community of Suriname, later carried to the Netherlands. It emerged from the music brought by Indian indentured labourers who arrived in Suriname from 1873 onwards, principally from the Bhojpuri-speaking regions of Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.

History

The name derives from baithak, a sitting gathering, because performers traditionally played seated on the floor. Baithak gana developed from Bhojpuri folk genres such as birha and from religious singing, adapting them to the conditions of the diaspora. Sung in Sarnami Hindustani, the local koiné of the Surinamese Hindustani community, it preserves linguistic and musical features that faded in India itself.

Music

The traditional ensemble consists of a singer who also plays the harmonium, accompanied by dholak (a double-headed hand drum) and dantaal, an idiophone of struck iron rods that provides the driving rhythm. Performances often involve a competitive exchange between two singers or groups, improvising verses on religious, romantic, or satirical themes. In the late twentieth century, baithak gana increasingly fused with modern styles, incorporating keyboards and drum machines, and became popular at weddings and cultural festivals in both Suriname and the Netherlands.

Cultural significance

Baithak gana is regarded as a key marker of Indo-Surinamese identity, maintaining a link to the ancestral culture of the indentured labourers while evolving into a distinctively Surinamese art form. Notable performers include singer and harmonium virtuosos who popularised the genre through radio broadcasts and recordings in Paramaribo.
