Four Pests campaign

The Four Pests campaign (Chinese: 除四害, Chú Sìhài) was a mass hygiene and pest-eradication campaign in the People's Republic of China, initiated in 1958 as part of the Great Leap Forward. Citizens were mobilized to eliminate four pests: rats, flies, mosquitoes, and sparrows (specifically the Eurasian tree sparrow).

The sparrow campaign

Sparrows were targeted because they ate grain seeds. Millions of people banged pots and pans to frighten sparrows into continuous flight until they fell dead from exhaustion; nests were destroyed and eggs broken. By some estimates hundreds of millions of sparrows were killed, driving the species close to extinction in China.

Ecological consequences

Without sparrows to eat them, insect pests — especially locusts — multiplied and devastated crops, contributing to the ecological imbalance that worsened the Great Chinese Famine (1959–1961). In 1960 Mao Zedong halted the sparrow campaign (bedbugs replaced sparrows among the four pests) and China imported some 250,000 sparrows from the Soviet Union to restore the population.

The episode is frequently cited as a classic example of an ecological intervention backfiring through unintended consequences.
