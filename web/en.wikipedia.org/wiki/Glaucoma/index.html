Glaucoma is a group of eye diseases that result in damage to the optic nerve (and retinal ganglion cells) and cause vision loss. The most common type is primary open-angle glaucoma, in which the drainage angle for the aqueous humour remains open but drains too slowly; less common is angle-closure glaucoma, in which the angle is blocked, and normal-tension glaucoma, in which damage occurs at statistically normal pressures. Vision loss from glaucoma, once it has occurred, is permanent; the disease has been called the "silent thief of sight" because peripheral vision is usually lost gradually and unnoticed until late in the course of the disease.

Signs, causes, and diagnosis

Most forms of open-angle glaucoma develop slowly and without pain or early symptoms. Acute angle-closure glaucoma, by contrast, is a medical emergency presenting with sudden eye pain, blurred vision, halos around lights, redness, nausea, and a mid-dilated unreactive pupil. The major risk factor is increased intraocular pressure (IOP, typically above 21 mmHg), caused by increased production or decreased outflow of aqueous humour; other risk factors include older age, a family history of the condition, African or Asian ancestry (depending on type), and high myopia. Diagnosis combines tonometry (pressure measurement), examination of the optic disc for characteristic cupping, visual field testing, and imaging of the retinal nerve fibre layer.

Treatment

Treatment aims to lower intraocular pressure, the only modifiable risk factor. First-line therapy is usually prescription eye drops — prostaglandin analogues such as latanoprost, beta blockers, alpha agonists, or carbonic anhydrase inhibitors. Laser procedures (trabeculoplasty for open-angle disease; peripheral iridotomy for angle closure) and surgery (trabeculectomy, tube shunts, and minimally invasive glaucoma surgery) are used when drops are insufficient. Glaucoma is the second-leading cause of blindness worldwide after cataracts, and because damage is irreversible, early detection through routine eye examinations is emphasised, particularly for those over 40 and those with affected relatives.
