Total least squares

FieldStatistics, numerical analysis
Also known asErrors-in-variables regression, orthogonal regression

Total least squares (TLS), also known in statistics as errors-in-variables regression, is a least-squares data-modeling technique in which errors are allowed in all variables, not just the dependent (observed) variable as in ordinary least squares. Instead of minimising vertical residuals, TLS minimises the orthogonal (perpendicular) distances from the data points to the fitted model.

Method

For a linear model, the TLS problem can be solved using the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the augmented data matrix: the solution is derived from the right singular vector corresponding to the smallest singular value. The approach goes back to work by Adcock and Pearson in the nineteenth century, and its modern matrix formulation was given by Gene Golub and Charles Van Loan in 1980. In one dimension the method corresponds to Deming regression and, when variances are equal, to fitting the first principal component line.

Applications

Total least squares is used in signal processing, system identification, computer vision and any setting — such as measurement of two noisy quantities — where the independent variables cannot be treated as exact.
