Canadian Eskimo Dog

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Canadian Eskimo Dog or Canadian Inuit Dog (Inuktitut: Qimmiq) is a breed of working dog from the Canadian Arctic. It is one of the oldest and rarest indigenous breeds of North America, traditionally used by Inuit for sled pulling, hunting, and protection against polar bears. It is the official animal of the territory of Nunavut.

Characteristics

The breed is a large spitz-type dog, powerfully built with a thick double coat in many colours, a curled tail, and upright ears. Males stand roughly 58–70 cm at the shoulder and may weigh 30–40 kg. The dogs are renowned for endurance, able to haul heavy loads long distances on very little food, and for hardiness in extreme cold.

History and decline

Dogs of this type accompanied the Thule ancestors of modern Inuit across the Arctic about a thousand years ago and were essential to Inuit survival. In the mid-20th century the population, once estimated in the tens of thousands, collapsed owing to disease, the adoption of snowmobiles, and controversial killings of sled dogs by authorities in the 1950s and 1960s — events later investigated by the Qikiqtani Truth Commission. By the 1970s only a few hundred pedigree animals survived; a breeding programme led by William Carpenter and the Canadian Eskimo Dog Foundation in Manitoba, with support from researchers and breeders, worked to rebuild the breed from remaining stock in the eastern Arctic.
