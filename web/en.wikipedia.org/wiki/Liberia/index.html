Liberia, officially the Republic of Liberia, is a country on the West African coast. It is bordered by Sierra Leone to its northwest, Guinea to its north, Ivory Coast to its east, and the Atlantic Ocean to its south and southwest. Liberia has a population of around 5.4 million and an area of 111,369 square kilometres. English is the official language, but more than twenty indigenous languages are spoken. The country's capital and largest city is Monrovia, named after US President James Monroe. Liberia began in the early nineteenth century as a colony for free-born and formerly enslaved Black Americans, sponsored by the American Colonization Society, and declared independence on 26 July 1847 — one of only two African countries, with Ethiopia, to preserve their sovereignty during the European Scramble for Africa.

History

The Americo-Liberian settlers — some 19,900 immigrants and recaptured Africans settled between 1822 and the American Civil War — established a republic modelled on the United States, but Americo-Liberians, never more than about five percent of the population, dominated the indigenous majority politically for over a century through the True Whig Party, which held power continuously from 1878 to 1980. This era brought the Firestone rubber concession (1926), one of the world's largest rubber plantations, and iron-ore mining wealth, alongside deep inequality. In 1980 Master Sergeant Samuel Doe seized power in a coup, ending Americo-Liberian rule; his regime's corruption and ethnic favouritism led to the First Liberian Civil War (1989–1997), launched by Charles Taylor's NPFL. Taylor won the 1997 election but faced a new rebellion, and the Second Liberian Civil War (1999–2003) ended with his exile and a UN peacekeeping mission; together the wars killed roughly 250,000 people and devastated the economy. Taylor was later convicted of war crimes in Sierra Leone by the Special Court and sentenced to 50 years' imprisonment.

Recent history

Ellen Johnson Sirleaf, elected in 2005, became Africa's first elected female head of state and oversaw reconstruction, debt relief, and the response to the West African Ebola epidemic of 2014–2016, which killed nearly 5,000 Liberians. Former football star George Weah won the 2017 election, achieving the country's first peaceful democratic transfer of power in decades, and lost the 2023 election run-off to Joseph Boakai, who took office in January 2024.

Geography and economy

Liberia has a hot, humid equatorial climate with heavy coastal rainfall and a coastline of mangrove swamps giving way to forested hills and plateaux inland. It retains much of the Upper Guinean rainforest, including Sapo National Park. The economy depends on rubber, iron ore, timber, gold, and diamonds, and on its flag-of-convenience ship registry — one of the two largest in the world by tonnage. Despite its resources, Liberia remains among the world's poorest countries, with infrastructure still recovering from the civil wars and Ebola crisis.

Culture

Liberian culture blends indigenous traditions of sixteen major ethnic groups — including the Kpelle (the largest), Bassa, Gio, Mano, and Kru — with the Southern-US-influenced culture of the Americo-Liberian settlers, visible in architecture, dress, and the Liberian variety of English. About 86% of Liberians are Christian and about 12% Muslim, with traditional beliefs also practised.
