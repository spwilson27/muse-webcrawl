Official language

An official language is a language having certain rights to be used in defined situations, often in the operations of government. The term is in contradistinction to a working language, which is used for day-to-day operations but not formally defined. Official languages are recognised in some countries' constitutions, statutes, or by government proclamation.

Some countries have a single official language, such as France; others, like Canada (English and French), India, South Africa (12 official languages), and Switzerland, have several. Some countries, including the United States and Australia, have no official language at the federal level, though a dominant language is used de facto. The United Nations has six official languages: Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, and Spanish.
