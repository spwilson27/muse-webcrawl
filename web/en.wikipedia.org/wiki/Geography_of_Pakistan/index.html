Geography of Pakistan

The geography of Pakistan encompasses a wide variety of landscapes, from the high mountains of the north to the Arabian Sea coast in the south. Pakistan covers about 881,913 square kilometres, making it the 33rd-largest country.

Northern Pakistan contains part of the Himalayas, Karakoram and Hindu Kush ranges, including K2, the world's second-highest mountain. The Indus River and its tributaries flow the length of the country and support extensive irrigated agriculture on the Punjab and Sindh plains.

Climate

Much of Pakistan is arid or semi-arid; the Thar Desert lies in the southeast and the Balochistan plateau in the west is dry and mountainous. The monsoon brings summer rains, while the north experiences cold winters with heavy snowfall.
