The Culinary Institute of America (CIA) is a private culinary and hospitality college headquartered in Hyde Park, New York. Founded in 1946 in New Haven, Connecticut, to train returning World War II veterans, it is widely regarded as one of the world's leading culinary schools.

History

The school began as the New Haven Restaurant Institute and moved to the former St. Andrew-on-Hudson Jesuit seminary in Hyde Park in 1972. Its main building, Roth Hall, houses several student-staffed, public restaurants. Over the decades the CIA expanded from a vocational cooking program into an accredited college offering bachelor's and master's degrees in culinary arts, baking and pastry arts, food business management, hospitality and related fields.

Campuses and programs

Besides Hyde Park, the CIA operates campuses in San Antonio, Texas (CIA San Antonio), Napa, California (CIA at Copia and the former Greystone), New York City, and Singapore. Students gain hands-on experience in teaching kitchens and in the institute's restaurants, including The Bocuse Restaurant, named for chef Paul Bocuse.

Notable alumni

Graduates include Anthony Bourdain, Grant Achatz, Charlie Palmer, Cat Cora, Michael Symon, Anne Burrell and many other prominent chefs and food writers.
