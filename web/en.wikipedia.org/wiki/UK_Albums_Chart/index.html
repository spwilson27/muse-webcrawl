UK Albums Chart

The UK Albums Chart is a weekly record chart compiled by the Official Charts Company (OCC) ranking the best-selling albums in the United Kingdom. It currently lists the top 100 albums based on combined sales of physical formats, digital downloads and streaming-equivalent units. The chart week runs from Friday to Thursday, reflecting the global release day adopted in 2015.

History

The first UK albums chart was published by Record Mirror in July 1956, listing the top five albums; the South Pacific soundtrack was an early long-running number one. Over the decades the chart was carried by various music papers and compiled by different organisations — including BMRB, Gallup (from 1983) and Millward Brown — before the Official Charts Company assumed responsibility. The chart's depth grew from a top 5 to the familiar Top 75 and later Top 100.

Compilation

Since February 2015, album-equivalent streams have counted toward the chart: the twelve most-streamed tracks from an album (with the top two tracks down-weighted) are converted at a rate of 1,000 streams to one album sale. The record for the most weeks at number one is held by the South Pacific soundtrack historically, while ABBA's Gold: Greatest Hits is among the longest-charting albums overall. The Beatles hold the record for the most number-one albums by an act, and Queen's Greatest Hits (1981) is the best-selling album in UK chart history.
