Ship's boat

A ship's boat is a utility boat carried by a larger vessel for transport, communication and work away from the ship. Ship's boats have included rowing and sailing craft such as launches, pinnaces, cutters, gigs, whalers and dinghies; modern ships carry motorised workboats and rigid inflatable boats.

Historical use

In the age of sail, a warship's boats performed duties impossible for the ship herself: ferrying men, water and provisions in harbour, landing parties ashore, laying out anchors in kedging, towing the ship in calms, and sounding channels. Boats were stowed nested on deck amidships or hung from davits, and were commanded by midshipmen or junior officers, for whom boat service was an important training and a common path to distinction — or to disaster in cutting-out expeditions.

Modern use

Contemporary ship's boats serve as tenders, rescue craft, and platforms for boarding parties, hydrographic survey and harbour work. Regulations also require ships to carry lifeboats and rescue boats, which are related but distinct, purpose-built survival craft.
