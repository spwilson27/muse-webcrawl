New York City blackout of 1977

The New York City blackout of 1977 was an electricity blackout that affected most of New York City on July 13–14, 1977. Unlike the Northeast blackout of 1965, which was widely remembered for orderly behaviour, the 1977 blackout was accompanied by widespread looting and arson during a period of financial crisis and a summer heat wave.

Cause

A series of lightning strikes on transmission lines and substations in Westchester County on the evening of July 13 tripped lines carrying power from the Indian Point nuclear plant and upstate. Through a sequence of equipment failures and operator decisions over about an hour, the Consolidated Edison system became isolated and collapsed at about 9:36 p.m., leaving some nine million people without power, in places for up to 25 hours.

Effects

During the night, looting and arson spread across poor neighbourhoods including the South Bronx, Harlem, Bedford-Stuyvesant and Bushwick. Roughly 1,600 stores were damaged in looting and over a thousand fires were set; more than 3,700 people were arrested. Estimated costs ran to hundreds of millions of dollars. The blackout struck while the city was gripped by fear of the "Son of Sam" serial killer and near bankruptcy, deepening its image of decline. In hip-hop history, the blackout is often credited with spreading DJ equipment looted from electronics stores, accelerating the genre's growth in the Bronx.
