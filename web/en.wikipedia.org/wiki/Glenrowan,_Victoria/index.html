Glenrowan is a small town in the Wangaratta local government area in Victoria, Australia. It is located 236 kilometres north-east of Melbourne on the Hume Highway, at the foot of the Warby Ranges near Wangaratta. The town is famous as the site of the final siege and capture of the bushranger Ned Kelly and his gang in June 1880.

History

Glenrowan was established around the railway station opened on the North East railway line in 1873 and was named after James and George Rowan, farmers in the district. In the early hours of 28 June 1880, the Kelly Gang took over Ann Jones's Glenrowan Inn, intending to derail a police train; the plan failed when schoolteacher Thomas Curnow warned the train. In the ensuing siege and shootout, gang members Joe Byrne, Dan Kelly and Steve Hart were killed, and Ned Kelly — wearing his famous bullet-proof armour — was captured after being shot in the legs. The siege site, the railway station and a large statue of Kelly in armour are now major tourist attractions, and the town trades heavily on its Kelly heritage.

Geography and economy

The surrounding district is agricultural, with grazing, cropping and viticulture; the Warby-Ovens National Park lies to the west. Glenrowan also gives its name to a wine region within the North East Victoria zone.
