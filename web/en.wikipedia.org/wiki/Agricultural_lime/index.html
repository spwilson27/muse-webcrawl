Agricultural lime

Agricultural lime, also called aglime, is a soil conditioner made from pulverized limestone or chalk. Its principal active component is calcium carbonate; the material may also contain dolomite (calcium magnesium carbonate). Agricultural lime is used to raise the pH of acidic soils, a practice known as liming.

Use

Over time, soils become acidic through leaching of basic cations, nitrogen fertiliser use, and acid rain. Excess acidity reduces the availability of nutrients such as phosphorus and molybdenum and can release toxic levels of aluminium and manganese. Spreading aglime neutralises this acidity, supplies calcium (and magnesium when dolomitic lime is used), and improves the activity of soil microorganisms and the effectiveness of many herbicides and fertilisers.

Quality and application

The effectiveness of a liming material is expressed by its calcium carbonate equivalent (CCE) relative to pure calcium carbonate, together with fineness of grinding: finer particles react faster with the soil. Lime is normally applied with a tractor-drawn spreader and incorporated into the topsoil; the amount required is determined by a soil test measuring current pH and buffer pH. Agricultural lime should not be confused with caustic quicklime (burnt lime) or hydrated lime, which react much more aggressively.
