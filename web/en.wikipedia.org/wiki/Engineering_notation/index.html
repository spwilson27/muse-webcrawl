Engineering notation

Engineering notation is a version of scientific notation in which the exponent of ten is always a multiple of three, so that the power of ten corresponds to an SI prefix such as kilo-, mega-, milli- or micro-. The coefficient (mantissa) consequently lies between 1 and 1000 (strictly, 1 ≤ coefficient < 1000).

Many calculators have an "ENG" mode that steps exponents in multiples of three.

Examples

The number 12,300,000 is written 12.3 × 106 in both scientific and engineering notation (12.3 million, or 12.3 M with the mega prefix). The number 0.000045 is written 4.5 × 10−5 in scientific notation but 45 × 10−6 in engineering notation — 45 micro-units. This alignment with SI prefixes makes engineering notation natural for electronics, where component values are specified in steps such as kilo-ohms and picofarads.

Comparison

Engineering notation differs from normalised scientific notation, in which the exponent may be any integer and the coefficient lies between 1 and 10. It sacrifices a little uniformity for direct readability in terms of metric prefixes.
