RIM-7 Sea Sparrow

Type: Surface-to-air missile
Origin: United States
Manufacturer: Raytheon
Length: 3.66 m (12 ft)
Diameter: 0.2 m (8 in)
Speed: Mach 2.5+
Guidance: Semi-active radar homing

The RIM-7 Sea Sparrow is a US ship-borne short-range anti-aircraft and anti-missile weapon system, primarily intended for defense against anti-ship missiles. It is derived from the AIM-7 Sparrow air-to-air missile and entered service in the 1970s.

Development

In the late 1960s the US Navy sought a lightweight point-defense system that could be fitted to smaller vessels. The solution adapted the proven AIM-7 Sparrow, fired first from modified ASROC launchers and later from the dedicated eight-cell Mk 29 launcher. The missile uses semi-active radar homing, requiring the launch ship to illuminate the target.

Variants and successors

Improved models added better guidance, folding wings for the box launcher, and compatibility with vertical launching systems. The Sea Sparrow Missile Consortium of NATO navies oversaw development. Its replacement, the RIM-162 Evolved Sea Sparrow Missile (ESSM), was developed from the RIM-7 line and offers greater range and manoeuvrability; ESSM Block 2 adds an active radar seeker.

Operators

The system has been used by the US Navy and many allied navies, and was also adapted into shore-based air defense in modified forms.
