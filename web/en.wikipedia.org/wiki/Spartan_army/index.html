Spartan army

Spartan hoplites in the phalanx

The Spartan army was the standing army of the polis of Sparta, one of the two leading city-states of classical Greece. Unlike other Greek states, whose armies were citizen militias, Sparta's full citizens — the Spartiates — were professional soldiers, freed from all other labour by the work of the enslaved helots and devoted from childhood, through the agoge training system, to military life. The result was the most feared land force in Greece from the sixth to the fourth centuries BC.

Organisation and training

Boys entered the agoge at seven, living communally under harsh discipline, minimal rations, and constant physical training; at twenty they joined a mess (syssitia) of about fifteen companions, with whom they ate and campaigned for life. The army fought as hoplites in the phalanx: armoured infantry in close formation, each man protected by a large round shield (aspis) bearing the lambda (Λ) for Lacedaemon, armed with a long thrusting spear (dory) and a short sword (xiphos). Units comprised enomotiai (files of about 32–36 men), grouped upward into pentekostyes, lochoi, and morai. Two hereditary kings, one of whom commanded in the field, led the army.

History

The army was forged in the Messenian Wars (8th–7th centuries BC), which gave Sparta its helot population and militarised constitution, traditionally credited to the lawgiver Lycurgus. At Thermopylae (480 BC) King Leonidas and his 300 Spartiates famously fought to the death; at Plataea (479 BC) Sparta fielded 5,000 Spartiates and 5,000 perioikoi in the largest Greek army of the Persian Wars. Victory over Athens in the Peloponnesian War (431–404 BC) made Sparta master of Greece.

Decline followed: the citizen body shrank dramatically (oliganthropia) as land concentrated in fewer hands, and the catastrophic defeat by Thebes at Leuctra (371 BC) — where some 400 of 700 Spartiates fell — broke Spartan power permanently. Sparta retained military prestige into the Hellenistic age but never recovered its classical strength.
