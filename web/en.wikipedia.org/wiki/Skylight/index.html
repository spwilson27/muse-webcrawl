Skylight

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Skylight
A roof window in a pitched roof

A skylight (sometimes called a rooflight) is a light-permitting structure or window, usually made of transparent or translucent glass, that forms all or part of the roof space of a building for daylighting and ventilation purposes. Skylights admit more light per unit area than vertical windows because overhead glazing receives light from a greater portion of the sky vault and is less obstructed by nearby buildings and trees.

History

Open skylights were used in Ancient Roman architecture, such as the oculus of the Pantheon. Glazed skylights became common in the Industrial Age as cast-plate glass manufacturing improved. In the mid-20th century, mass-produced unit skylights brought the feature to ordinary houses and commercial buildings; Danish entrepreneur Villum Kann Rasmussen founded the company that became VELUX, the best-known manufacturer of roof windows.

Types

Fixed unit skylights consist of a structural perimeter frame supporting glazing infill and do not open. Ventilating skylights can be opened, manually or by motor, and are common in bathrooms and kitchens where they release moisture. Tubular daylighting devices (light tubes) use a reflective tube to carry daylight from a small roof dome to a ceiling diffuser. Large roof structures also include ridge skylights, pyramid and barrel-vault systems, and custom structural skylights spanning atria.

Materials and performance

Glazing is usually glass or plastic such as acrylic or polycarbonate. Plastics are lighter and more impact-resistant but scratch more easily and may discolour. Modern units use double or triple glazing, low-emissivity coatings, and argon fills to limit heat loss in winter and solar gain in summer. Proper flashing and curb design are essential to prevent leaks, historically the most common complaint about skylights.

Benefits and drawbacks

Skylights reduce the need for artificial lighting, provide views of the sky, and can support passive ventilation when operable. Drawbacks include potential overheating in summer, heat loss in winter, glare, condensation, and the maintenance burden of roof penetrations. Energy codes in several jurisdictions limit total skylight area relative to roof area.
