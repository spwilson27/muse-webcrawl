Unitary matrix

DefinitionU*U = UU* = I
GroupUnitary group U(n)
Real analogueOrthogonal matrix
ApplicationsQuantum mechanics, signal processing

In linear algebra, an invertible complex square matrix U is unitary if its conjugate transpose U* is also its inverse — that is, if U*U = UU* = I, where I is the identity matrix. Equivalently, the columns (and rows) of a unitary matrix form an orthonormal basis of ℂn with respect to the usual inner product. Unitary matrices are the complex analogues of real orthogonal matrices.

Unitary transformations preserve lengths and inner products

Properties

A unitary matrix preserves the Hermitian inner product and hence lengths and angles: ‖Ux‖ = ‖x‖ for every vector x. Its eigenvalues all lie on the unit circle in the complex plane, so each has absolute value 1, and its determinant likewise has absolute value 1. Unitary matrices are normal, and therefore are diagonalisable by a unitary matrix according to the spectral theorem. The set of all n × n unitary matrices forms a group under multiplication, the unitary group U(n), and those with determinant 1 form the special unitary group SU(n).

Applications

Unitary matrices are fundamental in quantum mechanics, where the time evolution of a closed system is described by a unitary operator, ensuring that total probability is conserved; quantum logic gates are likewise unitary. They appear throughout physics and engineering in the QR decomposition, the discrete Fourier transform matrix, and scattering theory (the S-matrix).
