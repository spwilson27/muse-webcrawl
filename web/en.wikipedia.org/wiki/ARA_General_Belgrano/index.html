ARA General Belgrano

ARA General Belgrano (C-4) was an Argentine Navy light cruiser in service from 1951 until 1982. Originally built for the United States Navy as USS Phoenix (CL-46), a Brooklyn-class cruiser, she survived the attack on Pearl Harbor and served in the Second World War before being sold to Argentina in 1951. She was torpedoed and sunk by the British nuclear submarine HMS Conqueror during the Falklands War on 2 May 1982, with the loss of 323 lives — just over half of all Argentine military deaths in the war.

Early service

As USS Phoenix, the cruiser was in Pearl Harbor during the Japanese attack of 7 December 1941 but escaped undamaged, and went on to serve extensively in the Pacific, earning nine battle stars. Decommissioned in 1946, she was sold to Argentina in October 1951 along with her sister ship, which became ARA Diecisiete de Octubre. Renamed General Belgrano in 1956, she was the first ship in the world to be fitted with the Sea Cat anti-aircraft missile system in 1968.

Sinking

During the Falklands War, General Belgrano formed part of a pincer movement against the British task force. On 2 May 1982, HMS Conqueror fired three Mk 8 torpedoes, two of which struck the cruiser, which sank within an hour. Of the 1,093 crew aboard, 323 were killed. The sinking was controversial because it occurred outside the exclusion zone declared by Britain, although the ship was later confirmed by the Argentine Navy to have been a legitimate target engaged in operations against the task force.
