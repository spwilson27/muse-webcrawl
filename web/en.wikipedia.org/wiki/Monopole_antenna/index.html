Monopole antenna

A monopole antenna is a class of radio antenna consisting of a straight rod-shaped conductor, often mounted perpendicularly over some type of conductive surface, called a ground plane. The driving signal from the transmitter is applied between the lower end of the monopole element and the ground plane. This contrasts with a dipole antenna, which consists of two identical rod conductors with the signal applied between the two halves.

Operation

By the method of image charges, the ground plane acts as a mirror, so a monopole of length one quarter wavelength (λ/4) behaves like one half of a dipole. The common quarter-wave monopole has a radiation resistance of about 36.5 ohms and must be fed against an effective ground. Its radiation pattern is omnidirectional in the plane perpendicular to the rod, with maximum radiation at the horizon when mounted over a good ground, making it well suited to terrestrial communication.

Ground system

For low- and medium-frequency transmitting antennas, the "ground plane" is usually the Earth itself, supplemented by a radial system of buried wires to reduce losses. On vehicles and handheld devices, the metal body or chassis serves as the ground plane, as in the familiar whip antenna.

Applications

Monopoles are widely used as mast radiators for AM broadcasting, as whip antennas on cars and portable radios, and as ground-plane antennas for VHF and UHF services. Variants include the inverted-F antenna used in mobile phones and the folded monopole.
