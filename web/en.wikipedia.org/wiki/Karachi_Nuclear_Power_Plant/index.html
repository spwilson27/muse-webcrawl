Karachi Nuclear Power Plant

[Image placeholder: Karachi Nuclear Power Plant, coastal view]

The Karachi Nuclear Power Plant (KANUPP) is a nuclear power complex near Paradise Point on the coast of the Arabian Sea, about 25 kilometres west of Karachi, Pakistan. It was the first nuclear power plant in Pakistan and among the first in the Muslim world. The complex is operated by the Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission.

Unit 1

KANUPP-1 was a Canadian-supplied CANDU pressurised heavy-water reactor with a gross capacity of about 137 megawatts electric. Construction began in the 1960s with Canadian assistance, and the unit began commercial operation in 1972. After Canada withdrew support following India's 1974 nuclear test, Pakistan operated and maintained the plant largely on its own. The unit was permanently shut down in 2021 after nearly five decades of service.

Units 2 and 3

Two much larger Chinese-supplied Hualong One (HPR1000) pressurised water reactors, each of about 1,100 megawatts, were built at the site as KANUPP-2 and KANUPP-3. Construction began in 2015 and 2016 respectively; both units entered commercial operation in 2021–2022, greatly expanding Pakistan's nuclear generating capacity and making Karachi the country's largest nuclear power site.

Significance

The plant supplies electricity to the Karachi grid and is a symbol of Pakistan's civil nuclear programme, developed despite the country's position outside the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty and under International Atomic Energy Agency safeguards.
