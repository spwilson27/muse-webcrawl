Measurement uncertainty

In metrology, measurement uncertainty is the expression of the statistical dispersion of the values attributed to a measured quantity. All measurements are subject to uncertainty, and a measurement result is complete only when accompanied by a quantitative statement of its uncertainty. The uncertainty reflects doubt about the exactness of the result — it does not imply that the measurement is wrong.

Concepts

Uncertainty arises from random effects (such as noise and limited resolution) and from imperfectly corrected systematic effects (such as calibration errors). Uncertainty is distinct from error, the difference between a measured value and the true value, which is usually unknown. In the modern framework of the Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM), uncertainty is characterised by a standard deviation, called the standard uncertainty.

Evaluation

Standard uncertainties are evaluated by two methods. Type A evaluation uses statistical analysis of repeated observations. Type B evaluation uses other knowledge — calibration certificates, instrument specifications, prior data. For a quantity calculated from several measured inputs, individual standard uncertainties are combined by the law of propagation of uncertainty, involving partial derivatives and correlations, to give a combined standard uncertainty. Multiplying by a coverage factor k (commonly k = 2) gives an expanded uncertainty corresponding to a stated coverage probability, often about 95 per cent.
