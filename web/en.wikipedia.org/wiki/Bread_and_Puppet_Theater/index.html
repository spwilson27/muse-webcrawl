The Bread and Puppet Theater is a politically radical puppet theatre company founded in New York City in 1963 by German-born sculptor and choreographer Peter Schumann. It is famous for its enormous papier-mâché puppets, its street performances against war and injustice, and for serving free bread with aioli to audiences — reflecting Schumann's belief that theatre, like bread, is a necessity.

History

Beginning on New York's Lower East Side, the company became prominent in protests against the Vietnam War, its giant puppets becoming icons of the American peace movement. In 1974 the theatre moved to a farm in Glover, in Vermont's Northeast Kingdom, where it hosted the annual "Our Domestic Resurrection Circus" festival, drawing tens of thousands of visitors until the festival ended in 1998.

Work today

The company continues to tour and to perform each summer in Glover, where its museum houses one of the world's largest collections of giant puppets. Its aesthetic of "cheap art" — art made from inexpensive materials, for everyone — remains influential in political street theatre worldwide.
