DefinitionHeat absorbed by a gas relative to CO₂ over a set period
Standard horizon100 years (GWP100)
Reference gasCarbon dioxide (GWP = 1)

Global warming potential (GWP) is a measure of how much heat a greenhouse gas traps in the atmosphere over a specific time horizon, relative to the same mass of carbon dioxide, whose GWP is defined as 1. It was developed by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) to allow different greenhouse gases to be compared and aggregated as CO₂ equivalents in emissions inventories and climate policy.

Calculation

GWP depends on how strongly the gas absorbs infrared radiation and how long it persists in the atmosphere. Because gases decay at different rates, values depend on the time horizon chosen: methane, with an atmospheric lifetime of about 12 years, has a GWP of roughly 80 over 20 years but only about 27–30 over 100 years. The Kyoto Protocol and Paris Agreement reporting conventionally use 100-year values from IPCC assessment reports.

Criticism and alternatives

Critics note that a single GWP100 number can misrepresent the warming effect of short-lived gases, overstating the long-term impact of constant methane sources. Alternatives include the global temperature change potential (GTP) and GWP*, which relate methane emissions rates to CO₂-warming-equivalent emissions.
