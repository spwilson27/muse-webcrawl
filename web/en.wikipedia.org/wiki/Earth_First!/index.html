Earth First!

Earth First! is a radical environmental advocacy group that emerged in the southwestern United States in 1980. It was founded by Dave Foreman, Mike Roselle, Howie Wolke, Bart Koehler, and Ron Kezar in response to what they saw as the compromises of mainstream environmental organizations. Its motto is "No compromise in defense of Mother Earth."

The movement is decentralized, structured as a network of autonomous local groups rather than a hierarchical organization. Earth First! is associated with deep ecology and biocentrism, holding that all life has intrinsic value independent of its usefulness to humans.

Activities

Earth First! adherents have used tactics including civil disobedience, tree sitting, road blockades, and — controversially — "monkeywrenching," a term borrowed from Edward Abbey's novel The Monkey Wrench Gang (1975) for acts of sabotage against environmentally damaging machinery and development. The Earth First! Journal has been published since the movement's early years, and annual gatherings known as Round River Rendezvous bring activists together. Local groups worldwide adopted the name, and the movement influenced later direct-action environmental campaigns.
