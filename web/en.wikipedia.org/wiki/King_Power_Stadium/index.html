The King Power Stadium (also known as the Leicester City Stadium for UEFA competitions) is a football stadium in Leicester, England. It has been the home ground of Leicester City Football Club since 2002, when the club moved from its historic home at Filbert Street. The stadium is named after King Power, the Thai duty-free retail company owned by the club's late owner, Vichai Srivaddhanaprabha.

History

Leicester City had played at Filbert Street since 1891, but by the late 1990s the ground's limited capacity and difficulties of expansion led the club to seek a new home. The new stadium was built on a former power station site a short distance from Filbert Street and opened in 2002, initially under a sponsorship deal with the snack company Walkers, as the Walkers Stadium. In 2011 the naming rights passed to King Power.

The stadium was the setting for Leicester City's celebrated 2015–16 Premier League title-winning season, one of the most famous achievements in English football history. On 27 October 2018, a helicopter carrying club owner Vichai Srivaddhanaprabha crashed in the stadium car park shortly after take-off following a match, killing all five people on board; a statue of Vichai now stands outside the ground.

Other uses

The King Power Stadium has hosted England international matches, rugby union fixtures including matches at the 2015 Rugby World Cup, and concerts. Leicester City have announced plans to expand the stadium's capacity to around 40,000 as part of a wider redevelopment of the surrounding site.
