Fast & Furious

Fast & Furious (also known as The Fast and the Furious) is an American media franchise centred on a series of action films involving street racing, heists, spies, and family. Distributed by Universal Pictures, the franchise began with the 2001 film The Fast and the Furious, inspired by a 1998 Vibe magazine article about street racing in New York City. It has grown into one of the highest-grossing film franchises of all time, earning more than $7 billion worldwide, and includes a spin-off film, a television series, short films, live shows, theme park attractions, and video games.

Street racing and modified cars were the franchise's original focus before it shifted toward heists and espionage.

Overview

The first film follows undercover police officer Brian O'Conner (Paul Walker) infiltrating a crew of street racers led by Dominic Toretto (Vin Diesel). The early sequels continued the street-racing theme in Miami and Tokyo, but from Fast Five onward the series pivoted toward elaborate heist set pieces and globe-trotting espionage plots, with the ensemble cast — including Michelle Rodriguez, Tyrese Gibson, Ludacris, Dwayne Johnson, and Jason Statham — taking on paramilitary villains. The theme of "family" became the franchise's signature refrain.

Furious 7 was completed after Paul Walker's death in a car crash in November 2013 during production; his brothers served as stand-ins and the film ends with a tribute retiring his character. It became the franchise's highest-grossing entry at the time, earning over $1.5 billion.

Reception and impact

Reviews for the early films were mixed, but Fast Five was widely praised as a successful reinvention, and the series has been credited with sustaining a major franchise built around a diverse ensemble cast. Its depiction of car culture popularised import tuner cars, nitrous oxide systems, and street-racing aesthetics worldwide, while its escalating stunts — dropping cars from aircraft, driving between Abu Dhabi skyscrapers, and launching a car into orbit — have become a hallmark frequently parodied and celebrated.
