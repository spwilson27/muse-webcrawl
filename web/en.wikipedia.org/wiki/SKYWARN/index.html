SKYWARN

Founded1970s (1971 tornado programme origins)
OrganisationUS National Weather Service
PurposeTrained volunteer severe-weather spotting

SKYWARN is a programme of the United States National Weather Service (NWS) in which trained volunteers — including many amateur radio operators — report severe weather observations from the field. Spotter reports of tornadoes, hail, damaging winds, flooding and winter weather supplement radar data with ground truth.

History

Organised storm spotting grew from early local programmes after mid-20th-century tornado disasters; the NWS formalised spotter networks under the SKYWARN name in the 1970s. Today there are roughly 350,000–400,000 trained spotters in the United States.

Training and reporting

Local NWS forecast offices run free training classes covering storm structure, cloud features and reporting criteria. Reports reach forecasters via phone, web portals and amateur radio nets linked to NWS offices, and are used to issue and verify warnings. Spotters are taught never to endanger themselves; SKYWARN is distinct from storm chasing.
