Orlando Gibbons

Orlando Gibbons (baptised 25 December 1583 – 5 June 1625) was an English composer and organist of the late Tudor and early Jacobean periods, and one of the foremost figures of the English Virginalist School. A chorister at King's College, Cambridge, he became organist of the Chapel Royal in 1605 and held the post for the rest of his life; in 1623 he was also appointed organist of Westminster Abbey. Gibbons is celebrated for his keyboard music, consort music, madrigals and especially his Anglican church music.

Life

Gibbons was born in Oxford into a musical family and sang in the choir of King's College, Cambridge, from 1596. He took the degree of Bachelor of Music at Cambridge in 1606 and a doctorate at Oxford in 1622. He served James I and Prince Charles, composed for court occasions, and died suddenly at Canterbury in 1625, aged 41, while attending Charles I; he is buried in Canterbury Cathedral.

Music

Gibbons's church music includes full and verse anthems of enduring popularity, among them "This is the record of John", "Hosanna to the Son of David", "O clap your hands" and "Almighty and everlasting God", and services including the Short Service and Second Service. His hymn tunes, published in George Wither's Hymnes and Songs of the Church (1623), include "Song 1" and "Song 46".

His secular output includes the set of Madrigals and Motets of 5 Parts (1612), containing "The Silver Swan" — his best-known work — and contributions to The First Set of Madrigals by his contemporaries. His keyboard music, printed in Parthenia (1612/13) alongside Byrd and Bull, and his fantasias for viols are regarded as the summit of the English consort tradition.

Legacy

Unlike many of his contemporaries, Gibbons wrote almost no music for the theatre and little that was purely instrumental display; his reputation rests on the gravity and contrapuntal mastery of his sacred and chamber works. His sons and brothers continued the family profession, notably Christopher Gibbons, organist and composer of the Restoration era.
