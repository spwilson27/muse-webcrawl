Pseudoscience

Pseudoscience
Term coined1796 (early usage); popularised in the 19th–20th centuries
Key thinkersKarl Popper; Imre Lakatos; Paul Thagard; Mario Bunge
CharacteristicsUnfalsifiability, reliance on anecdote, resistance to revision

Pseudoscience consists of statements, beliefs, or practices that claim to be both scientific and factual but are incompatible with the scientific method. Pseudoscience is often characterised by contradictory, exaggerated, or unfalsifiable claims; reliance on confirmation bias rather than rigorous attempts at refutation; lack of openness to evaluation by other experts; absence of systematic practices when developing hypotheses; and continued adherence long after the pseudoscientific hypotheses have been experimentally discredited.

Phrenology, a popular 19th-century practice now regarded as pseudoscience

The demarcation problem

Distinguishing science from pseudoscience — the demarcation problem — has both philosophical and practical importance. Karl Popper proposed falsifiability as the criterion: a theory that cannot in principle be refuted by observation is not scientific. Imre Lakatos argued instead for evaluating research programmes by whether they predict novel facts. Paul Thagard proposed that a theory is pseudoscientific if it has been less progressive than its alternatives over a long period and its community of practitioners does not attempt to resolve the problems it faces.

Indicators

Common warning signs include vague or shifting claims, selective use of evidence, hostility to criticism, appeals to ancient wisdom or conspiracy, over-reliance on testimonials, and the use of scientific-sounding jargon without corresponding rigour. Examples commonly cited by scientists include astrology, homeopathy, phrenology, dowsing, and creation science.

Social significance

Pseudoscientific claims can cause harm when they displace effective medicine or distort public policy, and science educators and sceptical organisations work to improve public understanding of how scientific evidence is evaluated.
