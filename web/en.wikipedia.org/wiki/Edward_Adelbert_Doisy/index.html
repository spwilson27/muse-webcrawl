Edward Adelbert Doisy (November 13, 1893 – October 23, 1986) was an American biochemist. He received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1943 with Henrik Dam for their discovery of vitamin K and its chemical nature.

Biography

Doisy was born in Hume, Illinois, on November 13, 1893. He completed his B.S. degree in 1914 and his M.S. degree in 1916 from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. He completed his Ph.D. in 1920 from Harvard University. In 1919 he accepted a faculty appointment at Washington University in St. Louis, and in 1923 he moved to Saint Louis University School of Medicine, where he became professor and chairman of the new department of biochemistry, a post he held until his retirement in 1965.

Research

In 1929, Doisy and his colleagues isolated and characterised estrone, one of the first estrogens to be isolated, from the urine of pregnant women. His laboratory subsequently isolated estriol (1930) and estradiol (1935), contributing greatly to the understanding of female sex hormones.

Working on the antihemorrhagic factor discovered by the Danish scientist Henrik Dam, Doisy's group isolated vitamin K1 from alfalfa in 1939 and determined its chemical structure, and also isolated vitamin K2 from putrefied fish meal. Doisy shared the 1943 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Dam "for his discovery of the chemical nature of vitamin K."

Legacy

The department Doisy founded at Saint Louis University was later renamed the E. A. Doisy Department of Biochemistry and Molecular Biology. He died in St. Louis on October 23, 1986, at the age of 92.
