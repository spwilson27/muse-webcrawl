Qurban (Islam)

Qurbān (Arabic: قربان), also spelled Qorban and known as Uḍḥiyah (أضحية), is the ritual animal sacrifice performed by Muslims during the festival of Eid al-Adha. A sacrificial animal — typically a sheep, goat, cow, or camel — is slaughtered in commemoration of the Prophet Ibrahim's (Abraham's) willingness to sacrifice his son in obedience to God, an event in which, in Islamic tradition, God provided a ram to be sacrificed in the son's place.

Practice

The sacrifice is performed after the Eid prayer on the 10th of Dhu al-Hijjah and may continue during the following days of Tashreeq. The animal must meet requirements of age and soundness. The meat is traditionally divided into three parts: one for the family, one for relatives and friends, and one for the poor and needy, making charity a central element of the rite. Muslims who can afford it are expected to perform Qurban; jurists differ over whether it is obligatory (wajib) or a confirmed sunnah.

The word qurbān in Arabic signifies a means of drawing near to God. The ritual is also connected with the Hajj pilgrimage, though it is performed by Muslims worldwide, not only pilgrims. In many countries, charities now arrange Qurban donations so that sacrifices are carried out and the meat distributed in poorer regions.
