Von Economo neuron

Von Economo neurons, also called spindle neurons, are a specific class of mammalian cortical neurons characterised by a large spindle-shaped soma (cell body) and a single large apical axon and dendrite. They are named after the Austrian neurologist Constantin von Economo, who described them in 1925.

Distribution

In humans, von Economo neurons are concentrated in the anterior cingulate cortex and the fronto-insular cortex, regions associated with social cognition, self-awareness, and intuition. They are found in a limited range of species with large, complex brains, including great apes, elephants, and several cetaceans — a distribution often cited as an example of convergent evolution.

Function

Their large size suggests rapid conduction, and they are hypothesised to relay fast, intuitive social information across distant cortical areas. Abnormalities in these neurons have been reported in frontotemporal dementia, in which they are selectively lost early in the disease, and differences have been studied in autism and schizophrenia.
