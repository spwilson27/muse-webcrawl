Unconventional warfare

Unconventional warfare (UW) is broadly defined as military and quasi-military operations other than conventional warfare, typically conducted through, with, or by indigenous or surrogate forces. In US doctrine it is the core special-operations activity of enabling a resistance movement or insurgency to coerce, disrupt, or overthrow a government or occupying power.

TypeSpecial operations doctrine
Key practitionersUS Army Special Forces, SOE/OSS historically

Methods

UW encompasses guerrilla warfare, subversion, sabotage, intelligence activities, and unconventional assisted recovery. The US model describes phases from preparation and initial contact through infiltration, organisation, build-up, combat employment, and transition.

History

Examples include the British SOE and American OSS supporting European resistance in World War II, Jedburgh teams in France, US Special Forces with the Montagnards in Vietnam and with the Northern Alliance in Afghanistan in 2001, and support to Kurdish forces. UW differs from foreign internal defence, which supports a friendly government rather than a resistance.
