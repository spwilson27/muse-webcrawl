Watford is a town and borough in Hertfordshire, England, about 15 miles (24 km) northwest of central London, on the River Colne. It developed at a ford across the river — its name derives from the Old English for the ford used by hunters or, by some accounts, from a personal name combined with "ford." The town grew substantially in the 19th and 20th centuries as a centre for printing, brewing, and light industry, and as a commuter town for London.

History

Watford received a market charter in the 12th century, when the Abbot of St Albans was granted the right to hold a market. St Mary's Church dates from the same period. The arrival of the London and Birmingham Railway in 1838, with Watford Junction station, transformed the town into a railway and commuter hub. Brewing was a major industry; Benskins brewery was a large local employer until its closure in the 1950s. Printing also flourished, and Watford was home to major printing works serving London publishers.

Transport

Watford Junction is a principal station on the West Coast Main Line, with services to London Euston, Birmingham, and the north. The Metropolitan line of the London Underground serves Watford station, and the London Overground serves several local stations including Watford High Street. The M1 and M25 motorways pass nearby.

Sport and culture

The town is home to Watford F.C., nicknamed the Hornets, who play at Vicarage Road and reached the FA Cup final in 1984 and 2019. Cassiobury Park is the town's largest public open space. Warner Bros. Studios Leavesden, where the Harry Potter films were made, lies just outside the town and hosts the popular studio tour attraction.
