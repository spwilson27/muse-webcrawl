Gondophares I (Gundaphar, Guduvhara) was the founder of the Indo-Parthian kingdom in what is now Afghanistan, Pakistan, and northern India. He appears to have ruled from about 20 to 46 CE, initially as a Parthian vassal in Drangiana and Arachosia before asserting independence and taking the grand title "King of Kings" on his coinage.

Sources

Gondophares is known chiefly from his extensive coinage, which imitates Parthian and Indo-Scythian types and depicts Greek and Iranian deities, and from the Takht-i-Bahi inscription, dated in his regnal year 26. In Christian tradition he appears as "Gudnaphar" (Gaspar/Gathaspar in some versions) in the apocryphal Acts of Thomas, which recount the apostle Thomas's journey to his court in India — one of the roots of the tradition of Saint Thomas Christians on the Malabar Coast. Older scholarship sometimes doubted his historicity until the inscription confirmed his reign.

Kingdom and successors

His kingdom stretched from Sistan through the Kabul valley and Gandhara to the Punjab. After his death the realm fragmented among successors including Abdagases, Sarpedones, and Orthagnes, and the Indo-Parthian lands were gradually absorbed by the rising Kushan Empire under Kujula Kadphises.
