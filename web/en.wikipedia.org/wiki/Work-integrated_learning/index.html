Work-integrated learning

Work-integrated learning (WIL) is an educational approach in which students apply and develop academic knowledge within authentic work settings as a formal part of their curriculum. It is an umbrella term covering internships, co-operative education (co-op) placements, practicums, clinical placements, field education, apprenticeships, service learning and work-based projects.

Forms

In co-operative education, students alternate study terms with paid, full-time work terms in their field—a model pioneered at the University of Cincinnati in 1906 and widely used in Canada and elsewhere. Practicums and clinical placements are standard in professional programmes such as nursing, teaching and social work, where supervised practice is required for accreditation. Shorter internships and industry projects may be credit-bearing or co-curricular.

Benefits and challenges

Research associates WIL with improved graduate employability, career clarity and professional networks, and employers use placements as recruitment pipelines. Challenges include ensuring equitable access (unpaid placements can disadvantage low-income students), assuring placement quality, and coordinating partnerships among universities, employers and students.
