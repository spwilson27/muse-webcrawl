Ugo Cerletti (26 September 1877 – 25 July 1963) was an Italian neurologist and psychiatrist who co-invented, together with his colleague Lucio Bini, the method of electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) in 1938, one of the first somatic treatments in psychiatry.

Life and career

Cerletti was born in Conegliano in the province of Treviso. He studied medicine at the universities of Rome and Turin and specialised in neurology and psychiatry, studying under figures including Emil Kraepelin in Heidelberg and Alois Alzheimer in Munich. He held chairs at Bari, Genoa, and from 1935 at the Sapienza University of Rome, where he directed the Clinic of Nervous and Mental Diseases.

Electroconvulsive therapy

Building on the convulsive therapies of the era — insulin coma and the chemically induced seizures of Ladislas von Meduna — Cerletti pursued electrical induction as a more controllable method. Having observed that pigs at a Rome slaughterhouse were stunned by electric shocks through the head before slaughter, and having experimented extensively on animals, he and Lucio Bini developed a device and, in April 1938, performed the first successful treatment on a human patient: a man found wandering at Rome's railway station, diagnosed with schizophrenia, who improved markedly after a course of treatments.

The technique spread rapidly worldwide as an effective treatment for severe depression, mania, and catatonia. Cerletti later became uneasy about some of its indiscriminate uses, and he pursued the idea of "electroshock" chemical substances ("acroagonines") that he believed the shocked brain released. ECT, greatly refined with anaesthesia and muscle relaxants, remains in clinical use today.
