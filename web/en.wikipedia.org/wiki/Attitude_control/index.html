Attitude control

Attitude control
SensorsSun sensors, star trackers, gyroscopes, magnetometers
ActuatorsReaction wheels, thrusters, magnetorquers, control moment gyros
DisciplineAerospace engineering

Attitude control is the process of controlling the orientation (attitude) of an object with respect to an inertial frame of reference or another entity. For spacecraft, it is performed by the attitude determination and control system (ADCS), which senses the vehicle's orientation and applies torques to achieve and maintain the desired pointing — keeping solar arrays on the Sun, antennas on the Earth and telescopes on their targets.

Determination

Attitude is measured with sensors such as Sun sensors, horizon sensors, magnetometers, star trackers and inertial gyroscopes, often fused by estimation algorithms such as Kalman filters. Orientations are commonly represented by quaternions, Euler angles or direction-cosine matrices.

Control methods

  • Passive – spin stabilisation, gravity-gradient booms and permanent magnets align the craft without power.
  • Active – reaction and momentum wheels, control moment gyroscopes, magnetic torquers and small thrusters provide precise three-axis control.
  • Disturbances – gravity-gradient, aerodynamic, solar-radiation-pressure and magnetic torques must be counteracted or exploited.

Attitude control is equally essential for aircraft (via flight control systems), ships, submarines and camera gimbals, wherever orientation must be managed precisely.
