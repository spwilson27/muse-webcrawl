Magic Quadrant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Magic Quadrant (MQ) is a series of market research reports published by the information technology consulting firm Gartner that rely on proprietary qualitative data analysis methods to demonstrate market trends, such as direction, maturity and participants. Their analyses are conducted for several specific technology industries and are updated every one or two years. Once a market has been designated, a Magic Quadrant report is published; subsequent reports are named after the market in question.

Scoring

Gartner rates vendors upon two criteria: completeness of vision and ability to execute. Using a methodology which Gartner does not detail, these component scores lead to a vendor position in one of four quadrants:

  • Leaders score high on both completeness of vision and ability to execute. Typically, a leader has the big market share and credible customer references, and is a formidable competitor.
  • Challengers execute well today, with a large installed base, but have a less complete vision of market direction.
  • Visionaries understand where the market is going but have a smaller ability to execute, for example because of a smaller installed base or limited finances.
  • Niche players focus successfully on a small segment, or are unfocused and do not out-innovate or outperform others.

Criticism

The Magic Quadrant model has been criticised for its opacity: Gartner does not fully disclose the weightings applied to the evaluation criteria, and vendors have alleged a pay-to-play dynamic, since vendors that purchase Gartner services may receive more favourable treatment. A 2009 lawsuit by software vendor ZL Technologies alleged the rankings were arbitrary; the case was dismissed. Gartner has also issued other research formats, including the Hype Cycle, MarketScope (discontinued) and the Critical Capabilities report.
