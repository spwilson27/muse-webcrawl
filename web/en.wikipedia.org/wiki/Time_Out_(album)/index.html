Time Out is a studio album by the American jazz group the Dave Brubeck Quartet, released in 1959 by Columbia Records. It is best known for featuring compositions written almost entirely in unusual time signatures for jazz — such as 5/4, 9/8 and 6/4 — inspired by rhythms Brubeck had heard folk musicians play on tour in Eurasia. Despite the label's doubts, the album became one of the best-selling jazz records of all time and the first jazz album to sell more than a million copies.

Music

The album's most famous track, "Take Five", composed by alto saxophonist Paul Desmond, is written in 5/4 time and became the biggest-selling jazz single ever released. Brubeck's "Blue Rondo à la Turk" opens in a 9/8 rhythm borrowed from the Turkish aksak pattern before breaking into blues choruses in 4/4. Other tracks include "Strange Meadow Lark", "Three to Get Ready" (in alternating 3/4 and 4/4), "Kathy's Waltz", "Everybody's Jumpin'" and "Pick Up Sticks". The quartet — Brubeck on piano, Desmond on saxophone, Eugene Wright on bass and Joe Morello on drums — plays the odd metres with an ease that made them feel natural.

Reception and legacy

The abstract cover art is by S. Neil Fujita. Time Out peaked at No. 2 on the Billboard pop albums chart, was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame, and in 2005 was selected for the United States National Recording Registry. Its success led to follow-up albums exploring further metres, including Time Further Out (1961).
