The Battle of Albert (1–13 July 1916) was the opening phase of the Anglo-French Battle of the Somme, fought by the British Fourth Army and elements of the French Sixth Army against the German 2nd Army. It began after a seven-day preliminary bombardment and included the disastrous first day on the Somme (1 July 1916), the worst day in the history of the British Army, with some 57,000 casualties, over 19,000 of them killed.

Course

South of the Albert–Bapaume road, where the bombardment had been more effective and French heavy artillery supported the attack, British and French divisions achieved most of their objectives, capturing Mametz and Montauban. North of the road, assaults on strongpoints such as Serre, Beaumont-Hamel and Thiepval largely failed. In the following days the British captured La Boisselle, Contalmaison and Mametz Wood, setting up the next phase, the Battle of Bazentin Ridge on 14 July.

First use of mines and the tank prelude

Enormous mines were detonated under the German lines at 07:28 on 1 July, including at Lochnagar and Hawthorn Ridge; the Lochnagar crater remains a memorial today. The battle also saw the first use of the creeping barrage as a standard tactic in development.
