Poi (performance art)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Poi is a form of performance art in which the performer swings tethered weights through a variety of rhythmic and geometric patterns. A poi set consists of a pair of equal-length ropes or cords with handles at one end and soft weights — traditionally balls — at the other. "Poi" is both the equipment and the art form itself, and originates with the Māori people of New Zealand, in whose culture the poi is used in kapa haka performance accompanied by song (waiata poi).

History

In Māori tradition, poi were used by women in storytelling and song, and by men to train wrist flexibility and coordination for weapons. Balls of flax or raupō on flax cords evolved into the modern kapa haka poi. From the 1980s, circus and festival performers outside New Zealand popularised poi worldwide; the modern international community developed a large vocabulary of moves (weaves, butterflies, stalls, wraps, isolations) and prop variants, including fire poi with Kevlar wicks and programmable LED poi that paint light patterns in the air.

Modern culture and health

Poi is a central discipline of the "flow arts" movement, alongside hula hooping, staff and juggling, with international gatherings and online tutorials. A 2017 University of Auckland study found that a month of poi practice improved grip strength, balance and attention in older adults, prompting interest in poi as gentle exercise for wellbeing.
