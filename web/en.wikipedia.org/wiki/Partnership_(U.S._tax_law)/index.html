Partnership (U.S. tax law)

Under United States federal income tax law, a partnership is treated as a pass-through entity: the partnership itself does not pay income tax. Instead, each partner reports their distributive share of the partnership's income, gains, losses, deductions, and credits on their own tax return, whether or not anything is actually distributed. Partnership taxation is governed by Subchapter K of the Internal Revenue Code.

Key concepts

Partners maintain "outside basis" in their partnership interests and the partnership keeps "inside basis" in its assets. Contributions of property are generally tax-free under section 721, and distributions are generally tax-free to the extent of basis under section 731. Allocations of income and loss are respected if they have "substantial economic effect"; otherwise items are reallocated according to the partners' interests in the partnership.

Reporting

Partnerships file an informational return, Form 1065, and issue Schedule K-1 to each partner reporting their share of items. The Tax Equity and Fiscal Responsibility Act (TEFRA) and later the Bipartisan Budget Act of 2015 centralised partnership audit procedures at the entity level.
