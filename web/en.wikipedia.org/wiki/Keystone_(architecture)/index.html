Keystone (architecture)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Keystone

TypeArchitectural / structural element
PositionApex (crown) of an arch or vault
MaterialStone, brick
Related elementsVoussoir, springer, impost

A keystone (also called a capstone) is the wedge-shaped stone at the apex of a masonry arch or vault. It is the final piece placed during construction and locks all the other stones — the voussoirs — into position, allowing the arch to bear weight and transfer loads sideways into its supports.

Function

An arch under construction is supported by temporary wooden centring. As the voussoirs are laid from each springing point towards the crown, the keystone is driven into the final gap; being slightly wider at the top than the bottom, it wedges the ring tight and puts the arch into compression. Once the keystone is in place the centring can be struck (removed). Although all the voussoirs share the structural work, the keystone carries the least load of any of them — its importance is geometrical, completing and locking the ring. Because of this role, "keystone" has become a metaphor for the central, indispensable element of any structure, plan or organisation, and Pennsylvania is nicknamed the "Keystone State".

Decoration

Keystones are often enlarged, dropped below the line of the arch, or carved with scrolls, masks, heads or heraldic devices, making them a prominent decorative feature of doorways and windows in classical and Baroque architecture. In ribbed Gothic vaults the corresponding element is the carved boss at the intersection of the ribs.
