Gwynedd is a county in the north-west of Wales, created in 1974 and named after the old Kingdom of Gwynedd. Covering much of the historic counties of Anglesey (until 1996), Caernarfonshire and Merionethshire, it is the second-largest county in Wales by area and one of the most sparsely populated. Since 1996 it has been a unitary authority area governed by Gwynedd Council, the island of Anglesey having become a separate authority.

Geography

Gwynedd contains most of the Snowdonia (Eryri) National Park, including Yr Wyddfa (Snowdon), the highest mountain in Wales at 1,085 m, as well as the Llŷn Peninsula and the coast of Cardigan Bay. Principal settlements are the city of Bangor (site of Bangor University), the county town Caernarfon with its great Edwardian castle, and the towns of Pwllheli, Porthmadog, Dolgellau, Blaenau Ffestiniog and Bala.

History

The medieval Kingdom of Gwynedd, founded in the post-Roman period and associated with rulers such as Maelgwn, Rhodri the Great and the princes Llywelyn the Great and Llywelyn ap Gruffudd, was the strongest of the Welsh kingdoms and the last to fall to Edward I in 1282–83. The modern county was formed in 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972 and reduced to its present area in 1996. Gwynedd is a heartland of the Welsh language and of Welsh nationalism; Plaid Cymru has long been the dominant local party.

Economy and culture

The economy rests on tourism (centred on Snowdonia, the Ffestiniog and Welsh Highland heritage railways, and the Italianate village of Portmeirion), agriculture, slate-quarrying heritage (the Slate Landscape of Northwest Wales is a UNESCO World Heritage Site), higher education at Bangor, and the decommissioning nuclear station at Trawsfynydd. The National Eisteddfod and harp and folk traditions are strongly rooted in the county.
