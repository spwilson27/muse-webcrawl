Flap (aeronautics)

Flaps are a type of high-lift device used to reduce the stalling speed of an aircraft wing at a given weight. Flaps are usually mounted on the wing trailing edges of a fixed-wing aircraft. Flaps are used to reduce the take-off distance and the landing distance. Flaps also cause an increase in drag so they are retracted when not needed.

Principles

The general effect of flaps is to increase the camber of the wing, which increases the lift coefficient. For plain and split flaps the deflection also increases the effective angle of attack. Extending flaps increases drag, which can be useful during approach and landing because it allows a steeper descent without increasing airspeed.

Types

  • Plain flap – a simple hinged portion of the trailing edge.
  • Split flap – deflected from the lower surface of the wing.
  • Slotted flap – includes a gap that ducts high-energy air over the upper surface, delaying separation.
  • Fowler flap – slides backwards and down, increasing wing area as well as camber.
  • Krueger flap – a leading-edge device that folds out from the underside of the wing.

Use in flight

Flaps are typically extended in stages during the approach to landing and partially during take-off. Modern airliners use sophisticated multi-element flap systems controlled from the cockpit by a flap lever with detented positions.
