Pachinko

Pachinko (Japanese: パチンコ) is a mechanical arcade game and gambling device originating in Japan. A pachinko machine resembles a vertical pinball machine: the player fires small steel balls into the playfield, where they cascade through pins; balls entering certain pockets win additional balls.

History

Pachinko developed in the 1920s–1930s from Western bagatelle and pinball-type games, and exploded in popularity after the Second World War. Modern machines add electronic displays, video sequences and sophisticated "fever" modes.

Gambling and the industry

Gambling for cash is illegal in Japan, so winnings are paid in balls that are exchanged for prizes in the parlour; the prizes can then be sold for cash at a separate nearby shop, in the well-known "three-shop system". Pachinko parlours are a major leisure industry in Japan, employing hundreds of thousands of people and generating revenues rivalling those of the rest of the gambling world, though the number of parlours and players has declined from its 1990s peak.
