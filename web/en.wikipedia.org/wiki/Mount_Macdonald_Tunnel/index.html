The Mount Macdonald Tunnel is a railway tunnel through Mount Macdonald in the Selkirk Mountains of British Columbia, Canada, carrying the main line of the Canadian Pacific Railway beneath Rogers Pass. At 14.7 kilometres (9.1 miles) it is the longest railway tunnel in North America.

History

The tunnel was built to relieve the original Connaught Tunnel (1916) and the steep grades of the Rogers Pass route. Construction began in 1984 and the tunnel opened to traffic on 12 December 1988 at a cost of about $500 million. It reduced the ruling grade for westbound trains from 2.2% to 1%, allowing longer, heavier trains and removing the need for pusher locomotives on that segment.

Operation

The tunnel works in tandem with the Connaught Tunnel: the Macdonald Tunnel carries westbound trains on the lower, flatter route while eastbound trains generally use the Connaught. A sophisticated ventilation system, with doors at the portals and a mid-tunnel shaft, clears diesel exhaust from the single-track bore between trains.
