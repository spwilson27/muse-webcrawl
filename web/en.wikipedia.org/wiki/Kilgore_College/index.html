Kilgore College

Kilgore College is a public community college in Kilgore, Texas, in the piney woods of East Texas. Founded in 1935 during the East Texas oil boom, it serves roughly 5,000–6,000 students with associate degrees and workforce certificates. The college is best known nationally for the Kilgore College Rangerettes, the first collegiate women's precision dance team of its kind, founded in 1940 and famous for their high-kick routines at football halftime shows.

History

Kilgore College opened in 1935 as the town boomed after the discovery of the East Texas Oil Field in 1930–31. Gussie Nell Davis founded the Rangerettes in 1940 to bring spectacle to football games and — in the era's terms — to give young women a wholesome, disciplined activity; the team's combination of military drill precision, dance and the trademark high kick became an iconic Texas institution, performing at inaugurations, bowl games and abroad.

Academics and campus

The college offers transfer degrees in liberal arts and sciences and technical programs including process technology, nursing and other health sciences, and petroleum-related training for the regional oil and gas industry. Campus landmarks include the East Texas Oil Museum, which recreates a 1930s boomtown, and the Rangerette Showcase museum.

Athletics

The Rangers compete in NJCAA athletics; the football program has been a strong junior-college program and produced numerous players who transferred to four-year universities.
