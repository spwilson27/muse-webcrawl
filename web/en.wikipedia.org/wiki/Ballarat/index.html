Ballarat

Sturt Street, Ballarat

Ballarat is a city in the Central Highlands of Victoria, Australia, located about 110 kilometres west-northwest of Melbourne. With a population of roughly 113,000, it is the third-largest city in Victoria. The name derives from local Aboriginal words generally interpreted as "resting place".

History

The area was settled by Europeans in 1838 as a sheep run, but Ballarat's fortune was transformed by the Victorian gold rush: gold was discovered at Poverty Point in 1851, and within years the Ballarat goldfields were among the richest alluvial fields in the world, attracting tens of thousands of miners. Discontent over mining licence fees culminated in the Eureka Rebellion of December 1854, when miners built the Eureka Stockade and fought government troops; the rebellion is widely regarded as a pivotal event in the development of Australian democracy. Deep-lead and quartz mining continued for decades, and the wealth of the era endowed the city with grand Victorian architecture, the fine Sturt Street boulevard, and institutions such as the Art Gallery of Ballarat, founded in 1884 and the oldest regional gallery in Australia.

Modern city

Today Ballarat is a regional service, education and manufacturing centre, home to Federation University Australia. Tourist attractions include the Sovereign Hill open-air museum, which recreates the 1850s goldfields, the Ballarat Wildlife Park, and Lake Wendouree, the venue for rowing and canoeing at the 1956 Melbourne Olympics.
