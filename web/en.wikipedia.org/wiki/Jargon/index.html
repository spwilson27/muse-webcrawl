Jargon

Jargon is the specialized terminology associated with a particular field, profession or area of activity, understood mainly by members of that group and often opaque to outsiders. Jargon serves precision and efficiency within a group, signalling membership and compressing complex concepts into shorthand, but can also exclude, obscure and impress.

Jargon
Field: Linguistics, sociolinguistics
Related: Slang, argot, register, technical language

Etymology and history

The word comes from Old French jargon, "chatter, twittering of birds," used by Chaucer for unintelligible speech. In earlier usage it could mean thieves' cant or a pidgin trade language (as in Chinook Jargon of the Pacific Northwest). The modern sense of occupational vocabulary developed as professions, sciences and bureaucracies expanded.

Uses and criticism

Medical, legal, academic, military, corporate and computing communities each develop terms of art — e.g. legal "tort," computing "bandwidth" in its metaphorical sense. Within a field, jargon can be genuinely necessary where plain words lack a precise equivalent. Critics from Orwell onward have attacked jargon as pretentious or evasive, especially in management and politics, and plain-language movements press governments and professions to translate public-facing documents.

Related concepts

Jargon differs from slang (informal, often transient in-group speech), from argot (secret vocabulary of marginal groups), and from register (the whole variety of language used in a situation). Occupational jargons historically fed guild and trade secrecy. Learning a field's jargon is part of professional socialisation. See also buzzword, linguistics.
