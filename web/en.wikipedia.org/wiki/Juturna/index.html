Juturna

AbodeSprings and rivers of Latium
FestivalJuturnalia (11 January)
SiblingsTurnus

Juturna (also spelled Iuturna) was a Roman goddess of fountains, wells, and springs, associated particularly with a spring in Latium near the Numicus river and with the pool in the Roman Forum beside the Temple of Castor and Pollux.

Mythology

According to tradition, Juturna was a mortal woman, sister of Turnus, the Rutulian king who opposed Aeneas in Italy. Jupiter granted her immortality and dominion over springs and pools; in some accounts, Jupiter desired her and rewarded her silence with divinity. In Virgil's Aeneid, Juturna aids her brother Turnus against Aeneas but is finally compelled by Jupiter, through the agency of a Dira, to abandon him to his fate.

Cult

Her festival, the Juturnalia, was celebrated on 11 January and was particularly observed by water-workers (aquileges) and those involved with Rome's water supply. A temple to Juturna was vowed in the Campus Martius during the First Punic War, and the Lacus Juturnae in the Forum was a site of religious significance where the Dioscuri were believed to have watered their horses after the Battle of Lake Regillus.
