Lancaster University

Lancaster University (officially the University of Lancaster) is a public research university in Lancaster, Lancashire, England. It was established by royal charter in 1964, one of several plate glass universities created in the 1960s. The university was initially based in St Leonard's House in the city centre before moving to a purpose-built campus at Bailrigg in 1968.

History

Lancaster was the seventh plate glass university announced in 1961. The first students were admitted in 1964, and teaching began in the city centre; the Bailrigg campus, set in 300 acres of parkland south of the city, opened from 1968. George Pace designed many of the original buildings, organised around a central pedestrian spine.

Organisation

The university is collegiate, with nine colleges: Bowland, Cartmel, County, Furness, Fylde, Graduate College, Grizedale, Lonsdale and Pendle. Academic faculties include Arts and Social Sciences, Health and Medicine, Science and Technology, and the Lancaster University Management School, which is triple-accredited.

Reputation

Lancaster regularly ranks among the top universities in the United Kingdom and is known for research in areas such as environmental science, physics, management and linguistics. It operates international partnerships and teaching locations in several countries.
