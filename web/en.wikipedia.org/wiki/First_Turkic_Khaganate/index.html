The First Turkic Khaganate, also known as the Göktürk Khaganate, was a nomadic empire of the Göktürks stretching across the Eurasian steppe from Manchuria to the Black Sea. Founded by Bumin Qaghan in 552 after the overthrow of the Rouran Khaganate, it was the first empire to bear the name "Turk" as a political designation.

History

Bumin, chief of the Ashina clan, revolted against his Rouran overlords in 552 and founded the khaganate in the Altai Mountains region. His brother Istämi extended Turkic power westward, forming an alliance with the Sasanian Empire against the Hephthalites, whom they destroyed in the 560s. At its height the khaganate controlled the Silk Road trade routes and engaged in diplomacy with both the Byzantine Empire and China.

Succession disputes and civil war between the eastern and western wings, exacerbated by Sui dynasty intrigue, split the empire in 603 into the Eastern Turkic Khaganate and the Western Turkic Khaganate.

Legacy

The khaganate left behind the Orkhon inscriptions, the oldest known texts in a Turkic language, erected in the 8th century by its successor state. Its political model influenced later steppe empires, and its name endures in the modern ethnonym of the Turkic peoples.
