José Ortega y Gasset (1883–1955) was a Spanish philosopher and essayist, the leading Spanish thinker of the first half of the 20th century. His philosophy of "ratiovitalism" and his doctrine of perspectivism are summed up in his dictum "I am I and my circumstance".

Work

In Meditations on Quixote (1914), The Modern Theme (1923), and his best-known book, The Revolt of the Masses (1930), Ortega analysed the rise of mass society and the decline of liberal civilization. He founded journals including Revista de Occidente, which introduced much European thought to the Spanish-speaking world. Exiled during the Spanish Civil War, he returned to Spain in 1948.
