The Royal Gurkha Rifles (RGR) is a rifle regiment of the British Army, forming part of the Brigade of Gurkhas. It was formed on 1 July 1994 by the amalgamation of the four remaining Gurkha infantry regiments, and recruits Nepali soldiers — Gurkhas — who have served the British Crown since 1815.

History

The regiment was created from the 2nd, 6th, 7th and 10th Gurkha Rifles as the British Army's Gurkha presence was drawn down and the Hong Kong garrison closed. Its battalions have since served in Bosnia, Kosovo, Sierra Leone, Iraq and Afghanistan, where Gurkha soldiers won numerous gallantry awards.

Organisation

The 1st Battalion is based in Brunei, serving as part of the resident garrison, while the 2nd Battalion is based at Shorncliffe in Kent; the battalions rotate between the two postings. RGR soldiers are distinguished by the kukri, the curved Nepalese knife carried on operations and parade. The regiment's cap badge combines crossed kukris with elements of its predecessor regiments.
