The Georgia Institute of Technology (commonly referred to as Georgia Tech and GT) is a public research university and institute of technology in Atlanta, Georgia. It is part of the University System of Georgia and has satellite campuses in Savannah, Georgia; Metz, France; and Shenzhen, China. The school was founded in 1885 as the Georgia School of Technology as part of Reconstruction plans to build an industrial economy in the post-Civil War Southern United States.

History

Initially, the Georgia School of Technology offered only a degree in mechanical engineering. By 1901 its curriculum had expanded to include electrical, civil, and chemical engineering. In 1948 the school changed its name to the Georgia Institute of Technology to reflect its evolution from a trade school to a larger technical institute and research university. Women were admitted in 1952, and in 1961 Georgia Tech was the first university in the Deep South to admit African-American students without a court order. In 2014, Georgia Tech launched, with Udacity and AT&T, the first accredited online Master of Science in computer science degree delivered at scale (OMS CS).

Organization and academics

The university is organized into six colleges: the College of Computing, the College of Design, the College of Engineering, the College of Sciences, the Ivan Allen College of Liberal Arts, and the Scheller College of Business. Georgia Tech consistently ranks among the top public universities in the United States, particularly for engineering and computing. Its research enterprise exceeds $1 billion annually, anchored by the Georgia Tech Research Institute (GTRI), founded in 1934.

The 400-acre main campus in Midtown Atlanta hosted the Olympic Village and venues for swimming and diving at the 1996 Summer Olympics. Athletics and traditions

Georgia Tech's athletic teams are the Yellow Jackets, competing in NCAA Division I as members of the Atlantic Coast Conference. The football team plays at Bobby Dodd Stadium at Hyundai Field, one of the oldest stadiums in college football, and has won four national championships. Beloved traditions include the Ramblin' Wreck, a 1930 Ford Model A sport coupe that leads the football team onto the field; the fight song "Ramblin' Wreck from Georgia Tech"; the freshman RAT cap tradition; and the theft-prone "T" on Tech Tower.
