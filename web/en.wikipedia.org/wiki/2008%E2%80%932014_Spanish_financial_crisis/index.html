2008–2014 Spanish financial crisis

The 2008–2014 Spanish financial crisis, known in Spain as La Gran Recesión, was a major economic downturn beginning with the bursting of Spain's housing bubble and a crisis in its savings-bank sector, deepened by the eurozone sovereign-debt crisis.

Course

Years of cheap credit had fuelled a construction boom; when it collapsed, unemployment soared past 26% and bad loans imperilled the cajas (savings banks). In 2012 Spain received a European bailout of up to €100 billion to recapitalize its banks, including the nationalized Bankia. Austerity measures provoked mass protests, notably the 15-M (Indignados) movement.

Recovery

Spain returned to growth in 2014 and exited the bank assistance program, though unemployment and public debt remained high for years afterward.
