Yom HaShoah

Official name: Yom Hazikaron laShoah ve-laG'vurah (Holocaust and Heroism Remembrance Day)
Date: 27 Nisan (Hebrew calendar; adjusted to avoid Shabbat)
Observed by: Israel; Jewish communities worldwide
Established: 1951 (observed); 1959 (law)

Yom HaShoah (Hebrew: יום השואה, "Day of the Holocaust"), formally Yom Hazikaron laShoah ve-laG'vurah — Holocaust and Heroism Remembrance Day — is Israel's national day of commemoration for the approximately six million Jews murdered in the Holocaust by Nazi Germany and its collaborators, and for Jewish resistance during that period. It falls on the 27th day of Nisan in the Hebrew calendar, close to the anniversary of the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising, and is observed eight days before Israel's Independence Day.

Observance in Israel

The day opens with a state ceremony at Yad Vashem in Jerusalem on the preceding evening, at which six torches are lit by survivors representing the six million victims. At 10:00 a.m. a two-minute siren sounds across the country, during which traffic stops and the public stands in silent commemoration. Places of entertainment are closed by law, flags fly at half-mast, and radio and television broadcast documentaries, survivor testimony, and the reading of victims' names ("Unto Every Person There Is a Name").

History

The Knesset first designated the day in 1951; a 1959 law formalised its observance and the siren ritual. The date was chosen by the Knesset rather than the rabbinate, and some ultra-Orthodox communities, which object to formal mourning during the festive month of Nisan, instead commemorate the Holocaust on fast days such as the Tenth of Tevet. Outside Israel, Jewish communities hold ceremonies on Yom HaShoah, which is distinct from the United Nations' International Holocaust Remembrance Day on 27 January, the anniversary of the liberation of Auschwitz.
