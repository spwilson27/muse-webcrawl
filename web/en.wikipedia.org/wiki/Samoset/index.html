Samoset

Born: c. 1590, Maine

Died: c. 1653

Tribe: Abenaki (Penobscot region)

Known for: First Native American to make contact with the Plymouth Colony

Samoset (also Somerset, c. 1590 – c. 1653) was an Abenaki sagamore from the region of present-day Maine who was the first Native American to make contact with the Pilgrims of Plymouth Colony in March 1621.

Contact with Plymouth Colony

On 16 March 1621, Samoset walked alone into the Plymouth settlement and greeted the astonished colonists in English with the words "Welcome, Englishmen!" He had learned some English from fishermen and traders operating along the Maine coast. He stayed the night, explained local geography and peoples, and arranged a meeting with Massasoit, the sachem of the Wampanoag, who arrived days later with the interpreter Tisquantum (Squanto). The encounter led to a peace treaty between the Plymouth colonists and the Wampanoag that lasted for decades.

Later life

Samoset acted as an intermediary between the English and Native peoples of Maine. In 1625 he was a signatory to one of the earliest recorded land deeds in New England, conveying land at Pemaquid to John Brown. Little is recorded of his final years; he is thought to have died around 1653 in Maine.

Legacy

Samoset's greeting is a famous episode in early American history, regularly recounted in accounts of the founding of Plymouth Colony and the first Thanksgiving.
