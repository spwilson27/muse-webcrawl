Cimarron is a village in Colfax County, New Mexico, United States, lying on the Cimarron River at the eastern foothills of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains. A stop on the mountain branch of the Santa Fe Trail, it was a notorious frontier town of the American Old West and the centre of the vast Maxwell Land Grant.

History

The town grew up in the 1850s–60s on lands of the Maxwell Land Grant, where Lucien Maxwell built a mansion and grist mill (the Aztec Mill, still standing as a museum). Cimarron became county seat of Colfax County in 1872 and a haunt of ranchers, outlaws and gunfighters: Clay Allison was among its notorious residents, and the St. James Hotel, opened by Henri Lambert (once a cook for Abraham Lincoln), preserves a tin ceiling said to bear bullet holes from the town's violent years.

Conflicts between settlers, squatters and the land-grant company erupted in the Colfax County War of the 1870s. With the arrival of the railroad at nearby Springer and the sale of the grant, Cimarron declined, though cattle ranching continued.

Philmont Scout Ranch

Just south of Cimarron lies Philmont Scout Ranch, a 140,000-acre high-adventure camp of the Boy Scouts of America donated by oilman Waite Phillips beginning in 1938; its headquarters and the Villa Philmonte bring thousands of visitors through Cimarron each summer. The town is also a gateway to the Cimarron Canyon State Park and the Enchanted Circle scenic route toward Taos and Eagle Nest.

Geography

Cimarron occupies a valley where the Cimarron River emerges from the mountains onto the high plains. U.S. Route 64 passes through the village, connecting it with Raton to the east and Taos to the west through Cimarron Canyon.
