Video game bot

In video games, a bot is a type of artificial intelligence–based expert system software that plays a video game in the place of a human. Bots are used in a variety of genres, most commonly first-person shooters, where they serve as opponents or teammates for human players in practice matches or when multiplayer servers are unavailable.

Uses

Bots allow players to practice against computer-controlled opponents with adjustable difficulty, fill empty slots in multiplayer games, and extend the life of a game after its online community declines. Well-known examples include the bots of Quake III Arena, Unreal Tournament, and Counter-Strike, as well as cooperative teammates in games like Left 4 Dead, whose "AI Director" adjusts encounters dynamically.

Design

Shooter bots typically use pathfinding over waypoint graphs or navigation meshes, finite-state machines, or behavior trees for decision-making, and aim-assist models that simulate human reaction time and error. In other contexts the term also covers unauthorized third-party programs that automate play in online games — for example, gold-farming or leveling bots in MMORPGs — whose use generally violates a game's terms of service.
