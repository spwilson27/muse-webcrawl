Atmospheric wave

Atmospheric wave
TypesRossby, gravity, Kelvin, tidal
FieldAtmospheric dynamics

An atmospheric wave is a periodic disturbance in the fields of atmospheric variables — pressure, wind, temperature and geopotential height — that propagates through the atmosphere. Waves are fundamental to atmospheric dynamics: they transport energy and momentum, drive weather systems, and couple the lower atmosphere to the stratosphere and above.

Types of waves

  • Rossby (planetary) waves – very large-scale waves arising from the variation of the Coriolis effect with latitude; they meander through the mid-latitude flow, steering cyclones and blocking patterns.
  • Gravity (buoyancy) waves – oscillations in which buoyancy is the restoring force, generated by flow over mountains, convection and fronts; they include lee waves marked by lenticular clouds.
  • Kelvin waves – eastward-propagating waves trapped at the equator, important in the El Niño cycle and stratospheric oscillations.
  • Atmospheric tides – global oscillations with periods of a day and fractions thereof, driven by solar heating.

Significance

Breaking gravity waves deposit momentum that drives the quasi-biennial oscillation of the equatorial stratosphere and the circulation of the mesosphere. Rossby wave patterns govern the position of jet streams and the persistence of heatwaves, floods and cold outbreaks.
