Son Volt

Son Volt is an American rock band formed in 1994 in Belleville, Illinois, by singer-songwriter Jay Farrar following the breakup of the influential alternative country band Uncle Tupelo. Together with Jeff Tweedy's Wilco, Son Volt carried forward Uncle Tupelo's fusion of punk energy, country, and folk, and its debut album Trace (1995) is regarded as a landmark of the alt-country genre.

History

When Uncle Tupelo split in 1994, Farrar recruited drummer Mike Heidorn from the old band plus brothers Dave and Jim Boquist, and recorded Trace in late 1994. Released in September 1995, it featured the single "Drown" (a top-10 mainstream rock hit) and "Windfall", and was met with strong critical acclaim. Two further Warner Bros. albums followed — Straightaways (1997) and the more expansive Wide Swing Tremolo (1998) — after which the band went on hiatus around 2000 as Farrar pursued solo work.

Farrar revived Son Volt with an entirely new lineup for Okemah and the Melody of Riot (2005), a politically charged album invoking Woody Guthrie's birthplace. Later records include The Search (2007), American Central Dust (2009), Honky Tonk (2013), Notes of Blue (2017), Union (2019), Electro Melodier (2021), and Day of the Doug (2023), a tribute to Doug Sahm. The original line-up's chemistry remains the band's reference point, and "Windfall" and "Tear Stained Eye" remain staples of the Americana canon.
