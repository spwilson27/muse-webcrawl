Nonpartisanism
DefinitionAbsence of, or opposition to, political party affiliation in a political system or election
ExamplesMany US municipal elections; Nebraska legislature; Nunavut and Northwest Territories consensus government; historical non-partisan democracies

Nonpartisanism is a lack of affiliation with, and usually a lack of bias toward, a political party. In political science it describes systems, elections or officeholders that operate without formal party organisation, or officials who are expected to act impartially regardless of party sympathies.

Nonpartisan elections

In the United States, many local offices — mayors, city councils, school boards, judges — are elected on nonpartisan ballots that omit party labels, a legacy of Progressive Era reforms meant to weaken party machines. The Nebraska Legislature is the only US state legislature elected on a nonpartisan basis. Critics argue voters then rely more on incumbency and name recognition; supporters argue local issues are managerial rather than ideological.

Nonpartisan systems

Some governments function without parties altogether. Canada's territories of Nunavut and the Northwest Territories use "consensus government", in which MLAs sit as independents and choose the premier among themselves. Historically, George Washington's Farewell Address warned against parties, and the early American republic aspired — briefly — to nonpartisan governance. Small island states such as Nauru and Palau operate largely without formal parties, and many Gulf monarchies prohibit them.

Nonpartisan organisations

The term also describes bodies pledged to impartiality — e.g. nonpartisan election administrators, the Nonpartisan League (a historical US political movement, despite its name a major party force in North Dakota), and research organisations such as the Congressional Budget Office, whose analyses are expected to be party-neutral.
