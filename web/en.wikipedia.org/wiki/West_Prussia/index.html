West Prussia was a province of the Kingdom of Prussia and later of the German Empire, existing from 1773 to 1829 and again from 1878 to 1919. Its territory lay along the southern Baltic coast, roughly corresponding to today's Pomeranian Voivodeship in Poland and parts of the Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship.

History

West Prussia was formed after the First Partition of Poland in 1772, when Prussia annexed Royal Prussia, the part of Poland that had previously been under the Polish crown. It was created as a province in 1773. From 1829 to 1878 it was merged with East Prussia into a single province of Prussia, and then separated again in 1878.

The province's population was mixed, including German, Polish and Kashubian communities, with Danzig (modern Gdańsk) as its largest city and capital. Following the First World War, the Treaty of Versailles in 1919 divided the province: most of it became part of the re-established Poland, the city of Danzig became the Free City of Danzig, and a small eastern strip remained with Germany as part of East Prussia. After the Second World War, the remaining German territories were placed under Polish administration.
