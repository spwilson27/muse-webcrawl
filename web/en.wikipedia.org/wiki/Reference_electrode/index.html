Reference electrode

Image placeholder: a common laboratory reference electrode.

A reference electrode is an electrode which has a stable and well-known electrode potential. Its high stability is achieved by employing a redox system with constant (buffered or saturated) concentrations of each participant of the redox reaction. Reference electrodes provide the reference point against which the potential of a working or indicator electrode is measured in electrochemical cells.

Common types

The theoretical primary reference is the standard hydrogen electrode (SHE), assigned a potential of 0.00 V by convention. In everyday laboratory work it is replaced by more convenient secondary references such as the saturated calomel electrode (SCE, about +0.241 V versus SHE) and the silver/silver chloride electrode (about +0.197 V in saturated KCl), which are compact, robust and easy to maintain.

Applications

Reference electrodes are essential in potentiometry, pH measurement, corrosion studies, cyclic voltammetry and three-electrode electrochemical cells, where they allow the working electrode potential to be controlled and reported on a reproducible scale. Care is taken to minimise liquid-junction potentials and contamination between the reference filling solution and the sample.
