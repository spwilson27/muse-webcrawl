Pony car

Pony car is an American car classification for affordable, compact, highly styled automobiles with a youthful image, typically featuring long hoods, short rear decks, and a wide range of options allowing buyers to personalise their cars.

History

The class was created by the Ford Mustang, introduced on April 17, 1964, which combined sporty styling with economy-car mechanicals and an accessible price. Its enormous success prompted competitors: the Chevrolet Camaro and Pontiac Firebird (1967), the AMC Javelin (1968), and later Challenger and Barracuda variants from Chrysler.

Characteristics

Pony cars are usually rear-wheel drive, offered as two-door coupes or convertibles, with engines ranging from economical six-cylinders to powerful V8s. High-performance versions overlap with the muscle car category.

Modern era

After declining in the 1970s–1990s, pony cars were revived with retro-styled models such as the 2005 Ford Mustang, 2008 Dodge Challenger, and 2010 Chevrolet Camaro. The Mustang remains in production as the last traditional pony car from the original class.
