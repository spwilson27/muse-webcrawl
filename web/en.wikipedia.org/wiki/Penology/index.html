Penology

Penology (from Latin poena, "punishment") is the study of the punishment of crime and the management of prisons, a branch of criminology concerned with the philosophy, practice and effectiveness of penal systems.

Penology
Field: Criminology
Key thinkers: Beccaria, Bentham, Foucault
Topics: Deterrence, rehabilitation, recidivism

Theories of punishment

Penology examines justifications for punishment: retribution, deterrence, incapacitation, rehabilitation and restorative justice. Cesare Beccaria's On Crimes and Punishments (1764) argued for proportionate, certain punishment; Jeremy Bentham designed the panopticon and championed utilitarian reform.

History

Modern penology emerged with 18th–19th-century prison reformers such as John Howard and Elizabeth Fry, the penitentiary systems of Pennsylvania and Auburn, and 20th-century shifts between rehabilitative and punitive models. Michel Foucault's Discipline and Punish (1975) recast the prison as an instrument of social control.

Contemporary issues

Current debates cover mass incarceration, solitary confinement, private prisons, recidivism measurement and alternatives to custody.
