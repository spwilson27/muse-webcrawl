The 2023 review of Westminster constituencies was a periodic review of the parliamentary constituencies of the United Kingdom carried out by the four Boundary Commissions. The review redrew constituency boundaries to reflect population changes while keeping the total number of seats in the House of Commons at 650.

Background

The review was conducted under the Parliamentary Constituencies Act 2020, which retained 650 seats but changed the allocation formula so that each constituency (with a small number of protected exceptions) must have an electorate within five per cent of the United Kingdom electoral quota of 73,393. Earlier plans to reduce the Commons to 600 seats were abandoned.

Process

Each Boundary Commission published initial proposals in 2021, followed by public consultations and revised proposals. The commissions were required to submit their final reports by 1 July 2023. Under the revised rules the reports took effect automatically, without a vote in Parliament, through an Order in Council made in November 2023.

Changes

The allocation of seats shifted modestly from Wales and Scotland towards England, particularly the South East. Wales lost eight seats, falling from 40 to 32, while England gained ten. Many constituencies were substantially redrawn, and a number of long-standing seats disappeared or changed names.

Implementation

The new boundaries were first used at the 2024 general election, held on 4 July 2024. Analysts estimated that the boundary changes alone would have altered the result of the 2019 election only marginally.
