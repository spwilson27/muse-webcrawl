India–Bangladesh enclaves

The India–Bangladesh enclaves, also known as the chitmahals, were a complex group of enclaves and exclaves along the border of India and Bangladesh, mainly in the Cooch Behar district area. There were 111 Indian enclaves inside Bangladesh and 51 Bangladeshi enclaves inside India, some of them counter-enclaves and even a rare counter-counter-enclave, the world's only third-order enclave.

History

The enclaves are legendarily attributed to treaties and land grants arising from conflicts between the Kingdom of Cooch Behar and the Mughal Empire in the 17th and 18th centuries, and were left unresolved at the Partition of India in 1947. Residents of the enclaves were effectively stateless, lacking access to public services.

Resolution

Following the Land Boundary Agreement, implemented on 31 July 2015, India and Bangladesh exchanged most of the enclaves, and residents were given a choice of citizenship, resolving the decades-old problem.
