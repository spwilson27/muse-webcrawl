The Shunzhi Emperor (15 March 1638 – 5 February 1661), personal name Fulin, was the second emperor of the Manchu-led Qing dynasty and the first Qing emperor to rule over China proper proper. He ascended the throne at the age of five in 1643 after the death of his father, Huang Taiji, with his uncle Dorgon serving as prince regent.

Reign

Under Dorgon's regency, Qing forces entered Beijing in 1644 after the fall of the Ming dynasty to Li Zicheng's rebels, and the young emperor was enthroned in the Forbidden City on 30 October 1644. The conquest of the south was prosecuted by Manchu banners and defected Ming generals, while the regency imposed the queue hairstyle on Han men — an order that provoked fierce resistance and massacres at Jiangyin and Jiading. After Dorgon's sudden death at the end of 1650, the thirteen-year-old emperor assumed personal rule, purged Dorgon's faction, and worked to curb corruption and eunuch and banner abuses, relying on Han Chinese officials and on the German Jesuit adviser Johann Adam Schall von Bell at court.

Personal life and death

Shunzhi was deeply interested in Chinese culture and Chan Buddhism, and was devoted to his consort Donggo, whose death in 1660 devastated him. He died in February 1661, aged 22, traditionally of smallpox. A persistent legend holds that he instead abdicated and became a Buddhist monk, but historians accept his death. He was succeeded by his third son, Xuanye, who reigned as the Kangxi Emperor; Shunzhi was buried in the Xiaoling Mausoleum of the Eastern Qing tombs.
