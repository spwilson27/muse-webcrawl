Interactive voice response

Interactive voice response (IVR) is a technology that allows telephone callers to interact with a computer-operated phone system through voice commands and touch-tone keypad inputs, retrieving information or routing calls without a human operator.

Applications

IVR systems are widely used in banking, customer service, order-taking, call routing and telephone voting. Modern systems combine speech recognition and text-to-speech, and standards such as VoiceXML define how voice applications are written.

Criticism

Poorly designed IVR menus are a frequent source of customer frustration, and many jurisdictions have considered rules requiring easy access to a human agent. Conversational systems based on natural language processing increasingly supplement or replace rigid menu trees.
