Protected areas of Tanzania

The protected areas of Tanzania are extremely varied and among the most extensive in Africa: when national parks, game reserves, game controlled areas, the Ngorongoro Conservation Area and forest reserves are included, roughly a third of Tanzania's territory is under some form of protection.

National parks

Tanzania National Parks (TANAPA) manages more than twenty national parks. The most famous is Serengeti National Park, a World Heritage Site renowned for the annual migration of about 1.5 million wildebeest. Others include Kilimanjaro, Arusha, Tarangire, Lake Manyara, Ruaha (the largest), Gombe Stream (site of Jane Goodall's chimpanzee research), Katavi and Nyerere National Park, created in 2019 from the northern part of the Selous Game Reserve.

Ngorongoro Conservation Area

The Ngorongoro Conservation Area is a multiple-use area where Maasai pastoralists coexist with wildlife. Its centrepiece, the Ngorongoro Crater, is the world's largest inactive, intact and unfilled volcanic caldera and supports dense populations of lions, elephants, hippos and black rhinos.

Game reserves

Game reserves such as the Selous (one of Africa's largest faunal reserves) and Moyowosi are managed by the Tanzania Wildlife Management Authority and allow regulated hunting in some blocks, unlike national parks, where only photographic tourism is permitted.
