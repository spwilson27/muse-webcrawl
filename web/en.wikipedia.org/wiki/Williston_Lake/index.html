Williston Lake

TypeReservoir

Williston Lake is a reservoir in northern British Columbia, Canada, created by the construction of the W. A. C. Bennett Dam on the Peace River in 1968. With a surface area of roughly 1,773 square kilometres, it is the largest lake in British Columbia and one of the largest reservoirs in Canada.

Geography

The lake occupies the Rocky Mountain Trench and has three arms: the Peace Arm in the south, and the Parsnip and Finlay arms to the northwest. Its principal inflows are the Finlay, Omineca, Ingenika, Ospika, Parsnip, Manson, Nation and Nabesche rivers, and it drains eastward through the Peace River toward the Mackenzie River system.

History

The reservoir is named after Hubert Gardiner Williston, a provincial cabinet minister involved in the development of the Peace River power project. Filling the reservoir flooded a vast area of forest and displaced communities, including members of the Tsay Keh Dene and Kwadacha First Nations, whose lands and gravesites were inundated with little consultation — an enduring grievance that later led to compensation settlements.

Use and ecology

The lake stores water for the W. A. C. Bennett Dam's generating station, one of the largest hydroelectric facilities in Canada. The shoreline is largely undeveloped wilderness; the communities of Mackenzie and Hudson's Hope lie near its shores. The reservoir supports fisheries for species including bull trout, rainbow trout, kokanee and lake whitefish, though mercury levels and fluctuating water levels remain management concerns.
