FAFSA

The Free Application for Federal Student Aid is filed online at studentaid.gov.

The Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) is a form completed by current and prospective college students (undergraduate and graduate) in the United States to determine their eligibility for student financial aid. The FAFSA is administered by Federal Student Aid, an office of the U.S. Department of Education. Students submit information about their family's income, assets, and household, which is used to calculate a Student Aid Index (SAI), formerly the Expected Family Contribution (EFC).

Eligibility

Most students enrolled at eligible institutions who are U.S. citizens or eligible noncitizens can receive some form of federal aid. Results from the FAFSA determine eligibility for federal grants such as the Pell Grant, federal student loans (including Direct Subsidized and Unsubsidized Loans), and Federal Work-Study. Many states and colleges also use FAFSA data to award their own aid.

Application process

The application opens each year, traditionally on October 1, for the following academic year, and is usually submitted online. Applicants may authorize transfer of tax information directly from the Internal Revenue Service. Colleges listed on the form receive the results, called the Institutional Student Information Record, and prepare financial aid offers.

2024 simplification

The FAFSA Simplification Act produced a substantially redesigned form for the 2024–25 cycle, reducing the number of questions, replacing the Expected Family Contribution with the Student Aid Index, and expanding Pell Grant eligibility. The rollout was delayed from October to late December 2023 and was widely reported as troubled, with processing errors and delays affecting colleges and students.
