František Palacký

Born14 June 1798, Hodslavice, Moravia
Died26 May 1876, Prague, Bohemia
OccupationHistorian, politician

František Palacký (14 June 1798 – 26 May 1876) was a Czech historian and politician, the most influential person of the Czech National Revival. He is often called the "Father of the Nation" (Otec národa).

Early life

Palacký was born in Hodslavice in Moravia into a Protestant family. He studied at Bratislava (Pressburg) and worked as a tutor before moving to Prague in 1823, where he became involved in the circle of Josef Dobrovský and other Czech patriots. In 1827 he married Terezie Měchurová, daughter of a wealthy Prague lawyer, which gave him financial independence for his scholarly work.

Historian

Palacký's monumental work is the History of the Czech Nation in Bohemia and Moravia (Dějiny národu českého v Čechách a v Moravě), published in five volumes between 1836 and 1867. Drawing on archival research across Europe, it interpreted Czech history as a continual contact and conflict between Slavdom and Germanic peoples, and placed the Hussite movement at its moral centre. The history provided the Czech national movement with a powerful historical narrative and remains a foundational text of Czech historiography.

Politician

In the revolutionary year 1848 Palacký presided over the Slav Congress in Prague and declined an invitation to join the Frankfurt Parliament with a famous letter arguing that the Austrian Empire, if it did not exist, would have to be created in the interest of Europe — the basis of his programme of Austroslavism. He later sat in the Bohemian Diet and the Austrian Reichsrat, leading the Old Czech Party and advocating federalisation of the empire and Czech state rights. His political influence waned after the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867 excluded the Czechs; he famously remarked that "we were here before Austria, and we will be here after it."
