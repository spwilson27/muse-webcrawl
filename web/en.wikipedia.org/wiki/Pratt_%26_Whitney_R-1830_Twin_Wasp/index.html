Pratt & Whitney R-1830 Twin Wasp

The Pratt & Whitney R-1830 Twin Wasp is an American air-cooled radial piston engine with 14 cylinders arranged in two rows. Displacing 1,830 cubic inches (30 litres), it was among the most widely produced aircraft engines of all time, with about 173,000 built.

History

Introduced in the 1930s, the Twin Wasp powered many Second World War aircraft, including the B-24 Liberator, F4F Wildcat, Douglas DC-3/C-47 (in some versions) and the PBY Catalina. Licensed production took place in several countries.

Characteristics

Typical versions produced around 1,200 horsepower. Its reliability and wide availability made it a mainstay of both military and early commercial aviation.
