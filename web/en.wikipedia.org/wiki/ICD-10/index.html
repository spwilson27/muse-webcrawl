ICD-10 is the 10th revision of the International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems (ICD), a medical classification list maintained by the World Health Organization (WHO). It contains codes for diseases, signs and symptoms, abnormal findings, complaints, social circumstances, and external causes of injury or diseases.

Work on ICD-10 began in 1983, was endorsed by the Forty-third World Health Assembly in 1990, and came into use in WHO member states from 1994. The classification provides roughly 14,000–16,000 codes in the base WHO version, far more granular than ICD-9, and national modifications such as the American ICD-10-CM expand it to some 70,000 codes. The United States adopted ICD-10-CM for diagnosis coding on 1 October 2015.

Structure

Codes are alphanumeric: a letter followed by two digits forms a category (for example J45, asthma), with optional decimal extensions for increasing specificity. The 22 chapters group conditions by aetiology or body system, from Chapter I (Certain infectious and parasitic diseases, A00–B99) through Chapter XXI (Factors influencing health status, Z00–Z99), plus special-purpose codes in Chapter XXII (U codes).

Versions and successors

Many countries produced their own modifications, including ICD-10-CM and ICD-10-PCS in the US, ICD-10-CA in Canada and ICD-10-AM in Australia. The WHO released ICD-11 in 2018, and it came into effect on 1 January 2022, although ICD-10 remains in widespread use for billing and statistics worldwide.
