Halo: Combat Evolved

Halo: Combat Evolved is a 2001 first-person shooter video game developed by Bungie and published by Microsoft Game Studios. Released as a launch title for the Xbox console on November 15, 2001, it is the first instalment of the Halo franchise. Ports for Windows and Mac OS X followed in 2003.

Gameplay and story

Players assume the role of Master Chief, a cybernetically enhanced supersoldier accompanied by the artificial intelligence Cortana. After the starship Pillar of Autumn flees the fall of Reach, it discovers a mysterious ringworld — Halo — contested by the alien Covenant. The game popularised many conventions of console shooters: a two-weapon limit, regenerating health via an energy shield, dedicated melee and grenade buttons, and drivable vehicles. Its story unfolds as the Chief learns that Halo is a superweapon built by the Forerunners to contain the parasitic Flood.

Reception and legacy

Halo was a runaway success, selling over five million copies on Xbox and receiving widespread acclaim for its gameplay, multiplayer, and music by Martin O'Donnell and Michael Salvatori. It is credited with establishing the Xbox as a viable platform and with proving the first-person shooter could thrive on consoles. A remake, Halo: Combat Evolved Anniversary, was released in 2011, and the original was included in Halo: The Master Chief Collection (2014).
