The plains viscacha (Lagostomus maximus) is a species of viscacha, a rodent in the family Chinchillidae. It is the only species in the genus Lagostomus. It is found in Argentina, Bolivia, and Paraguay where it lives in grassland and is noted for its extensive communal burrow systems, called vizcacheras.

Description

The plains viscacha is a large rodent, with a head-and-body length of roughly 47 to 66 cm and a weight of up to about 8 kg. It has coarse fur that is grey or brown above and paler below, a blunt face with a distinctive black-and-white striped pattern across the muzzle, and strong hind legs adapted for digging. Males are considerably larger than females.

Behaviour

Plains viscachas are highly social, living in colonies that excavate large communal burrow systems used by many generations. They emerge at dusk to feed on grasses and other low vegetation. Colonies can significantly alter grassland vegetation and soil, and the animals have historically been persecuted as agricultural pests.

Relationship with other species

Burrowing owls and other animals frequently make use of viscacha burrow systems. The plains viscacha is a prey species for pumas, foxes, and birds of prey, and it is also hunted by humans for its meat and fur.
