Indomalayan realm

The Indomalayan realm is one of the eight biogeographic realms recognised in the WWF system. Formerly known as the Oriental realm, it extends across South and Southeast Asia, from Afghanistan and the Indian subcontinent through southern China to Indonesia and the Philippines.

Extent and habitats

The realm covers most of South and Southeast Asia east of the Indus and south of the Himalayas, including the Greater Sunda Islands but excluding New Guinea, which belongs to the Oceanian (Australasian) realm; the transition between the two follows the Wallace Line. Habitats range from tropical and subtropical moist broadleaf forests and mangroves to dry forests, grasslands and montane forests.

Fauna

Characteristic animals include the Indian rhinoceros, Asian elephant, Bengal tiger, orangutans, gibbons, peafowl and hornbills. The realm has high endemism, with distinctive families such as the leafbirds and fairy-bluebirds. Its forests are among the most biodiverse — and most threatened — on Earth.
