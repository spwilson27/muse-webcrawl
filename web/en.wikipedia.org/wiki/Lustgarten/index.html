The Lustgarten (German for "Pleasure Garden") is a park in central Berlin, on Museum Island in the Mitte district, in front of the Altes Museum and Berlin Cathedral. Originally laid out in the 16th century as the kitchen garden of the Berlin Palace (Stadtschloss), it was transformed in 1646 under the Great Elector Frederick William into a formal pleasure garden — at one point famous for the first potatoes and tomatoes grown in Brandenburg, displayed as curiosities.

History

In the 18th and 19th centuries the garden was progressively converted into a parade ground and civic square. Karl Friedrich Schinkel redesigned it in 1826–1829 in connection with his Altes Museum, adding the great granite bowl (Granitschale) in front of the museum in 1831. Under the Nazi regime the Lustgarten was paved over as a rally ground for mass demonstrations; in East Berlin it formed part of Marx-Engels-Platz beside the Palace of the Republic. After German reunification it was restored as a lawn park, following Schinkel's designs, and reopened in 1999.
