Grands Projets of François Mitterrand

The Grands Projets of François Mitterrand (also known as the Grands Travaux) were an ambitious programme of monumental public building projects undertaken in Paris during the presidency of François Mitterrand (1981–1995). Intended to reshape the capital for the bicentennial of the French Revolution and beyond, they became a defining legacy of his presidency.

Major projects

The most famous of the projects include the Louvre Pyramid designed by I. M. Pei; the Grande Arche de la Défense by Johann Otto von Spreckelsen; the Opéra Bastille; the Bibliothèque nationale de France (François Mitterrand Library); the Institut du Monde Arabe; the Parc de la Villette with its Cité des Sciences; the Ministry of Finance building at Bercy; and the Musée d'Orsay conversion across the river from the Louvre.

Reception

The projects were controversial for their cost and monumental style, earning Mitterrand the nickname "Tontonkhamun" in the press, but many are now regarded as landmark works of late 20th-century French architecture.
