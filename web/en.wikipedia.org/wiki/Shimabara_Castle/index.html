Shimabara Castle

LocationShimabara, Nagasaki, Japan
Built1618–1624
BuilderMatsukura Shigemasa
Demolished1871 (keep rebuilt 1964)

Shimabara Castle (島原城, Shimabara-jō), also known as Moritake Castle or Takaki Castle, is a Japanese castle in Shimabara, Nagasaki Prefecture. The five-storey white-walled castle, on a flat site near the Ariake Sea, was built by the daimyō Matsukura Shigemasa between 1618 and 1624 — an unusually grand fortress for a lord of its builder's modest rank.

History

The castle's construction imposed heavy taxes and forced labour on the local peasantry, in a domain where many were Christians subject to persecution under Matsukura Shigemasa and his son Katsuie. These grievances — crushing taxation and religious persecution — erupted in the Shimabara Rebellion of 1637–38, led by the teenage Amakusa Shirō, one of the largest revolts of the Edo period. After the rebellion was suppressed, the Matsukura were removed and the domain given to the Kōriki and later Fukōzu Matsudaira families.

The castle survived until the Meiji Restoration, when its buildings were dismantled in 1871. The five-storey main keep was reconstructed in reinforced concrete in 1964 and now houses a museum, with exhibits on Christian artefacts of the region, the Shimabara Rebellion, and local history. Several yagura turrets have also been rebuilt, and the site preserves long stone walls and moats unusual for the scale of the domain.
