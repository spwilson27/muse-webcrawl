Order of Saint Augustine

Order of Saint Augustine (OSA)
Founded: 1244
Type: Mendicant religious order (Catholic)
Rule: Rule of St Augustine
Motto: Anima una et cor unum in Deum

The Order of Saint Augustine (Latin: Ordo Fratrum Sancti Augustini, OSA), historically known as the Austin Friars, is a mendicant religious order of the Catholic Church. It was founded in 1244 when Pope Innocent IV united several groups of hermits in Italy under the Rule of St Augustine, and it spread rapidly across Europe as an order devoted to preaching, education and pastoral ministry.

History

The Augustinians became one of the great mendicant orders of the later Middle Ages, running universities and schools. Martin Luther was an Augustinian friar before the Reformation. The order was also prominent in overseas missions, including the early evangelisation of the Philippines, where Augustinians arrived in 1565.

Notable members

Famous Augustinians include Gregor Mendel, the founder of genetics, and Pope Leo XIV, elected in 2025, the first pope from the order. The order today is present in dozens of countries, engaged in parishes, schools and universities.
