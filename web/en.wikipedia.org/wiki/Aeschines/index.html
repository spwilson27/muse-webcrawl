Aeschines

Aeschines (Greek: Αἰσχίνης; 389–314 BCE) was a Greek statesman and orator of Athens, one of the ten Attic orators included in the Alexandrian canon. He was the great political rival of Demosthenes, advocating accommodation with Philip II of Macedon while Demosthenes urged resistance.

Aeschines
Born389 BCE, Athens
Died314 BCE, Samos
OccupationOrator, statesman

Career

Of humble origins — he worked as an actor and clerk — Aeschines rose through embassies to Philip, including the Peace of Philocrates (346 BCE). His feud with Demosthenes produced masterpiece speeches on both sides: Aeschines' Against Timarchus and On the Embassy, and his final contest, Against Ctesiphon (330 BCE), answered by Demosthenes' On the Crown. Losing decisively, Aeschines left Athens and taught rhetoric in Rhodes and Samos.
