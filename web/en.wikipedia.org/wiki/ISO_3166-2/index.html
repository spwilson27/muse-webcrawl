ISO 3166-2

ISO 3166-2 is part of the ISO 3166 standard, published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), which defines codes for the names of the principal subdivisions — such as provinces, states, regions, and departments — of all countries coded in ISO 3166-1. First published in 1998, ISO 3166-2 currently defines codes for subdivisions of over 240 countries and territories, comprising several thousand entries.

Format

An ISO 3166-2 code consists of two parts separated by a hyphen. The first part is the ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country code; the second part is a string of up to three alphanumeric characters, usually based on a national system of subdivision codes or names. For example, "US-CA" denotes California within the United States, and "FR-75" has denoted Paris within France.

Examples

  • GB-ENG — England (United Kingdom)
  • DE-BY — Bavaria (Germany)
  • JP-13 — Tokyo (Japan)
  • CN-BJ — Beijing (China)
  • AU-NSW — New South Wales (Australia)
  • CA-QC — Quebec (Canada)
  • IN-MH — Maharashtra (India)
  • BR-SP — São Paulo (Brazil)

Changes and maintenance

The ISO 3166 Maintenance Agency updates ISO 3166-2 when countries create, abolish, merge, or rename subdivisions. Changes were formerly announced in newsletters and are now published through ISO's Online Browsing Platform, with editions of the standard appearing in 2007, 2013, and 2020. Subdivision entries include the code, the subdivision name in the country's official language(s) and romanisations, the subdivision category (state, province, region, etc.), and the parent subdivision where applicable.

Related standards

ISO 3166-1 defines country codes (alpha-2, alpha-3, and numeric), while ISO 3166-3 lists codes for formerly used country names. Subdivision codes are used in postal addressing, shipping, statistics, software localisation, and in standards such as UN/LOCODE and various gazetteers.
