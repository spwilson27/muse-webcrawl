Dutch football league system

The Dutch football league system is the system of interconnected football leagues in the Netherlands, administered by the Royal Dutch Football Association (KNVB). It is arranged as a pyramid with promotion and relegation between levels, although movement between the professional and amateur sections was historically restricted and only fully opened in reforms of the 2010s.

Professional levels

The Eredivisie, founded in 1956, is the top division, contested by 18 clubs; the bottom club is relegated to the Eerste Divisie and others enter relegation play-offs. The Eerste Divisie (also called the Keuken Kampioen Divisie for sponsorship reasons) is the second tier and includes a number of reserve ("Jong") teams of Eredivisie clubs, which are ineligible for promotion.

Semi-professional and amateur levels

The Tweede Divisie, reintroduced in 2016, forms the third tier and the bridge between professional and amateur football, mixing semi-professional clubs with under-23 reserve sides. Below it, the Derde and Vierde Divisie and the regional Klassen are organised on Saturday and Sunday lines reflecting the Netherlands' traditional split between Saturday (often religious) and Sunday amateur football clubs.

Cup competition

Clubs from across the pyramid compete in the KNVB Cup, the country's main knockout competition, whose winner qualifies for European competition.
