Southampton F.C.

Southampton Football Club is an English professional football club based in Southampton, Hampshire. Founded in 1885 as St. Mary's Young Men's Association F.C., the club is nicknamed "The Saints" in reference to its origins as a church team. Southampton play their home matches at St Mary's Stadium, their home since 2001, having previously played at The Dell. Their colours are red and white stripes.

History

Southampton joined the Southern League in 1894 and reached the FA Cup finals of 1900 and 1902. They entered the Football League in 1920 and gained promotion to the First Division in 1966 under Ted Bates. The club's greatest triumph came in 1976, when, as a Second Division side managed by Lawrie McMenemy, they defeated Manchester United 1–0 in the FA Cup Final — Bobby Stokes scoring the winner — one of the competition's most famous upsets.

Southampton were founding members of the Premier League in 1992 and enjoyed a celebrated spell under Gordon Strachan in the early 2000s. Relegated in 2005 and again into League One in 2009 amid financial crisis, the club was rescued by Markus Liebherr and climbed back to the Premier League by 2012, reaching the 2017 EFL Cup Final and qualifying for the UEFA Europa League. The club is renowned for its academy, which produced players such as Gareth Bale, Theo Walcott, Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain, Luke Shaw and Adam Lallana.

Rivalries

The club's main rivals are Portsmouth, with whom they contest the South Coast derby.
