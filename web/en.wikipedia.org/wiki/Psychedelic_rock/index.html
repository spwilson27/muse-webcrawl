Psychedelic rock is a rock music genre that is inspired, influenced, or representative of psychedelic culture, which is centred on perception-altering hallucinogenic drugs such as LSD. The music incorporated new electronic sound effects, extended instrumental solos and improvisation, and drew on non-Western sources such as the ragas and drones of Indian music.

History

The genre emerged in the mid-1960s in the United States and Britain. In California, bands such as the Jefferson Airplane, the Grateful Dead and the Doors developed the style around the San Francisco scene and its acid-test parties, while Texas band the 13th Floor Elevators were among the first to use the word "psychedelic" on an album (1966). In Britain, Pink Floyd, the Beatles and others pushed studio experimentation; the Beatles' Revolver (1966) and Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band (1967) carried psychedelic techniques to a mass audience.

Landmark recordings include the Jimi Hendrix Experience's Are You Experienced (1967), the Doors' debut album (1967), and Pink Floyd's The Piper at the Gates of Dawn (1967). The style peaked around the 1967 "Summer of Love" and the Woodstock festival of 1969.

Characteristics

Musical hallmarks include modal melodies, drones, unusual timbres (sitar, Mellotron, Farfisa organ), heavy use of studio effects such as tape echo, phasing, reversed tapes and panning, extended jam-based structures, and lyrics evoking altered states, mysticism and surreal imagery. Album artwork and light shows formed a distinctive visual culture.

Decline and legacy

By the early 1970s many acts moved toward progressive rock, hard rock or country rock, but psychedelic rock profoundly influenced later genres including krautrock, space rock, stoner rock, shoegaze and the neo-psychedelia of bands such as Tame Impala and the Flaming Lips.
