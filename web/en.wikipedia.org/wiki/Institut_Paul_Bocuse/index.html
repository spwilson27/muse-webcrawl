Institut Paul Bocuse

Institut Paul Bocuse (now also known as Institut Lyfe) is a French school of culinary arts, hospitality management, and pastry located in Écully, near Lyon, France. It was founded in 1990 by chef Paul Bocuse, the emblematic figure of nouvelle cuisine, together with Gérard Pélisson, co-founder of the Accor hotel group. The institute trains several hundred students from dozens of countries in cooking, pastry, service, and hotel and restaurant management.

Programmes

The institute offers bachelor's and master's programmes in culinary arts, pastry arts, and international hospitality and restaurant management, taught in French and English. Teaching combines academic study with extensive practical work in training restaurants, including a gastronomic application restaurant, allowing students to practise cooking and service for real guests. A research centre attached to the school studies food, eating behaviour, and the culinary professions.

Setting and reputation

The main campus centres on the Château du Vivier, a nineteenth-century manor in Écully, supplemented by modern buildings on the site. Located near Lyon, long regarded as France's gastronomic capital, the school draws on a network of renowned chefs and partner institutions. Its alumni include chefs and hospitality executives working internationally. In 2023 the school adopted the additional name Institut Lyfe, reflecting its links with the Lyfe foundation that supports it, while retaining the Paul Bocuse name.
