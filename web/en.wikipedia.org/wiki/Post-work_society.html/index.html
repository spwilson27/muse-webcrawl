Post-work society

A post-work society is a society in which the centrality of paid employment in people's lives is greatly reduced, typically through automation and the provision of income or services independent of work, such as a universal basic income.

Ideas

Post-work theorists argue that automation and artificial intelligence can free people from necessary labour, and criticise the work ethic and the cultural expectation of full employment. Writers associated with the idea include André Gorz, Kathi Weeks, Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, whose Inventing the Future (2015) popularised the demand for full automation and a basic income.

Criticism

Critics question the feasibility of decoupling income from work, the pace of automation, and whether identity and social cohesion can be sustained without employment.
