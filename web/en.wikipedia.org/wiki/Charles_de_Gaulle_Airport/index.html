Charles de Gaulle Airport (IATA: CDG, ICAO: LFPG), also known as Roissy Airport, is the main international airport serving Paris, the capital of France. It is France's largest airport and one of the busiest in Europe, serving as the principal hub of Air France and a major cargo centre. The airport is named after French statesman Charles de Gaulle (1890–1970), leader of the Free French Forces and president of the Fifth Republic.

Terminals

The airport has three terminals. Terminal 1, designed by Paul Andreu and opened in 1974, is famous for its circular concrete design with a central courtyard and satellite boarding piers connected by moving walkways. Terminal 2 consists of a complex of halls (2A–2G) built from the 1980s onward, largely for Air France and SkyTeam partners; part of its Terminal 2E roof collapsed in May 2004 shortly after opening, killing four people, and was rebuilt. Terminal 3 serves charter and low-cost flights.

Transport links

The airport is served by the RER B suburban railway, TGV high-speed trains from its own station, long-distance buses and the A1 motorway. It lies within the communes of Roissy-en-France and neighbouring municipalities across the Val-d'Oise, Seine-Saint-Denis and Seine-et-Marne departments.

Statistics and role

Before the COVID-19 pandemic the airport handled more than 76 million passengers in 2019. It is one of the world's top ten busiest airports and a principal European connecting hub, and hosts one of FedEx's largest air hubs outside the United States.
