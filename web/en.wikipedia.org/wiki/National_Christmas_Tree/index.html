National Christmas Tree

LocationThe Ellipse, President's Park, Washington, D.C.
First lighting1923
First lit byPresident Calvin Coolidge
OrganiserNational Park Service

The National Christmas Tree is a large evergreen tree located in the northeast quadrant of the Ellipse, in President's Park near the White House in Washington, D.C. Each year since 1923, the tree has been decorated and lit in a ceremony in early December, traditionally by the President of the United States, as the centrepiece of the "Christmas Pageant of Peace".

History

The tradition began in 1923, when President Calvin Coolidge lit a 48-foot balsam fir decorated with more than 2,500 electric bulbs before a crowd of several thousand. For most of the twentieth century a cut tree was brought in each year; in 1973 a living Colorado blue spruce from Pennsylvania was planted on the Ellipse to serve permanently, though storm damage and disease have forced several replacements since. The current living tree was planted in 2021.

Pageant of Peace

Surrounding the national tree is the "Pathway of Peace", lined with smaller trees representing every U.S. state and territory, each decorated with ornaments made in that state. The Pageant of Peace includes nightly musical performances, a Nativity scene, Santa's workshop and a large model railway. During the Second World War the tree went unlit for security and energy reasons, and in 1979 it was fully lit only in honour of the American hostages in Iran — otherwise topped with a single light — before returning to full lighting in later years.
