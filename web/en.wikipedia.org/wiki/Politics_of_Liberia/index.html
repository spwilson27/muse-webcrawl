Politics of Liberia

The Capitol Building, Monrovia

The politics of Liberia take place in a framework of a presidential republic modelled closely on that of the United States, with a president as head of state and government, a bicameral legislature (the Senate and House of Representatives), and an independent judiciary. Liberia was founded by freed American slaves in the nineteenth century, and its 1986 constitution restored civilian rule after a period of military government.

History

From 1878 to 1980 Liberia was effectively a one-party state under the True Whig Party, dominated by Americo-Liberian settlers. Master Sergeant Samuel Doe seized power in a 1980 coup, ending that order. Civil wars from 1989 to 2003, involving warlord Charles Taylor—who was elected president in 1997 before being forced from power—killed an estimated 250,000 people and devastated the country. A UN peacekeeping mission, UNMIL, oversaw disarmament and reconstruction until 2018.

Recent politics

Ellen Johnson Sirleaf, elected in 2005 and re-elected in 2011, became Africa's first elected female head of state and oversaw post-war recovery and the response to the 2014–2016 Ebola epidemic. Former footballer George Weah won the 2017 election. In 2023 Joseph Boakai defeated Weah in a close run-off, and the peaceful transfer of power reinforced Liberia's reputation for competitive elections despite weak institutions, corruption, and severe poverty. The president serves a six-year term, renewable once.
