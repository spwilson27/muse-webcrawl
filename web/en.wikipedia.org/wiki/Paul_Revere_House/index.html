The Paul Revere House, built about 1680, was the colonial home of the American patriot Paul Revere during the time of the American Revolution. A National Historic Landmark, it is located at 19 North Square in Boston's North End and now operates as a museum operated by the Paul Revere Memorial Association. It is the oldest house still in existence in downtown Boston.

History

The three-storey timber-frame house was built around 1680 on the site of the parsonage of the Second Church, which had burned in the Great Fire of 1676. Its first owner was the wealthy merchant John Jeffs. The house is a rare surviving example of 17th-century urban architecture in Boston; its overhanging second storey and casement windows are characteristic of early New England building.

Paul Revere owned the house from 1770 to 1800. He lived there with his family when he set out on his famous "midnight ride" on 18 April 1775 to warn of the approaching British troops before the Battles of Lexington and Concord. After Revere sold it, the building became a tenement and housed shops at street level, including a candy store, a cigar factory, a bank, and a produce shop.

Restoration and museum

In 1902, Revere's great-grandson John P. Reynolds Jr. purchased the house to prevent its demolition. Funds were raised to restore it, and architect Joseph Everett Chandler returned it to its presumed colonial appearance, removing later additions. The house opened to the public on 18 April 1908, one of the earliest historic house museums in the United States. Today it is a stop on Boston's Freedom Trail and is furnished to reflect the Revere family's occupancy.
