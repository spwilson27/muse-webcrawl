Furnace

A furnace is a device used for high-temperature heating. The name derives from Latin fornax, meaning oven. Furnaces are used for applications including smelting and refining metals, firing ceramics, generating steam, and heating buildings. Industrial furnaces include blast furnaces for ironmaking, arc furnaces for steelmaking, and muffle furnaces for laboratory work.

In North American usage, a furnace is specifically a central heating appliance that heats air distributed through ducts, as distinct from a boiler that heats water. Domestic furnaces may burn natural gas, oil, or use electricity. Efficiency is measured by the annual fuel utilization efficiency (AFUE) rating; modern condensing gas furnaces exceed 90%.
