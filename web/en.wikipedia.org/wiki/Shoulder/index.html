Shoulder
Latin: articulus humeri
Bones: Humerus, scapula, clavicle
Joints: Glenohumeral, acromioclavicular, sternoclavicular

The human shoulder is the region where the arm joins the torso. It comprises three bones — the clavicle (collarbone), scapula (shoulder blade), and humerus (upper arm bone) — together with associated muscles, ligaments, and tendons. The shoulder is the most mobile joint complex in the body, trading stability for an exceptional range of motion.

Structure

The main joint, the glenohumeral joint, is a ball-and-socket joint between the head of the humerus and the shallow glenoid cavity of the scapula. The acromioclavicular and sternoclavicular joints link the scapula and clavicle to the skeleton. The rotator cuff — the tendons of the supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, and subscapularis muscles — centres the humeral head in the socket and powers rotation.

Movement

The shoulder permits flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, internal and external rotation, and circumduction. Coordinated motion of the glenohumeral joint and scapula (scapulohumeral rhythm) allows the arm to be raised overhead.

Clinical significance

Because of its shallow socket, the shoulder is the most commonly dislocated major joint. Other common problems include rotator cuff tears, impingement syndrome, adhesive capsulitis ("frozen shoulder"), and fractures of the clavicle.
