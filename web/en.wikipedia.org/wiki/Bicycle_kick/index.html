Bicycle kick

A bicycle kick, also known as an overhead kick or scissors kick, is a move in association football in which a player kicks the ball while both feet are off the ground and the body is airborne and horizontal or inverted, striking the ball backwards over their own head. It is one of the most spectacular techniques in the sport and is used both to shoot at goal and to clear the ball defensively.

The bicycle kick: striking the ball backwards while airborne.

Execution

The player throws their body backwards, raising the non-kicking leg first to gain height, then swings the kicking leg over in a pedalling motion resembling cycling — hence the name. Timing and body control are essential; a mistimed attempt risks a heavy fall or dangerous play.

History

The move's origins are disputed among several South American claimants. In Brazil it is called the bicicleta and is associated with Leônidas da Silva in the 1930s; in Chile and Peru similar claims attach to players such as Ramón Unzaga and to the chalaca tradition. Famous bicycle-kick goals include Pelé's in the 1968 film-starred era of Santos, Marco van Basten's volleys, Wayne Rooney's 2011 Manchester derby winner, and Gareth Bale's goal in the 2018 UEFA Champions League Final.

In other sports

Variations of the overhead kick appear in beach soccer, futsal and sepak takraw, where aerial acrobatic kicks are a central feature of play.
