Agricultural policy

Agricultural policy comprises the laws, regulations, and government programmes that govern domestic agriculture and trade in agricultural products. Its objectives typically include ensuring food security, stabilising farm incomes and food prices, supporting rural communities, promoting exports, and protecting the environment.

Instruments

Governments intervene in agriculture through price supports and deficiency payments, direct subsidies, crop insurance, import tariffs and quotas, marketing boards, research and extension funding, land and water regulation, and environmental schemes that pay farmers for conservation practices. International trade in farm goods is governed in part by the World Trade Organization's Agreement on Agriculture.

Major examples

The European Union's Common Agricultural Policy, established in 1962, has long been one of the EU's largest budget items, shifting over time from price support toward direct payments tied to environmental and rural-development goals. In the United States, policy is set principally through periodic farm bills covering commodity support, conservation, trade, nutrition, and rural development. Agricultural protectionism and subsidy levels remain contentious in global trade negotiations, particularly between developed and developing countries.
