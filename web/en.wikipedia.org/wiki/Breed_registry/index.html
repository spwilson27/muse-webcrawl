Breed registry

A pedigree recorded by a breed registry documents an animal's ancestry.

A breed registry, also known as a herdbook, studbook or register, is an official list of animals within a specific breed whose parents are known. Animals are usually registered by their breeders when they are still young, and the organisation that maintains the registry issues pedigrees and enforces breed standards. A registry is said to be "closed" if only offspring of registered parents may be entered, and "open" if animals meeting the standard may be admitted after inspection.

Functions

Breed registries preserve and improve purebred populations of livestock, horses, dogs and cats. They record pedigrees, sanction shows, maintain performance and health records, and set the breed standard against which animals are judged. Famous examples include the General Stud Book for Thoroughbred horses, first published in England in 1791, national kennel clubs such as The Kennel Club and the American Kennel Club for dogs, and herdbooks for cattle breeds such as the Hereford and Holstein.
