Jesus Seminar

Founded: 1985
Founder: Robert W. Funk
Sponsor: Westar Institute
Purpose: Historical Jesus research
Key publications: The Five Gospels (1993), The Acts of Jesus (1998)

The Jesus Seminar was a group of academic biblical scholars, founded in 1985 by New Testament scholar Robert W. Funk under the auspices of the Westar Institute, that sought to determine what the historical Jesus actually said and did. It became one of the most publicized — and controversial — enterprises of the modern "quest for the historical Jesus."

Method

The Seminar's roughly 150 participating scholars ("fellows") evaluated the sayings attributed to Jesus in the canonical gospels and the Gospel of Thomas, voting with colored beads: red for sayings Jesus "undoubtedly" uttered, pink for likely, gray for unlikely, and black for those judged inauthentic. They applied criteria of historical authenticity — including dissimilarity, multiple attestation, and coherence — together with assumptions about an oral tradition and a non-apocalyptic Jesus that critics frequently challenged.

Findings and publications

In The Five Gospels: The Search for the Authentic Words of Jesus (1993), the Seminar printed the gospels in its color-coded "Scholars Version" translation; it concluded that only about 18 percent of the sayings attributed to Jesus were likely authentic, and it did not regard the Gospel of John's sayings material as historical. A subsequent phase assessed Jesus' deeds in The Acts of Jesus (1998), finding few events, other than Jesus' baptism, his crucifixion under Pontius Pilate, and a small number of others, historically secure. The portrait that emerged — Jesus as an itinerant wisdom teacher and social provocateur rather than an apocalyptic prophet — was embraced by some scholars and the public, and sharply criticized by others, including Luke Timothy Johnson, N. T. Wright, and Dale Allison, who questioned its criteria, voting procedure, and presuppositions. After Funk's death in 2005 the Seminar continued in altered forms at the Westar Institute.
