Cao Pi (187 – 29 June 226), courtesy name Zihuan, was the first emperor of the state of Cao Wei in the Three Kingdoms period of China. He was the second son of Cao Cao, the warlord who had unified northern China under the nominal rule of the Han emperor. In 220, months after his father's death, Cao Pi forced Emperor Xian to abdicate and proclaimed himself emperor, ending the Han dynasty after four centuries.

Reign

Cao Pi adopted Chen Qun's nine-rank system for selecting officials, embedding aristocratic families in the bureaucracy. He launched three campaigns against Eastern Wu, all ultimately unsuccessful, and maintained tense relations with Shu Han. He also tightened control over imperial princes, a policy that weakened the dynasty's own clan.

Literary achievements

An accomplished writer, Cao Pi authored the Dianlun ("Authoritative Discourses"), whose chapter on literature is one of China's earliest works of literary criticism, and composed the celebrated poem cycle including the "Yan'ge xing" in the seven-syllable form. Together with his father and his brother Cao Zhi, he formed the literary circle known as the "Three Caos."
