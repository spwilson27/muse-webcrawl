Lough Mask

Lough Mask (Irish: Loch Measca) is a limestone lake in County Mayo and County Galway in the west of Ireland, north of Lough Corrib, to which it drains underground. Covering about 83 km², it is one of Ireland's largest lakes and one of its most celebrated wild brown trout fisheries.

Geography

The lake is roughly 16 km long and up to 6 km wide, with a maximum depth of about 58 metres in its western basin. It is fed by the Keel Canal, the Cloon River and streams from the Partry Mountains, and drains through subterranean channels — a famous natural feature is the "Pigeon Hole" near Cong, where the underground river can be seen. Villages on its shores include Ballinrobe, Clonbur, Tourmakeady and Partry.

History

The lough's islands hold early Christian and medieval remains. In 1880 the shores of Lough Mask saw a landmark of the Land War: the boycott of Captain Charles Boycott, land agent at Lough Mask House — the episode that gave English the word "boycott". The 1882 "Lough Mask murders" of a bailiff and his grandson were another notorious incident of the period.

Fishing and wildlife

Lough Mask is internationally renowned for wet-fly fishing for wild brown trout, especially during the mayfly season, and also holds ferox trout, pike and perch. It hosted the World Cup Trout Fly Fishing Championship. The lake is designated a Special Protection Area for its breeding gulls and other birds, and a Special Area of Conservation for its limestone habitats.
