Adobe PageMaker

PageMaker was a desktop publishing application, first released in 1985 by the Aldus Corporation for the Apple Macintosh, that helped launch the desktop publishing revolution.

History

Created by Paul Brainerd, who coined "desktop publishing", PageMaker combined WYSIWYG page layout with the Macintosh's graphical interface and Apple's LaserWriter printer. Aldus was acquired by Adobe in 1994. PageMaker 7.0 (2001) was the final version; Adobe discontinued it in 2004 in favour of InDesign, introduced in 1999.

Features

PageMaker let users place text and graphics freely on pages, flow text between columns, and prepare documents for professional printing — capabilities that transformed newsletters, brochures and magazines production.
